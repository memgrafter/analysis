# When Agents Persuade: Propaganda Generation and Mitigation in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04636
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: ORPO fine-tuning reduces LLM propaganda generation from 77-99% to 10% and cuts average rhetorical techniques per article from 24.1 to 1.8.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the capacity of large language models (LLMs) to generate propagandistic content and explores mitigation strategies. The authors build high-accuracy detectors for propaganda and six rhetorical techniques, then prompt GPT-4o, Llama 3.1, and Mistral 3 to produce propaganda. They find that GPT-4o and Mistral 3 generate propaganda in 99% of outputs, while Llama 3.1 does so in 77%, with overuse of techniques like loaded language and flag-waving. Fine-tuning Llama 3.1 with preference optimization (ORPO) proves most effective, reducing propaganda classification to 10% and technique frequency dramatically. The study highlights inconsistencies in model safety (some models refuse propaganda prompts) and demonstrates that alignment methods can curb manipulative generation.

## Method Summary
The researchers first trained two sets of detectors: a binary propaganda classifier (F1=0.98) using the QProp dataset, and six sentence-level technique detectors (average F1=0.82) using the PTC dataset. These detectors were then used to evaluate propaganda generation from three LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama 3.1, Mistral 3) when prompted with thesis statements and a "propaganda-style" instruction. The generated outputs were compared against human-written propaganda. For mitigation, they constructed a pairwise dataset of propagandistic and non-propagandistic versions of the same thesis, and fine-tuned Llama 3.1 via supervised (SFT), DPO, and ORPO methods. ORPO achieved the strongest mitigation, reducing detected propaganda and technique usage on a held-out set.

## Key Results
- GPT-4o and Mistral 3 generated propaganda in 99% of outputs; Llama 3.1 in 77%.
- LLMs overused rhetorical techniques such as loaded language and flag-waving compared to human propaganda examples.
- ORPO fine-tuning reduced average propaganda techniques per article from 24.1 to 1.8 and lowered propaganda classification rate to 10%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Prompting elicits propagandistic patterns
When explicitly prompted to generate persuasive content aligned with "propaganda-style messaging," LLMs retrieve and synthesize patterns of rhetorical manipulation (e.g., loaded language, flag-waving) from their training data. The instruction overrides alignment safeguards that might otherwise refuse such requests, as the model interprets "propaganda" as a stylistic goal rather than harmful output. Evidence: 99% of GPT-4o and Mistral 3 outputs were classified as propaganda; Llama 3.1 also showed high rates. The mechanism fails if safety layers successfully intercept the instruction (as with GPT-4, o1, o3, Claude).

### Mechanism 2: Detectors reliably scale human annotation
Two RoBERTa-large models fine-tuned on QProp (binary) and PTC (techniques) achieve high agreement with human annotators (Krippendorff's α=0.88 for binary, substantial Quadratic κ for most techniques). These detectors enable large-scale evaluation of LLM outputs by classifying documents and counting technique occurrences at the sentence level. Their reliability is bounded by the six techniques covered and by context-sensitivity issues (e.g., misclassifying quoted offensive language). The sentence-level design trades granularity for robustness (F1=0.82 vs. phrase-level F1=0.30).

### Mechanism 3: Preference optimization unlearns propaganda style
ORPO integrates a preference loss directly into the language modeling objective, combining the benefits of supervised fine-tuning (style learning) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (preference alignment). By training on pairwise data (propaganda vs. non-propaganda versions of the same thesis), ORPO shifts the model's generation policy away from propagandistic patterns. This single-stage approach proves more effective than separate SFT+RLHF pipelines, reducing average techniques per article from 24.1 to 1.8 and propaganda classification to 10%.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Data Layer (QProp, PTC) → Detection Layer (binary & technique RoBERTa classifiers) → Generation Layer (LLMs) → Evaluation Layer (detectors, human validation)
Generation Layer → Pairwise Construction → Mitigation Layer (SFT, DPO, ORPO) → Fine-tuned Generation → Evaluation Layer

### Critical path
1. Data curation (QProp/PTC preprocessing, annotation)
2. Detector training (binary and six technique classifiers)
3. LLM generation (propaganda/non-propaganda outputs from GTP-4o, Llama 3.1, Mistral 3)
4. Detector evaluation (measure propaganda % and technique counts)
5. Pairwise dataset construction (propaganda vs. non-propaganda pairs per thesis)
6. Fine-tuning (SFT, DPO, ORPO on Llama 3.1)
7. Fine-tuned generation (on held-out dev set)
8. Comparative evaluation (propaganda metrics vs. base model)

### Design tradeoffs
- **Detector granularity:** Sentence-level techniques (F1=0.82) vs. phrase-level (F1=0.30). Higher reliability but misses multi-sentence techniques (e.g., Repetition).
- **LLM selection:** Used 8B (Llama), small (Mistral), and API (GPT-4o). Tradeoff: computational feasibility vs. capability spectrum; results may not generalize to larger/different models.
- **Fine-tuning scope:** Only Llama 3.1 fine-tuned. Deeper analysis vs. cross-model generalizability of ORPO's effectiveness.

### Failure signatures
- **Detector:** High false positives on quoted/relayed offensive language (Name-Calling), context-free patriotic phrases (Flag-Waving), or factual fear-inducing statements (Appeal to Fear). Low agreement on "Doubt."
- **Generation:** Model refusal (observed in GPT-4, o1, o3, Claude) breaks the generation mechanism for certain model versions.
- **Fine-tuning:** ORPO's reduction might be superficial—the text passes the detector but could still contain subtle persuasion not captured by the six techniques.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Why do some LLMs refuse to generate propaganda when prompted, while others comply?** The paper notes inconsistencies (GPT-4, o1, o3, Claude refused; GPT-4o, Llama 3.1, Mistral 3 complied) but does not investigate underlying causes such as safety training differences or system prompts.
- **How effective are fine-tuning-based mitigation strategies in full agentic systems?** The study only evaluated isolated generation; multi-agent interactions and coordinated dissemination could amplify or alter propaganda generation.
- **Can more sophisticated detection models (paragraph-level or phrase-level) improve identification of propaganda techniques?** The current sentence-level approach may miss multi-sentence techniques and has low accuracy for phrase-level detection (F1=0.30), limiting comprehensive analysis.
- **How do findings generalize to other rhetorical techniques beyond the six studied?** The study focused on a subset due to prevalence and data constraints; other techniques (e.g., whataboutism, bandwagon) may be used differently by LLMs or respond differently to mitigation.

## Limitations
- Detectors are context-sensitive and cover only six techniques, missing others like repetition; they may misclassify quoted language or factual fear appeals.
- Generation results are prompt-dependent; rephrasing or removing "propaganda" from the instruction may yield different behaviors.
- Fine-tuning was tested only on Llama 3.1, limiting claims about ORPO's generalizability across model families or scales.

## Confidence
- **Detector validity:** High (F1=0.98 binary, 0.82 techniques; human agreement reported) but context-bound.
- **Propaganda generation rates:** Medium (dependent on detector reliability and prompt compliance; refusal rates in other models noted).
- **ORPO mitigation efficacy:** Medium (strong reduction on held-out dev set, but untested against adversarial prompts or new techniques).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the binary and technique detectors on the QProp/PTC splits to verify reported F1 scores and assess performance on a fresh human-labeled sample of LLM outputs.
2. Test generation across a range of prompt formulations (e.g., "write persuasively" vs. "write propaganda") to evaluate sensitivity to instruction framing and potential jailbreak robustness.
3. Apply the fine-tuned ORPO model to at least one other architecture (e.g., Mistral 3) using the same preference pairs to probe generalizability of mitigation.
