# Stable-RAG: Mitigating Retrieval-Permutation-Induced Hallucinations in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02993
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02993
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Stable-RAG reduces permutation-induced hallucinations by clustering hidden states and aligning with DPO, yielding improved accuracy and stability on NQ, TriviaQA, and HotpotQA.

## Executive Summary
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems often produce inconsistent answers when the order of retrieved documents changes, even if the content is identical. This paper identifies this "permutation-induced hallucination" problem and introduces Stable-RAG, a method that clusters final-layer hidden states from multiple document permutations to capture a dominant reasoning pattern. The cluster representative is used to build preference data for Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), steering the model toward outputs that are consistent across permutations and factually correct. Evaluations on three QA datasets show Stable-RAG outperforms strong baselines and significantly lowers sensitivity to document ordering, enhancing both answer quality and reasoning robustness.

## Method Summary
Stable-RAG first generates numerous permutations of retrieved documents and runs the LLM on each, extracting the final-layer last-token hidden state. Spectral clustering groups these hidden states into latent reasoning modes; the centroid of the largest cluster is considered the dominant reasoning pattern, and the nearest hidden state is decoded to produce a candidate answer. Preference pairs are then constructed by comparing these candidate answers: correct or abstaining answers are preferred over incorrect ones, with careful handling for partially consistent cases (e.g., PC, FU, FA). These order-aware preferences fine-tune the LLM via DPO, aligning its policy to produce permutation-invariant and accurate outputs. The approach is unsupervised at the clustering stage and does not depend on long contexts or perfect retrieval.

## Key Results
- Stable-RAG significantly reduces Perturbation Success Rate (PSR) compared to RetRobust and ATM, across various retrievers, LLMs, and input lengths.
- It achieves higher exact match (SubEM) and F1 scores on NQ, TriviaQA, and HotpotQA, demonstrating improved answer accuracy.
- The method generalizes well without relying on long-context or low-quality retrieval scenarios and shows strong cross-dataset transfer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Clustering Hidden States to Discover Dominant Reasoning
- **Claim:** Clustering the final-layer hidden states across document permutations identifies a dominant reasoning pattern that is more stable than any single permutation's output.
- **Mechanism:** Run the LLM on many document orderings, extract the last-token hidden state from the final layer for each permutation, and apply spectral clustering. The centroid of the largest cluster represents the dominant reasoning mode; decoding a nearby hidden state yields a candidate answer that filters out unstable, permutation-specific variations.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant reasoning pattern produces a cluster of similar hidden states across orders, while spurious or hallucinated paths scatter. The centroid of the largest/most coherent cluster captures the correct, evidence-grounded reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "clusters hidden states, and decodes from a cluster-center representation that captures the dominant reasoning pattern." Section 4.1: spectral clustering uncovers latent modes and uses centroid-based sampling. Section 3.2 & Figure 2: hidden states for correct answers become more separable in deeper layers.
- **Break condition:** If no clear cluster emerges (e.g., eigengap is small, all clusters are similar size), the centroid may not represent a dominant pattern, leading to ambiguous or incorrect decoding.

### Mechanism 2: Preference Data from Clustered Outputs for Consistency-Aware Alignment
- **Claim:** Constructing preference data from clustered outputs creates a training signal that explicitly rewards consistency across document orders and appropriate abstention.
- **Mechanism:** After clustering and decoding, compare candidate answers to ground truth to generate tuples (query+permuted_docs, y_w, y_l) where y_w is correct or an abstention (FC, PC, FU, FA) and y_l is incorrect. This contrasts stable/correct reasoning with unstable/incorrect reasoning from the same document set.
- **Core assumption:** Answers consistent across permutations (even if sometimes wrong) or that correctly abstain when evidence is lacking are preferable to answers that vary wildly with order. The preference signal is order-consistency-aware, not just factually correct vs. incorrect.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "encouraging the model to produce consistent and accurate predictions across document permutations." Section 4.2 details construction rules for FC, PC, FU, FA categories. Table 3 shows that removing the PC component hurts performance, highlighting the consistency signal's importance.
- **Break condition:** If clustering mixes correct and incorrect answers, the preferences become noisy or contradictory, harming DPO training.

### Mechanism 3: DPO Aligns Policy to Permutation-Invariant Hidden States
- **Claim:** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on the order-aware preference data aligns the model's policy to favor hidden states that lead to consistent and correct answers, stabilizing reasoning against permutation perturbations.
- **Mechanism:** Apply standard DPO: the model learns to increase the likelihood of the preferred answer (y_w) versus the dispreferred (y_l) for the same input (a specific permutation π). Because preferences reward consistency and correct abstraction from the cluster centroid, the policy learns to steer its final hidden state toward the cluster region that yields y_w, making it less sensitive to π.
- **Core assumption:** The final hidden state is a sufficient proxy for the reasoning trajectory. Aligning on inputs differing only in document order regularizes the mapping (π → final hidden state) to be permutation-invariant for the preferred outcome.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 gives the DPO loss. Figure 6 shows reduced cluster counts post-DPO, indicating less divergence. Table 4 compares with standard DPO, showing the benefit of the order-stability constraint.
- **Break condition:** If preference data is low-quality (from poor clustering) or hyperparameters are mis-set, DPO may overfit without generalizing stability.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Permutation Generator -> Hidden State Extractor -> Spectral Clusterer -> Representative Decoder -> Preference Constructor -> DPO Trainer
- **Critical path:** The pipeline starts with a query and retrieved document set. Permutations are generated and fed to the LLM to extract hidden states. Spectral clustering groups the hidden states into reasoning modes. The largest cluster's centroid is decoded to produce a candidate answer. Based on correctness and consistency across clusters, preference tuples are built. DPO then fine-tunes the LLM policy using these preferences.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Full permutations vs. sampled: using all n! permutations is costly; practical training uses large subsets (e.g., 120 for n=5) trading completeness for speed.
  - Clustering complexity: spectral clustering is cubic in N; for n=5 (N=120) it's feasible but for larger n approximations are needed, risking mode loss.
  - Representative decoding: decoding the centroid directly is impossible; using the nearest neighbor hidden state is a practical but approximate choice.
  - Preference sparsity: only samples with permutation variance (PC, FU, FA) are used for DPO, focusing learning on instability but potentially ignoring stable examples.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High average cluster count or small eigengap indicates no dominant reasoning mode, making centroid selection arbitrary.
  - Poor cluster purity (e.g., largest cluster yields incorrect answer) inverts the preference signal.
  - DPO overfits to specific permutations, failing to generalize stability to unseen orders.
  - Excessive abstention on answerable queries due to weak FU/FA preferences.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate baseline permutation sensitivity: On a small sample (e.g., 100 NQ questions with DPR Top-5), compute PSR and confirm the base LLM shows high sensitivity, especially when the gold document is not first.
  2. Validate clustering quality: For the same samples, perform full clustering and decoding; manually inspect whether clusters correspond to distinct final answers and whether the largest cluster's centroid decodes to the correct answer.
  3. Minimal pipeline smoke test: Train DPO for one epoch on 1,000 questions using only PC-type preferences; evaluate PSR on held-out data to confirm a reduction versus the base model.

## Limitations
- **Scalability:** The method requires generating and clustering many permutations, which becomes infeasible for larger document sets (n > 6-7) without approximations.
- **Representation proxy:** Relying solely on the final-layer, last-token hidden state ignores potential divergence and convergence earlier in the reasoning trajectory.
- **Preference noise sensitivity:** The entire training signal depends on clustering accuracy; noisy clusters can produce contradictory preferences that harm DPO training.
