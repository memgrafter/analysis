# OmniVideo-R1: Reinforcing Audio-visual Reasoning with Query Intention and Modality Attention

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05847  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05847  
- **Reference count:** 33  
- **Primary result:** OmniVideo-R1 achieves 44.8% on OmniVideoBench, a 7.8% absolute improvement over its base model and surpasses larger models while preserving visual-only accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often struggle with audio-visual reasoning; in fact, adding audio can sometimes degrade visual performance. OmniVideo-R1 addresses this by introducing a two-stage reinforcement learning framework that first grounds query‑relevant audio‑visual cues via self‑supervision (Query‑Intensive grounding) and then enforces synergistic fusion through contrastive rewards (Modality‑Attentive fusion). Built on Qwen3‑Omni‑30B‑A3B and trained on 88,173 curated audio‑visual samples, the model attains substantial gains across multiple benchmarks—most notably 44.8% on OmniVideoBench (vs. 37.0% base)—and retains or even improves visual‑only results like Video‑MME (73.6% vs. 70.5%). Ablations confirm the contributions of the two stages.

## Method Summary
**Data preparation:** Raw videos from LLaVA‑Video and Video‑Vista undergo a three‑stage filtering: quality assessment with Gemini‑2.5‑Pro (scoring semantic value, audio relevance, question clarity, redundancy), heuristic filtering (redundancy score = 1, question clarity ≥ 0.8, composite score ≥ 0.7), and categorization with Qwen‑3‑32B to balance categories. This yields 88 173 samples for the Query‑Intensive (QI) stage and a high‑dependency subset of 12 887 samples for the Modality‑Attentive (MA) stage.

**Training procedure:** Stage 1 (QI) optimizes a reward `R_QI = r_format + r_ans + 0.5·(r_cons + r_comp)` that encourages accurate temporal grounding and caption consistency using a self‑supervised signal from Qwen3‑VL. Stage 2 (MA) continues from the QI checkpoint on the MA subset; for each sample, three rollouts (audio+video, video‑only, audio‑only) are generated and a contrastive bonus `r_attn = 0.3` is awarded if the multimodal answer matches or exceeds the unimodal ones. Both stages use Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), a sequence‑level RL algorithm tailored for MoE models.

## Key Results
- **Audio‑visual benchmarks:** OmniVideoBench 44.8% (base 37.0%), and surpasses larger models such as Video‑SALMONN 2+‑72B.
- **Visual‑only benchmarks:** Video‑MME 73.6% (base 70.5%), showing maintained visual reasoning.
- **Ablations:** QI contributes +17.8% on OmniVideoBench; MA adds +12.4%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Query‑Intensive Grounding via Self‑Supervised Learning
**Claim:** Training the model to produce time‑aligned captions for relevant audio‑visual segments improves its ability to locate and reason over modality‑salient cues without manual segmentation labels.  
**Mechanism:** The model generates `<time>...</time><caption>...</caption>` pairs. A composite reward `R_QI` combines format compliance, final answer accuracy (`r_ans`), consistency between each time segment and its caption (`r_cons`, judged by Qwen3‑VL), and completeness (`r_comp`) assessing whether concatenated segments cover necessary information. This encourages accurate, minimal, and comprehensive temporal grounding.  
**Core assumption:** The model’s own caption‑generation consistency with extracted segments provides a scalable training signal for identifying query‑relevant moments.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “query‑intensive grounding based on self‑supervised learning paradigms”; Section 3.2 details `r_cons` and `r_comp`.  
**Break condition:** If the judger (Qwen3‑VL) is inconsistent or biased, the consistency signal becomes noisy; for queries without a clear temporal focus, grounding may be penalized incorrectly.

### Mechanism 2: Modality‑Attentive Fusion via Contrastive Rewards
**Claim:** A contrastive reward encouraging the multimodal input to outperform any single‑modality input forces the model to integrate audio and visual cues synergistically.  
**Mechanism:** For each training example, the model generates three responses conditioned on (i) full audio‑visual input, (ii) video‑only, and (iii) audio‑only. If the full‑input accuracy `r1_ans` is at least as high as both `r2_ans` and `r3_ans`, an additional reward `r_attn = α` (α = 0.3) is granted. This explicit comparison pushes the model to utilize both modalities rather than ignoring one.  
**Core assumption:** Ideal multimodal reasoning performance should be non‑inferior to unimodal reasoning on tasks that genuinely require both modalities.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “modality‑attentive fusion built upon contrastive learning paradigms”; Section 3.3 defines `r_attn`.  
**Break condition:** On unimodal‑dominant tasks, the multimodal input may not consistently beat unimodal inputs, causing `r_attn` to stay zero and potentially leading to underutilization of one modality or training instability.

### Mechanism 3: Two‑Stage Reinforcement Learning (GSPO) for Behavior Shaping
**Claim:** A staged RL approach first establishing query‑intensive grounding and then refining modality fusion yields higher gains than end‑to‑end or single‑stage training.  
**Mechanism:** Stage 1 (QI) trains on 88 K samples with `R_QI` to instill grounding behavior. Stage 2 (MA) fine‑tunes on a high‑quality 13 K subset requiring strong audio‑visual integration using `R_MA = r_format + r_ans + r_attn`. Both stages use Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), which operates at the sequence level to avoid token‑level KL issues in MoE models.  
**Core assumption:** The two skills (accurate grounding and effective fusion) are best learned sequentially, and the MA stage does not overwrite the QI behavior but builds upon it.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 dataset splits; Section 3 describes GSPO; Table 5 ablations show removing either stage degrades performance.  
**Break condition:** If the MA stage’s contrastive reward conflicts with the grounding learned in QI (e.g., pushing for different temporal selections), the model may regress on grounding quality. The staged training also risks catastrophic forgetting if stage 2 data is too small or dissimilar.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Base model: Qwen3‑Omni‑30B‑A3B → RL trainer: GSPO → Reward modules (format checker, answer scorer, consistency judger Qwen3‑VL‑235B, completeness evaluator, modality‑attention comparator). Data pipeline filters LLaVA‑Video/Video‑Vista via quality assessment (Gemini), categorization (Qwen‑3‑32B), and produces QI/MA splits. Rollout manager generates responses under AV, V‑only, A‑only conditions for the MA stage.

**Critical Path**  
1. Prepare a batch of audio‑visual samples with questions.  
2. For each sample, generate G rollouts under the current policy.  
3. Compute rewards: QI stage uses `R_QI`; MA stage uses `R_MA` with modality comparisons.  
4. Estimate group advantages (Eq. 3) and sequence importance ratios (Eq. 4).  
5. Perform GSPO update (Eqs. 1‑2).  
6. After QI stage, switch to MA stage using the filtered MA subset.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Sequence‑level vs token‑level RL: Sequence‑level avoids KL explosion in MoE but loses fine‑grained control.  
- Off‑the‑shelf judger vs custom: A large VL model provides reliable scores but adds compute cost and external bias.  
- Self‑supervised vs human‑annotated: Avoids labeling cost but relies on the model’s own generation quality; consistency checks act as a safeguard.  
- Contrastive reward magnitude (α): Too high risks overfitting; too low may not induce fusion.  
- Two‑stage vs joint training: Staging isolates objectives but requires careful dataset curation and risks forgetting.

**Failure Signatures**  
- High variance in group advantages → unstable loss; monitor and clip gradients, adjust β.  
- Consistency judge returns uniformly low scores → captions may be degenerate; check prompt and sampling temperature.  
- MA stage drops unimodal performance → observe “without audio” ablation; may need to adjust α or MA data selection.  
- Malformed `<time>`/`<caption>` tags → `r_format` weight may be insufficient; enforce stricter format compliance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Can OmniVideo-R1 be trained without any ground‑truth answer supervision?  
*Basis:* The authors state in the limitations that current methods rely on outcome‑based ground‑truth; exploring training without it is an important direction.  
*Why unresolved:* The framework uses ground‑truth answers for `r_ans` and `r_comp`; QI grounding still depends on answer labels.  
*Evidence needed:* Development of a fully unsupervised reward mechanism (e.g., cross‑modal consistency) that eliminates the need for answer keys.

**Open Question 2:** Does the contrastive modality reward universally ensure that audio‑visual fusion outperforms single modalities?  
*Basis:* The reward design assumes multimodal superiority but the paper does not test cases where audio is irrelevant, noisy, or contradictory.  
*Why unresolved:* Forcing superiority might cause over‑reliance on audio even when detrimental, leading to errors in real‑world settings with poor audio.  
*Evidence needed:* Systematic evaluation on perturbed audio variants (noise, reversed speech, unrelated sounds) to see if the model incorrectly prioritizes audio.

**Open Question 3:** How does the quality and bias of the external judger models impact the stability and final performance of OmniVideo-R1's self‑supervised rewards?  
*Basis:* `r_cons` and `r_comp` rely on Qwen3‑VL‑235B, yet no ablation or sensitivity analysis is provided.  
*Why unresolved:* If the judger exhibits systematic biases, the reward signal could misguide RL training, causing suboptimal convergence or reward hacking.  
*Evidence needed:* Ablations replacing the judger with models of varying capacities, introducing controlled noise, and correlating judge accuracy (via human evaluation) with final performance.

**Open Question 4:** Can the query‑intensive grounding mechanism scale to ultra‑long videos (e.g., several hours) without sacrificing temporal precision or introducing prohibitive redundancy?  
*Basis:* The completeness reward concatenates all grounded segments; experiments include videos up to 30 minutes but not longer; scaling to hour‑long content is unstudied.  
*Why unresolved:* For long videos, the number of relevant segments could grow, making concatenation computationally expensive and losing fine‑grained alignment; the design lacks explicit long‑range modeling.  
*Evidence needed:* Evaluate grounding accuracy, segment count, and redundancy on long‑video benchmarks (e.g., LVBench) as a function of video length; compare against hierarchical or adaptive grounding strategies.

**Open Question 5:** What is the optimal interplay between query‑intensive grounding (QI) and modality‑attentive fusion (MA), and could joint or alternating training yield further gains over the current sequential approach?  
*Basis:* The paper adopts a fixed two‑stage pipeline without exploring joint optimization or different schedules; ablations show both components help but their interaction remains unstudied.  
*Why unresolved:* Sequential training may cause catastrophic forgetting or suboptimal synergy: QI might learn grounding patterns that MA overwrites, or MA might rely on QI's initial grounding but fail to refine it.  
*Evidence needed:* Experiment with interleaved QI/MA training (alternating batches/epochs), varying the proportion of data and reward weights; measure grounding precision (via segment IoU with human annotations) and multimodal fusion metrics alongside final accuracy.

## Limitations
- **GSPO specification** – The algorithm is only cited; critical hyperparameters (KL penalty schedule, distributed setup) are undocumented, hindering exact replication.
- **Judger reliability** – The consistency/completeness rewards rely on Qwen3‑VL‑235B, a black‑box model whose scoring consistency and potential biases are not validated.
- **Dataset provenance** – The 88K corpus is built from LLaVA‑Video/Video‑Vista with proprietary Gemini scoring and heuristic thresholds; exact filtering logic and licensing are unclear, preventing reconstruction.

## Confidence
- **Query‑Intensive grounding:** Medium – Reward formulation and ablations are convincing, but the self‑supervised signal depends on a large external judger whose biases are not characterized.
- **Modality‑Attentive fusion:** Medium – Contrastive reward ablation shows gains, yet the fixed α = 0.3 and the assumption that multimodal must never underperform unimodal may not hold universally.
- **Two‑stage RL (GSPO):** Low – The algorithm is only cited; implementation details (KL penalty, distributed training) are absent, making exact replication uncertain.
