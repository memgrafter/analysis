# Policies Permitting LLM Use for Polishing Peer Reviews Are Currently Not Enforceable

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20450
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20450
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: All tested AI-text detectors misclassify a non-trivial fraction of AI-polished human-written peer reviews as AI-generated, rendering polishing-only policies unenforceable.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the enforceability of policies that permit LLM use solely for polishing peer reviews (e.g., grammar correction) while prohibiting full AI generation. The authors test five AI-text detectors—including commercial systems Pangram and GPTZero—on a dataset of 50,156 reviews spanning human-written, AI-polished, AI-generated, and mixed collaboration levels. All detectors produce non-zero false positive rates on AI-polished reviews (up to 3.4% for commercial systems), and no strategy (context injection, domain-specific signals) reduces these below the sub-0.1% threshold needed for reliable large-scale enforcement. Consequently, current polishing-only policies are not practically enforceable. The study also warns that public prevalence estimates of AI-generated reviews (e.g., 21% at ICLR 2026) likely overstate violations by incorrectly counting polished reviews as fully AI-generated.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a dataset of 50,156 peer reviews from the PeerRead corpus and synthetic generations covering five collaboration levels: human (H), human-written then AI-polished (H-AI), AI-written then human-edited (AI-HI), AI-written in a brainstorming role (AI-BP), and AI-written in an elaborating role (AI-EP). Generations use multiple LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-5, etc.). Evaluation uses five detectors: three zero-shot (LogLikelihood, Binoculars, Fast-DetectGPT) and two commercial (Pangram v3.0, GPTZero). Zero-shot detectors are calibrated on NeurIPS 2013–2015 human reviews to achieve 0% false positive rate (FPR); thresholds are then applied to evaluate FPR on H-AI and human reviews, and true positive rate (TPR) on AI* (all AI-generated). Additional experiments incorporate paper context, similarity-based referencing, and supervised classifiers (stylometric, RoBERTa). The core metric is FPR on H-AI (allowed reviews) and TPR on AI* (prohibited reviews); enforceability requires FPR ≪ 0.1% at scale.

## Key Results
- All five detectors misclassify a non-trivial fraction of AI-polished (H-AI) reviews as AI-generated; Pangram and GPTZero yield FPRs of 3.1% and 3.4% on the hard subset.
- Zero-shot detectors generally perform worse, with Fast-DetectGPT achieving 4.6% FPR on H-AI.
- Adding paper context or domain-specific signals provides only marginal improvements, still far above the required sub-0.1% FPR.
- Humanization techniques (making AI text more human-like) dramatically increase FPR on H-AI, e.g., Pangram’s FPR jumps to 65.6%, collapsing detector utility.
- Supervised classifiers fail to generalize to held-out LLMs, with FPR on H-AI rising to 11.5% for some unseen models, while TPR on AI* drops.
- The study concludes polishing-only policies are currently unenforceable and argues that prior prevalence estimates (like 21% AI-generated) likely overstate violations by misclassifying mixed-authorship (polished) reviews.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Detectors rely on statistical signatures (perplexity, embedding similarity, learned patterns) that are altered even by superficial AI polishing (grammar/flow improvements), causing AI-polished text to statistically resemble fully AI-generated text. The core assumption is that minor AI intervention changes text in detectable ways regardless of content origin. Evidence: all detectors misclassify H-AI reviews, with Pangram FPR = 3.1% and GPTZero FPR = 3.4% on the hard subset. If detectors achieved consistently <0.1% FPR on H-AI, polishing policies could become enforceable.

### Mechanism 2
AI-text detectors output binary or coarse categories (“AI” vs “Human”) that fail to capture human-AI collaboration; any AI involvement triggers an “AI” label, inflating violation counts. The core assumption is that prevalence studies use detector outputs as ground truth without calibrating for false positives on polished human text. Evidence: Pangram labels 92.6% of AI-HI (human input + AI elaboration) as “AI”; H-AI reviews also misclassified. If detectors reliably output “mixed” or “polished” labels with high precision, prevalence estimates could be corrected.

### Mechanism 3
The constrained domain and writing style of peer reviews, rather than helping, may exacerbate detection challenges by reducing stylistic diversity and making human-polished text appear atypical. Supervised detectors trained on limited peer-review distributions overfit to specific models or prompts; new LLMs or humanization techniques shift the distribution, causing missed detections or false positives on polished text. Evidence: RoBERTa classifier’s TPR on AI* drops to 52.2% on held-out Llama-3.1, or FPR on H-AI rises to 11.5% on held-out Qwen; humanization causes Pangram FPR on H-AI to jump to 65.6%. If a domain-adaptive detector can maintain <1% FPR on H-AI across unseen LLMs without retraining, enforcement could become feasible.

3. Try similarity-based detection: Compute cosine/n-gram/keypoint similarity to AI references; train XGBoost; evaluate FPR on H-AI.  
4. Try supervised training: Train RoBERTa on stylometric features; perform held-out model evaluation; check FPR on H-AI from unseen LLM.  
5. Stress test with humanization: Apply humanizer to H-AI/AI-BP; re-evaluate detectors.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- TPR vs FPR: Raising thresholds lowers FPR but also TPR; enforcement demands very low FPR on H-AI as the hard constraint.  
- Context vs simplicity: Paper context improves TPR slightly but increases FPR on H-AI and adds implementation complexity.  
- Generalization vs specificity: Supervised models overfit to training LLMs; maintaining low FPR on new LLMs requires continuous retraining.

**Failure signatures:**  
- FPR on H-AI > 1% in any detector configuration.  
- Held-out model evaluation shows >5% FPR on H-AI or <80% TPR on AI*.  
- Similarity distributions for H-AI and AI* overlap >50% (visual inspection).  
- Humanization causes >50% drop in TPR on AI* or >30% rise in FPR on H-AI.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What are the underlying linguistic or statistical features that cause AI-polished reviews to be misclassified as AI-generated by current detectors? The paper demonstrates non-zero false positive rates but does not diagnose the root causes. Resolving this requires systematic feature ablation or representation analysis.
- **Open Question 2:** Can supervised AI text detectors be made robust to unseen LLM models without high false positive rates on AI-polished text? Current supervised approaches exhibit unpredictable generalization failures; training on a diverse corpus of many LLMs with rigorous out-of-distribution evaluation could provide evidence.
- **Open Question 3:** Do manuscript-contextualized detection methods reduce false positives on AI-polished reviews to below 0.1% while maintaining high true positives on AI-generated reviews? Existing context-aware methods only offer marginal improvements; novel architectures would be needed to meet the stringent threshold.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the accurate prevalence of AI-polished reviews in peer review, given that current detectors overestimate AI-generated content? A large-scale manual audit or a validated detection method with known error bounds is required to obtain reliable estimates.

## Limitations
- **Commercial detector fidelity:** Pangram/GPTZero APIs and underlying models may have changed since the test date; reported FPRs depend on undisclosed thresholds and versions.
- **Calibration stability:** The 0% FPR calibration on NeurIPS 2013–2015 human reviews may not generalize to newer review styles or conferences; split randomness could shift thresholds.
- **Policy boundary ambiguity:** Real-world “polishing” is ill-defined; the paper’s discrete levels may not capture continuous human-AI collaboration, affecting external validity.

## Confidence
- **Enforceability failure:** High. Consistently non-zero FPR across multiple detectors directly supports the claim.
- **Prevalence overstatement:** Medium. Relies on assumption that prior studies used detectors with similar misclassification rates and lacked mixed labels; cited 21% estimate methodology is opaque.
- **Domain challenges:** Medium. Held-out model degradation and humanization collapse indicate robustness issues, but real-world review diversity may differ.
