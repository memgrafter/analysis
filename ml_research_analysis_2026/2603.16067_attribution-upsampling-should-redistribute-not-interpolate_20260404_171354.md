# Attribution Upsampling should Redistribute, Not Interpolate

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16067
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16067
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Universal Semantic-Aware Upsampling (USU) reframes upsampling as mass redistribution based on semantic segments, reducing attribution infidelity by orders of magnitude and improving boundary fidelity across datasets and models.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a critical flaw in attribution upsampling for explainable AI: traditional interpolation methods (bilinear, bicubic) degrade saliency maps by ignoring model semantics, causing aliasing and boundary bleeding. The authors reformulate upsampling as a mass redistribution problem constrained by four desiderata (mass conservation, monotonicity, score-invariant sensitivity, locality). They prove that interpolation violates three of these and derive a unique ratio-form operator, USU, that satisfies all. USU redistributes coarse attribution mass within each neighbourhood according to model-derived segment scores via a normalized exponential potential. An extended variant, Soft IWMR, relaxes conservation to correct coarse biases. Extensive experiments on ImageNet, CIFAR-10, and CUB-200 show USU drastically lowers infidelity (1–4 orders of magnitude) and improves multiple fidelity metrics, consistently across architectures and attribution methods.

## Method Summary
USU operates on a coarse attribution map *A* defined on a coarse grid of neighbourhoods *N*. For each neighbourhood, it computes the total mass *M*<sub>k</sub> = Σ<sub>x∈N<sub>k</sub></sub> *A*(x). A finer segmentation *S* with segment scores *s* (e.g., from masked perturbation) guides redistribution: within each neighbourhood, weights are assigned to pixels based on a potential φ(*s*), typically exponential, normalized per neighbourhood. The upsampled map ˜*A*(x) = *M*<sub>ν(x)</sub> · φ(*s*(σ(x))) / Σ<sub>y∈N<sub>ν(x)</sub></sub> φ(*s*(σ(y))), ensuring mass conservation and semantic alignment. Soft IWMR modifies neighbourhood masses using importance-weighted redistribution to handle systemic coarse biases. Hierarchical boundary refinement adaptively derives *S* from score heterogeneity via an H-map and recursive segmentation.

## Key Results
- USU achieves near-perfect IoU (up to 1.00) and pointing game accuracy (1.00) on synthetic shapes, far surpassing interpolation (~0.79 IoU).
- Across ImageNet, CIFAR-10, and CUB-200, USU reduces Infidelity by 1–4 orders of magnitude (e.g., VGG16/ImageNet: 6.91e6 → 1.27e5) and improves IROF, Monotonicity, Sparseness while lowering PixelFlipping and ROAD errors.
- USU consistently outperforms interpolation across seven attribution methods and multiple architectures, demonstrating robustness and broad applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Interpolation ignores semantics
Standard upsampling kernels (bilinear, bicubic) reconstruct smooth signals based solely on pixel coordinates, disregarding model relevance. This causes mass leakage across semantic boundaries, non-local contamination (ringing), and score-independent weighting that violates monotonicity. Attentive regions must be separated by semantic boundaries derived from model reasoning, not geometry.

### Mechanism 2: Ratio-form uniqueness from desiderata
Three desiderata—mass conservation, segment monotonicity, and locality—force the upsampling operator to be linear in neighbourhood mass and assign weights via a strictly monotone potential φ applied to segment scores. Locality implies Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA), which via the multiplicative Cauchy functional equation restricts φ to any strictly monotone function. Conservation then normalizes weights within each neighbourhood, yielding the unique ratio form: *w*<sub>k</sub>(x) ∝ φ(*s*(σ(x))).

### Mechanism 3: Hierarchical boundary refinement
A single segmentation scale is insufficient; boundaries must emerge adaptively from score heterogeneity. The H-map (convolution with a diagonal-difference kernel) detects local score transitions. Segments with high absolute H-map values are recursively refined to a finer segmentation. An adaptive mixing coefficient blends coarse and fine scores based on boundary strength, producing multi-scale scores where boundaries sharpen at score discontinuities.

- **Interpolation artifacts in signal processing**: Aliasing, ringing, and boundary bleeding arise from smooth kernels applied to piecewise-constant signals. *Why needed*: Explains why classical upsampling fails for saliency maps. *Quick check*: How do negative weights in bicubic interpolation create spurious high-attribution lobes?
- **Discrete choice theory (Luce's IIA)**: Locality implies IIA, which forces weight ratios between pixels to depend only on their segment scores, not on other pixels in the neighbourhood. *Why needed*: Drives the ratio-form derivation. *Quick check*: What functional equation arises from IIA and what is its monotone solution?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Coarse attribution *A* → neighbourhood masses *M*<sub>k</sub> → segmentation *S* with scores *s* → potential φ(*s*) → normalized weights *w*<sub>k</sub>(x) → fused attribution ˜*A*(x) = *M*<sub>ν(x)</sub> · *w*<sub>ν(x)</sub>(x). Optional: hierarchical refinement derives *S* adaptively.
- **Critical path**: From coarse map, compute *M*<sub>k</sub>; obtain *s* via model (e.g., masked perturbation); compute φ(*s*); normalize per neighbourhood; multiply by *M*<sub>k</sub>. For Soft IWMR, adjust *M*<sub>k</sub> using importance weights λ<sub>k</sub>.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - USU vs. Soft IWMR: USU enforces strict per-neighbourhood conservation; Soft IWMR relaxes it for global redistribution, useful when coarse attributions are biased.
  - Potential choice: Exponential φ(*s*) = exp((*s*−0.5)/ε) ensures constant conditioning; power-law alternatives violate D3 but may suit clustered scores.
  - Segmentation scale: Hierarchical refinement adapts scale; coarser scales group semantics, finer scales fragment regions.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Interpolation: boundary bleeding, ringing, identical outputs for different inputs.
  - Misaligned segmentation: if *S* does not match model boundaries, redistribution follows incorrect segments, causing fragmented explanations.
  - Soft IWMR over-redistribution: high ε<sub>Λ</sub> may shift mass to irrelevant neighbourhoods.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Synthetic validation**: Train RRL-constrained model on shapes with ground-truth attribution; compute IoU and pointing game for USU vs. bilinear. Expect USU near 1.0, interpolation ~0.79.
  2. **Desiderata verification**: On ImageNet/VGG16, compute D1 error (mass conservation violation). USU should be ~1e-7, interpolation ~1e-1.
  3. **Infidelity reduction**: For GradCAM on ImageNet/VGG16, upsample with bilinear vs. USU; compute Infidelity via Quantus. Expect 1–2 orders magnitude drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can the axiomatic characterization extend to operators where allocation weights depend on the mass being distributed (mass-dependent weighting), and what unique solution emerges?
- **Open Question 2**: Can we derive quantitative criteria based on coarse attribution statistics to predict when Soft IWMR's importance-weighted redistribution outperforms USU's strict conservation?
- **Open Question 3**: How robust is USU to segmentation boundary quality, and can alternative boundary determination methods (e.g., traditional image segmentation) match or exceed the proposed score-heterogeneity driven refinement?
- **Open Question 4**: What is USU's computational complexity relative to interpolation, and can approximate variants maintain theoretical guarantees while achieving practical efficiency?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters for tensor potential (ε), importance redistribution (ε<sub>Λ</sub>), and hierarchical refinement (θ, µ, τ, L) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Reliance on a segmentation *S* aligned with model semantics; misalignment with external segmenters (e.g., SAM) is not studied.
- Uniqueness proof assumes linearity in mass and IIA; relaxing these may invalidate the ratio-form guarantee.
- Segment score computation via masked perturbation lacks detail (mask style, inpainting, number of evaluations), impeding duplication.

## Confidence
- **Interpolation corrupts attribution signals**: **High** (Theorems 1‑3 and consistent empirical degradation).
- **USU uniquely satisfies D1‑D4**: **Medium** (rigorous proof but depends on strong assumptions).
- **Large-scale empirical gains**: **High** (improvements across three datasets, seven methods, multiple architectures; effect sizes 1‑4 orders).
- **Hierarchical boundary refinement necessity**: **Medium** (well-motivated, qualitative evidence, but quantitative ablation missing).
