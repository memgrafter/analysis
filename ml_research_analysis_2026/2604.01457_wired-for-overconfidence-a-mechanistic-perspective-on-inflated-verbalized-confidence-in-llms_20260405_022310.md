# Wired for Overconfidence: A Mechanistic Perspective on Inflated Verbalized Confidence in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01457
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01457
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit inflated verbalized confidence when wrong due to a compact circuit of mid-to-late layer components; targeted interventions improve calibration by up to 77.5% ECE reduction.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often display overconfidence, providing high verbalized confidence scores for incorrect answers. This paper mechanistically investigates the internal origins of this bias. The authors introduce Target-Set Logit Difference (TSLD) as a differentiable proxy for the model's internal confidence propensity. Using truth-injection counterfactuals—replacing a model's incorrect answer with ground truth—and Edge Attribution Patching with Integrated Gradients (EAP-IG), they identify a sparse Confidence Mover Circuit (CMC) composed of mid-to-late layer MLP blocks and attention heads that causally writes the overconfidence signal. Targeted inference-time interventions—mean ablation and activation steering—applied specifically to these components substantially improve calibration metrics (Expected Calibration Error, Brier score) across multiple models and datasets, demonstrating that overconfidence is rooted in identifiable, intervention-amenable circuitry.

## Method Summary
The study employs a two-step elicitation protocol: first, the model answers a question; second, it self-reports confidence as an integer 0–99. For incorrect answers, truth-injection counterfactuals are constructed by swapping the model's answer with the correct one while keeping all other tokens identical. The differentiable TSLD metric computes the mean logit difference between predefined high-confidence (e.g., 70–99) and low-confidence (e.g., 0–30) integer tokens at the final position, capturing internal confidence bias. To identify causal components, EAP-IG attributes changes in TSLD (∆TSLD) between clean (incorrect) and corrupted (truth-injected) prompts across the computational graph (linear projections). Attribution scores aggregate to component level (MLP blocks, attention heads), revealing a Confidence Mover Circuit concentrated in middle-to-late layers. Validation includes faithfulness (ablating top edges preserves ∆TSLD) and completeness (ablating all CMC edges reduces TSLD). Interventions apply either mean ablation (replacing a component's activation with the mean from corrupted-prompt activations) or activation steering (subtracting a scaled direction vector `v_conf`, the mean difference between clean and corrupt activations) at the final token position of top CMC components.

## Key Results
- A compact subcircuit of mid-to-late layer MLP blocks and attention heads is causally necessary and sufficient for driving inflated verbalized confidence, with consistent layer locations across PopQA, MMLU, and NQOpen datasets.
- Activation steering on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct reduces Expected Calibration Error by 77.5% and Brier score by 69.7% on PopQA; mean ablation reduces ECE by 96.9% for Llama/PopQA.
- Similar calibration gains are observed across models and datasets, though maximal intervention can sometimes hurt accuracy on knowledge-intensive tasks like MMLU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Verbalized overconfidence is driven by a differentiable internal signal, captured by the Target-Set Logit Difference (TSLD), which measures the model's internal preference for high versus low confidence tokens before decoding.
- **Mechanism:** TSLD computes the mean logit gap between predefined high-confidence (H={70,75,80,85,90,99}) and low-confidence (L={0,10,15,20,25,30}) integer token sets at the final position. This difference neutralizes global activation shifts and captures relative confidence propensity. A strong positive TSLD indicates a model-internal bias toward high-confidence outputs.
- **Core assumption:** The internal logit distribution at the confidence prediction position linearly separates confidence levels and reflects the model's latent confidence state, which correlates with final verbalized confidence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2]: Introduces TSLD as a differentiable proxy and shows it correlates strongly (r=0.815 for Qwen, r=0.734 for Llama) with changes in verbalized confidence after truth-injection counterfactuals.
  - [abstract]: States TSLD "captures internal confidence propensity."
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighboring papers focus on verbal confidence or calibration methods, not specific internal logit-based proxies like TSLD.
- **Break condition:** If the model's confidence token representation is non-linear or distributed across many layers in a way not captured by a simple logit difference at a single position, TSLD may fail to be a faithful proxy. Evidence: Models might encode confidence in earlier layers or via non-logit pathways not measured by final token logits.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Inflated verbalized confidence is causally driven by a compact subcircuit of mid-to-late layer MLP blocks and attention heads that writes the confidence-inflation signal at the final token position.
- **Mechanism:** Using truth-injection counterfactuals (replacing incorrect answers with ground truth) to create clean/corrupted pairs, Edge Attribution Patching with Integrated Gradients (EAP-IG) is applied. The difference in TSLD (∆TSLD) between conditions stratifies data. Attribution scores (product of activation difference and integrated gradient) identify edges whose flow change correlates with ∆TSLD. Aggregating to component level reveals a sparse Confidence Mover Circuit (CMC) concentrated in layers ~24-35 for Qwen and ~14-27 for Llama.
- **Core assumption:** The computational graph formalism (nodes as linear projections, edges as residual connections) accurately represents information flow for confidence computation, and the ∆TSLD signal isolates the pathway responsible for overconfidence suppression.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1]: Attribution heatmaps show concentration in mid-to-late layers, top-10 components are shared across datasets (≥80% overlap), and faithfulness/completeness validation confirms the circuit is both sufficient (~85%+ faithfulness) and necessary (~74-79% TSLD drop).
  - [abstract]: "a compact set of MLP blocks and attention heads, concentrated in middle-to-late layers, consistently writes the confidence-inflation signal."
  - [corpus]: Moderate; prior work (e.g., Teplica et al., 2025) identifies circuits for uncertainty, but this paper's specific finding of a fixed overconfidence circuit is novel. Neighbors like "How do llms compute verbal confidence" (Kumaran et al., 2026) probe confidence computation but not causal circuits for *over*confidence.
- **Break condition:** If the overconfidence signal is distributed across many more components or arises from emergent interactions not captured by linear attribution, the identified CMC may be an incomplete correlate. Evidence: High non-additivity in joint ablation (Figure 4) suggests interconnectedness but also potential for undiscovered parallel pathways.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Targeted inference-time intervention on the identified CMC components (via mean ablation or activation steering) substantially improves calibration, demonstrating the circuit's practical amenability to recalibration.
- **Mechanism:** Two intervention strategies are applied at the final token position only to the top-ranked CMC components: (1) Mean ablation replaces the component's activation with a mean computed from corrupted-prompt (truth-injected) activations. (2) Activation steering subtracts a scaled direction vector `v_conf` (mean difference between clean and corrupted activations for CMC components) from the component's activation: `h_out <- h_out - α * v_conf`. Both suppress the overconfidence signal without altering reasoning for the answer generation phase.
- **Core assumption:** The overconfidence direction `v_conf` estimated from the overconfident discovery subset (Bucket 1) generalizes to the full dataset and represents a linear, additive direction in activation space that primarily encodes inflated confidence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4]: Steering (α=0.5-0.6) reduces ECE by 77.5% and Brier score by 69.7% on PopQA for Qwen; mean ablation reduces ECE by 96.9% for Llama/PopQA. Reliability curves show movement toward the diagonal.
  - [abstract]: "Targeted inference-time interventions... substantially improve calibration."
  - [corpus]: Strong; multiple neighboring papers (e.g., "Taming Overconfidence in LLMs: Reward Calibration in RLHF", "Direct Confidence Alignment") pursue calibration via training or prompting, but this paper's *circuit-based inference intervention* is a distinct mechanistic approach.
- **Break condition:** If `v_conf` is not a true causal direction but confounds with other task-relevant signals (e.g., answer format, reasoning style), steering at optimal α might degrade accuracy or overall performance. Evidence: The non-monotonic ECE curve with increasing α (Figure 8) and the fact that maximal intervention (mean ablation) sometimes hurts MMLU performance suggest `v_conf` may contain approximations or impurity.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Differentiable Internal Proxies for Behavioral Metrics
- **Why needed here:** Verbalized confidence is a discrete integer output, not a differentiable scalar needed for gradient-based attribution. TSLD creates a continuous, differentiable signal from logits that correlates with the behavior of interest.
- **Quick check question:** If you wanted to attribute *uncertainty hedging in text*, how might you construct a differentiable proxy from the model's output distribution?

### Concept: Counterfactual Causal Tracing
- **Why needed here:** To isolate the causal effect of *answer correctness* on subsequent confidence, the paper uses "truth-injection": swapping an incorrect model answer with the ground truth while keeping all other tokens identical. This clean/corrupted pair design is essential for computing ∆TSLD and constructing the attribution baseline.
- **Quick check question:** Why is it critical to keep all tokens *except the answer* identical in the counterfactual? What confounder would a non-identical design introduce?

### Concept: Activation Steering for Inference-Time Control
- **Why needed here:** Steering provides a tunable, soft intervention that subtracts a precomputed direction (`v_conf`) from specific component activations at inference time. It's more stable than hard ablation (mean ablation) because it preserves instance-specific signals while attenuating the overconfidence bias.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between *mean ablation* (replacing activation) and *steering* (shifting activation) as intervention strategies? When might one fail while the other succeeds?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Question tokens → Step 1 (answer generation) → *store incorrect answer* → Step 2 (confidence computation): full prompt with stored answer + confidence query → forward pass until `POS END` → Target Components (mid-to-late layer MLP blocks and attention heads) → Output Layer (logits for confidence tokens) → argmax/verbalization. Intervention Hook: insert after `POS END` activation of each target component to apply mean ablation or steering before residual add.

**Critical path:**
1. Run Step 1 on a question, record the model's generated answer and store the `POS END` activation of all candidate CMC components.
2. For Step 2 on the same question: load the stored answer into the prompt. At `POS END` for each target component, apply intervention: either replace with `µ_ref` (mean of corrupted-prompt activations from Bucket 1) or subtract `α * v_conf`.
3. Continue forward pass through remaining layers to logits, decode confidence token.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Sparsity vs. Completeness:** A smaller top-k circuit is easier to intervene on but may miss contributing pathways; paper finds top-10 components yield major gains but not 100% correction, supporting a compact core plus distributed remainder.
- **Mean Ablation vs. Steering:** Mean ablation is simple and highly effective when signal is tightly concentrated (e.g., Llama/PopQA), but can be brittle (fails on MMLU). Steering offers tunable dose-response but requires selecting α and relies on a linear direction approximation.
- **Global vs. Per-Example Reference:** Both methods use a *global* reference (`µ_ref` or `v_conf`) computed from Bucket 1 examples. This is deployment-efficient but may not capture instance-specific nuances, potentially limiting maximum calibration gain.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Gradient Saturation:** EAP-IG is used instead of vanilla EAP because some important neurons may have near-zero gradient when saturated (fully activated). If your model shows poor attribution, check for saturation; EAP-IG's integrated gradients should help.
- **Non-Additive Joint Effects:** If ablating top components jointly yields much less reduction than sum of individual ablations (as in paper), it indicates heavy shared pathways. Interventions targeting only one may have limited effect.
- **Dataset-Specific Collapse:** The paper finds consistent circuits across datasets *within a model*. If your new dataset shows a completely different top-attribution pattern, the overconfidence mechanism may be task-dependent or your bucket stratification threshold (τ) may be inappropriate.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Replicate TSLD Correlation:** On your model/dataset, compute TSLD and verbalized confidence for incorrect examples. Run truth-injection counterfactuals (replace model's wrong answer with gold). Verify that ∆TSLD correlates with ∆verbalized confidence (expect r > 0.7).
2. **Run EAP-IG Attribution:** Using Bucket 1 examples (∆TSLD ≤ -τ), compute edge attribution scores. Generate heatmap (layer vs. component type). Confirm concentration in mid-to-late layers and identify top-10 components by score.
3. **Single-Component Ablation Test:** Ablate each of your top-10 components (replace `POS END` activation with corresponding corrupted-prompt activation). Measure mean |TSLD reduction|. Expect a small set (1-4) to show large reduction (>50% of baseline TSLD), while early-layer MLPs show ~0. This isolates the causal core from relay nodes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What comprises the full, non-compact system of confidence estimation beyond the identified Confidence Mover Circuits (CMCs)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state, "CMCs form a compact causal core of verbalized overconfidence, but not the full confidence-estimation system... The best results occur at moderate steering strengths rather than under maximal intervention. One interpretation is that these components form only part of a broader confidence-estimation system..." This directly identifies the existence of additional, uncharacterized components.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper successfully isolates a small, causally necessary set of components that drive a large portion of overconfidence. However, the non-additivity of joint ablation and the degradation of calibration under maximal intervention suggest remaining pathways or computations contribute to the confidence signal. The nature, location, and function of these other parts are not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic component-level ablation sweeping across all layers, not just the top-ranked ones, could map the remainder of the circuit. Furthermore, analyzing the representations and outputs of the residual stream after CMC intervention might reveal alternative confidence-coding subspaces or later-layer computations that remain intact.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does post-training (SFT, RLHF) specifically cause or strengthen the overconfident bias encoded in the CMCs, compared to the base pretrained model?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors write, "Post-training may strengthen a prior toward overconfident behavior. The models studied here are instruction-tuned variants... A natural next step is to compare pretrained, supervised fine-tuned, and RLHF-tuned checkpoints, or to track how verbal confidence-related circuits emerge over the course of post-training." This is presented as a clear, future research direction.
- **Why unresolved:** The study only examines final, instruction-tuned models. Without comparing to their base pretrained counterparts or intermediate checkpoints, it is impossible to attribute the presence or strength of the CMCs to the post-training process itself versus inherent properties of the base model architecture and pretraining data. The causal role of alignment procedures in shaping these circuits remains speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running the identical mechanistic pipeline (TSLD proxy, truth-injection, EAP-IG) on a suite of corresponding base (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B) and fine-tuned checkpoints (SFT, RLHF) for the same datasets. Comparing the presence, strength, and layer distribution of the CMCs across this developmental sequence would isolate the effect of post-training.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the mechanistic explanation for the structural misalignment between the compact, mid-to-late-layer confidence circuit and the more distributed, earlier-involved factual retrieval circuit?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper contrasts the two circuits (Fig. 9) and cites Teplica et al., noting that genuine uncertainty is generated in parts supporting factuality. They conclude the confidence circuit is "structurally misaligned" with factual computation, implying a failure in information routing. However, the *reason* for this architectural mismatch—whether due to training objectives, the nature of the confidence token output, or a separate learned heuristic—is not investigated.
- **Why unresolved:** The observation is descriptive. The paper does not test hypotheses for *why* the confidence signal decouples from the factual pathway. Is the CMC a superficial "cached" pattern learned from instruction data that bypasses the uncertainty computation? Or does the task of converting a complex reasoning state into a single scalar confidence score naturally localize to later, more integrated layers?
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Probing the inputs to the CMC components to see if they contain signals of the model's actual uncertainty or retrieval success/failure. If they do not, it suggests the circuit is indeed a default high-bias writer. If they do, the misalignment might be in how these signals are transformed. Causal interventions that route factual retrieval signals into the confidence pathway could also test if misalignment is a necessary condition for overconfidence.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How sensitive and robust is the Target-Set Logit Difference (TSLD) metric as a proxy for verbalized confidence, particularly with respect to the arbitrary choice of the fixed high (H) and low (L) confidence token sets?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] TSLD is defined using H={70,75,80,85,90,99} and L={0,10,15,20,25,30}. The paper validates it by correlating ∆TSLD with ∆verbal confidence but does not explore the sensitivity of the discovered circuits to different, equally plausible token set definitions (e.g., shifting all values by 10, or using a different binning strategy).
- **Why unresolved:** The choice of H and L is a methodological assumption. If the core findings (circuit location, intervention efficacy) are highly sensitive to this specific binning, the generalizability of the mechanistic claim is weakened. The paper presents TSLD as a stable proxy but does not prove its robustness to this design choice.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Repeating the core attribution and intervention analysis with multiple, systematically varied Target-Set definitions (e.g., sliding windows, different bin widths, percentile-based sets). If the same components are consistently identified and interventions remain effective across these variations, TSLD's validity is strengthened. High variance would indicate the results are an artifact of the specific token sets chosen.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How accurate is the average overconfidence direction vector `v_conf` used in activation steering as an approximation for the per-example, true direction needed to correct each individual instance's confidence?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Steering uses `v_conf` computed as the mean difference between clean and corrupt activations across all Bucket 1 examples. The paper notes that "excessive scaling amplifies this approximation error" as a possible reason for degradation under strong steering. This acknowledges that `v_conf` is a population-level average, not a per-example ground truth.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates a clear dose-response curve for steering, but the non-monotonicity at high α is attributed to this approximation error. The magnitude and nature of this error, and whether a per-example steering direction would eliminate the optimal-α window, are unknown. The steering method's success at moderate α suggests the average direction is useful but imperfect.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** For a subset of examples, compute a per-example `v_conf^i` (using a single clean-corrupt pair) and apply instance-specific steering. Compare calibration metrics against the global `v_conf` approach. If per-example steering yields strictly monotonic improvement with α or shifts the optimal α, it would confirm the limitation lies in the averaging. Alternatively, analyzing the variance of clean-corrupt activation differences across Bucket 1 could quantify the "signal-to-noise" ratio in `v_conf`.

## Limitations
- Unreported stratification threshold τ and tokenization details for confidence integers risk reproducibility and may affect circuit discovery.
- The Confidence Mover Circuit may be incomplete due to non-additive joint effects; generalizability beyond 3B instruction-tuned models is unclear.
- Interventions can hurt accuracy on some tasks (e.g., mean ablation on MMLU), suggesting the overconfidence direction `v_conf` may conflate signals.

## Confidence
- **TSLD proxy validity:** Medium (correlation >0.7 shown on two models only)
- **CMC identification:** Medium (consistent mid-to-late layer concentration but dataset overlap ~80%)
- **Intervention efficacy:** High (ECE reduced by 77.5% on PopQA, with similar gains across models/datasets)
