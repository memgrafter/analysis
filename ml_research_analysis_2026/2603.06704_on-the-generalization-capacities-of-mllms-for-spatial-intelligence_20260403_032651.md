# On the Generalization Capacities of MLLMs for Spatial Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06704
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06704
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** RGB-only MLLMs fail to generalize across cameras due to an irresolvable depth-focal length ambiguity; a Camera-Aware MLLM with three specific components achieves robust cross-camera performance.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit catastrophic failures on spatial tasks like 3D localization when evaluated under different camera intrinsics, even for simple image resizes. This stems from a fundamental geometric ambiguity: without knowledge of camera parameters, object scale and distance are indistinguishable. This paper identifies the root cause as the absence of intrinsic awareness in standard MLLM pipelines and proposes a Camera-Aware framework that explicitly encodes per-pixel ray directions derived from intrinsics, applies camera-aware geometric augmentation during training, and distills dense 3D priors from a pre-trained monocular depth model. Experiments show that naive MLLMs suffer large performance drops on resized inputs, while the proposed model maintains robust accuracy across diverse cameras and benchmarks, establishing intrinsic awareness as a prerequisite for reliable spatial intelligence in MLLMs.

## Method Summary
The proposed Camera-Aware MLLM framework modifies a standard MLLM (VG-LLM-4B, fusing Qwen2.5-VL and VGGT) by injecting geometric awareness into its visual processing stream. First, a dense camera ray embedding is computed from camera intrinsics (focal lengths, principal point) for each visual token, encoding the normalized line-of-sight direction via sinusoidal functions and fusing it with the 2D visual features. Second, camera-aware geometric augmentation synthetically varies intrinsics during training by applying random resize-and-crop transformations while consistently updating the intrinsic matrix, forcing the model to learn invariance. Third, a frozen monocular depth model (UniDepth v2) provides dense 3D point cloud priors that are distilling into an additional embedding added to the visual features. The LLM backbone and the pre-trained components are frozen; only the ray embedding module, geometric prior distillator, and the LLM are trained. At inference, if true intrinsics are unavailable, they are estimated using the same depth model.

## Key Results
- Naive MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, VG-LLM) suffer catastrophic performance degradation on resized test images (e.g., F1@0.25 drops from 39.1% to 31.6% on ScanNet-val ×1.2).
- The complete Camera-Aware model maintains robust accuracy under resizing (F1@0.25: 52.1%) and generalizes across diverse held-out cameras and spatial benchmarks (SPAR-Bench, VSI-Bench).
- Ablations confirm that all three components (ray embedding, geometric augmentation, geometric prior distillation) are necessary; removing any one causes a significant performance drop, especially on resized evaluations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Injecting a dense camera ray embedding into every visual token resolves the focal-depth ambiguity by grounding each pixel in its specific line-of-sight, enabling the model to disentangle object scale from camera geometry.
- **Mechanism:** For each visual token at grid position (i,j), compute normalized direction components (u-ij-c_x)/f_x and (v-ij-c_y)/f_y from camera intrinsics (f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y). Also include global focal lengths. Encode via sinusoidal embedding and fuse with visual features via addition.
- **Core assumption:** The per-pixel ray direction is the minimal sufficient geometry to disambiguate projection; without it, model reasoning is camera-specific.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "injecting a dense camera ray embedding derived from intrinsics into every visual token"; [section:4.2] Detailed computation of R_x[i,j], R_y[i,j] and fusion with F_vis.
- **Break condition:** If intrinsics are entirely unknown or estimated with high error, the embedding introduces incorrect geometric bias, harming performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Camera-aware geometric augmentation forces the model to learn invariant spatial reasoning by exposing it to a diverse virtual distribution of camera intrinsics during training, preventing overfitting to any single sensor setup.
- **Mechanism:** During training, randomly sample scaling factor s and principal-point shift (Δu, Δv). Apply resize-and-pad/crop to the image and update intrinsics: (f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y) → (s f_x, s f_y, s c_x - Δu, s c_y - Δv).
- **Core assumption:** Generalization requires explicit training on varied intrinsics; natural datasets have insufficient diversity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "applying camera-aware geometric augmentation to synthetically vary intrinsics during training"; [section:4.3] Fig. 5 illustrates the augmentation pipeline.
- **Break condition:** If augmentation hyperparameters do not cover test-time shifts or are not applied consistently to both image and intrinsics, the model remains fragile.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Distilling geometric priors from a pre-trained monocular metric depth estimation (MMDE) model provides dense 3D structural cues that compensate for lack of explicit 3D supervision, especially when camera intrinsics are unknown.
- **Mechanism:** For each image, run a frozen UniDepth v2 model to predict a dense point cloud (implied depth + intrinsics estimation). Embed this into E_geo and add to visual features.
- **Core assumption:** An MMDE model trained on massive cross-camera data embeds reliable geometric universals; distilling these transfers invariance to the MLLM without 3D annotations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "distilling geometric priors from a pre-trained monocular depth model"; [section:4.4] Describes using UniDepth v2 to predict point clouds and fuse E_geo.
- **Break condition:** If the MMDE model is trained on biased data or produces systematic errors, these inaccuracies are distilled and could mislead the MLLM.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Pinhole Camera Projection Model**
  - Why needed here: It formalizes the core ambiguity (Eq. 1,6). Without understanding how focal length, depth, and object size interact in projection, the paper's argument about irresolvability is inaccessible.
  - Quick check question: Given two images of the same object with different apparent sizes, what camera parameters could explain the difference if object size and distance are unknown?
- **Concept: Monocular Metric Depth Estimation (MMDE) Challenges**
  - Why needed here: The paper draws a direct analogy from MMDE's solution (intrinsic conditioning) to MLLMs. Knowing why MMDE failed before Metric3D/UniDepth contextualizes the novelty.
  - Quick check question: Why does canonicalizing image resolution (e.g., rescaling to fixed focal length) help but is insufficient for general MLLM training?
- **Concept: Data Augmentation as Synthetic Distribution Shift**
  - Why needed here: The paper's augmentation is not standard image augmentation but geometric transformation of the camera model. This concept distinguishes it from typical augmentation.
  - Quick check question: How does applying a random resize to an image correspond to changes in the camera's intrinsic matrix?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Image -> 2D Visual Encoder (ViT) -> F_vis -> + Camera Ray Embedding (from intrinsics) -> + Geometric Prior Distillator (from UniDepth v2) -> Modified Visual Tokens -> Projection Layer -> LLM Core (Qwen2.5-VL) -> Output
- **Critical path:** Image input → 2D Encoder → add Ray Emb → add Prior Dist → Project → LLM → output. The ray embedding step is the minimal insertion point that fundamentally changes geometry handling.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Efficiency vs. Generality: Using a frozen MMDE adds forward-pass cost but avoids needing intrinsics for every training image. A purely learned ray embedding would be lighter but might struggle without 3D supervision.
  - Dense vs. Sparse Conditioning: The paper injects per-token embeddings; a global camera embedding would not resolve per-pixel ray ambiguity.
  - Augmentation Range: Wider synthetic intrinsic ranges improve robustness but risk unrealistic geometries that confuse semantic learning.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Predictions systematically scale with image resize (e.g., all depths halved when image doubled).
  - Performance drops sharply on datasets with focal lengths outside training distribution.
  - Ablation removing any one component causes significant degradation on resized/eval sets.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Catastrophe:** Train a camera-agnostic MLLM on ScanNet, evaluate on ScanNet-val ×0.8/×1.2. Expect large F1 drop. Confirms sensitivity to intrinsic shift.
  2. **Cross-Camera Generalization:** Train on mixed-source data (ScanNet, ARKitScenes, etc.). Evaluate on each held-out dataset. Camera-agnostic baselines show unpredictable performance; camera-aware model maintains robust F1.
  3. **Ablation on Resized Test:** Train variants with (a) no additions, (b) only Ray Emb, (c) only Aug, (d) only Prior, (e) all three. Test on ScanNet-val ×1.2. Expect only full combination reaches ~52% F1@0.25. Validates necessity of all three.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** What are the operational limits of camera-aware geometric augmentation in terms of intrinsic parameter variation ranges before it causes negative transfer?
  - Basis: Camera-aware augmentation is core to the framework and ablation confirms its necessity.
  - Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates its benefit but does not characterize the boundary between beneficial and harmful augmentation intensities.
- **Question:** How sensitive is the overall performance to the fidelity of geometric priors distilled from the monocular depth model?
  - Basis: Geometric prior distillation is a key component and required per ablation.
  - Why unresolved: The framework relies on a single depth model (UniDepth v2); errors in its predictions may propagate and degrade spatial reasoning.
- **Question:** When camera intrinsics must be estimated rather than provided, how robust is the framework to estimation inaccuracies in real-world scenarios?
  - Basis: The framework uses a pre-trained MMDE to estimate intrinsics for images without them, but error tolerance is unexplored.
  - Why unresolved: Many internet images lack reliable EXIF data; poor intrinsic estimates could lead to failures.
- **Question:** Is the proposed camera-aware architecture sufficient for spatial intelligence in dynamic environments or interactive tasks requiring temporal reasoning and action?
  - Basis: The paper evaluates on static or short-video spatially-grounded tasks, but not on embodied AI or long-horizon planning.
  - Why unresolved: Dynamic scenes introduce motion, occlusion, and temporal dependencies not addressed by the current design.

## Limitations
- Reliance on a pre-trained monocular depth model (UniDepth v2) introduces potential bias; if its training distribution lacks certain camera types, those biases transfer to the MLLM.
- Requirement for camera intrinsics (or a robust estimator) is strict; in-the-wild images often lack reliable intrinsics, and estimation errors may degrade performance.
- Evaluation focuses on resizing shifts and limited benchmarks; generalization to extreme camera geometries (e.g., fisheye, ultra-wide) remains untested.
- Computational overhead from the dense ray embedding and frozen MMDE forward pass is not analyzed for real-time feasibility.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1:** Ignoring intrinsics creates an irresolvable depth-focal ambiguity, causing catastrophic cross-camera degradation. **Confidence: High** – rooted in pinhole geometry and empirically supported by consistent performance drops on resized inputs.
- **Claim 2:** The three-component Camera-Aware MLLM achieves robust cross-camera generalization. **Confidence: Medium-High** – strong cross-dataset results, but heavy reliance on UniDepth’s priors makes it unclear whether the MLLM itself learns invariant reasoning.
- **Claim 3:** All three components (ray embedding, augmentation, distillation) are individually necessary. **Confidence: Medium** – ablations show clear drops when any is removed, but interactions and potential redundancy across tasks are not fully explored.

## Next Checks
1. **Test on extreme intrinsics:** Evaluate the trained model on images from cameras with focal lengths/principal points far outside the augmentation range (e.g., ≤300 px or ≥2000 px focal lengths) to quantify failure boundaries.
2. **Distillation-only ablation:** Remove the geometric prior (E_geo) and drastically widen augmentation (e.g., scale 0.5–2.0, larger shifts) to see if augmentation alone can match the full model’s robustness; this isolates the unique value of the MMDE prior.
3. **Intrinsics noise sensitivity:** With ground-truth intrinsics available (e.g., ScanNet), inject Gaussian noise (varying σ) into focal lengths and principal points before ray embedding; measure F1 drop to establish tolerance thresholds for estimated intrinsics.
