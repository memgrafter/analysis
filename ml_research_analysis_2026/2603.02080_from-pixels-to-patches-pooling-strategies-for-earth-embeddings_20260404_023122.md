# From Pixels to Patches: Pooling Strategies for Earth Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02080
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02080
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Stats pooling (min/max/mean/std) improves spatial-split accuracy by up to +10% over mean pooling and reduces geographic generalization gap by over 50%; GeM pooling gives +5% improvement without dimensionality increase.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of aggregating dense pixel embeddings from geospatial foundation models into patch-level representations for tasks with coarse labels, where mean pooling discards useful within-patch variability and performance degrades under spatial distribution shift. The authors create EuroSAT-Embed, a dataset of 81,000 patch embeddings from three foundation models (AlphaEarth, OlmoEarth, Tessera), and benchmark 13 pooling methods under random and geographically disjoint splits. They find that distributional statistics (e.g., min/max/mean/std) significantly improve robustness: stats pooling achieves up to +10% higher spatial-split accuracy than mean pooling and reduces the geographic generalization gap by over 50%. Generalized Mean (GeM) pooling provides a +5% improvement without increasing dimensionality. These gains hold across all embedding sources and are more pronounced for higher-dimensional embeddings. The work provides practical guidance for leveraging frozen geospatial embeddings in downstream tasks.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 13 pooling operators (training-free and parametric) on the EuroSAT-Embed dataset. For each foundation model, 64×64 pixel embeddings are extracted for EuroSAT patches. Each pooling method is applied to the spatial dimensions of the embedding tensor to produce a fixed-dimensional patch vector. These vectors serve as input to downstream probes: a linear logistic regression classifier trained via L-BFGS on random-split training data, and a kNN classifier (k=5, cosine distance) that requires no training. Performance is measured by classification accuracy on random and geographically disjoint (longitude-based) test splits, as well as the generalization gap between them. The pooling methods include simple statistics (mean, max, std, min), composite statistics (stats, mean+std, percentiles), covariance matrix flattening, center-weighted mean, Generalized Mean (GeM), and two fitted methods (PCA, Bag-of-Visual-Words). All embeddings remain frozen; the focus is on the pooling operator as a drop-in component for frozen embedding products.

## Key Results
- Stats pooling (min/max/mean/std) improves spatial-split accuracy by up to +10% over mean pooling and reduces geographic generalization gap by over 50%.
- GeM pooling yields a +5% improvement without increasing embedding dimensionality.
- Gains from distributional statistics are larger for higher-dimensional embeddings (e.g., Tessera 512-d) than lower-dimensional ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Distributional Statistics Preserve Within-Patch Heterogeneity
Pooling methods that retain distributional heterogeneity (e.g., min/max/mean/std) reduce the geographic generalization gap compared to mean pooling. Mean pooling discards second-order and higher moments of the pixel embedding distribution within a patch, while class-discriminative signals for land-cover types may reside in variance or extremes (e.g., mixed urban neighborhoods show higher embedding variance than homogeneous forest patches). By concatenating statistics, the pooled vector retains a multi-dimensional summary of the embedding distribution, making it robust to spatial shifts where the absolute mean embedding changes but relative dispersion patterns remain class-identifying. The core assumption is that intra-patch embedding heterogeneity is semantically meaningful and partially consistent across geographic regions for the same land-cover class. Evidence: stats pooling reaches 88–91% on spatial splits versus 76–84% for mean pooling (average +7–10% gain) and reduces the gap by over 50% (Section 5). The mechanism draws inspiration from image retrieval literature (Radenović et al., 2018). A break condition is if within-patch variance is primarily noise (sensor artifacts, transient clouds) rather than class semantics, in which case statistics may amplify noise.

### Mechanism 2: Generalized Mean (GeM) Adaptive Interpolation
GeM pooling offers a near-optimal tradeoff by adaptively interpolating between mean and max pooling, improving spatial-split accuracy without increasing dimensionality. GeM computes \((1/HW) \sum_{i=1}^{HW} x_i^p\)^{1/p} per channel; with exponent \(p>1\) (paper uses \(p=3\)), it emphasizes salient activations while still considering all pixels, capturing both typical and peak semantic features within the patch. The core assumption is that an exponent \(p>1\) exists that optimally balances the robustness of mean pooling with the discriminative power of max pooling for a given GFM's embedding space. Evidence: GeM provides a +5% improvement over mean pooling across all embedding sources with no dimensionality increase (abstract). Break condition: if the optimal \(p\) is highly encoder- or task-specific, a fixed \(p=3\) may be suboptimal; however, the paper notes per-encoder tuning showed only marginal differences.

### Mechanism 3: Dimensionality Amplifies Heterogeneity Signal
Higher-dimensional embeddings (e.g., Tessera's 512-d) benefit more from distributional statistics because they likely encode finer-grained, multi-faceted semantic features per pixel, leading to greater intra-patch heterogeneity for a given land-cover class. Simple mean pooling aggressively compresses this high-dimensional heterogeneity into a single point, losing more signal. Richer statistics (e.g., std, min/max) are better equipped to summarize high-dimensional dispersion, capturing class-discriminative patterns lost by mean. Core assumption: higher-dimensional embeddings contain more class-relevant spatial heterogeneity that is discarded by mean pooling. Evidence: Tessera (512-d) shows the largest generalization gap under mean pooling (12%) while the lower-dimensional AEF (64-d) shows smaller gaps; stats pooling yields the largest absolute gains for Tessera (abstract, Section 5). Break condition: if high-dimensional embeddings are over-parameterized or contain redundant/noise dimensions, stats pooling may overfit to noise variance, especially with limited training data.

## Foundational Learning
**Spatial Autocorrelation & Geographic Distribution Shift**  
*Why needed:* The paper's spatial split (partitioning by longitude) mitigates train-test leakage caused by spatial autocorrelation—nearby locations having similar embeddings due to shared geography/climate, not just class label. Mean pooling's failure under this split suggests it is sensitive to this autocorrelation structure.  
*Quick check:* Why would a model trained on West African cropland patches fail when tested on Southeast Asian croplands, even though the label is the same? How might a stats-pooled embedding be more robust to this?

**Input-Label Resolution Mismatch**  
*Why needed:* The core problem is aggregating 64×64 pixel embeddings (10m resolution) into a single vector for a patch-level label that aggregates information over a larger area (e.g., 640m). This mismatch means the label is effectively a zonal statistic over many pixels, making the choice of pooling function a direct modeling decision about which zonal statistic (mean, variance, etc.) is predictive.  
*Quick check:* If your label is "annual crop" for a 640m patch, why is the variance of pixel embeddings within that patch potentially more informative than the mean?

**Training-Free vs. Parametric Pooling**  
*Why needed:* The paper contrasts fixed, training-free operators (mean, stats) with methods that require fitting parameters on the training set (PCA, BoVW). This distinction is critical for the "frozen embedding product" use case where the encoder is inaccessible and one must operate on precomputed embeddings.  
*Quick check:* What are the practical implications (storage, reproducibility, overfitting risk) of using a pooling method that requires fitting parameters on the training data versus one that is purely a function of the input patch?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Embedding Loader → Pooling Operator → Probe/Classifier  
**Critical path:** The system reads precomputed pixel embeddings, applies a pooling function to produce a fixed-dimensional patch vector, and feeds this vector to a downstream classifier (kNN or linear probe). No gradients flow back to the frozen embedding encoder.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Accuracy vs. Dimensionality:* Composite statistics (e.g., stats) often outperform mean pooling but increase feature dimension (e.g., 4×), raising memory/storage costs and potentially requiring stronger regularization in linear probes. GeM offers most of the gain with no dimension increase.  
- *Training-Free vs. Fitted:* Training-free methods are immediately portable and less prone to overfitting the train set's spatial structure. Fitted methods (PCA, BoVW) can capture dataset-specific distributions but risk poor generalization to new regions and necessitate storing/versioning fitted parameters.  
- *Encoder Interaction:* Pooling effectiveness varies significantly across foundation models; there is no universal best method—selection must be tailored to the embedding source.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- *Spatial split accuracy collapse:* Mean pooling accuracy often drops >10% from random to spatial split. A robust pooling method should exhibit a smaller gap (e.g., stats pooling gap ~6%).  
- *Parametric method instability:* BoVW achieves 92.3% on AlphaEarth but only 71.1% on OlmoEarth, indicating severe encoder-specific overfitting.  
- *Dimensionality mismatch:* Using high-dimensional outputs (e.g., covariance for 512-d Tessera) without sufficient training data or regularization leads to linear probe overfitting, manifested as a large random-split vs. spatial-split gap or poor kNN performance.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run linear probe with mean pooling on all three EuroSAT-Embed sources under random and spatial splits. Confirm reported base accuracies (e.g., ~80.6% spatial for mean linear probe) and generalization gaps.  
2. **Stats vs. GeM comparison:** For each source, compare stats pooling (4× dimension) and GeM pooling (1×) on the spatial split using linear probe. Verify stats yields higher absolute accuracy while GeM captures most of the improvement without dimension increase.  
3. **Encoder-dependence check:** On OlmoEarth (128-d), evaluate kNN with std pooling alone. Confirm a +11% spatial accuracy gain over mean pooling (75.0% vs 64.0%), demonstrating that for some encoders a single second-order statistic is highly effective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How do pooling strategies generalize to other Earth observation datasets (e.g., BigEarthNet, fMoW) and task formulations (e.g., multi-label classification, semantic segmentation) beyond single-label patch classification?  
*Basis:* The Limitations state evaluation was limited to EuroSAT (10 classes) with three embedding sources; broader coverage is needed.  
*Why unresolved:* No experiments on other datasets or tasks; it is unclear whether conclusions hold for different label structures, spatial granularities, or class distributions.

**Open Question 2:** Can learned pooling methods (e.g., attention-based aggregation, adaptive GeM) further improve performance over training-free statistical pooling, and what are the trade-offs in terms of labeled data requirements and overfitting?  
*Basis:* The Limitations propose that learned methods may improve but require task-specific training.  
*Why unresolved:* Only training-free and simple parametric methods were benchmarked; attention or adaptive GeM could capture non-linear interactions but may need more downstream data and risk overfitting, especially under spatial shift.

**Open Question 3:** What properties of geospatial foundation model embeddings (e.g., dimensionality, training objective, feature distribution) cause the observed variation in optimal pooling strategies across AlphaEarth, OlmoEarth, and Tessera?  
*Basis:* Results show strong encoder-specific patterns (e.g., BoVW excels on AlphaEarth but fails on OlmoEarth; Tessera gains most from stats), but the paper does not analyze underlying mechanisms.  
*Why unresolved:* Without understanding why encoders respond differently, it is difficult to predict or select the best pooling for a new GFM. No causal analysis of embedding characteristics (intrinsic dimensionality, clusterability, intra-class variance) is provided.

**Open Question 4:** How sensitive are pooling methods to their hyperparameters (e.g., GeM’s exponent p, number/positions of percentiles, covariance regularization), and can robust defaults be found that work across encoders and splits?  
*Basis:* The paper fixes hyperparameters (e.g., GeM p=3) based on preliminary sweeps but does not report the range explored; methods like percentiles or covariance have tunable aspects that could affect performance.  
*Why unresolved:* Practitioners need to know if small hyperparameter changes significantly alter results and whether a single configuration generalizes across models. The claim of marginal differences may be limited to their sweeps.

## Limitations
- Unspecified geographic split boundaries and covariance pooling implementation details hinder direct replication.  
- Corpus evidence for the proposed mechanisms is weak; the analysis relies on analogies to image retrieval literature rather than direct geospatial studies.  
- The core assumption that intra-patch heterogeneity is consistent across geographic regions remains untested.

## Confidence
- Stats pooling reduces geographic generalization gap: **High** (supported by consistent EuroSAT-Embed benchmarks across three GFMs).  
- GeM provides optimal 1× tradeoff: **Medium** (paper shows gains, but corpus support absent; optimal exponent p may be encoder/task-specific).  
- Higher-dimensional embeddings benefit more: **Medium** (evident in results, but could reflect noise amplification without sufficient data).

## Next Checks
1. **Split verification:** Obtain or reconstruct the precise longitude-based spatial split from EuroSAT-Embed metadata/Ekim et al. (2025) to confirm no leakage and reproduce reported gaps.  
2. **Covariance pooling audit:** Clarify whether covariance is computed across pixels per channel pair and the flattening order (row/column-major); test sensitivity to numerical stabilization (e.g., adding ϵ).  
3. **Encoder-tuning scope:** Run a fine-grained sweep of GeM exponent p (e.g., [1,2,3,4,5]) per GFM for both kNN and linear probes to validate the claim that p=3 is near-universally optimal.
