# Multi-Token Prediction via Self-Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06019  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06019  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Converts a pretrained autoregressive LM into a multi-token predictor via online self-distillation, achieving >3× speedup on GSM8K with <5% accuracy drop using confidence-adaptive decoding.

## Executive Summary
Autoregressive language models suffer from slow inference due to their token-by-token generation. This paper introduces a method to convert any pretrained model into a multi-token predictor without auxiliary components, using an online self-distillation objective. A frozen teacher (initialized from the same checkpoint) scores sequences generated by a student model within a single forward pass, forcing the student’s output distribution to collapse and emit coherent multi-token blocks. At inference, a confidence-adaptive decoder dynamically selects how many tokens to emit per step based on prediction confidence. On GSM8K, this yields more than 3× faster decoding with under 5% relative accuracy drop; aggressive thresholds achieve up to 5× speedup at higher accuracy cost, with positive transfer to other reasoning and generation benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The approach transforms a pretrained single-token autoregressive model into a multi-token predictor (MTP) by adding special `<MTP>` mask tokens to the vocabulary and training with an online self-distillation loss. During training, a frozen teacher and a student are initialized from the same checkpoint. For each sequence, random positions and chunk sizes $k \in [2, 16]$ are chosen; $k-1$ `<MTP>` tokens are inserted after the prefix token, and subsequent ground-truth tokens have their position IDs adjusted to skip the masks. A blocked causal attention mask ensures tokens within an MTP region attend causally only to earlier tokens in that region and to all previous ground-truth tokens, while standard causality applies elsewhere. The student’s logits at `<MTP>` positions are read via argmax to produce a deterministic $k$-token rollout $y'$. The teacher then scores the joint likelihood of $y'$ using its own next-token distribution. The loss $L_{\text{MTP}} = -P_{\theta_T}(y'|\text{prefix}) \log P_{\theta_S}(y'|\text{prefix})$ is backpropagated only through the student, encouraging its distribution to match the teacher’s argmax sequence. At inference, the student is provided with $k_{\text{max}}$ `<MTP>` tokens; the confidence-adaptive decoder (ConfAdapt) selects the largest $k' \leq k_{\text{max}}$ such that the maximum softmax probability at each of the first $k'$ positions exceeds a threshold $\tau$, then emits those $k'$ tokens via argmax. The process repeats until a stop token is generated.

## Key Results
- On GSM8K, MTP models achieve **>3× faster decoding** with **<5% relative accuracy drop** versus the single-token baseline using ConfAdapt ($\tau=0.90$).
- Aggressive confidence thresholds yield **up to 5× speedup** at higher accuracy cost, demonstrating a tunable speed-accuracy trade-off.
- Transfer improvements are observed on other reasoning (BBH) and generation (CNN/DailyMail) benchmarks, confirming the method’s broad applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Online Self-Distillation Collapses Student Distribution
- **Claim:** The online self‑distillation objective with a hard argmax teacher forces the student multi‑token prediction (MTP) model’s output distribution to collapse, enabling deterministic and coherent multi‑token generations without access to ground‑truth future tokens during training.
- **Mechanism:** At each training step, the student generates a sequence of *k* tokens in a single forward pass using an argmax readout (making the rollout deterministic). A frozen teacher model (initialized from the same checkpoint) then scores the joint likelihood of that exact sequence via the chain rule of probability under its own next‑token prediction distribution. The training loss weights the student’s log‑probability of its own rollout by the teacher’s score:  
  $L_{\text{MTP}} = -P_{\theta_T}(\mathbf{y}' \mid \mathbf{x}_{1:i}) \log P_{\theta_S}(\mathbf{y}' \mid \mathbf{x}_{1:i})$.  
  Because the teacher uses an argmax readout (hard distribution), the loss approaches zero only if the student’s distribution places nearly all its mass on the teacher’s argmax sequence, collapsing entropy.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher’s argmax sequence is coherent (high probability under a “good” LM) and the student model has sufficient capacity to match it.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] – “online self‑distillation objective: a student model generates *k* tokens per forward pass, and a frozen teacher scores the joint likelihood of this sequence using a hard argmax, forcing the student’s output distribution to collapse.”  
  [section 3.3] – Formal definition of Equation (3) and discussion that the hard teacher (“argmax”) “lowers the student model’s output entropy more rapidly” and is used for main runs.  
  [section A.3] – Figure 6 empirically correlates lower teacher loss (better quality) with higher student confidence (lower entropy), supporting entropy collapse as a training signal.  
  [corpus] – The citation “Self‑Distillation for Multi‑Token Prediction” (ArXiv 2603.23911) and others explore MTP but the exact online hard‑teacher method is introduced here; corpus evidence for this precise mechanism is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the teacher model is weak or misaligned with high‑quality text (e.g., early in its own training), the student may learn to replicate low‑quality or incoherent argmax outputs. The hard teacher also risks over‑collapsing the student, potentially harming diversity; however the adaptive decoder mitigates this by still sampling from softmax when confidence is low.

### Mechanism 2: Confidence‑Adaptive Decoding Leverages Correlation Between Confidence and Correctness
- **Claim:** Token‑level prediction confidence (maximum softmax probability) strongly correlates with correctness in reasoning tasks. Consequently, dynamically increasing the number of tokens decoded per forward pass when confidence exceeds a threshold yields a Pareto‑optimal speed‑accuracy trade‑off.
- **Mechanism:** During inference, the student predicts up to *k*<sub>max</sub> tokens. For each position *j* in that span, the maximum predicted probability *p*<sub>*j*</sub> is computed. The effective *k′* is chosen as the largest integer ≤ *k*<sub>max</sub> such that *p*<sub>*j*</sub> > *τ* for all *j* ≤ *k′*. All *k′* tokens are then emitted via argmax. The next forward pass operates on the new prefix (without the mask tokens), repeating the process. Higher *τ* means more conservative multi‑token emission, preserving accuracy but reducing speedup; lower *τ* increases speed at some accuracy cost.
- **Core assumption:** The top‑1 probability at a given position is a reliable indicator that the token (and its neighbors in the same block) will be correct under autoregressive evaluation.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] – “confidence‑adaptive decoder (ConfAdapt) dynamically sets *k* based on token prediction confidence.”  
  [section 4.6] – Detailed description of ConfAdapt and Figure 5 showing that ConfAdapt achieves pareto‑optimal trade‑offs across thresholds.  
  [section A.3] – Figure 6 plots teacher loss (quality) against student confidence; strong negative correlation is observed, empirically justifying the use of confidence as a proxy.  
  [corpus] – Related works like Kang et al. (2025) and Fu et al. (2025) use confidence for best‑of‑*n* reweighting, but ConfAdapt’s specific application to MTP is novel; corpus evidence for this exact adaptive scheme is absent.
- **Break condition:** On tasks where confidence is poorly calibrated (e.g., overconfidence on wrong tokens), aggressive *τ* values could cause large accuracy drops. The correlation also may not hold for tasks with highly entropic outputs (e.g., open‑ended generation), as seen in lower acceleration on CNN DailyMail.

### Mechanism 3: Hard Teacher Distribution Enforces Strict Entropy Reduction
- **Claim:** Using an argmax readout for the teacher (hard distribution) creates an almost‑delta target distribution, which forces the student’s MTP distribution to collapse more efficiently than using full softmax probabilities (soft teacher), thereby improving the speed‑accuracy trade‑off.
- **Mechanism:** In the loss, $P_{\theta_T}(\mathbf{y}' \mid \text{prefix})$ is computed via either softmax (soft) or argmax (hard). The hard teacher yields a reward of 1 only if the student’s argmax rollout exactly matches the teacher’s argmax rollout, and 0 otherwise (ignoring scaling). This sparse, high‑variance signal strongly incentivizes the student to put all probability mass on that exact sequence, rapidly reducing entropy. The soft teacher provides a smooth reward proportional to the teacher’s softmax probabilities, which can be spread across multiple plausible token sequences, slowing entropy collapse.
- **Core assumption:** Matching the teacher’s argmax rollout is sufficient for high‑quality generation; the teacher’s argmax is stable and coherent.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.3] – Explicitly defines the “hard teacher” variation and states “we adopt it for our main training runs.”  
  [section A.6] – Ablation comparing “Hard Teacher” (main) vs “Soft Teacher” shows the hard version yields better accuracy for a given acceleration (Tables 4–5).  
  [corpus] – The concept aligns with general entropy minimization findings (e.g., Wang et al., 2021; Agarwal et al., 2025), but the direct application to MTP via a hard teacher is not evidenced in the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the teacher’s argmax is not the highest‑quality sequence (e.g., due to teacher’s own limitations or dataset biases), the student may learn a suboptimal distribution. Additionally, the hard objective might be too strict, causing training instability if the student struggles to match the teacher.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Autoregressive next‑token prediction and its sequential inference bottleneck.  
  **Why needed here:** Understanding the baseline problem (one forward pass per token) explains why MTP—predicting multiple tokens per pass—can reduce latency, but only if the multi‑token predictions are coherent.  
  **Quick check question:** “If decoding *N* tokens with an MTP model that can emit up to *k* tokens per forward pass, what is the maximum number of forward passes required? Under what condition would it be fewer?”  
  **Answer:** At most ⌈*N* / *k*⌉ forward passes, achieved if every step emits exactly *k* tokens.

- **Concept:** Knowledge distillation with on‑policy rollouts (online distillation).  
  **Why needed here:** The method is fundamentally a distillation process where the student learns from a frozen teacher, but crucially uses the student’s own generative proposals rather than a fixed dataset, avoiding distribution shift.  
  **Quick check question:** “What is the key difference between offline and online language model distillation as used in this paper?”  
  **Answer:** Offline distillation uses a static dataset of teacher outputs; online distillation generates the student’s rollouts on‑policy, which are then scored by the teacher, ensuring the training distribution matches the deployment distribution.

- **Concept:** Confidence calibration of language models and its relationship to generation correctness.  
  **Why needed here:** ConfAdapt relies on the hypothesis that higher maximum predicted probability correlates with correct token sequences. Without this correlation, adaptive chunking would not reliably preserve accuracy.  
  **Quick check question:** “Why might a model’s confidence (top‑1 softmax probability) be higher for correct reasoning steps in grade‑school math than for incorrect ones?”  
  **Answer:** Correct reasoning steps are more predictable given the context, leading to sharper (higher‑probability) predictions; incorrect steps involve less predictable or contradictory continuations, yielding lower probabilities.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Teacher LM (frozen) -> Student MTP LM (trainable) -> ConfAdapt Decoder (inference)  
  Teacher LM: Copy of pretrained model; computes next‑token probabilities via softmax.  
  Student MTP LM: Same architecture as teacher with added `<MTP>` embeddings; predicts multiple tokens per forward pass.  
  ConfAdapt Decoder: Dynamically selects chunk size based on max‑probability threshold $\tau$.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. **Initialization:** Load pretrained checkpoint into both teacher and student; freeze teacher. Randomly initialize `<MTP>` token embedding from existing stats.  
  2. **Batch preparation:** Tokenize text; for each sequence, sample random MTP regions with random offsets and $k \in [2, 16]$. Insert $k-1$ `<MTP>` tokens after each prefix token and adjust position IDs for subsequent ground‑truth tokens. Construct blocked causal attention mask (standard outside MTP regions; causal within each MTP region).  
  3. **Training step:** Forward pass through student → obtain logits at all positions. For each MTP region, materialize $y' = \text{argmax}$ over the $k$ logits. Compute teacher’s joint likelihood: sequentially condition teacher on prefix + $y'$, multiplying per‑token softmax probabilities. Compute loss $L_{\text{MTP}} = - \log P_{\theta_S}(y') \cdot P_{\theta_T}(y')$. Back‑propagate only on student.  
  4. **Inference loop:** Start with user prompt. While not terminated: append $k_{\text{max}}$ `<MTP>` tokens to current sequence. Forward pass → get logits for the $k_{\text{max}}$ positions. Compute max probabilities; find largest $k' \leq k_{\text{max}}$ where all max probs > $\tau$. Emit $k'$ argmax tokens; remove all `<MTP>` tokens from KV cache; append emitted tokens to sequence; check stop tokens.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Hard vs soft teacher:** Hard teacher accelerates entropy collapse but may be harsher; soft teacher provides smoother gradients but underperforms in ablations.  
  - **Static *k* vs ConfAdapt:** Fixed *k* is simpler but ConfAdapt yields pareto‑optimal curves across speed‑accuracy.  
  - **Randomized *k* during training:** Exposes model to various chunk sizes, essential for ConfAdapt at inference; static *k* training leads to poorer results.  
  - **Bidirectional attention in MTP regions:** Bi‑directional theoretically gives more context but introduces distribution shift from inference (causal), and ablations show negligible gain.  
  - **Auxiliary NTP loss on prefix positions:** Adding standard next‑token loss on non‑MTP positions did not help (Table 5).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Incoherent multi‑token blocks:** Student entropy has not collapsed (e.g., early training); argmax tokens at different positions may be grammatically incompatible. Diagnose by checking per‑position max probabilities – uniformly low/medium indicates high entropy.  
  - **ConfAdapt mis‑calibration:** Confidence not correlated with correctness (e.g., on OOD tasks); decoder either never selects *k* > 1 (no speedup) or selects large *k* with severe accuracy drop. Plot confidence vs. correctness on validation set.  
  - **Teacher mismatch:** Weak teacher (low‑capability base LM) yields noisy argmax sequences, slowing convergence or causing degenerate outputs.  
  - **KV cache mishandling:** Forgetting to remove `<MTP>` tokens leads to attention over obsolete mask tokens, corrupting context.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **GSM8K speed‑accuracy:** Models trained on MetaMathQA with random $k \in [2,16]$ and hard teacher achieve >3× acceleration with <5% accuracy drop on GSM8K using ConfAdapt $\tau=0.90$ (L3.1‑8B‑Magpie: 3.3×, 64.1% vs 66.0% k=1).  
  2. **Teacher hardness ablation:** Replacing hard teacher with soft teacher reduces effective *k* at comparable accuracy (e.g., hard $\tau=0.90$ gives ~3.3×, soft gives ≤ 2.0×).  
  3. **Confidence‑quality correlation:** Plotting average student confidence vs teacher loss on validation set yields strong negative correlation ($r \approx -0.9$), confirming confidence as a quality proxy (Fig 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Entropy reduction methods:** What training methods can effectively reduce the output entropy of MTP models to near zero, enabling longer coherent token sequences per forward pass? The current online self‑distillation objective may not be optimal; alternatives like explicit entropy penalties or a second stage of online RL are proposed.
2. **Controlled stochasticity:** How can controlled stochasticity be incorporated into MTP models to maintain output diversity without sacrificing the speed‑accuracy tradeoff? The tension between low entropy requirements and modeling highly entropic natural text suggests future work on stochastic readouts or noise injection.
3. **Self-speculative decoding:** Can MTP models achieve lossless acceleration by verifying their own multi-token predictions through self‑speculative decoding? The authors propose using the model’s own proposals as drafts verified in subsequent passes.
4. **Alternative architectures:** Do alternative architectural designs for multi‑token prediction outperform the simple mask token extension in terms of training efficiency and inference speed‑accuracy tradeoff? Variants like separate prediction heads or parallel token modules (e.g., Medusa) remain unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Teacher quality dependence:** Student training hinges on the teacher’s argmax sequence quality; a weak or misaligned teacher yields poor targets.
- **Confidence calibration:** The ConfAdapt heuristic assumes confidence strongly correlates with correctness, which degrades on entropic/open‑ended tasks (e.g., CNN/DailyMail shows lower speedup).
- **Under‑specification:** Implementation details (tokenization, attention mask) are under‑specified, hindering exact reproduction; multi‑GPU requirements may limit accessibility.
- **No theoretical guarantee:** Entropy collapse is empirical; no proof that the objective forces zero entropy, and over‑collapsing may harm diversity or few‑shot performance.

## Confidence
- **Online self‑distribution collapses entropy:** Medium (Fig 6 shows confidence loss correlation but entropy not directly measured).  
- **Confidence‑adaptive decoding works:** High (strong empirical evidence across thresholds and tasks).  
- **Hard teacher superiority:** Medium (ablations favor hard teacher but limited to two datasets).

## Next Checks
1. **Teacher strength sensitivity:** Train students with teachers of varying sizes (e.g., 1B vs 8B) to map speed‑accuracy trade‑off dependence on teacher capability.
2. **Calibration on diverse benchmarks:** Evaluate max‑probability vs. correctness on tasks like HellaSwag and TruthfulQA to delineate ConfAdapt’s applicability boundaries.
3. **Track entropy during training:** Directly measure student output entropy (e.g., via held‑out samples) to confirm collapse and detect over‑collapsing.
