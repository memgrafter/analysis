# NextAds: Towards Next-generation Personalized Video Advertising

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02137  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02137  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** NextAds (closed-source) significantly improves personalization metrics (e.g., presentation score 7.12 vs. 1.77 for GenericAds on PCG-Bench) while maintaining video quality and product alignment, with noted trade‑offs in diversity and PCI product alignment.

## Executive Summary
NextAds introduces a paradigm shift from static retrieval‑based video advertising to a generation‑driven approach powered by GenAI. It conceptualizes a modular pipeline: Director (planning), Producer (generation), Verifier (utility/compliance checks), and Reflector (feedback‑driven refinement). Two concrete tasks are formalized—Personalized Creative Generation (PCG) and Personalized Creative Integration (PCI)—along with lightweight benchmarks (PCG‑Bench, PCI‑Bench). Feasibility experiments show that dynamic generation can produce highly personalized, high‑quality video ads, outperforming generic baselines. The work establishes a foundational framework for closed‑loop GenAI advertising, while highlighting challenges in diversity, scalability, and component completeness.

## Method Summary
NextAds targets two personalized video advertising tasks: PCG generates standalone video ads tailored to a user and product; PCI seamlessly embeds an ad into a host video. The system comprises four modules: Director (creates a CreativePlan from user data, product details, and serving context), Producer (generates video using a generative model conditioned on the plan), Verifier (evaluates quality, personalization, alignment, and compliance), and Reflector (uses verification rationales and online feedback to issue minimal revisions). A closed‑loop feedback mechanism allows iterative improvement across impressions. Benchmarks are built from Qilin and MicroLens datasets, with evaluation via a VLM judge (Gemini 2.5‑Flash) and VBench video quality metrics.

## Key Results
- **Large personalization improvement:** On PCG‑Bench, NextAds achieves a presentation score of 7.12 vs. 1.77 for the GenericAds baseline.
- **Quality and alignment maintained:** Generated videos meet high standards for visual quality and product representation.
- **Trade‑offs observed:** PCG shows a slight drop in diversity; PCI under low user‑product compatibility yields reduced product alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Shift to Dynamic Generation in Continuous Space**  
Instead of selecting from a fixed creative inventory, NextAds uses GenAI to synthesize a novel video at serving time conditioned on user data, product details, and context. This removes capacity constraints and enables infinite variations. Core assumption: modern video generation models can produce high‑fidelity, compliant creatives with sufficient speed; VLMs can accurately infer user preferences. Evidence includes recent progress in diffusion/transformer models and related image/banner generation work. Break condition: If generation quality, latency, or conditioning fidelity falls below platform standards, retrieval remains necessary.

**Mechanism 2: Modular Decomposition for Controllability and Auditability**  
The Director‑Producer‑Verifier‑Reflector structure isolates concerns, provides an interpretable intermediate plan, and reduces error attribution complexity. The Director outputs a structured plan, the Producer executes it, the Verifier performs dual‑track checks (utility and compliance), and the Reflector refines via minimal directives. This improves debugging and enables targeted updates. Core assumption: a structured plan enhances controllability and efficient refinement. Break condition: overly rigid plans or high false rejection rates degrade throughput and personalization.

**Mechanism 3: Closed‑Loop Evolution via Feedback Attribution**  
Post‑deployment user signals (skips, dwell) and pre‑deployment verification outcomes are attributed by the Reflector to distinguish plan‑level from execution‑level issues, triggering focused revisions (e.g., adjusting user profile, timing, or regenerating a segment). Over multiple impressions, the system can vary angles while preserving brand consistency, moving toward a generative campaign policy. Core assumption: user interactions can be reliably mapped to specific creative components for actionable refinements. Break condition: noisy attribution or delayed feedback misdirects refinements, causing creative decay or user fatigue.

## Foundational Learning
- **Generative Video Models (Diffusion, Transformer‑based)**  
  *Why needed:* The Producer component relies on these models to synthesize video from storyboard text and asset references. Understanding multi‑conditioning (text script, product image, style collage) is key.  
  *Quick check:* How do video generation models incorporate several conditioning inputs without overwriting critical details like product identity?

- **Vision‑Language Models (VLMs) for User & Context Modeling**  
  *Why needed:* The Director uses a VLM to infer user preferences (topics, style) from multimodal history and to summarize host videos for PCI.  
  *Quick check:* What risks arise when a VLM infers spurious correlations from watch history (e.g., linking a brand with an unrelated style), leading to inappropriate creative elements?

- **Automated Evaluation with VLMs (LLM‑as‑a‑Judge)**  
  *Why needed:* Benchmarks use a VLM (Gemini 2.5‑Flash) to score personalization, product alignment, integration quality, and diversity, as human evaluation is irreproducible and traditional metrics lack semantic depth.  
  *Quick check:* What biases might a VLM judge introduce when evaluating aesthetic alignment or integration seamlessness, and how can prompts be designed to mitigate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
User/Product/Context → Director → CreativePlan (UserProfile, Storyboard, AssetConstraints) → Producer → CandidateCreative → Verifier → {ACCEPT, REVISE, REJECT}. If REVISE/REJECT → Reflector → RevisionDirectives → Director or Producer → new CandidateCreative.

**Critical path:** The main flow follows the sequence above; the Reflector closes the loop by directing revisions when necessary.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
1. *Personalization vs. Diversity* – Over‑personalization risks creative fatigue and filter bubbles; controlled exploration is needed.  
2. *Generation Quality vs. Latency* – High‑fidelity video generation may exceed sub‑second serving constraints; caching, streaming, or ROI‑driven decisions are required.  
3. *Compliance Strictness vs. Flexibility* – Too strict verifiers reject effective borderline creatives; too lenient risks policy violations; calibrated thresholds and human‑in‑the‑loop are necessary.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Spurious conditioning: VLM injects irrelevant cues (e.g., competitor branding).  
- Forced personalization: Incoherent creatives when user preferences clash with product (e.g., soft aesthetic for a razor).  
- Diversity collapse: Strictly conditioned generation yields visually repetitive creatives.  
- Storyboard fidelity loss: Generated video deviates from planned scenes.  
- PCI integration disruption: Jarring transition or thematic mismatch at insertion point.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Benchmark replication* – Re‑run PCG‑Bench/PCI‑Bench evaluation with prompts and a VLM judge; verify if open‑source models show similar personalization gap.  
2. *Component isolation test* – Replace Director with a generic prompt (no user modeling) and measure personalization score drop to quantify Director’s contribution.  
3. *Failure mode injection* – Force generation on low compatibility pairs (e.g., pastel‑style user + industrial tool) and measure product alignment/integration quality to test fallback necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Robust user modeling:** How to handle noisy implicit feedback and temporal preference dynamics? The paper notes continual user modeling across sequential impressions as a key challenge.  
2. **Personalization‑diversity trade‑off:** How to avoid filter bubbles while maintaining creative effectiveness? The paper observes a slight diversity drop and calls for exploration strategies.  
3. **Verifier/Reflector design:** What architectures effectively ensure utility, compliance, and closed‑loop refinement with minimal latency? These components were left for future work.  
4. **User simulation for utility prediction:** How to design simulators that accurately predict true online utility of generated video ads? Existing simulators for recommenders don’t directly apply.  
5. **Targeting and auction redesign:** How should ad mechanisms jointly optimize with generation cost and expected lift? Traditional retrieval‑first auctions need augmentation for real‑time generation costs.

## Limitations
- **Implementation fidelity:** Exact prompts, generation hyperparameters, and PCI insertion mechanics are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- **System completeness:** Verifier and Reflector were not implemented in the feasibility study; their real impact on utility, compliance, and closed‑loop learning remains theoretical.
- **Evaluator bias & scalability:** Reliance on a single VLM judge introduces potential bias; serving‑time generation at platform scale (sub‑second latency, compute cost) is an unproven operational challenge.
