# Learning Image-Text Matching with Optimal Partial Transport

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14349
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14349
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: State-of-the-art image-text retrieval on Flickr30K and MS-COCO with ~2% R@1 improvement over prior methods using partial transport.

## Executive Summary
Cross-modal image-text matching suffers from redundant alignments and high computational cost. This paper introduces OMIT, a network that employs Optimal Partial Transport with Sinkhorn iterations to compute fine-grained similarities while filtering irrelevant fragments via local-to-global correspondence. OMIT achieves state-of-the-art results on Flickr30K and MS-COCO, notably improving R@1 by ~2% over prior methods. Ablation studies confirm partial matching consistently boosts performance across different feature backbones and marginal conditions. Visualizations show OMIT produces sparse, focused alignment plans, effectively reducing noise. The method maintains efficiency while significantly enhancing retrieval accuracy.

## Method Summary
OMIT uses Partial Sinkhorn Matching to compute cross-modal similarity. Given visual features V (Km×d) and text features T (Ln×d), it forms a local cost matrix C = 1 − V Tᵀ and extends it with global features v and t via dustbins. The transport plan is computed with Sinkhorn iterations (λ=0.02, T≈3) under partial marginals (ε=0.1 dustbin weight). Similarity is derived only from local fragment alignments. Training uses hinge-based triplet loss with hard negative mining, AdamW optimizer, and a two-stage learning rate schedule. The method achieves robust retrieval performance with sparse, focused alignments.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art R@1, R@5, R@10 on Flickr30K and MS-COCO datasets
- Improves R@1 by ~2% over prior methods
- Ablation studies confirm partial matching consistently boosts performance
- Produces sparse, focused alignment plans that reduce noise

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
OMIT addresses the inefficiency of dense alignment in cross-modal matching by using partial transport. By introducing dustbin-based global features and solving a relaxed transport problem, it filters out irrelevant fragments and focuses computation on salient correspondences. This selective alignment reduces noise and computational cost while preserving or improving retrieval accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Optimal Transport**: A framework for comparing probability distributions by minimizing transport cost. Why needed: Provides a principled similarity measure for cross-modal alignment. Quick check: Verify that transport cost matrices are correctly constructed from feature embeddings.
- **Sinkhorn Algorithm**: An iterative method to approximate optimal transport plans via entropy regularization. Why needed: Enables efficient computation of transport plans for large matrices. Quick check: Monitor convergence and numerical stability during iterations.
- **Partial Transport**: Extends optimal transport by allowing mass to be discarded to dustbins. Why needed: Filters irrelevant fragments in image-text pairs. Quick check: Ensure dustbin marginals are correctly set and sparse alignments are produced.
- **Triplet Loss with Hard Negative Mining**: A ranking loss that pushes negative pairs apart while pulling positive pairs together. Why needed: Improves retrieval by focusing on challenging negatives. Quick check: Validate that hard negatives are being selected and losses are updated accordingly.
- **Feature Projection to Common Space**: Mapping visual and text features into a shared 1024-dimensional space. Why needed: Enables meaningful cross-modal similarity computation. Quick check: Confirm feature dimensions match across modalities.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Image Features → Partial Sinkhorn Matching → Similarity → Triplet Loss → Backprop → Updated Features
- **Critical Path**: Feature extraction → partial transport alignment → similarity scoring → ranking loss → parameter update
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Partial transport reduces computation and noise but requires careful tuning of dustbin marginals; dense alignment is more comprehensive but noisier and costlier
- **Failure Signatures**: Noisy or non-sparse transport plans suggest incorrect marginal scaling; unstable Sinkhorn iterations indicate poor λ or T; poor retrieval implies misaligned feature spaces or insufficient hard mining
