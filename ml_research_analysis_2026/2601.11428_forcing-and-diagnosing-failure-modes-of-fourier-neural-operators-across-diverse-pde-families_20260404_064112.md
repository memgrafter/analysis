# Forcing and Diagnosing Failure Modes of Fourier Neural Operators Across Diverse PDE Families
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.11428
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11428
- Reference count: 1
- Primary result: Systematic stress‑testing reveals that FNOs are sensitive to distribution shifts (>10× error), parameter shifts (2–4×), resolution extrapolation (high‑frequency error concentration), and long‑horizon rollouts (compounding errors), while small input perturbations cause minimal impact.

## Executive Summary
Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) have emerged as a powerful class of neural architectures for learning solution operators of partial differential equations (PDEs). Despite their success, understanding their robustness under distribution shifts, resolution changes, long‑term rollouts, and input perturbations is crucial for reliable deployment. This paper addresses this gap by introducing a systematic stress‑testing framework that subjects trained FNOs to controlled perturbations: parameter shifts, boundary/terminal condition changes, resolution extrapolation with spectral analysis, iterative rollouts, and input noise. The study evaluates 1000 models across five PDE families (Nonlinear Schrödinger, Poisson, Navier‑Stokes, Black‑Scholes, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky) and quantifies failure using degradation factors (out‑of‑distribution error divided by in‑distribution baseline). The results provide a comparative atlas of failure modes, showing that FNOs degrade significantly under certain shifts, while remaining stable under others.

Key findings include: distribution shifts (e.g., changing Poisson boundary values or Black‑Scholes payoff discontinuities) inflate errors by over 10×; parameter shifts (e.g., altering nonlinearity or volatility) cause 2–4× increases; resolution extrapolation concentrates errors in high‑frequency modes, confirming spectral bias; iterative rollouts compound errors (e.g., 3.5× for Navier‑Stokes, 1.6× for Kuramoto–Sivashinsky); and small input perturbations generally do not amplify error (degradation ≈1). These insights offer concrete guidance for designing more robust operator learners.

## Method Summary
The experimental pipeline begins by training FNOs on each PDE family under a fixed in‑distribution regime (specific parameter ranges, boundary/terminal conditions, and resolutions). After training, the authors apply a battery of stress tests: (1) distribution shifts by modifying boundary conditions or payoff structures; (2) parameter shifts by varying physical parameters like nonlinearity strength or volatility; (3) resolution extrapolation by evaluating the model on higher resolutions and decomposing the error across Fourier modes; (4) iterative rollouts where the model’s own predictions are fed back as inputs over many time steps; and (5) input perturbations by adding small Gaussian noise. Performance is measured via relative error, and a degradation factor is computed for each perturbation type. This process is repeated over 1000 model instances (different random seeds, PDE families, and possibly architectures) to ensure statistical reliability.

## Key Results
- Distribution shifts (e.g., Poisson boundary values, Black‑Scholes discontinuous payoffs) inflate errors by >10×; parameter shifts (e.g., NLS nonlinearity, Black‑Scholes volatility) cause ~2–4× increase.
- Resolution extrapolation concentrates error in high‑frequency modes (spectral bias); iterative rollouts compound errors (Navier‑Stokes ~3.5×, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky ~1.6×).
- Small input perturbations yield degradation ≈1, indicating stability to noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The failure modes arise from inherent properties of FNOs and the training data distribution. Spectral bias—the tendency of neural networks to fit low frequencies first—manifests as high‑frequency error spikes when extrapolating to higher resolutions. Iterative rollouts amplify prediction errors because each step relies on the previous output, causing small inaccuracies to accumulate. Distribution shifts expose the model’s reliance on the training manifold; changes in boundary conditions or payoff structures fall outside the learned function space, leading to large degradations. Conversely, small input perturbations do not destabilize the model because the learned operator mapping is locally Lipschitz within the training regime, and the perturbations lie within a regime where the network’s response remains approximately linear.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)**: A neural network that learns mappings between function spaces by operating in Fourier space. It applies FFT to input, performs a linear transformation on low‑frequency modes, then IFFT to produce output. *Why needed*: Core architecture under study. *Quick check*: Does it use FFT? Yes.
- **Spectral bias**: Neural networks preferentially approximate low‑frequency components of a target function. *Why needed*: Explains high‑frequency error growth under resolution extrapolation. *Quick check*: When faced with a multimodal target, does the network fit smooth parts first? Yes.
- **Degradation factor**: Ratio of out‑of‑distribution error to in‑distribution baseline. *Why needed*: Normalized robustness measure. *Quick check*: Is the metric >1 for a perturbation that worsens performance? Yes.
- **Long‑horizon rollout**: Using the model recursively to simulate over many timesteps. *Why needed*: Tests stability and error propagation. *Quick check*: Does error increase with the number of rollout steps? Typically yes.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input -> FFT -> Fourier layer (linear transform on low‑freq modes, with spectral cutoff) -> IFFT -> Output, often with skip connections across multiple blocks.

Critical path: The Fourier layer’s linear operation in frequency domain is the heart of FNO; it approximates the integral kernel of the operator. The spectral cutoff (which frequencies to retain) is a crucial hyperparameter that balances expressivity and stability.

Design tradeoffs: A wider cutoff captures finer details but increases parameters and may harm generalization; a narrower cutoff enforces smoothness but struggles with sharp boundaries. More Fourier layers boost capacity but can exacerbate error compounding in rollouts. Padding choices affect handling of boundary conditions and resolution extrapolation.

Failure signatures: (1) Spike in high‑frequency error when testing on higher resolutions; (2) Sharp performance drop when boundary/terminal conditions differ from training; (3) Error blow‑up over long iterative rollouts; (4) Insensitivity to small input noise (stable degradation ≈1).

First experiments: (1) Train a 1‑D Poisson FNO with fixed Dirichlet boundaries; evaluate with different boundaries to see distribution‑shift failure. (2) Train on a low‑resolution sine wave; test on higher resolutions and plot error vs. frequency to confirm spectral bias. (3) Perform 100‑step rollout on Burgers’ equation; track error growth to observe compounding.

## Limitations
- Reported results aggregate over 1000 models without providing per‑PDE family variance, architecture specifics, or degradation factor distributions.
- Claim that small input perturbations do not amplify error contradicts known adversarial vulnerabilities; the magnitude and type of perturbations used are unclear.
- Mechanism behind boundary/terminal condition failures (e.g., role of spectral cutoff) is not dissected.

## Confidence
- Resolution extrapolation error concentrates in high frequencies: **High**
- Long‑horizon rollouts compound errors: **High**
- Distribution shifts cause >10× degradation: **Medium**
- Small input perturbations yield ≈1 degradation: **Low**
