# Fine-tuning Timeseries Predictors Using Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20063
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20063
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** GRPO RL fine-tuning with the actor-network paradigm and ≥50% layer freezing consistently yields lower MSE/MAE across Financial, Industrials, and Technology datasets, and enables effective cross-sector transfer learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the misalignment between supervised time-series forecasting models and domain-specific constraints (e.g., risk) by proposing reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning. Starting from a pre-trained financial forecaster, the authors backpropagate RL loss using PPO, CMAPPO, and GRPO. They find that using the pre-trained backbone directly as the actor network (outputting forecasts) outperforms a latent representation approach. Among the algorithms, GRPO—which uses group-relative advantage estimation without a separate critic—delivers the most consistent performance improvements, particularly when a majority of backbone layers are frozen. The method also demonstrates successful transfer learning, improving performance on sectors different from the model's original training data.

## Method Summary
The method fine-tunes a pre-trained transformer-based time-series forecaster via RL. The forecaster's backbone is repurposed as the policy network (actor) that directly outputs forecast vectors. The RL environment simulates a sliding window over historical data: the state is a window of features, the action is the forecast, and the reward is a bounded function of forecast error (e.g., `2*exp(-MSE-1)`). Three policy gradient algorithms are benchmarked: standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a critic, Centralized Critic Multi-Agent PPO (CMAPPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) which eliminates the critic by using group-relative advantages. Hyperparameters are tuned, and layer freezing strategies are explored to preserve general features while adapting to new objectives.

## Key Results
*   The **actor-network paradigm** (backbone directly outputs forecasts) reliably outperforms a latent action-head approach across all datasets.
*   **GRPO** achieves the best and most stable post-fine-tuning performance (lowest MSE/MAE), especially when freezing ≥50% of the backbone's layers.
*   **Cross-sector transfer learning** shows significant improvement when models fine-tuned on a sector different from their original supervised training data (e.g., a Financial model adapted for Industrials).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Direct Actor Paradigm Superiority
*   **Claim:** Using the pre-trained backbone as the actor network (directly outputting forecasts) is more effective than using it to produce a latent representation for a separate action head.
*   **Mechanism:** This allows policy gradients to update the backbone's weights directly, preserving its learned temporal features while aligning its output distribution with the RL reward. It avoids optimization complexity and potential misalignment from an intermediary network.
*   **Core Assumption:** The backbone's final layer is dimensionally and functionally suitable for the RL action space (forecast vector).
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "The actor-network paradigm... outperforms a latent representation approach."
    *   [section 4.5]: Table 3 shows actor paradigm improves MSE/MAE across datasets; latent paradigm often underperforms.
*   **Break Condition:** If the backbone's output layer is not configured for the required action distribution (e.g., point forecasts vs. stochastic policy).

### Mechanism 2: GRPO's Critic-Free Stability
*   **Claim:** GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation (without a separate critic network) yields superior fine-tuning performance and stability compared to PPO/CMAPPO.
*   **Mechanism:** GRPO computes advantages relative to a group of actions sampled from the current policy, using only the reward. This eliminates the need to train a separate value network (critic), which can struggle to learn accurate advantages during fine-tuning and destabilize backbone updates.
*   **Core Assumption:** Group-based relative scaling provides a sufficient, implicitly regularized learning signal.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "GRPO consistently yields the best post-fine-tuning performance... especially when freezing ≥50% of backbone layers."
    *   [section 6.1]: GRPO shows consistent improvement/preservation; PPO/CMAPPO often degrade, attributed to critic complexity.
*   **Break Condition:** Highly non-stationary or sparse rewards may make group-relative advantages uninformative, necessitating a critic for bootstrapping.

### Mechanism 3: RL-Guided Transfer Learning
*   **Claim:** RL fine-tuning enables effective transfer learning, improving performance on a sector different from the model's original training data.
*   **Mechanism:** The RL reward function encodes domain-specific constraints (e.g., risk), steering the pre-trained backbone's forecasts toward patterns relevant to the new sector. The backbone's general temporal features transfer, while RL adaptation specializes without needing new labeled forecast data.
*   **Core Assumption:** The pre-trained backbone contains representations generalizable across sectors, and the reward correctly captures the target sector's constraints.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "Transfer learning experiments demonstrate significant improvement when models are fine-tuned on a sector different from their original training data."
    *   [section 6.2]: Tables 6 & 7 show multiple cases (e.g., Financial→Industrials) with MSE/MAE improvement.
*   **Break Condition:** Source and target sectors are too dissimilar (different regimes/feature distributions) or the reward is mis-specified for the target, leading to degradation (seen in some Table 7 exceptions).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
    *   Why needed here: The paper benchmarks and extends PPO (via CMAPPO, GRPO). Understanding its clipped objective, advantage estimation (GAE), and actor-critic structure is essential to grasp the modifications and why GRPO (no critic) differs.
    *   Quick check question: How does the PPO clipping function prevent destructive policy updates, and what role does the critic’s value estimate play in computing advantages?
*   **Concept: Supervised Pre-training & Transfer Learning**
    *   Why needed here: The entire premise starts from a pre-trained supervised forecaster. One must understand how features learned on one dataset/sector might generalize and how fine-tuning adapts them.
    *   Quick check question: What is the difference between feature extraction (freezing layers) and fine-tuning (updating layers), and why might freezing early layers preserve general patterns?
*   **Concept: Time-Series Forecasting Evaluation (MSE, MAE)**
    *   Why needed here: These are the primary metrics for judging fine-tuning success. Understanding their sensitivity to outliers (MAE vs. MSE) helps interpret results like GRPO’s MAE improvements.
    *   Quick check question: In a financial forecasting context with potential extreme events, why might a model with lower MAE but similar MSE be considered more robust?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Pre-trained Backbone (Transformer Forecaster) -> RL Algorithm Wrapper (GRPO/PPO/CMAPPO) -> Environment (Sliding-window simulation). For PPO/CMAPPO: Critic Network (2-layer, 256 hidden) runs parallel to backbone.
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Start with a pre-trained backbone that outputs forecasts of correct dimension.
    2.  Implement the RL environment: state = historical feature window, action = forecast vector, reward = `2 * exp(-MSE(forecast, actual) - 1)`.
    3.  Choose GRPO algorithm. Set hyperparameters: group size = 8, total timesteps = 500k.
    4.  Apply layer freezing: freeze ≥50% of backbone layers (typically earliest layers).
    5.  Run fine-tuning, monitor validation MSE/MAE. Stop if performance degrades or plateaus.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Actor vs. Latent:** Actor is simpler, fewer parameters, direct gradient flow but risks catastrophic forgetting. Latent adds flexibility/exploration but adds hyperparameters and may underperform (Table 3).
    *   **Critic vs. No Critic (GRPO):** PPO/CMAPPO require a well-trained critic for stable advantages; this is difficult in fine-tuning and can hurt performance. GRPO avoids this but may be less sample-efficient in pure RL tasks.
    *   **Reward Shaping:** Bounding reward to [-1,1] (via `2*exp(-MSE-1)`) stabilizes training. Unbounded rewards (e.g., raw negative MSE) can cause gradient explosions.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Performance collapse after a few updates:** Likely due to too high learning rate, insufficient layer freezing, or unbounded/mis-specified reward.
    *   **GRPO underperforms PPO/CMAPPO:** Possibly because the reward is very sparse or the group size is too small for meaningful relative advantage.
    *   **No improvement over backbone:** Either the backbone is already optimal for the new domain, the RL task is ill-defined, or hyperparameters (timesteps, group size) are poor.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline reproduction:** Run the pre-trained backbone on your target dataset (no RL) to establish a reference MSE/MAE.
    2.  **Paradigm verification:** Fine-tune with PPO using both the *actor* and *latent* paradigms on a small subset (e.g., 50k timesteps). Verify actor paradigm matches Table 3’s superiority.
    3.  **Algorithm comparison with freezing:** Fine-tune with PPO, CMAPPO, GRPO on the same data, freezing 50% of backbone layers. Expect GRPO to show best validation performance with lowest variance.

## Limitations
*   The actor paradigm's superiority lacks theoretical justification; its success depends on the backbone's output layer being suitable for the action space.
*   GRPO's advantage is empirically robust but the hypothesis (critic instability) is not proven; no ablation isolates critic quality.
*   Transfer learning success is not guaranteed; notable exceptions in results show degradation when source/target sectors are too dissimilar or reward is mis-specified.
*   The method's effectiveness is contingent on the quality and domain relevance of the pre-trained backbone; a poorly pre-trained or mismatched backbone may not benefit from RL fine-tuning.

## Next Checks
1.  **Ablation on Critic Role:** Run identical fine-tuning with PPO but replace the critic with a *fixed* (pre-trained or random) value network to isolate the effect of critic mis-specification, testing the core GRPO hypothesis.
2.  **Domain Generalization Test:** Apply the exact method (GRPO, actor paradigm, 50% freeze) to a non-financial time-series dataset (e.g., energy demand, healthcare vitals) to validate transfer claims beyond financial/industrial/tech sectors.
3.  **Reward Sensitivity & Stability:** Empirically measure gradient norms and update stability across the three algorithms using the paper's `2*exp(-MSE-1)` reward versus an unbounded `-MSE` reward, directly testing the claim that bounded rewards prevent crashes.
