# Subspace Projection Methods for Fast Spectral Embeddings of Evolving Graphs

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.19439
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19439
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: G-REST (RSVD) reduces eigenvector approximation error and improves downstream task accuracy for dynamic graph spectral embeddings, with competitive runtime.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the computationally expensive task of updating leading eigenvectors of adjacency or Laplacian matrices as graphs evolve. Instead of recomputing eigendecomposition from scratch, the authors propose G-REST, an adaptive Rayleigh-Ritz projection method that constructs a subspace from the prior eigenspace and low-rank approximations of update-induced residuals, optionally leveraging randomized SVD for efficiency. Empirical evaluations on several datasets demonstrate that G-REST, especially with randomized SVD, yields significantly lower approximation errors compared to perturbation-based baselines while maintaining runtime comparable to or faster than state-of-the-art methods. Downstream applications in central node identification and graph clustering also benefit from the improved embeddings, underscoring the effectiveness of the projection-based update framework.

## Method Summary
G-REST builds an adaptive Rayleigh-Ritz projection subspace to iteratively update eigenvectors after graph modifications. The subspace integrates the existing eigenspace with low-rank approximations of residuals arising from edge/node additions. This design explicitly accounts for newly added nodes and employs randomized SVD to keep complexity low. The projection subspace is then used in a Rayleigh-Ritz step to obtain updated eigenvectors without full decomposition.

## Key Results
- G-REST with RSVD achieves significantly lower eigenvector approximation error (angle ψ) than perturbation-based baselines like TRIP and Residual Modes.
- Runtime is comparable to or faster than competing approaches such as TIMERS.
- Downstream tasks (central node identification via subgraph centrality, graph clustering via spectral clustering) show improved accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of G-REST stems from its adaptive subspace construction, which efficiently captures the directional changes induced by graph updates. By incorporating low-rank residual approximations, the method focuses computational effort on the most affected spectral components. Randomized SVD provides a controllable trade-off between accuracy and speed, making the subspace both informative and affordably sized. The Rayleigh-Ritz procedure then refines the eigenvectors within this subspace, yielding high-quality approximations with minimal overhead.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Prior eigenspace -> Residual estimation -> Low‑rank approximation (RSVD) -> Subspace construction -> Rayleigh‑Ritz -> Updated eigenvectors

**Critical Path**:
1. Retrieve current leading eigenvectors.
2. Compute residual matrix induced by graph updates (e.g., added edges/nodes).
3. Obtain low‑rank approximation of residual via RSVD.
4. Assemble projection subspace by combining prior eigenspace and residual basis.
5. Form projected matrix and solve small eigenproblem (Rayleigh‑Ritz) to get updated eigenvectors.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Subspace size: larger subspaces improve accuracy but increase cost.
- RSVD oversampling/iterations: more accuracy at the expense of runtime.
- Handling additions vs. deletions: algorithm may need adjustments for node/edge removals.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Accuracy rapidly degrades when updates are large relative to subspace dimension.
- Inconsistent results across runs indicate insufficient RSVD oversampling or iterations.
- Failure to capture effects of new nodes if residual model is too simplistic.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Vary subspace dimension and measure approximation error (angle ψ) on a small graph to understand the accuracy–complexity curve.
2. Compare deterministic SVD vs. RSVD in the residual step, evaluating error variance over multiple runs.
3. Apply G‑REST to a temporal graph dataset (e.g., evolving social network) and track clustering quality (NMI/ARI) over time against full recomputation.

## Limitations
- Empirical validation limited to a few datasets; scalability to millions of nodes not demonstrated.
- Assumes small incremental updates; performance under large structural changes is unknown.
- Randomized SVD introduces stochastic error; parameter sensitivity and stability across runs require further analysis.
