# Unlearning for One-Step Generative Models via Unbalanced Optimal Transport

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16489
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16489
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** UOT-Unlearn achieves state-of-the-art class unlearning for one-step generative models (CTM, Meanflow), attaining >85% PUL with low u-FID on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-256 without retain data.

## Executive Summary
Machine unlearning for one-step generative models (e.g., flow map models) is challenging because existing diffusion-based unlearning methods rely on iterative denoising and are incompatible. This paper introduces UOT-Unlearn, a plug-and-play framework that formulates class unlearning as an unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) problem. It uses a forget cost that heavily penalizes samples near a precomputed forget centroid in feature space and an f-divergence penalty to relax marginal constraints, thereby redistributing the forgotten class’s probability mass to remaining classes without needing real retain data. Evaluations on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-256 show superior trade-offs between unlearning efficacy (Percentage of Unlearning, PUL) and image quality (unlearned FID, u-FID), significantly outperforming baselines.

## Method Summary
UOT-Unlearn addresses class unlearning for one-step models where only a pretrained generator \(G_{\text{pre}}\) and a small forget-class sample set (e.g., 512 images for CIFAR-10) are available, with no retain data. The method formulates unlearning as transporting the pretrained data distribution \(p_{\text{pre}}\) to an unlearned distribution \(\nu\) that minimizes mass on the forget class. The transport cost \(c_{\text{ul}}\) (Eq. 18) combines: (1) a high penalty if a generated sample’s feature lies within an active forget region \(R_f\) (defined by cosine distance to forget centroid \(\mu_f\) being less than a margin \(m\)), and (2) a reconstruction penalty \(\tau \|G_{\text{pre}} - G_\theta\|^2\) otherwise. Optimization uses the semi-dual UOT formulation with an f-divergence penalty (KL for \(\Psi_2\), unspecified for \(\Psi_1\)), trained adversarially: a discriminator \(v_\phi\) minimizes \(\mathcal{L}_v = \mathbb{E}[\Psi_1^*(v_\phi(G_\theta)-c_{\text{ul}})] + \mathbb{E}[\Psi_2^*(-v_\phi(G_{\text{pre}}))]\), while the generator \(G_\theta\) minimizes \(\mathcal{L}_G = \mathbb{E}[c_{\text{ul}} - v_\phi(G_\theta)]\). Key hyperparameters include Adam with learning rates \(1.6\times10^{-4}\) (generator) and \(1.0\times10^{-4}\) (discriminator), batch size 128 (CIFAR-10) or 8 (ImageNet-256), and EMA for Meanflow.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10 with Consistency Trajectory Models (CTM): average PUL 85.51%, u-FID 6.96.
- On CIFAR-10 with Meanflow: average PUL 92.44%, u-FID 19.53.
- On ImageNet-256 (Meanflow, goldfish forgetting): PUL 85.08%, u-FID 20.16, maintaining high quality while effectively erasing the target concept.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by constructing an adversarial UOT objective that adaptively redistributes probability mass without requiring retain data. The forget cost \(c_{\text{ul}}\) creates a strong repulsion from the forget centroid in feature space, actively suppressing the target class. Meanwhile, the f-divergence penalty (e.g., KL) acts as a regularizer that prevents excessive deviation from the original distribution, preserving overall generation fidelity. This balance allows the generator to “forget” by moving mass to other classes while avoiding catastrophic collapse. The semi-dual formulation enables optimization using only the pretrained generator and forget samples, making it suitable for one-step models where no iterative diffusion process exists.

## Foundational Learning
- **Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT):** Allows marginal constraints to be relaxed via f-divergence penalties, essential for handling distribution shifts without exact match. *Why needed:* Enables soft mass conservation, preventing over-penalty when moving forget mass. *Quick check:* Verify that the KL penalty term in the objective approximates a soft marginal constraint.
- **f-divergence:** Measures difference between distributions; used here to regularize transport. *Why needed:* Controls the trade-off between forgetting strength and fidelity. *Quick check:* Confirm that the chosen \(\Psi_1, \Psi_2\) convex conjugates implement the intended penalty.
- **Class unlearning:** Task of removing a target class from a generative model without access to retain data. *Why needed:* Practical privacy/compliance scenarios where retain data cannot be used. *Quick check:* Evaluate PUL (classifier accuracy on forget class) and u-FID (quality on retain classes).
- **One-step generative models:** Models that map noise to samples in a single forward pass (e.g., CTM, Meanflow). *Why needed:* Diffusion-based unlearning methods fail here. *Quick check:* Ensure the method does not assume multiple denoising steps.
- **Feature extractor:** Pretrained network (ResNet) used to compute sample features for the forget cost. *Why needed:* Provides a semantic space to define forget region. *Quick check:* Check that features are normalized (cosine distance) and centroid \(\mu_f\) is averaged over forget samples.
- **Adversarial training for UOT:** Uses a discriminator to estimate the transport potential. *Why needed:* Solves the semi-dual UOT objective without explicit coupling. *Quick check:* Verify discriminator and generator losses follow the given \(\mathcal{L}_v\) and \(\mathcal{L}_G\).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pretrained generator \(G_{\text{pre}}\) -> Forget centroid \(\mu_f\) (from feature extractor) -> Cost \(c_{\text{ul}}\) -> Discriminator \(v_\phi\) and Generator \(G_\theta\) (adversarial updates)

**Critical path:**  
1. Extract features of forget samples using pretrained ResNet; compute centroid \(\mu_f\).  
2. For each batch: sample noise, generate \(x_{\text{pre}} = G_{\text{pre}}(\text{noise})\) and \(x_{\text{un}} = G_\theta(\text{noise})\).  
3. Compute \(c_{\text{ul}}\) based on feature distance to \(\mu_f\) (active forget region) or reconstruction error.  
4. Update \(v_\phi\) to minimize \(\mathcal{L}_v\), then update \(\theta\) to minimize \(\mathcal{L}_G\).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Forgetting strength (\(\lambda, m\)) vs. generation fidelity (\(\tau\)): Higher \(\lambda\)/smaller \(m\) increases forgetting but may degrade u-FID.  
- Feature extractor choice: More semantic layers improve separation but may be less robust; ResNet-56/50 used ad-hoc.  
- f-divergence selection: KL provides strong regularization; alternatives (e.g., \(\chi^2\)) may yield different trade-offs.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Over-forgetting: PUL and u-FID both drop → reduce \(\lambda\) or increase \(\tau\).  
- Under-forgetting: Low PUL despite good u-FID → increase \(\lambda\) or decrease \(m\).  
- Discriminator loss divergence → lower discriminator learning rate or add gradient clipping.  
- Generator collapse → check \(c_{\text{ul}}\) computation and feature extractor preprocessing.

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run with reported hyperparameters (λ=1.0, τ=1.0, m=0.34 for CTM-class8) on CIFAR-10; verify PUL and u-FID roughly match paper.  
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Sweep λ ∈ [0.5, 2.0] and m ∈ [0.1, 0.5] for CTM and Meanflow; plot PUL vs. u-FID to understand trade-off curve.  
3. **Feature extractor robustness:** Replace ResNet-56 with ResNet-18 or a different architecture; measure stability of \(\mu_f\) and final metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can UOT-Unlearn be extended to perform structured probability redistribution across semantically related class hierarchies in large-scale datasets? *Basis:* Future work mentions exploring structured redistribution in hierarchical datasets like ImageNet. *Why unresolved:* Current evaluations use flat class structures; hierarchical semantics unexplored.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the theoretical stability and convergence behavior of the UOT unlearning process when using alternative cost formulations beyond the current heuristic design? *Basis:* Future work calls for analyzing stability under alternative costs. *Why unresolved:* Forget cost (Eq. 18) is empirical; interaction with f-divergence terms and convergence properties unanalyzed.
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive is UOT-Unlearn to the choice of feature extractor and forget centroid construction, and what is the minimal data required to reliably define the forget concept? *Basis:* Method uses fixed subset (512/260 images) and pretrained ResNet, but robustness unstudied. *Why unresolved:* Practical deployment needs guidelines; choices are ad-hoc and may not generalize.
- **Open Question 4:** What are the quantitative effects of approximating the data distribution ν with the pretrained distribution p_pre (Eq. 15), and can the method leverage partial retain data when available? *Basis:* Optimization approximates ν ≈ p_pre due to no retain data, but this assumption is unanalyzed. *Why unresolved:* Approximation error and impact unknown; setting strictly prohibits retain data.
- **Open Question 5:** Can the UOT unlearning framework generalize beyond single-class forgetting to handle multi-concept erasure or fine-grained, non-class-aligned concepts? *Basis:* Evaluation focuses on single-class unlearning; extensions unexplored. *Why unresolved:* Real-world needs involve multiple or abstract concepts; interference between forget targets unknown.

## Limitations
- Exact f‑divergence for \(\Psi_1\) in the semi‑dual formulation is unspecified (only \(\Psi_2 = \text{KL}\) stated).
- Hyperparameters (\(\lambda, \tau, m\)) for most dataset‑model settings in Table 2 are omitted; only CTM‑class8 values provided.
- No official source code released, requiring full re‑implementation with risk of subtle deviations.
- Evaluation restricted to two one‑step architectures (CTM, Meanflow); generalizability to other frameworks untested.

## Confidence
- **UOT‑Unlearn enables practical class unlearning for one‑step models:** High (theoretical justification and clear experimental gains).
- **CIFAR‑10 numbers (CTM: PUL 85.51%, u‑FID 6.96; Meanflow: PUL 92.44%, u‑FID 19.53):** Medium (hyperparameter gaps could shift results, but effect size suggests robustness).
- **ImageNet‑256 result (Meanflow: PUL 85.08%, u‑FID 20.16):** Medium (large‑scale performance may be more sensitive to tuning, but still plausible).
