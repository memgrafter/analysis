# M3MAD-Bench: Are Multi-Agent Debates Really Effective Across Domains and Modalities?

- arXiv ID: 2601.02854

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces M3MAD-Bench, a unified benchmark for evaluating Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) methods across five domains (Knowledge, Mathematics, Medicine, Natural Sciences, Complex Reasoning) and both text-only and vision-language modalities using nine base models. Extensive experiments reveal that collaborative debate paradigms (such as LLM Debate) consistently achieve higher accuracy and robustness compared to adversarial approaches (e.g., Div-MAD) across all tested settings. Gains are more substantial on reasoning tasks than on pure knowledge recall. However, MAD methods impose notable token and computational overhead with limited performance improvements in many cases. Further analysis shows that increasing the number of debate agents yields greater benefits than extending the number of debate rounds, and that simply combining heterogeneous models does not automatically enhance performance.

## Method Summary
The study leverages M3MAD-Bench, a benchmark covering five domains (Knowledge, Mathematics, Medicine, Natural Sciences, Complex Reasoning) and two modalities (text-only, vision-language), evaluated on nine distinct base models. The benchmark facilitates comparison between collaborative (LLM Debate) and adversarial (Div-MAD) MAD configurations. No detailed reproduction notes were provided in the source material.

## Key Results
- Collaborative debates (LLM Debate) consistently outperform adversarial paradigms (Div-MAD) in accuracy and robustness across domains and modalities.
- Performance improvements from MAD are more pronounced on reasoning tasks than on knowledge recall tasks.
- MAD incurs significant token and time costs with limited gains; scaling the number of agents is more effective than increasing debate rounds; model heterogeneity alone does not yield better performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The superior performance of collaborative debates may stem from consensus-building mechanisms that reduce individual model errors and promote stable, agreeable outputs. Adversarial setups (Div-MAD) might introduce disruptive conflicts or oscillation without constructive resolution. ## Limitations
- Benchmark generalizability: M3MAD-Bench uses a limited set of nine models (mostly older or academic); findings may not generalize to latest proprietary models or niche domains.
- Token cost analysis: Inefficiency claims rely on absolute token counts without normalization for task difficulty or quality-per-token trade-offs; commercial pricing changes could affect cost-benefit conclusions.
- Model selection bias: The conclusion that heterogeneity does not help may be confounded by the specific model pairs tested; broader exploration of capability gaps and architectural families is needed.
