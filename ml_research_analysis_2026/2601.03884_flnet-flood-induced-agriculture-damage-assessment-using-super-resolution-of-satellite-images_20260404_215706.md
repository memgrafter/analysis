# FLNet: Flood-Induced Agriculture Damage Assessment using Super Resolution of Satellite Images

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03884  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03884  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** FLNet improves the F1-score for the critical "Full Damage" class from 0.83 (native 10m Sentinel-2) to 0.89, matching high-resolution commercial PlanetScope imagery while using only free Sentinel-2 data.

## Executive Summary
FLNet addresses the challenge of rapid, accurate flood damage assessment for smallholder farms, where 10m Sentinel-2 imagery suffers from mixed pixels and commercial 3m PlanetScope data is costly. The core method uses an EDSR super-resolution model, trained on real PlanetScope NDVI, to enhance Sentinel-2 NDVI from 10m to 3m before computing ΔNDVI (pre/post difference). A U-Net then classifies the ΔNDVI map into three damage classes (No, Partial, Full). The pipeline achieves an F1-score of 0.89 for "Full Damage," equal to PlanetScope performance, demonstrating a cost-effective path to high-fidelity, farm-level damage mapping.

## Method Summary
FLNet operates in two training phases. First, a preprocessor aligns and masks paired Sentinel-2 (10m) and PlanetScope (3m) NDVI images, extracting cropland patches. An EDSR network is trained to map 10m Sentinel-2 NDVI to 3m PlanetScope NDVI using ℓ1 loss. In the second phase, the frozen EDSR upscales pre- and post-flood Sentinel-2 NDVI to 3m. The super-resolved images are differenced to produce a 3m ΔNDVI change map. A U-Net is then trained on these ΔNDVI maps to classify damage, using cross-entropy loss. At inference, the same sequential steps (preprocessing → SR → ΔNDVI → U-Net) are applied to new Sentinel-2 pairs.

## Key Results
- **Full Damage F1-score:** Increases from 0.83 (native 10m Sentinel-2) to 0.89 with FLNet, matching PlanetScope accuracy.
- **Visual improvement:** Super-resolved ΔNDVI maps show sharper field boundaries and better retrieval of narrow damage strips compared to native 10m (Fig. 3).
- **SR quality:** EDSR achieves PSNR ~21 dB and SSIM >0.85 for pre-flood NDVI reconstruction against PlanetScope.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Super-resolution mitigates mixed pixels
The EDSR model learns a pixel-mapping from 10m to 3m space trained on real PlanetScope NDVI, injecting high-frequency spatial detail into coarse Sentinel-2 NDVI. This reduces spectral mixing within a single 10m pixel, leading to sharper ΔNDVI boundaries and improved classification of "Full Damage." Evidence: F1 improvement and visual sharpness figures. The mechanism fails if the SR model overfits and hallucinates unrealistic textures or if pre/post images are misaligned.

### Mechanism 2: ΔNDVI as a robust change feature
Computing the difference between pre- and post-flood SR NDVI yields a single-channel change map that emphasizes vegetation loss. Because ΔNDVI is derived after SR, it inherits improved spatial fidelity, and its simplicity forces the segmentation model to focus on the magnitude and pattern of change. Evidence: Use of ΔNDVI as the sole U-Net input. The approach breaks if damage does not produce a strong ΔNDVI signal (e.g., lodging without immediate NDVI drop) or if cloud/shadow residuals cause false signals.

### Mechanism 3: U-Net preserves fine boundaries
The U-Net’s encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections is particularly suited to maintain precise spatial details when segmenting the sharp ΔNDVI map. The skip connections directly transmit high-resolution edge information, enabling accurate delineation of narrow, fragmented fields. Evidence: Standard use of U-Net for pixel-wise tasks and reported boundary improvement. The model may propagate SR noise or artifacts if the ΔNDVI input is degraded.

## Foundational Learning
- **Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI)**  
  *Why needed:* NDVI is the fundamental radiometric indicator of vegetation health; ΔNDVI is the direct change signal for flood damage. All downstream analysis depends on accurate NDVI calculation from Red and NIR bands.  
  *Quick check:* Given Sentinel-2 Red=1000, NIR=3000, NDVI = (3000-1000)/(3000+1000)=0.5. If after flood Red=1500, NIR=2000, NDVI = (2000-1500)/(2000+1500)=0.2, ΔNDVI = -0.3.

- **Single-Image Super-Resolution (SISR) & EDSR**  
  *Why needed:* The core innovation uses SISR to virtually upgrade Sentinel-2 resolution. EDSR’s design (deep residual blocks without batch normalization) is key for reconstructing realistic NDVI textures without degradation.  
  *Quick check:* EDSR removes batch normalization from residual blocks compared to SRResNet; this preserves range flexibility and improves reconstruction quality for smooth indices like NDVI.

- **U-Net for Semantic Segmentation**  
  *Why needed:* The final task is pixel-wise three-class classification. U-Net’s skip connections preserve fine spatial boundaries, critical for small agricultural fields.  
  *Quick check:* A standard CNN like ResNet lacks a decoder path and skip connections, so it would downsample and lose boundary precision, resulting in blocky, inaccurate damage edges.

- **Mixed-pixel problem**  
  *Why needed:* At 10m resolution, a single pixel often covers multiple farm plots, blending spectral signals and obscuring damage. SR aims to unmix these signals.  
  *Quick check:* A 10m pixel containing 50% healthy crop and 50% damaged crop would show intermediate NDVI; SR attempts to separate these into distinct 3m pixels.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Pre-Processor → SR Module (EDSR) → Change Feature Module (ΔNDVI) → Segmentation Module (U-Net) → Post-Processor.

### Critical path
The pipeline is strictly sequential: preprocessing must produce co-registered, masked NDVI pairs; SR inference is applied separately to pre- and post-flood NDVI; ΔNDVI is computed; the U-Net classifies the result. Training involves two independent phases: (A) train EDSR on paired S2/PS NDVI; (B) freeze EDSR, generate SR ΔNDVI for all training samples, then train U-Net. The SR step is a prerequisite for the reported performance.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Single-channel ΔNDVI vs. Multi-spectral SR:** Using only ΔNDVI discards spectral information (e.g., Red Edge bands) but simplifies the task and reduces computation.
- **Real HR supervision (PlanetScope) vs. Synthetic:** Training on real 3m PS avoids domain shift from synthetic degradations, improving radiometric fidelity, but requires costly paired data and may still have sensor differences.
- **Fixed threshold labeling:** Labels are generated by applying thresholds to Δ(PS NDVI) followed by morphological smoothing. This is reproducible but may not capture complex, non-linear relationships between ΔNDVI and actual damage, especially for "Partial" class or confounders like harvesting.

### Failure signatures
1. **Sub-pixel misregistration:** Ghosting or false linear damage along field edges in ΔNDVI maps.
2. **Cloud/shadow residuals:** Isolated extreme ΔNDVI pixels where un-masked clouds or shadows remain.
3. **Over-smoothing by SR:** SR ΔNDVI looks nearly identical to bicubic upsampling; minimal F1 gain.
4. **Thresholding failure:** Overlap of ΔNDVI histograms between damage classes leads to noisy labels, capping UNet performance.

### First 3 experiments (to validate the claimed mechanism)
1. **Reproduce Table 2:** Train FLNet on the BFCD-22 (or equivalent) splits and confirm Sentinel-2 10m F1(Full) ≈0.83, SR+U-Net F1(Full) ≈0.89, PlanetScope F1(Full) ≈0.89.
2. **Ablate SR contribution:** Compare UNet performance when trained on (a) native 10m ΔNDVI, (b) bicubic-upsampled 10m ΔNDVI, (c) EDSR SR ΔNDVI. The gap between (b) and (c) isolates the EDSR effect.
3. **Visual & quantitative SR validation:** Compute PSNR/SSIM between SR(NDVI) and real PS(NDVI) on a held-out set. Expect PSNR ~21 dB, SSIM >0.85 (pre-flood). Measure edge gradient magnitude in ΔNDVI maps to quantify sharpness improvement over bicubic.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Cloud removal integration:** How can learned cloud removal be seamlessly integrated without distorting ΔNDVI values and causing false damage detection?
- **Generalization:** To what extent does FLNet generalize across diverse landscapes, crop types, and flood seasons beyond the single Bihar event?
- **Post-flood SR degradation:** Why does SR performance drop more for post-flood NDVI (SSIM 0.748) vs. pre-flood (0.860), and can this gap be closed?
- **Adaptive labeling:** Can fixed threshold labeling be replaced with a context-aware approach that accounts for local agriculture and phenology to reduce false positives (e.g., harvesting mistaken for damage)?

## Limitations
- **Dataset & transferability:** Results hinge on the BFCD-22 dataset; its public availability and single-event, single-region focus limit claims of broad applicability.
- **Label quality:** Damage labels come from fixed ΔNDVI thresholds, not manual annotation, potentially misrepresenting "Partial Damage" and conflating other changes (e.g., harvesting).
- **Causal attribution:** The paper does not isolate how much of the F1 gain is due to SR versus the ΔNDVI feature or U-Net architecture.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Super-resolution resolves mixed pixels and improves full-damage boundary fidelity. **Confidence:** Medium (supported by visual/F1 gains but lacking direct spatial metrics and comparison to SR-after-ΔNDVI).
- **Claim:** ΔNDVI is a robust, radiometrically consistent change feature. **Confidence:** High (standard in remote sensing; main risks are unmodeled confounders).
- **Claim:** U-Net on SR ΔNDVI excels due to preserved fine boundaries. **Confidence:** Medium (U-Net is standard, but the interaction effect is inferred, not explicitly tested).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate SR contribution:** Train the U-Net on native 10m ΔNDVI, bicubic-upsampled ΔNDVI, and EDSR SR ΔNDVI; isolate the EDSR effect.
2. **Validate SR-first vs. ΔNDVI-first:** Compare the pipeline’s SR(NDVI) → ΔNDVI against computing native ΔNDVI first then applying SR to the difference map.
3. **Human-aligned label verification:** On a manually annotated validation subset, measure agreement (e.g., Cohen’s κ) between fixed-threshold labels and human labels to quantify labeling noise and set a performance ceiling.
