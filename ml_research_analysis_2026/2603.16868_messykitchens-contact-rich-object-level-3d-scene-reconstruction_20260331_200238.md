# MessyKitchens: Contact-rich object-level 3D scene reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16868
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16868
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MessyKitchens dataset and Multi-Object Decoder achieve state-of-the-art object-level 3D reconstruction with mean depth error 1.62mm (49.7% improvement) and lowest inter-object penetration ratio (0.14).

## Executive Summary
We address the problem of physically plausible, object-level 3D scene reconstruction from a single image in cluttered environments, where accurate inter-object contacts are crucial. The core contributions are a new dataset, MessyKitchens, and a novel method called the Multi-Object Decoder (MOD). MessyKitchens provides 100 real-world kitchen scenes with high-fidelity ground truth for 130 scanned objects, including precise shapes, poses, and contact annotations. MOD extends the SAM 3D framework by incorporating multi-object self- and cross-attention to jointly refine object poses, thereby enforcing global scene consistency. Experiments show superior registration accuracy, minimal inter-object penetration, and consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art methods across multiple datasets, with strong zero‑shot generalization.

## Method Summary
**Dataset:** MessyKitchens was constructed by scanning real kitchen scenes using a high‑precision 3D sensor. It includes 130 individual objects (e.g., plates, utensils, containers) whose geometries were captured separately, along with their full 6‑DoF poses within 100 cluttered arrangements. Crucially, the dataset provides explicit contact annotations between objects, which serve as supervision for learning physically plausible placements.

**MOD Architecture:** The Multi‑Object Decoder takes as input the initial object proposals from SAM 3D, which supplies coarse masks and shape latents for each visible object. For each object, a token is formed that encodes appearance and geometric features. MOD then processes all object tokens through multiple layers containing two attention mechanisms:
- *Self‑attention* refines each object’s features in isolation, updating its pose based solely on its own context.
- *Cross‑attention* allows every object to attend to all others, explicitly modeling inter‑object relationships. This joint refinement step iteratively adjusts translations and rotations to reduce penetrations and respect annotated contacts. The final poses are fed to a shape decoder to produce fine‑grained geometry.

MOD is trained with a combination of geometric losses (chamfer distance, depth alignment) and a contact consistency loss that penalizes inter‑object penetrations.

## Key Results
- Mean depth error 1.62 mm (49.7% relative improvement) and inter‑object penetration ratio 0.14, establishing new benchmarks on MessyKitchens.
- MOD outperforms PartCrafter, MIDI, and SAM 3D: object IoU 0.445 vs. 0.409 and scene IoU 0.472 vs. 0.431 on MessyKitchens.
- Strong zero‑shot generalization to GraspNet‑1B and HouseCat6D, demonstrating robustness to unseen scene distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of MOD stems from its explicit modeling of inter‑object dependencies during pose refinement. Cross‑attention lets each object’s token incorporate information from all others, enabling the network to negotiate placements that avoid penetrations and respect support contacts. Self‑attention, in contrast, refines individual object features without interference, preserving identity‑specific details. By iterating these attentions, MOD gradually aligns objects to a globally consistent configuration while retaining accurate shapes. The contact annotations in MessyKitchens provide direct supervision for this process, guiding the model toward physically plausible solutions. Additionally, SAM 3D’s strong priors give a reliable initialization, ensuring that refinement starts near plausible poses.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Single‑image 3D reconstruction** – Recovering 3D geometry from one 2D view; necessary because the input is a monocular image. Quick check: What inherent ambiguities exist (e.g., scale, occlusion) and how does a dataset with ground truth help resolve them?
2. **Object‑level scene representation** – Decomposing a scene into individual objects with separate poses and shapes; enables reasoning about contacts and clutter. Quick check: Why is a holistic scene reconstruction insufficient for contact‑rich editing or manipulation?
3. **Physical plausibility (contacts and penetration)** – Ensuring reconstructed objects do not interpenetrate and respect support relationships; critical for downstream robotics/AR tasks. Quick check: How can penetration be quantified and penalized during training?
4. **Foundation models for 3D (e.g., SAM 3D)** – Pre‑trained models that provide robust object segmentation and shape priors; bootstrap learning without massive data. Quick check: What risks arise when fine‑tuning a foundation model on a narrow domain like kitchens?
5. **Attention mechanisms (self‑ and cross‑)** – Self‑attention updates an element’s representation from its own features; cross‑attention aggregates information across a set. Quick check: In MOD, why is cross‑attention essential for reducing inter‑object penetration?
6. **High‑fidelity scanned datasets** – Real‑world scans with accurate geometry and contact labels; enable supervised training and reliable evaluation. Quick check: What are the challenges of obtaining accurate contact annotations in densely cluttered scenes?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (simplified flow):
Input image → SAM 3D backbone → Object tokenizer → Multi‑Object Decoder (self‑attention layers + cross‑attention layers) → Pose refinement (6‑DoF updates) → Final scene assembly (poses + shapes) → (Training: Contact consistency loss)

Critical path: The input image passes through SAM 3D to obtain initial object proposals and shape latents. These are tokenized and fed into the Multi‑Object Decoder, where self‑attention captures per‑object context and cross‑attention mediates inter‑object constraints. After several refinement rounds, the updated tokens are decoded into refined poses and shapes, yielding the final reconstructed scene.

Design tradeoffs:
- *SAM 3D prior*: Provides strong initialization but ties MOD to SAM’s category coverage; performance may drop on unseen object types.
- *Cross‑attention complexity*: Scales quadratically with number of objects, trading accuracy for memory and speed; limiting detection count helps.
- *Decoder depth*: More layers improve refinement but increase overfitting risk and computation.

Failure signatures:
- Penetration ratio significantly higher than baseline, indicating poor cross‑object negotiation.
- Objects floating or unsupported, suggesting missing support cues.
- Large drops in IoU for categories absent from SAM 3D training.
- Inconsistent poses across objects within the same scene (e.g., jittery placements).

Three first experiments to onboard:
1. **Baseline replication**: Train MOD on MessyKitchens and evaluate object/scene IoU, depth error, and penetration ratio; compare against SAM 3D and other baselines.
2. **Ablation of cross‑attention**: Remove cross‑attention while keeping self‑attention; quantify the increase in penetration and decrease in contact accuracy to highlight the role of inter‑object reasoning.
3. **Zero‑shot domain transfer**: Run pre‑trained MOD on GraspNet‑1B without fine‑tuning; analyze performance breakdown by object category and clutter level to diagnose generalization weaknesses.

## Limitations
- MessyKitchens contains only 100 kitchen scenes, which may not capture the diversity of other cluttered environments (e.g., living rooms, offices).
- Ground truth obtained via scanning might contain subtle artifacts in dense clutter; no independent validation of pose and shape accuracy is reported.
- Evaluation relies on geometric metrics (depth error, IoU, penetration) that do not fully capture higher‑level physical properties such as stability under gravity or support relationships.

## Confidence
- Dataset fidelity: **Medium** – Scanning offers precise geometry but diversity and artifact‑free captures are not fully verified.
- MOD superiority over SOTA: **Medium** – Results are strong but lack statistical tests, ablations on every component, and cross‑dataset replications.
- Zero‑shot generalization: **Low** – Only summary metrics are provided; no per‑scene failure analysis or computational cost details.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate MOD on standard benchmarks (e.g., ScanNet, ShapeNet) using consistent metrics to verify cross‑dataset reliability and rule out overfitting to MessyKitchens.
2. Perform an ablation study that independently removes self‑attention and cross‑attention to quantify each component’s contribution to pose accuracy and contact consistency.
3. Deploy reconstructed scenes in a physics engine (e.g., PyBullet) to evaluate stability under gravity and perturbations; compare penetration ratios and object falls against baseline methods.
