# SimpleOCR: Rendering Visualized Questions to Teach MLLMs to Read

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22426
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22426
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** SimpleOCR achieves robust zero-shot gains (+5.4% on OOD math, +1.8% on ChartQA) using only 8.5K training samples, with no architectural changes.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often exhibit "modality laziness"—they ignore visual text despite strong OCR capabilities, relying instead on textual shortcuts. The authors diagnose this by presenting questions in a Visualized Question (VQ) format, where the question is rendered as an image. Qwen2.5-VL shows up to a 12.7% performance drop on VQ inputs, confirming its dependence on text shortcuts. SimpleOCR addresses this by transforming all training samples into VQ format with randomized text rendering, forcing the model to learn visual reading. No model architecture changes are required. The method yields substantial zero-shot improvements on standard benchmarks: on four OOD math benchmarks it outperforms the base model by 5.4% and RL-based GRPO by 2.7% while using only 8.5K samples (30× fewer than RL). On OCR-intensive tasks, it reaches 81.6% on ChartQA (+1.8%) and 69.1% on HallusionBench (+0.9%). Ablations demonstrate that full VQ training (100%) is essential to avoid optimization conflicts, and style randomization prevents overfitting. A slight performance dip on pure geometry tasks hints at a text bias. SimpleOCR also integrates seamlessly with RL strategies like NoisyRollout for complementary gains.

## Method Summary
SimpleOCR applies a data-level transformation that converts every training sample into a Visualized Question (VQ). The textual question is rasterized onto the associated image, often with randomized visual characteristics (font, color, layout). The model is trained exclusively on these VQ samples, compelling it to rely on visual processing and OCR rather than internal text generation shortcuts. The approach is architecture-agnostic and does not alter the model internals. Training on the fully transformed dataset avoids optimization conflicts that arise from mixing original (textual question) and VQ inputs, ensuring consistent learning signals.

## Key Results
- Outperforms base Qwen2.5-VL by 5.4% on OOD math benchmarks and by 2.7% over GRPO while using 30× fewer samples (8.5K).
- Achieves 81.6% on ChartQA (+1.8% over base) and 69.1% on HallusionBench (+0.9%) on OCR-intensive tasks.
- Full VQ training and style randomization are critical; a slight dip occurs on pure geometry tasks, suggesting potential bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SimpleOCR combats modality laziness by removing the textual shortcut entirely: the question appears only as visual content, forcing the model to OCR it. This compels the model to develop robust visual-text integration skills. Randomization across fonts, colors, and layouts prevents overfitting to a specific rendering style, enhancing generalization. Full VQ training guarantees consistent training signals and avoids conflicts between modalities. The resulting model learns to read text from images, a skill that transfers zero-shot to standard inputs where text might still be present visually, thus improving performance on OCR-heavy benchmarks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Modality Laziness**  
  - *Why needed:* MLLMs often bypass visual text despite OCR abilities, harming performance on image-text tasks.  
  - *Quick check:* Evaluate model on VQ inputs; a significant drop confirms reliance on textual shortcuts.
- **Visualized Question (VQ) Format**  
  - *Why needed:* Rasterizing the question onto the image eliminates internal text generation, forcing visual reading.  
  - *Quick check:* Convert all training questions to VQ and measure improvement on visual-text benchmarks.
- **Text Rendering Randomization**  
  - *Why needed:* Varying fonts, colors, and layouts avoids overfitting to a single style and improves robustness to diverse text appearances.  
  - *Quick check:* Train with no randomization vs. full randomization; test on unseen text styles for performance differences.
- **Full VQ Training**  
  - *Why needed:* Mixing original and VQ samples introduces conflicting training signals; full VQ ensures consistent learning objective.  
  - *Quick check:* Compare models trained on 0%, 50%, and 100% VQ data; full VQ should yield the best target performance.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer**  
  - *Why needed:* Demonstrates that learned visual reading skills apply to standard (non-VQ) inputs, validating the approach's practicality.  
  - *Quick check:* After VQ training, evaluate on benchmarks with normal text+image format; improvements indicate effective transfer.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Original Input (image + question text) → Text Renderer (rasterizes question onto image) → Combined Visual Input → MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) → Output
- **Critical path:** During training, each sample passes through the rendering pipeline, producing a VQ image that is fed to the MLLM. The model learns to decode the question from visual features. At inference, the model can handle both VQ and standard inputs because the acquired OCR skill is input-format agnostic.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Randomization vs. convergence:* Greater style randomness improves generalization but may slow training convergence.  
  - *Full VQ vs. mixed data:* Full VQ eliminates modality conflict but may shift data distribution, potentially reducing performance on non-text tasks (e.g., geometry).  
  - *Rendering complexity:* SimpleOCR uses basic text rendering, adding minimal computational overhead compared to model training.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Performance drop on pure geometry or non-text visual tasks indicates over-specialization on reading.  
  - Model fails when rendered text is extremely small, blurry, or heavily distorted beyond training distribution.  
  - Overfitting to a narrow set of fonts/colors if randomization is insufficient.
- **First experiments (3 exactly):**  
  1. Ablate VQ proportion: train with 0%, 50%, 100% VQ data to confirm full VQ necessity.  
  2. Randomization study: compare no, limited, and full randomization across unseen text styles.  
  3. Geometry impact: evaluate on geometry-intensive benchmarks to quantify negative transfer.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to Qwen2.5-VL; generalizability to other MLLMs remains unknown.
- Benchmarks lack real-world OCR tasks such as DocVQA or TextVQA, limiting assessment of broad OCR robustness.
- The observed performance dip on pure geometry tasks suggests a text bias that could hurt non-text visual reasoning.

## Confidence
- Modality laziness diagnosis: Medium (single-model evidence).  
- SimpleOCR gains on OCR tasks: Medium (consistent but on limited benchmarks).  
- Full VQ + randomization recipe: Medium (ablation-supported, but transferability to other models/tasks uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. Apply SimpleOCR to other MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.6, GPT-4V) and repeat key benchmarks.
2. Test on broader OCR datasets (TextVQA, DocVQA, OCR-VQA) to validate generalization.
3. Stress test with image corruptions (blur, rotation) and diverse text styles to evaluate robustness.
