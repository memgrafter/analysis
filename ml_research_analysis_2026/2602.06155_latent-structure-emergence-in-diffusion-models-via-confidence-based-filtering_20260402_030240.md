Latent Structure Emergence in Diffusion Models via Confidence-Based Filtering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06155
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06155
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Confidence-based filtering reveals latent class structure in diffusion noise seeds, enabling conditional generation without altering the model.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether the latent noise space of diffusion models encodes predictable structure for sample properties such as class labels. Using a confidence-based filtering approach, the authors generate images from random Gaussian seeds via a deterministic DDIM sampler, classify them with a pre-trained LeNet-5, and stratify the seeds by classifier confidence (logit margin). Experiments on MNIST show that high-confidence seeds exhibit strong class separability (LDA discriminability ~92% vs ~66% for low confidence) and cross-level label predictability, while low-confidence seeds show no discernible structure. The findings enable conditional generation by simply selecting high-confidence seeds of a desired class, without modifying the diffusion model. However, the reliance on deterministic sampling and a single dataset leaves open questions about generalizability to stochastic samplers and more complex data.

## Method Summary
The authors first generate 70,000 i.i.d. Gaussian seeds in ℝ⁷⁸⁴ and synthesize MNIST images using a pre-trained deterministic DDIM sampler. A pre-trained LeNet-5 classifier assigns each image a label and a confidence score defined as the margin between the top two logits. After balancing the dataset to equal class counts, seeds are stratified into 10 confidence levels: within each class, seeds are sorted by confidence and split into 10 equal-sized bins; the i-th bin across all classes forms level i (1 = highest confidence, 10 = lowest). For each level, a separate three-layer MLP is trained to predict the class from the seed, and its performance is evaluated on seeds from all levels to produce a cross-level accuracy matrix. Additionally, supervised LDA (reducing to 9 dimensions) followed by UMAP (to 2D) is applied for visualization, and the LDA discriminability is measured as the test accuracy of an LDA classifier. To relate accuracy to confidence, an MLP trained on level‑1 seeds predicts the classifier’s logits; on fresh seeds the predicted logits yield a label and confidence, which are compared to the classifier’s true label.

## Key Results
- High‑confidence seeds yield LDA discriminability of **91.91%**, while low‑confidence seeds achieve only **65.63%**, demonstrating strong class separability at high confidence.
- The cross‑level classification matrix reveals that models trained on high‑confidence seeds generalize well to other high‑confidence levels, but performance deteriorates sharply for low‑confidence seeds.
- LDA‑UMAP visualizations show well‑separated class clusters for high‑confidence seeds, whereas low‑confidence seeds overlap considerably.
- Conditional generation is possible by selecting high‑confidence seeds of a target class and feeding them to the fixed DDIM sampler, producing samples of that class without any model modification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The deterministic diffusion process (DDIM) induces a stable mapping from initial noise to final image. When a seed leads to an image that the classifier is highly confident about, that seed likely lies in a region of the latent space that maps to a distinct attractor basin associated with a particular class. The classifier’s confidence (logit margin) reflects how strongly the generated sample favors one class over the others, and thus serves as a proxy for the seed’s class purity. By sorting seeds by confidence, the authors isolate subsets of the latent space with varying degrees of class separation. High‑confidence seeds occupy more linearly separable manifolds, making linear discriminants (LDA) effective. Consequently, a latent classifier trained on such seeds can predict the class from the seed directly, and selecting seeds with the desired predicted class yields conditional generation. The mechanism hinges on the invertibility/stability of the deterministic diffusion ODE, which preserves class‑relevant information from seed to image.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Models (deterministic DDIM)**: *Why needed* – Provide a stable mapping from noise to image, essential for preserving seed structure through generation. *Quick check*: Verify that two runs with the same seed produce identical images.
- **Classifier Confidence (logit margin)**: *Why needed* – Quantifies how class‑deterministic a generated sample is; enables stratification of seeds by predictive quality. *Quick check*: Compute confidence for a batch; high‑confidence samples should be visually recognizable.
- **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)**: *Why needed* – Supervised linear projection that maximizes class separability; both a visualization aid and a discriminability metric. *Quick check*: Apply LDA to a labeled dataset (e.g., Iris) and achieve >90% test accuracy.
- **UMAP**: *Why needed* – Non‑linear dimensionality reduction for visualizing high‑dimensional latent embeddings. *Quick check*: Run UMAP on MNIST pixel vectors and observe digit‑wise clustering.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Noise Seed -> DDIM Sampler -> Image -> Pre‑trained Classifier -> Confidence -> Stratification -> Latent Classifier (MLP/LDA) -> Evaluation (LDA‑UMAP, cross‑level matrix)

**Critical Path**  
1. Generate random Gaussian seeds. 2. Synthesize images via DDIM. 3. Classify images to obtain labels and confidence. 4. Balance and stratify seeds into confidence levels. 5. Train separate latent classifiers per level. 6. Evaluate using cross‑level accuracy and LDA discriminability. 7. Visualize with LDA‑UMAP. The core insight is that confidence stratification reveals class‑separable latent subsets.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Deterministic (DDIM) vs. stochastic (DDPM) samplers: deterministic ensures reproducibility; stochasticity may obscure structure.  
- Confidence metric: logit margin is simple but may not capture true semantic confidence; alternatives (softmax probability, entropy) could alter stratification.  
- Stratification granularity: more bins yield finer resolution but fewer samples per bin; fewer bins risk merging distinct regimes.  
- Dimensionality reduction: LDA (linear, supervised) vs. PCA (unsupervised); UMAP parameters (n_neighbors, min_dist) strongly influence visualization.  
- Latent classifier capacity: larger MLPs fit better but risk overfitting; LDA provides a robust linear baseline.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Cross‑level accuracy matrix uniformly low (<20% on MNIST) across all levels.  
- LDA discriminability near random (10%) for all confidence levels.  
- UMAP plots for high‑confidence seeds show no visible class clustering.  
- Logit‑prediction model’s output confidence does not correlate with true classifier accuracy.

**First Experiments**  
1. *Confidence distribution*: Generate 70k seeds, compute confidence, verify that after balancing and binning each level has ~equal class counts and that average confidence decreases monotonically.  
2. *Cross‑level MLP*: For each level, train a three‑layer MLP (128‑128‑10, ReLU) for 10 epochs; evaluate on all levels; expect >90% accuracy for high→high and near‑random for low→low.  
3. *LDA discriminability & UMAP*: For levels 1 and 10, fit LDA, compute test accuracy, and produce LDA‑UMAP 2D plots; expect sharp separation for level 1 and heavy overlap for level 10.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What mathematical conditions guarantee that high‑confidence seeds form separable latent classes in diffusion models, and how does confidence relate to the invertibility and stability of the generative flow?
- Can confidence‑based filtering be extended to stochastic diffusion samplers like DDPM, and how does injected noise affect latent‑class separability?
- How can we reduce the computational cost of training latent classifiers for confidence‑based filtering, possibly via efficient seed sampling or adaptive selection?
- What is the sample complexity of confidence‑based conditional generation? How many seeds must be processed to obtain high‑quality samples from a desired class?

## Limitations
- Dependence on unreported hyperparameters (MLP architecture, UMAP parameters) and specific model checkpoints hinders exact replication.
- Evaluation limited to MNIST and deterministic DDIM; generalizability to stochastic samplers (e.g., DDPM) or more complex datasets remains unproven.
- High computational cost: generating and classifying tens of thousands of samples is expensive, with no theoretical sample complexity guarantees.
