# Characterizing Memorization in Diffusion Language Models: Generalized Extraction and Sampling Effects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02333  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02333  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Theorem 4.3 proves that increasing sampling resolution strictly increases memorization; empirically, diffusion models leak substantially less PII than autoregressive models under aligned evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates memorization in diffusion language models (DLMs), introducing a generalized (n,p)-discoverable extraction framework that accommodates arbitrary masking patterns and stochastic sampling trajectories. Theoretically, it proves (Theorem 4.3) that finer sampling resolution—more denoising steps—strictly increases the probability of exact memorization, with autoregressive models (ARMs) emerging as the limiting case of maximal resolution. Empirically, under aligned prefix-conditioned evaluation, DLMs leak substantially less personally identifiable information (PII) than similarly sized ARMs, even at larger scales. The framework unifies memorization analysis across architecture families and offers a rigorous tool for privacy risk assessment.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a generalized probabilistic extraction framework where memorization is defined as exact recovery of a masked token subset given observed context, supporting any mask pattern and stochastic decoding. For diffusion models, the recovery process is governed by the number of denoising steps (resolution N). The single-trial recovery probability p_z is estimated by averaging over R random trajectories; the required query budget n for (n,p)-discoverable extraction follows from the binomial tail. Empirically, they evaluate pretrained DLMs (e.g., LLaDA-8B, 1.1B) and a scale-matched ARM on SlimPajama and Enron PII data, using mask ratios 0.2–0.3 and varying N (1,2,5,10,per-token). Comparisons of exact memorization rates under fixed query budgets reveal the protective effect of diffusion training objectives.

## Key Results
- Finer sampling resolution (more denoising steps) strictly increases exact memorization probability (Theorem 4.3), with autoregressive models as the maximal-resolution limiting case.
- Under aligned prefix-conditioned evaluation, diffusion models leak substantially less PII than autoregressive models: e.g., with 10,000 queries, ARM-1.1B memorizes 213 emails (p=0.5) versus DLM-1.1B’s 16; LLADA-8B (179 emails at p=0.5) remains below the smaller ARM.
- Phone numbers often show zero memorization for diffusion models, indicating selective robustness for certain PII types.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Finer sampling resolution (more denoising steps) strictly increases the probability of exact memorization.
- **Mechanism:** Each denoising step recovers a subset of masked tokens. With more steps (higher resolution), the recovery process is more granular, allowing the model to leverage correct context earlier. This increases the conditional probability of correct recovery at each subsequent step, leading to a multiplicative boost in overall exact-recovery probability.
- **Core assumption:** Assumption 4.2 (Monotonicity of Recovery Probability): The probability of correctly recovering a masked token increases as the set of previously observed (correctly recovered) tokens expands. This captures the intuition that more correct context cannot hurt extraction fidelity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Theorem 4.3 establishes a monotonic relationship between sampling resolution and memorization: increasing resolution strictly increases the probability of exact training data extraction."
  - [section] Theorem 4.3 and its proof (Appendix A) formalize this for a fixed recovery order. Section 6.2 empirically validates the monotonic increase in log-recovery probability across resolutions {1,2,5,10,per-token}.
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors focus on efficiency, attacks, or other DLM properties; no direct anchor for the resolution-memorization link. Evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** The monotonicity relies on Assumption 4.2. If more context introduces uncertainty (e.g., due to accumulated errors from earlier incorrect predictions in a single trajectory), the relationship may fail during a specific extraction attempt. The theorem assumes a fixed, correct recovery order; stochastic variations in the recovery path might also weaken the guarantee in practice.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Under aligned prefix-conditioned evaluation, diffusion language models (DLMs) exhibit substantially lower (n, p)-discoverable memorization of PII compared to autoregressive models (ARMs) of similar scale.
- **Mechanism:** DLMs are trained with random masking objectives that disrupt the learning of long, contiguous token sequences that characterize PII like emails or phone numbers. In contrast, ARMs’ sequential next-token objective naturally aligns with the prefix-suffix extraction setup, making them more prone to verbatim reproduction of such patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The memorization gap is primarily due to architectural training objectives, not data or tokenization differences. The experimental setup controls for these factors (identical compute, data, tokenizer for 1.1B models; aligned finetuning for LLaDA-8B).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Under aligned prefix-conditioned evaluations, we further demonstrate that DLMs exhibit substantially lower memorization-based leakage of personally identifiable information (PII) compared to ARMs."
  - [section] Table 1 shows ARM-1.1B memorizing 213 emails vs. DLM-1.1B’s 16; LLADA-8B memorizes 179 emails under per-token reconstruction despite much larger scale. Section 6.3 discusses the alignment protocol.
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors discuss PII leakage (e.g., "Membership Inference Attacks...") and DLM properties, but do not directly compare ARM vs. DLM PII memorization under aligned conditions. Evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** If post-training (SFT/preference optimization) drives DLMs toward more sequential, prefix-suffix style generation patterns, this memorization gap could diminish. The paper’s conclusion explicitly flags this as future work.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The generalized (n, p)-discoverable extraction framework successfully measures memorization in DLMs by accommodating arbitrary masking patterns and stochastic sampling trajectories, whereas prior frameworks were limited to ARMs’ prefix-suffix structure.
- **Mechanism:** The framework defines memorization for any mask set M, where success means recovering *all* tokens in M given the observed context z_¬M. It accounts for DLM stochasticity by averaging single-trial recovery probabilities p_i^z over multiple random decoding trajectories (R trials) to estimate expected ˆp_z. The required query budget n is then derived via the standard 1-(1-p_z)^n ≥ p relationship.
- **Core assumption:** The averaging over R random trajectories provides a reliable estimate of the underlying expected recovery probability. The framework assumes that memorization is defined by exact recovery of the masked subset, not approximate matches.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We propose a generalized probabilistic extraction framework that unifies prefix-conditioned decoding and diffusion-based generation under arbitrary masking patterns and stochastic sampling trajectories."
  - [section] Definition 4.1 formalizes generalized exact memorization. Equation (8) defines p_z^ estimation over R trials. Proposition 4.4 shows ARM prefix-conditioned extraction is a special case (N=|M|).
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors propose sampling improvements ("Saber...") and membership attacks, but not a unified extraction framework. Evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** The estimation requires sufficient R trials to converge, which can be computationally expensive. For very low-probability events, even large R may not capture rare successful trajectories, leading to underestimation. The framework also assumes independence between queries and between recovery steps given context.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Probabilistic Extractable Memorization**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper moves beyond binary memorization (was a sequence duplicated?) to quantify *how many queries* are needed to extract a memorized sequence under stochastic decoding. This is crucial for realistic privacy risk assessment.  
  *Quick check question:* Given a single-trial extraction probability p_z = 0.001, how many independent queries n are needed to achieve at least p=0.5 success probability? (Answer: n ≥ log(1-0.5)/log(1-0.001) ≈ 693 queries.)

- **Concept: Sampling Resolution (N)**  
  *Why needed here:* Resolution is the key differentiator between ARMs (N = number of masked tokens) and DLMs (N << number of masked tokens, parallel recovery). The core theorem links N directly to memorization probability.  
  *Quick check question:* If a DLM uses N=5 steps to recover |M|=20 masked tokens, what is the typical number of tokens recovered per step? (Answer: Roughly 4 tokens per step on average, but actual number depends on the schedule.)

- **Concept: Generalized (n,p)-discoverable Extraction**  
  *Why needed here:* Defines memorization for any masking pattern, not just prefix-suffix. This is essential for DLMs where generation is non-sequential and any subset can be masked.  
  *Quick check question:* How does Definition 4.1 for DLMs reduce to the ARM definition from [21]? (Answer: When M is a suffix set and the DLM uses maximal resolution N=|M|, the iterative recovery becomes equivalent to left-to-right ARM decoding.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. **Mask Generator:** Produces random mask patterns M (subset of token indices) for evaluation. Must support arbitrary mask ratios and patterns.  
  2. **Generation Engine:** Runs the model’s stochastic sampling for N steps, following either greedy, random, or Gumbel-perturbed selection per step. Outputs reconstructed tokens for M.  
  3. **Probability Estimator:** For a given example and mask M, runs R independent trajectory samples to compute empirical p_z^ = (successful recoveries)/R.  
  4. **Query Calculator:** Given estimated p_z^ and target p, computes required query budget n via n ≥ log(1-p)/log(1-p_z^).  
  5. **Memorization Validator:** Compares train vs. test (same-domain but unseen) distributions of p_z^ to confirm memorization signal.

- **Critical path:** Data selection → Mask sampling → R trajectory generation (per mask) → p_z^ estimation → n calculation → aggregation over many examples → PII leakage statistics. The R trajectory loop is the most expensive computational step.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Resolution N vs. Compute:* Higher N (finer steps) increases p_z^ (per Theorem 4.3) but linearly increases generation cost. The "Max" (per-token) case is most expensive and corresponds to ARM-like behavior.  
  - *Stochasticity vs. Variance:* Using stochastic sampling (Gumbel/top-k) better reflects real deployment but increases variance in p_z^ estimates, requiring more R trials.  
  - *Mask Ratio:* Higher mask ratios lead to smaller absolute p_z^ but may better stress-test the model’s recovery capability. Paper uses r∈{0.2,0.25,0.3}.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High variance in p_z^ across different mask patterns for the same example, indicating unreliable memorization signal.  
  - No significant gap between train and test data distributions (Fig 5/9), suggesting the metric captures generalization, not memorization.  
  - DLM memorization rates that exceed matched-scale ARMs, which contradicts the paper’s main finding and may indicate faulty alignment or evaluation setup.  
  - p_z^ estimates near 0 or 1 with small R, leading to degenerate n values (0 or infinity).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate Estimation Accuracy:** Replicate Figure 2. For a fixed DLM, select 200 examples with moderate recovery rates. Compare empirical p_z from 100k random decodings against theoretical ˆp_z from 1,024 mask patterns. Check for high correlation (R² > 0.9).  
  2. **Confirm Resolution Monotonicity:** Replicate Figure 3. For a DLM (e.g., 1.1B), sample 200 mask-patterns with one-step recovery rate >0.01. Run 10k generations per pattern at resolutions N={1,2,5,10,per-token}. Plot median log(p_N/p_1) with bootstrap CIs. Expect clear upward trend.  
  3. **Aligned PII Comparison:** Replicate Table 1 core comparison. Using the same Enron PII prompts and n=10,000 queries, measure email memorization for a scale-matched ARM-1.1B vs. DLM-1.1B. Expect ARM to show significantly higher extractable counts at p=0.5. Also test LLADA-8B at "One" vs "Max" step to show resolution effect within a DLM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**  
  - *Question:* How do post-training methods like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and preference optimization quantitatively affect memorization in diffusion language models?  
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion states: "future work will measure how post-training (SFT and preference optimization) changes memorization in diffusion language models."  
  - *Why unresolved:* The study evaluates only pre-trained models; post-training could alter generation dynamics and memorization behavior, but this remains unmeasured.  
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Empirically fine-tune DLMs on standard datasets (e.g., Alpaca, HH-RLHF) and compare (n,p)-discoverable extraction rates pre- and post-training under aligned evaluation protocols.

- **Open Question 2**  
  - *Question:* Does post-training shift diffusion models toward more prefix-suffix-style generation patterns and thereby increase verbatim memorization and extractability?  
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion specifies: "test whether post-training drives DLMs toward more prefix–suffix-style generation, and whether this shift increases verbatim memorization."  
  - *Why unresolved:* Current evaluations use prefix-conditioned tasks, but post-training (e.g., instruction tuning) may bias models toward sequential decoding, changing memorization risk.  
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Analyze the distribution of masking patterns during generation after post-training (e.g., proportion of sequential vs. random token recovery) and correlate with changes in (n,p)-discoverable PII extraction.

- **Open Question 3**  
  - *Question:* How do sampling parameters beyond resolution (e.g., temperature, top-k, Gumbel noise scale) influence memorization trade-offs in diffusion language models?  
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Section 3.3 mentions Gumbel perturbation as the default sampler but does not explore other stochastic decoding strategies; the generalized framework could accommodate them.  
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper studies resolution effects extensively, but other sampling knobs may independently modulate memorization while affecting generation quality.  
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct controlled experiments varying temperature or top-k thresholds in DLM inference, measuring exact-recovery probabilities and comparing to generation metrics (e.g., perplexity, diversity).

- **Open Question 4**  
  - *Question:* How do arbitrary non-prefix masking patterns (e.g., random, block, structured) affect memorization extraction in diffusion models relative to autoregressive models?  
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The generalized (n,p)-discoverable framework (Definition 4.1) explicitly supports arbitrary masks, yet empirical validations focus largely on prefix-conditioned PII tasks.  
  - *Why unresolved:* The theoretical unification suggests different masks could yield distinct memorization behaviors, but this is not systematically tested.  
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply the generalized extraction protocol across a diverse set of mask patterns (random, center, checkerboard) on held-out data, comparing DLM and ARM extraction rates at identical mask ratios.

- **Open Question 5**  
  - *Question:* Is the monotonic relationship between sampling resolution and memorization (Theorem 4.3) universally valid, or does it depend on specific model architectures, training data distributions, or the validity of Assumption 4.2?  
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Theorem 4.3 proves monotonicity under Assumption 4.2 (recovery probability increases with more observed correct tokens), but the assumption may not hold universally.  
  - *Why unresolved:* The assumption is validated empirically on one model (DLM-1.1B) with limited mask patterns; its generalizability to larger models, different tokenizers, or datasets with varying sequential dependencies remains open.  
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Test Assumption 4.2 across multiple DLM scales (170M to 100B), diverse corpora (code, multilingual text), and under error propagation (tracking incorrect intermediate predictions), checking for violations of monotonicity.

## Limitations
- **Tokenization & PII detection:** Exact tokenizer details for scale-matched models are unspecified. Different subword splits for emails/phones could cause false negatives in memorization counts.
- **Training data provenance:** LLaDA-8B’s pretraining corpus is undisclosed; finetuning on Enron may not reflect its natural memorization. SlimPajama models’ preprocessing details (e.g., deduplication) are also unspecified.
- **Framework assumptions:** The (n,p)-discoverable estimation assumes independent recovery trials and fixed mask patterns; real-world extraction might violate these (e.g., correlated prompts). Low-probability events may be underestimated with finite R trials.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (Resolution increases memorization): **High**. Supported by Theorem 4.3 (proof in Appendix A) and empirical validation in Section 6.2 across multiple resolutions.
- Claim 2 (DLMs leak less PII than ARMs): **Medium**. Strong empirical evidence (Table 1, Figure 5) under controlled alignment, but acknowledges post-training effects (e.g., SFT) could narrow the gap—future work needed.
- Claim 3 (Generalized framework accuracy): **Medium-Low**. Theoretically sound (Definition 4.1, Proposition 4.4) but lacks direct corpus validation; prior frameworks were ARM-specific, so external comparison is weak.
