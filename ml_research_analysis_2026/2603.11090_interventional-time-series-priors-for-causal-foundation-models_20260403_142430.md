# Interventional Time Series Priors for Causal Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11090
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11090
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A Prior-data Fitted Network (PFN) trained on 100K synthetic TSCMs from CausalTimePrior achieves in-context causal effect estimation comparable to per-dataset VAR baselines without dataset-specific fitting, correctly distinguishing causal from non-causal queries.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces CausalTimePrior, a framework for generating synthetic temporal structural causal models (TSCMs) with paired observational and interventional time series. This addresses the lack of interventional data for training causal foundation models (PFNs) on temporal data. The prior supports configurable causal graphs, nonlinear dynamics, regime-switching, and multiple intervention types (hard, soft, time-varying). A proof-of-concept PFN trained on 100K such TSCMs demonstrates accurate in-context causal effect estimation on held-out models, achieving a Pred/GT ratio of 0.95 for causal queries and 0.46 for non-causal queries, with RMSE comparable to per-dataset VAR baselines. Ablation studies confirm that diverse intervention types improve generalization.

## Method Summary
CausalTimePrior defines a prior distribution over discrete-time TSCMs with time-lagged DAGs, nonlinear autoregressive mechanisms, and noise distributions. For each sampled TSCM, it generates paired data by forward simulation under observational conditions and under specified interventions (hard, soft, time-varying) that modify structural equations. The resulting tuples (X_obs, intervention spec, Y_int) train a Prior-data Fitted Network (PFN). The PFN architecture uses a GRU encoder for X_obs, separate encoders for intervention and query specifications, and a Gaussian prediction head. It is trained via negative log-likelihood to predict interventional outcomes given observational context and an intervention query.

## Key Results
- PFN trained on CausalTimePrior achieves Pred/GT ratio 0.95 for intervened queries and 0.46 for non-causal queries, showing accurate causal effect estimation and discrimination.
- RMSE (176.4) comparable to per-dataset VAR-OLS (176.5) without any dataset-specific fitting.
- Ablation: Training on all intervention types (hard, soft, time-varying) improves effect direction accuracy by 6.5% and effect size correlation by 0.13 compared to hard-only training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Synthetic paired observational and interventional time series can be generated from configurable TSCMs, enabling training of causal foundation models for time series where interventional data was previously unavailable.
- Mechanism: CausalTimePrior samples a TSCM (graph, mechanisms, noise) and generates data by forward simulation under observational conditions and under interventions that modify structural equations. This yields (X_obs, intervention spec, Y_int) tuples.
- Core assumption: The discrete-time TSCM framework with Markovian noise and acyclic time-lagged graphs covers a broad class of causal time series; the prior’s diversity matches the test distribution.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We propose CausalTimePrior, a principled framework for generating synthetic temporal structural causal models (TSCMs) with paired observational and interventional time series." [section 3] Details TSCM definition, prior components, intervention types, and data generation (Algorithm 1). [corpus] Identifies that existing generators (CausalTime, TimeGraph, CauseMe) lack interventions; only three (CAnDOIT, TECDI/RealTCD, CaTSG) support interventions but with limitations.
- Break condition: Real-world time series with non-Markovian confounding, continuous-time dynamics, or causal structures outside the prior’s support may not generalize.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Including diverse intervention types (hard, soft, time-varying) and regime-switching dynamics in the training prior improves the PFN’s generalization across intervention semantics and time-varying causal structures.
- Mechanism: The prior explicitly samples intervention type and regime-switching TSCMs (15% of training data) where causal graphs and mechanisms change via a sticky Markov chain. The PFN exposed to this mixture learns to interpret intervention specifications and adapt to regime changes in-context.
- Core assumption: Variation in intervention semantics and regime stability during pre-training confers robustness.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our prior supports configurable causal graph structures, nonlinear autoregressive mechanisms, regime-switching dynamics, and multiple intervention types (hard, soft, time-varying)." [section 3.5] regime-switching TSCMs. [Appendix F] Ablation: mixed-intervention training improves direction accuracy (+6.5%) and correlation (+0.13) over hard-only.
- Break condition: Test interventions or regime transitions qualitatively different (e.g., altering transition probabilities, long-term persistent effects) may degrade performance without prior coverage.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: A PFN trained on CausalTimePrior synthetic data can perform in-context causal effect estimation on held-out TSCMs, matching per-dataset baselines without sample-specific fitting.
- Mechanism: The PFN (GRU-based) encodes X_obs, intervention spec (I, tI, c), and query (τ) and outputs Gaussian parameters for Y_int. Trained via NLL over many TSCMs, it learns mapping from context to P(Y_int | do(X_I), X_obs).
- Core assumption: PFN architecture and capacity suffice to approximate the posterior predictive for any TSCM from the prior.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We demonstrate that PFNs trained on CausalTimePrior can perform in-context causal effect estimation on held-out TSCMs." [section 4] Reports Pred/GT ratio 0.95 for intervened queries vs 0.46 for non-causal, RMSE comparable to VAR-OLS (176.4 vs 176.5). [corpus] PFNs succeeded in tabular causal inference (Do-PFN, CausalFM) and time series forecasting (ForecastPFN, TimePFN), but temporal causal PFNs are novel.
- Break condition: Insufficient PFN capacity or overly complex prior relative to model size may cause failure.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  TSCM sampler -> intervention specifier -> data simulator -> PFN (temporal encoder + intervention encoder + query encoder + prediction head) -> training loop (loss computation, backpropagation).

- **Critical path**:
  1. Implement TSCM class with methods: sample_graph, sample_mechanisms, sample_noise, simulate_observational, simulate_interventional.
  2. Implement intervention application logic: hard (replace variable values, block incoming edges), soft (add shift), time-varying (assign trajectory c(t)).
  3. Build PFN: 2-layer GRU encoder (input dim=N, hidden=128), MLP embeddings for intervention and query, Gaussian prediction head.
  4. Training: sample batch of TSCMs and interventions, generate paired series, compute NLL loss, backpropagate (Adam, lr=1e-4, batch=32, epochs=15).
  5. Evaluation: hold-out TSCMs, feed observational context and intervention query, compare predicted mean to ground truth Y_int; compute metrics for query types (intervened, downstream, non-causal).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Graph prior: Erdős-Rényi is simple but may underrepresent specific motifs; structured priors improve coverage but add complexity.
  - Mechanism families: Simple parametric forms (sin, cos, square) ensure fast sampling; neural mechanisms increase diversity but risk instability.
  - Intervention diversity: All types increase prior complexity and cost but ablation shows benefits; limiting to hard simplifies training but harms generalization.
  - PFN architecture: GRU used for efficiency; transformers may capture long-range dependencies but are memory-intensive.
  - Regime-switching: 15% of training data adds graph sampling and Markov chain complexity; needed for non-stationarity but may require more capacity.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Simulation failures: NaNs/Infs due to exploding values; monitor and clip if needed (clipping thresholds unreported).
  - Poor causal discrimination: Similar predictions across query types -> model learning correlations, not causality. Check three-way metrics for small gaps.
  - OOD degradation: Performance drop on larger/denser graphs or complex mechanisms; track RMSE and effect correlation on OOD sets.
  - Intervention confusion: In shuffled-intervention control, non-causal query predictions should remain low; if they increase, model may rely on dataset biases.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Prior validation: Sample 100K TSCMs, compute statistics (graph size, edge density, intervention types, effect sizes), ensure simulation stability (0% divergence).
  2. Proof-of-concept PFN training: Train GRU-based PFN on 100K TSCMs, evaluate on 1K held-out; report three-way metrics (intervened/downstream/non-causal) and compare to per-dataset VAR-OLS and PCMCI+.
  3. Intervention type ablation: Train two PFNs: (a) mixed-intervention (all types) vs (b) hard-only; compare effect direction accuracy and correlation on same test set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can CausalTimePrior be extended to handle non-Markovian confounding and continuous-time dynamics? (Limitations: currently assumes Markovian noise and discrete-time; evidence needed: implement continuous-time SDE-based TSCMs and evaluate on such test sets.)
- **Open Question 2**: Does explicitly sampling canonical temporal causal motifs (e.g., confounders, mediators, colliders) improve prior coverage and PFN generalization compared to Erdős-Rényi? (Limitations: implicit coverage may be unbalanced; evidence needed: design motif-stratified prior and evaluate on motif-specific held-out sets.)
- **Open Question 3**: How well does the distribution of TSCMs generated by CausalTimePrior match real-world time series? (Limitations: not validated against real data; evidence needed: compare statistics between synthetic samples and real benchmarks like CauseMe.)
- **Open Question 4**: Can scaling the PFN to larger transformers and incorporating explicit temporal causal structure representations improve accuracy and robustness? (Limitations: current GRU is proof-of-concept; evidence needed: train transformer-based PFNs and evaluate on OOD scenarios.)

## Limitations
- Regime-switching TSCM details (number of regimes, transition probabilities) and time-varying intervention parameterization are unspecified.
- Prior may miss real-world complexities: non-Markovian confounding, continuous-time dynamics, latent variables.
- Evaluation only on synthetic TSCMs; no real-data validation.
- Small GRU (2‑layer, 128 hidden) may limit capacity for larger graphs/horizons.
- Simulation instability managed by unreported clipping, possibly biasing effects.
- Wide effect size distribution (mean 17.98, std 53.93) could hinder learning of weak effects.

## Confidence
- Synthetic prior generation is valid and diverse: *Medium* (Algorithm outlined, zero divergence claimed, but coverage not quantified.)
- Mixed intervention training improves generalization: *High* (Ablation shows clear gains: +6.5% direction accuracy, +0.13 correlation.)
- PFN achieves in‑context causal estimation matching per‑dataset baselines: *Medium* (Strong synthetic metrics, but simple architecture and no real‑data tests limit broader confidence.)
