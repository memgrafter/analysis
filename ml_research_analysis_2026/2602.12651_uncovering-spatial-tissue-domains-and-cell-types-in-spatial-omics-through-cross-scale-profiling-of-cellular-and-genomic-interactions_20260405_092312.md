# Uncovering spatial tissue domains and cell types in spatial omics through cross-scale profiling of cellular and genomic interactions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12651
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12651
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CellScape achieves state-of-the-art spatial domain segmentation with highest normalized mutual information (NMI) across multiple spatial transcriptomics datasets, including osmFISH (NMI 0.745), 10x Visium (mean NMI 0.650), and STARmap (NMI 0.794), while enabling robust multi-sample batch correction.

## Executive Summary
CellScape is a deep learning framework for spatial transcriptomics analysis that jointly models spatial cellular interactions and intrinsic gene regulatory relationships. It uses a dual-encoder architecture: a graph attention network processes spatial cell neighborhoods, while a convolutional neural network analyzes 2D gene co-expression maps. Trained with contrastive and reconstruction losses, it fuses these complementary views into unified embeddings. The method consistently outperforms existing approaches in spatial domain segmentation across diverse datasets, resolves fine cortical layers, and supports batch correction in disease models.

## Method Summary
CellScape ingests a gene expression matrix and 2D cell coordinates. It constructs a spatial graph (k‑NN or Delaunay) and a per‑cell 2D gene co‑expression map via optimal transport. Two encoders process these: a GAT encoder extracts spatial context, and a CNN encoder extracts intrinsic transcriptional patterns. Their embeddings are concatenated and trained using two self‑supervised losses: a masked reconstruction loss (scaled cosine error) recovers masked expression from context, and a multi‑positive contrastive loss attracts spatially adjacent cells. Gradients from both losses are merged with PCGrad. Downstream, spatial embeddings are reduced via PCA and clustered (Gaussian mixture) to define domains; intrinsic embeddings can reveal cell types. A spatial‑only variant (CellScape_CCI) is used for spot‑based data. Multi‑sample integration uses ComBat for gene‑level correction and block‑diagonal spatial graphs.

## Key Results
- Achieved highest NMI on osmFISH (0.745), 10x Visium (mean 0.650 across 12 slices), and STARmap (0.794), outperforming eight state-of-the-art methods.
- Accurately resolved fine cortical layers in mouse brain tissue.
- Enabled robust batch correction across multiple samples in Alzheimer’s disease mouse models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Joint modeling of spatial and genomic signals
**Claim:** Jointly modeling spatial cellular interactions and intrinsic genomic relationships yields superior spatial domain segmentation.
**Mechanism:** The GAT encoder captures neighbor‑to‑neighbor influences in tissue space, while the CNN encoder captures gene‑to‑gene regulatory patterns in a 2D co‑expression map. Fusing embeddings forces correlation between spatial position and transcriptional state, disentangling biological variation from noise.
**Break condition:** May degrade performance if spatial and genomic signals are redundant or one modality is extremely noisy.

### Specialized encoder architectures
**Claim:** Using GAT for spatial graphs and CNN for gene maps is critical due to differing data structures.
**Mechanism:** GAT applies adaptive attention over irregular spatial neighborhoods; CNN exploits local patterns in the 2D gene map. This specialization extracts optimal patterns before fusion.
**Break condition:** GAT unnecessary for dense regular grids; CNN harmful if gene co‑expression not spatially localizable.

### Self‑supervised training with contrastive and reconstruction losses
**Claim:** The combination provides a self‑supervised signal aligning spatial neighborhoods with transcriptional similarity.
**Mechanism:** Masked reconstruction forces recovery of expression from context; contrastive learning attracts spatial neighbors and pushes apart non‑neighbors. PCGrad balances losses during backpropagation.
**Break condition:** If spatial neighbors are transcriptionally dissimilar or gene map layout is poor, losses may misalign embeddings.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (gene expression + coordinates) -> spatial graph builder & gene map builder -> GAT encoder (spatial) & CNN encoder (intrinsic) -> embedding fusion -> losses (reconstruction + contrastive) -> PCGrad optimizer -> spatial embeddings for clustering (PCA + mclust) / intrinsic embeddings for cell types.

Critical path: Data preprocessing -> graph and map construction -> masking -> dual encoding -> fusion -> loss computation -> gradient merging via PCGrad -> embedding extraction -> downstream clustering and analysis.

Design tradeoffs:
- GAT vs. GCN: GAT adds parameters for adaptive neighbor weighting but may overfit small datasets.
- CNN on gene maps: relies on optimal transport placing co‑expressed genes adjacently; fails if patterns non‑local.
- Loss weighting: reconstruction vs. contrastive tradeoff; PCGrad helps but needs tuning.
- Spatial‑only variant (CellScape_CCI): used for spot‑based data where per‑cell gene maps are less meaningful.

Failure signatures:
- Blurred spatial domains despite loss convergence: contrastive weight too low or graph construction error.
- Z_intrinsic fails to separate known cell types: poor gene map layout or insufficient CNN capacity.
- Persistent batch effects in multi‑sample integration: ComBat ineffective or graph merging incorrect.
- Training instability (loss spikes): PCGrad misconfiguration or learning rate too high.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under what specific conditions (e.g., spot‑based vs. single‑cell resolution, tissue heterogeneity, noise levels) does incorporating genomic interactions fail to provide a measurable advantage over a purely spatial model? Evidence: CellScape benefits high‑resolution data but is statistically comparable to CellScape_CCI on Visium; no systematic characterization of thresholds.
2. How does the relative contribution of the spatial (GAT) versus intrinsic (CNN) encoder vary across different tissue architectures (e.g., layered cortex vs. heterogeneous tumor), and can branches be weighted automatically? Evidence: Fixed concatenation assumes synergy; not tested on disordered tissues where spatial proximity may be unreliable.
3. To what extent do the contrastive spatial loss and reconstruction loss interfere during training, and does PCGrad fully resolve gradient conflicts for all tissue types? Evidence: PCGrad is used but gradient conflict dynamics (e.g., cosine similarity) are not measured across datasets.
4. Can CellScape’s learned embeddings reveal causal or directional relationships (e.g., morphogen gradients, developmental trajectories), or are they purely correlative? Evidence: PAGA on embeddings suggests trajectories but static snapshot data cannot confirm causality; needs time‑series or perturbation validation.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (GAT layers/heads, CNN filters, masking ratio, temperature τ) not reported; must be extracted from code.
- Gene map construction via optimal transport assumes local co‑expression; may limit performance if patterns are non‑local.
- Unclear rule for switching to spatial‑only variant (CellScape_CCI) introduces application ambiguity.
- Break conditions suggest potential degradation in homogeneous tissues or with noisy modalities.

## Confidence
- Superior spatial domain segmentation (NMI gains): High
- Joint modeling of spatial and genomic signals: Medium
- Necessity of specialized encoders (GAT + CNN): Medium
- Efficacy of self‑supervised objectives (contrastive + reconstruction): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Heterogeneity stress test: Apply CellScape to both highly structured (e.g., cortex) and homogeneous (e.g., tumor) ST datasets; compare joint model against spatial‑only and genomic‑only ablations to map failure regimes.
2. Gene map robustness: Retrain CellScape using alternative gene map constructions (t‑SNE, UMAP, random placement); evaluate NMI and intrinsic embedding separation to test dependence on optimal transport.
3. Encoder architecture ablation: Replace GAT with a standard GCN and the CNN with an MLP; measure performance impact to quantify contributions of attention and convolutional inductive biases.
