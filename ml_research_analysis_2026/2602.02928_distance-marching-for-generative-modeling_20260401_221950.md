# Distance Marching for Generative Modeling
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02928
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02928
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Improves FID by 13.5% over recent time-unconditional baselines and surpasses flow matching in class-conditional ImageNet generation, achieving lower FID with 60% of the sampling steps and 13.6% lower FID across model sizes.

## Executive Summary
Distance Marching addresses target ambiguity in time-unconditional generative modeling by learning a distance-like scalar field \(u_\theta(x)\) and a direction field \(v_\theta(x)\). The method uses losses that prioritize closer targets (One-Step Loss and Directional Eikonal Loss) to align denoising directions with the data manifold. Two inference rules—sphere tracing and gradient descent—are derived from the fields. On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, it achieves a 13.5% FID improvement over time-unconditional baselines. For class-conditional ImageNet generation, it outperforms flow matching despite lacking time conditioning, reaching lower FID with only 60% of the sampling steps and a 13.6% reduction across model sizes. The learned distance metric also enables adaptive sampling and strong out-of-distribution detection.

## Method Summary
Distance Marching trains a backbone network (U-Net for CIFAR-10, SiT transformer for ImageNet) to predict, from a noisy sample \(x\), a scalar distance estimate \(u_\theta(x)\) and a direction vector \(v_\theta(x)\). Training data pairs \((x, x_1)\) are created by linear interpolation between a noise sample \(x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\) and a real image \(x_1\) with random coefficient \(t\). The network does **not** condition on \(t\); the distance field implicitly encodes the appropriate step size. The loss combines One-Step Loss (OSL), which normalizes denoising error by the squared distance to the target, and Directional Eikonal Loss (DEL), which regularizes \(v_\theta\) to align with the normalized displacement. During generation, starting from noise, samples are updated either via sphere tracing (\(x \gets x - \eta\, u_\theta(x) v_\theta(x)\)) or gradient descent (\(x \gets x - \eta\, v_\theta(x)\)). The distance prediction also allows adaptive stopping based on \(u_\theta\)’s magnitude, eliminating time conditioning and reducing target ambiguity.

## Key Results
- Improves FID by 13.5% over recent time-unconditional baselines on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet.
- For class-conditional ImageNet generation, surpasses flow matching with 60% of the sampling steps and 13.6% lower FID across model sizes.
- Learned distance metric enables adaptive sampling and strong out-of-distribution detection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Losses that Prioritize Closer Targets
- **Claim:** One-Step Loss (OSL) and Directional Eikonal Loss (DEL) reduce target ambiguity by focusing learning on closer data points, yielding denoising directions better aligned with the data manifold.
- **Mechanism:** OSL normalizes the denoising error by the squared distance to the target (plus a constant), causing samples nearer to the manifold to contribute more heavily to the gradient. DEL matches the predicted direction to the normalized displacement vector, with a constant to avoid division by zero and provide length-neutral regularization.
- **Core assumption:** For a given noisy point \(x\), the most informative training signal comes from the nearest true data point; farther points introduce conflicting orientation information because the same \(x\) could have originated from many possible \(x_1\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "losses that prioritize closer targets to reduce ambiguity and align denoising directions with the data manifold."
  - [section 4.2] "One-step loss explicitly concentrates supervision on nearby targets, while directional eikonal loss offers an adjustable intermediate concentration between one-step loss and standard flow matching."
  - [corpus] Corpus includes Energy Matching and Equilibrium Matching, which lack our distance-based reweighting and underperform (Tab. 1, Tab. 2), implying the importance of our loss design.
- **Break condition:** Nearest-neighbor search in high dimensions suffers from hubness; if the training relies on explicit nearest assignment (minibatch replacement), mode collapse may occur. Our method avoids explicit nearest neighbor assignment by using random interpolation, so the mechanism degrades if the interpolation distribution heavily favors large distances or if the dataset is extremely sparse.

### Mechanism 2: Time-Unconditional Field Parameterization
- **Claim:** Learning a scalar field \(u_\theta(x)\) (distance-like) and vector field \(v_\theta(x)\) (direction) that depend only on the current point eliminates the need for explicit time conditioning, simplifying the model and avoiding exposure bias.
- **Mechanism:** The training distribution pairs \((X, X_1)\) via linear interpolation with random coefficient \(T\). The network sees only \(X\) and predicts \(u_\theta(X)\) and \(v_\theta(X)\). During inference, updates use either \(v_\theta(x)\) (gradient descent) or \(u_\theta(x)v_\theta(x)\) (sphere tracing). The implicit mapping from \(x\) to the appropriate step size/direction replaces explicit time.
- **Core assumption:** The mapping from a point \(x\) to its corresponding noise level (or "time") is uniquely determined by the geometry of the data manifold; thus a time-conditional distribution can be captured by a single unconditional field.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "time-unconditional generative models learn time-independent denoising vector fields."
  - [section 3.1] "the spatial position \(x\) itself contains all the information required by the distance field \(u_\theta(x)\) with respect to a given data manifold."
  - [section 3.3] Training distribution definition and input use only \(x\).
- **Break condition:** Very high noise might correspond to regions where \(u_\theta\) is not smooth or monotonic with respect to actual noise level. If the data manifold has multiple disconnected components, a single scalar distance may not adequately encode the correct manifold component to target.

### Mechanism 3: Inference via Sphere Tracing and Gradient Descent
- **Claim:** The learned fields naturally support two inference rules—sphere tracing (adaptive step) and gradient descent (fixed step)—both of which converge reliably toward the data manifold even when \(v_\theta\) is not perfectly curl-free.
- **Mechanism:** Sphere tracing updates \(x_{i+1} = x_i - \eta u_\theta(x_i) v_\theta(x_i)\), using the distance magnitude to scale the step along the predicted direction. Gradient descent uses \(x_{i+1} = x_i - \eta v_\theta(x_i)\). The paper shows both converge empirically; the distance prediction also enables adaptive stopping.
- **Core assumption:** The direction field \(v_\theta\) is approximately aligned with the gradient of the underlying distance field, and the scalar \(u_\theta\) approximately equals (noise-adjusted) distance to the manifold.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "two principled inference methods: sphere tracing and gradient descent."
  - [section 3.1] Derivation from distance field properties and sphere tracing.
  - [section 5.5] Figure 16: Both DM (ST) and DM (GD) achieve better FID earlier than flow matching; DM (GD) reaches FM's final FID with <60% steps. Table 3 shows DM competitive with time-conditioned FM.
  - [section C.5] Figure 30: \(u_\theta\) converges to near \(\sqrt{c_0}\) during generation, indicating distance-like behavior.
- **Break condition:** If \(v_\theta\) contains strong rotational components, trajectories might cycle or meander. The paper notes they do not enforce \(v_\theta = \nabla u_\theta\); thus, convergence is not guaranteed by theory but observed empirically.

## Foundational Learning
### Target Ambiguity in Time-Unconditional Models
- **Why needed:** This is the fundamental problem Distance Marching solves. Removing time conditioning means a single noisy image \(x\) could correspond to many different denoising directions because \(x\) could have been generated from various noise levels \(T\) and different data points \(X_1\).
- **Quick check question:** Why does conditioning solely on \(x\) lead to ambiguous denoising targets? *(Answer: Because the forward process \(X = (1-T)X_0 + T X_1\) with random \(T\) and \(X_0, X_1\) makes the conditional distribution \(p(X_1, X_0 | X=x)\) multimodal.)*

### Distance Field Geometry
- **Why needed:** DM is inspired by unsigned distance fields in graphics. A distance field assigns to each point its Euclidean distance to a surface; its gradient points directly toward the surface and has unit norm, enabling one-step projection.
- **Quick check question:** Given an unsigned distance field \(d(x)\), what update moves a point to the surface in one step if \(\nabla d\) exists? *(Answer: \(x - d(x) \nabla d(x)\); see Eq. (2).)*

### Flow Matching Loss and Its Length Bias
- **Why needed:** To understand why standard flow matching fails in the time-unconditional setting—it weights the regression target by the displacement length \(\ell = \|X_1 - X_0\|\), causing longer pairs to dominate and biasing directions toward the data mean.
- **Quick check question:** In Theorem A.1, why does the flow matching minimizer become a length-weighted average of unit direction vectors? *(Answer: Because the loss \(\mathbb{E}[\|v - \Delta\|^2]\) with \(\Delta = \ell u\) gives minimizer \(\mathbb{E}[\ell u]\); larger \(\ell\) contributes more.)*

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map:
- **Backbone:** U-Net (CIFAR-10) or Transformer (SiT) for ImageNet. Takes only noisy sample \(x\) (no time/class input; class label \(y\) is separate).
- **Heads:**
  - *Direction predictor* \(v_\theta(x)\): output is same spatial shape as \(x\) (vector field). Usually a final linear layer from backbone features.
  - *Distance predictor* \(u_\theta(x)\): lightweight feed-forward network (two-layer CNN or MLP) attached to backbone features. Tiny relative parameter count (~0.01–0.2% extra).
- **Loss block:** Computes One-Step Loss (OSL) and Directional Eikonal Loss (DEL) from \((X, X_1)\) pairs; hyperparameters \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \epsilon, c_0\).
- **Training pipeline:** sample \((X, X_1)\) via linear interpolation; forward through backbone + heads; compute losses; backpropagate.
- **Inference engine:** sphere tracing or gradient descent loop with fixed step budget or adaptive stopping (using \(u_\theta\)).

### Critical Path:
1. **Prepare data**: Load real images.
2. **Construct training batch** (per Theorem 3.1):
   - Sample \(X_1\) from dataset.
   - Sample \(X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\).
   - Sample \(T \sim p_T\) (e.g., uniform on [0,1]).
   - Compute \(X = (1-T)X_0 + T X_1\).
3. **Forward pass**:
   - Extract features from backbone using \(X\).
   - Predict \(u = u_\theta(X)\) (scalar) and \(v = v_\theta(X)\) (vector).
   - Compute \(\text{denoise} = X - u \cdot v\).
4. **Compute losses**:
   - \(L_{\text{OS}} = \text{mean}( \|\text{denoise} - X_1\|^2 / (\|X - X_1\|^2 + \epsilon) )\).
   - \(L_{\text{DE}} = \text{mean}( \|v - (X - X_1)/\sqrt{\|X-X_1\|^2 + c_0}\|^2 )\).
   - \(L = \lambda_1 L_{\text{OS}} + \lambda_2 L_{\text{DE}}\).
5. **Backward & optimize** (AdamW).
6. **Validation**: Periodically generate samples from noise via GD/ST; compute FID.
7. **Inference**: Start from \(x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\);
   - *Sphere tracing*: repeat \(x \gets x - \eta \cdot u(x) \cdot v(x)\) until \(u(x)\) small or max steps.
   - *Gradient descent*: repeat \(x \gets x - \eta \cdot v(x)\).

### Design Tradeoffs:
- **OSL vs FM weighting:** OSL uses inverse distance weighting in denominator, making it locally sensitive; FM uses raw \(\|v - (X_1-X_0)\|^2\) with implicit length bias.
- **Direct \(v_\theta\) prediction vs \(v_\theta = \nabla u_\theta\):** Direct prediction avoids second-order derivative instability and heavy compute; but risks non-conservative fields. Empirically converges.
- **Hyperparameters:** \(\lambda_1\) (OSL weight) often smaller (0.1); \(\lambda_2\) (DEL) larger (1–30). \(\epsilon, c_0\) stabilize near manifold; too large oversmoothes.
- **Inference step size \(\eta\):** Sphere tracing typically uses smaller \(\eta\) (e.g., 0.022) than GD (e.g., 0.64). Needs tuning but robust per Fig 32.

### Failure Signatures:
- **Mode collapse / low-contrast images:** Caused by explicit nearest-neighbor re-matching (e.g., minibatch closest coupling). Symptom: generated images are grayish and lack diversity (Fig. 33c).
- **Artifacts under conditioning:** Using optimal transport coupling when class conditioning is present distorts posterior, causing unnatural artifacts (Fig. 33b).
- **Training instability:** Setting \(\epsilon=0\) or \(c_0=0\) can cause gradients to explode near manifold; watch for NaN loss or huge \(u_\theta\) values.
- **Poor distance estimation:** Systematic underestimation for large \(\|x-x_1\|\) (Fig. 14). May lead to insufficient step sizes early in sphere tracing.
- **Non-convergent trajectories:** If \(\|v_\theta\|\) does not decay during GD, decrease \(\eta\) or increase \(\lambda_2\) to improve direction quality.

### First 3 Experiments:
1. **2D Toy Validation** (Sec 5.1, C.1):
   - Train on 8-Gaussians → two-moons.
   - Visualize level sets of \(u_\theta\) and arrows of \(v_\theta\).
   - Run sphere tracing + HMC; compute Chamfer and Hausdorff distances. Target CD <0.01, HD <0.7 (Tab 1).
2. **Loss Component Ablation on CIFAR-10** (Sec 5.6):
   - Train three models with same U-Net: (a) OSL+DEL (DM), (b) FM loss only (\(\mathbb{E}[\|v - (X_1-X_0)\|^2]\)), (c) reweighted FM (\(\mathbb{E}[(1-t)^{-2}\|v-(X_1-X_0)\|^2]\)). Use sphere tracing to sample; tune \(\eta\) for each.
   - Compare FID after fixed epochs; expect DM lowest, reweighted FM middle, FM worst (Fig 12).
3. **Distance Predictor Accuracy** (Sec 5.4):
   - On CIFAR-10 validation, compute \(u_\theta(X)\) and reference \(\sqrt{\|X-X_1\|^2 + c_0}\).
   - Plot mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) binned by reference distance; expect <5% error for most samples, with underestimation increasing for larger distances (Fig 14).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Under what conditions does the learned direction field \( v_\theta \) (which is not constrained to be the gradient of a scalar potential) guarantee convergence of the sphere tracing or gradient descent generation process to the data manifold?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors note they do not enforce \( v_\theta = \nabla u_\theta \) and that "the resulting field may not be curl-free," yet they observe convergence empirically. They call convergence without structural constraints an open area for future investigation (Sec. C.5, Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** No theoretical convergence proof is provided for a non-conservative vector field in high-dimensional generative sampling. Current guarantees for flow matching assume time-conditioned, well-behaved vector fields.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theorem bounding the deviation of \( v_\theta \) from a gradient field with respect to sampling error, or an analysis showing that the specific loss design (OSL + DEL) implicitly regularizes \( v_\theta \) toward a curl-free field in practice.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the distance-like scalar field \( u_\theta \) be theoretically linked to an underlying energy or score function, and does its learned value semantically correspond to a meaningful "distance to the data manifold" beyond the empirical scaling observed?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state \( u_\theta \) can be interpreted as a noise level estimate that maps one-to-one to time (Sec. 3.1). They also mention the model is an energy-based model (Sec. 3.1). However, they do not establish a formal equivalence or analyze the learned \( u_\theta \) values relative to true manifold distance.
- **Why unresolved:** The relationship between the trained \( u_\theta \) and true unsigned distance (or energy) is not proven. The paper shows empirical alignment (Fig. 14) but notes systematic underestimation for large distances.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal analysis (e.g., under manifold assumptions) showing that minimizing Eq. (7) yields \( u_\theta \) approximating a monotonic function of the unsigned distance to the support. Alternatively, a controlled experiment on a known low-dimensional manifold could measure correlation between \( u_\theta \) and ground-truth distance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does the one-step loss (OSL), which reweights by inverse distance, consistently yield lower FID than reweighted flow matching (\( (1-t)^{-2} \)), even though both aim to reduce the influence of far targets? Is the improvement solely due to the explicit inverse-distance weighting, or are there interactions with the neural architecture and training dynamics?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors claim OSL "explicitly prioritizes closer targets" (Sec. 4.2) and show in Fig. 12 that OSL achieves lower FID than reweighted FM. They also show in Fig. 6 that reweighted FM still biases toward the data mean.
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical analysis (Theorem A.1) shows flow matching's length-weighting, but does not directly compare to OSL's inverse-distance weighting in a way that predicts FID differences. The interaction between the weight function, network capacity, and the high-dimensional posterior is not modeled.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study isolating the weight function (same architecture, same \( v_\theta \) predictor, only change the loss weights to match OSL's inverse-distance form versus FM's length-weight) on ImageNet, measuring FID and analyzing the resulting vector field geometry.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the distance predictor \( u_\theta \) provide a principled stopping criterion for adaptive sampling that is robust across datasets and architectures, and what is its theoretical connection to convergence guarantees?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors propose adaptive stopping when \( u_\theta \) increases for 10 consecutive steps (Tab. 4) and show it reduces steps by 29.7% with small FID increase. They state \( u_\theta \) serves as a "practical stopping signal" (Sec. 5.7).
- **Why unresolved:** The heuristic (increasing \( u_\theta \)) is not theoretically derived from the distance field properties. No analysis connects it to manifold distance or residual error. Its generalizability beyond the tested settings (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) and latent space VAEs is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A convergence analysis defining a terminal condition based on \( u_\theta \) (e.g., \( u_\theta < \tau \)) and proving that it bounds the distance to the manifold with high probability under assumptions on \( v_\theta \). Empirical validation on diverse datasets (e.g., LSUN, FFHQ) and non-latent models (pixel-space) would test robustness.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What causes the train–inference mismatch when using explicit target rematching (e.g., OT coupling) with additional conditions (like class labels), and how does the inverse-distance weighting in OSL mitigate this mismatch compared to nearest-neighbor strategies?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that rematching "can alter the posterior at a given noise location, creating a train–inference mismatch" (Sec. 4.1). They show OT coupling degrades ImageNet generation (Tab. 9). They argue OSL's inverse-distance weighting "implicitly prioritizes closer targets" without full rematching.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper describes the mismatch phenomenon but does not formalize it. The precise mechanism by which inverse-distance weighting avoids altering the conditional posterior \( p(X_1|X=x) \) in a way that harms generation is not analyzed. The trade-off between locality (OSL) and diversity is discussed but not quantified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An analysis of the posterior \( p(X_1|X=x, \text{condition}) \) under different rematching schemes (no rematching, OT, minibatch closest) versus OSL's implicit weighting, showing how the support or modes change. An experiment measuring coverage (e.g., mode dropping) alongside FID under these variants for class-conditional generation.

## Limitations
- Missing exact \(p_T(t)\) distribution parameters (e.g., skewness) and full distance predictor architecture (channel counts, normalization) hinder exact replication.
- Theoretical assumption that \(x\) uniquely determines noise level may fail on disconnected manifolds; convergence of sphere tracing is empirical as \(v_\theta\) is not forced curl‑free.
- Known failure modes: output scaling mismatch for \(v_\theta\), zero denominators (\(\epsilon\), \(c_0\)), and inadvertent nearest‑neighbor rematching cause instability or mode collapse.

## Confidence
- **FID superiority over time‑unconditional baselines**: High (strong results, comprehensive ablations).
- **Class‑conditional generation beating flow matching**: Medium (depends on underspecified details; needs independent verification).
- **Distance metric for adaptive sampling/OOD**: Medium (limited OOD evaluation; AUROC reported but baseline comparisons sparse).
- **Losses reduce target ambiguity**: Medium (ablations support, but direct gradient analysis absent).
- **Time‑unconditional field sufficiency**: Medium (empirical success; theoretical gaps noted).
