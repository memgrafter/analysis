# FiMI: A Domain-Specific Language Model for Indian Finance Ecosystem

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05794
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05794
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** FiMI Instruct achieves an 87% gain on domain tool-calling, with F1 scores up to 93.5% on transaction details (English), 92.6% on Hindi, and 94.7% on mandate summary (English); it restores instruction following to 74% IFEval while maintaining general benchmark performance comparable to the base model.

## Executive Summary
General-purpose large language models (LLMs) lack the specialized understanding required for India’s UPI payments ecosystem, leading to unreliable tool use. FiMI addresses this gap by continuously pre-training Mistral Small 24B on a curated corpus of 68 billion Indian finance tokens, followed by a two-stage instruction fine-tuning and a domain-specific supervised fine-tuning phase focused on multi-turn tool-driven conversations. The resulting FiMI Base improves finance reasoning by 20% over the base model, and FiMI Instruct boosts domain tool-calling accuracy by 87% (F1 up to 93.5% for transaction details) while preserving general capabilities (e.g., MMLU 78% vs. 79% baseline). An optimal data blend (60% domain, 25% CPT, 15% IFT) recovers instruction adherence to 74% IFEval, demonstrating a successful recipe for safe, reliable financial AI assistants.

## Method Summary
FiMI’s training pipeline comprises three stages. First, continuous pre‑training (CPT) on a 68 billion‑token finance‑focused corpus (English, Hindi, Hinglish, and synthetic QA) using a 3‑phase curriculum aligns the model with financial terminology while monitoring KL divergence to avoid catastrophic forgetting. Second, instruction fine‑tuning (IFT) on Smoltalk2 (19 B tokens) and the tulu‑3‑sft‑mixture builds core reasoning and tool‑calling skills. Third, domain‑specific supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) on 108 k synthetic UPI Help conversations—generated with SME‑validated prompts and filtered for structured accuracy—teaches the model robust multi‑turn workflows for transaction disputes and mandate management. Ablation experiments on data blends identified Blend3 (60% domain SFT, 25% CPT, 15% IFT) as optimal, balancing high tool accuracy with maintained instruction following.

## Key Results
- **Finance reasoning gain:** FiMI Base improves domain reasoning by 20% over Mistral Small 24B Base.
- **Tool‑calling accuracy:** FiMI Instruct achieves an 87% gain on domain tool-calling, with F1 scores of 93.5% (English transaction details), 92.6% (Hindi), and 94.7% (English mandate summary).
- **General benchmark preservation:** MMLU remains comparable (78% vs. 79% baseline); IFEval recovers to 74% with Blend3, while CPT Eval retains 65%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Continuous Domain‑Adaptive Pre‑training
- **Claim:** CPT on 68 B Indian finance tokens internalizes domain terminology and workflows, improving finance reasoning by 20%.
- **Mechanism:** A 3‑phase annealing curriculum gradually shifts data distribution from general to finance‑dominant, updating model weights while monitoring KL divergence to preserve general knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** Controlled mixing ratios and learning rates prevent catastrophic forgetting; moderate KL divergence (~0.75) indicates alignment without overfit.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states +20% reasoning gain; Section 3 details curriculum and KL monitoring; neighboring works (Multilingual Conversational AI for Financial Assistance, SNFinLLM) report similar gains.
- **Break condition:** KL divergence ≥1 or perplexity rise signals overfitting or forgetting.

### Mechanism 2: Instruction Tuning for Tool Calling
- **Claim:** IFT on reasoning‑dominant data establishes robust instruction following and tool use, raising IFEval from 0.29 to 0.79.
- **Mechanism:** Two‑stage IFT: Stage I (Smoltalk2, 19 B tokens) builds CoT and function‑call reasoning; Stage II (tulu‑3‑sft‑mixture) refines alignment, safety, and prompt adherence.
- **Core assumption:** Diverse instruction examples teach intent parsing and structured output generation, transferable to domain tool workflows.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 shows GSM8K (0.80→0.88) and HumanEval (0.35→0.72) improvements; Stage II boosts IFEval (0.29→0.79); prior work (Large Language Models Acing Chartered Accountancy) supports instruction tuning for domain tasks.
- **Break condition:** Insufficient tool‑calling examples or excessive safety tuning may degrade reasoning (observed MMLU‑Pro drop).

### Mechanism 3: Synthetic Domain SFT with SME Validation
- **Claim:** Domain SFT on synthetic UPI Help conversations achieves high tool‑calling F1 (up to 93.5%) and safety compliance.
- **Mechanism:** High‑fidelity synthetic data generated from product docs, social‑media‑derived personas, and tool definitions; validated via structural checks and LLM‑as‑judge for factuality, tool sequencing, and guardrail adherence.
- **Core assumption:** Rigorous SME validation and iterative filtering ensure synthetic data mirrors real user interactions and enforces domain constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 87% tool‑calling gain and up to 93.5% F1; Section 4.3 describes generation & validation; Section 4.8 shows Blend3 F1 of 93.47% on get_transaction_details; FinToolBench emphasizes tool‑use evaluation.
- **Break condition:** Inadequate edge‑case coverage or validation gaps lead to semantic errors; guardrails must be embedded to avoid safety failures.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Mistral Small 24B → CPT module (68 B curated tokens, 3‑phase curriculum) → FiMI Base → IFT module (Smoltalk2 + tulu‑3‑sft‑mixture, two stages) → Domain SFT module (108 K synthetic UPI Help conversations, Blend3) → FiMI Instruct → Evaluation/Inference.

**Critical path:** CPT → IFT → SFT → Evaluation. Each stage depends on prior checkpoints; the IFT checkpoint is reusable for other NPCI products.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Data blending:* Pure domain SFT loses instruction following; mixing CPT restores knowledge but not instruction; adding IFT data recovers both (Blend3 optimal).  
- *Synthetic vs. real:* Synthetic avoids PII but requires heavy validation; real data is risky.  
- *Model size:* Mistral 24B chosen for balance; smaller models lack depth, larger models are costly.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Catastrophic forgetting: Drop in IFEval/MMLU after domain fine‑tuning.  
- Tool instability: Incorrect tool selection or arguments, especially in Hindi.  
- Safety lapses: Non‑compliant outputs when system prompts are removed.  
- Overfitting: High training performance but poor real‑world generalization.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Blend1** (100% domain SFT): Tool F1 ~89%, IFEval 11.75%, CPT Eval 58.4%. *Conclusion:* Forgets instruction and general knowledge.  
2. **Blend2** (70% domain + 30% CPT): CPT Eval 65%, IFEval ~11.5%. *Conclusion:* Recovers general knowledge but not instruction following.  
3. **Blend3** (60% domain + 25% CPT + 15% IFT): IFEval 74.1%, tool F1 93.47%, CPT Eval 65%. *Conclusion:* Balanced performance; IFT essential for instruction recovery.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Architectural efficiency for agentic workloads:** What modifications can enhance FiMI’s efficiency (lower latency, memory) while maintaining domain tool‑calling accuracy?  
   *Basis:* Conclusion mentions future work on “architectural efficiency” and “smaller models optimized for agentic AI.”  
   *Why unresolved:* No exploration of compression, distillation, or sparse architectures.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments across model sizes, quantization, and MoE on domain vs. general benchmarks.

2. **Latent biases in synthetic dialogues:** Do synthetic financial dialogues contain semantic/procedural biases limiting robustness on novel, real‑world queries?  
   *Basis (inferred):* Synthetic pipeline uses fixed product docs and limited personas; unstated coverage of full user behavior variance.  
   *Why unresolved:* Evaluation uses manually curated adversarial set, not organic production logs.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* A/B testing on de‑identified real UPI Help logs comparing FiMI against synthetic‑only baseline.

3. **Mechanism for IFT restoring instruction adherence:** Why does including general IFT data (in Blend3) restore IFEval while preserving domain gains, unlike CPT‑only mixtures?  
   *Basis:* Section 4.8.3 shows Blend3 IFEval 74% vs. Blend2 11%, but no theoretical analysis.  
   *Why unresolved:* Interplay between domain knowledge, task behavior, and instruction formatting is not modeled.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Analytical probing (e.g., causal mediation) of model representations; ablation studies with controlled mixtures tracking forgetting/recovery dynamics.

4. **Transferability to other national financial ecosystems:** Can the FiMI methodology be reliably transferred to other countries (e.g., Brazil’s Pix, Indonesia’s QRIS) with different regulations, languages, and protocols?  
   *Basis (inferred):* Pipeline is tailored to India’s UPI; success tied to India‑specific data and workflows.  
   *Why unresolved:* Financial systems vary widely; methodology may fail due to mismatched semantics or linguistic diversity.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Replication study applying identical pipeline to another country’s domain using local data and benchmarks.

5. **Scaling relationship of CPT data volume:** What is the quantitative trade‑off between CPT corpus size and performance on domain vs. general benchmarks, and what threshold triggers catastrophic forgetting (KL divergence)?  
   *Basis (inferred):* Paper selects 68 B tokens without exploring the performance curve; KL threshold ≥1 is defined but not validated as universal predictor.  
   *Why unresolved:* No scaling experiments with varying CPT token counts; optimal specialization‑generalization point unknown.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Train multiple FiMI Base models with different CPT token volumes (e.g., 30 B, 68 B, 150 B), keeping post‑training Blend3 constant; evaluate full suite and correlate KL divergence with forgetting.

## Limitations
- **CPT data composition unspecified:** Exact sampling ratios from the 2.2 T filtered corpus to yield 68 B tokens are not reported, risking replication variance.
- **Synthetic generation parameters missing:** Full prompt templates, decoding settings, and SME validation criteria for the 108 k UPI Help conversations are undisclosed, affecting reproducibility.
- **Evaluation details limited:** The “Finance Reasoning” and “MMLU Finance” benchmark sets (questions, answers, LLM‑as‑judge config) are not public, hindering independent verification.
