# Modeling 3D Pedestrian-Vehicle Interactions for Vehicle-Conditioned Pose Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.08962  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08962  
- **Reference count**: 36  
- **Primary result**: A vehicle-conditioned 3D pedestrian pose forecaster that outperforms pedestrian-only baselines by up to ~21% MPJPE in multi-pedestrian scenes with vehicles.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses 3D pedestrian pose forecasting for autonomous driving by explicitly modeling interactions with surrounding vehicles. The authors enhance the TBIFormer architecture with a dedicated vehicle encoder and a Pedestrian-Vehicle Interaction Cross-Attention module using Trajectory-Aware Relative Position Encoding (TRPE). The Waymo-3DSkelMo dataset is extended with aligned 3D vehicle bounding boxes. Evaluations show consistent improvements over pedestrian-only baselines across varying numbers of pedestrians and vehicles, with larger gains in complex scenes. Ablations confirm the necessity of both the vehicle branch and the TRPE-guided fusion.

## Method Summary
The method processes aligned 3D pedestrian poses and vehicle bounding boxes from Waymo. Pedestrian motion is represented as frame-to-frame displacements of 3D joints; vehicle motion as displacements of 8 bounding box corners. Both sequences are compressed via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and temporally downsampled. A pedestrian encoder (TBIFormer with Temporal Body Partition Module) extracts features from DCT coefficients. A vehicle encoder groups corners into 12 edges, applies temporal/identity encoding, and projects via an MLP to match pedestrian feature dimensions. Core is the Pedestrian-Vehicle Interaction Cross-Attention (PVI-CA): pedestrian queries attend to vehicle keys/values with an additive bias B_TRPE from extended Trajectory-Aware Relative Position Encodings, which encode relative spatial and trajectory relationships. A Transformer decoder predicts future displacements, inverted via IDCT to produce 3D poses. Trained end-to-end with MPJPE loss.

## Key Results
- The full model consistently reduces MPJPE compared to the pedestrian-only TBIFormer baseline across 1-, 2-, and 3-pedestrian scenarios.
- With vehicles present, MPJPE reductions range from ~1.5% to ~21%, with larger gains in multi-pedestrian scenes and as vehicle count increases.
- Ablation studies show that both the vehicle branch and the TRPE-guided cross-attention are essential; removing TRPE or using a naive vehicle incorporation harms performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Aligned 3D Multi-Agent Data**  
Enhancing Waymo-3DSkelMo with spatially and temporally aligned 3D vehicle bounding boxes creates a unified spatiotemporal scene graph, enabling direct measurement of relative positions and motions. Core assumption: vehicle detections are accurate and register reliably with LiDAR-based pedestrian poses. Break condition: systematic misalignment corrupts TRPE calculations.

**Mechanism 2: Compact Vehicle Feature via DCT and Edge Grouping**  
Vehicle corner displacements are compressed via DCT to retain dominant trends and reduce noise. The 8 corners are logically grouped into 12 edges (mimicking body parts), with temporal/identity encoding followed by an MLP projection. This yields a structured representation of vehicle motion. Assumption: edge-level grouping is an effective structural prior for interactions. Break condition: aggressive DCT or poor grouping discards important maneuvers (e.g., sudden braking) or misrepresents salient vehicle parts.

**Mechanism 3: TRPE-Guided Cross-Attention**  
PVI-CA uses pedestrian features as queries and vehicle features as keys/values, with an additive bias B_TRPE from extended Trajectory-Aware Relative Position Encodings. This bias amplifies attention for vehicle components that are spatially proximate and on an interaction course, conditioned on recent trajectories. Assumption: relative position conditioned on motion is a strong predictor of influence. Evidence: removing TRPE degrades performance by ~10% MPJPE. Break condition: inaccurate trajectory estimation or flawed encoding misguides attention.

## Foundational Learning
- **3D Human Pose Representation (SMPL/Keypoints)**: The model operates on 3D joint coordinates from SMPL-based reconstruction, not 2D detections. Using displacements emphasizes pose changes over global translation. *Quick check*: How does representing poses as 3D displacements rather than absolute positions affect sensitivity to translation vs. local dynamics?
- **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) for Time-Series Compression**: DCT compresses motion sequences into a low-dimensional, invertible basis, preserving temporal structure better than moving averages or PCA while discarding high-frequency noise. *Quick check*: What is the trade-off between compression ratio and loss of phase information when using DCT for motion trajectories?
- **Cross-Attention with Relative Position Bias**: The fusion adds a bias matrix B to attention logits (QK^T + B)/√d_z before softmax, directly injecting spatial priors. This differs from concatenating relative embeddings to Q/K, which changes the feature space. *Quick check*: How does an additive bias compare to concatenation in representational capacity and training stability?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input (Pedestrian 3D poses X^1:T+1, Vehicle 3D boxes V^1:T+1) -> Preprocessing (Displacements Y^1:T, U^1:T; DCT; downsample to length L) -> Pedestrian Encoder (TBIFormer blocks + TBPM) -> Vehicle Encoder (Edge grouping + TPE/IE + MLP) -> Fusion (PVI-CA with TRPE: Q=H_p, K=V=H_v, bias=B_TRPE) -> Decoder (Transformer decoder + body queries) -> Output (Future displacements -> IDCT -> X^T+2:T+N+1).

**Critical path**: Aligned multi-agent data -> Vehicle feature extraction -> PVI-CA fusion with TRPE -> Decoder generation. Corruption of vehicle data at step 2 breaks conditionality.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Vehicle grouping (B_V)*: More groups (e.g., 12 edges) increase granularity but also capacity/overfitting risk; ablation shows minor gains from 1→12, suggesting simpler grouping may suffice.
- *TRPE complexity vs. robustness*: Trajectory-aware encoding is expressive but relies on accurate velocity estimation; a static distance bias would be more robust but less discriminative for dynamic interactions.
- *Conditioning strength*: Direct cross-attention fully conditions on vehicles; a gating mechanism might avoid over-reliance on vehicle motion when pedestrian intent dominates (e.g., distracted pedestrian).

**Failure signatures**:
- *Vehicle branch failure*: High MPJPE even with vehicles present, similar to "+ Vehicle Center" ablation. Check: Are vehicle features constant/zero? DCT degenerate? Edge grouping logical?
- *TRPE misguidance*: Performance worse than no vehicle branch ("Veh Branch w/o TRPE"). Check: Is B_TRPE providing negative/random biases? Relative position calculations unstable?
- *Scene segmentation mismatch*: Poor performance on specific ped/veh counts. Check: Training data distribution matches test split? Distance thresholds (15 m) appropriate?

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline replication**: Train and evaluate TBIFormer (pedestrian-only) on the same segmented dataset to establish strict baseline. Verify MPJPE/APE/FDE match Table 2 for [28] (Ped. only) columns.
2. **Vehicle branch sanity check**: Train full model but replace PVI-CA with identity (concatenate features after separate encoders). Isolates value of vehicle features before fusion.
3. **TRPE ablation replication**: Reproduce "+ Veh Branch w/o TRPE" entry from Table 3. Confirms that standard cross-attention already provides gain, and adding TRPE yields marginal improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can pedestrian-pedestrian and pedestrian-vehicle interactions be jointly modeled? The current segmentation may limit pedestrian-pedestrian dynamics.
- Does a learned vehicle representation (e.g., PointNet on raw point clouds) outperform hand-crafted bounding box grouping?
- How robust is the model to noisy vehicle detections in real perception? Robustness to perception noise is critical but not evaluated.
- Can the deterministic forecaster produce multimodal predictions? A single trajectory is insufficient for planning under uncertainty.
- Does the method generalize to unseen driving environments? Experiments only on Waymo; potential biases exist.

## Limitations
- Exact DCT basis size and TRPE bias formulation are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- The approach assumes perfect spatial-temporal alignment between pedestrian poses and vehicle boxes; misregistration would degrade performance.
- Evaluation metrics (MPJPE/APE/FDE) may not capture safety-critical interaction outcomes.

## Confidence
- **Alignment-based interaction modeling**: Medium – reliant on robust registration; limited external validation.
- **Vehicle encoder (DCT + 12-edge grouping)**: Medium – ablations show small but consistent gains; frequency loss trade-offs unexplored.
- **PVI-CA with TRPE**: High – removal causes ~10% MPJPE increase, strongly supporting bias contribution, though details are hidden.

## Next Checks
1. **Baseline & ablation replication**: Reproduce TBIFormer (ped-only) and the "-Veh Branch" / "-TRPE" ablations to confirm error gaps.
2. **TRPE necessity test**: Replace TRPE with a static Euclidean-distance bias; a significant regression would confirm trajectory-aware encoding is essential.
3. **Alignment robustness check**: Introduce controlled spatial (±0.2 m, ±0.5 m) and temporal (±1 frame) offsets to vehicle boxes and measure performance degradation.
