# TopoPrune: Robust Data Pruning via Unified Latent Space Topology

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02739
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02739
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Achieves superior accuracy and precision, especially at high pruning rates (e.g., 90% on ImageNet-1K), with statistically significant variance reduction (up to 6.5×) and robust cross-architecture transferability.

## Executive Summary
TopoPrune introduces a dual-scale topological framework for training‑free data pruning, addressing the geometric brittleness of existing methods. It first uses topology‑aware manifold projection (UMAP) to create a standardized global embedding, then applies differentiable persistent homology to optimize local topological complexity per class. The unified score balances global density and local persistence, yielding coresets that maintain high model accuracy while drastically reducing variance. Evaluations on CIFAR and ImageNet datasets show state‑of‑the‑art performance, particularly at extreme pruning rates (up to 90%), and demonstrate resilience across architectures and noisy feature spaces.

## Method Summary
TopoPrune operates on frozen feature embeddings from a pretrained model. A global UMAP projection yields a 2‑D manifold, from which a kernel density estimate provides a global density score. For each class, differentiable persistent homology (Vietoris‑Rips filtration) is applied: points are iteratively adjusted to maximize persistence lifecycles, and the Euclidean displacement of each point becomes its persistence score. Optionally, a training‑free Neighborhood Label Purity Score (NLPS) filters suspected mislabels. The final ranking combines density and persistence scores (weights α, β; default α=β=0.5), and stratified sampling selects a coreset preserving class proportions. The subset is then used to train a fresh model from scratch.

## Key Results
- **High accuracy at extreme pruning:** Outperforms baselines at 90% pruning on ImageNet‑1K, achieving superior test accuracy.
- **Low variance:** Reduces performance variance by up to 6.5× compared to existing methods, improving reliability.
- **Robustness:** Maintains strong cross‑architecture transferability and resists degradation under feature noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Global manifold projection via UMAP yields a standardized, architecture‑agnostic embedding whose density is a stable indicator of sample representativeness.

**Mechanism:** High‑dimensional features are transformed into a fuzzy simplicial set; UMAP then minimizes a cross‑entropy loss between high‑ and low‑dimensional similarities to produce a 2‑D manifold preserving both local and global topology. Density computed by KDE on this manifold reflects the intrinsic probability landscape.

**Core assumption:** The intrinsic topology of the dataset (e.g., connected components, holes) is preserved across different pretrained backbones, making the UMAP embedding stable under architectural changes.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "TopoPrune operates at two scales, first, a topology‑aware manifold projection... to establish a global low‑dimensional embedding."  
- [Section 3.1] "This process yields a standardized manifold embedding that preserves the data’s intrinsic shape."  
- [corpus] Weak evidence – none of the neighboring papers directly evaluate UMAP for cross‑architecture coreset stability; however, SPHENIC uses topology‑aware clustering which loosely aligns with the idea of robust manifold representations.

**Break condition:** Poorly chosen UMAP hyperparameters (e.g., too large `n_neighbors`) may collapse local structure into a distorted global view, making density scores unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Differentiable persistent homology optimizes local topological complexity, generating a persistence score that identifies samples whose removal would destabilize class‑wise manifold structure.

**Mechanism:** For each class, a Vietoris‑Rips filtration is built on the UMAP points. A multi‑parameter persistence loss (Hilbert‑decomposed signed measure between the filtration and a density measure) is minimized by gradient descent, repositioning points to increase feature lifespans. The Euclidean displacement of each sample during this process is its persistence score.

**Core assumption:** Maximizing persistence lifecycles directly correlates with structural importance of a sample to its local neighborhood, and the density‑preserving constraint prevents global distribution distortion.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "Subsequently, it employs differentiable persistent homology to perform a local topological optimization on the manifold embeddings, ranking samples by their structural complexity."  
- [Section 3.2] "Our objective is to maximize persistence life‑cycles... The Persistence Score for each sample yi is defined as the magnitude of its total displacement."  
- [corpus] Minimal direct evidence; multipers library cited in TopoPrune provides tools, but adjacent work focuses on analysis rather than data pruning.

**Break condition:** Excessive optimization steps (T) may over‑fit spurious noise; too few steps yield coarse scores that fail to discriminate fine‑grained structure, especially at high pruning rates.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A unified score linearly combining global density and local persistence yields high‑accuracy, high‑precision coresets by balancing prototypicality and boundary complexity.

**Mechanism:** Each sample receives `Score_dens` (Kernel Density Estimate on UMAP manifold) and `Score_pers` (displacement from PH optimization). The final `Score_unified = α·Score_pers + β·Score_dens` (with α=β=0.5) ranks samples. Stratified sampling on this score preserves class proportions.

**Core assumption:** The two topological descriptors are complementary and can be combined without nonlinear interaction to capture the full spectrum of sample importance.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "The unified score balances global density and local persistence."  
- [Section 3.3] "To create a comprehensive sample importance metric, we formulate a final score that unifies the global density and local persistence information as a weighted combination."  
- [corpus] Weak evidence – CCS and D2 combine diverse metrics but not topology; no direct empirical support from neighbor papers.

**Break condition:** Weight imbalance (α/β far from 0.5) can over‑emphasize atypical boundary samples or overly prototypical ones, degrading accuracy on imbalanced datasets.

## Foundational Learning
### Persistent Homology & Vietoris–Rips Filtration
**Why needed:** The local importance score relies on building a filtration of a point cloud and tracking topological features (connected components, loops) across scales, quantifying the structural role of each sample.  
**Quick check:** How does the birth–death pair of a feature relate to the scale of the filtration in a Vietoris–Rips complex?

### Manifold Approximation (UMAP)
**Why needed:** UMAP creates a low‑dimensional embedding that retains both local neighborhoods and global topology, enabling density estimation consistent across neural architectures.  
**Quick check:** What distinguishes UMAP’s fuzzy simplicial set approach from linear PCA in terms of preserving topological relationships?

### Differentiable Persistent Homology
**Why needed:** It transforms persistent homology from a static descriptor into an optimizable function, allowing gradient‑based adjustment of point positions to maximize persistence, which directly yields the persistence score.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Feature extractor → UMAP module → Per‑class PH optimizer → Mislabel filter (optional) → Stratified sampler

**Critical path:**
1. Load pretrained model and extract features for full dataset.
2. Apply UMAP globally, obtaining 2‑D coordinates and density values.
3. For each class subset:
   a. Initialize optimizable point set from UMAP coordinates.
   b. For `t = 1 to T`: compute multi‑parameter persistence loss, back‑propagate to update points.
   c. Compute per‑sample displacement as `||y_i – y'_i||²`.
4. Fuse density and displacement into unified score; optionally remove mislabeled samples via NLPS.
5. Perform stratified sampling on clean data to pick final coreset.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- UMAP hyperparameters (`n_neighbors`, `min_dist`) control trade‑off between local and global fidelity; defaults work well but may need tuning.
- Number of PH steps `T` must increase with pruning rate; higher `T` adds compute cost.
- α/β weighting: equal (0.5) is robust default; imbalanced datasets may benefit from higher β.
- NLPS is cheap but less accurate than AUM; AUM requires training dynamics but yields stronger coreset quality.

**Failure signatures:**
- At extreme pruning rates (>90%) accuracy drops if `T` too low or UMAP `min_dist` too small.
- High variance across seeds indicates UMAP instability; fix seed or increase `n_neighbors`.
- Mislabeled samples not filtered cause severe degradation; NLPS may miss some, suggesting AUM is preferable.
- Transfer to very different architectures fails if UMAP projection does not preserve comparable topology.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline benchmarking** – Compare TopoPrune against training‑free and training‑dynamic baselines on CIFAR‑10/100 and ImageNet‑1K at multiple pruning rates.
2. **Cross‑architecture transferability** – Use a small proxy (ResNet‑18/50) to select coresets, then train various target architectures (ResNet, EfficientNet, Swin) to evaluate robustness.
3. **Noise robustness** – Inject Gaussian noise into penultimate features, prune at various rates, and compare TopoPrune against Moderate and D2 to verify resilience.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can computational efficiency of topological autoencoders be improved to enable their practical use in large‑scale data pruning?**  
   **Basis in paper:** The authors rejected TopoAE and RTD due to prohibitive training costs (e.g., ~8 hours vs. UMAP's ~22 seconds on CIFAR‑100), opting for UMAP's algorithmic projection despite potential fidelity trade‑offs.  
   **Why unresolved:** The efficiency gap prevents evaluating whether learned manifold embeddings from autoencoders could yield superior coreset quality, limiting exploration of end‑to‑end topological learning for data pruning.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Development of optimized topological autoencoder architectures or training algorithms achieving latency within an order of magnitude of UMAP, validated by accuracy/stability benchmarks on ImageNet‑1K at high pruning rates.

2. **How can the weighting between global density and local persistence scores be adaptively determined for varying datasets and pruning rates?**  
   **Basis in paper:** The authors fixed α=β=0.5 after limited hyperparameter sweeps, noting in ablation studies that task‑specific tuning could improve performance, especially at high pruning rates and on complex datasets.  
   **Why unresolved:** A fixed balance may not optimally trade‑off global representativeness and local topological complexity across diverse data distributions, potentially missing accuracy gains.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Derivation of adaptive weighting schemes (e.g., based on intrinsic dimension or class separation metrics) that consistently outperform the fixed 50/50 split across benchmarks, with statistical significance testing.

3. **What specific neural network properties cause topological features to be more architecturally stable than Euclidean metrics?**  
   **Basis in paper:** The paper empirically shows topological scores have lower variance across architectures than Euclidean distances, citing stability theorems, but does not investigate which network characteristics (e.g., depth, attention) drive this stability.  
   **Why unresolved:** Understanding these mechanisms could inform future pruning method design and clarify why topology inherently captures invariances Euclidean geometry does not.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Controlled experiments isolating factors like network width or residual connections, measuring their impact on correlation of topological vs. Euclidean metrics across architectures, possibly using synthetic model families.

4. **Can training‑free proxies for mislabel detection approach the effectiveness of AUM without requiring training dynamics?**  
   **Basis in paper:** The proposed Neighborhood Label Purity Score (NLPS) outperformed other training‑free proxies but lagged behind AUM (e.g., 41.6% vs. 45.7% at 90% pruning on CIFAR‑100), limiting fully dynamic‑free operation.  
   **Why unresolved:** Training‑free methods are essential for leveraging frozen pretrained models; improving them could eliminate reliance on costly training dynamics while maintaining coreset quality.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Development of new geometric/topological proxies (e.g., using local persistence distributions) that statistically match AUM performance across datasets and pruning levels, validated via significance tests.

5. **Is the explicit decoupling of global manifold projection and local persistent homology optimization necessary, or can a unified multi‑parameter topological approach be both effective and efficient?**  
   **Basis in paper:** The methodology separates global (UMAP) and local (persistent homology) stages for tractability and clarity, but acknowledges multi‑parameter persistence could integrate these scales; trade‑offs in accuracy vs. complexity remain unexplored.  
   **Why unresolved:** A unified approach might better capture global‑local interactions, potentially improving score accuracy, but could increase computational cost or instability.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Implementation of a single multi‑parameter persistence framework jointly optimizing global and local structures, compared against decoupled TopoPrune in coreset quality and runtime at high pruning, to assess if benefits justify added complexity.

## Limitations
- UMAP hyperparameters (e.g., `n_neighbors`, `min_dist`) critically affect manifold stability; poor choices cause topology collapse and increased variance.
- Differentiable persistent homology lacks robust tuning guidelines (steps T, learning rate η), risking over‑ or under‑fitting, especially at high pruning rates.
- Linear fusion of global density and local persistence may be suboptimal on imbalanced or complex datasets, potentially degrading accuracy.
