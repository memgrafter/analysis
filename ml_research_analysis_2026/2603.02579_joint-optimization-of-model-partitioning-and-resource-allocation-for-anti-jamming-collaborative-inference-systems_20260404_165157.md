# Joint Optimization of Model Partitioning and Resource Allocation for Anti-Jamming Collaborative Inference Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02579
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02579
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Jointly optimizes computation resources, transmit power, and DNN partitioning to maximize revenue of delay and accuracy (RDA) under malicious jamming, with simulations showing superior performance over baselines while maintaining >80% accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles malicious jamming in device-edge collaborative deep neural network (DNN) inference systems. It introduces a logistic regression model to quantify how jamming and DNN partitioning jointly degrade inference accuracy. Based on this, the authors formulate a mixed-integer nonlinear optimization problem to maximize a unified "revenue of delay and accuracy" (RDA) metric by jointly optimizing computation resource allocation, device transmit power, and partitioning decisions. An alternating optimization algorithm decomposes the problem into convex and discrete subproblems solved via KKT conditions, convex optimization, and a quantum genetic algorithm. Simulations with ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 demonstrate that the proposed scheme outperforms several baselines (local-only, edge-only, fixed power, GA-based) in RDA and consistently maintains accuracy above 80% under varying jamming power and device computing capabilities.

## Method Summary
The authors model a collaborative inference system where devices offload partial DNN computation to an edge server, with a malicious jammer degrading wireless channel quality. Inference accuracy is expressed as a logistic function of signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) and the partitioning point. The optimization problem maximizes a weighted sum of normalized delay penalty and accuracy gain, subject to accuracy, energy, and resource constraints. Due to the problem's mixed-integer nonlinear nature, an alternating optimization (AO) approach is proposed: it iteratively optimizes computation resources (using KKT conditions), transmit power (via convex reformulation and CVX), and partitioning points (via a quantum genetic algorithm). The simulation uses ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 with five partitioning points, and the accuracy model parameters are obtained via data regression from experimental measurements.

## Key Results
- The proposed AO-based scheme achieves the highest revenue of delay and accuracy (RDA) compared to baselines: local-only, edge-only, fixed transmit power, and a GA-based partitioning approach.
- Inference accuracy remains above 80% even under high jamming power (up to 1.2 W) and varying device computing capabilities (1.4–2.2 GHz).
- The scheme effectively balances delay and accuracy trade-offs by dynamically adjusting transmit power, edge resources, and partitioning in response to jamming.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Inference accuracy degradation due to jamming and DNN partitioning is modeled as a logistic function of SINR and the partitioning point.
- **Mechanism:** A data regression approach fits experimental accuracy measurements across varying partitioning points \(k_n\) and SINR \(\phi_n\) to a logistic curve \(\text{Acc}_n(k_n, \phi_n) = \frac{A_{k_n}}{1 + e^{-\tau_{k_n}(\phi_n - \phi_{k_n})}} + b_{k_n}\). This quantifies the joint impact: smaller \(k_n\) (more offloading) increases intermediate feature data (IFD) size \(S_n(k_n)\), which, under jamming-reduced SINR, lowers accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** The logistic model accurately captures the empirical relationship for the specific DNN/dataset; fitting parameters are stable and generalizable.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We first analyze the effects of jamming and DNN partitioning on inference accuracy via data regression." [section II.A] "To quantify this joint impact, we derive a closed-form accuracy model via regression analysis..." The model is novel in combining jamming and partitioning effects.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Maximizing a weighted "Revenue of Delay and Accuracy" (RDA) metric jointly optimizes computation resources \(F\), transmit power \(P\), and partitioning \(K\) under constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The RDA \(\xi \cdot bT_n + (1-\xi) \cdot d\text{Acc}_n\) linearly combines normalized delay penalty (\(bT_n\)) and accuracy gain (\(d\text{Acc}_n\)). The optimization balances these by adjusting edge compute \(f^E_n\), device power \(p_n\) (to improve SINR \(\phi_n\)), and partitioning \(k_n\) (trade-off between local compute and transmission error). Constraints enforce minimum accuracy and resource limits.
- **Core assumption:** The RDA metric correctly represents system utility and the normalization scales meaningfully for weight \(\xi\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We introduce a revenue of delay and accuracy (RDA) metric... formulate an optimization problem to maximize..." [section II.B] Explicit definitions and problem \(P0\) with constraints.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An Alternating Optimization (AO) algorithm decomposes the intractable mixed-integer nonlinear problem (\(P0\)) into three solvable subproblems.
- **Mechanism:** AO iteratively fixes two variables to optimize the third:
  1. **Computation resource allocation (\(P1\)):** Convex; solved via KKT conditions yielding closed-form \(f^{E*}_n \propto \sqrt{L_E_n(k_n)}\).
  2. **Transmit power (\(P2\)):** Non-convex originally; reformulated into convex \(P2'\) by exploiting monotonicity of SINR constraints and energy constraint, solved with CVX.
  3. **DNN partitioning (\(P3\)):** Integer program; solved by Quantum Genetic Algorithm (QGA) using qubit encoding and rotation gates to search \(k_n\) space, with penalty terms for constraints.
- **Core assumption:** AO converges to a high-quality suboptimal solution; convex relaxations for \(P2\) are valid; QGA effectively explores discrete \(k_n\) space.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "To tackle this non-convex problem, we propose an efficient iterative algorithm that decomposes it into three subproblems... solved via KKT conditions, convex optimization methods, and the QGA." [section III] Detailed derivations and QGA description.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** DNN Partitioning for Collaborative Inference  
  **Why needed:** The core decision variable \(k_n\) splits the DNN between device and edge server. Understanding how layer execution workload \(L_D_n(k_n)\), \(L_E_n(k_n)\), and IFD size \(S_n(k_n)\) scale with \(k_n\) is essential to model delay and accuracy.  
  **Quick check:** If you move the partitioning point earlier (smaller \(k_n\)), what happens to local compute delay, transmission delay, and transmission error sensitivity? How does this affect the total RDA?
- **Concept:** SINR-Based Channel Modeling with Jamming  
  **Why needed:** Jamming reduces SINR \(\phi_n\), affecting both transmission delay and accuracy. The system must compensate via power control or partitioning adjustment.  
  **Quick check:** Explain how increasing transmit power \(p_n\) affects \(\phi_n\), transmission delay, energy consumption, and ultimately accuracy via the logistic function. What is the fundamental trade-off?
- **Concept:** Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) and Alternating Optimization  
  **Why needed:** The problem combines continuous (\(f^E_n, p_n\)) and discrete (\(k_n\)) variables in a nonlinear objective with nonlinear constraints. AO is a heuristic to handle such coupling by solving easier subproblems iteratively.  
  **Quick check:** Why is \(P3\) (optimizing \(k_n\) with \(f^E_n, p_n\) fixed) still an integer program? Why can't we just enumerate all \(k_n\) combinations for small \(K, N\)? When would enumeration become infeasible?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** System Model Module calculates all delay, energy, and accuracy components from inputs. Objective & Constraints Module computes RDA and checks constraints. Solver Loop (AO) iterates: Subsolver 1 (KKT) optimizes compute resources; Subsolver 2 (CVX) optimizes transmit power; Subsolver 3 (QGA) optimizes partitioning. Convergence Check stops when RDA change is below threshold or max iterations reached.
- **Critical path:** The AO iteration loop. Performance hinges on quality of each subproblem solution and their mutual convergence; QGA's ability to find good partitioning is often the bottleneck.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Accuracy vs Complexity:* QGA provides better global search for partitioning but has higher computational cost; for real-time systems, a faster heuristic might be needed.  
  - *Optimality vs Speed:* AO is suboptimal; a monolithic MINLP solver might find better solutions but is often intractable.  
  - *Model Fidelity:* The logistic accuracy model is simple and fast but may not capture complex interactions; a more accurate model would increase solver complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. AO does not converge / cycles: indicates strong coupling; remedy: add damping or more subproblem iterations.  
  2. QGA fitness plateaus at low value: premature convergence or large search space; remedy: increase population size, adjust rotation angles, or mutation rate.  
  3. CVX fails or returns boundary \(p_n = P_{max}\): constraints too stringent; problem may be infeasible; remedy: check constraint formulations, relax parameters, or re-evaluate logistic model.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate Accuracy Model:** For fixed \(k_n\), run inference with controlled jamming, measure accuracy vs. SINR, and compare to logistic fit.  
  2. **Test AO Convergence & Subproblem Solver:** With a small system (\(N=2, K=3\)), run Algorithm 1, log variables per iteration, verify KKT conditions, CVX monotonic improvement, and QGA fitness gains.  
  3. **Replicate Baseline Comparison:** Implement baselines (LC, ESC, FTP, GA-based) using paper’s parameters, vary device compute and jamming power, and reproduce Figures 4 and 5; document deviations from missing \(k_n=0\) parameters or P2 formulation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can advanced secure transmission techniques (e.g., beamforming, artificial noise) be integrated into the current joint optimization framework to further enhance anti-jamming resilience without compromising the delay-accuracy trade-off?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion states that "future work will focus on jointly optimizing model partitioning and anti-jamming secure transmission strategies for CI," implying the current scheme does not incorporate such techniques.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only optimizes transmit power and partitioning, omitting physical-layer security methods that could mitigate jamming more effectively.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and evaluate a modified system model that includes secure transmission schemes within the optimization problem, then compare RDA performance against the proposed baseline under varied jamming conditions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is the proposed logistic regression model for inference accuracy (as a function of SINR and partitioning point) universally applicable across different DNN architectures (e.g., transformers, MobileNet) and datasets (e.g., ImageNet, speech data)?
- **Basis in paper:** The model is derived solely from experiments with ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, and while the authors claim parameters can be adjusted, no validation on other models or datasets is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** The accuracy dynamics may vary significantly with network depth, structure, or data modality, potentially invalidating the logistic form or requiring per-model refitting.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct similar data regression experiments for multiple DNN architectures and datasets, quantify goodness-of-fit (e.g., R², error distributions), and assess whether the same functional form holds or if alternative models are needed.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How robust is the proposed AO-based algorithm against adaptive or collaborative jamming scenarios where the jammer dynamically adjusts its power or targets specific devices based on observed transmission strategies?
- **Basis in paper:** The system model assumes a static jammer with fixed power \(p_j\), and the optimization treats jamming as a constant interference term. No game-theoretic or adaptive adversarial modeling is considered.
- **Why unresolved:** In practice, jammers may employ reactive strategies or coordinate across devices, making the SINR model time-varying and potentially undermining optimality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulate adaptive jammer strategies and evaluate performance degradation compared to static jamming; alternatively, formulate a min-max game and compare solutions.

## Limitations
- **Accuracy model generalizability:** The logistic fit is derived only from ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 with five partitioning points; it may not hold for other DNN architectures, datasets, or extreme jamming.
- **Missing all-edge parameters:** The ESC baseline requires accuracy parameters for \(k_n = 0\), which are not provided; assuming \(k_n=0\) equals \(k_n=1\) is undocumented and could bias baseline comparisons.
- **Heuristic AO+QGA:** No optimality guarantees; performance may depend on initialization, QGA hyperparameters, and unspecified convergence criteria.
- **Undeclared layer workloads:** Exact per-layer computation and IFD sizes for ResNet-18 are omitted; reproduction requires inferring from an unspecified implementation.

## Confidence
- Logistic accuracy model: Medium (empirically fitted for one case; may not generalize)
- RDA metric & formulation: Medium (reasonable but linear weighting untested against real utility)
- AO decomposition (KKT/CVX/QGA): Medium (subproblem solvers sound; AO heuristic, QGA may converge poorly)
