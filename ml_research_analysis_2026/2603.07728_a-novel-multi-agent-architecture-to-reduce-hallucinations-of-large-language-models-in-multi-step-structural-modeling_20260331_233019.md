# A Novel Multi-Agent Architecture to Reduce Hallucinations of Large Language Models in Multi-Step Structural Modeling
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07728
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07728
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: Multi-agent architecture with specialized LLMs achieves near-perfect accuracy (100% on 18/20, 90% on 2/20) in generating error-free OpenSeesPy scripts for 2D frame analysis, while reducing inference time and cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of hallucinations and error accumulation in large language models (LLMs) when applied to multi-step structural modeling tasks using OpenSeesPy. The authors propose a novel multi-agent architecture that decomposes the end-to-end process into specialized agents: problem analysis, construction planning, parallel geometry assembly (node and element agents), load integration, and code translation, with embedded consistency checkpoints to catch and correct errors. Two different LLM backbones are selectively assigned: GPT-OSS 120B for complex reasoning tasks (analysis, planning, geometry) and Llama-3.3 70B for translation and mapping tasks. Evaluated on a benchmark of 20 frame problems, the system achieves 100% accuracy on 18 problems and 90% on the remaining two, far outperforming sequential and general-purpose LLM baselines. Inference time is reduced from 269–949 seconds to 75.4–194.2 seconds, and cost per problem remains low ($0.013–$0.023). The work demonstrates that modularizing LLM pipelines with domain-specific specialization and validation checkpoints can significantly improve reliability and efficiency in engineering automation.

## Method Summary
The task is to automatically generate executable OpenSeesPy scripts from textual descriptions of 2D frame structures (geometry, boundary conditions, loads, materials). The proposed architecture solves this by breaking it into seven pipeline stages:

1. **Problem analysis agent** parses the input text and outputs a structured JSON with geometry, supports, material properties, and load patterns.
2. **Construction planning agent** produces a stepwise plan describing the bay-by-bay and story-by-story assembly process, specifying step types (column, beam, etc.).
3. **Node agent** and **element agent** run in parallel to generate node coordinates and element connectivity based on the plan, outputting JSON lists.
4. **Connectivity mapping** is a deterministic function that converts node coordinates into element definitions (node ID pairs) and resolves duplicates.
5. **Load assignment agent** maps loads from the problem analysis to the appropriate elements, producing a loads JSON.
6. **JSON compiler** aggregates all JSON artifacts into a single data structure.
7. **Code translator agents** first convert the aggregated geometry into OpenSeesPy commands (node, element definitions) and then generate the complete script (including analysis, constraints, loads, and execution).

Consistency checkpoints are placed after the planning stage (validating total steps against geometry) and after geometry assembly (checking for duplicate nodes/elements, unmapped coordinates, orphan nodes). On failure, upstream agents are regenerated up to five times. The system uses no fine-tuning; performance relies on careful prompt engineering and deterministic post-processing.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: 100% error-free script generation on 18 of 20 benchmark problems; 90% on the remaining two (one failure due to planning mismatch, one due to load mapping error).
- **Efficiency**: Inference time reduced from 269–949 seconds (sequential and general LLM baselines) to 75.4–194.2 seconds; cost per problem ranges from $0.013 to $0.023.
- **Scalability**: The architecture scales to larger frames (e.g., 10-bay, 10-story) maintaining 80–100% accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of the architecture stems from three synergistic design choices. First, **task decomposition** isolates distinct reasoning challenges (semantic parsing, spatial planning, coordinate calculation, load mapping, code generation) into separate agents, preventing error propagation that occurs in monolithic LLM pipelines. Second, **LLM specialization** matches each agent to the LLM best suited for its cognitive profile: GPT-OSS 120B handles complex, multi-faceted reasoning tasks requiring deep structural understanding, while Llama-3.3 70B excels at deterministic translation and mapping tasks where consistency is paramount. This leverages the complementary strengths of large and mid-sized models. Third, **embedded consistency checkpoints** act as guardrails; they automatically validate intermediate outputs against structural constraints (e.g., total number of bays, absence of duplicate nodes) and trigger targeted regeneration before errors cascade. The combination of modularization, adaptive LLM assignment, and rigorous validation yields a robust system that maintains high accuracy while reducing both time and cost compared to naive approaches.

## Foundational Learning
The following concepts underpin the architecture and are essential for understanding or extending it:

- **Multi-Agent Systems**: Coordination of multiple specialized agents to solve complex tasks. *Why needed*: Enables separation of concerns and targeted LLM use. *Quick check*: Can the pipeline be described as a series of independent stages with well-defined inputs/outputs?
- **Consistency Checkpoints**: Automated validation steps that verify intermediate results against domain constraints. *Why needed*: Catches hallucinations before they propagate. *Quick check*: Are there explicit tests after planning and geometry assembly that can fail and trigger retries?
- **LLM Specialization**: Using different language models for different subtasks based on their strengths. *Why needed*: Optimizes performance and cost; large models for reasoning, smaller for translation. *Quick check*: Does the architecture assign GPT-OSS to reasoning agents and Llama to translation agents?
- **OpenSeesPy**: Domain-specific scripting language for structural analysis. *Why needed*: The final output must be executable by the OpenSees engine. *Quick check*: Are generated scripts syntactically valid OpenSeesPy commands?
- **JSON Interchange Format**: Structured data used to pass information between agents. *Why needed*: Provides a stable, machine-readable contract. *Quick check*: Do all agents output/consume well-defined JSON schemas?
- **Prompt Engineering**: Crafting instructions to guide LLM behavior without fine-tuning. *Why needed*: The system relies on zero-shot or few-shot prompting to achieve specialized behaviors. *Quick check*: Are agent roles and output formats defined via system prompts and examples?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Problem Analysis Agent -> Construction Planning Agent -> Node Agent & Element Agent (parallel) -> Connectivity Mapping -> Load Assignment Agent -> JSON Compiler -> Code Translator -> OpenSeesPy Execution

### Critical Path
The main execution flow from textual input to runnable script follows: (1) parse description, (2) plan construction steps, (3) generate nodes, (4) generate elements, (5) resolve connectivity, (6) assign loads, (7) compile JSON, (8) translate to code, (9) execute. Checkpoints may cause loops back to earlier stages but the ideal path is linear after planning.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Specialization vs. Overhead**: Multiple agents increase coordination complexity and API calls but reduce hallucinations. The authors accept this overhead for higher accuracy.
- **Model Selection**: Using a 120B parameter model for reasoning yields quality results but is expensive and slower; the hybrid approach keeps average cost low by using the smaller model for most translation steps.
- **Deterministic Post-processing**: Relying on Python functions for connectivity and compilation avoids LLM variability in these steps, improving reliability at the cost of additional development effort.

### Failure Signatures
- Duplicate node/element definitions in final script.
- Mismatch between planned steps and actual geometry counts.
- Load references to non-existent element IDs.
- Incorrect node y-coordinates due to cumulative height miscalculation.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Baseline run**: Execute the complete pipeline on a single benchmark problem using the reported LLM APIs and verify that the generated OpenSeesPy script runs without errors and produces expected analysis results.
2. **Checkpoint validation**: Intentionally corrupt the output of the planning agent (e.g., change number of bays) and observe that the consistency checkpoint triggers a regeneration, and that the system recovers within the retry limit.
3. **Specialization test**: Replace the hybrid LLM assignment with a single LLM (either GPT-OSS or Llama) across all agents and measure the drop in accuracy, runtime, and cost compared to the published hybrid configuration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can an automated prompt optimization framework be developed to adapt domain-specific structural modeling instructions to diverse LLM backbones without manual retuning?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 5 identifies that "the current prompt templates are effective for the selected backbone LLMs, they fail to maintain consistent reliability across other models" and calls for "an automated prompt optimization framework."
- **Why unresolved**: The architecture’s success depends on handcrafted prompts tuned for GPT-OSS 120B and Llama-3.3 70B. Prompt sensitivity is a known LLM challenge, so extending to new models (e.g., Gemini, Claude) requires costly per-model re-engineering.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A system that, given a target LLM and a set of structural problems, automatically generates or optimizes prompts for each agent role (analysis, planning, etc.) such that accuracy matches the current hybrid-LLM architecture. Validation would involve zero-shot transfer to at least three unseen LLM families on the benchmark.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can the multi-agent architecture be extended to handle structural systems with non-rectangular geometries, such as frames with diagonal bracing, cantilevers, or irregular bay configurations?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 5 states the architecture "is restricted to the structural analysis of rectangular frames with vertical columns and horizontal beams" and lacks capability for "diagonal bracing and cantilevers."
- **Why unresolved**: The node and element agents rely on step types and coordinate rules specific to orthogonal, bay-by-bay assembly. Non-orthogonal elements require new geometric reasoning and connectivity logic, risking hallucinations in node/element generation.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An extended architecture where agents parse textual descriptions mentioning diagonals or overhangs, generate valid node coordinates and element connectivity for such geometries, and produce executable OpenSeesPy scripts. Testing on a new benchmark with braced frames and cantilevers would confirm generalizability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can the code translation module be enhanced to support dynamic loading (e.g., seismic, wind) and nonlinear analysis (e.g., material plasticity, geometric nonlinearities) in OpenSeesPy?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 5 notes the system is "limited to linear elastic behavior under static loading conditions" and that incorporating dynamic/nonlinear analysis is "essential for broader applicability."
- **Why unresolved**: The current translation agents only script static linear elastic models. Dynamic analysis requires time series and load patterns; nonlinear analysis needs material models and convergence settings. Translating these from natural language without errors is an unaddressed challenge.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extended translation agents that generate correct OpenSeesPy commands for dynamic ground motion analysis or pushover nonlinear analysis. Successful execution and physically plausible results on benchmark problems (e.g., response spectrum analysis, cyclic loading) would validate the enhancement.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What intrinsic LLM properties determine task-specific performance (e.g., reasoning vs. translation) in multi-agent structural modeling, and how can this inform robust agent-LLM allocation strategies?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 4.2 shows GPT-OSS 120B excels at complex reasoning but fails in mapping/translation, while Llama-3.3 70B is reliable in translation but hallucinates in planning. The authors allocate accordingly but do not explain why.
- **Why unresolved**: The allocation is based on empirical trial; without understanding correlates (e.g., pretraining data, architecture, instruction-tuning), selecting LLMs for new agent roles as models evolve requires repeated benchmarking.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A correlational study linking LLM metrics (e.g., parameter count, benchmark scores on reasoning/coding tasks, tokenization schemes) to per-agent success rates across the 20-problem benchmark. A predictive model could then recommend LLM assignments for unseen agent-task combinations.

## Limitations
- **Prompt dependency**: The exact prompts and system messages for each agent are undisclosed, hindering exact replication of the 100% accuracy result.
- **Checkpoint opacity**: The specific validation logic, thresholds, and retry mechanisms for consistency checkpoints are not provided; only high-level descriptions exist.
- **Model ambiguity**: The reported models (“GPT-OSS 120B”, “Llama-3.3 70B Instruct Turbo”) are not clearly defined; publicly available equivalents may differ, affecting performance portability.

## Confidence
- **Accuracy claims (100%/90%)**: Medium confidence (due to missing artifacts and model specificity).
- **Architecture design (specialized agents + checkpoints)**: High confidence (clearly described and aligns with multi-agent principles).
- **Cost/time metrics (reduction from baselines)**: Medium confidence (empirical measurements are plausible but rely on unavailable model configurations).
