# Imagine How To Change: Explicit Procedure Modeling for Change Captioning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.05969
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05969
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ProCap achieves state-of-the-art results on CLEVR-Change (CIDEr 135.6), Spot-the-Diff (CIDEr 42.7), and Image-Editing-Request (CIDEr 40.6), outperforming prior methods by notable margins while also demonstrating improved robustness and inference speed.

## Executive Summary
ProCap is a two-stage framework that reformulates change captioning as dynamic procedure modeling. Unlike prior static approaches, it first trains a procedure encoder on keyframes sampled from synthesized intermediate frames via a caption-conditioned masked reconstruction task, capturing spatio-temporal dynamics. In the second stage, it replaces explicit keyframes with learnable procedure queries, enabling efficient end-to-end captioning without frame synthesis. ProCap sets new state-of-the-art on three benchmarks, with gains in robustness and speed.

## Method Summary
Stage 1 (Explicit Procedure Modeling) uses a pre-trained frame interpolation model to generate pseudo-intermediate frames between two input images. A confidence-based sampler selects a few keyframes, which are tokenized and fed to a transformer encoder. The encoder reconstructs masked visual tokens while being conditioned on the full caption and also optimized for cross-modal alignment and temporal consistency. Stage 2 (Implicit Procedure Captioning) freezes this encoder and inserts learnable query embeddings (representing virtual keyframes) between the encoded features of the before and after images. A text decoder then generates the change caption. This design avoids costly frame synthesis at inference while leveraging the procedural knowledge learned from explicit procedures.

## Key Results
- ProCap sets new SOTA on CLEVR-Change (CIDEr 135.6), Spot-the-Diff (CIDEr 42.7), and Image-Editing-Request (CIDEr 40.6).
- It outperforms prior methods by notable margins.
- The model shows improved robustness and higher inference speed compared to prior work.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ProCap works through three synergistic mechanisms. (1) Explicit procedure modeling approximates the continuous change process with a discrete set of keyframes, forcing the encoder to learn temporal dynamics and align them with the caption. (2) Learnable procedure queries allow the frozen encoder to implicitly infer the same procedural representation directly from static images, eliminating the need for inference-time frame synthesis while retaining efficiency. (3) Multi-granularity masking during pre-training trains the encoder to reconstruct visual tokens at different granularities (frames, patches, blocks) under caption guidance, yielding representations that capture both coarse semantic flows and fine details. Together, these mechanisms produce accurate and fluent change captions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Frame Interpolation (FI)**: Generates intermediate frames to make implicit dynamics explicit. *Why needed*: Provides a dense approximation of the change procedure for pre‑training. *Quick check*: Does the FI model produce physically plausible transitions? Strong constraints (e.g., optical flow) yield better downstream results.
- **Masked Multi‑modal Reconstruction**: Training objective where masked visual tokens are predicted conditioned on text. *Why needed*: Aligns visual dynamics with language semantics, preventing over‑reliance on static cues. *Quick check*: Keeping the caption fully visible ensures the encoder learns to ground reconstruction in the description.
- **Learnable Query Prompts**: Fixed‑size embeddings inserted between image features to simulate keyframes. *Why needed*: Enables efficient inference by avoiding explicit frame synthesis. *Quick check*: Queries are optimized during captioning training to directly elicit procedural features from the frozen encoder.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Image pair → CNN backbone (shared) → Patch embeddings → (Stage 1: Keyframes from FI + confidence sampler → Procedure Encoder → Reconstruction losses; Stage 2: Learnable procedure queries → Procedure Encoder → Text Decoder → Caption)

**Critical path**:  
*Stage 1*: FI generates 7 pseudo‑frames → Compute similarity scores (visual‑text or visual‑visual) → Normalize and sample top‑k keyframes (k=2) → Tokenize via VQGAN → Apply multi‑granularity masking to patch embeddings → Encoder reconstructs masked tokens → Joint losses: L_msm, L_align, L_csy.  
*Stage 2*: Extract patches of before/after images via CNN → Concatenate with k×n_I learnable query embeddings → Encoder processes → Decoder autoregressively generates caption with cross‑entropy loss. Training end‑to‑end; inference uses only learnable queries.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Keyframe count (k)*: More keyframes improve procedural detail but increase quadratic attention cost and may introduce noise.  
- *FI model choice*: Optical‑flow‑based FI offers geometric consistency but may fail on complex motions; diffusion‑based FI is more flexible but introduces noise that can degrade representation (Table 9).  
- *Query reuse*: Learnable queries boost efficiency but slightly underfit compared to using real keyframes (Table 14), creating a performance‑efficiency trade‑off.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Unrealistic FI outputs (e.g., artifacts) produce misleading keyframes → high reconstruction loss in Stage 1 and low CIDEr.  
- Mis‑configured masking probabilities (e.g., over‑using entire‑frame masks) or incorrect negative sampling weakens representation → measurable drop in validation metrics.  
- Over‑fitting to synthetic procedures on small real datasets (e.g., Spot‑the‑Diff) leads to poor generalization; monitor validation loss.  
- Learnable query size k mismatched to scene complexity: too few merges distinct changes; too many adds noise → non‑monotonic CIDEr (Table 8).  
- Query collapse: embeddings become indistinguishable → stagnating metrics during Stage 2 training.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Stage ablation** (Table 2): Removing Stage 1 or Stage 2 components isolates their contributions to SOTA.  
2. **Procedure query length analysis** (Table 3): Varying k (number of queries) to balance accuracy and inference speed.  
3. **Multi‑granularity masking ablation** (Table 11): Removing each masking strategy (entire, random, in‑block, out‑of‑block) shows their individual impact, guiding probability schedule.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **3D‑aware representations**: How can 3D scene modeling be integrated to handle extreme geometric discontinuities and produce physically plausible intermediate frames? Current FI fails under large viewpoint changes.
- **Optimal informative point**: What is the theoretically optimal criterion for selecting informative keyframes in a change procedure? The current confidence‑based heuristic lacks a firm foundation.
- **LLM integration**: Can large language models be combined with ProCap to leverage high‑level reasoning while preserving its temporal modeling and efficiency? Naive integration may discard learned dynamics.

## Limitations
- Frame interpolation model specifics (checkpoint, hyperparameters, fine‑tuning) are undisclosed; FI quality directly affects performance and reproducibility.
- Confidence‑based frame sampling details (exact scoring formula, CLIP/DINOv2 layers) are vague, leading to implementation variability.
- Negative sampling constructions for alignment and temporal consistency losses are only partially defined, requiring guesswork and potentially hurting results.

## Next Checks
1. **FI backbone robustness**: Train Stage 1 with multiple FI models (e.g., RIFE, diffusion‑based) and correlate FI quality metrics (LPIPS, FVD) with downstream CIDEr on CLEVR‑Change to determine sensitivity.
2. **Sampling transparency**: Re‑implement the confidence module using standard CLIP/DINOv2 features; compare keyframe selection distributions and final CIDEr against the paper’s reported values.
3. **Negative sampling sensitivity**: Ablate negative caption construction (random vs. hard negatives) and warping parameters for L_align and L_csy; measure impact on validation loss, CIDEr, and caption diversity.
