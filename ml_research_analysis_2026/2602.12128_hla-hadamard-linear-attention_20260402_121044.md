# HLA: Hadamard Linear Attention

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12128
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12128
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HLA reduces FLOPs by 75-89% and inference latency by 10-42% on video diffusion transformers with sequences up to 32,760 tokens, achieving comparable VBench scores (81.42 vs 83.31).

## Executive Summary
Hadamard Linear Attention (HLA) introduces a novel approach to linear attention that mitigates the low-rank approximation problem while retaining linear computational complexity. By applying a nonlinearity after pairwise interactions—computing element-wise products of multiple transformed query-key pairs—HLA achieves a higher-degree rational approximation of softmax attention. The method is efficiently implemented via tensor contractions, avoiding costly reshaping operations. Evaluated on a video diffusion transformer handling extremely long sequences (up to 32,760 tokens), HLA reduces FLOPs by 75-89% and latency by 10-42% with only a small drop in VBench score (81.42 vs 83.31). This demonstrates a promising direction for scaling Transformers to long contexts without sacrificing performance.

## Method Summary
HLA modifies standard linear attention by using multiple projection heads for queries and keys. For a sequence of length N and hidden dimension d, HLA computes F separate outer products between projected queries and keys: each query projection ϕ_q(Q) and key projection ϕ_{kf}(K) produce feature vectors, and their outer product is computed via einsum. The element-wise product across all F factors yields a high-order interaction tensor. This product is then contracted with the value tensor to produce the context, following equation 12. A normalization term η ensures the weights sum to one. The entire process maintains O(N) complexity because all operations are implemented as tensor contractions without materializing the N×N attention matrix. Optional value modulation can further refine the output. The projected features are designed to be non-negative (e.g., via ReLU) to satisfy the normalization assumptions.

## Key Results
- FLOPs reduction: 75–89% (e.g., 0.77 vs 7.21 TFLOPs) on sequences up to 32,760 tokens.
- Quality retention: VBench score of 81.42 compared to 83.31 for standard attention (1.9% relative drop).
- Latency improvement: Inference speedup of 10–42% depending on sequence length and hardware.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hadamard Linear Attention (HLA) approximates softmax attention with a higher-degree rational function than standard linear attention.
- Mechanism: HLA computes attention scores as element-wise products of F pairwise interactions from separate linear projections: ∏_{f=1}^F (ϕ_q(Q)·ϕ_{kf}(K)^T). This yields a rational function with numerator degree 2F+1 and denominator degree 2F, where F is the number of factors.
- Core assumption: The expressive power of attention is limited by the degree of its rational approximation; a higher degree can better model the sparse, high-magnitude peaks of softmax.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "applying a nonlinearity after pairwise interactions via element-wise product of multiple linear projections, yielding a higher-degree rational approximation of softmax."
  - [section 4.3] "The numerator has degree 2F+1, while the denominator has 2F. Standard linear attention is equivalent to a rational function of degree 3 for the numerator and 2 for the denominator."
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus papers (e.g., “Higher-order Linear Attention”) discuss degree in linear attention but do not specifically address HLA’s formulation.
- Break condition: If increasing F does not improve performance on target tasks, or if the computational cost O(d^F) becomes prohibitive for hidden dimension d.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: HLA achieves O(N) computational complexity without costly tensor reshaping.
- Mechanism: By representing the attention computation as a tensor contraction (A·V = T_q ⊗ C, Theorem 4.2), HLA uses einsum-style operations to compute outer products and contractions directly, avoiding explicit pairwise score matrices and reshaping steps.
- Core assumption: Modern hardware can efficiently execute these tensor operations; avoiding reshaping reduces memory overhead and latency.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "An efficient O(N) computation scheme derived using tensor contractions avoids costly reshaping."
  - [section 4.2] Detailed derivation and Algorithm 1 show the procedure using standard einsum patterns.
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus mentions linear efficiency in related works but not HLA’s specific avoidance of reshaping.
- Break condition: If tensor contractions are not optimized on a given platform, the expected linear scaling may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: HLA mitigates the low-rank constraint inherent in standard linear attention, reducing information loss per layer.
- Mechanism: In standard linear attention, rank(ϕ(Q)·C) ≤ min(rank(ϕ(Q)), rank(C)). HLA’s element-wise product of F interactions yields rank ≤ ∏_{f=1}^F rank(ϕ(Q)·ϕ_{kf}(K)^T), relaxing the bottleneck (Eq. 6).
- Core assumption: The low-rank constraint is a primary cause of performance degradation in linear attention; relaxing it improves model capacity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "linear attention ... suffering from low-rank approximation, causing performance loss. Hadamard Linear Attention (HLA) addresses this ..."
  - [section 4.1] "Since the bottleneck imposed by the low-rank constraint is relaxed, a network based on HLA instead of standard linear attention achieves less information loss at each attention layer."
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus paper “Token Statistics Transformer” discusses low-rank issues but not HLA’s relaxation.
- Break condition: If the rank relaxation does not translate to empirical gains (e.g., due to optimization challenges or task-specific factors).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. Input projections for Q, K, V (linear layers).
  2. HLA-specific projections: ϕ_q for Q; ϕ_{k1}, ..., ϕ_{kF} for K (F=2 or 3); optional ϕ_{v1}, ϕ_{v2} for value modulation.
  3. Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) applied to Q and K.
  4. Scale Q by √d.
  5. Core HLA: compute k_outerprods = ∏_{f=1}^F einsum('bhsd,bhse->bhsde', k_f, ...); context C = einsum('bhmde,bhmg->bhdeg', k_outerprods, V); q_outerprods similarly; attention = einsum('bhsd,bhde->bhse', q_outerprods, C).
  6. Normalization: η from summed q_outerprods and k_outerprods; divide q_outerprods by η.
  7. Optional value modulation: T = attention + ϕ_{v1}(attention) ⊙ ϕ_{v2}(V).
  8. Output projection.

- **Critical path**: The k_outerprods tensor (size N×d_φ^F) and context C (d_φ^F×d) dominate memory and compute. The two major einsum contractions are O(N d^F). For F=3, d_φ=128, d_φ^F = 2M, which can be large.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - F: Higher F increases expressive power (higher-degree rational) but costs O(d^F). Choose F=2 or 3 based on d and hardware.
  - d_φ (projection hidden dim): Larger d_φ improves approximation but increases memory quadratically/exponentially with F.
  - Value modulation: Adds two MLPs; may restore high frequencies but can attenuate if misdesigned (e.g., sigmoid).
  - Layer coverage: Replace only some attention layers (e.g., 10/30) to balance efficiency and quality.
  - Training: Stage-wise (first HLA layers frozen, then full) plus synthetic data helps convergence.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - NaNs/divergence: Often due to violation of non-negative constraint in ϕ projections; ensure ReLU or other non-negativity.
  - Poor accuracy despite low FLOPs: Insufficient capacity (small d_φ or F), inadequate training steps, or unsuitable layer selection.
  - Out-of-memory: d_φ^F too large; reduce d_φ or F, or use gradient checkpointing.
  - Low speedup than expected: Inefficient einsum patterns or non-contiguous tensors; verify with profiling.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Synthetic validation: On a toy task requiring nonlinear interactions (e.g., parity), compare linear attention (F=1) vs. HLA (F=2,3) to confirm higher-degree approximation improves accuracy.
  2. Scaling benchmark: Measure FLOPs and wall-clock time for sequence lengths from 1k to 32k on target hardware; confirm O(N) scaling and expected FLOP reduction (e.g., 75-89%).
  3. Value modulation ablation: Train HLA with and without equation (18) on a video diffusion subset; compare VBench scores to quantify its impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**
  - Question: How does HLA generalize to tasks beyond video generation, such as language modeling or image recognition?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates HLA exclusively on video diffusion models (Sections 5.1-5.2), despite mentioning broader applications in the introduction and related works.
  - Why unresolved: Without cross-domain validation, it is unclear if performance gains are specific to video data or transferable, limiting claims about HLA's versatility.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Benchmark HLA on standard datasets like GLUE (NLP) and ImageNet (vision), comparing against standard and linear attention baselines in terms of accuracy and efficiency.

- **Open Question 2**
  - Question: What is the optimal number of Hadamard product factors F for balancing approximation quality and computational cost across varying sequence lengths?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares F=2 and F=3 (Section 5.3, Table 2), noting that F=3 improves VBench scores but increases polynomial complexity (Section 4.3), yet does not explore F beyond 3 or characterize the trade-off.
  - Why unresolved: The relationship between F, performance, and FLOPs is not systematically studied; a higher F might yield diminishing returns or inefficiencies at different scales.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments with F=1,4,5,... on multiple sequence lengths, measuring metrics like VBench scores, FLOPs, and latency to identify optimal points.

- **Open Question 3**
  - Question: How does the rational function approximation of HLA quantitatively compare to softmax attention in terms of output error and attention matrix properties?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper theoretically shows HLA uses a higher-degree rational function (Lemma 4.3), but provides no empirical measurement of approximation error relative to softmax.
  - Why unresolved: While higher degree suggests better approximation, the actual closeness in outputs, rank, or sparsity is unvalidated, questioning if HLA truly mimics softmax behavior.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Compute error metrics (e.g., Frobenius norm) between HLA and softmax attention outputs on held-out data; analyze rank and sparsity patterns of attention matrices across layers.

## Limitations
- Architectural details of the ϕ projections (layer dimensions, activation functions, LayerNorm usage) are ambiguous, risking implementation errors that could break non-negativity assumptions and normalization.
- Training protocol uncertainties, including synthetic video generation parameters and exact step counts for the two-stage procedure, hinder faithful reproduction and may affect convergence.
- The role of the value modulation module (ϕ_v) in the final models is unclear; if omitted, the completeness of the theoretical formulation is not empirically validated.
