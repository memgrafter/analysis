# RAE-AR: Taming Autoregressive Models with Representation Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01545
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01545
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RAE-AR combines token normalization and Gaussian noise injection to enable high-quality image generation with representation autoencoders, achieving VAE-comparable results (e.g., DINOv2 gFID 15.1 -> 7.5).

## Executive Summary
Autoregressive (AR) image generation models struggle when using representation autoencoders (e.g., DINOv2, SigLIP2, MAE) due to high token-wise variance and amplified exposure bias. The authors propose RAE-AR, a simple yet effective approach that normalizes token distributions and injects Gaussian noise during training. On ImageNet, these modifications dramatically improve generation quality across multiple representation backbones, bringing them on par with traditional VAE-based AR models. The work highlights the importance of data scaling and robustness in continuous AR modeling.

## Method Summary
RAE-AR builds on a continuous AR framework (e.g., MAR). Images are encoded by a frozen representation encoder into a sequence of tokens. Before feeding them to the AR transformer, the tokens are normalized to zero mean and unit variance per spatial position, reducing token-wise variance. During training, isotropic Gaussian noise is added to the normalized tokens, simulating exposure bias and forcing the model to condition on perturbed inputs. The AR transformer processes the noisy sequence causally; a diffusion MLP predicts the noise for each token. At inference, tokens are generated sequentially via diffusion sampling using the previous denormalized tokens, then denormalized using stored statistics and passed to a pretrained decoder. The combination of normalization and noise injection mitigates both training difficulty and inference mismatch.

## Key Results
- DINOv2 latents: gFID improves from 15.137 to 7.494; IS rises from 108.384 to 165.588.
- SigLIP2 latents: gFID drops from 34.084 to 6.091.
- MAE latents: gFID decreases from 67.642 to 9.083.
- Results are comparable to VAE-based AR models, demonstrating generality across both causal and mask-based AR paradigms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Token Variance Reduction
High token-wise distribution variance impedes AR training. AR models model each token's distribution via an MLP; large token variance makes density estimation harder, increasing loss and slowing convergence. Token normalization (zero mean, unit variance per token) reduces this variance, easing the training objective and accelerating convergence. Evidence: lower token variance correlates with better generation (Table 3) and faster training curves (Figure 3).

### Mechanism 2: Exposure Bias Amplification
Autoregressive models suffer from training-inference distribution mismatch (exposure bias). During training they condition on ground-truth tokens, while at inference they condition on their own predictions. High-dimensional representation latents have many degrees of freedom, so small errors amplify into larger distribution shifts, compounding over the sequence. Noise injection during training simulates exposure bias, making the denoiser robust to perturbed conditioning tokens. Evidence: Noise injection improves DINOv2 gFID from 15.137 to 9.516, while normalization alone does not fix exposure bias (gFID 18.090).

### Mechanism 3: Synergistic Combination
Normalization and noise injection are complementary: normalization eases training, noise injection bridges the training-inference gap. Combining both yields the best results (e.g., SigLIP2 gFID 34.084 -> 6.091) and matches VAE-based AR performance. The synergy is distinct from other strategies (e.g., dimension-dependent noise schedules) that fail for AR models.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input image -> Representation encoder (frozen, e.g., DINOv2) -> Token sequence Z ∈ ℝ^{N×d} -> (optional) Token normalization: Z_norm = (Z - μ) / σ -> (training only) Gaussian noise injection: Z̃ = Z_norm + α·ε -> Causal AR transformer (width=1280) -> Feature sequence F ∈ ℝ^{N×d'} -> Diffusion MLP (per token) -> Predicted normalized token or noise -> Loss (MSE) -> In inference: sequential diffusion sampling -> Denormalized tokens -> Decoder -> Image.

**Critical path (inference):**  
1. Token 0: condition on [START] token → AR transformer → F_0 → diffusion MLP → Ẑ_0.  
2. For i = 1..N-1: use previously generated tokens [Ẑ_0,...,Ẑ_{i-1}] (denormalized but then re-normalized) → AR transformer (causal) → F_i → diffusion → Ẑ_i.  
3. After all N tokens, feed the sequence to the pretrained decoder to produce the image.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Token normalization reduces variance but discards absolute scale; must be combined with noise injection.  
- Noise level α balances robustness and signal preservation; too high degrades quality, too low insufficient. Typical α ∈ [0.05, 0.3].  
- Model capacity: AR transformer must be wide (e.g., 1280) to handle high-dimensional latents; diffusion MLP stays small.  
- Using dimension-dependent noise schedules (from related RAE work) harms AR performance.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training loss plateaus high without token normalization.  
- Generation quality poor (high gFID) without noise injection, even if normalization used.  
- Performance deteriorates when α exceeds optimal range.  
- Inconsistent gains if applying dimension-dependent schedules.  
- Out-of-memory due to large model width and sequence length; mitigated by checkpointing.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline: Train AR with DINOv2 latents, no normalization, no noise. Expect gFID ~15.1, IS ~108.4.  
2. +Normalization only: Add token normalization. Expect faster convergence but gFID ~18.1 (no improvement or worse).  
3. +Noise injection only: Add Gaussian noise (α=0.1) during training. Expect gFID ~9.5.  
These establish individual contributions; combining both yields RAE-AR.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction challenges:** Key hyperparameters for decoder training and AR diffusion steps are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.  
- **Mechanistic evidence:** The paper's analysis of token variance and exposure bias is correlational; causal validation is needed.  
- **Generalization:** Experiments are limited to ImageNet at 256×256; performance on other datasets or higher resolutions remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness of RAE-AR (normalization + noise):** High  
- **Token variance as a training impediment:** Medium  
- **Amplified exposure bias due to high-D latents:** Medium  
- **Generalization beyond ImageNet:** Low

## Next Checks
1. **Variance causality:** Artificially increase token variance (e.g., by scaling latent channels) and confirm that AR training loss rises and convergence slows, independent of other factors.  
2. **Exposure bias quantification:** Measure KL divergence between the distribution of ground-truth conditioning tokens and those seen during inference with/without noise injection; verify that noise reduces the divergence.  
3. **Noise schedule comparison:** Implement the dimension-dependent noise schedule from related work (e.g., Diffusion Transformers with Representation Autoencoders) and demonstrate that isotropic noise yields superior generation for AR models on the same backbone.
