# Navigating Time's Possibilities: Plausible Counterfactual Explanations for Multivariate Time-Series Forecast through Genetic Algorithms

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00855
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00855
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** The genetic algorithm successfully found satisfying counterfactual scenarios in over 90% of 30 experimental runs (28/30), providing actionable insights for preventing vacuum breaks with quantified scenario likelihoods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of generating plausible counterfactual explanations for multivariate time-series forecasts. Given a desired target outcome, the method seeks interventions on input variables that would lead to that outcome while remaining historically plausible. The proposed framework combines Granger causality tests to filter causally relevant variable relationships, quantile regressors to model distributional uncertainty for each variable, and a genetic algorithm to search over sequences of quantile selections (interventions) that satisfy outcome constraints within a tolerance. Evaluated on real-world industrial vacuum system data using a lightGBM forecast model, the approach achieved a success rate of over 90% (28 out of 30 runs) in finding valid counterfactuals, enabling actionable insights for preventing vacuum breaks.

## Method Summary
The method involves several stages: First, a primary forecasting model (lightGBM) is trained to predict the target variable. Second, Granger causality tests are applied to the historical data to identify directed, lagged relationships between variables, thereby reducing the search space to causally relevant features. Third, for each input variable, multiple quantile regressors are trained to capture its conditional distribution, using lagged values of itself and its Granger-causal parents. Fourth, a genetic algorithm is employed to search for a sequence of quantile choices (one per variable per future timestep) that minimizes a multi-objective fitness function: (1) the error between the resulting forecast and the desired target, (2) the dissimilarity from the current state, and (3) the negative log-likelihood of the scenario under the quantile models. The GA evolves a population of such quantile sequences over many generations, with each individual evaluated by recursively applying the quantile models to generate a full future trajectory and then querying the forecast model. The best individuals that meet the tolerance are returned as plausible counterfactual explanations.

## Key Results
- The genetic algorithm found satisfying counterfactual scenarios in 28 out of 30 experimental runs (>90% success rate).
- The generated scenarios provide actionable insights for preventing vacuum breaks in the industrial system.
- Example counterfactuals include quantified likelihoods (e.g., one scenario had a likelihood of 42.9%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Granger Causality Filtering
- **Claim:** Granger causality tests filter causally relevant variable relationships, reducing the search space for plausible counterfactuals.
- **Mechanism:** The method performs statistical tests (with p<0.05) to determine whether past values of one variable improve the forecast of another beyond its own history. Only variables that are found to Granger-cause the target or other exogenous variables are retained, constraining the auto-regressive models used to project futures.
- **Core assumption:** Linear Granger causality is a sufficient proxy for the underlying causal structure relevant to the forecast task.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states "We employ Granger causality to enhance the reliability of identified causal relationships, rigorously assessing their statistical significance." Section III details the pairwise tests and their use to focus only on causally related pairs. Corpus neighbors (e.g., "InvarGC", "GCAD") extend Granger to non-linear settings, suggesting the linear test may miss complex dependencies.
- **Break condition:** If key system drivers exhibit non-linear lagged effects not captured by linear tests, the filtered variable set may omit crucial intervention points, leading to implausible or impossible counterfactuals.

### Quantile Regression for Distributional Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Training multiple quantile regressors per variable captures distributional uncertainty, enabling the search over a range of plausible future trajectories.
- **Mechanism:** Independent models are trained to estimate specific conditional quantiles (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 0.9) using pinball loss. This creates a mapping from historical context to a spectrum of possible future values, which the GA can sample from via quantile selection.
- **Core assumption:** The historical quantile estimates provide a valid, stationary representation of plausible future variability for each variable under unchanged conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions "trains quantile regressors to model distributional uncertainty for each variable." Section III gives the pinball loss equation and describes the intervention policy as a vector of quantile choices. Figure 4 visualizes quantile bands.
- **Break condition:** Concept drift or regime shifts can make historical quantile estimates non-stationary, causing generated “plausible” futures to become unrealistic.

### Genetic Algorithm for Multi-Objective Search
- **Claim:** A genetic algorithm optimizes a multi-objective fitness function over quantile sequences to find counterfactual interventions that satisfy outcome constraints while remaining historically plausible.
- **Mechanism:** An individual encodes a sequence of quantile selections (one per variable per forecast step). The GA evolves a population toward individuals whose resulting forecast (via recursive quantile model application) minimizes: (1) error to target outcome, (2) divergence from current state, and (3) negative log-likelihood of the scenario.
- **Core assumption:** The three-objective formulation effectively balances achieving the counterfactual goal with maintaining plausibility and similarity to the present.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract describes employing a GA to search for quantile sequences satisfying constraints while maintaining plausibility. Section III defines the three objectives (o1, o2, o3) and the GA parameters. Section IV reports success in over 90% of runs.
- **Break condition:** The fitness landscape may have many local optima; with insufficient population size/generations or premature convergence, the GA may fail to find any satisfying counterfactual.

## Foundational Learning
- **Granger Causality**
  - *Why needed:* To identify which past variables contain predictive information for others, ensuring the auto-regressive forecasts for counterfactual scenarios only use causally relevant features and avoid spurious relationships.
  - *Quick check:* What is the key limitation of using linear Granger causality tests for a system known to have non-linear dynamics?
- **Quantile Regression**
  - *Why needed:* To model the full conditional distribution of each variable, not just its mean, allowing the counterfactual search to explore less probable but still plausible future paths.
  - *Quick check:* How does the pinball loss differ from mean squared error in its treatment of over- vs. under-predictions?
- **Genetic Algorithms for Multi-Objective Optimization**
  - *Why needed:* The search space (all quantile sequences) is high-dimensional and non-convex. A GA can heuristically explore this space for solutions that balance conflicting objectives (target match, plausibility, likelihood).
  - *Quick check:* Why might tournament selection and a high crossover probability (0.75) be chosen for this problem?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Pipeline → Forecast Model, Causal Filter, Quantile Regressors;  
Causal Filter → Quantile Regressors (defines lagged predictors);  
Quantile Regressors → Genetic Algorithm (provides per-variable projection models);  
Genetic Algorithm → Scenario Evaluator (supplies candidate X');  
Scenario Evaluator → Forecast Model (predicts Y');  
Forecast Model → Genetic Algorithm (fitness evaluation, closing the loop);  
Output ← Genetic Algorithm (final counterfactuals).

**Critical path:**  
1. Prepare multivariate time-series data (33 variables, 3-second intervals).  
2. Perform Granger causality analysis to obtain a directed graph of significant relationships (p<0.05).  
3. Train quantile regressors for each variable, using only Granger-causal lagged predictors.  
4. Configure GA parameters (population size, generations, mutation/crossover rates).  
5. Run GA search: initialize random quantile sequences, evaluate fitness by recursively projecting X' and computing forecast error, dissimilarity, and likelihood; evolve population over generations.  
6. Output counterfactual scenarios (X') that satisfy the target constraint within tolerance.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Linear vs. non-linear Granger:* Linear tests are fast and interpretable but may miss complex dependencies; non-linear tests are more accurate but computationally heavier and less standardized.  
- *Genetic Algorithm vs. gradient-based optimization:* GA is flexible and derivative-free, suitable for black-box models, but can be slow and may converge to local optima; gradient-based methods are efficient if the forecast model is differentiable.  
- *Independent vs. joint quantile models:* Independent models are modular and capture marginal distributions, but ignore cross-variable dependencies; joint models (e.g., copulas) could capture dependencies but are more complex to train and sample from.

**Failure signatures:**  
- GA fails to converge or plateaus at high fitness, yielding no valid counterfactual (check best fitness per generation).  
- Generated X' trajectories appear implausible (e.g., violate physical limits, show abrupt jumps); verify against historical ranges and Granger relationships.  
- Quantile models have poor coverage on held-out data (e.g., only 60% of true values lie within [q0.1, q0.9]); indicates mis-specification.  
- Excessive runtime due to large T (forecast horizon) or V (number of variables); consider stronger Granger filtering to reduce V.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate Granger Analysis:** Compute the causality matrix on a held-out block of data and have domain experts validate whether the significant directed links align with known process physics (e.g., pumps influencing pressure). Use Figures 3 and the process diagram as references.  
2. **Quantile Coverage Check:** For each of the 33 variables, compute the proportion of future held-out points that fall within the predicted [q0.1, q0.9] band. Aim for coverage close to 80% (the nominal interval width). Report any variables with severely mis-calibrated bands.  
3. **GA Parameter Sweep:** On a fixed counterfactual query (e.g., target pressure = X), run the GA with population sizes (50, 200, 500) and generations (50, 100, 200). Record the success rate (fraction of runs where o1 < ε) and average convergence speed (best fitness vs. generation). Identify parameters that balance reliability and efficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Local minima avoidance:** How can the genetic algorithm be enhanced to avoid convergence to local minima and ensure thorough exploration of the solution space? (The paper notes that sometimes the GA converges to local minima and cannot evolve further.)  
- **Domain knowledge integration:** What frameworks can effectively incorporate domain-specific knowledge to improve the accuracy and actionability of generated counterfactual explanations? (The paper suggests investigating integration of domain knowledge.)  
- **Extreme value forecasting:** How can quantile regression models be modified to better forecast extreme values, thereby improving counterfactual plausibility for rare but critical events like vacuum breaks? (The paper discusses difficulties in extrapolation and undervaluing extremes.)

## Limitations
- **Implementation ambiguities:** The paper omits key details: the exact autoregressive model used in the GA recursion, the precise quantile levels and likelihood computation, and which variables are considered "actionable."  
- **Core assumptions:** The approach assumes linear Granger causality suffices, that historical quantile distributions are stationary, and that the three-objective fitness balance is adequate without extensive tuning.  
- **Potential failure modes:** Local minima in GA, implausible counterfactuals due to poor causal filtering or quantile calibration, and high computational cost for large T or V.

## Confidence
- **Granger filtering reduces search space:** Medium (standard method but may miss non-linear causality; no external validation provided).  
- **Quantile regression enables plausible futures:** Medium (sound approach, but calibration and independence assumptions not fully verified).  
- **Genetic algorithm finds satisfying counterfactuals:** Medium-high (empirical success rate >90% on one dataset, but heuristic and may not generalize).
