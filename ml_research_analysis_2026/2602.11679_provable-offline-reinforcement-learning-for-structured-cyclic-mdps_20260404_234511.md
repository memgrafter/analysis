# Provable Offline Reinforcement Learning for Structured Cyclic MDPs

### Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11679  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11679  
- **Reference count**: 11  
- **Primary result**: CycleFQI learns optimal policies in cyclic MDPs with finite-sample guarantees, avoids the curse of dimensionality via stage‑wise decomposition, provides valid asymptotic inference, and empirically outperforms standard FQI on synthetic and Type 1 Diabetes datasets.

### Executive Summary
This paper tackles offline reinforcement learning (RL) in cyclic Markov decision processes (MDPs) where the environment consists of distinct stages with heterogeneous dynamics. It introduces CycleFQI, a modular fitted Q‑iteration algorithm that decomposes the cyclic problem into K stage‑wise sub‑problems linked by coupled Bellman equations. CycleFQI supports partial policy optimization, allowing some stages to follow predefined policies while optimizing others. Theoretical analysis provides finite‑sample suboptimality bounds and shows that, under Besov smoothness, the sample complexity scales separately with each stage’s dimension, avoiding the curse of dimensionality that plagues flattened FQI. The paper also develops a sieve‑based inference method that yields asymptotically valid confidence intervals for the optimal value vector. Empirical evaluations on synthetic data and a Type 1 Diabetes treatment simulation demonstrate that CycleFQI achieves higher cumulative rewards and more accurate value estimates compared to standard FQI.

### Method Summary
The paper proposes CycleFQI, a fitted Q‑iteration method tailored to cyclic MDPs. The problem setting consists of K stages, each defined by its own state space \(S_k \subseteq [0,1]^{d_k}\), finite action set \(A_k\), transition kernel \(P_k\), reward function \(R_k\), horizon \(H_k\), and deterministic terminal mapping \(\phi_k\) that sends the agent to the next stage. A stage‑specific discount \(\gamma_k\) is applied when leaving the stage, and the overall cycle discount is \(\gamma_{\text{cycle}} = \prod_{k=1}^K \gamma_k\). The learner receives an offline dataset \(D = \{D_1,\dots,D_K\}\), where each \(D_k\) contains transitions \((s,a,r,s')\) sampled from some unknown behavior policies. The learning objective is to compute a policy \(\pi = (\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K)\) that maximizes the constrained value under an update set \(U \subseteq \{1,\dots,K\}\): for \(k\in U\) we may choose \(\pi_k\) freely, while for \(k\notin U\) we must follow a fixed baseline \(\pi_k^\circ\). CycleFQI iterates \(M\) times: initialize \(Q_k^{(0)}\) arbitrarily; at iteration \(m\), for each stage \(k\) compute targets

\[
y_i^k = 
\begin{cases}
r_i^k + V_k^{(m-1)}(s_i'^k) & \text{if non‑terminal}, \\
r_i^k + \gamma_k V_{[k+1]}^{(m-1)}(\phi_k(s_i'^k)) & \text{if terminal},
\end{cases}
\]

where \(V_k^{(m-1)}(s) = \max_{a\in A_k} Q_k^{(m-1)}(s,a)\) for \(k\in U\) and under \(\pi_k^\circ\) otherwise. Then fit \(Q_k^{(m)} = \arg\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}_k} \sum_i (f(s_i^k,a_i^k) - y_i^k)^2\) using a suitable function class \(\mathcal{F}_k\) (e.g., random forests). After \(M\) iterations, the policy \(\pi_k^{(M)}\) is greedy on \(Q_k^{(M)}\) for \(k\in U\) and clones \(\pi_k^\circ\) otherwise. Additionally, the paper introduces a sieve‑based inference method to construct asymptotically valid confidence intervals for the optimal value vector.

### Key Results
- **Finite‑sample guarantee**: With probability \(1-\delta\), the suboptimality gap \(\|v^* - v^{(M)}\|_\infty \le 2\sqrt{C H^2/(1-\gamma_{\text{cycle}})^2}\cdot\varepsilon + 2HY/(1-\gamma_{\text{cycle}})\cdot\gamma_{\text{cycle}}^{\lfloor M/H\rfloor}\), where \(\varepsilon\) aggregates approximation and regression errors.
- **Dimensionality‑avoidance rate**: Under Besov regularity, CycleFQI achieves an error rate of \(\tilde{O}\big(\max_k n_k^{-\alpha_k/(2\alpha_k+1)}\big)\), which decouples across stages and circumvents the curse of dimensionality faced by flattened FQI.
- **Empirical performance**: On synthetic and Type 1 Diabetes datasets, CycleFQI yields higher cumulative rewards and more accurate value estimates than standard FQI; the sieve‑based inference attains nominal 95% coverage.

### Why This Works (Mechanism)

#### Mechanism 1: Cyclic Decomposition into Stage‑Wise Sub‑Problems
- **Claim**: The cyclic MDP is mathematically decomposed into K separate stage‑level MDPs, enabling per‑stage learning.
- **Mechanism**: Each stage \(k\) is treated as a finite‑horizon MDP with its own state/action spaces, transition kernel \(P_k\), reward \(R_k\), horizon \(H_k\), and terminal set \(T_k\). The cycle is closed by deterministic inter‑stage transitions \(\phi_k\) and discount factors \(\gamma_k\) applied at stage exits.
- **Core assumption**: Stages are *structurally distinct*—dynamics, rewards, and state‑action spaces differ sufficiently that a monolithic model is statistically inefficient.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  [abstract] “decomposes the cyclic process into stage‑wise sub‑problems.”  
  [section 2.1] Formal definition of stage‑specific MDP \(M_k\) and inter‑stage transitions.  
  [corpus] The average neighbor FMR (0.553) indicates a related literature on MDP decomposition, supporting the novelty of explicit cyclic coupling.
- **Break condition**: If stage‑specific features are insufficiently informative (e.g., all stages share nearly identical dynamics), the decomposition overhead may outweigh benefits.

#### Mechanism 2: Coupled Bellman Equations via Stage‑Specific Q‑Function Vector
- **Claim**: A vector of stage‑specific Q‑functions \(Q = (Q_1,\dots,Q_K)\) is updated synchronously, with each \(Q_k\) depending on \(V_{k+1}\) (or \(V_{[k+1]}\) cyclically), forming a coupled Bellman system.
- **Mechanism**: The Bellman operator \(T^U\) (Eqs. 1–2) defines \((T^U Q)_k\) using \(Q_k\) for within‑stage updates and \(V_{[k+1]}\) for terminal transitions. CycleFQI (Algorithm 1) solves K separate regression problems per iteration but circulates V‑values across stages via the cyclic index mapping.
- **Core assumption**: The cyclic dependence is correctly captured by the mapping \(\phi_k\) and discount factors \(\gamma_k\), and the fixed point \(Q^*_U\) exists uniquely (Proposition 2).
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  [abstract] “vector of stage‑specific Q‑functions…linked by coupled Bellman equations.”  
  [section 2.3] Definition of \(T^U\) and contraction property.  
  [section 3.1] Target computation in CycleFQI (Eq. 3) explicitly uses \(V_{[k+1]}\) from previous iteration.
- **Break condition**: If stage transitions \(\phi_k\) are stochastic or highly noisy, the deterministic coupling may induce bias; the theoretical bound assumes deterministic \(\phi_k\).

#### Mechanism 3: Partial Policy Optimization via Update Set \(U\)
- **Claim**: The framework allows optimizing policies only for a subset \(U \subseteq \{1,\dots,K\}\) while fixing predefined policies \(\pi_k^\circ\) for \(k \notin U\) (e.g., clinical protocols).
- **Mechanism**: The Bellman operator \(T^U\) (Eq. 1) mixes max‑operator for stages \(k \in U\) and expectation under \(\pi_k^\circ\) for others. The output policy \(\pi^{(M)}\) is greedy on \(Q^{(M)}\) for \(k \in U\) and clones \(\pi_k^\circ\) otherwise.
- **Core assumption**: The fixed policies \(\pi_k^\circ\) for \(k \notin U\) are feasible (they respect safety/clinical constraints) and their value functions lie within the approximation class \(\mathcal{F}_k\).
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  [abstract] “enables partial control, allowing some stages to be optimized while others follow predefined policies.”  
  [section 2.3] Formal definition via update set \(U\) in \(T^U\).  
  [section 6.2] Real‑world T1D experiment with update sets like “Morning/Day” only.
- **Break condition**: If \(\pi_k^\circ\) for \(k \notin U\) are far from optimal, the constrained optimum may be severely suboptimal compared to full control.

### Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Besov Regularity**  
  - **Why needed here**: Captures stage‑specific smoothness heterogeneity (e.g., abrupt decision boundaries in some stages, smooth in others) to derive tight non‑parametric rates. Enables Table 2 rates for diverse approximators.  
  - **Quick check question**: *“Can you verify that each \(Q_k^*(\cdot,a)\) belongs to \(B^{s_k}_{p_k,q_k}(S_k)\) with \(s_k > d_k/p_k\) for all actions \(a\)?”*

- **Concept: Distributional Coverage (Assumption 1)**  
  - **Why needed here**: Ensures offline data distribution \(\nu_k\) covers all state‑action pairs visited by any policy (even non‑stationary) within stage \(k\). Without it, errors amplify across cycles (Sec 4.1).  
  - **Quick check question**: *“Does your offline dataset satisfy \(\sup_{\beta_k\in B_k} (d\beta_k/d\nu_k)_\infty \le C\) for each stage? How would you test this in practice?”*

- **Concept: Fitted Q‑Iteration (FQI)**  
  - **Why needed here**: CycleFQI is a direct extension of FQI; understanding the base algorithm (iterative regression of Q‑values) is prerequisite.  
  - **Quick check question**: *“In standard FQI, what are the two main error sources? How does CycleFQI modify the target computation to account for stage transitions?”*

### Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data layer (stage‑wise datasets \(D_k\)) -> Model layer (function classes \(\mathcal{F}_k\)) -> Algorithm layer (CycleFQI) -> Theory layer (Bellman operator, Besov smoothness, approximation exponent, cycle discount).

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Initialize \(Q^{(0)}\) (e.g., zero or small random).  
  2. For \(m=1\dots M\): for each stage \(k\) in parallel:  
     - Compute targets \(y_i^k\) using \(Q^{(m-1)}\) (Eq. 3), requiring \(V_{[k+1]}^{(m-1)}\) (hence coupling).  
     - Fit \(Q_k^{(m)} \in \mathcal{F}_k\) to \((s_i^k, a_i^k, y_i^k)\).  
  3. Output \(\pi^{(M)}\): greedy on \(Q^{(M)}\) for \(k\in U\), else \(\pi_k^\circ\).

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - **Per‑stage model complexity**: \(D_k\) (capacity of \(\mathcal{F}_k\)) should balance estimation (\(\sqrt{D_k \log D_k / n_k}\)) and approximation (\(D_k^{-\alpha_k}\)) as in Theorem 5. Overly complex \(\mathcal{F}_k\) increases variance; too simple increases bias.  
  - **Joint vs. separate training**: CycleFQI uses K separate regressions per iteration vs. flattened FQI’s single regression on joint state‑action space. The former exploits structure to avoid curse of dimensionality (Sec 4.2.1).  
  - **Partial policy optimization (\(U\))**: Smaller \(U\) focuses learning but may limit global optimum; larger \(U\) increases coupling difficulty.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - **High approximation error**: If \(\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}_k} \|Q_k^* - f\|_\infty\) is large (e.g., \(\mathcal{F}_k\) too simple), the \(\varepsilon\) term in Theorem 4 dominates regardless of \(n_k\).  
  - **Poor coverage (Assumption 1 violation)**: If \(\nu_k\) under‑samples regions visited by the learned policy, the bound blows up because \(C\) in Assumption 1 becomes huge. Empirically, look for large off‑policy disparity between \(D_k\) and policy‑induced distributions.  
  - **Error accumulation across stages**: In early iterations, a mistake in \(Q_k^{(m)}\) propagates to \(Q_{k-1}^{(m+1)}\) via \(V_{[k+1]}\). Monitor \(\|Q^{(m)} - Q^{(m-1)}\|_\infty\); slow decay may indicate instability.

- **First 3 experiments** (following Sec 6):  
  1. **Synthetic cyclic MDP validation**: Compare CycleFQI vs flattened FQI on a 4‑stage MDP with varying \(d_k\). Verify Theorem 6’s dimension separation (use \(d_k=1,2,2,2\); expecting \(d_{\max}=2 \ll d_{\text{total}}=7\)).  
  2. **Ablation on update set \(U\)**: Train with \(U=\{\text{all}\}\), \(U=\{1,2\}\), \(U=\emptyset\) to show partial optimization works (Table 3 “Day/Evening only” should still beat random/FQI).  
  3. **Inference coverage check**: For the 3‑stage linear MDP (Sec 6.1.2), run \(N=200\) trials, compute \(D^2\) statistic, and verify Q‑Q plot aligns with \(\chi^2_K\) (Fig 2) and coverage approaches 95% as \(n\) increases (Table 4).

### Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can CycleFQI be extended to offline settings with partial coverage (distribution shift) without relying on the uniform coverage assumption?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The Discussion states: “Future work may extend this analysis by incorporating pessimistic penalties to address scenarios with partial coverage, leveraging the modular structure of CycleFQI.”
- **Why unresolved**: Assumption 1 (uniform coverage) is strong and often violated in practice; relaxing it is crucial for broader applicability of offline CycleFQI.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop a pessimistic variant of CycleFQI with finite‑sample bounds under weaker concentrability coefficients and demonstrate improved robustness on limited‑coverage benchmarks.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the CycleFQI framework be adapted to online reinforcement learning, where the agent must balance exploration and exploitation across stages?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The Limitations section notes: “While it does not directly target online learning scenarios, the foundational structure and theoretical guarantees laid out here provide a natural pathway for future extensions to such settings.”
- **Why unresolved**: The algorithm and theory are entirely offline and do not address exploration; online cyclic decision‑making introduces new challenges in staged exploration and sample efficiency.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Design an online version of CycleFQI (e.g., with exploration bonuses) and prove regret or PAC bounds; validate in a simulated cyclic environment with sequential data collection.

#### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does CycleFQI retain its theoretical guarantees and computational efficiency when using highly expressive function approximators like deep neural networks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The Limitations remark: “For highly expressive models, additional refinements may further enhance scalability, presenting a promising direction for future work.” Moreover, the current theory only covers Random Forests (Theorem 9) and general capacity‑controlled classes.
- **Why unresolved**: Deep networks introduce non‑convex optimization and generalization issues not covered by the existing analysis; scaling to high dimensions is essential for complex applications.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Prove convergence and generalization bounds for CycleFQI with deep approximators and implement it on high‑dimensional cyclic tasks, measuring both performance and scalability.

#### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can valid statistical inference for the optimal policy value in cyclic MDPs be performed without the margin condition and Besov smoothness assumptions currently required?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The sieve‑based inference (Section 5) depends on Assumption 4 (margin condition) and Assumption 6 (Besov regularity). These are strong assumptions that may not hold in many real problems.
- **Why unresolved**: The margin condition ensures a fast \(\sqrt{n}\) rate; its violation could invalidate asymptotic normality. Besov smoothness may be too restrictive for functions with discontinuities or high‑frequency features. Relaxing them would broaden the method’s use.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop an alternative inference approach (e.g., debiased or double‑machine‑learning) that achieves \(\sqrt{n}\) normality under weaker conditions and demonstrate correct coverage in simulations that violate the current assumptions.

### Limitations
- Strong theoretical assumptions (deterministic stage transitions, uniform coverage, Besov smoothness) may restrict applicability to real‑world cyclic tasks.
- Missing implementation details (RandomForest hyperparameters, stage identity encoding in flattened FQI) and lack of publicly available data (Type 1 Diabetes) hinder exact replication.
- Sieve‑based inference lacks practical guidance for choosing sieve dimensions; invalid choices can degrade coverage.
