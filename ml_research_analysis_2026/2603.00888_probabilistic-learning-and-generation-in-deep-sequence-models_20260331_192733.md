# Probabilistic Learning and Generation in Deep Sequence Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00888
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00888
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposes SGPA, OHSVGP, and pseudo‑video augmentation to improve uncertainty calibration, online learning, and generation in deep sequence models.

## Executive Summary
Deep sequence models (DSMs) such as Transformers and recurrent networks often lack well‑calibrated uncertainty estimates and struggle with online adaptation and video generation. This thesis bridges DSM inductive biases with probabilistic modeling, introducing three contributions: Sparse Gaussian Process Attention (SGPA) recasts attention as a sparse GP posterior, yielding better calibrated predictions and out‑of‑distribution detection without accuracy loss on text, image, and graph tasks. Online HiPPO SVGP (OHSVGP) uses HiPPO’s polynomial projections as interdomain inducing points, enabling efficient Bayesian updates and stronger long‑term memory in online and continual settings. Pseudo‑video augmentation extends image models by training on sequences of corrupted images, injecting self‑supervision that improves generated image quality. Together, these methods demonstrate that integrating probabilistic principles into DSMs enhances robustness and performance, opening avenues for mutual improvement between the two fields.

## Method Summary
The thesis develops three probabilistic methods tailored to deep sequence models. SGPA replaces scaled dot‑product attention with a kernel function mathematically equivalent to the posterior mean of a sparse variational Gaussian process; a decoupled formulation yields efficient uncertainty estimates per attention head. OHSVGP treats HiPPO’s time‑varying orthogonal polynomial projections as inducing variables in an interdomain GP, enabling online ELBO optimization via ODE recurrences without storing past data. For video generation, pseudo‑video augmentation constructs synthetic video trajectories from single images using deterministic corruptions (e.g., blur, noise); training a video autoencoder and prior on these trajectories yields an image generator when only the final frame is kept. Each method is derived from first principles and validated on diverse benchmarks, showing clear benefits in calibration, online adaptation, or generation fidelity.

## Key Results
- SGPA reduces negative log‑likelihood (NLL) and expected calibration error (ECE) while maintaining accuracy on CoLA, IMDB, CIFAR‑10/100, and ZINC, and improves out‑of‑distribution detection.
- OHSVGP achieves lower negative log‑predictive density (NLPD) and root mean‑square error (RMSE) than OSVGP and OVC on online time‑series (Solar, Audio) and continual tasks (Skillcraft, Powerplant, ERA5), with significant runtime savings.
- Pseudo‑video augmentation lowers FID (e.g., 12.58 vs 12.90 on CIFAR‑10) and raises PSNR compared to training image generative models (VQ‑VAE, DDPM) directly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Replacing scaled dot‑product attention with a kernel function interpreted as the posterior mean of a sparse Gaussian process (SGPA) yields better calibrated uncertainty without sacrificing accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The core equivalence is that kernel attention (with symmetric kernel K) computes the same output as the mean function of a sparse variational Gaussian process (SVGP) where queries are input locations, keys are inducing locations, and values are variational mean parameters. This allows placing a GP prior on attention outputs and using the SVGP posterior covariance for uncertainty. The ELBO is rewritten to treat each MHSA head as an independent (sparse) GP layer, enabling Bayesian updating through the Transformer stack.
- **Core assumption:** The kernel function in attention can meaningfully capture similarity in the representation space, and the sparse GP posterior variance derived from decoupled inducing points provides a well‑calibrated estimate of uncertainty at the attention output level.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.2 derives: `F = K_qk [v]_{:,d}` equals the SVGP mean `m_d` when reparameterizing `v = K_kk^{-1} mu`.
  - Section 3.3 introduces decoupled SGPA to make variance computation efficient and independent of input sequence length.
  - Experiments in Section 3.5 show lower NLL/ECE on text (CoLA, IMDB), image (CIFAR10/100), and graph (ZINC) tasks while matching accuracy.
  - Related Bayesian Transformer approaches (e.g., mean‑field VI on weights) underfit; SGPA exploits attention architecture directly.
- **Break condition:** If the kernel hyperparameters cannot be learned effectively on the target domain, or if the inducing point set is too small to capture the necessary representation space, calibration gains diminish and accuracy may drop. The method also assumes independence of output dimensions within an attention head (in decoupled SGPA), which may limit expressiveness for highly correlated output features.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Interpreting HiPPO's time‑varying orthogonal polynomial projections as inducing variables in an interdomain sparse variational GP (OHSVGP) preserves long‑term memory in online learning by compressing the history into a fixed‑dimensional state via ODE recurrence.
- **Mechanism:** HiPPO's basis functions `g^{(t)}_m(x) = (2m+1)^{1/2} P_m(2x/t - 1)` with uniform measure on `[0,t]` are used as interdomain inducing variables `u^{(t)}_m = ∫ f(x) φ^{(t)}_m(x) dx`. The prior cross‑covariance `K^{(t)}_{fu}` and inducing covariance `K^{(t)}_{uu}` are updated via ODEs derived from the HiPPO recurrence (Eq. 2.10‑2.11) or via RFF approximation. The online ELBO (Eq. 4.6) is then minimized using only new data points and the previous posterior over inducing variables, without revisiting old data.
- **Core assumption:** The polynomial basis and uniform measure are suitable for the target system's temporal dynamics, and the finite number of inducing variables (basis functions) can compress the relevant historical information needed for prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.3.1 defines interdomain inducing variables using HiPPO projections.
  - Section 4.3.2 shows how to compute `K^{(t)}_{uu}` via RFF: each Fourier feature evolves via the same A(t), B(t) matrices as HiPPO.
  - Section 4.6 shows OHSVGP outperforms OSVGP and OVC on online time‑series (Solar, Audio) and continual tasks (Skillcraft, Powerplant, ERA5), with dramatically lower runtime (Table 4.1) and less catastrophic forgetting.
  - Online sparse GP methods (OSVGP) suffer from inducing point shift; OHSVGP fixes basis functions adaptively.
- **Break condition:** OHSVGP requires careful sorting of input observations to create a pseudo‑time order when no natural order exists (Section 4.4). Poor sorting (e.g., kernel‑similarity minimization) leads to forgetting. It also assumes that the kernel is stationary (for RFF factorization) or that the non‑stationary kernel can still be approximated by the HiPPO basis structure. If the data distribution changes abruptly across tasks or requires non‑polynomial memory, HiPPO‑LegS may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training a video generative model on pseudo‑videos (sequences of original images corrupted by deterministic augmentations like blurring or Gaussian noise) injects self‑supervision into intermediate latent states, improving the quality of the final generated image compared to training the image model directly.
- **Mechanism:** Pseudo‑videos `z_{1:T}` are generated where `z_T = x` (target image) and `z_{t<T}` are corrupted versions via a Markov transition (first‑order or higher‑order). The video model (e.g., C‑ViViT for VQ‑VAE, video diffusion for DDPM) is trained to model `p(z_{1:T})` or `p(z_T | z_{<T})`. This imposes direct supervision on intermediate states (e.g., denoising a specific noise level), analogous to diffusion models' forward process. At generation, only the final frame is retained as the synthesized image.
- **Core assumption:** The corruption process defines a tractable and informative joint distribution over latent states such that optimizing the video model's likelihood improves the marginal/posterior for `z_T` beyond what an image‑only model can achieve.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 5.2 compares HV AE (flexible intermediate states) vs. diffusion (fixed noise corruption), noting diffusion's superior performance partly due to explicit self‑supervision.
  - Section 5.3 shows C‑ViViT (video version of VQ‑VAE) trained on 8 or 18‑frame blurred pseudo‑videos yields higher PSNR and lower FID on CIFAR10/CelebA than 1‑frame VQ‑VAE (Table 5.2).
  - Section 5.4 theoretically argues (Theorem 5.4.1) that using more past frames (higher‑order Markov transition) cannot increase reconstruction error if the conditional distributions differ; first‑order Markov is a special case that may be suboptimal.
  - Figure 5.6 and Table 5.3 confirm video diffusion models on high‑order Markov Gaussian noise pseudo‑videos outperform image diffusion on both CIFAR10 and CelebA.
  - Weak direct evidence; related work on sequential generative models and self‑supervised learning provides context but not direct validation of this pseudo‑video scheme.
- **Break condition:** The augmentation must not destroy all semantic content; if corruption is too severe, intermediate states become uninformative for reconstructing `z_T`. Also, the video model architecture must be capable of modeling the temporal dependencies introduced by the pseudo‑video. For some architectures (e.g., VQ‑VAE), blurring works better than Gaussian noise (Table 5.5) because the latter increases pixel variability, making discrete codebook assignment harder.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **SGPA Module:** Inputs (queries Q from previous layer output). Replace `softmax(QK^T/√d_k) V` with `K_{qk} V` where `K_{qk}` uses a base kernel on Q and (amortized) K from the input. Maintain global inducing keys `k_g` and variational parameters `{v_g, S_g}`. Output mean `K_{q k_g} v_g` and covariance `K_{qq} + K_{q k_g} K^{-1}_{k_g k_g} (S_g - K_{k_g k_g}) K^{-1}_{k_g k_g} K_{k_g q}`. Use reparameterization to sample outputs for next layer.
  - **OHSVGP:** Inputs are sequential scalar time indices (or sorted observations). Maintain `A(t), B(t)` matrices from HiPPO (Eq. 2.14‑2.15). For each task, update `K_{fu}^{(t)}` and `K_{uu}^{(t)}` via ODE recurrence (Eq. 4.10) or via RFF basis (Eq. 4.13‑4.17). Optimize `KL(q(u) || p(u))` and expected log‑likelihood using ELBO 4.6.
  - **Pseudo‑video Pipeline:** Start from image dataset. Choose corruption (blur, Gaussian noise). Generate T‑frame sequences `[corrupt(x), ..., x]`. Train video autoencoder (C‑ViViT) to reconstruct. Train video prior (VideoGPT or Phenaki) on latent codes. Generation: sample from prior, decode to video, take last frame.
- **Critical path:** SGPA: build decoupled kernel attention → integrate with Transformer layer stack → derive ELBO → implement reparameterized sampling → train with SGD. OHSVGP: choose HiPPO variant → implement recurrence for `K_{fu}` (and `K_{uu}` via RFF) → implement online ELBO update between tasks. Pseudo‑video: define corruption schedule → implement video model (adapt from image model) → train on pseudo‑videos → evaluate last‑frame quality.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *SGPA:* Decoupled inducing points reduce complexity from O(T²) to O(M_g²) but use fewer inducing points than input length, potentially underfitting if M_g is too small. Deep GP depth (number of attention layers) increases uncertainty propagation but worsens optimization.
  - *OHSVGP:* HiPPO‑LegS uses uniform weight on all history (best for long memory) but requires sorting input points if no natural order. Higher‑order Markov for pseudo‑videos needs more frames and more expressive models to capture joint distribution.
  - *Pseudo‑video:* More frames improve reconstruction but may overcomplicate prior learning. Blur vs. noise: blur preserves coherence (better for VQ‑VAE), noise may be better for diffusion (matches corruption model).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *SGPA:* ELBO collapses, training instability, underfitting (accuracy much below MLE). Check Cholesky decompositions, kernel matrix conditioning, and learning rates for deep kernel parameters.
  - *OHSVGP:* Catastrophic forgetting after a few tasks. Diagnostics: plot inducing state beliefs `q(u)` over time; should expand. Likely cause: poor sorting or insufficient `M`.
  - *Pseudo‑video:* Generated images are blurry or distorted. Diagnostic: compare reconstruction quality of video autoencoder; if last‑frame reconstruction is poor, pseudo‑video may be too corrupted or video model capacity insufficient.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *SGPA sanity check:* Replace one attention layer in a small Transformer (e.g., 2 layers) with SGPA using exponential kernel on a 1D synthetic regression with known uncertainty. Verify that predictive variance increases away from training data and matches SVGP baseline.
  2. *OHSVGP identity check:* On a simple 1D function `f(x)=sin(10x)` with evenly sampled points, apply OHSGPR with `M=10` inducing variables. Task 1: fit on first half; Task 2: fit on second half. Check that posterior after task 2 recovers the first half well (low NLPD on first half points).
  3. *Pseudo‑video reconstruction:* Train C‑ViViT on 4‑frame pseudo‑videos from CIFAR10 using Gaussian blur (as in paper). Compute FID and PSNR of last‑frame reconstructions vs. 1‑frame VQ‑VAE. Expect improvement in both metrics if mechanism holds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the Sparse Gaussian Process Attention (SGPA) framework be extended to autoregressive, decoder‑based Transformers?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The thesis conclusion (Chapter 6) identifies extending SGPA to decoder‑based architectures as a key future direction, noting the incompatibility of static GP priors with the dynamically updating context in models like GPT.
- **Why unresolved:** Decoder‑based models condition generation on an ever‑growing sequence of past tokens, requiring sequential Bayesian updates to replace the static ELBO training objective. The current SGPA framework, designed for context‑free encoders, does not support this online posterior adaptation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal derivation of an online ELBO for SGPA (as hinted in Chapter 6) and empirical validation on a standard autoregressive language modeling task (e.g., WikiText‑103), showing improved uncertainty calibration without sacrificing predictive performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the most effective data augmentation strategies for constructing pseudo‑videos to improve image generative models beyond the first‑order Markov chain?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Chapter 5 demonstrates that pseudo‑videos improve generative model performance but notes the design space is huge and suboptimal for methods like Gaussian blur in some cases (Table 5.5). The authors call this a key future research question.
- **Why unresolved:** While high‑order Markov noise augmentation shows promise (Section 5.4.2), the theoretical analysis only covers autoregressive video models and is limited to reconstruction loss. The optimal augmentation likely varies by model type (VQ‑VAE vs. diffusion), dataset, and task, and lacks a general guiding principle.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic empirical study across multiple model families (autoregressive, diffusion, flow matching), datasets (CIFAR‑10, ImageNet), and augmentation types (noise schedules, blur kernels, geometric transforms), measuring final image quality metrics (FID, IS) to identify general patterns or trade‑offs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the HiPPO framework be integrated with more flexible, deep Gaussian process architectures to enable efficient probabilistic state‑space modeling?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Chapter 4 introduces OHSVGP, which uses a fixed HiPPO recurrence for interdomain inducing points. The text hints (Chapter 6) that this could form "probabilistic SSM layers" but does not develop a full deep model incorporating multiple nonlinear layers and trainable recurrence parameters as in S4 or Mamba.
- **Why unresolved:** OHSVGP applies a linear, fixed‑parameter HiPPO recurrence to inducing points. A deep model would require stacking multiple such layers with nonlinear transformations between them, while also allowing the recurrence matrices to be learned (as in modern SSMs). The variational inference framework for such a deep, nonlinear temporal model is not established.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derival of a multi‑layer OHS‑GP model where each layer is an interdomain GP with HiPPO‑induced bases, using a variational objective that allows learned recurrence parameters. Empirical validation on long‑sequence benchmarks (e.g., the Long Range Arena or sequential image classification) would demonstrate feasibility and performance gains over OHSVGP or standard deep GPs.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What theoretically grounded sorting criteria exist for applying OHSVGP to multidomain continual learning without oracle access to the task ordering?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Chapter 4’s Figure 4.2 shows that OHSVGP performance heavily depends on the sorting criterion used to pseudo‑chronologize data in multidimensional continual tasks (e.g., 2‑moon dataset). The authors note that sorting‑by‑kernel‑similarity helps, but there is no general theory.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments use simple heuristics (e.g., maximizing/minimizing kernel similarity to previous points). Without a principled approach, OHSVGP risks catastrophic forgetting on real‑world continual learning problems where no natural temporal order exists (e.g., class‑incremental vision tasks). The impact of sorting on the induced kernel matrix properties and memory preservation is not analyzed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis linking sorting criteria to the spectral decay or condition number of the evolving kernel matrices K(t) uu. Empirical comparison of several learned sorting policies (e.g., via reinforcement learning to minimize posterior predictive variance) against the kernel‑similarity heuristic on standard continual learning benchmarks (Permuted MNIST, CIFAR‑100 incremental).

## Limitations
- SGPA requires careful kernel hyperparameter tuning and enough inducing points; decoupled head assumptions may limit expressiveness for correlated features and small inducing sets cause underfitting.
- OHSVGP is dependent on a meaningful pseudo‑time ordering; sorting quality directly affects retention and may fail on multidomain tasks without natural order, and it assumes a stationary kernel or that HiPPO polynomials can approximate non‑stationary dynamics.
- Pseudo‑video augmentation’s effectiveness hinges on the corruption preserving enough semantic content; too severe degradation harms reconstruction, and video model capacity must handle longer sequences.

## Confidence
- **SGPA calibration gains:** Medium (theoretical derivation solid, multi‑domain validation, but hyperparameter sensitivity)
- **OHSVGP online learning:** Medium‑High (strong inductive bias, consistent improvements, sorting dependency remains a fragility)
- **Pseudo‑video generation:** Low (evidence limited to few architectures, plausible but not broadly validated)
