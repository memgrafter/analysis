# VERDICT: Verifiable Evolving Reasoning with Directive-Informed Collegial Teams for Legal Judgment Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19306
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19306
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** VERDICT achieves SOTA on CAIL2018 (Law Art Acc 85.35%, Charge Acc 82.40%) and strong temporal generalization on CJO2025 (Law Art Acc 90.56%, Charge Acc 85.84%).

## Executive Summary
VERDICT is a traceable multi-agent framework for Legal Judgment Prediction (LJP) that simulates a virtual collegial panel through a draft-verify-revise workflow. It integrates five specialized roles and a Hybrid Jurisprudential Memory (HJM) that evolves Micro-Directives from verified case trajectories. On the CAIL2018 benchmark, VERDICT attains state-of-the-art accuracy (85.35% law article, 82.40% charge). On the temporally split CJO2025 dataset, it demonstrates robust generalization (90.56% law article, 85.84% charge), significantly outperforming baselines. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both the multi-agent topology and the HJM’s evolving directives, with HJM crucial for mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Method Summary
VERDICT employs five agents—Court Clerk, Judicial Assistant, Case-handling Judge, Adjudication Supervisor, and Presiding Judge—in an iterative draft-verify-revise pipeline. The Case-handling Judge, the core decision-maker, is fine-tuned via two-stage alignment: Protocol-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on filtered accurate samples to standardize output, followed by Logic-Driven Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using a preference set derived from corrected error trajectories. During inference, the Court Clerk extracts event points from case facts; the Judicial Assistant retrieves relevant statutes and precedents using vector search plus semantic reranking. The Case Judge drafts a verdict based on this context. The Supervisor reviews the draft, consults the Hybrid Jurisprudential Memory (HJM), and returns Pass/Reject with feedback. The Case Judge revises accordingly, up to a maximum of three iterations. The HJM stores precedent Standards and dynamically evolves Micro-Directives through Inductive Generation, Contrastive Refinement, and Directive Evolution, updating its graph topology via diffusion. Finally, the Presiding Judge emits the structured verdict. Retrieval is augmented by a statutory library and HJM with configurable weights (α=0.4, β=0.3, γ=0.3). The directive lifecycle includes a consolidation threshold (0.3) to merge similar high-confidence directives.

## Key Results
- **CAIL2018:** Law Article Accuracy 85.35%, Charge Accuracy 82.40%.
- **CJO2025** (future split): Law Article Accuracy 90.56%, Charge Accuracy 85.84%.
- **Ablation:** Removing either the multi-agent workflow or the HJM causes significant performance drops, especially on temporal generalization; HJM is key to reducing catastrophic forgetting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The multi-agent design mirrors a real-world collegial panel, allowing specialized agents to handle distinct legal reasoning tasks. This division yields more focused, interpretable outputs and enables structured critique via the Supervisor. The Hybrid Jurisprudential Memory continuously distills verified reasoning trajectories into high-confidence Micro-Directives, which guide retrieval and feedback. This evolution allows the system to adapt to new legal trends without forgetting past knowledge, addressing the core challenge of temporal drift in LJP. The draft-verify-revise loop ensures that each verdict undergoes iterative refinement, while RAG grounds decisions in up-to-date statutes and precedents, collectively producing accurate and legally sound judgments.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Input -> Court Clerk -> Event Points -> Judicial Assistant -> Retrieved Statutes & Precedents -> Case Judge (draft) -> Adjudication Supervisor (feedback & HJM query) -> HJM (update directives) -> [if Reject, back to Case Judge for revision] -> (after Pass or Tmax) -> Presiding Judge -> Final Verdict

Critical path:
1. Parse case facts into event points.  
2. Retrieve relevant legal context (statutes + precedents).  
3. Generate initial verdict draft.  
4. Supervisor evaluation: pass/fail and feedback.  
5. Revise (max 3 iterations).  
6. Emit final structured verdict.

Design tradeoffs:
- Multi-agent orchestration adds latency but yields interpretability and specialization.
- HJM evolution produces adaptive directives but risks noise; requires confidence-based consolidation.
- Proprietary backbone models (Qwen, DeepSeek) deliver strong performance but limit reproducibility; smaller open models may be substituted at a cost.
- Fixed iteration limit (Tmax=3) balances quality and compute; increasing T may yield marginal gains.

Failure signatures:
- Supervisor feedback is consistently vague or leads to non-convergent loops.
- HJM fragments into many low-confidence directives; check directive confidence histogram.
- Case Judge revisions do not meaningfully change outputs, indicating feedback is ignored.
- Significant performance gap between CAIL2018 and CJO2025 even with HJM, suggesting insufficient temporal coverage in HJM retrieval.

First experiments:
1. Fine-tune the Case-handling Judge alone (SFT + DPO) on CAIL2018 and evaluate test accuracy to reproduce the base model’s performance.  
2. Run the full multi-agent system with HJM disabled; compare results on CAIL2018 and CJO2025 to isolate HJM’s impact.  
3. Execute the complete VERDICT pipeline on both datasets, then vary the HJM consolidation threshold (e.g., set to 0) to demonstrate the effect of directive evolution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions. However, based on its limitations and discussion direction, the following questions arise:
- How can the multi-agent orchestration be optimized to reduce latency without sacrificing interpretability?
- What are the precise hyperparameters (e.g., graph diffusion k-hop, edge threshold τ) for HJM construction, and how do they affect performance across different legal domains?
- Can VERDICT's approach be adapted to legal systems with different procedural structures (e.g., common law vs. civil law)?
- To what extent do proprietary backbone models contribute to performance, and how do open-source alternatives compare?
- How does HJM evolve over longer time spans, and does it require periodic reinitialization?

## Limitations
- Undisclosed details of alignment datasets (`Dtrain`, `Dfault`) and the initial filtering model hinder exact replication.
- Missing quantitative specifications for HJM graph construction (edge threshold τ, k-hop, seed size K) and the meta-LLM used in directive evolution.
- Dependence on large proprietary models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, DeepSeek-V3) may limit accessibility and scalability.

## Confidence
- **CAIL2018 performance:** Medium — The reported SOTA result is based on proprietary models and undisclosed alignment details, making exact replication challenging.
- **CJO2025 temporal generalization:** Medium–Low — While the improvement is significant, the future split introduces domain shift; performance may vary on different temporal splits.
- **Ablation (multi-agent + HJM necessity):** Medium — Ablation results are convincing but rely on the same base model; independent verification is limited.
- **HJM mitigates catastrophic forgetting:** Medium — The paper provides evidence via CJO2025 gains, but direct measurement of forgetting on earlier data is not shown.
