# PET-TURTLE: Deep Unsupervised Support Vector Machines for Imbalanced Data Clusters

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03237  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03237  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PET-TURTLE improves clustering accuracy on imbalanced data by up to 18 percentage points (42.8%→60.6% on CIFAR10-PL) and yields ~15% relative gain over TURTLE across six imbalanced image datasets.

## Executive Summary
PET-TURTLE is an extension of the TURTLE deep clustering algorithm, designed to handle imbalanced data. It introduces two key modifications: a KL divergence term that regularizes the predicted label distribution toward a power-law prior, and the use of sparsemax instead of softmax to produce sparse logits. These changes specifically counteract TURTLE’s inherent bias toward balanced clusters and lead to better hyperplane estimation. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and real-world imbalanced datasets show substantial improvements: on CIFAR10-PL with α=2.0, accuracy increases from 42.8% to 60.6%; across six imbalanced image datasets, PET-TURTLE achieves an average 15% relative improvement over TURTLE; even on balanced datasets the sparse logits provide a modest ~3% boost. Confusion matrices on Food101-PL confirm reduced over-prediction of minority clusters, making PET-TURTLE a robust solution for imbalanced deep clustering.

## Method Summary
PET-TURTLE builds on the TURTLE framework, which employs an unsupervised support vector machine (SVM) within a deep network. The method modifies the original TURTLE objective in two ways: (1) a KL divergence term regularizes the predicted cluster distribution toward a power‑law prior, allowing the model to accommodate natural size imbalances among clusters; (2) the softmax activation is replaced by sparsemax, which produces sparse probability vectors and prevents the allocation of small probabilities to many clusters. The combined loss is minimized end‑to‑end on unlabeled data, using the network’s own predictions as supervision. These simple yet effective changes relax the balanced‑cluster assumption and improve the quality of the learned decision boundaries for imbalanced scenarios.

## Key Results
- On synthetic CIFAR10‑PL (α=2.0), clustering accuracy improved from **42.8%** (TURTLE) to **60.6%** (PET‑TURTLE).  
- Across **six real imbalanced image datasets**, PET‑TURTLE achieved an average **15% relative accuracy improvement** over TURTLE.  
- On **five balanced datasets**, PET‑TURTLE still gained **~3%** due to the sparsemax; confusion matrices on Food101‑PL showed reduced over‑prediction of minority clusters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The KL divergence with a power‑law prior explicitly shapes the output distribution to reflect an expected imbalance, counteracting the balanced bias of the original SVM‑based objective. Sparsemax introduces sparsity in the logits, yielding more confident cluster assignments and preventing the model from spreading probability mass thinly across all clusters. Together these modifications allow the unsupervised SVM to focus on clusters of varying sizes, leading to more accurate hyperplane estimation and improved performance, especially for minority clusters.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
`Input -> Encoder -> Logit Layer -> Sparsemax -> Cluster probabilities`  
`Loss = SVM_loss + λ * KL(probabilities || power-law prior)`

- **Critical path (training)**:  
Forward: Input → Encoder → Logits → Sparsemax → Probabilities.  
Compute unsupervised SVM loss using probabilities as soft assignments.  
Compute KL divergence between probabilities and fixed power‑law prior.  
Sum losses → backpropagate through encoder and logit layer.

Inference: same forward pass, take `argmax` of probabilities as cluster label.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Sparsemax* yields sparse assignments, which can improve separation but may reduce gradient flow and risk dead clusters.  
  - *Power‑law KL regularizer* encourages natural imbalance; λ too high forces artificial sizes, λ too low leaves balanced bias unaddressed.  
  - *Unsupervised SVM* provides margin‑based separation but depends on quality of probability estimates and can be noise‑sensitive.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - All samples collapse into one/few clusters → KL weight too low or SVM loss ineffective.  
  - Extremely sparse outputs with some clusters never assigned → over‑sparsification or poor initialization.  
  - KL term diverges or becomes NaN → numerical issues (e.g., zero probabilities in prior).  
  - Training loss plateaus at high value → component conflict, possibly λ set too high.

- **Three first experiments**:  
  1. **Sparsemax sparsity test**: Feed random logits and verify output has many zeros and sums to 1; compare entropy with softmax.  
  2. **KL regularizer sanity**: With a fixed power‑law prior, compute KL loss for synthetic distributions (uniform, power‑law); ensure it decreases as distribution approaches prior.  
  3. **Synthetic imbalance clustering**: Use a small 2‑D dataset with two clusters of unequal size; run PET‑TURTLE and TURTLE; check that minority cluster is recovered better by PET‑TURTLE.

## Limitations
- Dataset details (CIFAR10‑PL construction, six imbalanced/five balanced datasets) are undisclosed, impeding reproducibility.  
- Only one imbalance level (α=2.0) is evaluated; generalizability across the imbalance spectrum is unknown.  
- No comparisons to other state-of-the-art imbalanced clustering methods and no ablation studies isolating the contributions of the KL prior and sparsemax.
