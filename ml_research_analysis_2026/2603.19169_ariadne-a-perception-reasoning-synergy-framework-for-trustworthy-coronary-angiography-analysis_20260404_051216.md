# ARIADNE: A Perception-Reasoning Synergy Framework for Trustworthy Coronary Angiography Analysis

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID**: 2603.19169
* **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19169
* **Reference count**: 40
* **Primary result**: State-of-the-art segmentation (centerline Dice 0.838, clDice 0.8378) and 41% false positive reduction in coronary angiography analysis.

## Executive Summary
ARIADNE tackles the semantic-topological gap in coronary angiography by integrating perception and reasoning. Its perception module adapts the Sa2VA foundation model through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) guided by Betti number constraints, producing topologically coherent vessel trees, followed by hard sample focused training. The reasoning module uses a reinforcement learning agent with an explicit rejection mechanism to navigate stenosis candidates, mimicking clinical triage by deferring ambiguous cases like bifurcations. On 1,400 clinical angiograms, ARIADNE achieves a centerline Dice of 0.838 and clDice of 0.8378, while stenosis detection attains a true positive rate of 0.867 with only 0.85 false positives per image—a 41% reduction over geometric baselines. External validation on ARCADE and XCAD benchmarks confirms robust generalization across acquisition protocols.

## Method Summary
ARIADNE comprises two tightly coupled modules. The perception module builds on Sa2VA (InternViT-6B frozen, InternLM2, SAM-2 decoder) and undergoes three training stages: (1) supervised alignment with Dice loss; (2) DPO fine-tuning using preference pairs where the winning example is ground truth (β₀=1) and losing examples are high-Dice but topologically fragmented predictions (β₀>1); (3) hard sample focused training on the subset with Dice < 0.75 using a hybrid Dice+BCE loss. The pre-reasoning stage converts the binary segmentation into a continuous centerline via morphological thinning and computes local radii. Candidate stenosis points are selected based on narrow radius and high curvature. The reasoning module then employs a PPO-trained MLP policy that, for each candidate, observes a 16-dimensional state (radius profile, derivatives, Z-score, curvature over a window) and chooses actions: Left, Right, Confirm, or Reject. The reward heavily penalizes false negatives (−50) relative to false positives (−10), while rewarding correct rejections (+10), thereby optimizing reliability over coverage.

## Key Results
* Segmentation: Achieves centerline Dice 0.838 and clDice 0.8378 on 1,400 clinical angiograms; external validation yields clDice ≈ 0.786 on XCAD.
* Stenosis detection: TPR 0.867 with FPPI 0.85, improving PPV to 0.634 and reducing false positives by 41% vs. geometric baselines.
* Generalization: Robust performance on ARCADE and XCAD multi-center datasets, confirming adaptability across acquisition protocols.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: DPO alignment using Betti number constraints transforms Sa2VA from semantic segmentation to topologically coherent vascular reconstruction.  
**Mechanism**: Preference pairs are constructed where the winning example is ground truth (β₀=1) and losing examples are high-Dice but fragmented predictions (β₀>1). The DPO objective optimizes the policy to assign higher probability to connected masks without explicit topological loss engineering.  
**Core assumption**: The reference policy from Stage 1 contains sufficient visual feature representation to serve as a stable baseline; only the preference signal needs adjustment toward topological validity.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
[abstract] “The perception module employs DPO to fine-tune the Sa2VA foundation model using Betti number constraints as preference signals, aligning the policy toward geometrically complete vessel structures rather than pixel-wise overlap metrics.”  
[section 2.2] “The preferred (winning) sample y_w is the ground truth segmentation, which satisfies global geometric constraints by exhibiting β₀(y_w)=1… The non-preferred (losing) sample y_l consists of hard negative examples mined from the Stage 1 policy—specifically, predictions with high pixel-level overlap Dice(y_l,y*)>0.8 but topological violations β₀(y_l)>β₀(y_w).”  
[corpus] Corpus evidence weak; neighboring papers focus on segmentation or reconstruction but not DPO for topological alignment. This appears to be the first application.  
**Break condition**: If the reference policy is degenerate (Stage 1 performance too low), preference pairs will not meaningfully distinguish topological quality; DPO may fail to converge or will overfit to low-quality priors.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Hard Sample Focused Training (HSFT) consolidates topological reasoning under diagnostic ambiguity by concentrating optimization on anatomically challenging cases.  
**Mechanism**: After DPO alignment, the model is fine-tuned exclusively on samples with Dice < 0.75 (20.8% of dataset) using hybrid loss L_HSFT = L_Dice + λL_BCE (λ=0.5), balancing structural consistency with boundary precision.  
**Core assumption**: The hard samples identified by low Dice correlate with topological fragility; targeted optimization on this subset yields disproportionate improvement in overall robustness.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
[section 2.2] “To consolidate topological reasoning under diagnostic ambiguity, we implement HSFT in Stage 3 that concentrates computational resources on scenarios where clinical interpretation is most challenging… This progressive strategy achieves 5× resource efficiency by focusing training on the most diagnostically relevant subset of samples.”  
[section 4.1] Performance gains from Stage 2→Stage 3 show incremental but meaningful improvements (clDice 0.8298→0.8378).  
[corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; neighboring papers use hard sample mining but not combined with DPO for topological tasks.  
**Break condition**: If hard sample identification is inaccurate (e.g., low Dice due to annotation noise rather than true topological violation), HSFT will misallocate resources and potentially degrade performance on clean samples.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: The RL-based reasoning agent with explicit rejection mechanism reduces false positives by mimicking clinical triage, deferring ambiguous candidates (bifurcations, crossings) rather than forcing coverage.  
**Mechanism**: Stenosis detection is formulated as MDP with state encoding local radius profile, derivatives, Z-score, curvature. The Reject action transitions to next candidate without prediction. Reward function heavily penalizes false negatives (r_FN=−50) versus false positives (r_FP=−10) while rewarding correct rejections (r_TN=+10), optimizing for reliability over coverage.  
**Core assumption**: The perception module produces topologically continuous centerlines; otherwise, the agent’s local state cannot reliably distinguish pathological narrowing from fragmentation artifacts.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
[abstract] “The reasoning module employs a reinforcement learning agent with an explicit rejection mechanism to autonomously defer ambiguous candidates like bifurcations, reducing false positives.”  
[section 2.3] “Critically, the Reject action implements an explicit abstention mechanism… This rejection capability mirrors the clinical triage workflow where radiologists defer uncertain cases for secondary review rather than issuing potentially erroneous diagnoses.”  
[section 4.2] FPPI reduced to 0.85 vs. 1.89–2.45 in baselines; PPV improved to 0.634.  
**Break condition**: If segmentation topology is compromised (fragmented trees), candidate generation will produce spatially invalid or disconnected stenosis candidates, making RL navigation meaningless; the entire perception-reasoning dependency fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
  - *Why needed*: To inject topological constraints into a foundation model without requiring explicit differentiable topological loss functions, which are challenging to design for complex vascular connectivity.  
  - *Quick check*: How does DPO's implicit reward modeling differ from standard supervised training with a Dice loss in terms of gradient signal for topological properties?

- **Topological Consistency Metrics (Betti number β₀, clDice)**  
  - *Why needed*: Pixel-level Dice/IOU are insufficient; clinical workflows require continuous vessel trees for centerline extraction and hemodynamic analysis. β₀=1 ensures single connected component; clDice evaluates skeleton continuity.  
  - *Quick check*: Why can a segmentation achieve high Dice yet fail clinical use due to topological fragmentation?

- **Reinforcement Learning with Abstention**  
  - *Why needed*: Stenosis detection must distinguish true lesions from anatomical artifacts; deterministic thresholding fails at bifurcations/crossings. RL with rejection enables context-aware decision-making with explicit cost asymmetry.  
  - *Quick check*: How does the rejection mechanism change the effective operating point of the detector, and what clinical need does it address?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (512×512 X-ray angiography frames) → Sa2VA perception module (InternViT-6B frozen + InternLM2 with LoRA + SAM-2 decoder, three-stage training) → Binary segmentation → Morphological thinning → Centerline extraction → Candidate generation (P_cand = {p ∈ C | r(p) < μ_r − kσ_r ∧ ∇²r(p) > θ_curv}) → RL reasoning agent (MLP policy, 16-dim state) → Confirm/Reject actions → Final detections (with rejects filtered).

**Critical path**:  
Training: Data → Stage1 (Dice loss) → Stage2 (DPO with topological preference pairs) → Stage3 (HSFT on hard samples) → Freeze perception → Extract centerlines → Generate P_cand → Train RL agent (PPO) on candidate navigation.  
Inference: Image → Perception → Mask → Centerline → P_cand → RL agent per candidate → Filter rejects → Output.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- DPO vs. explicit topological loss: DPO avoids engineering complex, non-differentiable topological constraints but requires constructing high-quality preference pairs (β₀-based mining). Tradeoff: simplicity vs. pair quality sensitivity.
- MLP vs. LSTM: Feedforward chosen for efficiency and Markov assumption; LSTM didn't improve (F1 0.831 vs 0.854). Tradeoff: sequence modeling vs. local context sufficiency.
- Rejection threshold: Implicit in reward design; higher r_TN encourages more rejects, improving PPV but risking missed lesions (r_FN penalty must dominate).

**Failure signatures**:
- **Segmentation failure**: High Dice but low clDice; fragmented trees visible in spatiotemporal visualization; β₀ > 1 on validation samples.
- **Reasoning failure**: High FPPI at bifurcations; RL agent repeatedly Confirms on vessel crossings; PPV drops toward geometric baselines (~0.557). Check: Are centerlines continuous? Is P_cand contaminated by duplicates from fragmented trees?
- **Systemic failure**: DPO convergence unstable? Monitor KL divergence β term. If HSFT overfits hard samples, performance on easy samples may degrade (track per-subgroup Dice).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Progressive validation: Train perception module through 3 stages. Evaluate clDice and Dice on validation set after each stage. Expect: Stage2 jump in clDice, Stage3 modest Dice gain.
2. Ablation on DPO baseline: Compare against (a) Sa2VA supervised only, (b) Sa2VA + explicit topological regularizer (if implementable). Measure clDice gap to quantify DPO's unique contribution.
3. RL agent architecture ablation: Train MLP vs. LSTM policies with/without Reject action. Evaluate TPR/PPV/FPPI. Expect: MLP+Reject yields best balance; LSTM no gain; removing Reject spikes FPPI.

## Limitations
* Unspecified LoRA target layers and curvature threshold θ_curv hinder exact reproduction.
* DPO may fail if Stage1 predictions lack topological diversity; HSFT might misallocate resources due to annotation noise.
* Cascading errors from perception to reasoning are not assessed; RL agent robustness to fragmented centerlines unknown.
