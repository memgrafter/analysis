# Latent Semantic Manifolds in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.22301  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22301  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** LLM hidden states lie on a low‑dimensional latent semantic manifold with a Fisher‑derived metric; token generation induces a Voronoi tessellation, and the expressibility gap quantifies semantic distortion, scaling linearly with vocabulary size and revealing a universal hourglass intrinsic dimension pattern.

## Executive Summary

This paper addresses the mismatch between LLMs' continuous internal representations and discrete token outputs by developing a geometric framework where hidden states are points on a latent semantic manifold. The manifold inherits a Riemannian metric from the Fisher information of the token probability distribution, and token generation is interpreted as a Voronoi partition of this manifold. The expressibility gap is defined as the set of states with small margin between the top‑two logits, quantifying semantic distortion due to discretization.

The authors prove two key theorems: a rate‑distortion lower bound showing that the minimum distortion decays as \(N^{-2/k}\) with vocabulary size \(N\) and intrinsic dimension \(k\), and a linear volume scaling law for the expressibility gap via the coarea formula. Empirically, across six decoder‑only transformers ranging from 124M to 1.5B parameters, they observe a universal “hourglass” intrinsic dimension profile (peaking at \(k\approx15{-}22\), occupying only 1–3% of the ambient dimension), smooth manifold curvature, and linear expressibility gap scaling with slopes between 0.87 and 1.12 (\(R^2 > 0.985\)). A persistent “hard core” of predictions with small margin (40–51% with margin < 0.5) is stable across scales, providing a geometric decomposition of perplexity. These findings highlight fundamental limits of discrete language and offer guidance for model design, compression, and decoding.

## Method Summary

The methodology combines rigorous geometric analysis with large‑scale empirical measurements. Theoretically, it postulates that the hidden states of an LLM lie on a smooth submanifold \(M \subset \mathbb{R}^d\) (Hypothesis 4.1). The map \(h \mapsto p(t|h)\) (token probability) induces a Fisher information metric on \(M\) by pulling back the Fisher–Rao metric on the simplex: \(g_F = J_\Phi^\top (W^\top \Sigma_p W) J_\Phi\). Token selection (arg max logit) partitions \(M\) into Voronoi regions \(R_t\); the margin \(m(h) = \ell_1 - \ell_2\) measures distance to the boundary, and the \(\varepsilon\)-expressibility gap \(G_\varepsilon = \{ h : m(h) < \varepsilon \}\) quantifies ambiguous states. Using the coarea formula under assumptions of smooth boundary and bounded margin gradient, Theorem 10.5 proves \(\mu(G_\varepsilon) = (\varepsilon/\lambda) \mathcal{H}_{k-1}(\partial V) + O(\varepsilon^2)\), i.e., linear scaling. Additionally, Theorem 10.8 establishes a rate‑distortion lower bound: the minimum average distortion achievable with \(N\) tokens decays as \(N^{-2/k}\).

Empirically, the authors analyze six decoder‑only transformers (GPT‑2, OPT, Pythia; 124M–1.5B) on WikiText‑103. They estimate the intrinsic dimension \(k\) of each layer’s hidden states using TWO‑NN and MLE, revealing a consistent hourglass profile. Manifold smoothness is validated via local PCA curvature (residual variance) and second fundamental form approximations. The expressibility gap is measured by computing the empirical CDF \(\eta(\varepsilon)\) of margins; a log‑log fit yields the scaling exponent \(\beta\) and \(R^2\). Additional analyses include 2‑D manifold projections (UMAP, spectral embedding) and correlation of margin with prediction entropy. The results confirm the theoretical predictions: low intrinsic dimension, near‑linear gap scaling, and a substantial fraction of predictions near decision boundaries.

## Key Results
- Developed a manifold‑based framework: LLM hidden states live on a smooth latent semantic manifold with a Fisher‑derived metric; token generation induces a Voronoi tessellation; the expressibility gap quantifies semantic distortion.
- Proved two fundamental theorems: a rate‑distortion lower bound (distortion ∝ \(N^{-2/k}\)) and a linear volume scaling law for the expressibility gap via the coarea formula.
- Empirically validated across six transformers (124M–1.5B): universal hourglass intrinsic dimension (peak \(k\approx15{-}22\), 1–3% utilization), smooth curvature, linear expressibility gap scaling (\(\beta \in [0.87,1.12]\), \(R^2 > 0.985\)), and a persistent “hard core” of boundary‑proximal predictions (40–51% with margin < 0.5), which decomposes perplexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:** Claim: The Fisher information metric on the semantic manifold provides a semantically meaningful distance that reflects the distinguishability of token distributions. Mechanism: The language model defines a smooth map from hidden states \(h\) to token probabilities \(p(t|h)\). The Fisher–Rao metric on the probability simplex is pulled back to the manifold, yielding \(g_F = J_\Phi^\top (W^\top \Sigma_p W) J_\Phi\). Distances under \(g_F\) approximate KL divergence for nearby points, so geodesics correspond to semantically smooth variations that the model perceives as distinguishable. Core assumption: Hidden states lie on a smooth submanifold \(M\) of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) and the map \(h \mapsto p(\cdot|h)\) restricted to \(M\) is an immersion. Evidence anchors: abstract states the metric is “inherited from the Fisher information of the token distribution”; Section 5 gives the explicit definition and justification (Definition 5.1, Proposition 5.2). Break condition: Degeneracy of \(\Sigma_p\) (extremely peaked or flat token distributions) makes \(G(h)\) rank‑deficient, causing the metric to collapse; this requires a regularized metric or restricting analysis to well‑behaved regions.

**Mechanism 2:** Claim: Discrete token generation induces a Voronoi partition of the semantic manifold, and the expressibility gap linearly quantifies the volume of semantically ambiguous states near the boundaries. Mechanism: Greedy token selection (arg max logit) assigns each hidden state \(h\) to the nearest token embedding in Euclidean metric (under tied weights/no bias), defining Voronoi regions \(R_t\). The margin \(m(h) = \ell_1 - \ell_2\) measures confidence; \(m(h)=0\) defines the boundary \(\partial V\). The \(\varepsilon\)-expressibility gap \(G_\varepsilon = \{h : m(h)<\varepsilon\}\) collects ambiguous states. Using the coarea formula and assuming a smooth boundary with uniform margin gradient \(\lambda\), Theorem 10.5 proves \(\mu(G_\varepsilon) = (\varepsilon/\lambda)\cdot\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(\partial V) + O(\varepsilon^2)\) – linear volume growth. Core assumption: \(\partial V\) is a finite union of smooth \((k-1)\)-dimensional submanifolds with bounded curvature and \(\|\nabla m\| \ge \lambda > 0\) near the boundary. Evidence anchors: abstract mentions “tokens correspond to Voronoi regions… and a linear volume scaling law for the expressibility gap derived from the coarea formula”; Section 6 defines the partition (Definition 6.1, Proposition 6.2), Section 10 defines the gap (Definition 10.2) and proves linear scaling (Theorem 10.5). Break condition: The margin gradient bound may fail near plateaus where multiple tokens tie over an extended region or the boundary may become non‑smooth due to degenerate embedding arrangements, causing the \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) term to dominate for larger \(\varepsilon\).

**Mechanism 3:** Claim: The intrinsic dimension of the semantic manifold follows a universal hourglass profile (peak \(k\approx15{-}22\), utilizing only 1–3% of ambient dimension), which explains the effectiveness of low‑rank adaptation and links vocabulary size to distortion via a rate‑distortion bound. Mechanism: Across all tested transformers (124M–1.5B), the intrinsic dimension \(k(\ell)\) estimated by TWO‑NN and MLE rises in early layers, peaks in the middle, and contracts at the final layer. The low absolute values imply the manifold occupies a tiny fraction of \(\mathbb{R}^d\), making it highly compressible. Theorem 10.8 (rate‑distortion) shows that for a \(k\)-dimensional manifold, the minimum average semantic distortion with \(N\) tokens scales as \(N^{-2/k}\). Thus the small \(k\) reduces the curse of dimensionality and justifies using low ranks (\(r\approx20{-}30\)) in methods like LoRA, which need to span only the tangent space dimension, not the full width. Core assumption: Hidden states indeed lie near a low‑dimensional smooth manifold, and the estimators (TWO‑NN, MLE) are accurate for the given data and neighborhood scales. Evidence anchors: abstract explicitly reports the hourglass profile and low utilization; Section 13.2 (Figures 2–4, Table 2) presents empirical profiles across six models; the rate‑distortion bound is proved in Theorem 10.8. Break condition: If the intrinsic dimension were close to ambient (\(k \approx d\)), the manifold would be effectively full‑dimensional, falsifying the low‑dimensional structure; if the hourglass pattern were absent or highly model‑specific, the claim of universality would be invalid.

## Foundational Learning
- **Riemannian manifold and metric tensor** – *Why needed*: Provides the mathematical structure to define smoothness, distance, angles, volume, and curvature on the semantic space; essential for geodesics, the coarea formula, and the expression of the expressibility gap. *Quick check*: How does the Fisher metric \(g_F\) differ from the ambient Euclidean metric, and why is the former more appropriate for measuring semantic distance?
- **Fisher information metric** – *Why needed*: The specific metric used, derived from the token distribution’s statistical distinguishability; it encodes how sensitive the distribution is to changes in \(h\) and thus reflects semantic relevance. *Quick check*: In the softmax parameterization, why does the Fisher matrix factor as \(W^\top \Sigma_p W\), and what does each factor represent?
- **Voronoi diagram and margin** – *Why needed*: Token generation is modeled as a Voronoi tessellation of the manifold; the margin measures distance to the decision boundary and defines the expressibility gap. *Quick check*: What geometric property of the Voronoi boundary does the expression \(G_\varepsilon = \{h : m(h) < \varepsilon\}\) capture, and why does Theorem 10.5 imply a linear volume growth for small \(\varepsilon\)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input tokens → Embedding layer → Residual stream (hidden states evolving through attention and feed‑forward layers) → Unembedding matrix \(W\) → Logits → Softmax → Token probabilities. The Fisher metric is derived from the softmax output's covariance.

**Critical path:**  
1. Estimate intrinsic dimension of each layer’s hidden states (e.g., TWO‑NN) to verify the hourglass profile and locate the peak.  
2. Compute curvature (local PCA residual, second fundamental form) to ensure smoothness and regularity.  
3. Measure Voronoi margin \(m(h)\) on evaluation data; fit the log‑log slope of the expressibility gap CDF to confirm linear scaling.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Uniform vs. non‑uniform width: Uniform width over‑provisions middle layers (peak dimension) and under‑provisions early/final layers; non‑uniform width following the hourglass could reduce parameters.  
- Embedding size vs. intrinsic dimension: Very large \(d\) relative to \(k\) yields low utilization; reducing \(d\) to just above expected \(k\) (e.g., 2–3×) may suffice but risks manifold folding.  
- Vocabulary size vs. expressibility gap: Larger \(N\) reduces the gap (per Theorem 10.8) but increases model size; returns diminish as gains scale as \(N^{-2/k}\).  
- Decoder‑only vs. encoder‑decoder: The observed universal hourglass may be specific to decoder‑only LLMs; extending to encoder‑decoder or multimodal architectures could require different geometric considerations.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High intrinsic dimension (\(k > 0.5\,d\)) suggests breakdown of the manifold hypothesis.  
- Inconsistent curvature: spikes or heavy tails indicate non‑smooth structures (attention switches, memorized outliers).  
- Non‑unit scaling slope (\(\beta\) far from 1) in log‑log \(\eta(\varepsilon)\) indicates boundary irregularities or unbounded margin gradient.  
- Layer‑0 anomaly: inflated intrinsic dimension signals raw embeddings violate the manifold; persistence beyond layer 1 suggests inadequate structure development.  
- Expressibility gap deviates from linearity for larger \(\varepsilon\); \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) terms dominate.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Intrinsic dimension sweep: Apply TWO‑NN and MLE to hidden states from all layers of GPT‑2 (124M) on WikiText‑103 validation; plot \(k(\ell)\) to observe the hourglass, compute utilization.  
2. Curvature profiling: For each layer, compute local PCA residual variance and second fundamental form; verify curvatures are small (\(\sim10^{-5}\)) and stable across layers.  
3. Expressibility gap validation: For final‑layer hidden states, compute margin \(m(h)\), build empirical CDF \(\hat{\eta}(\varepsilon)\), perform log‑log fit for small \(\varepsilon\); expect slope \(\beta\in[0.87,1.12]\), \(R^2>0.985\), and report median margin and fraction with \(m<0.5\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Q1:** Do geometric properties (hourglass dimension, linear expressibility gap scaling) hold for models >1.5B and does intrinsic dimension increase with scale? *Basis:* Conclusion explicitly suggests extending to larger models and probing the connection between \(k\) and scaling laws. *Why unresolved:* Only models up to 1.5B were evaluated; scaling could alter manifold geometry. *Resolution:* Measure intrinsic dimension, curvature, and gap scaling across a diverse set of models from 1B to 100B+ on a held‑out corpus; test whether the hourglass persists and whether \(k\) correlates with parameter count or training compute.

- **Q2:** Are the observed universal geometric properties specific to decoder‑only LLMs, or do they generalize to encoder‑decoder and multimodal architectures? *Basis:* Conclusion recommends studying different modalities such as vision‑language models. *Why unresolved:* All six tested models are decoder‑only; encoder‑decoder objectives and multimodal fusion may produce different manifold dynamics. *Resolution:* Extract hidden states from representative encoder‑decoder (e.g., T5, BART) and vision‑language (e.g., BLIP, LLaVA) models; compute the same geometric metrics and compare to the decoder‑only baseline.

- **Q3:** How can geodesic distances on the semantic manifold be computed efficiently at inference time, given the high cost of evaluating the Fisher metric? *Basis:* Conclusion calls for developing efficient algorithms for geodesic computation. *Why unresolved:* The Fisher metric requires summing logits over the entire vocabulary (\(\sim10^5\)); solving geodesic ODEs directly is computationally prohibitive for applications like semantic interpolation or quantization. *Resolution:* Propose and evaluate approximations—e.g., low‑rank surrogates for \(G(h)\), local PCA‑based tangent‑space metrics, or diffusion‑map approximations—and validate accuracy against exact geodesics on toy manifolds or small‑scale LLMs.

- **Q4:** Under what conditions and how frequently does the latent semantic manifold hypothesis fail due to non‑smooth transitions (attention switching, polysemy resolution, memorization)? *Basis:* Section 15.1 identifies potential failure modes but does not quantify them. *Why unresolved:* The main analysis assumes smooth manifold structure; widespread breakdowns would limit the framework’s explanatory power and could bias geometric estimates. *Resolution:* Construct datasets with ambiguous or memorized inputs; detect high curvature spikes, discrete jumps in hidden‑state trajectories between layers, or deviations from manifold fitting; correlate with model behavior to estimate prevalence across architectures and tasks.

## Limitations
- Geometric assumptions (smooth manifold, regular boundary) may not hold globally, especially in low‑entropy predictions.
- Intrinsic dimension estimation hyperparameters are not disclosed, risking reproducibility; dataset dependence and estimator stability remain unknown.
- Empirical validation limited to six ≤1.5B models on a single dataset (WikiText‑103); scalability, multilingual applicability, and generalization to other architectures are untested.
