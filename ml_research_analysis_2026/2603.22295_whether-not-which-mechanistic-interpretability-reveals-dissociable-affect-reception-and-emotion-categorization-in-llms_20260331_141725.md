# Whether, Not Which: Mechanistic Interpretability Reveals Dissociable Affect Reception and Emotion Categorization in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22295
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22295
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** LLMs detect emotional significance from situational context alone (AUROC 1.000), but mapping to specific emotion categories partially depends on keywords (1-7% AUROC drop).

## Executive Summary
This paper uses clinical vignettes without emotion keywords and four mechanistic interpretability methods to test whether LLMs truly understand emotions or merely perform keyword matching. The results reveal two dissociable mechanisms: affect reception (detecting emotional significance) is keyword-independent and saturates in early layers with near-perfect accuracy, while emotion categorization (mapping to specific labels) partially depends on keywords, with larger drops in smaller models. Causal activation patching confirms that both stimulus types share representational space, transferring affective salience rather than category identity. This falsifies the keyword-spotting hypothesis and establishes clinical stimuli as a rigorous validity standard for emotion recognition in LLMs.

## Method Summary
The study applied four mechanistic interpretability methods (linear probes, activation patching, knockout, and geometric analysis) to six LLMs (1B-9B parameters) using clinical vignettes designed to be devoid of emotion keywords. Two stimulus sets were used: Set A (emotion keywords present) and Set B (keyword-free clinical vignettes). The analysis examined binary affect reception (emotional vs. neutral), 8-class emotion categorization, and geometric organization of representations across stimulus types. Activation patching tested whether representational spaces were shared, while knockout experiments identified critical layers for each task.

## Key Results
- Affect reception achieves AUROC 1.000 on keyword-free clinical vignettes, saturating in early layers (3-6) across all models
- Emotion categorization drops 1-7% in AUROC when keywords are removed, with larger drops in smaller models
- Cross-set activation patching transfers affective salience but not specific emotion identities
- Instruction tuning reorganizes existing representations to cluster by emotion rather than surface features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Binary Affect Reception
- **Claim:** Models detect emotional significance from situational context alone without emotion keywords
- **Mechanism:** Early-layer residual stream activations encode a binary affective salience signal distinguishing emotionally significant narratives from neutral ones with near-perfect accuracy (AUROC 1.000). This computation completes in the first 9-38% of network depth across all tested models.
- **Core assumption:** Emotional meaning can be extracted from non-lexical situational and behavioral cues, paralleling human affective appraisal that precedes categorical identification.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states AUROC 1.000 on Set B clinical vignettes with saturation in layers 3-6; section 5.1 shows binary probe AUROC of 1.000 on keyword-free stimuli.
- **Break condition:** If binary probes on rigorously keyword-free stimuli fail to achieve high AUROC or do not saturate early, the mechanism is falsified.

### Mechanism 2: Categorical Emotion Categorization
- **Claim:** Mapping detected affect to specific emotion categories partially depends on lexical shortcuts but also uses abstract representations that improve with scale.
- **Mechanism:** Mid-to-late layer residual stream activations encode a multi-class emotion signal. Keywords provide a categorical shortcut; their removal causes a 1-7% AUROC drop, with smaller models showing larger drops. Cross-set activation patching transfers affective salience but not category identity.
- **Core assumption:** Categorical emotion discrimination requires more complex, abstract computation than binary salience detection, making it more fragile when lexical cues are removed.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes 1-7% AUROC drops without keywords; section 5.2 shows 8-class AUROC drops of 4.6-6.7% (1B) vs. 1.1-1.9% (8B/9B); section 6.1 shows cross-set patching transfers only affective salience.
- **Break condition:** If 8-class AUROC on keyword-free stimuli matches keyword-rich performance across all scales, or if cross-set patching transfers specific emotion identities, the partial keyword dependence and dissociation are invalidated.

### Mechanism 3: Geometric Reorganization by Instruction Tuning
- **Claim:** Instruction tuning reorganizes existing emotion representations geometrically—clustering same-emotion across stimulus types—without creating the underlying emotional encoding.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-training encodes emotion information detectable by probes. Post-training with human feedback reshapes the representational geometry so that representations of the same emotion from different stimulus forms become more similar, increasing emotion-based clustering over stimulus-set clustering.
- **Core assumption:** Alignment does not install new capabilities but restructures pre-trained representations for functional accessibility.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states geometric organization is shaped by instruction tuning; section 8.2 shows Llama-8B Instruct has emotion silhouette (0.091) > set silhouette (0.066), while Base shows reverse (0.044 vs. 0.117).
- **Break condition:** If base models show the same geometric organization as instruct variants, or if instruct variants fail to show increased emotion-clustering, reorganization is not alignment-driven.

## Foundational Learning

- **Mechanistic Interpretability (probing, activation patching, knockout)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's conclusions rely on causal and correlational methods that require understanding how internal activations relate to model outputs.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between a linear probe (correlational) and activation patching (causal)? When would each be insufficient alone?

- **Clinical Psychology Vignette Design**
  - *Why needed here:* The core innovation is using keyword-free, situationally grounded stimuli to test emotional processing validity. This design rules out lexical confounds that plague standard NLP emotion datasets.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a cross-topic design (same emotion across different scenarios) necessary? What would a failure of this control imply?

- **Dissociation Paradigm**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's central thesis is a functional dissociation (affect reception vs. categorization) revealed by a selective impairment (keyword removal).
  - *Quick check question:* If both binary and 8-class performance drop equally when keywords are removed, does that support or refute a dissociation? What pattern would indicate a single mechanism?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Residual stream (h) -> early layers (affect reception) -> mid layers (categorization) -> extraction position
  MHSA (a) and FFN (m) components show different peak timings

- **Critical path:**
  1. Input → early layers (0-30% depth): Affect reception signal builds rapidly, saturating by layer 3-6
  2. Mid layers (30-70%): Categorical emotion information consolidates; peak probe layers for 8-class classification
  3. Late layers (70-100%): Refinement; FFN component peaks here
  4. Extraction position: Model reads accumulated representation to produce label

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Scale: Small models show concentrated, fragile processing; large models show distributed, robust processing
  - Keywords: Provide shortcut for categorization, reducing computational burden
  - Base vs. Instruct: Base models have comparable encoding; instruct models show better geometric organization

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Binary probe fails on Set B (AUROC ≈ 0.5): Affect reception mechanism absent
  - 8-class probe shows no Set A vs. Set B drop: No keyword dependence in categorization
  - Cross-set patching fails (success ≈ chance): Representational spaces are incompatible
  - Knockout shows no critical layers for Set B: Either redundant processing or weak emotion signal

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate binary detection on clinical vignettes: Train linear probe on emotional vs. neutral Set B stimuli, expect AUROC ≥0.99 and early-layer saturation (L3-L6)
  2. Run keyword removal contrast for 8-class: Compare Set A and Set B 8-class probe AUROC, expect 1-7% drop, larger in small models
  3. Perform same-emotion cross-set activation patching: Patch from keyword-rich to keyword-free text, expect ~75-87.5% success transferring affective salience

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Do scale-dependent architecture shifts (distributed processing, improved cross-set transfer, shrinking keyword dependence) persist in 70B+ and proprietary LLMs?
- Basis: The paper states in Limitations that whether these trends continue, plateau, or change character in 70B+ models remains an open question.
- Why unresolved: Only 1B-9B models were tested; larger models may show different patterns.
- What evidence: Replicate the full analysis pipeline on frontier-scale models using the same clinical vignettes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is the negative-emotion advantage (stronger cross-topic clustering for grief vs. ecstasy) robust across taxonomies and larger stimuli sets, and what explains it?
- Basis: Section 7.4 notes whether this parallel reflects shared information-theoretic properties or is coincidental remains an open question.
- Why unresolved: The effect appeared in only two models with low power (4 vignettes per domain).
- What evidence: Increase vignette count and test alternative taxonomies via cross-topic permutation tests.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can clinical vignette methodology become a rigorous standard for emotion circuit validation, and how should the stimulus set be independently validated and expanded?
- Basis: Section 9.5 calls for clinical stimulus methodology to become a standard validity check, with a planned follow-up with multiple clinicians to address the single-designer limitation.
- Why unresolved: Current vignettes designed by one psychologist; community adoption and multi-rater validation are pending.
- What evidence: Independent replication studies using released stimuli, multi-clinician validation, and expansion to more emotions and topics.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Does instruction tuning create a distinct representational accessibility mechanism, or does it merely reorganize pre-existing affect reception/categorization pathways?
- Basis: Section 9.1 says the geometric shift warrants dedicated investigation; section 8.2 notes alignment restructures what pre-training already built.
- Why unresolved: Geometric changes are shown, but causal separability from the two main mechanisms is unclear.
- What evidence: Targeted causal perturbations that dissociate geometric reorganization from binary vs. categorical performance across base/instruct models.

## Limitations

- The clinical vignette methodology has not been validated against human emotion recognition benchmarks
- Potential probe overfitting and relatively small effect sizes (1-7% AUROC drops) for categorization mechanism
- Causal patching experiments cannot definitively rule out different internal processes that coincidentally achieve similar outputs

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Binary affect reception mechanism (AUROC 1.000, early saturation, keyword independence)
- **Medium Confidence**: Partial keyword dependence in categorization (1-7% drops, scale effects, causal patching results)
- **Medium Confidence**: Geometric reorganization by instruction tuning (Silhouette scores, cosine gaps, cross-set clustering)

## Next Checks

1. **Human benchmark comparison**: Have human raters classify the same clinical vignettes by emotion and compare performance to LLM probes to validate whether detected "emotion" corresponds to genuine affective meaning.

2. **Adversarial keyword manipulation**: Systematically insert emotion keywords into keyword-free vignettes and measure exact magnitude of categorization performance changes to quantify lexical versus contextual contributions.

3. **Cross-linguistic replication**: Test the same dissociation paradigm on models trained on non-English corpora to strengthen claims of fundamental mechanistic differences if affect reception remains keyword-independent while categorization shows varying keyword dependence.
