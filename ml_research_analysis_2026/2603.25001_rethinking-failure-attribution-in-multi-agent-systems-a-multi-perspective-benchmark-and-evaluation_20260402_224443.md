# Rethinking Failure Attribution in Multi-Agent Systems: A Multi-Perspective Benchmark and Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25001
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25001
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Multi-perspective failure attribution benchmarks with high-temperature LLM sampling yield human-aligned, diverse attributions, challenging prior claims of LLM limitation due to flawed single-cause evaluation assumptions.

## Executive Summary
Existing benchmarks for failure attribution in multi-agent systems (MAS) unrealistically assume a single, deterministic root cause per failure. This work introduces MP-Bench, the first benchmark capturing multiple valid failure steps and their reasoning through expert annotations. Using nDCG for ranking consensus and LLM-Judge for reasoning quality, evaluations show that large language models (LLMs), when sampled multiple times at high temperature, can identify diverse, human-aligned failure attributions. For instance, Claude-Sonnet-4.5 achieves strong scores (nDCG@5 exp: 0.5916 hand-crafted, 0.8142 multi-LLM; reasoning >7.0). Results surpass random baselines and contrast with prior near-random outcomes on deterministic benchmarks, suggesting earlier LLM limitations were artifacts of evaluation design. Ensembling diverse LLM families further improves performance.

## Method Summary
Reproduction notes are not provided. The method centers on MP-Bench, a new benchmark built from simulated MAS tasks. It defines failure attribution as identifying multiple critical steps leading to failure, not a single root cause. Expert annotators label these steps and provide reasoning. Evaluation uses two metrics: nDCG (to measure alignment with human consensus rankings of failure steps) and LLM-Judge (an LLM-based assessor of reasoning quality). LLMs are prompted to generate failure attributions, which are sampled multiple times at high temperature to encourage diversity. Performance is aggregated across samples and compared against random and prior deterministic benchmark results.

## Key Results
- LLMs at high temperature produce diverse, human-aligned failure attributions; Claude-Sonnet-4.5 achieves nDCG@5 (exp) of 0.5916 on hand-crafted tasks and 0.8142 in multi-LLM settings, with reasoning scores >7.0.
- Results significantly exceed random baselines and contrast sharply with prior near-random LLM performance on single-cause benchmarks, indicating evaluation assumptions—not model capability—caused earlier failures.
- Combining diverse LLM families in an ensemble improves attribution quality beyond individual models.

## Limitations
- Scope limited to simulated tasks; real-world MAS (e.g., robotics) not evaluated.
- High-temperature sampling essential; deterministic runs may fail to replicate results.
- Metrics (nDCG, LLM-Judge) rely on LLMs, risking circular evaluation.
