# InfMem: Learning System-2 Memory Control for Long-Context Agent

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02704  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02704  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** InfMem improves average absolute accuracy by +10.17 to +11.84 points over MemAgent on ultra-long QA (32k–1M tokens) while reducing inference time by 3.9× via adaptive early stopping.

## Executive Summary
InfMem introduces a control-centric agent for multi-hop reasoning over ultra-long documents where critical evidence is sparse. It executes a PreThink–Retrieve–Write loop that actively monitors memory sufficiency, performs targeted in-document retrieval, and applies evidence-aware joint compression to update a bounded memory. A practical SFT-to-RL training pipeline aligns these discrete control decisions with end-task correctness. Evaluations on ultra-long QA benchmarks show significant accuracy improvements and substantial inference-time reductions compared to prior memory-augmented agents.

## Method Summary
The core is a streaming loop with a fixed memory budget *M*. At each coarse chunk, **PRETHINK** decides to STOP or RETRIEVE; if continuing, it emits a retrieval query and *k*. **RETRIEVE** globally fetches fine-grained units from a pre-built index. **WRITE** then jointly compresses the new chunk and retrieved evidence into a new memory that respects the budget, prioritizing composition-critical facts and bridging links. Training uses a two-stage recipe: first, supervised fine-tuning distills a teacher model’s trajectories to learn the protocol; second, verifier-based reinforcement learning (GRPO) aligns long-horizon decisions with a reward combining exact-match, early-stop shaping, and binary verifiers for valid function calls and memory updates.

## Key Results
- On ultra-long QA (32k–1M tokens), InfMem improves average absolute accuracy by **+10.17 (Qwen3-1.7B)**, **+11.84 (Qwen3-4B)**, and **+8.23 (Qwen2.5-7B)** points over MemAgent.
- Reduces **inference time by 3.9× on average** (up to 5.1×) via learned early stopping without sacrificing accuracy.
- Gains transfer to natural long-document QA benchmarks (LongBench), demonstrating generalizable evidence management.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Active Control Loop
- **Claim:** The PreThink-Retrieve-Write loop actively monitors memory sufficiency, performs targeted retrieval, and applies evidence-aware joint compression, enabling selective preservation of low-salience bridging evidence under fixed memory.
- **Mechanism:** At each streaming step, PRETHINK conditions on current memory and question to decide STOP or RETRIEVE; if continuing, it emits a retrieval query and a top‑k count. RETRIEVE globally fetches fine‑grained units from the entire document. WRITE integrates retrieved evidence with the current chunk via bounded joint compression, prioritizing links required for multi‑hop synthesis. This monitor‑seek‑update‑stop cycle repeats until PRETHINK signals sufficiency.
- **Core assumption:** Passive, monotonic compression loses critical evidence; active, state‑dependent control curates a working memory for ultra‑long reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 describe the loop and joint compression; empirical gains on ultra‑long benchmarks validate the approach over passive baselines.

### Mechanism 2: Evidence‑Aware Joint Compression
- **Claim:** WRITE jointly compresses the current chunk and retrieved evidence, selectively retaining only answer‑relevant facts and bridging links, avoiding memory drift and preserving critical information within a strict overwrite budget.
- **Mechanism:** WRITE parses the question, previous memory, current chunk, and retrieved context to extract entities, relations, and verifiable facts necessary for downstream composition, discarding speculative content, and writes a new memory that respects the fixed token budget *M*. Retrieval is used “for writing’’ to shape the update, ensuring retrieved evidence is integrated compactly while preserving previously stored essential facts.
- **Core assumption:** Under bounded memory, the model can identify which pieces are composition‑critical for the specific question and compress away everything else.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 explicitly define evidence‑aware composition; prompt templates instruct memory updates that “help to answer the problem.’’

### Mechanism 3: Training Recipe (SFT → RL)
- **Claim:** A two‑stage training pipeline—first supervised fine‑tuning on protocol‑valid trajectories, then verifier‑based reinforcement learning—stably aligns the agent’s discrete control decisions with end‑task correctness and early‑stopping efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 (SFT) distills a teacher model (e.g., Qwen3‑32B) executing the full loop; the student learns valid structured outputs and bounded memory maintenance. Stage 2 (RL) uses GRPO with rollout‑level rewards: outcome exact‑match, early‑stop shaping, and binary verifiers (function‑call validity, memory‑budget compliance). Advantages are computed across a group of rollouts for long‑horizon credit assignment.
- **Core assumption:** Outcome‑based rewards with protocol verifiers can guide exploration in a high‑dimensional discrete decision space where intermediate actions are not directly supervised.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4 describes the verifiable pipeline; Section 4.2 defines reward components; Section 5.2 reports GRPO hyperparameters.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: System‑2 Cognitive Control**  
  *Why needed:* The architecture is framed as a System‑2 controller engaging in explicit, state‑dependent memory management, unlike passive streaming. This clarifies why PRETHINK monitors sufficiency and plans retrieval rather than automatically writing every chunk.  
  *Quick check:* What characterizes a System‑2 decision in the InfMem loop, and how might it differ from a System‑1 behavior?

- **Concept: Bounded Memory Streaming Agents**  
  *Why needed:* The problem assumes a fixed‑size overwrite memory and single‑pass processing, yielding O(1) memory and O(n) computation. This constraint necessitates compression and early stopping.  
  *Quick check:* How does the memory budget *M* impact the trade‑off between information retention and computational cost?

- **Concept: Verifiable Reinforcement Learning for Agentic Workflows**  
  *Why needed:* Control decisions (stop/retrieve, query, memory update) are discrete and affect long‑horizon success. SFT alone cannot learn optimal timing; RL with dense shaping and verifiers aligns the entire rollout with the final answer reward.  
  *Quick check:* Why are binary verifiers (function‑call, memory) introduced alongside the outcome reward, and what would happen if they were omitted?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  PRETHINK (state‑dependent controller) -> RETRIEVE (global tool) -> WRITE (memory updater) -> ANSWER (final generator).  
  A pre‑built BM25 index over fine‑grained chunks supports RETRIEVE. An underlying LLM backbone generates all structured outputs. The training pipeline uses a teacher model (SFT) and GRPO (RL).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Initialize empty memory *m₀*.  
  2. For each coarse streaming chunk *cₜ* (t = 1… T):  
     a. PRETHINK(q, *mₜ₋₁*) → (*aₜ, uₜ, kₜ*).  
     b. If *aₜ* == STOP → break.  
     c. Else: *rₜ* ← RETRIEVE(*uₜ, kₜ*).  
     d. *mₜ* ← WRITE(q, *mₜ₋₁, cₜ, rₜ*; *M*).  
  3. Upon termination: ŷ ← ANSWER(q, *m_final*).  
  Retrieval is global; reading proceeds sequentially; memory updates overwrite under budget.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Retrieval chunk size vs. top‑k: Smaller chunks increase granularity but fragment evidence; larger chunks preserve context but reduce diversity under fixed *M*. Paper found 500‑token chunks optimal.  
  - Early‑stopping aggressiveness: Conservative stops improve accuracy but increase latency; aggressive stops reduce latency but risk premature exits. Default 3‑stop balances Pareto frontier.  
  - SFT vs. RL: SFT ensures protocol stability; RL optimizes long‑horizon reward but requires careful shaping (early stop) and verifiers to avoid invalid rollouts.  
  - Memory budget *M*: Tighter budgets force aggressive compression, risking loss of weak links; larger budgets preserve more but increase overhead.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - PRETHINK failure: Repeated early STOPs → answers wrong on multi‑hop queries, indicating mis‑calibrated sufficiency monitoring.  
  - RETRIEVE failure: Irrelevant retrieved chunks → memory pollution → answer degradation, especially with ambiguous entities.  
  - WRITE failure: Truncated/budget‑exceeding updates → protocol violation (training) or rationale loss (inference); over‑compression drops bridging facts → sudden performance drops on cross‑chunk links.  
  - RL mis‑alignment: Over‑weighted early‑stop reward → immediate stops (low latency, collapsed accuracy); under‑weighted → never stops (high latency, marginal gains).  
  - Evidence accumulation failure: WRITE fails to extract key sentences from retrieved context → answer incorrect despite gold evidence presence.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Cross‑backbone ultra‑long QA evaluation (Table 1): Tests on HopQA, SQuAD, MuSiQue, 2Wiki with lengths 32k–1M; InfMem outperforms MemAgent by >10 points absolute and reduces inference time 3.9×.  
  2. LongBench QA transfer (Table 2): Evaluates on natural long‑document QA; gains transfer beyond synthetic distractors, showing generalizable evidence management.  
  3. Inference efficiency (early stopping) (Figure 3): Wall‑clock time vs. accuracy trade‑off; InfMem achieves higher accuracy and lower latency than MemAgent, confirming learned stopping policy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How well does the InfMem framework transfer to long-context tasks beyond question answering, such as summarization or code generation, where the notion of "evidence sufficiency" and reward signals differ? *(Basis: Evaluation restricted to QA benchmarks; training pipeline uses verifiable exact‑match rewards and protocol verifiers designed for QA correctness.)*
- **Open Question 2:** Under what conditions does InfMem's joint compression fail to preserve multi-hop bridging evidence, leading to loss of critical links across distant segments? *(Basis: No detailed analysis of memory content retention over time; rewards supervise only final answer, not intermediate memory fidelity.)*
- **Open Question 3:** How does InfMem's performance vary with retrieval unit granularity (e.g., sentences vs. paragraphs) and retrieval mechanism (BM25 vs. dense search) in ultra‑long contexts? *(Basis: Paper uses BM25 over paragraph‑level units and fixes retrieval chunk size to 500 tokens based on one ablation; does not explore other sizes or methods.)*
- **Open Question 4:** Does the SFT→RL recipe remain effective when scaling InfMem to much larger models (e.g., 70B+) or different architectures (e.g., MoE, encoder‑decoder), and how should reward weights be adjusted? *(Basis: Experiments limited to Qwen3-1.7B/4B and Qwen2.5-7B; hyperparameters fixed across scales; teacher is 32B, scaling may weaken teacher‑student gap.)*
- **Open Question 5:** To what extent does the early stopping mechanism cause premature termination on complex multi‑hop questions, and how does this trade‑off vary with query difficulty? *(Basis: Reward shaping term R_early = γ^{d-1} incentivizes stopping at first apparent sufficiency; a "3‑stop" variant indicates awareness of premature stopping risk; no analysis of stopping behavior vs. question complexity.)*

## Limitations
- **Training details unspecified:** Reward component weights and exact verifier logic are omitted, blocking faithful RL replication.
- **Scalability claims untested on truly massive documents:** No discussion of hardware constraints or retrieval/indexing efficiency beyond 1M tokens.
- **Compression fidelity not probed:** No analysis of *what* gets discarded by evidence‑aware joint compression, especially subtle bridging facts under tight budgets.

## Confidence
- **Active control loop mechanism:** High – clear specification and ablations against passive baselines.
- **Evidence‑aware compression:** Medium – relies on LLM’s ability to judge “composition‑critical” facts without direct validation of retained vs. dropped content.
- **SFT→RL training recipe:** Medium – approach sound but missing hyperparameters (reward weights, shaping) limits reproducibility confidence.
- **Efficiency gains from early stopping:** High – wall‑clock measurements provided and consistent across backbones.
