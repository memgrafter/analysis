# INTRYGUE: Induction-Aware Entropy Gating for Reliable RAG Uncertainty Estimation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.21607
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21607
*   **Reference Count:** 37
*   **Primary Result:** A training-free method that corrects entropy-based hallucination detectors in RAG by gating with SinkRate from induction heads, achieving AUROC up to 0.82 across six LLMs and four benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Standard entropy-based uncertainty estimation fails for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) because induction heads—which copy correct context to produce grounded outputs—collidentally activate separate "entropy neurons" that inflate predictive entropy. This creates false uncertainty signals on accurate responses. INTRYGUE addresses this by computing an aggregated SinkRate score from the model's top induction heads, which serves as a proxy for successful context utilization. The final uncertainty score is the product of this SinkRate and the standard aggregated entropy, amplifying uncertainty when context use is weak and suppressing it when strong. This simple, training-free gate reliably improves hallucination detection without added computational cost.

## Method Summary
For a given model and query, INTRYGUE first identifies the top-*k* induction heads by pre-computing an `InductionScore` on a synthetic repeated sequence. During response generation, it records attention matrices for these heads. After generation, for each head, it computes `SinkRate`—the proportion of attention mass on early-sequence tokens—over the response spans. These per-head SinkRates are aggregated (using `min` or `mean`) into a scalar `s`. Separately, it aggregates token-wise predictive entropy from the output logits (using `min`, `max`, or `mean`) into a scalar `e`. The final INTRYGUE uncertainty score is `s * e`. A threshold on this score, calibrated on a small validation set of labeled examples, classifies outputs as hallucinated or grounded.

## Key Results
*   INTRYGUE achieves AUROC scores of 0.82 on CoQA and 0.77 on MS MARCO, consistently matching or exceeding a wide range of entropy-, attention-, and sampling-based baselines.
*   The method demonstrates strong generalization across four RAG benchmarks and six LLMs ranging from 4B to 13B parameters.
*   It maintains computational efficiency comparable to a simple entropy calculation, as the added cost is a one-time head identification and low-overhead attention aggregation during generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:** Standard entropy-based uncertainty is unreliable in RAG because induction heads that promote grounded outputs also collaterally trigger a separate set of neurons ("entropy neurons"). These neurons increase the variance of pre-softmax logits via LayerNorm scaling, thereby inflating predictive entropy regardless of output correctness. Mean-ablation of top induction heads decreases the activation of entropy neurons, confirming a causal link from induction activity to spurious entropy inflation. The failure mode is specific to decoder-only Transformers where this mechanistic coupling exists.

**Mechanism 2:** The aggregated SinkRate from top induction heads is a reliable, mechanistic proxy for the degree of successful context utilization ("induction"). SinkRate measures the proportion of attention mass that "sinks" or concentrates on specific early-sequence tokens (often utility tokens). Low average SinkRate indicates induction heads are actively attending to relevant context positions to copy information (successful induction). High SinkRate indicates deactivation or misdirection, suggesting a failure to utilize context. This metric shows consistent discrimination between grounded and hallucinated outputs (AUROC 0.63–0.74) and causal ablations of induction heads increase loss, establishing their role.

**Mechanism 3:** Gating (multiplying) the aggregated predictive entropy by the aggregated SinkRate disentangles the two competing effects on entropy. The multiplicative gate amplifies the uncertainty score when SinkRate is high (induction failed, entropy inflation is likely spurious) and suppresses it when SinkRate is low (induction succeeded, entropy inflation is a valid side-effect of correct grounding). This corrects for the false uncertainty signals identified in Mechanism 1. The design uses `min`/`max` aggregation for long outputs to capture peak failures and `mean` for short, single-answer outputs.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
RAG Pipeline (provides context C) -> Generator LLM (decoder-only Transformer, outputs logits/attentions) -> Induction Head Identifier (pre-computes top-k heads via `InductionScore`) -> SinkRate Calculator (computes `SinkRate` per head during generation) -> Entropy Calculator (computes per-token entropy) -> Score Combiner (`f(SinkRates) * g(Entropies)`) -> Final Uncertainty Score.

**Critical path:**
1.  **Initialize:** Pre-identify top-`k` induction heads for the target model using a synthetic repeated sequence.
2.  **Generation Loop:** For each token in the response: run forward pass, store attention matrices for top-`k` heads.
3.  **Post-generation:** For each head, compute `SinkRate` over response positions; aggregate to `s`. Compute aggregated entropy `e` from token-wise entropies.
4.  **Output:** `score = s * e`. Threshold `score` to classify as hallucination.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Aggregation (`f`, `g`):** `min` on entropy is robust to short correct spans in long hallucinations; `mean` is smoother for short answers. `f` typically uses `min` on SinkRate for sensitivity to any failed head.
*   **Number of Heads (`k`):** Paper shows stability (Fig 6a). A small `k` (3-10) balances signal and noise; too many may include non-induction heads.
*   **Threshold Calibration:** Requires a small labeled validation set from the same task/model for optimal performance.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **High score on grounded output:** SinkRate was high (induction failed) despite correctness. Could be due to model using parametric knowledge or incorrect head identification.
*   **Low score on clear hallucination:** SinkRate was low (induction *seemed* successful) but output is wrong. Likely because the model inductively copied from an *incorrect* retrieved context. INTRYGUE measures faithfulness to provided text, not objective truth.
*   **No separation (AUROC ~0.5):** Model may lack clear induction heads, head identification failed, or SinkRate is not discriminative for the model/dataset.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Replicate Head Identification:** Compute `InductionScore` on a synthetic repeated sequence (e.g., random tokens `S=(s1...sL, s1...sL)`). Verify heads with scores >0.5 exist and are concentrated in middle layers.
2.  **Validate SinkRate-Hallucination Correlation:** On a held-out set of 100 RAG examples (50 grounded, 50 hallucinated), compute average SinkRate (over top-5 heads). Perform Mann-Whitney U-test; expect p-value < 0.05 and AUROC > 0.6.
3.  **Gate Sanity Check:** For 50 examples, compute: (a) `MaxEntropy`, (b) `min(SinkRates)`, (c) `INTRYGUE = min(SinkRate) * MaxEntropy`. Plot histograms. Expect (c) to show improved separation where (a) has poor separation (the bottom-left quadrant).

## Limitations
*   INTRYGUE measures faithfulness to the retrieved context, not factual truth; it can be overconfident on outputs that inductively copy from an incorrect context.
*   Its efficacy depends on the existence and correct identification of classic induction heads via a synthetic calibration sequence, which may not generalize to all model architectures or fine-tuning regimes.
*   The method requires a small labeled validation set for threshold calibration, limiting zero-shot deployment.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1** (induction heads trigger entropy neurons): Medium. Mean-ablation shows causality, but the precise neural pathway may not generalize.
*   **Mechanism 2** (SinkRate as induction proxy): Medium-High. Strong correlation and causal ablations support it, but relies on the attention-sinking pattern holding universally.
*   **Mechanism 3** (entropy gating efficacy): High. Consistent AUROC improvements across diverse LLMs and benchmarks demonstrate robust empirical validity.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-architecture test:** Apply INTRYGUE to encoder-decoder (e.g., T5) or non-Transformer models to verify the induction-entropy coupling and gating effectiveness.
2.  **Robustness of head selection:** Vary the number of top-`k` induction heads and the synthetic sequence length `L` used for `InductionScore`. Evaluate AUROC stability on a fixed validation set to gauge sensitivity.
3.  **Alternative attention metrics:** Replace `SinkRate` with other attention concentration metrics (e.g., attention entropy, context-token focus ratio). If performance is similar, the specific `SinkRate` definition may be non-essential.
