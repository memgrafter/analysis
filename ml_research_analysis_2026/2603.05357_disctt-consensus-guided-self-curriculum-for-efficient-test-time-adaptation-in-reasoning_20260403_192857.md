# DiSCTT: Consensus-Guided Self-Curriculum for Efficient Test-Time Adaptation in Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05357
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05357
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** DiSCTT consistently outperforms strong baselines, achieving higher accuracy with lower variance and up to 50% reduction in computation and wall-clock time across six reasoning benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
DiSCTT addresses the inefficiency of test-time adaptation for reasoning LLMs by dynamically allocating optimization strategies based on instance-level epistemic uncertainty. This uncertainty is estimated from consensus among sampled reasoning trajectories without external labels. Inputs are partitioned into easy (high consensus) and hard (low consensus) subsets. Easy samples are refined via supervised fine-tuning using the majority-agreed pseudo-label, while hard samples undergo reinforcement learning with a correctness-gated, population-relative novelty reward and relevance-aware semantic gating. A periodic recomputation of partitions creates a difficulty-aware self-curriculum that stabilizes learning as the model evolves. Across multiple mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks, DiSCTT yields superior accuracy, reduced variance, and significant computational savings, alongside robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method Summary
Given an unlabeled test dataset, DiSCTT first samples *M* independent reasoning completions per input using the current policy. A consensus score is computed as the proportion of completions sharing the majority final answer. Inputs with consensus ≥ *ρ* (e.g., 0.45) are sent to supervised fine-tuning (SFT); others to reinforcement learning (RL). SFT minimizes cross-entropy against the majority-agreed completion. RL employs GRPO with a reward that (i) zeroes out trajectories not matching the majority answer, (ii) adds a novelty term (average Jensen–Shannon divergence from a reference distribution over majority completions), and (iii) multiplies by a relevance gate (average cosine similarity of reasoning steps to the input). After a fixed number of cycles (2 SFT epochs + 10 RL epochs), the policy re-samples all inputs and recomputes partitions, forming a dynamic curriculum. This alternating schedule repeats for several cycles, enabling efficient parameter updates during inference.

## Key Results
- Outperforms strong baselines (TTRL, EVOL-RL) on six reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME-2024, AMC, GPQA, HotpotQA, MMLU).
- Achieves higher average accuracy with lower variance across model scales (0.5B–7B).
- Reduces computation and wall-clock time by up to 50% compared to uniform RL approaches.
- Demonstrates robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Agreement among sampled reasoning trajectories serves as an online, label-free estimator of instance-level epistemic uncertainty, enabling difficulty-aware partitioning.
- **Mechanism:** For each input, the policy samples *M* independent completions. The consensus score *c_j* is computed as the proportion of completions sharing the majority final answer. Inputs with *c_j ≥ ρ* (threshold) are routed to supervised fine-tuning (SFT); others to reinforcement learning (RL).
- **Core assumption:** High trajectory agreement correlates with low epistemic uncertainty and solvable problem instances, while disagreement indicates genuine difficulty or reasoning instability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "epistemic uncertainty estimated from agreement among sampled reasoning trajectories"
  - [Section 3.2] Formal definition of consensus ratio *c_j* and partitioning rule.
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus contains papers using consensus for selection (e.g., self-consistency) but not explicitly for *difficulty estimation* at test time. Assumption: consensus-difficulty link is not independently validated in corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the consensus threshold *ρ* is mis-calibrated for a dataset, the difficulty partition becomes noisy, causing inappropriate routing (e.g., hard problems sent to SFT).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Periodic recomputation of the difficulty-aware partition creates a dynamic self-curriculum that adapts to the model's evolving competence, stabilizing test-time learning.
- **Mechanism:** After every *K* training steps, the current policy regenerates completions for all inputs, recomputes consensus scores, and repartitions *D* into *D_easy* and *D_hard*. The partition is reused for intermediate steps, decoupling difficulty estimation from per-gradient updates.
- **Core assumption:** The model's progression makes previously difficult instances become easier (or vice versa), and a slower-moving curriculum (periodic updates) prevents destabilizing non-stationarity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "periodically recomputes partitions, enabling stable and efficient self-improvement"
  - [Section 3.3] Describes periodic regeneration with interval *K* and closed-loop curriculum.
  - [corpus] Partial: Curriculum learning exists, but corpus lacks direct evidence for *test-time periodic repartitioning* as a stabilizer.
- **Break condition:** If *K* is too small, the curriculum changes too frequently, causing label noise in routing; if too large, the system cannot adapt to rapid policy shifts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A correctness-gated, population-relative novelty reward with relevance-aware semantic gating stabilizes RL on low-consensus inputs by constraining exploration to majority-aligned, semantically relevant deviations.
- **Mechanism:** For *D_hard*, RL uses GRPO with reward *R(y_i) = 1[a_i = a_maj] × (α + β·JSD_nov(ri)) × (ε + (1−ε)·g_rel(ri))*. Only trajectories matching the majority answer are rewarded. Novelty (JSD from the majority-conditioned reference distribution *p̄_maj*) is scaled by semantic relevance *g_rel* (average cosine similarity of reasoning steps to input embedding).
- **Core assumption:** Majority answer *a_maj* is a reliable pseudo-label; encouraging deviation from the *population* of majority-aligned trajectories (not arbitrary diversity) yields informative exploration without collapse. Relevance gating prevents off-topic "creative" deviations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "consensus-regularized objective that encourages diversity under relevance constraints"
  - [Section 3.5] Full reward decomposition and definitions of JSD_nov and g_rel.
  - [Section 4.4] Ablation (Table 4) shows each component (correctness gate, novelty, relevance) incrementally improves accuracy.
  - [corpus] Partial: EVOL-RL uses global novelty without consensus gating; DiSCTT's correctness gate and population-relative novelty are novel combinations. Corpus does not evaluate relevance gating.
- **Break condition:** If majority answer *a_maj* is incorrect (e.g., all samples fail on a hard problem), the correctness gate blocks all updates, causing learning stagnation. If relevance gating is too harsh, gradients vanish.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)**  
  Why needed here: DiSCTT updates model parameters *during inference* on unlabeled test data. Understanding TTA's goal—adapting to distribution shift without external supervision—is fundamental to framing the problem.  
  Quick check question: How does DiSCTT differ from traditional fine-tuning in its use of supervision?

- **Concept: Epistemic Uncertainty vs. Aleatoric Uncertainty**  
  Why needed here: DiSCTT uses consensus to estimate *epistemic* uncertainty (reducible, model-dependent) to route inputs. Distinguishing this from irreducible aleatoric noise is key to understanding why consensus is a valid signal.  
  Quick check question: Why might token-level probability entropy be a poor proxy for epistemic uncertainty in multi-step reasoning?

- **Concept: Curriculum Learning**  
  Why needed here: DiSCTT implements a *self-curriculum* where difficulty is dynamically reassessed. Grasping curriculum principles—presenting easier concepts first—explains the SFT-then-RL sequence and periodic repartitioning.  
  Quick check question: What is the risk of a static (non-recomputed) easy/hard split during test-time training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Sampler → Consensus Calculator → Router → (SFT Module or RL Module) → Parameter update  
  Curriculum Manager triggers periodic repartitioning.
- **Critical path:** Input → Sampler → Consensus Calculator → Router → (SFT or RL) → Parameter update → (every *K* steps: Curriculum Manager → all inputs re-sampled → repartition).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  Threshold ρ: Lower → more SFT (stable, but less exploration); higher → more RL (exploratory, high variance).  
  Re-routing interval K: Smaller → faster adaptation but overhead; larger → stable but slow to adapt.  
  Reward components: Removing correctness gate risks reinforcing incorrect modes; removing relevance gating risks semantically irrelevant novelty.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  High variance on easy benchmarks (e.g., MMLU) → ρ too low, RL applied unnecessarily.  
  No improvement on hard benchmarks (e.g., AIME-2024) → ρ too high, insufficient RL or majority answer often wrong.  
  Performance degradation over time → K too small causing chaotic repartitioning; or reward mis-specification (e.g., relevance gate too weak).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate consensus threshold sensitivity:** Run DiSCTT on MATH-500 with ρ ∈ {0.3, 0.45, 0.6}. Measure routing ratios, accuracy, and variance. Expect optimal ρ ~0.45 (as in paper).  
  2. **Ablate reward components:** Starting from base model, sequentially add: (a) correctness gate only, (b) + population-relative novelty, (c) + relevance gating. Measure accuracy on GPQA. Expect incremental gains as in Table 4.  
  3. **Test re-routing interval K:** Compare K = {1, 5, 20} curriculum cycles on AMC. Track accuracy dynamics and routing stability. Expect intermediate K (e.g., 5) best balances adaptivity and stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can DiSCTT's consensus-guided self-curriculum be effectively extended to multimodal reasoning tasks (e.g., visual or audio-linguistic settings)?  
  Basis: Conclusion explicitly lists "extensions to other modalities" as future work. Unresolved because evaluation is limited to text-based benchmarks; transferability unknown.  
  Resolution: Implement DiSCTT on multimodal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MMBench, AudioCaps) and compare against baselines.

- **Open Question 2:** Do tighter or more nuanced uncertainty estimates (beyond simple consensus agreement) improve the difficulty-aware routing and overall adaptation efficiency?  
  Basis: Conclusion suggests exploring "tighter uncertainty estimates". Current method uses basic agreement ratio; finer-grained quantification (e.g., token-level entropy, Bayesian averaging) not explored.  
  Resolution: Replace consensus score with alternative proxies, run controlled experiments across benchmarks, measure routing accuracy, final performance, and stability.

- **Open Question 3:** What are the theoretical properties (e.g., convergence, stability, sample complexity) of the difficulty-aware self-curriculum and periodic re-routing mechanism?  
  Basis: Conclusion calls for "theoretical characterizations of curriculum-driven test-time learning dynamics." Only empirical results presented; no formal analysis explains why alternating SFT/RL with dynamic partitioning yields stable, efficient adaptation.  
  Resolution: Develop an analytical framework (e.g., tabular or linear setting) to model curriculum evolution, proving convergence guarantees or identifying instability risks relative to uniform test-time training.

## Limitations
- Implementation details missing: reasoning step segmentation, variable-length JSD_nov handling, tie-breaking for majority answers, GRPO hyperparameters.
- Performance sensitive to threshold ρ and re-routing interval K; mis-calibration causes inappropriate routing or curriculum instability.
- Risk of learning stagnation when majority answer incorrect or relevance gating overly restrictive.

## Confidence
- Consensus-based uncertainty estimation: Medium confidence.
- Periodic self-curriculum: Medium confidence.
- Correctness-gated RL reward: High confidence for correctness gate, Medium for population-relative novelty and relevance gating.
- Overall efficiency and robustness: Medium confidence.
