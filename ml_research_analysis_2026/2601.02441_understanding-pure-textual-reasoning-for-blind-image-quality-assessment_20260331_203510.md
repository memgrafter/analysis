# Understanding Pure Textual Reasoning for Blind Image Quality Assessment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02441
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02441
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Self-Consistency reduces the image-text performance gap to near parity (PLCC/SRCC difference ~0.02/0.03) and text-only inference achieves results competitive with state-of-the-art deep BIQA models.

## Executive Summary
Blind Image Quality Assessment (BIQA) aims to predict human perceptual quality scores without a reference image. This paper studies how textual reasoning contributes to BIQA by comparing three learning paradigms—Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Self-Consistency, and Autoencoder-like—within a multimodal large language model (MLLM) framework. Using Qwen-VL-2.5-7B-Instruct and training on the KONIQ dataset, the authors measure the performance gap between image-conditioned and text-only score prediction across six BIQA benchmarks. The key findings are: naive CoT yields negligible improvement; Self-Consistency closes the gap to minimal differences (0.02 in PLCC, 0.03 in SRCC) and its text-only performance rivals dedicated deep learning BIQA models; the Autoencoder-like approach moderately reduces the gap and produces more natural quality-related explanations. Robustness checks show that removing score-related semantic words causes only a minor drop, indicating that the model learns genuine quality reasoning rather than lexical shortcuts.

## Method Summary
The backbone is the pre-trained MLLM Qwen-VL-2.5-7B-Instruct. Training employs Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm that maximizes a reward based on the absolute difference between predicted and ground-truth MOS. Three paradigms differ in information flow and loss weighting: Chain-of-Thought (I→R→S) uses separate stages with no quality supervision on the caption; Self-Consistency performs two passes on the same image (I→(R,S) then R→S) and encourages consistency between scores; Autoencoder-like learning first generates an explanation conditioned on the true score ((I,S*)→R) and then reconstructs the score from the explanation (R→S*). All paradigms can be tuned via weights α (image-conditioned loss) and β (text-only loss). At inference, the model may run either the image-conditioned pass or the text-only pass (using generated captions) to assess performance.

## Key Results
- Chain-of-Thought provides negligible improvement in closing the image-text performance gap.
- Self-Consistency reduces the gap to PLCC/SRCC differences of 0.02/0.03 across six datasets, and its text-only results are competitive with state-of-the-art BIQA models.
- Autoencoder-like learning moderately narrows the gap and yields more natural, quality-related textual explanations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Chain-of-Thought**: CoT enforces a strict sequential flow: image → caption → score. The caption generation is not supervised by quality, so the text may lack fine-grained quality cues. When the image is removed for score prediction, the model lacks sufficient information, resulting in negligible gap reduction.

**Self-Consistency**: This paradigm uses two passes on the same image: first generates caption and score (conditioned on image), second predicts score from caption alone. Both predictions receive reward from the ground-truth MOS, optionally with a consistency bonus. This joint optimization forces the caption to retain quality information that remains predictive in the text-only branch, effectively aligning the textual representation with the score manifold while still leveraging visual cues during training. The result is a drastic reduction of the image-text gap and strong text-only performance.

**Autoencoder-like**: The model first generates an explanation given the true score, thereby biasing the caption to include quality-justifying content. Later it predicts the score from the explanation alone. This bidirectional flow acts like an autoencoder for quality information, encouraging the text to become a predictive container. The gap reduction is moderate, but explanations appear more natural and quality-focused.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Qwen-VL backbone → two-stage inference (image-conditioned pass: I → (R, S1); text-only pass: R → S2) → reward computation (|S-MOS| with tolerance) → losses (cross-entropy + regression) weighted by α, β → GRPO update (group-relative advantages, KL penalty).

Critical path (Self-Consistency):
1. Encode image and generate caption R and score S₁.
2. Use R as input to generate score S₂.
3. Compute rewards r₁ and r₂ from MOS; optionally add consistency bonus.
4. Normalize rewards within the sampled group to obtain advantages.
5. Update model policy with GRPO objective, controlling deviation from reference model.

Design tradeoffs:
- α vs β balances reliance on image vs text.
- Number of samples per image (N) and number of score predictions per caption (M) affect reward estimation vs compute.
- Reward shaping (tolerance t, consistency bonus) influences learning dynamics.
- GRPO hyperparameters (clip range ϵ, KL weight β) require tuning for stability.

Failure signatures:
- Persistent large image-text performance gap (>0.1 PLCC).
- Attention analysis shows no shift toward quality tokens; image tokens dominate text-only tests.
- Removing score-related words drastically reduces text-only performance, indicating lexical shortcut reliance.
- Training instability (KL divergence explosion, stagnant group rewards).

## Limitations
- Exact GRPO hyperparameters (group size N, KL penalty, clip range) are not specified, hindering precise replication.
- Token-attention analysis method is underspecified (selection of heads/layers, distinction between image and text tokens).
- DeQA-normalization procedure across datasets is unclear, potentially affecting cross-dataset comparisons.

## Confidence
- Paradigm Performance Gaps: High (consistent across six datasets with clear tables)
- Mechanistic Explanations: Medium/Low (plausible but not causally proven)
- Generalizability Beyond Qwen-VL: Low (no evidence the findings transfer to other MLLMs)
