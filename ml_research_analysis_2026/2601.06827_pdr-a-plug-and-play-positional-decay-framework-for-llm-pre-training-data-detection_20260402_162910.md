# PDR: A Plug-and-Play Positional Decay Framework for LLM Pre-training Data Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06827  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06827  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PDR improves likelihood-based pre-training data detection, boosting Min‑k%++ by up to 4.7 AUROC points for LLaMA‑13B on WikiMIA‑128.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Positional Decay Reweighting (PDR), a training‑free framework for membership inference attacks (MIA) on large language models. PDR addresses the key limitation of existing likelihood‑based methods: they uniformly aggregate token scores, ignoring the information‑theoretic decay of memorization signals across token positions. By applying monotonically decreasing positional weights to token‑level scores, PDR amplifies early high‑entropy tokens where memorization is most concentrated. Comprehensive experiments across multiple LLMs (LLaMA, Pythia, GPT‑NeoX) and benchmarks (WikiMIA, MIMIR) show consistent AUROC improvements with negligible computational overhead. PDR seamlessly integrates with diverse base scorers (Loss, Min‑k%, FSD), establishing a robust positional prior for black‑box detection.

## Method Summary
PDR modifies token‑level scoring functions by introducing positional decay weights \( w(t) \) that decrease monotonically with position \( t \). For full‑sequence methods (Loss, Ref), the final score is \( s_{\text{PDR}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} w(t) \cdot \text{score}_t \). For outlier‑based methods (Min‑k%, Min‑k%++), the set \( S_k \) of the lowest \( k\% \) scoring tokens is first identified using unweighted scores; reweighting is then applied only to those tokens: \( s_{\text{PDR}} = \frac{1}{|S_k|}\sum_{t\in S_k} w(t) \cdot \text{score}_t \). Three decay families are supported: linear \( w(t)=1-\alpha\cdot(t-1)/(T-1) \), exponential \( w(t)=\exp(-\alpha\cdot(t-1)) \), and polynomial \( w(t)=(1-(t-1)/(T-1))^\alpha \). The approach requires only the model’s token‑level outputs, adds negligible computation, and needs no training or additional data.

## Key Results
- PDR lifts Min‑k%++ AUROC by up to **4.7 points** on LLaMA‑13B (WikiMIA‑128).
- Gains are **consistent** across models, sequence lengths, and base methods, with LPDR (linear) often performing best.
- PDR integrates smoothly with the FSD framework, extending its benefits to fine‑tuning‑based amplification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Memorization signals are heavily concentrated in high‑entropy initial tokens and decay as context accumulates.
- **Mechanism:** In autoregressive generation, early tokens have high predictive uncertainty (high entropy). An unusually high probability for such a token distinguishes memorized members from non‑members. As context grows, entropy decreases and the probability gap between members and non‑members narrows.
- **Core assumption:** Token‑level entropy generally decreases with position in typical text sequences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Explicit statement of the decay pattern.
  - [section 4.1] Figure 1(a) shows average token entropy decreasing with position; Figure 1(b) shows the largest log‑probability gap between members and non‑members at early positions.
- **Break condition:** Non‑monotonic or increasing entropy profiles (e.g., code mixed with prose) violate the assumption and may reduce PDR’s effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Applying monotonically decreasing positional weights amplifies the discriminative memorization signal.
- **Mechanism:** By increasing the contribution of early tokens, the weighted score rises more for member samples (where high‑probability memorized tokens often appear early) than for non‑members, widening the score separation.
- **Core assumption:** The most informative tokens for MIA are disproportionately located near the start of a sequence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Formal definition of `sPDR-Loss` and variants with weighting.
  - [section 5.3] Figure 5 visualizes larger score shifts for members vs. non‑members after PDR; Figure 15 shows increased separation of score distributions.
- **Break condition:** If the most discriminative tokens appear mid‑sequence or at the end, uniform decay weights could suppress the signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** PDR is a training‑free, plug‑and‑play prior that consistently enhances diverse likelihood‑based detection methods.
- **Mechanism:** PDR acts as a lightweight wrapper around existing scoring functions, requiring only token‑level outputs and a simple arithmetic adjustment during aggregation. No fine‑tuning, reference models, or extra data are needed.
- **Core assumption:** The positional prior is complementary and robust across base methods and model architectures.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Claims of training‑free, plug‑and‑play nature and integration with FSD.
  - [section 5.2] Tables 1 & 2 show consistent AUROC improvements when PDR (especially LPDR) is combined with Loss, Ref, Min‑k%, Min‑k%++, and FSD across multiple LLMs and datasets.
- **Break condition:** Base methods that lack token‑level outputs cannot use PDR; performance may degrade if aggregation is already optimized for positional effects.

## Foundational Learning
- **Autoregressive generation and token‑level scoring**  
  Why needed: Likelihood‑based MIA relies on the chain rule \( p(x)=\prod_{t=1}^{T} p(x_t \mid x_{<t}) \); token‑level log‑probabilities are the raw material for PDR’s reweighting.  
  Quick check: Given a sequence \( x=(x_1,x_2,x_3) \), write \( \log p(x) \) and explain how a typical MIA score (e.g., average log‑likelihood) is computed from token‑level terms.

- **Token‑level entropy and predictability**  
  Why needed: PDR’s motivation hinges on entropy decreasing with position; early high‑entropy tokens are more informative for memorization detection.  
  Quick check: If \( H(p(\cdot|x_{<t})) \) is high at position \( t \), what does that imply about model uncertainty? How would a memorized \( x_t \) affect the entropy and the log‑probability?

- **Likelihood‑based Membership Inference Attack (MIA) scoring functions**  
  Why needed: PDR modifies existing aggregators (Loss, Min‑k%, Min‑k%++); understanding their original formulations is essential to see where and how reweighting is injected.  
  Quick check: Contrast Loss (average all token log‑probs) with Min‑k% (average lowest k%). Why might Min‑k% be more sensitive to outlier tokens, and how does PDR’s post‑selection reweighting preserve that sensitivity?

## Architecture Onboarding

Input Tokenizer -> Target LLM (M) -> Positional Weight Generator -> Base Scoring Module -> PDR Wrapper -> Score Output

**Critical path:**
1. Tokenize input to \( x_1\ldots x_T \).
2. Forward pass through \( M \) to obtain \( \log P(x_t|x_{<t}) \) for all positions.
3. Compute decay weights \( w(1)\ldots w(T) \) based on chosen function and \( \alpha \).
4. For full‑sequence methods (Loss, Ref): multiply each token’s score by \( w(t) \), then average.  
   For outlier methods (Min‑k%, Min‑k%++): first identify set \( S_k \) of the lowest k% unweighted scores; then average \( w(t)\cdot\text{score}_t \) only for \( t\in S_k \).
5. Output final membership score.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Decay function/α:** Linear is simplest and robust; exponential emphasizes very early tokens; polynomial offers adjustable curvature. The paper finds \( \alpha=1 \) for linear works well generally, but optimal \( \alpha \) may vary with sequence length and dataset (shorter sequences often benefit from gentler decay).
- **Order of operations for Min‑k%:** Reweighting must occur *after* outlier selection; reweighting before selection alters the outlier set and harms performance (see ablation in Table 3).
- **Computational overhead:** Only element‑wise multiplication and summation—negligible compared to the model forward pass. No training or extra data required.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Uniform distribution of lowest‑probability tokens across positions: PDR may amplify uninformative early tokens or suppress informative late ones.
2. Marginal gains on very short sequences (e.g., \( T=32 \)): positional signal is weaker.
3. Degradation on highly heterogeneous corpora (e.g., ArXiv, HackerNews in MIMIR) where entropy profiles are not monotonically decreasing.
4. Incorrect order for Min‑k%: reweighting before selection leads to performance drops.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Replicate entropy trend:** For LLaMA‑13B, compute average token entropy vs. position on WikiMIA and a contrasting corpus (e.g., GitHub code). Verify downward trend; identify deviations.
2. **Decay hyperparameter sweep:** On a validation set (e.g., Pythia‑6.9B, WikiMIA‑64), evaluate PDR with linear, exponential, polynomial decays over \( \alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\} \). Confirm \( \alpha=1 \) for linear is robust; note dataset‑specific optima.
3. **Integrate with base method:** Implement PDR as a wrapper around Min‑k%++. Validate that for a member/non‑member pair with similar original scores, reweighting shifts the member’s score higher (as in Figure 5). Measure AUROC improvement on a held‑out set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multilingual & code‑switched generalisation:** How does PDR perform on non‑English or code‑mixed data? The evaluation is English‑centric; tokenization differences may alter entropy decay patterns.
2. **Weak signals on homogeneous data:** On highly homogeneous benchmarks like MIMIR, baseline performance is near random and PDR yields only modest gains on some sub‑sets. Can PDR be strengthened for such subtle distributional shifts?
3. **Optimal decay function & hyperparameter selection:** What is the best decay type and \( \alpha \) for a given model and sequence length? Current choices are heuristic; a principled or automated selection method is lacking.
4. **Adaptive per‑sample weighting:** Can we design a stable adaptive weighting scheme that respects each sequence’s local entropy dynamics, improving over fixed decay? Prior attempts (e.g., per‑sample entropy weights) were volatile.

## Limitations
- The core assumption of monotonically decreasing entropy may fail on data with volatile structures, limiting PDR’s applicability.
- Implementation details for WikiMIA preprocessing (tokenisation, padding, truncation) are not fully specified, risking inconsistent reproduction.
- Min‑k%++ depends on position‑wise calibration statistics \( \mu_t,\sigma_t \) from non‑member samples; noisy estimates from small or unrepresentative calibration sets can yield unstable scores.
- PDR requires token‑level outputs; it cannot be applied to purely black‑box APIs that only provide sequence‑level probabilities.

## Confidence
- **Entropy decay hypothesis:** Medium (empirical evidence across several datasets but not universally proven).  
- **PDR improves detection performance:** High (consistent AUROC gains across multiple LLMs, benchmarks, and base methods).  
- **PDR is training‑free and plug‑and‑play:** High (design and experiments confirm negligible overhead and seamless integration).
