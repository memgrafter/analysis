# From Gradients to Riccati Geometry: Kalman World Models for Single-Pass Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13423
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13423
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** KWM matches Adam's perplexity on WikiText-2 (88.4 vs 88.7) but with better robustness (shifted PPL 98.2 vs 110.3) and 30% less catastrophic forgetting on continual benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Kalman World Models (KWM) recasts neural network training as recursive Bayesian filtering, replacing backpropagation with Kalman gain updates on parameter and activation states. This approach yields equivalent natural gradient descent under Gaussian approximations, while incorporating uncertainty quantification and Riccati geometry for stability. Empirical results on language modeling and continual learning show comparable perplexity to Adam with improved robustness to distribution shift and reduced catastrophic forgetting. KWM leverages Koopman operator lifting to avoid local linearization and employs low-rank structured covariances for scalability, unifying parameter learning and activation correction within a single filtering pass.

## Method Summary
KWM transforms neural network training into a state estimation problem where parameters and activations are latent states. At each step, the model computes an innovation (prediction error) and updates states using a Kalman gain derived from the current covariance estimate. The covariance evolves via a Riccati equation, with structured approximations (block-diagonal, low-rank) to maintain scalability. For transformers, activation-level innovation correction is applied during decoding to adapt hidden states online. The Koopman operator lifts nonlinear dynamics into a linear feature space, avoiding repeated Jacobian linearizations. Implementation requires choosing covariance structures, process/observation noise levels, and Koopman encoder architecture.

## Key Results
- Comparable perplexity to Adam on WikiText-2 (88.4 vs 88.7).
- Superior robustness under distribution shift: shifted PPL 98.2 vs 110.3.
- Reduced catastrophic forgetting: 30% improvement on continual benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The Kalman gain update is equivalent to a natural gradient step under locally linear Gaussian observation models.
**Mechanism:** The Kalman gain \(K_t = P_t H_t^\top (H_t P_t H_t^\top + R)^{-1}\) reduces to \(K_t \approx F^{-1} H_t^\top R^{-1}\) when the parameter covariance \(P_t\) approximates the inverse Fisher information \(F^{-1}\) and observation noise \(R\) is small, yielding \(\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + F^{-1} \nabla \theta \ell_t\).
**Core assumption:** The observation model is locally linear in parameters and noise is Gaussian.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] States the equivalence to natural gradient descent under Gaussian approximations.
- [section] Theorem 1 and Section 4 derive the equivalence via matrix inversion lemma and Fisher information expression \(F = H_t^\top R^{-1} H_t\).
- [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "Recursive KalmanNet") explore similar connections but do not directly prove this equivalence; corpus evidence is indirect.
**Break condition:** Non-Gaussian observation noise or strong nonlinearity where local linearization fails, causing the gain to deviate from Fisher-preconditioned updates.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Learning is reframed as sequential Bayesian state estimation rather than deterministic loss minimization.
**Mechanism:** Parameters \(\theta_t\) are treated as latent states evolved via \(\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} + \xi_t\) and updated through recursive posterior inference \(p(\theta_t | y_{1:t}) \propto p(y_t | \theta_t) p(\theta_t | y_{1:t-1})\), with Gaussian approximations yielding Kalman updates that propagate uncertainty.
**Core assumption:** The posterior over parameters can be approximated as Gaussian.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] Describes "training becomes online filtering; error signals become innovations."
- [section] Section 5 formalizes the posterior recursion and Gaussian approximation (Eq. 25–34), linking to Riccati geometry.
- [corpus] Corpus includes Bayesian filtering papers (e.g., "Bayesian Kalman View of In Context Learning") that align conceptually but do not address this specific parameter-state fusion.
**Break condition:** Multi-modal or heavy-tailed posteriors that violate Gaussianity, or when parameter dynamics \(\xi_t\) are not mean-zero Gaussian.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Transformer activations are corrected online via innovation terms, turning the model into a nonlinear observer.
**Mechanism:** Hidden states \(h_t\) are treated as latent variables with prior \(p(h_t | y_{1:t-1}) \approx \mathcal{N}(\mu_t, \Sigma_t)\). Innovation \(\tilde{y}_t = e_{y_t} - \text{softmax}(W \mu_t)\) drives a Kalman-style update \(h_t \leftarrow \mu_t + K_t \tilde{y}_t\), where \(K_t\) scales with activation covariance \(\Sigma_t\).
**Core assumption:** The softmax observation model is locally linearizable and activations follow Gaussian random walk dynamics.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] Mentions "activation-level innovation correction" for transformers.
- [section] Section 6 derives the update (Eq. 48–51) and connects to predictive coding.
- [corpus] Neighbor work on "Transformers as Implicit State Estimators" uses Kalman views for ICL but focuses on state estimation, not weight-free activation correction.
**Break condition:** High-dimensional activations where Jacobian \(H_t = W^\top J_{\text{softmax}}\) is ill-conditioned or when the linearization error accumulates over long sequences.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Augmented state \(z_t = [x_t^\top, \theta_t^\top]^\top\) (activations + parameters), observation model \(y_t = h(\theta_t, x_t) + v_t\), innovation \(\tilde{y}_t = y_t - \hat{y}_t\), Kalman gain \(K_t\) from covariance \(P_t\) and Jacobian \(H_t\), Riccati update for \(P_t\).

### Critical path
1. Forward pass to get predictions \(\hat{y}_t\) and Jacobian \(H_t\).
2. Compute innovation \(\tilde{y}_t\).
3. Compute gain \(K_t = P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top (H_t P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top + R)^{-1}\).
4. Update state \(\theta_t \leftarrow \theta_{t|t-1} + K_t \tilde{y}_t\) (and activations if using correction).
5. Update covariance via Riccati equation.

### Design tradeoffs
- Full covariance (\(O(p^2)\)) vs. structured (block-diagonal, low-rank, Kronecker) for scalability.
- Local linearization (EKF) vs. Koopman lifting (avoids stepwise Jacobians but requires learned \(\phi\)).
- Parameter-only vs. activation-level updates (latter adds test-time adaptation cost).

### Failure signatures
- Covariance collapse (\(\lambda_{\min}(P_t) \to 0\)) causing numerical instability.
- Gain explosion (\(\|K_t\| \gg 1\)) from ill-conditioned \(H_t^\top R^{-1} H_t\).
- Diverging error dynamics if \(\rho(I - K_t H_t) \ge 1\) (violates observability/excitation).
- Poor Koopman features leading to large projection errors in lifted space.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Sequential MNIST** – Validate long-term memory and convergence stability with recurrent networks.
2. **WikiText-2** – Compare perplexity and robustness under distribution shift (e.g., token dropout) for transformer models.
3. **Permuted MNIST / streaming language tasks** – Measure catastrophic forgetting via average performance drop on previous tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How does KWM scale to modern LLM parameter counts (billions) while maintaining computational feasibility and stability?
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that "empirical evaluation at frontier LLM scale has not yet been performed."
**Why unresolved:** Current experiments use models with ≤6 layers and 512 hidden size; scaling to billion-parameter transformers introduces covariance overhead and distributed implementation challenges not addressed.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implement KWM with structured covariances (e.g., low-rank) on a ≥1B parameter model, measure memory/time per step, convergence speed, and perplexity relative to Adam; analyze stability under distribution shift.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What is the minimal sufficient rank *r* for low-rank covariance approximations that preserves stability and natural-gradient-like convergence in billion-parameter models?
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper identifies "determining minimal sufficient rank *r* for billion-parameter models remains an open problem."
**Why unresolved:** Rank *r* trades off curvature tracking (stability/convergence) against computational cost; aggressive truncation may degrade performance, but no guidance on thresholds for large models.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation varying *r* (e.g., 4, 16, 64, 256, 1024) in large-scale experiments, measuring final perplexity, robustness metrics (shifted PPL), and catastrophic forgetting; correlate with Fisher spectrum approximations.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can we derive theoretical guarantees that learned Koopman operators provide accurate linear embeddings of nonlinear neural dynamics, ensuring filtering stability and accuracy?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper claims filtering is "exact in lifted space" but the approximation lies in finite-dimensional truncation; no bounds on how well learned *K* captures invariant subspaces.
**Why unresolved:** Filtering accuracy and stability depend critically on the quality of the lifting function *ϕ* and operator *K*; without guarantees, performance may degrade for highly nonlinear architectures or unseen dynamics.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Provide generalization bounds linking reconstruction loss (Eq. 131) to operator approximation error; validate by measuring trajectory prediction error on synthetic systems with known Koopman spectra; correlate spectral radius of *K* with convergence rates in real networks.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Do Kalman-style updates guarantee convergence to competitive minima in highly nonconvex deep learning landscapes without strong convexity assumptions?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] Global convergence (Theorem 2) assumes *µ*-strong convexity; the paper notes that "global convergence guarantees do not hold without additional structural assumptions" in nonconvex regimes.
**Why unresolved:** Deep networks are inherently nonconvex; local stability (Section 7) does not preclude convergence to poor local minima or divergence in practice.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Extensive empirical study on standard deep learning benchmarks (ResNets, transformers) with random restarts, measuring frequency of converging to minima matching Adam's final loss; develop new nonconvex analysis tools (e.g., based on smoothness or overparameterization).

### Open Question 5
**Question:** What is the computational overhead of activation-level innovation correction in large autoregressive transformers, and how does it trade off with robustness gains under distribution shift?
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] Activation correction is introduced to mitigate hallucination but not benchmarked for computational cost; Section 13 only reports perplexity, not latency or FLOPs per token.
**Why unresolved:** Correcting hidden states at each decoding step adds per-token matrix operations; may be prohibitive for long sequences or real-time generation, yet potential robustness benefits are unclear.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Measure per-token latency and memory overhead during decoding for varying batch sizes and sequence lengths; correlate with robustness metrics (e.g., accuracy on shifted datasets, hallucination rates); develop efficient approximations (e.g., low-rank gains in activation space).

## Limitations
- The paper provides high-level descriptions but omits critical hyperparameters (e.g., process/observation noise magnitudes, covariance structures) and architectural details for Koopman lifting and activation-level updates.
- Key assumptions—local linearity, Gaussian posteriors, and linear Gaussian observation models—may not hold for deep transformers, risking suboptimal performance or instability.
- Covariance approximations (low-rank, Kronecker) need validation for scalability and accuracy.
