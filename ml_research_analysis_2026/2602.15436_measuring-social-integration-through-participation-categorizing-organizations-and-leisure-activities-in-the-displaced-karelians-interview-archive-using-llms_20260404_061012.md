# Measuring Social Integration Through Participation: Categorizing Organizations and Leisure Activities in the Displaced Karelians Interview Archive using LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15436
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15436
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** An LLM ensemble achieved 76.8% F-score on coarse labels (93.9% of human baseline) and 73.7% on full granularity, matching human reliability for clear categories but showing systematic errors on ambiguous or culturally-specific entities, with performance varying by dimension (highest for group size, lowest for physical activity).

## Executive Summary
The study tackled the challenge of analyzing 350,000 extracted mentions of leisure activities and organizational memberships from Finnish Karelian evacuee interviews—71,000 unique terms too unstructured for quantitative social integration research. The authors developed a four-dimensional classification framework (category, group size, regularity, physical intensity) and created a gold-standard annotation. Testing open-weight LLMs, an ensemble of seven optimized prompts achieved 76.8% F-score on coarse labels and 73.7% on full granularity, approaching human consensus. The method labeled all 60,647 unique entities, producing a structured resource. LLMs reliably handled clear categories but faltered on ambiguous or culturally-specific entities, with systematic weaknesses in judging physical activity intensity.

## Method Summary
The task was a multi-label classification of historical entity strings (e.g., "Martha Club", "Cross-country skiing") into four orthogonal dimensions: Q1-Category (18 labels, multi-label allowed), Q2-Group Size, Q3-Regularity, Q4-Physical Intensity. Input data comprised pre-extracted entities from 89,339 Karelian interviews, with a gold-standard test set of 150 entities labeled by four annotators via majority vote. The approach used zero-shot prompting of the Mistral-Large-Instruct-2407 model. An original prompt was iteratively refined via error analysis, yielding seven optimized prompts. For each entity, all prompts were run in parallel, and a majority vote determined the final label per question. Performance was evaluated using macro-averaged F1-score against the human baseline (~78.6% full, ~81.8% coarse).

## Key Results
- The 7-prompt ensemble achieved 76.8% F-score on coarse labels (93.9% of human baseline) and 73.7% on full granularity.
- LLMs matched human reliability for clear categories but exhibited systematic errors on ambiguous or culturally-specific entities (e.g., Finnish abbreviations, folk dances).
- Performance varied by dimension: highest for group size (Q2), lowest for physical activity intensity (Q4), reflecting lower inter-annotator agreement on Q4.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** A multi-dimensional, multi-label framework decomposes "participation" into four orthogonal aspects (category, group size, regularity, physical intensity), enabling structured quantification without forcing high-cardinality single categories. This design captures distinct sociological facets relevant to health outcomes and aligns with the LLM's sequential prompting. Evidence: the schema is explicitly designed to address health research considerations (Section 4.1), and its independence is supported by moderate inter-annotator agreement variation across questions.

**Mechanism 2:** An ensemble of varied prompts applied to a single LLM mitigates stochastic output variance and prompt-specific biases by assuming uncorrelated errors across differently phrased instructions. Majority voting across seven prompts yields a modest but consistent gain over a single prompt (~1.2 percentage points). Evidence: the ensemble improved F-score from 73.3% (repeated original) to 73.7% (full schema) (Section 5), and iterative prompt engineering produced diverse instructions (Section 4.5).

**Mechanism 3:** Coarsening label granularity (e.g., collapsing "Small group" and "Large group" into "Social") substantially improves performance (3.1 pp gain to 76.8% F-score) by removing distinctions that are inherently ambiguous even for humans and lack clear signal in the entity string. Evidence: inter-annotator agreement was lowest for Q4 (κ=0.579) and improved with coarsening, especially for Q2 (Section 5.1).

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-Label vs Multi-Class Classification:** Why needed: Q1 (Categories) is inherently multi-label (e.g., "Board of the Sports Club" is both administrative and sports-related). Forcing single-label would miss key sociological facets. Quick check: Why would labeling it only as "Sports/Physical activity" be insufficient for analyzing social integration? *Answer:* It misses the administrative leadership aspect, which may correlate with different social networks and responsibilities.

- **Inter-Annotator Agreement Metrics (Cohen’s Kappa):** Why needed: Establishes the human consensus ceiling for LLM performance and identifies which dimensions are objectively codable. Quick check: If two annotators have κ=0.6 on Q4 (Physical Intensity), what does that imply? *Answer:* The dimension is fundamentally ambiguous for these historical entities, so we should temper expectations for LLM accuracy and incorporate uncertainty in downstream analyses.

- **Statistical Voting Ensembles:** Why needed: Majority voting across multiple LLM prompts improves robustness by averaging out individual prompt errors, assuming errors are not perfectly correlated. Quick check: If Prompt A and Prompt B are highly correlated in missing "Lotta Svärd" classifications, would a 2-prompt vote help? *Answer:* No; correlated errors mean the same mistakes persist, so voting provides little gain on those entities.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input (entity string + optional hierarchy) -> Prompt Bank (7 templates) -> LLM Inference Engine (Mistral-Large-Instruct via vLLM) -> Error Handler (JSON validation, retries) -> Voting Module (majority per question) -> Output (annotated dataset)

**Critical path:** Entity Extraction (prior work) -> Hierarchy Extraction (LLM-based) -> Prompt Generation (optimized) -> 7-way Parallel Inference -> JSON Parsing & Validation -> Majority Voting -> Dataset Assembly

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Granularity vs Accuracy: Fine-grained labels capture nuance but reduce LLM accuracy and human agreement; coarse labels boost reliability at analytical cost.
- Single vs Multi-Question Prompt: Holistic prompt (all questions together) used for simplicity; separate prompting offered no clear advantage for Mistral.
- Prompt Diversity vs Cost: 7 prompts yield ~1.2 pp gain over single prompt; 7× inference cost must be weighed against marginal reliability improvement for large-scale runs.

**Failure signatures:**
- High Model-Human Disagreement Entities: Culturally specific abbreviations (e.g., "2.KKK/KKR"), rare folk dances, OCR errors, or entities where humans also disagree.
- Dimension-Specific Weakness: Systematic underperformance on Q4 (Physical Intensity), especially for "Creative/Artistic" and "General social group" categories.
- Overconfidence vs Uncertainty: LLM tends to guess a label (e.g., "Large group") where humans correctly select "Cannot be determined" (e.g., "Seniors’ circle").

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Run the original single prompt (Appendix A.1) on the 150-entity test set; verify ~72.7% F-score (full schema).
2. Voting implementation: Run the 7-prompt ensemble on the test set; measure per-dimension F-score expected: ~73.7% full, ~76.8% coarse.
3. Error pattern analysis: Compute model-human disagreement rates by Q1 category; replicate the drop for "Creative/Artistic" on Q4; manually inspect top 10 high-disagreement entities for cultural blind spots.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Optimizing LLMs for culturally-specific entities:** How can LLMs reduce systematic errors like uncertainty avoidance and improve classification of culturally-specific historical terms? *Basis:* Error analysis shows failures on Finnish-specific items. *Why unresolved:* General-purpose LLMs have knowledge gaps. *Resolving evidence:* Few-shot prompting with cultural examples, integration of historical knowledge bases, or domain-adapted fine-tuning.
- **Generalizability of the framework:** To what extent is the four-dimensional schema transferable to other historical or cultural contexts? *Basis:* Framework tailored to Karelian refugees. *Why unresolved:* Validity and necessary adaptations for different social structures remain untested. *Resolving evidence:* Application to diverse archives, measuring inter-annotator agreement and LLM performance, iterative schema refinement.
- **Downstream utility of multi-label categories:** Does multi-label classification for activity categories yield tangible improvements in social integration research vs. single-label? *Basis:* 29.2% of entities had multiple Q1 labels, but no direct comparison. *Why unresolved:* Benefit is assumed, not empirically demonstrated. *Resolving evidence:* Parallel analyses using multi- vs. single-label datasets comparing effect sizes in health outcome models.
- **Incorporating uncertainty into sociological models:** How should LLM classification uncertainty be quantitatively integrated to ensure robust estimates? *Basis:* Study produces labels but does not address uncertainty propagation. *Why unresolved:* Downstream use may ignore reliability differences across entities/dimensions. *Resolving evidence:* Develop reliability scores, simulate datasets with known error distributions, test outcome model robustness.

## Limitations
- **Missing optimized prompts:** Exact text of the seven prompts used in the ensemble is undisclosed, blocking precise replication of the 73.7% full-schema result.
- **Missing test set:** The 150-entity gold standard with 4-annotator consensus labels is not public, preventing direct evaluation of claims.
- **Unclear hierarchy preprocessing:** Details of the LLM step that reduced 64,200 to 52,982 organizations (model, prompt) are vague, affecting input consistency.
- **Systematic errors persist:** LLMs show weaknesses on ambiguous and culturally-specific entities, particularly for physical activity intensity (Q4).

## Confidence
- **Framework validity:** High (grounded in prior health research, supported by inter-annotator agreement metrics).
- **Ensemble efficacy:** Medium (modest ~1.2 pp gain; error independence across prompts assumed but not fully verified).
- **Coarsening benefit:** High (3.1 pp improvement strongly evidenced, aligns with lower human agreement on fine-grained dimensions).
- **Overall pipeline replicability:** Medium (performance claims rely on undisclosed optimized prompts and test set).

## Next Checks
1. **Baseline replication:** Use the original prompt (Appendix A.1) on any obtainable subset of entities to confirm F-score ≥72% (full schema), validating model/prompt parity.
2. **Ensemble approximation:** Create 3–5 simple prompt variants (e.g., reordering questions, synonym swaps) and measure majority-vote gain; if gain is <1 pp, the reported 7-prompt improvement may be prompt-specific.
3. **Error pattern audit:** Manually inspect 50 high-disagreement entities (where model ≠ human majority) to verify systematic failures on Finnish-specific terms/abbreviations and overconfidence on ambiguous Q4 labels.
