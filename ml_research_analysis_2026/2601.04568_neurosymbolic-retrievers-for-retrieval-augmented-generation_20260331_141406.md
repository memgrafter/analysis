# Neurosymbolic Retrievers for Retrieval-augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04568
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04568
- **Reference Count:** 18
- **Primary Result:** KG-Path RAG achieved 84.7% accuracy in depression detection; Proknow-RAG reached 89.1% accuracy in suicide risk detection, with both enhancing interpretability and clinical relevance.

## Executive Summary
Traditional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems often lack transparency in reasoning, a critical drawback for high-stakes domains like mental health. This paper introduces Neurosymbolic RAG, integrating symbolic knowledge graphs with neural retrieval to improve both performance and interpretability. Three methods are proposed: MAR modulates query embeddings with interpretable symbolic features; KG-Path RAG uses graph traversal and joint optimization for path-aware retrieval; Proknow-RAG reorders evidence using clinical questionnaires to enforce procedural workflows. Evaluated on the IMHI dataset for mental health risk assessment, KG-Path RAG and Proknow-RAG demonstrate strong accuracy (84.7% and 89.1% respectively) and superior interpretability compared to neural baselines, with Proknow-RAG additionally showing high procedural correctness and computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The paper presents three neurosymbolic RAG approaches tailored for mental health risk assessment:

1. **MAR (Modulated Attention Retriever):** Accumulates symbolic features φ(t) from clinical knowledge graphs across conversation turns. These features modulate query/document embeddings via a learned transformation: e' = e + α·W·φ, where α dynamically scales modulation based on feature complexity (e.g., symptom count, graph connectivity, risk scores). This aims to make retrieval decisions interpretable by tying them to specific clinical concepts.

2. **KG-Path RAG:** Maps queries to knowledge graph nodes, performs BFS to gather 1-2 hop neighbors, and enriches the query using a language model (e.g., T5-3B) that conditions on the original query and extracted KG concepts. Retrieval scores combine neural similarity (cosine similarity of bi-encoder embeddings) and graph-based importance (PageRank). The system jointly optimizes retrieval loss and a graph ranking loss (L = L_retrieval + β·L_graph-rank, β=0.40) with graph-guided negative sampling, ensuring that retrieved documents are not only relevant but also supported by explicit KG paths.

3. **Proknow-RAG:** Builds upon KG-Path RAG by reordering the retrieved passages according to the sequential structure of clinical assessment instruments (e.g., PHQ-9, GAD-7). This procedural reordering ensures that evidence is presented in the order clinicians expect, enhancing workflow alignment and interpretability.

All methods rely on a clinical knowledge graph encoding symptoms, conditions, and questionnaire relationships, and operate on multi-turn conversational inputs from the IMHI dataset.

## Key Results
- **KG-Path RAG** achieved **84.7% accuracy** in depression detection, significantly outperforming neural baselines.
- **Proknow-RAG** achieved **89.1% accuracy** in suicide risk detection, excelling in procedural correctness and computational efficiency.
- Both methods improved interpretability and clinical relevance as evaluated through human assessment, demonstrating the value of neurosymbolic integration in high-stakes domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Neurosymbolic RAG works by leveraging structured symbolic knowledge (knowledge graphs and clinical instruments) to guide neural retrieval, addressing the opacity of purely neural systems. The mechanisms are:

- **Symbolic grounding:** Queries and documents are linked to explicit clinical concepts (e.g., symptoms, disorders) via a knowledge graph, enabling traceable reasoning paths (KG-Path RAG) or interpretable modulation (MAR).
- **Joint optimization:** KG-Path RAG trains the retriever with a graph-based loss, aligning embedding similarity with graph connectivity. This ensures retrieved documents lie on plausible paths within the KG, making the retrieval process more explainable.
- **Procedural alignment:** Proknow-RAG enforces the order of evidence presentation based on established clinical questionnaires, mirroring expert workflows and reducing cognitive load for clinicians.

The integration of symbolic constraints not only boosts accuracy by focusing on relevant, high-quality evidence but also provides transparency: users can inspect the KG paths or the symbolic features that influenced retrieval, building trust in high-stakes applications.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graphs (KGs):** Structured representations of clinical entities (symptoms, disorders) and their relationships.  
  *Why needed:* Provide symbolic backbone for mapping free text to interpretable concepts and enabling path-based reasoning.  
  *Quick check:* Ensure KG covers key mental health concepts (e.g., depression, anxiety) and relationships (e.g., "symptom X is part of disorder Y").

- **Embedding Modulation:** Adjusting vector representations by adding symbolic feature contributions.  
  *Why needed:* Allows neural retrievers to incorporate discrete, human-understandable features without sacrificing end-to-end learning.  
  *Quick check:* Verify that modulated embeddings for similar clinical cases converge (e.g., queries with similar symptom sets have closer vectors).

- **Joint Retrieval-Graph Optimization:** Simultaneously training neural retrieval and graph-based ranking objectives.  
  *Why needed:* Prevents the retriever from drifting from graph structure, maintaining coherence between neural and symbolic spaces.  
  *Quick check:* Monitor retrieval loss and graph ranking loss during training; both should decrease together.

- **Procedural Workflows:** Predefined sequences (e.g., questionnaire items) that structure evidence presentation.  
  *Why needed:* Aligns system output with domain expert practices, improving usability and safety.  
  *Quick check:* Reorder retrieved passages for a sample case; confirm the order matches standard clinical protocols (e.g., PHQ-9 item order).

- **Graph Traversal (BFS):** Exploring neighbor nodes up to a fixed hop count.  
  *Why needed:* Efficiently expands query context with related concepts while controlling complexity.  
  *Quick check:* For a given query, run BFS and manually assess if 1-2 hop neighbors are clinically relevant.

- **Bi-encoder Retrieval:** Separate encoders for queries and documents to compute similarity.  
  *Why needed:* Enables fast approximate nearest neighbor search (e.g., with FAISS) at scale.  
  *Quick check:* Encode a set of known relevant documents and verify they rank highly for related queries.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** User Query -> Symbolic Feature Extraction (φ) -> KG Traversal (BFS) -> Query Enrichment (LM) -> Bi-encoder Retrieval -> Scoring (Cosine + PageRank) -> [for KG-Path RAG] -> Joint Optimization (L_retrieval + L_graph-rank) -> Top-k Retrieval. For Proknow-RAG: KG-Path RAG output -> Procedural Reordering (Questionnaire mapping) -> Final ranked list.

- **Critical Path:**  
  1. Map query to KG nodes (feature extraction).  
  2. Perform BFS to gather relevant KG concepts (1-2 hops).  
  3. Enrich query with LM using original query + KG concepts.  
  4. Compute retrieval scores (neural + graph).  
  5. Apply joint optimization during training.  
  6. For Proknow-RAG, reorder results by questionnaire sequence.

- **Design Tradeoffs:**  
  - **Graph depth vs. noise:** Deeper BFS yields more context but risks irrelevant concepts. The paper limits to 1-2 hops to balance relevance and precision.  
  - **Weighting (α, β):** Tradeoff between neural similarity and graph-based ranking; β=0.40 was chosen empirically. Over-reliance on graph may degrade retrieval if KG is incomplete.  
  - **Procedural rigidity vs. flexibility:** Fixed questionnaire order ensures consistency but may miss contextually important out-of-order evidence.

- **Failure Signatures:**  
  - **Irrelevant KG neighbors:** BFS returns semantically unrelated concepts → impoverished enrichment. Detect by inspecting neighbor precision.  
  - **Mis-modulated embeddings:** α values not correlating with clinical complexity or φ extraction errors → biased retrieval. Detect by visualizing embedding clusters.  
  - **Reordering mismatch:** Proknow-RAG produces sequences that violate clinical protocols → low procedural correctness scores.

- **First 3 Experiments:**  
  1. **KG Validation:** Load the clinical KG (if available) and run BFS on sample queries; manually verify neighbor relevance.  
  2. **Baseline KG-Path RAG:** Implement the full pipeline (without joint optimization first) on a small subset of IMHI; measure retrieval accuracy vs. a neural baseline.  
  3. **Proknow-RAG Reordering:** Take outputs from experiment 2 and apply questionnaire-based sorting; evaluate order correctness with a clinician on 10 cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can symbolic feature accumulation in retriever embeddings provide transparent, justifiable document selection that aligns with expert reasoning in multi-turn clinical conversations?
- **Basis in Paper:** Explicitly stated as RQ1. The paper describes MAR's modulation networks using accumulated features φ(t) from a clinical KG.
- **Why Unresolved:** The paper lacks empirical validation of transparency from clinicians. Interpretability is claimed but not quantitatively assessed with domain experts.
- **Resolution Evidence:** Controlled user studies with licensed clinicians assessing comprehensibility and clinical validity of modulated embeddings and justifications, compared to baselines. Ablation correlating modulation strength αₜ with perceived complexity.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does jointly optimizing knowledge graph traversal and neural retrieval (KG-Path RAG) yield more traceable and semantically coherent retrieval paths than sequential query enrichment, without prohibitive computational cost?
- **Basis in Paper:** Explicitly stated as RQ2. KG-Path RAG uses joint optimization via a graph-based ranking loss to make paths explicit.
- **Why Unresolved:** The contribution of joint optimization vs. KG quality is not disentangled. No human evaluation of path traceability (e.g., time to verify connections).
- **Resolution Evidence:** Comparative human evaluation of path traceability between KG-Path RAG and baselines using metrics like verification time or agreement on path relevance. Analysis of computational overhead across graph scales.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can procedural knowledge infusion (Proknow-RAG) generalize beyond mental health to enforce valid workflows in other high-stakes domains (e.g., legal reasoning, scientific peer review) where sequential protocols are critical?
- **Basis in Paper:** Explicitly stated as RQ3. Proknow-RAG reorders evidence using clinical questionnaires.
- **Why Unresolved:** Evaluation limited to mental health tasks from IMHI; not tested in other domains with different procedural structures.
- **Resolution Evidence:** Case studies applying Proknow-RAG to non-mental-health domains (e.g., legal statute elements) with metrics on adherence to procedural templates and expert workflow alignment.

### Open Question 4 (inferred)
- **Question:** How does the transparency afforded by these neurosymbolic methods impact user trust and decision-making in real-world, high-stakes settings compared to state-of-the-art black-box RAG systems?
- **Basis in Paper:** Inferred from motivation emphasizing trust erosion; paper calls for clinician-centered evaluation of appropriateness, consistency, and safety, but current work lacks trust measures.
- **Why Unresolved:** All evaluations are offline accuracy plus limited human study on interpretability/clincial relevance; no measurement of trust or behavior change.
- **Resolution Evidence:** Longitudinal or simulated decision-support studies with clinicians comparing trust surveys, time to decision, and error rates in treatment recommendations between neurosymbolic and neural RAG.

### Open Question 5 (inferred)
- **Question:** What are the scalability and maintenance implications of deploying neurosymbolic RAG systems that depend on curated knowledge graphs and clinical instruments in dynamic, real-world environments?
- **Basis in Paper:** Inferred; methods assume static, high-quality KGs and validated questionnaires, but no discussion of operational challenges like knowledge drift.
- **Why Unresolved:** Experiments use fixed dataset and KGs; unknown how systems perform with updated medical evidence or incomplete symbolic structures.
- **Resolution Evidence:** Stress testing on temporally split datasets (e.g., newer studies not in original KG) and perturbation analysis (adding noise, removing edges) to estimate degradation and expert maintenance labor.

## Limitations
- **Missing KG details:** The exact structure, node/edge types, and weights of the clinical knowledge graph are not provided, blocking faithful implementation of traversal, PageRank, and complexity computation.
- **Underspecified feature extraction:** The mechanism for automatically extracting symbolic features φ(t) from raw conversational text is not described, making MAR's modulation irreproducible without assumptions.
- **Scalability and maintenance unknowns:** The paper does not address how the system handles knowledge evolution, incomplete graphs, or cost of expert curation in real-world deployment.
