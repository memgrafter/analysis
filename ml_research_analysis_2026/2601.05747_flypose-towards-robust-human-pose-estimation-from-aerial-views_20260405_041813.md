# FlyPose: Towards Robust Human Pose Estimation From Aerial Views

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05747
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05747
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A lightweight top-down aerial pose system improving detection by 6.8 mAP and pose by 16.3 mAP, running at ~20ms on Jetson Orin AGX, with successful quadrotor deployment.

## Executive Summary
FlyPose tackles the challenge of real-time human pose estimation from UAVs, where low resolution, steep viewing angles, and occlusion degrade performance. The system uses a top-down pipeline: a lightweight RT-DETRv2-S detector finds people, and a fine-tuned ViTPose-S estimates 17 COCO keypoints. Central to its success is multi-dataset training across diverse aerial datasets (including a new FlyPose-104 set) to build robustness to scale, viewpoint, and sensor modality (RGB/thermal). The pipeline runs efficiently on embedded hardware (Jetson Orin) and was flight-tested onboard a quadrotor, demonstrating practical viability for aerial robotics applications.

## Method Summary
FlyPose is a two-stage top-down pipeline. First, RT-DETRv2-S with a ResNet-18 backbone detects persons in full-HD frames (1280px input). The detector is trained incrementally: fine-tuned on VisDrone, then on a combined set of nine aerial detection datasets (including thermal), and finally augmented with COCO-Person data while switching to Normalized Wasserstein Distance (NWD) loss to improve small-object robustness. Second, cropped person patches are resized to 256x192 and fed to ViTPose-S, which is fine-tuned on UAV-Human v1 with downscaling augmentation (5–20%) to simulate small-scale persons. Both models are optimized via TensorRT (FP32) for the Jetson Orin AGX, achieving a total latency of ~20ms per frame for single-person scenes.

## Key Results
- **Detection:** Average 6.8 mAP improvement across Manipal-UAV, VisDrone, HIT-UAV, and FlyPose-104 test sets.
- **Pose:** 16.3 mAP improvement in 2D pose estimation on the UAV-Human dataset.
- **Latency:** ~20 ms end-to-end inference on a Jetson Orin AGX Developer Kit.
- **Deployment:** Successfully integrated and flown onboard a quadrotor UAV.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multi-dataset training with aerial and frontal-view imagery yields a person detector robust to varying scales, viewpoints, and modalities (RGB/thermal).
- **Mechanism:** The detector (RT-DETRv2-S) is exposed to a diverse distribution of person appearances during training, forcing it to learn features invariant to these variations rather than overfitting to any single dataset's characteristics.
- **Core assumption:** A model trained on a sufficiently diverse mixture of aerial datasets will generalize better to unseen aerial scenes than a model trained on any single dataset or a mix that excludes frontal data.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper reports an average 6.8 mAP improvement across four aerial test sets after multi-dataset training (Section 4.1, Table 1). Progressive mAP gains are shown after each training stage, with the highest weighted average (28.21) after combining all aerial data before a minor tweak with COCO reintroduction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning a heavyweight pose estimator (ViTPose-H) on aerial data, then distilling to a smaller variant (ViTPose-S), retains accuracy gains while meeting real-time constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The large model (ViTPose-H) learns the complex mapping from aerial patches to keypoints. Its knowledge is transferred to the smaller ViTPose-S via fine-tuning, which directly benefits from aerial-specific training even without explicit distillation.
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge of aerial pose distributions can be effectively encoded in a smaller architecture through fine-tuning, without requiring a fundamentally new architecture.
- **Evidence anchors:** ViTPose-H achieves the highest aerial mAP (73.18) but is too slow for Jetson. ViTPose-S, after fine-tuning on UAV-Human, achieves 65.76 mAP with 6.54ms Jetson latency, making it the deployed choice (Section 4.2, Table 2).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A top-down pipeline (detect-then-estimate) is computationally feasible for onboard deployment because it concentrates computation on person regions and allows independent optimization of detection and pose modules.
- **Mechanism:** Decoupling person localization from keypoint estimation lets each module be optimized separately for its task and hardware constraints. The pose estimator processes only cropped patches, reducing background noise and enabling use of highly optimized single-purpose engines (TensorRT).
- **Core assumption:** The computational cost scales linearly with the number of detected persons and is dominated by the per-person pose cost, which is acceptable for typical drone encounter rates.
- **Evidence anchors:** The pipeline runs at ~20ms total latency (13ms detection + 6.54ms pose for one person) on Jetson Orin (Section 4.3). The authors note latency increases with more persons but anticipate batching can offset costs.

## Foundational Learning
### Top-down vs. Bottom-up Pose Estimation
- **Why needed here:** The entire FlyPose pipeline is top-down. Understanding this paradigm is crucial to grasp why detection is a separate, critical first stage, how errors propagate, and why scaling behavior with people count matters in aerial scenes.
- **Quick check question:** What is the primary computational and accuracy advantage of a top-down pipeline in an aerial context with often few, small persons?
### Multi-Dataset Training & Domain Shift
- **Why needed here:** The core improvement comes from training on multiple, heterogeneous aerial datasets. An engineer must understand the challenges of merging datasets (different sensors, altitudes, annotation styles) and how it combats overfitting to a narrow "aerial" domain while potentially harming frontal-view performance.
- **Quick check question:** Why does adding the COCO-Person dataset *after* aerial training improve Average Recall (AR) but slightly decrease average mAP on aerial test sets?
### Embedded Deployment Constraints (Jetson Orin, TensorRT)
- **Why needed here:** The research is driven by real-time onboard constraints. An engineer must understand the trade-offs between model complexity (parameters, FLOPs) and measurable on-hardware latency, including engine conversion (TensorRT) and precision (FP32).
- **Quick check question:** Why is the reported inference latency for ViTPose-S on the Jetson (6.54ms) dramatically lower than on the A6000 GPU (110.23ms), and what does this imply for model selection?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** RTSP Frame → Preprocess (CUDA padding/rescaling) → RT-DETRv2-S inference → Crop person patches → Preprocess patches (resize to 256x192) → ViTPose-S inference → NMS on heatmaps → Keypoint output.

**Critical path:** The sequential flow from raw video frames to final keypoints, where detection must complete before pose estimation can begin on each crop.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Accuracy vs. Latency:** ViTPose model size (S/B/L/H) directly trades mAP for Jetson latency; ViTPose-S was chosen as the Pareto frontier for <7ms pose inference.
- **Detection Recall vs. False Positives:** Lowering detection confidence increases recall but raises false positives and total latency (more pose inferences). Cluttered backgrounds cause false positives.
- **Dataset Diversity vs. Frontal Performance:** Multi-dataset training improves aerial robustness but hurts COCO validation performance; reintroducing COCO mitigates this at a slight cost to some aerial metrics.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Detection Failure:** Small, distant persons (<~20px height) are missed; background clutter causes false positives.
- **Pose Failure:** Facial keypoints (nose, eyes, ears) have consistently lower OKS; strong camouflage and multi-person proximity cause keypoint confusion; thermal imagery shows more left-right identity swaps.
- **System Failure:** One-time 300ms RTSP stream initialization delay; total latency scales linearly with number of detected persons if not batched.

**First experiments:**
1. **Preliminary Benchmarking:** Evaluate off-the-shelf detection (RT-DETRv2 variants) and pose models (ViTPose, RTMPose, AlphaPose) on VisDrone (detection) and UAV-Human (pose) to establish baseline accuracy-latency curves.
2. **Incremental Multi-Dataset Training:** Iteratively train the detector: (a) on VisDrone only, (b) on combined 9 aerial datasets, (c) add COCO-Person back in. Measure mAP/AR on all held-out test sets after each step to assess generalization and catastrophic forgetting.
3. **Pose Model Selection & Fine-tuning:** Finetune ViTPose-S/B/L/H on UAV-Human train-set. Measure mAP on UAV-Human test and absolute latency on Jetson Orin (TensorRT FP32). Select the smallest model meeting a minimum accuracy threshold (~65+ mAP) for deployment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Synthetic 3D Data:** Does adding synthetic 3D pose projections improve aerial pose accuracy? No experiments validate this; synthetic projections may not capture aerial-specific occlusions and scales.
2. **Flight Metadata:** Can flight altitude and camera metadata enhance keypoint accuracy for small-scale persons? Current models ignore these priors; integration method and real-time impact unknown.
3. **Batching Trade-offs:** What are the accuracy and latency trade-offs when batching multiple person detections in top-down aerial pose estimation? Single-person latency reported; multi-person batching may affect throughput and memory usage on edge devices.
4. **Thermal Robustness:** How can pose estimation be made robust to thermal imagery challenges like left-right keypoint swaps? Thermal modality lacks symmetry cues; current RGB-trained model generalizes poorly.

## Limitations
- Training and deployment details (hyperparameters, TensorRT flags, preprocessing) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Multi-person latency scaling is not characterized; batching is needed for crowded scenes but trade-offs are unmeasured.
- Small-person detection (<20px height) remains weak, limiting high-altitude operation.
- Thermal generalization depends on training mix; performance on unseen thermal datasets is uncertain.
- The new FlyPose-104 dataset's isolated impact is unknown; it may not add unique value beyond existing aerial data.

## Confidence
- **Multi-dataset detection robustness:** High (consistent mAP gains across 4 aerial test sets).
- **ViTPose-S pose accuracy & latency:** High (6.5ms Jetson latency, 65.8 mAP on UAV-Human).
- **Top-down onboard deployment:** Medium (single-person latency OK; multi-person scalability unproven).
- **RGB/thermal generalization:** Medium (tested on one thermal dataset only; swap issues noted).
- **FlyPose-104 dataset value:** Low (new dataset; no ablation study isolates its contribution).

## Next Checks
1. Profile end-to-end latency for 1–20 persons on Jetson Orin with/without TensorRT batching to quantify scaling and identify optimal batch size.
2. Ablate FlyPose-104: retrain detector without it (other data fixed) and compute mAP differences on the four test sets to measure its marginal utility.
3. Conduct flight tests at 10–30m altitude; evaluate detection recall and pose accuracy for tiny persons; test super-resolution preprocessing to potentially recover small-scale detail.
