# Conformal Prediction for Risk-Controlled Medical Entity Extraction Across Clinical Domains

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.00924  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00924  
- **Reference count**: 23  
- **Primary result**: A conformal prediction framework provides finite-sample false discovery rate (FDR) guarantees for LLM-based medical entity extraction, revealing a reversal in model miscalibration across domains (underconfident on FDA drug labels, overconfident on radiology reports) and achieving ≥90% coverage with 9–13% rejection.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the unsafe deployment of large language models (LLMs) in clinical entity extraction due to poorly calibrated confidence scores. It introduces a conformal prediction framework that offers finite-sample false discovery rate (FDR) guarantees, adapting automatically to a model's miscalibration without relying on well-calibrated probabilities. Applied to FDA drug labels and MIMIC-CXR radiology reports, the study uncovers a critical domain-dependent reversal: models are underconfident on structured drug labels but overconfident on free-text radiology reports. Despite this heterogeneity, the method achieves the target coverage (≥90%) with manageable rejection rates (9–13%). Further analysis shows that per-category thresholds expose substantial hidden variation, and model-specific thresholds reveal that FDR control depends more on confidence discriminability than on raw extraction accuracy.

## Method Summary
The pipeline proceeds as follows: First, an LLM (e.g., GPT-4.1, Llama-4-Maverick) extracts entity spans from clinical text. For each span, a confidence score \(\hat{p}_e\) is computed as the geometric mean of per-token softmax probabilities. A verification module labels each extracted entity as correct or incorrect: for FDA labels, an LLM-as-judge (GPT-5-mini) uses FactScore (score=3 threshold); for radiology, exact span-and-type match against physician annotations is used. Nonconformity scores are defined as \(s_e = \text{logit}(\hat{p}_e)\). The verified entities are split into calibration and test sets. For each domain and entity category (e.g., FDA section, RadGraph type), a threshold \(\tau\) is chosen on the calibration set as the smallest score where the empirical FDR does not exceed a predefined \(\alpha\) (Equation 3). At inference, entities with \(s_e \ge \tau\) are accepted; others are rejected. This guarantees that, under exchangeability, the expected FDR among accepted entities is \(\le \alpha\).

## Key Results
- **Miscalibration reversal**: Models are underconfident on structured FDA drug labels (predicted confidence < empirical accuracy) but overconfident on free-text radiology reports (predicted confidence > accuracy).
- **FDR control with moderate rejection**: Target coverage (≥90%) is achieved with rejection rates of 9–13% overall; per-model rates vary widely (e.g., Llama-4-Maverick rejects 19.6% vs. GPT-4.1 rejects 59.3% at \(\alpha=0.10\)).
- **Per-category thresholds reveal hidden risk**: Global thresholds mask significant variation; for instance, the FDA “Drug Interactions” section requires a threshold that rejects 59.8% of entities, while “Adverse Reactions” rejects 0%, and in RadGraph, some categories (OBS-U) are fully rejected while others are nearly fully accepted.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **FDR-controlling conformal prediction works by setting thresholds from empirical nonconformity scores on a calibration set, automatically adapting to miscalibration**. The threshold \(\tau\) is chosen so that the empirical FDR on the calibration set does not exceed \(\alpha\). Entities with score \(s_e \ge \tau\) are accepted. This guarantees that, under exchangeability, the expected FDR among accepted entities is \(\le \alpha\), regardless of the underlying confidence distribution, as long as the nonconformity score meaningfully ranks entities by error risk (higher score → lower risk). The guarantee holds without assuming well-calibrated probabilities. If scores fail to discriminate between correct and incorrect entities, the threshold may force near-universal rejection or acceptance, breaking the utility.

2. **The direction of miscalibration reverses across clinical domains due to document structure and extraction difficulty**. On structured, formulaic documents (FDA drug labels), model token probabilities are conservative, leading to underconfidence. On free-text, variable-format documents (radiology reports), the model assigns high confidence to linguistically plausible but semantically incorrect extractions, causing overconfidence. The conformal threshold \(\tau\) adjusts to the baseline error rate: underconfident models require permissive thresholds (low \(\tau\)), while overconfident models require strict thresholds (high \(\tau\)). This reversal is attributed to systematic differences in how document structure influences the correlation between internal probabilities and factual correctness.

3. **Per-category thresholds expose heterogeneity masked by global thresholds**. Error rates and confidence discriminability vary across extraction categories within a domain (e.g., FDA sections, RadGraph entity types). Computing a separate threshold \(\tau_c\) per category adapts locally to these differences. A global threshold averages across categories, risking acceptance of high-risk entities from poor-performing categories while rejecting low-risk ones from high-performing categories. This granularity is only beneficial if categories exhibit meaningful variation and if the calibration set contains sufficient samples per category to estimate reliable thresholds.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: LLM Extraction -> Confidence Aggregator (geometric mean of token probabilities) -> Verification Module (for calibration set) -> Conformal Calibrator (threshold per domain/category) -> Inference Accept/Reject.

**Critical path**: Extraction produces spans and token log-probs → Confidence Aggregator computes per-span confidence → Verification provides correctness labels for calibration entities → Conformal Calibrator computes nonconformity scores and selects thresholds on calibration split → Thresholds applied to test entities for acceptance decision.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Global vs. per-category thresholds: Global thresholds require less calibration data but can mask high-risk categories; per-category thresholds offer finer risk control but need sufficient samples per category.  
- Verification ground truth: LLM-as-judge scales to unlabeled data but introduces judge bias; exact-match is objective but limited to annotated datasets.  
- Confidence aggregation: Geometric mean is sensitive to low-probability tokens (may better indicate errors) but could over-penalize minor token variations.

**Failure signatures**  
- Excessive rejection at all \(\alpha\) levels indicates confidence scores lack discriminative power between correct/incorrect entities.
- Wide gap between models’ rejection rates at the same \(\alpha\) suggests differences in confidence discriminability.
- Per-category thresholds varying by orders of magnitude signal strong category-specific error profiles; a single threshold would be dangerously inappropriate.
- Calibration curves far from the diagonal indicate severe miscalibration that conformal prediction must compensate for via extreme thresholds.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Run baseline extraction on domain-specific test sets and compute standard metrics (F1, precision, recall) against available ground truth.  
2. For each entity category, bin entities by predicted confidence, compute empirical accuracy per bin, plot calibration curves, and calculate ECE to identify under/overconfidence patterns.  
3. Split verified data into calibration/test sets. For a range of \(\alpha\) values, compute global and per-category thresholds on the calibration set; apply to the test set and report rejection rate, coverage, and achieved precision.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Surrogate confidence for black-box LLMs**: What methods (e.g., confidence prompts, output entropy) can replace token log-probabilities to enable conformal prediction for models like GPT-4 that do not expose logits?
- **Comparison with post-hoc calibration**: How does conformal prediction’s FDR guarantee compare practically to temperature scaling or Platt scaling for medical entity extraction?
- **Clinical workflow impact**: How does conformal filtering affect clinician review burden and error propagation in real-world medical entity extraction tasks?
- **Temporal distribution shift**: Can adaptive conformal methods (e.g., sliding-window calibration) maintain FDR guarantees as medical language evolves over time?

## Limitations
- **Reproduction blockers**: Unreported details include exact model versions/API endpoints for GPT-4.1 and Llama-4-Maverick (log-prob extraction), random seeds for data splits, FDA label sampling criteria, and RadGraph prompt templates.
- **Verification bias**: Using GPT-5-mini as a judge for FDA entities may introduce systematic errors; exact-match for RadGraph is objective but limited to annotated subsets.
- **Per-category threshold instability**: Small category sample sizes (e.g., RadGraph OBS-U) can yield unreliable threshold estimates, limiting the benefit of granular risk control.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (conformal FDR guarantee): **High** – solid theoretical foundation, standard implementation.
- Mechanism 2 (domain reversal): **Medium** – empirically observed in this study but may not generalize beyond FDA and radiology.
- Mechanism 3 (per-category thresholds): **Medium** – data-dependent; gains depend on category heterogeneity and sample size.
