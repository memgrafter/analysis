# Model Merging via Data-Free Covariance Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01329
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01329
- **Reference count**: 26
- **Primary result**: ACTMat achieves fully data-free model merging with average accuracy gains of +3.1% on T5-Base and +5.3% on T5-Large over the previous best data-free method.

## Executive Summary
Principled model merging methods like RegMean require access to training samples to estimate per-layer activation covariances, which is often infeasible for public expert models. This paper introduces ACTMat, a method that approximates these covariances directly from difference matrices (task vectors) under conditions of small cross-term error, uncorrelated activations and gradients, and covariance stationarity. When combined with RegMean’s interference minimization framework, ACTMat yields a fully data-free merging rule. Evaluated across vision (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14) and language (T5-Base, T5-Large, OLMo-3-7B) models, ACTMat consistently outperforms prior data-free state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant accuracy improvements (+3.1% and +5.3% on T5-Base/Large) and also surpassing baselines on reasoning tasks.

## Method Summary
ACTMat operates within the task arithmetic framework. For each fine-tuned model, the task vector \(\Delta_t = W_t - W_0\) is computed for every linear layer. Theoretically, under full-batch gradient descent with negligible cross-term, correlation, and drift errors, the activation covariance \(C_t\) can be estimated as \(\hat{C}_t = \Delta_t^\top \Delta_t\) up to a common scaling factor. The RegMean merging rule then minimizes activation interference: for each linear layer, merged weights are \(W^* = \sum_t W_t \hat{C}_t \left( \sum_{t'} \hat{C}_{t'} \right)^\dagger\). Non-linear layers (e.g., LayerNorm) are averaged. For attention mechanisms, the Q/K/V projections are treated as separate linear layers, necessitating a custom implementation to capture distinct covariances. The entire process is data-free and avoids the computational overhead of collecting validation data and performing covariance estimation passes.

## Key Results
- ACTMat eliminates data dependency for covariance estimation while matching or exceeding RegMean’s performance.
- On T5-Base and T5-Large across 7 QA datasets, ACTMat achieves average accuracy gains of +3.1% and +5.3% over the best prior data-free method (TSV).
- For OLMo-3-7B fine-tuned via RL on reasoning tasks, ACTMat surpasses both data-free and data-dependent baselines on AIME, HumanEval, and IFEval.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach rests on three theoretical pillars:

1. **Covariance Estimation from Task Vectors**  
   - Claim: The activation covariance of a layer can be approximated from the task vector without data.  
   - Mechanism: Under full-batch gradient descent, the task vector accumulates gradient outer products. Theorem 3.1 shows that if cross-term error, correlation error between activations and gradient norms, and drift error (covariance non-stationarity) are small, then \(\Delta_t^\top \Delta_t\) is proportional to the true covariance \(C_t\).  
   - Core assumption: The three errors are negligible during fine-tuning.  
   - Evidence: Theorem 3.1, Figure 2 (error measurements on ViT-B/16), and empirical outperformance of baselines.  
   - Break condition: If any error becomes large (e.g., correlated gradients from Adam), the approximation degrades.

2. **Scale Invariance of RegMean**  
   - Claim: RegMean’s solution is invariant if all covariances are scaled equally; thus approximations scaled by \(\kappa_t\) with \(\kappa_t \approx 1\) yield identical or near-identical merged weights.  
   - Mechanism: Proposition 3.2 proves that if \(C_t = \kappa_t \hat{C}_t\) with all \(\kappa_t\) equal, the minimizer using \(\hat{C}_t\) equals the true minimizer. Empirically, Figure 3 shows \(\kappa_i/\kappa_j\) ratios cluster around 1.  
   - Core assumption: The scaling factors \(\kappa_t\) are nearly identical across tasks.  
   - Evidence: Proposition 3.2, Figure 3.  
   - Break condition: Wide variation in \(\kappa_t\) leads to divergence from RegMean optimum.

3. **Robustness Bound with Approximate Covariances**  
   - Claim: The layer-wise interference objective yields an upper bound on negative transfer; this bound degrades gracefully with covariance estimation error.  
   - Mechanism: Theorem 3.3 extends prior work, showing expected negative transfer is bounded by layer-wise interference errors plus terms involving \(\|C_t - \hat{C}_t\|_F\). Thus, error in covariance estimation directly limits performance drop.  
   - Core assumption: Lipschitz continuity of layer functions and loss; linear layers use exact closed-form minimizer.  
   - Evidence: Theorem 3.3, Equation (8), and competitive results against RegMean with data.  
   - Break condition: Large covariance errors make bound loose and merged performance may drop sharply.

## Foundational Learning
- **Task Arithmetic and Model Merging**  
  *Why needed*: ACTMat uses task vectors (differences between fine-tuned and pretrained weights) as its starting point. Understanding this basis clarifies why interference occurs in naive merging.  
  *Quick check*: What is a task vector, and why does naive averaging of task vectors cause interference?

- **Interference Minimization (RegMean)**  
  *Why needed*: RegMean formulates merging as minimizing the ℓ₂ activation mismatch per layer, leading to a closed-form solution that requires activation covariances. ACTMat approximates these covariances to avoid data.  
  *Quick check*: How does the RegMean objective differ from simple task arithmetic, and why is it more principled?

- **Full-Batch Gradient Descent and Covariance**  
  *Why needed*: The derivation of ACTMat assumes full-batch updates and links the accumulated gradient matrix to activation covariance. This connection is non-trivial and underpins the Δ⊤Δ approximation.  
  *Quick check*: In full-batch gradient descent, what is the relationship between the weight update and the input covariance matrix?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Task-vector extractor → ACTMat estimator → Merging rule → Parameter combiner.

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Obtain pretrained checkpoint \(W_0\) and fine-tuned checkpoints \(\{W_t\}\).  
  2. For each linear layer, compute task vector \(\Delta_t = W_t - W_0\).  
  3. Estimate covariance proxy \(\hat{C}_t = \Delta_t^\top \Delta_t\).  
  4. Sum across tasks: \(S = \sum_t \hat{C}_t\).  
  5. Compute merged weights: \(W^* = \sum_t W_t \hat{C}_t \, S^\dagger\).  
  6. For non-linear layers (e.g., LayerNorm), average parameters across tasks.  
  7. Repeat for all linear layers, with special handling for attention (split Q/K/V projections).  
  8. Save and evaluate the merged model.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  *Pros*: Fully data-free; no storage or compute for data collection; retains RegMean’s theoretical scale invariance; simple linear algebra operations.  
  *Cons*: Relies on three error terms being small (not guaranteed for all fine-tuning regimes); only applies to linear layers; requires careful implementation for attention; scaling-factor equality (\(\kappa_t\)) is an empirical assumption.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Merged accuracy drops below simple averaging or task arithmetic.  
  - Matrix \(S\) is near-singular, causing unstable pseudoinverse or NaNs.  
  - Large gap between ACTMat and RegMean when data is available.  
  - Layer-wise \(\hat{C}_t\) norms vary widely, indicating scaling factor violation.  
  - High measured cross-term, correlation, or drift errors (e.g., angular distance > 0.5).  
  Diagnosis: compute \(\kappa_t\) distribution, check condition number of \(S\), compare with data-estimated covariances if possible.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Vision merging**: ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14 fine-tuned on 8 image classification datasets; compare average accuracy to baselines.  
  2. **Language merging**: T5-Base and T5-Large fine-tuned on 7 multiple-choice QA datasets; measure accuracy gains over prior data-free methods.  
  3. **Reasoning merging**: OLMo-3-7B RL-fine-tuned on math, code, instruction tasks; evaluate on AIME, HumanEval, IFEval against data-free and data-dependent baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Why do the error terms remain small?**  
  *Basis*: The authors explicitly state that developing a theoretical understanding of why cross-term, correlation, and drift errors are small in practice is future work.  
  *Why unresolved*: Errors are measured empirically (e.g., Figure 2) but no formal bounds link them to training dynamics.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Proved bounds on each error as functions of hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) and data statistics, validated across diverse architectures.

- **When are scaling factors \(\kappa_t\) approximately equal?**  
  *Basis*: ACTMat assumes \(C_t \approx \kappa_t \Delta_t^\top \Delta_t\) and relies on \(\kappa_i/\kappa_j \approx 1\) (Figure 3).  
  *Why unresolved*: No derivation of \(\kappa_t\) from fine-tuning dynamics; its variability across task dissimilarities, optimizers, or model sizes is unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Analytical expression for \(\kappa_t\) and large-scale experiments quantifying its distribution under varied fine-tuning regimes.

- **How to extend ACTMat to non-linear layers?**  
  *Basis*: ACTMat is formulated only for linear layers; non-linear components are averaged heuristically.  
  *Why unresolved*: Modern architectures contain many non-linear operations (e.g., LayerNorm, attention softmax); no covariance-like quantities are defined for them.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Definitions of covariance proxies for non-linear transforms (e.g., via Jacobian expectations), with theoretical error analysis and empirical gains when merging such layers.

## Limitations
- The covariance approximation \((\Delta^\top \Delta \propto C)\) relies on three error terms being small; this may fail with optimizers like Adam (correlated gradients), very small datasets (high covariance drift), or long training runs (non-stationarity).
- ACTMat applies only to linear layers; success depends on handling attention projections correctly, but the paper’s “custom implementation” details are sparse, creating a replication barrier.
- The scale-invariance property (Proposition 3.2) requires all scaling factors \(\kappa_t\) to be equal; this is empirically observed but not guaranteed, especially across heterogeneous tasks.

## Confidence
- Covariance approximation (Mechanism 1): **Medium** (theoretical derivation solid, empirical support on ViT-B/16, but strong assumptions on uncorrelated gradients/activations).
- Scale invariance guarantee (Mechanism 2): **Low** (equality of \(\kappa_t\) is an empirical observation, not a general property).
- Robustness bound (Mechanism 3): **Medium** (formal bound provided, but tightness in practice unknown).
- Overall empirical claims: **Medium** (strong results on reported benchmarks, but limited to ViT, T5, OLMo and specific tasks; unknown generalizability).
