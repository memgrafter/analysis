# MLLM-based Textual Explanations for Face Comparison

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16629
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16629
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can achieve accurate face verification decisions under surveillance/extreme pose but frequently generate explanations based on hallucinated or non-verifiable facial attributes; a likelihood-ratio framework reveals poor evidential separability of these explanations in semantic embedding space.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies the reliability of textual explanations produced by MLLMs like GPT-4o for face verification in challenging conditions (e.g., surveillance, extreme pose). The core finding is that while MLLMs can make correct match/non-match decisions—especially when provided auxiliary face recognition (FR) scores or decisions—their explanatory reasoning often relies on attributes not actually visible in the images (hallucinations). To quantify explanation reliability independent of decision correctness, the authors propose a likelihood-ratio (LR) framework that models the distribution of explanation embeddings under genuine/impostor hypotheses using Gaussian Mixture Models. Evaluated on the IJB-S dataset, the framework shows that incorporating FR information improves categorical verification accuracy (e.g., ~75% genuine, ~98% impostor for GPT-4o) but does not consistently yield more visually faithful explanations. LR analysis confirms that explanation separability remains poor under unconstrained conditions, highlighting a fundamental gap in MLLM-based explainable face recognition.

## Method Summary
The authors design a two-phase framework to evaluate MLLM-generated textual explanations. In the training phase, they use a curated dataset (BUPT-CBFace) with ground-truth labels to generate "grounded" explanations via an MLLM, then encode these explanations using a frozen text embedding model (`text-embedding-3-small`). After dimensionality reduction via PCA (retaining 97% variance), they train two Gaussian Mixture Models (4 components, full covariance) to model the distributions of genuine and impostor explanation embeddings. In the testing phase, they evaluate explanations from MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini) on the IJB-S dataset under different prompting strategies (no-score, score-only, score+decision, where scores come from six FR systems). Each test explanation is embedded, projected via the same PCA, and assigned a likelihood-ratio score `Sexpl = Λ(z)/(1+Λ(z))` based on its likelihood under the two GMMs. This score measures evidential strength: higher values indicate explanations more typical of genuine pairs. They analyze `Sexpl` via ROC-AUC and cluster separability metrics (Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin) to assess whether textual explanations are semantically distinguishable between genuine and impostor hypotheses.

## Key Results
- Providing MLLMs with auxiliary FR similarity scores and/or binary decisions improves categorical verification accuracy on IJB-S, with GPT-4o reaching up to ~75% genuine and ~98% impostor accuracy under the score+decision prompt.
- However, this improvement does not translate into more visually grounded or faithful explanations; MLLMs continue to produce hallucinated or non-verifiable facial attributes.
- The likelihood-ratio framework shows poor separability between genuine and impostor explanation embeddings under unconstrained (surveillance/extreme pose) conditions, with only modest gains when FR decisions are included in prompts, confirming a fundamental reliability gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Incorporating auxiliary face recognition (FR) information (similarity scores and/or binary decisions) into MLLM prompts improves categorical face verification accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The MLLM leverages the numerical or categorical FR outputs as additional context when generating its verification decision, anchoring its reasoning to a model specialized for the visual task rather than solely on its internal linguistic priors.
- **Core assumption:** The FR system's output provides a reliable signal about the pair's identity relationship that the MLLM can utilize to override potential biases from its general-purpose training.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Although such information improves categorical verification performance..." [section V-A] Confusion matrices in Figure 3 show improved genuine/impostor classification for GPT-4o and Gemini when provided with FR scores and decisions. [corpus] Neighbor papers explore MLLM performance but do not provide direct evidence; evidence is primarily from the target paper.
- **Break condition:** This improvement does not consistently translate into more visually grounded or faithful textual explanations, as hallucinations persist.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A likelihood-ratio (LR) framework, which models the distribution of textual explanation embeddings under genuine and impostor hypotheses using Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs), can quantify the evidential strength of an explanation independently of the ground-truth label.
- **Mechanism:** Explanations are converted to embeddings via a frozen text encoder, dimensionality-reduced with PCA, and their likelihood is evaluated under separately trained GMMs for genuine and impostor classes. The likelihood ratio serves as a normalized score of how typical the explanation is for each hypothesis.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic space captured by the text embedding model meaningfully separates class-consistent (visually grounded) explanations from non-class-consistent (hallucinated or generic) ones, and this separation can be modeled probabilistically.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We introduce a likelihood-ratio–based framework that measures the evidential strength of textual explanations." [section III-B] Details the LR calculation: `Sexpl = Λ(z)/(1+Λ(z))` from GMM likelihoods. [section V-D] ROC curves in Figure 6 show varying separability (AUC) across prompting strategies.
- **Break condition:** The framework measures separability in the semantic embedding space, which does not guarantee direct visual grounding; linguistically plausible but incorrect explanations may still score highly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multi-level prompting (varying inclusion of FR scores/decisions) systematically influences the separability of MLLM-generated explanation embeddings in the semantic space, as measured by cluster metrics and LR performance.
- **Mechanism:** Providing more task-specific information (e.g., hard FR decisions) during prompt construction steers the MLLM's explanation generation toward producing outputs that are more class-discriminative when embedded, improving statistical separation between genuine and impostor explanation clusters.
- **Core assumption:** The MLLM's output distribution is conditionally dependent on the provided contextual information (prompt), and this dependency manifests in the semantic structure of the generated text.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III-C] Defines the prompting hierarchy (grounded, no-score, score-only, score+decision). [section V-C] Table I shows improved Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin, and Fisher ratio metrics for prompts with FR decisions vs. no-score. Figure 5 visualizations show clearer separation in t-SNE plots with score+decision. [section V-D] LR-ROC curves (Figure 6) show better performance for score+decision prompts.
- **Break condition:** Improved cluster separability and LR scores do not correlate perfectly with the absence of non-verifiable attributes in the explanations; some top-scoring explanations may still contain hallucinations.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  MLLM API Client -> Text Generator (MLLM) -> Frozen Text Embedding Model (`text-embedding-3-small`) -> PCA Module -> Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs: P0, P1) -> LR Calculator -> Comparison Interface
- **Critical path:**  
  **Training Phase:** Generate grounded explanations on BUPT-CBFace -> Encode with text model -> Apply trained PCA -> Train class-conditional GMMs (4 components, full covariance).  
  **Testing Phase:** Generate prompted explanations on IJB-S -> Encode -> Apply same PCA transform -> Evaluate GMM likelihoods -> Compute LR scores (`Sexpl`) -> Analyze separability/accuracy.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *PCA variance threshold (97%):* Higher dimension retains more signal but risks overfitting GMMs; lower dimension reduces noise but may lose discriminative semantics.
  - *GMM components (4) & covariance (full after PCA):* Full covariance captures correlated features post-PCA but requires more data; diagonal is simpler but less expressive.
  - *Text embedding model:* Choice (e.g., `text-embedding-3-small`) directly affects semantic space geometry; a model tuned for similarity search might be suboptimal for modeling class distributions.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low cluster separation metrics: Silhouette coefficient near 0, high Davies-Bouldin index in Table I, overlapping t-SNE clusters (Figure 5c).
  - Poor LR discriminability: ROC AUC ~0.5 in Figure 6, indicating `P0(z) ≈ P1(z)`.
  - High "uncertain" verdict rate: Especially on genuine pairs with extreme pose (Figure 3, Figure 4), indicating MLLM lack of confidence.
  - Disconnect between decision accuracy and LR score: High categorical accuracy but low LR separability, suggesting correct decisions may stem from luck or FR guidance, not faithful explanation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate GMM training: Use the provided code to generate explanations on the BUPT-CBFace subset with ground-truth prompts, train the PCA and GMMs, and verify separation metrics on the training set (expect high Silhouette ~0.30 from Table I).
  2. Prompting strategy ablation: On a small IJB-S holdout, run the no-score, score-only, and score+decision prompts for a single MLLM. Compare the t-SNE plots of the resulting embeddings and LR scores to reproduce the trend in Figure 5 and Table I.
  3. LR vs. FR score correlation: For the same IJB-S pairs, plot `Sexpl` against the provided FR similarity score. Assess whether higher FR scores correlate with higher `Sexpl` for genuine pairs but not necessarily for impostors, probing if LR captures explanation quality beyond the raw FR signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can MLLM-generated textual explanations be systematically validated for direct visual grounding against image evidence, moving beyond embedding-based separability metrics?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors state: "a key limitation is that embedding-based separability does not directly validate visual grounding" and "Linking textual attributes directly to visual evidence remains an important direction for future work."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current evaluation (likelihood ratio) measures semantic consistency of explanations but cannot confirm if mentioned attributes (e.g., "prominent jawline") are actually visible and verifiable in the input images, especially under challenging conditions.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A new evaluation framework that rigorously grounds each textual claim against pixel-level evidence (e.g., via human annotation, segmentation masks, or verifiable attribute detectors) to quantify hallucination rates.

- **Open Question 2:** To what extent does the proposed likelihood-ratio (LR) score from textual explanations correlate with human judgment of explanation faithfulness or evidential strength in face verification?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The LR framework is introduced as a "proxy measure of explanation reliability rather than a ground-truth faithfulness metric." Its practical utility for assessing trustworthiness in real-world applications (e.g., forensic review) is unstated.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper demonstrates LR separability but does not validate whether higher LR scores actually correspond to explanations that humans would deem more trustworthy, accurate, or useful.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A user study where evaluators rank explanations by perceived reliability, with correlation analysis against LR scores and verification accuracy.

- **Open Question 3:** Why does incorporating auxiliary FR information (scores/decisions) improve categorical verification performance but not consistently enhance explanation faithfulness in MLLMs?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors observe: "Although such information improves categorical verification performance, it does not consistently lead to faithful explanations."  
  *Why unresolved:* The underlying mechanism is unclear—whether the MLLM uses FR signals to override flawed visual reasoning or merely adjusts its textual output to align with provided scores without truly grounding explanations in images.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Analysis of MLLM internal attention patterns or probing experiments that decouple FR guidance from visual content (e.g., providing mismatched scores) to see if explanations become more visually grounded or merely score-consistent.

- **Open Question 4:** Does using multiple diverse FR model scores in prompts introduce noise that harms explanation quality more than it helps, and is there an optimal aggregation strategy?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Results show: "using a single high-performing FR model score (KPRPE) yields better separation than using multiple model scores, as multi-model inputs introduce additional variability."  
  *Why unresolved:* The trade-off between aggregating complementary information from multiple FR models (potential diversity benefit) and introducing conflicting signals (variability harm) is not quantified for explanation quality.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments varying the number, agreement level, and performance of FR models included in prompts, measuring both LR separability and human-rated faithfulness.

- **Open Question 5:** Can MLLMs be fine-tuned or prompted to consistently produce visually verifiable explanations under extreme pose and surveillance conditions, or are fundamental model limitations the primary barrier?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper shows drastic performance drops on IJB-S (extreme pose/surveillance) even with ground-truth labels during generation, and notes "hallucinated or weakly grounded explanations remain prevalent." This suggests a distribution shift that may not be easily addressed by prompt engineering alone.  
  *Why unresolved:* It is unknown whether the failures are due to insufficient training data diversity, architectural limitations in visual-language alignment, or inherent biases in MLLM pretraining that favor linguistic priors over fine-grained visual evidence in low-quality images.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Fine-tuning studies on curated datasets of hard face pairs with verifiable attribute annotations, evaluating whether explanation faithfulness (via visual grounding metrics) improves without sacrificing verification accuracy.

## Limitations
- Findings rely on proprietary MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini), which may not generalize to other models or future updates.
- Training data (BUPT-CBFace) is not publicly available, hindering full replication of GMM training.
- Prompt design and FR model specifics are partially opaque without the codebase; minor deviations could affect results.
- The LR framework uses textual embeddings; high separability does not guarantee explanations are visually grounded or free of hallucinations.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1:** Incorporating auxiliary FR information improves categorical verification accuracy -> **High** (direct empirical evidence on IJB-S).
- **Claim 2:** LR framework quantifies evidential strength of explanations -> **Medium** (proxy metric not validated against ground-truth explanation quality).
- **Claim 3:** Multi-level prompting improves embedding separability -> **Medium** (correlative results; causality not firmly established).
