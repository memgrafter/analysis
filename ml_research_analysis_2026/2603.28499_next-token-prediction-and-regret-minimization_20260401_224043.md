# Next-Token Prediction and Regret Minimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28499
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28499
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Robust Polya‑urn switching guarantees sublinear regret for any next‑token predictor, with TV distance decaying as |A|T^{-α} (unbounded) or Δ^{-α} (bounded), and empirically reduces Next‑Token TV to ~0.02 on NanoDO transformers.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores how next‑token predictors, widely used in language modeling, can be harnessed for adversarial online decision‑making while guaranteeing low cumulative regret. The authors introduce a simple robustification technique: monitor the empirical regret of an arbitrary predictor and, whenever it exceeds that of a Pólya urn baseline, replace the predictor’s output with the urn’s prediction. This ensures that the quantal best response of any adversary incurs only sublinear regret. Theoretically, for unbounded context windows, any predictor is exponentially close in total variation distance to a low‑regret model, with TV ≤ |A|T^{-α} and worst‑case regret O(√T log(|A|T)). For bounded contexts of length L, extending the context by Δ reduces regret to O(1/√Δ) with TV ≤ Δ^{-α}, though some predictors may require impractically large Δ. Experiments with NanoDO transformers confirm that the robustified model (ROBUST BERNOULLI) achieves vanishing regret across distributions while maintaining a Next‑Token TV distance of approximately 0.02, substantially lower than the 0.15 observed for a pure Polya‑urn predictor.

## Method Summary
The proposed method wraps an arbitrary next‑token predictor with a regret monitoring and switching mechanism. In each round, the wrapper computes predictions from both the base predictor and a Pólya urn, which is a randomized predictor known to achieve sublinear regret. The wrapper maintains an empirical estimate of the cumulative regret of each strategy based on realized losses. If the base predictor’s empirical regret exceeds that of the urn by a margin, the wrapper switches to using the urn’s prediction; otherwise it forwards the base predictor’s output. This ensures that the quantal best response of any adversary yields sublinear regret. The analysis extends to both unbounded and bounded context settings by leveraging the exponential convergence of the urn in total variation and by allowing context extensions to simulate longer memory.

## Key Results
- Sublinear regret is guaranteed for any base predictor through the Polya‑urn switching mechanism.
- For unbounded contexts, TV distance decays as |A|T^{-α} and worst‑case regret is O(√T log(|A|T)); for bounded contexts, extending by Δ yields TV ≤ Δ^{-α} and regret O(1/√Δ).
- Empirical evaluation on NanoDO transformers shows the robustified predictor attains vanishing regret and reduces Next‑Token TV distance to ~0.02 compared to ~0.15 for a pure Polya urn.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Pólya urn serves as an anytime low‑regret benchmark. By ensuring that the combined predictor’s empirical regret never significantly exceeds the urn’s, the wrapper inherits the urn’s regret guarantees. The switching rule is conservative: it only falls back to the urn when the base predictor is demonstrably underperforming, thus preserving the base predictor’s performance when it is already good. The theoretical analysis shows that the urn’s predictions converge rapidly to the distribution that minimizes regret, which bounds the total variation distance between the combined predictor and an optimal low‑regret model. In bounded‑context settings, extending the context provides more information, allowing the predictor to approximate the optimal distribution more closely, which yields the stated trade‑offs between context extension and regret.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Base Predictor (BP) and Pólya Urn (PU) run in parallel. The Regret Tracker (RT) accumulates losses and computes empirical regrets for BP and PU. The Switcher (S) receives these regret values and selects either BP’s prediction or PU’s prediction to forward as the final output, which is sent to the environment. The environment’s feedback (loss) updates RT.  
Sequence: Context → BP (p_t) and PU (q_t) → RT updates regrets → S compares R_t(BP) and R_t(PU) → selects prediction → Output → Environment → loss → RT.

**Critical path**  
1. Receive context from environment.  
2. Compute BP’s prediction (may be computationally heavy).  
3. Compute PU’s prediction (lightweight).  
4. Update RT with losses from previous round.  
5. Compare current empirical regrets; if BP’s ≤ PU’s + margin, choose BP; else choose PU.  
6. Transmit chosen prediction.  
All steps must complete within the decision deadline.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Accuracy vs robustness:** When BP is strong, the system uses BP and maximizes accuracy; when BP is weak, fallback to PU prevents large regret but may incur higher short‑term loss.  
- **Computation:** Adding RT and PU increases overhead; BP may dominate cost.  
- **Switch threshold:** The margin (if any) for switching balances responsiveness and stability; too sensitive causes flickering, too insensitive delays fallback.  
- **Theoretical assumptions:** reliance on quantal best response and well‑calibrated probabilities; deviations may weaken guarantees.

**Failure signatures**  
- Consecutively high regret even after switching to PU, suggesting PU itself is not low‑regret for the current sequence.  
- Rapid back‑and‑forth switching indicates noisy regret estimates or an unstable BP.  
- Persistent use of PU without re‑engaging BP may indicate BP is permanently rejected, possibly missing recoverable performance.  
- In bounded contexts, if regret does not decay as expected with Δ, the underlying predictor may be fundamentally far from low‑regret models.

**First experiments**  
1. **Baseline comparison:** Run ROBUST BERNOULLI against pure PU and unmodified BP on synthetic adversarial sequences and on NanoDO transformers; record cumulative regret and TV distance.  
2. **Switch frequency analysis:** Log the proportion of rounds where PU is selected under varying adversary strengths and distributional regimes; correlate switch frequency with regret accumulation.  
3. **Threshold sensitivity:** Vary the switching margin (e.g., add constant or multiplicative slack) to assess impact on finite‑sample regret and stability; identify a robust default.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on idealized assumptions (perfect predictor calibration, exact Pólya‑urn performance); real predictors may violate these and degrade regret bounds.
- For bounded contexts, some predictors are inherently Θ(1)‑far from any low‑regret model, requiring potentially large context extensions Δ to achieve small regret, which may be impractical.
- Experimental validation is limited to NanoDO transformers and a narrow set of distributions; broader evaluation across larger language models and diverse tasks is lacking.
