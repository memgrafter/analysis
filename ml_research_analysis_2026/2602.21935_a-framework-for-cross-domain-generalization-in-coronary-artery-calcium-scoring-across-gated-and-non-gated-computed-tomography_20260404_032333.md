# A Framework for Cross-Domain Generalization in Coronary Artery Calcium Scoring Across Gated and Non-Gated Computed Tomography

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21935
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21935
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: A self-supervised Vision Transformer (CARD-ViT) trained only on ECG-gated CTs generalizes to non-gated CTs, achieving 0.707 accuracy and Cohen’s κ=0.528 on Stanford non-gated data, matching AI-CAC (trained on non-gated).

## Executive Summary
We propose a framework for coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring that operates robustly across ECG-gated and non-gated computed tomography (CT) scans without requiring non-gated training data. The core is CARD-ViT, a Vision Transformer pretrained via DINO on gated CT slices in a self-supervised manner. A segmentation decoder transforms the learned features into pixel-wise calcium probability maps, from which lesion-specific Agatston scores are computed. Evaluated on internal and external gated test sets, the model attains >0.87 κ. On the Stanford non-gated test set, it matches the performance of AI-CAC (κ=0.528) despite never seeing non-gated images during training. This demonstrates that self-supervised pretraining on gated scans can bridge the domain gap to non-gated CTs, enabling opportunistic cardiovascular screening where non-gated scans are more common but often unlabeled.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of three stages: (1) Self-supervised pretraining of a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone on a large corpus of gated CT slices using DINO, which distills knowledge from a teacher to a student network with augmentations to learn invariant representations. (2) Supervised fine-tuning with a segmentation decoder (e.g., U-Net style) on the gated data, using pixel-level calcium annotations to produce binary calcium masks. (3) A post-processing module that identifies lesions and computes Agatston scores from the mask and CT intensities. The segmentation head is artery-agnostic, deliberately avoiding vessel-specific outputs to mitigate label imbalance (e.g., underrepresented left main artery). All training employs only gated scans; non-gated data appear solely at test time. This design encourages the model to capture calcium morphology independent of gating-related signals.

## Key Results
- Internal gated test set: Accuracy 0.910, Cohen’s κ 0.871.
- External gated test set: Accuracy 0.874.
- Stanford non-gated test set: Accuracy 0.707, Cohen’s κ 0.528, statistically indistinguishable from AI-CAC (trained directly on non-gated data).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The self-supervised DINO pretraining forces the ViT to model higher-level anatomical and pathological features from gated CTs while being invariant to superficial variations. This yields representations that are robust to motion blur and intensity shifts characteristic of non-gated acquisitions. The segmentation decoder, fine-tuned on gated masks, learns to map these features to calcium locations. Because the pretrained features are already semantically rich, the decoder does not overfit to gating-specific artifacts. Moreover, the artery-agnostic output avoids imposing structure that might not transfer well, allowing the model to focus on the essential appearance of calcifications. Consequently, the model generalizes to the non-gated domain despite the absence of non-gated training examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-supervised learning (DINO):** A method for training vision models without labels using knowledge distillation and multi-crop augmentations. **Why needed:** To pretrain CARD-ViT on abundant unlabeled gated CTs, learning representations that capture calcium patterns without relying on pixel-level annotations. **Quick check:** Visualize attention maps on unseen scans to verify focus on coronary regions.
- **Vision Transformer (ViT):** Processes images as sequences of patches with self-attention. **Why needed:** Provides global receptive field enabling the model to integrate context across the coronary tree, which is crucial for identifying scattered calcifications. **Quick check:** Compare patch-based vs. CNN backbone in a pilot study.
- **Domain generalization:** The ability of a model to perform well on unseen target domains without adaptation. **Why needed:** Non-gated CTs constitute a different acquisition domain; the framework must generalize from gated training. **Quick check:** Simulate domain shift by applying synthetic motion blur to gated test set and measure performance drop.
- **Agatston scoring:** Clinical standard quantifying calcification based on area and density. **Why needed:** The end task is to produce this score for cardiovascular risk stratification. **Quick check:** Correlate predicted Agatston with ground-truth on a held-out gated set (should be high).
- **Artery-agnostic segmentation:** Predicting a unified calcium mask without artery labels. **Why needed:** Reduces bias from imbalanced vessel annotations (e.g., few left main examples) and simplifies training. **Quick check:** After inference, project mask onto known vessel trees; measure proportion of mask overlapping each artery.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input CT Slice -> CARD-ViT (Pretrained ViT) -> Segmentation Decoder -> Calcium Mask -> Agatston Scoring Module -> Output Risk Score.
Critical path: The input slice is tokenized into patches; CARD-ViT extracts hierarchical features; the decoder progressively upsamples these features to a full-resolution probability map; thresholding yields binary lesions; the scoring module sums area × density factor per lesion to produce the Agatston score.
Design tradeoffs:
- Artery-agnostic vs. vessel-specific: Artery-agnostic avoids vessel imbalance but loses explicit artery assignment, potentially affecting lesion grouping.
- Self-supervised pretraining on gated only: Saves annotation effort but risks missing domain-specific cues; adding non-gated data could improve but requires labels.
- Decoder capacity: A lightweight decoder reduces parameters and overfitting but may underutilize pretrained features; a deeper decoder may overfit to gated noise.
Failure signatures:
- Motion blur in non-gated scans leads to missed small calcifications (false negatives) or spurious signals (false positives).
- Heavy calcification can cause segmentation overflow, merging adjacent lesions and inflating Agatston.
- Stent artifacts may be misclassified as calcium due to high density.
Three first experiments:
1. Ablation: Train CARD-ViT with (a) DINO pretraining, (b) supervised pretraining on gated labels, (c) random init. Compare non-gated test accuracy/κ to quantify self-supervision benefit.
2. Hybrid baseline: Fine-tune the pipeline on a small mixed dataset (e.g., 10% non-gated + gated) and contrast performance with the proposed framework; this measures the cost of excluding all non-gated data.
3. Failure mode analysis: Manually inspect 50 cases with largest scoring errors on Stanford non-gated set; categorize primary failure cause (motion, beam-hardening, stent, adjacent structure) to guide targeted augmentations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the segmentation accuracy of CARD-ViT on non-gated CT scans?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that "the lack of pixel-level annotations for non-gated scans, which restricts segmentation evaluation to gated data" (Discussion).
- Why unresolved: Without ground-truth segmentation masks for non-gated scans, we cannot quantify the model's ability to localize calcifications in the target domain, limiting an understanding of failure modes and potential clinical errors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Manual annotation of a representative subset of the Stanford non-gated dataset (or a new multi-center non-gated cohort) at the pixel level, followed by quantitative evaluation (Dice, IoU, Precision, Recall) of CARD-ViT predictions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can domain adaptation techniques further improve cross-domain generalization on non-gated CT scans beyond the current self-supervised transfer?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Future work will focus on ... domain adaptation for non-gated performance" (Discussion).
- Why unresolved: The current framework achieves comparable classification accuracy to AI-CAC but shows lower sensitivity in high-risk (400+) and intermediate categories (11–400). It remains uncertain whether explicit adaptation (e.g., adversarial training, self-training with pseudo-labels) can close this gap without extensive non-gated labels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement and compare multiple domain adaptation methods on the Stanford non-gated test set, measuring changes in Cohen’s κ and per-category sensitivity/specificity, while controlling for any use of non-gated supervision.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the artery-agnostic segmentation design hide vessel-specific calcium patterns, and does the observed vessel annotation imbalance bias the model’s performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Although we predict an artery-agnostic calcium mask, labeled training slices are vessel-imbalanced (LM underrepresented) ... this motivates artery-agnostic outputs to reduce vessel-specific bias" (Discussion); vessel-stratified evaluation is listed in future work.
- Why unresolved: No per-vessel analysis was performed, leaving unclear whether certain arteries (e.g., left main) are systematically under- or over-segmented, which could affect risk stratification.
- What evidence would resolve it: A retrospective analysis on gated test sets with vessel-level annotations: compute vessel-wise segmentation metrics and scoring errors, and compare with a multi-output vessel-specific model trained with imbalance-aware sampling to isolate the effect of artery-agnostic vs. artery-specific heads.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does CARD-ViT generalize across different CT scanner manufacturers, reconstruction kernels, and non-gated acquisition protocols at multiple external centers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "center-specific ECG-gating and reconstruction may shift motion blur and calcium appearance, motivating multi-center validation" (Discussion); multi-center validation is a stated future direction.
- Why unresolved: Performance was validated only on a single external non-gated dataset (Stanford) and one internal gated set. Real-world deployments will face heterogeneous scanners and protocols; current results may not hold under such variability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Collection of a multi-center non-gated CT cohort from ≥3 hospitals with diverse scanner models and acquisition parameters, manual CAC annotation, and evaluation of the model’s accuracy/κ across sites, with statistical analysis (e.g., ANOVA) to identify protocol factors influencing performance.

## Limitations
- Non-gated data are absent during training, preventing evaluation of segmentation accuracy and domain adaptation impact on the target domain.
- External validation is limited to a single non-gated institution; multi-center and multi-vendor generalization remain unproven.
- The study lacks comparison to a hybrid-trained model and does not analyze motion artifact handling explicitly; clinical deployment considerations (failure modes, efficiency) are not addressed.

## Confidence
- Cross-domain generalization (non-gated performance matching AI-CAC): Medium confidence.
- Architectural novelty (CARD-ViT + segmentation decoder): Medium confidence.
- Clinical utility for opportunistic screening: Low confidence.

## Next Checks
1. Ablation study: Train comparison models (DINO-pretrained, supervised pretrained, random init) with identical architectures and evaluate non-gated test performance to isolate the contribution of self-supervised pretraining.
2. Hybrid training baseline: Fine-tune the pipeline on a small mixed dataset (10% non-gated + gated) and compare accuracy/κ on the Stanford non-gated set against the proposed framework.
3. Failure mode analysis: Manually review 50 mis-segmented/mis-scored cases from the Stanford non-gated set, categorize errors (motion, beam-hardening, stent, adjacent structure), and quantify their impact on Agatston discrepancy.
