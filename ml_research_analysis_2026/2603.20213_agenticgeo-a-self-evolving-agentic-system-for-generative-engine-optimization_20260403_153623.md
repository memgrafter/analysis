# AgenticGEO: A Self-Evolving Agentic System for Generative Engine Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20213

## Executive Summary
Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) aims to adapt content for ranking in AI-driven search interfaces. Existing GEO methods rely on static heuristics or engine-specific fine-tuning, which are brittle and require costly, repeated feedback from target engines. AgenticGEO reconceptualizes GEO as a content-conditioned control problem addressed by a self-evolving agentic framework. It combines a MAP-Elites archive to maintain diverse, compositional rewriting strategies with a lightweight Co-Evolving Critic that approximates engine feedback, enabling adaptive, multi-turn content rewriting with dramatically reduced interaction costs.

## Method Summary
AgenticGEOformulates GEO as a control problem where an agent iteratively rewrites content to maximize a proxy reward signal. The core architecture maintains a MAP-Elites archive—a quality-diversity algorithm—that stores and evolves a population of distinct rewriting strategies based on their performance across multiple dimensions (e.g., keyword coverage, readability). A Co-Evolving Critic, trained on historical engine feedback, provides a surrogate reward signal to guide strategy selection and rewriting without direct engine calls. This design allows the system to autonomously explore and refine strategies, selecting and combining them compositionally for new queries.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming 14 baselines with average gains of 26.08–27.71% on in-domain evaluations.
- Demonstrates robust cross-domain transfer, with improvements up to 74.89% on out-of-distribution datasets.
- Maintains 98.1% of peak performance while using only 41.2% of the generative engine feedback typically required, showing high sample efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system succeeds by decoupling strategy evolution from direct engine dependency. The MAP-Elites archive ensures a broad repertoire of rewriting approaches is always available, preventing overfitting to any single engine's quirks. The Co-Evolving Critic acts as a cheap, differentiable proxy that approximates the engine's ranking behavior, enabling massive parallel exploration. Together, they create a feedback loop where the critic's predictions are periodically aligned with minimal real engine data, while the archive's diversity allows the agent to adapt strategies to new query contexts without catastrophic forgetting. This results in a system that continuously self-improves with minimal external supervision.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** User Query -> Strategy Retrieval (MAP-Elites Archive) -> Multi-Turn Rewriting Agent -> Co-Evolving Critic (Reward Estimation) -> Feedback Loop (Periodic Real Engine Alignment) -> Archive Update

**Critical path:** The iterative loop between the rewriting agent applying a strategy and the critic evaluating the result is central. This loop runs autonomously for many steps, with occasional "alignment events" where real engine feedback updates the critic and archive elite status.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- *Archive diversity* vs. *convergence speed*: A highly diverse archive may require more iterations to find optimal strategies.
- *Critic accuracy* vs. *update cost*: Frequent critic retraining with real feedback improves fidelity but increases engine calls.
- *Compositional complexity* vs. *interpretability*: Combining multiple strategies boosts performance but makes debugging harder.

**Failure signatures:** 
- Declining correlation between critic predictions and real engine feedback over time.
- Archive population collapsing to a few similar strategies (loss of diversity).
- Semantic drift in rewritten content detected by inconsistent performance on auxiliary relevance metrics.

**First three experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication**: Run AgenticGEO on a single engine and dataset, comparing to a non-adaptive heuristic baseline to confirm basic efficacy.
2. **Critic ablation**: Disable the Co-Evolving Critic (use random or fixed rewards) to quantify the sample efficiency gains from surrogate modeling.
3. **Archive analysis**: Visualize the MAP-Elites archive after training to inspect strategy diversity and identify clusters of successful approaches.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to only two generative engines (likely ChatGPT/Perplexity) and three specific datasets, limiting generalizability.
- The Co-Evolving Critic's accuracy as a feedback surrogate is unvalidated on novel query distributions or other engines.
- Semantic consistency relies on automatic metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) which may not capture meaningful meaning preservation.

## Confidence
- **High**: Self-evolving framework reduces feedback dependence (clear methodology, ablations).
- **Medium**: MAP-Elites ensures diverse strategies (plausible design, but diversity metrics unreported); Co-Evolving Critic approximates feedback (performance supports, but black-box validation missing); Semantic preservation (automatic metrics cited, human evaluation absent).
- **Low**: Robust cross-domain generalization (transfer tests limited to specific datasets; no zero-shot on entirely unseen domains).

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate across additional generative engines** (e.g., Bing Chat, You.com) to verify performance consistency beyond the two originally tested.
2. **Quantify critic accuracy** by comparing its predicted feedback scores against actual engine responses on a held-out query set, reporting correlation (e.g., Spearman’s ρ).
3. **Conduct human evaluation** of rewritten content for semantic fidelity and relevance on a sample of cross-domain examples, using pairwise preference judgments against original content.
