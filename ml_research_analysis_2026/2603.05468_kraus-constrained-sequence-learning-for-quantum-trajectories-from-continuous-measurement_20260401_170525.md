# Kraus Constrained Sequence Learning For Quantum Trajectories from Continuous Measurement

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2603.05468
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05468
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: A Kraus-structured output layer enforces CPTP constraints on sequence models, yielding physically valid quantum state updates. Kraus-LSTM improves state estimation fidelity by 7% over unconstrained models and adapts rapidly to non‑stationary Hamiltonian switches, while Transformer‑based approaches fail catastrophically.

## Executive Summary
Real‑time reconstruction of quantum states from continuous measurement is essential for feedback control but standard solvers require exact models and are sensitive to parameter drift. Neural sequence models can learn dynamics but unconstrained predictors may violate physical constraints like positivity. This paper introduces a Kraus‑structured output layer that maps any backbone’s hidden state to a completely positive trace‑preserving (CPTP) quantum operation by construction, ensuring physical validity. Evaluated on synthetic trajectories with stochastic measurements and Hamiltonian switching, the Kraus layer consistently improves performance across backbones. Kraus‑LSTM achieves the strongest result, improving fidelity by 7% over its unconstrained counterpart. In non‑stationary regimes, gated recurrent models (LSTM/GRU) show the most robust adaptation with minimal fidelity degradation after switches, whereas models with smoothness biases (Mamba, Neural ODE) exhibit larger drops. The Transformer catastrophically fails, revealing incompatibility between global attention and recursive physical constraints.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to attach a Kraus head to any sequence model backbone. The backbone processes the measurement record \(y_{0:t}\) into a hidden state \(h_t\). The Kraus head then: (a) linearly projects \(h_t\) to a complex matrix \(V \in \mathbb{C}^{r d \times d}\) (with Kraus rank \(r=2\) for a qubit), (b) applies a QR decomposition on \(V\) to obtain an orthonormal matrix \(Q\) satisfying \(Q^\dagger Q = I\) (Stiefel manifold projection), (c) reshapes \(Q\) into Kraus operators \(\{K_i\}\), and (d) updates the quantum state via \(\rho_{t+1} = \frac{\sum_i K_i \rho_t K_i^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(\sum_i K_i \rho_t K_i^\dagger)}\). The map is CPTP by construction. Numerical stabilizers (Hermiticity projection and trace renormalization) correct floating‑point drift. The model is trained on trajectories generated from the stochastic master equation (SME) for a single qubit under continuous homodyne detection, with random Hamiltonian switches. Loss is mean Frobenius error between predicted and true density matrices; no explicit physicality penalty is used. Training uses AdamW with learning‑rate scheduling and early stopping based on training Bures distance.

## Key Results
- Kraus‑constrained models guarantee physical validity and improve fidelity; Kraus‑LSTM yields a 7% gain over unconstrained and achieves the highest overall performance.
- Gated RNNs (LSTM/GRU) adapt very quickly to abrupt Hamiltonian switches; Phase 1→Phase 2 fidelity drops are ≈ 0.005, while smoothness‑biased models (Mamba, Neural ODE) incur drops > 0.01 due to inertial lag.
- Transformer‑based architecture fails catastrophically (fidelity drop ~0.203), indicating that global attention is incompatible with recursive physical constraints in this setting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: CPTP Guarantee via Kraus Structure
- **Claim:** The Kraus output layer ensures updates are completely positive and trace‑preserving by construction, preventing error accumulation from unphysical predictions.
- **Mechanism:** The layer maps hidden state \(h_t\) to a complex matrix \(V \in \mathbb{C}^{r d \times d}\), then applies a reduced QR decomposition to obtain an orthonormal matrix \(Q\) whose columns are reshaped into Kraus operators \(K_i\). This enforces \(\sum_i K_i^\dagger K_i = I\), guaranteeing the induced map \(\rho_{t+1} = \frac{\sum_i K_i \rho_t K_i^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(\sum_i K_i \rho_t K_i^\dagger)}\) is CPTP in exact arithmetic. Numerical stabilizers (Hermiticity projection, trace renormalization) correct floating‑point drift (Sec. 5.1).
- **Core assumption:** Discrete‑time evolution conditioned on the measurement record can be captured by a CPTP map; a low‑rank Kraus parametrization (e.g., \(r=2\) for a qubit) suffices.
- **Break condition:** If the hidden dimensionality is too low, the QR step may produce ill‑conditioned operators near singularity, causing fidelity collapse despite formal CPTP.

### Mechanism 2: Gated Recurrent Adaptation via Latent Reset
- **Claim:** Gated architectures (LSTM/GRU) quickly adapt to abrupt Hamiltonian switches by effectively resetting their latent state, minimizing fidelity degradation.
- **Mechanism:** Forget/reset gates can drive activations toward extreme values (≈ 0), collapsing the recurrent state to a function of the current measurement alone: \(h_t \approx \tanh(W x_t)\). This “latent restart” lets the Kraus head apply a fresh CPTP map aligned with the new regime within ~10–20 timesteps (Sec. 10, Table 3).
- **Core assumption:** The optimal filtering response to an orthogonal parameter switch is rapid discarding of regime‑specific latent features, consistent with the Markovian property of the SME.
- **Break condition:** For non‑orthogonal switches or gradual drift, aggressive resetting may over‑discard useful memory, harming steady‑state tracking; vanishing gradients in ungated RNNs prevent effective reset.

### Mechanism 3: Smoothness Bias Induces Inertial Lag
- **Claim:** Inductive biases that enforce temporal smoothness (Mamba’s linear recurrence, Neural ODE’s continuous dynamics) mismatch the white‑noise measurement process, causing lag after switches.
- **Mechanism:** These models maintain state as a weighted average (Mamba) or a continuous flow (Neural ODE), which resists discontinuous updates. After a switch, they blend incompatible pre‑ and post‑switch statistics over 50–100 timesteps before converging (Sec. 10).
- **Core assumption:** The homodyne measurement record is statistically white; optimal filtering requires trusting rapid fluctuations rather than smoothing them.
- **Break condition:** If measurement noise is temporally correlated (e.g., 1/f noise), smoothness priors may become beneficial, reversing the performance hierarchy.

## Foundational Learning

### Density Matrix Physicality
- **Why needed here:** The Kraus layer’s physicality guarantee hinges on preserving positivity, trace = 1, and Hermiticity throughout recursive updates; violations cause instability and non‑physical rollouts.
- **Quick check:** Given a 2×2 matrix with eigenvalues {1.2, -0.1}, what simple operation fixes trace and positive‑semi‑definiteness?  
  *Answer: Project onto the PSD cone (set negative eigenvalues to zero) and renormalize trace to 1.*

### Stochastic Master Equation (SME)
- **Why needed here:** The SME defines the ground‑truth stochastic dynamics (Lindbladian drift + measurement back‑action) that the neural filters must emulate; understanding SME explains why gated reset helps after Hamiltonian switches.
- **Quick check:** In the SME \(d h = L(h)dt + \sqrt{\eta}\,\mathcal{H}[O](h)\,dW_t\), what role does the \(\mathcal{H}[O]\) superoperator play?  
  *Answer: It encodes the measurement back‑action, causing state diffusion orthogonal to the measurement direction.*

### Inductive Bias in Sequence Models
- **Why needed here:** The paper’s core conclusion ties performance to alignment between architecture (gating, attention, smoothness) and the stochastic, Markovian nature of quantum trajectories.
- **Quick check:** Why might self‑attention be unsuitable for modeling Markovian SDEs with abrupt parameter changes?  
  *Answer: Attention aggregates information globally, smoothing over abrupt transitions and breaking the Markov property.*

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
1. **Backbone** (RNN/GRU/LSTM/TCN/Mamba/Transformer/Neural ODE): Processes measurement record \(y_{0:t}\) → hidden state \(h_t\).  
2. **Kraus Head**: (a) Linear projection: \(h_t \to V \in \mathbb{C}^{r d \times d}\); (b) Stiefel projection via QR decomposition (reduced) → orthonormal \(Q\) with \(Q^\dagger Q = I\); (c) Reshape \(Q\) → Kraus operators \(\{K_i\}\); (d) Apply update: \(\rho_{t+1} = \frac{\sum_i K_i \rho_t K_i^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(\sum_i K_i \rho_t K_i^\dagger)}\).  
3. **Numerical stabilizers**: Hermiticity projection and trace renormalization (correct floating‑point drift).  
4. **Loss**: Mean Frobenius error \(\|\rho_{\text{pred}} - \rho_{\text{true}}\|_F^2\) (no explicit physicality penalty).

**Critical path:** Input \(y_t\) → backbone recurrent/attention step → \(h_t\) → Kraus head (V→QR→{K_i}) → apply map to previous \(\rho_t\) → \(\rho_{t+1}\). Training: loss backpropagates through Kraus head (differentiable QR) into backbone.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Memory vs. context: Global attention (Transformer) scales quadratically, limiting batch size; recurrence (LSTM/GRU) scales linearly.
- Kraus rank \(r\): Higher \(r\) increases expressivity but also parameter count (∝ \(r d^2\)); for multi‑qubit systems, low‑rank approximations become essential.
- Hardware: Kraus heads increase memory (operator materialization); paper notes A100 required vs. RTX 4050 for baselines.

**Failure signatures:**
- Transformer: Catastrophic fidelity drop (~ -0.203), manifold collapse toward \(\rho = I/2\); caused by stateless attention + recursive drift + memory constraints.
- Vanilla RNN: Slight degradation under Kraus (Δ ≈ -0.004) due to vanishing gradients preventing effective reset.
- Mamba/Neural ODE: Larger Phase 2 fidelity drops (> 0.01) from smoothness‑induced inertial lag.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline fidelity comparison**: Train unconstrained vs. Kraus variants across backbones on switching dataset; expect Kraus‑LSTM/GRU to show largest absolute fidelity gain (+0.0686, +0.0193).
2. **Switching adaptation test**: Measure fidelity before/after random Hamiltonian switch (σˣ→σʸ); gated models should show < 0.01 drop, while Mamba/Neural ODE drop > 0.01.
3. **Physicality stress test**: Run long‑horizon rollouts (T=2000) without ground truth; check trace error and min eigenvalue. Unconstrained models accumulate violations; Kraus models stay within 10⁻⁴ tolerance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What causes the fidelity degradation in Vanilla RNN under Kraus constraints, and can it be mitigated?** The authors suspect an interaction between vanishing gradients and the QR‑based Stiefel projection, but causality is unproven. Controlled ablations (replacing QR with unconstrained Kraus head, gradient clipping) could resolve.
2. **How can Kraus‑constrained models be efficiently parametrized and trained for high‑dimensional quantum systems?** Scaling to many qubits requires low‑rank Kraus approximations or geometry‑aware parametrizations to manage exponential Hilbert space growth.
3. **Do Kraus‑constrained models maintain physical validity and tracking accuracy under non‑Markovian noise (e.g., 1/f noise)?** The dataset uses Markovian noise; non‑Markovian environments could violate the discrete Kraus map assumption.
4. **Does providing the true initial state ρ₀ to unconstrained baselines reduce the performance gap?** Current unconstrained baselines are stateless sequence‑to‑state regressors, while Kraus models recursively update from ρ₀; this asymmetry may bias comparisons.

## Limitations
- Ambiguities in experimental details: measurement record formulation (dy increment vs. integrated), scaling, baseline inputs, Neural‑ODE integration, and hyperparameter choices.
- Limited evaluation of extreme scenarios: break conditions (low hidden dimension, non‑orthogonal switches, correlated noise) remain untested.
- Hardware requirements (A100 GPU) hinder accessibility and replication on consumer hardware.

## Confidence
- Kraus CPTP guarantee: High (mathematically sound, experiments confirm).
- Gated RNN rapid adaptation: Medium (strong for orthogonal switches; generalizability uncertain).
- Smoothness bias lag: Medium (consistent but alternative explanations possible).
- Transformer failure: Medium (observed but may be influenced by training setup).
