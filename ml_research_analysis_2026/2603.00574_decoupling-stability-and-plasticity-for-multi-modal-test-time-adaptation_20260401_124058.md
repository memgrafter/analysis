# Decoupling Stability and Plasticity for Multi-Modal Test-Time Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00574
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00574
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DASP achieves state-of-the-art multi-modal test-time adaptation, improving average accuracy by up to 5.0% over prior arts on Kinetics50-C and VGGSound-C.

## Executive Summary
In multi-modal test-time adaptation (MM-TTA), models must adapt to distribution shifts in one modality while preserving performance on the other, facing the stability-plasticity dilemma: negative transfer on clean modalities and catastrophic forgetting on corrupted ones. This paper introduces Decoupling Adaptation for Stability and Plasticity (DASP), which first diagnoses modality bias using an interdimensional redundancy score computed on fused features, then applies asymmetric adaptation: biased modalities update a full-rank plastic adapter to capture domain-specific changes; unbiased modalities update a low-rank stable adapter with KL regularization to retain source knowledge. Evaluated on Kinetics50-C and VGGSound-C with video and audio corruptions, DASP substantially outperforms prior TTA methods, achieving 65.2% and 39.7% average accuracy respectively, with strong robustness in continual adaptation scenarios.

## Method Summary
DASP addresses MM-TTA by diagnosing which modality is biased under test-time corruption without relying on source statistics. The diagnosis uses a redundancy score \(R(Z)\) that measures average squared normalized covariance among dimensions of the unified latent space \(Z\); higher \(R\) indicates instability (bias). Based on a threshold \(\delta\), modalities are partitioned into a biased set \(G\). Each modality has two adapters: a stable adapter \(\phi^s\) (low-rank bottleneck, rank 32) and a plastic adapter \(\phi^p\) (full-rank linear, rank 768). For biased modalities, only \(\phi^p\) is updated; for unbiased modalities, \(\phi^p\) is bypassed and \(\phi^s\) is updated with an additional KL divergence loss between the adapted and source model predictions, preserving source behavior. The total loss combines diversity regularization (\(L_{\text{div}}\)), entropy minimization (\(L_{\text{ent}}\)), and KL (\(L_{\text{kl}}\)). The framework runs online with Adam, resetting adapters episodically or continuing across corruptions.

## Key Results
- **Kinetics50-C (video corruptions):** DASP achieves 65.2% average accuracy, surpassing TSA (63.8%) by 1.4%.
- **VGGSound-C (audio corruptions):** DASP reaches 39.7% average accuracy, a 5.0% improvement over TSA (34.7%).
- **Continual interleaved corruption:** DASP demonstrates superior robustness against forgetting compared to prior methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Redundancy Diagnosis via Interdimensional Correlations
**Claim:** The redundancy score \( R(Z) = \frac{1}{D(D-1)} \sum_{i \neq j} C_{ij}^2 \) (where \(C\) is the normalized covariance matrix) reliably identifies biased modalities without source data.  
**Mechanism:** Distribution shifts cause feature dimensions to become spuriously correlated, increasing interdimensional redundancy. A robust source latent space exhibits linear disentanglement (low redundancy), and shifts degrade this property consistently across modalities.  
**Evidence anchors:** [Abstract] notes higher redundancy in biased vs. unbiased modalities; [Section 3.2] provides theoretical linkage to decorrelation and empirical validation in Fig. 3 (strong correlation between redundancy and accuracy drop); neighboring literature confirms the stability-plasticity dilemma but not this metric.  
**Break condition:** If certain corruptions preserve decorrelation or batch size is too small (making \(R\) noisy), diagnosis may fail.

### Mechanism 2: Asymmetric Adaptation with Decoupled Adapters
**Claim:** Updating only the plastic adapter for biased modalities and only the stable adapter (with KL) for unbiased modalities resolves negative transfer and catastrophic forgetting.  
**Mechanism:** Biased modalities require plasticity to capture domain-specific shifts; thus the full-rank plastic adapter (high capacity) is updated while the stable adapter remains frozen. Unbiased modalities require stability; their low-rank stable adapter (limited capacity) is updated with KL regularization to stay close to source predictions, while the plastic adapter is bypassed.  
**Evidence anchors:** [Abstract] describes asymmetric updates; [Section 3.3] formalizes routing (Eqs. 5‑11) and shows a 5.9% drop without asymmetry; related works independently explore stability-plasticity separation.  
**Break condition:** Misdiagnosis of bias or if domain-specific features actually reside in stable adapter parameters.

### Mechanism 3: Parameter Decoupling for Knowledge Separation
**Claim:** Low-rank stable adapters internalize domain-agnostic knowledge, while full-rank plastic adapters externalize domain-specific knowledge, enabling simultaneous adaptation and preservation.  
**Mechanism:** The capacity constraints force the stable adapter to learn only robust, domain-invariant features, whereas the plastic adapter can model complex, modality-specific realignment. KL regularization on stable adapter outputs further anchors it to source behavior.  
**Evidence anchors:** [Section 3.3] explicitly states the externalization/internalization principle; [Section 4.3] ablations show removing either adapter harms performance; neighboring papers use alternative isolation strategies.  
**Break condition:** If domain-specific information occupies low-frequency components that the low-rank adapter would capture, or if corruption affects both feature types equally.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input -> Modality encoders (f_a, f_v) -> Fusion (f_u) -> Fused features Z_m -> Redundancy calculator & Asymmetric router (δ) -> [Biased: Plastic adapter ϕ^p_m; Unbiased: Stable adapter ϕ^s_m] -> Adapted features ˜z_m -> Classification head (f_c) -> Predictions

### Critical Path
For each mini‑batch: (1) forward pass yields Z_m; (2) compute redundancy r_m and biased set G via δ; (3) route each modality through its designated adapter (Eqs. 5‑6); (4) compute total loss \(L = L_{\text{div}} + \lambda_{\text{ent}} L_{\text{ent}} + \lambda_{\text{kl}} L_{\text{kl}}\) (L_kl only for m∉G); (5) backpropagate and update only selected adapter parameters.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Stable adapter rank (32):** Limited capacity reduces overfitting but may underfit if domain‑invariant features are high‑rank.
- **Plastic adapter rank (768):** Full capacity maximizes adaptation but risks interference if misapplied.
- **Redundancy threshold (δ = 0.05):** Heuristic sensitivity; Fig. 5 shows robustness but extreme distributions may need adjustment.
- **KL weight (λ_kl = 1.0):** Strong enough to preserve source behavior without stifling required adaptation.

### Failure Signatures
- Unstable redundancy scores across batches → increase batch size or use momentum buffer.
- All modalities flagged biased → check batch‑norm layers or corruption severity; consider higher δ.
- All modalities flagged unbiased → check for unusually mild corruption or feature collapse; lower δ.
- Performance collapse on unbiased modality → verify KL regularization active and stable adapter gradients exist.
- No improvement on biased modality → verify plastic adapter is being updated and has sufficient capacity.

### First Experiments
1. **Redundancy sanity check:** On clean source data, R should be near zero; on severely corrupted data, R should increase significantly and correlate with accuracy drop (reproduce Fig. 3 pattern).
2. **Gradient flow verification:** For a batch with known corruption (e.g., corrupted video), confirm only the video’s plastic adapter receives gradients; for clean audio, only the audio’s stable adapter updates and KL loss is non‑zero.
3. **Ablation replication:** Removing either adapter (stable or plastic) should degrade performance; reversing asymmetric logic (updating stable for biased, plastic for unbiased) should cause >5% accuracy drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does interdimensional redundancy consistently correlate with modality bias across diverse multi‑modal datasets and architectures beyond audio‑video classification?
2. How robust is DASP to the predefined redundancy threshold δ, and can an adaptive thresholding strategy be developed for unknown target distributions?
3. Can the asymmetric adaptation framework scale to systems with more than two modalities, and how should bias diagnosis be generalized for N modalities?
4. What theoretical guarantees exist for the stability‑plasticity decoupling, and how close does it approach optimal trade‑offs in continual test‑time adaptation?
5. How does DASP handle simultaneous corruption of multiple modalities, where redundancy scores may all increase and diagnosis becomes ambiguous?

## Limitations
- **Diagnosis robustness:** The redundancy score lacks specified variance filtering thresholds; its reliability depends on batch size and may fail for certain corruptions that preserve decorrelation.
- **Adapter implementation gaps:** Exact architectural details (dimensions of low‑rank layers, presence of biases/activations) are not provided, risking incorrect reproduction.
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluations are limited to two synthetic corruption benchmarks (Kinetics50‑C, VGGSound‑C); performance on real‑world distribution shifts remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Redundancy diagnosis):** Medium (empirical correlation strong, but break conditions not fully explored).
- **Mechanism 2 (Asymmetric adaptation):** High (rigorous ablations and explicit loss formulation provide strong evidence).
- **Mechanism 3 (Parameter decoupling):** Low (relies on capacity arguments without direct feature‑space validation).
