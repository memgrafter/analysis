# OmniVL-Guard: Towards Unified Vision-Language Forgery Detection and Grounding via Balanced RL
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.10687
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10687
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A unified multimodal forgery detection and grounding model achieves state-of-the-art in-domain performance (96.2% binary accuracy, 54.3% image IoU) and significant zero-shot gains (e.g., +17% on MMFakeBench) via a balanced RL framework with self-evolving CoT data.

## Executive Summary
OmniVL-Guard presents a unified framework for detecting and localizing forgeries across text, images, and videos within a single multimodal large language model. It addresses a key training imbalance where simpler classification tasks dominate gradients, starving finer-grained localization tasks. The solution combines two innovations: (1) a Self-Evolving Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation pipeline that automatically synthesizes high-quality, multimodal reasoning data for training, and (2) Adaptive Reward Scaling Policy Optimization (ARSPO), a reinforcement learning method that uses non-linear reward mapping and dynamic task-weight adjustment to balance learning across heterogeneous tasks. Evaluated on diverse in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks, the approach demonstrates superior performance and robust generalization, establishing a new state-of-the-art for omnibus vision-language forgery analysis.

## Method Summary
The framework employs a two-stage training procedure on a curated `FSFR` dataset. First, **Self-Evolving CoT Generation** constructs the `FSFRsft` dataset. It starts with a small, high-quality seed of CoT-annotated examples generated by top-tier MLLMs. The base model is then fine-tuned on this seed. Over three bootstrapping iterations, the current model generates CoTs on remaining samples; these are filtered for task accuracy (e.g., IoU ≥ 0.75) and logical consistency, validated by a *different* SOTA MLLM. A final collaborative synthesis step handles hard, long-tail cases. Second, **ARSPO** performs reinforcement learning on `FSFRrl`. It optimizes a multi-task reward where classification uses linear mapping, while all localization tasks use an exponential mapping `g(x)=e^{3x}` to amplify gradients for high-quality outputs. Crucially, an adaptive algorithm monitors per-task performance trends against a warm-up baseline and dynamically rescales task-specific coefficients during training to boost lagging tasks and decay mastered ones, counteracting difficulty bias.

## Key Results
*   **In-domain SOTA:** Achieves 96.2% binary classification accuracy, 54.3% image IoU, 63.8% text F1, and 59.2% video tIoU, outperforming prior best by 2.7-37.8% absolute.
*   **Zero-shot generalization:** Yields large gains on out-of-domain benchmarks, e.g., 79.4% on MMFakeBench (+17%) and 63.6% on FakeSV (+8%).
*   **Ablation验证:** Both core components of ARSPO—Task-Based Reward Mapping (TBRMF) and Dynamic Coefficient Adjustment (DCA)—contribute positively to final performance, with optimal exponential steepness found at `a=3`.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Self-Evolving CoT Generation produces high-quality reasoning chains that overcome cold-start and hindsight bias for RL in forgery detection.
*   **Mechanism:** It iteratively refines a small seed dataset using evolving model generations, filters samples by accuracy and logical consistency via multiple MLLMs, and supplements with collaborative hard-CoT synthesis for long-tail cases.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions overcoming the cold-start challenge. Section 3 details the pipeline (Figure 2) and FSFR statistics. Corpus evidence is weak; neighbors lack this specific self-evolving approach.
*   **Break condition:** Poor initial seed quality or lax filtering leads to noise accumulation, causing degenerate reasoning chains and RL divergence.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** ARSPO balances multi-task RL by using non-linear reward mapping for localization tasks and dynamic coefficient adjustment to counteract difficulty bias.
*   **Mechanism:** Theoretical analysis (Eq.4) shows gradient sensitivity depends on reward derivative. ARSPO applies exponential mapping `g(x)=e^{ax}` for localization to amplify gradients for high-quality responses. It also adaptively adjusts task weights based on relative progress (Algorithm 1) to support lagging tasks.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes ARSPO. Section 4 derives Eq.4, discusses convex mapping (Figure 3), and presents Algorithm 1. Section 5.2 ablates TBRMF/DCA contributions and finds optimal `a=3` (Figure 4). Corpus evidence is weak; neighbors focus on general RL, not multi-task forgery balancing.
*   **Break condition:** An excessively high exponential slope amplifies noise, causing overfitting; mis-set dynamic thresholds (`ϵ`, `τ`) can cause coefficient oscillations and training instability.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The unified OmniVL-Guard framework, combining Self-Evolving CoT and ARSPO, achieves state-of-the-art performance and generalization by learning shared forensic representations.
*   **Mechanism:** The FSFR dataset provides CoT-annotated examples across all tasks and modalities. The model undergoes SFT cold-start on this data, then ARSPO dynamically balances the interplay between binary classification and fine-grained grounding.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports SOTA results. Section 5.1 shows consistent in-domain and zero-shot gains (Tables 2,3). Section 5.3 confirms ARSPO's advantage beyond simple balancing. Corpus evidence is moderate; UniShield proposes unified detection/localization but without RL.
*   **Break condition:** Inadequate coverage of all modalities/tasks in training data, or insufficient backbone model capacity, can lead to underfitting on specific tasks and diminished zero-shot transfer.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning  
  **Why needed here:** The model must generate intermediate forensic reasoning (e.g., describing visual tampering cues) before a final judgment, improving interpretability and providing a dense RL signal.  
  **Quick check question:** How does a CoT example for image tampering differ structurally from a direct classification answer?
*   **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning (Policy Optimization)  
  **Why needed here:** Supervised fine-tuning alone suffers from difficulty bias; RL with reward shaping enables exploration and allows balancing gradients across heterogeneous tasks.  
  **Quick check question:** In RL for language models, what is the role of the advantage function and how is it typically normalized within a group?
*   **Concept:** Multi-task learning with dynamic weighting  
  **Why needed here:** Binary classification is inherently easier than fine-grained localization; fixed loss weighting would let the former dominate. Dynamic adjustment is needed to equilibrate learning progress.  
  **Quick check question:** Why might a fixed loss weighting scheme fail in multi-modal forgery detection, even if all tasks have equal loss scales?
*   **Concept:** Exponential reward mapping for gradient reshaping  
  **Why needed here:** Localization metrics (IoU, F1, tIoU) have bounded ranges and less steep sensitivity compared to classification logits. Exponential mapping amplifies rewards for high-quality outputs, increasing gradient signal for harder tasks.  
  **Quick check question:** What is the mathematical effect on the gradient of using `g(x)=e^{ax}` versus `g(x)=x` for a reward signal `x`?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input modalities (text, image, video) -> Unified MLLM backbone (Qwen3VL-8B with LoRA adapters) -> Output JSON with fields: `classification`, `explanation` (CoT), `localization` (bounding boxes/word indices/temporal segments). Two-stage training: (1) SFT on `FSFRsft` (cold-start) -> (2) RL with ARSPO on `FSFRrl`.

Critical path: 1) Data preparation (construct `FSFRsft`, `FSFRrl` from source datasets). 2) SFT cold-start: fine-tune base model on `FSFRsft`. 3) RL phase: run ARSPO loop (Algorithm 1) for `T_warm` steps, then every `T` steps compute per-task trends, adjust coefficients `l_k`, and perform policy update with combined reward. 4) Evaluate on test set `Dt`.

Design tradeoffs: Exponential reward mapping amplifies high-quality signals but risks amplifying noise if the slope `a` is too high. Dynamic coefficient adjustment requires careful tuning of thresholds (`α_boost`, `α_decay`, `τ_high`) to avoid oscillations. LoRA reduces memory footprint but may limit adaptation compared to full fine-tuning.

Failure signatures: 1) RL divergence or mode collapse (repetitive/empty outputs) due to incorrectly scaled rewards or aggressive coefficient changes. 2) Stagnant localization metrics while classification improves, indicating ARSPO imbalance not corrected. 3) Poor zero-shot OOD performance, suggesting artifacts in `FSFRsft` from MLLM generation biases.

First 3 experiments: 1) Ablate ARSPO components (TBRMF, DCA) to measure individual contribution to final performance. 2) Study the effect of exponential reward slope `a` (e.g., 1-5) on localization metrics (IoU, F1, tIoU) to identify optimal steepness. 3) Cross-backbone generalization test: train the same `FSFRsft`/ARSPO pipeline with InternVL3.5-8B and compare in-domain and OOD results to assess data unbiasedness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Is the fixed exponential reward slope parameter (a=3) universally optimal across all four core tasks (binary classification, image/text/video localization)?** The authors adopt a=3 based on ablations limited to text and image localization without evaluating video localization or binary classification. Different tasks may have distinct optimal slopes due to varying metric scales and noise.
*   **Do the dynamic adjustment hyperparameters (α_boost, α_decay, thresholds ϵ_mom, ϵ_rescue, τ_high) generalize across diverse model architectures and dataset distributions?** The heuristic thresholds are fixed for the Qwen3VL-8B backbone and FSFR dataset; their robustness to other data regimes and model families is unexplored.
*   **Is the three-iteration termination point in the Self-Evolving CoT generation pipeline generalizable?** Appendix I shows no gain from a fourth iteration with Qwen3VL-8B, but saturation points may shift with models of different reasoning capabilities or source data distributions.
*   **Can the current architecture effectively handle forgeries requiring simultaneous reasoning across all three modalities in a single cohesive analysis?** The framework defines separate tasks per modality and does not evaluate a joint tri-modal reasoning scenario, which is common in real-world misinformation.

## Limitations
*   The exact `FSFR` dataset splits are not public; reconstruction from source datasets involves significant curation uncertainty and potential preprocessing mismatches.
*   Precise ARSPO hyperparameters (`α_boost`, `α_decay`, `τ_high` per task) and the specific SOTA MLLMs used for CoT verification are omitted, risking implementation drift and sensitivity to these choices.
*   Self-Evolving CoT may accumulate noise if seed filtering criteria are too lax; ARSPO’s exponential reward mapping could amplify this noise, and dynamic adjustments may oscillate if thresholds are mis-set.

## Confidence
*   **Self-Evolving CoT Generation:** Medium. Pipeline described and ablations show gains, but reliance on unverified MLLM choices and weak corpus novelty reduces certainty.
*   **ARSPO Balancing Mechanism:** Medium. Theoretical justification and component ablations are sound, yet hyperparameter sensitivity and lack of comparison to other multi-task RL methods limit robustness.
*   **Unified Framework & Generalization:** Medium-High. Strong in-domain results are compelling, but zero-shot gains depend on `FSFR` data quality, which may contain model-specific artifacts.
