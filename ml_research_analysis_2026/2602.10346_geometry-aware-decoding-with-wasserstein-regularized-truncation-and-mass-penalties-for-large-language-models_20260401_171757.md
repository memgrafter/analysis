# Geometry-Aware Decoding with Wasserstein-Regularized Truncation and Mass Penalties for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10346
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10346
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Top-W decoding outperforms state-of-the-art truncation methods across multiple benchmarks, achieving up to 33.7% relative accuracy gain on GSM8K and winning the majority of judge-based evaluations with ~5.4% runtime overhead.

## Executive Summary
Large language models face a trade-off between diversity and coherence during text generation, with existing truncation-based samplers (e.g., top-k, nucleus) relying on probability and entropy while ignoring token embedding geometry. This paper introduces Top-W, a geometry-aware decoding rule that minimizes a Wasserstein-1 distance between the original and cropped next-token distributions, explicitly balancing geometric faithfulness, entropy, and retained probability mass. Top-W uses an efficient alternating refinement with a closed-form prefix-scan update, avoiding combinatorial search. Across four benchmarks (GSM8K, GPQA, AlpacaEval, MT-Bench) and three instruction-tuned models, Top-W consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving up to 33.7% relative accuracy gain on GSM8K and winning the majority of (temperature, model) settings on judge-based evaluations. It also improves creative writing scores while adding only about 5.4% runtime overhead.

## Method Summary
Top-W is implemented as a logits processor that crops the next-token distribution before sampling. Given model logits ℓ, temperature T, hyperparameters λ=2.2, β=2.8, candidate pool size m=1200, and maximum alternations T_alt=3:
1. Compute probabilities p_i ∝ exp(ℓ_i / T).
2. Form candidate pool C as the indices of the top m tokens by p.
3. Initialize S as the smallest prefix of tokens sorted by p whose cumulative probability exceeds an absolute threshold (e.g., 0.9); if the total probability of C is below the threshold, S = C.
4. For up to T_alt iterations:
   - **f‑step**: For i∈C, compute f_i = −min_{j∈S} d(i,j), where d is the whitened cosine distance between token embeddings (pre‑computed).
   - **S‑step**: Compute ϕ_i = f_i + λ log p_i.
     * If β ≥ λ: sort C by ϕ descending; for each prefix k, compute Γ_k = Σ_{r≤k} p_{(r)} and J_k = (Σ_{r≤k} p_{(r)} ϕ_{(r)}) / Γ_k + (β−λ) log Γ_k. Select the smallest k that maximizes J_k and set S to that prefix.
     * If β < λ: pick i maximizing f_i + β log p_i and set S = {i}.
   - If S stabilizes, break early.
5. Mask logits: ℓ_i = −∞ for i∉S, renormalize, and sample the next token from the resulting softmax.

## Key Results
- Top-W yields substantial relative accuracy gains on reasoning benchmarks: up to 33.7% on GSM8K and improvements on GPQA.
- In judge‑based evaluations (AlpacaEval, MT‑Bench), Top-W wins the majority of (temperature, model) settings, indicating overall higher response quality.
- Creative writing scores increase while the computational overhead is modest, approximately 5.4% longer per token compared to top‑p.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Top-W’s effectiveness arises from three interconnected mechanisms:

### Mechanism 1: Wasserstein–entropy–mass objective
**Claim**: The objective F_{λ,β}(S) jointly balances geometric faithfulness, entropy (creativity/coherence), and retained probability mass.  
**Mechanism**: For a candidate crop S, the objective adds: (i) a Wasserstein‑1 term W₁(p, q_S) using token‑embedding distances to penalize moving mass to distant tokens, (ii) λ·H(q_S) to penalize high‑entropy (overly diffuse) crops, and (iii) −β log Γ_S to reward retaining more mass. The embedding‑induced metric d(i,j) is assumed to reflect semantic similarity, so W₁ acts as a semantic distortion measure.  
**Evidence**: Abstract states the use of Wasserstein distance over token‑embedding geometry; Section 3.1 (Eq. 5, Lemma 3.1) factorizes W₁; prior work (e.g., Top‑H) confirms entropy–mass trade‑offs in decoding.  
**Break condition**: Poorly calibrated embedding metric (e.g., high anisotropy not mitigated by whitening) undermines the semantic meaning of W₁; mis‑tuned (λ,β) can over‑penalize entropy or mass.

### Mechanism 2: Alternating refinement with prefix‑scan
**Claim**: Alternating f‑step and S‑step solves the intractable OT‑regularized truncation with O(n) per‑step cost by leveraging a prefix‑structure theorem.  
**Mechanism**: For a fixed feasible potential f (1‑Lipschitz), the S‑step reduces to maximizing G_f(S) = (∑_{i∈S} p_i ϕ_i(f))/Γ_S + (β−λ) log Γ_S, where ϕ_i(f)=f_i+λ log p_i. Theorem 3.4 shows that when β≥λ, the optimizer is always a prefix of tokens sorted by ϕ_i(f); this avoids combinatorial search over 2^{|V|} subsets.  
**Evidence**: Lemma 3.2 derives the surrogate; Theorem 3.4 establishes the prefix regime; Section 4.2 notes the S‑step becomes a one‑dimensional scan over prefix length.  
**Break condition**: If β<λ, singleton collapse may cause overly sharp truncation; the prefix scan also relies on the candidate pool containing the global top‑ϕ tokens, otherwise the restricted scan may miss the true optimum.

### Mechanism 3: Anchored potential from embedding distances
**Claim**: Geometry is injected via an anchored potential f_S(i)=−dist(i,S), which is the pointwise minimal 1‑Lipschitz extension with f_S(j)=0 for j∈S, biasing ϕ‑scores toward tokens near the current set.  
**Mechanism**: In the f‑step, instead of solving the Kantorovich–Rubinstein dual (expensive), we set f(i)=−min_{j∈S} d(i,j). This is feasible (1‑Lipschitz) and makes ϕ_i = −dist(i,S)+λ log p_i. Tokens far from S get strongly negative scores, encouraging the next S‑step to include nearby high‑probability tokens and avoid isolated picks.  
**Evidence**: Lemma 4.2 proves dist(·,S) and −dist(·,S) are feasible and extremal; the implementation uses f_S(i)=−dist(i,S); tokens farther from S receive a more negative score.  
**Break condition**: If the embedding metric d(i,j) is not smooth or if S becomes fragmented, dist(i,S) may not reflect meaningful semantics; the alternating loop may converge slowly if geometry is noisy.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
The pipeline flows as: logits → temperature scaling → probability distribution p → top‑m candidate pool C → alternating loop (f‑step: compute f_i = −min_{j∈S} d(i,j); S‑step: prefix scan on ϕ_i) → final crop S → mask logits (set ℓ_i = −∞ for i∉S) → sample next token.

### Critical path
Main computational cost lies in the distance queries of the f‑step (O(m·d) per iteration). The critical path is: pool selection → embedding lookup/whitening → alternating loop (dominated by distance computation) → final mask.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Candidate pool size (m)**: Larger m improves exactness but increases O(m²) alternating cost; typical m=1200 balances speed and coverage.
- **β relative to λ**: β≥λ avoids singleton collapse; larger β increases retained mass (more diversity) but risks coherence loss.
- **Alternation count (T_alt)**: More iterations approach the fixed point better but with diminishing returns; paper uses T_alt=3.
- **Metric choice**: Whitened cosine distance is cheap and reduces anisotropy; raw L2 may be dominated by a few embedding dimensions.

### Failure signatures
- **Degenerate crops**: β≤λ leads to singleton collapse repeatedly, reducing diversity.
- **Poor diversity‑coherence balance**: λ too high → overly sharp crops; λ too low → behavior similar to top‑p.
- **Runtime spikes**: m too large (e.g., >5000) makes the O(m²) loop prohibitive; high embedding dimension slows matrix multiplies.
- **Geometry misalignment**: If embeddings no longer reflect semantics (e.g., after domain shift), the distance penalty may push the crop toward linguistically unrelated tokens.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline comparison on reasoning tasks**: Run GSM8K and GPQA with Top‑W vs. top‑p, Min‑p, Top‑H at fixed temperatures T∈{1.0,1.5,2.0}. Expect Top‑W to show stable accuracy gains as T increases.
2. **Parameter sweep on β**: Fix λ=2.2, vary β across {2.0,2.5,2.8,3.0,3.5} on LLaMA‑3.1‑8B at T=1.5. Plot GSM8K accuracy and creative scores; anticipate accuracy peaks at moderate β (e.g., 2.8) while creative scores improve with larger β.
3. **Runtime overhead measurement**: Measure time per token for Top‑W versus baselines across models. Expect ~5% overhead with m=1200, T_alt=3; also test m={500,2000} to confirm quadratic scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can Top‑W's λ and β be set automatically across models and tasks without manual tuning?  
2. Is the specific embedding‑based metric optimal for Top‑W, or can alternative geometries significantly improve performance?  
3. What are the convergence properties and optimality guarantees of Top‑W's alternating refinement scheme?  
4. How does the candidate pool size m affect Top‑W's performance‑efficiency trade‑off, and what is the minimal m that preserves most benefits?

## Limitations
- Unspecified details (warm‑start threshold, exact whitening formula, tie‑breaking) and the ad‑hoc choice of pool size m=1200 hinder exact reproducibility and leave the efficiency‑accuracy trade‑off under‑explored.
- The assumption that embedding distances reflect semantic similarity may fail under high anisotropy or domain shift, potentially harming geometry‑awareness.
- Evaluation is restricted to short‑form reasoning and instruction tasks; long‑form generation and diverse domains remain unexamined.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority** (GSM8K +33.7%, majority wins on AlpacaEval/MT‑Bench): **High**
- **Prefix‑scan efficiency and ~5% overhead**: **Medium**
- **Geometry‑awareness as key driver**: **Low** (no ablation isolating geometry)
- **Hyperparameter choices** (λ=2.2, β=2.8): **Medium** (sensitivity shown but not exhaustive)
