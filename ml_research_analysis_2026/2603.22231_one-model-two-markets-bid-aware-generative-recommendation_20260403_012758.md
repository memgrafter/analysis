# One Model, Two Markets: Bid-Aware Generative Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22231  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22231  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** GEM-Rec integrates ad monetization into generative recommendation using control tokens and bid-aware decoding, achieving up to 28× revenue lift while preserving organic relevance.

## Executive Summary
Generative recommender systems excel at producing coherent, context-aware item sequences but traditionally lack mechanisms for advertising monetization. GEM-Rec closes this gap by introducing two special control tokens—`<ORG>` and `<AD>`—that explicitly decouple the decision of *whether* to show an ad from *which* item to show. The model is trained on unified organic and ad interaction logs, learning a joint probability distribution over slot type and item content. At inference, a Bid-Aware Decoding layer injects real-time bid information into the generation logits via a scalar parameter λ, steering the system toward high-value items without requiring retraining. Theoretical guarantees ensure allocative monotonicity (higher bids lead to greater exposure) and organic integrity (organic rankings remain unaffected by bid modulation). Experiments on a synthetic marketplace across four datasets demonstrate that revenue increases monotonically with λ, up to 28× after bid shocks, while organic ranking quality (NDCG) stays stable. A smooth Pareto frontier between total NDCG and revenue is shown, and all generated ad identifiers are valid.

## Method Summary
GEM-Rec is a generative retrieval model that generates recommendation sequences token-by-token, where each token corresponds to a component of a hierarchical Semantic ID. The vocabulary is augmented with two control tokens, `<ORG>` and `<AD>`, to indicate the type of slot being generated. During training, the model learns a factorization: first predicting the slot type (ad or organic) given the user history, then retrieving an item conditioned on that slot type. This joint modeling on unified logs implicitly captures the intersection of user relevance and ad feasibility. At inference, for ad slots the model applies Bid-Aware Decoding: before sampling the next token, the logits are boosted by λ·log(1+b) where b is the maximum bid associated with the candidate prefix (or slot). This modulation increases the likelihood of choosing high-bid items while preserving the generative decoding process. The approach avoids retraining and works with any pre-trained generative recommender that uses hierarchical item codes.

## Key Results
- Revenue increases monotonically with bid pressure λ (up to 28× after bid shocks).  
- Organic NDCG remains stable under bid modulation.  
- Generated ad identifiers maintain 100% validity.  
- Demonstrates a smooth Pareto frontier between total NDCG and revenue.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** GEM-Rec factorizes the decision of *whether* to show an ad from *which* item to show by introducing control tokens (`<ORG>`, `<AD>`) into the sequence.
- **Mechanism:** During training on unified organic/ad logs, the model learns a joint probability distribution `P(x_t | H_{<t}) = P(f_t | H_{<t}) * Π_k P(c_{t,k} | H_{<t}, f_t, c_{t,<k})`. The first term (`P(f)`) learns the contextual appropriateness of an ad slot (Ad Satisfaction Modeling). The second term (`P(c|f)`) retrieves items conditioned on the decided slot type (Mode-Conditional Retrieval).
- **Core assumption:** The historical interaction logs contain a clear signal of *when* ads were successfully shown (i.e., they passed both a platform filter for bid value and a user filter for relevance). The model can learn this latent feasibility policy from the data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...introduce control tokens to decouple the decision of whether to show an ad from which item to show."
  - [section 3.2] "We augment the vocabulary with two special control tokens: F={<ORG>, <AD>}... By training on successful interaction logs, the model implicitly captures the intersection of high semantic relevance and high economic value."
  - [corpus] Evidence is weak; neighboring papers (e.g., Yang et al., 2024; Ju et al., 2025) focus on standard generative retrieval, not commercial tokenization.
- **Break condition:** If the training logs do not reflect a coherent, learnable signal for ad placement timing (e.g., ads are shown randomly, or the "successful" logs are extremely sparse/noisy), the `P(f)` term fails to learn a meaningful slot allocation policy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Bid-Aware Decoding injects real-time bid information directly into the generation logits at inference time without retraining, steering the model toward high-value items.
- **Mechanism:** A scalar parameter `λ >= 0` controls the strength of modulation. **Slot-Level:** The logit for `<AD>` is boosted by `λ * log(1 + b_max)`, where `b_max` is the maximum bid among eligible candidates, making ad slots more likely when valuable inventory exists. **Item-Level:** For each token in a candidate Semantic ID's hierarchical prefix, its logit is boosted by `λ * log(1 + B(c))`, where `B(c)` is the maximum bid under that prefix branch. This is implemented via a pre-computed lookup table.
- **Core assumption:** The hierarchical Semantic ID structure (where prefixes represent coarse semantic clusters) allows a single `B(c)` value to efficiently approximate the value of all items sharing that prefix. Also, the boosting function `log(1 + b)` is a valid monotonic transformation suitable for logit space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "We boost this score using the maximum bid...˜z_{<AD>} = z_{<AD>} + λ·log(1+b_max)" and "Boost each candidate token using max bid under its prefix branch...˜z_c = z_c + λ·log(1+B(c))".
  - [section 4.3] The proof of Proposition 1 relies on this specific multiplicative form to ensure monotonicity.
  - [corpus] Neighbors discuss bid learning (e.g., "High-dimensional Bid Learning for Energy Storage") but not inference-time logit modulation in generative retrieval; evidence is absent.
- **Break condition:** If the prefix-based bid aggregation `B(c)` is a poor proxy for the actual bid of the specific target item (e.g., high variance of bids within a prefix cluster), the item-level boost may misdirect the beam search away from the literal highest-bidding item.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The GEM-Decoding mechanism provides **Allocative Monotonicity** (higher bids increase exposure probability) and **Organic Integrity** (organic rankings unaffected by bid modulation) by construction.
- **Mechanism:** Monotonicity holds because the boosted slot probability `P(<AD>)` is a strictly increasing function of `b_max`, and within the sponsored branch, beam search scores are a sum of log-probabilities where the target item's score is non-decreasing in its own bid while competitors' scores are non-increasing (due to the normalizing partition function). Organic Integrity holds because bid-based logit modification is *gated* by the sampled flag `f=<AD>`; if an organic slot is generated, the decoding proceeds with unmodified logits.
- **Core assumption:** The beam search is deterministic with fixed tie-breaking, and the set of eligible ad candidates `I_elig` is fixed and independent of bids (i.e., the admissibility filter is applied *before* bid modulation). The proof also assumes a one-to-one mapping between items and full Semantic ID sequences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Proposition 1 (Allocative Monotonicity) and Proposition 2 (Structural Consistency, including Organic Integrity) are stated and proven in Appendix C.
  - [abstract] "...guarantees allocative monotonicity (higher bids increase exposure) and organic integrity (organic rankings unaffected by bid modulation)."
  - [corpus] The theoretical guarantees align with mechanism design principles (monotonicity for strategy-proofness) but the specific proof structure for hierarchical generative decoding is novel; corpus provides general context but no direct parallel.
- **Break condition:** If the decoding uses stochastic sampling instead of deterministic beam search, the monotonicity proof breaks because the allocation becomes a random variable whose expectation may not be monotone. Also, if `I_elig` changes with bids (e.g., a dynamic relevance filter that depends on `b`), the monotonicity assumption is violated.

## Foundational Learning

- **Control Tokens (`<ORG>`, `<AD>`)**  
  - **Why needed here:** To explicitly represent the distinction between organic and ad slots, enabling the model to learn when it is appropriate to show an ad versus organic content.  
  - **Quick check:** Verify the model's output sequence starts with either `<ORG>` or `<AD>` before any item tokens; confirm training logs contain both token types (c.f. [section 3.2]).

- **Hierarchical Semantic IDs**  
  - **Why needed here:** Discretizes items into a tree of semantic tokens, allowing efficient lookup and bid aggregation at prefix level.  
  - **Quick check:** Ensure each item is represented by a fixed-length token sequence; inspect the depth and branching factor of the semantic tree.

- **Bid-Aware Logit Modulation**  
  - **Why needed here:** To incorporate real-time bid information into the generative process without retraining, steering generation toward high-value items.  
  - **Quick check:** During inference, confirm that for ad slots the logits are increased by λ·log(1+b) for candidate prefixes (c.f. [section 4.1]).

- **Allocative Monotonicity**  
  - **Why needed here:** Guarantees that higher bids lead to higher exposure probability, aligning advertiser incentives with platform revenue.  
  - **Quick check:** Run a sensitivity test: fix a candidate set, vary one item's bid, and check that its selection probability does not decrease as bid increases (c.f. Proposition 1).

- **Organic Integrity**  
  - **Why needed here:** Ensures that organic rankings remain unaffected by bid pressure, preserving user experience and relevance.  
  - **Quick check:** Generate organic recommendations across different λ values; verify that the item ordering is identical (c.f. Proposition 2).

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component Map**  
  User History → Encoder → Slot Type Predictor → (branch)  
  → Organic Path: Item Retriever → Output  
  → Ad Path: Bid Modulation Module → Candidate Generator → Hierarchical Beam Search → Output

- **Critical Path**  
  1. Control token (`<ORG>`/`<AD>`) prediction from user history.  
  2. If ad, compute maximum bids per Semantic ID prefix and apply λ·log(1+B(c)) boost.  
  3. Perform beam search over hierarchical tokens to generate full item IDs.  
  Overall latency is dominated by beam search; bid lookup adds O(1) per prefix.

- **Design Tradeoffs**  
  - Decoupling slot decision from retrieval allows independent optimization but may suffer if training ad placement policy differs from deployment.  
  - Prefix-based bid aggregation is efficient but can misrank items when intra-prefix bid variance is high.  
  - Deterministic beam search is required for theoretical guarantees but is computationally heavier than sampling.  
  - Semantic ID compression reduces inventory size but introduces discretization error; granularity trades off relevance vs. bid approximation quality.

- **Failure Signatures**  
  - Low accuracy on `<AD>` token prediction indicates poor slot allocation learning.  
  - Revenue does not increase with λ, suggesting bid modulation is ineffective (e.g., due to poor prefix proxies).  
  - Organic rankings change when λ varies, violating organic integrity.  
  - Memory errors when constructing prefix lookup tables for very large inventories.

- **First Three Experiments**  
  1. **Control Token Accuracy:** Train on synthetic logs with known ad policy; evaluate accuracy of `<AD>` vs `<ORG>` prediction. Target >95%.  
  2. **Bid Monotonicity Sweep:** Fix candidate set, vary item bids and λ; check that selection probability of each item is non‑decreasing in its own bid and λ.  
  3. **Organic Integrity Test:** Generate top‑10 organic recommendations for a user set across λ∈{0,1,5}; compute Kendall τ between λ=0 and others. Expect τ=1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can GEM-Rec be extended to implement a dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) payment mechanism (e.g., VCG-style) while maintaining inference-time bid modulation and provable allocative monotonicity?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly state in Section 4.2 and Appendix E that they leave a full DSIC implementation to future work, noting the technical challenge of computing truthful payments on top of practical decoding algorithms with approximations like beam search.
- **Why unresolved:** truthful payments require determining the minimum bid that would yield the same allocation, which is complex in autoregressive generation with search heuristics; counterfactual decoding is computationally expensive.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretically sound DSIC extension for GEM-Decoding with empirical validation showing advertisers weakly prefer truth-telling under the new payment rule, while preserving organic integrity and revenue properties.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does decoupling the bid pressure into separate parameters λ_slot (controlling ad slot frequency) and λ_item (controlling item selection within ads) yield a finer, more controllable trade-off between ad load, revenue, and ad relevance than the single λ parameter?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors suggest this in Section 5.8, noting that independent scaling parameters would allow more precise calibration of ad frequency versus bid-aware ranking.
- **Why unresolved:** The current unified λ provides a scalar control knob; separate parameters could enable multi-dimensional optimization but require re-analyzing theoretical guarantees (monotonicity, organic integrity) under a two-parameter scheme.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An extended framework with λ_slot and λ_item; empirical Pareto analysis showing a 2D surface of (ad rate, ad relevance, revenue) outcomes, demonstrating improved platform flexibility without compromising monotonicity guarantees.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does GEM-Rec's architecture and bid-aware decoding mechanism maintain its theoretical guarantees (allocative monotonicity, organic integrity) and performance benefits when trained and evaluated on real-world marketplace logs with heterogeneous auction formats (e.g., second-price, hybrid) and strategic bid shading?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses a synthetic marketplace because standard benchmarks lack bid logs and unified organic/ad interaction history; the simulation simplifies many real-world complexities (exogenous fixed bids, first-price only, no strategic bidding).
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world data introduces distribution shifts, noisy bids, and strategic advertiser behavior that could break monotonicity assumptions or degrade relevance; the theoretical proofs assume fixed candidate sets and deterministic beam search.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Deployment on a controlled A/B test or offline evaluation on a dataset with real bids, multi-slot auctions, and evidence of bid shading; analysis of whether higher bids still monotonically increase exposure and whether organic rankings remain invariant.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does GEM-Rec scale to large-scale industrial inventory (millions of items) and long-sequence user histories, given the computational overhead of hierarchical beam search with logit modulation at every token step?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The experiments use medium-sized datasets (Steam, Amazon categories) with relatively small inventories; the hierarchical decoding strategy (flag sampling + content beam search) and prefix-aware bid aggregation require maintaining lookup tables and running beam search over semantic tokens, which may not scale efficiently.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not discuss latency, throughput, or memory requirements at production scale; prefix lookup tables could become huge, and beam search over structured semantic IDs may be slower than atomic ID retrieval.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Complexity analysis of decoding time and memory as functions of inventory size and semantic depth; production-scale benchmarks comparing GEM-Rec latency against baseline TIGER and traditional two-tower models; potential optimizations (e.g., caching, hierarchical pruning).

## Limitations
- **Training Data Assumption:** The core mechanism relies on historical logs reflecting a coherent, learnable signal for *successful* ad placements (where ads passed both platform bid filters *and* user relevance filters). Real-world logs are often noisy, with sparse or confounded signals (e.g., ads shown due to budget pacing, not pure relevance). This threatens the model’s ability to learn a meaningful `P(f)` for slot allocation.
- **Bid Aggregation Approximation:** Item-level boosting uses a single max-bid `B(c)` per hierarchical Semantic ID prefix. This assumes low intra-prefix bid variance. In domains with high bid heterogeneity (e.g., arbitrary product listings), this proxy could misdirect generation away from the literal highest-bidding eligible item.
- **Theoretical Guarantees' Strict Prerequisites:** Monotonicity and organic integrity proofs assume deterministic beam search with fixed tie-breaking and a static, bid-independent eligibility set `I_elig`. Real systems often use stochastic decoding (e.g., sampling) and dynamic eligibility filters (e.g., real-time relevance thresholds that could indirectly depend on bids), breaking the guarantees.

## Confidence
- **High:** Core factorization architecture (control tokens, joint modeling) is theoretically sound and clearly presented.
- **Medium:** Bid-Aware Decoding mechanism and its proposed monotonicity effect. The math is correct *under stated assumptions*, but real-world adherence to assumptions (e.g., static `I_elig`) is questionable.
- **Low:** Generalizability of the implicit "ad satisfaction" learning from unified logs. Evidence is largely paper-specific; corpus shows minimal parallel work on commercial tokenization in generative retrieval, suggesting this is a novel and unproven assumption in practice.

## Next Checks
1. **Test Ad Token Learning Under Log Noise:** Retrain GEM-Rec on synthetic logs with controlled noise (e.g., random ad injections, perturbed click signals) to quantify the degradation in `<AD>` token prediction accuracy and downstream revenue lifting. This directly tests the break condition of "noisy/sparse ad signals."
2. **Measure Proxy Error of Prefix-Based Boosting:** On a dataset with per-item bid data, compute the correlation between `B(c)` (max bid in prefix) and the true bid of the highest-bidding item *within that prefix* during inference. High variance would indicate a systemic misalignment between the boosting heuristic and true bid ranking.
3. **Stress-Test Organic Integrity with Stochastic Decoding:** Run inference with top-*k* sampling or temperature >0 while modulating bids. Measure if organic rankings (for `<ORG>` slots) change significantly across different `λ` values. Any change violates the structural consistency guarantee and reveals sensitivity to decoding stochasticity.
