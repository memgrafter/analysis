# One-for-All Model Initialization with Frequency-Domain Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07523
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07523
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A single pre-trained model can initialize diverse downstream architectures via low-frequency DCT coefficient transfer, accelerating convergence by up to 15× in vision and reducing training FLOPs by 40.5% in language tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FRONT, a training-free method to initialize arbitrary-sized models from a single pre-trained checkpoint. It reveals that a model's foundational, task-agnostic knowledge ("learngene") is concentrated in the low-frequency components of its weight matrices. By extracting these components via the 3D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and adapting them to target architectures through simple truncation or padding in the frequency domain, FRONT enables flexible model scaling without additional training costs. An optional spectral refinement step further improves transferability. Experiments across vision and language benchmarks demonstrate significant convergence speedups and robust cross-architecture initialization, challenging the paradigm of monolithic pre-trained checkpoints.

## Method Summary
FRONT operates in three stages. First, it applies a 3D-DCT to each relevant weight tensor (e.g., QKV projections in ViTs), decomposing it into frequency coefficients. The low-frequency coefficients (within a retention ratio `r`) are retained as the compact "learngene," while high-frequency components are discarded via a hard mask. Second, this spectral tensor is adapted to the target model's dimensions by zero-padding (if the target is larger) or truncation (if smaller) along each axis. Finally, an inverse 3D-DCT reconstructs the full-sized initialized weight tensor. An optional refinement phase adds a spectral regularizer during source model training to encourage knowledge concentration in the lowest frequencies, improving transfer purity. The entire process requires no gradient updates on the target model.

## Key Results
- In vision tasks (ImageNet), FRONT reduces convergence epochs by up to 15× compared to training from scratch.
- In language tasks (GLUE), it lowers required training FLOPs by an average of 40.5% while maintaining competitive accuracy.
- The method generalizes across architectures (ViTs, ResNets, BERT, GPT) and scales (depth/width), including heteromorphic transfers like BERT-to-GPT initialization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A model's foundational, task-agnostic knowledge ("learngene") is concentrated in the low-frequency components of its weight matrices.
- **Mechanism:** After applying 3D-DCT to weight tensors, the spectral magnitude is highest in low-frequency bands. This information remains stable across fine-tuning and model scaling, whereas high-frequency components are volatile and task-specific. The DCT's energy compaction property separates coarse, structural information (generalizable features) from fine-grained details (task-specific noise).
- **Core assumption:** Low-frequency subspaces encode universal inductive biases shared across tasks and scales.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "a model’s foundational, task-agnostic knowledge – its 'learngene' – is encoded within the low-frequency components"; [section 1, Figure 1] low-frequency similarity to pre-trained model persists across tasks/scales, high-frequency drops.
- **Break condition:** Severely undertrained source models or radical domain shifts (e.g., ImageNet → medical segmentation) may require higher `r` or extensive fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Low-frequency DCT coefficients can be transferred to target models of arbitrary size via truncation/padding in frequency domain.
- **Mechanism:** The linear, invertible DCT allows adaptation by resizing the coefficient tensor. Truncation discards high-frequency coefficients; padding adds zeros. The IDCT then reconstructs a full weight matrix whose effective structure is smoothly adjusted. This works because DCT basis functions are universal and fixed across model sizes.
- **Core assumption:** The low-frequency weight subspace is shared across architectures within a family (e.g., all Transformers).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.5] "flexibly accommodate varying architectures via zero-padding or truncation"; [section 4.2.1, Tables 1 & 2] successful initialization for DeiTs with varying depth/width.
- **Break condition:** Cross-family transfers (e.g., Transformer → MLP) are untested and likely fail due to mismatched tensor semantics. Extreme scaling (e.g., 100× parameters) introduces excessive zero-padded high frequencies, harming initialization.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A spectral regularizer during source model training purifies the learngene by forcing knowledge into lower frequencies, improving transfer robustness.
- **Mechanism:** A regularization term `L_reg` penalizes energy in frequency coefficients with a soft, exponentially decaying mask `M_r+`, applying stronger penalties to higher frequencies. This nudges the optimizer to store important parameters in the lowest bins, making subsequent hard truncation less lossy.
- **Core assumption:** Standard training inadvertently stores task-specific details in mid/high frequencies; explicit regularization can "purify" the generalizable core.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4] definition of `L_reg` and `M_r+`; [section 4.4.1, Figure 5] brief refinement (20 epochs) matches/exceeds 150-epoch FROM-SCRATCH FRONT+.
- **Break condition:** Over-regularization (high `λ`/low `γ_d`) suppresses too much information, making the learngene too coarse; under-regularization (`λ≈0`) reverts to direct extraction, which may be suboptimal.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) & Spectral Analysis**
  - **Why needed:** The method relies on DCT to decompose weight tensors into frequency components, separating generalizable low-frequency knowledge from task-specific high-frequency noise.
  - **Quick check:** Why is DCT preferred over DFT for real-valued weight matrices, and what does "low-frequency" intuitively represent in the reconstructed weight matrix?

- **Concept: Transformer & CNN Architecture Internals**
  - **Why needed:** Correct application of 3D-DCT requires understanding weight tensor shapes (e.g., ViT QKV: `[L, D, 3D]`). Misinterpreting axes corrupts the spectral decomposition.
  - **Quick check:** For a ViT `W_qkv` tensor of shape `[L, D, 3D]`, what do the three DCT dimensions conceptually correspond to after transformation?

- **Concept: Model Scaling Laws (Depth/Width)**
  - **Why needed:** The core value is initializing variable-sized models. Scaling laws predict how capacity changes inform whether truncation or padding occurs and help interpret performance tables.
  - **Quick check:** If you pad a low-frequency tensor in the frequency domain, what is the likely effect on the spatial-domain weight matrix's rank and smoothness?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Source Model -> [DCT -> Mask (r)] -> Learngene G -> [Pad/Truncate for L',d'] -> IDCT -> Target Init Weights -> Train -> Evaluate
  (Optional refinement inserts a training step between Source Model and DCT)

- **Critical path:**
  `Source Model -> DCT -> Mask(r) -> G -> Pad/Truncate -> IDCT -> Target Init Weights -> Train -> Evaluate`

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **`r` (frequency ratio):** Lower `r` increases compression but risks info loss; higher `r` includes more noise. Optimal range ~0.5–0.83 for ViTs.
  - **Refinement vs. cost:** FRONT (direct) is zero-cost; FRONT+ (from-scratch) is expensive (150 epochs); FRONT++ (fine-tune) is balanced (~20 epochs).
  - **Architecture family:** Works best intra-family (ViT→ViT, ResNet→ResNet). Cross-family transfers are untested and likely problematic.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Worse-than-scratch performance on small datasets (CUB, Cars) suggests `r` is too low or source is undertrained.
  - Identical FRONT and scratch training curves from epoch 1 indicate incorrect DCT axes or `r`≈1.
  - NaN/Inf during IDCT suggests extreme padding/truncation ratios or all-zero masked `G`.
  - Poor convergence on models with different block structures (e.g., Parallel Attention ViT) signals architecture incompatibility.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Spectral Stability Replication:** Fine-tune pre-trained model on two downstream tasks. Apply 3D-DCT to weights at epochs 0, 10, 50. Plot cosine similarity of low vs. high-frequency components to initial weights. Expect: low-freq ~1.0, high-freq drops quickly.
  2. **Direct Initialization Sanity Check:** Use DeiT-Ti source. Extract `G` with `r=0.5`. Initialize same-size DeiT-Ti. Train 10 epochs on ImageNet. Expect: accuracy ~60%+, far ahead of He-Init (~34%).
  3. **Width Expansion Test:** Source: DeiT-S (D=384). Target: DeiT-B (D=768). Pad in feature dimension. Train target 10 epochs. Expect: accuracy >70% (Table 2). If ~random (1%), padding logic is incorrect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What theoretical mechanism explains why low-frequency DCT components encode task-agnostic knowledge? (No formal theory provided; analogy to signal processing energy compaction.)
- What are the boundaries of heterogeneous transfer? Can FRONT initialize models with fundamentally different inductive biases (e.g., CNNs to Transformers)? (Only tested on Transformer-variants; cross-family untested.)
- Can optimal refinement hyperparameters (`λ`, `γ_d`, epochs) be automatically determined? (Manual grid search used; no automated method proposed.)
- Does FRONT extend to audio, video, or graph neural networks? (Evaluated only on vision/language; other modalities untested.)
- Should `r` be adapted per layer/component? (Uniform `r` used; adaptive schemes unexplored.)

## Limitations
- **Bias/LayerNorm handling:** Unclear whether biases and normalization parameters are included in DCT; omission may discard important signal.
- **Hyperparameter generalization:** Spectral refinement parameters (`λ`, `γ_d`) tuned only on ViT-ImageNet; effectiveness on other architectures/tasks unknown.
- **Cross-family transfer failure risk:** Assumes shared weight tensor semantics within a family; transfers between fundamentally different structures (e.g., CNN → Transformer) are not supported.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (low-frequency learngene concentration):** *Medium*. Strong empirical evidence (Figure 1) but no formal theory; depends on pre-training quality and task similarity.
- **Mechanism 2 (frequency-domain adaptation):** *High* for intra-family transfers (ViT→ViT, ResNet→ResNet) based on tables; *Low* for inter-family (no evidence).
- **Mechanism 3 (spectral refinement):** *Medium*. Ablations show gains but optimal `λ`/`γ_d` are fragile; over/under-regularization harms performance.
