# Conformalized Signal Temporal Logic Inference under Covariate Shift

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27062
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27062
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** Proposes a two-stage framework combining density-ratio weighting and Jensen-Rényi regularization to refine STL formulas under covariate shift, achieving lower misclassification rates and valid weighted conformal coverage with improved inefficiency.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning interpretable Signal Temporal Logic (STL) formulas for trajectory classification when training and deployment data distributions differ (covariate shift). It introduces a two-stage method: first, a differentiable learner (TLINet) produces an initial STL formula from source data; second, using a small amount of deployment-side data, the formula is refined by reweighting the loss with a kNN-estimated density ratio and regularizing with Jensen-Rényi divergence to align robustness distributions. The resulting formula is calibrated via weighted conformal prediction, providing finite-sample coverage guarantees under shift. Empirical evaluations on naval surveillance and robot motion datasets show reduced misclassification rates and valid, more efficient prediction sets compared to unshifted training baselines.

## Method Summary
The core problem is binary classification of trajectories using an STL formula φ_θ when P_train(X) ≠ P_dep(X) but P_train(Y|X) = P_dep(Y|X). The solution has three stages. First, TLINet is trained on D_train to minimize logistic loss over STL robustness ρ_φθ(X). Second, with a limited deployment dataset D_dep, the formula is refined by minimizing a combined objective: the original training loss plus a density-ratio-weighted loss on D_dep (using a kNN estimator of ω(X)=p_dep/p_train) plus a Jensen-Rényi divergence term that aligns the empirical distributions of robustness values over D_train and D_dep. Refinement terminates based on effective sample size (ESS) of the weights. Third, for deployment, a weighted conformal calibration threshold T_wcp is computed on a calibration set D_cal using the same density ratios, applied to nonconformity scores s = -Yρ_φθ(X). Prediction sets C(X) = {Y: s ≤ T_wcp} thus enjoy coverage guarantees under P_dep.

## Key Results
- Reduces misclassification rate (MCR) on deployment distribution compared to a formula trained only on source data.
- Achieves empirical coverage matching target miscoverage levels via weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift.
- Lowers prediction set inefficiency (average set size) relative to unrefined models, improving operational utility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Density-ratio weighting corrects the mismatched training and deployment distributions in both refinement and calibration. A kNN estimator in a fixed embedding space approximates ω⋆(X)=p_dep(X)/p_train(X). This weight scales the loss on D_dep and adjusts the weighted empirical CDF during conformal calibration, effectively reweighting expectations to align with P_dep. Success depends on a reliable embedding that preserves density relationships and a sufficient amount of deployment data; failure occurs if the kNN estimate is high-variance or concentrated.

### Mechanism 2
Jensen-Rényi divergence (JRD) regularization prevents overfitting to noisy density-ratio estimates by encouraging similar marginal distributions of robustness scores ρ_φθ over D_train and D_dep. Adding λ_JRD·JRD(Ê_train^θ, Ê_dep^θ) to the refinement objective stabilizes learning by promoting a formula whose robustness distribution generalizes better across shifts. An inappropriate λ_JRD—too small or too large—harms MCR by either under-regularizing or distorting the decision boundary.

### Mechanism 3
Weighted conformal prediction leverages the same density ratios to compute a quantile threshold T_wcp from a weighted empirical CDF of nonconformity scores on D_cal. Under the covariate shift assumption (P(Y|X) invariant) and with accurate ω⋆(X), this threshold inherits finite-sample coverage guarantees for P_dep. The guarantee's finite-sample properties degrade if the effective sample size of the weights is small, leading to variable thresholds and under-coverage.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** TLINet (initial STL learner) -> Density-ratio estimator (kNN) -> Shift-aware refinement module (weighted loss + JRD) -> Weighted CP calibrator -> Deployment predictor
- **Critical path:** Train φ_θ0 on D_train → Estimate ω(X) on D_train ∪ D_dep using kNN → Refine φ_θ by minimizing L(θ) with weighted loss and JRD → Compute T_wcp on D_cal with same ω(X) → Deploy C(X) = {Y: -Yρ_φθ(X) ≤ T_wcp}
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Stability vs. adaptation:* High λ_JRD stabilizes but may under-adapt; aggressive ω(X) (wide range) risks overfitting D_dep.
  - *Computation vs. ESS:* More D_dep improves density estimation but increases refinement cost; ESS criterion balances this.
  - *Embedding choice:* The fixed embedding f(X) for kNN must preserve relevant trajectory geometry; poor choice degrades density-ratio accuracy.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - ESS(D_dep) ≪ |D_dep| during refinement → weight concentration, high variance.
  - Coverage on deployment test set << 1-α → weight estimation error or violated covariate shift assumption.
  - MCR(P_dep) worse than initial φ_θ0 after refinement → overfitting to D_dep or mis-specified λ_JRD.
- **First 3 experiments:**

  2. Plot empirical coverage vs. inefficiency for weighted CP on refined model at target α.
  3. Ablate ESS termination criterion (ε variation) to assess impact on final MCR and coverage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-class extension:** How to extend the framework to multi-class STL inference while retaining guarantees under covariate shift? (Conclusion identifies this as future work.)
- **Advanced adaptation:** What domain adaptation techniques (e.g., adversarial training, invariant risk minimization) better handle severe shift compared to density-ratio weighting and JRD? (Conclusion calls for investigating more advanced strategies.)
- **Weight error sensitivity:** How does density-ratio estimation error affect weighted CP coverage, and can robust weighting schemes mitigate this? (Not analyzed; inferred need for theoretical and empirical study of weight error impact.)

## Limitations
- The critical embedding function f(X) for kNN density estimation is not specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Jensen-Rényi divergence implementation details (binning, kernel choices) are omitted, leaving practical computation ambiguous.
- Datasets (Naval Surveillance, VIMA) are not publicly released with preprocessing scripts, preventing independent verification.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on accurate ideal density ratios; in practice, kNN estimates may have high error with limited deployment data.
- Covariate shift assumption (invariant P(Y|X)) may not hold in real-world deployments, limiting applicability.

## Confidence
- **MCR reduction under shift:** *Medium*. Two datasets show improvement; limited diversity raises overfitting concerns.
- **Weighted CP coverage validity:** *Medium-High*. Theoretical foundation is sound, but empirical weight accuracy is uncertain.
- **Interpretability of output formula:** *High*. TLINet produces symbolic STL; refinement preserves structure.
- **Inefficiency reduction:** *Medium*. Gains shown on test datasets; generalization to other domains unclear.
