# PRECTR-V2:Unified Relevance-CTR Framework with Cross-User Preference Mining, Exposure Bias Correction, and LLM-Distilled Encoder Optimization

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.20676
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20676
*   **Reference count:** 39
*   **Primary result:** Offline AUC 0.7674 / GAUC 0.6933 (exceeding PRECTR by 0.0093/0.0041) and online per capita orders +1.39% / GMV +3.18%.

## Executive Summary
PRECTR-V2 is a unified relevance-CTR framework for e-commerce search that addresses three core production challenges: cold-start personalization, exposure bias, and the efficiency-accuracy trade-off of text encoders. It mines cross-user preferences within query categories to augment sparse low-activity user data, corrects bias through synthetic hard negatives and a calibrated pairwise loss, and replaces a frozen BERT with a lightweight, LLM-distilled encoder for end-to-end optimization. The system yields significant offline gains in AUC/GAUC and robust online improvements in orders and GMV while reducing PCOC deviation.

## Method Summary
The framework integrates three novel components. First, for cold-start users, it samples sequences from top frequent users in the same query category and applies Multi-Head Target-Attention to derive user-level and category-level relevance embeddings, weighted by a MoE network to produce an incentive score τ. Second, exposure bias is mitigated by constructing synthetic hard negatives: positives with high relevance scores are paired with negatives where the relevance label is randomly lowered (to 1–3) and Gaussian noise is added to the item embedding. A pairwise ranking loss with a margin and a dynamic truncation weight (zeroing loss if batch-average scores exceed a threshold) balances ranking improvement with CTR calibration. Third, a parameter-efficient 3-layer transformer encoder is pre-trained via relevance classification SFT and knowledge distillation from Qwen-7B (using RAG-based templates). This encoder replaces a frozen BERT and is fine-tuned jointly with the CTR model, satisfying latency constraints while improving semantic alignment.

## Key Results
*   Offline AUC 0.7674 and GAUC 0.6933, surpassing PRECTR by +0.0093 and +0.0041.
*   Online A/B test: +1.39% per capita orders and +3.18% GMV growth.
*   PCOC deviation reduced from 2.3% to 1.7% after debiasing regularization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cross-User Preference Mining for Cold-Start
*   **Claim:** Mitigates the sparse-behavior problem for low-activity users by transferring global relevance preferences within a query category.
*   **Mechanism:** For a cold-start user u, their short click sequence S<sub>u</sub> is augmented with cross-user sequences S<sub>q</sub> sampled from top-N frequent transaction users in the same query category. All queries/items are encoded by a lightweight transformer. Target-Attention uses the current query as Q and the two sequences as K, V to produce user-level (r<sub>user</sub>) and category-level (r<sub>cate</sub>) relevance embeddings. A MoE gating network weights two expert functions to output a final incentive score τ that calibrates the CTR prediction.
*   **Break condition:** If query-category detection is poor or sampled cross-user behaviors are thematically noisy, τ becomes unreliable and degrades personalization.

### Mechanism 2: Exposure Bias Correction via Synthetic Hard Negatives
*   **Claim:** Synthetic negatives and a calibrated pairwise loss correct for position/exposure bias while preserving absolute CTR calibration (PCOC).
*   **Mechanism:** Positives with click=1 and rsl=4 are paired with negatives created by (a) lowering the rsl label (randomly to 1–3 using p<sub>1</sub>, p<sub>2</sub>) and (b) adding Gaussian noise to the item text embedding. The model f computes a pair-wise loss: ∑log(1+exp(max(0, margin – (f(x⁺) – f(x⁻))))). A dynamic weight w(x) zeros the loss if the batch-average positive score exceeds a threshold, preventing over-penalization.
*   **Break condition:** Inappropriate noise scale, label drop probabilities, margin, or threshold values cause either insufficient bias correction or PCOC collapse.

### Mechanism 3: LLM-Distilled Lightweight Encoder
*   **Claim:** A small, trainable encoder replaces frozen BERT, enabling joint optimization of semantics and CTR while meeting latency SLAs.
*   **Mechanism:** A 3-layer transformer (~2M params) is pre-trained in two stages: (1) Relevance classification SFT using cross-entropy on rsl labels; (2) Knowledge distillation from Qwen-7B via MSE of text embeddings, augmented with RAG-based templates. The combined loss 𝓡 = 𝓡<sub>distill</sub> + 𝓡<sub>SFT</sub> yields a teachable student. This encoder is inserted into the CTR model and fine-tuned end-to-end with a reduced learning rate.
*   **Break condition:** Domain mismatch between LLM training data and e-commerce text, or insufficient student capacity, degrades embedding quality and offline AUC/GAUC.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Cross-User Preference Transfer  
  *Why needed:* Cold-start users have too few interactions to model personal relevance; global patterns within the same query category provide a proxy.  
  *Quick check:* Does the sampling use a similarity threshold on query embeddings, or simply rely on category labels?
*   **Concept:** Synthetic Hard Negative Sampling  
  *Why needed:* Training data is dominated by high-relevance exposures; synthetic negatives expose the model to the low-relevance tail, reducing overfitting.  
  *Quick check:* Are negatives constructed by only modifying the rsl label and adding noise while keeping all other features identical to the positive?
*   **Concept:** Knowledge Distillation for Encoder Efficiency  
  *Why needed:* A full BERT is too heavy for online serving; a distilled tiny encoder retains semantic richness and meets latency constraints.  
  *Quick check:* Is the student encoder trained with both classification (SFT) and embedding (distillation) losses, and is its parameter count compatible with the serving envelope?
*   **Concept:** Mixture-of-Experts for Calibration  
  *Why needed:* A single scoring function may not effectively combine user-specific and category-level signals; MoE allows adaptive weighting.  
  *Quick check:* Are the two expert functions simple (e.g., log(1+exp) and -log(1+exp)) and does the gating network use only the combined embeddings as input?
*   **Concept:** Pairwise Ranking with Dynamic Truncation  
  *Why needed:* A standard pairwise loss can distort absolute probability calibration (PCOC); dynamic truncation prevents over-penalizing already well-ranked positives.  
  *Quick check:* Is the dynamic weight w(x) computed per-batch, and does it zero out loss for all samples if the mean positive score exceeds a fixed threshold?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Cold-Start Module -> Debias Module -> Lightweight Text Encoder -> Base Feature MLP -> CTR Heads
*   **Critical path:** Input features -> generate <query,item> text -> if cold-start, augment with cross-user sequences -> compute text embeddings via lightweight encoder -> derive r<sub>user</sub>, r<sub>cate</sub> -> get τ -> feed embeddings + other features into MLP -> output P(rsl|x) and P(click|rsl,x) -> combine for final CTR score -> apply pairwise loss on positive/hard-negative pairs during training.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Latency vs expressiveness (smaller encoder risks semantic loss; distillation/SFT mitigate); pairwise ranking vs calibration (stronger ranking improves order metrics but may distort PCOC; margin/dynamic truncation seek balance); cross-user sample size (more samples improve coverage but increase compute and potential noise; 50 is a pragmatic default).
*   **Failure signatures:** Cold-start: high variance in τ for same user across sessions, or GAUC drop for new-user segment; Debias: PCOC deviation >2% indicates calibration failure, or pairwise loss saturates quickly; Encoder: drop in validation relevance-classification accuracy or large KL divergence between teacher/student embeddings; Global: online per-capita orders or GMV lower than baseline post-rollout.
*   **First 3 experiments:** 1) Cold-start ablation: Remove cross-user augmentation; measure AUC/GAUC on low-activity users (≤5 clicks). 2) Hard-negative hyper-parameter sweep: Grid-search p<sub>1</sub>, p<sub>2</sub>, noise std, margin, dynamic threshold; track PCOC and overall AUC. 3) Encoder integration test: Replace frozen BERT with distilled encoder; run offline evaluation and profile inference latency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   How does the granularity of query categories affect the efficacy of cross-user preference mining for cold-start users?
*   To what extent do the hyperparameters in exposure bias correction (e.g., p1, p2, threshold, margin) generalize across diverse data distributions and temporal shifts?
*   Does the lightweight encoder retain sufficient semantic richness for relevance modeling compared to frozen BERT, despite a 98% reduction in parameters?
*   What are the primary factors contributing to the residual PCOC deviation (1.7%) after applying debiasing regularization, and can it be further reduced without harming ranking performance?

## Limitations
*   Query-category definition for cross-user mining is unclear; ambiguous granularity may affect sampling relevance.
*   RAG-based LLM distillation pipeline (retrieval source, templates, prompts) is underspecified, hindering reproducibility.
*   Synthetic negative hyper-parameters and dynamic truncation threshold lack sensitivity analysis across distributions.

## Confidence
*   Cross-User Preference Mining: **Medium** – novel approach described in detail, but cross-user transferability not externally validated.
*   Exposure Bias Correction: **Medium** – pairwise loss with synthetic negatives plausible, yet realism of generated negatives uncertain.
*   LLM-Distilled Encoder: **Medium** – distillation design sound, but domain alignment of teacher embeddings not demonstrated.

## Next Checks
1.  Cold-start ablation: Remove cross-user augmentation; measure GAUC on low-activity segment (≤5 clicks) to isolate impact.
2.  Debias hyper-parameter sweep: Vary noise std, margin, and dynamic weight threshold; track PCOC deviation and overall AUC to identify stable configurations.
3.  Encoder reliability test: Evaluate student encoder on relevance classification accuracy and KL divergence vs. Qwen-7B; benchmark inference latency against SLA (e.g., ≤50 ms).
