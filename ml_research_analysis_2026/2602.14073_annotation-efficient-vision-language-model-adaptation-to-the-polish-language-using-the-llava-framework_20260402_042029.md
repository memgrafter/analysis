# Annotation-Efficient Vision-Language Model Adaptation to the Polish Language Using the LLaVA Framework

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.14073
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14073
*   **Reference count:** 27
*   **Primary result:** A fully automated translation and filtering pipeline with Polish-native LLM backbones yields a +9.55 percentage point gain on Polish MMBench (79.35% vs 69.80%) and superior linguistic correctness in human evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to adapt vision-language models (VLMs) to Polish by leveraging a fully automated pipeline. Existing English-centric multimodal datasets are translated into Polish using a strong multilingual model (Tower+ 72B), and low-quality translations are filtered via the COMET metric. The adapted models use Polish-specific instruction-tuned language models (PLLuM, Bielik) as backends and a SigLIP2 vision encoder. Training follows a two-stage procedure with LoRA for parameter efficiency. The resulting models significantly outperform prior English-aligned VLMs on Polish benchmarks and demonstrate high linguistic correctness in human evaluations, proving that large-scale automated translation can effectively bootstrap VLMs for low-resource languages with minimal manual annotation.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a scalable, annotation-free data pipeline for Polish VLM adaptation. English instruction-tuning datasets (e.g., LLaVA-Instruct, A-OKVQA) are translated to Polish using Tower+ 72B. Each translated question-answer pair is scored by a reference-less COMET model; pairs falling below dataset-specific thresholds are discarded to ensure linguistic quality. Synthetic OCR data is added via SynthDoG-PL to improve text-in-image understanding. Models are built using the LLaVA-Next architecture, pairing a SigLIP2 vision encoder with a Polish-native LLM (PLLuM-12B or Bielik-11B). Training is two-stage: Stage 1 trains only the randomly initialized MLP projector on 595K filtered image-caption pairs. Stage 2 unfreezes the vision encoder and applies LoRA (rank 128) to the LLM, training on a 2M-sample mixture of 85% Polish-translated and 15% original English instructions.

## Key Results
*   **Polish MMBench:** The proposed model achieves 79.35% accuracy, a 9.55 percentage point improvement over the LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-13B baseline (69.80%).
*   **Human Evaluation (XM3600):** The model wins against Pixtral-12B in linguistic correctness with up to 84% win rate in head-to-head comparisons.
*   **Efficiency:** High performance is achieved with minimal manual annotation and parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning, preserving the Polish LLM's linguistic knowledge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Large-scale automated translation with lightweight quality filtering can bootstrap high-quality multimodal training data for low-resource languages without extensive manual annotation.
*   **Mechanism:** A strong multilingual LLM (Tower+ 72B) translates English-centric VLM datasets into Polish. A reference-less COMET metric filters out low-quality QA pairs per dataset via thresholding, controlling noise while scaling data creation.
*   **Evidence:** [abstract] describes the fully automated pipeline; [section 4.1.1] details translation, COMET filtering, and threshold variation; neighboring work on multilingual VLMs (e.g., *Maya*) supports this approach.
*   **Break condition:** Systematic "translationese," semantic drift, or mis-calibrated thresholds (too inclusive/exclusive) degrade performance, especially on culturally specific or morphologically complex tasks.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Using Polish-native, instruction-tuned LLM backbones (PLLuM, Bielik) preserves morphological and syntactic competence crucial for Polish, allowing the vision-language adapter to focus on cross-modal alignment.
*   **Mechanism:** The LLaVA-Next MLP projector maps visual features into the embedding space of a Polish-optimized LLM. The LLM's pre-existing knowledge of Polish inflection and idiom enables coherent output generation once visual grounding is learned.
*   **Evidence:** [section 2.3] discusses Polish linguistic complexity and notes PLLuM/Bielik are optimized for Polish; [section 6.1/figures] show our models produce grammatically correct Polish while baselines like Vicuna fail.
*   **Break condition:** The Polish LLM backbone may lack sufficient multimodal instruction-tuning exposure, leading to incoherent outputs despite correct morphology if projector training is inadequate.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A two-stage training strategy (projector-only pre-alignment, then vision encoder + LoRA on LLM) efficiently transfers visual knowledge to the Polish LLM while preserving its linguistic prior and managing computational cost.
*   **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains only the MLP projector on captions, aligning visual features with the frozen Polish LLM's token space. Stage 2 unfreezes the vision encoder and applies LoRA to the LLM on a diverse instruction mix, preventing catastrophic forgetting.
*   **Evidence:** [section 3] describes the two-stage process; [table 5] lists learning rates and trainable parameters; results show gains over full fine-tuning baselines with fewer trained LLM parameters.
*   **Break condition:** Inadequate Stage 1 pre-alignment (too few samples, poor captions) hinders Stage 2; LoRA rank too low underfits cross-modal patterns, too high risks overfitting or high cost.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** LLaVA-style vision-language architecture
    *   **Why needed:** The work reproduces and adapts the LLaVA-Next framework. Understanding the roles of the frozen vision encoder, trainable MLP projector, and language model is essential to grasp where Polish adaptation occurs.
    *   **Quick check:** In LLaVA, what is the purpose of the MLP projector, and why is it typically the only component trained in the first stage?
*   **Concept:** Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)
    *   **Why needed:** The paper uses LoRA to adapt the Polish LLM backbone during instruction tuning, avoiding full fine-tuning. This is a core design choice for efficiency and preservation of linguistic knowledge.
    *   **Quick check:** How does LoRA achieve parameter efficiency, and what do the rank (r) and alpha (α) parameters control?
*   **Concept:** Machine translation quality estimation (COMET)
    *   **Why needed:** The pipeline relies on COMET, a reference-less metric, to filter automatically translated multimodal data. Understanding its role and limitations is key to evaluating the data pipeline's robustness.
    *   **Quick check:** What does a COMET score measure, and why might it be preferred over simple BLEU for filtering conversational QA pairs?
*   **Concept:** Morphological complexity in NLP
    *   **Why needed:** Polish's rich inflection (cases, gender, aspect) is a primary motivator for using native LLMs. This concept explains why a generic English-aligned model would fail even with perfect vision.
    *   **Quick check:** Provide an example of how Polish case inflection would affect a VLM's answer to "Where is the cat?" (e.g., "W zmywarce" vs. "W zmywarkę").

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    SigLIP2 Vision Encoder -> MLP Projector -> Polish LLM Backbone (PLLuM/Bielik)
    (Projector trained in both stages; Vision Encoder unfrozen in Stage 2; LLM adapted via LoRA in Stage 2)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Acquire English VLM datasets (LLaVA-Instruct, etc.).
    2.  Translate all QA turns with Tower+ 72B.
    3.  Compute COMET score per QA pair; drop below dataset-specific threshold.
    4.  Pre-train MLP projector on 595K filtered captions (Stage 1).
    5.  Prepare Stage 2 mixture: 85% Polish-translated, 15% English-original.
    6.  Train vision encoder (unfrozen) + LoRA on LLM with the instruction mixture (Stage 2).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Translation vs. Manual Curation:** Chose scalable automated translation over slow manual Polish data creation. Tradeoff: Risk of translation artifacts vs. speed/coverage.
    *   **SigLIP2 vs. CLIP:** Selected SigLIP2 for stronger multilingual visual-text alignment. Tradeoff: Potential compute overhead vs. better OCR/cultural text handling.
    *   **LoRA vs. Full Fine-tuning:** Used LoRA (r=128) on LLM to preserve Polish linguistic prior and reduce memory. Tradeoff: Possibly lower capacity for new cross-modal patterns vs. efficiency/stability.
    *   **85:15 Polish:English mix:** Maintained some English data to avoid catastrophic forgetting of English capabilities. Tradeoff: Dilutes Polish specialization vs. retains broad usability.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Data-level:** COMET threshold too low → noisy translations; too high → poor coverage. Synthetic OCR lacks real-world variance → fails on real Polish text.
    *   **Training-level:** LoRA rank too low → can't learn new connections; vision encoder frozen too long → poor alignment.
    *   **Evaluation-level:** High score on translated MMBench but fails on authentic Polish cultural references due to dataset bias.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate translation filtering:** Translate 1,000 LLaVA-Instruct samples with Tower+72B, apply COMET at thresholds 0.4/0.6/0.8. Manually inspect 50 pairs per threshold to estimate precision/recall of quality.
    2.  **Projector alignment sanity check:** Train *only* the projector on the 595K filtered captions for 1 epoch. Generate captions for 100 held-out images; check for coherent Polish (even if visually inaccurate).
    3.  **LoRA rank sweep:** After Stage 1, run a small Stage 2 with LoRA ranks {64, 128, 256} on a 10% instruction subset. Evaluate on a 200-sample Polish MMBench slice to identify optimal rank within memory constraints.

## Limitations
*   The exact COMET filtering thresholds per dataset are undisclosed and likely hand-tuned, limiting generalizability.
*   The sampling strategy to achieve the precise 85:15 Polish-English mix in Stage 2 is unspecified.
*   Evaluation relies on a machine-translated then human-corrected MMBench, which may not fully capture authentic Polish cultural/linguistic nuance.

## Confidence
*   **Translation-based data bootstrapping:** Medium. Pipeline is clear and results are positive, but break conditions (translationese, cultural drift) lack direct ablation. COMET's downstream utility correlation is assumed.
*   **Polish-native LLM backbone necessity:** Medium. Strong qualitative evidence shows Vicuna failures on morphology, but no direct backbone ablation study exists. Claim hinges on assumed linguistic competence of PLLuM/Bielik.
*   **Two-stage training efficacy:** High. Procedure and parameters are explicit, and results show clear gains. LoRA choice is justified by efficiency and memory constraints.
