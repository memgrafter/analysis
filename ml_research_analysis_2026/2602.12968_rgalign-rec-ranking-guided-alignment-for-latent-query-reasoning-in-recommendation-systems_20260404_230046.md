# RGAlign-Rec: Ranking-Guided Alignment for Latent Query Reasoning in Recommendation Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12968
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12968
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Offline on Shopee: GAUC +0.12% (3.52% error reduction), Recall@3 +0.56%, NDCG@3 +0.35%; Online A/B: QE-Rec CTR@3 +0.98%, IHR +1.43%; RGAlign-Rec adds CTR@3 +0.13%, IHR +0.30%.

## Executive Summary
RGAlign-Rec tackles the semantic gap and objective misalignment in e-commerce chatbot proactive intent prediction. It introduces a three-stage Ranking-Guided Alignment (RGA) framework that couples a large language model (LLM) reasoner with a Query-Enhanced (QE) ranking model. First, a QE-Rec model is trained using frozen LLM last-token embeddings. Second, QE-Rec acts as a reward to select optimal queries from multiple teacher LLMs via best-of-N sampling; the selected queries then fine-tune the LLM through ranking-guided supervised fine-tuning (RG-SFT) and contrastive learning (RG-CL). Third, QE-Rec is retrained with the aligned LLM in a closed loop. On Shopee data, the approach yields offline gains in GAUC, Recall@3, and NDCG@3, and online A/B improvements in CTR@3 and IHR.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three stages. Stage 1: Train a Query-Enhanced Ranking model (QE-Rec) with a frozen LLM reasoner (Qwen3-4B). User features are verbalized into text, processed by the LLM, and the last-token pooling embedding is projected by a query tower. QE-Rec combines user, intent, and query representations via weighted cosine similarities and is trained with a ListNet KL loss and a confidence predictor. Stage 2: Use the trained QE-Rec as a reward to score candidate queries generated by multiple teacher LLMs; the best query (by NDCG@3) is selected and used to fine-tune the LLM via RG-SFT and RG-CL, aligning the LLM’s outputs and hidden states with ranking objectives. Stage 3: Retrain QE-Rec from scratch using embeddings from the aligned LLM, producing the final RGAlign-Rec system.

## Key Results
- Offline on Shopee: GAUC +0.12% (3.52% error reduction), Recall@3 +0.56%, NDCG@3 +0.35%.
- Online QE-Rec (first stage): CTR@3 +0.98%, IHR +1.43%.
- RGAlign-Rec (full) incremental gains: CTR@3 +0.13%, IHR +0.30%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Last Token Pooling (LTP)
- **Claim**: Last Token Pooling extracts a high-fidelity semantic representation from the LLM that is better suited for ranking than alternative pooling methods.
- **Mechanism**: The LLM reasoner processes feature verbalization and uses the hidden state of the final token as the user's latent query embedding. This embedding is projected by the query tower and combined with user and intent embeddings in the scoring function.
- **Assumption**: The final token in a decoder-only LLM aggregates global context for generation, making its hidden state an effective bottleneck for semantic compression.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract mentions "last-token-pooling embeddings".
  - Section 3.2 describes LTP in detail and contrasts with mean pooling and BGE.
  - Corpus: limited direct citations; related work exists but LTP-specific evidence is primarily from this paper.
- **Break condition**: If the LLM architecture is not decoder-only or if the task requires token-level nuances, LTP may fail.

### Mechanism 2: Ranking-Guided Alignment (RGA)
- **Claim**: RGA uses the QE-Rec model as a reward to select optimal queries and fine-tune the LLM reasoner, aligning its output distribution with ranking objectives.
- **Mechanism**: Multi-LLM sampling generates candidate queries; QE-Rec scores them (via NDCG); best queries are selected for RG-SFT and RG-CL, which adjust the LLM's generation policy and hidden states towards ranking-optimal representations.
- **Assumption**: The QE-Rec model provides a reliable, differentiable proxy for downstream ranking performance that can guide LLM alignment.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract states "Ranking-Guided Alignment (RGA)" and "utilizes downstream ranking signals as feedback".
  - Section 4.2 details the RGA process including best-of-N, RG-SFT, and RG-CL.
  - Corpus: Related work on RLHF/RLAIF for recommendation exists, but using ranking model as reward is a novel twist; no direct citations yet.
- **Break condition**: If QE-Rec is poorly calibrated or if the best-of-N pool lacks diversity, alignment may converge to suboptimal queries.

### Mechanism 3: Closed-loop Calibration
- **Claim**: Closed-loop calibration iteratively refines both the LLM reasoner and QE-Rec, ensuring mutual enhancement and preventing semantic drift.
- **Mechanism**: After RGA, the aligned LLM generates new embeddings; QE-Rec is retrained with these, forming a feedback loop that synchronizes the two components.
- **Assumption**: The iterative process converges to a stable equilibrium where semantic and ranking spaces are aligned.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract mentions "closed-loop alignment framework".
  - Section 4.3 describes the closed-loop calibration stage.
  - Corpus: No existing work on closed-loop between generative reasoner and ranking model; this is a novel contribution.
- **Break condition**: If the loop oscillates or one component overfits, performance may degrade; early stopping is needed.

## Foundational Learning

### Last Token Pooling (LTP)
- **Why needed**: To convert the LLM's sequential output into a fixed-dimensional embedding that captures the overall semantic intent from user features.
- **Quick check**: Why might the last token's hidden state be more effective than mean pooling for a decoder-only LLM in a recommendation task?

### Reward Model from Ranking (QE-Rec)
- **Why needed**: To provide a scalable, automated feedback signal that reflects task-specific ranking performance, avoiding costly human annotations.
- **Quick check**: How does using NDCG from QE-Rec as a reward differ from traditional human preference labels in RLHF?

### Contrastive Alignment (RG-CL)
- **Why needed**: To align the geometric structure of the LLM's latent space with the ranking-optimized semantic space, which is more continuous and robust than token-level preference optimization.
- **Quick check**: Why might representation-level contrastive learning be less prone to reward hacking compared to DPO?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Raw user features (profile, behavior, context) -> Feature verbalization -> LLM reasoner (Qwen3-4B) -> Last-token pooling -> Query tower (MLP) -> Query embedding. Parallel: User tower (hierarchical encoder) -> User embedding; Intent tower (ID + frozen HLLM) -> Intent embedding. Scoring: s = 0.7·cos(z_u,z_i) + 0.3·cos(z_i,z_q). RGA: multi-LLM sampler -> QE-Rec reward (NDCG@3) -> best-of-N selector -> RG-SFT + RG-CL on LLM. Closed-loop: aligned LLM -> recompute embeddings -> retrain QE-Rec.

**Critical path**:
1. Stage 1: Train QE-Rec with frozen LLM (LTP) and fixed intent encoder.
2. Stage 2: Use QE-Rec as reward; generate queries from multiple LLMs; select best; apply RG-SFT and RG-CL to align LLM.
3. Stage 3: Retrain QE-Rec using embeddings from aligned LLM; deploy RGAlign-Rec.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- LTP vs. BGE: LTP is end-to-end trainable and captures decoder-specific context but is tied to LLM architecture; BGE uses external encoder, decoupling semantics but may misalign with ranking.
- Best-of-N strategies: V1 (full) maximizes candidate diversity but risks self-fitting; V2 (non-Qwen3) forces external diversity but reduces data scale.
- RG-CL vs. RG-DPO: CL operates on hidden states, providing smoother gradients and better scalability; DPO models token-level preferences but may suffer from noisy pairs and gradient instability.

**Failure signatures**:
- LTP embeddings fail to improve over random: indicates LLM cannot capture relevant semantics or feature verbalization is inadequate.
- RG-SFT leads to performance drop: suggests overfitting to teacher LLM queries or reward over-optimization.
- Online CTR gain erodes after closed-loop: indicates instability in iterative retraining, possibly due to distribution shift.

## Limitations
- Private Shopee data blocks external replication; surrogate datasets may not match distribution.
- Teacher LLMs (Gemini/GPT-5) inaccessible; open‑source substitutes likely differ in capability.
- HLLM intent encoder unspecified; arbitrary choice may skew results.

## Confidence
- LTP effectiveness: Medium (Evidence from ablation studies shows gains, but mechanism's dependence on decoder-only LLMs is not thoroughly validated across architectures; future checks are needed.)
- RGA mechanism: Medium (The use of QE-Rec as a reward is novel and yields improvements, yet the relative contributions of RG-SFT and RG-CL are not isolated, and the best-of-N sampling strategy could introduce bias.)
- Closed-loop calibration: Low (No convergence analysis or stability guarantees are provided; the possibility of oscillation or overfitting across iterations is noted as a failure signature, and the approach requires careful early stopping.)

## Next Checks
1. LTP architecture dependence: Replace Qwen3-4B with LLaMA-3-8B (decoder-only) and T5-XL (encoder-decoder); compare LTP vs. mean/BGE pooling.
2. Reward model sanity: Human annotators rank best-of-N queries selected by QE-Rec vs. random samples; measure agreement.
3. Loop stability protocol: Run 0–5 closed-loop iterations on held-out set; track GAUC/NDCG variance and embedding drift.
