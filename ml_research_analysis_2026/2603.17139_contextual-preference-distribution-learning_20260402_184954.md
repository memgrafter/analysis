## Executive Summary
Contextual Preference Distribution Learning (CPDL) addresses uncertainty arising from context-dependent human preferences in decision-making. It learns a model that maps contextual features to full preference distributions using a bounded-variance score function gradient estimator, producing a maximum likelihood estimate. The learned distributions can generate scenarios for downstream risk-averse optimization. In a synthetic ridesharing environment, CPDL reduces average post-decision surprise by up to 114× compared to a risk-neutral approach with perfect predictions and by up to 25× versus leading risk-averse baselines, while also achieving the lowest test loss across all evaluated methods.

## Method Summary
The authors did not provide reproduction notes. Based on the brief description, CPDL constructs a model that predicts preference distributions conditioned on context. Training employs a score function gradient estimator with bounded variance to maximize the likelihood of observed preferences. The resulting distribution model is then used to sample scenarios for a risk-averse optimization module. ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Context -> Encoder -> Distribution Parameter Network -> Preference Distribution -> Scenario Sampler -> Risk-Averse Optimizer

**Critical Path**:  
The system receives contextual input, encodes it into features, passes these to a network that outputs parameters of a preference distribution. This distribution is sampled to produce scenarios, which are fed to an optimizer that selects actions minimizing a risk-averse criterion.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Variance reduction vs. bias in the gradient estimator.
- Model capacity vs. risk of overfitting to simulated data.
- Number of sampled scenarios vs. computational budget.
- Flexibility of the preference distribution family vs. tractability of optimization.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Unstable training (diverging or exploding gradients).
- Poor calibration of predicted distributions (high held-out negative log-likelihood).
- Scenario set failing to capture decision-relevant tail outcomes.
- Optimizer consistently yielding suboptimal actions compared to baselines.

**First Experiments**:
