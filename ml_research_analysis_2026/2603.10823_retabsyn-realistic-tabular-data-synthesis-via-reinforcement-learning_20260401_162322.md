# ReTabSyn: Realistic Tabular Data Synthesis via Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10823
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10823
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** ReTabSyn consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (GReaT, TVAE, TabSyn, PTA, SynRL) on small-data, imbalanced, and distribution-shifted tabular benchmarks by targeting the conditional distribution via reinforcement learning.

## Executive Summary
ReTabSyn addresses the limitations of deep generative models in low-data, imbalanced tabular settings by shifting the objective from modeling the full joint distribution to prioritizing the conditional distribution \(P(y|X)\), which is critical for downstream utility. It employs an oracle-free preference construction strategy (perturbing target labels and correlated features) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to fine-tune a language model generator. This two-stage pipeline (augmentation + DPO) yields synthetic data with high downstream task performance, statistical fidelity, and reduced privacy leakage. Extensive experiments across challenging regimes (sample sizes 32–512, 1% class prevalence, and covariate shift) confirm substantial gains over existing methods.

## Method Summary
ReTabSyn uses a two-stage pipeline. First, real data are augmented via SMOTE-like interpolation within categorical buckets to enlarge the manifold and prevent overfitting; the augmentation factor \(M(N)\) scales dynamically with the original sample size \(N\). A GPT-2 language model, serializing rows with the GREAT framework (random feature order, target as completion), is pre-trained on the augmented data to obtain a base policy \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\). Second, preference pairs are constructed from the *original* real samples: with 70% probability (Type I) the target label is flipped while features are fixed; with 30% probability (Type II) a feature in a strongly correlated pair is perturbed while its partner is held constant. The generator is then fine-tuned with DPO (β=0.1, λ=0.1) to increase the log-likelihood margin between chosen and rejected completions, thereby sharpening \(P(y|X)\) while regularizing with reference policy \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\). Synthetic data is produced by sampling from the fine-tuned model.

## Key Results
- Outperforms SOTA baselines (GReaT, TVAE, TabSyn, PTA, SynRL) on small-data (32–512 rows), extreme class imbalance (1% prevalence), and distribution-shifted splits.
- Achieves higher AUROC than real data in some small-data regimes; on Adult under 1% imbalance, PR-AUC 0.906 vs. SMOTE 0.891; on shifted splits, AUC 0.863 (real 0.868).
- Improves fidelity (mean correlation similarity 0.83) and reduces privacy leakage (membership inference AUC 0.62) compared to SMOTE (0.83).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conditional Distribution Prioritization
- **Claim:** Prioritizing \(P(y|X)\) over the full joint maximizes downstream utility in low-data tabular settings.
- **Mechanism:** Theoretical bound (Theorem 1) shows excess risk dominated by regression mismatch \(\|\tilde{\eta}-\eta\|\). ReTabSyn constructs preference pairs that reward correct labels given features (Type I) and penalize unrealistic feature co-occurrences (Type II), directly aligning the generator with the decision boundary.
- **Core assumption:** Feature mismatch term negligible due to expressive hypothesis class; true conditional dominates.
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 1; ablation showing sharpening conditional improves AUROC.

### Mechanism 2: Oracle-Free Preference Construction
- **Claim:** Deterministic perturbations yield high-purity supervision for DPO, eliminating bias from external reward models.
- **Mechanism:** For each real row, chosen–rejected pairs are created by flipping \(y\) (Type I) or perturbing a feature in a strongly correlated pair (Type II). Schema validation ensures structural consistency.
- **Core assumption:** Tabular schemas provide reliable constraints; minimal perturbations generate meaningful contrast.
- **Evidence anchors:** Superiority over RL-guided baselines (PTA, SynRL) that use external classifiers; ablation on preference mix.

### Mechanism 3: Two-Stage Pipeline
- **Claim:** Augmentation followed by DPO fine-tuning yields high-fidelity, utility-aligned synthesis.
- **Mechanism:** SMOTE-like augmentation expands manifold; base LM learns joint via MLE. DPO fine-tuning sharpens conditional while regularization (\(\beta,\lambda\)) prevents manifold deviation.
- **Core assumption:** Interpolation within buckets preserves \(P(y|X)\); base generator adequate; DPO hyperparameters balance alignment and diversity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Ablation removing either stage hurts AUROC and fidelity; dynamic augmentation schedule outperforms fixed large factors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Distribution Prioritization**:  
  *Why needed:* Downstream task risk is dominated by \(\|\tilde{\eta}-\eta\|\) when hypothesis class is expressive; focusing on \(P(y|X)\) directly reduces this term.  
  *Quick check:* If a classifier perfectly predicts \(y\) from \(X\), does the exact distribution of \(X\) matter for test error? (No, as long as the classifier is well-specified.)

- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**:  
  *Why needed:* DPO fine-tunes the generator using preference pairs without a separate reward model, providing a stable, closed-form loss for conditional alignment.  
  *Quick check:* How does DPO differ from PPO-based RLHF? (DPO directly optimizes policy against a reference model; no reward model training.)

- **Tabular Data Augmentation via Interpolation**:  
  *Why needed:* Deep generators require more data; augmentation enlarges manifold coverage while respecting mixed-type constraints.  
  *Quick check:* Why interpolate only within categorical buckets? (To respect discrete feature domains and avoid invalid category combinations.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Real Data → Augmentation Module → Base Generator (pre-trained) → Preference Constructor → DPO Fine-Tuner → Synthetic Data → Evaluation Suite

### Critical Path
Real data → augmentation → pre-train base generator → construct preference pairs from real data → DPO fine-tuning → sample synthetic data → evaluate.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Augmentation aggressiveness:** Dynamic schedule (e.g., \(M(N)=30\) for \(N\le128\)) adapts to scarcity; fixed large \(M\) risks unrealistic samples.
- **Preference mix:** 70% Type I vs. 30% Type II balances utility (AUROC) and fidelity (correlation similarity). All-target boosts AUROC but hurts correlation recall; all-feature improves fidelity but lowers utility.
- **DPO regularization:** β controls preference strength; λ prevents deviation from reference. Mis-set values cause over-alignment or collapse.
- **Base generator choice:** LM-based serialization handles mixed types naturally but may be less sample-efficient than specialized architectures.

### Failure Signatures
- **Degraded downstream utility:** Check augmentation size (too small → overfitting) and DPO steps (too few → weak conditional alignment).
- **Low correlation similarity:** Excessive DPO regularization or poorly chosen correlated feature pairs (ignoring non-linear dependencies).
- **High membership inference AUC:** Over-aggressive augmentation (copying real rows) or insufficient diversity pressure from DPO.
- **Constraint violations:** Perturbations not covering constraint-related features; verify that at least one involved variable is perturbed.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablation on augmentation size:** Fixed \(N\times1000\) underperforms dynamic schedule; \(N\times20\) yields diminishing returns, showing size must scale with data scarcity.
2. **Ablation on preference pair types:** All target pairs (\(\text{proportion}=1\)) boost AUROC but hurt correlation; all feature pairs (\(\text{proportion}=0\)) improve fidelity but lower utility; default 0.7/0.3 balances both.
3. **Small-data evaluation:** ReTabSyn consistently outperforms baselines (including GReaT, TVAE, TabSyn) across \(N=32\)–\(512\), even surpassing real-data performance at very small \(N\), demonstrating synergy of augmentation and DPO.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does ReTabSyn's conditional alignment generalize to other generative backbones beyond language models, such as diffusion models?
- **Basis in paper:** Conclusion states: "For future work, we plan to (i) test ReTabSyn with alternative backbones (e.g., diffusion) to assess generality and scaling."
- **Why unresolved:** Current implementation uses GPT-2; diffusion models have different inductive biases and training dynamics, and it is unknown if DPO fine-tuning yields similar utility gains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement ReTabSyn with a diffusion-based tabular generator (e.g., TabDDPM) on the same small-data benchmarks and compare downstream performance (AUROC/PR-AUC), fidelity, and computational overhead.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can explicit constraints for fairness, privacy, and adversarial robustness be incorporated into ReTabSyn's oracle-free framework without degrading conditional alignment?
- **Basis in paper:** Future work includes: "(ii) incorporate explicit constraints for fairness, privacy, and adversarial robustness."
- **Why unresolved:** Current preference construction targets feature-target and feature-feature dependencies; adding constraints may require modifying perturbation logic and DPO objectives, potentially creating trade-offs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Define constraint-violation detection (e.g., rule-based checks) to generate rejected pairs, integrate these into the DPO loss, and evaluate fairness metrics (e.g., demographic parity) and privacy leakage (membership inference attacks) on benchmark datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What principled controls exist within ReTabSyn to mitigate bias and support ethical synthetic data practices?
- **Basis in paper:** Future work: "(iii) study principled controls to mitigate bias and support ethical data practices."
- **Why unresolved:** Paper does not address bias amplification; preference optimization might reinforce biases in real data if perturbations are not bias-aware, leading to unfair synthetic distributions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Modify preference labeling to balance conditional distributions across sensitive groups (e.g., via reweighting), and measure bias metrics (e.g., equalized odds) on synthetic data and downstream models while maintaining utility.

## Limitations
- Augmentation schedule \(M(N)\) not fully specified (bucket formation, mixed-type distance) → risk of poor manifold coverage.
- Feature perturbation rules (pair selection, perturbation method) underspecified → may produce invalid rejections.
- DPO-Positive implementation details (regularization term) ambiguous → training instability.

## Confidence
- **Conditional distribution prioritization:** **High**
- **Oracle-free preference construction:** **Medium**
- **Two-stage pipeline (augmentation + DPO):** **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. Vary \(M(N)\) (e.g., 5–200) on small datasets (\(N=32,128,512\)) to find optimal augmentation scaling for AUROC/fidelity.
2. Replace heuristic perturbations with a learned counterfactual generator (e.g., conditional GAN) and measure utility/privacy trade-offs.
3. Grid search DPO hyperparameters (\(\beta,\lambda\) in {0.05,0.1,0.2}) to identify stable configurations and report variance.
