# Decomposing Retrieval Failures in RAG for Long-Document Financial Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17981  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17981  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** A hierarchical page-then-chunk retrieval method with a domain fine-tuned page scorer achieves 0.55 page recall@5 on FinanceBench, surpassing baselines (0.46) and reaching 0.81 on metrics-generated questions—exceeding the oracle document bound (0.68).

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies *within-document retrieval failures* as a critical bottleneck in financial RAG: even when the correct document is retrieved, the specific page or chunk containing the answer is often missed. Oracle analysis reveals that page recall peaks at only ~60% under perfect document retrieval, exposing a large gap. The authors propose a hierarchical solution: first retrieve pages using a bi-encoder fine-tuned on financial filings, then retrieve chunks exclusively from those pages. On 150 FinanceBench questions, the method lifts page recall to 0.55 (vs. 0.46 best baseline) and numeric answer accuracy to 0.30 (vs. 0.12). Performance is especially strong on “metrics-generated” questions (0.81 page recall), demonstrating the value of exploiting page-level semantic coherence in structured financial documents.

## Method Summary
The approach decomposes retrieval into three granularities—document, page, chunk—and defines oracle candidate pools to isolate failure modes. The core system is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a page retriever uses a fine-tuned bi-encoder (initialized from BGE-M3) to embed queries and pages via contrastive learning (Multiple Negatives Ranking loss) with in-batch negatives; (2) a chunk retriever (base BGE-M3) searches only within chunks belonging to the top-P retrieved pages. Training uses 5-fold document-level splits to prevent leakage. PDFs are preprocessed into pages (truncated to 2000 chars) and fixed-size chunks (1024 tokens, 128 overlap). The generator (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) is held constant. Baselines include dense (BGE-M3), sparse (BM25, SPLADE), hybrid, query reformulation (HyDE, Multi-HyDE), and reranking.

## Key Results
- Page recall@5 improves from 0.46 (best baseline) to 0.55 with the page scorer.
- On metrics-generated questions, page recall@5 reaches 0.81, exceeding the Oracle Document bound of 0.68.
- Numeric answer accuracy rises from 0.12 to 0.30; chunk-level Max BLEU@5 / ROUGE-L achieve 0.33 / 0.46.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Within-document retrieval failures are a primary bottleneck in financial RAG, persisting even after the correct document is retrieved.
- **Mechanism:** The paper decomposes retrieval performance at three granularities (document, page, chunk) and establishes empirical upper bounds via *oracle settings*. The oracle document setting restricts the search space to the gold filing, isolating the headroom due to imperfect page/chunk discovery. The oracle page setting further restricts to gold pages, isolating the chunk-level gap.
- **Core assumption:** The corpus is pre-chunked with metadata linking chunks to their source page and document, and gold annotations exist for the answer-bearing page.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "correct document is retrieved but the page or chunk that contains the answer is missed" and "page recall peaks at 60% even with perfect document retrieval".
  - [section 3.3] Formal definitions of `Cdoc(d⋆)` and `Cpage(d⋆, P⋆)` oracle candidate pools.
  - [corpus] Neighboring work (e.g., "Hybrid Document-Routed Retrieval", "Metadata-Driven RAG") also target within-document retrieval, implicitly validating the gap's significance.
- **Break condition:** If document recall is high but page recall remains near-random (e.g., <0.1 for a 50-page filing), the gap is confirmed and not merely a metric artifact.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A domain fine-tuned page scorer, treating pages as a coherent intermediate unit, significantly improves within-document retrieval over direct chunk matching.
- **Mechanism:** A bi-encoder (initialized from BGE-M3) is fine-tuned via contrastive learning (MNR loss) to embed queries and pages in a shared space. At inference, top-P pages are retrieved first; subsequent chunk retrieval is constrained to chunks from only those pages. This reduces noise from irrelevant sections before fine-grained search.
- **Core assumption:** Financial pages exhibit semantic coherence (e.g., a table and its surrounding discussion) that a generic embedder fails to capture, and this coherence is learnable from page-level QA pairs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "fine-tune a bi-encoder specifically for page-level relevance on financial filings, exploiting the semantic coherence of pages."
  - [section 4.7] Training uses `S = {(q, (d⋆(q), p+)) | p+ ∈ P⋆(q)}` positives with in-batch negatives.
  - [section 5.2] The method achieves 0.55 PageRec@5, beating the best baseline (0.46) and exceeding Oracle Document (0.60) on metrics questions.
- **Break condition:** If page-level training yields no improvement over a pre-trained page encoder on a held-out filing type (e.g., Earnings transcripts in Table 4), the assumed coherence does not generalize or the training signal is insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The benefit of page-level filtering is highly dependent on document type and question type, due to structural regularity.
- **Mechanism:** Structured, templated documents (e.g., 10-Ks) have consistent page layouts and section ordering, making page embeddings more predictive. Unstructured or conversational documents (e.g., Earnings calls) break this pattern. Numeric/"metrics" questions often target specific tables in predictable sections (e.g., balance sheets), making them well-suited for page scoring.
- **Core assumption:** Answer location correlates with document metadata (type) and question intent (type), and this correlation is learnable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.3] On 10Ks (n=112), PageScorer achieves 0.62 vs. Oracle Document 0.56; on Earnings calls (EC), it achieves 0.10 vs. Oracle 0.64.
  - [section 5.3 & Figure 3] On "Metrics Generated" questions, PageScorer reaches 0.81 PageRec@5, exceeding Oracle Document (0.68).
- **Break condition:** If performance on 10Ks drops to baseline levels when controlling for document length, the mechanism is not exploiting page coherence but merely proxy signals like document ID.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval Granularity Decomposition**  
  *Why needed here:* To diagnose *where* retrieval fails (document vs. page vs. chunk) and set realistic upper bounds. Without this, a high document recall could mask poor evidence retrieval.  
  *Quick check:* If Oracle Document page recall is 0.60 on a dataset, what does a system's 0.40 page recall tell us about its within-document capability versus its document-finding skill?
- **Concept: Bi-encoder Contrastive Fine-tuning**  
  *Why needed here:* To adapt a generalist text embedder (BGE-M3) to the financial domain's lexical and structural patterns at the page level. The MNR loss with in-batch negatives is the standard efficient approach.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the positive pair `(query, gold_page)`? What role do other pages in the batch play as negatives, and what is a key risk of this sampling strategy?
- **Concept: Hierarchical Retrieval (Coarse-to-Fine)**  
  *Why needed here:* Long financial filings (hundreds of pages) make brute-force chunk search noisy. A cheap, high-recall coarse filter (pages) reduces the search space for the expensive, precise chunk retriever.  
  *Quick check:* How does "Page-then-Chunk" differ from the "Parent-Child" hierarchical method evaluated in Section 4.5? What is the assumed unit of coherence in each?
- **Concept: Oracle Evaluation for Gap Analysis**  
  *Why needed here:* To quantify the maximum achievable performance given perfect retrieval at a coarser granularity, isolating the specific failure mode being addressed.  
  *Quick check:* If Oracle Document recall is 1.0 but Oracle Page recall is 0.6, what does that imply about the corpus structure and the difficulty of the task?
- **Concept: Domain-Specific Coherence in Financial Documents**  
  *Why needed here:* Financial filings exhibit strong internal consistency (tables with captions, MD&A sections) that can be exploited by a page-level retriever but is missed by generic embedders.  
  *Quick check:* Give an example of a financial page where a generic embedder might fail to connect a query about "net profit" with a page containing a table titled "Consolidated Statements of Income".

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. Page Encoder & Index: `Eθp` model + offline embeddings for every `(doc_id, page_num)` stored in vector DB.  
  2. Chunk Encoder & Index: Pre-trained `Echunk` (likely BGE-M3) + offline chunk embeddings with `(doc_id, page_num)` metadata stored in vector DB.  
  3. Page Retriever: At query time, embed query with `Eθp`, perform nearest-neighbor search over page index, return top-P pages.  
  4. Chunk Filter: Given top-P pages, filter full chunk index to `C_filt = {c | (d_c, p_c) ∈ top-P pages}`.  
  5. Chunk Retriever: Embed query with `Echunk`, search over `C_filt`, return top-k chunks for generation.  
  6. Oracle Evaluator: Utility to run retrieval over `Cdoc(d⋆)` or `Cpage(d⋆, P⋆)` for upper-bound analysis.  
- **Critical path:** Train page encoder → build page index → for each query: page retrieval → chunk filtering → chunk retrieval → generation. The page retrieval step is the new critical dependency.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **P (pages retrieved):** Higher P increases recall but adds noise and latency. Ablation suggests P=20 is a sweet spot.  
  - **Chunk size:** Larger chunks (1024 tokens) help financial context but may dilute page-focused signals; fixed chunking ignores layout spanning pages.  
  - **Page embedding normalization:** Truncating to 2000 chars loses long tables; tradeoff for consistent embedding length.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low page recall on non-10K docs indicates overfitting to annual report structure.  
  - High document recall but dropped numeric match suggests retrieved chunks lack the specific numeric table.  
  - Page recall > Oracle Document on metrics questions may indicate gold annotation gaps or synonymous table retrieval.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Reproduce Oracle Bounds:** Using BGE-M3 on the 150-question subset, compute Oracle Document and Oracle Page metrics to confirm the reported gaps (Table 2).  
  2. **Ablate Page Retriever P-value:** For a trained page scorer, vary P ∈ {5,10,20} and measure PageRec@5 and chunk metrics (Table 6). Verify the claimed sweet spot at P=20.  
  3. **Breakdown by Question Type:** Run the trained page scorer on the three FinanceBench question types (Domain, Metrics, Novel). Replicate Figure 3 to confirm strong performance on "Metrics Generated" and diagnose weaknesses on "Novel".

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How does the proposed page scorer generalize to the full FinanceBench dataset and other financial QA benchmarks with diverse document types (e.g., 10-Qs, 8-Ks, earnings calls) beyond the 150-question, 10-K-heavy subset?  
  **Basis:** The conclusion states the study evaluates 150 questions predominantly from 10-K filings, limiting generalization. Section 5.3 shows performance drops on Earnings calls.  
  **Why unresolved:** Results indicate overfitting to annual report structure; broader evaluation needed to assess practical utility across SEC filings.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Full evaluation on complete FinanceBench or other benchmarks (SEC-QA, FinQA); ablation controlling for document type prevalence in training.
- **Question:** Can the page scorer's training be improved by replacing random in-batch negatives with hard negative mining, and what is the resulting impact on page recall and downstream generation?  
  **Basis:** Limitations note reliance on random in-batch negatives; hard negative mining could improve results.  
  **Why unresolved:** Random negatives may be semantically too easy or contain false negatives, impairing embedding quality for fine-grained page discrimination.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Implement hard negative mining (e.g., using BM25 to find similar but non-gold pages); compare page recall, chunk metrics, numeric accuracy against in-batch approach.
- **Question:** Does integrating the page scorer with other proven techniques—such as cross-encoder reranking or query reformulation—yield synergistic gains that exceed the sum of their parts for within-document retrieval?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion lists combining page scoring with reranking or query augmentation as a promising direction; paper evaluates components separately but not in combination.  
  **Why unresolved:** Page scoring reduces noise at coarse granularity, reranking refines at chunk level; their complementary potential is unknown.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Evaluate combined pipelines (e.g., Page Scorer → Chunk Retrieval → ReRanker); measure page recall, BLEU/ROUGE-L, numeric accuracy; statistical testing for significance.
- **Question:** Are the current evaluation metrics (ROUGE-L, numeric match) sufficient to capture answer correctness in financial contexts, or do they systematically underestimate failures due to subtle numerical or contextual errors?  
  **Basis:** Limitations acknowledge that ROUGE-L and numeric matching may not fully capture correctness in financial contexts.  
  **Why unresolved:** Financial answers require precise numbers, units, periods; ROUGE-L may reward paraphrased wrong answers; numeric tolerance may accept incorrect rounding.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Fine-grained error analysis categorizing error types; adopt stricter metrics (exact numeric match, unit consistency, LLM-based verification) correlated with human judgments.
- **Question:** Can the hierarchical page-then-chunk approach be extended to multi-document scenarios where the answer requires aggregating evidence across multiple filings (e.g., comparing year-over-year data or cross-referencing different report types)?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion suggests extending to multi-document questions requiring cross-filing context aggregation; current method assumes single gold document per question.  
  **Why unresolved:** Page scorer trained on single-document positives; multi-document evidence distribution changes ranking objective.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Adapt training to include multi-document positive pairs; modify scorer to rank pages from a multi-document corpus; evaluate on a dataset with multi-document gold evidence.

## Limitations
- Evaluation uses only 150 FinanceBench questions and one LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct), limiting statistical power and generalizability across generators.
- The page scorer performs well on 10-Ks but poorly on earnings calls (e.g., 0.10 vs. 0.64 oracle), indicating overfitting to structured annual reports.
- Pages are truncated to 2000 characters, discarding key tabular data; the chunk retriever's encoder (base vs. fine-tuned) is unspecified.

## Confidence
- Within-document retrieval bottleneck: **High** (oracle gap ~40%).  
- Page scorer improves retrieval: **Medium** (average gain but uneven across document types).  
- Benefit depends on document/question type: **Medium** (supported but dataset small).
