# APEX-EM: Non-Parametric Online Learning for Autonomous Agents via Structured Procedural-Episodic Experience Replay

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29093
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29093
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: APEX-EM achieves 89.6% accuracy on KGQAGen-10k (+48.3pp) and 83.3% success on BigCodeBench (+29.4pp), demonstrating large gains from non‑parametric online learning.

## Executive Summary
Large language model agents typically lack persistent procedural memory, often re‑deriving solutions from scratch even for structurally identical tasks. APEX‑EM introduces a non‑parametric online learning framework that accumulates complete procedural‑episodic execution traces in a Procedural Knowledge Graph (PKG) without updating model weights. The system uses a hybrid retrieval strategy—combining semantic similarity, structural signature matching, and graph traversal—and stores both successful and failed experiences with rich error annotations to serve as positive and negative in‑context examples. Evaluated on BigCodeBench and KGQAGen‑10k, APEX‑EM yields substantial absolute improvements and enables cross‑domain transfer even when tasks share no lexical overlap. Ablations reveal that rich judge feedback is critical for structured query generation but negligible for code generation, highlighting the task‑dependent nature of feedback utility.

## Method Summary
APEX‑EM’s PRGII workflow orchestrates five phases. **Plan** decomposes a task, extracts entities, and computes a structural signature (ordered operation sequence). **Retrieve** performs hybrid search: semantic vector similarity on the task description, longest common subsequence (LCS) on structural signatures, and graph traversal via relations like `derived_from` and `uses_entity` in the PKG; top‑3 successful and top‑2 failed experiences are returned. **Generate** produces an artifact conditioned on the retrieved examples. **Iterate** applies a validator to refine the artifact up to five times. **Ingest** evaluates the final artifact with a teacher model, assigns a quality score, and stores the experience (success or failure) as a PKG node with layered reflections (Goal, Procedure, Evidence, Execution Trace, Error Registry, Patch Reflections). The PKG grows monotonically; no weight updates occur.

## Key Results
- KGQAGen‑10k: 89.6% accuracy from 41.3% baseline (+48.3pp), surpassing oracle retrieval upper bound of 84.9%.
- BigCodeBench: 83.3% success rate from 53.9% (+29.4pp), exceeding prior memory‑based methods.
- Ablations: Rich judge feedback yields +10.3pp on KGQAGen but negligible effect on BigCodeBench; binary signal partially compensates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Storing complete procedural‑episodic traces (full causal chain) enables reliable plan replay and adaptation, surpassing summaries or distilled reflections.
- **Mechanism:** The **Procedural Knowledge Graph (PKG)** encodes each execution as a node with five reflection layers (Goal, Procedure, Evidence, Error, Patch). This preserves the full causal chain—planning steps, artifacts, iterations, errors, quality scores—making each experience a "replayable unit" rather than a narrative hint. During retrieval, the agent receives the entire structured trace, allowing direct template adaptation.
- **Core assumption:** A replayable, fully detailed procedural record contains sufficient information for an LLM to adapt the plan to a new but structurally similar task without re‑deriving intermediate steps.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "stores complete execution traces ... enables cross‑domain transfer"; [section 3.3] details the five layers and states: "Without this structure, retrieved memories provide narrative guidance rather than replayable procedures"; [section 5.2.1] and [section 5.6] show performance gains and frozen memory generalizing.
- **Break condition:** If the structural signature is too coarse or domain‑specific, the retrieved plan may be inapplicable. Signature extraction failure or low LCS overlap (τ_σ = 0.6 threshold) leads to irrelevant retrieval.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Hybrid retrieval combining semantic search, **structural signature matching**, and **PKG traversal** enables cross‑domain transfer where surface‑level semantic similarity fails.
- **Mechanism:** Retrieval uses three parallel strategies: (1) semantic similarity on task embeddings, (2) longest common subsequence on ordered operation sequences (structural signatures), (3) graph traversal via `derived_from`, `uses_entity`, `structurally_similar_to` edges. Candidates are re‑ranked by a composite score. Structural signatures act as domain‑agnostic "moves" (e.g., `entity_resolution → temporal_filter → aggregation`), matching procedures across domains with zero lexical overlap.
- **Core assumption:** Abstract procedural patterns (operation sequences) are more stable and transferable across domains than surface‑level task descriptions or entity names.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "hybrid retrieval ... enabling cross‑domain transfer"; [section 3.4.2] defines `sim_σ` via LCS and gives cross‑domain example; [section 5.2.3] shows entity graph retrieval (A3) leading on HLE where semantic similarity is low; [corpus] indicates neighbor work lacks explicit structural matching for cross‑domain.
- **Break condition:** When tasks share no common high‑level procedural pattern (signature LCS below threshold) or the Operation taxonomy is too impoverished; graph traversal may retrieve noisy edges.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** **Dual‑outcome indexing** (storing successes *and* failures with structured error annotations) creates a procedural equivalent of hindsight experience replay, allowing the agent to avoid known failure modes.
- **Mechanism:** Experiences are gated by quality score `q` (successful if `q ≥ θ`). Both successes and failures are stored in the same PKG schema. Failures populate the `Error Registry` (error class, root cause, recovery procedure) and `Patch Reflections` (program deltas). During retrieval, both positive (`P⁺`) and negative (`P⁻`) examples are provided, with negatives serving as guardrails.
- **Core assumption:** Structured failure annotations (type, cause, fix) are more useful than binary failure signals for preventing repeated errors, especially in complex domains.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Successful experiences serve as positive in‑context examples; failures as negative examples with structured error annotations"; [section 3.3] "Failed experiences additionally populate the Error Registry and Patch Reflections"; [section 5.4] ablations: removing rich judge feedback causes -10.3pp on KGQA but ≈0 on BCB; [corpus] neighbor work typically stores only successes or scalar utilities.
- **Break condition:** If the Teacher/ Judge misdiagnoses errors, negative examples may mislead; if `Patch Reflections` are too specific, they may not generalize.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Experience Replay (Reinforcement Learning)**  
  *Why needed here:* APEX‑EM frames its memory as an "experience replay buffer for LLM agents". Understanding RL replay (storing (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples) clarifies the analogy: APEX‑EM stores high‑level procedural plans instead of low‑level transitions, and uses in‑context learning instead of gradient updates.  
  *Quick check question:* How does APEX‑EM's "non‑parametric online learning" differ from gradient‑based RL replay in terms of knowledge update mechanism?

- **Concept: Procedural vs. Episodic Memory (Cognitive Science)**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper’s premise is that LLM agents lack "procedural‑episodic memory". Tulving’s distinction underpins the design: *procedural* (how to do) is encoded in the Procedure Reflection; *episodic* (specific experience) is the full trace with quality score. The dual‑outcome store merges both.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is storing both the reusable procedure template *and* the specific execution trace (errors, artifacts) necessary for cross‑task transfer?

- **Concept: Knowledge Graphs & Graph Traversal**  
  *Why needed here:* The PKG is a typed, multi‑relational graph. Understanding graph concepts (nodes, edges, traversal) is essential to grasp entity resolution, structural signature extraction, and multi‑hop retrieval (`derived_from`, `uses_entity`). The graph enables world‑change propagation and structural matching.  
  *Quick check question:* How does the `uses_entity` edge support automatic plan freshness when world entities (e.g., a sports team’s roster) change?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  PKG (Procedural Knowledge Graph) ↔ stores Experience, Entity, Operation, Sub‑Task, TaskTopic nodes with edges (FOLLOWED_BY, uses_entity, similar_to, structurally_similar_to, derived_from).  
  PRGII Workflow orchestrates: Plan → Retrieve → Generate → Iterate → Ingest.  
  Retrieval Stack: vector index (semantic) ∪ graph store (structural/traversal) ∪ metadata index ∪ experience store (full records).  
  Domain Adapters & Validators: pluggable components (e.g., CodeDomainAdapter, SPARQLValidator).

- **Critical path:**  
  `Task t` → **Plan** → `P(t) = (TU, E, S, σ)` → **Retrieve** → `P⁺, P⁻` (based on `sim_sem`, `sim_σ`, graph proximity) → **Generate** → `a₁` → **Iterate** (`Vd(aⱼ, E)` → refine) → **Ingest** → `O_d(a_final, E)` → compute `q` → **Add/Merge** into PKG (creating `Experience` node with all layers, linking to `Entity`/`Operation` nodes). Memory grows monotonically; no weight updates.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  Non‑parametric vs. parametric: avoids catastrophic forgetting and fine‑tuning costs, but relies on retrieval quality and context window limits; no theoretical convergence guarantees.  
  Structured vs. unstructured storage: PKG enables precise querying (by error class, signature) and world‑change propagation, but requires taxonomies (Operation types, error classes) that may need manual design.  
  Judge dependency: rich feedback (Opus) helps on KGQA/HLE but adds latency/cost; binary signal suffices for code. Inaccurate judges corrupt `Error Registry` and `Patch` layers.  
  Storage bloat vs. coverage: every execution is stored; compaction (archiving dominated plans via `supersedes`) is necessary but may discard useful edge cases.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  Low cross‑domain transfer: absent `structurally_similar_to` edges or poor signature extraction; monitor LCS distribution across retrieved pairs.  
  Memory bloat / slow retrieval: PKG growth without compaction degrades graph traversal; monitor node/edge count and query latency.  
  Judge feedback leakage: teacher includes ground‑truth answers; system memorizes answers, not procedures; audit `F_T` strings.  
  Stuck iteration loops: identical failures across iterations indicate `V_d` or refinement prompt failure; detect via identical `stderr` or `f_j`.  
  Entity resolution conflicts: low `τ_r` threshold duplicates `Entity` nodes; monitor `EntityResolver` cache hit rate.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Cold‑start + seed memory test:** Initialize empty PKG, run 5–10 seed tasks per domain; measure epoch‑1 success rate with/without seed memory to quantify bootstrapping value.  
  2. **Isolate retrieval strategies:** On held‑out set, run ablations with only semantic, only entity‑graph, and full retrieval; measure success rate and average LCS of top retrieved pairs to validate structural matching hypothesis.  
  3. **Judge feedback quality test:** Use high‑quality (Opus) vs. noisy judge (GPT‑4o‑mini) on subset; compare learning curves and `Error Registry` consistency to assess feedback sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Performance on MemRL’s remaining benchmarks (ALFWorld, Lifelong Agent Bench) and whether structural signatures help in embodied/long‑horizon environments.  
*Basis:* [explicit] Future work lists evaluating on these benchmarks. Results on BigCodeBench, KGQAGen‑10k, and a subset of HLE are reported; full comparison pending.  
*Why unresolved:* Direct comparison data absent; unknown if structural transfer benefits persist where semantic retrieval is already strong or continuous learning is needed.  
*What would resolve it:* Run PRGII on ALFWorld and Lifelong Agent Bench with same frozen backbone configurations used for MemRL; report Last SR and CSR over 10 epochs; analyze structural matching’s added value.

**Open Question 2:** Can the hybrid retrieval scoring function (Equation 5) be learned rather than using fixed weights to automatically balance semantic similarity, structural matching, graph proximity, quality, and recency?  
*Basis:* [explicit] Authors propose investigating learned retrieval policies, contrasting fixed composite score with MemRL’s Q‑value‑based utility.  
*Why unresolved:* Fixed weights (α,β,γ,δ,ϵ) are hand‑tuned; potential for a learned policy to dynamically adjust per task/domain remains unexplored.  
*What would resolve it:* Implement a meta‑learned retrieval policy (e.g., a small network taking task and candidate features); train on validation set; measure gains in SR/CSR and retrieval accuracy vs. fixed heuristic.

**Open Question 3:** Why does rich qualitative judge feedback benefit KGQAGen‑10k (+10.3pp) but not BigCodeBench, and can we predict when such feedback is critical?  
*Basis:* [explicit] Ablation shows negligible benefit on code generation but large gain on structured queries.  
*Why unresolved:* Root cause not analyzed; hypotheses include binary vs. nuanced errors, judge oracle availability, or pattern complexity.  
*What would resolve it:* Fine‑grained error analysis of failed BCB vs. KGQA tasks, categorizing error types and correlating feedback specificity with improvement; or test on a benchmark with mixed error types to infer predictive factors.

**Open Question 4:** What are the formal limitations of non‑parametric online learning—under what conditions does it fail to converge or degrade with memory growth (e.g., negative interference, staleness, signature collisions)?  
*Basis:* [inferred] Paper highlights monotonic improvement but acknowledges no formal convergence guarantees; discusses memory maintenance but not worst‑case failures.  
*Why unresolved:* Without theoretical analysis, unknown if performance can plateau, oscillate, or decline as memory grows, especially with conflicting experiences or ambiguous signatures.  
*What would resolve it:* Stress‑test with synthetic/adversarial tasks triggering signature collisions or requiring forgetting outdated info; measure SR/CSR vs. memory size beyond 300 experiences; analyze “supersedes” edge creation rate.

**Open Question 5:** How does the offline‑to‑online pipeline (seeding memory with high‑quality experiences from powerful models, then operating with a smaller model) affect long‑term knowledge integrity, bias propagation, and diversity?  
*Basis:* [explicit] Authors mention supporting an offline‑to‑online pipeline but do not evaluate it.  
*Why unresolved:* Questions remain about whether weaker models can reuse seeded experiences without retraining, whether online contributions dilute or conflict with seeded knowledge, and whether procedural patterns become biased.  
*What would resolve it:* Two‑phase experiments: seed memory with X experiences from a strong model on held‑out tasks; run online learning with a weaker model on new tasks; measure initial boost and subsequent evolution; compare control without seeding; analyze provenance of retrieved experiences over time.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed teacher/judge prompt design and rubric; feedback quality likely impacts `Error Registry` fidelity and failure utility.
- Underspecified structural signature extraction algorithm and full Operation taxonomy; cross‑domain transfer claims depend on this mapping’s correctness.
- No formal convergence guarantees; potential for performance degradation due to negative experiences, staleness, or signature collisions as memory grows.

## Confidence
- Complete trace replay (Mechanism 1): High
- Hybrid retrieval for zero‑shot transfer (Mechanism 2): Medium
- Dual‑outcome memory with rich failures (Mechanism 3): Low

## Next Checks
- **Signature consistency test:** Use a fixed Operation taxonomy (from examples), run signature extraction on 100 tasks; measure inter‑annotator agreement (LLM‑prompted) and LCS variance within same‑ vs. cross‑domain pairs. Target: >0.8 agreement, cross‑domain LCS ≥0.6 for claimed transfers.
- **Entity resolution ablation:** Vary `τ_r` (0.7–0.95) on a subset; measure duplicate `Entity` nodes for same real‑world object and retrieval precision of `uses_entity` links. Expect a sweet spot balancing fragmentation vs. over‑merging.
- **Judge feedback fidelity audit:** Generate `Error Registry` entries using Opus and a weaker judge (GPT‑4o‑mini). Compute agreement on error class/root cause; measure downstream impact on success rate when mixing feedback sources. Expect >0.7 agreement for critical error classes.
