# Expert Mind: A Retrieval-Augmented Architecture for Expert Knowledge Preservation in the Energy Sector

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14541
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14541
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: A RAG system that captures tacit energy expert knowledge through multimodal elicitation and provides cited conversational queries, targeting >85% factual accuracy, >20% reduction in onboarding time, and >15% decrease in expert consultation hours.

## Executive Summary
Expert Mind addresses the irreversible loss of tacit knowledge from retiring energy sector experts using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline. It captures expertise through structured interviews, think-aloud sessions, and document ingestion, extracts structured artifacts with LLMs, stores them in a vector database, and enables cited conversational queries. Ethical safeguards (informed consent, right to erasure) are integrated architecturally. The system aims for >85% factual accuracy, >50 weekly queries, >20% reduction in onboarding time, and >15% decrease in expert consultation hours.

## Method Summary
The system follows a multi-stage pipeline: Capture (interviews, think-aloud, document ingestion) → Transcription (Whisper) → LLM-based extraction → Human validation → Vector embedding and storage → Query processing (retrieval-augmented generation with citations). Ethical constraints (consent scopes, erasure propagation) are enforced through design rather than policy. The architecture is domain-agnostic but tailored to the energy sector’s tacit knowledge challenges.

## Key Results
- Factual accuracy >85%
- >20% reduction in onboarding time
- >15% decrease in expert consultation hours

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Multimodal knowledge capture elicits tacit knowledge that conventional documentation misses.
- **Mechanism**: Structured interviews (following cognitive task analysis) and think-aloud sessions externalize expert reasoning by prompting verbalization during scenario-based problem-solving and real-time observation, respectively. This targets the "externalization" quadrant of the SECI model.
- **Core assumption**: Experts can articulate sufficient heuristic and diagnostic knowledge when prompted by appropriate protocols, and this verbalized output is a valid proxy for their internal tacit knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "addresses the knowledge elicitation problem through structured interviews, think-aloud sessions"
  - [section III.A] describes the capture modalities and their purpose for surfacing procedural and diagnostic knowledge.
  - [corpus] Weak support. Neighbors discuss expert systems and RAG but not specific elicitation techniques. Assumption: efficacy of think-aloud and CTA is established in cited literature [4,5].
- **Break condition**: Experts who are "poor verbalizers" (acknowledged in Section VI.A) produce corpora too sparse for effective extraction, regardless of protocol quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A RAG pipeline with human-in-the-loop validation converts raw captured modalities into a faithful, queryable knowledge base.
- **Mechanism**: 1) Transcribe audio/video (Whisper). 2) Use an LLM (Claude) to extract structured artifacts (facts, heuristics, patterns). 3) Expert validates artifacts pre-ingestion. 4) Embed artifacts in a vector store with metadata. 5) Retrieve semantically similar artifacts for a user query and generate a cited response. The validation step is posited to prevent hallucination propagation.
- **Core assumption**: LLM extraction from transcripts can reliably identify and structure the four defined artifact types, and expert validation is both feasible and effective at correcting errors.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "extracts structured artifacts like decision criteria using LLMs, stores them in a vector database... enables conversational queries with source citation."
  - [section III.B] details the extraction pipeline and the "human-in-the-loop validation step... essential for maintaining knowledge fidelity."
  - [corpus] Moderate support. Neighbors [17] discuss domain adaptation of RAG for QA, and [6] covers multimodal RAG. However, none address the specific extraction-to-validation loop for tacit knowledge. Assumption: this validation loop is a unique, effective safeguard.
- **Break condition**: Validation is skipped due to expert time constraints, or experts are inconsistent reviewers, allowing low-quality artifacts into the vector store.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Embedding ethical and legal constraints (consent, IP, erasure) as first-class architectural requirements ensures responsible deployment and expert participation.
- **Mechanism**: System design mandates specific features: pre-capture written consent with defined scope, metadata for provenance disclosure, a complete erasure propagation mechanism, and separate consent for voice cloning. These are not policy add-ons but implemented functional components (e.g., metadata indexing for deletion).
- **Core assumption**: Treating ethics as an implementation constraint—rather than external policy—is necessary and sufficient to manage the unique risks of digitizing a cognitive profile in an industrial setting.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "ethical safeguards such as informed consent and right to erasure integrated into the design."
  - [section IV] explicitly details how each principle (Informed Consent, IP, Bias Disclosure, Right to Erasure, Voice/Likeness) is "instantiated in system design decisions."
  - [corpus] Weak support. Neighbors [65454, 29146] discuss ethical AI frameworks in general but not their enforcement via system architecture. Assumption: this architectural integration is a novel and effective approach.
- **Break condition**: Organizational pressure overrides a designed constraint (e.g., erasure is delayed beyond SLA, consent scope is expanded post-capture).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Capture Layer (Structured Interviews, Think-Aloud Sessions, Corpus Ingestion) → Processing Layer (Whisper ASR, Claude API Extraction, Human Validation Interface) → Persistence Layer (Vector Store with Metadata Index, File Store) → Query Layer (REST API, Web Frontend, LLM Orchestrator).  
**Critical path**: Raw Input (Audio/Text/Video) → Transcription → LLM Extraction → Expert Validation → Vector Embedding & Storage → User Query → Vector Retrieval → Context Assembly → LLM Generation → Cited Response.  
**Design tradeoffs**:
- Accuracy vs. Ethics: Maximizing knowledge capture may conflict with an expert's "right to erasure" or desire for limited scope; system enforces ethical bounds, potentially sacrificing some captured knowledge.
- Fidelity vs. Scalability: Human validation ensures quality but creates a bottleneck; scaling requires efficient validation workflows or accepting lower confidence artifacts.
- Generality vs. Depth: Domain-agnostic architecture may miss domain-specific nuances that a purpose-built system might encode.
**Failure signatures**:
- High correction rate in validation logs (>10%) indicates poor LLM extraction or unclear capture sessions.
- Low query resolution rate with frequent "no relevant context found" suggests gaps in the knowledge base or retrieval issues.
- Erasure requests not completed within SLA indicate architectural coupling issues between vector DB, metadata index, and derived artifacts.
**First 3 experiments**:
1. Transcription & Extraction Sanity Check: Process 5 validated interview transcripts; measure artifact extraction completeness, false positive rate, and LLM confidence distribution; goal: baseline extraction quality.
2. Retrieval Relevance Audit: For 50 curated domain queries, run retrieval only; have experts blind-rate top-5 artifact relevance; goal: diagnose retrieval failures.
3. Ethical Compliance Workflow Test: Simulate full lifecycle including erasure request; verify complete removal from all stores; goal: validate technical implementation of right to erasure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can knowledge artifacts from multiple experts be fused into a coherent, cross-validated knowledge graph without contradictions or dilution of individual expertise?**  
   *Basis*: Future work identifies "multi-expert fusion" as a natural extension.  
   *Why unresolved*: Current architecture stores individual artifacts separately; no reconciliation mechanism.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Pilot study measuring graph coherence when fusing artifacts from 3–5 experts on overlapping scenarios.

2. **Can the system capture and represent emergent, team-level decision-making processes that are not explicitly verbalized by any single individual?**  
   *Basis*: Limitations state the system does not model collective, distributed knowledge from team interactions.  
   *Why unresolved*: Capture focuses on individuals; group dynamics and shared mental models are out of scope.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Analyze recorded team meetings to identify absent decision patterns and test if queries surface them post-hoc.

3. **Will the human validation layer reliably achieve the projected >85% factual accuracy, or will expert fatigue and validation bias limit its effectiveness?**  
   *Basis*: Expected outcomes target >85% factual accuracy with human validation raising it above that level.  
   *Why unresolved*: Validation protocol's impact untested; no data on reviewer agreement or scalability.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Measure inter-rater reliability during validation and correlate validation effort with final accuracy in a pilot.

4. **What technical mechanisms guarantee complete erasure of all synthetic derivatives (e.g., embedded vectors, generated responses) upon an expert’s withdrawal request?**  
   *Basis*: Ethical framework mandates a "complete erasure pathway" across all derived artifacts.  
   *Why unresolved*: No implementation details for tracing and deleting distributed synthetic artifacts.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Forensic audit after simulated erasure request checking residual data in logs, backups, and LLM memory.

5. **How can continual learning be integrated to incorporate new expert experiences post-digitization without catastrophic forgetting of existing knowledge artifacts?**  
   *Basis*: Future work proposes "integration of continual learning mechanisms".  
   *Why unresolved*: Architecture is batch-oriented; no incremental update or retention strategy.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Experiment simulating periodic artifact additions and measuring retention of historical query accuracy and embedding stability.

## Limitations
- Efficacy of capture protocols: Experts with poor verbalization may produce sparse transcripts, limiting artifact extraction; domain-specific tacit knowledge may resist structured elicitation.
- Validation scalability: Human-in-the-loop validation may be bypassed due to time constraints, risking artifact quality.
- Ethical adherence in practice: Organizational pressures may override designed constraints like timely erasure or strict consent scopes.
