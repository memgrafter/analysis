# Curriculum-Driven 3D CT Report Generation via Language-Free Visual Grafting and Zone-Constrained Compression

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23308
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23308
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Macro F1 of 0.429 on CT-RATE validation, surpassing prior SOTA (U-VLM, 0.414) by 3.6%.

## Executive Summary
Automated radiology report generation from 3D CT volumes faces challenges of extreme sequence lengths, severe class imbalance, and LLMs ignoring visual tokens. This paper presents Ker-VLJEPA-3B, a four-phase curriculum learning framework that integrates a frozen, self-supervised visual encoder (LeJEPA ViT-Large, trained without text) into a Llama 3.2 3B decoder. Key innovations include zone-constrained cross-attention to compress slices into 32 spatially-grounded tokens, a positive-findings-only training strategy to prevent posterior collapse, and warm bridge initialization for weight transfer across phases. On the CT-RATE benchmark (2,984 validation volumes), the model achieves a macro F1 of 0.429, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (U-VLM, 0.414) by 3.6%. Ablation studies attribute 56.6% of the generation quality to patient-specific visual token content, underscoring the effectiveness of the visual grafting approach.

## Method Summary
The method processes pre-computed LeJEPA slice embeddings (up to 600 slices per volume) through a zone-constrained cross-attention module that partitions the z-axis into 32 contiguous zones, producing one token per zone. A global self-attention layer then enables inter-zone communication. The resulting tokens are projected by a JEPA predictor with norm calibration to match the LLM’s embedding dimension. These projected tokens are grafted into the LLM (Llama 3.2 3B Instruct) via special <|visual_region|> placeholders in the embedding layer, and additionally processed by gated cross-attention adapters inserted at layers 7, 14, and 21. Training proceeds in four curriculum phases: (1) train the visual encoder with classification/MIL losses; (2) contrastive bridge training on positive-findings-only text with PCA-whitened embeddings; (3) generative fine-tuning on positive-findings text with warm bridge initialization (loading converged bridge and LoRA weights from a prior Phase 3 run); (4) narrative adaptation on raw reports with EWC to preserve visual grounding while fine-tuning only LoRA adapters.

## Key Results
- Macro F1: 0.429 on CT-RATE validation (vs. U-VLM 0.414, +3.6%).
- Per-class threshold optimization: 0.448 (+8.2%).
- Ablation: 56.6% of generation quality derives from patient-specific visual token content.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Zone-Constrained Cross-Attention for Spatial Compression
- **Claim:** Zone-constrained cross-attention compresses variable-length CT slice sequences into a fixed set of 32 spatially-grounded visual tokens, preserving anatomical localization while respecting LLM context limits.
- **Mechanism:** The z-axis is partitioned into 32 contiguous zones. Each of 32 learnable region queries attends via multi-head cross-attention only to slices within its assigned zone, producing one token per zone. A subsequent global self-attention layer enables inter-zone communication.
- **Core assumption:** Pathological findings are spatially correlated with gross anatomical zones along the craniocaudal axis, so compressing within zones retains clinically relevant spatial cues.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section III.A.1 define the zone partitioning (Eq.2) and attention (Eq.3); ablation in Table XI shows a large F1 drop (-0.076) when this component is removed; neighboring works lack this specific spatial grounding.
- **Break condition:** If key pathologies consistently span zone boundaries or zone boundaries do not align with consistent anatomical landmarks across patient variants, the spatial grounding assumption fails and compression may lose critical localization information.

### Mechanism 2: Positive-Findings-Only Training
- **Claim:** Positive-findings-only training prevents posterior collapse by eliminating gradient domination from normal-text tokens, forcing the LLM to rely on visual tokens for all generation steps.
- **Mechanism:** Training text is reformulated to contain only natural language descriptions for the binary abnormality labels that are positive for a given scan. For normal scans, a short template is used, ensuring every non-template token corresponds to a finding the visual encoder should have detected.
- **Core assumption:** The catastrophic gradient imbalance (~90% normal tokens in raw reports) teaches the LLM to ignore visual features and rely on linguistic priors for normal anatomy description, causing "posterior collapse" where visual information is discarded during generation.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions the strategy; Section III.C.3 explains the gradient imbalance; Table XI shows the single largest improvement (+0.123 F1) from adding this strategy; the specific formulation as "normal-text gradient domination" is not evidenced in neighboring papers.
- **Break condition:** If the positive-findings text is insufficiently descriptive or stylistically divergent from ground-truth reports, the model may fail to learn authentic narrative structure; if normal findings still contain subtle, linguistically complex descriptions, they might re-introduce harmful gradients.

### Mechanism 3: Warm Bridge Initialization
- **Claim:** Warm bridge initialization transfers converged vision-to-LLM projection and cross-attention weights from a prior curriculum phase, overcoming the "bridge reset problem" and providing immediate convergence in subsequent phases.
- **Mechanism:** After training Phase 2, the weights of 27 bridge components (3 layer projectors, 3 cross-attention adapters) and 392 LoRA tensors from a previous Phase 3 run are loaded into the new Phase 3 training run, overwriting their random initialization.
- **Core assumption:** The randomly initialized bridge parameters at the start of Phase 3 constitute an independent bottleneck that limits performance regardless of upstream (Phase 2) representation quality. Converged bridge weights from a prior optimal Phase 3 provide a favorable initialization.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract describes warm bridge; Section III.C.3 explains the bridge reset problem; Table VI shows cold start epoch 1 F1 = 0.360 vs. warm bridge epoch 1 F1 = 0.425; improving Phase 2 alone (cold bridge + better P2) yielded only 0.424, demonstrating the bridge reset's dominance; the technique is novel and not present in neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If the distribution of visual tokens from an improved Phase 2 is substantially different from the Phase 2 used to train the saved bridge components (e.g., due to different preprocessing or encoder updates), the transferred weights may be misaligned and hinder rather than help convergence.

## Foundational Learning
**Self-Supervised Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**  
Why needed: Provides modality-pure visual representations (LeJEPA) trained without any text/label supervision, avoiding entangling visual features with linguistic biases present in contrastive (CLIP-style) or segmentation-supervised encoders.  
Quick check: Why is training the visual backbone without any text supervision hypothesized to yield better representations for downstream radiologist-style report generation?

**Anisotropy in LLM Embeddings**  
Why needed: Pre-trained LLM text embeddings exhibit severe anisotropy (cosine similarity ~0.95), making standard contrastive learning ineffective. PCA whitening is required to create an isotropic space for InfoNCE loss to work.  
Quick check: What property of LLM embedding spaces renders a naive contrastive loss between visual and text embeddings nearly useless, and how is it mitigated?

**Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Learning**  
Why needed: Phase 4 fine-tuning on raw narrative text (dominated by normal descriptions) risks corrupting the visual grounding learned in Phase 3. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) selectively constrains updates to preserve critical parameters.  
Quick check: In the curriculum, what is the architectural distinction between parameters that control "what to attend to" (visual grounding) versus "how to generate text" (style), and how is each protected during Phase 4?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Frozen LeJEPA → Zone compression → JEPA predictor (with norm cal) → LLM embedding graft + gated x-attn at L7/L14/L21 → Autoregressive decode.

**Critical Path:** The data flow proceeds from frozen LeJEPA slice embeddings through zone-constrained cross-attention (with zone partitioning and global self-attention) to produce 32 tokens. These are projected by the JEPA predictor (with norm calibration) and inserted as <|visual_region|> tokens into the LLM embedding layer. Additional gated cross-attention adapters at layers 7, 14, and 21 process these tokens. The LLM autoregressively generates the report, with cross-attention active at every decode step.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Frozen visual encoder vs. end-to-end:* Sacrifices potential fine-grained feature adaptation for stability and to preserve modality-pure representations.
- *Aggressive compression (600→32 tokens) vs. fidelity:* Zone-constrained approach preserves spatial order but discards intra-zone detail; alternative volumetric projectors might retain more detail but exceed context limits.
- *Positive-findings-only training vs. natural language coverage:* Eliminates posterior collapse but requires a separate Phase 4 to re-introduce normal text style, adding training complexity.

**Failure Signatures:**
- *Posterior collapse:* Generation F1 peaks at epochs 1-4 then crashes to ~0.15. Diagnose by monitoring val F1 vs. epoch; check if visual token ablation (zeroed) causes catastrophic drop.
- *Bridge reset:* Phase 3 cold start shows epoch 1 F1 ~0.360 and slow climb; warm bridge should show epoch 1 F1 >0.425.
- *Anisotropy mismatch:* InfoNCE loss fails to decrease; check discriminability (d') of whitened space; if d' remains low (~1), PCA whitening is not working.
- *Cross-attention skip bug:* If generation F1 is abnormally low (~0.12) but prefill performs better, verify cross-attention hooks fire on every decode step.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Validate zone-constrained output: Feed a volume with known anatomical landmarks (e.g., apex, diaphragmatic dome). Extract the 32 tokens. Apply a linear probe to predict slice z-index. Expect near-perfect accuracy, confirming each token's zone-specific grounding.
2. Reproduce positive-findings training effect: Train two Phase 3 models identical except one uses raw reports, one uses positive-findings-only. Compare validation F1 trajectories. Expect the raw-report model to collapse by epoch 4, while the positive-findings model sustains >0.40.
3. Measure warm bridge impact: From a single converged Phase 2 checkpoint, launch two Phase 3 runs: one cold (random bridge init), one warm (load saved 416 bridge+LoRA params). Compare epoch-1 F1 and convergence curves. Expect warm bridge to start >0.42 and converge faster.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does Ker-VLJEPA-3B generalize to multi-center CT data with varying scanner models and patient demographics? The paper states that multi-center validation would strengthen generalizability claims.
2. Can end-to-end training from raw CT voxels, rather than frozen slice embeddings, improve report generation accuracy? The current design uses a frozen encoder for stability, but joint optimization might allow better adaptation.
3. How do clinical radiologists perceive the quality, accuracy, and workflow utility of the generated reports? The authors acknowledge the lack of a formal observer study.
4. What techniques can enhance model precision while maintaining high recall to balance false positives and false negatives for clinical decision support? The current model’s high recall comes at the cost of lower precision.
5. Is the modality-agnostic curriculum framework effective for integrating self-supervised encoders from non-imaging domains, such as genomic sequences or sensor data? The zone-constrained design is tailored for volumetric CT.

## Limitations
- Warm bridge initialization procedure is underspecified, including how to obtain the prior Phase 3 checkpoint and exact parameter mapping for the 416 bridge+LoRA tensors.
- The preprocessing pipeline for extracting LeJEPA slice embeddings from raw DICOM data (slice selection, z-coordinate handling) is unreleased, blocking replication on new volumes.
