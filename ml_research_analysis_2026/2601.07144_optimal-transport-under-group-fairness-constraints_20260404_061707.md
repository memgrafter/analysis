# Optimal Transport under Group Fairness Constraints
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.07144
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07144
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Three algorithms—FairSinkhorn (exact fairness), penalized OT (convex trade-off), and cost learning (reusable cost)—with theoretical guarantees and synthetic validation for fair optimal transport.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses group fairness in optimal transport for matching problems. Fairness is encoded as meeting predefined target probabilities for matching individuals across sensitive attribute groups. The authors introduce three methods: FairSinkhorn, a modified Sinkhorn algorithm that enforces exact fairness via an additional projection step; a penalized OT approach that adds a convex quadratic fairness penalty to the entropic objective; and a bilevel cost learning method that optimizes the ground cost so the resulting OT plan satisfies fairness and can be reused. Theoretical contributions include a finite-sample complexity bound of O(1/√n) for penalized OT and a generalization bound for cost learning that scales as exp(5RΘ/ε)/√n. Empirical evaluation on synthetic datasets demonstrates the trade-offs between transport cost and fairness, with FairSinkhorn achieving perfect fairness at high cost, penalized OT offering a smooth trade-off, and cost learning providing comparable performance with dramatically faster inference on new data. The work provides both practical algorithms and statistical guarantees for fair matching.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three algorithms for group-fair optimal transport. FairSinkhorn modifies the Sinkhorn iteration by projecting onto the linear manifold defined by fairness constraints on the coupling’s block sums, thereby ensuring exact fairness. The penalized OT approach adds a convex quadratic penalty λ·LF(Π) to the entropic objective, where LF measures deviation from the target block sums, yielding a strongly convex problem solved via a generalized conditional gradient method. Cost learning employs bilevel optimization: the inner problem computes entropic OT with a parametric cost, while the outer problem adjusts parameters to minimize fairness loss plus regularization, enabling the learned cost to be applied to unseen data. The authors provide theoretical statistical guarantees for the latter two methods and validate all three on synthetic data, illuminating the cost–fairness trade-off and the reusability of learned costs.

## Key Results
- FairSinkhorn enforces perfect fairness but often at the expense of higher transport cost.
- Penalized OT offers a smooth trade-off between cost and fairness, supported by an O(1/√n) finite-sample complexity guarantee.
- Cost learning produces a reusable cost function that attains a similar trade‑off with orders‑of‑magnitude faster inference on new data; theoretical generalization bound scales as exp(5RΘ/ε)/√n.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: FairSinkhorn – Exact Fairness via Projected Sinkhorn
**Claim:** Computes an exact group‑fair optimal transport plan efficiently.  
**Mechanism:** Alters the classical Sinkhorn iteration by inserting a projection step onto the linear manifold defined by fairness constraints on the coupling (i.e., block sums equal the target matrix F). Dual variables include Lagrange multipliers, yielding iterative scaling of the transport kernel with an intermediate block‑projection.  
**Core assumptions:** The fairness target F must be a feasible coupling between the group marginals p and q (F ∈ Π(p, q)), and the underlying measures have compact support (Assumption 3.1). Under these conditions, a unique optimal solution exists (Proposition 3.2).  
**Evidence:** The abstract introduces FairSinkhorn; Section 3.3 details the algorithm (Algorithm 1) and the existence/uniqueness guarantee.  
**Break condition:** If F is infeasible (e.g., due to incompatible group marginals), the projection step cannot find a feasible point, causing the algorithm to stall or violate constraints. Numerical rounding errors may accumulate for very small ε, leading to infeasible intermediate matrices.

### Mechanism 2: Penalized OT – Convex Trade‑off with Finite‑Sample Guarantees
**Claim:** Provides a convex relaxation that smoothly trades off transport cost against fairness violation, accompanied by finite‑sample statistical guarantees.  
**Mechanism:** Adds a convex quadratic penalty λ·LF(Π) to the entropic OT objective, where LF(Π) = Σₛ,𝓌 (Tr[Πᵀ Bₛ𝓌] − Fₛ𝓌)² measures deviation from the fairness target. The resulting strongly convex problem is solved via a generalized conditional gradient algorithm: each iteration solves a Sinkhorn subproblem with cost C + ∇LF(Π_t).  
**Core assumptions:** Penalty coefficient λ > 0. For statistical bounds (Theorem 4.2), the ground cost c is infinitely differentiable (Assumption 4.1) and measures are compactly supported.  
**Evidence:** The abstract mentions the penalized OT approach and finite‑sample guarantees; Section 4.1 formulates the problem (5) and states the O(1/√n) sample complexity result (Theorem 4.2).  
**Break condition:** An inappropriate λ leads to extreme outcomes: too small yields large fairness violations; too large forces impractically high transport cost. Convexity relies on the penalty being convex; using a non‑convex fairness loss would break the theoretical foundation.

### Mechanism 3: Cost Learning – Reusable Ground Cost via Bilevel Optimization
**Claim:** Cost learning via bilevel optimization produces a reusable ground cost c_θ such that the resulting entropic OT plan is fair on unseen data, with a theoretical bound on the fairness deviation.  
**Mechanism:** The inner level solves standard entropic OT with cost C_θ to obtain Π_ε(c_θ). The outer level adjusts θ to minimize LF(Π_ε(c_θ)) plus a regularization D(c_θ, c_base). Gradients are computed by differentiating through the OT solver (implicit or iterative differentiation). After training, c_θ can be applied to new samples without retraining.  
**Core assumptions:** The parametric cost family is uniformly bounded (Assumption 4.3) and data distributions are compactly supported. The bilevel objective is smooth but non‑convex; convergence to a good optimum is not guaranteed.  
**Evidence:** The abstract outlines the cost learning strategy and its generalization guarantee; Section 4.2 defines the bilevel problem (7) and presents Theorem 4.4 bounding fairness deviation by O(exp(5RΘ/ε)/√n); Section 5.4 shows empirical generalization despite a training‑test gap.  
**Break condition:** Non‑convexity may cause the optimizer to get stuck in a local minimum that does not satisfy fairness. The generalization bound contains an exponential factor in 1/ε, meaning the theoretical guarantee degrades quickly if ε is small. Overfitting (e.g., overly complex neural net) can lead to large fairness gaps on unseen data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Entropic Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn)**  
  *Why needed:* All three methods rely on entropic OT to ensure a unique, differentiable coupling and enable efficient computation via Sinkhorn iterations.  
  *Quick check:* "Why does adding entropy to optimal transport improve numerical stability and uniqueness?"

- **Group Fairness as Target Matching Probabilities**  
  *Why needed:* The paper’s fairness notion directly translates to linear equality constraints on the OT plan’s block sums, providing a clear formulation.  
  *Quick check:* "What conditions must the fairness target F satisfy to be consistent with the group marginal distributions p and q?"

- **Bilevel Optimization and Hypergradient Estimation**  
  *Why needed:* Cost learning requires optimizing parameters of the cost function that appear inside the solution of an inner OT problem; this bilevel structure necessitates gradients through the OT solver.  
  *Quick check:* "How can one compute the gradient of an OT solution with respect to the cost parameters, and why is implicit differentiation preferred in this context?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Data loader -> Cost calculator -> OT solver (FairSinkhorn / Penalized OT / Cost learning) -> Metric reporter -> Hyperparameter sweeper

**Critical path**  
- *FairSinkhorn:* Load data → compute cost matrix → run FairSinkhorn iterations → output fair coupling.  
- *Penalized OT:* Load data → initialize coupling (e.g., from vanilla OT) → iterate generalized conditional gradient, each iteration calling Sinkhorn on modified cost → output coupling.  
- *Cost learning:* Load data → initialize cost parameters θ → repeat: inner Sinkhorn solves OT with C_θ → compute loss = fairness loss + regularization → backpropagate hypergradient → update θ → after training, apply Sinkhorn with learned c_θ to any new data.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Exact vs. approximate: FairSinkhorn enforces strict constraints but may incur high transport cost; penalized OT offers a smooth trade‑off via λ.  
- Convexity: Penalized OT remains convex, guaranteeing a global optimum; cost learning is non‑convex, risking suboptimal solutions but gains reusability and potential interpretability (e.g., Mahalanobis cost).  
- Reusability: Cost learning yields a cost function that can be deployed on any future dataset; penalized OT must be resolved each time.

**Failure signatures**  
- FairSinkhorn: algorithm fails to converge within max iterations, or block sums after projection deviate from F beyond tolerance; indicates infeasible F or numerical instability (e.g., very small ε).  
- Penalized OT: fairness loss plateaus above the desired threshold despite increasing λ; Sinkhorn subproblems may not converge, or the GC iteration stagnates.  
- Cost learning: training fairness loss does not decrease; hypergradient norms explode or vanish; validation fairness degrades significantly compared to training (overfitting).

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Compare FairSinkhorn, vanilla Sinkhorn (with varying ε), and penalized OT on a simple two‑Gaussian dataset to visualize the cost–fairness trade‑off.  
2. Evaluate penalized OT and cost learning (Mahalanobis and MLP) on two synthetic geometries (Gaussian and Circles) to show how data separability affects achievable fairness–cost curves.  
3. Assess reusability and generalization: Learn a cost on a training set (50k/5k) and compute fairness on test sets of varying sizes; compare inference time and fairness against penalized OT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Downstream fairness impact**  
   - *Question:* How do fairness constraints in optimal transport affect fairness in downstream tasks that use the resulting matches?  
   - *Basis:* The conclusion states: “A promising direction for future work is to study how fairness constraints in OT can lead to fairer outcomes downstream.”  
   - *Why unresolved:* The paper only analyzes the fairness of the transport plan itself, not its impact on subsequent decision‑making pipelines.  
   - *Resolution needs:* Theoretical results linking transport‑level fairness violations to downstream disparity metrics, plus empirical studies on real‑world pipelines (e.g., allocation, recommendation) that incorporate fair OT matches.

2. **Convergence of FairSinkhorn**  
   - *Question:* What is the convergence rate of FairSinkhorn compared to standard Sinkhorn?  
   - *Basis:* Algorithm 1 is introduced without any convergence analysis.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Theoretical guarantees on efficiency and scalability, especially under exact fairness constraints, are missing.  
   - *Resolution needs:* Proof of convergence (e.g., linear rate) with explicit dependency on ε, dimensionality, and structure of the fairness target F, alongside empirical iteration counts on benchmark datasets.

3. **Non‑convex optimization robustness in cost learning**  
   - *Question:* How can we effectively solve the non‑convex bilevel optimization in cost learning to avoid poor local minima and ensure robust fairness guarantees?  
   - *Basis:* Section 4.3 notes the non‑convexity and risk of local minima but relies on generic gradient‑based solvers without assurances.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Lack of algorithmic guarantees hinders trust and limits practical use in high‑stakes fairness applications.  
   - *Resolution needs:* Development of specialized optimization techniques (e.g., safe initialization, stochastic variance reduction, trust‑region methods) with convergence guarantees under smoothness assumptions, plus robustness analyses.

4. **Generalization to real‑world matching data**  
   - *Question:* How well do the proposed fair OT methods generalize to real‑world matching datasets with high‑dimensional features, complex correlation structures, and noisy sensitive attributes?  
   - *Basis:* Experiments are confined to two synthetic settings (Gaussian and Circles).  
   - *Why unresolved:* Synthetic data may not capture challenges like missing values, outliers, or distribution shifts; observed cost‑fairness trade‑offs might change in practice.  
   - *Resolution needs:* Extensive experiments on diverse real‑world matching benchmarks (e.g., school choice, kidney exchange, online labor markets), including ablations on sensitivity to fairness target specification and comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art fairness baselines.

## Limitations
- All methods assume compact support and a feasible fairness target F; violations can cause algorithmic failure (e.g., infeasible projections in FairSinkhorn).  
- Cost learning’s non‑convex optimization lacks global optimum guarantees, and its generalization bound scales as exp(5RΘ/ε)/√n, which may be loose in practice.  
- Empirical evaluation is restricted to synthetic data, limiting understanding of real‑world performance and robustness.

## Confidence
- **FairSinkhorn (Exact Fairness):** High – Theoretical existence/uniqueness (Proposition 3.2) and algorithm are rigorously defined, but confidence drops if assumptions fail.  
- **Penalized OT (Convex Trade‑off):** Medium – Convexity and finite‑sample O(1/√n) bound (Theorem 4.2) are solid, yet empirical validation remains synthetic.  
- **Cost Learning (Reusable Cost):** Medium – Generalization bound and synthetic results support claims, but non‑convexity and exponential dependence on 1/ε weaken theoretical assurances.
