# Score-based diffusion models for diffuse optical tomography with uncertainty quantification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03449  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03449  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A data-driven diffusion prior yields posterior samples with significantly lower variance than classical model-based reconstructions, even under severe ill‑posedness, limited‑view geometries, and out‑of‑distribution targets; regularized UCoS further improves robustness and uncertainty localization compared to diffusion posterior sampling and Gaussian priors.

## Executive Summary
Diffuse optical tomography (DOT) poses a highly ill‑posed inverse problem: estimating internal optical properties from noisy boundary measurements. This paper adapts score‑based diffusion models to sample from the Bayesian posterior for DOT. It evaluates the Unconditional representation for the Conditional Score (UCoS) framework, which avoids repeated forward model evaluations during sampling by learning an unconditional score that incorporates measurements via a simple affine transformation. A novel regularization mixes this data‑driven score with a model‑based Gaussian posterior score, approximating the score of a geometric mixture of densities. Experiments on simulated and real DOT data show that the data‑driven prior dramatically reduces posterior variance while staying centered on the true solution, and the mixed‑score variant adds robustness in limited‑view and out‑of‑distribution scenarios.

## Method Summary
The method treats DOT as a Bayesian inverse problem with a Gaussian noise model and a prior distribution µ over optical parameters. A linearized forward operator A maps parameters to measurements. The posterior µ_y is proportional to the product of the likelihood and prior. To sample from µ_y, the authors use the UCoS framework: a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) is trained to approximate the unconditional score r(η, t; µ) of the prior at a transformed variable η = R_t^{-1} \tilde{x}_t (which depends on y only through an affine shift ξ_t(x,y)). Training minimizes a denoising score matching loss using synthetic data (10 000 images with 1–3 circular inclusions). During reverse‑time SDE sampling, the conditional score s(x,t;µ_y) is computed from r and ξ_t without re‑evaluating A. The regularized variant adds a convex combination of the learned score and the closed‑form score of a Gaussian OU posterior, controlled by a mixing weight α (e.g., 0.5 in simulation, 0.4 in experiment). This geometric mixture approximation improves stability when the data‑driven prior is poorly matched or measurements are limited.

## Key Results
- The data‑driven prior yields posterior samples with far lower variance than a classical Gaussian OU prior, while maintaining low bias, even under severe ill‑posedness and limited‑view geometries.
- Regularized UCoS outperforms plain UCoS, diffusion posterior sampling (DPS), and Gaussian priors in limited‑view and out‑of‑distribution scenarios, with better‑localized uncertainty and higher fidelity.
- The approach succeeds on real experimental DOT data, though occasional failures (missing/hallucinated inclusions) are observed and partially mitigated by regularization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper’s success rests on three intertwined mechanisms:

1. **Efficient Posterior Sampling via Unconditional Score Representation**  
   UCoS rewrites the conditional score s(x,t;µ_y) in terms of an unconditional score r(η,t;µ) and an affine shift ξ_t(x,y) that encodes the measurement. This offloads the expensive forward operator A to the training stage (where it appears in the transformed training targets) and avoids repeated A‑evaluations during sampling. The transformation relies on A being linear (or linearized) and its adjoint being efficiently applicable.

2. **Regularization via Geometric Mixture of Scores**  
   The regularized UCoS mixes the learned score s_θ with a model‑based Gaussian posterior score s(·;ν_y). Theorem 2.6 shows that for small diffusion times t, this convex combination equals the score of a geometric mixture of the corresponding smoothed densities. Intuitively, the Gaussian term steers sampling toward well‑behaved, smooth solutions when the data‑driven prior is mis‑calibrated (e.g., limited‑view or out‑of‑distribution cases).

3. **Data‑Driven Prior Reduces Posterior Variance**  
   The training distribution µ captures realistic spatial structures (localized, piecewise‑constant inclusions) that a simple Gaussian OU prior cannot. The diffusion model learns this complex prior, so when conditioned on measurements the posterior concentrates on plausible solutions that fit the data, rejecting the high‑variance samples produced by the overly smooth Gaussian prior. The variance is naturally localized around inclusion locations and amplitudes.

## Architecture Onboarding
The implementation consists of the following components:

**Component map:**  
Data generator (simulates (x, y) pairs) → Training loop (denoising score matching on FNO) → Sampling engine (reverse‑time SDE) → [Optional] Regularization module (computes Gaussian posterior score and mixes).

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate training data (FE solves for A).  
2. Train FNO to approximate r_θ by minimizing Eq. 12.  
3. For a new measurement y, compute posterior mean and covariance of a smooth OU prior (if regularizing).  
4. Run reverse SDE (500 steps) with score mixing (α) to draw posterior samples.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *FNO capacity:* More layers/modes improve prior capture but risk overfitting; the paper uses 10 layers, 32 channels, 17 modes for 32×32 images.  
- *Regularization weight α:* Higher α increases robustness to limited‑view/OOD but can bias smoothness in‑distribution; tuned per scenario (0.5 sim, 0.4 exp).  
- *Number of sampling steps:* More steps improve quality but increase compute; k=500 balances cost and stability.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Plain UCoS in limited‑view/OOD: hallucinated or missing inclusions, high variance spread.  
- Over‑regularized (α too high): overly smooth reconstructions, merged close inclusions.  
- DPS baseline: very high sample variance, inconsistent inclusion counts.  
- Training instability: near‑zero DSM loss but poor samples indicates memorization or mode collapse.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Full‑view, single circular inclusion (in‑distribution): verifies low‑variance samples and accurate mean.  
2. Limited‑view, two circular inclusions: tests robustness; UCoS should localize inclusions, regularized UCoS sharper, DPS/Gaussian high variance.  
3. Full‑view, OOD target (elliptical/triangular): stresses prior mismatch; UCoS may approximate shapes with multiple circles, regularized variant may merge them; bias quantified against ground truth.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Robustness to modelling errors and real data:** How to further improve UCoS against failures on experimental data (missed/hallucinated inclusions)? The paper does not diagnose specific causes (forward model inaccuracies, noise mismatch, prior shift).  
2. **Theoretical justification for mixed score in infinite dimensions:** Theorem 2.6 holds only in finite dimensions and for small t; the practical infinite‑dimensional, full‑time‑range regularization lacks rigorous foundation.  
3. **Prior representation of complex real tissues:** Training data uses only simple circular inclusions; real tissues exhibit far greater morphological diversity. The prior may mislead, especially under model mismatch. Validating with more diverse (e.g., MRI‑derived) training data remains open.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter details (FNO learning rate schedule, batch size, exact OU covariance parameters) are omitted, hindering exact replication.  
- Experimental data preprocessing (frequency‑domain conversion, noise calibration) is incompletely described, limiting cross‑dataset validation.  
- Regularization parameter α is fixed per scenario without sensitivity analysis; optimal choice for new settings is unclear.

## Confidence
- **UCoS enables efficient posterior sampling:** High (Theorem 2.5 and computational advantage).  
- **Regularized UCoS improves robustness:** Medium (empirical gains in limited‑view/OOD but theory restricted and α selection heuristic).  
- **Data‑driven prior reduces variance:** High (quantitative improvements over Gaussian OU consistently demonstrated).  
- **Generalizability to other inverse problems:** Low (only linearized DOT evaluated; nonlinear modalities may break the linear assumption).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter replication:** Train the FNO with the reported architecture (10 layers, 32 channels, 17 modes) using a systematic grid of learning rates/weight decay; verify DSM loss convergence and that posterior sample quality matches paper figures.  
2. **Out‑of‑distribution stress test:** Introduce targets with shapes absent from training (triangular, star‑shaped) and run both plain and regularized UCoS; measure bias and shape distortion; assess whether α=0.5 (sim) remains optimal.  
3. **Ablation against baselines:** For identical limited‑view and OOD scenarios, draw ∼100 samples from UCoS (varying α), DPS, and Gaussian OU using the same random seeds; compute voxel‑wise variance maps and mean bias to isolate the contribution of the data‑driven prior versus score mixing.
