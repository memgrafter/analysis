# Aligning Language Models from User Interactions
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12273
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12273
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO) improves alignment on real multi-turn interactions; e.g., AlpacaEval 2.0 LC winrate for Qwen3-8B rises from 49.3 to 51.9.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of learning from naturally occurring multi-turn user interactions, which are plentiful but usually discarded due to the absence of explicit feedback signals. The core contribution is Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO), a method that leverages the model’s in-context learning ability: after a user’s follow-up message is observed, the model’s hindsight distribution (conditioned on that follow-up) often reflects improved alignment. SDPO computes token-level advantages as the log‑ratio between the hindsight and original policies and then distills the hindsight distribution back into the current policy via a KL divergence loss. Experiments on real‑world WildChat data show consistent improvements on alignment and instruction‑following benchmarks without degrading other capabilities. The approach also supports personalization and continual adaptation purely from user interactions.

## Method Summary
The task is defined over interaction tuples (x, y, o): x is conversation history, y is the assistant’s response, and o is a user follow‑up. No explicit reward is provided; instead, the follow‑up serves as a hindsight signal. The key insight is that reprompting the model with (x, o) yields a distribution over y, πθ(y|x,o), that is better aligned than the original πθ(y|x). Token‑level advantages are A_i = log πθ(y_i|x,o,y<i) – log πθ(y_i|x,y<i). The policy is optimized to maximize the expected advantage, equivalent to minimizing the token‑level KL divergence between the current policy and a detached hindsight policy. Training involves for each tuple: compute original log‑probs of y, compute hindsight log‑probs using a template (“The following is a future user message. Use this to guide your answer to the user prompt: o”), derive advantages, and update πθ with a policy gradient loss (or directly with a summed KL loss). An off‑policy variant uses a surrogate objective over logged data without importance sampling. Hyperparameters follow Table 5: learning rate 2e‑6, batch size 32, 2 epochs, cosine decay with 5% warmup.

## Key Results
- AlpacaEval 2.0 LC winrate for Qwen3‑8B: 49.3 → 51.9 (+2.6)
- IFEval prompt‑level score: 83.9 → 85.0 (+1.1)
- ArenaHard‑v2 (Hard Prompt): 14.0 → 15.5 (+1.5)
- SDPO enables effective personalization and continual adaptation without degrading general performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SDPO relies on the observation that a user’s follow‑up often encodes implicit feedback about the preceding assistant response. By reprompting the model with that follow‑up, the model’s hindsight distribution over the original response becomes more aligned with user preferences because the additional context clarifies the intended outcome. The token‑level log‑ratio (advantage) between the hindsight and original distributions acts as a learned reward: tokens that increase in probability under hindsight are those that better satisfy the user. Optimizing the policy to increase expected advantage—or equivalently, minimizing KL divergence to the hindsight distribution—distills this implicit feedback into the model. This bootstrapping uses the model’s own in‑context learning as a reward model, removing the need for external supervision.

## Foundational Learning
- **In‑context learning**: The model adjusts predictions based on context without weight updates. *Why needed*: Enables computing a hindsight policy simply by reprompting with the user’s follow‑up. *Quick check*: Does the distribution for a given response change when the follow‑up is added as context?
- **Policy gradient**: An RL method that updates the policy to maximize expected reward. *Why needed*: SDPO’s objective is a policy gradient with token‑level advantages. *Quick check*: Is ∇θ E[log πθ(y) · A] an unbiased estimator of the gradient of expected reward?
- **KL divergence**: Measures difference between two distributions. *Why needed*: SDPO distills the hindsight distribution via a KL loss, preventing large deviations. *Quick check*: Does minimizing KL(πθ || π_hindsight) encourage πθ to match π_hindsight on its support?
- **Token‑level advantage**: Per‑token log‑probability difference between hindsight and original policies. *Why needed*: Provides fine‑grained guidance for the policy at the token level. *Quick check*: Are advantages usually positive for tokens that are refined by the user’s follow‑up?
- **Self‑distillation**: Training a model to match its own distribution under altered conditions. *Why needed*: SDPO uses the model’s own hindsight distribution as a target, avoiding external reward models. *Quick check*: Does the model improve when trained to match its own distribution under a different context?
- **Off‑policy learning**: Updating a policy with data collected from a different behavior policy. *Why needed*: Allows use of logged interactions without requiring the current policy to generate the responses. *Quick check*: Does the surrogate objective remain valid without importance sampling when data is off‑policy?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Data (x,y,o) -> OriginalLogProbs -> HindsightLogProbs -> TokenAdvantages -> LossComputation -> Optimizer -> UpdatedModel

Critical path:
1. Load a batch of interaction tuples.
2. Compute original token log‑probs for y given x.
3. Construct hindsight prompts using the template and compute hindsight token log‑probs given (x, o).
4. Calculate per‑token advantages (log‑ratio).
5. Compute loss (policy gradient or KL divergence).
6. Backpropagate and update model parameters.

Design tradeoffs:
- On‑policy vs. off‑policy: Off‑policy uses logged data, improving sample efficiency but potentially introducing bias; on‑policy yields cleaner gradients but requires generating y at each step.
- Computational cost: Two forward passes per tuple (original andindsight) double the inference overhead; gradient checkpointing may be needed.
- Advantage scaling/clipping: Raw advantages can be noisy; the paper did not mention scaling, but clipping or normalization may stabilize training.
- Loss choice: Policy gradient version (REINFORCE‑style) vs. direct KL minimization—different bias‑variance tradeoffs.

Failure signatures:
- Advantages near zero across a validation batch → indicate feedback‑poor data or ineffective hindsight template.
- Training loss diverges or becomes NaN → likely numerical instability from extreme advantage values or learning rate too high.
- Downstream benchmark scores drop → catastrophic forgetting; consider reducing learning rate or adding regularization.
- Slow or no improvement → batch size too small, learning rate too low, or insufficient epochs.

Three first experiments:
1. Compute the distribution of absolute token advantages on a validation set; verify that a significant proportion are positive and non‑negligible.
2. Train SDPO on a small subset (e.g., 1 k examples) for one epoch and measure change on a held‑out AlpacaEval prompt set.
3. Compare off‑policy SDPO (using logged y) with on‑policy SDPO (sampling y from the current policy) under identical hyperparameters to assess stability and sample efficiency.

## Limitations
- Exact data preprocessing (conversation splitting, truncation to last 5 turns, chat template usage) is unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Off‑policy training details (use of importance sampling, additional KL penalties) are unclear; potential bias from logged behavior policy is not quantified.
- Evaluation settings (benchmark versions, judge models, prompts) vary across papers; precise versions are needed for fair comparison.
