# Specific Emitter Identification via Active Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04502  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04502  
- **Reference count:** 12  
- **Primary result:** An active learning-enhanced semi-supervised training scheme significantly improves specific emitter identification accuracy under limited labeling budgets, with dataset-dependent selection strategies (BALD for WiFi, K-center for ADS-B) and optimal contrastive loss weight α=0.1.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the high annotation cost in specific emitter identification (SEI) by proposing a three-stage semi-supervised framework augmented with active learning (AL). The method first leverages self-supervised contrastive learning with a dynamic dictionary to learn robust representations from abundant unlabeled RF signals. It then fine-tunes on a small labeled set using a joint loss that combines contrastive and cross-entropy objectives. Finally, an AL module iteratively selects the most valuable unlabeled samples for labeling, using either uncertainty (BALD) or representativeness (K-center) criteria depending on dataset complexity. Experiments on ADS-B and WiFi datasets demonstrate that this approach achieves markedly higher recognition accuracy than conventional supervised and semi-supervised methods when labeled data is scarce, confirming that appropriate AL strategy selection is crucial and that a small contrastive loss weight (α=0.1) yields the best performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed scheme consists of three sequential stages. **Stage 1** employs a MoBY-inspired contrastive learning setup: a query encoder is trained with gradient descent, while a momentum-updated key encoder supplies negative samples stored in a queue (dynamic dictionary). Augmented signal pairs (phase-rotated) form positives, and InfoNCE loss enforces representation invariance. **Stage 2** adds a classifier head and trains on the initial small labeled set with a weighted sum of cross-entropy loss and the same contrastive loss (α=0.1). **Stage 3** initiates an AL loop: the current model evaluates the unlabeled pool, selects the top-K samples per round using either BALD (MC dropout uncertainty) or K-center (feature-space coverage), acquires their labels (simulated oracle), appends them to the labeled set, and retrains from the previous checkpoint using the joint loss. This cycle repeats until the labeling budget is exhausted.

## Key Results
- The three-stage pipeline with active learning substantially outperforms conventional CNN and semi-supervised baselines under identical labeling budgets on both ADS-B and WiFi datasets.
- Optimal AL strategy is dataset-dependent: uncertainty-based (BALD) excels on the simpler WiFi distribution, while representativeness-based (K-center) is superior for the complex, overlapping ADS-B distribution.
- The joint loss with a small contrastive weight (α=0.1) achieves the best accuracy; larger α values degrade performance by weakening supervised classification signal.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Self-supervised contrastive learning with a dynamic dictionary extracts robust, discriminative feature representations from large amounts of unlabeled RF signal data.
- **Mechanism:** The query/key branch architecture uses augmented signal pairs. The query encoder updates via gradients; the key encoder updates via momentum and its outputs are stored in a queue. InfoNCE loss treats the augmented pair as positive and all other queue entries as negatives, forcing the model to learn representations invariant to phase rotation while distinguishing different emitters.
- **Core assumption:** Hardware impairments forming the RF fingerprint are preserved through phase rotation; same-emitter instances should be closer in feature space than different-emitter instances.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "self-supervised contrastive learning is employed with a dynamic dictionary update mechanism"; [section III.B] details query/key branch, momentum update, queue, and loss (Eqs. 3-5).
- **Break condition:** Augmentations that destroy discriminative hardware features; if phase rotation is inappropriate or channel effects dominate, representations may not be emitter-specific.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint optimization of contrastive and cross-entropy losses on a small labeled set enhances feature separability and strengthens classification boundaries, improving generalization under label scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** Building on Stage 1’s pre-trained encoder, a classifier head is added. Total loss: L = (1-α)L_CE + αL_CL. Cross-entropy directly optimizes classification; contrastive loss preserves the discriminative feature structure from Stage 1. Small α=0.1 lets supervised signal dominate while retaining beneficial separation.
- **Core assumption:** Stage 1’s feature space is a good initialization; preserving its topology via L_CL prevents catastrophic forgetting or overfitting to the small labeled set.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "jointly optimizes contrastive and cross-entropy losses... to enhance feature separability"; [section III.C] defines joint loss (Eq. 7) and its purpose; [section IV.C, Figure 6] empirical validation that α=0.1 is optimal.
- **Break condition:** Setting α too high (e.g., ≥0.5) weakens CE supervision, degrading performance. If Stage 1 fails completely, Stage 2 cannot recover.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Active learning module selects the most valuable unlabeled samples for expert annotation using a combination of uncertainty (BALD) and representativeness (K-center) criteria, maximizing performance gain per labeled sample.
- **Mechanism:** After supervised training, the model evaluates the unlabeled pool. **Uncertainty (BALD):** Uses MC dropout to estimate predictive entropy; high BALD = high model disagreement = informative near-decision-boundary samples. **Representativeness (K-center):** Selects samples maximally far (cosine distance) from already selected set to cover the feature manifold. Optimal strategy is dataset-dependent: BALD for simpler WiFi, K-center for complex ADS-B.
- **Core assumption:** Informativeness can be estimated by boundary proximity (uncertainty) or global coverage (representativeness); optimal choice correlates with dataset complexity (class overlap, noise).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "selects the most valuable samples... based on the uncertainty and representativeness criteria"; [section III.D] defines BALD (Eq. 8) and K-center (Eq. 9); [section IV.C, Figures 3-5] show BALD underperforms on ADS-B but excels on WiFi; K-center works better on ADS-B.
- **Break condition:** Using a fixed strategy (e.g., always BALD) on complex, overlapping datasets like ADS-B leads to redundant/outlier selection, harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)  
  **Why needed:** To learn a representation space where augmented views of the same RF signal are pulled together (positive) and all others pushed apart (negatives), without labels. This pre-trains the encoder on abundant unlabeled data.  
  **Quick check:** In the MoBY-inspired architecture, what is the purpose of the momentum-updated key encoder and the queue, and how does it differ from updating both branches with gradients?

- **Concept:** Semi-Supervised Learning (Consistency/Co-Training)  
  **Why needed:** The overall scheme is semi-supervised: Stage 1 uses unlabeled data only; Stage 2 uses a small labeled set; Stage 3 iteratively expands the labeled set. This reduces the need for a large initial labeled corpus.  
  **Quick check:** How does the proposed three-stage scheme differ from a standard pseudo-labeling semi-supervised approach where the model's predictions on unlabeled data are used as targets?

- **Concept:** Active Learning (Acquisition Functions)  
  **Why needed:** To strategically select which unlabeled samples should be labeled by an expert, maximizing the accuracy improvement per expensive annotation. It directly addresses the core challenge of labeling cost.  
  **Quick check:** What is the fundamental trade-off between an uncertainty-based acquisition function (like BALD) and a representativeness-based one (like K-center), and what dataset property dictates which is superior?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Encoder (3 conv blocks with 1x7, 1x5 kernels, BN, ReLU, max-pool) -> Projection Head (2 dense layers, 256→128) -> Predictor (2 dense layers, 256→128, only in query branch). The Classifier (3 FC layers with dropout) attaches to encoder/projection output during supervised stages. The Dynamic Dictionary is a fixed-length queue (V=512) storing key branch projections from past batches.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Initialize model with random weights.  
  2. **Stage 1 (Self-supervised):** Train encoder, proj_head, predictor on all unlabeled data using only L_CL. Key encoder updated by momentum (m=0.99).  
  3. **Stage 2 (Supervised):** Add classifier. Train on initial small labeled set with joint loss L = (1-α)L_CE + αL_CL (α=0.1).  
  4. **Stage 3 (AL Loop):**  
     a. Freeze model; extract features from all unlabeled samples.  
     b. Compute acquisition scores (BALD via MC dropout or K-center via cosine distance).  
     c. Select top-K samples, acquire true labels (simulated oracle), add to labeled set.  
     d. Retrain from previous checkpoint on updated labeled set using joint loss.  
     e. Repeat until labeling budget exhausted.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  **α value:** Low α (0.1) preserves classification strength; high α risks weakening CE supervision.  
  **AL Strategy:** BALD is computationally cheaper (per-sample) but fails on complex distributions. K-center requires all features for distance computation (O(U²)) but ensures coverage.  
  **Dictionary Size (V):** Larger V provides more negatives but increases memory and may include less relevant negatives.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Stage 1 loss decreases but linear evaluation accuracy on held-out labeled set does not improve → representations not discriminative for SEI.  
  2. In Stage 3, BALD selector on complex ADS-B leads to accuracy drop → uncertainty estimates corrupted by noise/overlap.  
  3. Joint loss (Stage 2) diverges or CE loss dominates → α may be too low or L_CL scale mismatch.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate Figure 6: Train full pipeline with α ∈ {0,0.01,0.1,0.5,1} on WiFi. Plot accuracy vs. total labeled samples to confirm α=0.1 optimal.  
  2. AL Strategy Swap: On ADS-B, force BALD selection from first round. Compare final accuracy against paper’s K-center result and baseline CNN to quantify penalty of mismatched strategy.  
  3. Ablation - Stage 1 Necessity: Skip Stage 1 (random init), go directly to Stage 2 with joint loss on initial labeled set. Compare accuracy after first AL round against full pipeline to isolate self-supervised pretraining benefit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why does uncertainty-based active learning (BALD) underperform on complex datasets like ADS-B compared to representativeness-based (K-center)?  
  **Basis:** Paper shows BALD worse than conventional methods on ADS-B (Fig.3), attributes to disturbed uncertainty estimates but does not analyze specific mechanisms (e.g., miscalibration, outliers).  
  **Resolution evidence:** Controlled experiments varying class overlap/noise; visualization of selected samples; calibration metrics on ADS-B vs. WiFi.

- **Open Question 2:** How can we automatically determine the optimal weighting factor α for the joint loss without grid search?  
  **Basis:** Paper empirically finds α=0.1 best on WiFi (Fig.6) but provides no theoretical basis or generalization guideline.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Analytical study of loss landscapes/ gradient interactions; correlation of optimal α with data metrics (intra-class variance, number of classes) across multiple datasets.

- **Open Question 3:** Can combining uncertainty and representativeness criteria in active learning lead to further improvements, and how should they be integrated?  
  **Basis:** Paper compares both separately and notes complementarity (Fig.5 discussion) but does not test hybrid approaches.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Experimental evaluation of hybrid AL methods (e.g., weighted sum, sequential) on both datasets; analysis of selection sets for coverage and boundary refinement.

- **Open Question 4:** To what extent does the three-stage scheme depend on initial self-supervised pretraining, and could supervised training with more labels substitute for it?  
  **Basis:** Scheme starts with self-supervised pretraining; claims to mitigate overfitting but does not ablate Stage 1.  
  **Resolution evidence:** Ablation removing Stage 1 (random init or supervised pre-training); comparison with fully supervised models using same labeled sample count; feature quality evaluation (linear probe).

## Limitations
- Dataset sources and preprocessing steps for ADS-B [10] and WiFi [11] are unspecified, requiring guesswork for faithful reproduction.
- Active learning protocol details (number of rounds, batch size, sampling without replacement) are implicit; simulated oracle labeling may not reflect real annotation constraints.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (MC dropout T, dictionary size V, temperature τ) is unexplored; default values may be suboptimal for some scenarios.

## Confidence
- **Contrastive pretraining yields RF-invariant features:** Medium (sound mechanism but RF-specific validation limited to paper).
- **Joint loss with α=0.1 optimally balances objectives:** High (ablation in Fig.6 shows clear peak on WiFi; expected on ADS-B).
- **AL strategy selection is dataset-dependent:** Medium (validated on two datasets; broader generality unknown).
- **Overall three-stage pipeline outperforms baselines:** High (comprehensive experimental comparisons reported).
