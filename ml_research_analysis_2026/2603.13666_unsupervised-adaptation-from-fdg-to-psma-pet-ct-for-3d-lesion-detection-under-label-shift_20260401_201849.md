# Unsupervised Adaptation from FDG to PSMA PET/CT for 3D Lesion Detection under Label Shift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13666
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13666
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Improves AP@0.1 from 0.225 (source-only) to 0.613 on AutoPET 2024 (FDG→PSMA), outperforming conventional self-training and showing FROC gains.

## Executive Summary
Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for 3D medical lesion detection faces unique challenges when moving from FDG to PSMA PET/CT, notably label shift: PSMA lesions are smaller and differently distributed. This paper introduces a self-training framework that specifically addresses label shift in lesion size and count. Two core mechanisms are proposed: adaptive anchor shape updates via exponential moving average (EMA) to align anchor shapes with the smaller target lesions, and prior-guided pseudo-label selection using size-bin quotas derived from an estimated target histogram to maintain the natural size distribution. On the AutoPET 2024 dataset (501 labeled FDG studies, 369 unlabeled PSMA studies), the method yields a dramatic increase in AP@0.1 from 0.225 to 0.613, surpassing conventional self-training and demonstrating better FROC curves. This work illustrates that lightweight, well-designed mechanisms can effectively tackle label shift in detection UDA without complex adversarial training.

## Method Summary
The approach begins with fully supervised pretraining of an M3DRetinaNet detector on the labeled FDG data. After pretraining, an adaptation loop runs for R rounds (200). Each round alternates between: (1) a supervised gradient step on a batch of source (FDG) data; (2) inference on all target (PSMA) volumes using the current model; (3) pseudo-label selection guided by a size-based prior (a volume histogram and mean lesions-per-subject estimate) to assign per-subject budgets and per-bin quotas; (4) EMA updates of the priors and of anchor shapes computed via k-means on the selected pseudo-boxes; and (5) a supervised gradient step on a batch sampled from the newly pseudo-labeled target set. The loop gradually shifts the detector toward the target distribution while preserving source knowledge. Anchor adaptation focuses on better coverage of small lesions, and the quota system ensures the pseudo-labeled set reflects the target's size prevalence, mitigating the bias toward larger FDG-like lesions.

## Key Results
- AP@0.1 increases from 0.225 (source-only) to 0.613 after adaptation.
- The proposed method outperforms conventional self-training (e.g., top-50% confidence selection) across AP@0.1, AP@0.25, AP@0.5.
- FROC curves show improved lesion detection sensitivity at comparable false positive rates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Anchor Shape Adaptation via EMA
Source-derived anchor shapes are poorly aligned with the smaller PSMA lesions, leading to low positive anchor coverage and high regression residuals. After each round, k-means clustering on the union of selected pseudo-labeled boxes estimates new anchor shape centroids. These are updated via exponential moving average: s ← (1−β)s_prev + β * s_new, smoothing updates and preventing drift from noisy pseudo-labels. Evidence includes abstract and section 2.2. Break condition: biased or extremely noisy pseudo-labels could move anchors away from the true distribution, degrading detection.

### Prior-Guided Pseudo-label Selection with Size-bin Quotas
Without guidance, fixed-confidence selection preferentially retains larger, high-uptake lesions (FDG-like) and ignores the many small PSMA lesions. The method estimates a target lesion size histogram h (over volume bins) and mean lesions per subject μ from selected pseudo-labels, updated via EMA. Each subject receives a per-subject budget N_allow = λμ, distributed across bins proportionally to h. Within each bin, top-confidence detections are taken up to the bin's quota. This enforces a size distribution matching the estimated target prior. Evidence: abstract and section 2.3. Break condition: if early pseudo-labels are sparse or biased, histogram may converge slowly or to a wrong distribution, causing under-selection in some bins.

### Alternating Supervised Updates
Training only on noisy pseudo-labeled target data risks catastrophic forgetting of robust source features. The method alternates supervised gradient steps: first on a source batch, then on a target pseudo-labeled batch, while updating priors and anchors after target inference. This anchors the model with clean source loss while gently nudging it toward the target. Evidence: section 2.4. Break condition: if early pseudo-labels are very inaccurate (low precision), alternating may still contaminate the model; bootstrapping requires sufficiently accurate initial pseudo-labels.

## Foundational Learning
- **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA)**: Technique to adapt a model from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain without accessing target labels. *Why needed*: The core problem is adapting the detector from labeled FDG to unlabeled PSMA. *Quick check*: What is the key difference between covariate shift and label shift, and which one is targeted here? (Answer: Covariate shift concerns input distribution change; label shift concerns output/label distribution change; this paper targets label shift in lesion size/prevalence.)
- **Label Shift**: A specific UDA scenario where the marginal distribution of labels changes between domains while the conditional P(features|label) remains the same. *Why needed*: The paper explicitly addresses that lesion size distribution and prevalence differ between FDG and PSMA. *Quick check*: How would a fixed confidence threshold for pseudo-label selection fail under label shift? (Answer: It would preferentially select labels that are common in the source, distorting the target label distribution.)
- **Anchor-based Object Detection (RetinaNet)**: Detection framework that uses a set of pre-defined anchor boxes at multiple scales and aspect ratios; classification and regression are performed relative to these anchors. *Why needed*: The detector (M3DRetinaNet) uses anchors, and misalignment between anchor shapes and target lesion sizes harms performance. *Quick check*: In RetinaNet, what role do anchor shapes play in classification and regression? (Answer: Anchors provide reference boxes; classification predicts objectness per anchor, and regression adjusts anchor to true box.)
- **Self-training**: Semi-supervised learning where a model is iteratively retrained on its own high-confidence predictions (pseudo-labels) for unlabeled data. *Why needed*: The adaptation framework generates pseudo-labels on the target and trains on them. *Quick check*: What is the typical failure mode of self-training when pseudo-labels are biased? (Answer: The model can reinforce its own errors, leading to drift away from the true target distribution.)
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**: An averaging technique that weights recent values more heavily, governed by a decay factor; used for smoothing. *Why needed*: EMA stabilizes updates to anchor shapes and priors against noisy pseudo-labels. *Quick check*: How does EMA differ from a simple running average, and why is it useful here? (Answer: EMA applies a constant decay to older values, making it more responsive to recent changes; it smooths noise while tracking gradual shifts.)
- **Free-Response ROC (FROC)**: Evaluation metric for detection tasks that plots sensitivity (e.g., lesion detection rate) against number of false positives per scan. *Why needed*: Primary evaluation metric for lesion detection sensitivity vs. false positives per scan. *Quick check*: What does a point on an FROC curve represent, and why is it suitable for lesion detection? (Answer: Each point shows the average number of true detections at a given false positive rate per scan; it captures the trade-off relevant to clinical use where false positives are costly.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Source Pretraining → Alternating Training Loop (Inference → Pseudo-label Selection using priors → Anchor Adaptation & Prior Estimation → Supervised Source update → Supervised Target update). The Anchor Adaptation module updates anchor shapes via k-means+EMA on selected pseudo-boxes; Prior Estimation maintains EMA-smoothed histogram h and mean lesions μ; Pseudo-label Selector uses h and μ to enforce size-bin quotas.
- **Critical path**: 1. Pretrain M3DRetinaNet on FDG with anchors from k-means on FDG boxes. 2. For round = 1 to R=200: a) infer on all PSMA with current model; b) select pseudo-labels via quota system (using current h, μ); c) update μ, h, and anchors S via EMA; d) train one epoch on FDG; e) train one epoch on pseudo-labeled PSMA. 3. Evaluate on PSMA with final anchors.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Bin granularity (B)*: More bins capture finer size distributions but risk sparse counts; fewer bins are robust but may merge distinct modes.
  - *EMA momenta (α_μ, α_h, β)*: Higher values increase stability but slow adaptation; lower values adapt faster but amplify noise.
  - *Selection factor λ schedule*: Starting too high admits many false positives; starting too low starves the model. Linear ramp (0.1→0.8) balances exploration and exploitation.
  - *Efficiency vs. alignment*: Anchor adaptation and histogram estimation add overhead but are lightweight compared to adversarial UDA discriminators for 3D dense proposals.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - *Anchor drift*: Anchor volumes become abnormally small or large; indicates noisy pseudo-labels overwhelming EMA.
  - *Quota starvation*: Many bins receive zero quota; histogram h has near-zero values for certain bins; suggests initial bias or insufficient detections.
  - *Training instability*: Loss spikes or divergence during target updates; suggests pseudo-label precision is too low; raise confidence threshold τ or reduce λ.
  - *Poor small-lesion recall*: Despite mechanisms, AP@0.1 remains low for small lesions; check bin boundaries and whether small lesions are consistently selected.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablation – Anchor adaptation only: Enable anchor EMA updates but use fixed-confidence pseudo-label selection (no prior-guided quotas). Expected: modest AP gain, especially on small lesions, but bias toward larger lesions persists.
  2. Ablation – Prior-guided selection only: Freeze anchors at source values but use size-bin quotas with fixed source histogram (no EMA update). Expected: improves recall across sizes but may not fully align anchor coverage for smallest lesions.
  3. Baseline comparison – Conventional self-training: Use top-50% confidence selection and static source anchors. Expected: underperforms on small lesions due to label shift, validating the necessity of both mechanisms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can an adaptive binning method that accounts for lesion clustering improve pseudo-label selection accuracy for label shift? (Fixed bins may group clustered lesions inaccurately.)
- How does incorporating model uncertainty into pseudo-label selection affect robustness and performance under label shift? (Current selection uses only confidence; uncertainty could reduce noise impact.)
- Can an adaptive binning strategy driven by information-theoretic criteria automatically optimize the trade-off between granularity and reliability? (Fixed bin number may be suboptimal.)
- How can the proposed label shift mitigation be extended to multi-class lesion detection with distinct size and prevalence distributions? (Current one-class simplification; multi-class requires per-class quotas and size priors.)

## Limitations
- The method is sensitive to pseudo-label noise in early rounds; if confidence threshold τ is too low or initial detections are poor, anchor adaptation and histogram updates may diverge.
- Critical architectural and training details (e.g., exact M3DRetinaNet configuration, source pretraining hyperparameters) are omitted, hindering exact reproducibility.
- Evaluation is limited to the AutoPET 2024 dataset; generalizability to other tracers, scanners, or institutions remains unproven.

## Confidence
- Anchor shape EMA adaptation: Medium (supported by ablation but sensitive to noise)
- Prior-guided pseudo-label selection: Medium (theoretically plausible, relies on accurate histogram estimation)
- Overall UDA improvement: High (large AP gain on standard benchmark with ablations)
