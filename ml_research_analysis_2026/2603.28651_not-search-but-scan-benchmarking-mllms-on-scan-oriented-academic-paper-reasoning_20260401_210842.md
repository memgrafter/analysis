# Not Search, But Scan: Benchmarking MLLMs on Scan-Oriented Academic Paper Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.28651
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28651
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The best MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro on OCR-text) achieves only 30.3 points; no category exceeds 50; reasoning-enhanced models improve >10% absolute; RAG yields negligible gains.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces *ScholScan*, a new benchmark for scan-oriented academic paper reasoning, which requires models to perform exhaustive full-document consistency checking without pre-specified targets. The dataset comprises 1,800 instances across nine error types, 715 papers, and includes fine-grained annotations of evidence locations and reasoning chains. Evaluation of 15 multimodal LLMs (24 input/config combinations) and 8 RAG systems reveals uniformly low performance—top scores around 30—and highlights a severe bottleneck in constructing long causal reasoning chains. Reasoning-enhanced models (e.g., Qwen‑3‑Thinking) outperform their base counterparts by over 10% absolute, while RAG fails to provide meaningful improvements. The work establishes scan-oriented tasks as a challenging paradigm that exposes critical weaknesses in current MLLMs’ document-level understanding and suggests new directions for building research‑assistant systems.

## Method Summary
The ScholScan benchmark is built from ICLR and Nature papers, with synthetic error instances generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro and validated by humans. Each instance asks the model to scan an entire paper (provided as either page images or OCR text) for a specific error category, without hinting at where errors might occur. Annotations include the existence of an error, the precise evidence spans, and a reasoning chain of causal steps. For evaluation, models generate open-ended answers; a GPT‑4.1 extractor parses these into structured outputs (existence, evidence count, reasoning steps, unrelated errors). The final score combines detection accuracy, evidence localization (Dice), reasoning completeness, and a penalty for hallucinated errors. The study tests 15 MLLMs and 8 RAG configurations, comparing image and text inputs and analyzing per‑category performance, reasoning chain length, and cross‑paper consistency.

## Key Results
- Top scores: Gemini 2.5 Pro reaches 30.3 on OCR text; GPT‑5 reaches 19.2 on images; no category exceeds 50 points.
- Reasoning‑enhanced models (e.g., Qwen‑3‑Thinking) consistently outperform base models by >10% absolute across both input modalities.
- RAG systems provide negligible gains; most degrade performance, with Recall@5 below 0.5 and no improvement in overall scores.
- Most open‑source models score near zero, indicating the task’s extreme difficulty.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The scan-oriented paradigm better approximates real research workflows by removing pre-specified targets, forcing models to engage in exhaustive document scanning and active evidence construction rather than targeted retrieval.
- **Mechanism**: Without target cues, models cannot rely on relevance-based retrieval shortcuts. They must process entire documents, maintain a global evidence view, and perform cross-referencing across sections to detect inconsistencies. This mirrors how human researchers read papers.
- **Core assumption**: Real-world academic research requires comprehensive document understanding and proactive discovery of implicit problems, not just retrieval of known answers.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "current work on academic paper reasoning is largely confined to a search-oriented paradigm centered on pre-specified targets, with reasoning grounded in relevance retrieval, which struggles to support researcher-style full-document understanding, reasoning, and verification."
  - [section 1]: "models must move beyond reactive question answering and toward proactive discovery of implicit problems."
  - [corpus]: Neighboring papers (e.g., MC-Search, Taming the Untamed) also explore retrieval for complex reasoning, indicating this is an active research direction but current methods remain search-oriented.
- **Break condition**: If the task becomes too open-ended without clear success criteria, the paradigm loses discriminative power and evaluation reliability.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The 9-category error taxonomy with fine-grained annotations enables process-aware evaluation that can diagnose specific capability gaps rather than just aggregate performance.
- **Mechanism**: By separately measuring detection, evidence localization, and reasoning completeness (and penalizing hallucinated errors), the framework identifies whether failures stem from scanning ability, logical integration, or evidence grounding. This allows targeted improvement.
- **Core assumption**: Understanding failure modes is more valuable than a single score for guiding future research on document-level reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "ScholScan provides fine-grained annotations for both evidence location and reasoning steps, enabling a comprehensive evaluation framework that assesses model performance in terms of both process and outcome."
  - [section 4.3]: Correlation analysis shows "CF exhibits consistently low correlations with other error categories" and "DI does not exhibit strong correlations with SG, MO, or DHP," indicating distinct skill dimensions.
  - [section 4.4]: "As reasoning steps increase, both reasoning and overall scores steadily decline, revealing a clear bottleneck in MLLMs' ability to construct long causal chains."
- **Break condition**: Inconsistent annotation quality across the 1,800 instances would undermine the reliability of fine-grained attribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Reasoning-enhanced architectures (thinking models) provide iterative refinement that better simulates the extraction‑then‑reasoning cycle required for scan tasks.
- **Mechanism**: Longer context windows combined with explicit chain‑of‑thought reasoning allow models to maintain evidence across document spans, integrate multiple pieces of information, and build coherent causal chains. This addresses the need for multi‑step consistency checking.
- **Core assumption**: Scan-oriented tasks require multi‑step evidence integration; single‑pass generation without explicit reasoning is insufficient for reliable performance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "reasoning-enhanced variants (e.g., Qwen‑3‑Thinking) outperform base models by >10% absolute."
  - [section 4.2]: "Reasoning‑enhanced models demonstrate clear advantages. Across both input configurations, reasoning‑enhanced variants consistently achieve higher scores."
  - [section 4.3, Figure 5]: "Stronger models often scan up to 8 times more evidence and execute 3.5 times more reasoning steps than the reference answers."
- **Break condition**: Very long reasoning chains degrade coherence; Figure 6 shows performance declines as reasoning steps increase, indicating a scalability limit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal document understanding**
  - **Why needed here**: The benchmark requires models to process both OCR text and document images, with formulas and layouts critical for certain error categories. Understanding the gap between text and image inputs is central to the paper's findings.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does image input sometimes outperform OCR text for CF (Computation & Formulae) errors, but generally underperform for other categories?

- **Concept: Evaluation design for generative tasks**
  - **Why needed here**: The paper's scoring function combines detection, evidence location, reasoning process, and hallucination penalties. Understanding this design is essential to interpret the results and why RAG fails.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the evidence location Dice score penalize incorrect evidence selection, and why does this matter for scan-oriented tasks?

- **Concept: Error taxonomies for academic writing**
  - **Why needed here**: The 9 error categories structure the entire benchmark. Knowing the definitions (RQD, DI, SG, MO, DHP, CF, IC, RCA, LE) is necessary to understand what capabilities are being tested and the correlation analysis.
  - **Quick check question**: What distinguishes a Design & Identifiability error from a Sampling & Generalizability error in the context of experimental validity?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  Input Processor -> Query Engine -> Model Under Test -> Extractor Module -> Scorer
  (Additionally, RAG Systems can be inserted between Query Engine and Model Under Test)

- **Critical path**:
  1. Prepare 715 papers (image + OCR text) and 1,800 question instances across 9 error types.
  2. Run each model with both input configurations, collecting open-ended responses.
  3. Extract evidence and reasoning from responses using the extractor prompt.
  4. Compute final scores and analyze per-category performance.
  5. For RAG experiments, integrate retrieval before model input and repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Image vs OCR text**: Text generally performs better except for formula-heavy CF errors where visual information is essential. Image processing is a current bottleneck.
  - **Single-paper vs Cross-paper**: Cross-paper errors test citation consistency but are harder to construct and may rely on paraphrasing quality.
  - **Precision vs Recall**: The metric heavily penalizes unrelated errors (P_unrelated_err), favoring conservative detectors but potentially missing subtle issues.
  - **Annotation cost**: High-quality, multi-stage human validation was required to reduce hallucinations in synthetic data.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Zero-score patterns**: Either complete omission (model finds no error) or hallucination overload (model invents errors and misses the target).
  - **Reasoning chain collapse**: Score drops rapidly when reasoning steps exceed 3-4, indicating difficulty maintaining long causal chains.
  - **RAG degradation**: Most RAG methods lower performance compared to baseline, suggesting retrieval introduces noise or misalignment for consistency-checking tasks.
  - **Open-source model collapse**: Many open-source models score near zero, indicating the task complexity exceeds current capabilities.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline scan capability**: Run a single proprietary model (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro) on both image and text inputs across all 9 categories to establish a performance baseline and confirm the text/image gap.
  2. **RAG integration test**: Implement BM25 and VRAG-RL with the best base model (Qwen3-Thinking for text, Llama 4 Maverick for image) to verify the paper's finding that RAG provides negligible gains.
  3. **Reasoning chain analysis**: Use the scoring breakdown to plot performance vs. number of required reasoning steps and evidence locations, replicating Figure 6 to diagnose whether your implementation shows the same bottleneck patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can scan-oriented task paradigms be systematically extended to academic disciplines beyond the natural sciences covered in ScholScan?
- What architectural or algorithmic innovations are necessary to improve MLLM performance on target-suppressed inputs in scan-oriented tasks?
- Why do existing RAG methods yield negligible improvements in scan-oriented tasks, and how can retrieval mechanisms be redesigned to support cross-document consistency checking?
- What fundamental cognitive or strategic differences exist between human expert scanning and MLLM scanning processes that explain the latter's inefficiency and low accuracy?

## Limitations
- Synthetic data construction (LLM + human) may introduce biases.
- Closed extractor (GPT-4.1) parameters undisclosed; affects reproducibility.
- Unreleased models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) prevent exact replication; open-source near-zero scores may be prompt-sensitive.
- Dataset limited to ICLR/Nature English papers; domain coverage narrow.

## Confidence
- **Scan paradigm reveals systematic MLLM weaknesses** – High
- **Reasoning-enhanced models improve performance** – High
- **RAG negligible for scan tasks** – Medium
- **Fine-grained taxonomy diagnoses distinct gaps** – Medium
- **Synthetic data quality** – Low

## Next Checks
1. **Transparent replication**: Run the benchmark on open-source MLLMs (e.g., Llama-4, Qwen-3) using the exact prompts and extraction code; verify ranking stability and reasoning-chain collapse.
2. **OCR fidelity analysis**: Measure formula/table retention in OCR text across multiple engines; correlate with CF/MO/RCA scores to isolate visual understanding limits.
3. **RAG design sweep**: Test diverse retrievers (BM25, dense, hybrid), chunk sizes, and query strategies to confirm whether any RAG setup yields gains; if not, RAG is fundamentally mismatched to scan tasks.
