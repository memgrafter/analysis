# Unbiased Approximate Vector-Jacobian Products for Efficient Backpropagation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.14701
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14701
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Replaces exact vector-Jacobian products with unbiased randomized approximations via sketching, reducing computational/memory costs while maintaining convergence; accuracy drop under 2% at 5–10% sampling budget on MNIST/CIFAR-10 models.

## Executive Summary
Deep neural network training incurs high computational and memory costs, particularly during backpropagation. This paper introduces a method to replace exact vector-Jacobian products (VJPs) with randomized, unbiased approximations using sketching matrices. The approach reduces per-iteration FLOPs and communication overhead, especially in pipeline parallelism, while preserving the convergence properties of stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Theoretical analysis shows that variance from approximations propagates through the network, potentially damping or amplifying based on Jacobian spectra. Experiments on multi-layer perceptrons (MNIST), BagNet-17, and Vision Transformers (CIFAR-10) demonstrate that diagonal data-dependent sketching (e.g., ℓ1-score) yields strong accuracy-cost trade-offs: at low sampling budgets (p=0.05–0.1), test accuracy degradation is typically under 2% while compute and communication are reduced proportionally. Uniform masking consistently underperforms, highlighting the benefit of importance weighting. Optimal spectral sketches minimize local distortion but are not always dominant due to their local-only nature and computational cost.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the backward pass of linear layers (fully-connected or 1×1 convolutions). Instead of computing the exact vector-Jacobian product \(J^\top g\), it uses a sketching matrix \(R\) with \(\mathbb{E}[R]=I\) to compute an unbiased approximation \((JR)^\top g = J^\top(R^\top g)\). For each layer, importance scores per output channel are derived from the batch gradients (e.g., ℓ1 norm of \(G\)). Given a fixed sampling budget \(r\) (approximately \(r = p \times d_\text{out}\)), a convex optimization (Alg. 1) yields probabilities \(p_j\) that minimize variance under an unbiasedness constraint. Exactly \(r\) channels are then sampled without replacement using correlated Bernoulli trials (Alg. 2). The selected gradient columns are rescaled by \(1/p_j\), and the approximate gradient for the layer is assembled. This reduces FLOPs and communication by a factor proportional to \(p\), while preserving unbiasedness and thus SGD convergence. The approach is applied to all linear layers except the final classification head, and can be combined with standard optimizers and architectures.

## Key Results
- Unbiased sketching of VJPs reduces computational and communication costs without biasing gradients, maintaining SGD convergence.
- Diagonal importance sampling (e.g., ℓ1-score) achieves <2% accuracy degradation at 5–10% sampling budget on MLP, BagNet-17, and ViT.
- Variance propagation analysis shows errors may dampen or amplify depending on Jacobian spectra; optimal spectral sketches minimize local distortion but simpler proxies often suffice in practice.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Unbiased Gradient Approximation**  
Replace exact VJPs \(J^\top g\) with approximations \((JR)^\top g\) using random \(R\) where \(\mathbb{E}[R]=I\), yielding conditional unbiasedness \(\mathbb{E}[\hat{J}g \mid g]=Jg\). This reduces per-iteration cost without biasing gradients, and SGD convergence holds as long as added variance \(V\) is bounded and independent.

**Mechanism 2: Variance Propagation**  
Proposition 2.2: Variance at a node decomposes into local distortion plus propagated error from predecessors, scaled by squared Jacobian norms. Thus approximation error can dampen or amplify along the backward pass depending on the Jacobian spectra. Exploding norms (e.g., in unstable RNNs) break convergence.

**Mechanism 3: Optimal Importance Sampling**  
For a fixed sketching rank \(r\), minimizing variance under unbiasedness leads to a convex program: sample coordinates with probabilities \(p_i^* \propto \sqrt{w_i}\), where importance weights \(w_i\) come from second-moment matrices of the Jacobian and gradient statistics. Diagonal sketches in the eigenbasis of \(\Gamma_B^{1/2} J^\top J \Gamma_B^{1/2}\) are optimal locally (RCS). However, practical proxies like ℓ1-norm are cheaper and often perform similarly.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Forward pass unchanged; backward pass modified at linear layers (fully‑connected or 1×1 convs) to replace \(J_{ij}^\top \hat{g}_j\) with \((J_{ij}R)^\top \hat{g}_j\). Sketching matrix \(R\) is chosen per layer based on importance scores (e.g., ℓ1 of output gradients). Key components: (1) score computation (e.g., \(\|G[:,j]\|_1\) per output channel), (2) probability solver (Alg. 1), (3) correlated exact‑r sampler (Alg. 2), (4) masked gradient assembly with rescaling.

**Critical path:**  
During backprop for a linear layer: cache forward inputs/weights → compute output gradient \(G\) → compute scores → compute \(p_j^*\) → sample indices \(I\) → construct \(\hat{G}\) by zeroing columns not in \(I\) and rescaling by \(1/p_j\) → complete backward with \(\hat{G}W\) and \(W^\top\hat{G}\). The score computation and sampling must be fast relative to the matmul they approximate.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Variance vs. compute/comm:* Lower sampling budget \(r\) reduces FLOPs/bandwidth but increases variance \(V\), possibly requiring more epochs.  
- *Proxy vs. optimal scores:* ℓ1/ℓ2 proxies are cheaper but suboptimal vs. spectral (RCS). RCS uses SVD of \(\Gamma_B^{1/2}J^\top J\Gamma_B^{1/2}\) (costly) and may not dominate empirically due to local‑only optimization.  
- *Diagonal vs. full sketches:* Diagonal masks (per‑column) are simpler and memory‑efficient; full spectral sketches can capture cross‑coordinate importance but require more complex implementations.  
- *Per‑layer uniform vs. adaptive:* Applying the same budget everywhere is simpler; adapting per‑layer based on local Jacobian sensitivity might improve overall accuracy.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Training instability:* High variance \(V\) may cause divergence or noisy loss curves; reduce learning rate or increase \(p\).  
- *Accuracy drop beyond expected:* If local Jacobian norms are large (e.g., deep networks with residual connections), error amplification may cause >2% accuracy degradation even at \(p=0.1\).  
- *Implementation mismatch:* Using independent Bernoulli instead of correlated exact‑r sampler leads to variable rank across steps, slightly hurting low‑budget performance.  
- *Score miscomputation:* Using forward activations instead of backward gradients for importance weights degrades performance.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline verification:** Train 4‑layer MLP on MNIST without approximation; cross‑validate learning rate to obtain target test accuracy (expect ~98%).  
2. **Proxy comparison sweep:** For each budget \(p \in \{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5\}\), run MLP with per‑column masks using uniform, ℓ1, ℓ2, Var, G‑SV scores. Plot accuracy vs. \(p\); expect ℓ1 to be on upper envelope.  
3. **Scaling to larger models:** Apply Diagonal Sketching (DS) and RCS to BagNet‑17 and ViT on CIFAR‑10. Verify that accuracy degradation is marginal (e.g., <2%) at \(p=0.05\)–0.1. Also test placement (all layers vs. only first/last).

## Limitations
- The method assumes bounded Jacobian norms and independent sketching noise; these may not hold for unstable architectures (e.g., deep RNNs) or when gradients are correlated.
- Optimal spectral sketching (RCS) is computationally expensive and only locally optimal; cheaper proxies (ℓ1) often work as well in practice, limiting the advantage of optimal spectra.
- Experiments are limited to modest models (MLP, BagNet‑17, ViT) on MNIST/CIFAR‑10; scalability to larger datasets and models (e.g., ImageNet, GPT) remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Unbiasedness and convergence preservation:** Medium – solid derivation but relies on boundedness assumptions.
- **Variance propagation via Jacobian spectra:** High – Proposition 2.2 provides direct theoretical grounding.
- **Importance sampling vs. uniform masking:** Medium – experimental evidence on a few models.
- **Optimality of spectral sketches (RCS) locally:** High – theoretically proven and empirically validated on small benchmarks.
