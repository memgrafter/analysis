# Argument Reconstruction as Supervision for Critical Thinking in LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17432
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17432
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Training on argument reconstruction yields significant gains on 7 critical thinking tasks (e.g., +2.6 F1 on ArgsNovel, +1.5% Acc on ReClor, +51.3% F1 on WebisArgQuality20), with strong data efficiency in low-resource settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) can improve critical thinking by learning argument reconstruction—a fundamental human skill of extracting explicit and implicit premises and conclusions. The authors introduce GAAR, an automatic engine that reconstructs arbitrary arguments (including fallacious ones) into valid premise-conclusion structures, and use it to create the Arguinas dataset (2,850 pairs). GAAR achieves perfect validity and superior faithfulness compared to baselines. Models finetuned on Arguinas for argument reconstruction outperform non-reconstruction models across seven downstream critical-thinking benchmarks, with the largest gains observed. The approach proves particularly data-efficient in low-resource regimes, demonstrating that structured supervision from high-quality reconstructions can endow LLMs with transferable reasoning skills.

## Method Summary
GAAR is a multi-stage pipeline that processes raw argument texts (with optional titles and backgrounds) into structured outputs: lists of explicit and implicit premises plus a conclusion. Its stages include fallacy detection, type-specific reconstruction guidance, formalization into first-order logic, validity checking and premise pruning via a SAT solver, streamlining via back-translation, and a fine-grained faithfulness judgment. Using Claude Sonnet 4.5 as the core LLM, GAAR generates the Arguinas dataset. For training, the authors finetune Qwen3 base models and Qwen2.5 instruct models in two regimes: (1) pre-adaptive finetuning—first on Arguinas, then on each downstream task; and (2) continued finetuning—directly on Arguinas without downstream adaptation. Evaluation across seven critical-thinking benchmarks (e.g., ReClor, ArgsNovel, WebisArgQuality20) uses standard metrics (Accuracy, Macro F1) with multiple generations per test instance.

## Key Results
- GAAR achieves perfect validity and superior faithfulness over baselines like AAR and direct prompting.
- Pre-adaptive finetuning on Arguinas yields consistent improvements on all seven downstream tasks (e.g., +2.6 Macro F1 on ArgsNovel, +1.5% Accuracy on ReClor), with the largest gains in low-resource settings.
- Continued finetuning alone increases performance on WebisArgQuality20 by 51.3% F1 and shows strong data efficiency, confirming that reconstruction training transfers broadly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Training on argument reconstruction provides structured supervision that teaches LLMs to explicitly identify and link premises and conclusions, building a foundational skill for critical thinking.
- **Mechanism**: GAAR generates high-fidelity premise-conclusion structures with validity guarantees, forcing the model to learn logical inference patterns rather than surface-level text matching. This structured output serves as a "thinking scaffold" during training.
- **Core assumption**: Explicitly modeling inferential relationships improves downstream reasoning because it compels the model to internalize logical dependencies.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Models trained on argument reconstruction outperform those without on seven critical thinking tasks.
  - GAAR achieves perfect validity and superior faithfulness over baselines.
  - Arguinas is uniquely general (includes fallacies, multiple domains) compared to neighboring datasets.
- **Break condition**: If GAAR's reconstructions become invalid or unfaithful, the supervision signal degrades and downstream gains diminish.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The skills from reconstruction transfer broadly because argument analysis is domain-agnostic; learning to dissect logical structure in one context improves evaluation across diverse reasoning tasks.
- **Mechanism**: Reconstruction forces the model to separate content from logical form—extracting implicit warrants, identifying assumptions, and pruning irrelevant details. This meta-skill of "structural unpacking" applies to legal reasoning, argument quality judgment, novelty detection, etc.
- **Core assumption**: Critical thinking is unified by a common core of structural analysis, not task-specific heuristics.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Pre-adaptive finetuning on Arguinas improves performance across seven heterogeneous downstream tasks.
  - Continued finetuning on Arguinas alone also boosts performance, indicating retained structural knowledge without further adaptation.
  - Neighboring papers focus on narrower argument tasks; this work's general approach yields broader transfer.
- **Break condition**: If downstream tasks require content-specific knowledge not captured by structural patterns (e.g., domain expertise), transfer may plateau without supplementary training.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: GAAR's iterative refinement loop with fine-grained faithfulness criteria ensures high-quality synthetic data, making the approach data-efficient—especially in low-resource settings.
- **Mechanism**: The engine's multi-stage validation (validity check, premise pruning, three-part faithfulness judgment) filters out flawed reconstructions. This yields a dataset where each sample reliably represents an argument's logical skeleton, allowing models to learn from fewer examples.
- **Core assumption**: Clean, consistent supervision is more valuable than large quantities of noisy data for learning reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Ablation studies show that removing fine-grained faithfulness criteria or fallacy-handling logic degrades quality.
  - Arguinas pre-adaptation is most beneficial in low-resource regimes for tasks like ReClor and UKPConvArg2.
  - Neighboring datasets (AAAC, EntailmentBank) are less effective, likely due to synthetic simplicity and lack of fallacies.
- **Break condition**: If the cost of generating high-fidelity reconstructions becomes prohibitive or the engine's assumptions fail for certain argument types, data efficiency gains may reverse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Argument Reconstruction**  
  Why needed here: The entire premise is that reconstructing arguments (extracting explicit/implicit premises and conclusion) is the key skill that enhances critical thinking. Models must first learn this mapping from raw text to structured logic.  
  Quick check question: Given an argument, can you identify its main conclusion and at least two implicit premises that bridge gaps in the reasoning?

- **Concept: Validity vs. Faithfulness**  
  Why needed here: GAAR distinguishes *validity* (premises logically entail conclusion) from *faithfulness* (reconstruction preserves original intent). A valid but unfaithful reconstruction misrepresents the argument; a faithful but invalid one fails to capture proper inference. Both dimensions are essential for quality supervision.  
  Quick check question: Is a reconstruction that adds a strong but unintended premise still faithful? What about one that misses an essential implicit premise?

- **Concept: Pre-adaptive vs. Continued Finetuning**  
  Why needed here: Two distinct training scenarios are evaluated. *Pre-adaptive* uses Arguinas as an intermediate step before downstream finetuning; *continued* directly finetunes an instruction-tuned model on Arguinas without further adaptation. Understanding the difference explains when and why the approach works.  
  Quick check question: Why might continued finetuning improve a model's performance on a downstream task without any further training on that task?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Raw arguments → GAAR Engine (multi-stage pipeline) → Arguinas dataset (argument-reconstruction pairs) → Finetuning (Base models: pre-adaptive; Instruct models: continued) → Downstream evaluation on seven critical-thinking benchmarks.

- **Critical path**: From raw argument collection → running GAAR to synthesize Arguinas → pre-adaptive or continued finetuning → evaluation on downstream tasks. The validity and faithfulness of GAAR's outputs are the linchpins; if these degrade, all downstream benefits are questionable.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Cost vs. Quality**: Selecting Claude Sonnet 4.5 as GAAR's base model balanced reconstruction fidelity and API expense.
  - **Generality vs. Depth**: GAAR uses both general (4-type) and specific (Walton's 60 schemes) argument taxonomies to cover diverse real-world arguments, adding complexity.
  - **Iteration vs. Efficiency**: The engine's iterative loop (feedback from validity/faithfulness stages) improves quality but increases latency and token usage.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - GAAR produces low validity or faithfulness on held-out domains (e.g., very short social media arguments).
  - Downstream tasks show no improvement or regression after Arguinas finetuning, especially on tasks requiring non-structural knowledge.
  - Continued finetuning degrades instruction-following or general capabilities.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Reconstruction Quality Benchmark: Run GAAR vs. baselines on a diverse sample; measure validity (SAT solver) and faithfulness (LLM judge). Expect GAAR >95% validity and >50% faithful win rate.
  2. Pre-adaptive Finetuning on ReClor: Finetune Qwen3-4B-Base on Arguinas then ReClor; expect at least +1.5% accuracy gain in low-resource settings.
  3. Continued Finetuning on WebisArgQuality20: Finetune Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on Arguinas only; expect significant F1 improvement (+51.3%).

## Limitations
- GAAR's perfect validity may not generalize to unseen domains (e.g., social media, non-English arguments) or complex fallacies.
- Downstream improvements rely on unspecified prompts, parsing, and hyperparameters, posing reproducibility risks.
- The causal link between reconstruction training and critical thinking is inferred; gains could stem from data diversity rather than structural learning.
