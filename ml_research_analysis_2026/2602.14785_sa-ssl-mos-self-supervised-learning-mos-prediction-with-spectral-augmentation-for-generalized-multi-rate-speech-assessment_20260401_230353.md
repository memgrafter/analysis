# SA-SSL-MOS: Self-supervised Learning MOS Prediction with Spectral Augmentation for Generalized Multi-Rate Speech Assessment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14785
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14785
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Two-step SA-SSL-MOS achieves best AudioMOS test performance (MSE 0.377, LCC 0.848, SRCC 0.750) and substantially improves generalization across six out-of-domain datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles multi-rate speech quality assessment (SQA) for audio sampled between 16 kHz and 48 kHz. Standard self-supervised learning (SSL) models are typically trained on 16 kHz audio, discarding perceptually important high-frequency information in higher-rate recordings. The proposed SA-SSL-MOS uses a parallel-branch architecture: one branch processes downsampled 16 kHz audio with a pretrained SSL model (Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-2B) to capture contextual features, while a second branch upsamples to 48 kHz and processes a log-magnitude spectrogram with a 2D CNN to preserve high-frequency details. A two-step training strategy first pre-trains on the large 48 kHz NISQA dataset, then fine-tunes on the small multi-rate AudioMOS dataset. This approach yields state-of-the-art performance on the AudioMOS test set and demonstrates robust generalization across diverse out-of-domain datasets, outperforming models trained solely on AudioMOS and a baseline SSL-only model.

## Method Summary
The method employs a dual-branch neural network. The SSL branch downsamples input audio to 16 kHz and extracts representations from layer 9 of a frozen Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-2B model, which are then processed by a three-layer 1D convolutional Feature Processing Module. The spectrogram branch upsamples the same input to 48 kHz, computes a log-magnitude spectrogram (STFT window 320, shift 160, FFT 320), and passes it through a Spectrogram Processing Module based on the DNSMOS Pro CNN encoder. The two embeddings are concatenated into a 640-dimensional vector and fed through three fully connected layers, which output the parameters (mean μ and variance σ²) of a Gaussian posterior for MOS prediction. Training uses a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss. The two-step procedure first trains all parameters on the large NISQA dataset (48 kHz, ~5k samples) for 30 epochs, then fine-tunes on the smaller AudioMOS training set (320 samples) for 3 epochs with a lower learning rate.

## Key Results
- **AudioMOS test set**: Two-step SA-SSL-MOS achieves the best utterance-level performance among compared models (MSE 0.377, LCC 0.848, SRCC 0.750).
- **Generalization**: The two-step training strategy yields large improvements on six out-of-domain test sets (e.g., TCD-VoIP LCC 0.860 vs. 0.391 for an AudioMOS-only baseline).
- **Consistency**: SA-SSL-MOS consistently outperforms the baseline SSL-only model on non-Chinese datasets, demonstrating the benefit of incorporating high-frequency information via the spectrogram branch.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The parallel-branch architecture yields better MOS predictions than SSL-only because it captures both robust long-range temporal patterns (from SSL) and fine high-frequency spectral details (from spectrogram) discarded by 16 kHz downsampling.
- **Mechanism**: The SSL branch operates on 16 kHz audio, leveraging Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-2B's representations. The spectrogram branch operates on 48 kHz audio, preserving frequencies up to 24 kHz via a high-resolution STFT and 2D CNNs. Concatenation allows the final layers to weight these complementary features.
- **Core assumption**: High-frequency components (above 8 kHz) contain perceptually relevant cues for assessing high-fidelity speech quality; discarding them harms performance.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] explicitly states the goal of incorporating high-frequency features via parallel branches; [section 2.1] details the two-branch design; comparative results in Tables 2 and 3 show SA-SSL-MOS outperforming SSL-only on most datasets.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Two-step training (pre-train on large single-rate NISQA, then fine-tune on small multi-rate AudioMOS) substantially improves generalization to out-of-domain test sets compared to training only on AudioMOS.
- **Mechanism**: Pre-training on NISQA teaches the spectrogram branch and fusion layers to map high-resolution spectra to MOS scores using abundant data. Limited fine-tuning adapts the model to the multi-rate distribution and rating scale without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Core assumption**: Out-of-domain test distributions are more similar to NISQA than to the small AudioMOS training set; thus pre-training provides a better prior.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] mentions the two-step scheme improves generalization with limited multi-rate data; Tables 2 and 3 show consistent, large gains for two-step over AudioMOS-only training across all six out-of-domain sets (e.g., TCD-VoIP LCC 0.860 vs. 0.391).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The spectrogram branch is data-hungry; it requires large-scale pre-training to learn meaningful high-frequency representations and otherwise degrades performance relative to SSL-only.
- **Mechanism**: The Spectrogram Processing Module (SPM) uses many CNN parameters and needs diverse examples to learn invariances to high-frequency distortions (e.g., bandwidth limitation, codec artifacts). With only the small AudioMOS dataset (~320 samples), the SPM cannot converge and introduces noise.
- **Core assumption**: Modeling complex high-frequency distortions reliably requires substantial training data.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 2.1] states pre-training enables the spectral branch to learn rich representations; Table 2 shows SA-SSL-MOS trained only on AudioMOS performs worse than SSL-only trained on AudioMOS (MSE 0.375 vs. 0.282), while the two-step variant surpasses both (MSE 0.377), demonstrating the SPM's data requirement.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Self-supervised learning (SSL) for speech
  - *Why needed*: The SSL branch relies on a pretrained Wav2Vec2 model; understanding its contrastive or masked prediction objective explains why it learns robust representations from raw audio without labels.
  - *Quick check*: What objective does Wav2Vec2 use during pre-training, and why does that lead to useful representations for downstream tasks like SQA?
- **Concept**: Spectrogram and time-frequency analysis
  - *Why needed*: The spectrogram branch converts audio to a 2D time-frequency representation; knowledge of STFT parameters (window length, hop size, FFT size) is essential to understand how high-frequency resolution is preserved.
  - *Quick check*: How does increasing the FFT size in STFT affect the spectrogram's frequency resolution, and what time-resolution trade-off does it introduce?
- **Concept**: Transfer learning and fine-tuning
  - *Why needed*: The two-step training is a transfer learning paradigm; understanding the rationale for a lower learning rate and few fine-tuning epochs explains how catastrophic forgetting is avoided.
  - *Quick check*: Why is a smaller learning rate used during fine-tuning than during pre-training, and what would happen if the fine-tuning rate were too high?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input audio → SSL Branch: downsample 16 kHz → Wav2Vec2-XLS-R-2B (layer 9) → Feature Processing Module (3× 1D conv) → flatten. Spectrogram Branch: upsample 48 kHz → STFT (window 320, shift 160, FFT 320) → log-mag spectrogram → Spectrogram Processing Module (CNN encoder) → global pool → flatten. Concatenate (640-D) → MOS Mapping Module (3 FC layers) → linear heads (μ, σ²).
- **Critical path**: Both branches process the same utterance in parallel; their embeddings are concatenated and passed through fully connected layers to predict MOS and uncertainty. The SSL branch's Wav2Vec2 model is frozen.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Parallel branches increase computational cost (especially spectrogram branch). Fixed upsampling to 48 kHz may interpolate unnecessarily for lower native rates. Simple concatenation fusion assumes equal informativeness without cross-branch attention. Two-step training requires a large, compatible single-rate dataset for pre-training.
- **Failure signatures**: Very high predicted variance (σ²) indicates out-of-distribution inputs. Performance drop on non-English/acoustically mismatched data suggests spectrogram branch overfitting to NISQA's domain. Overfitting if fine-tuning exceeds a few epochs. Dimension mismatch between branch outputs prevents concatenation.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablate the spectrogram branch: train SA-SSL-MOS with only the SSL branch under the two-step regime to isolate the spectrogram branch's contribution.
  2. Ablate pre-training data: replace NISQA with another large 48 kHz dataset in the two-step training to evaluate robustness of the pre-training effect.
  3. Fine-tuning epoch sweep: vary fine-tuning epochs (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10) on AudioMOS to identify overfitting; the paper reports optimal at 3 epochs.

## Limitations
- Key architectural details (exact layer sizes for processing modules, dropout, weight decay) are missing, hindering faithful reproduction.
- The spectrogram branch's effectiveness is inconsistent; it degrades on Chinese datasets, indicating domain bias from NISQA pre-training.
- Two-step training relies on availability of a large, representative single-rate dataset; alternative regimes for data-scarce scenarios are not explored.
- Upsampling lower-rate audio to 48 kHz may introduce interpolation artifacts; their impact is unanalyzed.
- The core hypothesis that high-frequency information is crucial lacks direct validation (e.g., controlled band-limiting experiments).
- Predicted uncertainty (variance) is not evaluated for reliability.

## Confidence
- **Parallel-branch architecture superiority**: Medium. Improvements are shown on many but not all datasets; no isolated ablation of the fusion itself.
- **Two-step training improves generalization**: High. Consistently large gains across six out-of-domain sets are clearly demonstrated.
- **Spectrogram branch is data-hungry**: Medium. The contrast between AudioMOS-only and two-step training supports this, but no systematic data scaling experiments.
- **Overall SOTA performance on AudioMOS**: High. Direct metric comparisons against baselines are provided.

## Next Checks
1. **Branch isolation ablation**: Train and evaluate models with only the SSL branch and only the spectrogram branch (both under two-step) to quantify each branch's independent contribution and verify synergy from fusion.
2. **Controlled bandwidth experiment**: Evaluate the trained model on artificially band-limited test sets (e.g., cutoffs at 4, 8, 12 kHz) to directly test its reliance on high-frequency cues and the spectrogram branch's role.
3. **Pre-training dataset diversity**: Replace NISQA with a different large 48 kHz corpus (e.g., different language, recording conditions) in the two-step pipeline to assess the robustness of the pre-training effect and potential for negative transfer.
