# Beyond Musical Descriptors: Extracting Preference-Bearing Intent in Music Queries

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12301
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12301
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: LLMs reliably extract explicit musical descriptors and predict preference intent with up to 89% accuracy, but struggle with context-dependent categories and referential roles.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces MusicRecoIntent, a manually annotated corpus of 2,291 Reddit music queries with labels for musical descriptors and their preference-bearing roles. It evaluates several LLMs using a two-stage prompting approach: first extract descriptors across seven categories, then classify intent as positive, negative, or referential. Results show high performance for well-defined descriptors (e.g., genre, mood) and strong intent accuracy (89%), but lower scores for ambiguous categories like Listening Context and Instrument. The study highlights challenges in segmentation granularity and referential intent confusion.

## Method Summary
The authors compiled a dataset of 2,291 music recommendation requests from Reddit, annotating each with descriptor spans and one of three intent labels (+ positive, - negative, ~ referential). They applied zero-shot prompting using Ollama with seven LLM variants (Gemma, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen). The pipeline consists of two sequential prompts: the first identifies descriptor spans across seven categories; the second assigns intent labels to each extracted descriptor. Evaluation used exact-match and partial-match F1 for extraction, and accuracy for intent. No model fine-tuning was performed; inference was done with default parameters (temperature=0, top_p=1).

## Key Results
- Gemma3:27B achieved exact-match F1 of 0.69 and partial-match F1 of 0.76 for descriptor extraction; intent prediction accuracy reached 89%.
- Highest extraction scores were for named entities (0.84), genre (0.82), and mood (0.82); lowest were for Listening Context (0.41) and Instrument (0.46).
- Referential intents were frequently misclassified as positive, especially for named entities (17% error).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Explicit Descriptor Extraction
- **Claim:** LLMs reliably extract explicit, well-defined musical descriptors (e.g., named entities, genre, mood) because these categories often have stable, surface-level lexical patterns.
- **Mechanism:** The models match token sequences to frequently co-occurring terms in their training data. This works best for categories with clear boundaries and less contextual dependency.
- **Core assumption:** Explicit descriptors have consistent linguistic realizations across queries, enabling detection via pattern matching rather than deep reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** High exact-match F1 for NE (0.84), Genre (0.82), Mood (0.82), Country (0.92), Decade (0.85); dataset includes many examples for these categories (e.g., 1,870 NEs).
- **Break condition:** Performance drops for descriptors requiring understanding of compound phrases or implicit references (e.g., “summer trip to Norway” for Listening Context).

### Mechanism 2: Two-Stage Prompting
- **Claim:** Decomposing the joint task into extraction followed by intent classification improves overall accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** First prompt isolates descriptor spans; second prompt classifies intent using local query context, reducing cognitive load per step.
- **Core assumption:** Descriptor intent can be determined reliably from immediate context once the span is identified.
- **Evidence anchors:** 89% overall intent accuracy with Gemma3:27B; high per-class accuracy for positive (94%) and negative (90%).
- **Break condition:** Fails when intent depends on relationships between multiple descriptors or pragmatic cues beyond the isolated phrase (e.g., negation scope over a compound descriptor).

### Mechanism 3: Annotation Ambiguity Drives Disagreements
- **Claim:** Disagreements between annotators and LLMs are primarily due to ambiguous guidelines on segmentation and interpretation, not solely model deficiency.
- **Mechanism:** Lack of rigid rules for span boundaries leads to inconsistent decisions for subjective, context-bound categories (e.g., should “indie pop” be one or two descriptors?).
- **Core assumption:** The task definition lacks precision, making consistent automation difficult without refined guidelines.
- **Evidence anchors:** Low inter-annotator agreement for Instrument (66.1%) and Listening Context (66.8%); segmentation disagreements are the most frequent error type in model outputs.
- **Break condition:** For highly idiomatic or complex syntactic structures, even human consensus is elusive, limiting achievable model performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Named Entity Recognition (NER) in Domain-Specific Text**  
  *Why needed:* The task involves identifying not only standard entities (artists, songs) but also domain-specific ones (genres, moods) that are often polysemous or expressed as common nouns.  
  *Quick check:* In “songs like Kendrick Lamar's Maad City”, how should spans be annotated for artist and track title? Consider variations like “Kendrick Lamar” vs. “Kendrick Lamar's”.

- **Preference-Bearing Intent Classification**  
  *Why needed:* Extracting a descriptor is insufficient; the system must determine if the user wants that attribute (+), its opposite (-), or something similar (~).  
  *Quick check:* For “rock music without guitar”, what is the intent label for “guitar” and why? How would you handle “I hate rap but want to explore the genre”?

- **Contextual Descriptor Granularity and Scope**  
  *Why needed:* Categories like Listening Context and Instrument involve multi-word expressions with fuzzy boundaries. Models must decide appropriate span size.  
  *Quick check:* Annotate “wedding first dance song” as a Listening Context descriptor. Should it be one span or split? What principle decides?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Parser → Descriptor Extraction Module → Intent Prediction Module → Post-processor

**Critical path:** Extraction → Intent → Output. Intent prediction depends entirely on the quality of extraction; errors in spans propagate.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Two-stage vs. end-to-end: Two-stage allows independent optimization and easier error analysis but risks error propagation. End-to-end might capture cross-dependencies but requires more complex training.  
- Prompt engineering vs. fine-tuning: The paper uses zero-shot prompts; fine-tuning could improve consistency but reduces flexibility.  
- Granularity control: Without strict rules, segmentation varies. A post-processing heuristic (e.g., treat Listening Context as maximal noun phrases) could help.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low partial-match F1 on Instrument/Listening Context indicates segmentation issues.  
- High confusion between referential (~) and positive (+) intent for named entities.  
- Over-detection of generic terms like “music”, “song” shows lack of domain filtering.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline extraction sanity check:** Run extraction on 50 queries with high-agreement categories (NE, Genre). Compute exact/partial-match F1; expect >0.80 partial match.  
2. **Granularity stress test:** Evaluate extraction on 30 queries with compound Listening Context descriptors. Manually review span boundaries; identify systematic splitting/merging failures.  
3. **Intent confusion analysis:** For 100 correctly extracted named entities, compare predicted vs. gold intent in queries containing “like” or “similar to”. Quantify ~ vs. + error rate; if >20%, refine intent prompt examples.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Referential vs. Positive Intent Confusion** – The paper shows 17% of referential cases are misclassified as positive, especially for named entities, but does not investigate whether prompt design, model biases, or linguistic ambiguity cause this. Evidence: controlled error analysis varying surface forms and prompts.
2. **Segmentation Ambiguity** – Low inter-annotator agreement for Instrument (66.1%) and Listening Context (66.8%) indicates guideline ambiguities; the paper does not propose refined rules. Evidence: new annotation guidelines (e.g., maximal noun phrases) tested for improved agreement and model consistency.
3. **Exact vs. Partial Match Gap** – Larger gaps for context-dependent categories may stem from annotation noise or model limitations, but the paper does not disentangle them. Evidence: evaluation against a consensus gold subset or boundary-tolerant metrics.
4. **Domain Generalization** – The dataset is Reddit-specific; no tests on other platforms or languages. Evidence: evaluation on diverse music query sources (e.g., voice assistants, non-English).
5. **Mitigation for Rare Intents** – Negative intents are extremely rare (49) and referential confusion persist; the paper does not explore fine-tuning or data augmentation. Evidence: supervised fine-tuning experiments, class balancing, prompt engineering.

## Limitations
- Dataset restricted to Reddit music requests; unknown transfer to other genres or platforms.
- Low inter-annotator agreement for Instrument (66.1%) and Listening Context (66.8%) indicates guideline ambiguities, limiting both human and model performance.
- Prompt implementation details (system roles, tokenization) not fully specified; results may vary across LLM interfaces.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Explicit descriptor extraction):** High for NE/Genre/Mood; Low for Listening Context/Instrument.
- **Mechanism 2 (Two-stage prompting benefit):** Medium – high intent accuracy shown but no ablation against alternatives.
- **Mechanism 3 (Annotation ambiguity impact):** Medium – IAA and error analysis support, but model errors also contribute; causes not fully separated.

## Next Checks
1. Clarify annotation guidelines: Redefine segmentation rules (e.g., require maximal noun phrases for Listening Context) on a subset; measure impact on IAA and model extraction F1.
2. Prompt sensitivity analysis: Systematically paraphrase extraction and intent prompts (vary example ordering, wording); assess output stability across random seeds; report metric variance.
3. Pipeline vs. joint modeling: Fine-tune a sequence-to-sequence model (e.g., T5) to perform both extraction and intent end-to-end; compare performance, especially on low-agreement categories.
