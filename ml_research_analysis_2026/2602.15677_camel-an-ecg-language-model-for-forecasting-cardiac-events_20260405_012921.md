# CAMEL: An ECG Language Model for Forecasting Cardiac Events
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.15677
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15677
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A specialized ECG tokenization and 5-stage curriculum training pipeline enables a generalist LLM (MedGemma-4B) to achieve state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on both cardiac event forecasting and classification benchmarks, significantly outperforming prior ECG language models and supervised models.

## Executive Summary
CAMEL is a framework that adapts a generalist large language model (MedGemma-4B) for electrocardiogram (ECG) analysis, specifically targeting the novel task of forecasting future cardiac events from raw ECG signals. It introduces a per-second-per-lead tokenization strategy to represent long, multi-lead ECG recordings as a sequence of discrete tokens compatible with an LLM's context window. The model is trained through a curated 5-stage curriculum, progressing from basic ECG comprehension to complex multi-turn reasoning and finally forecasting. Evaluated on the new ECGForecastBench and existing ECGBench, CAMEL demonstrates superior zero-shot performance, suggesting that LLM-based approaches can effectively learn temporal and clinical patterns in ECGs for predictive tasks.

## Method Summary
CAMEL's core innovation is a two-part methodological pipeline tailored for ECG data within an LLM paradigm. First, it employs a specialized ECG encoder that tokenizes each 1-second segment of each ECG lead into separate tokens, creating a "language" of ECG that can be processed sequentially by an LLM. This addresses the challenge of representing long-duration, multi-lead signals. Second, it implements a 5-stage curriculum training process: (1) ECG comprehension (learning basic signal patterns), (2) statistics calculation, (3) single-turn question answering, (4) multi-turn reasoning, and (5) the target forecasting task. The LLM backbone (MedGemma-4B) is adapted using LoRA, allowing efficient fine-tuning on this structured curriculum without modifying the entire model.

## Key Results
*   On the ECGForecastBench forecasting benchmark, CAMEL achieves a +12.4% absolute F1 score gain over fully supervised models and a +21.1% gain over prior zero-shot ECG language models.
*   On the ECGBench classification benchmark, it achieves a +7.0% absolute average gain, matching or surpassing both prior ELMs and supervised baselines in in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings.
*   The approach demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization across six tasks and nine diverse datasets, indicating robustness to varying ECG sources and conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's claims of effectiveness rest on three interconnected design choices. First, the **per-second-per-lead tokenization** creates a structured, sequential representation that preserves temporal granularity and multi-lead relationships, which is theoretically well-suited for an autoregressive LLM's processing. Second, the **5-stage curriculum** scaffolds learning, ensuring the model first acquires foundational ECG knowledge before tackling the complex sequence-to-sequence forecasting task, which may prevent poor local minima. Third, **starting from a powerful generalist LLM (MedGemma-4B)** provides a strong prior for language-like pattern matching and reasoning, which is then efficiently specialized via LoRA to the ECG domain. The combination appears to allow the model to learn both short-term morphology and long-term temporal dynamics crucial for prediction.

## Foundational Learning
*   **ECG Tokenization:** Converting continuous ECG voltage signals into discrete, sequential tokens per second per lead. *Why needed:* To make raw, long-duration, multi-lead signals consumable by an LLM's fixed context window and token-based architecture. *Quick check:* Measure information retention by reconstructing tokenized signals back to analog and computing signal-to-noise ratio.
*   **Curriculum Learning for Medical Signals:** Progressively training on tasks of increasing complexity (comprehension -> statistics -> QA -> reasoning -> forecasting). *Why needed:* To build robust, hierarchical representations and avoid catastrophic forgetting when learning the final, difficult forecasting objective. *Quick check:* Ablate the curriculum order or remove stages and measure degradation on the final forecasting task.
*   **LLM Adaptation for Sequential Forecasting:** Using an autoregressive decoder-only LLM (via LoRA) to predict future ECG segments or derived events. *Why needed:* Leverages the LLM's inherent strength in sequence modeling and in-context learning for a novel clinical prediction task. *Quick check:* Compare against a non-autoregressive model (e.g., CNN) with similar parameters trained end-to-end on the same forecasting objective.
*   **Zero-Shot Clinical Benchmarking:** Evaluating on pre-defined, held-out datasets without task-specific fine-tuning. *Why needed:* To assess true generalization and domain adaptation capability of the pretrained model, simulating real-world deployment where labeled data for a new hospital or device is scarce. *Quick check:* Perform few-shot fine-tuning (e.g., 1-10 examples per class) on the benchmark datasets and measure the performance delta from zero-shot.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Raw Multi-Lead ECG Input -> ECG Encoder (Tokenizer per sec/lead) -> Token Sequence -> MedGemma-4B (LLM Backbone) + LoRA Adapters -> Text/Token Output (Forecast/Answer)

**Critical Path:** Raw signal is transformed into a long token sequence by the specialized encoder. This sequence is fed into the frozen, powerful MedGemma-4B model, with only the LoRA modules being trained through the curriculum stages. The final stage's training objective aligns the LLM's output generation with the forecasting task.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Using a generalist LLM offers strong inductive biases for sequence reasoning but may be over-parameterized and inefficient for pure signal processing. The tokenization scheme is simple and interpretable but may lose fine-grained waveform morphology within each second compared to higher-frequency tokenization. The curriculum requires significant, carefully curated training data but avoids the need for a single massive, noisy, multi-task dataset.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance on very long-term forecasts (>30 seconds) indicating context window limitations. Degradation on ECG data from novel acquisition devices or filtering methods not seen in training, suggesting the tokenizer's feature representation is brittle. Inability to follow complex multi-turn reasoning chains in clinical dialogues, pointing to gaps in the curriculum's "multi-turn reasoning" stage.

**Exactly 3 First Experiments:**
1.  **Tokenization Ablation:** Replace the per-second-per-lead tokenization with a standard ECG representation (e.g., 1D CNN feature tokens) and retrain from stage 3 onward, comparing forecasting performance on ECGForecastBench.
2.  **Backbone Swap:** Replace MedGemma-4B with a smaller, encoder-only model (e.g., a BioBERT variant) and apply the same curriculum, measuring the performance drop to quantify the contribution of the decoder-only LLM architecture.
3.  **Stage-Skip Ablation:** Train models that skip stage 2 (statistics) and stage 4 (multi-turn reasoning) individually, then evaluate on tasks requiring statistical knowledge (e.g., "What is the heart rate?") and conversational reasoning to isolate each stage's contribution.

## Limitations
*   **Method Necessity:** The claimed advantage of the specific tokenization and 5-stage curriculum over more recent "encoder-free" or supervised pre-training approaches is not conclusively proven, as comparisons may not cover the full spectrum of contemporary alternatives.
*   **Benchmark Validation:** The large reported gains rely on the new ECGForecastBench and splits of ECGBench; without public, detailed dataset splits and protocols, concerns about potential benchmark-specific overfitting or data leakage remain.
*   **Clinical Utility Gap:** The zero-shot evaluation paradigm, while a strength for research, does not directly address real-world clinical deployment scenarios where a small amount of high-quality, local fine-tuning data is typically available and expected.

## Confidence
*   **Method Novelty (specialized tokenization + curriculum):** Medium. The approach is clearly described but conceptually overlaps with existing ideas in input representation and staged training; independent replication is needed to confirm its unique added value.
*   **Zero-Shot SOTA Performance:** Low to Medium. The absolute gains are substantial, but confidence is lowered by the recency of the work (no citations yet), the novelty of the forecasting benchmark, and the lack of external validation on completely unseen real-world datasets.
*   **Generalization Across Datasets/Tasks:** Medium. Claims are based on nine datasets, but the summary does not allow assessment of demographic diversity, device heterogeneity, or potential hidden correlations that could inflate results.

## Next Checks
