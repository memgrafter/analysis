# Generative Agents Navigating Digital Libraries
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22529
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22529
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Agent4DL generates realistic search sessions with query term overlap τ=0.87 and improves IR models (MRR 41.32, nDCG@3 35.19), demonstrating utility for digital library research.

## Executive Summary
Digital libraries suffer from a scarcity of real user search behavior data due to privacy constraints, hindering research and system design. This paper introduces Agent4DL, an LLM-based user simulator that leverages the ReAct (Reasoning + Acting) framework to generate diverse, realistic search sessions—including queries, clicks, and stopping decisions—tailored to specific user profiles. The simulator incorporates three specialized modules: Profile (capturing academic traits like depth, breadth, recency bias, and interdisciplinarity, plus distilled research interests), Memory (storing factual and emotional experiences to inform stopping behavior), and Interaction (managing the ReAct loop).

Agent4DL’s simulated behavior closely mirrors real users, achieving a query term overlap of τ=0.87. When the generated sessions are used to train retrieval models (e.g., RoBERTa), they significantly outperform baselines such as BM25 and models trained on existing small datasets (SUSS/EconBiz), with MRR 41.32 and nDCG@3 35.19 on a 3000-session dataset. Moreover, the simulator replicates known phenomena like information overload and the open access effect, underscoring its value for IR research and library system evaluation.

## Method Summary
Agent4DL is built upon the ReAct paradigm, orchestrating a cycle of reasoning and action that iteratively refines a search session. At initialization, each agent receives a Profile derived from real user interaction logs: four academic traits are quantified via formulas based on time spent, topic diversity, publication year, and field diversity, while research interests are summarized from a sample of interacted documents using ChatGPT. During a session, the Interaction module prompts an LLM with the current context (history), the agent’s Profile, and relevant memories (retrieved from the Memory module) to produce a reasoning step that determines the next action—issue a query, click a result, or stop. The Digital Library Environment responds to queries with paginated result snippets and to clicks with full document content (or abstracts), enabling the agent to observe outcomes and write both factual and emotional memories. The loop continues until a stop decision is made, producing a complete session log. Finally, the Data Pipeline extracts (query, clicked document, stopping point) tuples suitable for training or evaluating IR models.

## Key Results
- Query term overlap with real users: τ=0.87, indicating high lexical and topical alignment.
- Training RoBERTa on 3000 simulated sessions yields MRR 41.32 and nDCG@3 35.19, surpassing BM25 and models trained on sparse real data (SUSS/EconBiz).
- The simulator reproduces information overload (decreasing per-resource time as resource volume grows) and the open access effect, validating its ability to model known user behaviors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Personalized user profiles grounded in academic traits (depth, breadth, recency bias, interdisciplinarity) and distilled research interests enable the generation of consistent, human-like querying and interaction behavior.
- **Mechanism:** The profile module initializes agents with static behavioral tendencies (quantified traits) and dynamic interest summaries. These guide the ReAct reasoning process, biasing query formulation and document selection toward a coherent personal research narrative rather than generic or random actions.
- **Core assumption:** Human search behavior in academic digital libraries exhibits stable, measurable trait-based patterns that can be abstracted from historical interaction data and used to condition an LLM's output.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1.1] Explicitly defines the four academic traits using formulas based on historical user-document interactions (time spent, topic diversity, publication year, field diversity) and describes segmenting users into tiers for each. It also details using ChatGPT to summarize research interests from 10 interaction history documents.
  - [abstract] States the simulator generates "diverse, realistic search sessions including queries, clicks, and stopping decisions tailored to specific user profiles."
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper *USimAgent: Large Language Models for Simulating Search Users* (FM: 0.57) also focuses on LLM-based user simulation, indicating the broader research context, but does not provide direct evidence for *this specific trait-based mechanism*. Assessment: Weak/moderate corpus support for the general approach, but not the specific trait definitions.
- **Break condition:** If the trait quantification formulas from real interaction data fail to produce statistically distinguishable agent behavior cohorts in simulation (e.g., "deep divers" do not spend more simulated time per document than "quick scanners").

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A dual memory system storing factual (queries, clicks) and emotional (satisfaction, frustration) experiences allows agents to model dynamic stopping behavior that reflects information overload and evolving search satisfaction.
- **Mechanism:** The memory module writes interaction outcomes and associated emotional states. During the ReAct reasoning step, the agent retrieves relevant past experiences and performs emotion-driven reflection. This internal state assessment, combined with the "depth" trait, directly informs the decision to continue or stop the search session.
- **Core assumption:** Human searchers' decisions to terminate a session are significantly influenced by a real-time cognitive-affective assessment of progress and overload, which can be approximated by tracking and reflecting on emotional valence linked to past outcomes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1.2] Describes the memory module's three operations (retrieval, writing, reflection) and explicitly states it records "concrete search behaviors" (factual) and "psychological responses... including satisfaction with results or frustration with complex queries." It links this to "evaluating search experience" and "abandon search sessions."
  - [section 5.1] Provides empirical support by replicating the information overload phenomenon: as resource volume increased, simulated per-resource time decreased and accessed resources initially rose then plateaued, matching expected behavior.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is indirect. The concept of emotional influence on search is referenced ([12] on cognitive styles) but not the specific memory-reflection mechanism. Assessment: Weak corpus support; the mechanism is primarily paper-specific.
- **Break condition:** If the emotion annotation or reflection prompt consistently fails to produce stopping decisions that correlate with simulated overload metrics (e.g., no negative correlation between number of results viewed and subsequent satisfaction scores).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ReAct (Reasoning + Acting) framework synergizes deliberative planning with environment interaction, enabling agents to generate context-aware queries and clicks that evolve based on observed search results and internal state.
- **Mechanism:** In each timestep *t*, the agent uses the full context history *C_t* (previous reasoning, queries, observations) to generate new reasoning *r_t*. This reasoning determines the action (stop, query, click). The action changes the environment (new results or clicked document content), generating a new observation *o_t*, which updates the context for the next cycle. This loop allows for query refinement, strategic exploration, and adaptation.
- **Core assumption:** The stepwise, observable reasoning process of ReAct, when prompted with domain-specific instructions and agent state (profile + memory), can approximate the iterative, goal-directed nature of human exploratory search.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] "We use the ReAct method [34] to include reasoning and action steps." It defines the context update cycle *C_t -> r_t -> action -> o_t -> C_{t+1}* and states prompts "guide the LLM to perform reasoning tasks, generating stops, queries, clicks, and observations."
  - [table 2] Shows high Term Overlap (τ=0.87) and BERTScore between agent-generated and real user queries, indicating the reasoning loop produces contextually relevant and thematically consistent query refinements.
  - [corpus] The core ReAct method [34] is a well-cited, general LLM agent framework. The neighbor paper *USimAgent* also uses LLM agents for simulation, suggesting the architectural paradigm has traction. Assessment: Strong anchor from the paper's own description and results; corpus supports the general method's relevance.
- **Break condition:** If ablating the ReAct loop (e.g., using a single-turn prompt without history) causes a significant drop in query diversity, session length, or alignment metrics (τ, nDCG) compared to the full model.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: ReAct Paradigm for Agentic Workflows**
- **Why needed here:** Agent4DL's core engine is the ReAct loop. Understanding the separation and chaining of "Reasoning" (thoughts) and "Acting" (tool use/environment change) is critical to grasp how the simulator produces sequential, dependent behavior rather than independent actions.
- **Quick check question:** How does the context history *C_t* in the ReAct loop prevent an agent from repeating the same query or clicking on irrelevant documents after already viewing an abstract?

**Concept: User Profiling in Information Retrieval (Traits & Interests)**
- **Why needed here:** The simulator's personalization hinges on translating static historical data into a probabilistic profile. Understanding how academic traits (depth, breadth, etc.) are operationally defined and how interests are distilled is key to predicting agent behavior distribution.
- **Quick check question:** What is the difference between a user's "breadth" trait (set of topics) and their "research interests" (natural language summary), and how might each differently influence the first vs. tenth query in a session?

**Concept: Simulation-Based Evaluation & Data Augmentation**
- **Why needed here:** The paper's value proposition is using simulation to overcome real data scarcity. This requires understanding how simulated sessions can be treated as training data (e.g., for a RoBERTa ranker) and what validity threats exist (e.g., simulator bias, hallucination).
- **Quick check question:** If a RoBERTa model trained on 3000 Agent4DL sessions outperforms one trained on real SUSS data (Table 1), what are two possible interpretations of this result regarding the quality of Agent4DL's data versus a potential flaw in the real SUSS dataset?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
The system comprises five components:  
- **Profile Module**: Inputs (real user interaction logs). Output (JSON profile: trait tiers + research interest summary). Static at session start.  
- **Memory Module**: Interface (write: store action, observation, emotion; retrieve: get relevant memories based on current query/reasoning). Stores factual and emotional vectors.  
- **Interaction Module**: Contains the policy. Uses **ReAct Engine** (LLM + prompt templates) that consumes *C_t* (history), Profile, Retrieved Memories. Produces *r_t* (reasoning), action (STOP, QUERY, CLICK).  
- **Digital Library Environment**: Simulates the search system. For QUERY: returns paginated results (title, snippet, metadata). For CLICK: returns full document abstract/text. Prunes documents if LLM hallucinates discipline.  
- **Data Pipeline**: Post-processes sessions into (query, clicked doc, stopping point) tuples for IR model training/evaluation.

**Critical Path**  
Initialize Agent(Profile) → [ReAct Loop: Reason(Profile, Memory, History) → Decide Action → Execute(Env) → Observe → Write(Memory) → Update History] until STOP → Output Session Log.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- LLM Cost/Realism vs. Metadata Dependency: High-fidelity behavior requires rich document metadata for the environment to return meaningful snippets. Poor metadata leads to agent "hallucination" or irrelevant actions. Tradeoff: Use real APIs (EconBiz) vs. synthetic generation.  
- Zero-Shot Generalization vs. Dataset-Specific Tuning: Agent4DL is designed zero-shot (no training on target library's logs). This increases generalizability but may underfit specific interface quirks (e.g., position bias) compared to a SimIIR 2.0 HMM trained on that dataset (Table 3).  
- Emotion as Proxy vs. Ground Truth: Emotional states are LLM-generated, not measured. This enables modeling but is a black box. Break condition: if emotion scores show no variance or don't correlate with session length.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Anachronistic/Superficial Queries: Agent generates queries using terms from very recent papers when its profile is initialized with a "historical researcher" (high recency bias = negative). Indicates trait-interest misalignment.  
- Stopping Prematurely on Overload: In the information overload experiment (Fig 1), if *N_accessed* keeps increasing linearly without plateau, the overload reflection mechanism is failing.  
- Position Bias Blindness: Agent clicks result #1 on every page regardless of snippet relevance, suggesting it's not using the retrieved memory or reasoning to evaluate snippets, just defaulting to top position. (Paper notes this as a limitation).

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. Profile Validation: For 100 agents initialized from real Sowiport users, compare the distribution of their simulated trait metrics (e.g., avg. time/doc for "deep divers") against the *ground-truth* metrics calculated from the users' real historical logs. Use KS-test; expect non-significant difference for well-defined traits.  
2. Memory-Ablation Stopping Test: Run identical search tasks for agents with (a) full memory, (b) memory write disabled (no emotion/facts recorded), (c) memory retrieval disabled. Measure average session length and stopping accuracy against real user session endpoints. Expect (a) > (b) and (c) in alignment, especially for overload scenarios.  
3. Query Divergence Under Perturbation: For a fixed agent profile and search topic, run 10 sessions. Compute pairwise τ/BLEU between all generated queries. Then, slightly perturb the initial query (add one synonym) and re-run 10 sessions. Measure if intra-session similarity remains high (>τ=0.7) while inter-session similarity between perturbed and unperturbed groups drops, testing consistency vs. adaptability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** To what extent do Agent4DL's simulated search sessions capture the full complexity of real user cognitive processes, such as evolving information needs, learning during search, and nuanced emotional dynamics beyond self-reported satisfaction?

*Basis in paper:* RQ1 asks about accuracy in simulating real user behaviors; the paper validates alignment via behavioral metrics (query term overlap τ=0.87, click/stopping accuracy) but does not deeply probe cognitive or emotional processes beyond basic satisfaction reporting in the memory module.

*Why unresolved:* Behavioral alignment (queries, clicks) does not guarantee cognitive fidelity; real users undergo mental model shifts and coping strategies that are not measured or compared in the evaluation, limiting understanding of deeper simulation validity.

*What evidence would resolve:* Comparative studies using think-aloud protocols or physiological measures (e.g., eye-tracking) during real and simulated sessions, analyzing alignment in cognitive states (e.g., confusion, insight) and emotional trajectories over time.

**Open Question 2:** Can Agent4DL be systematically extended to simulate and provide insights for other unresolved phenomena in digital libraries, such as serendipitous discovery, collaborative search, or cross-lingual information needs?

*Basis in paper:* RQ2 asks if Agent4DL can offer insights on unresolved problems; the paper presents two initial case studies (information overload, open access effect) but explicitly confines discussion to potential paths, implying many other phenomena remain unexplored.

*Why unresolved:* The current framework is designed for individual, query-driven sessions; phenomena like serendipity or collaboration require different interaction models, user types, and evaluation metrics that are not implemented or tested.

*What evidence would resolve:* Developing new modules for multi-agent collaboration or browsing behaviors, then validating against empirical studies or observational data on these phenomena, measuring predictive accuracy for outcomes like novel discovery or group search efficiency.

**Open Question 3:** How does the omission of scholarly information-seeking behaviors like citation chaining, colleague consultation, or serendipitous browsing—acknowledged as limitations—affect the realism and research utility of Agent4DL's simulated data?

*Basis in paper:* Section 7 lists these omissions as key limitations; the action space currently covers queries, clicks, stopping, but excludes these common academic practices.

*Why unresolved:* Simulated sessions may systematically diverge from real scholarly workflows, potentially biasing any research that uses Agent4DL data to train or evaluate systems for academic search, especially for studies on exploration or knowledge synthesis.

*What evidence would resolve:* Extending Agent4DL to incorporate these behaviors, then comparing simulated session characteristics (e.g., document diversity, session length, citation networks) against real search logs that include such actions, quantifying differences in distribution and downstream task performance.

**Open Question 4:** What techniques can enable Agent4DL to operate effectively in digital libraries with sparse or inconsistent metadata, which is common in many collections, without sacrificing simulation fidelity?

*Basis in paper:* Section 7 notes reliance on rich metadata as a limitation; the document relevance generation and topic assignment depend on complete metadata, which is not uniformly available.

*Why unresolved:* Metadata sparsity could lead to poor relevance judgments, unrealistic queries, and biased user profiles, limiting the simulator's applicability to less-curated libraries and potentially exacerbating biases in generated data.

*What evidence would resolve:* Evaluating Agent4DL on datasets with artificially reduced metadata or on real sparse-metadata libraries, measuring fidelity drops (e.g., query relevance, click alignment), and testing augmentation methods (e.g., metadata inference from full text, weak supervision) to recover performance.

**Open Question 5:** How can Agent4DL mitigate LLM-related issues like hallucinations and outdated domain knowledge, which affect query generation and relevance assessment in fast-evolving academic fields?

*Basis in paper:* Section 7 reports struggles with domain-specific knowledge and recent developments, leading to anachronistic queries; the current pruning approach may reduce coverage without solving currency.

*Why unresolved:* Hallucinations and outdated knowledge undermine trust in simulated behaviors for studying contemporary research trends, and may produce systematically biased sessions that misrepresent user strategies in cutting-edge domains.

*What evidence would resolve:* Fine-tuning the underlying LLM on up-to-date, field-specific corpora or integrating retrieval-augmented generation with current databases, then evaluating on recent user logs for accuracy in query topicality, document relevance, and temporal alignment.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt templates and document relevance generation details omitted, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Emotional states are LLM-generated without ground truth; may introduce systematic bias.
- Agents show position bias (defaulting to top results), reducing realism in click behavior.
- Trait definitions derived from specific academic libraries may not generalize to other domains.

## Confidence
- Trait-based personalization (Mechanism 1): Medium
- Memory/Emotion for stopping (Mechanism 2): Medium
- ReAct loop (Mechanism 3): High
- Simulated data utility (overall): High
