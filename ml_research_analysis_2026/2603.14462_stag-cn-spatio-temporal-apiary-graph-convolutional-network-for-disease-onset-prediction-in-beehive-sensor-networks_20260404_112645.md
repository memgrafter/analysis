# STAG-CN: Spatio-Temporal Apiary Graph Convolutional Network for Disease Onset Prediction in Beehive Sensor Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14462  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14462  
- Reference count: 27  
- Primary result: STAG-CN achieves an F1 score of 0.607 at a three‑day forecast horizon and, after threshold calibration, reaches F1 0.946; LOGO AUROC is 0.846.

## Executive Summary
Honey bee colony losses threaten pollination services, and existing monitoring systems treat hives as isolated, ignoring disease spread pathways. This paper introduces STAG-CN, a graph neural network that models inter‑hive relationships via a dual adjacency matrix combining physical co‑location and climatic sensor correlation. The model processes temporal sensor data through a spatial–temporal–spatial architecture, achieving an F1 score of 0.607 at a three‑day forecast horizon. Ablation studies show that the climatic adjacency alone matches the full model’s performance, indicating that shared environmental response patterns are more predictive than spatial proximity. Cross‑apiary validation reveals strong ranking ability (AUROC 0.846), though classification thresholds require site‑specific calibration.

## Method Summary
STAG-CN operates on a spatio‑temporal graph where nodes represent hive‑session units and edges encode relationships derived from physical co‑location (same apiary group) and climatic similarity (Pearson correlation of group‑level sensor means). The architecture consists of two stacked ST‑Blocks; each block contains a temporal convolutional network (TCN) with causal dilated convolutions, followed by a Chebyshev graph convolution (order K=3), and another TCN, with residual connections and layer normalization. Input features are daily aggregated sensor statistics (8 channels × 4 stats = 32 features) over a 7‑day lookback window. After the final ST‑Block, temporal pooling extracts the last time step, and a two‑layer MLP produces disease onset probabilities. Training uses focal loss to handle class imbalance (5.71% positives) and Adam optimization with early stopping. The dual adjacency matrix is fixed (λ=0.5 blend) and normalized, avoiding overfitting on the small dataset (44 samples). Evaluation employs expanding‑window temporal cross‑validation and leave‑one‑group‑out (LOGO) to assess generalization across apiaries.

## Key Results
- STAG‑CN achieves F1 = 0.607 at a 3‑day forecast horizon with 7‑day lookback.
- Climatic‑only adjacency (λ=0) yields identical F1 (0.607), while physical‑only (λ=1) drops to 0.274, underscoring the dominance of environmental correlation.
- LOGO cross‑validation yields AUROC = 0.846; after threshold calibration (τ=0.05), F1 recovers to 0.946, demonstrating strong discriminative ability that generalizes across apiaries but requires site‑specific threshold tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Climatic adjacency captures predictive environmental patterns
**Claim:** Shared environmental response patterns (climatic adjacency) are more predictive of disease onset than physical co‑location (physical adjacency).  
**Mechanism:** The climatic adjacency matrix encodes pairwise Pearson correlations of mean sensor time series across apiary groups. Hives with similar environmental responses (e.g., temperature/humidity trends) likely experience conditions that favor pathogen proliferation. Graph convolutions using this adjacency propagate correlated signals, amplifying disease‑predictive patterns that are invisible when hives are modeled in isolation.  
**Core assumption:** Disease emergence is more strongly associated with correlated environmental stressors (affecting multiple hives similarly) than with mere physical proximity between hives.  
**Evidence:**  
- Ablation study: climatic‑only (λ=0) achieves F1=0.607, equal to the combined model, while physical‑only (λ=1) yields F1=0.274 (Table V).  
- The dataset’s disease concentration in one group may contribute to this pattern.  
**Break condition:** If disease transmission is dominated by direct contact (e.g., Varroa mites via drifting bees), physical adjacency might become more predictive. Current dataset bias limits assessment.

### Mechanism 2: Spatial graph convolutions dominate; temporal convolutions add minimal value
**Claim:** The spatial GCN component alone matches the full spatio‑temporal model’s performance, suggesting that temporal dynamics are less informative given data constraints.  
**Mechanism:** The GCN‑only baseline (spatial only) achieves identical F1 (0.607) to STAG‑CN. The temporal convolutional networks may lack sufficient temporal variation (only 44 samples, 7‑day window) to learn complementary temporal patterns.  
**Core assumption:** In sparse‑label, small‑sample regimes, spatial network structure provides a stronger signal than temporal sequences.  
**Evidence:**  
- Section VI‑A reports that STAG‑CN and GCN‑only both achieve F1 = 0.607; the paper attributes this to the small temporal window and limited sample size.  
- Complex baselines like Graph WaveNet (F1=0.274) underperform, indicating overfitting on small data.  
**Break condition:** With more samples or longer sequences, temporal patterns (e.g., slow temperature shifts) could provide incremental gains over spatial‑only models.

### Mechanism 3: Discriminative ability generalizes across apiaries but requires calibration
**Claim:** STAG‑CN’s risk scoring generalizes to unseen apiaries (high AUROC), but the default classification threshold (0.5) yields poor F1; per‑apiary threshold calibration recovers high F1.  
**Mechanism:** Leave‑one‑group‑out (LOGO) cross‑validation yields AUROC = 0.846, showing the model ranks diseased hives higher than healthy ones in held‑out groups. The low F1 at τ=0.5 reflects poor probability calibration, not a failure of the learned representations. Calibrating on a small held‑out slice of the target apiary (τ=0.05) raises F1 to 0.946.  
**Core assumption:** The disease risk function generalizes in ranking, but the optimal decision boundary varies across apiaries due to differences in baseline risk or sensor noise.  
**Evidence:**  
- LOGO result: AUROC = 0.846, F1 = 0.180 at τ=0.5 (Table X).  
- After calibration (τ=0.05), F1 = 0.946.  
**Break condition:** If disease manifestations differ fundamentally across regions (e.g., pathogen strains), the representations may not generalize even in ranking (AUROC would drop). The current single‑season, single‑country dataset limits this test.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for Spatial Dependencies**  
  *Why needed:* STAG‑CN uses Chebyshev spectral graph convolutions to aggregate features from neighboring hives, capturing inter‑hive relationships ignored by isolated models.  
  *Quick check:* How does a Chebyshev convolution of order K=3 approximate spectral filtering on the graph Laplacian, and what does “three hops” mean in this apiary context?

- **Causal Dilated Convolutions for Temporal Sequences**  
  *Why needed:* The temporal convolutional network (TCN) blocks process sensor time series with causal, dilated kernels to expand the receptive field without mixing future into past, enabling forecasting.  
  *Quick check:* Why is causality enforced in the TCN kernels, and how does dilation help with long‑range dependencies in a 7‑day window?

- **Focal Loss for Imbalanced Binary Classification**  
  *Why needed:* With only 5.71% positive labels, focal loss down‑weights easy negatives and focuses training on hard/positive examples, improving class imbalance handling.  
  *Quick check:* What is the effect of the focusing parameter γ=2.0 on the loss landscape, and why might this be preferable to simple class weighting?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (N hives, W days, F features) → Linear projection (F → C_h=64) → [ST‑Block (TCN1 → ChebConv(K=3) → TCN2) × 2] → Temporal pooling (last step) → Classifier (2‑layer MLP) → Sigmoid output. Dual adjacency matrix (λ=0.5 blend) is shared across all GCN layers.

**Critical path:** Sensor time series → linear projection → ST‑Block 1 → ST‑Block 2 → temporal pooling → classifier. Spatial operations occur via ChebConv inside each ST‑Block; TCNs handle temporal dynamics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Fixed vs. adaptive adjacency: Fixed predefined dual matrix outperforms adaptive (embedding/attention) on small data, avoiding overfitting.  
- Chebyshev vs. diffusion: Chebyshev convolutions are stable with few nodes (N=49); diffusion methods (e.g., Graph WaveNet) overfit (F1=0.274).  
- Early vs. late fusion: Adding image‑derived features early reduced performance (F1 0.607 → 0.454), likely due to noise on 44 samples.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High variance across temporal CV folds (±0.435 F1) indicates instability from small sample size.  
- LSTM baseline fails completely (AUROC=0.042), confirming the necessity of graph structure.  
- Inclusion of image features hurts performance, signaling that annotation metadata is not complementary at this scale.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Adjacency ablation (λ sweep):** Replace dual adjacency with climatic‑only (λ=0) and physical‑only (λ=1). Expect climatic‑only to match full model (F1 ≈ 0.607) and physical‑only to drop significantly (F1 ≈ 0.274).  
2. **Baseline comparison:** Implement Graph WaveNet and DCRNN baselines; expect underperformance (F1 ~0.274) due to small data, validating that simpler spatial‑only models suffice.  
3. **LOGO CV with calibration:** Train on 5 apiary groups, test on held‑out group. Compute AUROC (should be ~0.846) and F1 at τ=0.5 (should be low ~0.180). Then calibrate τ on first 30% of held‑out predictions; F1 should recover to ~0.946.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi‑season and geographic generalization:** How does STAG‑CN perform across different seasons and diverse geographic apiaries? The current evaluation uses only 79 days from a single summer season, with disease concentrated in one group.
2. **Visual features from hive images:** Can learned representations from hive inspection photographs provide more informative features than the annotation‑derived statistics currently used? The dataset includes images, but the study did not process actual image content.
3. **Adaptive adjacency on larger data:** Does incorporating adaptive adjacency learning (node embeddings or attention) improve performance when trained on larger datasets, where overfitting is less likely? The current small‑data ablation showed no benefit, likely due to overfitting.
4. **Ensemble with interpretable rules:** Can an ensemble that combines STAG‑CN’s risk scores with interpretable threshold‑based rules yield more practical and trustworthy decision support for beekeepers? STAG‑CN’s calibrated thresholds improve F1, but adding domain rules may enhance usability.

## Limitations
- Extremely small dataset (44 samples) from a single season/location leads to high variance and limits reliability.
- Physical adjacency relies on node‑group mappings that are not fully disclosed, making exact replication difficult.
- Model outputs are poorly calibrated; default threshold yields very low F1, requiring per‑apiary threshold tuning.
