# RetroReasoner: A Reasoning LLM for Strategic Retrosynthesis Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12666
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12666
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RetroReasoner (8B) improves exact-match prediction and template diversity via explicit strategic bond-disconnection reasoning and reinforcement learning with round-trip reward.

## Executive Summary
RetroReasoner addresses the lack of explicit chemical reasoning in retrosynthesis prediction by modeling a chemist’s strategic bond-disconnection process. It employs a two-stage training pipeline: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on SyntheticRetro data, which provides structured reasoning chains (product analysis → candidate substructure → strategic bond disconnection → synthetic equivalent mapping), and reinforcement learning (RL) with a round-trip accuracy reward derived from a forward synthesis model. On an in-distribution test set, RetroReasoner (8B) outperforms prediction-only and prior reasoning baselines, showing significant gains in accuracy, feasibility, and diversity, especially on rare templates and atoms. RL further refines outputs for better feasibility and diversity.

## Method Summary
RetroReasoner uses a two-stage training approach. First, SFT trains a Qwen3-8B model on data generated by SyntheticRetro, which produces structured reasoning steps and linking texts for each reaction. The model learns to generate both the reasoning chain and the final reactants. Second, RL fine-tuning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) optimizes a reward based on round-trip accuracy: a separate forward model predicts the product from the proposed reactants, and reward is given if it matches the target product. This encourages chemically consistent and diverse outputs beyond exact-match memorization.

## Key Results
- With SFT, Exact@100 increases by 5.6% relative to Prediction-Only (0.734 vs 0.678) and Template Diversity rises by 1.336 (3.898 vs 2.562).
- RL further improves Exact@1 by 2.8% and Round-trip@1 by 1.4% over SFT.
- On rare-template and rare-atom/token subsets, RetroReasoner shows larger gains in Feasible Ratio and Template Diversity compared to Prediction-Only, demonstrating broader and more diverse feasible reactant proposals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Explicit Strategic Bond-Disconnection Reasoning
- **Claim:** Modeling a chemist’s stepwise bond-disconnection process improves accuracy and diversity.
- **Mechanism:** The model decomposes retrosynthesis into four structured steps that mirror expert reasoning: product analysis, candidate substructure identification, strategic bond disconnection, and synthetic equivalent mapping. This reduces the complexity of direct product-to-reactant mapping and provides interpretability.
- **Core assumption:** A chemically motivated reasoning chain enables more effective learning of transformations than end-to-end prediction.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 1 state the approach models chemist strategy. Ablation in Section 6.6.1 shows removing later steps (using only product analysis) drops Exact@1 and diversity.

### Mechanism 2: Reinforcement Learning with Round-Trip Reward
- **Claim:** RL with round-trip accuracy as reward yields more feasible and diverse proposals than exact-match reward.
- **Mechanism:** A forward model converts predicted reactants back to a product; reward = 1 if the round-trip product matches the target. This encourages predictions that are chemically consistent and explores a broader valid space rather than narrow exact matches.
- **Core assumption:** Round-trip validation correlates with synthetic viability and approximates existence of at least one feasible reactant set.
- **Evidence:** Abstract reports RL gains over SFT. Section 6.6.4 (Table 5) shows exact-match reward increases Feasible Ratio but decreases diversity, confirming round-trip’s role.

### Mechanism 3: Diverse Linking Texts in SFT
- **Claim:** Training with multiple reasoning paths (linking texts) per reaction improves generalization.
- **Mechanism:** SyntheticRetro generates n=15 different linking texts per reaction, each providing a unique natural language connective between structured steps. A different linking text is used each epoch, exposing the model to a richer distribution of rationalizations and preventing overfitting to a single explanation.
- **Core assumption:** Multiple plausible reasoning trajectories exist for the same transformation; training on one narrow path harms generalization.
- **Evidence:** Section 4.3 and C.1 describe the n=15 scheme. Section 6.6.3 (Figure 3) shows n=15 yields higher Exact@1 and step accuracies than n=1.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Retrosynthetic analysis (synthons, synthetic equivalents)
  - **Why needed:** The reasoning process builds on bond disconnection to form synthons and mapping to actual reactants; essential to interpret structured outputs.
  - **Quick check:** Given a product and a disconnection between two atoms, what is the difference between synthons and their synthetic equivalents? Example: Disconnecting a C–O bond in an ester yields an acylium synthon and an alkoxide synthon; their synthetic equivalents are实际 reactants like an acid chloride and an alcohol.

- **Concept:** SMILES
  - **Why needed:** Molecules are represented as SMILES strings throughout input/output, metrics, and round-trip validation; parsing errors invalidate evaluation.
  - **Quick check:** Convert SMILES "CCO" to structure and identify functional group. Issues when generating SMILES with LLM: invalid syntax, incorrect atom/bond counts, non-canonical forms.

- **Concept:** Reinforcement learning with verifiable reward (RLVR)
  - **Why needed:** RL optimizes a non-differentiable reward (round-trip accuracy) that cannot be trained via supervised learning; understanding RLVR clarifies need for policy gradient methods like GRPO.
  - **Quick check:** Advantage of round-trip verifier over exact match? It encourages exploration of chemically consistent routes rather than narrow memorization; verifier affects exploration by rewarding any route that round-trips correctly.

- **Concept:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on structured rationales
  - **Why needed:** SFT establishes the initial policy that generates reasoning and reactants before RL refinement; without it, RL may not converge.
  - **Quick check:** Why SFT alone insufficient? It lacks exploration; RL with a verifiable reward further optimizes for feasibility and diversity beyond the static SFT distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
SyntheticRetro generator (uses LocalMapper, rule extractors, GPT-oss-20B) → generates structured reasoning data (n=15 per instance) → RetroReasoner policy (initialized from Qwen3-8B; SFT then RL with GRPO) → predicted reactants → validated by Round-trip forward model (Qwen3-0.6B/8B; trained separately on reversed I/O) → round-trip reward computation.

**Critical path:**  
1. Build SyntheticRetro generator with rule-based extractors and linking text prompt.  
2. Generate training data: process RXN SMILES, filter valid instances, produce n=15 rationales each.  
3. SFT RetroReasoner on rationales with cross-entropy loss, cycling linking texts per epoch.  
4. Train round-trip forward model on reactants→product pairs.  
5. RL fine-tuning: initialize from SFT checkpoint, compute round-trip reward via cached forward model, optimize with GRPO.  
6. Evaluate on held-out sets using Exact@k, Round-trip@k, Feasible Ratio, Template Diversity.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- SFT diversity vs noise: n=15 improves generalization but increases data cost and risk of inconsistent rationales.  
- Round-trip vs exact-match reward: Round-trip encourages feasible, diverse outputs but requires high-accuracy forward model; exact-match is simpler but penalizes valid alternatives.  
- Model size: Larger base (8B vs 1.7B) learns reasoning better but needs more compute; 8B shown necessary.  
- Temperature: >1.2 yields diverse SMILES but breaks validity quickly; chosen 1.2 balances diversity and validity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Accuracy drop on rare templates: low Template Diversity; RetroReasoner mitigates but still declines.  
- Invalid SMILES at high temperature: Feasible Ratio plummets; monitor with RDKit validity.  
- Structured step errors: Misidentification of functional groups/bonds leads to incorrect synthons; monitor step-wise accuracy.  
- Overfitting during RL: If forward model inaccurate, reward mis-specified; watch for Feasible Ratio increase but Exact@1 decrease.  
- Computational bottleneck: Round-trip reward computation dominates; caching needed but memory-intensive.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **In-distribution comparison:** Train RetroReasoner (SFT+RL) and Prediction-Only on USPTWO train; test on 500 held-out instances. Expected: SFT outperforms Prediction-Only on Exact@100 (+5.6% rel) and Template Diversity; RL further improves Exact@1 over SFT.
2. **Hard case evaluation:** Select rare reaction template and rare atom/token subsets (100 each). Expected: RetroReasoner shows larger gains in Feasible Ratio and Template Diversity vs Prediction-Only.
3. **Ablation studies:**  
   a) Remove linking texts (w/o L12,L23,L34) → drop in Exact@1 and diversity.  
   b) Use only product analysis (only R1) → similar drop, showing need for full reasoning.  
   c) Reduce linking text diversity (n=1) → lower Exact@1 and step accuracies.  
   d) Replace round-trip reward with exact match (w/ Rexact) → Feasible Ratio increases but diversity decreases sharply.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **How can RetroReasoner incorporate environmental synthesis conditions (temperature, humidity, pressure) into its reasoning to produce experimentally feasible routes?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7 (Limitations) states it does not consider environmental conditions.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Conditions are essential for practical execution; ignoring them may yield routes that fail in real labs.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Collect a dataset with condition annotations; modify model to conditionally generate retrosynthesis; evaluate on a benchmark requiring correct condition prediction; compare with expert-annotated feasible routes.

2. **Can RetroReasoner’s reasoning be extended to include electron-transfer mechanisms and to handle macromolecules like polymers and crystals?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7 notes current reasoning does not extend to electron transfer and cannot handle polymers or crystals.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Electron-level details matter for redox/pericyclic reactions; polymers/crystals are key for materials science; current scope is narrow.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Integrate electron-flow representations or quantum descriptors into reasoning steps; build/curate datasets of polymer/crystal reactions; test on domain-specific benchmarks; measure improvements in route validity.

3. **How do inaccuracies in the round-trip forward model affect reinforcement learning, and how can the reward be made more robust to such errors and missing constraints?**  
   - *Basis:* Discussion “Validity of Using the Round-trip Accuracy” identifies forward model errors could reinforce incorrect predictions and ignores conditions, thermodynamics, yields.  
   - *Why unresolved:* RL updates depend on reward; biased forward model may promote invalid routes or penalize feasible ones, limiting real-world utility.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Sensitivity studies varying forward model accuracy; design hybrid rewards (e.g., combining round-trip with confidence scores, thermodynamic scores, expert heuristics); validate against known feasible routes with yield/condition data.

4. **Does multi-turn reasoning (test-time scaling) in RetroReasoner improve performance on full retrosynthetic planning tasks that require multiple disconnections?**  
   - *Basis:* Discussion “Applicability to Retrosynthetic Planning” hypothesizes scaling inference computation through multi-turn exploration will yield gains but remains untested.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Paper evaluates single-step prediction; practical planning needs iterative, tree-like search; benefit of compute-scaled inference unknown for chemistry.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Implement iterative application via tree search on multi-step planning benchmarks (USPTO-FULL, PLAN); compare route metrics (length, cost, success rate) with/without multi-turn exploration; analyze scalability with increased inference compute.

## Limitations
- Dataset quality depends on GPT-oss-20B and rule-based extractors; hidden errors may propagate to SFT.
- Round-trip reward relies on forward model accuracy; its sensitivity to forward mistakes is not quantified.
- Evaluation is limited to the USPTWO split; out-of-distribution generalization is unknown.
- SMILES validity degrades at higher temperatures, affecting feasibility.
- Lack of human validation of reasoning chains limits interpretability claims.
- Training costs and hyperparameter choices may affect reproducibility.

## Confidence
- Explicit strategic bond-disconnection reasoning improves accuracy and diversity: **High** (supported by Tables 2–4, Figure 3).
- RL with round-trip reward yields feasible, diverse outputs: **Medium** (Table 5; alternative rewards not tested, forward model dependency unexamined).
- Linking-text diversity (n=15) aids generalization: **High** (Figure 3).
- Robustness to rare templates/atoms: **Medium** (Table 3; only two rare subsets evaluated).

## Next Checks
1. **Forward model corruption analysis:** Systematically degrade forward model accuracy (e.g., drop top-k predictions, add noise) and measure impact on RL policy performance to assess reward robustness.
2. **Human evaluation of reasoning chains:** Recruit domain experts to annotate ≥200 generated rationales, judging correctness of each reasoning step and overall synthetic feasibility; correlate with automatic metrics.
3. **Cross-dataset transfer:** Train on USPTWO and zero-shot test on external reaction database (e.g., Pistachio) to evaluate generalization beyond original distribution.
