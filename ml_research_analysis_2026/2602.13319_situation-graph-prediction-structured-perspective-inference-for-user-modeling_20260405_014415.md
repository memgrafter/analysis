# Situation Graph Prediction: Structured Perspective Inference for User Modeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13319
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13319
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: SGP with RA-ICL achieves substantial gain in structured perspective inference (Soft F1: 0.145 → 0.424) and reveals a significant normalized latent-surface gap (+0.53).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Situation Graph Prediction (SGP), a structured inverse inference task aimed at reconstructing users' internal states (goals, emotions, context) from multimodal digital artifacts. Because real-world labeled traces are unavailable, it proposes a structure‑first synthetic data generation pipeline: first sample a valid, ontology‑aligned perspective graph, then condition an LLM to generate artifacts consistent with that graph. This guarantees aligned (artifact, graph) pairs without manual annotation. In a diagnostic study with 75 pilot pairs using GPT‑4o and retrieval‑augmented in‑context learning (RA‑ICL), overall Soft F1 improves dramatically from 0.145 to 0.424. Moreover, after entropy normalization to adjust for vocabulary sparsity, the latent‑surface performance gap widens from +0.14 (zero‑shot) to +0.53 (with retrieval), demonstrating that inferring psychological states remains a much harder problem than extracting observable elements and validating the synthetic strategy.

## Method Summary
The core method is a two‑stage synthetic data generation process that inverts the assumed generative model of user traces. First, a valid Situation Graph is sampled from an ontology‑based prior, ensuring compliance with a DUL‑derived schema that distinguishes surface (observable) from latent (psychological) predicates. Second, an LLM generates multimodal artifacts (e.g., text, images) conditioned on the sampled graph, producing (𝑋𝑡, 𝐺𝑡) pairs with guaranteed consistency. For inference, artifacts are first decomposed into text using modality‑specific modules; then a reasoning agent (GPT‑4o) predicts graph triplets, either zero‑shot or with RA‑ICL (retrieving the top‑k semantically similar synthetic demonstrations). Performance is measured via Strict/Soft F1 and Predicate Violation Rate, with results decomposed into surface and latent subsets to compute the latent‑surface gap (ΔLS). Entropy normalization accounts for differences in vocabulary size between the two subsets.

## Key Results
- RA‑ICL boosts SGP performance: Soft F1 from 0.145 (zero‑shot) to 0.424 (with retrieval).
- Entropy‑normalized latent‑surface gap widens dramatically: from +0.14 zero‑shot to +0.53 with retrieval, indicating significant difficulty of latent inference.
- Synthetic generation ensures aligned (artifact, graph) pairs by construction, enabling evaluation without real labels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Structure‑first synthetic generation creates a reliable supervision signal by aligning latent perspective graphs (𝐺𝑡) with observable artifacts (𝑋𝑡) by construction.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline inverts the hypothesized generative process: it first samples a valid, ontology‑compliant Situation Graph from the schema’s prior, then conditions an LLM to generate multimodal artifacts that are *consistent* with that graph. This ensures every training/validation pair (𝑋𝑡, 𝐺𝑡) has a guaranteed causal link, sidestepping the impossibility of annotating real user traces.
- **Core assumption:** The chosen ontology (DUL‑based schema) is sufficiently expressive to represent the key latent perspective variables (goals, emotions, context) that would manifest in observable digital traces.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "structure‑first synthetic generation strategy that aligns latent labels and observable traces by design."; [section 4] "First, we sample a valid Situation Graph 𝐺𝑡... Next, we treat artifact generation as a conditional rendering task... This inversion aligns supervision with evidence by construction."; [corpus] The nearest papers focus on knowledge graphs and recommendation, not on *generating* artifacts from latent graphs. Evidence for this specific mechanism is weak from the corpus; it is primarily an original contribution anchored in the paper’s own methodology.
- **Break condition:** The generated artifacts fail to be plausibly consistent with the seed graph (e.g., an `Emotion:Stressed` graph produces cheerful, relaxed communications), or the ontology’s vocabulary is too sparse/constraining to cover the diversity of real‑world manifestations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Representing perspective as an ontology‑aligned, typed knowledge graph (vs. free text) provides a structured, actionable target for inference that disentangles *surface extraction* from *latent state inference*.
- **Mechanism:** The schema explicitly separates nodes and predicates into "surface" (observable: participants, locations, times) and "latent" (psychological: emotions, valence). This allows decomposing the monolithic prediction task (recover 𝐺𝑡) into sub‑problems and measuring the performance gap (ΔLS) between them. The typed constraints also act as a weak supervision signal during generation and inference.
- **Core assumption:** The surface‑latent dichotomy in the schema accurately reflects the inferential challenge in real user modeling; i.e., observable cues are imperfect proxies for internal states.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] "Crucially, the schema distinguishes *surface* from *latent* attributes... latent predicates... encoding unobservable internal perspective."; [section 6.2] "We decompose... into *latent* and *surface* subsets to separate extraction from state inference."; [abstract] "we observe a gap between surface‑level extraction and latent perspective inference."
- **Break condition:** The surface/latent distinction is artificial or does not generalize; in practice, "latent" states are directly observable (e.g., a post explicitly says "I am furious"), or true latent variables are not captured by the predefined predicate types.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Retrieval‑Augmented In‑Context Learning (RA‑ICL) with synthetic demonstrations provides a scalable, no‑training proxy for supervision that highlights the semantic gap, as demonstrations primarily improve pattern‑matching for surface elements.
- **Mechanism:** Providing the model (GPT‑4o) with a small set of (𝑋𝑡, 𝐺𝑡) examples retrieved by semantic similarity helps it learn *mappings from artifact patterns to graph structures*. Because surface elements (e.g., a location name) have high lexical/semantic overlap with the source text, they are easier to "copy" or align from demonstrations. Latent inferences require abstracting beyond demonstrated patterns, a harder reasoning step.
- **Core assumption:** The demonstrations in the support set are high‑quality, consistent with the query’s underlying graph structure, and the retrieval metric (text‑embedding‑3‑large) effectively finds relevant precedents.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 6.1] "retrieve the top‑𝑘=3 semantically similar (𝑋,𝐺) pairs... provide them as demonstrations. These serve as proxy supervision."; [section 6.2] "retrieval‑augmented demonstrations provide more actionable signal for extracting observable elements than for inferring internal states."; [table 1] Surface F1 improves more (+0.322) than Latent F1 (+0.206) with RA‑ICL, and the entropy‑normalized gap widens significantly.
- **Break condition:** The retrieved demonstrations are semantically misaligned (e.g., matching on superficial keywords but different latent states), providing misleading signal. Or, the foundation model is already saturated on the task, and ICL provides no benefit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Perspective‑Aware AI (PAi)**
  - **Why needed here:** It defines the paradigm shift from modeling static preferences to evolving internal states (goals, emotions, context). SGP is presented as a core technical task *for* PAi.
  - **Quick check question:** How does PAi differ from traditional user modeling (e.g., collaborative filtering) in its representation of "user"?
- **Concept: Situation Graph (𝐺𝑡)**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the structured, symbolic output target of SGP—an ontology‑grounded representation of a user’s perspective at time *t*.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the four semantic strata in the schema, and which one contains the "latent" psychological predicates?
- **Concept: Structure‑First Synthetic Generation**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the proposed solution to the data bottleneck: creating aligned (𝑋𝑡, 𝐺𝑡) pairs without real labels by inverting the generation process.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the two sequential steps in this pipeline, and why is the order critical?
- **Concept: Latent vs. Surface Inference Gap (ΔLS)**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the key diagnostic metric used to argue that SGP is non‑trivial; the gap measures the extra difficulty of inferring unobservable states.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is entropy normalization necessary to interpret the raw latent‑surface F1 gap fairly?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Ontology & Schema:** Defines node/edge types and constraints (DUL‑based). Source of truth for graph validity.
  2. **Synthetic Data Generator:** Two‑stage module: (a) samples a valid 𝐺𝑡 from the schema prior; (b) prompts an LLM to generate multimodal artifacts 𝑋𝑡 conditioned on 𝐺𝑡.
  3. **Inference Pipeline:** (a) *Modality Decomposer*: uses VLMs/transcribers to convert raw artifacts to text; (b) *Reasoning Agent* (e.g., GPT‑4o): consumes decomposed artifacts + schema + optional demonstrations to output triplet set ˆ𝑇𝑡.
  4. **Evaluator:** Computes metrics (Strict/Soft F1, PVR) by comparing ˆ𝑇𝑡 to ground truth 𝑇𝑡, with decompositions into surface/latent subsets.
- **Critical path:** Schema Design → Synthetic Data Generation → Modality‑Specific Preprocessing → In‑Context Prompt Assembly → LLM Inference → Structured Parsing → Metric Calculation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Synthetic vs. Real Data:* Synthetic data provides labels and privacy but risks oversimplification and distribution shift. Pilot data (N=75) is for diagnostics, not training.
  - *Schema Complexity:* A richer ontology captures more nuance but increases generation difficulty, graph validity constraints, and prediction space size.
  - *RA‑ICL k‑value:* More demonstrations (higher *k*) provide more supervision signal but increase prompt length/cost and risk of retrieval noise.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Predicate Violation Rate (PVR): The agent frequently proposes edges that violate type constraints (e.g., connecting an `Emotion` node to a `Location` with an invalid predicate).
  - Near‑zero or negative ΔLS (raw or normalized): The model performs equally well/better on latent than surface elements, suggesting the task or evaluation is flawed (e.g., latent states are trivially extractable, or surface extraction is artificially hard).
  - Low consistency across 5 folds: Performance is highly sensitive to the specific retrieved demonstrations, indicating poor generalization or that the task is not learnable from a few examples.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic Data Sanity Check:** For a manually inspected sample, verify that every artifact in 𝑋𝑡 contains clear evidence for at least one node/predicate in its paired 𝐺𝑡, and that no artifact contradicts any part of 𝐺𝑡.
  2. **Zero‑shot vs. RA‑ICL Ablation:** Run the diagnostic protocol with *k*=0 and *k*=3 on the same test folds. Confirm the expected pattern: RA‑ICL improves overall Soft F1 and increases the raw ΔLS gap.
  3. **Entropy Normalization Validation:** Compute the Shannon entropy for the latent object distribution (8 values) and surface object distribution (106 values) across the full synthetic dataset. Apply the normalization factor and confirm it transforms the near‑zero raw gap into a substantial positive gap, demonstrating the confound of vocabulary size.

## Limitations
- Synthetic artifact realism: No human validation that generated artifacts are plausible or cover real‑world diversity.
- Small‑scale, single‑model evaluation (N=75, GPT‑4o only) and lack of real labeled test data.
- Potential gaps in ontology coverage and classification of surface vs. latent.

## Confidence
- Structure‑first synthetic alignment: Medium
- Latent‑surface inference gap: Medium
- RA‑ICL amplifies the gap: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Human plausibility study: Sample 200 synthetic (𝑋,𝐺) pairs; ask human raters to judge whether artifacts logically cohere with the latent graph and whether latent states are inferable.
2. Cross‑model replication: Repeat diagnostic using at least two additional LLMs (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Llama‑3.1‑70B) with identical settings; report consistency of ΔLS gap and RA‑ICL gains.
3. Semi‑real validation: Collect a small set of real user digital traces (e.g., forum posts, emails), manually annotate perspective graphs using the schema, and evaluate zero‑shot/RA‑ICL SGP. Compare performance and gap to synthetic results to quantify synthetic‑to‑real transfer.
