# Token-level Data Selection for Safe LLM Fine-tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01185
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01185
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Token-level selection (TOSS) yields ~20% average win-rate improvement over sample-level methods, with up to 30% higher safety win rates and 11% higher utility win rates.

## Executive Summary
Fine-tuning large language models on custom data often compromises their safety alignment. Existing sample-level data selection methods yield poor trade-offs between safety and utility. This paper introduces TOSS, a novel framework that operates at the token level to identify and discard safety-critical tokens before fine-tuning. TOSS computes a risk score for each token based on the difference in negative log-likelihood between a safety-degraded model (trained on harmful data) and a utility-oriented model (trained on task-specific data). An iterative variant, TOSS-Pro, refines the safety-degraded model progressively for more accurate scoring. Experiments on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Llama-2-7B-Chat-hf show TOSS significantly outperforms sample-level baselines, improving win rates by ~20% on average, with up to 30% higher safety and 11% higher utility. TOSS-Pro further boosts safety by up to 6%. The approach transfers across models sharing a tokenizer, demonstrating practical efficacy.

## Method Summary
TOSS comprises three main stages. First, two reference models are trained from the base model: a safety-degraded model on a small set of harmful examples (D_h) and a utility-oriented model on a small set of task-relevant examples (D_u). Second, each token in the custom fine-tuning dataset D_cus is scored using the difference in negative log-likelihood between these reference models: S(token) = loss_utility - loss_safety; high scores indicate tokens that are likely to degrade safety while being non-essential for utility. All tokens across the entire dataset are pooled and globally ranked, and the top-d% (e.g., d=0.1) are masked (i.e., their labels are set to ignore during loss computation). Third, the base model is fine-tuned on D_cus with the mask applied, using a standard objective (e.g., cross-entropy) but ignoring masked tokens. TOSS-Pro extends this process with an iterative loop: after an initial masking, the samples with the highest token scores are added to D_h, the safety-degraded model is retrained, and token scoring is repeated for T iterations before the final masking. This token-level granularity allows adaptive discarding: harmful samples lose many tokens while benign samples retain most, leading to a superior safety-utility trade-off compared to sample-level selection.

## Key Results
- Average win-rate improvement of ~20% over sample-level methods (e.g., SEAL, DSIR) as judged by GPT-4o.
- Up to 30% higher safety win rates and 11% higher utility win rates compared to baselines.
- TOSS-Pro provides additional safety gains of up to 6% over the non-iterative TOSS.
- Transferability experiments show that token selection computed on one Llama model benefits other sizes sharing the same tokenizer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Token Scoring via Loss Difference
- **Claim**: Token-level scoring via the loss difference between a safety-degraded model and a utility-oriented model effectively identifies tokens that contribute to safety degradation.
- **Mechanism**: For each token in D_cus, compute its negative log-likelihood under both reference models. The score is S = loss_utility - loss_safety. High S indicates the safety-degraded model predicts the token easily (low loss) while the utility model struggles (high loss), signaling a safety risk.
- **Core assumption**: Safety degradation manifests at the token level, and the loss patterns distinguish harmful from utility-relevant tokens.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] describes scoring via loss difference; Section 4.1 formalizes S; ablations (Figure 4) show token-level superiority.
- **Break condition**: If tokens with high S are actually benign or essential for utility, the mechanism reduces utility or fails to improve safety.

### Mechanism 2: Global Ranking of Token Scores
- **Claim**: Pooling tokens across the entire dataset and discarding the top-d% globally adaptively removes more tokens from harmful samples than from benign ones.
- **Mechanism**: After computing scores for all tokens in D_cus, they are sorted globally (not per sample). The highest-scoring tokens are masked regardless of which sample they belong to. This yields a variable discard ratio per sample proportional to its risk density.
- **Core assumption**: Harmful samples contain a higher concentration of high-risk tokens than benign samples.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] mentions global ranking; Section 4.1 explains limitation of fixed per-sample ratios; Figure 4 (Left) shows global ranking outperforms local ranking.
- **Break condition**: If risk tokens are uniformly distributed, global ranking offers no advantage; but Section 3 analysis shows non-uniform distribution (Figure 2).

### Mechanism 3: Progressive Refinement (TOSS-Pro)
- **Claim**: Iteratively refining the safety-degraded model improves token risk identification.
- **Mechanism**: Start with an initial safety-degraded model trained on D_h. After scoring and masking, select samples with the highest average token scores and add them to D_h. Retrain the safety-degraded model on the expanded D_h. Repeat for T rounds before the final scoring and masking.
- **Core assumption**: The safety-degraded model’s ability to recognize unsafe tokens can be enhanced by exposing it to increasingly informative harmful examples discovered through token scoring.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions TOSS-Pro; Section 4.2 details the procedure; Table 3 shows TOSS-Pro improves safety over TOSS while random updates hurt performance.
- **Break condition**: If added samples are noisy or too narrow, the refined model may overfit and misidentify tokens, reducing effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Base Model (f_θ) -> (split) -> Safety-degraded Model (f_θ^h) trained on D_h; Utility-oriented Model (f_θ^u) trained on D_u -> both applied to Custom Dataset (D_cus) -> Token Scorer computes per-token S(y) = loss_utility - loss_safety -> Mask Generator globally ranks tokens and produces binary mask M_cus (discard top d%) -> Selective Fine-tuning Module trains f_θ on masked D_cus (labels = -100 for masked tokens) -> Final Model.  
For TOSS-Pro: after Token Scorer, high-score samples are identified -> added to D_h -> Iterative Updater retrains f_θ^h -> loop back to Token Scorer for T iterations before Mask Generator.

**Critical path**:  
1. Train f_θ^h on D_h and f_θ^u on D_u (using LoRA, 2 epochs each).  
2. Score every token in D_cus using S.  
3. Generate global mask M_cus by sorting all S and discarding top-d% tokens.  
4. Fine-tune f_θ on D_cus with M_cus applied.  
*For TOSS-Pro*: between steps 2 and 3, perform T iterations: select top-k samples by token scores, augment D_h, retrain f_θ^h, recompute S.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Token-level granularity improves safety-utility trade-off but adds computational overhead from training and running two reference models.  
- Choice of reference datasets (D_h, D_u) must be representative yet small; poor selection degrades scoring quality.  
- Discard ratio d controls the safety-utility balance; higher d increases safety but may reduce utility.  
- TOSS-Pro may yield extra safety gains at the cost of additional training cycles; diminishing returns or overfitting are possible.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High attack success rate (ASR) on safety benchmarks indicates ineffective masking.  
- Low utility win rate on downstream tasks (e.g., SLIMORCA) suggests over-discarding or mis-specialized reference models.  
- Mask distribution highly skewed: many benign samples have high discard ratios or many harmful samples retain many tokens.  
- TOSS-Pro iterations do not improve or worsen performance, implying poor sample selection or noisy updates.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Main Evaluation**: Compare TOSS and TOSS-Pro against baselines (Standard SFT, Random, SafeInstr, DSIR, SEAL) on Llama models using safety benchmarks (HEx-PHI, ANTHROPIC HH test) and utility benchmark (SLIMORCA) via win rates judged by GPT-4o.  
2. **Ablation Studies**: Evaluate the contribution of each component: global vs. local ranking, token-level vs. sample-level selection, and ablations where only one reference model is used.  
3. **Transferability Test**: Demonstrate that token masks computed on one model (e.g., Llama-3-8B) can be reused on other models sharing the same tokenizer (Llama-3.2-1B, 3B) without retraining reference models.

## Limitations
- Exact sizes of the reference datasets (D_h, D_u) are not specified; arbitrary sampling may lead to inconsistent token scoring and hinder reproducibility.
- TOSS-Pro’s hyperparameter k (number of samples added per iteration) is undisclosed, making iterative refinement hard to replicate exactly.
- Baseline implementations (e.g., SEAL) are complex and lack official code; recreated versions may underperform, potentially inflating the reported advantages of TOSS.
- Transferability is assessed only within the Llama family; it is unclear whether the approach generalizes to architectures with different tokenizers or model structures.

## Confidence
- Token-level scoring efficacy: High
- TOSS-Pro iterative refinement: Medium
- Cross-model transferability: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Reference model validation**: Generate responses from f_θ^h on a held-out set of harmful prompts to confirm higher compliance relative to the base model; similarly, evaluate f_θ^u on task-relevant prompts to confirm utility preservation.
2. **Discard ratio sweep**: Fine-tune with various d (e.g., 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) and plot safety (ASR) vs. utility (win rate) to verify monotonic trade-off and identify optimal operating point.
3. **TOSS-Pro iteration audit**: After each iteration, measure the safety-degraded model’s compliance rate on a fixed harmful set; it should increase monotonically. Also inspect the added samples to ensure diversity and avoid narrow pattern overfitting.
