# Query-Mixed Interest Extraction and Heterogeneous Interaction: A Scalable CTR Model for Industrial Recommender Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09387
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09387
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HeMix outperforms strong baselines, yielding +3.61% GMV and +2.78% PV_CTR in online A/B tests; scales effectively to 1.5B parameters.

## Executive Summary
HeMix addresses two critical challenges in industrial CTR/CVR prediction: sparse multi‑field inputs and ultra‑long user sequences. It introduces a Query‑Mixed Interest Extraction module that jointly models context‑aware and context‑independent interests, and a HeteroMixer block that replaces self‑attention with an efficient multi‑granularity heterogeneous interaction. Trained on billions of samples and deployed on the AMAP platform, HeMix delivers significant online gains and demonstrates steady improvements as model size increases, supporting a favorable scaling law.

## Method Summary
HeMix first embeds all sparse features (user, item, cross, and sequence item attributes). Non‑sequential features are concatenated, passed through an MLP, and split into N_NS tokens. For sequences, mixed queries—comprising a subset of non‑sequential tokens (dynamic) and fixed learnable vectors—attend to global historical and real‑time behaviors via Mixed Hetero Attention, producing separate context‑aware and context‑independent sequence tokens. All tokens are then processed by a stack of L HeteroMixer blocks. Each block performs HeteroMixing (grouping by origin, low‑rank MLPs per group, reconstruction) followed by a per‑token HeteroFFN. Final tokens are mean‑pooled and fed to a predictor. The design is parameter‑efficient and parallelizable, enabling controlled scaling.

## Key Results
- Offline: Consistent AUC and Logloss improvements over strong baselines (e.g., RankMixer) on billions of samples for CTR and CVR.
- Online: A/B test on AMAP shows +3.61% GMV, +2.78% PV_CTR, and +2.12% UV_CVR versus DLRM.
- Scaling: Larger models (up to 1.5B parameters) yield steady accuracy gains without architectural changes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HeMix’s effectiveness arises from three synergistic mechanisms.

1. **Query‑Mixed Interest Extraction** – Jointly extracts context‑aware (candidate‑dependent) and context‑independent (candidate‑invariant) interests from ultra‑long sequences. Dynamic queries (from non‑sequential tokens) and fixed learnable queries attend to global and real‑time behaviors via Mixed Hetero Attention, separating short‑term and long‑term signals. Ablation (Table 3) shows removal of fixed queries degrades AUC by ~0.0005, and dropping either sequence type causes larger drops, confirming each component’s contribution.

2. **HeteroMixer Block** – Replaces quadratic self‑attention with a three‑stage pipeline: multi‑head token fusion, heterogeneous mixed‑token interaction via low‑rank MLPs, and group‑aligned reconstruction. Grouping tokens by semantic origin (e.g., user, item) enables efficient multi‑granularity interactions. Empirical results (Table 4) show HeteroMixer outperforms RankMixer and Transformer; scaling experiments (Figure 3) demonstrate superior efficiency.

3. **Scalable Modular Architecture** – Both components are parameter‑efficient and parallelizable; HeteroMixer reduces complexity to O(N·dr) from O(N²). Total parameters scale linearly with depth L, width d_T, and token count N, allowing controlled expansion. Scaling up to 1.5B parameters consistently improves performance (Figure 3), indicating a scaling law for CTR models.

## Foundational Learning
Three key concepts underpin HeMix’s design:

- **Tokenization in Recommender Systems**  
  *Why needed*: Converts heterogeneous sparse features into unified dense tokens. HeMix’s “interact‑then‑split” for non‑sequential fields preserves semantic boundaries better than naïve concatenation.  
  *Quick check*: Why is AutoSplit insufficient for non‑sequential features, and how does “interact‑then‑split” preserve semantic boundaries?

- **Heterogeneous Feature Interactions**  
  *Why needed*: Recommender features originate from different domains (user, item, context) with mismatched semantics; homogeneous pairwise attention wastes capacity. HeteroMixer groups tokens by origin and applies dedicated interactions.  
  *Quick check*: What is the key difference between RankMixer’s token mixing and HeteroMixer’s group‑aligned reconstruction?

- **Scaling Laws for Recommendation Models**  
  *Why needed*: Justifies investment in larger models by showing predictable returns. HeMix must demonstrate smooth improvement with parameter count, unlike DLRM where scaling gives marginal gains.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Sparse Features → Embedding Lookup → Non‑sequential Tokenization (Concat → MLP → Split) → Sequence Tokenization (Mixed Hetero Attention on Global/Real‑time) → Combined Tokens → L × HeteroMixer Block (HeteroMixing + HeteroFFN) → Mean Pooling → Prediction (MLP/LR) → Output.

**Critical path**: Sequence tokenization (Query‑Mixed Interest Extraction) is critical; errors propagate to all subsequent layers, followed by the depth of HeteroMixer blocks which determines capacity.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Token count N vs. compute: More tokens capture finer semantics but increase HeteroMixer cost O(N·dr). N=60 in the paper; increasing may help but costs more.
- Low‑rank rank dr: Lower dr reduces parameters/flops but risks underfitting group interactions; dr ≪ N·d_h must be tuned.
- Fixed query vectors Q^G, Q^R: Add parameters to extract candidate‑invariant patterns; ablation shows they help, so removing them risks loss of context‑independent signals.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Sequence tokenization failure**: If ablations “w/o Global Sequence”, “w/o Real‑time Sequence”, or “w/o Mixed‑Queries” cause major AUC drop (>0.001), sequence modeling is critical. High cosine similarity among extracted sequence tokens indicates poor disentanglement.
- **Interaction failure**: If HeteroMixer underperforms Transformer or RankMixer at similar parameter budget, grouping or low‑rank design may be too restrictive; consider increasing dr or M.
- **Scaling failure**: If larger models (e.g., >500M params) do not improve over smaller ones, optimization may be unstable; check learning rate, normalization, and regularization.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Tokenization ablation**: Train HeMix‑Small with (a) full, (b) no fixed queries, (c) no mixed queries (AutoSplit), (d) no global seq, (e) no RT seq. Expect full best; major drop from removing sequences.
2. **Interaction architecture swap**: Replace HeteroMixer block with RankMixer and Transformer blocks (same L, d_T, N). Verify HeteroMixer > RankMixer > Transformer on an AMAP sample.
3. **Scaling curve**: Train HeMix with parameter budgets ~100M, 300M, 600M, 1B by varying L, d_T, N. Plot AUC vs #Params and GFLOPs vs AUC; expect monotonic improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Fixed query invariance** – How do fixed query vectors maintain context‑independence while effectively capturing user interests? They are used globally, so they risk assimilating candidate‑specific signals during training. Ablations show benefit but learning dynamics and invariance mechanisms are not elaborated. Resolution: Analyze attention patterns and query embeddings across candidates; test regularization for invariance.
2. **Low‑rank sensitivity** – What is the sensitivity to the low‑rank rank dr in HeteroMixing, and does optimal dr vary with data sparsity or sequence length? The paper uses a fixed dr ≪ N·dh without ablation. Resolution: Systematically vary dr on datasets with different sparsity; plot performance vs dr and GFLOPs.
3. **Scaling law quantification** – Does HeMix exhibit a quantifiable scaling law similar to LLMs, and which dimension (depth, width, token count) maximizes performance per compute for CTR? The paper shows empirical scaling but no power‑law fit or compute‑optimal point. Resolution: Controlled experiments scaling each dimension independently; fit scaling exponents; compute AUC per GFLOP.

## Limitations
- **Replication‑blocking unknowns**: Exact hyperparameters (query split ratio, HeteroMixer dr, per‑token FFN sharing), temporal split logic, and precise model dimensions for the 100M/1.5B variants are not disclosed.
- **External validation gap**: Results are on internal AMAP data only; no independent replication on public benchmarks, limiting generalizability.
- **Scalability practicality**: Online deployment uses only ~100M parameters despite 1.5B experiments, and training requires 100 GPUs with warm‑started embeddings, which may not be feasible for smaller organizations.

## Confidence
Confidence in the three core mechanisms is assessed as **Medium** due to strong empirical evidence but limited isolation and external validation.

- Query‑Mixed Interest Extraction: Medium (ablation supports contribution, but novelty vs. related work not strongly contrasted).
- HeteroMixer Block: Medium (outperforms baselines, but low‑rank impact unablated; no direct comparison to dense mixing).
- Scalable Modular Architecture: Medium (scaling shown, but limited data points; online use of smaller models questions practical scaling).

## Next Checks
1. **Query‑type isolation ablation**: Train HeMix with only dynamic queries, only fixed queries, and mixed queries on an AMAP sample to verify joint modeling necessity (expect significant drop for single‑query variants).
2. **Low‑rank vs. dense HeteroMixer**: Replace low‑rank MLP with dense MLP of equal parameter count in HeteroMixer; compare AUC, Logloss, and training stability to assess expressiveness loss.
3. **Cross‑dataset scaling law**: Implement HeMix‑Small (~100M) and HeMix‑Large (~1B) on public datasets (Criteo/Avazu) with artificially long sequences; plot AUC vs. parameters and GFLOPs to check if monotonic improvement holds universally.
