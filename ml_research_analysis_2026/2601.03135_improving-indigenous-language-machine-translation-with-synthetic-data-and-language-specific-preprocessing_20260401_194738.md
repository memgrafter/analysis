# Improving Indigenous Language Machine Translation with Synthetic Data and Language-Specific Preprocessing

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03135
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03135
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Synthetic data augmentation and language-specific preprocessing yield consistent chrF++ improvements for Guarani-Spanish and Quechua-Spanish, but gains are limited for highly agglutinative Aymara.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the data scarcity challenge for neural machine translation of low-resource indigenous languages of the Americas (Aymara, Guarani, Quechua). The authors augment small curated parallel corpora with synthetic sentence pairs generated via forward translation of Spanish captions using the NLLB-200 model. They apply meticulous language-specific preprocessing, including orthographic normalization and tokenizer adjustments, before fine-tuning the mBART model. Results show consistent improvements in chrF++ for Guarani (+2.00) and Quechua, while Aymara's absolute performance remains low despite a gain, highlighting morphological complexity as a persistent barrier.

## Method Summary
The method integrates three main steps: (1) **Synthetic data generation:** Forward translation of the MultiScript30k Spanish caption dataset into Aymara and Guarani using NLLB-200 to augment the small AmericasNLP 2023 parallel corpora. (2) **Language-specific preprocessing:** Conservative cleaning and deterministic orthographic normalization are applied to both authentic and synthetic data, tailored to each language's known artifacts (e.g., merging split digraphs in Quechua, normalizing Guarani digraphs). The Guarani tokenizer is explicitly extended with language-specific characters. (3) **Fine-tuning:** The `facebook/mbart-large-50` model is fine-tuned on the preprocessed and augmented training sets, with early stopping on development BLEU. Ablation experiments isolate the impact of synthetic data and preprocessing steps.

## Key Results
- For **Guarani–Spanish**, augmenting with synthetic data increases chrF++ from 42.00 to 44.00 (+2.00).
- For **Quechua–Spanish**, deterministic orthographic normalization yields substantial chrF++ gains by resolving systematic intra-word spacing artifacts.
- For **Aymara–Spanish**, synthetic data provides a +3.97 chrF++ gain, but absolute performance remains low (~30 chrF++), indicating that generic preprocessing is insufficient for highly agglutinative languages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Forward-translated synthetic data mitigates data scarcity by increasing training volume and linguistic diversity, leading to better model generalization.
**Mechanism:** NLLB-200 translates a large Spanish caption dataset into the target indigenous language. The resulting diverse synthetic pairs, despite potential noise, expose the fine-tuned mBART model to varied structures, regularizing learning and reducing overfitting on limited authentic data.
**Break condition:** If synthetic data introduces severe semantic distortions or domain mismatch, performance could degrade. Aymara's low absolute score suggests a break condition for highly agglutinative languages where synthetic morphology is poorly modeled.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Language-specific orthographic normalization reduces preprocessing noise and token fragmentation, improving character-level metrics.
**Mechanism:** Deterministic rules merge split morphemes or fix inconsistent spacing (e.g., Quechua's "ch aypiqa" → "chaypiqa"). This creates more consistent word forms, enabling subword tokenizers to produce linguistically meaningful units that boost character n-gram overlap.
**Break condition:** If removed "noise" is actually valid morphological marking or dialectal variation, normalization could erase meaningful information and harm translation quality.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Tokenizer extension with language-specific multi-character units preserves crucial phonological contrasts otherwise fragmented.
**Mechanism:** The base mBART tokenizer is extended with frequent Guarani digraphs (e.g., "ch", "mb") and nasal vowels. Resizing the embedding layer prevents these units from being split, maintaining their integrity within the multilingual representation space.
**Break condition:** If added tokens are too infrequent, they may remain unused or cause overfitting to surface forms.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Curated AmericasNLP parallel corpora + MultiScript30k Spanish captions -> NLLB-200 (forward translation) -> Language-specific preprocessing (normalization, filtering) -> mBART tokenizer (extended for Guarani) -> `facebook/mbart-large-50` (fine-tuned) -> chrF++ evaluation.
- **Critical path:** 1) Preprocess curated data per language. 2) Generate synthetic Aymara/Guarani data with NLLB-200 and filter. 3) Merge synthetic into training sets. 4) Extend tokenizer (Guarani) and resize embeddings. 5) Fine-tune mBART with early stopping. 6) Evaluate on dev set.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Synthetic Data Volume vs. Quality:* More data increases diversity but adds noise; the paper uses a fixed out-of-domain source for scalability.  
  *Generic vs. Language-Specific Preprocessing:* Aymara received a conservative pipeline to avoid over-normalization, trading off fixing known artifacts.  
  *Tokenizer Modification Cost:* Extending vocabulary increases model size slightly and may require more data to adapt new tokens; applied only to Guarani.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Low absolute chrF++* (e.g., Aymara ~30) indicates fundamental modeling limits (morphology, tokenization).  
  *Performance drop after preprocessing* suggests over-aggressive rules removing valid forms.  
  *High validation loss* during training signals synthetic data quality issues or hyperparameter mismatch.  
  *Excessive <unk> tokens* in target language text indicates insufficient vocabulary coverage.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1) **Baseline Replication:** Fine-tune mBART on curated-only data for Guarani and Aymara; record chrF++.  
  2) **Synthetic Augmentation Impact:** Repeat with curated + synthetic data; measure Δ chrF++.  
  3) **Preprocessing Ablation (Quechua):** Train on curated+augmented with and without orthographic normalization; compare scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does synthetic data augmentation consistently improve MT for all low-resource indigenous languages, or are there language-specific limits? The paper shows varied improvements, suggesting morphological complexity may moderate benefits, but the exact factors are not isolated.
2. What specific preprocessing aspects are most critical for morphologically rich indigenous languages, and can they be generalized? The paper uses different pipelines but does not ablate individual steps or test cross-language strategy transfer.
3. To what extent do automatic character-level metrics like chrF++ correlate with human assessments of translation quality for indigenous languages? The paper acknowledges chrF++'s limitations for agglutinative languages but provides no human evaluation data.
4. Can morpheme-aware preprocessing or segmentation mitigate subword tokenization fragmentation in highly agglutinative languages like Aymara? The authors propose this as a future direction without empirical comparison.
5. Does incorporating multimodal context (e.g., images from MultiScript30k) improve translation robustness for low-resource indigenous languages? This is suggested as future work without experimentation.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data filtering criteria are unspecified, leaving potential noise unquantified.
- Orthographic normalization rules are described only at a high level; exact transformation coverage is unknown.
- Evaluation relies exclusively on automatic chrF++, which may not capture semantic or cultural adequacy; no human evaluation is provided.
- Aymara's low absolute scores suggest the approach may not generalize to highly agglutinative languages, and tokenizer extensions were only tested for Guarani.

## Confidence
- **Synthetic data augmentation consistently improves translation quality:** Medium (supported for two languages, but noise and Aymara results unclear).
- **Language-specific preprocessing yields substantial gains:** Medium-Low (strong for Quechua, but rules underspecified and not generalized).
- **The combined approach is effective and scalable across indigenous languages:** Low (Aymara's low scores, lack of broader evidence, missing ablations).

## Next Checks
1. **Preprocessing fidelity:** Re-implement the exact orthographic normalization for each language and measure token-fragmentation metrics (e.g., subwords per token) before and after to confirm claimed reductions.
2. **Synthetic data quality:** Forward-translate MultiScript30k with NLLB-200, apply the paper's length-ratio filter, then manually evaluate a stratified sample of 200 pairs for semantic adequacy and fluency to estimate error rate.
3. **End-to-end replication:** Fine-tune mBART on curated-only and curated+augmented data for Guarani and Aymara using reported hyperparameters and proper token extension/tags; verify chrF++ deltas on the official dev set. Repeat the Quechua normalization ablation.
