# NOTAI.AI: Explainable Detection of Machine-Generated Text via Curvature and Feature Attribution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05617
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05617
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** On a balanced subset of the RAID benchmark, the ensemble achieves an F1 score of 0.963, outperforming individual components.

## Executive Summary
NOTAI.AI is an explainable system for detecting AI-generated text. It combines three families of features—Conditional Probability Curvature (a statistical curvature measure), a fine-tuned ModernBERT neural detector, and various stylometric metrics—within an XGBoost meta-classifier. Local explanations are generated via SHAP and translated into natural language by a large language model, making the reasoning accessible to non-experts. The system is deployed as an interactive web application. Evaluated on a balanced subset of the RAID benchmark, NOTAI.AI achieves an F1 score of 0.963, significantly surpassing any single-signal detector. Feature importance analysis identifies CPC, Type-Token Ratio, and the ModernBERT score as the most influential signals.

## Method Summary
NOTAI.AI employs a four-stage pipeline. First, it extracts 17 features from input text: a ModernBERT detection score, Conditional Probability Curvature (estimated using a proxy language model, GPT-Neo 1.3B), and a suite of readability and stylometric measures (e.g., Type-Token Ratio, hapax legomena, punctuation frequencies). Second, these features are fed into a supervised XGBoost classifier trained on a balanced subset of the RAID dataset (human-written texts equally represented with AI-generated ones). Third, SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) is applied to the trained model to compute per-instance feature attributions. Finally, an instruction-tuned LLM (Gemma-3-27b-it) is prompted with the SHAP values, raw features, and prediction to produce a concise natural-language rationale distinguishing evidence for AI vs. human authorship. The complete pipeline is served through a Flask-based web interface that also allows interactive feature ablation.

## Key Results
- F1 score of 0.963 on a balanced RAID test set, outperforming best single-feature detector (F1 0.888).
- Ensemble leverages complementary signals; top three most important features are CPC, Type-Token Ratio, and ModernBERT score.
- The framework provides local, instance-level explanations that are both technically grounded (SHAP) and user-friendly (LLM-generated summaries).
- Deployed as an interactive web application for real-time analysis and evidence inspection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Combining Orthogonal Feature Families**  
The detector integrates three distinct feature sources—neural (ModernBERT), statistical (CPC), and stylometric—within a supervised meta-classifier. Each source captures different artifacts of the generative process; for example, CPC measures the smoothness of the probability landscape under perturbations, while stylometry encodes surface-level lexical patterns. Because these signals are largely independent, the XGBoost ensemble can learn nonlinear interactions that improve robustness across diverse generation settings (e.g., varying sampling temperatures). Performance gains are evidenced by the ensemble's F1 (0.963) far exceeding any single-feature baseline (max 0.888). The mechanism fails if features become highly collinear or if one family dominates to the point others add no predictive value.

**Mechanism 2: LLM-Mediated Translation of SHAP Attributions**  
SHAP provides precise per-feature contributions, but the raw values are not directly accessible to non-experths. NOTAI.AI passes structured SHAP data (including top positive/negative features) to Gemma-3-27b-it with a carefully designed prompt, producing a natural-language summary that separates AI-supporting and human-supporting evidence. This two-layer approach preserves fidelity to the model’s decision logic while delivering an understandable rationale. The break condition occurs if the LLM hallucinates influences not present in SHAP, oversimplifies interactions, or generates generic text that does not reflect the specific instance.

**Mechanism 3: Non-Linear Interaction Modeling by XGBoost**  
The meta-classifier automatically captures high-order interactions among the 17 features. SHAP dependence plots reveal complex patterns: CPC exhibits a U-shaped effect (extreme negative values have little impact, values near zero push toward AI, positive values toward human); Type-Token Ratio shows a sigmoid-like threshold (~0.56) that flips the decision; ModernBERT score acts as a sharp confirmatory signal only at very high confidence. These learned boundaries allow the model to use features context-dependently, explaining the ensemble's superior performance. The mechanism relies on representative training data; if the RAID subset does not generalize (e.g., to new generators or domains), the intricate boundaries may fail.

## Foundational Learning
**Conditional Probability Curvature (CPC)**  
*Why needed:* CPC is the core statistical signal from Fast-DetectGPT. It measures the second derivative of the log-probability under perturbations—how sharply the likelihood changes. Negative CPC indicates a smooth, predictable probability landscape typical of AI-generated text.  
*Quick check:* If perturbing a text and recomputing its log-probability yields very similar scores across perturbations, what does that imply about its CPC value and likely origin?

**SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) for Tabular Data**  
*Why needed:* SHAP provides mathematically grounded, local feature attributions that sum to the difference from the baseline prediction. These values are the foundation for the LLM-generated rationales.  
*Quick check:* In a model output, if Feature A has a SHAP value of +0.3 and Feature B has -0.1 for a specific prediction, what does the net contribution of these two features imply for the final score relative to the baseline?

**Stylometric Features for Authorship Attribution**  
*Why needed:* Traditional forensic linguistics metrics (e.g., Type-Token Ratio, hapax legomena, punctuation patterns) capture surface-level regularity. AI text often shows unusual patterns (e.g., high lexical diversity without burstiness) that help discrimination.  
*Quick check:* Would you expect AI-generated text to have a higher or lower hapax legomena ratio than human-written text of comparable length, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Parser -> Feature Extraction Engine (parallel modules: ModernBERT inference, CPC calculator using GPT-Neo 1.3B, stylometric analyzer) -> Feature Aggregator -> Meta-Classifier (XGBoost) -> SHAP Explainer -> LLM Rationalizer (Gemma-3-27b-it) -> Web Interface (Flask/JS).  

**Critical path:** Input Text → Feature Extraction (slowest step) → XGBoost Predict → SHAP Compute → LLM Call → UI Render. Feature extraction and LLM call are the main latency bottlenecks.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Supervised vs. Zero-Shot:* The supervised ensemble uses labeled data for higher accuracy (F1 0.963) vs. zero-shot CPC-only methods (F1 ~0.888).  
- *Ensemble Complexity vs. Interpretability:* Combining 17 features boosts performance but obscures the global decision logic; mitigated by SHAP+LLM explanations.  
- *LLM Explanation Fidelity vs. Cost:* A 27B LLM produces fluent rationales but adds latency/cost; a smaller model or rules could be faster but less natural.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Feature Extraction Failure:* A sub-module returns NaN or times out; accuracy drops.  
- *SHAP-Model Mismatch:* Using a SHAP explainer fitted on a different XGBoost model version yields meaningless attributions.  
- *LLM Hallucination:* LLM output misrepresents SHAP data (e.g., unsupported claims).  
- *Domain Shift Degradation:* Performance plummets on out-of-distribution text (non-English, new LLMs).  

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity Check – Feature Extraction:** Run a known human and AI sample through the extraction pipeline; verify all 17 feature values are plausible and free of NaNs.  
2. **SHAP Consistency Test:** On a fixed test set, compute correlation between each feature's raw value and its SHAP value; ensure directionality matches expected patterns (e.g., positive CPC → negative SHAP for AI class).  
3. **LLM Explanation Validation:** Have human evaluators rate the accuracy of Gemma's summaries for 20 diverse samples against the SHAP evidence; target average rating >4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to natural, imbalanced, and shifted domains:** The evaluation used a balanced RAID subset; performance under real-world skew, unseen generators, or adversarial paraphrasing remains unknown. Cross-domain or full-dataset evaluations are needed.
2. **Utility of micro-level (token/phrase) explanations:** While the system provides feature-level rationales, it is unclear whether finer-grained localization would improve user understanding and trust. A user study comparing token-level heatmaps to the current approach is required.
3. **Extension to multilingual and human-AI co-authored text:** The framework was developed for English, fully human or AI texts. Its effectiveness on mixed authorship or other languages is an open question, requiring adaptation of features and training data.
4. **Impact of downsampling on reported performance:** The balanced subset discards most AI samples, potentially inflating F1. Evaluation on the original imbalanced RAID would reveal true robustness.
5. **Generative reasons for non-linear SHAP patterns:** The observed U-shape for CPC and threshold for TTR are described but not causally explained. Controlled generation experiments could determine whether these patterns reflect genuine human vs. machine differences or dataset artifacts.

## Limitations
- The assumed orthogonality of the three feature families (neural, CPC, stylometric) is not strongly validated; high collinearity could undermine the ensemble's rationale.
- The LLM-based explanation layer may produce hallucinations or oversimplifications; its fidelity to SHAP values has not been systematically measured.
- Evaluation on a curated balanced subset of RAID limits generalizability; performance under real-world distributional shifts and on the full imbalanced dataset remains uncertain.
