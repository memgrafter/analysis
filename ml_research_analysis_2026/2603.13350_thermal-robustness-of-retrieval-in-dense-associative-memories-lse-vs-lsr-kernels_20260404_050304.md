# Thermal Robustness of Retrieval in Dense Associative Memories: LSE vs LSR Kernels
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13350
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13350
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Both LSE and LSR kernels share zero-temperature critical load α_c(0)=0.5, but differ in finite-temperature robustness: LSR achieves perfect retrieval at any temperature below a load threshold α_th, while LSE sustains retrieval at high temperatures only at very low loads; at equal sharpness, LSR is substantially more thermally robust.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the thermal robustness of retrieval in dense associative memories (DAMs) with exponential capacity, focusing on two continuous spherical models: the log-sum-exp (LSE) and log-sum-ReLU (LSR) kernels. Using Monte Carlo simulations, the authors map the retrieval phase boundaries in temperature-load space. Both kernels have zero-temperature critical load α_c(0)=0.5. However, the LSE kernel can sustain retrieval at arbitrarily high temperatures for sufficiently low load, whereas the LSR kernel exhibits a sharp threshold α_th = (1 - b^{-1})^2/2 below which retrieval is perfect at any temperature. At equal sharpness β_net (i.e., b = Nβ_net), LSR demonstrates substantially greater thermal robustness than LSE due to its finite support that suppresses distant spurious patterns. Within the retrieval basin, the measured equilibrium alignment matches Boltzmann predictions.

## Method Summary
Monte Carlo simulations were performed using Metropolis-Hastings on the unit sphere. For each load α, the system size N was adapted to keep the number of stored patterns M = e^{αN} within a computationally feasible range (M∈[20k,500k]), with N decreasing linearly from ~990 at α=0.01 to ~24 at α=0.55. Random patterns were generated as uniformly distributed unit vectors. The state vector x was initialized near a target pattern with initial overlap φ_init∈[0.75,1]. For each temperature T, independent Markov chains were run: after an equilibration period (n_eq steps) proposals were made using x' = normalize(x + σ η) with η Gaussian and σ = 2.4T/√N, accepted with probability min(1, exp(-ΔH/T)). For the LSR kernel, proposals yielding overlap φ^μ < φ_c = 1-1/b for all patterns incur infinite energy and are rejected. After equilibration, the alignment φ = (x·ξ_1)/N was sampled over n_samp steps and averaged over trials (number of trials decreasing from 512 to 64 as α increases). The resulting average φ(α,T) was used to delineate the retrieval region.

## Key Results
- Both LSE and LSR kernels have zero-temperature critical load α_c(0)=0.5.
- LSE kernel: retrieval survives arbitrarily high temperatures at sufficiently low load; at higher loads, retrieval fails at moderate temperatures.
- LSR kernel: perfect retrieval at any temperature for loads below a threshold α_th = (1 - b^{-1})^2/2; for typical sharpness values, this threshold approaches α_c, yielding near-perfect retrieval across almost the entire load range.
- At equal sharpness β_net (b = Nβ_net), LSR shows substantially greater thermal robustness than LSE because its finite support completely suppresses contributions from distant spurious patterns.
- Within the retrieval basin, the measured equilibrium alignment φ_eq agrees with the analytical Boltzmann prediction across temperatures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The LSR kernel exhibits a sharp load threshold α_th = (1 - b^{-1})^2/2 below which retrieval is perfect at any temperature.  
- **Mechanism:** The Epanechnikov kernel has finite support, meaning only patterns with overlap φ_µ exceeding φ_c = 1 - 1/b contribute to the energy. For random patterns on the N‑sphere, overlaps are approximately Gaussian with variance 1/N. The expected number of stored patterns with φ_µ > φ_c is M * P(φ > φ_c). When this expected number is less than 1 (i.e., α < α_th), spurious local minima are absent and the retrieval basin remains unique regardless of temperature. Above α_th, spurious minima appear and thermal activation can cause retrieval failure.  
- **Core assumption:** Patterns are i.i.d. uniformly distributed on the sphere; Gaussian approximation holds for large N; threshold based on expectation of a Poisson count.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section Page 3: “Because the kernel has finite support, spurious patterns with φ_µ < φ_c do not contribute… expected number of patterns exceeding φ_c becomes appreciable only when α > α_th = (1−b^{-1})^2/2.”; Corpus: “Dense Associative Memory with Epanechnikov Energy” (arXiv:2506.10801) confirms LSR based on Epanechnikov kernel.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The LSE kernel can sustain retrieval at arbitrarily high temperatures for sufficiently low load α.  
- **Mechanism:** At low α, the pattern density on the sphere is low. The LSE kernel weights decay exponentially with squared distance, so only patterns with sufficiently high overlap actively compete with the target. With low pattern density, the probability of having spurious patterns close enough to create competing local minima is very small. Consequently, the retrieval basin remains the unique global minimum and is stable against thermal fluctuations, even when temperature is high enough to cause significant thermal broadening.  
- **Core assumption:** Patterns are random; energy landscape dominated by target; thermal fluctuations do not induce transitions because no other minima exist within thermal noise scale.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section Page 3: “At the low‑α end, the pattern density is too low to create a dense net of local minima, so the average alignment is governed by the proximity to the retrieval pattern. However, its value can be far from unity due to thermal broadening.”; Corpus: minimal additional.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** At equal sharpness β_net (b = Nβ_net), LSR demonstrates substantially greater thermal robustness than LSE.  
- **Mechanism:** The finite support of LSR eliminates contributions from distant patterns (φ_µ < φ_c). In LSE, every pattern contributes exp(-β_net d^2/2), which, while small for distant patterns, is non-zero. At moderate α, the exponential number of distant patterns produces a cumulative background in the energy landscape, acting as an effective noise floor that raises entropy and flattens the basin. LSR maintains a sharper well and thus a higher critical temperature for retrieval.  
- **Core assumption:** Cumulative effect of many small contributions in LSE creates measurable flattening; this is absent in LSR due to hard cutoff.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section Page 4: “When compared at equal β_net, i.e., b = N ≫ 1, the LSR kernel shows substantially greater thermal robustness than LSE, since its finite support suppresses contributions from distant spurious patterns entirely.”; Corpus: “Dense Associative Memory with Epanechnikov Energy” supports finite support benefits.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pattern generator -> patterns; State vector x (initialized near target); Energy function (LSE or LSR); Monte Carlo sampler (Metropolis-Hastings with renormalization); Measurement module (computes average φ); Adaptive-N controller (sets N based on α); Grid manager (batches (α,T) pairs).

**Critical path:**  
1. Choose kernel and parameters (β_net or b), define (α,T) grid.  
2. For each α, compute adaptive N(α) and generate M patterns.  
3. For each (α,T), run independent MC trials: initialize x, perform n_eq equilibration steps (with rejection for LSR outside support), then sample φ over n_samp steps.  
4. Average φ over trials.  
5. Plot phase diagram (retrieval region where φ near 1).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Larger N improves accuracy but increases memory; adaptive N reduces N at high α, risking finite-size artifacts.  
- Using all M patterns is exact but costly; pre-selecting active patterns (those with φ_µ > φ_c) for LSR reduces cost but may bias if state drifts.  
- MC parameters (n_eq, n_samp, σ) trade runtime for convergence; σ tuned to achieve reasonable acceptance.  
- Kernel sharpness: larger β_net (or b) sharpens basin and aids thermal robustness but may shrink basin size if too large.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Non-monotonic or unstable φ across MC runs → poor mixing.  
- φ lower than predicted φ_eq → insufficient equilibration or approximation error.  
- Phase boundary drifts with changes in N → adaptive-N artifacts.  
- Out-of-memory at high M → need chunking or pre-selection.  
- Failure to recover α_c(0)=0.5 at low T → bug in energy or overlap.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Zero-temperature capacity check: set T=0.025, sweep α from 0.3 to 0.55; expect retrieval failure near α=0.5.  
2. Phase boundary mapping: for β_net=1 (LSE) and b≈3.41 (LSR with typical N≈24), generate (α,T) grids and plot φ; verify LSE shows retrieval at high T for low α, while LSR shows sharp threshold near α_th.  
3. Boltzmann equilibrium validation: deep inside retrieval basin (e.g., α=0.05, T=1.0 for LSE; α=0.1 for LSR), compute theoretical φ_eq and compare with MC average; discrepancies indicate sampling issues.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What is the self-overlap q in the non-retrieval phase for LSE and LSR kernels, and how does it vary with T and α?** The paper only measures average alignment φ; measuring q would help characterize the paramagnetic, spin-glass, or mixed phases.
2. **Does the finite support approximation of the LSR kernel, by pre-selecting only patterns with overlap φ > φ_c, accurately approximate the full energy for states far from the retrieval basin?** The approximation might neglect contributions from patterns just below the threshold when the state drifts, potentially altering the energy landscape and dynamics.
3. **How do finite-size effects, due to the adaptive-N scheme reducing N at high load α, affect the reliability of the mapped phase boundaries?** The simulation uses a single N per α; finite-size scaling or fixed N across α would clarify whether boundaries shift with N.
4. **How does the regularization parameter ε in the LSR kernel influence the retrieval phase boundary and thermal robustness?** The value of ε is not varied; for distant patterns the kernel contribution is ε, which could affect the sum when M is large, altering the effective threshold α_th or introducing spurious minima.

## Limitations
- The adaptive-N scheme reduces N at high α, introducing finite-size artifacts that may shift phase boundaries.
- Unspecified simulation parameters (ε, σ, N(α) interpolation, trial counts) leave room for reproducibility issues and may affect results.
- Hard-wall rejections in the LSR kernel near the support threshold could impede mixing and bias the measured alignment.
