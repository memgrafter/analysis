# Self-Verification is All You Need To Pass The Japanese Bar Examination

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03144
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03144
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** A fine-tuned GPT-4.1 model with self-verification passes the Japanese bar exam’s original multiple-choice format, scoring 96/175 and exceeding the 93 passing threshold.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can pass the Japanese bar examination’s multiple-choice section while preserving its authentic, complex format—which requires joint evaluation of multiple statements per question and strict answer string constraints. The authors propose a self-verification approach: fine-tune a model on format-faithful data (without decomposing questions) and prompt it to first generate an answer, then verify it against the question using the same model. This method, applied to GPT-4.1, achieves an official exam scale score of 96/175, crossing the passing mark. In contrast, baselines using decomposed training or multi-agent architectures fail to reach 93, underscoring the importance of format-preserving supervision and self-verification for high-stakes legal reasoning.

## Method Summary
The task is to answer the Japanese bar exam’s multiple-choice questions in their original multi-proposition format (e.g., selecting combination options like “112”). The training data comprises 460 authentic questions from 2019–2023 (R1–R5), and testing uses the full 2024 exam (R6), totaling 175 points. The method involves fine-tuning GPT-4.1 on these questions without decomposition. During inference, a two-step self-verification is used: first, generate an initial answer with a strict prompt; second, use a verification prompt to compare the question and initial answer, outputting a revised answer only if clearly inconsistent. Baselines include zero/few-shot prompting, fine-tuning on a decomposed dataset (JBE-QA), and a multi-agent pipeline with specialized roles (Retriever, Verifier, etc.). Evaluation uses exact-match accuracy and the official partial credit scheme: for groups of *n* questions, one error yields *n-2* points; two or more errors yield 0; all correct yields *n*.

## Key Results
- The self-verification model achieves an official exam scale score of 96/175, surpassing the 93 passing threshold.
- With self-verification, average score is 94.7 and exact-match accuracy is 49.35%.
- This is the first demonstration of an LLM passing the exam without altering its original format.
- Baselines—including fine-tuning on decomposed data and multi-agent systems—fail to reach the passing score.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success hinges on two synergistic factors: (1) **Format-faithful supervision** during training, which teaches the model to handle the joint proposition constraints and strict answer formatting as a holistic unit, preserving the exam’s representational integrity. (2) **Self-verification** at inference, where the model leverages its own consistency-checking ability to catch and correct errors without external tools or decomposition. This simple mechanism avoids error propagation seen in multi-agent pipelines and induces a global consistency check that a single forward pass lacks. The model’s strong pretrained legal knowledge, combined with fine-tuning on authentic examples, allows it to recognize when its initial answer conflicts with the question’s logical requirements.

## Foundational Learning
- **Format-Faithful Supervision**  
  *Why needed:* Preserves the exam’s native multi-proposition structure; decomposing questions (as in JBE-QA) breaks joint constraint learning.  
  *Quick check:* Train on original-format data and test on original format; decomposed training should degrade performance.

- **Self-Verification**  
  *Why needed:* Adds a consistency check without architectural complexity; corrects reasoning slips that a single pass misses.  
  *Quick check:* Compare initial vs. final answer accuracy; improvement indicates effective verification.

- **Joint Proposition Constraints**  
  *Why needed:* The exam requires evaluating multiple statements together; error in one can affect others via partial credit rules.  
  *Quick check:* Measure accuracy on questions with high inter-statement dependency; format-faithful training should help.

- **Partial Credit Scheme**  
  *Why needed:* Official scoring is non-standard (n-2 rule); model must optimize for group-wise correctness, not just per-statement accuracy.  
  *Quick check:* Compute both exact-match and partial-credit scores; the latter aligns with passing criteria.

- **Consistency Checking**  
  *Why needed:* The verification step exploits the model’s ability to detect contradictions between question and answer.  
  *Quick check:* Analyze verification prompts; successful ones should highlight factual or logical mismatches.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Model -> Answer Generation -> Verification -> Final Answer

**Critical path:**  
1. Fine-tune GPT-4.1 on 460 authentic exam questions (2019–2023) with strict format prompts.  
2. For each test question:  
   a. Run answer-generation prompt to get initial answer.  
   b. Run verification prompt with question and initial answer to get final answer (revise only if clear inconsistency).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Single-model self-verification vs. multi-agent: Simpler, avoids error propagation, but relies on model’s internal consistency ability.  
- Format preservation vs. decomposition: Faithful format maintains representational integrity but requires handling complex output; decomposition simplifies training but hurts transfer.  
- Conservative verification: Prompt instructs to revise only if “clearly inconsistent”; too aggressive harms correctness, too passive wastes the check.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High rate of malformed outputs (e.g., extra text) indicates prompt insufficiency or model ignoring format constraints.  
- Final accuracy worse than initial suggests over-correction in verification.  
- Multi-agent pipeline performs poorly due to error propagation (e.g., noisy retrieval).  
- Overfitting on small training set (460 examples) leads to test degradation.

**Three first experiments:**  
1. **Scale ablation:** Replicate with smaller open-source models (e.g., 7B, 13B) to see if self-verification bridges capability gaps.  
2. **Verification ablation:** Compare performance with and without the verification step to measure its isolated contribution.  
3. **Format fidelity test:** Fine-tune on decomposed JBE-QA data and evaluate on original format; expect significant drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Free-response extension:** Can self-verification and format-faithful training work on the essay (論文式) portion, which requires structured legal argumentation and citations?  
2. **Decomposition failure cause:** Why does training on decomposed data (JBE-QA) fail on the original format—due to lost joint constraint representations or inability to re-compose at inference?  
3. **Model scale dependence:** Does self-verification enable smaller, weaker models to pass, or is it only effective with strong pretrained knowledge like GPT-4.1?  
4. **Multi-agent failure cause:** Why do multi-agent architectures underperform a single fine-tuned model—due to error propagation, representational mismatch, or retrieval disruption?  
5. **Verification mechanism:** Does self-verification primarily correct reasoning errors, enforce format compliance, or both? And does it sometimes introduce new errors?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameters unreported:** Fine-tuning details (learning rate, epochs, batch size) are absent, risking deviation in replication.  
- **Model version ambiguity:** “GPT-4.1” is a future API model; its availability and exact capabilities are unknown, preventing direct replication with current models.  
- **Evaluation specifics missing:** Grouping logic for partial credit calculation is not described; without correct metadata, official score cannot be faithfully verified.

## Confidence
- **Self-verification enables passing the exam:** Medium (results tied to unreleased model and unclear training details).  
- **Decomposed training and multi-agent systems fail:** Low (baseline implementations under-specified; failure causes not isolated).  
- **Format-faithful supervision is critical:** Medium (core contrast plausible but depends on experimental parity).
