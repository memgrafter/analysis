# Text Before Vision: Staged Knowledge Injection Matters for Agentic RLVR in Ultra-High-Resolution Remote Sensing Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14225  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14225  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** Staged knowledge injection (text-only cold-start SFT + VQA pre-warming) achieves 60.40% Pass@1 and 96.25% Pass@32 on XLRS-Bench, outperforming larger general-purpose models.

## Executive Summary
Ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing understanding requires multimodal models to localize tiny task-relevant regions within massive image spaces. Standard reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) using zoom-in tools can address this, but suffers from instability due to the vast visual action space and sparse rewards. The authors propose a staged knowledge injection recipe: first, cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with Earth-science text-only QA, automatically generated from textbooks and verified via a knowledge graph, to instill domain concepts and reasoning structures; second, pre-warming SFT on the same challenging UHR image-text examples that will later appear in RL, to stabilize the policy before agentic RLVR. This two-stage preparation equips the model with spatial and task priors that make tool-based exploration effective.

On the XLRS-Bench benchmark, the approach (using QwenVL2.5 as base) achieves 60.40% Pass@1 and 96.25% Pass@32, significantly surpassing larger general-purpose models like GPT-5.2 and Intern-S1. Ablations reveal that text-only cold-start primarily boosts the reasoning boundary (Pass@32), while combining it with VQA data in cold-start further improves average performance (Pass@1) and stabilizes gains. The results demonstrate that structured domain priors are essential for successful agentic RLVR in UHR RS, and that a “text-before-vision” curriculum unlocks effective tool use.

## Method Summary
The method builds on a base multimodal language model (QwenVL2.5) and applies a three-stage training pipeline over domain-specific and general data.  

**Stage 1:** Cold-start SFT for one epoch on a large corpus of 148,777 Earth-science text-only QA pairs (with chain-of-thought reasoning) generated from textbooks and papers, optionally combined with the domain UHR VQA dataset SuperRS-VQA. This stage injects domain knowledge and reasoning structures without visual complexity.  

**Stage 2:** Pre-warming SFT on the same SuperRS-VQA examples to bootstrap spatial and task representations.  

**Stage 3:** Agentic RLVR using the GRPO algorithm, where the model interleaves textual reasoning with actions of a zoom-in tool (selecting image regions to crop and inspect). The RL training mixes the general DeepEyes-47K dataset and domain SuperRS-VQA. The reward combines a binary correctness signal, a format compliance bonus, and a tool bonus of 1.0 only if the final answer is correct; this conditional design prevents reward hacking when the tool is misused. The staged regimen ensures that by the time RL begins, the model already possesses strong domain priors and a basic policy for tool use, turning RL into fine-grained refinement rather than blind exploration.

## Key Results
- The staged approach yields state-of-the-art results on XLRS-Bench: **60.40% Pass@1** and **96.25% Pass@32**, beating larger models such as GPT-5.2 and Intern-S1.  
- Cold-start SFT with Earth-science text-only QA substantially raises the reasoning boundary (**Pass@32**), demonstrating that textual domain knowledge alone guides visual evidence retrieval.  
- Combining text-only and VQA data in cold-start SFT stabilizes training and improves **Pass@1**, indicating synergy between conceptual priors and visual grounding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Text-only cold-start injects domain priors that guide visual search.
Despite lacking images, high-quality Earth-science QA provides dense domain concepts, mechanistic explanations, and decision rules. These align with the model's pretrained language–vision interface and reduce the exploration space for zoom-in actions by pre-instilling “where to look” and “how to reason” heuristics. **Evidence:** The abstract states text-only QA is a primary driver of UHR visual reasoning gains; Section 3.3 shows text-only cold-start yields larger Pass@32 gains than image–text VQA; and ablations confirm steady Pass@1 improvement as text data scales. **Break condition:** If the model's language–vision alignment were degraded, transfer would fail.

### Mechanism 2: Pre-warming on hard UHR VQA stabilizes subsequent RL.
SFT on the same challenging examples that will appear in RL creates an initial policy that encodes basic spatial patterns and task-specific zoom-in heuristics. This bootstraps the policy so that RL performs refinement rather than blind exploration in the massive pixel space, avoiding reward sparsity and instability. **Evidence:** Section 3.2 and Fig. 3 show that SFT+RL on the same data outperforms either alone; the hard-example pre-warming strategy explicitly reuses SuperRS-VQA in both stages. **Break condition:** If the UHR VQA data is too diverse or lacks consistent patterns, SFT on a subset may not generalize to RL samples.

### Mechanism 3: Agentic RLVR requires domain priors to be effective and stable.
Without structured priors from text cold-start and VQA pre-warming, RL must explore a vast, sparse visual action space with only a binary correctness signal, leading to instability or performance collapse. The priors provide a high-quality initial policy and dense reward within the tool-augmented space. **Evidence:** Table 1 shows GRPO with tools but without domain data yields only marginal gains (Pass@1=50.01 vs. SFT-only 48.26), while with domain data it reaches 60.40; Section 3.2 demonstrates that adding VQA only in RL (without pre-warming) yields unstable gains. **Break condition:** If the reward were dense or action space drastically reduced, RL might succeed without priors.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Data Pipeline: Textbook/paper corpus → cleaning/normalization → QA/CoT generation → knowledge graph verification → Earth-science text QA corpus.  
Training Pipeline: Base MLLM (QwenVL2.5) → Cold-start SFT (Earth-science text QA ± SuperRS-VQA) → Pre-warming SFT (SuperRS-VQA) → Agentic RLVR (GRPO + zoom-in tool, mixing DeepEyes-47K and SuperRS-VQA).

**Critical path**  
Data construction must precede cold-start SFT. Cold-start SFT must precede pre-warming SFT. Pre-warming SFT must precede Agentic RLVR. Deviating (e.g., RL before SFT) leads to instability.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Automation vs. Noise: Fully automated textbook/paper parsing scales but risks hallucinations; knowledge graph filtering mitigates but may reduce dataset size.  
- Text vs. VQA Emphasis: Text-only cold-start maximizes reasoning boundary (Pass@32) but initial Pass@1 gains are smaller than text+VQA; combining them yields best of both but increases training cost.  
- RL Data Composition: Using general-domain RL data (DeepEyes-47K) alongside domain VQA stabilizes training but may dilute domain-specific signal if not balanced.

**Failure signatures**  
- Pass@1 collapses after RL: Indicates RL stage without adequate prior (missing text cold-start or pre-warming), leading to overfitting or reward hacking.  
- Pass@32 remains low despite scaling text data: Suggests knowledge graph filtering is too aggressive or base model's language–vision alignment insufficient for transfer.  
- Tool use becomes degenerate: Model either never uses zoom-in or uses it randomly, indicating SFT failed to instill tool-conditioned policies.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Ablation 1:** Compare cold-start SFT with Earth-science text QA only vs. SuperRS-VQA only vs. both. Hypothesis: text-only yields higher Pass@32; combination yields highest Pass@1.  
2. **Ablation 2:** Compare introducing SuperRS-VQA in cold-start SFT only, RL only, both (pre-warming), or neither. Hypothesis: pre-warming (both) yields most stable Pass@1 gains.  
3. **Scaling experiment:** Vary volume of text QA used in cold-start SFT while keeping RL fixed. Hypothesis: Pass@1 increases linearly; Pass@32 plateaus as boundary saturates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** What are the optimal proportions and sequential ordering of Earth-science text-only QA versus UHR image-text VQA during cold-start SFT to maximize both pass@1 and pass@32? The paper used a single combination without exploring mixing ratios or schedules, so the precise configuration that balances reasoning structure and visual grounding is unknown. Controlled experiments varying text fraction and ordering are needed.

**Open Question 2:** How does the structural granularity and specificity of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in Earth-science text QA mediate its transfer to UHR visual evidence acquisition? The paper attributes gains to “procedural scaffolding” from CoT but did not systematically vary CoT structure or granularity. It is unclear which aspects (step count, mechanistic depth, spatial explicitness) are critical. Ablations generating text QA with controlled CoT variations and measuring impact on zoom-in trajectories would resolve this.

**Open Question 3:** What specific categories of “long-tail” Earth-science domain concepts and discriminative rules are missing from current datasets that limit further expansion of the reasoning boundary (pass@32) in UHR RS? The paper speculates pass@32 plateaus and likely requires additional long-tail concepts beyond their text-VQA corpus, but provides no analysis of failure cases to identify these gaps. Error analysis clustering incorrect trajectories by task type and targeted data augmentation could resolve this.

**Open Question 4:** Does the “Text-Before-Vision” staged injection recipe generalize to agentic RL setups with tools other than zoom-in (e.g., segmentation masks, spectral calculators) or different RL algorithms (e.g., PPO vs. GRPO variants)? All experiments use the same tool-augmented RL baseline (DeepEyes with GRPO), so robustness to alternative tool environments or RL implementations is unknown. Replication with other tools and algorithms would test generalizability.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (SFT learning rate/batch size, GRPO settings) are unreported, hindering exact replication and potentially causing unstable training.  
- Earth-science text QA pipeline details (textbook/paper sources, LightRAG thresholds, generation prompts) are vague, threatening data quality, domain coverage, and reproducibility.  
- Zoom-in tool implementation specifics (tokenization, action space, max iterations) are unspecified, impairing integration with agentic RL and possibly affecting performance.

## Confidence
- Text-only cold-start improves reasoning boundary: Medium  
- Pre-warming on hard VQA stabilizes RL: Medium  
- RL requires domain priors to succeed: Medium  
- Foundational concepts’ causal roles: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Alignment probe:** Evaluate zero-shot VQA on a held-out Earth-science dataset before/after text-only SFT; a significant accuracy gain would support transfer of textual domain knowledge to visual reasoning.  
2. **Tool usage audit:** During RL rollouts, log zoom-in invocation frequency; pre-warmed models should show consistent (>30%) tool use, while models without pre-warming show random or absent calls.  
3. **Data pipeline ablation:** Rebuild the Earth-science text QA corpus without knowledge graph verification; a marked Pass@1 drop would confirm the knowledge graph’s role in ensuring domain fidelity.
