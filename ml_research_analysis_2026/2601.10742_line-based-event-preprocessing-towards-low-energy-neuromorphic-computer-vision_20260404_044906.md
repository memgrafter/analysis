# Line-based Event Preprocessing: Towards Low-Energy Neuromorphic Computer Vision
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.10742
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10742
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Line-based event preprocessing reduces theoretical energy consumption in neuromorphic computer vision by extracting sparse features via an SNN line detector, maintaining or improving classification accuracy while significantly cutting synaptic operations.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a bio-inspired line detection module as preprocessing for event-based neuromorphic vision. By converting raw event streams into sparse representations of line intersections, the approach reduces the number of synaptic operations in the downstream classifier, which is used as a proxy for energy consumption. Evaluated on three benchmark datasets (PokerDVS, N-MNIST, DVS128 Gesture), the method achieves favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-offs. Notably, on the complex DVS128 Gesture dataset, optimal parameters yield up to 7× improvement in energy efficiency (accuracy per synaptic event), and cumulative preprocessing strategies increase accuracy by 9 percentage points while reducing synaptic events by over 80%. The work demonstrates that carefully designed event preprocessing can lead to substantial energy savings in neuromorphic systems without sacrificing performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed system consists of a preprocessing layer implementing an SNN-based line detector followed by a linear classifier. The line detector receives events from a dynamic vision sensor (DVS) and uses diagonally spaced synaptic connections (step k) to detect line intersections within a specified sensor region (e.g., central quarter, cross). A Winner-Takes-All mechanism within each detector population ensures sparse output. The classifier is a fully-connected layer of LIF neurons trained via Backpropagation Through Time to read out the preprocessed spike trains. The energy efficiency is evaluated using the total number of synaptic events (SE) during inference as a theoretical proxy for energy consumption. Hyperparameters (k, synaptic strength ω) are tuned to optimize either accuracy or efficiency, and cumulative strategies that combine multiple detector regions are explored. Experiments on three datasets compare accuracy, SE, and efficiency (A/SE) against a baseline without preprocessing.

## Key Results
- The preprocessing approach reduces synaptic events while maintaining or improving classification accuracy across all three datasets.
- On DVS128 Gesture, optimal parameter selection yields up to 7× higher energy efficiency (accuracy per synaptic event) compared to no preprocessing.
- Cumulative preprocessing strategies (combining multiple detector regions) increase accuracy by 9 percentage points and reduce synaptic events by over 80% on DVS128 Gesture.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Event Preprocessing Reduces Synaptic Operations
- **Claim**: Event preprocessing reduces the number of synaptic operations (SE) in the downstream classifier, lowering theoretical energy consumption.
- **Mechanism**: A bio-inspired SNN line detection module processes the raw event stream, producing a sparse event-based representation (line intersections) instead of the original dense event flow. This smaller set of preprocessed events is fed to the classifier, causing fewer total synaptic activations (SE = SE_P(k) + SE_C).
- **Core assumption**: Neuromorphic hardware energy is dominated by synaptic event processing; reducing SE proportionally reduces energy.
- **Evidence**: Abstract states preprocessing reduces theoretical energy consumption; Section 2.3 approximates energy by SE; Section 3 notes reducing synaptic operations is key for low energy.
- **Break condition**: If preprocessing introduces more synaptic events than it saves (SE_P(k) > reduction in SE_C), total energy increases. This occurs with poor hyperparameters (e.g., low k in "whole sensor" strategy).

### Mechanism 2: Diagonal Synaptic Pattern as Spatial Filter
- **Claim**: The line detection synaptic pattern (k-spaced diagonals) acts as a spatial filter, extracting stable geometric primitives (line intersections) while discarding noise.
- **Mechanism**: Input neurons on specific diagonals connect to detector neurons. Only events along these diagonals contribute to detector activation. Tuning k controls spatial density, directly altering sparsity of preprocessed output.
- **Core assumption**: Relevant discriminative information for the tasks is encoded in line-like structures formed by event clusters.
- **Evidence**: Section 2.1 describes diagonal connections at every k positions and Winner-Takes-All; Section 4.1 (Fig. 6) shows higher k reduces SE; Section 4.2 shows drastic SE reduction vs. standard layers.
- **Break condition**: If k is too high, pattern becomes too sparse to capture meaningful lines, causing accuracy collapse.

### Mechanism 3: Cumulative Strategies Enhance Feature Diversity
- **Claim**: Cumulative preprocessing strategies combine complementary spatial receptive fields, improving accuracy beyond baseline while maintaining high efficiency.
- **Mechanism**: Different strategies monitor different sensor regions (center, corners, cross). Their output event streams are summed before the classifier, providing a richer representation.
- **Core assumption**: No single strategy captures all relevant linear features for complex datasets like DVS128 Gesture.
- **Evidence**: Section 5.2 reports 9-point accuracy increase on DVS128 Gesture; Fig. 10 shows cumulative strategies achieve higher accuracy and efficiency; Section 6 notes 6-9 point accuracy gains.
- **Break condition**: If added synaptic events from combined preprocessors outweigh accuracy gain, efficiency degrades.

- **Why needed**: Input data is asynchronous events (x, y, polarity, timestamp). The system handles this sparse temporal stream directly.

- **Why needed**: The core metric is E = A/SE, where SE proxies energy. This model must be understood to interpret trade-offs.

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
1. Input Interface: Encodes AER events from DVS camera. For split polarities, splits into two parallel populations.
2. Preprocessing Layer: 4, 8, or 16 LIF neuronal populations (detectors) depending on strategy. Each receives inputs via fixed k-spaced diagonal pattern from its assigned sensor region. Includes intra-detector Winner-Takes-All inhibition.
3. Classifier: Single fully-connected linear layer of N_labels LIF neurons. Receives all spikes from active detector neurons.
4. Output/Readout: Rate-based decoding; label neuron with most spikes during sample window is prediction.
5. Hyperparameter Controller: Sets k (diagonal step) and ω (synaptic weight strength).

#### Critical Path
Raw event stream → Encoding/Splitting → Preprocessing Layer (Diagonal summation + WTA inhibition) → Sparse detector spike train → Fully-connected classifier → Label spike counts → Inference decision.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- k (Diagonal Step): High k = fewer synapses/events (lower energy) but coarser spatial sampling (risk accuracy loss). Low k = finer sampling but higher energy. Optimal k dataset-dependent.
- Polarity Handling: Merged = half input neurons/synapses (lower energy) but loses polarity-specific patterns. Split = preserves more information (higher accuracy) at doubled synaptic cost.
- Strategy (Region): Central quarter = fewest synapses but discards periphery. Cross/Corner = more synapses but captures more context. Choice depends on event clustering.
- Parametrisation Goal: Best accuracy (tune k, ω for max A) vs. Best efficiency (tune for max A/SE).

#### Failure Signatures
- Accuracy collapse (A ≈ 1/N_labels): Usually from k too high or ω too low. Check detector output rasters.
- No efficiency gain (SE ≈ SE_no_preprocess): From k=1 (too many synapses) or using Whole sensor strategy (detectors rarely fire but classifier still fully connected).
- High energy, low accuracy: Common with Whole sensor strategy; requires high ω to fire, produces many events, but spatial pattern poorly matched to centrally-clustered data.
- Training instability: Classifier fails if preprocessing output too sparse or too bursty. Monitor preprocessed event rate per sample.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. Hyperparameter Sweep (PokerDVS): For each strategy and polarity, train classifier across grid of k and ω. Plot accuracy vs. efficiency. Identify Pareto fronts. Verify >50% of (k, ω) pairs for non-trivial strategies beat baseline in efficiency while satisfying A > A_T.
2. Architecture Scalability Analysis: For fixed (k, ω), compute total neurons and incoming synapses per neuron for each strategy as sensor size scales. Plot as in Fig. 4. Confirm all strategies drastically reduce incoming synapses per neuron vs. fully-connected baseline.
3. Cumulative Strategy Test (DVS128 Gesture): Combine top 2-3 individual strategies by summing detector outputs. Train and evaluate with best accuracy parametrisation. Replicate finding that cumulative strategies can increase accuracy and efficiency over single-strategy best.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scaling to larger datasets/tasks?** Future work mentioned; not evaluated for segmentation or high-resolution data (Prophesee 1 MP). Needs implementation on such tasks.
- **Simultaneous accuracy/efficiency improvement replicable?** Observed only on DVS128 Gesture; need to test on other complex neuromorphic tasks (e.g., detection, action recognition).
- **Impact of additional neuronal/synaptic parameters?** Only k and ω varied; other parameters (membrane time constant, refractory period, inhibitory weights, delays) fixed. Requires systematic sensitivity analysis.
- **Theoretical energy saving valid on real hardware?** Simulated only; need deployment on SpiNNaker/FPGA to measure actual power and latency.

## Limitations
- Line detector implementation fidelity: exact connectivity pattern and border-handling inferred from prior work, not fully specified, risking reproducibility.
- Energy model validity: assumption that synaptic events directly proxy hardware energy may not hold due to additional costs (memory access, routing, idle power).
- Generalization: results shown for single-layer linear classifier; unknown if benefits persist with deeper SNNs or different neuron models.

## Confidence
- Efficiency-Accuracy Trade-off: High
- Line Detection as Sparse Filter: Medium
- Cumulative Strategy Benefit: Low-Medium
