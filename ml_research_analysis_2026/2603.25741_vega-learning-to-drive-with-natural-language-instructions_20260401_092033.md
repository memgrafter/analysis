# Vega: Learning to Drive with Natural Language Instructions
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25741
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25741
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Vega achieves state-of-the-art planning on NA VSIM (86.9 EPDMS v2, 87.9 PDMS v1) with strong instruction-following via joint image-action generation.

## Executive Summary
Vega addresses the lack of flexible instruction-following in vision-language-action models for autonomous driving. It introduces a unified model that jointly predicts future images and actions conditioned on natural language instructions. The authors build the InstructScene dataset with ~100k annotated scenes and propose an architecture combining autoregressive and diffusion paradigms. On NA VSIM benchmarks, Vega sets new records, demonstrating superior planning and visual outcome generation.

## Method Summary
Vega is a vision-language-world-action model that integrates an autoregressive Transformer for sequence modeling and a diffusion branch for generative prediction. Given past camera images, actions, and a natural language instruction, it assembles a sequence of visual and action tokens, then uses a Mixture-of-Transformers to enable joint attention across modalities. The model is trained to denoise both future images and actions simultaneously, using a weighted loss. Classifier-free guidance aids instruction adherence at inference. The InstructScene dataset provides the necessary triplet (image, instruction, trajectory) and future image targets.

## Key Results
- Achieved 86.9 EPDMS on NA VSIM v2 (89.4 with best-of-N).
- Achieved 87.9 PDMS on NA VSIM v1 (89.8 with best-of-N).
- Demonstrated robust instruction-following in both trajectory prediction and visual ground truth generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Vega's mechanism hinges on joint modeling of future states and actions within a unified Transformer. The autoregressive branch processes the history and instruction to capture contextual dependencies, while the diffusion branch generates plausible futures by iteratively denoising. By sharing attention across modalities via Mixture-of-Transformers, the model aligns language instructions with visual and action representations. The dual prediction tasks provide complementary supervision: images offer dense perceptual guidance, actions provide kinematic constraints. Training with classifier-free guidance dropout encourages learning of instruction-specific features, enhancing controllability.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Past images, past actions, instruction -> Integrated Transformer (autoregressive + diffusion branches) -> Future images, actions
Critical path: 1) Tokenize and encode inputs (images via VAE/SigLIP, text via Qwen tokenizer, actions as continuous). 2) Interleave past image and action tokens, append instruction tokens. 3) Duplicate latent tokens for clean (condition) and noisy (prediction) copies. 4) Apply blocked lower-triangular attention masks across modalities. 5) Predict noise for future image and action tokens via diffusion loss.
Design tradeoffs: Autoregressive vs. diffusion parallelism; duplicate tokens increase memory but separate conditioning; joint loss weighting balances image and action learning.
Failure signatures: Diverging loss (incorrect masking/scaling), low PDMS (missing CFG or misaligned instructions), poor image generation (wrong VAE or noise schedule).
First three experiments:
1. Train on a small subset (e.g., 1k scenes) to verify loss decreases and basic generation quality.
2. Ablate CFG by evaluating with and without guidance to measure instruction-following impact.
3. Run NAVSIM planning-only evaluation on a few scenes to compute PDMS manually and compare to paper.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Are the NA VSIM v1 and v2 metrics sufficient for evaluating instruction-following capabilities, or do they bias against certain driving strategies?  
   Basis: Authors note NA VSIM v1's unbalanced metrics favor risk-averse policies, and Vega's lower relative performance may stem from this bias.  
   Why unresolved: Misaligned metrics may not reflect compliance with diverse instructions, potentially misleading research.  
   Evidence needed: New metrics that directly quantify instruction adherence across behaviors, then re-evaluate models.

2. Does the InstructScene dataset's automatic annotation introduce biases that limit diversity and real-world relevance?  
   Basis: Instructions are generated via VLM and rule-based methods, which may inherit biases and reduce coverage of nuanced scenarios.  
   Why unresolved: Biased data could hinder generalization to unseen instructions or environments.  
   Evidence needed: Analyze instruction diversity (linguistic complexity, scenario types) and test on human-annotated sets to measure performance drops.

3. How well does Vega generalize to ambiguous, complex, or out-of-distribution instructions?  
   Basis: Paper shows qualitative examples but lacks systematic evaluation of challenging instructions, assuming dataset coverage suffices.  
   Why unresolved: Real-world users issue varied unclear commands; without testing, practical reliability is uncertain.  
   Evidence needed: Held-out test set with intentionally diverse/complex instructions from humans, quantify accuracy, safety, compliance.

4. What is the specific contribution of future image generation to action planning, and could alternative world models improve supervision?  
   Basis: Ablations show minimal performance difference across future frame choices, suggesting joint modeling—not image fidelity—may be key.  
   Why unresolved: Unclear if image generation is necessary or optimal; other signals like segmentation or occupancy might help.  
   Evidence needed: Ablate image generation entirely or replace with different prediction targets (e.g., BEV maps, object motions) and compare planning metrics.

## Limitations
- Dataset claims (InstructScene size/diversity) have medium confidence due to incomplete documentation of annotation prompts and rule thresholds.
- Model architecture & training details (e.g., transformer layers, diffusion schedule) have low confidence, hindering exact reproduction.
- Evaluation results are high confidence but depend on precise NAVSIM setup; deviations may affect reported scores.

## Confidence
- Evaluation results (PDMS/EPDMS numbers): High
- Dataset claims (InstructScene annotation quality/diversity): Medium
- Model architecture & training specifics (e.g., MoT pattern, noise schedule): Low
