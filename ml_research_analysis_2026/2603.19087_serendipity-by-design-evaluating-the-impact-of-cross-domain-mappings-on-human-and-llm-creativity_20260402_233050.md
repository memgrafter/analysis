# Serendipity by Design: Evaluating the Impact of Cross-domain Mappings on Human and LLM Creativity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19087  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19087  
- **Reference count:** 4  
- **Primary result:** Cross-domain mapping boosts human originality but not LLM originality, though LLMs generate more original ideas than humans in both conditions; semantic distance predicts creativity for both.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether prompting for analogical transfer from random, remote source domains enhances creative idea generation. Human participants and seven large language models (LLMs) generated novel product features under two prompt conditions: a cross-domain mapping instruction (translate a property from a randomly assigned source) and a user-need prompt (identify an unmet need). Humans produced significantly more original ideas when given cross-domain prompts, while LLMs showed no overall prompt effect. However, across both conditions, all seven LLMs generated ideas rated as more original than human ideas. Furthermore, for both humans and LLMs, the semantic distance between the target product and the inspiration source—computed via Wikipedia embeddings—positively predicted originality, suggesting that remote association is a general creativity lever.

## Method Summary
The experiment used 10 daily products (e.g., backpack, smartphone) and 26 source domains (e.g., cactus, symphony orchestra). For each of 100 unique target–source pairs, humans (N=140) and seven LLMs generated ideas under two conditions: cross-domain mapping (describe a source property then translate it to the target) and user-need (identify an unmet need then propose a solution). Human participants generated 7 ideas per pair in the cross-domain condition and 70 per product in the user-need condition; LLMs generated 1 idea per pair for cross-domain and 10 per product for user-need. All raw outputs were rephrased by a fixed LLM (o3) into uniform 1–2 sentence product descriptions to remove framing effects. Independent human raters (N≈1002) evaluated each rephrased idea on originality, feasibility, usefulness, and investment worthiness (5-point Likert). Analyses used linear mixed-effects models with random intercepts for participants and ideas, and semantic distance was calculated via sentence-transformer embeddings on Wikipedia text.

## Key Results
- Cross-domain mapping significantly increased originality of human-generated ideas compared to user-need prompts (p<.01), while LLMs showed no statistically significant prompt effect overall.
- Across both conditions, all seven LLMs (including o3, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4) produced ideas rated as more original than human ideas (all p<.001 in user-need condition).
- Greater semantic distance between target and source predicted higher originality for both humans (β=.1, p<.01) and LLMs (β=.18, p<.001).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Cross-domain mapping increases human creative originality by forcing a semantic leap that overcomes cognitive fixation on familiar, domain-specific solutions.  
**Mechanism:** The intervention imposes an explicit analogical structure-mapping process (Gentner, 1983), requiring participants to first articulate a property of a remote source and then translate it to the target. This procedural constraint disrupts the default retrieval of close associates and compels engagement with a distant semantic neighborhood.  
**Core assumption:** Humans suffer from "knowledge fragmentation" and "steep associative hierarchies" that bias retrieval toward conventional, low-distance ideas unless externally prompted to scan broader memory.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract]: "humans reliably benefit from randomly assigned cross-domain mappings, while LLMs, on average, generate more original ideas than humans and do not show a statistically significant effect of cross-domain mappings."  
- [page 3, "Eliciting Novel Ideas"]: Detailed protocol where humans first "described a property of the source" before proposing the feature, explicitly instantiating the mapping process.  
**Break condition:** If the randomly assigned source domain shares salient perceptual features with the target (low semantic distance) or if the participant lacks accessible knowledge about the source, the forced mapping may yield trivial or infeasible translations that do not enhance originality.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Higher semantic distance between the target product and inspiration source predicts greater originality for both humans and LLMs, indicating that remote association is a general driver of creative ideation.  
**Mechanism:** Concepts represented further apart in a high-dimensional embedding space are less likely to have strongly co-occurred in training data or memory. Bridging them requires accessing more idiosyncratic or latent properties of each concept, which can recombine in novel ways.  
**Core assumption:** Semantic distance in a static embedding space is a valid proxy for the "remoteness" of an association that challenges conventional thinking.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [page 4, Results]: "combinations that paired more semantically distant concepts tended to receive higher originality ratings (human β=.1, t=2.721, p < .01; LLMs β=.18, t=4.81, p < .001)."  
- [page 5, Figure 3(b)]: Scatterplot showing positive relationship between semantic distance and mean originality.  
**Break condition:** If semantic distance becomes too extreme (e.g., abstract concept vs. concrete product), the mapping may fail to produce a coherent or interpretable feature, potentially reducing perceived originality or feasibility.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** LLMs generate more original ideas than humans on average, even under a user-need prompt, because their architecture inherently accesses a broader, flatter associative hierarchy without needing an explicit cross-domain intervention.  
**Mechanism:** Training on a massive, cross-disciplinary corpus allows LLMs to sample from a vastly larger and more diverse set of token co-occurrences than human experiential memory. This reduces "fixation" on local, high-frequency associations by default.  
**Core assumption:** The breadth of LLM training data directly translates to a flatter, more "remote" base distribution of generated tokens compared to human semantic memory, which is shaped by embodied experience and cultural priors.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [page 3, "LLM idea generation"]: LLMs were prompted identically under both conditions, with no additional constraint in the user-need condition.  
- [page 4, Results]: "Across both conditions, LLMs generated ideas were rated as more original than human generations." Also: "in the user-need condition... all LLMs were rated as more original than humans."  
**Break condition:** For highly specialized or esoteric target domains where LLM training data is sparse or noisy, the baseline associativity may degrade, and the advantage over humans could diminish. Human expert knowledge might then dominate in originality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Associative Hierarchies (Mednick, 1962)  
  **Why needed:** Explains the core theoretical reason cross-domain mapping should work for humans (flattening steep hierarchies). Without this, the "fixation" problem being addressed is undefined.  
  **Quick check:** If a person's associative hierarchy for "chair" immediately generates "table, legs, seat" before "crystal, vibration, symphony," what does that imply about their creative potential for furniture design?

- **Concept:** Structure-Mapping Theory (Gentner, 1983)  
  **Why needed:** Provides the cognitive mechanism for *how* a cross-domain mapping transfers useful information (aligning relational structure, not just attributes). The paper's human protocol explicitly instantiates this.  
  **Quick check:** Why is mapping the *relation* "holds weight" from "column" to "leg of a chair" more generative than mapping the attribute "made of stone"?

- **Concept:** Semantic Distance via Embeddings  
  **Why needed:** The study operationalizes the key independent variable (remoteness of inspiration) using a specific computational method. Understanding this is crucial to interpreting the core quantitative finding.  
  **Quick check:** If two concepts have low Jensen-Shannon divergence between their embedding distributions, what does that suggest about their likelihood of being paired in a creative ideation task?

## Architecture Onboarding
Task Engine -> Prompt Generator -> Idea Generator -> Rephraser Module -> Rating Pipeline -> Analysis Backend

**Critical path:** Task/Prompt Generation → Raw Idea Collection (Human/LLM) → Rephrasing for Normalization → Human Rating → Statistical Analysis (Originality ~ Condition + Source + Distance).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Rephrasing: Eliminates framing artifacts but destroys the *process* evidence (the analogy itself), forcing inference from the final feature alone.  
- Semantic distance: Uses a single, general-knowledge model (Wikipedia) which may not capture the *functional* or *perceptual* analogies participants actually made.  
- Rating: Uses crowd-sourced "Shark Tank" framing, which prioritizes investment worthiness and may bias originality ratings toward "surprising but plausible" over "wildly impossible."

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low inter-rater reliability (ICC) on originality ratings, indicating the construct is not being assessed consistently.  
- Non-positive correlation between semantic distance and originality in follow-up studies, suggesting the distance metric is invalid for the task domain.  
- LLM-generated ideas in the *user-need* condition showing *lower* originality than humans, contradicting the core "baseline associativity" assumption.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replication with Qualitative Coding: Re-run the core design but have expert raters code each idea for *type of mapped feature* (perceptual vs. functional/mechanistic vs. structural). Test if the human/LLM difference in mechanism explains the condition effect asymmetry.  
2. Semantic Distance Threshold Test: For LLM-generated ideas only, perform a fine-grained analysis splitting the cross-domain condition by deciles of semantic distance. Test if the prompt effect is only significant for the highest-distance bin.  
3. Iterative Workflow Prototype: Implement a 2-iteration setup where ideas from the first round are fed back as context for a second-generation round. Compare originality gains from different sequence conditions to test if the intervention's effect is transient or cumulative.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Do humans and LLMs systematically differ in the *types* of properties they transfer during cross-domain mapping (e.g., perceptual vs. functional), and does this difference explain the divergence in originality outcomes?  
- **Question 2:** Does incorporating iterative refinement (e.g., critique, revision) alter the effectiveness of cross-domain mapping for humans and LLMs, and can it mitigate the feasibility-originality trade-off?  
- **Question 3:** For LLMs, is there a *threshold of semantic distance* beyond which an explicit cross-domain mapping prompt becomes necessary to stimulate originality gains comparable to those seen in humans?

## Limitations
- **Rephrasing destroys process:** The o3 rephrasing step removes evidence of *how* analogies were mapped (perceptual vs. relational), preventing direct mechanism tests.  
- **Semantic distance proxy:** Wikipedia embeddings may not capture functional or structural analogies humans/LLMs actually use; "distance" could reflect dataset biases rather than true creative leap.  
- **Rating framing bias:** "Shark Tank" investment worthiness emphasis may penalize highly novel but infeasible ideas, understating true originality.

## Confidence
- **Cross-domain mapping boosts human originality:** High (p<.01, robust across products)  
- **Semantic distance predicts originality:** High (consistent positive β for both groups)  
- **LLMs > humans in baseline originality:** High (all 7 models scored higher in user-need condition)  
- **LLMs show no prompt effect:** Medium (aggregate non-significant, but some models show marginal trends; variability unmodeled)

## Next Checks
1. **Qualitative coding replication:** Recruit expert raters to code each idea’s *analogy type* (perceptual/functional/structural). Test if human originality gain is driven by relational mappings, while LLMs default to surface features.  
2. **Embedding space validation:** Compute semantic distance using multiple embedding sources (e.g., ConceptNet, domain-specific corpora). Correlate each with originality to confirm Wikipedia generalism isn’t driving the effect.  
3. **Iterative generation test:** Run a two-round study where first-round ideas (from any condition) seed a second round. Measure whether cross-domain exposure creates cumulative originality gains, especially for LLMs.
