# Towards plausibility in time series counterfactual explanations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.08349  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08349  
- **Reference count**: 39  
- **Primary result**: Achieves perfect or near-perfect validity and significantly lower DTW distances (higher plausibility) compared to baselines, at the cost of higher L1/L2 perturbations.

## Executive Summary
Generating counterfactual explanations for time series classifiers that remain realistic over time is challenging. The paper introduces a method that optimizes counterfactuals directly in the input space using gradient-based techniques. The loss function incorporates validity (ensuring the counterfactual belongs to the target class), sparsity, proximity in L1/L2, and a novel soft dynamic time warping (soft-DTW) term. This soft-DTW aligns the counterfactual with its k-nearest neighbors from the target class, thereby preserving plausible temporal patterns.

Across eight benchmark datasets, the approach achieves perfect or near-perfect validity and substantially improves plausibility metrics: DTW distances to target class samples are much lower than those from reference methods, and Isolation Forest scores approach 1.000. However, the method incurs higher L1 and L2 distances, indicating a trade-off between temporal realism and input-space proximity. Limitations include hyperparameter sensitivity, lack of statistical significance testing, and reliance on proxy metrics without human evaluation.

## Method Summary
The proposed counterfactual generation method employs gradient-based optimization over the input time series. The loss combines four terms: (i) validity, typically using a classifier’s confidence for the target class; (ii) sparsity to restrict the number of changed points; (iii) proximity to keep the counterfactual close to the original in L1/L2 norm; and (iv) a novel soft-DTW term that measures alignment with the k‑nearest neighbors of the target class. The soft‑DTW provides a differentiable way to enforce temporal realism. Optimization iteratively updates the candidate counterfactual to minimize the sum of these weighted losses until convergence or a stopping condition.

## Key Results
- **Validity**: Perfect or near‑perfect across datasets (e.g., 1.000 on CBF).  
- **Plausibility**: Dramatically lower DTW distances (e.g., 0.016 vs. 0.302 for M‑CELS on TwoLeadECG) and Isolation Forest scores close to 1.000.  
- **Trade‑off**: Higher L1 and L2 distances compared to baselines, indicating larger input perturbations for temporal realism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works because the soft‑DTW term encourages the counterfactual to follow the same temporal dynamics as real examples from the desired class. By sampling k‑nearest neighbors, the loss pulls the counterfactual into a dense region of the target class manifold, avoiding unrealistic warps. Gradient‑based optimization then fine‑tunes the signal to satisfy all loss components simultaneously, balancing validity with plausibility. The sparsity and proximity terms prevent over‑alteration, while the validity term ensures class change.

## Foundational Learning
- **Counterfactual Explanations**: Minimal perturbations that change a model’s prediction to a desired class. *Why needed*: To provide interpretable, actionable insights for model decisions. *Quick check*: Can you explain why a model changed its output by altering only a few features?  
- **Time Series Classification**: Learning to label sequences of temporal data. *Why needed*: Many real‑world problems involve sequential data (e.g., ECG, sensor readings). *Quick check*: Train a classifier on a time series dataset and evaluate its accuracy.  
- **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)**: An elastic distance measure aligning time series by non‑linear stretching. *Why needed*: To compare time series with phase shifts or varying lengths. *Quick check*: Compute DTW between two sine waves shifted in time and verify it is lower than between unrelated waves.  
- **Soft‑DTW**: A smooth, differentiable variant of DTW enabling gradient‑based optimization. *Why needed*: To incorporate temporal alignment as a gradient‑friendly loss term. *Quick check*: Use a library to compute gradients of soft‑DTW with respect to input series.  
- **Gradient‑based Optimization**: Iterative parameter updates using derivatives of a loss. *Why needed*: To efficiently minimize complex, differentiable objectives. *Quick check*: Optimize a simple neural network with backpropagation and verify loss decreases.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
X_original → X_cf_initial → X_cf  
  X_cf → classifier → validity  
  X_cf → k‑NN(target_class) → softDTW  
  X_cf → L1/L2(X_original) → sparsity/proximity  
[validity, softDTW, sparsity/proximity] → sum_loss → optimizer (e.g., Adam) → X_cf_updated → (loop until convergence) → X_cf_final

**Critical Path**  
1. Initialize counterfactual X’.  
2. While not converged:  
   a. Compute validity loss via classifier.  
   b. Retrieve k‑nearest neighbors from target class.  
   c. Compute soft‑DTW between X’ and neighbors.  
   d. Compute L1/L2 between X’ and original X.  
   e. Combine all losses.  
   f. Compute gradient of total loss w.r.t. X’.  
   g. Update X’ using optimizer.  
3. Return final X’.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Validity vs. Proximity: Strong class change may require larger perturbations.  
- Proximity vs. Plausibility: Minimizing L1/L2 alone yields unrealistic series; soft‑DTW improves realism but increases perturbation size.  
- Computational Cost: Soft‑DTW is O(n²) and gradient computation is expensive, limiting scalability to long series.

**Failure Signatures**  
- No convergence after many iterations.  
- Final X’ still not classified as target class.  
- Extremely high L1/L2, indicating over‑perturbation.  
- Unrealistic warping patterns visible despite low soft‑DTW.  
- Out‑of‑memory errors for long series.

**First Experiments**

## Limitations
- Trade‑off: Higher L1/L2 distances required for temporal plausibility; practical impact on interpretability unclear.  
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: The choice of k and soft‑DTW parameters may significantly affect results, but the paper does not analyze this.  
- Evaluation limitations: Reliance on proxy metrics (DTW, Isolation Forest) without human validation raises questions about real‑world plausibility.

## Confidence
- Validity (near‑perfect): Medium  
- Plausibility (lower DTW, high IF scores): Medium  
- Trade‑off (higher L1/L2): Medium
