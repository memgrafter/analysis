# Beyond Token Eviction: Mixed-Dimension Budget Allocation for Efficient KV Cache Compression
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20616
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20616
- **Reference count**: 28
- **Primary result**: Achieves full-attention accuracy on LongBench with only 6.25% of the KV cache, and 100% needle-retrieval accuracy with as little as 0.26% KV on 50K context sequences; reduces per-token decoding latency to 55% of full attention.

## Executive Summary
The KV cache in transformer-based language models grows linearly with sequence length, presenting a major bottleneck for long-context inference. Existing token eviction methods operate at a coarse granularity, either retaining a token's full key/value vectors or discarding them entirely. This paper introduces MixedDimKV, a fine-grained compression technique that allocates variable numbers of latent dimensions to each token. The allocation is driven by a conservative upper-bound estimate of each token's contribution to attention output deviation under compression. The per-token dimension assignment is optimized by solving a discrete constrained minimization via bisection search over a Lagrangian dual variable, a strategy justified by a negligible duality gap in long-context settings. An extension, MixedDimKV-H, incorporates external head-level importance profiles to further improve allocation. Evaluations on LongBench and the Needle-in-a-Haystack test demonstrate that MixedDimKV attains performance comparable to full attention while using a fraction of the KV cache (down to 0.26% at 50K context) and significantly reduces decoding latency.

## Method Summary
MixedDimKV operates on a per-layer, per-head basis. For each token, it computes a loss score for several candidate compression ratios (e.g., 0%, 12.5%, 25%, 100%) using a conservative upper bound on the deviation of the attention output when that token's KV vectors are compressed via PCA. The loss integrates the effect across all queries in a local observation window (the last α tokens). The dimension allocation problem is then formulated as minimizing the total loss subject to a fixed total dimension budget. Due to the separability of the objective, the problem reduces to independently selecting a ratio for each token based on a Lagrangian multiplier λ. The authors solve for λ using bisection, exploiting the monotonicity of the total dimension as a function of λ and the negligible duality gap for long sequences. The chosen ratios determine how many dimensions each token retains; tokens sharing the same ratio are packed contiguously to maintain efficient attention computation. The PCA projections are performed per-head; only the projection basis for the maximum retained dimension is stored, and lower-dimensional representations are obtained by slicing this basis. MixedDimKV-H further divides the global budget among heads according to external importance scores (e.g., from head-wise profiling) and then applies the same intra-head allocation within each head's quota.

## Key Results
- With only 6.25% of the original KV cache size, MixedDimKV matches full-attention performance on average across LongBench datasets.
- In the Needle-in-a-Haystack benchmark at 50K context length, MixedDimKV achieves 100% retrieval accuracy while using as little as 0.26% of the KV cache.
- MixedDimKV reduces per-token decoding latency to 55% of that of full attention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works because it replaces binary eviction with a continuous-spectrum compression, guided by a theoretically grounded loss approximation. The conservative upper-bound loss score effectively ranks tokens by their sensitivity to compression; tokens that are more frequently attended to or that carry more information (e.g., needles) receive higher dimensions. The Lagrangian relaxation with bisection efficiently finds an allocation that maximizes total retained importance under a strict memory budget, and the negligible duality gap ensures optimality in practice for long sequences. Head-wise PCA restricts compression to act within each head's subspace, avoiding interference between heads and simplifying implementation. MixedDimKV-H leverages the observation that not all heads are equally important; budgeting at the head level prevents wasteful allocation to low-impact heads and allows the intra-head algorithm to fine-tune per-token dimensions further. Together, these design choices yield substantial memory savings with minimal accuracy degradation.

## Foundational Learning
- **KV Cache**: Why needed: Enables O(1) attention for previously computed tokens during decoding, avoiding recomputation. Quick check: Decoding step fetches cached keys/values for all prior tokens.
- **Token Eviction**: Why needed: Simplest way to reduce cache size by dropping entire tokens, typically based on recency or importance. Quick check: Sliding window keeps only the most recent tokens.
- **PCA-based Compression**: Why needed: Reduces dimensionality of KV vectors via linear projection, preserving most variance. Quick check: Truncated SVD on covariance matrix yields projection matrix.
- **Lagrangian Relaxation**: Why needed: Converts a constrained discrete optimization into unconstrained problems with a multiplier λ, enabling efficient incremental solution. Quick check: Increase λ penalizes dimension usage, shifting selection to smaller ratios.
- **Attention Output Deviation Bound**: Why needed: Provides a closed-form, computable upper bound on how much the softmax output changes when KV are compressed, enabling token ranking without costly recomputation. Quick check: Derived from triangle inequality and softmax's Lipschitz property.
- **Head-wise Importance**: Why needed: Different attention heads capture different linguistic features; distributing budget proportionally avoids starved important heads. Quick check: Importance can be estimated via average attention magnitude or gradient-based saliency.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Token extraction -> Loss scoring (for each candidate ρ) -> Bisection optimization (λ search) -> Dimension assignment per token -> Token packing (contiguous groups) -> PCA compression with shared basis -> Attention with variable-dim KV. For MixedDimKV-H: Global head quota allocation -> Per-head MixedDimKV.
Critical path: Accurate loss scoring → feasible allocation → correct projection slicing → packed attention.
Design tradeoffs: Candidate ratio set granularity vs. grouping complexity; local window size Q vs. compute overhead; per-head independent PCA vs. inter-head joint compression; memory savings vs. fragmentation.
Failure signatures: All tokens assigned 0% or 100% (token eviction collapse); total assigned dimensions overshoot/undershoot budget; loss scores not monotonic with ratio; Needle-in-a-Haystack accuracy drop; excessive number of contiguous groups causing kernel launch overhead.
First experiments: 1) Verify loss scoring: select a few tokens, compute L_t(ρ) for all ratios, confirm monotonic decreasing trend and correct boundary values (0% and 100%). 2) Test bisection: on a short sequence, manually vary λ and observe changes in total assigned dimensions; ensure convergence to within 1% of target budget. 3) End-to-end sanity check: run MixedDimKV on a single LongBench task with a small set of ratios (e.g., {0%, 100%}) to confirm that it reduces to token eviction and yields expected performance drop.

## Limitations
- Implementation details are underspecified: exact bisection parameters (tolerance, initial λ bounds), query window size α, and whether PCA covariance is recomputed online or cached.
- The candidate compression ratios {0%, 12.5%, 25%, 100%} are chosen heuristically; their optimality for other models or datasets is unverified.
- The claim of negligible duality gap for long sequences is based on theoretical reasoning but lacks empirical validation across contexts and budgets.

## Confidence
- 6.25% KV parity with full attention on LongBench: **Medium**
- 100% accuracy at 0.26% KV on Needle-in-a-Haystack: **Medium**
- 45% decoding latency reduction: **Medium**
- MixedDimKV-H outperforms HeadKV given same head importance: **Low**

## Next Checks
1) **Bisection robustness**: Systematically vary tolerance (e.g., 1e-3, 1e-5) and rounding strategy (floor/round/ceil) on a calibration set; report variance in dimension assignments and downstream LongBench scores.
2) **Local window ablation**: Evaluate performance with different α (32, 64, 128) and query sampling strategies (last tokens vs. random) on Needle-in-a-Haystack; analyze impact on dimension distribution.
3) **PCA implementation cost**: Compare runtime and memory overhead of computing SVD online during prefill versus caching and reusing covariance statistics across decoding steps; ensure subspaces for lower ratios are subsets of the maximal basis.
