# DyGnROLE: Modeling Asymmetry in Dynamic Graphs with Node-Role-Oriented Latent Encoding

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.23135  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23135  
- **Reference count:** 15  
- **Primary result:** Achieves highest Macro F1 on 7 of 8 DTGB datasets for future edge classification, with a 7.3-point gain on Yelp and escaping majority-class collapse on Stack Overflow.

## Executive Summary
Dynamic graph models often process source and destination nodes symmetrically, which limits performance on directed edge classification tasks, especially under low-label regimes. DyGnROLE addresses this by explicitly disentangling node roles through three innovations: separate embedding vocabularies for directional neighbor frequency counts, role-semantic positional encodings (RSPE), and a dual-CLS pooling strategy. It further leverages a self-supervised pretraining objective, Temporal Contrastive Link Prediction (TCLP), with historical false-negative masking to align the separate role spaces. On the DTGB benchmark (8 datasets, 10k labels), DyGnROLE substantially outperforms baselines, with ablation studies confirming the critical role of pretraining and asymmetry quantification scores (0.82–1.02) demonstrating effective role separation.

## Method Summary
DyGnROLE operates in two stages. First, for each directed edge (u, v, t), it samples the 10 most recent one-hop neighbors for both u and v (with timestamps < t). For each neighbor, it computes four counts: within-source, within-destination, cross u→v, and cross v→u. These counts, along with frozen TinyBERT text embeddings and Fourier time encodings, are projected and augmented with four role-specific learned positional embeddings (RSPE). During pretraining, source and destination neighbor sequences are processed independently by a shared Transformer encoder; their distinct [CLS] tokens yield role-specific embeddings z_u and z_v. The TCLP loss (InfoNCE with τ=0.07) attracts embeddings from the same interaction while repelling others, using a precomputed mask to avoid penalizing historical false negatives. During finetuning, sequences are concatenated with dual [CLS] tokens, and a two-layer MLP classifies the concatenated [z_u; z_v] using cross-entropy on 10k labeled edges.

## Key Results
- DyGnROLE achieves the highest Macro F1 on 7 of 8 DTGB datasets, with a 7.3-point absolute gain on Yelp (33.0% vs. 25.7%).
- Ablation shows removing pretraining causes severe performance collapse (e.g., Yelp: 33.0% → 14.0%), while other components (NFE, RSPE, dual-CLS) contribute dataset-specific improvements.
- Quantitative asymmetry analysis reveals DyGnROLE generates distinct role-specific representations (asymmetry scores 0.82–1.02) while all baselines remain perfectly symmetric (0.00).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Shared parameters for source/destination nodes create a representational bottleneck that degrades performance on directed edge classification, especially with limited labels.
- **Mechanism:** Separate embedding vocabularies for within- and cross-sequence neighbor frequencies capture distinct structural motifs for each role. These counts are embedded via dedicated tables, forcing the model to learn non-commutative, direction-specific patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Functional asymmetry in directed graphs manifests as statistically distinguishable frequency patterns in local neighborhoods; a shared embedding space cannot preserve these distinctions without explicit disentanglement.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "separate embedding vocabularies... capture the distinct structural and temporal contexts unique to each role."
  - [section 3.2] Formal definition of four count types and embedding via separate tables with UNK for rare counts.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "FlowNet" and "TIDFormer" discuss modeling directional/spatio-temporal dynamics but do not validate separate frequency vocabularies as a solution.
- **Break condition:** If the graph is undirected or role-specific frequency distributions are statistically indistinguishable, this mechanism adds unnecessary parameters without benefit.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Permutation-invariant Transformers lack inherent signals to distinguish a node’s role as source or destination within an interaction, leading to symmetric representations.
- **Mechanism:** Role-semantic positional encodings (RSPE) inject four learnable embeddings (E_src_node, E_dst_node, E_src_neighbor, E_dst_neighbor) added to projected features based on the node’s functional role and position (query vs. neighbor). This explicitly marks the query node and its neighbors as belonging to either the source or destination stream.
- **Core assumption:** Functional roles can be encoded as special tokens in the latent space, and self-attention will leverage these to generate role-contextualized representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "role-semantic positional encodings... explicitly distinguish query and neighbor roles."
  - [section 3.2] Precise rules: E_src_node added to prepended source node, E_dst_node to prepended destination node, etc.
  - [section 5.3] Asymmetry scores for DyGnROLE (0.82–1.02) vs. baselines (0.00) show RSPE helps generate distinct role-specific representations.
- **Break condition:** If interactions are inherently symmetric or the downstream task does not require role-conscious representations (e.g., predicting existence of any link), RSPE may not improve performance and could introduce noise.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** In low-label regimes, specialized role-aware modules cannot learn meaningful distinctions from scratch; pretraining on a self-supervised objective aligned with the downstream task is essential to encode role-specific inductive biases.
- **Mechanism:** Temporal Contrastive Link Prediction (TCLP) treats an observed interaction (u,v) as a positive pair. Source and destination CLS embeddings from the same interaction are attracted (via normalized dot product), while embeddings from different interactions are repelled, with historical neighbors masked as negatives to avoid penalizing valid past relationships. This aligns the separate role spaces geometrically.
- **Core assumption:** Predicting plausible future links requires learning a representation space where valid source-destination pairs are proximate; this objective implicitly teaches the model the causal logic of directed interactions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "self-supervised pretraining objective... enables the model to learn role-specific representations without requiring annotated data."
  - [section 3.3] Full TCLP loss formulation and historical false negative masking via precomputed masks.
  - [section 5.2] Ablation: removing pretraining ("w/o Pretrain") causes performance collapse to majority-class levels on multiple datasets (e.g., Yelp: 33.0% → 14.0%).
- **Break condition:** If abundant labeled data is available for the downstream task, pretraining may offer diminishing returns; if the graph dynamics are static or link formation is random, TCLP’s contrastive signal may not transfer.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Static text features -> Frequency embeddings (4 tables) -> Time encoding -> Feature projection (independent linear) -> RSPE addition -> Dual-CLS tokens -> Transformer encoder (L=2, shared) -> Dual-CLS extraction -> [Pretraining: TCLP loss] -> Finetuning: MLP classifier.

**Critical path:**  
Input (historical neighbors) → Feature construction (text, time, frequencies) → Projection & RSPE → Transformer (independent streams in pretraining, concatenated in finetuning) → Dual-CLS extraction → (Pretraining: TCLP alignment) → Finetuning: MLP classification. Role separation is enforced at feature construction (frequencies), RSPE injection, and CLS extraction.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Separate vocabularies vs. parameter efficiency: Four frequency embedding tables increase parameters (~4× vocab size × d_c) but capture directional motifs. The UNK token for rare counts prevents overfitting.
- Independent vs. concatenated Transformer streams: Pretraining uses independent streams to force role disentanglement; finetuning concatenates to allow cross-role attention for classification. This two-phase design trades early separation for later integration.
- TCLP pretraining cost vs. label efficiency: Pretraining on all historical edges is computationally heavy but essential for low-label regimes (10k labels). The precomputed mask adds storage overhead but avoids recomputing historical neighbors each epoch.

**Failure signatures:**
- Symmetric representations: Asymmetry score (1−cosine(z_u_src, z_u_dst)) near 0.0 on validation set, indicating role collapse.
- Majority-class collapse: Macro F1 ≈ Weighted F1 and matches the majority class baseline (e.g., 44.6% on Stack U for baselines). This occurs if pretraining is omitted or RSPE/dual-CLS is disabled.
- Overfitting to frequency counts: If validation performance degrades while training performance remains high, the UNK threshold (N_min=10,000) may be too low for small datasets, causing noise fitting.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce main results (Table 1):** Train DyGnROLE and all baselines on DTGB with 10k labels. Verify Macro F1 gains on 7/8 datasets, especially large margins on Yelp (+7.3 points) and Stack U (+9.3 points).
2. **Ablation study (Figure 2):** Run remove-one variants (w/o NFE, w/o RSPE, w/o CLS, w/o Pretrain). Confirm w/o Pretrain causes the largest drop (often to majority-class levels), and other components contribute dataset-specific gains.
3. **Asymmetry quantification (Table 2 & Figure 3):** Compute asymmetry scores via node swapping and probe internal frequency embeddings. Verify DyGnROLE scores 0.82–1.02 while all baselines score 0.00, confirming role separation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
While the paper does not have an explicit "open questions" section, the discussed limitations and suggested future work imply several open questions:
- How can the historical false-negative mask generation for TCLP be made more scalable and memory-efficient for large graphs?
- What is the optimal design for the four frequency embedding streams (e.g., parameter sharing, concatenation order) to balance performance and efficiency?
- How does DyGnROLE generalize to other dynamic graph benchmarks beyond DTGB, and what is its label efficiency across different labeled-edge budgets?

## Limitations
- Scalability of historical false-negative mask generation for TCLP is memory-intensive on large graphs and underspecified in implementation details.
- The exact design of the four frequency embedding streams (e.g., sharing tables, concatenation order) is ambiguous, hindering faithful reproduction and parameter-efficiency analysis.
- Evaluation is restricted to eight DTGB datasets, limiting generalizability claims beyond these domains.
