# Pretraining and Benchmarking Modern Encoders for Latvian

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.15005
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15005
- **Reference count**: 17
- **Primary result**: The lv-deberta-base model (111M parameters) achieves state-of-the-art results across a unified Latvian benchmark suite, outperforming larger multilingual baselines.

## Executive Summary
Latvian, a low-resource Uralic language, has historically lacked dedicated pretrained encoder models. This paper introduces a suite of Latvian-specific encoders—RoBERTa, DeBERTaV3, and ModernBERT—trained from scratch on a carefully curated 6.43 billion-word Latvian corpus. The best-performing model, lv-deberta-base (111M parameters), sets new state-of-the-art results on a comprehensive Latvian benchmark covering diverse NLP tasks. Notably, it achieves the highest COPA commonsense reasoning score (MCC 52.5), superior Universal Dependencies parsing (BLEX 89.1), and leading Word Sense Disambiguation accuracy (78.9%). All models and evaluation resources are publicly released to stimulate further research and application in Latvian language technology.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed a high-quality monolingual Latvian corpus by aggregating and cleaning web-crawled text, resulting in 6.43 billion words. Three encoder architectures—RoBERTa, DeBERTaV3, and ModernBERT—were pretrained using the masked language modeling (MLM) objective. After pretraining, each model was fine-tuned on a unified Latvian benchmark suite encompassing tasks such as text classification, commonsense reasoning, parsing, and word sense disambiguation. Performance was compared against existing multilingual baselines and prior Latvian-specific models to demonstrate the effectiveness of language-specialized pretraining.

## Key Results
- lv-deberta-base achieves overall state-of-the-art performance on the unified Latvian benchmark suite.
- Top scores on individual tasks: COPA commonsense reasoning (MCC 52.5), Universal Dependencies parsing (BLEX 89.1), and Word Sense Disambiguation (sense accuracy 78.9%).
- The Latvian-specific models outperform larger multilingual encoders despite having fewer parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Language-specific pretraining allows the model to fully allocate its capacity to Latvian linguistic patterns rather than sharing capacity across many languages. The large curated corpus ensures exposure to diverse Latvian constructions, while the DeBERTaV3 architecture’s disentangled attention mechanism captures nuanced token relationships. This specialization yields deeper latent representations that transfer more effectively to downstream Latvian NLP tasks compared to generic multilingual models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**: Essential self-supervised objective for pretraining; the model learns to predict masked tokens in Latvian sentences. *Quick check*: Compute perplexity on a held-out Latvian validation set.
- **Corpus Curation**: Ensures high-quality, in-language training data; cleaning and deduplication remove noise. *Quick check*: Verify language identification accuracy and duplicate rate after processing.
- **Transfer Learning**: Leverages pretrained representations for diverse downstream tasks. *Quick check*: Fine-tune on a small task-specific dataset and observe performance gain over random initialization.
- **Encoder Architecture (DeBERTaV3)**: Provides disentangled attention and improved gradient flow; outperforms standard transformers. *Quick check*: Compare MLM convergence speed and final perplexity against a RoBERTa baseline.
- **Benchmark Evaluation**: Unified suite measures model capabilities across multiple Latvian NLP tasks. *Quick check*: Run all benchmark tasks and record macro-averaged scores.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw Latvian Text -> Cleaning -> Tokenization (SentencePiece) -> DeBERTaV3 Encoder (MLM pretraining) -> Task-specific Heads (fine-tuning)

**Critical path**: The sequence that must succeed to achieve SOTA results: (1) Assemble and clean the 6.43B-word Latvian corpus, (2) Pretrain a DeBERTaV3 encoder with MLM, (3) Fine-tune on each downstream benchmark task.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Corpus quality vs. quantity*: A smaller but highly curated corpus reduces noise versus massive multilingual data.
- *Model complexity vs. efficiency*: DeBERTaV3 offers better accuracy but requires more computation than RoBERTa.
- *Parameter budget*: 111M parameters balance expressiveness and practicality for Latvian NLP community.

**Failure signatures**:
- MLM loss fails to converge or validation perplexity remains high, indicating data quality or hyperparameter issues.
- Large performance gap on morphologically rich tasks (e.g., parsing) suggests tokenizer or architecture limitations.
- Zero-shot performance on out-of-domain topics degrades sharply, showing corpus representativeness gaps.

**First experiments**:
1. Train a RoBERTa model on the same corpus and compare MLM perplexity and downstream performance to DeBERTaV3.
2. Ablate the cleaning step by training on raw web-crawled data and measure the resulting change in benchmark scores.
3. Fine-tune on a simple tag‑ging task (e.g., POS tagging) to verify that pretrained representations transfer effectively.

## Limitations
- The 6.43B-word corpus, while large, may still underrepresent certain Latvian domains compared to massive multilingual datasets, potentially limiting robustness.
- The unified Latvian benchmark suite may not align with newer evaluations such as LAG-MMLU, risking overfitting to a specific test set.
- The study focuses exclusively on Latvian; transferability to other low-resource Uralic languages remains untested.

## Confidence
- SOTA claims on Latvian benchmarks: **High**
- Superiority over multilingual models: **Medium**
- Approach generalizability to other languages: **Low**
