# CVPD at QIAS 2026: RAG-Guarded LLM Reasoning for Al-Mawarith Share Computation and Heir Allocation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24012
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24012
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: The system achieved a MIR-E score of 0.935 and ranked first on the QIAS 2026 blind-test leaderboard, confirming that retrieval-grounded, schema-aware generation substantially enhances reliability for high-precision Arabic legal tasks.

## Executive Summary
Islamic inheritance law (Al-Mawarith) requires complex multi-stage legal and numerical reasoning under varying school-specific rules. This paper presents a RAG pipeline that combines a deterministic symbolic inheritance calculator to generate 100K synthetic cases with full traces, hybrid retrieval with cross‑encoder reranking, and schema‑constrained output validation. The system achieved a MIR‑E score of 0.935 on the QIAS 2026 blind‑test leaderboard, securing first place and demonstrating the effectiveness of retrieval‑grounded, schema‑aware generation for Arabic legal reasoning tasks.

## Method Summary
The pipeline runs entirely without model fine‑tuning. First, an offline rule‑based generator samples heir configurations and, using a deterministic inheritance calculator, resolves all QIAS stages (eligibility, blocking, share assignment, taʿsīl adjustments) to produce a “golden trace.” These traces are rendered into Arabic Q&A pairs, forming a synthetic corpus of 100 K examples. Second, the corpus together with curated jurisprudential PDFs is indexed in a hybrid retrieval system that fuses dense vector search and BM25 via reciprocal rank fusion (α=0.7, β=0.3, k=60) and reranks the top results with a cross‑encoder. Third, for an input query, the top‑K retrieved precedents are injected into a prompt for Qwen3.5‑9B; the model outputs reasoning and an `<answer>` JSON block. Fourth, an output parser extracts the JSON, fills non‑critical missing fields with defaults, and validates four constraints: presence of required keys (`Ckeys`), correct data types (`Ctypes`), permissible categorical labels (`Clabels`), and mass balance (`Cmass`, sum within ε=5). Only compliant outputs are submitted.

## Key Results
- Achieved a MIR‑E score of 0.935 on the QIAS 2026 blind‑test leaderboard, ranking first.
- Synthetic corpus of 100 K cases with full traces guarantees legal and numerical consistency by construction.
- Hybrid retrieval focusing on curated PDFs yields significantly higher quality than web‑only or mixed sources (Table 4).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A large‑scale, rule‑grounded synthetic corpus with full intermediate reasoning traces provides jurisprudentially valid and numerically consistent supervision, enabling the LLM to learn the multi‑stage legal‑arithmetic structure.
- **Mechanism:** A deterministic symbolic inheritance calculator resolves every case (heir eligibility, blocking, share assignment, adjustments, taʿsīl) before any natural language is generated. This creates a “golden trace” for each ~100K synthetic case, rendered into Arabic Q&A pairs for retrieval indexing.
- **Core assumption:** Encoded juristic rules (matching QIAS configuration) cover all legally valid cases needed for evaluation; arithmetic consistency is guaranteed by the solver.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “a deterministic inheritance calculator… ensures legal and numerical consistency by construction.”
  - [section 3.2] “For each sampled heir configuration, a deterministic inheritance calculator resolves all QIAS stages… prior to any natural‑language generation.”
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers describe dataset creation but not this specific synthetic generator’s rule completeness.
- **Break condition:** If the rule set misses a madhhab variant or edge‑case interaction present in the blind test, the retrieval corpus lacks a relevant precedent, causing reasoning failure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Hybrid retrieval (dense + BM25) with cross‑encoder reranking, prioritizing curated PDF sources, retrieves semantically precise and legally coherent precedents, grounding the LLM’s generation in relevant, high‑quality examples.
- **Mechanism:** Query `q` fetches candidates via dense cosine similarity and BM25 lexical matching. Results are fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) and reranked by a cross‑encoder. The top‑K documents provide in‑context examples.
- **Core assumption:** Curated jurisprudential PDFs contain legally sound, high‑precision explanations more valuable than noisy web sources, even if web sources have better keyword overlap.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “hybrid retrieval (dense and BM25) with cross‑encoder reranking.”
  - [section 4.4] Table 4: PDF‑only retrieval achieves significantly higher combined, semantic, and success scores than Web‑only or mixed sources.
  - [section 4.4] “PDF‑only produces 190/200 high‑quality retrievals and no poor cases.”
- **Break condition:** If the query concerns a highly specialized or under‑documented scenario not in the PDF corpus, retrieval fails to provide a usable precedent, forcing the LLM to rely on error‑prone parametric knowledge.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Schema‑constrained output validation (keys, types, labels, mass balance) with rule‑based defaults enforces submission compliance and corrects minor LLM generation slips, making the system robust to formatting and minor reasoning drift.
- **Mechanism:** After generation, a parser extracts structured data. A validator checks: presence of critical keys, correct data types, permissible categorical values, and that post‑taʿsīl shares sum within tolerance. Non‑critical missing fields get default values.
- **Core assumption:** The four constraint categories (`Ckeys`, `Ctypes`, `Clabels`, `Cmass`) capture the majority of legal and structural correctness requirements for MIR‑E scoring; minor omissions can be safely patched.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “schema‑constrained output validation.”
  - [section 3.3.3] “The validation procedure evaluates four categories of constraints: V(y) = Ckeys ∧ Ctypes ∧ Clabels ∧ Cmass.”
  - [appendix C] Details on default value filling and mass tolerance (ε=5) for rounding.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM produces a logically inconsistent output that passes all four checks (e.g., a blocked heir incorrectly listed as receiving a share, or an ‘awl/radd flag mismatch), the validator will not catch it, and the error propagates to the final MIR‑E score.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Islamic Inheritance Stages (Heirs, Blocking/Hajb, Shares, ‘Awl/Radd, Taʿsīl)**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire pipeline is engineered around these discrete, weighted stages (per MIR‑E scoring). A developer must understand what each stage represents and how they logically sequence to design the synthetic generator, retrieval queries, and validation rules.
  - **Quick check question:** *In a case where a daughter and a full sister are present, how does the principle of `sudur` (blocking by presence of a closer heir) affect the sister’s eligibility, and which subsequent share calculations change?*

- **Concept: Symbolic Calculator / Deterministic Solver**
  - **Why needed here:** This is the core of the synthetic data engine. The system’s correctness hinges on the calculator’s rules being perfect and exhaustive for the target configuration. A developer must understand its inputs (heir set, madhhab config) and guaranteed outputs (all stage values).
  - **Quick check question:** *For a given spouse and two sons, what are the exact sequential outputs of the calculator for: 1) fixed shares, 2) residual pool, 3) ‘awl application if shares exceed 1, 4) final post‑taʿsīl percentages?*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Rule-Based Generator & Symbolic Calculator → Knowledge Base → Hybrid Retriever & Reranker → Prompted LLM (Qwen3.5-9B) → Output Parser & Validator

**Critical path:**  
`Input Query` → **[Retrieval]** → `Retrieved Precedents` → **[LLM Generation]** → `Raw Structured Output` → **[Validation]** → `Final Compliant Submission`. Failure at validation triggers a fallback (e.g., use default values, possibly re‑run generation with a warning).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **PDF vs. Web Corpus:** PDFs give higher semantic precision (Table 4) but lower coverage of edge cases. Web increases coverage but adds noise that can degrade retrieval quality. The system defaults to PDF‑only for robustness.
- **Validation Strictness:** Aggressive patching (`Cmass` tolerance ε=5, default values) improves submission completeness but risks hiding deeper LLM reasoning errors that MIR‑E might still penalize later.
- **Retrieval k & K:** Larger *k* in fusion increases diversity but may inject noise. Reranker *K* must balance context richness with prompt length limits for the 9B model.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Retrieval Failure:** High lexical overlap but low semantic similarity (Web‑only pattern). Manually spot‑check top‑5 results for a sample of “complex” and “very complex” dev questions.
- **Generation Failure:** Output missing critical keys (`heirs`, `shares`), or `<answer>` JSON malformed. Check log for parser exceptions.
- **Validation Failure:** `Cmass` violation (total shares ≠ 100 ± 5) or `Clabels` violation (invalid `awl_or_radd` value). These indicate LLM drift from the schema or incorrect arithmetic.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Synthetic Data Sanity Check:** Generate 100 cases with the calculator. Visually inspect 10 random outputs and their traces to confirm all stages are populated and arithmetic holds (e.g., sum of fixed+residual = 1 before adjustments).
2.  **Retrieval Quality Audit:** Run the hybrid retriever on the full 200‑dev set. Measure the **retrieval success rate** (`Scomb ≥ 0.5`) as in Table 4. If below 90%, tune RRF weights (α, β) or investigate the embedding model’s quality on Arabic legal text.
3.  **Validator Stress Test:** Take 50 LLM outputs known to be imperfect (from dev set). Run only the validator. Record which constraints fail most often. If `Ckeys` fails frequently, prompt engineering is needed; if `Cmass` fails, the LLM’s arithmetic within the context is unstable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the symbolic rule profiles be extended to cover all major Sunni madhahib and civil‑law variants, and what impact would this have on performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that “Jurisprudential coverage: current rule profiles do not cover all major Sunni schools and civil‑law variants” as a limitation.
- **Why unresolved:** Covering all variants requires extensive jurisprudential knowledge and formalization, which is non‑trivial.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing additional madhahib rule sets and evaluating on diverse test cases from those schools.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can confidence‑aware retrieval with source attribution mitigate the harm caused by web evidence on semantic precision?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note “Source sensitivity: Web evidence can harm semantic precision when fusion is loosely controlled” and propose “confidence‑aware retrieval with source attribution” as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Need to design effective confidence scoring and attribution mechanisms for legal texts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare retrieval quality and downstream MIR‑E scores using confidence‑weighted fusion versus current RRF.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent can blocking‑graph constraints reduce errors in multi‑generation hajb reasoning?
- **Basis in paper:** Error analysis shows 42% errors in blocking logic; the authors suggest “integrate blocking‑graph constraints for neuro‑symbolic error reduction”.
- **Why unresolved:** Blocking dependencies are complex; formalizing them as a graph and integrating with LLM generation is challenging.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Build a blocking graph module and measure reduction in hajb‑related errors on the dev set.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does augmenting MIR‑E with expert juristic review yield a more comprehensive measure of explanation faithfulness and usability?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that “MIR‑E only partially captures explanation faithfulness and practical usability” and propose “augment MIR‑E with expert juristic review”.
- **Why unresolved:** Requires coordination with Islamic law scholars and developing new evaluation protocols.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a human evaluation study where experts rate the faithfulness and usability of system outputs, and correlate with MIR‑E.

## Limitations
- Synthetic corpus completeness depends on the symbolic calculator’s encoded rules; unknown coverage for rare madhhab variants or complex hajb chains may hurt blind‑test performance.
- Retrieval component specifics (embedding model, cross‑encoder, PDF source list) are opaque, making full reproduction and tuning difficult.
- LLM prompt template and generation hyperparameters are unreported, risking implementation drift that could affect output quality.
