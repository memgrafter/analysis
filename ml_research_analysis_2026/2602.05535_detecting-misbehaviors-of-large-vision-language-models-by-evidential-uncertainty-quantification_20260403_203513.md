# Detecting Misbehaviors of Large Vision-Language Models by Evidential Uncertainty Quantification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.05535
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05535
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: EUQ detects misbehaviors by decomposing epistemic uncertainty into conflict (CF) and ignorance (IG), improving AUROC by up to 10.5% and AUPR by 5.5% over strong baselines.

## Executive Summary
Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) often generate unreliable content such as hallucinations, jailbreak responses, adversarial failures, and out-of-distribution errors, primarily due to epistemic uncertainty stemming from internal conflicts or missing information. Existing uncertainty quantification methods fail to distinguish these underlying causes. This paper introduces Evidential Uncertainty Quantification (EUQ), a training-free framework that reinterprets features from the LVLM output head as evidence within Dempster-Shafer theory. EUQ analytically decomposes uncertainty into two components: conflict (CF) capturing contradictory evidence, and ignorance (IG) reflecting absent evidence. Evaluated on four LVLMs across diverse misbehavior categories, EUQ consistently outperforms baseline methods, achieving up to 10.5% higher AUROC and 5.5% higher AUPR. Further analysis reveals that hallucinations produce high CF while OOD failures yield high IG, enabling interpretable diagnosis. EUQ requires only a single forward pass and no model retraining, making it both efficient and practical for real-world deployment.

## Method Summary
EUQ treats the pre-logits vector **Z** (the input to the final linear projection layer of an LVLM) as evidence about the correctness of each generated token. The parameters of the projection layer (**W**, **b**) are reused in closed form to derive an evidence mapping **E = A ⊙ Z⊤ + B**, where **A** and **B** are computed via the Least Commitment Principle, ensuring the mapping respects the original projection. The evidence weights are split into positive (**e⁺**) and negative (**e⁻**) components. For each token, simple belief functions are constructed and combined using Dempster’s rule, yielding a conflict score **CF** (sum of conflicting mass) and an ignorance score **IG** (mass assigned to the frame of discernment). Token-level scores are averaged across the sequence to produce sentence-level uncertainty estimates. The entire pipeline runs in a single forward pass after model generation, with **A** and **B** precomputed offline, making it training-free and computationally lightweight.

## Key Results
- EUQ achieves AUROC improvements up to 10.5% and AUPR up to 5.5% compared to state-of-the-art baselines across four LVLMs (DeepSeek-VL2-Tiny, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, MoF-Models-7B) and four misbehavior types (hallucination, jailbreak, adversarial, OOD).
- Misbehavior diagnosis via CF/IG: Hallucinations produce high CF (internal contradictions) while OOD failures yield high IG (knowledge gaps); jailbreaks and adversarial attacks show mixed patterns.
- The method incurs negligible overhead (<1ms per token) and remains effective across varying temperatures, model scales, and without any retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Pre-logits features in the LVLM output head can be interpreted as evidence for uncertainty quantification, enabling training-free detection of misbehaviors.
- **Mechanism**: The pre-logits feature vector **Z** is mapped via an affine transformation **E = A ⊙ Z⊤ + B** to produce evidence weights **e_ij** that quantify support/contradiction for each output token. Parameters **A**, **B** are derived analytically from the existing linear projection layer’s weights **W** and bias **b** using a closed-form solution under the Least Commitment Principle.
- **Core assumption**: The projection layer inherently encodes a linear combination of features that correlates with evidence for predictions; thus, reinterpreting its parameters in a Dempster-Shafer framework yields meaningful uncertainty estimates.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “interpret features from the model output head as either supporting (positive) or opposing (negative) evidence”; [section 3.1] “Z is interpreted as evidence”; [corpus] Related works also use evidential ideas, but EUQ uniquely leverages pre-logits with closed-form parameter extraction.
- **Break condition**: Fails if the LVLM’s output head is non-linear or does not expose pre-logits. Assumes linearity; a non-linear projector would require redesign.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Decomposing epistemic uncertainty into conflict (CF) and ignorance (IG) distinguishes misbehavior types (e.g., hallucinations vs. OOD failures) by their evidential patterns.
- **Mechanism**: Positive (**e⁺**) and negative (**e⁻**) evidence weights from **Z** are aggregated into simple mass functions **m⁺** and **m⁻**. Dempster’s combination yields **CF** = Σ_{S1∩S2=∅} m⁺(S1)m⁻(S2) (contradiction) and **IG** = Σ_j m⁻_j(H) (missing information).
- **Core assumption**: Misbehaviors arise from epistemic uncertainty that manifests as contradictory evidence (high CF) or absent evidence (high IG). The assignment of mass from **Z** faithfully reflects these components.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “decomposes uncertainty into conflict (CF) and ignorance (IG) by … Dempster-Shafer theory”; [section 3.3] Equations (11) and (12) define CF and IG analytically; [corpus] Prior work uses conflict for uncertainty but EUQ adapts it to LVLMs with CF/IG decomposition.
- **Break condition**: If misbehavior causes do not map cleanly to conflict/ignorance (e.g., aleatoric noise dominates), CF/IG may not correlate with errors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The evidence mapping parameters can be computed directly from the LVLM’s existing projection layer, making the approach training-free and computable in a single forward pass.
- **Mechanism**: Under the Least Commitment Principle, solve min_{A,B} ‖A⊙Z⊤ + B‖²₂ subject to **1⊤B = b·1**. The closed-form solution (Lemma 1) is **A\* = W - μ₀(W)**, **B\* = -(A\* - μ₁(A\*))⊙Z⊤**, where **W**, **b** come from the LVLM’s linear layer.
- **Core assumption**: The linear projection’s parameters already encode the mapping needed; reinterpreting it as evidence fusion does not require additional learning.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “training-free framework”; [section 3.2 Lemma 1] Closed-form derivation and equations; [corpus] Efficiency analysis highlights single-pass inference.
- **Break condition**: If the bias term **b** is large and the centering approximation fails, performance may degrade. Also depends on numerical stability of the estimation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST)**: A mathematical framework for representing and combining uncertain evidence via belief functions and plausibility. *Why needed*: EUQ uses DST to model positive and negative evidence and to derive conflict and ignorance measures from Dempster’s combination rule. *Quick check*: What does the mass assigned to the full frame of discernment represent in DST?
- **Epistemic vs. Aleatoric Uncertainty**: Epistemic uncertainty stems from limited knowledge and can be reduced with more data; aleatoric uncertainty is inherent randomness. *Why needed*: EUQ targets epistemic uncertainty as the root of misbehaviors, decomposing it into conflict (contradictory knowledge) and ignorance (missing knowledge). *Quick check*: How might a model’s ignorance (IG) differ from conflict (CF) as sources of epistemic uncertainty?
- **LVLM Output Head Architecture**: In decoder-based LVLMs, the last hidden state is fed into a linear projection layer (often followed by softmax) to produce token logits. *Why needed*: EUQ requires access to the pre-logits **Z**, i.e., the input to this projection, to compute evidence. *Quick check*: In a typical LVLM, what is the sequence of operations from the last hidden state to the final token probability distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Pre-logits (Z) → Evidence mapping (E = A⊙Z⊤+B) → Split into positive (e⁺) and negative (e⁻) evidence → Construct mass functions (m⁺, m⁻) → Dempster’s rule → Conflict (CF) and Ignorance (IG) → Token aggregation → Sentence-level scores

### Critical path
During inference, after the LVLM generates a response, the pre-logits **Z** for each token are extracted. The precomputed matrices **A** and **B** (derived from the projection layer’s **W**, **b**) are applied to **Z** to obtain evidence weights. Positive and negative evidence are separated, transformed into simple BBAs, and combined via Dempster’s rule to compute token-level CF and IG. The final sentence-level score is the average of token scores. This entire process adds a single pass of lightweight post-processing with no retraining.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Advantages**: Training-free, minimal computational overhead (<1 ms per token), single forward pass, and provides interpretable uncertainty decomposition.
- **Limitations**: Requires internal access to pre-logits, which may be unavailable in closed-source models; assumes a linear projection layer (non-linearity would break the closed-form solution); CF/IG decomposition may not capture misbehaviors dominated by aleatoric uncertainty.

### Failure signatures
- High CF, low IG → predictions likely contain internal contradictions (e.g., hallucinations).
- High IG, low CF → predictions likely stem from lack of knowledge (e.g., OOD failures).
- Low CF and IG → model is confident and likely correct.
- Both high → ambiguous or complex error cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Closed-source adaptation** – How can EUQ be applied to models without internal representation access? The paper acknowledges this limitation but offers no solution.  
2. **Multi-class generalization** – The method is evaluated on binary or multiple-choice tasks; it is unclear how CF/IG behave in large output spaces or open-vocabulary generation.  
3. **Layer selection rationale** – Certain decoder layers better distinguish misbehaviors, but the underlying architectural or functional reasons are not explained.  
4. **Non-monotonic scale effects** – Detection performance peaks at very small and very large model scales but declines at intermediate sizes; the cause of this pattern remains unclear.  
5. **Long-text token dynamics** – For extended generations, the evolution of CF/IG across token positions is unexplored; understanding per-token patterns could enable early detection.

## Limitations
- The approach depends on access to pre-logits, whose extraction details vary across LVLM architectures and are not fully specified for all models.
- Dataset splits and evaluation protocols (e.g., OOD benchmarks, correctness thresholds like ROUGE-L > 0.5 or HarmBench usage) are incompletely described, hindering exact replication.
- Baseline comparison hyperparameters (sampling temperatures, number of generations, semantic similarity models) are omitted, raising concerns about fairness of reported performance margins.
