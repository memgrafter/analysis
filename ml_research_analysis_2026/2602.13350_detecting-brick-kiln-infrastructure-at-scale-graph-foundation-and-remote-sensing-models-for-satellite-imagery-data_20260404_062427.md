# Detecting Brick Kiln Infrastructure at Scale: Graph, Foundation, and Remote Sensing Models for Satellite Imagery Data
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13350
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13350
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** ClimateGraph, a region-adaptive anisotropic graph neural network, achieves macro F1-score 0.79, improving over isotropic GNN baselines by up to 17 points.

## Executive Summary
Brick kilns in South Asia contribute significantly to pollution and forced labor, yet large-scale monitoring remains limited. This paper addresses the gap by curating a dataset of 1.3 million high-resolution satellite tiles across five cities and introducing ClimateGraph, a graph-based model that captures directional spatial layouts of kilns. ClimateGraph outperforms isotropic graph neural network baselines and is compared against foundation models (RemoteCLIP, Rex-Omni) and a classical remote sensing pipeline. Results show that while ClimateGraph best exploits cross-region context, foundation models offer flexible zero-shot detection, and remote sensing provides an efficient, training-free alternative—each with distinct trade-offs in accuracy, data requirements, and robustness.

## Method Summary
The authors propose three detection approaches. **ClimateGraph** constructs a geospatial graph from points of interest (POIs) with multi-modal environmental features; edges encode proximity and bearing angles, and anisotropic attention—using a learnable Fourier-series kernel and geometry-aware weighting—propagates messages directionally. **Foundation models** include RemoteCLIP, a contrastively pretrained vision-language model that uses prompt engineering and logistic regression calibration for zero-shot tile classification, and Rex-Omni, a multimodal model predicting bounding boxes via token sequences. The **classical remote sensing pipeline** computes a Normalized Difference Brick Kiln Index (NDBKI) from satellite imagery, applies annual temporal compositing, Otsu thresholding, building-height filtering, and morphological closing—requiring no training data.

## Key Results
- ClimateGraph macro F1: 0.79 (vs. GCN/GAT: 0.62, SAGEConv: 0.78).
- RemoteCLIP zero-shot F1 (calibrated): 0.446–0.526.
- Rex-Omni zero-shot F1: 0.15–0.25.
- Remote sensing F1 by city: 0.166 (Kathmandu) to 0.650 (Gazipur).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** ClimateGraph's region-adaptive anisotropic attention improves detection by explicitly modeling directional spatial layouts of brick kilns. The model constructs a graph where edges encode both proximity and bearing angles, using a learnable Fourier-series kernel to weight neighbor contributions based on orientation. This allows differentiation between isotropic (circular) and anisotropic (rectangular) kiln patterns, capturing discriminative structures relative to neighboring features like roads. Evidence includes the model's design equations and outperformance over isotropic GNNs. The approach may fail if directional priors are noisy in dense urban fabrics, as suggested by lower performance in Kathmandu.

**Mechanism 2:** Foundation models like RemoteCLIP enable zero-shot detection by transferring semantic knowledge from large-scale satellite image-text pretraining. During inference, hand-crafted prompts describing kiln morphology are compared to image embeddings via cosine similarity, with a logistic regression calibrator. This assumes alignment between textual semantics (e.g., "circular brick kiln") and visual patterns despite domain shift. Evidence includes the model's satellite-specific pretraining and prompt-based inference. Performance drops if kiln variants are under-represented in pretraining, as seen with Rex-Omni's low scores.

**Mechanism 3:** The classical remote sensing pipeline uses deterministic spectral-temporal-geometric priors without training. It computes the NDBKI to highlight red-fired brick signatures, uses annual composites to capture active periods, applies Otsu thresholding, filters with building-height data to avoid urban false positives, and consolidates fragments morphologically. This relies on kilns having distinct spectral signatures and spatial isolation. Performance varies widely, failing in cities where kilns blend into urban fabric (e.g., Kathmandu) due to similar rooftop colors or attached housing.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
- *ClimateGraph:* POI sampling -> Graph construction (k-NN + bearing) -> Anisotropic GNN layers (Fourier kernel + geometry attention) -> Linear classifier.
- *RemoteCLIP:* Tile resize -> ViT-B/32 vision encoder -> Text encoder -> Cosine similarity -> Logistic regression calibrator.
- *Rex-Omni:* Tile resize -> Qwen2.5-VL vision encoder -> Transformer decoder -> Tokenized bbox sequence -> NMS filtering.
- *Remote Sensing Pipeline:* Annual NDBKI80 composite -> Otsu threshold -> Local maxima -> Building-height filter -> Morphological closing -> Polygonization.

Critical path:
1. Data preprocessing: Align tiles to common CRS and radiometric calibration; for graphs, POI generation is the initial step.
2. Feature engineering: For ClimateGraph, raster sampling onto POIs must yield valid features; imputation strategy needs validation.
3. Model initialization: Load pretrained weights for foundation models; random init for ClimateGraph matching feature dimensions.

Design tradeoffs:
- Graph vs. tile-based: Graph models (ClimateGraph) capture long-range context but depend on robust POI seeding; tile-based models are simpler but ignore cross-tile dependencies.
- Training-free vs. learned: Remote sensing is instant but brittle; foundation models offer flexibility but require prompt engineering; ClimateGraph needs labels but generalizes better.
- Compute vs. accuracy: Remote sensing fastest (~0.5s/tile); foundation models moderate (0.2–8s/tile); ClimateGraph training heavy but inference efficient.

Failure signatures:
- ClimateGraph: Sudden drop in specific cities (e.g., Kathmandu) indicates violated anisotropic assumptions; check graph density and bearing distributions.
- RemoteCLIP: Low calibration performance (F1 <0.4) suggests prompt-domain mismatch; inspect embedding separation via t-SNE.
- Rex-Omni: Low success rate (<15%) and erratic bboxes indicate poor satellite grounding; test with natural images to isolate domain issues.
- Remote sensing: High false positives in urban areas or low recall in fragmented kilns; visually inspect NDBKI composites and adjust thresholds.

First experiments:
1. Graph sanity check: Visualize edge bearings for a 100-POI subgraph from Lahore; confirm kiln POIs show directional clustering (circular uniform, rectangular bimodal).
2. Prompt sensitivity test: For RemoteCLIP, test 5–10 prompt variations on a 100-tile calibration set per city; identify terms ("chimney", "circular") correlating with higher F1.
3. Remote sensing parameter sweep: Vary Otsu threshold multiplier (±20%) and building height threshold (2–5m) on Gazipur and Kathmandu tile sets; plot precision-recall to find optimal operating points.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does ClimateGraph generalize to new geographic regions beyond the five training cities? Current evaluation lacks hold-out regions, risking overfitting to known contexts.
2. Can foundation models achieve competitive zero-shot detection without any calibration? Current reliance on small supervised calibration sets undermines data-scarce applicability.
3. Why does the remote sensing baseline fail in dense urban areas like Kathmandu, and how can it be enhanced? Current NDBKI and height priors cannot isolate kilns blending into urban fabric.
4. Does ClimateGraph's anisotropic attention capture interpretable directional patterns related to kiln siting (e.g., wind, roads)? Analysis of learned kernels vs. GIS layers is needed for trust and domain insights.

## Limitations
- POI extraction methodology is unspecified, critically affecting graph construction.
- ClimateGraph training hyperparameters and feature preprocessing details are missing.
- RemoteCLIP calibration data composition is undisclosed, hindering zero-shot replication.
- Remote sensing baseline thresholds and morphological parameters are not quantified, requiring empirical tuning.

## Confidence
- ClimateGraph's anisotropic attention yields superior detection (macro F1 0.79): Medium (results strong but reproducibility constrained).
- Foundation models provide zero-shot detection: Low (performance inconsistent, prompt engineering sensitive).
- Classical remote sensing offers training-free detection: Medium (pipeline clear but brittle across cities).

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate ClimateGraph with alternative POI strategies (uniform grid, building centroids) to test graph construction sensitivity.
2. Ablate the anisotropic kernel (use isotropic attention) and compare per-city F1 to quantify directional modeling contribution.
3. Sweep NDBKI threshold and building-height filter on per-city validation sets to produce precision-recall curves, clarifying reported performance range.
