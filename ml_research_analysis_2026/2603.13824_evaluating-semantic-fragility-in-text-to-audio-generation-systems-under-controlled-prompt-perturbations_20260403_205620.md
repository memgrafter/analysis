# Evaluating Semantic Fragility in Text-to-Audio Generation Systems Under Controlled Prompt Perturbations
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13824
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13824
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Text-to-audio models exhibit semantic robustness in embedding space but persistent acoustic divergence under minimal prompt variations, indicating fragility in semantic-to-acoustic realization.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the robustness of state-of-the-art text-to-audio generation systems to semantically equivalent prompt variations. The authors construct a dataset of 75 prompt groups across three perturbation types—Minimal Lexical Substitution (MLS), Intensity Shifts (IS), and Structural Rephrasing (SR)—and generate audio using MusicGen-small, MusicGen-large, and Stable Audio 2.5. Evaluation combines high-level semantic metrics (CLAP embedding similarity) with acoustic and temporal measures (log-Mel spectrogram distances, MFCC-DTW, chroma-DTW). Results show that larger models (MusicGen-large) achieve higher CLAP similarity (e.g., 0.77 for MLS, 0.82 for IS), indicating better semantic consistency. However, all models exhibit persistent divergence in spectral and temporal metrics, even when semantic similarity remains high. This decoupling suggests that fragility primarily arises during the semantic-to-acoustic realization stage rather than in the multi-modal embedding alignment.

The study highlights a critical gap between understanding the “what” (the semantic concept) and generating the “how” (the acoustic details) in audio synthesis. While scaling model size improves semantic robustness, it does not resolve acoustic instability, pointing to the need for architectural or training interventions that explicitly couple semantic representations to fine-grained acoustic parameters. The findings call for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that capture both semantic and acoustic facets of robustness.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses 75 prompt groups: 30 MLS pairs (near-synonym substitutions), 15 IS groups (ordered intensity scales, yielding 45 adjacent comparisons), and 30 SR pairs (syntactic rephrasing). For each prompt, one audio sample is generated per model with random seed fixed to 0. Outputs are resampled to a common rate (implicitly 16 kHz for CLAP) and temporally aligned. Acoustic features are extracted using standard settings (log-Mel: n_fft=2048, hop=512, n_mels=128; MFCC: n_mfcc=20; chroma: 12 bins). Pairwise distances are computed: L1 and RMSE for log-Mel spectrograms, DTW with Euclidean metric for MFCC/chroma sequences, and cosine/L2 for CLAP audio embeddings. Metrics are averaged across all pairs within each perturbation condition and model. Paired statistical tests (t-tests) compare model scales, focusing on CLAP similarity changes.

## Key Results
- MusicGen-large significantly outperforms MusicGen-small on semantic consistency (CLAP cosine: MLS 0.77 vs. 0.59, IS 0.82 vs. 0.68; p<.001 for IS, p=.0014 for MLS).
- All models show substantial acoustic divergence: average log-Mel L1 distances exceed 15 and MFCC-DTW costs surpass 140, even for high CLAP similarity, indicating fragility in spectral and temporal realization.
- Structural rephrasing (SR) does not benefit from scaling (Cohen’s dz=0.07, p=.699), suggesting syntactic robustness may require different capabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Embedding stability decoupled from acoustic output
**Claim:** Semantic alignment in the shared embedding space (e.g., CLAP) remains relatively stable under prompt perturbations, even when acoustic outputs diverge.  
**Mechanism:** Text-to-audio models use a pretrained multi-modal encoder (like CLAP) to map text prompts and generated audio into a common latent space via contrastive learning. This space captures high-level semantic concepts rather than low-level acoustic details. Minor lexical or syntactic changes often preserve the high-level concept, leading to similar embedding vectors.  
**Core assumption:** Contrastive training creates a smooth semantic manifold where synonymous or intensity-modulated prompts project to nearby regions, decoupling semantic similarity from precise acoustic realization.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes "acoustic and temporal analyses reveal persistent divergence across all models, even when embedding similarity remains high." A section states "CLAP-based similarity serves as a high-level indicator of semantic consistency..." And neighboring work (e.g., AQAScore) uses embedding metrics as a standard, though potentially insufficient.  
**Break condition:** If minimal lexical substitutions cause a statistically significant drop in CLAP cosine similarity (e.g., below 0.6) across model scales, the embedding alignment mechanism itself is fragile.

### Mechanism 2: Fragility in semantic-to-acoustic realization
**Claim:** The fragility originates primarily in the semantic-to-acoustic realization stage, where the model transforms semantic representations into discrete acoustic tokens or spectrograms.  
**Mechanism:** After text encoding, the generative core (autoregressive transformer for MusicGen, diffusion for Stable Audio) must synthesize concrete acoustic parameters. This process is highly sensitive to tiny variations in the conditioning embedding or internal state, leading to divergent sample paths. Larger models may have smoother mappings but still exhibit non-deterministic acoustic realization.  
**Core assumption:** The acoustic generation decoder operates in a high-dimensional, stochastic space where small input perturbations can amplify into large output differences due to the lack of a robust, deterministic inverse mapping from semantics to acoustics.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract: "These findings indicate that fragility arises primarily during semantic-to-acoustic realization rather than multi-modal embedding alignment." A section notes "autoregressive token-level conditioning... allows localized variations in textual embeddings to propagate throughout sequential generation, potentially amplifying minor semantic differences into perceptible acoustic variation." Adjacent work (e.g., Tango 2, BATON) focuses on alignment via preference optimization, suggesting the realization step is a known pain point.  
**Break condition:** If increasing model scale consistently reduces log-Mel L1/RMSE and MFCC-DTW distances proportionally to CLAP similarity gains, the realization stage would not be the primary fragility source.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupling between embedding and acoustic metrics
**Claim:** The decoupling between embedding-level similarity and acoustic/temporal metrics reveals that robustness is multi-faceted; a model can appear semantically robust in embedding space while being acoustically fragile.  
**Mechanism:** Evaluation metrics operate at different abstraction levels: CLAP captures semantic concept agreement; log-Mel captures spectral energy distributions; MFCC-DTW captures timbral and temporal structure. The generation pipeline may optimize for semantic alignment via contrastive loss without explicit constraints on fine-grained acoustic stability, causing a gap between high-level and low-level consistency.  
**Core assumption:** Perceptual audio quality and semantic intent are not perfectly correlated; a model can generate semantically "correct" but acoustically divergent outputs (e.g., different instrumentation for "happy").  
**Evidence anchors:** A section: "These results show that MusicGen-small maintains only partial semantic stability while exhibiting substantial timbral and structural variation." Another: "MusicGen-large achieves stronger alignment across structural and semantic representations, although spectral variability remains present." The Sound Scene Synthesis challenge paper mentions controllability and evaluation as persistent challenges, implying multi-level metrics are needed.  
**Break condition:** If training a model to minimize log-Mel distance between perturbed prompt outputs significantly improves CLAP similarity, the metrics would be tightly coupled, contradicting the observed decoupling.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Contrastive Language–Audio Pretraining (CLAP)
**Why needed here:** CLAP is the primary semantic similarity metric; understanding its contrastive training explains why synonymous prompts yield similar embeddings but not identical audio.  
**Quick check question:** Why might two audio samples from semantically similar prompts have high CLAP similarity but different spectrograms?

### Concept: Autoregressive vs. Diffusion Audio Generation
**Why needed here:** The paper compares MusicGen (autoregressive on discrete tokens) and Stable Audio (diffusion); the generation algorithm influences sensitivity to prompt variations.  
**Quick check question:** How could token-level conditioning in an autoregressive model amplify small prompt variations?

### Concept: Multi-level Audio Similarity Metrics (log-Mel, MFCC-DTW)
**Why needed here:** These metrics detect acoustic divergence that CLAP misses; knowing what each measures (spectral energy vs. timbral-temporal structure) is key to interpreting fragility.  
**Quick check question:** What does a high MFCC-DTW cost between two audio samples suggest about their perceptual difference?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Prompt → Text Encoder → Conditioning Embedding → Acoustic Generator → Audio Representation → Decoder → Waveform → Feature Extraction → Metric Computation.  
**Critical path:** Prompt → Text Encoder → Conditioning Embedding → Acoustic Generator → Audio Representation → Decoder → Waveform → Feature Extraction → Metric Computation.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Embedding alignment vs. acoustic fidelity:* Optimizing for CLAP similarity may not optimize for spectral/temporal stability.  
- *Model scale vs. efficiency:* Larger models improve semantic robustness but not acoustic robustness, with higher compute cost.  
- *Prompt generality vs. control:* Avoiding genre/instrumentation constraints tests general fragility but may miss domain-specific robustness.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- High CLAP cosine similarity (>0.77) paired with high log-Mel L1 (>15) or MFCC-DTW (>140) indicates semantic-acoustic decoupling.  
- Stable Audio shows low MFCC-DTW but low CLAP similarity, suggesting temporal stability without semantic consistency.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline replication: Generate audio for 5 prompt groups from the paper’s MLS/IS/SR sets for MusicGen-small; compute all metrics to confirm approximate values.  
2. Perturbation isolation: Hold one perturbation type constant (e.g., MLS) and vary only the lexical substitution pair to measure intra-group variance versus inter-group variance.  
3. Scaling check: Compare MusicGen-small vs. large on the same 10 prompt groups to verify the paper’s claim that scaling improves CLAP similarity but not log-Mel/MFCC-DTW.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
What architectural or training mechanisms cause persistent acoustic divergence (spectral/temporal) despite stable CLAP embedding similarity under prompt perturbations? The paper infers that fragility arises during semantic-to-acoustic realization but does not analyze model internals, leaving the causal factors unexplained. To resolve, studies that isolate the text encoder and acoustic decoder contributions, probe intermediate representations, or apply controlled interventions are needed.

### Open Question 2
To what extent do the observed objective metric divergences correspond to perceptually noticeable differences? The paper's qualitative analysis by a single author is not a formal perceptual evaluation, so the auditory salience of the acoustic discrepancies remains unknown. A controlled listening experiment with multiple participants, correlating subjective ratings with objective distances, would address this.

### Open Question 3
Do the robustness patterns generalize to other architectures and domain-specific prompts? The evaluation is limited to three models and a modest dataset of general prompts, restricting inference about broader applicability. Testing additional models (e.g., AudioLDM, Tango) and domain-specific prompt sets would clarify generalizability.

### Open Question 4
Why does increased model capacity improve semantic consistency for lexical and intensity perturbations but not for structural rephrasing? The paper reports the statistical result without hypothesizing the cause; it is unclear whether SR taps different linguistic pathways or if the examples were insensitive. Analyzing the specific SR pairs, testing different syntactic transformations, and probing the text encoder's syntactic limits could elucidate.

## Limitations
- Small prompt set (75 groups) and lack of genre/instrumentation constraints limit generalizability.
- Missing critical generation parameters (audio duration, sampling rate, CLAP model version) and unspecified feature extraction details (FFT size, hop length, pre-DTW alignment) hinder faithful replication.
- Absence of formal perceptual evaluation; reliance on a single listener’s qualitative analysis leaves the perceptual impact of acoustic divergence uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Embedding stability under prompt perturbations:** High.
- **Primary fragility in semantic-to-acoustic realization:** Medium.
- **Multi-faceted decoupling between embedding and acoustic metrics:** High.
