# Spatial-Morphological Modeling for Multi-Attribute Imputation of Urban Blocks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10923  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10923  
- **Reference count:** 7  
- **Primary result:** The SM+IDW composite imputer achieves MAE 0.1629 and robust R² 0.5038 on the Saint Petersburg dataset, outperforming all baselines including SMV-NMF.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the problem of missing morphological data (FSI, GSI) at the urban block level, which is critical for urban planning analyses. It introduces the Spatial-Morphological (SM) imputer, a two-stage method: first, blocks with observed FSI/GSI are clustered via K-means to define morphological types; second, a CatBoost classifier predicts cluster membership for blocks with missing values using land-use composition and site area, with imputation based on cluster medians. While SM alone yields moderate accuracy, combining it with local spatial interpolation (IDW or sKNN) significantly improves performance. On a Saint Petersburg dataset, the SM+IDW composite achieves the best MAE (0.1629) and robust R² (0.5038), demonstrating the complementary value of integrating global morphological priors with local spatial context for robust, context-aware imputation.

## Method Summary
The SM imputer proceeds as follows: (1) Using blocks with complete FSI/GSI, perform K-means clustering in standardized (FSI, GSI) space to identify `k` morphological types; compute median FSI and GSI for each cluster. (2) Train a CatBoost classifier on these observed blocks to predict cluster membership from features: fractional shares of seven land-use types and site area. (3) For blocks with missing FSI/GSI, apply the classifier to obtain cluster probability distributions; impute FSI and GSI as probability-weighted averages of the cluster medians. (4) Combine these SM estimates with local spatial estimates derived from observed neighboring blocks via IDW or sKNN through simple averaging. This hybrid leverages global morphological structure (from clustering) and local spatial autocorrelation (from neighbors) to improve accuracy.

## Key Results
- The SM+IDW composite achieves MAE 0.1629 and robust R² 0.5038, the best performance among all evaluated methods.
- SM alone provides moderate accuracy, but integration with local spatial methods (IDW or sKNN) yields a significant improvement.
- The composite outperforms the multi-view baseline SMV-NMF, highlighting the advantage of combining morphological and spatial approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Global morphological patterns as a structured prior.**  
- **Claim:** Clustering observed blocks by FSI/GSI identifies distinct morphological types that serve as a prior for imputation.  
- **Mechanism:** K-means on standardized (FSI, GSI) partitions blocks into `k` clusters; each cluster's centroid (median FSI/GSI) defines a prototypical built-form profile.  
- **Core assumption:** Blocks with similar land-use composition and site area belong to the same morphological cluster, implying functional purpose determines built-form intensity.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Methodology describes clustering on observed FSI/GSI; related work (SpaceMatrix) supports morphological clustering; one-way ANOVA shows land-use groups differ in built-form intensity.  
- **Break condition:** Clusters are indistinct (high intra-cluster variance, overlapping), suggesting morphology is not well-captured by finite types.

**Mechanism 2: Land-use and site area as proxies for morphological cluster.**  
- **Claim:** Land-use composition and site area predict cluster membership, enabling imputation for missing blocks.  
- **Mechanism:** CatBoost classifier trained on observed blocks uses land-use shares and site area to predict cluster probabilities; missing blocks are imputed by weighting cluster medians by these probabilities.  
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between functional mix/size and built-form density is stable and learnable city-wide, and is the primary driver of morphological type.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Methodology section describes classifier training; one-way ANOVA indicates significant built-form differences across land-use groups; corpus shows land-use's causal influence on density.  
- **Break condition:** Classifier has low accuracy (e.g., cross-validated F1 < 0.4) or outputs uniform probabilities, indicating land-use/site are insufficient discriminators.

**Mechanism 3: Spatial composite corrects residual errors.**  
- **Claim:** Combining SM estimates with local spatial interpolation (IDW/sKNN) yields superior accuracy by addressing residual spatial non-stationarity.  
- **Mechanism:** SM output (probability-weighted median) is blended with a local estimate (IDW: inverse-distance weighted average of observed neighbors; sKNN: average of `k` spatial nearest neighbors) via simple averaging.  
- **Core assumption:** SM errors are spatially autocorrelated; local observed values capture immediate spatial context not encoded in global morphological priors.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states combination yields superior performance; discussion notes complementary advantages; Moran's I (~0.55) confirms spatial autocorrelation in FSI/GSI.  
- **Break condition:** Residuals from SM imputation show negligible spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I ~ 0), or local methods perform poorly, indicating no remaining local spatial pattern.

## Foundational Learning
- **Morphological Clustering (SpaceMatrix):**  
  *Why needed:* The SM imputer's global prior relies on partitioning FSI/GSI space into meaningful urban form types. This unsupervised step defines the "morphological types" that mediate between land-use and built-form.  
  *Quick check:* If you plot cluster centroids in (FSI, GSI) space, what urban forms might they represent (e.g., high-FSI/low-GSI = tower-in-park)? Do they align with known typologies?

- **Spatial Autocorrelation:**  
  *Why needed:* Justifies using local spatial methods (IDW, sKNN). The paper's empirical motivation (Moran's I ~ 0.55) shows nearby blocks have similar FSI/GSI, making neighbor-based imputation plausible.  
  *Quick check:* If Moran's I for FSI were negative (e.g., -0.3), how would that change the rationale for IDW/sKNN? Would a different spatial method be appropriate?

- **Multi-Attribute Imputation & Baselines:**  
  *Why needed:* The task is simultaneous imputation of FSI and GSI. Understanding baselines like SMV-NMF (spatial multi-kernel clustering + NMF) clarifies the novelty of combining global morphology with local spatial smoothing.  
  *Quick check:* How does SMV-NMF's approach differ from SM+IDW conceptually? Why might the latter be more robust at high missing rates?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Observed FSI/GSI -> K-means Clustering -> Cluster Medians & Assignments  
  Land-use/Site Area + Cluster Assignments -> CatBoost Classifier -> Trained Classifier  
  Missing Blocks (Land-use/Site) -> Trained Classifier -> Cluster Probabilities  
  Cluster Probabilities + Cluster Medians -> SM Prediction -> SM Estimates  
  SM Estimates + Observed Neighbors (FSI/GSI) -> IDW/sKNN -> Local Estimates  
  SM Estimates + Local Estimates -> Simple Average -> Final Imputation

- **Critical path:** Clustering (2) -> Classifier Training (3) -> SM Prediction (4) -> Spatial Composite (5). Clustering is prerequisite; classifier must be trained before SM prediction; spatial composite needs both SM output and spatial neighbor index.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **K-means `k` selection:** More clusters capture finer gradations but risk overfitting and empty clusters; fewer are robust but may merge distinct forms. Paper does not specify selection method.  
  - **Classifier choice:** CatBoost handles mixed features well but is a black box; simpler logistic regression offers interpretability but may sacrifice accuracy.  
  - **Composite weighting:** Simple averaging is unoptimized; learned weighting (e.g., based on local spatial density) might improve performance.  
  - **Local method choice:** IDW requires tuning `power`; sKNN requires `k`. Paper evaluates both; sKNN may be less sensitive to distance decay.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Clustering failure:** Elbow plot shows no clear knee; silhouette scores <0.2; centroids too close.  
  - **Classifier failure:** Cross-validation accuracy <40%; predicted probabilities near-uniform.  
  - **Spatial composite failure:** No improvement over pure SM or pure local method.  
  - **Implementation error:** Using imputed neighbor values (instead of observed) in IDW/sKNN, creating feedback loop.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate cluster structure:** Run K-means on Saint Petersburg training subset for `k=3,5,7`. Plot clusters in (FSI, GSI) space, compute silhouette scores. Goal: Confirm well-separated, interpretable morphological types.  
  2. **Validate classifier's predictive power:** Perform 5-fold cross-validation of CatBoost cluster classifier. Report accuracy, macro-F1, and confusion matrix. Goal: Verify land-use/site are strong predictors of cluster ID; check for systematic misclassifications.  
  3. **Test composite hypothesis:** On held-out test set, compute MAE for (a) SM only, (b) IDW only, (c) SM+IDW. Expect (c) < (a) and (c) < (b). If not, inspect residuals for spatial autocorrelation in SM errors. Goal: Empirically verify complementary effect.

## Limitations
- **Clustering validity:** Choice of `k` and interpretability of (FSI, GSI) clusters are not rigorously justified; low silhouette scores could undermine the morphological type premise.  
- **Classifier generalization:** No cross-validation metrics for CatBoost cluster classifier; accuracy on unseen blocks, especially under varying missingness patterns, is unknown.  
- **Composite mechanism:** Simple averaging is an unoptimized heuristic; the reason for superiority (e.g., error decorrelation) is not analytically decomposed, leaving optimal weighting unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (Global morphological patterns as prior): Medium. Clustering is performed but not validated against real-world urban types.  
- **Mechanism 2** (Land-use as proxy): Low-Medium. Classifier training performance is implied but not reported; causal assumption may not hold uniformly.  
- **Mechanism 3** (Spatial composite advantage): High for empirical result on dataset (clear MAE/R² improvement). Low-Medium for general mechanism (residual spatial autocorrelation not quantified, no ablation on error type).
