# Active In-Context Learning for Tabular Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27385
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27385
- Reference count: 20
- Result: Tab-AICL outperforms strong baselines on 18/20 tabular classification benchmarks, with TabPFN-Hybrid significantly ahead on 8/20 and Proxy-Hybrid cutting cost ~20×.

## Executive Summary
Tabular Active In-Context Learning (Tab-AICL) tackles the cold-start challenge in active learning for tabular data by exploiting the in-context learning capabilities of TabPFN, a pretrained tabular foundation model. Rather than incrementally retraining a model, Tab-AICL maintains a growing labeled context that conditions TabPFN’s predictions, yielding calibrated uncertainties even with very few labels. The paper introduces four acquisition strategies: Margin (uncertainty-based), Coreset (diversity-based), Hybrid (uncertainty filter plus k-means diversity), and Proxy-Hybrid (which uses a logistic regression proxy to reduce the number of expensive TabPFN evaluations). Across 20 classification datasets with budgets up to 100 labeled samples, Tab-AICL methods achieve higher normalized area under learning curves (AULC) than retrained CatBoost and XGBoost on 18 datasets; Hybrid significantly outperforms CatBoost-Margin on 8 datasets after FDR correction. The Proxy-Hybrid variant reduces TabPFN evaluations by approximately 20× while preserving most of the performance, offering a practical trade-off.

## Method Summary
The Tab-AICL framework operates as follows. Starting from a small initial labeled set (one instance per class), an active learning loop iteratively selects batches of unlabeled points for labeling until a budget (e.g., 100 labeled samples) is exhausted. At each iteration, the current labeled context L is provided to TabPFN, which produces probabilistic predictions for the unlabeled pool U. Instead of updating model parameters, TabPFN leverages its pretrained transformer to condition on L, delivering calibrated uncertainties via attention. Four acquisition strategies query points from U: (1) TabPFN-Margin selects points with smallest margin between top-2 class probabilities; (2) TabPFN-Coreset greedily picks points to maximize coverage in preprocessed feature space; (3) TabPFN-Hybrid first retains the N_cand most entropic points (using TabPFN entropy), then applies k-means (k=B) to choose nearest-to-centroid points, combining uncertainty and diversity; (4) TabPFN-Proxy-Hybrid first fits a logistic regression proxy on L, ranks U by proxy entropy, shortlists the top α-fraction (α=0.05) and then applies the Hybrid procedure on this shortlist, drastically cutting TabPFN evaluations. Baselines include CatBoost and XGBoost retrained from scratch each round with fixed hyperparameters and margin acquisition. Preprocessing (standardization, imputation, ordinal encoding) is fitted only on the initial pool to avoid leakage and applied consistently. Performance is measured by normalized AULC (Cohen’s κ) up to 100 labels, with statistical tests (Wilcoxon + FDR). The experiments reveal that Hybrid and Proxy-Hybrid offer robust performance across dataset types, while pure margin excels on clean data and proxy-hybrid remains resilient to outliers.

## Key Results
- Tab-AICL methods achieve higher normalized AULC than retrained CatBoost/XGBoost on 18 out of 20 classification benchmarks.
- TabPFN-Hybrid significantly outperforms CatBoost-Margin on 8 datasets after Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction.
- Proxy-Hybrid reduces the number of TabPFN evaluations by approximately 20× with minimal loss in AULC relative to full Hybrid.
- No single acquisition rule dominates: Margin works best on clean datasets, Hybrid on clustered manifolds, and Proxy-Hybrid is robust to outliers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reliable uncertainty via TabPFN in-context learning
- **Claim:** Tabular foundation models like TabPFN provide calibrated uncertainty estimates for active learning in cold-start regimes by leveraging in-context learning (ICL) without task-specific parameter updates.
- **Mechanism:** TabPFN is pretrained on synthetic tabular tasks; at inference, it conditions on the provided labeled context (L) and generates probabilistic predictions for unlabeled points via attention over the context. This yields calibrated uncertainties even with very few labeled examples because the model’s prior from pretraining regularizes predictions.
- **Core assumption:** The pretraining distribution sufficiently covers the diversity of real tabular data manifolds, so ICL can generalize to new tasks from minimal context.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section II.A describe TabPFN’s ICL approach; corpus on cold-start AL challenges supports the need but not direct evidence of calibration.
- **Break condition:** Context length exceeds TabPFN’s maximum window (e.g., >1,000 points), degrading quality; or test distributions deviate significantly from synthetic pretraining tasks.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid acquisition improves sample efficiency on structured data
- **Claim:** Combining an uncertainty filter with a diversity step (hybrid acquisition) improves sample efficiency on datasets with clustered or geometric structure by avoiding fixation on outliers while covering manifold regions.
- **Mechanism:** First, select high-uncertainty (high-entropy) candidates from the pool using TabPFN predictions. Then, run k-means on their preprocessed features and query points nearest to centroids. This ensures the batch targets both ambiguous regions and spatial spread.
- **Core assumption:** High-entropy points are more likely to lie near decision boundaries; diversifying among them prevents redundant labeling within the same submanifold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III.C.2 describes TabPFN-Hybrid; Section V.B shows strong performance on clustered datasets (e.g., Vehicle).
- **Break condition:** When uncertainty is miscalibrated (e.g., noisy/imbalanced pools), high-entropy points may be outliers rather than boundary points, leading to wasted labels.

### Mechanism 3: Proxy shortlisting cuts computational cost
- **Claim:** Using a lightweight proxy model (logistic regression) to shortlist candidates before TabPFN evaluation drastically reduces computational cost while preserving most of the hybrid acquisition’s benefits.
- **Mechanism:** At each iteration, fit a logistic regression on current context L, rank all pool points by proxy entropy, retain top α-fraction as shortlist, then apply TabPFN-based entropy and diversity only on this subset. This cuts TabPFN evaluations by ~1/α (≈20× with α=0.05).
- **Core assumption:** The linear proxy’s entropy ranking correlates sufficiently with TabPFN’s entropy over the full pool, so the shortlist retains most high-value candidates.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III.C.3 and Section V.C report runtime reduction and comparable AULC to full Hybrid.
- **Break condition:** If the proxy model is mis-specified (e.g., linear separator inappropriate), the shortlist may discard informative non-linear regions, harming performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Data flow: Initial pool U → Preprocessing pipeline (fit on U, transform features) → static pool features. Labeled context L0 (one per class) starts AL loop. AL loop controller orchestrates: Scoring – if Proxy-Hybrid, Proxy model (logistic regression) fits on L to produce entropy ranking → shortlist S; TabPFN (conditioned on L) scores S or all U. Acquisition strategy (Margin/Coreset/Hybrid/Proxy-Hybrid) selects batch B from scored points. Oracle labels B → L ← L ∪ B, U ← U \ B. Components: TabPFN model, Acquisition strategies, Preprocessing pipeline, AL loop controller, optional Proxy model.

### Critical path
1. Initialize L0 with one random sample per class.
2. Preprocess U and test set using pipeline fitted on initial U only.
3. While |L| < budget (e.g., 100) and U not empty:
   a. Score unlabeled pool (or shortlist) using TabPFN conditioned on L.
   b. Select batch B via chosen acquisition rule.
   c. Simulate oracle labeling: add B to L, remove from U.
4. Evaluate test performance (Cohen’s κ) after each round; compute normalized AULC.

### Design tradeoffs
- Batch size (B): Smaller B yields more frequent context updates but higher total TabPFN calls; larger B amortizes annotation cost but increases per-iteration context length (quadratic attention cost). B=10 used in paper.
- Proxy shortlisting fraction (α): Smaller α drastically reduces TabPFN calls but risks missing informative points; α=0.05 gives ~20× reduction, with a clamp to avoid too-small shortlists (N_min=200, N_max=2000).
- Acquisition rule choice: No universal best; depends on dataset structure. Hybrid and Proxy-Hybrid are more robust across types.

### Failure signatures
- TabPFN errors due to context overflow (exceeding max window ~1000 points) – monitor context length; consider reducing B or using proxy.
- Initialization failure: ensure L0 includes all classes; otherwise zero variance breaks acquisition.
- Proxy-Hybrid shortlist too small: if |S_short| < B after proxy ranking, k-means may fail; enforce minimum shortlist size via clamp (N_min).
- Preprocessing leakage: If imputation/scaling is refit after queries, results become overly optimistic; ensure preprocessing fits only on initial U.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline sanity check:** Run TabPFN-Random vs. CatBoost-Margin on Iris/Ionosphere to confirm TabPFN’s cold-start advantage without active selection.
2. **Strategy comparison:** On a clustered dataset (e.g., Vehicle), compare all four Tab-AICL strategies; expect Hybrid and Proxy-Hybrid to lead.
3. **Cost validation:** Measure TabPFN evaluation count for Proxy-Hybrid vs. full Hybrid on a large pool (e.g., CoverType subsampled); confirm ~20× reduction with α=0.05.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automatic transition from ICL to retraining:** How to decide when to switch from Tab-AICL (in-context) to retraining a scalable model (e.g., CatBoost) as the labeled set grows? The paper identifies this as a future direction but provides no criterion.
2. **Context compression/distillation:** How can the labeled context used by TabPFN be compressed or distilled to reduce its quadratic computational overhead while preserving predictive performance? No method is presented.
3. **Meta-selection of acquisition rules:** Can the choice of acquisition rule be automated by evaluating early AL iterations, thus overcoming the dataset-dependent performance of any single rule? No dynamic selection mechanism is implemented.
4. **Extension to regression and multi-label:** Will Tab-AICL be effective for regression and multi-label tabular problems, and how should acquisition functions be adapted? The current work focuses on classification.

## Limitations
- Reproduction details are incomplete: dataset metadata inference, TabPFN context window size, and exact inference call signatures are unspecified, risking inconsistent implementations.
- Generalisability is uncertain: evaluation on only 20 datasets; no comparison with newer tabular foundation models (e.g., FT-Transformer) or state-of-the-art active learning strategies.
- Statistical robustness may be limited: paired Wilcoxon with FDR correction might still yield false positives; the chosen datasets could be biased.

## Confidence
- Overall performance (Tab-AICL outperforms baselines on 18/20): High
- Uncertainty reliability (TabPFN provides calibrated cold-start uncertainty): Medium
- Hybrid acquisition (uncertainty+diversity improves sample efficiency on clustered data): Medium
- Proxy-Hybrid (proxy shortlisting cuts cost ~20× with minimal loss): Medium

## Next Checks
