# Contextual and Seasonal LSTMs for Time Series Anomaly Detection
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09690
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09690
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: CS-LSTMs achieve state-of-the-art performance on univariate time series anomaly detection, with Best F1 improvements of 3.1–10.5% and Delay F1 gains of 0.7–16.1%, while reducing inference time by 40% and demonstrating strong cross-domain transferability.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CS-LSTMs, a dual-branch LSTM framework for univariate time series anomaly detection. The system jointly model seasonal patterns via frequency-domain analysis (S-LSTM) and local contextual variations via overlapping windows (C-LSTM). A wavelet-based noise decomposition strategy mitigates interference from anomalies during training, and a masked probabilistic loss further stabilizes learning. Evaluated on Yahoo, KPI, WSD, and NAB datasets, CS-LSTMs attains state-of-the-art results, particularly on subtle anomalies, with significantly faster inference than Transformer-based competitors. The approach also shows robust cross-domain transferability.

## Method Summary
CS-LSTMs processes a raw univariate series through three stages. First, wavelet transform decomposes the signal; high-frequency coefficients are thresholded using a median absolute deviation estimate to produce a denoised reference series. Second, two branches generate predictive distributions: the S-LSTM branch splits the historical window into non-overlapping segments, applies FFT to each, concatenates the resulting frequency vectors, and feeds the sequence to a single-layer LSTM to output a mean and variance. The C-LSTM branch uses overlapping windows (stride 1), also transforms them via FFT, and processes them with a separate LSTM to produce its own distribution parameters. Third, training minimizes a masked negative log-likelihood loss that combines the predicted Gaussian parameters with targets that are the raw values for normal points (mask=1) and the denoised values for anomalous points (mask=0). The model is trained with Adam (batch size 512, up to 30 epochs, early stopping). During inference, the anomaly score is the deviation of the next observed value from the fused predictive distribution, and anomalies are flagged when this score exceeds a threshold determined by point adjustment.

## Key Results
- Best F1 improvements range from 3.1% to 10.5% across standard benchmarks (Yahoo, KPI, WSD, NAB), with Delay F1 gains of 0.7–16.1%.
- Inference time is reduced by 40% relative to Transformer-based anomaly detectors.
- CS-LSTMs exhibit superior cross-domain transferability: a model trained on one dataset generalizes well to others without adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CS-LSTMs improve detection of subtle anomalies by jointly modeling evolving periodic patterns (frequency domain) and local temporal variations (time domain) in a dual-branch architecture.
- **Mechanism:** The S-LSTM branch transforms historical windows via FFT to capture long-term periodic evolution, while the C-LSTM branch uses overlapping windows to model short-term contextual shifts. Their predictions are fused to estimate a robust normal range.
- **Core assumption:** Univariate series anomalies often manifest as deviations from either slowly changing periodicity (segment anomalies) or abrupt local trends (point anomalies), which cannot be captured by a single-domain model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "jointly leverage contextual dependencies and seasonal patterns... integrating both time-domain and frequency-domain representations"
  - [section 4.1.2] S-LSTM "models the temporal evolution of periodic patterns" via FFT windows.
  - [section 4.1.3] C-LSTM "captures locally changing trends" via overlapping windows.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., "Seasonal-Trend Decomposition Approach") also separate components but differ in architecture.
- **Break condition:** If periodicity is static or anomalies are purely global spikes without local context, the dual-branch overhead may not justify gains.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A wavelet-based noise decomposition strategy mitigates interference from anomalies and noise during training, enabling more accurate normal pattern learning.
- **Mechanism:** Wavelet coefficients are soft-thresholded using MAD-estimated universal thresholds to suppress high-frequency noise while preserving underlying signal structure, yielding a denoised reference series `ˆx`.
- **Core assumption:** Anomalies and noise act as contamination in the training data; removing non-stationary high-frequency components isolates the "normal" generative process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "built upon a noise decomposition strategy to mitigate the impact of anomalies"
  - [section 4.1.1] Details wavelet decomposition (Eq.2-5), MAD noise estimation (Eq.3), and soft-thresholding (Eq.4).
  - [section 5.4.2] Ablation shows this strategy outperforms pooling/STL decomposition in accuracy and speed.
  - [corpus] No direct match; TFAD uses STL but CS-LSTMs' wavelet approach is novel for this context.
- **Break condition:** If the series has no high-frequency noise or anomalies are low-frequency trends, decomposition may erode signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A masked negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss with noise-decomposed targets forces the model to learn normal distributions robustly despite unlabeled anomalies.
- **Mechanism:** For each point, the loss combines the predicted Gaussian (µ, σ²) with a hybrid target: original value if normal (mask=1), denoised value if anomalous (mask=0). This prevents anomalous points from distorting the learned distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Anomaly labels are unavailable during training; using a robust denoised estimate for suspected anomalies provides a cleaner learning signal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "masked probabilistic loss"
  - [section 4.2] Defines loss D(µ, σ, x, ˆx) (Eq.6) and explains anomaly mask usage.
  - [appendix F] Proves optimal µ is the hybrid target (Eq.18), showing the loss encourages alignment with denoised normal values.
  - [corpus] Not explicitly tested; other papers (e.g., DONUT) use reconstruction loss without explicit masking.
- **Break condition:** If noise decomposition fails (e.g., over-smoothing), the hybrid target itself becomes biased.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Univariate Time Series (UTS) characteristics.
  - **Why needed here:** UTS has only one variable per timestamp, limiting cross-variable correlations and making context essential for distinguishing normal fluctuations from anomalies.
  - **Quick check question:** Why can't a simple threshold on absolute values detect anomalies in UTS? (Answer: Normal variation range is context-dependent; see Figure 3.)
- **Concept:** Frequency-domain analysis (Fourier Transform).
  - **Why needed here:** Periodicity evolution is more naturally observed in frequency domain; FFT converts local windows into frequency vectors for S-LSTM.
  - **Quick check question:** How does FFT help model changing periodicity compared to raw time values? (Answer: Magnitude of frequency components directly indicates dominant cycles.)
- **Concept:** LSTM for temporal sequence modeling.
  - **Why needed here:** Both branches use LSTM to learn temporal dependencies from sequences of windows (frequency vectors or raw segments).
  - **Quick check question:** Why use LSTM over a simple MLP for the sequence of windows? (Answer: LSTM captures long-range dependencies across the window sequence.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input:** Raw univariate series x(0:t)
  2. **Preprocessing:** Noise decomposition (wavelet → MAD threshold → denoised ˆx)
  3. **Dual-branch input generation:** 
     - S-LSTM: split historical window into non-overlapping segments → FFT → frequency vectors zˢ.
     - C-LSTM: split into overlapping segments → FFT → frequency vectors zᶜ.
  4. **Prediction branches:** 
     - S-LSTM(LSTM on zˢ) → (µˢ, σˢ)
     - C-LSTM(LSTM on zᶜ) → (µᶜ, σᶜ)
  5. **Fusion:** Concatenate µˢ/σˢ and µᶜ/σᶜ (implicitly via summed loss, not explicit fusion layer).
  6. **Loss:** Masked NLL D(µ, σ, x, ˆx) computed separately for each branch, summed.
  7. **Scoring:** Anomaly score based on deviation of xₜ₊₁ from predicted distribution (µ, σ) of fused output.
- **Critical path:** Noise decomposition → windowing/FFT for both branches → parallel LSTM inference → loss calculation → backprop. The two LSTM branches are independent until loss aggregation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Window sizes:** Seasonal window must cover at least one full period; larger windows risk noise. Context window balances local detail and information sufficiency (Figure 7).
  - **Denoising:** Wavelet-based is efficient and robust (Table 7), but choice of wavelet basis and levels affects performance; paper uses default parameters.
  - **Dual-branch vs. single:** Adds parameters/computation (~600K) but gains robustness across anomaly types (Table 5 ablation).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Missed subtle anomalies:** If seasonal window too small (misses period) or too large (dilutes local change); see Figure 7 degradation.
  - **High false positives:** Over-aggressive wavelet thresholding removes valid signal, making normal points appear anomalous.
  - **Slow inference on long series:** FFT and dual-branch increase per-step cost, though still faster than Transformers (Table 4).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation of branches:** Remove C-LSTM or S-LSTM; performance drops on specific anomaly types (Table 5).
  2. **Denoising strategy comparison:** Replace wavelet with pooling or STL; wavelet balances accuracy and speed (Tables 6,7).
  3. **Window size sweep:** Vary seasonal/context windows; find dataset-dependent optimal ranges (Figure 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Is there a principled, automated method to select optimal window sizes for S-LSTM and C-LSTM branches across diverse univariate time series characteristics, eliminating the current reliance on dataset-specific heuristics based on sampling rate?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper acknowledges dataset-specific hyperparameter settings due to differences in "intrinsic information density" (Appendix A) and shows F1 scores vary with window size (Figure 7), yet provides only broad rules-of-thumb (e.g., seasonal window = total window / 5–7).
- **Why unresolved:** The current rules are derived empirically from a limited set of IT monitoring datasets; their universality for series with different periodicities, noise levels, or sampling frequencies remains unverified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic study across a wider range of UTS benchmarks (e.g., varying period lengths, missing rates, trend strengths) that derives or validates an automated window selection algorithm, possibly based on statistical properties like autocorrelation or spectral entropy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Could alternative or adaptive noise decomposition strategies beyond wavelet shrinkage with MAD thresholding further improve robustness, particularly for time series with complex non-stationarities or non-Gaussian noise?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors propose a wavelet-based noise decomposition as a core component and compare it only against "Pooling Decomposition" and "STL Decomposition" (Section 5.4.2), stating their method strikes the best balance.
- **Why unresolved:** The comparison excludes other state-of-the-art denoising or robust decomposition techniques (e.g., robust singular spectrum analysis, Bayesian change point detection for trend extraction), leaving open whether the wavelet-MAD combination is optimal or merely sufficient for the tested datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive benchmark integrating multiple decomposition methods (e.g.,<V>,<R>) into the CS-LSTMs framework and evaluating on synthetic data with controlled noise types and real-world series with known artifact patterns.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the relative contribution of the S-LSTM (frequency-domain periodicity modeling) versus C-LSTM (local context modeling) branches to detecting different anomaly types (e.g., point vs. segment, abrupt vs. gradual), and is there risk of redundancy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The dual-branch architecture is motivated by two challenges, and ablation studies remove entire branches (Table 5), but the paper does not analyze branch-specific detection performance or error patterns on a per-anomaly-type basis.
- **Why unresolved:** Without granular analysis (e.g., which branch flags which anomalies first, or how branch outputs correlate during normal vs. anomalous segments), the claimed complementarity remains an assumption rather than a quantified design insight.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An error analysis study on a labeled dataset with fine-grained anomaly typology, measuring branch-wise precision/recall for each type, and visualizing branch attention or prediction uncertainties in the frequency vs. time domains.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can CS-LSTMs be adapted for cross-domain transfer when the target domain has limited or no labeled anomalies and significant distribution shift (e.g., different seasonal patterns, trend dynamics), beyond the demonstrated direct zero-shot transfer?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.3 evaluates transferability by training on Yahoo/KPI and testing on other datasets without any adaptation, noting CS-LSTMs "consistently achieve the best F1 scores," but does not explore fine-tuning, domain adversarial training, or unsupervised adaptation techniques.
- **Why unresolved:** Industrial deployment often faces new systems with unseen dynamics; the current transfer protocol assumes the source model generalizes well, but performance drops (still best) suggest room for improvement with targeted adaptation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments introducing small amounts of unlabeled target data for self-supervised fine-tuning or using domain adaptation modules, measuring gains over zero-shot transfer on challenging domain shifts (e.g., from web traffic to IoT sensor data).

## Limitations
- Dual-branch overhead may not be justified when periodicity is static or anomalies are purely global spikes.
- Wavelet denoising can over-smooth low-frequency trends, potentially distorting the normal pattern.
- Evaluation is limited to univariate time series; multivariate extensions remain untested.
