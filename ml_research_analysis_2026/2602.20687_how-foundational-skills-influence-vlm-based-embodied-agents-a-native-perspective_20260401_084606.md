# How Foundational Skills Influence VLM-based Embodied Agents:A Native Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20687
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20687
- **Reference count**: 3
- **Primary result**: NativeEmbodied benchmark reveals severe deficiencies in Navigation and Spatial Alignment for 15 VLMs, with best Search success at 34.9% and most models below 50% on Alignment.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NativeEmbodied, a novel embodied agent benchmark that employs a native action space consisting of primitive movements to properly evaluate low-level control skills. By decoupling three high-level tasks from four foundational low-level skills (Navigation, Spatial Alignment, Planning, Perception), the benchmark isolates critical capabilities missing in current vision-language models (VLMs). Evaluation of 15 VLMs shows catastrophic failures in Navigation and Spatial Alignment, while Perception is relatively mature. Ablation studies confirm that Navigation and Planning are the main bottlenecks for long-horizon tasks, and that enabling reasoning improves planning but degrades basic execution, exposing a fundamental trade-off between cognitive and motor skills in native settings.

## Method Summary
The paper does not provide reproduction details. However, the method revolves around constructing the NativeEmbodied benchmark, which defines a native action space of discrete primitive commands (e.g., move forward, turn, pick, place) and designs four foundational low-level skills. Three high-level tasks (Search, Rearrangement, Mobile Manipulation) are embedded within this space to allow targeted evaluation and ablation of specific skills.

## Key Results
- Navigation and Spatial Alignment are critically underperforming: best Search success rate only 34.9% (GPT-o3), most models below 50% on Alignment.
- Ablation reveals Navigation and Planning as primary bottlenecks for long-horizon tasks; Perception capabilities are mature and not limiting.
- Enabling reasoning improves planning but degrades basic execution, indicating a trade-off between cognitive and motor skills in native settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by forcing VLMs to execute tasks through a native action space of primitive commands, which exposes a mismatch between typical VLM training (static image-text corpora) and the fine-grained motor control required for embodied tasks. The decoupling of foundational skills via ablation reveals that Navigation and Planning are the primary bottlenecks because current VLMs lack explicit spatial reasoning and multi-step sequence optimization capabilities when operating at the control level. The observed trade-off—where reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought) improves planning but degrades execution—likely stems from reasoning introducing latency or misalignment with the real-time, precise timing requirements of primitive actions, or from internal representation conflicts between high-level cognition and low-level motor commands.

## Foundational Learning
- **Navigation**  
  *Why needed*: Enables autonomous movement through an environment to reach target locations.  
  *Quick check*: Can the agent navigate to a specified goal using only primitive movements (e.g., forward, turn) without collisions or Stuck?
- **Spatial Alignment**  
  *Why needed*: Allows the agent to correctly orient itself or objects relative to the environment.  
  *Quick check*: Can the agent align its facing direction or an object's pose to match a given orientation using only rotation and translation primitives?
- **Planning**  
  *Why needed*: Supports sequencing multiple primitive actions to achieve long-term goals.  
  *Quick check*: Can the agent generate and execute a multi-step plan to reach a distant goal while avoiding obstacles?
- **Perception**  
  *Why needed*: Provides accurate interpretation of visual input (objects, distances, semantics).  
  *Quick check*: Can the agent reliably detect and localize task-relevant objects from raw pixels?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Observation (RGB-D + optional language) -> VLM (vision-language encoder) -> Reasoning (internal) -> Action head -> Discrete primitive action space  

**Critical path**: Sensory input is processed by the VLM to extract features and reason about the task, then mapped to a primitive action command that directly controls the agent.  

**Design tradeoffs**: Native action space offers fine-grained control but increases sequence length; reasoning enhances planning but may degrade real-time execution; pre-trained VLMs may not align with low-level motor control.  

**Failure signatures**: Repeated collisions or getting stuck (navigation), incorrect orientation (spatial alignment), inefficient long sequences (planning), misidentification of objects (perception).  

**First experiments**:  
1. Baseline evaluation of each VLM on the four foundational skills individually to quantify capabilities.  
2. Ablation of reasoning (e.g., disabling chain-of-thought) to isolate its impact on planning versus execution.  
3. Composite long-horizon tasks integrating multiple foundational skills to identify most challenging skill combinations.

## Limitations
- The benchmark may not fully isolate foundational low-level skills, as high-level task definitions could implicitly constrain or bias low-level execution patterns.  
- Simulation environments (e.g., Habitat) might not capture real-world physical complexities, limiting generalizability of motor-skill failures.  
- The "native" action space design choices may disadvantage models optimized for standard high-level action outputs, potentially conflating architectural limitations with genuine skill deficiencies.

## Confidence
- **High**: NativeEmbodied introduces a novel native action space and experimental decoupling of skills.  
- **Medium**: VLMs exhibit severe deficiencies in Navigation and Spatial Alignment on this specific benchmark.  
- **Medium**: Ablation identifies Navigation and Planning as primary bottlenecks for long-horizon tasks.  
- **Low**: Findings generalize beyond the provided benchmark and simulation setting to real-world deployment.

## Next Checks
1. Cross-benchmark replication: Re-evaluate top-performing VLMs on other native-action embodied benchmarks (e.g., versions of BEHAVIOR, Romero) to confirm if Navigation/Alignment deficits persist across diverse low-level action interfaces.  
2. Sim-to-real transfer test: Deploy the best and worst models from the study on a physical robot (e.g., LoCoBot) performing simplified native navigation/spatial alignment tasks to quantify the simulation-to-reality gap in failure modes.  
3. Architectural ablation: Fine-tune a single VLM backbone (e.g., LLaVA) with mixture-of-experts heads separately for perception, planning, and motor control, then measure whether improved low-level execution causally reduces long-horizon task success without degrading reasoning.
