# Bridging the Trust Gap: Clinician-Validated Hybrid Explainable AI for Maternal Health Risk Assessment in Bangladesh

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.07866
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07866
*   **Reference Count:** 13
*   **Primary Result:** A hybrid explainable AI framework combining fuzzy logic rules and SHAP explanations achieved 88.67% accuracy and was preferred by 71.4% of validating clinicians, with 54.8% expressing trust for clinical use.

## Executive Summary
This study develops and clinically validates a hybrid explainable AI (XAI) system to predict maternal health risks in resource-constrained settings like Bangladesh, aiming to overcome low trust in black-box AI. The framework integrates ante-hoc fuzzy logic rules—encoding clinical guidelines—with post-hoc SHAP feature attributions within an XGBoost model. Trained on 1,014 patient records, the model achieved high predictive performance (ROC-AUC 0.9703). A validation study with 14 Bangladeshi healthcare professionals demonstrated a clear preference (71.4%) for the hybrid explanation format over alternatives, with a majority (54.8%) indicating trust for potential clinical deployment. The work highlights that transparent, multi-modal explanations can bridge the trust gap, while also identifying critical barriers like missing clinical features and connectivity issues.

## Method Summary
The researchers built a hybrid XAI pipeline. First, they engineered a "fuzzy risk score" feature by encoding 12 clinical guidelines (e.g., related to age, blood pressure) into a fuzzy inference system with membership functions. This score, along with 7 other clinical and socioeconomic features (including regional healthcare access), trained an XGBoost classifier. For explanations, the system provided: 1) the fuzzy rule that fired for a given prediction, 2) global and local SHAP plots showing feature contributions, and 3) a LIME-based local explanation. The model's performance was assessed via standard metrics. Trust and usability were evaluated through a within-subject survey and qualitative interviews with 14 clinicians, who rated clarity, trust, and preference for different explanation formats (hybrid vs. SHAP-only vs. LIME-only).

## Key Results
*   The hybrid XGBoost model achieved 88.67% accuracy and a ROC-AUC of 0.9703 on the held-out test set.
*   clinician validation showed a 71.4% preference for the hybrid explanation format over SHAP-only or LIME-only alternatives.
*   SHAP analysis identified "regional healthcare access" as the top predictor, while the engineered fuzzy risk score ranked third and showed a modest positive correlation (r=0.298) with actual risk outcomes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Hybrid explanations build trust by providing intuitive, rule-based reasoning (fuzzy logic) that mirrors clinicians' mental models.
*   **Mechanism:** The ante-hoc fuzzy layer encodes clinical guidelines as if-then rules with gradual membership functions (e.g., "IF Age is Advanced AND BP is Stage 2 HTN THEN risk is High"). This produces a transparent, causal narrative for each prediction that clinicians can verify against their expertise, reducing the "black-box" perception.
*   **Core assumption:** Clinicians trust explanations that reflect familiar, structured clinical reasoning patterns more than statistical feature weights alone.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The paper states it uses a "hybrid explainable AI framework... combining ante-hoc fuzzy logic with post-hoc SHAP explanations" and reports "71.4% preference for the hybrid explanation format." Section III.B describes the 12 fuzzy rules. Section IV.D includes qualitative feedback where clinicians valued "transparent reasoning showing *why* predictions were made."

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** SHAP (post-hoc) explanations complement fuzzy rules by quantifying the relative influence of *all* features, including non-linear interactions learned by XGBoost, thus providing a complete picture of model behavior.
*   **Mechanism:** SHAP values assign each feature a contribution score for a specific prediction, highlighting which data points most drove the risk assessment. This addresses the "what matters" question beyond the limited variables in any single fuzzy rule, revealing dependencies clinicians might overlook.
*   **Core assumption:** Clinicians need to understand both the *logical pathway* (fuzzy rules) and the *feature importance hierarchy* (SHAP) to fully interrogate and accept an AI's output.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes "SHAP analysis identified regional healthcare access as the top predictor." Section IV.B directly compares hybrid explanations (A) against SHAP-only (B), finding a clear preference for the hybrid, implying SHAP alone was insufficient for trust.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The synergy between ante-hoc (fuzzy) and post-hoc (SHAP) explanations creates a self-validating loop: fuzzy rules ground the model in clinical knowledge, while SHAP validates that the learned model respects or adjusts that knowledge in data-driven ways, enhancing perceived reliability.
*   **Mechanism:** The engineered fuzzy risk score is included as a feature. If XGBoost assigns it high SHAP importance (it ranked 3rd), it signals that the model's predictions align with the symbolic rule system. This alignment is empirically shown by the correlation (r=0.298) between fuzzy scores and actual risk.
*   **Core assumption:** Trust increases when two independent explanation modalities (knowledge-based rules and data-driven attributions) converge on similar conclusions about what drives risk.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the fuzzy score ranked third, "validating clinical knowledge integration (r=0.298)." Section V.A discusses how the multi-modal approach addresses different explanatory questions ("why," "what matters," "for this specific case").

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Fuzzy Logic for Clinical Reasoning**
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire ante-hoc layer depends on translating vague, linguistic clinical guidelines into mathematical membership functions and inference rules. Without this, the "transparent reasoning" component cannot exist.
    *   **Quick check:** How would you design a fuzzy membership function for "advanced maternal age" that reflects clinical uncertainty about the exact risk threshold?
*   **Concept: SHAP Values for Feature Attribution**
    *   **Why needed here:** SHAP explains the *post-hoc* XGBoost model. Understanding its additive, game-theoretic foundation is key to interpreting why features like "healthcare access" dominate predictions.
    *   **Quick check:** If the SHAP value for "Blood Pressure" is +0.15 in a high-risk prediction, what does that number represent in practical terms?
*   **Concept: Clinical Validation Study Design for XAI**
    *   **Why needed here:** The paper's core contribution is the *clinician-validated* trust outcome. This requires understanding within-subject designs, Likert scales, and qualitative coding.
    *   **Quick check:** Why is it a limitation that all 14 participants had <5 years experience, and how would that bias the preference for fuzzy rule explanations?
*   **Concept: Hybrid XAI Architecture**
    *   **Why needed here:** The novelty lies in strategically combining two distinct explanation paradigms (symbolic ante-hoc and statistical post-hoc) to address different clinician cognitive needs.
    *   **Quick check:** What are the risks of having both a fuzzy rule and a SHAP plot that seem to contradict each other for the same prediction?
*   **Concept: Feature Engineering in Low-Data Settings**
    *   **Why needed here:** The study used only 1,014 records. Creating a robust engineered feature (the fuzzy score) from limited, potentially noisy data is a key methodological challenge.
    *   **Quick check:** How might you validate that the engineered fuzzy risk score is clinically meaningful before including it in the predictive model?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Data Ingestion → Feature Engineering (incl. Fuzzy Score Module) → XGBoost Training → SHAP/LIME Explainer → Explanation Composer (merges fuzzy rules + SHAP plots + clinical params) → Web/Mobile UI for Clinician.
*   **Critical path:** 1) Implement fuzzy inference system with 12 rules based on guidelines. 2) Train XGBoost on 8 features (6 clinical + healthcare access + fuzzy score). 3) Generate global SHAP summary and local LIME plots. 4) Package all three explanation types into a single case-view interface. 5) Run clinician A/B test to measure preference and trust.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** *Simplicity vs. completeness:* Fuzzy rules cover few variables; more rules increase transparency but may cause conflicts. *Local vs. global focus:* LIME provides case-specific detail but may be unstable; SHAP is consistent but can be overwhelming. *Performance vs. interpretability:* XGBoost is high-performing but not inherently interpretable; the hybrid trades pure interpretability for accuracy.
*   **Failure signatures:** 1) Low hybrid preference (<50%) in validation indicates explanations are not complementary or are confusing. 2) Fuzzy-SHAP rank mismatch (low SHAP for fuzzy score) signals model divergence from clinical logic. 3) High "maybe/conditional trust" suggests missing critical features (as clinicians noted). 4) Clarity scores <2.5 for complex cases indicate UI/visualization failure.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Fuzzy rule sanity check:** Manually compute fuzzy risk scores for 10 varied synthetic cases. Verify outputs align with clinical intuition (e.g., older patient with high BP should score higher). If not, tune membership functions.
    2.  **SHAP ranking validation:** After training, extract global SHAP values. Confirm healthcare access and fuzzy score are top 3 features. If not, investigate feature scaling or potential data leakage.
    3.  **Explanation preference pilot:** Deploy a minimal survey with 3-5 clinician colleagues showing only two explanation types (Hybrid vs. SHAP-only) for one case. Measure preference and clarity. If hybrid is not clearly preferred, revise the fusion UI before full validation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question:** How do explanation preferences and trust thresholds vary with clinician seniority and specialty beyond the early-career sample studied?
    *   **Basis:** The study limitations note all 14 participants had <5 years experience; senior clinicians may have different explanation needs and trust thresholds, but this was not tested.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The current validation only captures perspectives from early-career professionals in specific roles, limiting understanding of how experience and specialization affect XAI utility and trust.
*   **Question:** Does incorporating the clinician-identified missing features (obstetric history, gestational age, fetal parameters) improve predictive performance, explanation quality, and trust compared to the current model?
    *   **Basis:** Clinicians identified these as "critical gaps." The model currently uses a dataset lacking these features.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper does not evaluate whether adding these features changes SHAP/fuzzy rule outputs, model accuracy (ROC-AUC), or clinician trust. It remains unknown if these features are merely desired for completeness or are essential for clinically valid predictions.
*   **Question:** Can an offline-capable, local-language mobile implementation of the hybrid XAI framework overcome the identified connectivity and training barriers to achieve practical adoption in rural Bangladeshi settings?
    *   **Basis:** Connectivity (50%) and training (28.6%) were cited as key adoption barriers. Future work proposes "offline-capable mobile implementations with local language support."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The current study used an online survey platform; the feasibility, usability, and trust implications of a deployed, offline system in low-connectivity environments remain untested.
*   **Question:** What specific components of the hybrid explanation (fuzzy rules, SHAP, LIME, integrated parameters) are most responsible for building trust, and can explanations be adaptively tailored to individual clinician cognitive styles?
    *   **Basis:** While hybrid explanations were preferred (71.4%), the paper states trust is "multi-faceted" and mentions different users may need different explanations. It does not isolate the causal contribution of each component or test adaptivity.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The preference result is aggregate; it is unclear which modality drives trust for which user, and whether a one-size-fits-all hybrid is optimal or if personalized explanation generation would further enhance trust and utility.

## Limitations
*   **Feature completeness:** Clinicians identified missing critical predictors (obstetric history, gestational age), questioning whether the model's explanation addresses the full clinical decision space.
*   **Generalizability:** Validation limited to 14 junior Bangladeshi clinicians (<5 years experience); results may not generalize to senior practitioners or other low-resource settings.
*   **Weak alignment signal:** The fuzzy risk score’s low correlation with actual risk (r=0.298) suggests limited empirical validation of the rule-based layer’s clinical relevance.

## Confidence
*   **Model performance claim (88.67% accuracy, AUC 0.9703):** High
*   **Clinician preference for hybrid explanations (71.4%):** Medium
*   **Healthcare access as top SHAP predictor:** Medium
*   **Fuzzy score ranking/clinical utility:** Low

## Next Checks
1.  **Feature gap assessment:** Quantify the predictive value of clinician-missing features via ablation study; if data permits, retrain model including obstetric history/gestational age and re-evaluate trust metrics.
2.  **Generalizability test:** Replicate the clinician validation in a distinct low-resource setting (e.g., rural India) with mixed experience levels to check preference/trust stability.
3.  **Rule clinical validity audit:** Conduct a Delphi study with independent obstetric experts to score the 12 fuzzy rules against current guidelines, measuring alignment beyond the original development team’s input.
