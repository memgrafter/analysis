# DECADE: A Temporally-Consistent Unsupervised Diffusion Model for Enhanced Rb-82 Dynamic Cardiac PET Image Denoising

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07759
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07759
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** DECADE achieves superior unsupervised denoising on low-count (15%) Rb-82 dynamic PET, outperforming self-supervised UNet with SSIM 0.73 vs. 0.68, PSNR 30.64 vs. 27.82, and the lowest MBF percentage error (11.61% rest, 8.49% stress).

## Executive Summary
DECADE is an unsupervised diffusion model for denoising dynamic Rb-82 cardiac PET frames, which are notoriously noisy and lack paired clean training data. It employs a two-stage approach: first pre-training a 3D diffusion model on low-noise mean static images to learn a clean anatomical prior, then fine-tuning it with ControlNet conditioned on temporally smoothed consecutive frames to enforce consistency. During sampling, deep posterior sampling (DPS) quantitatively anchors the generation to the original noisy frame. Evaluated on Vision 450 and Quadra datasets, DECADE significantly reduces noise while preserving critical myocardial blood flow (MBF) and myocardial flow reserve (MFR) quantification metrics compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
DECADE addresses the unsupervised dynamic PET denoising problem via a two-phase diffusion framework. **Phase A** pre-trains a 3D denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) U-Net on low-noise mean static PET images (2-7 minute summed frames), establishing a generative prior for clean cardiac anatomy and tracer distribution. **Phase B** freezes this pre-trained model and attaches a trainable ControlNet branch. The ControlNet is fine-tuned on dynamic frames, using three consecutive Gaussian-smoothed noisy frames as conditional input to inject temporal context and consistency. For inference on a new noisy dynamic frame `y`, the sampling process starts from noise and uses the standard reverse diffusion step. A deep posterior sampling (DPS) regularization term is applied at each timestep, pulling the intermediate estimate toward `y` to preserve quantitative fidelity. The method switches from the unconditional pre-trained U-Net (early timesteps, `t > 950`) to the temporal ControlNet (later timesteps, `t ≤ 950`) to balance low-frequency recovery with high-frequency detail and temporal coherence.

## Key Results
*   On the Quadra dataset (15% count vs. full-count reference), DECADE achieved SSIM 0.73, PSNR 30.64, and NMSE 0.29, surpassing a self-supervised UNet (0.68, 27.82, 0.40).
*   It yielded the lowest MBF percentage error among compared methods (11.61% for rest, 8.49% for stress).
*   Ablations confirmed the necessity of all components: pre-training on static images, temporal ControlNet conditioning, and DPS guidance during sampling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Pre-training a 3D diffusion model on low-noise mean static PET images provides a prior for clean cardiac anatomy, enabling unsupervised generalization to dynamic frames.
*   **Mechanism:** The model learns the data distribution of pseudo-clean static images during Phase A, establishing a generative prior that captures typical myocardial structures and tracer uptake patterns without requiring paired noisy-clean dynamic data.
*   **Core assumption:** The anatomical and uptake distribution in mean static images sufficiently represents the clean target distribution for dynamic frames.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "unsupervised learning approach involves pre-training a diffusion model on mean static images with reduced noise to capture the clean data distribution."
    *   [section II.B]: "Phase A... leverages widely available low-noise static cardiac PET images for pre-training to learn the clean data distribution."
*   **Break condition:** If the statistical distribution of mean static images diverges significantly from clean dynamic frames (e.g., due to rapid tracer washout in early frames), the prior may misguide denoising, causing hallucinations in low-uptake regions.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Fine-tuning the pre-trained model with ControlNet using Gaussian-smoothed consecutive frames injects temporal context, reducing artifacts and ensuring temporal consistency.
*   **Mechanism:** During Phase B, three consecutive noisy frames (smoothed to reduce noise) are used as conditional inputs via ControlNet. The trainable copy learns to align the static-prior model with dynamic data while preserving temporal coherence, as anatomical structures change smoothly.
*   **Core assumption:** Adjacent frames share sufficient anatomical consistency that smoothing them provides a reliable temporal constraint.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "fine-tunes it with temporal consistency via ControlNet, conditioning on consecutive Gaussian-smoothed frames."
    *   [section II.E]: "Three consecutive Gaussian-smoothed noisy frames are used as control inputs to guide the denoising process and ensure temporal consistency."
*   **Break condition:** If temporal smoothing over-blurs rapid kinetic changes (e.g., ventricular filling), the model may underestimate fast intensity variations, leading to smoothed time-activity curves.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Deep posterior sampling (DPS) during the diffusion reverse process incorporates the original noisy frame as a quantitative anchor, preserving measurement fidelity while allowing generative denoising.
*   **Mechanism:** At each sampling step, a regularization term `ρ∇||y−x̂₀||²` pulls the intermediate denoised estimate toward the noisy input `y`, ensuring consistency with observed data. An exponential decay scheduler emphasizes this constraint early (recovering low-frequency structure) and relies more on the diffusion prior later (recovering high-frequency details).
*   **Core assumption:** The noisy measurement `y` provides a valid constraint for `x₀` despite noise, as the forward model is approximately Gaussian after reconstruction.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "During sampling, it incorporates image guidance from the original noisy frame via deep posterior sampling."
    *   [section II.D]: "For PET denoising... image consistency is enforced during the sampling process using DPS."
    *   [section II.D, Eq. 14]: Explicit formulation of the DPS update step.
*   **Break condition:** If the initial noisy frame `y` is extremely low-count (<5% counts), the gradient may be too noisy, causing overfitting to noise or local minima.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Pre-trained 3D U-Net (DDPM) -> ControlNet (trainable copy) -> DPS Sampler (with guidance from original noisy frame `y` and condition `c` from 3 smoothed frames)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Train pre-trained 3D U-Net on mean static images (Phase A).
    2.  Freeze U-Net, attach and fine-tune ControlNet on dynamic frames using 3 consecutive smoothed frames as condition (Phase B).
    3.  For inference on a noisy frame `y` with temporal context `c`: initialize noise `x_T`; for `t=1000→1`:
        *   Predict noise: `ϵ = ϵ_θ(x_t, t)` if `t>950` else `ϵ = ϵ_θ(x_t, t, c)`.
        *   Compute `x̂₀ = (x_t + (1−ᾱ_t)ϵ)/√ᾱ_t`.
        *   Standard DDIM reverse step to `x_{t-1}`.
        *   Apply DPS correction: `ρ = exp(−0.05(1000−t)) * 500 / ‖y−x̂₀‖₂²; x_{t-1} = x_{t-1} − ρ * ∇_{x_t} ‖y−x̂₀‖₂²`.
    4.  Return final `x̂₀`.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Memory vs. Temporal Detail:* Using 3 consecutive 3D frames increases GPU memory but improves consistency. Using only the current frame saves memory but risks artifacts.
    *   *Noise Level vs. Quantification:* Heavy DPS constraint preserves low-frequency quantification but may limit high-frequency perceptual quality; the exponential decay scheduler balances this.
    *   *Pre-training Data Domain:* Static images are abundant and cleaner but mismatched to early dynamic frames (rapid changes); this mismatch is mitigated by Phase B fine-tuning.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Hallucinated structures in low-uptake regions** (e.g., septum, cavities) if DPS weight too low or ControlNet under-trained.
    *   **Over-smoothed time-activity curves** if temporal smoothing (Gaussian on consecutive frames) is too aggressive.
    *   **Quantification bias** (over/under-estimation of MBF) if DPS constraint weight `ρ` is mis-calibrated for the noise level of input `y`.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Ablation of DPS:** Run sampling with only the pre-trained/ControlNet model (no `y`-guidance). Expect degraded SSIM/PSNR and MBF error as shown in Table IV (DPS Only row).
    2.  **Temporal control ablation:** Compare DECADE (3-frame ControlNet) vs. "single frame control" (using only current smoothed frame). Expect lower SSIM and visible artifacts in cavity boundaries (Fig. 6, cyan/purple arrows).
    3.  **Switch timestep `t_c` sensitivity:** Vary `t_c` (e.g., 900, 950, 1000). Expect early switch (lower `t_c`) to degrade high-frequency recovery; late switch (higher `t_c`) to cause artifacts from unconditional model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How does increasing the number of consecutive frames used for temporal conditioning (beyond three) affect DECADE's denoising performance, especially in challenging low-uptake regions? The paper states only three consecutive frames are currently used and that utilizing temporal information from the entire acquisition could enhance learning.
*   **Open Question 2:** Can incorporating anatomical priors from co-registered CT or segmentation masks further improve DECADE's performance by providing stronger structural constraints during sampling? The authors acknowledge CT scans are available and could provide accurate anatomical contours.
*   **Open Question 3:** Would pre-training the diffusion model on a mixture of early dynamic frames (with higher counts) and static images improve generalization to all dynamic phases compared to pre-training on static images alone? The limitation section notes early frames are less noisy but not used for pre-training because "clean images for early frames do not exist."
*   **Open Question 4:** What is the sensitivity of DECADE's performance to the choice of the intermediate time step `t_c` (switching from unconditional to ControlNet), and could an adaptive or learned `t_c` improve results? The paper sets `t_c = 950` empirically without justification or sensitivity analysis.
*   **Open Question 5:** Does DECADE generalize to other PET tracers (e.g., 15O-water, 18F-flurpiridaz) with different kinetics and noise characteristics without retraining or with minimal fine-tuning? The paper states validation was only on 82Rb data and notes "rapid tracer kinetics" as a specific challenge.

## Limitations
*   **Architectural underspecification:** Exact 3D U-Net depth, channel dimensions, and ControlNet condition embedding details (kernel sizes, channel dimensions) are not provided, blocking exact replication.
*   **Static-to-dynamic transfer assumption:** The core premise that mean static images provide a valid prior for dynamic frames lacks direct validation; distribution shift between static and early-phase dynamic frames is a significant risk.
*   **Parameter calibration for low counts:** The DPS constraint weight schedule was likely tuned for ~15% count data; its robustness for ultra-low-count (<5%) regimes is unknown.

## Confidence
*   **Static Pre-training Prior:** Medium. Mechanistically plausible and supported by pre-training results, but direct evidence for effective static-to-dynamic transfer is inferential.
*   **Temporal Consistency via ControlNet:** Medium-High. ControlNet is a proven framework; its specific application to temporal smoothing in dynamic PET is novel but logically sound.
*   **Deep Posterior Sampling (DPS) for Fidelity:** High. DPS is a well-established inverse problem solver; its integration is clearly described. Confidence is moderate for dynamic PET due to potential TAC distortion if `ρ` is mis-calibrated.

## Next Checks
1.  **Ablation: No Static Pre-training.** Train Phase B (ControlNet fine-tuning) from random initialization without Phase A. Expect significant degradation in SSIM/PSNR and MBF error, confirming the necessity of the static prior.
2.  **Boundary Condition Test for ControlNet.** Compare DECADE's default frame-padding (likely replication) against explicit mirror-padding or skipping edge frames. Measure artifact incidence at sequence boundaries (first/last 2 frames).
3.  **DPS Weight Sensitivity at Ultra-Low Counts.** On a held-out set simulated at 1-5% counts, vary the base weight `w` (e.g., 100, 500, 2000) and decay rate. Monitor NMSE and MBF bias to identify failure thresholds.
