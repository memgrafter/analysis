# Pupillometry and Brain Dynamics for Cognitive Load in Working Memory

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10614
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10614
- **Reference Count:** 24
- **Primary Result:** Feature-based pupillometry models (XGBoost) achieve competitive accuracy (binary: 61.3%, 4-class: 52.3%) with high computational efficiency, while EEG models reach near-perfect multiclass accuracy (~99.8%) but risk overfitting.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates practical cognitive load monitoring by comparing pupillometry and EEG. Using the OpenNeuro Digit Span dataset, the authors apply Catch-22 feature extraction combined with classical machine learning (Random Forest, XGBoost, SVM) and deep learning models, incorporating SHAP for interpretability. Pupillometry-based XGBoost models yield competitive classification accuracy while being computationally lightweight, whereas EEG models approach perfect multiclass accuracy but may overfit. The work demonstrates that interpretable, feature-based pupillometry can serve as a portable, effective proxy for EEG in real-world cognitive load assessment, though challenges remain for low-engagement states and multimodal fusion.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes the OpenNeuro ds003838 dataset (64 participants). Pupillometry (120 Hz) undergoes confidence-based filtering and task-aligned epoching; EEG (1000 Hz, Fz channel) is artifact-rejected and normalized. Catch-22 extracts 22 time-series features per epoch. Classical ML (RF, XGBoost, SVM) and deep models (CNN, LSTM) are trained on these features or raw sequences, with SMOTE for class imbalance. Evaluation uses accuracy, F1-scores, and training time. SHAP explains tree-based model decisions.

## Key Results
- Pupillometry XGBoost: binary (just listen vs. memory) accuracy 61.3%, 4-class (graded load) accuracy 52.3%.
- EEG feature-based models: multiclass accuracy 99.8%, but cross-validation/test similarity suggests possible overfitting.
- Pupillometry classical ML outperforms deep learning (CNN/LSTM) in efficiency and often accuracy; deep models train orders of magnitude slower and sometimes underperform.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Catch-22 Feature Efficacy
Catch-22 computes 22 minimally redundant descriptors (distributional, spectral, dynamical) from pupil epochs, capturing systematic load-dependent changes (e.g., dilation magnitude, temporal irregularity). This transforms raw high-frequency data into a low-dimensional, discriminative representation suitable for classical ML. The core assumption is that pupil dynamics reflect locus coeruleus–norepinephrine activity, manifesting in measurable statistical properties.

### Mechanism 2: SHAP Interpretability Validation
SHAP allocates feature importance for tree ensembles (e.g., XGBoost). Aggregated SHAP values identify pupillometry features like `DN_HistogramMode_5/10` (distribution shape), `CO_trev_1_num` (asymmetry), and `SB_BinaryStats_mean_longstretch1` (sustained dilation runs) as top predictors. These align with known psychophysiological mechanisms (baseline shifts, slope asymmetry) of cognitive effort, validating model decisions.

### Mechanism 3: Classical ML vs. Deep Learning Efficiency
Feature-based models avoid deep learning's data-hungry nature on the modest dataset (~64 subjects). Catch-22 pre-extracts invariant properties, reducing dimensionality and variance. Classical ML generalizes better, trains faster (XGBoost ~1.5s vs. LSTM ~900s), and often achieves higher accuracy than CNN/LSTM on raw sequences, which overfit or underfit.

  *Quick check:* What confidence thresholds are used for sample and epoch exclusion, and what artifact do they primarily target?
- **Catch-22 Feature Extraction**  
  *Why needed:* Provides fixed-length, interpretable representation from variable-length temporal signals; essential for classical ML and apparently more robust than raw data for this dataset.  
  *Quick check:* Name three categories of properties (e.g., distributional, spectral) that Catch-22 captures, and give one SHAP-top pupillometry feature.
- **SHAP for Tree-Based Interpretability**  
  *Why needed:* Explains model decisions, linking predictions to physiologically meaningful features and building trust in neurophysiological monitoring.  
  *Quick check:* What does `SB_BinaryStats_mean_longstretch1` likely quantify in cognitive load?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Acquisition (OpenNeuro ds003838) -> Preprocessing (pupillometry confidence filtering, EEG artifact rejection) -> Feature Extraction (Catch-22) -> Classification (RF, XGBoost, SVM, CNN, LSTM) -> Interpretability (SHAP) -> Evaluation (accuracy, F1, time).
- **Critical path:** Preprocessing alignment (ensuring pupillometry timestamps match events) and consistent feature extraction parameters are most failure-prone; errors propagate downstream.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Accuracy vs. Efficiency:* XGBoost balances both; EEG models are nearly perfect but may overfit; pupillometry is portable but less accurate.
  - *Interpretability vs. Performance:* Deep models sacrifice interpretability and speed for marginal or no accuracy gain.
  - *Modality:* Pupillometry chosen for wearability; EEG as high-performance benchmark.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - "Just Listen" class consistently has F1 <0.20, indicating poor separability of low-engagement states.
  - EEG multiclass CV/test accuracy nearly identical and extremely high (~99.8%) suggests possible data leakage or overfitting.
  - Pupillometry deep learning (CNN/LSTM) performs near chance on 4-class (41.4%/46.2%) while XGBoost reaches 52.3%.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate preprocessing and Catch-22 extraction on one subject's pupillometry epoch; verify feature vector shape (22,) and finiteness.
  2. Benchmark XGBoost on binary pupillometry; target ~61% accuracy, ~1.5s training; inspect SHAP top features.
  3. Test "Just Listen" separability by training a binary model on just listen vs. memory/5; expect ~50% accuracy if features are inadequate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multimodal fusion for low-engagement detection:** How to combine EEG and pupillometry to improve detection of states like "Just Listen"? Unresolved because unimodal F1 <0.20; requires feature-, decision-, or deep-level fusion with SHAP analysis.
- **Beyond Catch-22 features for low engagement:** Which additional features (e.g., micro-saccades, spectral asymmetry) can better distinguish low-load states? Unresolved due to consistent poor recall; requires engineering and validation.
- **Attention-based hybrid deep models:** Can lightweight attention modules balance accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability? Unresolved; current deep models are slower and less interpretable, classical models may miss temporal patterns.
- **Generalizability to mental health applications:** Are pupillometry/EEG cognitive load biomarkers generalizable to depression diagnosis? Unresolved; only healthy participants studied; requires patient data replication and comparison with clinical scales.

## Limitations
- Train-test split ratio and stratification method (subject-wise vs. epoch-wise) unspecified; risk of data leakage, especially given EEG's near-perfect scores.
- Pupillometry binocular aggregation strategy (average vs. concatenate) ambiguous; affects reproducibility.
- SMOTE variant used for imbalance correction undisclosed; may influence results for minority classes.
- Random seeds for splits and model initialization not provided; replication variance possible.
- Findings based on a single dataset (OpenNeuro ds003838, 64 subjects); limited generalizability.
- "Just Listen" condition consistently poorly classified, indicating feature inadequacy or task confound.
- Claim that feature-based pupillometry universally outperforms deep learning may not hold with larger datasets or improved architectures.

## Confidence
- **Pupillometry + Catch-22 + classical ML yields competitive accuracy:** Medium. Supported by internal results but lacks external validation and suffers small sample size.
- **EEG feature models achieve near-perfect multiclass accuracy:** Medium for reported numbers, Low for generalizability due to overfitting indicators and split ambiguity.
- **Pupillometry outperforms deep learning:** Medium. Paper shows advantage, but deep learning could improve with more data or tuning; binary/multiclass comparisons slightly inconsistent.
- **SHAP provides physiologically meaningful insights:** Low. SHAP top features align with known mechanisms, but no independent physiological validation presented.

## Next Checks
1. **Subject-wise stratified evaluation:** Implement splits where no subject appears in both train and test; recompute metrics to detect leakage and assess true generalizability.
2. **Compare binocular aggregation strategies:** Test both averaging and concatenation of pupillometry features; determine impact on accuracy and whether paper's choice affects conclusions.
3. **External replication:** Apply the exact pipeline to an independent working-memory pupillometry dataset; verify if similar accuracy and SHAP feature importance patterns hold.
