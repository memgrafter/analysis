# Statistical Learning Theory in Lean 4: Empirical Processes from Scratch

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02285
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02285
- **Reference count**: 24
- **Primary result**: First formalization of empirical process-based generalization analysis in Lean 4, yielding sharp minimax rates for linear and ℓ1-constrained regression.

## Executive Summary
This work builds a verified foundation for statistical learning theory in Lean 4, addressing the absence of formalized empirical process tools for generalization analysis. Through a human–AI collaboration—humans devise proof strategies and decompose theorems, while AI (Claude Code Opus-4.5) executes the tactical proof construction—the authors develop approximately 30,000 lines of Lean 4 code from scratch. The formalization introduces a Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality and complete Gaussian concentration toolbox, proves Dudley’s entropy integral theorem for sub-Gaussian processes, and constructs a localized empirical process framework. Applied to least-squares regression, this yields sharp minimax rates: O(σ² r/n) for linear regression (with design matrix rank r) and O(R² log d/n) for ℓ1-constrained regression. The process also uncovered implicit assumptions (e.g., measurability, integrability) often omitted in textbook statements, resulting in a reusable, fully verified library for machine learning theory.

## Method Summary
The project follows a dependency-driven development order. Starting from measure-theoretic probability, the team first develops the Gaussian analysis pipeline: Efron–Stein inequality → Gaussian Poincaré inequality → logarithmic Sobolev inequality → Gaussian Lipschitz concentration. In parallel, they build the metric entropy toolkit: covering numbers and Dudley’s chaining argument → entropy integral theorem for sub-Gaussian processes. Finally, these two branches are combined into a localized empirical process framework, yielding a master error bound that is instantiated for linear and ℓ1-constrained regression. The human–AI workflow allocates high-level decomposition to humans and low-level proof search to AI, ensuring rigorous oversight while leveraging automation. The codebase depends on the latest Lean 4 Mathlib and targets total verification (no `sorry` or `axiom`).

## Key Results
- First formalization of the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality and a complete Gaussian concentration toolbox.
- First formal Dudley entropy integral theorem for sub-Gaussian processes.
- Localized empirical process framework applied to least-squares regression, producing sharp minimax rates O(σ² r/n) for linear regression (rank r) and O(R² log d/n) for ℓ1-constrained regression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success stems from a disciplined decomposition of complex theorems into manageable lemmas and a strictly enforced dependency order. By constructing the Gaussian concentration and Dudley entropy integral components in parallel, the longest sequential paths are shortened. The human–AI partnership ensures that high-level strategic choices (e.g., selecting dyadic nets, defining localized balls) are made by domain experts, while the AI handles the generation of routine proof steps, reducing human fatigue and error. Crucially, the formalization process forced the explicit addition of usually implicit hypotheses (such as integrability and path continuity), which both guarantees correctness and reveals gaps in standard textbook presentations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Measure-theoretic probability**: Why needed: Formalizes probability spaces, random variables, and expectations; all subsequent definitions rely on measurable functions and integrals. Quick check: Verify all random variables are `Measurable` and expectations are defined via `Integral`.
- **Efron–Stein inequality**: Why needed: Provides variance control for functions of independent random variables; basis for concentration and Poincaré inequalities. Quick check: Lemma `efronStein` states `Variance (f X) ≤ ∑ᵢ ess supₓ Variance (f X | Xᵢ = xᵢ)`.
- **Gaussian Poincaré inequality**: Why needed: Relates variance of smooth functions to squared gradient norm; a prerequisite for deriving logarithmic Sobolev inequality. Quick check: `gaussianPoincare` shows `∫ ‖∇f‖² dγ ≤ ∫ f² dγ - (∫ f dγ)²`.
- **Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (LSI)**: Why needed: Implies Gaussian concentration; central to proving Lipschitz concentration and controlling deviations. Quick check: `gaussian_logSobolev_W12_pi` states `Ent_γ(f²) ≤ 2 ∫ ‖∇f‖² dγ`.
- **Covering numbers & metric entropy**: Why needed: Quantify complexity of function classes; essential for Dudley’s theorem and capacity control in learning bounds. Quick check: Definition `coveringNumber F eps d` returns a `WithTop Nat` satisfying the ε-covering property.
- **Empirical processes & localized Gaussian complexity**: Why needed: Bridge between probabilistic tools and learning theory; enable conversion from entropy integrals to prediction error bounds. Quick check: `LocalGaussianComplexity` combines supremum of empirical process with a localized ball; `master_error_bound` then controls estimation error.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Measure Theory → Probability → (Efron–Stein → Gaussian Poincaré → Gaussian LSI → Gaussian Concentration) and (Metric Spaces → Covering Numbers → Dudley Chaining → Entropy Integral → Sub-Gaussian Processes) → Localized Empirical Process → Master Error Bound → Regression Applications.
- **Critical path**: The overall schedule is determined by the longer of the two parallel branches (Gaussian analysis or Dudley entropy) plus the final integration. Neither branch can begin without basic measure theory, and the integration step waits for both to finish.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Human–AI division: humans handle decomposition and high-level strategy; AI performs tactical proof search, balancing oversight with efficiency.
  - Building from scratch: avoids unverified external dependencies but increases initial effort.
  - Dyadic net construction: trades a slight constant-factor complexity for a clear recursive proof structure.
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - Integrability mismatches when extending smooth/compact support results to Lipschitz functions (missing density lemmas).
  - Covering number edge cases (`n ≤ 1` yielding `0`) leading to type errors; always require hypotheses `0 < ε` and `0 ≤ R`.
  - Inaccurate constants in Dudley’s theorem if projection step is misimplemented (e.g., using `ε_k` instead of `3/2 ε_k`).
  - Unproved path continuity assumptions on restricted functions.
  - Critical inequality not automatically satisfied; must derive from capacity control.
- **Exactly 3 first experiments**: 
  1. Set up Lean 4 environment, clone the repository, and achieve a clean build (no `sorry`).
  2. Replicate the Gaussian pipeline: formalize `condExpExceptCoord`, prove `efronStein`, `gaussianPoincare`, `gaussian_logSobolev_W12_pi`, and `gaussian_lipschitz_concentration`.
  3. Replicate Dudley’s theorem: define `IsENet`, `coveringNumber`, `DyadicNets`, and prove the three-stage chaining argument to obtain `dudley`.
