# SMGI: A Structural Theory of General Artificial Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07896
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07896
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Formalizes AGI as stability-preserving extensibility of the learning interface via a typed meta-model and four admissibility obligations.

## Executive Summary
Existing AGI theories typically fix the learning interface, lacking formalism for systems that dynamically evolve their representation, hypothesis space, evaluators, and memory across tasks. SMGI addresses this by introducing a typed meta-model θ = (r, H, Π, L, E, M) that explicitly encodes these components as first-class dynamic entities. General intelligence is characterized as admissible coupled dynamics (θ, T_θ) satisfying four obligations: (i) structural closure under admissible task transformations, (ii) dynamical stability (Lyapunov control), (iii) bounded statistical capacity (PAC-Bayes/MDL), and (iv) evaluative invariance under regime shifts. This reframes AGI as a problem of designing systems that extend their learning interface while preserving stability, capacity, and coherence, yielding a generalization bound that unifies sequential complexity, drift, and transformation magnitude.

## Method Summary
SMGI defines a meta-model θ with six typed components: representation map r, hypothesis class H, prior Π, evaluator family L, environment family E, and memory M. The system's behavior is governed by coupled dynamics T_θ acting on the structural state S_θ = R(θ)×H(θ)×Π(θ)×M(θ). Admissibility requires that for every transformation τ in a declared class T, (i) the interface remains well-typed (closure), (ii) trajectories remain bounded (stability via Lyapunov witness V), (iii) statistical capacity grows in a controlled way (sequential PAC-Bayes/MDL), and (iv) a protected evaluator core C ⊆ L stays invariant. The theory thus shifts focus from static algorithms to designing θ and T_θ such that these four obligations hold simultaneously across all admissible tasks and regime switches.

## Key Results
- **Structural generalization bound**: Excess risk R(Q) is bounded by a sum of sequential PAC-Bayes complexity, Lyapunov drift term (c_V∑E[V(s_t)]/n), transformation magnitude L_ℓ ε_max, and a capacity term √(KL(Q||P)+ln(2√n/δ)/(2(n-1))), tightly coupling generalization to stability and transformation.
- **Strict inclusion theorem**: Classical frameworks—empirical risk minimization, reinforcement learning, Solomonoff induction, and frontier agent pipelines—are shown to be structurally restricted instances of SMGI, where some components are frozen or obligations trivially hold, demonstrating SMGI's expressive breadth.
- **AGI as extensibility criterion**: The theory redefines AGI not by capability but by the ability to extend the learning interface while preserving the four obligations, providing a formal, testable condition for general intelligence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Structural closure under transformations
The meta-model θ declares a transformation class T acting on the environment family E(θ). For any τ ∈ T, the representation r must satisfy alignment conditions (e.g., r(τ·z) = Γ(τ)(r(z))) so that the induced dynamics T_θ,τ maps the invariant set S*_θ into itself, preserving well-typedness of H, Π, and M. Break condition: r not T-aligned or sup_e D_E(e, τ(e)) > ε_max.

### Dynamical stability via Lyapunov witness
A nonnegative function V: S_θ → ℝ₊ with constants α ∈ (0,1], β ≥ 0 satisfies E[V(s_{t+1})|ℱ_t] ≤ (1-α)V(s_t) + β along all admissible trajectories, bounding drift despite memory updates and regime switches. Witness must cover full structural state S_θ; break condition: absence of certified V or regime-induced unbounded oscillations.

### Evaluative invariance through protected core
A subset C ⊆ L of evaluators must remain projectively invariant: Π_C(ℓ_{t+1}) = Π_C(ℓ_t). Updates are permitted only via a certified operator CertUpdate_Φ that enforces constraints Φ on an audit set, decoupling descriptive adaptation from normative consistency. Break condition: no protected core or regime switches arbitrarily replace ℓ_t without satisfying Φ.

## Foundational Learning
- **Typed meta-model θ**: Explicitly separates representation, hypothesis, prior, evaluator, environment, and memory as first-class typed components. Enables well-typed statements about transformation and updates. Quick check: When τ changes task distribution, which components may change, and which must preserve a certificate? (E(θ) may change; r, H, M must stay well-defined; C ⊆ L invariant.)
- **Coupled dynamics (θ, T_θ)**: Separates structural ontology from behavioral semantics. Admissibility is a property of the pair, allowing stability and invariance to be evolution properties. Quick check: Can a stable θ with poorly specified T_θ satisfy obligations? No; all obligations are properties of the realization.
- **Admissibility obligations (i)–(iv)**: Minimal checkable constraints defining SMGI: (i) closure, (ii) stability, (iii) bounded capacity, (iv) invariance. Quick check: Which obligation fails if hypothesis class grows without complexity control during task switching? (iii).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: θ (r, H, Π, L, E, M) -> T_θ (state transition kernel, regime switch operator, combined evaluator) -> S*_θ (nonempty invariant set under all τ ∈ T)

Critical path: First design θ with typed components. Then define T_θ and provide witnesses: V (Lyapunov), C(θ) (capacity functional), Φ (protected evaluator constraints). Declare transformation class T with bounds ε_max, L_r, L_ℓ. Verification: check Graph(Sem_θ) ∩ SMGI ≠ ∅, i.e., at least one realization (θ, T_θ) satisfies all obligations.

Design tradeoffs:
- Expressivity vs control: Larger regime set K or richer memory strata increase generality but require tighter drift and invariance guarantees.
- Global vs local invariants: Universal constant c_V may be loose; per-regime constants require more certificates.
- Program-prior (Π) complexity: Using description-length |θ| forces explicit complexity accounting for all components, potentially costly.

Failure signatures:
- Violating (i): Under τ ∈ T, state leaves S*_θ (e.g., r produces invalid output, memory under-specified).
- Violating (ii): Unbounded growth of V(s_t) over long horizons or after regime switches.
- Violating (iii): Capacity functional C(θ(s)) diverges during evolution, or sequential PAC-Bayes term blows up; may cause sudden performance collapse on prior regimes.
- Violating (iv): Protected constraint Φ fails on audit trajectories after regime update or during operation (e.g., safety evaluator forgotten).

First 3 experiments:
1. Closure certification under synthetic T: Use tabular POMDP with latent memory; define bounded τ changing observation function; prove S*_θ invariant and r T-aligned; verify with rollouts.
2. Stability witness validation on S*_θ: Identify candidate V(s) = ||s||² + complexity_penalty; measure drift E[V(s_{t+1})|s_t]; check α, β for battery of τ; stress-test with worst-case z-sequences.
3. Evaluative invariance audit: Implement two regimes with opposing losses (speed vs accuracy); define Φ as safety constraint; write CertUpdate that accepts new ℓ only if Φ holds on adversarial test suite; run across many regime switches and measure constraint violation rate.

## Limitations
- High theoretical complexity: Six typed components and four obligations make practical instantiation uncertain; required certificates (Lyapunov V, invariant core, bounded τ) may be unobtainable for open-ended learning.
- Lack of empirical validation: Strict inclusion theorem and generalization bound remain unapplied to modern AGI systems; minimal external uptake (avg citations 0.0).
- Loose theoretical constants: Bound parameters (ε_max, c_V) likely optimistic; predictive utility untested in realistic settings.

## Confidence
- Obligation-based AGI definition: Medium (formal but not empirically decidable)
- Generalization bound: High within proof assumptions; Medium for real-world relevance
- Strict inclusion theorem: Medium-High for demonstrated cases (ERM, RL, Solomonoff); Low for broad reframing claim across contemporary systems
- Practical instantiability: Low (no known implementations)
