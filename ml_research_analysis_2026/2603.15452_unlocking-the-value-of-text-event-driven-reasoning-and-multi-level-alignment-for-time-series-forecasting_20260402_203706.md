# Unlocking the Value of Text: Event-Driven Reasoning and Multi-Level Alignment for Time Series Forecasting
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15452  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15452  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** VoT achieves state-of-the-art performance on 10 real-world datasets, ranking first in 19 of 20 metrics against strong multimodal baselines, with particularly strong gains in event-sensitive domains like Energy and Social Good.

## Executive Summary
Time series forecasting often fails to capture abrupt, event-driven changes (e.g., economic crises) because numerical data alone lacks contextual signals. This paper introduces VoT, a dual-branch method that combines exogenous news text with endogenous statistical text to improve forecasting. The event-driven branch uses Historical In-Context Learning (HIC) to guide a large language model in extracting predictive signals from news, while the numerical branch aligns endogenous text with time series patterns via contrastive learning. A final Adaptive Frequency Fusion dynamically merges the two branches' outputs across frequency bands. Extensive experiments show VoT significantly outperforms existing multimodal forecasting methods.

## Method Summary
VoT is a dual-branch architecture. The Event-driven Reasoning branch processes exogenous news text through a generative pipeline (template generation → summarization → reasoning) using an LLM. During training, reasoning errors are corrected with ground truth and stored in a knowledge base; at inference, similar historical summaries are retrieved and their corrections injected as prompts (HIC) to improve accuracy. The Numerical branch uses a PatchTST encoder and aligns endogenous text (automatically generated from time series statistics) with decomposed time series components (trend/seasonal) via contrastive learning (Endogenous Text Alignment, ETA). Both branches produce forecasts, which are fused by Adaptive Frequency Fusion (AFF): outputs are transformed via FFT, partitioned into low/mid/high bands with adaptive thresholds, combined with learned weights, and inverted via iFFT to the final prediction. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-train PatchTST, train alignment module, then fine-tune all components.

## Key Results
- VoT achieves state-of-the-art performance across 10 real-world datasets from MM-TSFLib and a new Weather dataset.
- Ranks first in 19 of 20 metrics compared to strong multimodal baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical contributions of HIC, ETA, and AFF, with especially strong gains in Energy and Social Good domains.
- Demonstrates robustness to moderate noise/sparsity in exogenous text (10-20% degradation).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Event-driven reasoning with Historical In-Context Learning (HIC)
**Claim:** HIC extracts predictive signals from exogenous text and corrects LLM errors.  
**Mechanism:** A three-step pipeline (template generation → summarization → reasoning) converts raw news into structured insights. During training, reasoning errors are corrected with ground truth and stored in a knowledge base. At inference, similar historical summaries are retrieved via embedding similarity; their corrections are injected as prompts to guide the LLM, reducing event-related forecasting errors.  
**Core assumption:** External events drive abrupt time series changes; LLMs can reason about these events when supplied with relevant, error-informed historical examples.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 detail the generative pipeline and knowledge base construction/retrieval. Corpus shows neighboring papers explore LLM reasoning but not the specific retrieve-and-correct design.  
**Break condition:** For novel events without historical parallels, retrieved examples may be dissimilar or noisy, causing HIC to misguide the LLM and degrade performance sharply.

### Endogenous Text Alignment (ETA)
**Claim:** ETA bridges the semantic gap between time series and endogenous text via decomposed pattern extraction and contrastive learning.  
**Mechanism:** Time series statistics (mean, trend, seasonality) are converted into descriptive text. Dual-query attention extracts trend/seasonal text components, which are cross-aligned with decomposed time series (trend/seasonal) through contrastive learning. Aligned text features are projected back into the series space and fused with the original representation for numerical prediction.  
**Core assumption:** Structured textual descriptions of statistical properties can be semantically aligned with their corresponding time series patterns, enriching the representation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states endogenous text integration. Section 3.4.1 provides equations and architecture (decomposed pattern extraction, contrastive loss). Corpus suggests the explicit decomposition-and-align strategy appears novel with indirect support.  
**Break condition:** If time series decomposition is inappropriate (e.g., non-additive seasonality, irregular patterns), the forced trend/seasonal split may break alignment and introduce noise.

### Adaptive Frequency Fusion (AFF)
**Claim:** AFF dynamically combines predictions from event and numerical branches across frequency bands, leveraging complementary strengths.  
**Mechanism:** Both branch outputs are transformed to the frequency domain (FFT). The spectrum is partitioned into low, mid, and high bands (adaptively). Learnable weights for each band of each branch produce a fused spectrum, which is inverted (iFFT) to the final prediction.  
**Core assumption:** The event branch better captures low-frequency (trend) shifts, while the numerical branch excels at high-frequency fluctuations. Adaptive weighting allows dataset-specific fusion policies.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions dynamic combination across bands. Section 3.4.2 gives formulation (Equations 11-13) and notes adaptive partitioning. Corpus indicates frequency-domain fusion is used but adaptive per-band weighting for multimodal branches is less common; support mainly from paper's experiments.  
**Break condition:** Mis-specified frequency bands or overfitted weights on small validation sets can hurt performance relative to the better single branch.

## Foundational Learning
- **Time Series Decomposition (Trend/Seasonal)**  
  *Why needed:* ETA relies on additive decomposition to separate components that align with corresponding text descriptions.  
  *Quick check:* If the series exhibits non-additive interactions (e.g., multiplicative seasonality), what modifications might be needed?
- **In-Context Learning with LLMs**  
  *Why needed:* HIC uses retrieved historical summaries as in-context examples to steer LLM reasoning without fine-tuning.  
  *Quick check:* How does the similarity metric choice (e.g., cosine on embedding space) affect the relevance of retrieved examples?
- **Frequency Domain Analysis (Fourier Transform)**  
  *Why needed:* AFF operates on the frequency spectrum to decide where each branch contributes most.  
  *Quick check:* What are the trade-offs of using FFT versus more advanced spectral methods (e.g., wavelets)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Inputs (X, T_ex, T_en) -> Event Branch (Template Generator → Summary Generator → Reasoning LLM + HIC: Knowledge Base + Retriever) -> Y_event;  
  Inputs (X, T_en) -> Numerical Branch (PatchTST encoder + ETA: Dual-query attention, contrastive alignment) -> Y_num;  
  Y_event & Y_num -> AFF (FFT → band split → learnable weights → iFFT) -> Y_final.
- **Critical path:** Data flows from historical series X and exogenous text T_ex through event branch to produce Y_event; X and endogenous text T_en flow through numerical branch to produce Y_num. AFF fuses Y_event and Y_final into Y_final. HIC runs in parallel with event reasoning, retrieving corrections from knowledge base built during training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Latency (LLM API calls) vs. event information gain.  
  - Simplicity of linear decomposition vs. expressiveness of more complex pattern extraction.  
  - Fixed vs. adaptive frequency band thresholds (adaptive may overfit).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Event branch fails on novel events (no similar history in knowledge base).  
  - ETA contrastive loss fails to converge → poor alignment, numerical predictions degrade.  
  - AFF weights collapse to extreme values → effectively discarding one branch.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Ablation study: remove HIC, ETA, or AFF to measure individual contribution.  
  2. Text replacement: compare exogenous text to random/statistics-only text.  
  3. Frequency analysis: filter predictions into low/high bands to verify complementary patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How does VoT's performance degrade when exogenous text sources are sparse, incomplete, or of significantly lower quality (e.g., noisy, biased, or insufficiently descriptive) compared to the curated datasets used in the paper?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper's experiments assume the availability of rich, aligned exogenous text and test robustness only to moderate noise/sparsity (10-20%). It does not evaluate scenarios with severe text scarcity (<5% coverage) or qualitative degradation (e.g., misinformation, irrelevant content).  
**Why unresolved:** VoT's Event-driven Reasoning branch fundamentally relies on exogenous text to extract event signals. Severe text limitations would likely cripple the HIC module's ability to retrieve meaningful corrections and the reasoning pipeline's ability to generate accurate forecasts, but the paper does not probe this boundary.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments where exogenous text is artificially down-sampled, replaced with synthetic noise, or replaced with text from unrelated domains. Performance metrics (MSE/MAE) would be tracked against text availability/quality degradation curves to identify failure thresholds.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What are the theoretical and practical limits of the Historical In-Context Learning (HIC) mechanism when confronted with truly novel, unprecedented events that have no historical analogs in the training knowledge base?  
**Basis in paper:** HIC's effectiveness is predicated on retrieving *similar* historical examples for error-informed guidance. The paper demonstrates robustness to imperfect retrieval (e.g., 100th-most similar) but does not consider the scenario where the current event is fundamentally dissimilar to any training example (e.g., a first-of-its-kind financial crisis or a novel pandemic).  
**Why unresolved:** The knowledge base is constructed solely from training data. For a novel event, retrieval may yield irrelevant corrections, potentially introducing harmful biases or failing to provide useful guidance. The paper does not analyze how (or if) the system defaults in such cases.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Creating controlled test sets with simulated "black swan" events (e.g., injecting an extreme shock pattern not present in training). HIC's retrieval similarity scores and the quality of the retrieved correction (e.g., via human evaluation or automated consistency checks with ground truth) would be measured to determine a similarity threshold below which HIC harms performance.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** To what extent are the Adaptive Frequency Fusion (AFF) weights interpretable, and do they correlate with measurable dataset characteristics (e.g., event volatility, text richness, seasonal strength) in a way that could inform model selection or fusion strategy design?  
**Basis in paper:** The AFF module learns dataset-specific frequency-band weights to adaptively combine branch outputs. The paper states that different datasets exhibit varying degrees of correlation with events, resulting in different frequency component distributions, and provides frequency-domain visualizations.  
**Why unresolved:** While the necessity of adaptive fusion is demonstrated via ablation, the learned weight vectors themselves are not analyzed. It is unclear whether, for example, high `w_event` in the low-frequency band consistently corresponds to datasets with more pronounced structural breaks, or whether the weight patterns can be used to diagnose a dataset's reliance on textual vs. numerical information.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Post-hoc analysis of AFF weight distributions across all 10 datasets, correlating them with external dataset statistics (e.g., kurtosis of the series, text-to-series topic coherence, frequency of named entities in exogenous text). This could reveal if the model learns meaningful, human-interpretable fusion strategies or if the adaptation is dataset-specific in an opaque way.

## Limitations
- Reliance on LLM APIs for the event branch introduces latency, cost, and potential prompt engineering sensitivities beyond HIC corrections.
- ETA's additive decomposition assumption may not hold for series with non-additive interactions (e.g., multiplicative seasonality), limiting robustness.
- Adaptive band partitioning in AFF is underspecified, risking overfitting to validation sets.
- Reproduction gaps include missing hyperparameters (contrastive temperature, AFF thresholds), exact prompt templates, and unclear handling of API limits/costs.

## Confidence
- Event-driven reasoning (HIC): Medium (ablation support, but retrieval noise could degrade)
- Endogenous Text Alignment (ETA): Medium (ablation positive, yet decomposition constraints limit robustness)
- Adaptive Frequency Fusion (AFF): Medium (ablation beneficial, but adaptivity may overfit)
- Overall SOTA performance: High (consistent gains across 10 datasets vs. strong baselines)

## Next Checks
1. **Retrieval sensitivity analysis for HIC:** Vary similarity thresholds and measure performance on events lacking close historical parallels to identify failure thresholds.
2. **Evaluate ETA under non-additive patterns:** Test with multiplicative seasonality or non-linear patterns; compare against alternative decompositions (e.g., STL).
3. **Replace adaptive band partitioning with fixed thresholds:** Use fixed partitions (e.g., low 0-10%, mid 10-70%, high 70-100%) to isolate benefits of adaptivity and check for overfitting.
