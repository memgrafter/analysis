# Egocentric Bias in Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15892  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15892  
- **Reference count:** 12  
- **Primary result:** 91.3% of 103 vision-language models (VLMs) perform below the 25% chance level on Level-2 visual perspective taking (L2 VPT), with 75.88% of errors reproducing the camera viewpoint.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FlipSet, a diagnostic benchmark designed to isolate the cognitive component of Level-2 visual perspective taking (L2 VPT)—specifically, the ability to mentally rotate a 2D string by 180° from an agent’s viewpoint. Evaluating 103 VLMs in a zero-shot setting reveals a pervasive egocentric bias: the vast majority of models score below chance and most errors are egocentric (i.e., the model reports its own perspective rather than the agent’s). Control experiments show that the models possess the constituent skills—Theory of Mind (ToM) accuracy 90.4% and mental rotation (MR) accuracy 26.1% (above chance)—yet fail catastrophically when these must be combined (L2 VPT accuracy 10.3%). A strong correlation exists between MR and L2 VPT performance (r=0.746), but 91.7% of models perform below the product of their isolated ToM and MR scores, indicating a fundamental compositional deficit in binding social awareness to spatial operations. The findings suggest current VLMs rely on coarse-grained associative patterns rather than integrated spatial-social reasoning.

## Method Summary
FlipSet comprises 28 stimulus items (e.g., alphanumeric strings like “81”, “nod”) displayed on a white card in Arial font. In each image, a plush monkey faces the back of the card, so the string is mirrored from the monkey’s perspective. For each item, 12 counterbalanced four‑choice layouts are generated, assigning the correct rotated string, the egocentric (unrotated) string, a confusable distractor, and a random distractor to options A–D. This design cleanly separates 180° mental rotation from three‑dimensional scene complexity. The authors evaluate 103 VLMs zero‑shot: an image and the question “What does the monkey see on the card?” are provided, and the model’s textual response is mapped to one of the four choice strings via exact matching. Errors are classified as Egocentric, Confusable, Random, or Fail. To decompose abilities, separate control tasks measure ToM (e.g., false‑belief stories) and MR (e.g., rotated shapes) accuracy.

## Key Results
- **Systematic egocentric bias:** 91.3% of models fall below the 25% chance baseline; egocentric errors account for 75.88% of all mistakes.  
- **Compositional deficit:** Integrated L2 VPT accuracy is only 10.3%, while 91.7% of models underperform relative to the product of their isolated ToM and MR scores.  
- **Component correlation:** Mental rotation correlates strongly with L2 VPT performance (r=0.746), highlighting the role of spatial transformation but also the missing integration mechanism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark works because it removes confounding 3D factors and forces a pure 180° rotation on a simple 2D stimulus. Counterbalanced layouts prevent models from exploiting superficial cues like relative positions of choices. The four‑choice design with distinct error categories isolates the specific failure modes, particularly the egocentric bias. By evaluating zero‑shot without demonstrations or chain‑of‑thought, the test probes inherent model capabilities rather than task‑specific adaptation. This clean isolation reveals whether a VLM can truly adopt another’s visual perspective, exposing the compositional gap between social reasoning and spatial transformation.

## Foundational Learning
**Level-2 Visual Perspective Taking (L2 VPT)** – The ability to infer what another agent sees from a different viewpoint, requiring mental rotation. *Why needed:* It is the core cognitive skill under investigation. *Quick check:* Can the model correctly report the string as seen by the monkey after a 180° rotation?

**Egocentric Bias** – The tendency to default to one’s own perspective when reasoning about others. *Why needed:* It is the dominant error mode observed. *Quick check:* What percentage of incorrect answers correspond to the camera‑view string?

**Mental Rotation (MR)** – The capacity to mentally manipulate spatial representations. *Why needed:* A prerequisite for L2 VPT; measured separately. *Quick check:* Does the model exceed chance on a control task that asks it to identify a rotated shape?

**Theory of Mind (ToM)** – The ability to attribute mental states (e.g., beliefs, perceptions) to others. *Why needed:* Social awareness component; measured in isolation. *Quick check:* Does the model answer false‑belief questions correctly?

**Compositional Generalization** – Combining known skills (ToM + MR) to solve a novel integrated task. *Why needed:* The failure to achieve L2 VPT despite competent components indicates a deficit here. *Quick check:* Does integrated performance match or exceed the product of the separate ToM and MR scores?

**Diagnostic Benchmarking** – Using controlled, minimal tasks to isolate specific cognitive processes. *Why needed:* FlipSet’s design strips away 3D complexity to target pure rotation. *Quick check:* Does the stimulus design ensure no 3D cues are available?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Image → Vision Encoder (e.g., CLIP) → Projection Layer → Language Model (decoder) → Text Output.

**Critical Path:** The image is encoded into visual features; these are aligned with the text embedding space; the language model processes the combined prompt and generates the answer. The binding of social perspective (the monkey’s location) to a spatial transformation (180° rotation) must occur within the language model’s reasoning, likely relying on the quality of the visual representation.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Frozen vs. fine‑tuned encoders:* Frozen encoders preserve general knowledge but may lack fine‑grained spatial precision; fine‑tuning can adapt but needs task‑specific data.  
- *Global pooling vs. spatial feature maps:* Global embeddings discard spatial layout; spatial maps retain locality but increase computational cost and may require explicit reasoning modules.  
- *Single‑view vs. multi‑view training:* Single‑view data encourages view‑invariance but does not teach explicit transformations; multi‑view data could encourage model‑based spatial reasoning but is harder to collect.  
- *Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) vs. direct prediction:* CoT can decompose reasoning but may become disconnected from spatial structure, as demonstrated by the amplified egocentric bias.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Egocentric error rate > 50% (strong self‑perspective projection).  
- Random/Fail rate > 20% (inability to engage with the visual task).  
- Inconsistent accuracy across counterbalanced layouts (positional bias).  
- Degraded performance when CoT prompting is used (amplified bias).

**First Experiments:**  
1. **Zero‑shot L2 VPT evaluation:** Run the full FlipSet (or a balanced subset) with a fixed prompt and image preprocessing; record overall accuracy and error distribution.  
2. **Component decomposition:** Administer isolated ToM and MR control tasks; compute product of component scores and compare to integrated L2 VPT to quantify compositional deficit.  
3. **Prompt variation test:** Compare zero‑shot, few‑shot, and CoT prompts to assess robustness and the effect of explicit reasoning on bias amplification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Architectural modifications for integration** – How can VLMs be designed to bind theory‑of‑mind awareness with mental rotation operations? The paper notes a “compositional deficit” and suggests innovations like explicit spatial buffers or 3D‑aware encoders.  
2. **CoT failure mode** – Why does chain‑of‑thought prompting often worsen egocentric bias in L2 VPT? The paper finds that verbal reasoning can become detached from spatial structure.  
3. **Role of visual representation granularity** – Do coarse‑grained encodings (e.g., CLIP) fundamentally limit fine‑grained spatial reasoning? The paper posits that richer, multi‑view or 3D representations might help.  
4. **Generalization to 3D scenes** – Does the egocentric bias and compositional deficit persist when L2 VPT involves complex 3D arrangements, depth, and occlusion? FlipSet isolates 2D rotation; generalizing to ecologically valid settings remains open.

## Limitations
- The full FlipSet dataset (images and layout mappings) is not publicly available, preventing exact replication.  
- The complete list of 103 evaluated VLMs is undisclosed, hindering precise re‑evaluation.  
- Prompt wording, image preprocessing, and handling of model‑specific chat templates are unspecified, introducing variability.  
- Exact string matching for response parsing may misclassify verbose outputs.  
- Stimuli are restricted to 2D strings; conclusions about general L2 VPT in 3D contexts may be overreaching.

## Confidence
- **Systematic egocentric bias:** Medium confidence (robust aggregate pattern but dependent on unreleased benchmark).  
- **Compositional deficit (performance below product of ToM and MR):** Medium confidence (statistic based on component measures with noise).  
- **High correlation between MR and L2 VPT (r=0.746):** High confidence (large correlation across many models).  
- **FlipSet as a clean diagnostic:** Medium confidence (isolation of rotation is sound, but real‑world validity limited to 2D tasks).

## Next Checks
1. Release the complete FlipSet dataset (images and layout specifications) to enable exact replication and further analysis.  
2. Re‑evaluate a diverse subset of the 103 models using a fully standardized protocol (fixed prompt, image preprocessing, robust parsing) to verify the main metrics.  
3. Systematically vary option letter assignments across layouts to measure sensitivity to positional bias and ensure counterbalancing effectiveness.
