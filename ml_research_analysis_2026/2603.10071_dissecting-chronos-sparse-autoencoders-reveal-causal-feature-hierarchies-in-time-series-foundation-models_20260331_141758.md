# Dissecting Chronos: Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Causal Feature Hierarchies in Time Series Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10071
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10071
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: Sparse autoencoder features from Chronos-T5-Large are universally causally relevant to forecasting, with a depth-dependent hierarchy where mid-encoder change-detection features are most critical (max ΔCRPS=38.61) and final encoder features can be ablated without harm.

## Executive Summary
This study applies sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to the Chronos-T5-Large time series foundation model to interpret its internal representations. By training TopK SAEs across six layers and conducting 392 single-feature ablation experiments on the ETT dataset, the authors establish that every extracted feature is causally necessary for accurate probabilistic forecasting (measured by CRPS). They reveal a hierarchical organization: early encoder layers encode low-level frequency patterns, the mid-encoder (block 11) concentrates critically important change-detection features, and the final encoder aggregates a semantically rich but less essential summary. Notably, progressively ablating features from the final encoder paradoxically improves forecast quality, suggesting these features may carry pretraining biases suboptimal for the target domain.

## Method Summary
The reproduction involves training separate TopK SAEs (d_sae=8192, k=64) on residual stream activations from six target layers of Chronos-T5-Large, using a mixture of ~100k time series windows (ETT and synthetic diagnostics). A heuristic taxonomy labels features by correlating their activations with known synthetic temporal properties (e.g., trend, seasonality). Causality is tested via single-feature ablation: a feature's sparse code is zerod, decoded to a modified activation, patched into the forward pass, and the change in CRPS (ΔCRPS) on ETT windows is measured. Progressive ablation cumulatively removes features sorted by decoder-norm to assess layer-wise importance.

## Key Results
- All 392 single-feature ablations yielded strictly positive ΔCRPS, confirming universal causal relevance.
- The mid-encoder (block 11) exhibited the highest causal impact, with a maximum single-feature ΔCRPS of 38.61 and a heavy-tailed distribution of effects.
- Progressive ablation of the final encoder (block 23) paradoxically reduced CRPS (−0.89), while ablating mid-encoder features catastrophically degraded performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Sparse autoencoder (SAE) features extracted from Chronos activations are universally causally relevant to its forecasting performance.
- **Mechanism:** By training a TopK SAE on a model's residual stream activations, the learned dictionary vectors represent approximately monosemantic features. Zeroing a specific feature's activation during inference (single-feature ablation) removes that concept from the model's computation. If the forecast quality (CRPS) degrades, the feature's information was irreplaceable by other features, establishing causal necessity.
- **Core assumption:** The SAE's sparse code is a sufficient decomposition; no critical information is lost in the reconstruction or resides in the residual error term not captured by any feature.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Through 392 single-feature ablation experiments, we establish that every ablated feature produces a positive CRPS degradation, confirming causal relevance." [section 3.1] "Across 392 single-feature ablation experiments spanning three encoder layers, every ablation produced a strictly positive ∆CRPS." [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "Sanity Checks for Sparse Autoencoders") investigate whether SAEs beat random baselines, indicating this causal validation is a key community benchmark, but no direct evidence for time series models exists prior to this work.
- **Break condition:** If a significant fraction of features (e.g., >5%) show non-positive ∆CRPS, the SAE is learning non-causal or redundant representations. This could occur if the SAE's expansion factor is too low or training is unstable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Chronos-T5 encodes temporal concepts in a depth-dependent hierarchy, with different layers specializing in different types of features.
- **Mechanism:** SAEs applied to different layers reveal distinct concept taxonomies. Early layers focus on local, low-level properties (high-frequency patterns). The mid-encoder becomes a hub for detecting abrupt changes (level shifts, noise), which are critical for adapting to non-stationarities. The final encoder aggregates a rich, semantic summary of the entire input window for the decoder's use.
- **Core assumption:** The heuristic correlation-based taxonomy correctly maps feature activation patterns to ground-truth temporal properties on synthetic diagnostic data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "reveals a depth-dependent hierarchy: early encoder layers encode low-level frequency features, the mid-encoder concentrates causally critical change-detection features, and the final encoder compresses a rich but less causally important taxonomy." [section 3.2] Table 2 shows starkly different concept distributions: Encoder block 5 is dominated by 'freq. high', block 11 by 'level shift ↑' and 'noise', block 23 by 'seasonality' and diverse concepts. [corpus] Weak support. Neighbors like "From Atoms to Trees" discuss hierarchical features in LLMs but not time series. The assumption of concept labeling validity is not challenged by corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the concept distribution is statistically uniform across all layers, the hierarchy is an artifact of the taxonomy or SAE training. This could be tested by comparing layer-wise feature similarity matrices.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Causal importance is inversely related to semantic richness; the layer with the most diverse, labeled concepts (final encoder) is paradoxically less critical and can even be ablated without harm.
- **Mechanism:** The mid-encoder's concentrated change-detection features are indispensable for the core forecasting task on ETT data. The final encoder's richer representation may contain features specialized for the diverse pretraining distribution of Chronos, which are suboptimal for the specific ETT domain. Ablating them acts as an implicit domain adapter, removing conflicting signals.
- **Core assumption:** The decoder-norm ranking used for progressive ablation is a valid proxy for feature importance, and the ETT benchmark is representative of the model's intended deployment contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "The most critical features reside in the mid-encoder (max ∆CRPS=38.61), not in the semantically richest final encoder layer, where progressive ablation paradoxically improves forecast quality." [section 3.3] Figure 1 shows CRPS rising catastrophically for block 11 but *decreasing* for block 23 under progressive ablation. [corpus] No direct evidence. The "paradoxical improvement" aligns with literature on redundancy and domain adaptation (e.g., in "Sanity Checks for SAEs" they discuss feature utility vs. interpretability), but this specific inverse relationship is novel.
- **Break condition:** If progressive ablation on the final encoder degrades performance on a broader, more diverse benchmark (e.g., the full Chronos eval suite), the improvement on ETT is a dataset-specific anomaly, not a general principle.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**
  - **Why needed here:** SAEs are the primary tool for decomposing the dense, entangled activations of a TSFM into potentially interpretable, sparse features. Understanding their TopK objective and training (dictionary learning) is essential to grasp how "features" are defined.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the trade-off between the SAE's expansion factor (d_sae / d_model) and the likelihood of finding interpretable, dead, or superimposed features?
- **Concept: CRPS (Continuous Ranked Probability Score)**
  - **Why needed here:** CRPS is the key evaluation metric. It measures the quality of probabilistic forecasts. Understanding that ∆CRPS = CRPS_ablated - CRPS_original is crucial; a positive value means worse performance after ablation, indicating the feature's causal role.
  - **Quick check question:** Why use CRPS instead of MSE for a probabilistic model like Chronos, and how does a single-feature ablation affect the distributional forecast?
- **Concept: Chronos Tokenization**
  - **Why needed here:** Chronos represents time series as sequences of discrete tokens (4,096 bins). This tokenization is the "language" the T5 processes. The features discovered by SAEs operate on these token-level embeddings and their contextual activations.
  - **Quick check question:** How does quantizing continuous values into discrete bins affect the nature of the features a SAE might learn (e.g., bin boundaries vs. smooth trends)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Chronos-T5-Large → Activation Collection → SAE Trainer (per layer) → Feature Extractor → Ablation Engine → Evaluation Harness → ΔCRPS Metrics & Taxonomy Labels.
- **Critical path:**
  Collect Activations → Train SAE (per layer) → Extract Features → Run Single-Feature Ablations (392) → Compute ∆CRPS → Classify Features → Run Progressive Ablation (per layer sorted by decoder norm) → Compare Curves. The causal claim hinges on the integrity of the "Patch Modified Activation" step; any divergence from the original forward pass besides the ablated feature invalidates the experiment.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **SAE Scale:** Larger d_sae finds more features but increases dead feature rate and computational cost. k=64 balances sparsity and reconstruction.
  - **Ablation Speed vs. Power:** Ultra-fast config (256 windows, 4 samples) allows sweeping many features but has high variance. Extended config (1k windows, 8 samples) is needed for reliable estimates in noisy layers.
  - **Taxonomy Coverage:** Heuristic correlation labeling is fast but labels only ~17% of features. A supervised probe would improve coverage but requires more labeled data.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **SAE Training:** Reconstruction loss plateaus at a high value; >50% dead features (never activated); feature activations are not sparse (many >k).
  - **Ablation Experiments:** ∆CRPS distribution has a long left tail (negative values); progressive ablation curve is non-monotonic for a layer claimed to be critical.
  - **Taxonomy:** Very few features labeled (>0.5% per layer); concept distribution is nearly identical across all layers.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **SAE Reconstruction Sanity:** Train a SAE on enc.11 with a small dataset. Verify training loss decreases and that the mean activation per feature on a held-out set is sparse (most near zero).
  2. **Single-Feature Ablation Spot-Check:** Take the top 10 features from enc.11 by decoder-norm. Run the full ablation pipeline. Confirm all ∆CRPS > 0 and are significantly larger than the noise floor (estimated from random feature ablations).
  3. **Progressive Ablation Replication:** For enc.11 and enc.23, run progressive ablation with 64 features. Verify the paper's pattern: CRPS increases sharply for enc.11 and decreases/plateaus for enc.23. If not, investigate data window selection or decoder-norm ranking stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Would increasing the SAE expansion factor beyond 8× produce more interpretable features or alter the observed depth-dependent hierarchy of causal importance?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors list "SAEs at larger expansion factors" as a future work direction.
  - **Why unresolved:** The current experiments use an expansion factor of 8×; it is unknown whether larger dictionaries reduce feature superposition and change the distribution of causal impacts across layers.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Train SAEs with expansion factors 16× and 32× on the same six layers, then compare the labeling rate of features, the tail heaviness of ΔCRPS distributions, and the layer-wise hierarchy.
- **Open Question 2:** Do other time series foundation models (e.g., TimesFM, MOMENT, Moirai) exhibit a similar mid-encoder bottleneck, where the most causally critical features are concentrated in middle layers rather than the final encoder?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper explicitly proposes "cross-architecture comparisons" as future work.
  - **Why unresolved:** The study only examined Chronos-T5-Large; different architectures (decoder-only, etc.) may process temporal information differently, leading to distinct internal representations and importance patterns.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the same SAE training and single-feature ablation pipeline to at least two other TSFMs, then compare the mean/max ΔCRPS across layers, feature taxonomy distributions, and progressive ablation curves to see if the mid-encoder dominance is universal.
- **Open Question 3:** What explains the paradoxical improvement in CRPS when progressively ablating features in the final encoder (block 23), while ablating earlier layers degrades performance?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper reports the negative ΔCRPS (−0.89) and hypothesizes it may stem from features that generalize across pretraining domains but are suboptimal for ETT.
  - **Why unresolved:** The exact mechanism—whether the final encoder contains domain-specific noise, redundant representations, or actively harmful features for the ETT distribution—remains unknown.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** (a) Test progressive ablation on multiple datasets from different domains to check if improvement is dataset-specific; (b) Analyze activation statistics of the most ablated features to correlate them with pretraining domain characteristics; (c) Fine-tune Chronos on ETT and repeat ablation to see if the paradox diminishes.
- **Open Question 4:** What are the functional roles of the majority (82.8%) of SAE features that remain unlabeled by the current heuristic taxonomy, despite showing positive causal impact?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note the heuristic taxonomy labels only 17.2% of features and propose "supervised probes for improved taxonomy coverage" as future work.
  - **Why unresolved:** Unlabeled features may encode complex or higher-order temporal patterns that fall outside the eleven coarse categories, but their causal relevance suggests they are important for forecasting.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop more sophisticated labeling methods (e.g., supervised probes predicting temporal properties, clustering with LLM-generated descriptions). After labeling, correlate concepts with ablation impact to map the functional landscape of unlabeled features and potentially expand the taxonomy.

## Limitations
- The paradoxical improvement from final encoder ablation is observed only on the narrow ETT benchmark; it may not generalize to other datasets or the full Chronos evaluation suite.
- The heuristic feature taxonomy covers only ~17% of SAE features, leaving the majority of causally relevant representations uninterpreted and possibly skewing the layer hierarchy narrative.
- The causal necessity claim assumes the SAE's sparse decomposition is lossless, but no control experiments (e.g., random feature ablation or denoising SAE baseline) are reported to rule out training artifacts.

## Confidence
- **High:** Universal causal relevance (all ΔCRPS > 0) is directly measurable and statistically robust with the extended ablation setup.
- **Medium:** Depth-dependent feature hierarchy and concept distributions; the labeling is heuristic but the statistical pattern across layers is strong.
- **Low:** The final encoder's paradoxical improvement and the inverse causal-semantic relationship; based on a single dataset and narrow ablation protocol, lacking broader validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Dataset Consistency:** Replicate progressive ablation on the full Chronos benchmark (multiple datasets). Verify if final encoder improvement is ETT-specific.
2. **Taxonomy Coverage:** Increase synthetic diagnostic complexity or use supervised probes to label more features. Re-analyze ablation impacts on newly labeled subset.
3. **SAE Stability Control:** Train a denoising SAE and a random feature ablation baseline. Compare their ΔCRPS distributions to the TopK SAE to confirm claims are not artifacts of training.
