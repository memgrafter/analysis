# Neural Probabilistic Amplitude Shaping for Nonlinear Fiber Channels

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02716
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02716
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Neural probabilistic amplitude shaping (NPAS) achieves a 0.5 dB signal-to-noise ratio gain over sequence selection and matches the performance of non-deployable neural probabilistic shaping, with superior stability at larger blocklengths.

## Executive Summary
Neural probabilistic amplitude shaping (NPAS) introduces a deployable framework to directly learn joint amplitude distributions for nonlinear fiber channels, addressing the limitation of traditional probabilistic amplitude shaping (PAS), which optimizes only marginal symbol probabilities and ignores beneficial temporal dependencies. NPAS uses an autoregressive neural network to model conditional amplitude distributions while independently assigning sign bits via forward error correction, maintaining full PAS compatibility. On a 205 km single-span dual-polarized 64-QAM link, NPAS delivers a 0.5 dB effective signal-to-noise ratio improvement over sequence selection and matches the performance of non-deployable neural probabilistic shaping, with enhanced training stability at larger blocklengths due to its reduced optimization space.

## Method Summary
NPAS employs an autoregressive LSTM encoder that, given past unsigned amplitudes, outputs a categorical distribution over the next amplitude. During training, Gumbel-Softmax sampling enables differentiable approximation of the arithmetic distribution matcher (ADM) mapping bits to amplitude sequences. Sign bits are sourced separately from a systematic FEC encoder, preserving PAS interface compatibility. The model is trained end-to-end using a differentiable averaged-manifold (AM) channel model, with a mismatched Gaussian demapper computing log-likelihood ratios and an adjusted binary cross-entropy loss relative to transmitted bit labels. At inference, the trained encoder freezes; the ADM uses its conditional distributions to deterministically map information bits to amplitude sequences, which are combined with live FEC sign bits for transmission. The system is evaluated via split-step Fourier method (SSFM) simulations on a 205 km single-span dual-polarization 64-QAM WDM link.

## Key Results
- NPAS achieves a 0.5 dB effective SNR gain over conventional PAS with sequence selection (ESS+Sel.).
- NPAS matches the nonlinear mitigation performance of neural probabilistic shaping (NPS) while being PAS-compatible and thus deployable.
- NPAS exhibits superior training stability and continued performance improvement at larger blocklengths (e.g., L=32) compared to NPS, which plateaus or degrades.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Traditional PAS fails to fully mitigate nonlinear interference because it optimizes only marginal symbol probabilities, ignoring beneficial temporal dependencies in the symbol sequence.
- **Mechanism:** Fiber nonlinearity (e.g., self-phase modulation) depends on the instantaneous power of *multiple* consecutive symbols. Shaping only per-symbol statistics (marginals) discards the potential to coordinate symbol amplitudes over time to reduce peak-to-average power ratio or other nonlinearity-exacerbating patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The channel's nonlinearity exhibits memory over a finite duration (captured by the AM model's sum), so joint amplitude distributions can exploit this memory beneficially.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "traditional PAS shapes only marginal symbol probabilities, even though nonlinear fiber channels are sensitive to both individual symbol distributions and their temporal dependencies."
  - [section 1]: "Finite-blocklength PAS can inherently introduce beneficial symbol correlations... Sequence selection methods exploit this... but... limited."
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbors discuss neural shaping but do not directly anchor this causal claim. The claim is internal to this paper's stated innovation.
- **Break condition:** If the channel's nonlinearity were effectively memoryless (e.g., only linear noise + instantaneous nonlinear phase rotation without inter-symbol crosstalk), optimizing marginals would suffice.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** NPAS achieves deployable joint-distribution shaping by constraining learning to unsigned amplitudes, allowing sign bits to be sourced independently from a systematic FEC encoder, thus maintaining full PAS compatibility.
- **Mechanism:** The NPAS autoregressive model outputs conditional distributions over the *magnitude* (amplitude) part of the constellation. The independent I/Q sign bits are supplied by the FEC parity bitstream. This separation means the ADM and FEC encoder interface remains identical to conventional PAS, while the learned amplitude distribution captures temporal correlations.
- **Core assumption:** The nonlinearity's sensitivity to temporal dependencies is primarily in the amplitude (power) trajectory, not the sign pattern. Separating amplitude and sign learning does not sacrifice significant nonlinearity mitigation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "NPAS introduces a PAS-compatible framework using an autoregressive neural network to directly learn joint amplitude distributions while assigning sign bits via forward error correction."
  - [section 2]: "NPAS consists of an autoregressive neural model that generates a sequence of conditional distributions over **unsigned** complex symbols... the corresponding sign bits ¯s... are supplied by the FEC parity stream, ensuring full compatibility with the PAS architecture."
  - [corpus]: The neighbor "Neural Probabilistic Shaping..." (paper_id 46107) uses *signed* symbols, highlighting NPAS's architectural divergence for PAS compatibility.
- **Break condition:** If sign bits were found to carry significant temporal nonlinearity-mitigation potential (e.g., specific sign sequences reduce interference), the separation would cause a performance penalty relative to a unified model.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** NPAS demonstrates superior training stability and continued performance improvement at larger blocklengths compared to NPS because its optimization space (joint amplitude distributions) is ~4^L times smaller than the full signed constellation space (for QAM).
- **Mechanism:** For a 2D QAM constellation with M possible *unsigned* amplitude states (e.g., square-root of M for one dimension), the cardinality of the sequence space is M^L. For full signed symbols, it is (2M)^L = 4^L * M^L. A dramatically smaller hypothesis space for the autoregressive model reduces the difficulty of the optimization problem and mitigates overfitting or divergence during end-to-end training, especially as L increases.
- **Core assumption:** The performance gain from optimal joint shaping on amplitudes saturates at a lower complexity than the gain from optimizing full signed symbols. The marginal benefit of optimizing signs decreases with L, while the optimization cost grows exponentially.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "superior stability at larger blocklengths due to its reduced optimization space."
  - [section 3 & Fig 2(a)]: "For L≤8, NPS edges out NPAS... Beyond that, NPS plateaus and then degrades, while the proposed NPAS remains stable and continues to improve. This effect is attributed to the 4^L times smaller search space of NPAS."
  - [corpus]: No direct anchor; this is a novel claim of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If the marginal benefit of optimizing signs continued to increase linearly with L, the stability advantage would not outweigh the performance ceiling, making NPS preferable despite training difficulty.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Probabilistic Amplitude Shaping (PAS)**
  - **Why needed here:** NPAS is a direct extension/alternative to PAS. Understanding PAS's core function—using a distribution matcher to map bits to non-uniformly probable constellation points, while FEC carries sign bits—is essential to grasp NPAS's architectural constraint and goal.
  - **Quick check question:** In a standard PAS system for QAM, what part of the constellation point is determined by the distribution matcher, and what part by the FEC encoder?
- **Concept: Autoregressive Modeling & Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs/LSTMs)**
  - **Why needed here:** NPAS's core engine is an autoregressive RNN that models p(a_t | a_{1:t-1}). This is how temporal dependencies are learned and sampled during ADM operation.
  - **Quick check question:** How does an autoregressive model generate a sequence, and what is the role of the recurrent state?
- **Concept: Differentiable Channel Model & End-to-End Learning**
  - **Why needed here:** NPAS is trained via backpropagation through a simulated channel (the AM model). The engineer must understand that the "channel" in training is a differentiable proxy, not the true SSFM.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is a differentiable channel model necessary for training the NPAS encoder, and what is the role of the loss function (adjusted BCE)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input bits → NPAS Encoder (LSTM) → Conditional amplitude distributions → ADM (with Gumbel-Softmax during training) → Unsigned amplitude sequence → Sign Injector (combine with FEC sign bits) → Power normalization → Channel model (AM during training, SSFM during evaluation) → Mismatched Gaussian demapper → LLRs → Adjusted BCE loss → Backprop.
- **Critical path:**  
  - **Training:** Bitstream → LSTM (stateful) → Gumbel-Softmax sampled amplitudes → combine with sampled/known signs → power norm → AM channel → demapper → BCE loss → backprop through ADM (straight-through) and LSTM.  
  - **Inference:** Trained LSTM **freezes**. Information bits → ADM (using frozen conditionals) → deterministic amplitude sequence → combine with live FEC sign bits → transmit over SSFM channel.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **PAS Compatibility vs. Expressivity:** Constraining to unsigned amplitudes ensures FEC compatibility but may limit performance compared to a unified model (like NPS), especially at very short blocklengths (L≤8).  
  - **Blocklength (L) vs. Complexity & Performance:** Larger L captures more temporal patterns and improves stability (for NPAS) but increases ADM complexity and latency. The paper finds L=32 as a practical balance.  
  - **Channel Model Fidelity:** Training uses the differentiable AM model for efficiency. Performance gap to full SSFM during evaluation indicates model accuracy; a less accurate AM model harms transfer.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Training Divergence:** BCE loss increases or becomes NaN. Likely causes: learning rate too high, Gumbel temperature annealing schedule failed, or AM model gradients are unstable.  
  - **No Gain over Uniform/PAS:** The learned distributions collapse to uniform or fail to capture useful temporal correlations. Check: RNN capacity (hidden size), block length L too small, or channel memory neighbors (k) insufficient.  
  - **Poor Transfer to SSFM:** Performance with the true SSFM channel during evaluation is significantly worse than with the AM model during training. Indicates an inaccurate differentiable proxy.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate Baseline AIR Curves:** Train NPAS and NPS (for baseline) at L=32, k appropriate for memory. Evaluate vs. launch power using SSFM. Confirm ~0.5 dB SNR gain over ESS (PAS) and sequence selection (ESS+Sel.) baselines (with 64 candidates, AM metric).  
  2. **Ablation on Amplitude-Only Constraint:** Train a variant where the RNN outputs distributions over *signed* symbols (like NPS) but still tries to interface with a PAS-style FEC (requiring a hacky sign bit injection). Compare performance and training stability to vanilla NPAS. Expect NPS to have edge at low L but NPAS to be more stable at higher L.  
  3. **Sensitivity to Channel Memory (k):** Train NPAS with varying k (number of neighbor blocks in training). Evaluate performance. Expected: k too small fails to capture relevant channel memory; k too large increases computational cost without benefit beyond the channel's effective memory length.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does neural probabilistic amplitude shaping (NPAS) perform in multi-span optical fiber transmission systems compared to single-span links?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The results presented here are limited to a single-span link, and extending the comparison to multi-span transmission is subject of future investigation.  
  *Why unresolved:* The evaluation is confined to a single-span scenario, whereas multi-span systems involve cumulative nonlinear effects, dispersion management, and amplifier noise that could impact NPAS's joint distribution learning and overall performance.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the quantization rate loss associated with the arithmetic distribution matcher (ADM) when employing Gumbel-Softmax trained neural models in NPAS?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Because NPAS and NPS are trained via Gumbel-Softmax sampling, the ADM rate loss is not yet quantified; therefore, we leave ESS’s rate losses, including those from sequence selection, undeducted to provide an optimistic AIR bound for ESS as our benchmark.  
  *Why unresolved:* The lack of quantified rate loss prevents a fair comparison of NPAS's practical performance and hinders assessment of its deployability in rate-sensitive systems.
- **Open Question 3:** How does the autoregressive neural network architecture in NPAS, such as hidden state size or recurrent unit type, influence the trade-off between nonlinear interference mitigation and training stability across different blocklengths?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] NPAS employs an autoregressive recurrent neural network... and offers markedly superior stability for larger blocklengths due to its reduced optimization space, but the specific architectural factors are not explored.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper uses a fixed architecture (single-layer LSTM, hidden size 256), leaving open how design choices affect performance especially as blocklength increases, which is critical for scalability.
- **Open Question 4:** Can NPAS be generalized to higher-order modulation formats (e.g., 256-QAM) and probabilistic constellation shaping with larger amplitude sets while preserving its performance advantages over traditional PAS and sequence selection?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The numerical results are limited to dual-polarized 64-QAM transmission; while NPAS shows gains, its applicability to other modulation formats is not addressed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Higher-order modulations have more amplitude levels, potentially increasing the complexity of joint distribution learning and affecting PAS compatibility; the paper does not test beyond 64-QAM.

## Limitations
- **ADM implementation details** (mapping algorithm, rate loss) are unspecified, hindering exact bitrate replication.
- **Training hyperparameters** (Gumbel temperature schedule, learning rate, batch size, side block configuration) and optimal launch power selection method are missing.
- **Channel model fidelity:** training on differentiable AM vs. evaluation on SSFM; the performance gap due to model mismatch is unquantified.
- **Experimental scope limited** to a single 205 km WDM link; generalizability to other setups is unknown.

## Confidence
- *Marginal-only PAS is inadequate*: **Medium**. Theoretical plausibility is strong, but the paper does not isolate temporal correlation benefits from blocklength effects.
- *NPAS maintains PAS compatibility*: **High**. The unsigned amplitude + independent sign architecture is clearly defined and contrasted with NPS.
- *Stability at large L due to reduced optimization space*: **Medium**. Empirical trends support the claim, but causal attribution to space size is inferential, not proven.
