# A Semi-Supervised Pipeline for Generalized Behavior Discovery from Animal-Borne Motion Time Series

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02618
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02618
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** The method recovers withheld behaviors as distinct clusters with low containment scores (Oc < 0.3) in most cases; negative control yields high containment (>0.3), validating the novelty threshold.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of discovering novel animal behaviors from short inertial measurement unit (IMU) and GPS motion snippets in a semi-supervised setting with limited labeled data and severe class imbalance. The proposed pipeline first learns an embedding space using a lightweight temporal CNN trained on the labeled subset. It then performs label-guided K-means clustering on all embeddings (both labeled and unlabeled) with one extra cluster beyond the known classes to capture potential novel behaviors. Novelty is determined by a kernel density estimation (KDE) based highest-density region (HDR) containment score, which measures the overlap between a discovered cluster and the distributions of known classes; low best-match containment indicates novelty. In experiments withholding one behavior at a time, the pipeline typically isolates the withheld class into a distinct cluster with containment scores below 0.3, except for the "Manouvre" class which scores 0.496. A negative-control experiment with no novel behavior consistently yields containment scores above 0.3 for the extra cluster, confirming the threshold's effectiveness. This approach offers a quantitative and interpretable framework for generalized category discovery in ecological time series.

## Method Summary
The problem is framed as Generalized Category Discovery (GCD): given a small labeled set of known animal behaviors and a larger unlabeled pool that may contain both known and novel behaviors, the goal is to cluster the data and identify which clusters correspond to novel categories. The dataset consists of 4,338 samples from gulls, each a 20-step (1 s) multivariate time series with 4 channels (3-axis acceleration, GPS speed). Nine expert-annotated behaviors exhibit severe class imbalance. Preprocessing normalizes GPS speed (divide by 22 m/s) and clips acceleration to [–2, 2] g.

The pipeline comprises three stages:
1. **Representation learning**: A lightweight temporal CNN (three 1D convolution layers, batch normalization, ReLU, dropout 0.25, global average pooling) is trained on the labeled subset \(D_L\) for 2000 epochs using cross-entropy loss with AdamW (learning rate 3e-4). The pre-softmax logits serve as embeddings \(z = f_\theta(x)\).
2. **Semi-supervised clustering**: All samples (labeled and unlabeled) are embedded and clustered with K-means, where \(K = K_{\text{known}} + 1\). Centroids for the known classes are initialized as the mean embeddings of labeled samples from each class; an additional centroid is randomly initialized. Standard K-means updates are applied to all points, allowing labeled samples to move.
3. **Novelty decision**: For the extra cluster (the one that originated from the random centroid), a Gaussian KDE is fit to its 2D t-SNE projection (perplexity 30, all embeddings jointly). For each known class, a KDE is also fit to its t-SNE points. The 0.95 highest-density regions (HDRs) are estimated via Monte Carlo sampling (2000 points) from each KDE. Containment scores measure the fraction of one cluster's mass inside the other's HDR, taking the maximum directional containment \(\text{Contain}(c,k) = \max(C_{c\to k}, C_{k\to c})\). The best-match containment \(O_c = \max_k \text{Contain}(c,k)\) is compared to a threshold \(O_c < 0.3\); if below, the extra cluster is declared novel.

Two evaluation protocols are used:
- **Existing novel class discovery**: One class is withheld entirely from \(D_L\) and appears only in the unlabeled pool. Success is measured by whether that class is captured by the extra cluster (withheld-class accuracy) and whether its containment score is below threshold.
- **Negative control**: All classes are present in \(D_L\) (split arbitrarily), and the unlabeled pool contains only known classes. The extra cluster should yield containment scores above 0.3, indicating no spurious novelty detection.

## Key Results
- **Withheld behaviors**: For most classes, the pipeline isolates the withheld class into the extra cluster with containment scores \(O_c < 0.3\) and high withheld-class accuracy, demonstrating effective discovery. The exception is "Manouvre", with \(O_c = 0.496\), indicating difficulty in separating this behavior.
- **Negative control**: When no novel behavior exists, the extra cluster consistently has \(O_c > 0.3\), validating the threshold for avoiding false positives.
- **Interpretability**: The containment score provides a clear, quantitative criterion for novelty, and the clustering preserves known class structure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Supervised embedding learning on limited labeled data produces separable representations where known behaviors form distinct clusters.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight temporal CNN is trained with cross-entropy loss on the labeled subset. The pre-softmax logits serve as embeddings, optimized to maximize inter-class separation and intra-class compactness for the known taxonomy.
- **Core assumption:** The nine expert-annotated behaviors have discriminative motion patterns that a modest encoder can capture, even with severe class imbalance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "learns an embedding from labeled data"
  - [section 3.3] "train a lightweight classifier and use its pre-softmax outputs as the representation"
  - [section 4.2] t-SNE visualizations show clear separation for most withheld classes when using supervised embeddings.
- **Break condition:** If the encoder is too weak or behaviors are too similar (as seen with MOMENT and Chronos2 embeddings in Appendix B), the embedding space may not separate classes, causing the clustering stage to fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Label-guided semi-supervised K-means with one extra cluster isolates a withheld behavior into a distinct cluster while preserving known class structure.
- **Mechanism:** K-means is initialized with centroids derived from labeled embeddings of known classes. An additional centroid is added and optimized jointly over all embeddings (labeled + unlabeled). Labeled points constrain known class centroids, while the extra centroid attracts unlabeled samples not well explained by any known centroid.
- **Core assumption:** The withheld behavior occupies a cohesive region in embedding space that is not covered by the known class centroids.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "performs label-guided K-means clustering on all embeddings (allocating one extra cluster)"
  - [section 3.4] "allocate one additional free cluster beyond the number of known classes to capture structure not well explained by the labeled classes"
  - [section 4.2] "the semi-supervised K-means stage typically isolates the withheld behavior into a distinct cluster" and Table 1 shows matching cluster assignments.
- **Break condition:** If the novel behavior is transitional or overlaps heavily with known classes (e.g., Manouvre), the extra cluster may merge with existing ones or become diffuse, failing to isolate the withheld class.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The KDE+HDR containment score provides an interpretable novelty statistic: a low best-match overlap (Oc < 0.3) indicates a cluster not explained by any known class distribution.
- **Mechanism:** For each discovered cluster c, Gaussian KDE estimates its density in 2D t-SNE space. For each known class k, α-HDRs (regions with top α probability mass) are computed. Containment(c, k) measures the fraction of cluster c’s mass lying inside k’s HDR (or vice versa). The best-match Oc = max_k Contain(c, k) is low if c’s high-density region does not align with any known class’s high-density region.
- **Core assumption:** Known classes have distinct, compact high-density regions in the embedding projection; a novel behavior will occupy a region with minimal overlap.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "KDE-based highest-density region (HDR) containment score"
  - [section 3.5] Formal definition of Contain(c, k; α) and Oc.
  - [section 4.2] Withheld classes typically yield Oc < 0.3 (Table 1); [section 4.3] negative control yields Oc > 0.3 (Table 2).
- **Break condition:** If KDE is unstable for small or multi-modal clusters, or if t-SNE distortion changes relative densities, Oc may not reflect true separability. Also, if a novel behavior is embedded near known classes, containment may be spuriously high.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Generalized Category Discovery (GCD)
  - **Why needed here:** The task matches GCD: unlabeled data contains both known and unknown classes, requiring clustering and novelty decision.
  - **Quick check question:** How does GCD differ from closed-set classification and pure novel class discovery (NCD)?
- **Concept:** Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) and Highest Density Regions (HDR)
  - **Why needed here:** The containment score relies on nonparametric density estimation and HDRs to quantify distributional overlap in a low-dimensional projection.
  - **Quick check question:** Why use α-HDRs instead of full density overlap (e.g., Bhattacharyya coefficient) for novelty detection?
- **Concept:** Semi-supervised clustering with label guidance
  - **Why needed here:** The pipeline must respect known class structure while allowing an extra cluster for potential novelty, balancing supervision and discovery.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the effect of the number of extra clusters on the discovery process?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Encoder -> Semi-supervised K-means -> KDE+HDR module -> Decision logic
- **Critical path:**
  1. Train encoder on \(D_L\).
  2. Embed all samples (\(D_L \cup D_U\)) with trained encoder.
  3. Run label-guided K-means with \(K = \text{|known classes|} + 1\).
  4. For the extra cluster, compute \(O_c\) against all known class distributions.
  5. If max \(O_c < 0.3\), mark cluster as novel behavior.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Using t-SNE for KDE simplifies computation but may distort global density relationships; original embedding space would require more complex density estimation.
  - Fixed single extra cluster assumes at most one novel behavior per unlabeled pool; multiple novelties would need iterative or hierarchical extension.
  - Threshold 0.3 is dataset-specific (calibrated via negative control); may not transfer without re-calibration.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor embedding separation in t-SNE plots (encoder limitation).
  - Extra cluster has high \(O_c\) in withheld-class trials (e.g., Manouvre at 0.496), indicating failure to isolate novelty.
  - Known classes split into multiple clusters, suggesting over-clustering or insufficient label guidance.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Withhold-one-class validation:** Remove a full behavior from \(D_L\), place it only in \(D_U\); check if it forms a distinct cluster with low \(O_c\).
  2. **Negative-control validation:** \(D_U\) contains only known classes; extra cluster should have high \(O_c (>0.3)\) to confirm no spurious novelty detection.
  3. **Deployment simulation:** Split labeled data 50/50; use half as \(D_L\) and half plus sliding unlabeled windows as \(D_U\); apply pipeline and monitor \(O_c\) for new clusters in stream context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can we replace the low-dimensional KDE+HDR containment score with a robust method operating directly in the embedding space?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5 states the containment statistic inherits limitations of density estimation in a low-dimensional embedding projection, and KDE/HDR estimates can be less stable for small or multi-modal clusters.
- **Why unresolved:** The t-SNE projection may distort embedding geometry, and KDE instability leads to unreliable novelty decisions for small discovered clusters.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A high-dimensional distribution overlap measure (e.g., kernel-based divergence) with theoretical stability guarantees and improved withheld-class accuracy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What self-supervised objectives are effective for generalized category discovery from short, imbalanced time series without false negatives?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5: "A promising direction is burst-aware self-supervised learning ... objectives tailored to discovery rather than forecasting. Positive-only self-distillation methods ... may reduce false negatives under imbalance."
- **Why unresolved:** Standard contrastive learning fails due to false negatives from imbalance and short windows; reconstruction objectives lack discriminability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A self-supervised method pretrained on unlabeled gull motion data that yields embeddings with clearer class separation (e.g., higher silhouette score) and lower containment scores for withheld behaviors.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can hierarchical models that aggregate across bursts capture transitional behaviors like Manouvre that are not separable from single bursts?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5: "hierarchical modeling could encode within-burst motion and then aggregate across bursts with time-gap information ... better capture behavioral transitions."
- **Why unresolved:** Single-burst embeddings cannot distinguish transitions; Manouvre overlaps with other flight modes due to lack of temporal context.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A hierarchical model using multiple consecutive bursts and time-gap features that isolates Manouvre as a distinct cluster with low containment score in withheld-class trials.

## Limitations
- t-SNE projection may distort true embedding densities, compromising KDE-based containment scores.
- The novelty threshold \(O_c < 0.3\) is calibrated on a single dataset; its transferability to other animal motion data is unknown.
- The pipeline assumes at most one novel behavior; multiple simultaneous novelties would require extensions.

## Confidence
- Withheld class discovery: Medium
- Threshold effectiveness: Medium
- Label-guided K-means isolation: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Systematic withheld-class analysis**: For each of the nine behaviors, perform 10 random 50/50 splits of labeled data; compute mean/std of \(O_c\) and withheld-class accuracy. Identify patterns (sample size, class similarity) that predict success or failure.
2. **Robustness to t-SNE and KDE choices**: Vary t-SNE perplexity (10, 30, 50), learning rate, and iterations; recompute \(O_c\). Also test alternative density estimation (e.g., Gaussian Mixture Models in original embedding space). Determine whether the 0.3 threshold remains discriminative.
3. **Multiple novelty scenarios**: Withhold two distinct behaviors simultaneously; run the pipeline with \(K = K_{\text{known}} + 2\) extra clusters. Check if both novelties are isolated (low \(O_c\) each) or merged. If needed, propose an iterative extension (e.g., remove discovered novel cluster and re‑cluster remainder) and evaluate recovery.
