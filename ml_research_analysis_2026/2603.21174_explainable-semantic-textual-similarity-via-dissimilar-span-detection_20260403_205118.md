# Explainable Semantic Textual Similarity via Dissimilar Span Detection
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21174
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21174
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Introduces Dissimilar Span Detection (DSD) to explain STS; LLMs (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, F1-Diff 0.640) and supervised models (roberta-base, F1-Diff 0.574) perform best on SSD, but overall scores remain low; integrating DSD into paraphrase detection improves accuracy by up to 8 percentage points.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a key limitation of Semantic Textual Similarity (STS)—its lack of interpretability—by proposing the Dissimilar Span Detection (DSD) task. DSD identifies specific text spans that differ semantically between a pair of sentences, providing localized explanations for similarity scores. To support research, the authors construct the Span Similarity Dataset (SSD) via a semi-automated pipeline that uses an LLM to generate candidate annotations, which humans then validate. They evaluate a range of methods: unsupervised approaches adapted from XAI (LIME, SHAP), a novel Embedding-DSD that uses n-gram replacement, and supervised token classification models. Results indicate that large language models and fine-tuned transformers achieve the highest DSD performance on SSD, yet absolute scores are modest, underscoring the task's difficulty. A downstream experiment on paraphrase detection demonstrates that incorporating DSD can improve accuracy by up to 8 percentage points, providing evidence of practical utility despite current limitations in span detection accuracy.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is framing STS explainability as a span-level detection problem rather than generating free-text rationales. The authors first build the Span Similarity Dataset (SSD), containing 1,000 sentence pairs with manually validated dissimilar spans marked using `{{` and `}}`. The dataset is created through a semi-automated LLM-human pipeline: an LLM proposes sentence pairs with span modifications and initial labels; human annotators correct span boundaries and labels, ensuring quality at scale. For evaluation, they compute unigram-level F1 after aligning predicted and gold spans by minimizing offset differences. Baselines include unsupervised methods: LIME-DSD and SHAP-DSD (applying perturbation-based feature importance to an embedding similarity function), and the novel Embedding-DSD, which systematically replaces n-grams from one sentence into the other and measures the impact on embedding similarity. Supervised approaches cast DSD as token classification (BIO schema) and fine-tune models like BERT and RoBERTa using 5-fold cross-validation. A downstream task integrates DSD into paraphrase detection by rejecting pairs with any dissimilar spans, showing improved accuracy.

## Key Results
- On the SSD test set, Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieves the highest F1-Diff (0.640) among LLMs; the supervised roberta-base model leads token classifiers (F1-Diff 0.574); Embedding-DSD with `all-mpnet-base-v2` reaches F1-Diff 0.423, outperforming LIME (0.294) and SHAP (0.223).
- Overall DSD performance remains low, with the best methods below 0.65 F1-Diff, indicating the task is challenging and under-solved.
- Downstream, adding DSD to a paraphrase detection pipeline (using PAWS-Wiki) yields accuracy improvements of up to 8 percentage points, demonstrating practical value.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Span-Level Interpretability as a Complement to Aggregate STS Scores
Providing localized dissimilar span annotations alongside an STS score helps users understand *why* two texts are similar or dissimilar, addressing the black-box nature of a single similarity score. DSD reframes explainability from generating textual rationales to highlighting specific spans that share a semantic role (e.g., agent, action) but differ in value (e.g., dates). This direct, visual annotation is more actionable than a cosine similarity number. Evidence: The abstract states DSD helps users identify words that negatively affect similarity; downstream paraphrase gains plausibly confirm annotation correctness. However, if span annotations are inaccurate (as suggested by low F1-scores), interpretability collapses.

### Mechanism 2: Semi-Automated Dataset Creation via LLM-Human Pipeline
A pipeline where an LLM proposes span modifications and replacements, followed by human validation, efficiently creates a large, high-quality dataset for a fine-grained annotation task where pure manual labeling is slow. The LLM acts as a fast proposer of candidate variations; humans ensure label accuracy, balancing scale and quality. Evidence: Section 3.2 details the 4-step pipeline; Section 3.3 reports substantial inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ ≥ 0.66 for span boundaries, 0.87 for labels), supporting task coherence. Break condition: if LLM proposals are off-task or human validation is noisy, dataset validity suffers.

### Mechanism 3: Task-Specific Perturbation Sensitivity via n-gram Replacement Analysis
Embedding-DSD outperforms general XAI methods (LIME, SHAP) because its perturbation strategy—replacing n-grams from sentence1 into sentence2—aligns with the semantic substitution nature of DSD. Unlike random masking, Embedding-DSD uses linguistically valid perturbations and measures their impact on embedding similarity, aggregating per unigram to estimate contribution to overall difference. Evidence: Section 4.1 describes the algorithm; Table 7 shows Embedding-DSD (F1-Diff 0.423) exceeds SHAP (0.223) and LIME (0.294). Performance depends on the embedding model's sensitivity to semantic change.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Accuracy vs. Speed/Cost:** LLMs yield highest F1-Diff but are slow/expensive; token classifiers are fast after fine-tuning but require labeled SSD; Embedding-DSD is mid-tier in speed and accuracy.
- **Generalist vs. Specialist:** LIME/SHAP are model-agnostic but provide weaker signals; Embedding-DSD is specialized for DSD but tied to the embedding model's capabilities.
- **Supervision:** Unsupervised methods need no SSD training but require careful threshold tuning; supervised methods need SSD but avoid thresholding at inference.

### Failure Signatures
- **High `F1-NoDiff` + Low `F1-Diff`:** Method over-predicts "no dissimilar spans" (like `No-DSD`), indicating threshold too high or model under-sensitive.
- **`Naive-DSD` performing well:** Suggests the task is largely about lexical non-overlap, not deep semantics; may indicate dataset bias or a model too weak for true semantic judgment.
- **LLM output format errors:** Models may not follow strict `{{span}}` output format (e.g., DeepSeek R1 in Table 7), requiring robust parsing/retry logic.
- **Span boundary mismatch:** Predicted spans omit necessary context (e.g., annotating only "lower" instead of "close lower" for a date change), signaling poor contextual understanding.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Dataset Expansion:** How can the Span Similarity Dataset (SSD) be expanded in size and linguistic diversity, and how would this affect DSD model performance? The conclusion notes SSD can be expanded but provides no roadmap.
2. **Downstream Applications:** How can DSD be integrated into other downstream STS-dependent tasks (e.g., fake news detection, annotation error detection, text clustering) to improve their accuracy? Only paraphrase detection is evaluated.
3. **Multilingual & Paragraph-Level:** How can DSD be extended to multilingual settings and longer textual units (e.g., paragraphs), where span alignment and ordering are more complex? The paper acknowledges SSD is English-only and sentence-level.
4. **Complete Span Alignment:** How can DSD systems be extended to jointly detect equivalent spans, dissimilar spans, and spans appearing in only one sentence, enabling complete span alignment? The limitations note current DSD focuses solely on dissimilar spans.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Scope & Generalization:** SSD contains only 1,000 sentence pairs, limiting diversity; low overall F1 scores (max ~0.64) suggest DSD remains a challenging, under-solved task.
- **Prompt/Threshold Opacity:** LLM prompts for SSD creation are incompletely specified; unsupervised method thresholds were tuned on private validation splits, hindering exact replication.
- **Utility vs. Accuracy Trade-off:** Downstream paraphrase gains (↑8%) suggest DSD annotations are useful, but poor span-detection accuracy raises questions about whether benefits stem from coarse filtering rather than fine-grained explanation.

## Confidence
- **DSD task definition & utility:** Medium – Supported by downstream improvement but limited by low detection scores.
- **SSD dataset quality:** High – Inter-annotator agreement (κ ≥ 0.66) and semi-automated pipeline lend credibility.
- **Embedding-DSD superiority over LIME/SHAP:** Medium – Results are dataset-specific; threshold sensitivity not fully explored.
- **LLM performance claims:** Medium – Dependent on specific model versions and unreleased prompts.
