# BATQuant: Outlier-resilient MXFP4 Quantization via Learnable Block-wise Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16590
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16590
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Recovers up to 96.43% of full-precision accuracy on multimodal benchmarks under aggressive W4A4KV16 quantization and exceeds 99% recovery under W4A8KV16, establishing new SOTA on Qwen3 models.

## Executive Summary
BATQuant tackles the critical problem of performance collapse in MXFP4 quantization for large language and multimodal models. The collapse is caused by global rotations (e.g., Hadamard) spreading outlier energy across hardware-defined quantization blocks, creating harmful bimodal activation distributions that waste the narrow 4-bit floating-point range. BATQuant's solution is a strictly block-wise affine transformation that aligns with the hardware's quantization granularity, confining outlier energy within its native block. This is paired with a Global and Private Kronecker (GPK) decomposition for parameter efficiency and block-wise learnable clipping to suppress residual outliers. Evaluated on Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, BATQuant significantly outperforms prior methods like QuaRot, BRQ, and FlatQuant across both multimodal and reasoning tasks under mixed-precision settings.

## Method Summary
BATQuant is a post-training quantization (PTQ) method that operates per linear layer. It introduces a learnable block-wise affine transformation applied to activations, where the transformation block size strictly matches the hardware's MXFP quantization block (e.g., 32 elements). To keep parameters manageable, each block's affine matrix is factorized via GPK into a globally shared matrix and a small block-specific private matrix. Additionally, per-block clipping thresholds are made learnable to aggressively suppress any remaining outliers. Calibration involves feeding small batches (128 sequences) through the model, computing the MSE between full-precision and quantized outputs, and updating the transformation and clipping parameters with AdamW for 5 epochs. After calibration, the inverse transformation is fused into the weights, which are quantized offline. During inference, activations are transformed, clipped, and quantized on-the-fly before matrix multiplication with the quantized weights.

## Key Results
- On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct under W4A4KV16, BATQuant achieves up to 96.43% recovery of full-precision accuracy on multimodal benchmarks.
- Under the less aggressive W4A8KV16 setting, it exceeds 99% recovery.
- It consistently outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods (QuaRot, BRQ, FlatQuant) across both multimodal and complex reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Block-wise affine transformation prevents cross-block outlier energy transfer that disrupts local scaling in MXFP formats.
- **Mechanism:** By restricting learnable transformations to the exact hardware quantization block size (e.g., 32 elements), any outlier-induced energy is confined within its native block. This contrasts with global rotations which spread outlier energy across blocks, creating new outliers and bimodal distributions that waste the limited MXFP4 range.
- **Core assumption:** Outlier energy crossing quantization block boundaries corrupts the independent scaling factor calculation for each block.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "restricts transformations to align with MXFP granularity to prevent cross-block outlier propagation"; [section 3.1] "By restricting the transformation scope to the size of the MXFP block... ensures that outlier redistribution occurs only locally."
- **Break condition:** Transformation block size mismatched with hardware quantization block size (g). Ablation (Figure 6) shows performance degradation when block size ≠ 32.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Global and Private Kronecker (GPK) decomposition maintains parameter efficiency while preserving per-block adaptation capacity.
- **Mechanism:** GPK factorizes each block's affine matrix into a global shared basis (A) and a block-specific private matrix (B_i). This reduces parameters from O(N*g) to O(g₁² + k*g₂²) where g = g₁*g₂. The shared matrix provides a common basis, while small private matrices allow fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** A single global transformation basis suffices for most statistical smoothing, with only minor block-specific adjustments needed.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Global and Private Kronecker (GPK) decomposition to effectively reduces storage and runtime overhead"; [section 3.1] Table 1 shows >74% parameter reduction; Equation (2) defines P_i = B_i ⊗ A.
- **Break condition:** Poor GPK split (g₁ vs g₂). Ablation (Figure 7) shows non-monotonic performance; too large g₁ limits block adaptation, too small g₁ risks optimization instability.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Block-wise learnable clipping dynamically suppresses residual outliers within each quantization block surviving the affine transformation.
- **Mechanism:** For each block, clipping thresholds are scaled by learnable parameters (α_min, α_max) applied to the block's min/max values. This allows aggressive clipping of extreme values while preserving the core distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Even after optimal affine transformation, some blocks retain problematic outliers requiring explicit threshold adjustment.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "incorporate Block-wise Learnable Clipping to suppress residual outliers"; [section 3.1] Equations (3) and (4) define per-block clipping with sigmoid constraints; Table 4 ablation shows gains from adding clipping.
- **Break condition:** Clipping parameters miscalibrated, causing over-clipping (information loss) or under-clipping (outliers persist). Ablation (Table 4) shows marginal but consistent gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Post-Training Quantization (PTQ)**
  - *Why needed:* BATQuant is a PTQ method; its goal is to minimize accuracy drop without retraining, by optimizing transformations on a small calibration set.
  - *Quick check:* What is the key difference between PTQ and Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) in terms of data and compute requirements?
- **Concept: Microscaling Floating-Point (MXFP) Format**
  - *Why needed:* BATQuant's design is predicated on MXFP's block-wise scaling (e.g., 32 elements per scaling factor). MXFP4's tiny 7-value grid (E2M1) makes bimodal distributions catastrophic.
  - *Quick check:* Why does a bimodal distribution waste bit-width in a 4-bit floating-point format with a small, non-uniform set of representable values?
- **Concept: Outlier Phenomenon in LLM Activations**
  - *Why needed:* The core problem is outliers corrupting block-wise scaling. A few extreme values can dominate a block's dynamic range, forcing a large scaling factor that quantizes the majority inaccurately.
  - *Quick check:* If an activation block contains one value 1000x larger than all others, what happens to the quantization step size (σ) for that block in an MXFP format?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Calibration Data -> Per-Layer Optimization (A, {B_i}, {α}) -> Offline Fusion (W_quant = Q(W @ (B_i^{-1} ⊗ A^{-1}))) -> Inference (X -> GPK Transform (A then B_i) -> Clipping (α) -> Q(X) -> MatMul(W_quant)). KV cache uses separate transformations (P_k, P_v) per token head.
- **Critical path:**
  1. **Calibration:** For each layer, feed 128 calibration batches. Compute MSE between FP32 and quantized outputs (with A, B_i, α active). Update parameters via AdamW (lr=2e-3, cosine decay, 5 epochs, batch size 4).
  2. **Offline Fusion:** Fuse P^{-1} into weights, quantize weights to target MXFP format. Store A, {B_i}, and {α} for activation path.
  3. **Inference:** For each activation, apply GPK transformation, apply clipping, quantize to MXFP, then compute with quantized weight.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Block Granularity:** Must match hardware MXFP block size (32). Smaller transforms are ineffective; larger mixes statistics across blocks.
  - **GPK Parameter Split (g₁ vs g₂):** Larger g₁ (smaller g₂) means more shared global transform, less per-block capacity. Smaller g₁ increases private capacity but risks overfitting/optim instability. Default g₁=8 from ablation (Figure 7).
  - **Clipping Sensitivity:** Learnable clipping adds minimal params but requires stable calibration. Too aggressive hurts accuracy; too little fails to suppress outliers.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Metric:** Sudden drop >5% on reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH) under W4A4KV16 vs. BF16.
  - **Check:** Visualize per-block activation histograms. Presence of bimodal peaks or blocks with extreme kurtosis indicates failed transformation/clipping.
  - **Diagnostic:** Compute variance of per-block MXFP scaling factors (σ). High variance suggests cross-block energy transfer (Mechanism 1 failure).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run RTN, QuaRot, BRQ, FlatQuant on Qwen3-8B/VL to confirm published performance gaps (Tables 2, 3).
  2. **Ablation - Block Size:** Fix all else, vary affine transform block size (g={16,32,64,128}) while keeping MXFP block=32. Expect peak at 32, drop otherwise (Figure 6).
  3. **Ablation - GPK Split:** Fix all else, vary GPK g₁ in {1,2,4,8,16,32}. Expect non-monotonic curve with optimum at g₁=8 (Figure 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How does BATQuant's performance generalize to hardware platforms with MXFP quantization block sizes (e.g., g) different from the 32-element granularity it was designed for?
- **Question 2:** Does the architecture-specific optimization in BATQuant hinder its applicability to non-Qwen model families (e.g., Llama, GPT, Mixture-of-Experts)?
- **Question 3:** What is the theoretical limit and potential interference between BATQuant's learned transformations and GPTQ's weight quantization, given that the paper consistently combines them but does not disentangle their effects?
- **Question 4:** To what extent do the learnable clipping parameters risk overfitting to the small calibration set (128 samples), potentially harming out-of-distribution robustness?
- **Question 5:** Can the principles of BATQuant be extended to other low-bit MXFP formats (e.g., MXFP6, MXFP8) or asymmetric quantization without fundamental redesign?

## Limitations
- **Hardware emulation gap:** Results rely on microxcaling software emulation; actual MXFP4 hardware may exhibit different rounding/noise, affecting recovery rates.
- **Calibration protocol opacity:** Sampling strategy, tokenization details, vision-text pairing, and handling of non-divisible dimensions remain unspecified, risking reproducibility.
- **Generalization beyond Qwen:** Claims are based solely on Qwen3 series; applicability to other architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder, different norms) is untested.

## Confidence
- **Overall SOTA recovery:** Medium (benchmarks strong but unverified; depends on unknown calibration details and hardware assumptions).
- **Block-wise alignment mechanism:** Medium-High (Figure 6 ablation robustly shows block size mismatch degrades performance).
- **GPK efficiency:** High (Table 1 clearly shows >74% parameter reduction; Figure 7 validates g₁=8 split).
- **Learnable clipping:** Medium (Table 4 shows consistent but marginal gains; isolating its effect from affine transformation is challenging).
