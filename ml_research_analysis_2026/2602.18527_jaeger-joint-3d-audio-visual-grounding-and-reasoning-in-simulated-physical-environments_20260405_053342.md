# JAEGER: Joint 3D Audio-Visual Grounding and Reasoning in Simulated Physical Environments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18527
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18527
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: JAEGER achieves a median angular error of 2.21° for single-source direction-of-arrival estimation and 99.2% accuracy on multi-speaker joint audio-visual reasoning tasks.

## Executive Summary
Current audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs) are limited to 2D perception (RGB video, monaural audio), which hinders reliable 3D source localization and spatial reasoning. The JAEGER framework addresses this by integrating RGB-D visual streams with multi-channel first-order ambisonics (FOA) audio. A key innovation is the Neural Intensity Vector (Neural IV), a learned audio representation that provides robust directional cues even with overlapping sources and reverberation. Trained and evaluated on SpatialSceneQA—a new benchmark of 61k instruction-tuning samples from simulated 3D environments—JAEGER with Neural IV achieves state-of-the-art results: sub-degree direction-of-arrival estimation, accurate 3D grounding of sound-emitting objects (3D IoU 0.32), and exceptional joint reasoning accuracy (99.2%). Ablations confirm that explicit depth and FOA audio are indispensable components. The work demonstrates that explicit 3D modeling is essential for robust spatial perception and reasoning in physical environments.

## Method Summary
JAEGER extends AV-LLMs to 3D by fusing RGB-D video with spatial audio from FOA microphones. The system processes visual input through a depth-aware encoder to obtain 3D scene understanding. Audio is handled by an encoder that extracts features from multi-channel FOA signals and projects them into the Neural Intensity Vector (Neural IV), a compact learned representation encoding directional information. The Neural IV is combined with visual features and fed into a large language model (LLM) that performs joint reasoning and grounding, answering spatial questions and localizing sound sources in 3D space. The model is trained end-to-end on the SpatialSceneQA dataset, which comprises diverse 3D scenes with precise object and sound source annotations, using instruction-tuning to align the LLM with multimodal spatial tasks.

## Key Results
- Single-source direction-of-arrival median angular error: 2.21°.
- Overlapping sources direction-of-arrival median angular error: 13.13°.
- 3D visual grounding of sound-emitting objects: 3D IoU = 0.32, median center error = 0.16 m.
- Multi-speaker joint audio-visual reasoning accuracy: 99.2%.
- Ablation: removal of depth or FOA audio causes catastrophic performance drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
JAEGER’s success stems from the explicit 3D representations and the Neural IV. Depth information anchors objects in 3D space, while FOA audio provides full spherical directional cues. The Neural IV distills the complex acoustic scene, including overlapping sources and reverberation, into a robust directional embedding. By feeding these aligned multimodal features into an LLM, the system can perform complex spatial reasoning that requires understanding relationships between visual entities and sound sources. The end-to-end training on a large-scale synthetic dataset ensures coherence between perception and reasoning components.

## Foundational Learning
Key concepts underpinning JAEGER:

- **RGB-D imaging**: combines color (RGB) and depth data to provide 3D structure of a scene. *Needed for* explicit 3D object localization. *Quick check*: Verify depth maps align with RGB frames and metric accuracy.
- **First-order ambisonics (FOA)**: a compact representation of sound field captured by a tetrahedral microphone array, encoding directional information in four channels. *Needed for* full-sphere audio localization without beamforming. *Quick check*: Decode a known direction sound to confirm vector orientation.
- **Neural Intensity Vector (Neural IV)**: a learned embedding from FOA features that encodes sound direction. *Needed for* robust localization under reverberation and source overlap. *Quick check*: Visualize IV vectors for single sources at different angles; they should be consistently oriented.
- **Multimodal fusion**: integrating visual and audio streams into a unified representation for the LLM. *Needed for* joint reasoning. *Quick check*: Test model performance when one modality is dropped; both should contribute.
- **Large Language Model (LLM)**: used as a reasoning engine over multimodal tokens. *Needed for* flexible instruction following and spatial question answering. *Quick check*: Probe with text-only queries in the spatial domain to ensure LLM prior knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
RGB-D input -> Visual Encoder -> Visual tokens;  
FOA audio input -> Audio Encoder -> Neural IV -> Audio tokens;  
Visual tokens + Audio tokens -> LLM -> Question answering / 3D grounding output.

**Critical path**:  
1. RGB-D frames are encoded into visual features with explicit 3D coordinates.  
2. FOA audio is converted to Neural IV vectors.  
3. Features are tokenized and concatenated.  
4. LLM processes the multimodal prompt to produce answers and optionally 3D bounding box predictions.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *FOA vs. microphone arrays*: FOA offers a compact, spherical representation but may suffer from spatial aliasing at high frequencies; the Neural IV learns to compensate.  
- *Explicit depth vs. monocular depth estimation*: Using raw depth sensors adds cost but guarantees metric accuracy; monocular estimates would introduce noise.  
- *End-to-end training vs. modular*: End-to-end simplifies pipeline but requires large multimodal datasets; modular approaches may be more interpretable.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Large angular errors (>10°) in single-source DOA.  
- Incorrect association between speakers and visual objects (low grounding IoU).  
- Sensitivity to reverberation: performance degrades sharply when reverberation time exceeds training distribution.  
- Inability to handle more than a certain number of overlapping sources (e.g., >3).

**First three validation experiments**:  
1. Ablate depth: replace depth with zeros or a learned monocular depth estimator to quantify depth contribution.  
2. Replace FOA with monaural audio to confirm spatial audio necessity.  
3. Test Neural IV vs. traditional direction-of-arrival algorithms (e.g., beamforming) on the same features to isolate learned representation benefits.

## Limitations
- **Simulation-to-reality gap**: Training and evaluation are purely in simulated environments; real-world acoustic and visual variability is unaddressed.
- **Benchmark novelty and scope**: SpatialSceneQA is newly created and lacks external human validation; synthetic question distribution may not reflect real-world spatial reasoning challenges.
- **Component interpretability**: The Neural IV is a black-box representation; its internal mechanisms and failure modes under novel acoustic conditions are not well understood.

## Confidence
- 3D Audio-Visual Framework Design: High  
- State-of-the-Art on SpatialSceneQA: Medium-High  
- Robustness to Overlapping Sources: Medium  
- Generalization to Real Physical Worlds: Low  

## Next Checks
1. **Real-World Dataset Evaluation**: Apply JAEGER to an existing real-world 3D audio-visual dataset (e.g., with microphone arrays and RGB-D cameras) to assess performance degradation due to simulation bias.  
2. **Cross-Dataset Generalization Test**: Train on SpatialSceneQA and evaluate zero-shot on a different 3D audio-visual QA benchmark to check for overfitting.  
3. **Acoustic Robustness Stress Test**: Introduce controlled acoustic variations (reverberation time, absorption coefficients, non-FOA microphone setups) during evaluation to identify failure thresholds of the Neural IV.
