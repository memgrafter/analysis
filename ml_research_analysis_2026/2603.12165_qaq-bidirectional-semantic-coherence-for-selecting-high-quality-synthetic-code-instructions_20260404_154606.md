# QAQ: Bidirectional Semantic Coherence for Selecting High-Quality Synthetic Code Instructions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12165
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12165
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: Selecting the middle 50–75% percentile of samples by stratified Reverse Mutual Information (RMI) on 25% of the WarriorCoder dataset achieves performance comparable to training on the full dataset.

## Executive Summary
Synthetic code instruction data often contains noise and hallucinations, where methods like Instruction-Following Difficulty (IFD) fail to separate intrinsic task complexity from low-quality samples. The QAQ method addresses this by introducing bidirectional semantic coherence, measuring how well an answer predicts its query (Q|A) via Reverse Mutual Information (RMI). By stratifying RMI by question complexity and using disagreement between strong and weak models, QAQ identifies samples that are both valid and challenging. On the WarriorCoder dataset, selecting just 25% of data with stratified RMI matches full-data training performance on HumanEval+ and MBPP+, significantly outperforming IFD and random baselines.

## Method Summary
QAQ is a data selection pipeline for synthetic code instructions. First, for each query-answer pair, it computes the Reverse Mutual Information (RMI = log PPL(Q) – log PPL(Q|A)) using a strong model (e.g., DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Base). RMI quantifies how much the answer reduces uncertainty about the query. Second, samples are stratified into decile bins based on query perplexity PPL(Q) to control for inherent question complexity. Within each bin, RMI values are normalized into ranks. The core selection then chooses samples whose normalized RMI rank falls in the middle range (e.g., 0.5–0.75), filtering out both low-quality misaligned pairs (low RMI) and defective high-RMI “echo” samples. An extension uses the difference in normalized RMI ranks between a strong and a weak model (“cognitive gap”) to select samples that are valid yet challenging.

## Key Results
- Stratified RMI selection (50–75% percentile, 25% of data) achieves pass@1 scores of 72.56 on HumanEval+ and 58.47 on MBPP+, essentially matching full-data training (72.56 / 59.52).
- This selection substantially outperforms IFD, random sampling, and top-RMI baselines.
- The cognitive gap approach (high disagreement between strong/weak models) yields a subset with only 13.85% overlap with consensus-based selection and improves HumanEval+ performance by over 3 points compared to selecting samples both models agree are high-quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Bidirectional semantic coherence (Q|A) better identifies valid query-answer pairs than unidirectional generation difficulty (A|Q/IFD) because it detects misalignment where the answer does not meaningfully constrain the query.
- **Mechanism**: RMI = logPPL(Q) – logPPL(Q|A) quantifies how much the answer reduces uncertainty about the query. Low RMI occurs when the answer provides no predictive signal about the query (e.g., off-topic or nonsensical queries paired with generic answers). The reverse direction captures whether the answer is a specific explanation of the query.
- **Core assumption**: A high-quality code instruction sample requires that the answer be sufficiently specific to reconstruct the core of the query. Trivial or misaligned pairs yield low RMI.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract: “low RMI indicates semantic misalignment”; Section 3.1: “low RMI suggests the answer does not relate well to the question”; Corpus: Strong correlation with known failure modes (e.g., greeting “Hi!” paired with irrelevant answer yields negative RMI).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Extremely high RMI samples often contain defect patterns (e.g., keyword echoing/paraphrasing) that provide limited learning signal, and filtering these extremes improves training efficiency.
- **Mechanism**: When the answer contains verbatim or near-verbatim phrases from the query (including fabricated jargon), PPL(Q|A) becomes extremely low, driving RMI very high. These samples are “easy” for the model in the reverse direction due to surface-level cues rather than deep semantic understanding.
- **Core assumption**: Very high RMI indicates the model exploits shallow pattern matching (repetition) rather than capturing a challenging, semantically rich instruction.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract: “excessively high RMI may contain defect patterns that LLMs easily recognize”; Section 4.2: Case study with nonsense “gritty sulla matrix” echo yields RMI=3.37 and poor downstream value; Section 4.2: Performance drop when using RMI Top-25% vs. mid-range (50-75%).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Model disagreement in stratified RMI rankings (cognitive gap) isolates samples that are both valid (recognized by strong model) and learnable (challenging for weak model), outperforming consensus-based selection.
- **Mechanism**: Compute normalized RMI ranks within question complexity bins separately for a strong (Ms) and weak (Mw) model. The difference Diff = rs – rw selects samples where Ms gives high rank (valid/challenging) but Mw gives low rank (not trivially easy). This disentangles data quality from inherent difficulty.
- **Core assumption**: A strong model's high RMI indicates genuine semantic alignment, while a weak model's low RMI indicates the sample is not trivially predictable. The intersection (high Diff) yields valuable training examples.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract: “disagreement between strong and weak models to identify samples that are valid yet challenging”; Section 3.3: Table 2 interpretation of four agreement/disagreement cases; Section 4.3: Diff-High (25% data) outperforms Sum-High (consensus) by 3+ points on HumanEval+; only 13.85% overlap between selected sets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Perplexity as inverse probability**
  - *Why needed here*: RMI is defined via PPL(Q) and PPL(Q|A). Understanding that lower perplexity means higher likelihood under the model is essential to interpreting RMI values and their extremes.
  - *Quick check question*: If PPL(Q|A) is much lower than PPL(Q), what does that imply about the relationship between Q and A according to the model?
- **Concept: Mutual information asymmetry in practice**
  - *Why needed here*: The paper hinges on the empirical fact that I(Q;A) estimated via conditional perplexity in direction A|Q (IFD) versus Q|A (RMI) captures different signals due to length, information density, and model biases.
  - *Quick check question*: Why might a model find it easier to generate a short answer given a long query than to generate a long query given a short answer?
- **Concept: Model disagreement as a signal beyond averaging**
  - *Why needed here*: The cognitive gap hypothesis treats disagreement not as noise but as informative about sample characteristics (validity vs. difficulty). This contrasts with typical ensembling that relies on consensus.
  - *Quick check question*: If two models of different capabilities disagree heavily on a sample's quality score, what are two possible interpretations?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw dataset -> Score computation (RMI) -> Stratification (by PPL(Q)) -> Within-bin ranking -> Disagreement module (Diff = rs - rw) -> Selection (threshold τ) -> Curated subset S.

**Critical path**: Compute PPL(Q) and PPL(Q|A) for each sample using strong and weak models -> Bin samples by PPL(Q) into K deciles -> For each bin, compute normalized RMI rank r for each model -> Calculate Diff = rs - rw -> Apply threshold τ to extract final subset S (e.g., τ=0.1 for ~25% retention).

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Binning fineness (K): Smaller K mixes complexity levels; larger K enforces tighter matching but risks sparse bins. Stability found for K∈[5,50].
- Model pair selection: Requires capability gap but not necessarily code-specificity. Larger gap may increase noise if weak model is too weak to meaningfully compute RMI.
- Threshold τ: Directly controls subset size; must be tuned for desired retention fraction.

**Failure signatures**:
- RMI distribution shows no clear bimodality/outliers → dataset may lack targeted defect patterns.
- Extremely low correlation (ρ<0.5) between strong/weak model RMI ranks → models mismatched or data domain too dissimilar.
- Selected subset performs worse than random → flawed scoring (template issues) or dataset lacks exploitable Q|A patterns.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. RMI sanity check: Compute RMI on a small labeled subset (e.g., 100 samples with known misaligned pairs). Verify low RMI for misaligned and very high RMI for echo/repetition cases.
2. Binning stability: For K∈{5,10,20,50}, check that within-bin RMI distributions are roughly uniform and that performance of the 50–75% selection is stable.
3. Model disagreement validation: Compute rs and rw for a held-out set. Confirm that Diff-high samples are rated high by strong model but not weak model, and that Diff-low samples are rated low by both.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the QAQ framework generalize effectively to non-code domains and seed-based synthetic datasets? The paper tested only on two seedless synthetic code datasets (WarriorCoder, Magpie); efficacy on other paradigms (e.g., Evol-Instruct, Alpaca) is untested.
- What is the optimal strategy for selecting the strong and weak model pair to maximize the informativeness of the “cognitive gap” (Diff) signal? The paper tested only a few pairs (e.g., DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B with smaller models); principles for balancing capability gap, architectural similarity, and data overlap are unknown.
- Can a curriculum learning approach that dynamically adjusts the QAQ selection criteria during fine-tuning improve training efficiency and final model performance compared to static one-time selection? QAQ performs static selection before training; a dynamic curriculum matching data difficulty to the model’s evolving capability might be superior.
- To what extent do inherent, model-specific biases in RMI computation (arising from pretraining data and objectives) affect the reliability and consistency of the “cognitive gap” signal, and can these biases be mitigated? Cross-model RMI correlations range 0.63–0.80, but the systematic impact on subset quality is not quantified.

## Limitations
- WarriorCoder source/preprocessing undisclosed; hinders exact replication.
- Results rely on DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B; generalizability to other models uncertain.
- RMI sensitive to chat templates and tokenization; minor formatting changes may alter rankings.

- Stratified 50–75% selection: Medium
