# Algorithmic Simplification of Neural Networks with Mosaic-of-Motifs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14896  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14896  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** MoMos maintains accuracy within ~1% of full-precision baselines on CIFAR-10 while achieving up to 9.4× higher algorithmic compression rates than quantization-aware training.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates why large neural networks are highly compressible despite being over-parameterized. It hypothesizes that training induces **algorithmic simplicity**—the emergence of structured, repeatable patterns (motifs) in weights—which lowers Kolmogorov complexity relative to random initialization. To test this, the authors propose **Mosaic-of-Motifs (MoMos)**, a method that partitions weights into blocks and forces reuse of a small set of motifs during training. Theoretically, this constrained hypothesis class guarantees a shorter worst-case description length. Empirically, across MLP, ResNet20, and Tiny-ViT on CIFAR-10, MoMos achieves accuracy comparable to full-precision models (e.g., Tiny-ViT: 82.59% vs 81.85% for quantization-aware training) with significantly higher compression rates. Analysis shows that trained models exhibit lower Block Decomposition Method (BDM) complexity than their initializations, and distortion between original and MoMos-reconstructed weights decreases over training, confirming that optimization naturally discovers reusable structure.

## Method Summary
MoMos enforces algorithmic simplicity by constraining the weight parameterization during training. Let `w ∈ ℝⁿ` be the flattened weight vector. The method partitions `w` into `m = ⌈n/s⌉` blocks of size `s` via a partition function `ψ`. From the current blocks, it randomly selects `k` motifs (including a zero motif for sparsity), where `k = max(1, ⌊c·m⌋)` and `c` is the relative motif capacity. Each block is assigned to its nearest motif (L2 distance) forming a mosaic `M: [m] → [k]`. The reconstructed weight vector `bw` concatenates the assigned motifs and removes padding. After each optimizer step, `w` is replaced by `bw`, projecting it onto the constrained hypothesis class `H_{s,k}` (a union of linear subspaces defined by motif reuse). Training proceeds with standard optimizers (AdamW or SGD) and early stopping on validation loss. The process ensures that weights are represented by a small reusable codebook, thereby reducing algorithmic complexity while attempting to preserve model performance.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy preservation:** Across MLP, ResNet20, and Tiny-ViT on CIFAR-10, MoMos achieves validation/test accuracy within ~1% of full-precision baselines, even at low motif capacity (e.g., `c=0.5%` for Tiny-ViT).
- **High compression rates:** MoMos yields up to 9.4× higher Relative Algorithmic Compression (RAC) than quantization-aware training with just 0.5% motif capacity, demonstrating superior parameter reuse.
- **Complexity reduction:** For >90% of 200 pretrained models, the BDM complexity ratio (trained vs. random initialization) is less than 1, indicating that training lowers algorithmic complexity. Reconstruction distortion (`‖w−bw‖₂`) decreases during MoMos training, confirming recovery of the original model’s performance within the constrained space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Training induces algorithmic simplicity by creating repeatable block structures ("motifs") in weight matrices, lowering Kolmogorov complexity relative to random initialization.  
**Mechanism:** The MoMos parameterization partitions weights into blocks of size `s` and restricts each block to be selected from a small set of `k` reusable motifs, enforcing repeatability. This constrained hypothesis class `H_{s,k}` guarantees a shorter worst-case description length than the trivial `n·q` bit encoding (Proposition 3.3).  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3 formalize the hypothesis class and prove `K(bw) ≤ k·s·q + m·⌈log₂k⌉ + ζ`. The BDM complexity ratio `r_BDM < 1` for most trained models (Figure 2) supports that optimization discovers repeatable patterns.  
**Break condition:** If motifs cannot be reused across many blocks (`k ≈ m`), the constraint becomes overly restrictive and accuracy collapses (observed for large `s` and low `c` in Figure 4).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The constrained optimization domain remains sufficiently rich (contains many distinct linear subspaces) to learn effective models despite enforced repetition.  
**Mechanism:** Different mosaics `M` define distinct linear subspaces `H_{s,k}(M)` via equality constraints (`b_i = b_j` for positions sharing a motif index). The number of distinct subspaces `α(m,k)` grows exponentially with the number of blocks `m` (Corollary 3.9) and is non-decreasing in `k`. The union `H_{s,k} = ∪_M H_{s,k}(M)` thus provides a rich optimization landscape.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 analyzes distinctness via partitions `P(M)`. Empirically, small block sizes `s` (large `m`) maintain baseline accuracy even at very low capacity `c` (Figure 4), confirming that the space of reusable structures is vast.  
**Break condition:** When `s` is large (small `m`) and `k` very small, `α(m,k)` becomes limited, leading to underfitting and accuracy drops (e.g., `s=128` at `c<0.5` in Figure 4).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Empirically, trained models exhibit lower algorithmic complexity (per BDM) than their random initializations, and MoMos models recover this structure while enforcing it during training.  
**Mechanism:** BDM approximates Kolmogorov complexity by summing CTM frequencies of repeated blocks. MoMos explicitly constructs weights from repeated motifs, ensuring low BDM by design. Training with MoMos projection minimizes distortion `‖w − bw‖₂`, allowing the unconstrained model `w` to adapt to the repeatable structure.  
**Evidence anchors:** BDM complexity ratio `r_BDM < 1` for >90% of pretrained models (Figure 2). Distortion `d` decreases and validation loss stabilizes during MoMos training (Figure 3), showing successful recovery.  
**Break condition:** Poor motif initialization or aggressive mosaic assignment can cause distortion divergence, leading to permanent accuracy loss (e.g., `ResNet20, s=4, c=0.5` in Table 1).

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov complexity:** The length of the shortest program that outputs a given object. Needed to formalize "algorithmic simplicity" and argue that training reduces `K(w)`. Quick check: "If `K(w)` is the Kolmogorov complexity of weight matrix `w`, why is the trivial upper bound `n·q` bits not a measure of learned simplicity?"
- **Block Decomposition Method (BDM):** An approximation of Kolmogorov complexity for binary strings, summing CTM frequencies of occurring blocks plus `log(m_i)` for repetitions. Needed to measure complexity reduction in real-valued weights after binarization. Quick check: "How does BDM differ from entropy in measuring weight structure? Why might two weight matrices have similar distributions but different BDM scores?"
- **Constrained hypothesis classes (`H_{s,k}`) as unions of linear subspaces:** `H_{s,k}` is the set of weight vectors formed by reusing `k` motifs across `m` blocks of size `s`. It equals `∪_M H_{s,k}(M)`, where each `H_{s,k}(M)` is a linear subspace of dimension ≤ `s·k_M`. Needed to theoretically show the constrained space remains expressive. Quick check: "Given a mosaic `M`, what does `dim(H_{s,k}(M)) = s·k_M` mean? How does changing `k` vs. `s` affect the number of distinct subspaces `α(m,k)`?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Block partition (`ψ`) → Motif set `Z` (size `k`) → Mosaic `M` (assignment map) → Reconstruction (`ψ⁻¹`) → Replaced parameters

**Critical path:**  
1. After each optimizer step, flatten all parameters into `w` and pad to length divisible by `s`. Split into `m` blocks of size `s` via `ψ`.  
2. Form motif set `Z`: include zero motif `z₀=0`; sample `k−1` distinct block indices uniformly from `[m]` and set `z_i` to those blocks.  
3. For each block `b_i`, find nearest motif: `M(i) = argmin_j ‖b_i − z_j‖₂²`.  
4. Reconstruct `bw` by concatenating `z_{M(1..m)}` and removing padding.  
5. Replace model parameters with `bw`; repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- `s` (block size): Smaller `s` increases `m`, enriching the optimization domain (`α(m,k)↑`) but requires more motifs for same capacity `c`. Larger `s` improves compression potential but risks underfitting.  
- `c = k/m` (relative capacity): Lower `c` yields higher compression but may lose accuracy. Tune `c` with `s` to balance RAC and performance.  
- Motif initialization: Random sampling per iteration is simple; alternatives (e.g., k-means) might reduce distortion but add compute.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Distortion divergence:** `‖w − bw‖₂` increases during training → mosaic assignment unstable or `k` too small. Increase `c` or `s`.  
- **Accuracy collapse:** Validation accuracy significantly below FP32 baseline → optimization domain too restricted. Decrease `s` or increase `c`.  
- **No BDM reduction:** `r_BDM ≈ 1` → model not learning repeatable structure. Verify zero motif inclusion; try larger `c`.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **BDM complexity ratio sweep:** Train a model (e.g., Tiny-ViT) with MoMos across various `(s, c)` and compute `r_BDM` at convergence. Expect `r_BDM < 1` for all successful runs.  
2. **Distortion tracking:** Log `d = ‖w − bw‖₂` per epoch for `(s=4, c=0.1)` across MLP/ResNet20/Tiny-ViT. Expect monotonic decrease/stabilization alongside validation loss.  
3. **Accuracy-capacity curve:** For fixed `s` (e.g., `2,4,8`), sweep `c ∈ [0.005, 0.8]` and plot validation accuracy. Expect small `s` to maintain baseline accuracy down to very low `c`; large `s` to require `c > 0.5`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Is the reduction in algorithmic complexity during training the primary cause of neural network compressibility, or is it a correlated byproduct of other optimization dynamics? The paper shows correlation but not causation; competing theories (e.g., lottery ticket hypothesis) are not contrasted.
- **Open Question 2:** How can we design more faithful and scalable approximations of Kolmogorov complexity for high-precision, real-valued weights? BDM is defined for binary strings and may be misleading; new proxies adapted for deep learning are needed.
- **Open Question 3:** Can motif selection and assignment be optimized (e.g., via learned codebooks or differentiable assignments) instead of random sampling and nearest-neighbor projection? The paper leaves open whether learning motifs would improve the accuracy-compression trade-off.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the precise theoretical relationship between block size `s`, motif capacity `c`, and recoverable accuracy? Empirically, small `s` enriches the optimization space, but no formal bounds link `(s,c)` to target accuracy loss.
- **Open Question 5:** How can MoMos be efficiently implemented on modern hardware to realize inference speedups? Current libraries lack support for reusable block operations; co-design with quantization or sparsity patterns is speculative.

## Limitations
- BDM is an unvalidated proxy for Kolmogorov complexity; its correlation with true compressibility is unclear.
- Missing experimental details (weight binarization method, BDM block size, motif sampling specifics) impede faithful reproduction.
- Evaluation restricted to CIFAR-10 and three small architectures; generalizability to large-scale vision/NLP tasks is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Training induces algorithmic simplicity via motif reuse (BDM reduction):** Medium (empirical but proxy limitations)  
- **Hypothesis class `H_{s,k}` remains sufficiently expressive:** High (extensive architecture sweeps confirm robustness)  
- **MoMos achieves high RAC with minimal accuracy loss:** Medium (results strong but limited dataset/models)  
- **Algorithmic simplicity fundamentally explains compressibility:** Low (indirect evidence; needs stronger causal link)
