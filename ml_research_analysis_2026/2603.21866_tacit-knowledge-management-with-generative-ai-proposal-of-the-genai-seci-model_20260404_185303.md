# Tacit Knowledge Management with Generative AI: Proposal of the GenAI SECI Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21866
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21866
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** A conceptual GenAI SECI model that retains Nonaka’s four conversion modes and positions generative AI as an auxiliary tool for human-led symbol grounding, supported by a proposed Digital Knowledge Twin system architecture.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a gap in systematic models for integrated tacit and explicit knowledge management using generative AI, as prior work largely confined AI to explicit knowledge handling. It proposes the GenAI SECI model, introducing "Digital Fragmented Knowledge"—partial, fragmented field knowledge in cyberspace—and applies generative AI to the Externalization, Combination, and Internalization processes of the SECI framework to collect, structure, and recommend these fragments. The core result is a conceptual model that preserves the original SECI's four conversion modes while positioning AI as a tool that assists human-led symbol grounding in workshops, contrasting with prior models that treat AI as a new actor. A concrete system architecture, the Digital Knowledge Twin, is presented as an implementation pathway. The model emphasizes simplicity and workshop-based collaborative internalization but requires empirical validation across workplaces.

## Method Summary
The paper’s method is a conceptual model design that extends Nonaka’s SECI framework with generative AI capabilities. The task is to implement a system that processes "Digital Fragmented Knowledge" (e.g., voice messages, photos, sensor data from workplaces) to support knowledge creation. Inputs are multimodal fragments captured from workplace events. The objective is to facilitate human internalization—where knowledge "clicks into place"—through collaborative workshops. The proposed procedure involves three automated processes within the Digital Knowledge Twin architecture: (1) **Externalization (A)**: semi-automatic aggregation of raw multimodal inputs into coherent knowledge fragments; (2) **Combination (B)**: loose structuring of fragments by linking them to operational processes or objects via an extended knowledge graph; (3) **Internalization (C)**: automatic classification and recommendation of fragments to stimulate discussion in workshops. Human participants remain central to symbol grounding and insight generation.

## Key Results
- The GenAI SECI model retains the original SECI’s four conversion modes (Socialization, Externalization, Combination, Internalization) while integrating generative AI into Externalization, Combination, and Internalization as an auxiliary tool.
- The model introduces "Digital Fragmented Knowledge" as a new form of cyberspace-based field knowledge and positions AI for collection, structuring, and recommendation, with humans leading symbol grounding in workshops.
- A system blueprint called the Digital Knowledge Twin is proposed to implement the model, emphasizing simplicity, workshop-centric internalization, and loose knowledge graph structuring.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model works by leveraging generative AI to handle the growing volume of fragmented digital traces from workplaces (e.g., sensor logs, photos, voice notes), which traditional SECI cannot systematically process. AI aggregates these fragments into usable summaries (Externalization), links them to existing operational knowledge via a knowledge graph (Combination), and recommends relevant fragments to trigger reflective discussion in workshops (Internalization). This offloads labor-intensive curation to AI while preserving human primacy in meaning-making (symbol grounding), avoiding the theoretical complication of treating AI as a new knowledge actor. The workshop setting provides the social Ba (shared space) for internalization, where participants connect recommended fragments to their tacit experiences.

## Foundational Learning
- **SECI model**: Nonaka’s framework of knowledge conversion modes (Socialization, Externalization, Combination, Internalization). *Why needed*: Provides the theoretical scaffold for integrating AI into knowledge processes. *Quick check*: Can you describe each mode and its role in creating knowledge?
- **Tacit vs. explicit knowledge**: Tacit is personal, context-bound, and hard to formalize; explicit is codified and transmissible. *Why needed*: The model targets tacit knowledge management, a historically difficult area. *Quick check*: Provide an example of each from a workplace.
- **Digital Fragmented Knowledge**: Partial, multimodal digital traces (e.g., a photo of a machine part, a voice memo about an anomaly). *Why needed*: Represents a new, abundant form of knowledge source in digital workplaces. *Quick check*: List three types of such fragments from a manufacturing setting.
- **Generative AI as auxiliary tool**: AI assists but does not replace human cognition in symbol grounding. *Why needed*: Maintains human agency while exploiting AI efficiency. *Quick check*: Contrast this with an "AI-as-actor" SECI extension.
- **Symbol grounding**: The process of linking symbols (e.g., words, data) to real-world referents and meaning. *Why needed*: Central to internalization; humans uniquely perform this. *Quick check*: Explain how a maintenance technician grounds the term "vibration anomaly" during a workshop.
- **Workshop-based collaboration**: Structured group sessions for sensemaking and knowledge sharing. *Why needed*: Provides the social Ba for Internalization in the model. *Quick check*: Describe a workshop protocol that could trigger internalization.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Raw Multimodal Inputs -> Externalization (A) -> Knowledge Fragments -> Combination (B) -> Knowledge Graph -> Internalization (C) -> Workshop Recommendations

**Critical path:** A (aggregation) → B (graph linking) → C (recommendation) → human workshop.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Automation vs. human oversight: High automation in A/B/C risks incoherent fragments or noisy graphs; requires human curation loops.
- Loose vs. tight structuring: A loosely linked graph maximizes recall but may reduce precision; a tightly curated graph may miss unexpected connections.
- Generality vs. domain specificity: The model aims for cross-domain applicability but may need domain-specific fine-tuning of prompts and ontologies.

**Failure signatures:**
- Aggregated fragments are inaccurate, generic, or miss critical multimodal context.
- Knowledge graph is too sparse (few links) or too noisy (spurious links), making retrieval ineffective.
- Recommended fragments fail to stimulate meaningful workshop discussion (e.g., too obvious, irrelevant, or lacking novelty).

**First experiments:**
1. Implement Externalization: Use an LLM with a fixed prompt to aggregate a dataset of multimodal workplace traces (e.g., voice transcript + photo + sensor data). Have domain experts rate 100 output fragments for accuracy and completeness.
2. Build the knowledge graph: Define a simple domain ontology (e.g., {Process, Object, Problem, Solution}). Use the LLM to extract entities/relations and build a Neo4j graph. Test retrieval against 20 benchmark queries (precision@5, recall@5).
3. Simulate an internalization workshop: Recruit 8-10 participants for a sensemaking task. Treatment group receives AI-recommended fragments; control group uses raw fragments. Compare coded discussion metrics (insight novelty, relevant discussion duration).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does the GenAI SECI model generalize across diverse workplace domains (e.g., manufacturing, healthcare, agriculture, maintenance)?**  
  *Basis in paper:* The conclusion explicitly states that empirical validation across diverse settings is needed.  
  *Why unresolved:* The model remains conceptual and untested in different sectors.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic multi-domain field experiments measuring knowledge creation and performance impacts.

- **What are the precise theoretical boundaries, properties, and taxonomy of "Digital Fragmented Knowledge"?**  
  *Basis in paper:* The conclusion notes further conceptual development is required.  
  *Why unresolved:* The concept is newly introduced but lacks a clear definition or structure.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A rigorous classification framework supported by empirical categorization of real-world fragments.

- **How must the GenAI SECI model evolve to remain valid as generative AI capabilities advance?**  
  *Basis in paper:* The conclusion states that continuous reassessment will be essential as AI evolves.  
  *Why unresolved:* The model assumes current AI functionalities; future AI may alter human-AI roles.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Longitudinal studies tracking AI progress and experiments with next-generation models to update the framework.

- **Does the Digital Knowledge Twin system effectively implement the GenAI SECI model in practice, particularly for externalization, combination, and internalization?**  
  *Basis in paper:* The system is mentioned as "under development" with full integration and evaluation as future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* The architecture is proposed but not realized or evaluated.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Deployment of a complete prototype in actual workplaces with user studies and empirical metrics on fragment quality, recommendation accuracy, and internalization outcomes.

## Limitations
- The paper provides no implementation specifics (e.g., LLM prompts, fine-tuning, hyperparameters) for the three core processes.
- It lacks operational definitions or metrics for "symbol grounding" and "internalization," relying on anecdotal insight generation.
- Empirical validation is absent; the model is conceptual with no real-world workplace studies.
- The assumption of human primacy (AI as auxiliary) is theoretical and not tested against alternative actor-based models.

## Confidence
- **High:** The conceptual contrast with prior "AI-as-actor" SECI extensions and the clear articulation of integrating Digital Fragmented Knowledge into SECI modes.
- **Medium:** The Digital Knowledge Twin architecture logically implements the model’s processes but remains an unevaluated blueprint.
- **Low:** Claims about improving internalization via fragment recommendation in workshops lack empirical support; the assumed workshop protocol and its effectiveness are speculative.

## Next Checks
- **Aggregation Quality Benchmark:** Implement Externalization with a fixed LLM and multimodal dataset. Have domain experts (n≥3) rate 100 output fragments vs. source data for accuracy, completeness, and conciseness. Target: >85% expert agreement on "accurate and complete."
- **Graph Retrieval Stress Test:** Build a knowledge graph for a closed domain (e.g., a specific manufacturing line). Create 20 known-question/answer pairs. Measure precision@5 and recall@5 of the graph-based retrieval system against this ground truth.
- **Workshop Impact Pilot:** Run a controlled experiment (2 groups, 8-10 participants each) on a sensemaking task. Treatment group receives AI-recommended fragments; control group uses raw fragments or none. Compare coded discussion metrics: insight novelty (modified Sensemaking rubric), duration of relevant discussion, and self-reported "aha moment" frequency.
