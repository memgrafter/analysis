# Federated Inference: Toward Privacy-Preserving Collaborative and Incentivized Model Serving
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02214
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02214
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Federated Inference (FI) enables collaborative inference with privately owned models using SMPC for privacy, but incurs 50–200× computational overhead; ensemble effectiveness degrades under severe non-IID heterogeneity (e.g., soft voting 28.6% vs single best 40.5% on CIFAR-10), and label-free reward schemes often misalign with true model merit.

## Executive Summary
This paper formalizes Federated Inference (FI) as a setting where multiple organizations collaboratively perform inference using their privately owned models without sharing data or parameters. It instantiates FI via FedSEI, a system combining Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC) for privacy and ensemble aggregation for utility. The study finds that SMPC introduces significant computational overhead (50–200×) and that wide-area network latency dominates deployment costs. Furthermore, the benefits of model ensembling are highly dependent on the data heterogeneity across clients; under severe non-IID conditions, ensembles can perform worse than the single best local model. Finally, designing fair incentives without access to ground-truth labels during inference is fundamentally challenging, as common label-free reward schemes misalign with actual model contribution, especially in non-IID settings.

## Method Summary
FedSEI operationalizes FI using additive secret sharing across multiple non-colluding computing parties. A client secret-shares its input and broadcasts shares to all parties. Each party holds a share of every model's parameters. All parties jointly evaluate each model and perform a secure weighted aggregation (e.g., soft voting) on the protected predictions. The final aggregated output share is sent to the client for reconstruction. An on-chain smart contract handles job registration, fee escrow, and reward distribution using signatures from the computing parties. The system assumes a semi-honest threat model where computing parties are curious but follow the protocol, and no single party or colluding subset below a threshold can learn secrets.

## Key Results
- SMPC-based inference incurs a **50–200× computational overhead** on CPU compared to plaintext execution, with overhead scaling with model size and number of parties.
- In geographically distributed deployments, **network Round-Trip Time (RTT) dominates latency**, leading to minute-scale inference times for cross-continent setups, making FI impractical for low-latency applications.
- **Ensemble effectiveness is context-dependent**; under severe non-IID data (Dirichlet α=0.05 on CIFAR-10), soft voting accuracy (28.6%) can be significantly worse than using the single best model (40.5%).
- **Label-free reward allocation schemes** (uniform, confidence-based, agreement-based) show poor alignment with true model merit (oracle based on test accuracy), particularly under high data heterogeneity, with some schemes performing worse than uniform splitting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC) based on additive secret sharing enables inference-time privacy preservation by keeping client inputs, model parameters, and intermediate predictions confidential from all parties, including the executing environment.
- **Mechanism:** Model parameters and client inputs are split into random shares distributed across multiple computing parties. All computations (model evaluation, ensemble aggregation) occur on these shares using SMPC protocols that satisfy the closure property (e.g., adding shares yields a valid share of the sum). Plaintext reconstruction only occurs for the final output on the client side.
- **Core assumption:** The system operates under a semi-honest (honest-but-curious) threat model with bounded collusion (fewer than |P| malicious computing parties), and model owners are mutually distrustful.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Explicitly states FI requires "inference-time privacy preservation" and instantiates FedSEI "combining SMPC for privacy."
  - [section 4.1] Details additive secret sharing and the closure property as the SMPC foundation.
  - [corpus] The corpus signals related work using SMPC/HE for privacy in distributed inference (e.g., FEDINF [19]), providing contextual support that SMPC is a recognized primitive for such settings, though not directly cited by the paper.
- **Break condition:** SMPC introduces 50–200× computational overhead on CPU (Section 5.1), and geographic network latency dominates wide-area deployments, leading to minute-scale inference times (Section 5.2), making it impractical for low-latency applications.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Collaborative inference via ensemble aggregation of privately owned models can yield meaningful performance gains, but effectiveness is highly context-dependent and can degrade under severe non-IID data heterogeneity.
- **Mechanism:** Protected predictions from each model (⟨𝑦𝑖⟩) are combined using a secure aggregation function (e.g., weighted average) executed entirely within the SMPC domain. The aggregation weights can be uniform (hard/soft voting) or input-dependent (entropy, spectral, TTA-based).
- **Core assumption:** Local models possess complementary knowledge due to heterogeneous training data distributions; naive aggregation will improve utility over the single-best model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States that "meaningful performance gains through collaboration" is a core requirement and finds "ensemble effectiveness is context-dependent and can degrade under severe non-IID heterogeneity."
  - [section 5.3] Provides concrete results: on CIFAR-10 with α=0.05, soft voting achieves 28.6% vs. single best 40.5% with LeNet, demonstrating degradation.
  - [corpus] Related works (e.g., [20], [32]) explore ensemble/online methods for edge AI, showing this is a common collaboration strategy, though the target paper highlights its fragility in FI.
- **Break condition:** When local data distributions are extremely skewed (e.g., Dirichlet α << 0.1), ensemble predictions can be dominated by models that are consistently wrong on certain queries, leading to catastrophic accuracy drops relative to the single-best local model.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Label-free reward allocation schemes (uniform, confidence-based, agreement-based) often fail to align rewards with true model merit (measured by individual accuracy), especially under severe non-IID conditions, posing a fundamental challenge for sustainable incentive design.
- **Mechanism:** Since ground-truth labels are unavailable at inference time, rewards are distributed using observable but imperfect signals: uniform sharing, inverse prediction entropy (confidence), or agreement with the ensemble output. These are compared against an "ideal merit share" oracle based on test accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** It is possible to infer relative contribution from model outputs alone in a privacy-preserving manner, without needing labels or access to other models' internals.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Notes "Label-free reward allocation schemes often misalign with true model merit, especially in non-IID settings."
  - [section 5.4] Quantifies misalignment using a "Reward Fairness" metric; under severe non-IID (α=0.05), all schemes show substantial deviation from perfect merit alignment, sometimes worse than uniform.
  - [corpus] Works like [30] and [67] discuss FL incentives, but the label-free constraint is unique to FI; corpus provides broader context on incentive challenges in collaborative learning.
- **Break condition:** Under high label skew (α ≤ 0.1), the correlation between any observable output statistic (confidence, agreement) and true model accuracy becomes weak or adversarial, causing reward schemes to systematically over- or under-reward contributors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC)**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the primary cryptographic tool for enforcing the confidentiality constraints (input, model, intermediate values) that define FI. Understanding its overhead properties (communication rounds, bandwidth) is critical for feasibility analysis.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the round complexity and primary bottleneck for ReLU activation in secret-sharing MPC?
- **Concept: Non-IID Data Heterogeneity in Federated Settings**
  - *Why needed here:* FI assumes models are trained on disjoint data. The degree of label/feature skew (Dirichlet α) directly determines whether ensemble aggregation is beneficial or harmful, as shown in Section 5.3.
  - *Quick check question:* How does extreme label skew (α=0.05) affect the accuracy of a soft-voting ensemble relative to the best single model?
- **Concept: Incentive Mechanism Design without Verifiable Ground Truth**
  - *Why needed here:* FI operates without access to labels during inference, making standard performance-based rewards impossible. This necessitates studying label-free proxies and their failure modes (Section 5.4, 6).
  - *Quick check question:* Why are confidence-based or agreement-based reward schemes unreliable under severe non-IID data distributions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Client -> SMPC Parties -> Client -> Incentive Layer
  Model Owners -> SMPC Parties
- **Critical path:**
  Client input secret-sharing -> SMPC parties compute ⟨𝑦𝑖⟩ for all models -> Secure weighted aggregation (⟨𝑦⟩) -> Shares sent to client -> Client reconstructs 𝑦 -> On-chain verification (signatures) -> Reward distribution.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Privacy vs. Latency: Stronger SMPC (more parties) increases computation/communication overhead (Section 5.1). Geographic distribution drastically increases latency due to network RTTs (Section 5.2).
  - Ensemble Utility vs. Heterogeneity: Uniform aggregation is robust but suboptimal; query-dependent weighting (entropy, TTA) can help in some non-IID regimes but may fail catastrophically under severe skew (Section 5.3).
  - Incentive Fairness vs. Privacy/Simplicity: More nuanced reward schemes require more observable signals (agreement, confidence), which may leak information or be manipulable. Uniform allocation is private and simple but may not reflect contribution (Section 5.4, 6).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Latency failure: End-to-end inference time exceeds application tolerance (e.g., >1 second for real-time, >minutes for batch). Diagnose by measuring network RTT vs. local computation (Table 7).
  - Accuracy failure: Ensemble accuracy falls below the single-best local model's accuracy on a validation set of non-IID data. Check Dirichlet α and aggregation strategy (Table 8, Figure 4).
  - Incentive failure: The Reward Fairness metric (𝓕) is significantly below 1.0, especially under non-IID (α < 0.3), indicating systematic misallocation (Figure 5).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. SMPC Computational Overhead (Table 5): Measure plain vs. SMPC (1P) inference latency for MLP/CNN models on CPU to isolate protocol cost. Expect 50–200× slowdown.
  2. Geographic Network Latency Impact (Table 7): Deploy 3 SMPC parties across AWS regions (same zone, inter-zone, inter-continent) and measure end-to-end latency for LeNet/ResNet-18. Expect minutes for cross-continent.
  3. Ensemble Accuracy under Non-IID (Table 8): Train K local models on Dirichlet-partitioned CIFAR-10 (vary α and K). Evaluate hard/soft voting and label-free weighted strategies on test set. Expect severe degradation for α=0.05.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can hybrid cryptographic protocols (e.g., combining SMPC with HE/TEEs) reduce the 50–200× computational overhead of privacy-preserving inference while maintaining the same confidentiality guarantees?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 8.1 states that "hybrid designs that selectively protect sensitive layers while executing others in plaintext may offer improved efficiency under acceptable privacy guarantees" and identifies the need to "understand the trade-offs between security assumptions, performance, and deployability."
- Why unresolved: Current SMPC-based inference uniformly applies expensive cryptographic operations to all layers, but the sensitivity of different model components (e.g., final classification layers vs. early feature extractors) varies. Without empirical studies on hybrid protocols across diverse model architectures and threat models, the efficiency-privacy Pareto frontier remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic evaluation comparing end-to-end latency and accuracy of hybrid vs. pure SMPC implementations on standard benchmarks (e.g., ResNet, BERT) under a quantified leakage budget (e.g., permissible information exposed by partially unprotected layers). Resolution requires demonstrating a practical configuration where overhead drops below, say, 50× while satisfying a formally defined privacy policy.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can non-ensemble collaboration mechanisms (e.g., model fusion or routing-based approaches) consistently outperform ensemble aggregation under severe non-IID data heterogeneity without requiring access to raw data or labels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 8.2 explicitly states that "ensemble inference does not universally yield performance gains under severe non-IID data heterogeneity" and proposes exploring "model fusion" and "routing mechanisms" as alternatives that could "offer computational advantages and different robustness properties."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows soft voting can degrade accuracy by over 11 percentage points compared to the single best model under extreme non-IID conditions (CIFAR-10, α=0.05). Alternative strategies like fusion or routing remain conceptually promising but unproven in the FI setting, where adaptation must occur without ground-truth labels or shared data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluations of a concrete fusion/routing algorithm (e.g., a secure multi-party variant of model soups or a privacy-preserving MoE router) across multiple non-IID regimes. Success would be demonstrated if the method reliably matches or exceeds the best local model performance while operating under FI's privacy constraints, with results showing consistent gains where standard ensembles fail.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What reward allocation mechanism can achieve fair compensation proportional to model contribution in label-free FI settings, especially under severe non-IID heterogeneity where current schemes (confidence, agreement) misalign with true merit?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 8.3 identifies the fundamental challenge: "inference-time collaboration typically lacks access to ground-truth labels, making it difficult to objectively measure individual model contributions." Section 5.4 empirically shows that under α=0.05, label-free schemes often perform worse than uniform allocation and deviate substantially from ideal merit shares.
- Why unresolved: The inherent ambiguity stems from the absence of a verifiable performance signal. Proxies like confidence or agreement are shown to be unreliable under label skew. Without a robust, manipulation-resistant metric that operates solely on protected predictions, sustainable economics for FI remain elusive.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal contribution metric that (a) is computable from only the ensemble's protected output and each model's protected prediction, (b) correlates strongly with oracle merit across diverse non-IID datasets in controlled experiments, and (c) resists gaming in a game-theoretic analysis. Validation would require showing significantly higher reward fairness (e.g., >0.9 on the paper's 𝓕 metric) under severe skew compared to baselines.

### Open Question 4
- Question: For geographically distributed deployments, can topology-aware protocol optimizations or hierarchical MPC designs mitigate the minute-scale latency caused by wide-area network RTT, without compromising the SMPC security model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5.2 establishes that "geographic network conditions, rather than model complexity, impose the fundamental constraint" with cross-continent deployments showing 200+ second latencies. Section 8.1 suggests "topology-aware execution or hierarchical protocol designs may mitigate latency."
- Why unresolved: The current SMPC implementation treats all parties equally in a single flat session, forcing synchronous communication across distant nodes. Adjusting the computation graph (e.g., grouping nearby parties, pre-computing shares) or altering the secret-sharing scheme could reduce round complexity, but may weaken the collusion resistance assumptions or require new cryptographic constructions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A prototype FI system that integrates a network-aware scheduler or hierarchical MPC protocol (e.g., local SMPC groups with aggregate outputs shared globally), evaluated on a real wide-area testbed. Latency reduction must be quantified (e.g., >50% improvement for global deployments) while formally arguing that the new design maintains at least the same security threshold (e.g., t-out-of-N collusion resilience) as the baseline.

## Limitations
- Dirichlet partitioning details (α handling, zero-class enforcement) and training data augmentation specifications are missing, which impacts reproducibility of non-IID ensemble and incentive results.
- SMPC configuration (fixed-point precision, CrypTen version, network optimizations) is not fully documented; reported overheads may be sensitive to these parameters.
- Advanced ensemble weighting strategies (spectral, TTA) lack details on calibration set size and augmentation parameters, hindering exact replication.
- The threat model assumes semi-honest parties with bounded collusion; robustness against active adversaries or realistic malicious behavior is not evaluated.
- Incentive fairness evaluation uses an oracle based on test labels; real-world deployment without labels may yield different dynamics and participant strategies.

## Confidence
- **SMPC privacy guarantee:** High. Based on well-established cryptographic principles of additive secret sharing with a clear semi-honest, bounded-collusion model.
- **Ensemble degradation under severe non-IID:** Medium. Empirical results on CIFAR-10 with LeNet are clear, but generalizability to other datasets, model architectures, and aggregation rules requires further validation.
- **Reward misalignment in label-free settings:** Medium. Simulation results demonstrate the problem under controlled non-IID splits, but real participant behavior and potential strategic manipulation could alter outcomes.

## Next Checks

2. Benchmark the SMPC computational overhead across two independent MPC frameworks (e.g., CrypTen and MP-SPDZ) on a standardized hardware platform (x86/ARM) with 2–5 parties to validate the claimed 50–200× slowdown range and identify configuration-sensitive bottlenecks.
3. Conduct a small-scale live testnet deployment with at least 5 independent model owners executing real inference jobs; measure reward fairness from actual on-chain distributions and compare it to the simulated oracle-based fairness under the same query workload.
