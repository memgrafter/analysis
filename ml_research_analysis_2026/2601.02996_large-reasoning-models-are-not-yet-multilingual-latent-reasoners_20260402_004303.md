# Large Reasoning Models Are (Not Yet) Multilingual Latent Reasoners

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02996
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02996
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Latent reasoning is detectable across languages but varies significantly by resource level and task difficulty, with a shared internal mechanism converging toward an English-centered pathway.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large reasoning models (LRMs) perform latent reasoning independent of explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) articulation across multiple languages. The authors use a truncation-based methodology on two multilingual math benchmarks—MGSM (easier) and Multilingual AIME (harder)—across 11 languages. They find clear evidence of latent reasoning, defined as the early emergence of correct answers before they appear explicitly in the reasoning trace. However, this phenomenon is stronger in high-resource languages and almost disappears on the harder AIME benchmark. Representational analysis via logit lens indicates a shared internal mechanism that converges toward an English-centered computational pathway, particularly for correctly solved instances. Controlled perturbation experiments (NumEdit, Paraphrase) confirm that latent reasoning contributes beyond mere memorization.

## Method Summary
The core method is truncation analysis: for each problem, the model generates a full CoT trace, which is then systematically truncated at increasing ratios (by step). At each truncation point, the model is prompted to predict the final answer given only the partial trace. Early correct predictions—that cannot be explained by copying the gold answer from the visible trace—signal latent reasoning. Two key metrics are computed: AUTC (area under the truncation accuracy curve) and LRS (latent reasoning score, derived from AUTC relative to an upper bound). Representational analysis uses logit lens to project hidden states to the output space, tracking the rank of the gold answer token across layers. Cosine similarity between hidden states of different languages (aligned by problem instance) tests for shared pathways. Perturbation experiments (altering numbers or paraphrasing) probe the role of memorization.

## Key Results
- Latent reasoning is evident across languages but follows a clear resource gradient: high-resource languages show stronger signals (higher LRS), while low-resource languages show weaker or absent signals. On the harder AIME benchmark, LRS drops sharply for all languages.
- Logit lens analysis reveals highly similar rank trajectories of the gold answer across all languages, indicating a shared computational dynamics that converges toward an English-centered internal pathway, especially for correct solutions.
- Perturbation experiments (NumEdit, Paraphrase) show partial memorization but confirm that latent reasoning remains a distinct contributor, as performance drops less under paraphrase than under numeric edits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Latent reasoning is operationally identified by measuring how early correct answers emerge under progressive reasoning-trace truncation, beyond what can be explained by answer copying.
- **Mechanism:** The model generates a full CoT. The trace is truncated at increasing ratios, and the model predicts the final answer from the partial trace. If accuracy rises well before the gold answer token appears explicitly in the truncated trace, this indicates the answer was computed in latent space prior to articulation.
- **Core assumption:** Early correctness not correlated with the gold-in-trace rate reflects genuine latent computation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Finds clear evidence of latent reasoning using truncation.
  - [section 4.1]: Describes prompting model for numerical prediction based on truncated trace.
  - Builds on prior work (Lanham et al., 2023; Pfau et al., 2024) showing answers often emerge before explicit articulation.
- **Break condition:** If early accuracy improvements perfectly correlate with gold-in-trace rate approaching 1, then early correctness is merely copying.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multilingual latent reasoning converges toward a shared, English-centered internal pathway despite surface language differences.
- **Mechanism:** Using logit lens, the rank of the gold answer is tracked across model layers for each language. Highly similar rank trajectories across languages indicate shared computational dynamics. Hidden-state cosine similarity with English representations shows systematic alignment, especially for high-resource languages and correctly solved instances.
- **Core assumption:** Language-invariant rank trajectories and elevated similarity to English suggest core reasoning happens in a shared subspace shaped by English-centric post-training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Logit lens shows similar rank trajectories and convergence toward English-centered pathway.
  - [section 5.1]: All languages exhibit highly similar ranking curves for a fixed model.
  - [section 5.2]: High-resource languages show higher similarity to English, suggesting convergence.
- **Break condition:** If non-English languages show higher similarity to each other than to English, or if rank trajectories diverge significantly, the hypothesis fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Latent reasoning capability is resource-dependent and fragile under increased task difficulty.
- **Mechanism:** Comparing LRS across language resource tiers and two benchmarks of different difficulty (MGSM vs. AIME) reveals disparities. High-resource languages show stronger early answer emergence; low-resource languages show weaker signals. AIME shows dramatically reduced LRS across all languages.
- **Core assumption:** Quality of multilingual representations and training data availability determines ability to compute answers in latent space before articulation; harder tasks require longer explicit traces.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Strength varies: stronger in resource-rich, weaker in low-resource, less observable on harder benchmarks.
  - [section 4.3]: Latent reasoning is stronger in high-resource languages; on AIME, AUTC and LRS drop sharply.
  - [table 1]: Shows resource gradient (e.g., English LRS 0.38→0.03 from MGSM→AIME for 7B).
- **Break condition:** Uniform LRS across resources or stronger latent reasoning on AIME than MGSM would invalidate the claim.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Processor -> Reasoning Generator -> Truncation Controller -> Answer Elicitor -> Representation Prober -> Similarity Calculator -> Perturbation Engine -> Metric Aggregator
- **Critical path:**
  Problem ingestion -> Full CoT generation -> Truncation at multiple ratios -> Answer prediction per truncation -> Correctness evaluation -> Logit lens probing -> Hidden-state extraction -> Cross-lingual similarity computation -> Metric calculation -> Statistical analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Step vs. token truncation:* Step-based aligns with human-interpretable reasoning units but may miss finer-grained dynamics; token-based would be more precise but less interpretable and computationally heavier.
  - *Model size coverage:* Three sizes (7B, 14B, 32B) balance computational cost with capacity gradients; larger models might show different patterns but are prohibitively expensive.
  - *Benchmark selection:* MGSM (grade-school) shows clear latent signals; AIME (competition) shows minimal signals, limiting generalizability to harder tasks.
  - *Language set:* 11 languages provide resource diversity but still limited; many low-resource languages remain unrepresented.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Truncation phase:* If gold-in-trace rate is near 1.0 at low truncation ratios (<20%), early accuracy is likely due to explicit answer copying, not latent reasoning.
  - *Similarity analysis:* If cosine similarity with English is consistently lower than similarity with other languages for all languages, the English-centered hypothesis fails.
  - *Perturbation check:* If NumEdit match ratio remains high (>50%) in w/o-Trace condition, memorization dominates and latent reasoning signals may be artifacts.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run full truncation analysis on MGSM with R1-Qwen-32B across all 11 languages to reproduce main latent reasoning patterns and verify resource gradients.
  2. Repeat truncation on Multilingual AIME with same model to confirm fragility under increased difficulty (expect near-zero LRS).
  3. Perform logit lens analysis on MGSM for German (high-resource) and Swahili (low-resource) to verify rank trajectory similarity and English alignment patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does token-level truncation provide finer-grained insights into latent reasoning dynamics than step-level truncation?
- **Basis:** Limitations section notes truncation at step level; token-level may reveal more precise dynamics.
- **Why unresolved:** Step-based truncation may mask critical transitions within individual reasoning steps.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Perform token-level truncation experiments (every 1–5 tokens) on MGSM and AIME, compute AUTC/LRS at each granularity, compare curves.

- **Open Question 2:** How does latent multilingual reasoning scale with model size beyond 32B parameters?
- **Basis:** Limitations: experiments limited to distilled models up to 32B; larger models may differ.
- **Why unresolved:** Larger models may exhibit stronger or qualitatively different latent reasoning, potentially reducing resource disparities.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Evaluate state-of-the-art LRMs (70B+) on same benchmarks, measure LRS and cross-lingual similarity, analyze scaling trends.

- **Open Question 3:** What mechanisms during English-centric post-training cause latent reasoning pathways to converge toward English across languages?
- **Basis:** Limitations: underlying causes not investigated; future work could use data attribution or mechanistic interpretability.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding mechanisms is key to improving multilingual latent reasoning and mitigating English bias.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Apply data attribution (e.g., influence functions) to identify training examples; use mechanistic interpretability (e.g., activation patching) to locate and test English-specific circuits.

## Limitations
- Step segmentation method not specified; cross-lingual sentence splitting may be inconsistent.
- Logit lens details ambiguous (tuned vs. raw, pre/post layer norm), affecting rank trajectory interpretation.
- Prompt-hacking prefixes for all languages are missing; replication requires exact strings.
- Truncation mapping for AIME unclear due to skewed reasoning lengths (per-instance vs. global).
- Language set limited (11) and skewed toward high/mid-resource; generalizability uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (latent reasoning via truncation): High. Gold-in-trace control robustly separates latent computation from copying; findings consistent across languages and scales.
- **Mechanism 2** (English-centered pathway): Medium. Similarity analyses are correlational; confounders (e.g., English-heavy post-training) could drive observations.
- **Mechanism 3** (resource dependence & fragility): High. Clear resource gradients and AIME collapse are statistically strong and reproducible.

## Next Checks
1. **Token-based truncation:** Repeat analysis using fixed token counts instead of sentence steps to test granularity robustness.
2. **Full similarity matrix:** Compute pairwise cosine similarities among all languages; verify English is the central hub, not an artifact.
3. **Extend to diverse low-resource languages:** Add 3–5 additional low-resource languages (e.g., Yoruba, Quechua, Khmer) to test resource gradient.
