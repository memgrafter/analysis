# Gen-Fab: A Variation-Aware Generative Model for Predicting Fabrication Variations in Nanophotonic Devices

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11505  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11505  
- Reference count: 27  
- Primary result: Gen-Fab predicts fabrication variations in nanophotonic devices using a conditional GAN with noise injection, achieving 89.8% IoU on out-of-distribution test structures, outperforming deterministic and ensemble baselines, and capturing distributional alignment via lower KL and Wasserstein distances.

## Executive Summary
Fabrication variations in nanophotonic devices, such as over-etching, under-etching, and corner rounding, cause significant deviations between the designed layout and the fabricated structure. Accurately predicting the distribution of these outcomes is crucial for robust design but remains challenging due to the inherent stochasticity of nanofabrication processes. Gen-Fab addresses this by adapting a conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN) based on Pix2Pix, with a key innovation: injecting a latent noise vector at the generator’s bottleneck. This allows the model to produce multiple, diverse Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) predictions from a single GDS layout, thereby modeling aleatoric uncertainty. On out-of-distribution test structures, Gen-Fab achieves the highest intersection-over-union (IoU) score of 89.8%, surpassing deterministic U-Net (85.3%), MC-Dropout U-Net (83.4%), and ensemble U-Nets (85.8%). It also attains lower Kullback-Leibler divergence and Wasserstein distance compared to all baselines, demonstrating superior alignment with the real fabrication distribution and effective uncertainty modeling. Feature-space analysis confirms strong generalization to unseen geometries. Future work aims to integrate Gen-Fab with fabrication facilities for continuous updates and to embed it within inverse design loops for robust layout optimization.

## Method Summary
Gen-Fab is a conditional generative adversarial network that maps binary GDS layout images to distributions of realistic SEM images. The generator is a U-Net encoder-decoder with skip connections; its bottleneck concatenates a d-dimensional latent noise vector (sampled from a standard normal distribution) that is spatially tiled to match the feature map dimensions. This injection enables the generator to model a conditional distribution rather than a deterministic mapping. The discriminator is a PatchGAN with a 70×70 receptive field, operating locally to enforce texture realism. The training objective combines an adversarial loss with a weighted L1 reconstruction loss (λ = 100) to balance structural fidelity and detail diversity. The model is trained on the ANT-NanoSOI dataset, which contains paired GDS-SEM images. To augment the limited data, the authors apply 90°, 180°, and 270° rotations to the original 31 pairs, yielding 124 training samples. During each iteration, a new noise vector is sampled per image. After training, the discriminator is discarded; for inference, multiple SEM predictions are generated by sampling different noise vectors. This setup yields both sample quality and diversity, crucial for capturing fabrication variability.

## Key Results
- Achieves 89.8% IoU on out-of-distribution test structures, the highest among compared models (U-Net: 85.3%, MC-Dropout U-Net: 83.4%, ensemble U-Nets: 85.8%).
- Significantly lower distributional divergence: KL divergence and Wasserstein distance between generated and real SEM sets are reduced, indicating better alignment with the true fabrication spread.
- Demonstrates generalization to unseen geometries (Cross, Square, Target) through procedural training; variance maps and feature-space separation confirm effective learning of local deformation patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent Noise Injection Enables One-to-Many Mapping
- **Claim:** Injecting a latent noise vector *z* at the generator's bottleneck enables the model to produce multiple, diverse fabrication outcomes from a single GDS layout, capturing aleatoric uncertainty.  
- **Mechanism:** The noise vector is tiled spatially to match the bottleneck's feature dimensions and concatenated with the deterministic layout features. During training, a different *z* is sampled per iteration, forcing the generator to learn that multiple valid outputs exist for one input. The discriminator receives the (layout, generated SEM) pair, ensuring each sampled output is locally realistic.  
- **Core assumption:** Fabrication variability can be modeled as a latent variable added to a deterministic mapping, and PatchGAN's local discrimination is sufficient to enforce realistic edge variations without collapsing to a single output.  
- **Break condition:** If the noise injection fails to induce diversity (e.g., all outputs remain near-identical across *z*), the mechanism breaks, likely due to too small λ (L1 loss dominates), too small latent dimension *d*, or discriminator overfitting.

### Mechanism 2: Adversarial-L1 Balance for Sharpness and Fidelity
- **Claim:** The adversarial loss combined with a weighted L1 loss balances sharp, realistic textures (from GAN) with structural fidelity to the input layout (from L1), avoiding blurriness or mode collapse.  
- **Mechanism:** The generator minimizes a weighted sum: *L_G = L_GAN + λ L1*. L1 ensures the mean prediction aligns with ground-truth geometry, while the discriminator pushes individual samples toward the real SEM distribution, adding high-frequency details and plausible deviations.  
- **Core assumption:** A deterministic reconstruction loss (L1) on every sample, even with noise, prevents the generator from ignoring the layout condition and generating completely unrelated structures. λ=100 is sufficient to balance these forces for this domain.  
- **Break condition:** If λ is too low, outputs become blurry (GAN dominates); if too high, diversity collapses (L1 dominates). Unstable adversarial training (e.g., discriminator loss near zero) would also break this balance.

### Mechanism 3: Procedurally Diversified Training Data Generalizes to OOD Geometries
- **Claim:** Training on procedurally generated, diverse GDS–SEM pairs from the ANT-NanoSOI dataset, combined with data augmentation (rotations), enables the model to generalize to out-of-distribution test geometries (Cross, Square, Target) by learning fabrication-agnostic edge deformation patterns.  
- **Mechanism:** The training set uses Fourier-based random pattern generation with varied spatial frequencies and feature sizes, plus rotations. This exposes the model to a wide spectrum of geometric primitives and curvatures, not specific device types. The PatchGAN's local processing further helps transfer local edge behaviors to new shapes.  
- **Core assumption:** Fabrication variations (etching, corner rounding) are local phenomena that depend on feature size/shape but not global structure type. A sufficiently diverse training set allows the model to learn these local physics rather than memorizing specific layouts.  
- **Break condition:** If test structures introduce fundamentally new variation modes not present in training (e.g., different material stack, resolution), performance would degrade sharply. The Fréchet distance (FD = 7265.92) indicates a significant domain shift; further shift beyond that would break the mechanism.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGANs)**  
  *Why needed:* Gen-Fab is built on Pix2Pix, a cGAN. Understanding the adversarial min‑max game between generator *G* (produces SEM from layout+*z*) and discriminator *D* (judges realism of (layout, SEM) pairs) is essential to interpret training dynamics and failure modes.  
  *Quick check:* How does adding a noise vector *z* to the *G*'s bottleneck change the optimization objective compared to deterministic Pix2Pix?

- **U-Net Architecture & Skip Connections**  
  *Why needed:* Both the generator (U-Net encoder-decoder) and the baseline models are U‑Nets. Skip connections preserve high-resolution layout details (edges, corners) in the decoder, critical for pixel-aligned SEM prediction.  
  *Quick check:* Why are skip connections crucial for translating precise GDS layouts to SEM images, and what would happen if they were removed?

- **Distributional Metrics (KL Divergence, Wasserstein Distance)**  
  *Why needed:* The paper argues deterministic models fail to capture fabrication *distributions*. KL and Wasserstein measure alignment between the model's generated set and real SEM distributions, not just single-image accuracy.  
  *Quick check:* Why is a low IoU on a *single* matched pair insufficient to claim the model captures fabrication variability? How do KL-D/W-D complement IoU?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
GDS layout (2048×2048 binary) -> Generator (U-Net with 8 down/up-sampling layers, InstanceNorm; latent *z* tiled and concatenated at bottleneck) -> Generated SEM -> Discriminator (PatchGAN, 70×70 receptive, 5 conv layers, LeakyReLU) -> Adversarial loss + λ·L1 (λ=100) -> Adam optimizer updates G and D. Noise sampler provides *z* per training step.

**Critical Path**  
1. Preprocess ANT‑NanoSOI pairs: align GDS‑SEM, resize to 2048×2048, apply rotations for augmentation.  
2. For each iteration: sample batch of (layout X, real SEM Y), sample *z* ∼ N(0,I) per X, compute ˆY = G(X,z).  
3. Update *D* to maximize log D(X,Y) + log (1 − D(X,ˆY)).  
4. Update *G* to maximize log D(X,ˆY) + 100 · L1(Y,ˆY); resample *z* every step.  
5. Monitor losses; stop after ∼10k–20k steps (best model at 10k). Discard *D*.  
6. Inference: for new X, sample *z*¹…*z*ᴹ → ˆY¹…ˆYᴹ.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *λ* (L1 weight): Higher → sharper, more deterministic outputs; lower → more diverse but blurrier; λ=100 found optimal.  
- Latent dimension *d*: Larger may increase diversity but risks unstable training; optimal range 16–64.  
- PatchGAN receptive field: Smaller (70×70) enforces local texture realism; larger patches risk global structure collapse.  
- Data augmentation: Rotations improve orientation robustness but may not cover all OOD geometries.

**Failure Signatures**  
- **Mode collapse**: Generated SEMs for a single layout become near-identical across *z*; diagnose via low pixel‑wise variance across M samples.  
- **Discriminator dominance**: *D* loss → 0 quickly, *G* loss rises/stagnates; indicates *D* too strong—reduce capacity or add regularization.  
- **Blurriness**: *G* outputs smooth, lacking edge detail; likely λ too high or *D* too weak.  
- **OOD degradation**: Performance drops on small features (e.g., Cross‑25 vs Cross‑100); check feature‑space Fréchet distance for domain shift.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Convergence check**: Plot *G* and *D* losses per step; expect stable oscillations, not *D* loss collapsing to zero.  
2. **Baseline comparison on one structure**: Train U‑Net, MC‑Dropout U‑Net, and an ensemble of 35 U‑Nets. For a single OOD layout (e.g., Cross‑50), compute Greedy‑match IoU and visualize variance map; Gen‑Fab should show higher IoU and variance pattern closer to real SEMs.  
3. **Noise ablation**: Train Gen‑Fab with *d* ∈ {8,16,32,64,128}, with/without rotation augmentation. Evaluate average Greedy‑match IoU on OOD set; expect moderate *d* (16–64) with augmentation to win.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can Gen‑Fab be integrated with fabrication facilities for continuous model updates and effective process drift tracking?  
2. How can Gen‑Fab be effectively embedded within inverse design optimization loops to produce layouts inherently robust to fabrication variations?  
3. Can Gen‑Fab generalize to predict variations in multilayer photonic stacks and complex 3D nanophotonic components beyond the 2D planar structures studied?  
4. Do alternative generative frameworks (e.g., diffusion models, normalizing flows) offer superior trade‑offs in diversity, fidelity, and training stability compared to the cGAN approach for fabrication variation modeling?  
5. Can the latent space of Gen‑Fab be made interpretable to enable independent control over distinct fabrication variation modes (e.g., corner rounding vs. line‑edge roughness)?

## Limitations
- Proprietary ANT‑NanoSOI dataset; alignment and augmentation details not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.  
- Noise injection mechanism at bottleneck underspecified (tiling, concatenation details) leading to potential reproducibility issues.  
- Out‑of‑distribution evaluation limited to six test structures; unknown transferability to other materials, processes, or multilayer stacks.

## Confidence
- Diverse outputs from noise injection: Medium  
- Outperformance on OOD IoU and distribution metrics: High  
- Generalization via procedural training: Medium  
- λ=100 optimality: Low  

## Next Checks
1. Perform a latent dimension sweep (d = 8, 16, 32, 64) and measure output diversity (pixel‑wise variance) and IoU; expect optimal diversity‑fidelity trade‑off at d = 16–32.  
2. Monitor discriminator and generator losses during training; if discriminator loss approaches zero within a few steps, implement gradient penalty or adjust λ to maintain stability and avoid mode collapse.  
3. Test cross‑fabrication generalization by evaluating Gen‑Fab on a second independent dataset (e.g., nanophotonic devices from a different foundry) without retraining; measure degradation in IoU and distribution metrics.
