# ProtoDCS: Towards Robust and Efficient Open-Set Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23653
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23653
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** ProtoDCS achieves state-of-the-art open-set test-time adaptation for VLMs, with 67.98% known-class accuracy on CIFAR-10-C (vs. DPE's 64.00%), AUROC 95.59% (vs. 86.38%), and OSCR 66.71%, while adding only ~16 MB memory overhead over zero-shot CLIP.

## Executive Summary
Open-set test-time adaptation (OSTTA) for vision-language models (VLMs) requires adapting to covariate-shifted in-distribution (csID) data while filtering out-of-distribution (csOOD) samples. Existing methods fail due to brittle hard-coded thresholds for separation and unsafe entropy minimization that causes overconfidence on OOD data. ProtoDCS introduces a two-stage probabilistic separation using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to replace static thresholds, and an evidence-driven uncertainty-aware loss for safe prototype-level updates. By freezing the VLM backbone and adapting only lightweight prototypes, it achieves robust performance on CIFAR-10-C/100-C and Tiny-ImageNet-C with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
ProtoDCS processes an unlabeled test stream in three stages. First, a **First-Check** uses dynamic dual-thresholds on an openness score to populate a diversity-aware visual cache and select trustworthy samples for adaptation. Second, **Evidence-Driven Adaptation** optimizes residual matrices added to global prototypes using an uncertainty-aware loss (modeling aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty) plus a cross-modal alignment loss, while keeping the backbone frozen. Third, a **Final-Verification** fits a two-component GMM on a sliding window of re-evaluated openness scores to produce a probabilistic csOOD decision. csID samples are predicted by a dual-source classifier combining adapted textual and visual prototypes; high-quality csID predictions update the global textual prototype via a cumulative moving average and indirectly the visual prototype via the cache.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10-C, ProtoDCS achieves 67.98% known-class accuracy, 95.59% AUROC, and 66.71% OSCR, surpassing prior state-of-the-art DPE.
- It demonstrates strong efficiency: ~55.86 samples/sec throughput and only a 16 MB memory increase over zero-shot CLIP.
- Results hold on larger-scale Tiny-ImageNet-C and closed-set benchmarks, confirming robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Probabilistic Separation via Two-Stage GMM
**Claim:** ProtoDCS replaces brittle hard thresholds for csID/csOOD separation with a probabilistic, adaptive two-stage verification that handles ambiguous boundary samples in VLMs' compact embedding space.
**Mechanism:** The First-Check stage uses a dynamic dual-threshold on an openness score to segregate samples: a strict threshold populates a diversity-aware visual cache for pure csID prototypes; a looser threshold passes trustworthy samples for refinement. The Final-Verification stage then applies a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) on re-evaluated openness scores (using temporary prototypes) to produce a posterior probability for csOOD, adaptively modeling the evolving distribution.
**Core Assumption:** The openness score distribution of csID and csOOD samples is bimodal, and a GMM can capture this separation more robustly than static thresholds, especially under covariate shift.
**Break Condition:** If the GMM fails to converge (e.g., insufficient samples in sliding window W_g < 100) or if the openness score distributions for csID/csOOD are not bimodal (e.g., severe overlap), the fallback to a static threshold (Θ_c) may reintroduce brittleness.

### Mechanism 2: Safe Adaptation via Evidence-Driven Uncertainty Loss
**Claim:** ProtoDCS prevents overconfidence and semantic drift during adaptation by replacing entropy minimization with an evidence-driven uncertainty-aware loss that explicitly models aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty.
**Mechanism:** For trustworthy samples, ProtoDCS optimizes learnable residual matrices (ΔP_t, ΔP_v) added to global prototypes. The loss combines: (1) **Uncertainty loss** based on evidential deep learning (α = ReLU(z)+1), minimizing aleatoric uncertainty (expected entropy) and epistemic uncertainty (inverse total evidence); and (2) **Alignment loss** (InfoNCE) to preserve cross-modal consistency between visual and textual prototypes.
**Core Assumption:** Modeling both uncertainty types yields better-calibrated predictions that distinguish OOD/boundary samples from reliable csID data, enabling safe prototype updates.
**Break Condition:** If aleatoric uncertainty is systematically underestimated (λ_AU too low), or if the alignment loss weight (λ_align) is too high, prototype drift may occur, harming csID accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Prototype-Level Efficiency via Frozen Backbone
**Claim:** ProtoDCS achieves computational efficiency for VLMs by confining all adaptation to the prototype level, avoiding backpropagation through the frozen backbone.
**Mechanism:** The VLM backbone (E_v, E_t) remains frozen. Updates occur only on small residual matrices (ΔP_t, ΔP_v) and class-wise visual cache queues (C_v). Textual prototypes evolve slowly via cumulative moving average (CMA). Visual prototypes update more responsively but only via the diversity-filtered cache.
**Core Assumption:** Prototype-level adaptation suffices to correct for covariate shift in VLMs' well-structured embedding space, and modality-specific update strategies (slow text, fast visual) respect semantic stability vs. visual adaptability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Evidential Deep Learning:** Converts model logits into Dirichlet evidence parameters (α = ReLU(z)+1) to separate aleatoric (data noise) and epistemic (model uncertainty) uncertainty. Enables the uncertainty-aware loss that prevents overconfident updates on ambiguous/OOD samples.

- **Prototype Learning for VLMs:** Uses class-centric anchor vectors in the shared embedding space that can be incrementally updated without modifying backbone parameters. Visual prototypes are more amenable to frequent updates than textual ones under covariate shift because visual features are more susceptible to corruption while language semantics remain stable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input -> VLM Feature Extraction (frozen E_v, E_t) -> Openness Score Calculation -> First-Check Filters (Θ_a, Θ_b) -> Visual Cache Update & Residual Optimization -> Temporary Prototypes (P'_t, P'_v) -> Re-evaluated Openness Score -> GMM Verification (or fallback Θ_c) -> Dual-Source Prediction -> Global Prototype Update (CMA for P_t, cache for P_v).

**Critical Path:** For each sample x: (1) Compute f_v, initial S_open. (2) Update sliding window W_c (size 100) and set dynamic thresholds Θ_a=30th, Θ_b=60th percentile. (3) If S_open < Θ_a, update visual cache C_v via Algorithm 2. (4) If S_open < Θ_b, initialize ΔP_t=0, ΔP_v=0; compute L = L_uncertainty + λ_align*L_align; one gradient step on residuals; obtain P'_t, P'_v. (5) Recompute S'_open with P'_t; update W_g (size 100). (6) If n_proc ≥ 100, fit 2-component GMM on W_g; else use fallback Θ_c=0.7. (7) Classify as csID if P(c_csOOD|S'_open) < 0.5. (8) For csID, predict with dual-source; if AU < Θ_q, update global P_t via CMA and reset residuals.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Separation strictness (Θ_a, Θ_b) vs. adaptation recall: Stricter Θ_a yields purer cache but discards useful csID samples; looser Θ_b risks csOOD contamination requiring careful GMM verification.
- Modal asymmetry: Slow, stable text updates (CMA) vs. fast, selective visual updates (cache) balances semantic preservation with visual domain adaptation.
- GMM window size (W_g): Larger W_g gives stable distribution estimates but slower adaptation; smaller W_g is responsive but noisy.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor csID accuracy despite high AUROC: Over-filtering (Θ_a too high, or Θ_q too strict) discarding valid csID samples.
- Degrading OSCR/AUROC over time: GMM misfitting (non-bimodal scores) or cache corruption (τ_sim too low), allowing csOOD to influence prototypes.
- High variance in cold-start phase: Expected until W_g fills; prolonged instability suggests W_c/W_g sizes inadequate for shift magnitude.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Ablate Uncertainty Loss Components: Replace EDUA loss with entropy minimization; observe drop in OSCR and calibration, confirming overconfidence risk.
2. Ablate Separation Mechanism: Disable First-Check → sharp AUROC drop; disable GMM → degradation under shift, proving double-check necessity.
3. Hyperparameter Sensitivity Sweep: Vary Θ_a, Θ_b, τ_sim, W_g; identify robust ranges and failure modes (e.g., W_g=10 causes unstable GMM).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How to reduce ProtoDCS cold-start instability before GMM activation?** GMM requires 100 samples; fallback threshold yields high variance. Early errors cause negative adaptation, unsafe for online use. Resolve with source-prior GMM or few-sample alternatives.
- **Does ProtoDCS remain robust with multiple diverse OOD distributions?** Current tests use one OOD source; GMM assumes two components. Real OOD may be multi-modal, breaking assumption. Evidence needed: evaluate on mixed OOD datasets; extend GMM to more components.
- **Can ProtoDCS hyperparameters be set automatically without manual tuning?** Fixed values (W_c=100, Θ_a=30th percentile) chosen after limited search; tuning per dataset is impractical. Evidence needed: develop self-adaptive rules from online stream statistics.
- **Why does the evidence-driven uncertainty loss prevent overconfidence in open-set TTA?** No theoretical justification for its safety properties. Evidence needed: formal relationships between loss and calibrated uncertainty, with proof bounds.

## Limitations
- GMM-based separation hinges on the bimodal openness score assumption; this may fail under severe non-stationary shifts or overlapping csID/csOOD distributions.
- The visual cache's diversity and quality thresholds (τ_sim, Θ_q) require careful tuning; inappropriate values lead to cache corruption or under-adaptation.
- Hyperparameters (e.g., α, β for dual-source prediction) are dataset-dependent, limiting out-of-box deployment.

## Confidence
- **High:** Mechanism 2 (uncertainty-aware loss) and Mechanism 3 (prototype-level efficiency) are strongly supported by ablations, formal evidential framework, and reported metrics.
- **Medium:** Mechanism 1 (two-stage GMM separation) is well-described but relies on unverified implementation details (GMM fitting protocol, fallback logic) and the bimodal assumption, with risks if distributions overlap.

## Next Checks
1. **Test GMM robustness under distribution shift:** Generate test streams with unimodal or heavily overlapping S_open distributions (e.g., mixed corruption severities). Measure AUROC/OSCR degradation when GMM is active vs. an optimized fixed threshold, validating the bimodal assumption.
2. **Stress-test visual cache contamination:** Inject a high rate of csOOD samples that pass First-Check (Θ_b) into the stream. Monitor per-class cache purity (via ground-truth labels in controlled setup) and resulting drop in known-class accuracy, verifying the cache's diversity filter and quality threshold efficacy.
3. **Ablation of uncertainty loss coefficients:** Systematically sweep aleatoric (λ_AU) and epistemic (λ_EU) weights, and alignment weight (λ_align), on a challenging shift (e.g., severe CIFAR-10-C). Plot accuracy-uncertainty calibration curves to identify the safe parameter region where underconfidence on OOD does not suppress csID adaptation.
