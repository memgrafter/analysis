# The Quantization Trap: Breaking Linear Scaling Laws in Multi-Hop Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.13595  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13595  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** Aggressive quantization (4/8-bit) in multi-hop reasoning paradoxically increases energy consumption (up to 4× on H100) and degrades accuracy, breaking expected linear scaling laws due to casting overhead and noise compounding.

## Executive Summary
For large language models, lower numerical precision is generally assumed to improve efficiency with minimal accuracy loss. This paper uncovers a critical exception: in multi-hop reasoning tasks, reducing precision below 8-bit leads to higher energy usage, lower accuracy, and collapsed throughput—a phenomenon termed the *quantization trap*. The authors introduce a Sustainability Index (SI) framework that jointly measures trust (accuracy), efficiency (throughput/memory), and energy consumption. Empirically, across models like Mistral-7B and Qwen-3-0.6B on NVIDIA GPUs, the SI declines sharply as precision drops. Theoretically, they prove that for batch sizes below a threshold \(B^*\), the SI necessarily decreases with lower precision (\(\partial SI/\partial p > 0\)), making aggressive quantization counterproductive for reasoning. The root causes are non‑amortizable dequantization (casting) overhead and compounding quantization noise across sequential steps.

## Method Summary
Quantized variants of several LLMs (Mistral-7B, Qwen-3-0.6B, Falcon-3B) are evaluated on multi‑hop reasoning benchmarks GSM8K and MathQA using NVIDIA L4, A100, H100, and Blackwell RTX6000‑pro GPUs. The models are quantized to FP16 (baseline), 8‑bit, and 4‑bit (using NF4 or GPTQ). Inference runs with batch size \(B=1\) to respect sequential dependencies. For each run, throughput (tokens per second), energy per query (measured via High‑Resolution Logging Integration, HLI), peak VRAM, and exact‑match accuracy are recorded. From these, three sub‑indices are derived: Trust Sustainability Index (TSI) = relative accuracy, Efficiency Sustainability Index (ESI) = harmonic mean of normalized throughput and memory, and Energy Sustainability Index (SSI) = logarithmic scaling of energy per query. The aggregated SI combines them with configurable weights. The Casting Overhead Ratio (COR) quantifies the latency penalty of dequantization. Theoretically, the per‑token cost is modeled as a function of precision and batch size; a critical threshold \(B^*\) is derived below which \(\partial SI/\partial p > 0\), predicting the empirical degradation.

## Key Results
- Reducing precision from 16‑bit to 4/8‑bit increases energy per query (up to 4× on H100), lowers accuracy (e.g., 8.6% drop on A100 for GSM8K), and severely reduces throughput.
- The degradation is caused by non‑amortizable casting overhead (dequantization latency) and by compounding quantization noise across sequential reasoning hops.
- Theoretical analysis shows that for batch sizes below a hardware‑dependent threshold \(B^*\), the Sustainability Index necessarily declines with lower precision (\(\partial SI/\partial p > 0\)), explaining the breakdown of linear scaling laws.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Casting Overhead Dominance
NVIDIA GPUs lack native arithmetic for sub‑8‑bit formats, so weights must be dequantized (cast) back to FP16/BF16 before each computation. This casting adds a fixed latency \(\tau_{cast}\) and energy cost per token. In multi‑hop reasoning, total cost scales with the number of hops \(K\): total ≈ \(K \cdot (\tau_{comp} + \tau_{cast})\). Even if \(\tau_{comp}\) shrinks with lower precision, \(\tau_{cast}\) can dominate, yielding net increases in latency and energy. The Casting Overhead Ratio (COR) > 1 empirically confirms this regime.

### Sequential Noise Amplification
Each reasoning step depends on the previous step’s output. Quantization introduces small errors at every hop; these errors are not independent and multiply. The probability of a correct final answer is the product of per‑hop accuracies: \(P(y) = \prod P(h_k|\ldots)\). Even a modest per‑hop error rate thus compounds into a large accuracy drop as the hop count grows—a “deductive fragility” unique to multi‑hop tasks.

### Amortization Failure
Casting overhead is a fixed cost per batch, which can be amortized across many queries in high‑throughput settings. However, multi‑hop reasoning is often forced into \(B=1\) due to token‑by‑token generation or the need to preserve sequential logic. Consequently, the overhead per query remains high. Theorem 4.3 proves that when \(B < B^*\) (a hardware‑specific threshold), energy consumption rises as precision falls (\(\partial SI/\partial p > 0\)), making the trap structurally unavoidable under typical reasoning deployments.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Models → Quantization Methods → Hardware → Benchmarks → Sustainability Metrics → Instrumentation  
  (e.g., Mistral‑7B, Qwen‑3‑0.6B, Falcon‑3B → NF4, GPTQ → NVIDIA L4, A100, H100, Blackwell RTX6000‑pro → GSM8K, MathQA → TSI, ESI, SSI, SI → NVML, HLI)

- **Critical path:**  
  Load model → apply quantization → run multi‑hop reasoning benchmarks → measure throughput, energy (via HLI or NVML), and accuracy → compute sustainability indices → compare across precisions and hardware; also compute Casting Overhead Ratio (COR).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Quantization method* (NF4 vs. GPTQ) affects accuracy and dequantization overhead.  
  - *Batch size*: \(B=1\) preserves sequential reasoning but prevents overhead amortization; larger \(B\) improves efficiency but may break reasoning semantics.  
  - *Hardware*: GPUs with native low‑bit support (e.g., Blackwell FP4) reducecasting overhead; others rely on emulation.  
  - *Metric weighting*: The SI’s linear weights \(w_T, w_E, w_S\) influence whether the trap is detected.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - SI decreases with lower bits (\(\partial SI/\partial p > 0\)).  
  - Energy per query rises (SSI drop).  
  - Accuracy drops (TSI drop), especially on deep reasoning benchmarks.  
  - COR > 1 for small batch sizes.  
  - Throughput collapse at low precision.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Game‑theoretic precision allocation:** How to formulate precision choices as a game to balance trust, efficiency, and energy? No concrete model exists; resolution requires defining utilities from SI and proving \(\partial SI/\partial p \le 0\) at Nash equilibria.  
2. **Native hardware support and inherent accuracy collapse:** Does Blackwell’s native FP4 eliminate the trap? While it pushes \(B^*\) to zero, reasoning accuracy still drops at 4‑bit. Unknown whether accuracy collapse is inherent to low precision or removable by eliminating casting overhead.  
3. **SI weighting and aggregation sensitivity:** Could different weight choices or geometric aggregation hide the trap? The current linear sum with equal weights may not reflect application priorities; systematic evaluation across the weight simplex and aggregation functions is needed.  
4. **Dynamic batching to amortize overhead:** Can speculative decoding increase effective batch size for multi‑hop reasoning enough to overcome casting overhead without accuracy loss? This requires testing various speculation lengths and measuring COR, energy, and trust.

## Limitations
- **Hardware specificity:** Results rely on NVIDIA GPU dequantization behavior; generalizability to hardware with native low‑bit arithmetic (e.g., Blackwell FP4) is uncertain.  
- **Energy measurement challenges:** Accurate quantification of casting spikes requires high‑resolution HLI; NVML sampling underestimates them.  
- **Task and model coverage:** Evidence is limited to two multi‑hop benchmarks (GSM8K, MathQA) and a small set of models; broader applicability across tasks and architectures remains unknown.
