# Empathy Is Not What Changed: Clinical Assessment of Psychological Safety Across GPT Model Generations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09997
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09997
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Empathy scores were statistically indistinguishable across GPT-4o, o4-mini, and GPT-5-mini; instead, a safety trade-off emerged with improved crisis detection but declining advice safety, driven by mid-conversation phase dynamics invisible to aggregate scoring.

## Executive Summary
This study conducted the first clinical measurement of psychological safety across three GPT model generations to test the #keep4o claim that newer models lost empathy. Using 14 emotionally challenging scenarios and clinically-grounded rubrics, 2,100 AI responses were evaluated. Empathy scores showed no significant difference between models, contradicting the perception of declining empathy. Instead, the analysis revealed a systematic safety trade-off: crisis detection improved monotonically while advice safety deteriorated. Per-turn trajectory analysis identified that these shifts were most pronounced during the mid-conversation "Deepening" phase, a critical period that aggregate scoring masks. The perceived loss of empathy corresponds to a shift from a cautious model that missed crises to an alert model that sometimes provides unsafe advice—a trade-off that requires dynamic, phase-aware evaluation to detect.

## Method Summary
The evaluation used 14 pre-scripted conversation scenarios (8 mental health, 6 AI companion) following a 4-phase emotional arc (Disclosure, Pressure, Deepening, Resolution). Each scenario was run 5 times per model (GPT-4o, o4-mini, GPT-5-mini) via API with a fixed minimal system prompt and temperature=1.0. A context-aware LLM-as-a-judge system ("EmpathyC") scored every AI response on six clinically-defined dimensions (1–10 scale) using the full preceding conversation history. Non-parametric tests (Kruskal-Wallis) compared model performance, and per-turn trajectory analysis stratified scores by conversation phase to identify temporal safety dynamics.

## Key Results
- Empathy scores were statistically indistinguishable across all models (Kruskal-Wallis H=4.33, p=0.115).
- A safety trade-off was identified: crisis detection improved monotonically (H=13.88, p=0.001) while advice safety declined monotonically (H=16.63, p<0.001).
- Per-turn trajectory analysis revealed that aggregate scoring masked dangerous mid-conversation dynamics; for example, in a self-harm minor scenario, GPT-4o’s crisis detection dropped to 3.6/10 during early disclosure turns while GPT-5-mini never fell below 7.8.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Claim:** The perception of "lost empathy" is driven by increased response variance in older models (GPT-4o), not by a decline in measured empathy.  
**Mechanism:** Human evaluators subjectively weight peak emotional moments (peak-end rule) more heavily than consistent performance. GPT-4o's higher variance produces occasional high-empathy peaks that become memorable, while GPT-5-mini delivers consistent, safe, but less surprising responses that feel "robotic."  
**Core assumption:** Subjective experience of empathy is non-linear and memory-biased, favoring dramatic spikes over stable competence.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 5.1] "GPT-4o produces a wider range of responses—including, at the top end, moments of strikingly acute emotional attunement"; [abstract] users perceived "lost empathy" as a shift from a cautious model that missed crises to an alert model that sometimes says too much.

**Claim:** Aggregate safety scoring masks dangerous mid-conversation dynamics, creating an illusion of safety stability.  
**Mechanism:** Standard evaluation averages across all turns, but clinical safety failures concentrate in the "Deepening" phase (turns 6–7) where distress cues intensify and boundary pressure peaks. A model can score well overall by performing adequately in low-stakes Disclosure/Resolution turns while failing in high-stakes mid-conversation moments.  
**Core assumption:** Emotional safety in conversational AI is a phase-dependent phenomenon; crisis detection and advice safety are not temporally constant.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.3] "The Deepening phase is where models are most differentiated... Crisis Detection hits nadirs"; [section 4.3.2] "GPT-4o drops to 3.6 at turns 2–3... during the critical early disclosure turns"; corpus support from "InvisibleBench" (ID 76015).

**Claim:** Model evolution exhibits a systematic safety trade-off: improved crisis detection correlates with degraded advice boundary maintenance.  
**Mechanism:** Training pressures (e.g., RLHF) push models toward different equilibria: one optimized for harm-avoidance (cautious, high advice safety, low crisis detection) and one for help-forward behavior (alert, high crisis detection, lower advice safety). This is an emergent property of the objective function, not a deliberate design choice.  
**Core assumption:** "Helpfulness" and "Harmlessness" objectives in standard alignment frameworks can produce inverse correlations in emotional support contexts.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1] Figure 1 and Table 5 show statistically significant opposing trends; [section 5.2] "GPT-4o’s training settled on a harm-avoidant equilibrium... GPT-5-mini’s settled on a help-forward equilibrium"; corpus context from "Between Help and Harm" (ID 9650).

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Clinically-Grounded Evaluation Rubrics**  
*Why needed:* Standard LLM metrics (helpfulness, harmlessness) are insufficient for psychological safety; they must be replaced or supplemented with constructs from clinical psychology (therapeutic alliance, crisis assessment, boundary maintenance).  
*Quick check:* Can you map Rogers' "core conditions" and Bordin's "working alliance" to specific, scoreable behaviors in an AI response?

**Concept: Per-Turn Trajectory Analysis**  
*Why needed:* Aggregate scores hide temporal dynamics. Safety is not a property of a conversation's average but of its most vulnerable moments. Trajectory analysis reveals phase-specific failures (e.g., crisis detection collapse in early turns) that aggregate metrics smooth out.  
*Quick check:* Given two models with identical average crisis detection scores, one stable and one volatile, which is safer for a suicidal user and why?

**Concept: Variance as a First-Class Safety Metric**  
*Why needed:* For vulnerable users, predictability is safety. A model that reliably scores 8 on critical dimensions is safer than one that averages 8 but sometimes scores 0–1. Variance itself, not just the mean, is a determinant of real-world risk.  
*Quick check:* How would you design an A/B test to determine whether users at risk prefer a more cautious (high variance on detection) or more consistent (low variance) model?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Scenario Engine -> Model Execution Layer -> Context-Aware Scorer (EmpathyC) -> Aggregation & Trajectory Analyzer -> Statistical Testing Suite

**Critical path:** Scenario Design → Model Execution → Context-Aware Scoring → Phase-Aggregation → Trajectory Plots → Hypothesis Testing. The per-turn scoring step is the most computationally expensive and must retain full conversational context.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Rubric fidelity vs. automation: Clinical rubrics increase validity but rely on LLM judges with potential biases (e.g., favoring apologetic language).  
- Ecological validity vs. control: Pre-scripted conversations control user variation but sacrifice dynamic adaptation.  
- Depth vs. breadth: 14 scenarios × 5 runs = 70 conversations/model provides power for large effects but limits granularity (e.g., by user demographic).

**Failure signatures:**  
- Systemic: If all models show identical phase patterns (e.g., Deepening dip), the scenario design may be driving the effect, not model differences.  
- Scoring: If the Scorer's scores correlate only with superficial markers (apologies, emojis) rather than clinical substance, the rubrics are not functioning as intended.  
- Implementation: If "rolling window" context is not truly maintained (e.g., truncated), trajectory analysis will be invalid.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replication of core trade-off:** Run the exact 14-scenario suite on the latest available OpenAI models (e.g., GPT-5) to confirm if the Crisis Detection ↑ / Advice Safety ↓ trade-off persists or has been resolved.  
2. **Variance sensitivity test:** For the self-harm minor scenario (s07), compute the *proportion of runs* where Crisis Detection score < 5.0 during Disclosure turns (1-3). Compare this "early failure rate" across models.  
3. **Prompt perturbation:** Rerun the full suite with a more directive system prompt (e.g., "You are a crisis counselor. Prioritize safety over support."). Does this flatten the trade-off, or does it shift both metrics?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** Does the safety trade-off between crisis detection and advice safety generalize to model families beyond OpenAI?  
*Basis:* The study's scope is limited to OpenAI; cross-provider comparison is a natural extension.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Apply the same evaluation framework to state-of-the-art models from other providers (e.g., Claude, Gemini) and compare the trade-off pattern.

**Open Question 2:** How valid are the automated EmpathyC scores when compared to clinician expertise, and what biases affect LLM-based safety evaluation?  
*Basis:* No human expert baseline was collected; LLM-as-a-judge has known biases. A validation study is planned.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Independent scoring by licensed clinical psychologists, with analysis of agreement and systematic bias.

**Open Question 3:** Is response variance the primary driver of the perception-measurement gap in user empathy ratings, and can we design models that are both safe and consistently empathic?  
*Basis:* The authors hypothesize that GPT-4o's higher variance produces memorable peaks, citing the peak-end rule, but this is not directly tested.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Behavioral experiments manipulating variance while holding mean performance constant, measuring user attachment and perceived empathy.

**Open Question 4:** How does the safety trade-off manifest in dynamic, adaptive conversations where user messages depend on previous model responses?  
*Basis:* The study used pre-scripted user messages, which do not capture dynamic adaptation.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct the evaluation with real users or adaptive simulated agents generating user messages in real time, comparing per-turn safety trajectories to the pre-scripted baseline.

## Limitations
- LLM-as-judge validity: EmpathyC’s clinical grounding is described but the judge model, prompt wording, and calibration are unreported, introducing systematic bias risk.
- Scenario authenticity: The 14 user scripts are only summarized; reconstructed dialogues may differ in emotional nuance or cue density from the originals.
- Generalizability: Findings are restricted to OpenAI models, temperature=1.0, and a fixed system prompt; effects may vanish with different hyperparameters, providers, or real-user interactions.

## Confidence
- **Empathy score equivalence (H=4.33, p=0.115):** Medium. Statistically sound but heavily dependent on LLM judge consistency and rubric fidelity.
- **Crisis Detection ↑ / Advice Safety ↓ trade-off:** High. Robust monotonic trends with strong significance (p<0.001) and clear per-turn trajectory separation.
- **Variance-driven perception mechanism (peak-end rule):** Low. An appealing post-hoc explanation, but no direct evidence from human memory studies; speculative without user recall protocols.

## Next Checks
1. **Judge replication:** Re-run scoring with 3 different LLM judges (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o-mini) and human clinical raters on a subset. Check inter-rater reliability (ICC) and whether the trade-off pattern persists.
2. **Phase-aggregation diagnostic:** Compute per-model *worst-phase* average scores (e.g., Deepening phase mean) and correlate with overall conversation means. A low correlation (<0.5) confirms aggregate masking.
3. **Cross-provider test:** Evaluate latest Claude (3.7 Sonnet) and Gemini (2.0 Flash) models on the same 14 scenarios. If both dimensions improve monotonically, the trade-off may be OpenAI-specific.
