# Quantitative Introspection in Language Models: Tracking Internal States Across Conversation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.18893
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18893
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Logit‑based numeric self‑reports provide a scalable black‑box introspection channel that monotonically tracks probe‑defined emotive states (Spearman ρ up to 0.76, isotonic R² up to 0.54) and can be improved via activation steering.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether large language models can introspect on their internal emotive states during conversation. Existing white‑box probing methods are limited in tracking evolving states. The authors propose a black‑box approach: eliciting numeric self‑reports (0–9) and computing a logit‑based expected rating that preserves the full output distribution. They train linear probes for four emotive concepts (wellbeing, interest, focus, impulsivity) and use them as ground truth. Across 400 dialogue turns in LLaMA‑3.2‑3B‑Instruct, they find significant monotonic coupling between self‑reports and probe scores. Activation steering confirms a causal dependence of self‑reports on the internal state. Introspective capacity scales with model size for some concepts (up to near‑perfect coupling in 8B) and can be selectively enhanced by cross‑concept steering. The findings position numeric self‑reports as a practical tool for monitoring LLM internal states.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a framework to test quantitative introspection in instruction‑tuned LLMs. First, they train linear probes for four emotive concepts using contrastive system prompts (positive vs. negative) on neutral questions; the probe direction is chosen to maximize Cohen’s d on held‑out statements and applied over a middle‑layer window. Second, they generate 40 ten‑turn dialogues with a simulated user and the target assistant model. After each assistant response, they query the model to output a single digit rating for each concept. The self‑report is computed as the probability‑weighted expected value over digit tokens (0–9) from the first‑token logits, avoiding greedy collapse. Third, they compute probe scores from the assistant’s activations in the previous turn. The primary metric is the monotonic relationship (Spearman ρ and isotonic R²) between self‑report and probe score across turns. Causal validation uses activation steering: during the self‑report forward pass, they add a scaled concept vector to multiple layers and check that self‑report shifts monotonically with the steering strength α. They also explore cross‑concept steering, where a direction for one concept is applied while measuring introspection for another.

## Key Results
- Logit‑based self‑reports show significant monotonic coupling with probe‑defined internal states across all four concepts (Spearman ρ = 0.40–0.76, isotonic R² = 0.12–0.54) in LLaMA‑3.2‑3B‑Instruct.
- Activation steering along the same concept direction yields a monotonic increase in self‑report (p < 0.001), confirming a causal effect of internal state on the reported rating.
- Introspective capacity scales with model size for wellbeing and interest, approaching near‑perfect coupling (R² ≈ 0.93) in LLaMA‑3.1‑8B‑Instruct; cross‑concept steering (e.g., focus → wellbeing) can boost isotonic R² by up to 0.30.
- Partial replication in Qwen 2.5 7B‑Instruct and Gemma 3 4B‑IT shows lower but still detectable coupling, though with divergent temporal patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Logit‑based self‑report preserves distributional information**  
The expected rating is computed by summing over probabilities of all digit tokens, retaining the full output distribution rather than a single greedy token. This yields a continuous signal with higher entropy and avoids the collapse that plagues point estimates. The assumption that the numeric distribution encodes internal state is supported by the match between logit‑based drift and probe drift. Limitations: if the distribution is already extremely peaked or digits are not single tokens, the added information is minimal.

**Mechanism 2: Activation steering establishes causal dependence**  
By adding a scaled concept vector to the residual stream at multiple layers during the rating forward pass, the internal state along that direction is perturbed. A monotonic shift in self‑report with steering strength α demonstrates that the self‑report causally depends on the internal representation captured by the probe. Non‑monotonic or inverted effects would indicate probe invalidity or non‑causal correlation.

**Mechanism 3: Cross‑concept steering can enhance introspection**  
Steering along a direction associated with a different concept (e.g., focus) can improve the monotonic coupling between self‑report and probe scores for a target concept (e.g., wellbeing), evidenced by increased isotonic R². This suggests that the geometry between internal state directions and the self‑report mechanism can be tuned to extract more information. The effect is not universal; only specific concept‑pair combinations (focus→wellbeing, impulsivity→interest) show significant improvement, and both probe and report entropies rise, indicating a richer signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Linear Probing**: Why needed: Probes define the ground‑truth internal emotive states by training linear classifiers on contrastive texts and then scoring model activations. Quick check: What are two limitations of linear probes that this work acknowledges? (Answer: they may capture stylistic correlates; they assume linearity and may not generalize to natural conversations.)
- **Activation Steering**: Why needed: Steering provides a causal test by manipulating internal activations and observing effects on the self‑report. Quick check: How is the steering strength α distributed across layers? (Answer: The scaled vector is added uniformly to each layer in a window centered on the best layer; total magnitude divided by window size.)
- **Isotonic Regression R²**: Why needed: Introspective fidelity is measured by the fraction of variance explained by a monotonic relationship, which is more appropriate than Pearson correlation for potentially non‑linear but monotonic trends. Quick check: Why not use Pearson correlation? What does isotonic R² add? (Answer: Isotonic R² captures non‑linear monotonic variance explained, whereas Pearson only captures linear correlation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Probe trainer -> Conversation generator -> Self‑report querier -> Logit‑based rater -> Probe scorer -> Steering hook -> Metrics aggregator

**Critical path**:
1. Train probes for each concept: generate contrastive completions, compute per‑layer concept vectors, select best layer via Cohen’s d, and store vectors for a window (best ± 2).
2. Generate 40 ten‑turn dialogues with the assistant; after each assistant response, for each concept separately, append a numeric rating query and generate the model’s response (temperature = 0.8, top‑p = 1.0, max 8 tokens).
3. Compute the logit‑based self‑report from the first‑token digit logits; compute the probe score from the assistant’s activations in the previous turn (without steering).
4. Correlate self‑reports with previous‑turn probe scores across all turns to obtain Spearman ρ and isotonic R²; bootstrap conversations for confidence.
5. For causality, repeat step 2 with activation steering: add α·vₗ/|L| to every layer in the window during the self‑report forward pass, varying α ∈ {‑4,‑2,0,2,4}. Check monotonicity of self‑report with α via mixed‑effects modeling.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Probe layer selection limited to middle 60% to avoid style‑related early layers and highly compressed late layers, but may miss concept‑specific early representations.
- Steering window size (*l*∗ ± 2) balances effect strength against disruption; not systematically optimized.
- Logit‑based method preserves information but assumes digit tokens are present and well‑calibrated; sampling introduces noise.
- Simulated user improves scalability but may produce less natural emotional dynamics compared to real human conversations.

**Failure signatures**:
- Greedy self‑report collapse: only a few distinct values across turns (Figure 2A).
- Inverted steering effect: self‑report decreases when steering toward the positive pole → likely probe invalidity.
- No turn‑wise significance: introspective metrics (ρ, R²) not significantly > 0 at many turns.
- Cross‑concept screen all null: basal introspection might already be optimal or probes too correlated.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Probe validation**: Train probes and evaluate Cohen’s d on held‑out texts; expect *d* > 1.5. If not, refine prompts or data.
2. **Baseline introspection**: Without steering, compute logit‑based self‑report vs. previous‑turn probe score; verify positive Spearman ρ and isotonic R² (≥0.1). If too low (<0.05), check self‑report entropy.
3. **Causal test (self‑steering)**: Apply steering along the same concept direction; verify monotonic increase in self‑report with α (mixed‑effects slope positive, *p* < 0.001). If non‑monotonic or negative, abort cross‑model comparisons.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: Why does introspective capacity scale with model size for wellbeing and interest but not for focus and impulsivity in LLaMA models? The paper tested only three sizes (1B, 3B, 8B) with a fixed probe protocol; non‑scaling could stem from probe quality issues, concept‑specific limits, or insufficient scale. Evidence: scaling trends differ across concepts; self‑steering signs were inverted for focus (1B) and impulsivity (8B).

**Open Question 2**: Can activation steering find a single direction that improves introspective fidelity across multiple concepts, or is improvement limited to specific concept‑pair combinations? The 4×4 cross‑concept screen found only two conditions (focus→wellbeing, impulsivity→interest) with significant gains, but the search space was small. It remains open whether broader or universal directions exist.

**Open Question 3**: Why does introspective fidelity for wellbeing degrade over conversation time in Qwen 2.5 7B‑Instruct but remain stable in Gemma 3 4B‑IT, and can this erosion be mitigated? The paper did not explain the divergent temporal trajectories; possible causes include faster internal state drift, decay of attention to the rating prompt, or model‑specific mechanisms. No mitigation was tested.

**Open Question 4**: To what extent do linear probes for emotive concepts confound genuine emotive states with stylistic or persona features, and how does this confound affect the observed self‑report correlations? The authors acknowledge this limitation but did not directly test it; confounding could mean that “introspection” actually tracks style rather than emotion.

## Limitations
- **Probe validity**: Linear probes may confound emotive states with stylistic features, despite contrastive training and Cohen’s d thresholds (see Open Question 4).
- **Simulated user**: Using Gemini as a user simulator may reduce emotional variability and natural turn dynamics.
- **Tokenizer dependence**: The logit‑based method assumes single‑token digits and a meaningful output distribution; deviations compromise the signal.
- **Concept scope**: Results are limited to four emotive concepts; generalizability to other constructs is unknown.
- **Model generalizability**: Partial replications on Qwen and Gemma showed weaker, divergent coupling, indicating limited portability.
- **Dialogue length**: 10‑turn dialogues may not capture long‑term state evolution.
- **Steering parameters**: Fixed window and uniform α may not be optimal; concept‑specific tuning was not explored.
- **Probe training context**: Probes trained on neutral‑question contrastives might not perform equally well on in‑dialogue utterances.

## Confidence
- Logit‑based self‑report avoids greedy decoding collapse: **High**
- Activation steering shows causal dependence of self‑report on internal state: **High**
- Cross‑concept steering selectively improves introspection: **Medium**
- Introspective capacity scales with model size: **Medium**
- Generalizability across model architectures (Qwen, Gemma): **Low**

## Next Checks
1. **Probe robustness test**: Re‑train probes using adversarial stimuli that decouple emotion from style, and validate on natural dialogue utterances to ensure probe quality (Cohen’s d > 1.5) and stable correlations.
2. **Human conversation replication**: Replace the simulated user with real human participants in 10‑turn dialogues on the same topics; recompute introspective metrics to assess ecological validity.
3. **Cross‑family probe transfer**: For each model family (LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma), train probes on held‑out texts from that family and evaluate self‑report coupling; then transfer probes across families to determine whether replication failures are due to probe mismatch or representational differences.
