# Color When It Counts: Grayscale-Guided Online Triggering for Always-On Streaming Video Sensing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22466  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22466  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A grayscale-always, color-on-demand system achieves 91.6% of full-color video QA accuracy while using only 8.1% of RGB frames, enabling energy-efficient always-on sensing.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the high energy cost of continuous RGB video capture on edge/wearable devices by proposing a "grayscale-always, color-on-demand" paradigm. A low-power grayscale camera runs continuously to preserve temporal structure, while a training-free online trigger selectively activates the RGB camera based on a lightweight analysis of grayscale frame affinities. The trigger uses a quadratic program to measure per-frame novelty within a sliding window and a credit-budgeted controller to manage long-term RGB usage. Dynamic token routing further reduces inference compute by processing grayscale frames with fewer visual tokens. On streaming video benchmarks, the system recovers nearly full baseline accuracy with a small fraction of RGB frames, demonstrating substantial color redundancy and enabling practical always-on sensing.

## Method Summary
The proposed system operates causally on a streaming video input. Each timestep, a new grayscale frame is encoded by a frozen CLIP vision encoder to obtain a feature. A sliding window of recent features forms an affinity matrix, which is used in a quadratic program that assigns diversity weights to all frames in the window; the current frame's weight is its "importance score." A credit controller accumulates a budget at a target rate and grants a pseudo-budget to the QP, ensuring long-term RGB usage stays near a desired average. If the current frame's score exceeds a threshold and credit is available, the RGB camera is triggered. Regardless of trigger, the frame (grayscale or RGB) is routed to a frozen multimodal LLM (e.g., InternVL) with asymmetric token allocation: grayscale frames are resized to 224×224 and pooled to 64 tokens, while triggered RGB frames are resized to 448×448 and pooled to 256 tokens. All tokens are projected to the LLM dimension and concatenated temporally for streaming inference.

## Key Results
- Achieves 91.6% of full-color baseline accuracy on StreamingBench while using only 8.1% of RGB frames.
- Reduces total visual token count by 91.9% via dynamic token routing.
- Demonstrates that color is highly redundant for many streaming video understanding tasks; sparse RGB frames suffice when temporal continuity is maintained.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Continuous grayscale monitoring with sparse RGB triggers exploits color redundancy while preserving temporal structure, enabling energy-efficient always-on sensing. A low-power grayscale camera runs continuously, providing a frame at each timestep. A trigger analyzes a sliding window of recent grayscale frames to decide whether to activate the RGB camera for the current frame. The grayscale stream maintains temporal continuity at minimal power. The core assumption is that for many video understanding tasks, color information is only necessary at specific moments; the majority of semantic content can be inferred from luminance and motion. *Evidence anchors:* The abstract states that sparse RGB frames suffice for comparable performance when temporal structure is preserved via continuous grayscale streams. Section 1 and 3.1 describe the paradigm and problem formulation, emphasizing the grayscale-always, color-on-demand approach. The corpus shows related work on streaming video understanding (e.g., LiveStar, StreamMind) but these do not exploit grayscale/RGB asymmetry; this work introduces a novel sensor-level optimization. *Break condition:* If the video content consistently requires color, the trigger may activate RGB too frequently, diminishing energy savings, or if grayscale features fail to capture cues that predict color relevance.

### Mechanism 2
The online trigger uses a lightweight quadratic program on a windowed grayscale affinity matrix to causally detect frame-level novelty, indicating when color is likely needed. For each new grayscale frame, features from the last W frames are extracted via a frozen CLIP encoder. Their cosine similarities form an affinity matrix, transformed to be positive semi-definite. A quadratic program assigns diversity weights to the window, encouraging spread across dissimilar frames. The weight of the current frame is its importance score; if it exceeds a threshold and credits are available, RGB is triggered. The core assumption is that frame diversity in the grayscale feature space correlates with the presence of information that benefits from color. *Evidence anchors:* Section 3.2 details the sliding window, affinity matrix construction (Eq. 2), and QP formulation (Eq. 3). It states the quadratic form "penalizes allocating weight to mutually similar frames" and "encourages spreading the fixed budget across frames that are temporally diverse." The abstract mentions "lightweight quadratic programming to detect chromatic redundancy causally." Corpus papers like EON-1 focus on near-sensor feature extraction but not on grayscale affinity; this QP-based trigger is novel. *Break condition:* The QP relies on the quality of grayscale features; if these are not discriminative (e.g., low resolution, poor lighting), the affinity matrix may misrepresent novelty, leading to false positives/negatives.

### Mechanism 3
A credit-budgeted online controller stabilizes long-term RGB usage, allowing short bursts around novel events while adhering to an average rate. The controller maintains a credit balance b_t. Each frame, credits accumulate at a target rate r and are consumed when RGB is triggered (u_t=1). The available pseudo-budget m_t for the QP is derived from b_t (via clipping). This ensures total RGB frames over T is ≤ rT + C, where C is capacity. The core assumption is that uniform RGB sampling is suboptimal; an adaptive budget that permits occasional bursts better matches the non-uniform distribution of color-critical moments. *Evidence anchors:* Section 3.2 describes the credit update (Eq. 4), budget mapping (Eq. 5), and trigger decision (Eq. 6). It notes that "summing the credit update yields PT ut ≤ rT+C, ensuring long-term budget compliance." The abstract mentions "credit-budgeted control" to "jointly reduce sensing and inference cost." Corpus works like EgoTrigger [29] use audio triggers without a long-term budget mechanism; this credit system is a distinctive design. *Break condition:* If the target rate r is set too low, important color moments may be missed; if too high, energy savings diminish. The controller parameters (C, r) require tuning for different scenarios.

## Foundational Learning

### Streaming video understanding
**Why needed here:** The system processes an unbounded stream of frames in real-time, making per-frame decisions without access to future content. This requires causal processing and memory management.  
**Quick check:** What are the key differences between streaming video understanding and offline video analysis? (Answer: Need for online decisions, limited temporal context, low latency.)

### Training-free adaptation
**Why needed here:** The trigger must run on resource-constrained edge devices without additional training or supervision. It leverages pre-trained models (CLIP, MLLM) and simple optimization (QP).  
**Quick check:** What does "training-free" imply about the system's deployment? (Answer: No model updates or fine-tuning required; uses off-the-shelf components.)

### Token routing for efficiency
**Why needed here:** To further reduce compute, grayscale frames are processed at lower resolution (fewer tokens) than triggered RGB frames, while preserving temporal order. This asymmetric allocation cuts inference cost.  
**Quick check:** How does dynamic token routing reduce computational load? (Answer: By assigning fewer visual tokens to grayscale frames, which are less informative, while allocating more tokens to RGB frames when they are critical.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Grayscale camera -> CLIP visual encoder -> Sliding window buffer -> Affinity matrix builder -> QP solver -> Credit controller -> Trigger decision module -> Dynamic token router -> MLLM decoder

### Critical path
1. At each timestep t, acquire grayscale frame g_t.
2. Extract CLS feature f_t via CLIP.
3. Update sliding window; compute affinity matrix A_t.
4. Solve QP to get weight vector w_t; extract current frame score s_t.
5. Update credit b_t (accumulate r, subtract u_{t-1}).
6. Compute pseudo-budget m_t from b_t.
7. If s_t ≥ θ and b_t ≥ 1, set u_t=1 (trigger RGB); else u_t=0.
8. Route g_t (or captured c_t if u_t=1) to tokenizer with resolution determined by u_t.
9. Concatenate token block with previous ones and feed to MLLM decoder.
10. Repeat for next frame.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Temporal discontinuity vs. power saving**: Hard switching between grayscale (low-res) and RGB (high-res) introduces visual artifacts, but is necessary for energy efficiency.
- **Window size W**: Larger windows capture longer-term context but increase QP complexity O(W^2). Paper uses W=30.
- **Trigger latency**: The QP and controller add per-frame overhead; the system assumes 1 fps sampling to keep latency manageable.
- **Token allocation**: Fixed token counts (64 vs 256) are chosen heuristically; adaptive token counts could further optimize.

### Failure signatures
- **High RGB ratio**: If average RGB usage approaches 100%, the trigger is ineffective; check affinity matrix and credit parameters.
- **Task-specific accuracy drop**: Certain tasks (e.g., counting, spatial reasoning) may degrade due to resolution switches; monitor per-task performance.
- **QP infeasibility**: If the pseudo-budget m_t exceeds window size, the QP constraint may become infeasible; ensure correct clipping.
- **Temporal inconsistency**: The MLLM might misinterpret abrupt resolution changes; observe outputs for artifacts like frame duplication or miscounts.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline comparison**: Run the system with grayscale-only, full RGB, and ColorTrigger on StreamingBench to verify the reported trade-off (e.g., 8.1% RGB with 91.6% performance recovery). Use default parameters.
2. **Ablation study**: Disable dynamic token routing (use uniform 256 tokens for all frames) and compare accuracy and total token count to isolate its contribution.
3. **Parameter sensitivity**: Vary window size W (e.g., 15, 30, 60) and threshold θ (0.2, 0.3, 0.4) to observe impact on RGB ratio and accuracy, ensuring chosen values are near optimal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can ColorTrigger mitigate temporal discontinuities from abrupt grayscale-to-RGB transitions (resolution, chromaticity, token density) without sacrificing causality, training-free deployment, or real-time constraints?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly identify temporal discontinuities from hard frame insertions as a limitation affecting fine-grained temporal reasoning (Sec. 9). They note that spatial resolution changes (224×224 vs. 448×448), color content shifts (L-channel vs. RGB), and token density mismatches (64 vs. 256 tokens) create perceptual "jumps."
- **Why unresolved:** The current design relies on hard insertions for efficiency, but the impact on tasks requiring smooth temporal coherence is not fully quantified or addressed. Alternative integration methods (e.g., feature-space smoothing) might violate strict causality or require retraining.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic studies on temporal-sensitive tasks (e.g., action segmentation, motion tracking) comparing the baseline against transition-smoothing techniques (e.g., grayscale upsampling, adaptive token blending) while measuring accuracy, latency, and power overhead.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What granular task- and context-dependent factors determine color necessity in streaming video understanding, enabling more principled, data-driven triggering policies?
- **Basis in paper:** Figure 4 shows heterogeneous performance gains across tasks (e.g., Attribute Perception saturates early, Spatial Understanding is flat). The paper notes color redundancy is substantial but does not characterize which visual features (e.g., object color prevalence, scene texture, motion patterns) or contexts (e.g., indoor vs. outdoor) drive this variability.
- **Why unresolved:** The trigger uses a uniform vision-based criterion (affinity matrix) for all queries. Understanding the underlying taxonomy of color dependency could lead to adaptive thresholds or features tailored to task families, improving RGB allocation efficiency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale annotation or automated detection of color-critical moments across diverse datasets, correlated with video content descriptors (color histograms, object detectors, scene graphs). This would inform a more nuanced triggering model validated on per-task breakdowns.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can question semantics (e.g., explicit color mentions in the query) be incorporated into the training-free, causal trigger to improve RGB acquisition precision without adding prohibitive latency?
- **Basis in paper:** The trigger is designed to be question-agnostic, relying solely on grayscale feature affinities (Sec. 3.2). However, the authors observe performance fluctuations across tasks and note that some questions inherently require color (e.g., "What color is the...?"). The framework does not exploit query text to modulate triggering.
- **Why unresolved:** Integrating query understanding risks violating the training-free and low-latency constraints. A lightweight, query-aware gating mechanism might improve accuracy but requires empirical validation on its cost-benefit trade-off.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a variant that conditions the QP’s threshold or budget on a binary indicator derived from a frozen text encoder (e.g., detecting color-related keywords). Evaluate on color-critical vs. neutral queries, measuring changes in RGB usage, accuracy, and added inference time.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the grayscale-always, color-on-demand paradigm achieve significant real-world battery life extension on commercial wearable hardware under continuous, all-day usage scenarios?
- **Basis in paper:** The feasibility analysis (Sec. 6.1) estimates power savings based on component datasheets (e.g., Himax HM01B0 vs. Sony IMX681) and BLE transmission assumptions, projecting >10-hour operation. However, no empirical battery tests on devices like Meta Ray-Ban are reported.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical gaps (e.g., sensor startup latency, driver overhead, variable radio conditions) and real-world usage patterns (e.g., sporadic Queries, network traffic) may diminish projected gains. The paper lacks on-device power measurements.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Deploy the full pipeline (grayscale capture, trigger, VLM offloading) on actual smart glasses, measure battery drain under controlled continuous operation with varying RGB rates, and compare against always-on RGB baselines across realistic scenarios (indoor, outdoor, moving).

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does ColorTrigger generalize to MLLMs with built-in temporal attention mechanisms that assume uniform spatial dimensions across frames, and can its dynamic token routing be adapted without breaking such architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** The supplementary material (Sec. 7) describes adapting ColorTrigger to Qwen3-VL, which uses temporal window attention. This required upsampling grayscale frames to match RGB resolution, negating token-level savings and highlighting architectural incompatibility.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper’s token routing assumes independent frame processing (as in InternVL). For models with cross-frame attention, asymmetric tokenization may disrupt learned temporal interactions, and the authors did not provide a general solution.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design a token routing scheme that maintains consistent spatial dimensions (e.g., via learned padding or feature projection) while still reducing effective compute. Evaluate on multiple temporal-attention models, measuring accuracy, computational cost, and attention pattern changes.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Prompt/template and token-count mapping are unspecified; risk of dimension mismatches and context overflow.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Trigger performance depends on window size, threshold, credit cap, target rate; defaults may not generalize.
- **Color-redundancy assumption:** For color-critical tasks, RGB may trigger frequently, reducing energy savings.

## Confidence
- **Overall trade-off:** Medium – depends on uncertain components (prompt, token routing) and hyperparameter tuning.
- **QP trigger + controller:** Medium – sound theory but sensitive to feature quality and solver stability.
- **Dynamic token routing:** Low – token counts (64/256) lack clear derivation; encoder details hidden.
- **Grayscale continuity:** High – strong empirical evidence supports sufficiency.
