# When LLM Judge Scores Look Good but Best-of-N Decisions Fail

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12520
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12520
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Global correlation metrics can be high while within-prompt ranking for best-of-N selection is weak due to prompt-level baseline effects inflating scores.

## Executive Summary
LLM judges are commonly validated using global metrics like overall correlation with human or oracle judgments. However, in deployment scenarios such as best-of-N selection, where the judge must rank candidates within a specific prompt, global metrics may not predict performance. This paper decomposes judge and oracle scores into prompt-level baselines and candidate-level deviations, revealing that prompt-level effects can inflate global agreement while masking poor within-prompt ranking. Evaluation using within-prompt correlation, tie rates, and recovery shows that a judge with high global correlation (r=0.47) performs poorly within prompts (r=0.27, 67% ties). Pairwise judging mitigates ties and improves recovery, emphasizing that deployment should rely on within-prompt metrics rather than global scores.

## Method Summary
The analysis decomposes scores from an LLM judge and an oracle (e.g., GPT-4) into additive components: a prompt-level baseline (capturing prompt difficulty) and candidate-level deviations (reflecting quality). A best-of-N benchmark with 5,000 prompts and 4 candidates per prompt is used. Evaluation metrics include within-prompt correlation (r_within), tie rates (fraction of prompts with equal scores), recovery (fraction of oracle gain over random captured by the judge), and top-1 accuracy (selecting the oracle's best candidate). Pairwise judging is implemented by comparing all candidate pairs to reduce quantization ties from set-wise scoring.

## Key Results
- A judge with global correlation r=0.47 had within-prompt r=0.27, 67% tie rate, 21.0% recovery, and 31.6% top-1 accuracy in best-of-4.
- Pairwise best-of-2 reduced tie rates to 3.9% and increased recovery to 61.2%.
- Achieving 50% recovery requires a within-prompt correlation of approximately r_within≈0.4, based on extrapolation.
- Adding "easy pairs" to pairwise evaluations inflates global metrics but does not improve within-prompt performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism involves the additive decomposition of scores: prompt-level baselines vary across prompts and can dominate global variance, leading to high overall correlation even if candidate deviations are poorly ranked within each prompt. Within-prompt metrics isolate candidate deviations, revealing true ranking capacity. Pairwise judging reduces ties by avoiding score quantization from set-wise scoring, as judges often assign similar absolute scores to all candidates for a prompt, making distinctions only in relative comparisons. This exposes finer-grained preferences and improves selection accuracy relative to the oracle.

## Foundational Learning
1. **LLM-as-Judge Paradigm**: Using LLMs to evaluate text responses. *Why needed*: Scalable alternative to human evaluation. *Quick check*: Correlate judge scores with human judgments on a held-out set.
2. **Best-of-N Selection**: Generating multiple candidates per prompt and selecting the best. *Why needed*: Improves output quality in applications like code generation. *Quick check*: Measure oracle agreement when selecting the highest-scoring candidate.
3. **Global vs. Within-Prompt Correlation**: Correlation computed across all prompts versus averaged per prompt. *Why needed*: Global metrics can be inflated by prompt-level effects, masking within-prompt failures. *Quick check*: Compute both; a large gap indicates baseline dominance.
4. **Prompt-Level Baseline Effects**: Systematic score offsets due to prompt difficulty or phrasing. *Why needed*: Explains why judges score entire prompts higher or lower regardless of candidate quality. *Quick check*: Decompose scores; baseline variance should correlate with prompt features.
5. **Tie Rate in Set-Wise Judging**: Fraction of prompts where candidates receive identical scores. *Why needed*: High ties force random selection, reducing efficacy. *Quick check*: Count prompts with no score differences among candidates.
6. **Recovery Metric**: (Judge's gain over random) / (Oracle's gain over random). *Why needed*: Measures practical utility in best-of-N selection. *Quick check*: Compare judge's selected candidate quality to random baseline and oracle best.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Prompts -> Candidate Responses -> LLM Judge (Scores) -> Best-of-N Selection; Oracle (Ground Truth) -> Evaluation Metrics  
**Critical Path**: For each prompt, generate N candidates, score all with judge, select highest score (or via pairwise comparisons), compare to oracle's best to compute metrics.  
**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Set-wise judging is efficient but prone to score quantization and high ties; pairwise judging reduces ties but increases computational cost (O(N^2) comparisons).
- Global metrics are easy to compute but may not reflect deployment; within-prompt metrics are more relevant but require per-prompt analysis.
- Using a strong oracle (e.g., GPT-4) as ground truth assumes its superiority, which may not hold for all domains.
**Failure Signatures**:
- High global correlation coupled with low within-prompt correlation.
- Tie rates exceeding 50% in set-wise judging for best-of-N with N>2.
- Recovery below 30% for moderate N (e.g., best-of-4).
**First Experiments**:
1. On a sample benchmark, compute global correlation and within-prompt correlation; expect a significant gap if baseline effects are present.
2. Measure tie rates for set-wise vs. pairwise judging; verify pairwise reduces ties substantially.
3. Calculate recovery and top-1 accuracy; correlate with within-prompt correlation to check the linear relationship.

## Limitations
- The decomposition model assumes additive prompt baselines and candidate deviations, which may not generalize to all judge architectures or response modalities.
- Findings are based on a single 5k-prompt, best-of-4 text benchmark; performance may vary for different N, domains (e.g., code), or judge models.
- The boundary between legitimate difficulty and metric inflation when adding easy pairs to pairwise evaluations is not operationalized.

## Confidence
- **High**: Global metrics can decouple from within-prompt ranking due to prompt-level baseline effects; benchmark data demonstrates this dissociation.
- **Medium**: Specific thresholds (within-prompt r≈0.27, recovery 21% for best-of-4) are accurate for the evaluated judge and benchmark but may not universalize.
- **Low**: The linear extrapolation that 50% recovery requires r_within≈0.4 is suggestive from one data point and lacks broad validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Benchmark Replication**: Apply the decomposition and within-prompt analysis to at least two additional best-of-N benchmarks (e.g., code generation, instruction following) with varying N to test the correlation-recovery relationship.
2. **Controlled Baseline Test**: Construct a synthetic dataset with fixed prompts and uniformly varied response quality to isolate candidate deviations; verify if decomposition correctly recovers deviations and if within-prompt metrics predict selection accuracy.
3. **Pairwise vs. Set-wise Deployment Test**: In a live best-of-N deployment, compare a pairwise protocol (all pairs) against set-wise ranking for the same judge, measuring final selection accuracy against an oracle and operational tie rates to quantify cost-performance trade-offs.
