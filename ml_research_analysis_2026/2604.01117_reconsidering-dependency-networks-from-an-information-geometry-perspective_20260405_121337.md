# Reconsidering Dependency Networks from an Information Geometry Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01117
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01117
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** The paper introduces an information-geometric framework for dependency networks, interpreting pseudo-Gibbs sampling as m-projections, deriving a full conditional divergence (FC) upper bound, and proving consistency of node-wise learning; experiments confirm the bound is tight (differences ~0.001 nat).

## Executive Summary
Dependency networks are graphical models learned via pseudo-Gibbs sampling but lack a closed-form joint distribution, which has hindered theoretical analysis. This paper uses information geometry to reinterpret each pseudo-Gibbs update as an m-projection onto a full conditional manifold. This yields the full conditional divergence (FC) and an FC-limit upper bound that locates the stationary distribution relative to the true distribution. Learning is recast as independent node-wise optimisation: parameters are set directly from empirical conditionals, and structure is searched greedily to minimise a cost combining conditional entropy and an MDL-like penalty. A convergence proof shows that under standard conditions the learned model’s stationary distribution converges to the true distribution as the number of samples grows. Experiments on synthetic Ising and Bayesian network data empirically validate the tightness of the bound.

## Method Summary
The core insight is that a pseudo-Gibbs update for node *i* is the m-projection of the current distribution onto the manifold *E*(θᵢ) of distributions whose conditional for *X*ᵢ matches the learned θᵢ. This geometric view motivates the full conditional divergence *FC*(*p*‖*q*) = Σᵢ cᵢ *KL*(*p*‖*E*ᵢ(*q*)) and the bound *FC*(*p*‖π) ≤ *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*) = Σᵢ cᵢ *KL*(*p*‖*E*(θᵢ)). Learning decomposes per node: for a chosen summary function *Y*ᵢ (partition of *X*<sub>−*i*</sub>), parameters are set to empirical conditionals θᵢ(*X*ᵢ∣*y*ᵢ) = *p*<sub>*D*</sub>(*X*ᵢ∣*y*ᵢ). Structure learning greedily searches for *Y*ᵢ (via leaf splits/merges) to minimise cost(*Y*ᵢ) = *H*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>(*X*ᵢ∣*Y*ᵢ)) + *R*(*k*ᵢ,*N*), where *R* penalises model complexity. Under ergodicity of pseudo‑Gibbs, existence of a lossless *Y*ᵢ in the candidate set, and vanishing penalty, the stationary distribution π(*N*) converges to the true *p*<sup>\*</sup> as *N* → ∞ (Theorem 6). Thus, dependency networks become a consistent, decomposable alternative to standard Bayesian networks.

## Key Results
- Information-geometric interpretation: pseudo-Gibbs updates are m-projections onto full conditional manifolds.
- New divergence measure: full conditional divergence (*FC*) and an *FC*-limit upper bound that characterises the stationary distribution.
- Learning reformulated as independent node-wise optimisation; consistency proof provided.
- Empirical validation: on Ising and Bayesian network datasets the observed *FC*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>‖π) and *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>) differ by as little as 0.001 nat.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Each pseudo-Gibbs update for node *i* is an m-projection of the current distribution onto the full conditional manifold *E*(θᵢ).
- **Mechanism:** In information geometry, an m-projection minimises KL divergence to a manifold. Applying θᵢ yields the distribution *p*(*X*<sub>−*i*</sub>) θᵢ(*X*ᵢ∣*X*<sub>−*i*</sub>), which is exactly the m-projection of the current distribution onto *E*(θᵢ). This recasts the Markov transition as moving to the nearest distribution that respects θᵢ’s conditional.
- **Core assumption:** *E*(θᵢ) is both *e*-flat and *m*-flat (Theorem 1).
- **Break condition:** If the manifold is not *m*-flat (e.g., for some continuous or non‑exponential family distributions), the projection may be non‑unique or no longer correspond to the update.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The stationary distribution π lies within a small *FC*-neighbourhood of the true *p* if the divergences to each *E*(θᵢ) are small.
- **Mechanism:** The full conditional divergence *FC*(*p*‖*q*) = Σᵢ cᵢ *KL*(*p*‖*E*ᵢ(*q*)) sums the KL divergences to the conditionals of *q*. Theorem 3 proves *FC*(*p*‖π) ≤ Σᵢ cᵢ *KL*(*p*‖*E*(θᵢ)) = *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*). Hence, if each θᵢ closely matches *p*(*X*ᵢ∣*X*<sub>−*i*</sub>), *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*) is small, forcing π close to *p* in the *FC* sense.
- **Core assumption:** Pseudo‑Gibbs sampling is ergodic (unique stationary π exists).
- **Break condition:** The bound may become loose if the conditional manifolds *E*(θᵢ) are poorly aligned, or if ergodicity fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Learning decomposes into independent node optimisations; under conditions, π(*N*) → *p*<sup>\*</sup> as *N* → ∞.
- **Mechanism:** Parameter learning sets θᵢ = *p*<sub>*D*</sub>(*X*ᵢ∣*Y*ᵢ) (Theorem 4). Structure learning searches *Y*ᵢ to minimise cost(*Y*ᵢ) = *H*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>(*X*ᵢ∣*Y*ᵢ)) + *R*(*k*ᵢ,*N*). Theorem 6 proves that if the candidate set eventually includes a lossless *Y*ᵢ (preserving all information about *X*ᵢ) and the penalty *R* vanishes for that *Y*ᵢ, then *KL*(*p*<sub>*D*(*N*)</sub>‖π(*N*)) → 0 a.s., and under positivity π(*N*) → *p*<sup>\*</sup>.
- **Core assumption:** Strong law of large numbers for data; candidate set contains a lossless information source for all large *N*; *p*<sup>\*</sup>(*x*) > 0 for all *x*.
- **Break condition:** Convergence fails if no lossless candidate exists (e.g., bounded search depth), if *R* does not vanish, or if *p*<sup>\*</sup> has zero‑probability states.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Information Geometry** (m-/e-flat manifolds, m-projection)
  - *Why needed:* Provides the mathematical language to interpret pseudo‑Gibbs as sequential m‑projections, enabling the *FC* analysis.
  - *Quick check:* What manifold property ensures the m‑projection of *p* onto *E*(θᵢ) is *p*(*X*<sub>−*i*</sub>) θᵢ(*X*ᵢ∣*X*<sub>−*i*</sub>)?
- **Concept: Full Conditional Divergence (FC)**
  - *Why needed:* A structured divergence that respects the conditional independences of the network, parallel to pseudo‑likelihood; used to bound the distance between *p* and π.
  - *Quick check:* How does *FC*(*p*‖*q*) differ from *KL*(*p*‖*q*) in terms of factorisation, and why is that difference important?
- **Concept: Ergodicity of Pseudo‑Gibbs Sampling**
  - *Why needed:* Guarantees a unique stationary distribution π and that Monte Carlo averages converge to expectations under π.
  - *Quick check:* What practical conditions on the conditionals {θᵢ} and the graph might ensure ergodicity for random‑scan pseudo‑Gibbs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Node *i* → Summary function *Y*ᵢ → Conditional table θᵢ → Pseudo‑Gibbs sampler → Stationary distribution π.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Preprocess data to compute empirical counts for all (*X*<sub>−*i*</sub>, *X*ᵢ) tuples.
  2. Initialise *Y*ᵢ to a degenerate (e.g., constant) function.
  3. Greedy structure search: generate candidates (split/merge leaves), evaluate cost via leaf sums (Eq. 17), accept if improvement > penalty.
  4. After structure fixed, set θᵢ(*X*ᵢ∣*y*ᵢ) = empirical conditional *p*<sub>*D*</sub>(*X*ᵢ∣*y*ᵢ).
  5. Run pseudo‑Gibbs (random scan) to generate samples from π.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Search depth vs. computational cost: deeper trees capture more conditional structure but increase *k*ᵢ and risk overfitting; greedy search may get stuck in local minima.
  - Penalty *R* choice: MDL penalty *R*(*k*,*N*) = (*k* log *N*)/(2*N*) balances fit and complexity; too small → overfit, too large → underfit.
  - Random vs. sequential scan: random scan yields a homogeneous chain (easier analysis); sequential may mix faster but requires subsequencing to obtain stationary distribution.
  - Leaf splitting criterion: using only conditional entropy *H*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>(*X*ᵢ∣*Y*ᵢ)) favours simpler structures; the penalty controls complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Non‑ergodic chain:** Mixing time diverges; π not reached. Check by monitoring multiple chains – do they agree?
  2. **Large FC‑limit gap:** Validation shows *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>) >> *FC*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>‖π) – bound may be loose, model may not approximate *p*<sup>\*</sup>.
  3. **Search stagnation:** Greedy algorithm stops improving quickly; may need merge operations or different candidate generation (e.g., adding/removing parents).
  4. **Zero‑probability states:** If *p*<sup>\*</sup> has zeros, convergence may not be pointwise; ensure training data covers all state combinations or smooth estimates.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ising model comparison:** Train on samples from a known Ising model (e.g., 4×3 grid). Compare *FC*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>‖π) and *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>) to verify bound tightness; also compare π marginals to *p*<sup>\*</sup> via KL.
  2. **Bayesian network datasets:** Sample from a Bayesian network (e.g., 12‑node, 21‑edge). Evaluate how well the dependency network captures joint dependencies not explicit in the learned conditionals.
  3. **Bound sensitivity:** Vary number of samples *N* and structure complexity (max depth). Measure how *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>) decreases with *N* and correlates with *KL*(*p*<sup>\*</sup>‖π).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Closed‑form stationary distribution under structural constraints:** Can π be expressed in closed form for special dependency networks (e.g., tree‑structured conditionals or consistent conditionals)? The paper left the lack of a joint closed form as a major theoretical obstacle.
  - *Resolution path:* Derive π for restricted families where the m‑projection orbit becomes tractable.
- **Accurate inference for rare events:** How to improve conditional queries for rare clamped configurations (small *p*<sup>\*</sup>(*x*<sub>*C*</sub>))? Current weighting in the FC average downweights such events.
  - *Resolution path:* Develop importance sampling or stratification techniques that reweight MCMC samples to account for rare events.
- **Extension to continuous variables:** The analysis is restricted to finite discrete spaces; can it be extended to continuous domains?
  - *Resolution path:* Reformulate key definitions (manifolds, m‑projections, FC) using densities; prove analogous theorems for smooth distributions (e.g., exponential families); validate empirically.

## Limitations
- Exact generative parameters for the Ising4×3 and RB12‑21 datasets are not provided; regenerated data may differ from originals, affecting FC gap measurements.
- Ergodicity of pseudo‑Gibbs chains is not guaranteed; poor mixing could invalidate π estimation and bound evaluation.
- Greedy structure learning lacks transparent stopping criteria and penalty scaling details, risking suboptimal learned networks.
- The tightness of the FC‑limit bound is only validated on two small synthetic models; its behaviour on larger or real‑world networks remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Geometric interpretation (m-projection):** High – supported by rigorous theorems (1–2).
- **FC‑limit bound (theoretical):** High – proven inequality (Theorem 3).
- **FC‑limit bound (empirical tightness):** Medium – consistent on two small datasets, but limited scope.
- **Consistency of learning:** Medium – proof assumes ergodicity, existence of lossless candidates, and vanishing penalty; these may not always hold in practice.

## Next Checks
1. **Mixing diagnostics:** Run multiple pseudo‑Gibbs chains from diverse initial states; compute Gelman‑Rubin statistics and effective sample sizes to confirm ergodicity and reliable π estimation.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary the MDL penalty factor and maximum split depth; re‑measure *FC*(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>‖π), *KL*(*p*<sup>\*</sup>‖π) and *FC*<sub>lim</sub>(*p*<sub>*D*</sub>) to assess robustness.
3. **Cross‑dataset replication:** Evaluate on additional discrete distributions (e.g., a 10×10 Ising grid, UCI datasets like Adult) to test whether the *FC*‑limit bound remains tight across varied structures.
