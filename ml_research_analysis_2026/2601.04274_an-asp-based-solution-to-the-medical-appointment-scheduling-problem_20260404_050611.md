# An ASP-based Solution to the Medical Appointment Scheduling Problem

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04274
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04274
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Generates optimal, conflict-free schedules in under 0.04 seconds while strictly respecting accessibility constraints and optimizing preferences for vulnerable populations.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles inefficiencies in centralized medical appointment systems (e.g., Italy’s CUP) by introducing a declarative constraint optimization model using Answer Set Programming (ASP). The system integrates Blueprint Personas to encode patient-specific clinical, social, and environmental constraints, enabling personalized scheduling for vulnerable groups. A microservice architecture with asynchronous batching processes requests, and experimental results on synthetic scenarios show the solver produces optimal schedules in milliseconds while handling hard constraints like accessibility and soft objectives like travel distance. The approach demonstrates feasibility for real-time deployment but requires further validation on larger scales and with real-world data.

## Method Summary
The method formalizes scheduling as an ASP problem where feasible schedules correspond to answer sets satisfying encoded constraints. Patient, clinic, doctor, and slot data are transformed into ASP facts. Hard constraints (e.g., no double-booking, accessibility for disabled patients) are enforced via integrity constraints; soft objectives (distance, wait time, sensory penalties, preference bonuses) are optimized using a weighted `#minimize` statement. The system employs a two-microservice architecture: MS1 (Flask API) receives requests and pushes facts to a queue; MS2 batches facts every 60 seconds, compiles a full ASP program, invokes the Clingo solver, and stores results asynchronously. Blueprint Personas act as templates that inject specific constraints and preference weights based on patient archetypes, personalizing the optimization without altering core solver logic.

## Key Results
- Solves realistic scenarios (50 patients, 6 clinics, 500 slots) in under 0.04 seconds, with grounding below 10,000 atoms.
- Guarantees strict adherence to hard constraints (e.g., disabled patients never assigned to inaccessible clinics).
- Optimizes multi-criteria soft objectives (travel, sensory comfort, preferences) via a weighted sum, producing globally optimal schedules.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Declarative ASP Constraint Optimization
- **Claim:** The ASP declarative model efficiently generates feasible, optimal schedules by hard-coding all constraints and objectives, pruning invalid solutions during solving.
- **Mechanism:** ASP encodes the scheduling problem as a logic program with choice rules for assignments, integrity constraints to eliminate infeasible combinations (e.g., double-booking, accessibility violations), and a `#minimize` statement to optimize a weighted sum of soft objectives (distance, wait time, sensory penalties, preference bonuses).
- **Core assumption:** The combinatorial space of possible assignments can be systematically searched by a solver like Clingo, and optimality relative to the defined weighted objectives is both meaningful and computationally attainable for the problem scale.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "employing Answer Set Programming (ASP) to model scheduling as a declarative constraint optimization problem... Experimental evaluation... generates optimal, conflict-free schedules in under 0.04 seconds while respecting hard constraints."
  - [section 3.1] "This formalization naturally maps to an Answer Set Programming (ASP) model, where feasible assignments correspond to answer sets satisfying the constraints and optimization goals."
- **Break condition:** If grounding grows super-linearly (e.g., O(p·c·s) as claimed) for large instances (hundreds of patients, thousands of slots), computation time may exceed real-time thresholds, making the system non-responsive.

### Mechanism 2: Blueprint Personas for Personalization
- **Claim:** Integrating Blueprint Personas enables personalized, ethically-aligned scheduling for vulnerable populations by transforming complex patient archetypes into machine-readable constraints and preference weights.
- **Mechanism:** Personas are encoded as structured facts (e.g., `disabled(p1)`, `sensory_preference(p2, "light")`) that trigger specific hard constraints (e.g., `accessible(clinic)`) and influence soft objective weights via inference rules (e.g., `sensory_penalty/4`). This abstraction separates persona definition from solver logic.
- **Core assumption:** Key social, clinical, and environmental attributes of patient groups can be reliably distilled into a finite set of computable predicates that significantly impact scheduling outcomes and equity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "personalizes scheduling for vulnerable populations by integrating Blueprint Personas."
  - [section 3.2] "Blueprint Personas... encapsulate real-world profiles combining clinical, social, cognitive, and behavioral attributes... This modeling enables the system to reason over patient-specific constraints."
- **Break condition:** If real-world patient heterogeneity exceeds the expressivity of the predefined persona templates, the system may generate suboptimal or inequitable schedules for outliers, as patient-side financial constraints are not yet modeled.

### Mechanism 3: Layered Microservice with Asynchronous Batching
- **Claim:** The layered microservice architecture with asynchronous batching supports scalability and integration by decoupling the user-facing API from the computationally intensive ASP solving phase.
- **Mechanism:** A Flask-based service (MS1) receives requests and formats facts. A second service (MS2) batches facts over a 60-second window, compiles a complete ASP program, and invokes Clingo. Results are stored asynchronously, allowing immediate user feedback.
- **Core assumption:** 60-second batching is sufficient to aggregate demand while keeping solution latency acceptable for end-users, and the ASP program's grounding/solving time scales predictably within this window.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] "MS2: collects facts into a batch queue and, every 60 seconds, compiles them into a complete ASP program; this program is solved using Clingo... Asynchronous threading prevents overload during peak times, offering immediate user feedback and delayed result delivery."
  - [section 5.4] "In tests conducted with a custom Python generator, using 50 patients, 6 clinics, and 500 slots, the grounded atom count stayed below 10,000, and solving time was under 0.1 seconds."
- **Break condition:** Under a surge of concurrent requests overwhelming the batch queue or if grounding time exceeds the batching interval, the system will accumulate backlog, violating the "real-time" promise. The 60-second window is a tuned parameter that may not hold for all load profiles.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Answer Set Programming (ASP) semantics**
  - **Why needed here:** To understand how choice rules, constraints, and optimization statements (`#minimize`) interact to define the solution space. Misunderstanding stable model semantics could lead to incorrect expectation of what solutions are generated.
  - **Quick check:** If a patient has a `need` for 3 sessions and there are 5 available slots matching all other constraints, how does the choice rule `{appointment(...)}` ensure exactly 3 appointments are selected?
- **Concept: Constraint Satisfaction vs. Optimization**
  - **Why needed here:** The system separates "hard constraints" (must be satisfied) from "soft objectives" (to be minimized). An engineer must know which parts of the model are non-negotiable vs. tunable trade-offs.
  - **Quick check:** What is the logical consequence if a `disabled` patient is assigned to a non-`accessible` clinic? Does the solver find an alternative, or does the model become unsatisfiable?
- **Concept: Declarative vs. Procedural Problem Encoding**
  - **Why needed here:** ASP requires describing *what* constitutes a valid/optimal schedule, not *how* to construct it. Engineers used to imperative algorithms must shift to modeling domain rules and letting the solver search.
  - **Quick check:** The `sensory_penalty` rule computes a penalty. To *completely forbid* assignments where sensory mismatch exceeds a threshold, would you modify this rule or add a new constraint? How?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Client -> MS1 (Flask API) -> Queue (Redis/RabbitMQ) -> MS2 (Scheduler Service) -> Clingo (Solver) -> DB (MySQL)
- **Critical path:** Request arrives → MS1 authenticates/validates → facts pushed to queue → every 60s MS2 dequeues batch → constructs ASP program → calls Clingo → parse answer sets → update DB → status available for client polling/notification. Synchronous acknowledgement ends at MS1; solving is asynchronous.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Centralized ASP Model vs. Distributed Solving: Centralization guarantees global optimality but becomes a bottleneck; federated adaptation may sacrifice optimality for scale.
  - Fixed 60s Batching vs. Event-Driven: Batching amortizes solver startup cost and enables multi-request optimization but adds up-to-60s latency; event-driven is faster for low load but inefficient under bursts.
  - Full Replanning vs. Incremental Update: Replanning from scratch is simple; incremental solving (Clingo-supported) could speed up similar batches at the cost of complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Grounding explosion: Solver log shows huge atom count and times out; likely cause: missing pre-filtering of irrelevant clinics/slots.
  - Inconsistent model (UNSAT): Clingo returns "UNSATISFIABLE"; likely cause: conflicting hard constraints in batch (e.g., two high-urgency patients needing one accessible slot) or bug in constraint encoding.
  - Suboptimal schedule (high objective score): Schedule respects constraints but ignores preferences; likely cause: missing preference effect facts or weight misconfiguration in `#minimize`.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Constraint Sanity Check:** Create one disabled patient and one non-accessible clinic. Verify the solver never assigns that patient to that clinic.
  2. **Persona Activation Test:** Use a patient with `sensory_preference(p, "light")` and two clinics with opposing environmental conditions. Verify the schedule picks the clinic with lower sensory penalty even if farther.
  3. **Batching & Throughput:** Simulate 10, 50, 100 concurrent requests using the synthetic generator. Measure batch cycle time vs. solve time, and end-to-end latency per request.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scalability in dynamic contexts:** What is the computational scalability of the ASP system for hundreds/thousands of patients in dynamic emergency contexts? The paper states further experimentation is required under extreme loads and frequent emergencies, as testing only reached 50 patients.
- **No-show prediction:** How can predictive analytics for patient no-shows be incorporated into the ASP optimization to reduce wasted slots? Future work suggests this could enhance robustness, but the current model lacks no-show uncertainty.
- **Neurodivergent commuting:** What additional constraints and persona attributes are needed to model neurodivergent patients' urban commuting preferences (e.g., transport, crowding, sensory conditions)? Travel factors beyond clinic environment are currently unmodeled, and extending the framework is proposed.

## Limitations
- **External validation gap:** The system’s validation relies on authors' prior work and a single operational example; corpus analysis shows limited peer-reviewed corroboration (low FMR, zero citations among neighbors).
- **Weight justification:** The ad hoc weighting in the `#minimize` objective (e.g., `Distance*10000` vs. `WaitTime`) lacks empirical trade-off analysis with stakeholders, risking misaligned optimization.
- **Scalability boundary:** The 60-second batching window and claimed linear grounding are tested only up to 50 patients; the break point for super-linear growth or queue backlog under real operational scales remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **High:** Core ASP mechanism for constraint satisfaction and small-scale optimality (backed by sub-0.04s experimental timings).
- **Medium:** Microservice batching architecture for moderate loads (evidence from synthetic tests up to 50 patients).
- **Low:** Personalization via Blueprint Personas for broad vulnerable populations (relies on authors' internal framework; no independent validation of persona coverage).
