# RSHallu: Dual-Mode Hallucination Evaluation for Remote-Sensing Multimodal Large Language Models with Domain-Tailored Mitigation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10799
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10799
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RSHallu addresses hallucinations in RS-MLLMs with a novel taxonomy, dual-mode evaluation, and mitigation strategies, improving hallucination-free rates by up to 21.63 percentage points while preserving downstream task accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Remote sensing multimodal large language models (RS-MLLMs) are prone to hallucinations—outputs inconsistent with input imagery—which poses risks in high-stakes applications. This paper introduces RSHallu, a comprehensive framework to evaluate and mitigate hallucinations specifically in RS contexts. The core innovations include: (1) a novel taxonomy that extends beyond object-level errors to include image-level hallucinations (modality, resolution, scene semantics); (2) dual-mode evaluation with online cloud auditing (Qwen-VL-Max with chain-of-thought) and a local compact checker fine-tuned on RSHalluCheck; and (3) mitigation strategies combining domain-tailored fine-tuning on RSHalluShield with training-free techniques (decoding-time logit correction, RS-aware prompting).

Evaluations across representative RS-MLLMs show that the mitigation approaches improve hallucination-free rates by up to 21.63 percentage points while maintaining or even enhancing performance on downstream remote sensing visual question answering (RSVQA) and visual grounding (RSVG) tasks. The work underscores the importance of domain-specific hallucination handling and provides practical tools for reliable RS-MLLM deployment.

## Method Summary
RSHallu addresses RS-MLLM hallucinations through a structured pipeline. First, it defines a fine-grained taxonomy with five categories: image attributes (modality, resolution), image scenes (land-use classification), object existence, object attributes, and object relations. This taxonomy guides the construction of three datasets: RSHalluEval (2,023 expert-annotated QA pairs) for evaluation, RSHalluCheck (15,396 auto-generated QA pairs) for training a local hallucination checker, and RSHalluShield (30,000 balanced QA pairs) for mitigation via fine-tuning.

Second, the framework proposes dual-mode evaluation: an online mode leveraging Qwen-VL-Max with chain-of-thought prompting to compare model outputs against ground truth; and a local mode using a compact model (e.g., Qwen2-VL) fine-tuned on RSHalluCheck, enabling cost-effective, privacy-preserving auditing without ground truth. For mitigation, RSHallu integrates (a) supervised fine-tuning on RSHalluShield to rebalance response distributions, and (b) training-free plug-and-play methods—decoding-time logit correction that selectively aggregates attention-biased layers, and RS-aware prompting that injects global image context and counterfactual warnings. The combined strategies are evaluated by re-measuring hallucination-free rates on RSHalluEval and by ensuring downstream RSVQA/RSVG accuracy is preserved.

## Key Results
- **Evaluation suite:** RSHalluEval provides 2,023 QA pairs covering all hallucination types, with expert annotations to benchmark RS-MLLMs.
- **Dual-mode evaluator:** The online Qwen-VL-Max with CoT achieves high correlation with human judgments, while the locally fine-tuned checker reaches ~87% accuracy and can operate without ground truth.
- **Mitigation gains:** Applying fine-tuning on RSHalluShield, decoding correction, and RS-aware prompting improves overall hallucination-free rates by up to 21.63 percentage points (e.g., on LLaVA-1.5) without sacrificing RSVQA/RSVG performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Image-Level Hallucination Captures Domain Flaws
**Claim:** Formalizing RS-specific hallucinations via a taxonomy that includes image-level errors (attributes, scene) captures inconsistencies beyond object-centric failures in RS-MLLMs.  
**Mechanism:** The taxonomy extends conventional object-level hallucination (existence, attributes, relations) by introducing image-level categories that address RS-domain peculiarities: multimodal inputs (e.g., panchromatic vs. color), variable resolution, and global scene semantics. This forces models to reason about holistic image properties rather than only local objects.  
**Core assumption:** RS imagery has unique global-semantic and acquisition-driven features (modality, resolution) that general-domain taxonomies miss, and failures here constitute a distinct hallucination mode.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "introduce image-level hallucination to capture RS-specific inconsistencies beyond object-centric errors (e.g., modality, resolution, and scene-level semantics)"; Section 3 details examples of models misidentifying modality/resolution and misclassifying land-use scenes.  
**Break condition:** If image-level hallucination metrics do not correlate with real-world RS application failures (e.g., emergency response) in follow-up studies.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Mode Evaluation Balances Precision and Practicality
**Claim:** Dual-mode hallucination checking (online cloud auditing vs. local compact checker) balances evaluation accuracy, cost, and reproducibility for RS scenarios.  
**Mechanism:** Online mode uses a powerful cloud-based MLLM (Qwen-VL-Max) with chain-of-thought prompting for high-precision, reference-answer comparison. Local mode deploys a fine-tuned compact checker (on RSHalluCheck) for privacy, cost-efficiency, and reproducibility without ground truth. Both operationalize the taxonomy via automated QA scoring.  
**Core assumption:** RS practitioners face trade-offs between auditing precision (cloud) and deployment practicality (local); a single checker cannot optimally satisfy both.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "enable dual-mode checking—online cloud auditing via Qwen-VL-Max with chain-of-thought prompting and local checking via a compact checker fine-tuned on RSHalluCheck"; Section 4 (Tables 2–4) compares CoT benefits and fine-tuned checker accuracy/throughput.  
**Break condition:** If local checker fine-tuning fails to generalize to unseen RS-MLLMs or imagery distributions, or if cloud API costs/provenance preclude widespread use.

### Mechanism 3: Mitigation by Data Rebalancing and RS-Aware Interventions
**Claim:** Mitigation succeeds by combining (a) training-friendly fine-tuning on a domain-tailored dataset (RSHalluShield) with balanced normal/misleading QA pairs, and (b) training-free plug-and-play strategies (decoding-time logit correction, RS-aware prompting) that target RS-specific hallucination triggers.  
**Mechanism:** RSHalluShield provides supervised examples of correct vs. misleading RS responses, rebalancing the model's response distribution. Decoding correction intervenes in the logit layer by selecting reference layers with stronger visual-attention bias (via attention-weight metric) and applying weighted correction. RS-aware prompting injects global image context and counterfactual awareness to guide generation.  
**Core assumption:** RS hallucinations stem from both data bias (imbalanced training) and decoding/attention flaws; addressing both yields larger gains than either alone.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "introduce a domain-tailored dataset RSHalluShield (30k QA pairs) for mitigation... propose training-free plug-and-play strategies... decoding-time logit correction and RS-aware prompting"; Section 5 details the 1:1 normal-to-misleading ratio; Section 6 defines layer selection (Fig.9, Eq.1) and shows results (Tables 5–6).  
**Break condition:** If mitigation on RSHalluShield degrades performance on non-RS tasks, or if decoding correction introduces latency/instability in production.

## Foundational Learning
### Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) Architecture
**Why needed here:** RSHallu assumes familiarity with visual encoders, LLM backbones, and cross-attention fusion to understand where hallucinations originate (e.g., modality bias, insufficient visual grounding).  
**Quick check:** In an MLLM, where does the "modality gap" between visual features and text tokens most likely cause hallucination?

### Hallucination Taxonomy in Vision-Language Models
**Why needed here:** The paper's new image-level categories build on existing object-level schema (existence, attribute, relation). Understanding prior work (e.g., CHAIR, MMHalBench) is key to seeing RSHallu's novelty.  
**Quick check:** How does "image scene hallucination" differ from "object relation hallucination" in RS contexts?

### Remote Sensing Image Characteristics
**Why needed here:** RS modalities (panchromatic, multispectral), resolutions, and scene types (urban, agricultural) drive the need for image-level evaluation. Without this, the taxonomy seems arbitrary.  
**Quick check:** Why might a model that accurately identifies cars in an RS image still hallucinate about the image's overall land-use class?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Taxonomy Module → Dataset Construction (RSHalluEval, RSHalluCheck, RSHalluShield) → Dual-Mode Evaluator (Online & Local) → RS-MLLM Evaluation & Mitigation → Re-evaluation

Detailed:
- Taxonomy Module defines hallucination categories (image attributes, image scenes, object existence, object attributes, object relations).
- Dataset Construction produces three specialized corpora.
- Dual-Mode Evaluator includes online cloud auditing (Qwen-VL-Max + CoT) and local checker fine-tuned on RSHalluCheck.
- Mitigation Module: (1) Fine-tuning on RSHalluShield; (2) Training-free strategies (decoding correction, RS-aware prompting).
Both evaluation and mitigation feed RS-MLLMs; outcomes are measured by re-running RSHalluEval and downstream RSVQA/RSVG.

### Critical Path
1. Define fine-grained hallucination taxonomy for RS.
2. Construct RSHalluEval (expert QA), RSHalluCheck (auto QA for checker), and RSHalluShield (balanced auto QA for mitigation).
3. Implement and calibrate the dual-mode evaluator (choose online or local based on trade-offs).
4. Apply mitigation (fine-tuning and/or plug-and-play strategies) to target RS-MLLM.
5. Re-evaluate hallucination-free rates using RSHalluEval.
6. Validate that downstream task accuracy (RSVQA/RSVG) is maintained.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Online vs. Local evaluation:** Cloud mode (Qwen-VL-Max) offers higher accuracy (lower ES) but incurs latency, cost, and privacy concerns; local mode is fast and private but lags on image-attribute and object-existence detection.
- **Training vs. Training-free mitigation:** Fine-tuning on RSHalluShield yields larger HF gains but requires model access and compute; plug-and-play methods (decoding, prompting) are accessible for black-box APIs but show smaller, model-dependent improvements.
- **Dataset scale vs. quality:** RSHalluCheck/Shield are auto-generated at scale (15k/30k) enabling fine-tuning, yet may inherit biases from Qwen-VL-Max; RSHalluEval is expert-annotated and smaller (2k) to ensure reliable benchmarking.

### Failure Signatures
- **Evaluator misalignment:** Local checker exhibits higher error scores than online on image attributes, object existence, and object attributes (Table 4), leading to unreliable scores when cloud access is unavailable.
- **Decoding correction underperformance:** Simple layer averaging or mismatched thresholds (p, r) can degrade performance; paper reports average selection sometimes worse than target (Table 5).
- **Prompting backfire:** The overall perception strategy harms LLaVA-1.5 while helping Qwen2-VL and VHM, likely due to LLaVA-1.5's weaker visual grounding (Table 6).
- **Downstream regression:** Fine-tuning on RSHalluShield risks overfitting to its QA format, potentially reducing performance on RSVQA/RSVG; however, the paper reports maintained/increased accuracy, but this remains a risk for other models/tasks.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline audit:** Run a suite of RS-MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5, GeoChat, VHM, Qwen2-VL) on RSHalluEval using both online and local evaluators; compute overall and per-category hallucination-free (HF) rates to confirm prevalence of image-level errors.
2. **Evaluator calibration:** Validate the local checker by measuring its accuracy on the RSHalluCheck validation set; compare its predictions against expert scores on a RSHalluEval subset; demonstrate the benefit of chain-of-thought prompting for the online cloud evaluator.
3. **Mitigation validation:** Fine-tune Qwen2-VL on RSHalluShield; apply decoding correction and RS-aware prompting to LLaVA-1.5 and VHM; re-evaluate on RSHalluEval; verify that HF_all improves by >5–10 pp without dropping >2% on downstream RSVQA and RSVG test sets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can we improve the accuracy of local hallucination checkers for image-level and object-level hallucinations to match the performance of online cloud-based auditing?  
**Basis in paper:** In Section 4.4.2, Table 4 shows the fine-tuned local checker (Qwen2-VL) has higher error scores (ES) for image attributes, object existence, and object attributes than the online Qwen-API-CoT. The text states: "However, the local deployment strategies significantly lag behind the online invocation strategies in terms of image attributes, object existence, and object attributes."  
**Why unresolved:** Local checkers are essential for low-cost, reproducible, and privacy-preserving evaluation, but their lower accuracy on critical hallucination types limits reliability when cloud access is unavailable. The paper offers no solution to close this gap.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Developing enhanced training data or model architectures that specifically target the underperforming categories, and empirically demonstrating reduced error scores on RSHalluEval across all types, matching online performance.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How can we design a decoding-time hallucination mitigation strategy that is universal across different RS-MLLM architectures and question types without requiring per-model hyperparameter tuning?  
**Basis in paper:** In Section 7.5.1, the authors manually set different thresholds (p) and recall rates (r) for each model (LLaVA-1.5, GeoChat, VHM). They also note the effect is "particularly prominent for object existence questions" (binary), implying limited generality. The text details: "For LLaVA-1.5, p is set to 0.3 and 0.05, while r is configured as 0.8 and 0.5... For GeoChat, p=0.2 and r=0.3... For VHM, p takes values of 0.05 and 0.2..."  
**Why unresolved:** Manual tuning reduces plug-and-play usability and may not generalize to new models or non-binary questions. A universal strategy would be more practical for diverse RS-MLLMs.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Proposing an adaptive mechanism that automatically selects reference layers and correction parameters (e.g., via validation data or input statistics), and showing consistent improvement across multiple models and hallucination types without hand-tuning.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Why does the overall perception prompting strategy harm the performance of some RS-MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5) while helping others, and how can we design prompting strategies that are robust across models of varying capabilities?  
**Basis in paper:** In Section 7.5.2, Table 6 shows the overall perception strategy reduces overall HF for Qwen2-VL and VHM but reduces it for LLaVA-1.5. The text explains: "After utilization, the general performance of LLaVA-1.5 declined, whereas the performance of Qwen2-VL and VHM marginally increased. This may be due to the relatively weak visual extraction and anti-interference abilities of LLaVA-1.5..."  
**Why unresolved:** The failure mode is not deeply analyzed, and no principled adjustment for model strength exists. This limits applicability to weaker or older RS-MLLMs.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting ablations to identify harmful prompt aspects (e.g., length, noise), designing adaptive prompting based on model capability or input complexity, and testing on a broader range of model architectures and sizes.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How can we generate high-quality, style-consistent training data for image scene hallucinations to effectively mitigate them via fine-tuning, given that automatic annotation leads to style mismatches with expert evaluations?  
**Basis in paper:** In Section 7.6.1, fine-tuning on RSHalluShield failed to improve (and slightly worsened) image scene hallucinations. The authors attribute this to style mismatch: "This might be because RSHalluShield is automatically annotated, where the answers to such questions usually consist of a single sentence describing the image scene. In RSHalluEval, however, expert annotations for these types of questions often consist of a single word representing the scene’s category."  
**Why unresolved:** Scene-level hallucinations are a key RS-specific type, but automatic data generation produces answers that do not align with evaluation style, rendering fine-tuning ineffective for this category.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Developing improved automatic annotation that generates scene answers as single-word categories, or curating a manually annotated scene-hallucination dataset, and demonstrating improved HF_IS after fine-tuning.

## Limitations
- Public availability of RSHallu datasets and model checkpoints remains unconfirmed, hindering independent verification.
- Details of the Qwen-VL-Max API (e.g., logit access, cost, rate limits) are insufficient for exact reproduction of the online evaluator and decoding correction.
- The automatic generation process for RSHalluCheck and RSHalluShield (prompt templates, filtering) lacks full procedural transparency, which may affect dataset fidelity.

## Confidence
- *Taxonomy extension (image-level errors):* **Medium**
- *Dual-mode evaluation design:* **High**
- *Mitigation efficacy (training-free + fine-tuning):* **Medium**
