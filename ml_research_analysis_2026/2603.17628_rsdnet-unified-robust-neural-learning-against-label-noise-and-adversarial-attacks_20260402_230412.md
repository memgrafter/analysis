# rSDNet: Unified Robust Neural Learning against Label Noise and Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.17628
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17628
- **Reference Count:** 6
- **Primary Result:** A unified robust learning framework using S-divergences that achieves state-of-the-art robustness to both label noise and adversarial attacks while maintaining clean accuracy parity.

## Executive Summary
rSDNet proposes a unified robust learning framework based on S-divergences within a minimum-divergence estimation paradigm. The resulting loss function automatically down-weights aberrant observations, such as mislabeled samples or adversarially perturbed features. The approach is theoretically supported with proofs of Fisher consistency, classification calibration, and robustness guarantees under uniform label noise and infinitesimal feature contamination. Empirical evaluations on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 show that rSDNet matches standard cross-entropy accuracy on clean data and significantly outperforms existing robust losses under various contamination conditions, including label noise and adversarial attacks like FGSM, PGD, CW, and DeepFool.

## Method Summary
rSDNet employs a parametric family of S-divergences to construct a robust loss that is minimized in a minimum-divergence estimation framework. This loss automatically reduces the influence of observations that diverge significantly from the bulk distribution, effectively down-weighting both noisy labels and adversarially contaminated features. The method is controlled by two hyperparameters: β (>0) and λ (<0), which regulate the robustness-efficiency trade-off. The theoretical analysis establishes Fisher consistency and Bayes optimality under contamination models, providing a unified solution for two distinct corruption types.

## Key Results
- Clean accuracy on MNIST (98.1%) matches standard cross-entropy, demonstrating no sacrifice on uncorrupted data.
- Under 50% uniform label noise, rSDNet achieves 95.4% accuracy on MNIST, comparable to MAE but with better stability.
- Against adversarial attacks, rSDNet attains the highest PGD adversarial accuracy (99.6%) and improves clean accuracy relative to conditional cross-entropy (CCE).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The S-divergence loss inherently reduces the contribution of samples that are outliers relative to the learned distribution. In the context of label noise, mislabeled points are assigned lower weights during training. For adversarial perturbations, contaminated features similarly exhibit divergence from clean data distributions, leading to down-weighting. This unified mechanism allows a single loss to mitigate both corruption types without requiring separate detection or adaptation modules.

## Foundational Learning
- **S-divergence:** A parametric divergence measure generalizing common losses; needed to flexibly control robustness; quick check: verify reduction to cross-entropy at β=0.
- **Fisher consistency:** Guarantees minimizer recovers Bayes optimal classifier; needed for theoretical soundness; quick check: confirm consistency on synthetic data with known posteriors.
- **Classification calibration:** Ensures well-calibrated probability estimates; needed for reliable uncertainty; quick check: test reliability diagrams on clean held-out sets.
- **Robustness guarantees:** Theoretical bounds under uniform noise/infinitesimal contamination; needed for trust in worst-case scenarios; quick check: simulate uniform noise and validate accuracy decay curve.
- **Minimum-divergence estimation:** Framework for robust empirical risk minimization; needed to formalize down-weighting; quick check: compare objective values with and without contamination.
- **Uniform label noise:** Symmetric noise model; needed for tractable theory; quick check: evaluate on instance-dependent noise to test assumptions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Data -> Robust Loss Module (S-divergence computation) -> Gradient Calculation -> Parameter Update -> Classifier
- **Critical path:** Loss computation automatically assigns sample weights based on divergence before backpropagation.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Higher β/|λ| increases robustness but may reduce clean accuracy; parameter ranges must be tuned per dataset.
- **Failure signatures:** Sharp accuracy drop under non-uniform label noise or large-norm feature attacks beyond infinitesimal assumption; output probabilities may become miscalibrated if β too high.
- **First experiments:**
  1. Train on MNIST with increasing uniform label noise rates (0%, 30%, 50%, 70%) to plot robustness curve.
  2. Evaluate adversarial robustness using PGD with ε=0.03 and 10 steps, comparing to CCE and MAE baselines.
  3. Sweep β in [0, 0.2] and λ in [-1, -0.1] on CIFAR-10 with 40% noise to identify optimal trade-off region.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (uniform label noise, infinitesimal feature contamination) may not hold in real-world scenarios with instance-dependent noise or large attacks.
- Evaluation limited to three small datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10); scalability to large-scale data (e.g., ImageNet) is unknown.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (β, λ) lacks thorough analysis; recommended ranges may not generalize across datasets or corruption types.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical properties:** High (proofs are provided and verifiable)
- **Label noise robustness:** Medium (limited empirical validation, mostly 50% uniform noise)
- **Adversarial robustness:** Low-to-Medium (standard attacks only; no adaptive or adversarial training comparisons)
- **Clean accuracy parity:** High on MNIST, Medium on CIFAR-10
- **Hyperparameter generalizability:** Medium (ranges suggested but not extensively tested)
