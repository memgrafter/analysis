# Diagnosing Translated Benchmarks: An Automated Quality Assurance Study of the EU20 Benchmark Suite
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01957
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01957
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A scalable three-step automated QA pipeline (structural audit, COMET profiling, LLM-as-judge) reveals stark task-driven quality differences in machine-translated benchmarks (e.g., HellaSwag vs. ARC) and yields diagnostic indicators to prioritize human review.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of ensuring the quality of machine-translated benchmark datasets, using the EU20 suite (5 tasks across 20 European languages) as a case study. It implements a fully automated, three-step quality assurance pipeline: first, a structural audit repairs corrupted or incomplete entries; second, a neural metric (COMET) profiles sentence-level translation quality for multiple translation services; third, LLM-based span-level error annotation identifies dominant failure modes. Results demonstrate that translation quality is highly task-dependent, with datasets like HellaSwag showing pervasive accuracy/mistranslation errors and low COMET scores, while ARC maintains high quality. The approach provides a scalable alternative to exhaustive human review, generating actionable diagnostics for dataset maintainers.

## Method Summary
The core method is a three-step automated pipeline applied to the EU20 benchmark suite. Step 1 performs a structural corpus audit against four integrity criteria (answer-index alignment, field completeness, split consistency, cross-language coverage), surgically re-translating only defective fields via API. Step 2 computes reference-free and reference-based xCOMET-XXL scores for all (language, dataset) pairs, comparing translation services (DeepL, ChatGPT, Google) using paired bootstrap tests and Friedman/Nemenyi rankings, and correlates scores with sentence length. Step 3 samples items for span-level error labeling using three LLM judges with a GEMBA-ESA prompt and MQM taxonomy (Accuracy/Mistranslation, Fluency, Other), aggregating by majority vote to compute error rates per 1,000 items.

## Key Results
- Structural audit repaired significant violations (e.g., 327 empty-answer samples in HellaSwag validation), achieving 100% compliance on integrity criteria.
- COMET profiling revealed strong task-driven divergence: HellaSwag had low xCOMET-XXL scores (median 0.42–0.65) and high accuracy/mistranslation error rates (>600/1k items), while ARC scored highly (0.97–0.98) with low errors (90–250/1k). EU20 (DeepL) significantly outperformed Okapi (ChatGPT) on MMLU, ranking first across five languages.
- Sentence length negatively correlated with translation quality, most strongly in HellaSwag (Spearman ρ ≈ -0.75). Reference-based COMET on MMLU corroborated reference-free trends.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structural Integrity Enforcement
- **Claim:** Structural integrity violations (missing fields, split inconsistencies) directly compromise benchmark validity by making samples incomparable or un-scorable.
- **Mechanism:** An automated audit verifies four criteria (A-D). Defective entries are surgically repaired via targeted re-translation, preserving provenance and overwriting only flagged fields.
- **Core assumption:** Format and metadata consistency are prerequisites for meaningful cross-lingual comparison.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "structural corpus audit with targeted fixes"; Section 3 defines criteria and Table 1 quantifies violations (e.g., 327 empty answers). *Weak evidence:* Impact of repairs alone on downstream LLM scores is not measured.
- **Break condition:** Repair loop terminates when all splits achieve 100% compliance. Process fails if source-target ID mapping becomes irreparably ambiguous.

### Mechanism 2: Scalable Quality Profiling with COMET
- **Claim:** Sentence-level neural metrics (COMET) provide scalable, diagnostic indicators of translation reliability that correlate with task design and output length.
- **Mechanism:** Reference-free and reference-based xCOMET-XXL scores are computed per (language, dataset). System differences are analyzed via median gaps, win-rates, and paired bootstrap CIs. Sentence length is correlated with scores.
- **Core assumption:** COMET captures semantic/fluency adequacy relevant to benchmark utility; score distributions reflect systematic translation challenges.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "quality profiling using a neural metric (COMET)"; Section 4 shows task-driven patterns (Figure 1) and negative length-quality correlations; service comparisons (Figures 2-4).
- **Break condition:** Profiling becomes non-diagnostic if COMET scores saturate (ceiling effects) or the length-quality correlation vanishes across tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Span-Level Error Diagnosis via LLM-as-Judge
- **Claim:** Span-level error annotation (using LLM-as-judge with MQM taxonomy) reveals that accuracy/mistranslation errors dominate in low-COMET datasets, providing interpretable failure modes.
- **Mechanism:** Three LLM annotators apply GEMBA-ESA to label error spans by MQM category and severity. Majority agreement defines a present error. Rates per 1,000 items are aggregated.
- **Core assumption:** LLM-based error detection, while noisy, converges with sentence-level metrics to identify the *type* of degradation (adequacy vs. fluency).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes "LLM-as-judge span-level error detection (GEMBA-ESA)"; Section 5 shows A+M dominates error mass in HellaSwag (Table 4) and aligns with COMET (Figure 5).
- **Break condition:** Error landscape loses meaning if annotator agreement falls below a stable threshold (e.g., <2/3) or category distributions become uniform across datasets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Machine Translation Quality Estimation (QE)**
  - **Why needed:** The analysis relies on neural QE metrics (COMET) to score translations at scale without human references. Understanding reference-free vs. reference-based QE is key to interpreting quality profiles.
  - **Quick check:** What is a fundamental limitation of using a reference-based QE metric when the "reference" is itself a machine translation (e.g., Global-MMLU)?
- **Concept: Benchmark Structural Integrity**
  - **Why needed:** The first QA step ensures samples are parsable and comparable (correct answer indices, non-empty fields). Broken structure invalidates any subsequent quality score.
  - **Quick check:** Why would a missing "choices" field in a multiple-choice benchmark be more critical than a minor grammatical error in the question text?
- **Concept: Non-Parametric Statistical Comparison for NLP Metrics**
  - **Why needed:** The paper uses paired bootstrap for median differences and Friedman/Nemenyi for system ranks, which are standard for bounded metric scores with non-normal distributions.
  - **Quick check:** When comparing two translation systems on the *same* set of source segments, why is a paired test (like bootstrap) mandatory instead of an unpaired test?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Structural Auditor -> COMET Profiler -> LLM Judge Aggregator -> Statistical Analysis Module
- **Critical path:** 1) Run structural audit and apply repairs -> 2) Compute COMET scores for all (lang, dataset) -> 3) For shared subsets, compute pairwise system deltas/ranks -> 4) Sample items for LLM-as-judge -> 5) Aggregate span errors -> 6) Correlate error categories with COMET scores.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Reference-free vs. reference-based:* Ref-free scales to all languages but captures system-specific stylistic alignment. Ref-based (using human-edited refs) is more valid but limited to languages with gold references.
  - *LLM judge cost vs. granularity:* Three LLMs improve agreement but increase cost/time. One LLM is cheaper but more prompt-sensitive.
  - *Repair scope:* Full re-translation is expensive. Surgical fix (only defective fields) is efficient but risks inconsistent style between original and re-translated fragments.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Structural:** High rates of missing cross-language samples (NL) or fields (NC) persist after audit → systematic pipeline failure (e.g., API quota limits).
  - **COMET profiling:** All datasets cluster near ceiling (~0.98) → metric saturation, cannot distinguish quality.
  - **LLM judge:** Low majority agreement (<60%) on "Clean" vs. error categories → prompt/model instability; requires re-engineering or human calibration.
  - **Triangulation failure:** Dataset has high COMET but also high A+M error rate → suggests COMET is missing a critical error type; requires metric inspection.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Structural Audit Smoke Test:** Run auditor on 100-sample subset per dataset. Verify violations (Table 1) are reproducible and repair script successfully fills empty "choices" fields without altering IDs.
  2. **COMET Service Comparison on ARC:** For CORE-10 languages, compute ref-free xCOMET-XXL on EU20/Okapi overlap. Replicate median difference heatmap (Figure 2). Check bootstrap CIs for ∆ are non-zero for ≥8/10 languages.
  3. **Error-COMET Correlation:** For HellaSwag, compute Spearman ρ between sentence length and xCOMET-XXL per language. Confirm strong negative correlation (ρ < -0.6) for Germanic languages (e.g., de, da, sv) as in Figure 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** To what extent do the quality trends and error patterns identified in EU20 generalize to other multilingual benchmark suites, especially those covering non-European languages or different task types?
  - **Basis:** "Our study is centered on EU20 (five tasks and 20 European languages) so conclusions may not transfer to other domains or non-European languages."
  - **Why unresolved:** The study is confined to a specific set of tasks and languages; without broader validation, universality is unknown.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Replicate QA pipeline on diverse benchmarks (e.g., Asian, African languages; code/math tasks) and compare quality landscapes.
- **Open Question 2:** How reliably do automated QA metrics (COMET, LLM-as-judge) predict human judgment of translation quality at the span level across the full spectrum of tasks and languages in EU20?
  - **Basis:** "We rely on automated quality assurance as a proxy for human review... Human verification is limited to a reference-based check on a subset of MMLU."
  - **Why unresolved:** Human evaluation was only on a small, single-task subset; reliability for other tasks/languages is unverified.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Comprehensive human evaluation on a stratified sample of all tasks/languages, then correlate automated scores with human judgments.
- **Open Question 3:** To what degree do machine-translated benchmarks suffer from semantic infidelities and cultural inappropriateness that escape detection by current structural and neural quality metrics?
  - **Basis:** "structural audits ensure format integrity... and TQE-based evaluations cannot guarantee full semantic faithfulness or cultural appropriateness."
  - **Why unresolved:** QA pipeline focuses on surface/sentence-level alignment, not deeper semantic equivalence or cultural contextualization.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Design targeted probes or human studies for semantic distortions/cultural mismatches and correlate with existing metrics.

## Limitations
- LLM-as-judge reliability is uncertain; agreement metrics (Cohen’s κ) between the three annotators are unreported, and prompt/model drift could bias error classification.
- The impact of structural repairs alone on downstream LLM benchmark scores is not experimentally isolated.
- For high-quality translations (e.g., ARC), xCOMET-XXL scores cluster near ceiling (~0.98), potentially masking finer-grained differences relevant to benchmark validity.

## Confidence
- **Structural integrity necessity:** High – Criteria are clearly defined, violations quantified, and repairs are deterministic.
- **Task-driven quality divergence (HellaSwag vs. ARC):** Medium – Supported by COMET medians and error rates, but without human validation on a shared sample, the *interpretation* of "usable" remains indirect.
- **LLM error diagnosis triangulation:** Low – Relies on uncalibrated LLM judges; no human-annotated gold spans for verification, and severity heuristics are not operationally defined.

## Next Checks
1. **Inter-annotator agreement audit:** Re-run GEMBA-ESA on a common subset (e.g., 500 HellaSwag items) and compute pairwise Cohen’s κ. If κ < 0.6 for key categories (A+M), the error landscape is unreliable.
2. **Repair A/B test:** For a language like German, create two HellaSwag variants: (a) only structurally repaired entries; (b) full re-translation of the same items. Compare LLM accuracy on both against the original broken version to isolate the structural fix effect.
3. **COMET ceiling assessment:** On ARC, compute the gap between xCOMET-XXL reference-free scores and reference-based scores (using human-edited MMLU refs as proxy). If the gap is consistently < 0.05 across languages, the metric is saturated and further distinctions are likely noise.
