# A theory of learning data statistics in diffusion models, from easy to hard

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12901  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12901  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Diffusion models learn data statistics in a fixed order of increasing complexity, with sample complexity scaling linearly for pairwise statistics (\(\Theta(d)\)) and cubically for fourth-order cumulants (\(\Theta(d^3)\)) under independence, governed by the *diffusion information exponent* \(k^*\).

## Executive Summary
The paper reveals a *distributional simplicity bias* in diffusion models: they first capture simple pairwise correlations before tackling higher-order structure. This phenomenon is established both empirically—on U‑Nets trained on CIFAR‑10 and CelebA—and theoretically, via a single‑neuron denoiser on a mixed cumulant model. The key invariant is the *diffusion information exponent* \(k^*\), defined as the smallest order at which the Hermite expansion of the data likelihood ratio is non‑zero. For independent latent variables, the sample complexity to learn the fourth cumulant is \(\Theta(d^3)\), while pairwise needs only \(\Theta(d)\). Positively correlated latent variables can accelerate higher‑order learning to \(\Theta(d\,\text{polylog}\,d)\). The study also underscores the critical role of projected stochastic gradient descent (pSGD); without projection, unconstrained SGD can stagnate near zero norm due to a radial contraction term. Overparameterization and depth in multi‑layer networks can mitigate this stagnation.

## Method Summary
The authors adopt a teacher‑student framework: data are generated from a controlled model (mixed cumulant spikes in covariance and fourth‑order moments) and the student is a single‑neuron denoiser \(S_w(x) = -x - \sigma(w\cdot x)w\). By expanding the denoising loss in the Hermite basis, they derive the spherical gradient dynamics and identify the leading interaction term, which depends on the diffusion information exponent \(k^*\). This yields analytic predictions for sample complexity and learning curves. Empirical validation includes synthetic experiments confirming the sample‑complexity thresholds, tests of projected vs. unconstrained SGD, and demonstrations of the simplicity bias on real image datasets. The methodology hinges on expressing the loss gradient via an effective nonlinearity \(F_\sigma\) and separating radial and directional components.

## Key Results
- Diffusion models exhibit a *simplicity bias*: statistics are learned in a fixed order, from pairwise to higher‑order, as quantified by the diffusion information exponent \(k^*\).
- Sample complexity for projected SGD scales as \(\Theta(d^{k^*-1})\); concrete instantiations: \(\Theta(d)\) for pairwise (\(k^*=2\)), \(\Theta(d^3)\) for fourth‑order cumulant with independent latents (\(k^*=4\)).
- Projected SGD is essential: unconstrained SGD can converge to a trivial solution \(\|w\|=0\) for activations with negative radial term \(\Lambda(0)<0\) (e.g., odd sigmoids).
- Correlation between the latent sources of pairwise and higher‑order statistics accelerates learning of the higher‑order spike from \(\Theta(d^3)\) to \(\Theta(d\,\text{polylog}\,d)\).
- Overparameterization (width/depth) can overcome the stagnation issue even for unconstrained SGD, though the precise mechanism remains open.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: The learning dynamics are governed by the diffusion information exponent \(k^*\), the smallest order at which the Hermite expansion of the data likelihood ratio \(L_t\) has a non‑zero coefficient. This determines the initial power‑law growth of the overlap \(\alpha = w\cdot v\): \(\dot\alpha \propto \alpha^{k^*-1}\). Hence the sample complexity scales as \(\Theta(d^{k^*-1})\). For the mixed cumulant model with independent latents, the covariance spike has \(k^*=2\) (linear scaling) and the fourth‑cumulant spike has \(k^*=4\) (cubic scaling).  
**Core assumptions**: Data distribution admits a Hermite decomposition; the denoiser nonlinearity \(\sigma\) yields non‑zero coefficients at the relevant orders; initialization on the unit sphere.  
**Evidence**: Theoretical derivation (Section 4.1) and empirical verification on synthetic and image data.  
**Break condition**: If the lowest non‑zero Hermite coefficient appears at \(k^*=1\), all statistics are learned at linear rate. If the effective nonlinearity coefficient \(c_F^{k^*-1}=0\), the dynamics may stall.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Unconstrained SGD can stagnate near \(\|w\|=0\) due to an additional radial contraction term in the gradient, while projected SGD (pSGD) eliminates this term and enables efficient learning.  
**Mechanism**: The unconstrained gradient contains a radial component \(\Lambda(\|w\|)w\) with \(\Lambda = (1+\mathbf{c}_L^2) c_{\tilde F}^1(\|w\|) + c_G^0\). For many common activations (e.g., odd sigmoids with appropriate scaling) \(\Lambda(0)<0\), making \(\|w\|=0\) an attractive fixed point. pSGD keeps \(\|w\|\) constant, so only the signal term drives the dynamics.  
**Core assumptions**: Activation yields \(\Lambda(0)<0\) and the leading signal coefficient is sub‑linear (\(c_L^1=0\)); initialization near \(\|w\|=1\).  
**Evidence**: Analytical form of \(\Lambda\) (Section 4.3), Proposition 4.7 proving convergence to zero, and Fig. 2 illustrating \(\Lambda\) for various \(\sigma\).  
**Break condition**: Scaling \(\sigma\) so that \(\Lambda(\|w\|)>0\) makes the radial term repulsive and unconstrained SGD may escape zero. Overparameterization can also counteract contraction.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Positive correlation between the latent variables that generate the covariance and fourth‑cumulant spikes reduces the sample complexity for learning the fourth cumulant from \(\Theta(d^3)\) to \(\Theta(d\,\text{polylog}\,d)\).  
**Mechanism**: With independent latents, the Hermite expansion of \(L_t\) lacks cross‑terms, so the gradient for the cumulant direction evolves as \(\dot\alpha_v \propto \alpha_v^3\) (since \(k^*=4\)). When the latents \(\lambda\) (covariance) and \(\nu\) (cumulant) satisfy \(\mathbb{E}[\lambda\nu]>0\), cross‑terms couple the overlaps \(\alpha_u\) and \(\alpha_v\). Once \(\alpha_u\) is learned (in \(\Theta(d)\) samples), it drives \(\alpha_v\) linearly.  
**Core assumptions**: Data contain both spikes; scalar latents have non‑zero correlation; the denoiser can align to both directions.  
**Evidence**: Proposition 4.6 (part 2) proves weak recovery within \(\Theta(d\,\text{polylog}\,d)\) under correlation and matching sign initialization.  
**Break condition**: Independence or negative correlation removes acceleration; sign mismatch (\(w_0\) opposite to \(v\)) can prevent recovery even with correlation.

## Foundational Learning

- **Hermite Polynomials & Expansions**  
  Any square‑integrable function of a standard Gaussian can be expanded in the orthogonal Hermite basis, decomposing contributions by order (e.g., pairwise, fourth‑order).  
  *Why needed*: Identifying the diffusion information exponent \(k^*\) and deriving gradient dynamics rely on Hermite coefficients of the likelihood ratio \(L_t\) and the effective nonlinearity \(F_\sigma\).  
  *Quick check*: For standard normal \(x\), \(x^2 = h_2(x) + h_0(x)\) where \(h_2(x)=x^2-1\), \(h_0(x)=1\). The order‑2 coefficient is 1.

- **Diffusion Denoising Objective**  
  Diffusion models are trained to denoise data corrupted by Gaussian noise. The denoising score‑matching loss yields an effective nonlinearity \(F_\sigma\) that connects the training objective to statistical estimation.  
  *Why needed*: The gradient of the loss, expressed via \(F_\sigma\), determines learning dynamics and the radial term \(\Lambda\).  
  *Quick check*: For linear activation \(\sigma(z)=z\), the optimal linear denoiser matches the score of Gaussian data, giving \(F_\sigma=0\).

- **Spherical Gradient & Information Exponent**  
  Spherical gradient flow (constant \(\|w\|\)) isolates directional dynamics. The diffusion information exponent \(k^*\) is the smallest index of a non‑zero Hermite coefficient in the data likelihood ratio. It generalizes the supervised information exponent to diffusion.  
  *Why needed*: \(k^*\) directly governs learning rate and sample complexity via \(\dot\alpha \propto \alpha^{k^*-1}\).  
  *Quick check*: In a single‑index model \(y=\sigma^*(w^*\cdot x)+\text{noise}\) with MSE loss, if \(\sigma^*\) has a non‑zero third derivative at zero, the information exponent is typically 3 (first non‑zero Hermite coefficient appears at order 3).

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map** (plain text):  
MCM Sampler or Image Dataset -> Denoiser (rank‑1 autoencoder or multi‑layer U‑Net) -> pSGD Trainer (or SGD with optional projection) -> Monitoring (overlaps, clone loss)

**Critical Path**  
1. Implement the mixed cumulant model sampler with controllable spike strengths \(\beta_u,\beta_v\) and latent correlation \(\rho=\mathbb{E}[\lambda\nu]\).  
2. Build the denoiser: enforce unit‑norm \(w\) (renormalize each step) and define activation \(\sigma\).  
3. Compute the loss gradient analytically via \(-\nabla_{\text{sph}}L = (1-ww^\top)F_\sigma(x\cdot w)\) or use automatic differentiation.  
4. Train with pSGD: update \(w\), then project onto the unit sphere; record overlaps \(\alpha_u = w\cdot u\), \(\alpha_v = w\cdot v\).  
5. Diagnose simplicity bias by comparing test loss on real data vs. Gaussian clones (matched mean/covariance).  
6. Vary dimension \(d\), sample size \(n\), latency correlation \(\rho\), activation \(\sigma\), and network width/depth.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Projection**: Renormalization is cheap but crucial; skipping it may cause stagnation for activations with \(\Lambda<0\).  
- **Activation**: Odd sigmoids (e.g., \(-\tanh\)) often have \(\Lambda(0)<0\); one can manually rescale to flip the sign, but overparameterization offers a more automatic fix.  
- **Overparameterization**: Adding width or depth can counteract the radial contraction without changing \(\sigma\), but introduces additional parameters that may complicate the analysis.  
- **Diffusion time**: The coefficients \(c_L^k\) and \(c_F^k\) depend on the noise level \(t\); the theory assumes a fixed \(t\), whereas the full diffusion schedule involves many times.  
- **Finite‑\(d\) effects**: Theoretical thresholds include polylog factors; in practice, larger samples may be needed due to small‑\(d\) deviations.

**Failure Signatures**  
- **Norm collapse**: \(\|w\|\) steadily decreases to near zero (unconstrained SGD with \(\Lambda<0\)).  
- **No overlap growth**: \(w\cdot v\) remains at initialization scale (\(\sim 1/\sqrt{d}\)) despite many samples, indicating insufficient \(n\) for \(k^*>1\) or vanishing coefficients.  
- **Clone loss parity**: Test loss on real data and Gaussian clones stays identical throughout training; the model never exploits higher‑order structure.  
- **Instability**: Loss explodes or becomes NaN, often due to learning rates exceeding stability bounds.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Sample‑complexity scaling for fourth‑cumulant**  
   Train single‑neuron pSGD on MCM with \(\beta_u=0\) (only cumulant spike) at \(d\in\{50,100,200\}\). Sweep sample size \(n\) across \(0.5d^2, d^2, 2d^2, 0.5d^3, d^3, 2d^3\). Measure final \(|w\cdot v|\). Expect recovery only when \(n \gg d^3\), confirming the \(k^*=4\) cubic barrier.

2. **Projected vs. unconstrained SGD**  
   Repeat experiment 1 with activation \(\sigma = -\tanh\) (scaled). Compare pSGD (renormalization) vs. vanilla SGD. Observe that pSGD achieves high overlap while unconstrained SGD sees \(\|w\|\to0\) and overlap collapse.

3. **Effect of latent correlation**  
   Use MCM with both spikes (\(\beta_u>0, \beta_v>0\)). Set latent correlation \(\rho=0\) (independent) and \(\rho=0.5\) (positively correlated). Use total samples \(n = c d\) sufficient for pairwise learning. Track \(\alpha_u\) and \(\alpha_v\) during training. With \(\rho=0\), \(\alpha_v\) should remain near zero until \(n\sim d^3\); with \(\rho>0\), \(\alpha_v\) should rise soon after \(\alpha_u\) stabilizes, demonstrating acceleration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *What is the precise mechanism by which overparameterization and depth enable diffusion models to learn higher‑order statistics, counteracting the stagnation predicted for narrow networks under unconstrained SGD?*  
   The paper shows empirically that wider/deeper networks recover the spike despite a negative contraction term \(\Lambda\), but no theory explains this.

2. *How can the diffusion information exponent \(k^*\) be computed for realistic architectures beyond the single‑neuron denoiser, and what does it reveal about learning complex data distributions?*  
   The exponent is derived analytically only for a single‑unit model; for multi‑layer networks the expanded loss involves many order parameters, making \(k^*\) nontrivial to define.

3. *Why does the teacher‑student scenario with a matched denoiser become suboptimal due to an attracting fixed point at \(w=0\), contradicting conventional wisdom?*  
   The analysis links suboptimality to the MSE loss and the specific integration of Gaussian noise via Stein’s lemma, but the fundamental principle remains unclear.

4. *Can we design algorithms that achieve the information‑theoretic sample complexity (∼ \(d^{k^*/2}\)) for learning higher‑order statistics in diffusion models, thus closing the gap with the ∼ \(d^{k^*-1}\) bound for projected SGD?*  
   Current theory for SGD yields thresholds \(\Theta(d^{k^*-1})\), while lower bounds suggest \(\Theta(d^{k^*/2})\) is possible; it is unknown whether a practical algorithm can attain this.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis is restricted to a single‑neuron denoiser and a fixed diffusion time; extension to deep U‑Nets across the full training schedule is unproven.
- Empirical validation is limited to CIFAR‑10 and CelebA; generalizability to other data domains remains untested.
- The necessity of projected SGD is proven only for specific activations; its impact with modern optimizers like Adam is unclear.
- The paper assumes Gaussian forward noise and a mixed cumulant data model; real‑world data may exhibit heavy tails or structure not captured by Hermite expansions.
- The beneficial effect of overparameterization is demonstrated empirically but lacks a theoretical explanation.

## Confidence
- **Ordered learning & diffusion information exponent**: High (theorem supported by experiments).  
- **Projected SGD criticality**: Medium (derivation solid, but real‑world optimizer impact unknown).  
- **Correlation acceleration**: Medium (proved for synthetic data; real‑world correlation uncertain).
