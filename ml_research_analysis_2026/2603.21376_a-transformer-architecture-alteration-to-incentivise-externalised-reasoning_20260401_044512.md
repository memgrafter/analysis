# A transformer architecture alteration to incentivise externalised reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21376  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21376  
- **Reference count:** 18  
- **Primary result:** Introduced an early‑exit transformer that, via self‑distillation SFT and RL with a compute penalty, improves accuracy on theory‑of‑mind tasks (47% → 55–60%) while reducing average per‑token compute (98% → ~95% of layers) with stable coherence.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents an early‑exit transformer architecture designed to encourage externalised reasoning by minimising per‑token internal computation. Stochastic early‑exit heads are inserted at intermediate layers. Training proceeds in two stages: first, self‑distillation supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) calibrates exit probabilities using the KL divergence between intermediate‑layer and final‑layer next‑token distributions; second, reinforcement learning with RLOO optimises a reward that combines task performance with a penalty on the average exit layer index. Experiments on Qwen3‑4B and DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill‑Qwen‑1.5B show that the method yields higher accuracy, lower compute, and adaptive depth allocation, while coherence remains near baseline. The approach aims to make model reasoning more transparent by forcing more tokens to be processed in the external context.

## Method Summary
The architecture augments a base transformer with small linear exit heads attached every N layers. During self‑distillation SFT, target exit probabilities are derived from the KL divergence between each intermediate layer’s next‑token distribution and the final layer’s (transformed via a temperature‑modulated sigmoid). The model (LoRA adapters and exit heads) is trained to match these targets while also matching the final layer’s token distribution (via KL). In the second stage, RL fine‑tuning (RLOO) optimises a composite reward: task reward minus λ × (average exit layer index) minus β × KL regulariser to the base model. This incentivises earlier exits without sacrificing accuracy. At inference, a stochastic exit decision is sampled at each exitable layer; if exit occurs, the residual stream is passed directly to the unembedding layer, skipping remaining layers.

## Key Results
- On Qwen3‑4B (theory of mind), RL improved accuracy from 47% to 55–60% while average compute per token decreased from 98% to ~95% of total layers (some runs to 90%).
- Coherence remained stable but slightly below the unmodified baseline.
- On DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill‑Qwen‑1.5B, calibration successfully matched the target exit distribution, and models learned to vary computational depth adaptively, using fewer layers for predictable tokens.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: KL‑based exit calibration
**Claim:** Stochastic early‑exit heads, calibrated via self‑distillation using KL divergence between intermediate and final layer predictions, learn to identify tokens where shallow computation suffices to match full‑depth output.  
**Mechanism:** For each intermediate layer, the KL divergence between its next‑token distribution and the final layer’s is transformed via a temperature‑modulated sigmoid to produce a target exit probability. The model is trained to match these targets (cross‑entropy on exit decisions) while also matching the final layer’s token distribution (KL).  
**Core assumption:** Low KL divergence between an intermediate layer’s predictions and the final layer’s is a reliable heuristic that the token could be predicted accurately without deeper computation.  
**Evidence anchors:** The paper’s Section 2 describes the KL‑based target construction (eqs. 3‑4) and loss function (eq. 5); the abstract mentions self‑distillation SFT calibrates exit probabilities using KL divergence.  
**Break condition:** If tokens requiring nuanced reasoning coincidentally produce low KL divergence (e.g., due to superficial pattern matching), the model may exit prematurely, corrupting output coherence.

### Mechanism 2: RL compute penalty
**Claim:** Reinforcement learning (RLOO) with an explicit reward penalty on average exit layer index successfully incentivises the policy to produce earlier exits while maintaining task performance.  
**Mechanism:** The total reward combines a task reward (e.g., accuracy) with a penalty proportional to the average exit layer index across generated tokens (eq. 1). A KL regulariser (β) prevents output distribution drift from the base model.  
**Core assumption:** The reward signal disentangles task performance from compute usage, allowing the policy to find early‑exit solutions for many tokens without compromising harder ones.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states RL includes a reward term penalising average exit layer index; Figure 2 shows training dynamics where average compute decreases while accuracy improves.  
**Break condition:** If λ is too high, accuracy may be sacrificed; if the policy cannot find early‑exit pathways for certain contexts, it may converge to a suboptimal trade‑off.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive computation depth
**Claim:** The architecture enables token‑level adaptive computation depth, reserving full compute only for tokens where earlier layers’ predictions diverge significantly from the final layer.  
**Mechanism:** Exit decisions are sampled stochastically per token based on learned logits. The model learns to adjust computational depth dynamically; tokens with high predictability achieve high exit probability at shallow layers, while unpredictable or complex tokens trigger deeper passes.  
**Core assumption:** Token predictability (approximated by layer‑wise predictive convergence) is a robust signal for allocating computational depth.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes models learn to adaptively vary computational depth; token‑level visualisation (Section 3) shows simpler tokens exiting earlier.  
**Break condition:** For tokens that are locally predictable but globally crucial, early exit could degrade long‑term coherence; the myopic next‑token objective may not align with global sequence quality.

## Foundational Learning

### Self‑distillation (Knowledge Distillation)
**Why needed:** To provide a training signal for early‑exit probabilities without requiring ground‑truth “correct” exit layers. The model uses its own full‑depth output as a teacher to define when an intermediate layer’s prediction is “good enough.”  
**Quick check:** How does using the final layer’s distribution as a target avoid requiring human‑labeled exit decisions?

### Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) as an externalised reasoning trace
**Why needed:** The core safety premise is that externalising reasoning steps makes model cognition more monitorable. The architecture aims to force more tokens into the CoT by limiting per‑token internal computation.  
**Quick check:** Why would reducing internal computation per token necessarily increase the length of the externalised CoT?

### Reinforcement Learning from Custom Reward Shaping (RLOO)
**Why needed:** Standard next‑token prediction loss does not encourage earlier exits; it only cares about output correctness. RL optimises a composite objective (accuracy + compute penalty) that cannot be expressed in a standard MLE fine‑tuning loss.  
**Quick check:** What is the role of the KL regulariser (β) in preventing the RL‑updated policy from deviating catastrophically from the base model’s language distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Base Transformer (frozen) → LoRA Adapters (trained) → Early‑Exit Heads (trained) → Exit Sampler (per‑token stochastic decision) → (if exit) skip remaining layers → Unembedding Layer. RL Reward Calculator interacts during training to compute task reward and compute penalty.

### Critical path
1. Run base model on training data to record per‑layer KL divergences to final layer.  
2. Compute target exit distributions from KL divergences using the temperature‑modulated sigmoid.  
3. Train LoRA adapters + exit heads with SFT loss (token prediction KL + exit cross‑entropy).  
4. Initialise RL policy from the SFT checkpoint.  
5. Run RL with RLOO: collect samples, compute rewards, update policy to maximise R_total.

### Design tradeoffs
- **λ (exit penalty weight):** Higher λ forces more externalisation but risks accuracy/coherence loss; requires task‑specific tuning.  
- **KL factor (SFT):** Controls strictness of exit calibration; too low causes incoherent early exits, too high yields minimal compute savings.  
- **Exit interval (every N layers):** Finer intervals allow more adaptive exits but increase parameter overhead and calibration complexity.  
- **Stochastic vs. deterministic exits:** Stochasticity aids exploration during RL but introduces variance; deterministic inference (thresholding) may be more predictable.

### Failure signatures
- **Post‑SFT:** Exit distribution diverges from KL‑derived targets; coherence scores drop sharply (e.g., KL factor 0.25).  
- **During RL:** Task accuracy declines monotonically with compute reduction; high variance across runs indicates unstable training.  
- **Inference:** Tokens that require deep computation (e.g., logical deductions) consistently exit early, leading to factual errors or incoherent sequences.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Calibration verification:** On a held set, compare learned exit distribution against KL‑derived targets; goal: high correlation.  
2. **SFT coherence sweep:** Vary KL factor, evaluate coherence/completeness on a small sample; goal: select factor that minimises quality drop while achieving >5% average layer savings.  
3. **RL ablation:** Train with λ=0 vs. λ>0; goal: confirm that compute reduction is driven by the reward term, not just SFT distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Does the early‑exit mechanism generalise to domains and tasks beyond GSM8K and theory‑of‑mind, and would it maintain effectiveness on larger, more capable models?**  
   *Basis:* “Extension to different domains… we still need to run cross‑domain validation… validating our method with more complex datasets on larger models will be beneficial in future work.”  
   *Why unresolved:* Only evaluated on two small models (1.5B, 4B) and two narrow task distributions.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Systematic evaluation across a suite of benchmarks (math, coding, QA) on models of varying scales (e.g., 8B, 70B, 405B), measuring accuracy, compute reduction, and output quality degradation relative to baseline.

2. **Does externalising computation via early exits actually improve the faithfulness or monitorability of chain‑of‑thought, or does it simply shift obfuscation to other forms?**  
   *Basis:* “We stop short of verifying the intended effects on CoT monitorability… We leave the evaluation of faithfulness for future work.”  
   *Why unresolved:* Only accuracy and coherence measured; faithfulness and monitorability not evaluated.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Human or automated evaluation of faithfulness (e.g., does the externalised trace predict internal decisions?) and monitorability (can a weaker model detect misaligned reasoning?) comparing early‑exit models to standard CoT models on adversarial or deceptive tasks.

3. **Is KL divergence between intermediate and final layer logits a reliable proxy for “token predictiveness” or ease‑of‑prediction, and does the self‑distillation target inadvertently encode model‑specific biases?**  
   *Basis:* The calibration stage uses D_KL(p_layer || p_final) to define target exit probabilities (Appendix A, eq 3‑4), assuming low KL implies the token could be predicted early.  
   *Why unresolved:* No validation that low KL corresponds to human notions of “easy” tokens or that this heuristic is task‑agnostic; the distribution might be shaped by model‑specific quirks.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Ablate the calibration target: compare KL‑based exits to alternatives (entropy, margin, or human‑annotated difficulty) and measure correlation with external metrics of token difficulty (e.g., surprisal, agreement across model variants).

4. **Do stochastic early exits accumulate errors or destabilise generation over long sequences, and how does the RL reward structure affect the learned exit policy’s robustness?**  
   *Basis:* The model samples exit decisions stochastically per token; coherence remained “slightly below baseline” and showed “instability in later training steps.”  
   *Why unresolved:* No per‑sequence failure analysis; stochastic exits might cause cascading errors if an early exit leads to a wrong token that later layers cannot correct.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Error analysis by exit depth: track token error rates after early vs. late exits, and measure sequence‑level degradation (coherence breakdowns, self‑consistency across multiple samples). Ablate λ penalty to see if higher eagerness increases catastrophic failures.

5. **Does the two‑stage training (self‑distillation SFT then RL) introduce any “alignment tax” or unintended capabilities shift beyond the intended externalisation, such as reduced instruction‑following or increased hallucination?**  
   *Basis:* “Coherence remained stable but slightly below the unmodified baseline… care is needed to ensure overall model performance does not drop.” KL regulariser used to “prevent the model from drifting too far from its original behaviour.”  
   *Why unresolved:* Focus on within‑task accuracy and a narrow coherence measure; broader capabilities (factuality, safety compliance, multilingual ability) not evaluated.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Comprehensive benchmarking across diverse capability suites (e.g., MMLU, TruthfulQA, BBH) before and after early‑exit training, with controlled variation of λ and β to map the capability frontier trade‑offs.

## Limitations
- The core assumption that low intermediate‑to‑final layer KL divergence reliably signals “predictable” tokens remains unvalidated beyond the paper’s own training distribution.
- RL reward shaping shows high variance and instability in training; the claimed preservation of task performance is only tested on theory‑of‑mind tasks with small models.
- Coherence degradation is observed but not analysed; generalisation to larger models and complex reasoning tasks is uncertain.

## Confidence
- KL‑based exit calibration: Medium  
- RL compute penalty: Medium  
- Adaptive depth (per‑token exit decisions): Low  

## Next Checks
1. **Task Generalisation:** Replicate on a held‑out, complex reasoning benchmark (e.g., MATH, GPQA). Does the accuracy gain (47%→60%) hold, or does the KL heuristic fail on problems requiring multi‑step deduction?  
2. **Failure Mode Analysis:** For sequences where early exit leads to factual error or incoherence, manually inspect the KL divergence at exit layers. Does the metric fail for tokens that are locally predictable but globally crucial?  
3. **Robustness Sweep:** Systematically vary the RL penalty weight (λ) and SFT KL factor. Map the Pareto frontier of compute savings vs. accuracy/coherence loss to characterise sensitivity and identify stable operating points.
