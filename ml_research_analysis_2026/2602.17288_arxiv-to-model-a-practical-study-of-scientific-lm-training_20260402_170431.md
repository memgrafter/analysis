# ArXiv-to-Model: A Practical Study of Scientific LM Training
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17288
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17288
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Preprocessing and dataset scale critically impact training stability, with a 200GB corpus yielding stable convergence at 52.18B tokens and ~4.2 validation perplexity.

## Executive Summary
This study documents an end-to-end pipeline for training scientific language models from raw arXiv LaTeX sources. Through 24 experimental runs, it demonstrates that preprocessing decisions determine usable token volume and symbolic stability, while storage I/O can bottleneck training on moderate hardware. A 1.36B-parameter model trained on 52.18B tokens from a 200GB cleaned corpus achieved stable convergence (~4.2 validation perplexity), contrasting with unstable optimization on a smaller 20GB subset. The work emphasizes practical engineering challenges in scientific LM pretraining.

## Method Summary
The pipeline comprises: (1) metadata filtering and archive validation; (2) LaTeX extraction and cleaning to reconstruct full documents; (3) dataset assembly with deduplication and sampling; (4) tokenization using a LLaMA-compatible SentencePiece model; (5) dense decoder-only transformer training (1.36B params) on 2×A100 GPUs. Key innovations include domain-aware cleaning to preserve mathematical symbols and a curriculum that gradually introduces formal content. Storage optimization and I/O management were critical for sustained GPU utilization.

## Key Results
- Preprocessing critically impacts usable token volume and symbolic stability, directly affecting optimization stability.
- Storage/I/O throughput can become the primary bottleneck, rivaling GPU compute on constrained infrastructure.
- Stable convergence achieved with 52.18B pretraining tokens from a 200GB corpus, yielding final validation perplexity ~4.2.
- Comparison between 20GB (unstable loss) and 200GB (smooth loss) regimes demonstrates the decisive impact of dataset scale.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mechanism 1: Preprocessing determines token volume and quality. Heterogeneous LaTeX archives require robust extraction; data loss from filtering or extraction failures reduces effective dataset size below the threshold for stable optimization. Evidence: 24-run analysis showing extraction as primary data loss source; contrast between 20GB and 200GB runs.

Mechanism 2: Tokenization design affects symbolic stability. General tokenizers fragment mathematical symbols, inflating sequence length and disrupting formal pattern learning. Domain-aware tokenization preserves symbolic units, improving compression and stability. Evidence: explicit design objectives to preserve math; reported token count from 200GB corpus implying efficient compression.

Mechanism 3: Storage I/O can bottleneck training. High-bandwidth streaming of large compressed archives may throttle GPU utilization if disk throughput is insufficient. Evidence: limitations section citing early I/O bottlenecks; final run achieving >95% utilization after optimizations.

## Foundational Learning
- **LaTeX Project Structure & Extraction:** Understanding multi-file projects with \input/\include is essential to reconstruct full documents from arXiv tarballs. Quick check: How do you handle a missing file referenced by \input?
- **Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) & SentencePiece:** Tokenizer vocabulary and merge rules determine symbol fragmentation; for scientific text, fragmentation hampers learning of formal structures. Quick check: Why might a web-trained tokenizer produce multiple tokens for \frac{\partial x}{\partial t}?
- **Chinchilla Scaling Laws:** The T≈20×P guideline informs the data-versus-model size tradeoff; this study deviates to prioritize domain stability. Quick check: For a 1B model with fixed GPU-hours, should you train on 20B or 100B tokens per Chinchilla?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Ingestion & Filtering → LaTeX Processing & Cleaning → Dataset Assembly & Sampling → Tokenizer → Model → Training Loop → Monitoring & Evaluation.

Critical path: Raw arXiv tarball → Cleaned, tokenized, shuffled JSONL stream → Stable loss curve. Highest risks: extraction/cleaning data loss and initial training instability with new data/tokenizer.

Design tradeoffs:
- Tokenizer: Chose LLaMA-compatible general tokenizer for stability over domain-trained for better compression.
- Architecture: Dense transformer vs. MoE; chose dense for simpler scaling on 2 GPUs.
- Curriculum: Staged exposure (text→symbols) vs. mixed; chose staged to stabilize early optimization.
- Data scale: Trained to 52B tokens (beyond Chinchilla’s 27B) to prioritize robustness.

Failure signatures:
- Preprocessing: Sudden token drop after filtering; high archive validation failure rate; many zero-token documents.
- Tokenization: Average sequence length >1.5x expected; common math operators fragmented into ≥2 sub-tokens.
- Training (early): Loss spikes/plateaus in first 1% steps; gradient norm explosion (>10) or vanishing (<1e-6); train/eval loss divergence.
- Training (hardware): GPU utilization <70%; high I/O wait; sustained disk queue length.
- Tokenizer mismatch: Embedding_dim error; nonsensical early generations; perplexity >1000 on simple math text.

First 3 experiments:
1. Smoke Test on 1GB Subset: Run full mini-pipeline on 100 tarballs for 1k steps. Verify no crashes, reasonable token density (~0.5-0.8 tokens/char), and initial loss decrease.
2. Tokenizer Sanity Check: Tokenize 100 raw LaTeX docs. Compute avg tokens/doc and fragmentation rate for 50 common operators. Compare to web text baseline.
3. Stability Probe on 20GB: Train final config on 20GB subset for 5k steps. Monitor loss curve for monotonic decrease vs. oscillation.

## Limitations
- Quantitative link between tokenization fragmentation and training stability not directly measured.
- I/O bottleneck specifics (throughput thresholds, hardware) not profiled in detail.
- No evaluation of downstream task performance or scalability to larger models (e.g., 7B+) and longer training (100B+ tokens).

## Confidence
- Preprocessing volume/quality determines stability: High.
- Tokenizer design impacts symbolic stability: Medium.
- I/O throughput as primary bottleneck: Medium.
- Deviation from Chinchilla optimality for stability: Medium.
