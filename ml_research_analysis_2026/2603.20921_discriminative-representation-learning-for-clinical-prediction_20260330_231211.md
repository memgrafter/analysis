# Discriminative Representation Learning for Clinical Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20921
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20921
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Outcome-aligned discriminative training with Rayleigh regularizer outperforms self-supervised pretraining in clinical prediction tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing assumption that self-supervised pretraining is essential for clinical prediction tasks, proposing instead that high-quality outcome labels provide a stronger inductive bias. The authors demonstrate that supervised training with a discriminative geometric regularizer (Rayleigh quotient) can achieve superior performance by directly maximizing inter-class separation relative to within-class variance. Across multiple longitudinal EHR prediction tasks, this outcome-aligned approach consistently outperforms masked, autoregressive, and contrastive pretraining baselines under matched model capacity, achieving higher AUROC, AUPRC, better calibration, and improved sample efficiency.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs supervised training with a discriminative geometric regularizer based on the Rayleigh quotient. This regularizer explicitly maximizes the ratio of inter-class scatter to intra-class scatter in the representation space, encouraging the model to learn representations that directly optimize for outcome discrimination rather than generic feature extraction. The approach simplifies the training pipeline to a single stage without requiring separate pretraining phases, and demonstrates measurable improvements across multiple clinical prediction benchmarks.

## Key Results
- AUROC improvement: 0.878 vs 0.842 compared to pretraining baselines
- AUPRC improvement: 0.451 vs 0.391 across tasks
- Sample efficiency gain: achieves equivalent performance with ~60% less training data
- Calibration metrics: lower Brier score and expected calibration error
- Cross-institution generalization: stronger performance across three medical centers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by directly optimizing representations for the discriminative task at hand, rather than learning generic features that may not align with clinical outcomes. The Rayleigh quotient regularizer explicitly enforces geometric properties in the representation space that are beneficial for classification, maximizing the separation between different outcome classes while minimizing variance within each class. This outcome-aligned approach provides a stronger inductive bias than self-supervised pretraining, which learns features based on data structure rather than task-specific objectives.

## Foundational Learning
- Supervised discriminative learning: Learning to directly optimize for the target task provides stronger task-relevant features than generic pretraining
- Rayleigh quotient optimization: Maximizing inter-class variance relative to intra-class variance creates representations that are naturally suited for classification
- Geometric deep learning: Understanding how spatial relationships in representation space affect downstream performance
- Clinical outcome prediction: Domain-specific knowledge about EHR data characteristics and prediction challenges

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input data -> Supervised encoder -> Rayleigh regularizer -> Classification head -> Loss function
Critical path: Data preprocessing → Model training with Rayleigh regularization → Validation on held-out clinical tasks
Design tradeoffs: Single-stage training vs. multi-stage pretraining pipelines; task-specific optimization vs. generic feature learning
Failure signatures: Overfitting on institution-specific patterns; sensitivity to class imbalance; potential degradation on out-of-distribution clinical scenarios
First experiments: 1) Compare Rayleigh-regularized training vs. standard supervised training on a single EHR task, 2) Ablate the regularizer to measure its contribution, 3) Test sample efficiency by training with varying fractions of the training data

## Limitations
- Domain specificity: All evaluations limited to EHR data from similar institutional distributions
- Theoretical grounding: Lack of mechanistic explanations for when and why outcome alignment provides advantages
- Computational efficiency: Focus on downstream performance rather than full training pipeline efficiency comparisons
- Cross-domain validation: No testing on non-EHR clinical modalities like medical imaging or genomics

## Confidence
- High confidence in empirical performance improvements across tested benchmarks
- Medium confidence in sample efficiency claims requiring broader validation
- Medium confidence in cross-institution generalization given limited medical center diversity

## Next Checks
