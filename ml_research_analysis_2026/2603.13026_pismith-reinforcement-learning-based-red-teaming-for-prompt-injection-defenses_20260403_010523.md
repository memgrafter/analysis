# PISmith: Reinforcement Learning-based Red Teaming for Prompt Injection Defenses

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13026
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13026
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** PISmith achieves near-perfect attack success rates (1.0 ASR@10, 0.87 ASR@1) against state-of-the-art prompt injection defenses, significantly outperforming existing baselines.

## Executive Summary
Prompt injection attacks threaten the security of LLM applications, but current defenses are often assessed against non-adaptive attacks, creating a false sense of security. PISmith is a reinforcement learning-based red-teaming framework that trains an attack LLM to optimize injected prompts in a black-box setting. It addresses extreme reward sparsity inherent in such tasks through adaptive entropy regularization (to sustain exploration) and dynamic advantage weighting (to amplify gradients from successful rollouts). Evaluated on 13 benchmarks covering QA, RAG, and long-context tasks, PISmith achieves 1.0 ASR@10 and 0.87 ASR@1 against Meta-SecAlign-8B, substantially outperforming seven baselines including RL-Hammer and search-based methods. In agentic settings, it reaches perfect success rates against GPT-4o-mini and near-perfect against GPT-5-nano. However, the study reveals a trade-off: no current state-of-the-art defense simultaneously maintains high task utility and robust resistance to such adaptive attacks.

## Method Summary
PISmith frames prompt injection red-teaming as an on-policy reinforcement learning problem. The attack policy (based on Qwen3-4B-Instruct) generates candidate injected prompts conditioned on the user task, context, and injection goal. For each batch, the policy samples multiple rollouts; each contaminated context (original context concatenated with injected prompt) is sent to the defended target LLM (black-box). A judge LLM (GPT-4o-mini) produces a binary reward (1 for success, 0 otherwise). Instead of standard GRPO, which suffers from vanishing gradients under extreme sparsity, PISmith augments the loss with two mechanisms: adaptive entropy regularization, which maintains policy entropy by dynamically scaling an entropy bonus based on the mean reward; and dynamic advantage weighting, which multiplies the advantage of successful rollouts by a factor that decays as successes become common. Both mechanisms share a threshold τ and are co-adapted. Training uses 100 samples from Dolly Closed QA, group size 15, learning rate 1e-5, for 10 epochs, totaling ~15,000 target queries. At test time, 10 candidate prompts are generated per instance and ASR@10/ASR@1 are computed.

## Key Results
- PISmith achieves 1.0 ASR@10 and 0.87 ASR@1 against Meta-SecAlign-8B, outperforming RL-Hammer (0.70/0.48) and search-based methods.
- In agentic environments, it reaches 1.0 ASR@10 against GPT-4o-mini and 0.95 ASR@10 against GPT-5-nano.
- Ablation studies confirm that both adaptive entropy and dynamic advantage are essential; removing either reduces ASR@1 to ≤0.57.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Adaptive Entropy Regularization**: Sustains exploration under extreme reward sparsity by dynamically scaling an entropy bonus based on current mean reward. When mean reward is low, a high entropy coefficient prevents entropy collapse; as reward increases, the coefficient decays to allow exploitation. Evidence: Figure 1b shows sustained entropy vs. collapse in standard GRPO; ablation in Figure 3 shows performance drop without it.
- **Dynamic Advantage Weighting**: Amplifies gradients from successful rollouts proportionally to their rarity. When mean reward is below a threshold, advantages of successful rollouts are scaled up by a factor >1, ensuring that scarce success signals drive updates. Evidence: Ablation in Figure 3 shows ASR@1 drops from 0.87 to 0.57 without boosting.
- **Co-adaptation via Mean Reward**: Both mechanisms are controlled by the same mean reward signal, creating a coupled system: high entropy produces diverse rollouts that eventually yield successes; weighting then amplifies those successes to rapidly improve the policy. As successes become common, both mechanisms gracefully decay to standard GRPO. Evidence: Ablation removing either component leads to failure, demonstrating interdependence.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Reward Sparsity in On-Policy RL**
- *Why needed:* PISmith faces extreme reward sparsity because strong defenses yield almost no initial successes; standard GRPO fails because policy gradients vanish when advantages are near zero and entropy collapses.
- *Quick check question:* Why can’t we simply increase the entropy bonus coefficient β to a fixed high value throughout training?

**Concept: KL Divergence vs. Pure Entropy Regularization in Red-Teaming**
- *Why needed:* Standard GRPO includes a KL divergence term that anchors the attack policy to a harmless reference policy, hindering discovery of effective attacks. PISmith replaces it with pure entropy regularization to allow more aggressive exploration without such anchoring.
- *Quick check question:* How does decoupling −H(π) from H(π,πref) fundamentally change the optimization objective for an attacker?

**Concept: Black-Box Attack Assumptions**
- *Why needed:* PISmith assumes only query access to the defended LLM (no logits/gradients), so it must rely on binary success signals and on-policy sampling; this constrains advantage computation and policy updates.
- *Quick check question:* What would change if the attacker had white-box access to the target LLM’s gradients?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Attack Policy (πϕ) → Target LLM (defended) → Responses → Reward Model (GPT-4o-mini) → GRPO Trainer (computes advantages, applies adaptive entropy & dynamic weighting) → Policy gradient update → Attack Policy.

**Critical path:**
1. For each training batch (user instruction, context, injection goal), sample K=15 injected prompts from the current attack policy.
2. Construct contaminated contexts by concatenating each injected prompt with the original context.
3. Query the target defended LLM with each contaminated context to obtain responses.
4. Use the reward model (GPT-4o-mini) to assign a binary reward (1/0) to each response.
5. Compute the mean reward ¯r and standard deviation σ_r across the K rollouts.
6. Calculate base advantages A_i = (r_i - ¯r) / (σ_r + ϵ) for each rollout.
7. Determine adaptive parameters: entropy coefficient β(¯r) and advantage scaling γ(¯r) based on mean reward using predefined thresholds and decay schedules.
8. Apply dynamic weighting: for successful rollouts (r_i=1), multiply A_i by γ(¯r); failed rollouts keep original advantages.
9. Compute the GRPO loss with an entropy bonus (−β(¯r)·H(π)) applied only if entropy H(π) is below a cap Hcap, and perform a clipped policy gradient update.
10. Repeat for N epochs; monitor ¯r and entropy to ensure proper exploration-exploitation transition.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- No dual-target training: PISmith trains directly on the defended target, reducing training cost by 2× compared to methods that also train on an undefended model, but this requires robust adaptive mechanisms to handle initial sparsity.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: The adaptive mechanisms rely on several hyperparameters (β_max, γ_max, τ, H_cap) that must be tuned; the paper uses τ=0.5, β_max=0.01, γ_max=5.0, H_cap=0.5 based on validation performance.
- Single success amplification: The dynamic weighting amplifies all successes equally when mean reward is low, which may overemphasize a single lucky pattern early in training and reduce generalizability.

**Failure signatures:**
- Entropy collapse (H(π) → 0) despite adaptive bonus: β(¯r) too low or H_cap too restrictive. Increase β_max or relax H_cap.
- Mean reward ¯r stagnates near 0 for many steps: γ(¯r) may be too low or τ mis-set. Increase γ_max or lower τ.
- Policy degenerates into gibberish: entropy bonus too strong or cap too high. Reduce β_base or lower H_cap.
- High variance in ASR across benchmarks: training data too narrow; increase diversity of seed data.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Sanity check: Train on a known-vulnerable defense (e.g., no defense) for 1 epoch; verify that mean reward rises quickly, entropy remains above cap, and γ(¯r) decays as ¯r exceeds τ.
2. Ablation validation: Disable adaptive entropy (fix β=β_base) and/or dynamic weighting (fix γ=1); expect ASR@1 to drop to ~0.09 and 0.57 respectively on Meta-SecAlign-8B.
3. Transfer test: After training on Dolly Closed QA, evaluate on a held-out long-context benchmark (e.g., HotpotQA-Long); expect high ASR@10 (>0.95) if generalization works, else investigate prompt template or training data diversity.

## Limitations
- The exact GRPO implementation (advantage normalization, clipping, reference policy) is unspecified, which may cause reproduction variance.
- Agent evaluation splits depend on RL-Hammer's unpublished split, limiting replicability.
- Training details (gradient accumulation, scheduler, on-policy adherence) are omitted, potentially affecting convergence.
- Limited diversity of seed data (only 100 Dolly samples) may hinder generalization to new task distributions.

## Confidence
- **High:** Adaptive entropy and dynamic advantage mechanisms are strongly supported by ablations and theoretical justification; ASR gains over 7 baselines are large and consistent.
- **Medium:** Co-adaptation hypothesis is plausible but relies on a single shared signal (mean reward); independent verification needed.
- **Low:** The claim that no state-of-the-art defense simultaneously maintains high utility and resists PISmith holds only for currently evaluated defenses; future work may produce robust ones.
