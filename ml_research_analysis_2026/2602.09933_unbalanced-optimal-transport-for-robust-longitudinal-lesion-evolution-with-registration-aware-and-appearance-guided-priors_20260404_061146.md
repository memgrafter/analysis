# Unbalanced optimal transport for robust longitudinal lesion evolution with registration-aware and appearance-guided priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09933
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09933
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** UOT-based matching significantly outperforms distance-only bipartite baselines on lung CT, achieving higher edge-detection F1 (0.859 vs. 0.825), improved lesion-state weighted recall (0.886 vs. 0.860), and superior lesion-graph component F1 (0.842 vs. 0.802).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of matching lesions across longitudinal CT scans when lesions appear, disappear, merge, or split. The proposed method uses unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) to model mass imbalance naturally, avoiding forced one-to-one assignments. It integrates a composite cost that combines normalized geometry, registration trust from Jacobian, and optional appearance consistency. A tumor-load-aware prior adjusts marginal penalties based on global volume change. After solving the UOT problem, the transport plan is sparsified to directly yield a graph with event labels. On a lung cancer cohort, the method improves correspondence accuracy and topology consistency compared to standard bipartite matching.

## Method Summary
The framework treats lesion correspondence as a mass transport problem where total mass need not be conserved. Lesion instances from two timepoints are represented by masses (volumes) and 3D centroids. A cost matrix is constructed from a size-normalized geometric distance, modulated by registration trust (Jacobian-derived) and appearance similarity (ZNCC). Unbalanced OT relaxes marginal constraints via KL penalties (λ, µ), allowing mass creation and deletion. A tumor-load-aware prior sets λ and µ asymmetrically based on global volume change ratio ρ: if growth is observed, µ is reduced to favor appearances; if shrinkage, λ is reduced. The entropically regularized UOT is solved via Sinkhorn iterations. The resulting soft plan Γ is pruned using relative row/column thresholds to produce a sparse bipartite graph, from which lesion states (persistent, new, disappearing, merging, splitting) are inferred from node degrees.

## Key Results
- Edge-detection F1 improved to 0.859 from 0.825 (distance-only bipartite).
- Lesion-state weighted recall reached 0.886 vs. 0.860.
- Lesion-graph component F1 was 0.842 vs. 0.802.
- The UOT approach natively models many-to-one and one-to-many flows, enhancing merge/split detection and graph topology consistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) enables handling of unequal lesion counts and mass variation across timepoints, naturally modeling appearance, disappearance, merging, and splitting.
- **Mechanism:** UOT relaxes the mass conservation constraints of balanced OT by adding KL divergence penalties (λ, µ) on the marginals. This allows some mass from source lesions (T0) to be "lost" (disappearances) and some mass to be "gained" at target (T1) for new lesions, without forcing a one-to-one assignment.
- **Core assumption:** Lesion correspondence can be framed as a mass transport problem where total mass need not be preserved; sparse flows in the transport plan Γ represent matches, and mass imbalance represents creation/deletion.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) that accommodates unequal lesion mass"; [section 2.2] Minimization objective with KL terms: ⟨Γ, C⟩+λKL(Γ1∥a) +µKL(Γ⊤1∥b)−ε H(Γ).
- **Break condition:** If λ and µ are set too high, the solution reverts to near-balanced transport, losing ability to handle mass changes. If too low, the plan becomes overly sparse with many false appearances/disappearances.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A composite cost function blending normalized geometry, registration trust (Jacobian), and appearance consistency (ZNCC) guides the transport plan toward semantically meaningful matches in the presence of registration errors and intensity variations.
- **Mechanism:** Each pairwise cost C_ij is the geometric distance scaled up by factors when registration is untrustworthy (low Jacobian confidence) or appearance is inconsistent (low ZNCC). This penalizes matches in unreliable regions, making low-cost assignments more likely to be true correspondences.
- **Core assumption:** Local image reliability cues can be quantified and used to modulate a base geometric distance; reliable regions should have lower matching cost.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "transport cost blends (i) size-normalized geometry, (ii) local registration trust from the deformation-field Jacobian, and (iii) optional patch-level appearance consistency"; [section 2.3] Cost formulation: Cij = c_geom_ij (1+w_J[1−τ_ij]) (1−w_S¯s_ij).
- **Break condition:** If registration error is spatially systematic and not captured by Jacobian (e.g., global bias), τ_ij may be misleading. If w_S is too high, appearance variations (e.g., contrast change) could break true matches.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A tumor-load-aware asymmetry prior adjusts the marginal relaxation parameters (λ, µ) based on global volume change ρ, making the model more sensitive to expected direction of mass change (new lesions if growth, disappearances if shrinkage).
- **Mechanism:** Compute ρ = (sum follow-up volumes)/(sum baseline volumes). If ρ>1 (growth), reduce µ to lower penalty for new mass in T1, making "appearance" cheaper. If ρ<1 (shrinkage), reduce λ to ease "disappearance". This biases the UOT solution toward the clinically plausible flow direction.
- **Core assumption:** The net change in total lesion volume across the patient is an informative prior for the likelihood of new vs. disappearing lesions; global tumor burden trends correlate with local event types.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "tumor-load-aware asymmetry prior adjusts marginal penalties based on global burden change"; [section 2.4] Explicit formulas: "If ρ >1... we lower µ"; "If ρ <1... we lower λ".
- **Break condition:** If ρ is measured from noisy/partial segmentations, the prior could be wrong (e.g., missed lesions cause apparent shrinkage). The linear adjustment may not capture nonlinear relationships between global and local changes.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input Preprocessing -> Registration Trust Module -> Appearance Module -> Cost Matrix Assembly -> UOT Solver -> Pruning & Graph Extraction -> Evaluation Module

Critical path: Lesion extraction -> Cost computation (using registration & appearance) -> UOT solve -> Pruning -> Graph labeling. Any failure in lesion extraction or cost mis-calibration propagates to the final graph.

Design tradeoffs:
- *Computational cost:* Sinkhorn iterations scale with |L0|x|L1|; for many lesions, use sparsification before solving or limit candidate pairs.
- *Parameter sensitivity:* λ/µ adjustment from ρ is linear; a more sophisticated model (e.g., learned) might help but loses interpretability.
- *Prior integration:* Adding appearance (ZNCC) and Jacobian trust adds robustness but introduces more hyperparameters (w_J, w_S) and requires reliable registration fields.

Failure signatures:
- Many false "new" or "disappearing" lesions: λ/µ poorly tuned, or cost matrix failing to connect true matches due to poor geometry normalization or over-penalization from trust/appearance.
- Missed merges/splits: Pruning thresholds too aggressive, or many-to-one flows in Γ not surviving relative pruning.
- All lesions labeled "persistent": λ and µ too high, or ρ-based adjustment not applied/overridden.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Ablation of Cost Components:** Run with (a) geometry-only, (b) +Jacobian, (c) +Appearance, (d) full. Expect edge F1 to increase with each addition, especially on cases with registration artifacts or intensity changes.
2. **Parameter Sensitivity for ρ-Adjustment:** Fix λ_base, µ_base and vary the strength of ρ-based scaling (e.g., multiply by factor α). Plot lesion-state recall vs. α. Expect optimal α around 1.0 (as used) and drop-off for α=0 (no adjustment) or α very high (over-correction).
3. **Pruning Threshold Sweep:** Vary τ_row and τ_col (e.g., 0.1 to 0.9). Monitor trade-off between edge F1 and number of predicted merge/split events. Too low thresholds yield many sparse edges (false splits), too high yields few edges and missed merges.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the UOT-based matching perform when integrated with automatic lesion detection pipelines, given that the current method relies on manual segmentations? The conclusion states: “Future work includes evaluating the method on automatically detected (rather than annotated) lesions…”. The method assumes accurate lesion masks; detections introduce false positives and missed lesions, which may break the transport assumptions.
- **Open Question 2:** On data with lower quality non-rigid registration, how robust is the method and does the Jacobian-based registration trust term become more valuable? The conclusion mentions: “on data with lower registration quality, where larger gains are expected.” The Jacobian term had only minor impact in the stable registration scenario; its potential benefit under registration uncertainty remains speculative.
- **Open Question 3:** Can more advanced appearance similarity metrics (e.g., learned deep feature distances) further improve the transport cost beyond ZNCC? The ablation shows that adding patch-level ZNCC consistency yields the best scores, but the authors used only ZNCC, implying richer appearance models may offer additional gains.
- **Open Question 4:** Across which anatomical sites or disease types does the proposed UOT framework maintain its advantage over distance-only bipartite matching? The method was evaluated exclusively on lung lesions in melanoma patients; lesion morphology and growth patterns vary by cancer type and anatomical location, so the transport cost design might need adjustments for other contexts.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (w_J, w_S, ε, τ_row, τ_col) and exact implementation details (patch size, Jacobian scaling) are not reported, hindering full reproducibility.
- Evaluation is limited to a single lung CT cohort; generalizability to other organs, modalities, or noisier segmentations is unknown.
- The tumor-load-aware prior uses a simple linear adjustment (ρ→λ,µ) that may fail for heterogeneous disease patterns or inaccurate volume estimates.

## Confidence
- *UOT mechanism for mass imbalance and event modeling:* High – theoretical basis is sound and core to the method.
- *Composite cost (geometry + Jacobian trust + ZNCC):* Medium – ablations show benefit, but exact contribution and sensitivity to weight choices are not fully quantified.
- *Tumor-load-aware asymmetric marginal penalty:* Low – lacks isolated ablation evidence and relies on a simplistic global prior.
- *Overall performance on lung CT:* Medium – strong empirical results on one dataset, but limited external validation and potential overfitting to dev-set hyperparameters.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate the tumor-load prior:** Fix λ and µ to baseline values and disable ρ-based scaling; compare edge F1 and state recall to the full method to isolate the prior’s contribution.
2. **External validation on a different lesion type:** Apply the pipeline to a longitudinal brain MRI multiple sclerosis dataset, using the same hyperparameters (or retuned) to assess transferability and identify modality-specific weaknesses.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary w_J, w_S, τ_row, τ_col, and ε on a held-out validation split; map performance surfaces and report robust parameter ranges, especially for the pruning thresholds.
