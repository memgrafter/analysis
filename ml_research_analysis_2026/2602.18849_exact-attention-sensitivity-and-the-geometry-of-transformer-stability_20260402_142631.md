# Exact Attention Sensitivity and the Geometry of Transformer Stability

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18849  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18849  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Transformer training brittleness stems from exact softmax sensitivity (∥J_softmax∥_{∞→1} = θ(p)/τ) and architectural gradient flow, not attention sharpening; pre-LN stabilizes via an additive identity path, while post-LN compounds LayerNorm Jacobians causing exponential gradient decay/spikes; DeepNorm’s N^{-1/4} scaling arises from quartic attention projections; θ(p)≈1 persists throughout training, necessitating warmup for sensitivity throttling.

## Executive Summary
This paper explains why transformer training can be brittle by developing a stability theory built on two pillars: an exact formula for softmax sensitivity and a geometric analysis aligned with token-wise computation. The exact softmax Jacobian norm ∥J_softmax∥_{∞→1} equals θ(p)/τ, where θ(p) measures the balanced-mass factor of the attention distribution. Combined with a block-∞/RMS norm geometry, the framework reveals that pre-LayerNorm (pre-LN) preserves an additive identity gradient path, ensuring stable gradients, whereas post-LayerNorm (post-LN) forces gradients through multiplicative LayerNorm Jacobians, leading to exponential decay or spikes. The theory predicts DeepNorm’s N^{-1/4} scaling from the four projection matrices in the attention pathway and explains why learning-rate warmup is needed: to throttle sensitivity during early high-drift phases since θ(p) remains near 1 (high sensitivity) throughout training. Experiments on 774M-parameter GPT-2 models confirm the exactness of the softmax identity (relative error <10⁻¹³), the stability gap between pre-LN and post-LN, and the persistence of θ(p)≈1.

## Method Summary
The authors trained 774M-parameter GPT-2-style transformers (d=1280, N=36 layers, H=20 heads, dh=64, dff=5120, sequence length 1024) on the FineWeb corpus, comparing pre-LN (Xℓ+1 = Xℓ + MHA(LN(Xℓ))) and post-LN (Xℓ+1 = LN(Xℓ + MHA(Xℓ))) architectures. Training used AdamW (β=0.9,0.95, weight decay 0.1), peak LR 3e-4, 500-step linear warmup, cosine decay to 0, batch size 64 (micro-batch 4, grad accum 16), gradient clipping norm 1.0, and bfloat16 for 5000 steps. Key metrics included training loss, layer-wise gradient norms, the balanced-mass factor θ(p) computed via greedy bisection on sampled attention distributions, and the product of projection operator norms Gℓ = ∥W_O∥₂∥W_Q∥₂∥W_K∥₂∥W_V∥₂ via power iteration. Theorem 4.2 (exact softmax sensitivity) was verified for small L (≤16) by exhaustive search and for L=1024 by greedy approximation.

## Key Results
- The exact identity ∥J_softmax(u/τ)∥_{∞→1} = θ(p)/τ holds with numerical relative error <10⁻¹³, confirming the theoretical derivation.
- Pre-LN models converge stably (final loss ~51.5) while post-LN models diverge or plateau at higher loss (~61.2) due to vanishing early-layer and spiking late-layer gradients.
- Across training, θ(p)/τ ≈ 1.0 persists for both architectures, indicating attention never sharpens enough to reduce sensitivity, contradicting a common stabilization hypothesis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Exact Softmax Sensitivity Governs Instability
The operator norm of the softmax Jacobian is exactly θ(p)/τ, where θ(p) ∈ [0,1] captures how evenly the attention distribution can be bisected. High θ(p) (near 1) means small logit perturbations redistribute probability mass significantly, making the system sensitive. This sensitivity propagates through the network via token-wise convex combinations, with the ℓ∞→ℓ₁ norm capturing the relevant worst-case effect. Evidence: Theorem 4.2 proves the exact equality; Section 7 experiments show θ(p)≈1 throughout training, so sensitivity remains high. Break condition: If θ(p)→0 (e.g., strictly one-hot attention), sensitivity vanishes, but this is not observed in practice.

### Mechanism 2: Pre-LN vs. Post-LN Gradient Flow Structure
Pre-LN includes an additive identity in the layer Jacobian (∂X^{ℓ+1}/∂X^ℓ = I + J_MHA), allowing gradients to bypass the multi-head attention (MHA) sublayer via the residual connection. Post-LN multiplies the LayerNorm Jacobian on the outside (∂X^{ℓ+1}/∂X^ℓ = J_LN(Y^ℓ)(I+J_MHA)), forcing all gradients through projection-like operators that annihilate constant directions. Over depth, this product causes exponential decay in early layers and gradient spikes in later layers. Evidence: Theorem 5.4/5.5 formally derive the Jacobian structures; Figure 1 empirically shows post-LN gradient norms vanishing in middle layers and spiking at output. Break condition: Increased depth without compensation (e.g., DeepNorm scaling) makes post-LN gradients decay to near-zero in early layers, preventing learning; pre-LN’s identity path remains robust.

### Mechanism 3: DeepNorm’s N^{-1/4} Scaling from Quartic Pathway
The attention pathway involves four multiplicative projection matrices (W^Q, W^K, W^V, W^O), contributing a quartic term β⁴ to the Lipschitz constant, where β is their typical operator norm. To prevent end-to-end sensitivity from growing with depth N, each layer’s contribution must be O(1/N), requiring β = O(N^{-1/4}). DeepNorm implements this via initialization scaling and residual multiplier α ≈ (2N)^{-1/4}. Evidence: Theorem 6.1 derives the N^{-1/4} exponent; Section 7 validates the prediction on 774M models. Break condition: If the number of multiplicative maps m changes (e.g., shared Q/K gives m=3), the required scaling is N^{-1/m}; using N^{-1/4} for m≠4 causes instability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Block-∞/RMS Norm**  
  *Why needed:* Aligns geometric analysis with token-wise transformer computation (LayerNorm acts per token, attention does row-stochastic mixing). This norm measures worst-case token RMS magnitude, yielding length-independent Lipschitz bounds, unlike Frobenius norm which introduces spurious √L factors.  
  *Quick check:* Why does Frobenius norm produce spurious √L factors while block-∞/RMS does not?
- **Concept: Balanced-mass factor θ(p)**  
  *Why needed:* Precisely quantifies softmax sensitivity beyond entropy; it measures the maximum bisectable mass p(S)(1−p(S)) over subsets S of logits. High θ(p) means the distribution is “bisectable” (e.g., uniform or structured top-k), leading to high sensitivity even if non-uniform.  
  *Quick check:* Can a non-uniform attention distribution still have θ(p)=1? Give an example.
- **Concept: Jacobian-based gradient flow**  
  *Why needed:* Stability is determined by the product of layer Jacobians (∂X_m/∂X_ℓ), not just per-layer Lipschitz bounds. The additive I term in pre-LN vs. multiplicative LayerNorm projectors in post-LN explains the stability gap.  
  *Quick check:* What structural feature in the pre-LN Jacobian allows gradients to bypass sublayer transformations?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input → LayerNorm → Q/K/V projections → attention scores → softmax (θ(p)/τ) → output projection (W^O) → residual add (pre-LN) or LayerNorm (post-LN) → FFN (similar structure) → next layer.
- **Critical path:**  
  During backpropagation, gradients flow from output through MHA Jacobian (value path + attention path). In pre-LN, the residual add before LayerNorm creates an identity branch letting gradients bypass MHA and LN Jacobians. In post-LN, LayerNorm appears multiplicatively on the outside, forcing all gradients through its Jacobian, which annihilates constant directions and compounds sensitivity.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  1. **Pre-LN vs. post-LN:** Pre-LN is inherently stable due to identity path; post-LN requires careful scaling (e.g., DeepNorm) or initialization to compensate for LayerNorm Jacobian product.
  2. **Projection scaling:** For m multiplicative maps in the sensitivity pathway, scale each by N^{-1/m}. Standard attention (m=4) → N^{-1/4}; shared W^Q=W^K (m=3) → N^{-1/3}; linear attention (m=2) → N^{-1/2}.
  3. **Temperature vs. LR warmup:** Temperature warmup (τ_high → τ_low) throttles attention sensitivity directly (1/τ factor) without affecting FFN gradients, potentially more targeted than LR warmup.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **Post-LN without scaling:** Gradient norms vanish in early layers (< layer 30) and spike in final layers; training loss plateaus higher (e.g., ~60 vs. 51.5).
  2. **Misapplying DeepNorm scaling:** Using N^{-1/4} when m≠4 (e.g., tied Q/K) leads to exploding (β too large) or vanishing (β too small) gradients.
  3. **Assuming θ(p) decay:** Expecting attention to sharpen (θ(p)→0) during training might lead to omitting warmup prematurely; θ(p)≈1 persists, so architectural compensation is always needed.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Exact softmax verification:** For L=8,16, compute ∥J_softmax∥_{∞→1} and θ(p)/τ on 500 random logits via exhaustive search; expect relative error <10⁻¹³.
  2. **Pre-LN vs. post-LN gradient norms:** Train two identical models differing only in LN placement; track layer-wise gradient norms—expect post-LN to show vanishing in middle layers and spike at output.
  3. **θ(p) dynamics:** Sample attention from all layers/heads every 500 steps during training; compute θ(p) via greedy bisection—expect θ(p)/τ ≈ 1.0 consistently, not decaying.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Do findings about persistent θ(p)≈1 and architecture-driven stability generalize to transformer variants with alternative normalization (e.g., RMSNorm) or sparse/MoE architectures?  
  *Basis:* Paper focuses on LayerNorm and dense transformers; limitations section states extensions to RMSNorm and MoE are open. Core theory relies on LayerNorm properties and dense projections; RMSNorm changes Jacobian properties, MoE adds routing paths.  
  *Why unresolved:* RMSNorm alters LayerNorm Jacobian; MoE introduces new multiplicative maps. The path-length exponent principle may need revision.
- **Question 2:** Is the “path-length exponent principle” (scale each multiplicative map by N⁻¹/ᵐ) necessary and sufficient for stabilizing any deep architecture with an m-ic sensitivity pathway, or are there hidden dependencies on initialization, optimizer, or data?  
  *Basis:* Principle derived from Theorem 6.2, validated for m=4 (DeepNorm). Paper makes testable predictions for m=3 (shared Q/K) and m=2 (linear attention) but does not test them.  
  *Why unresolved:* Derivation assumes comparable operator norms β and a specific depth-compounding bound. Unknown if non-uniform β, different nonlinearities, or AdamW interactions require adjustments. Sufficiency for arbitrary m not proven.
- **Question 3:** What is the precise mechanistic role of “temperature warmup” (τ high → τ low) vs. learning rate warmup? Is it independently necessary, or redundant once projection norms and magnitudes are controlled?  
  *Basis:* Paper proposes temperature warmup as direct throttling of 1/τ factor (Section 6.3), contrasting with θ(p)≈1 persistence. Hypothesis stated but not tested; experiments use fixed τ=1.  
  *Why unresolved:* Interaction with projection norm growth (Figure 2) not isolated. Unknown if τ warmup alone can substitute for LR warmup.
- **Question 4:** Why do trained transformers maintain θ(p)≈1 across layers and training time? Is this universal across scales, datasets, and training paradigms (e.g., instruction tuning, vision transformers)?  
  *Basis:* Key empirical finding (Section 7) on 774M GPT-2 on FineWeb; authors note it rules out attention sharpening but provide no theoretical reason for persistence.  
  *Why unresolved:* No explanation whether high θ(p) is functionally necessary or a side effect. Without broader experiments across model scales, modalities, and objectives, universality is unknown.
- **Question 5:** How does block-∞/RMS norm compare to other norms (ℓ₂/Frobenius, ℓ₁) in predicting actual training stability? Could a better-aligned norm yield tighter guarantees?  
  *Basis:* Paper motivates block-∞/RMS by criticizing Frobenius for spurious √L factors (Section 3.1). Lemma 3.2 shows attention is nonexpansive under this norm, but Lipschitz bounds still contain growing terms like ¯B²_U. Not shown that this norm correlates better with stability metrics.  
  *Why unresolved:* Analysis is normative; not demonstrated that controlling block-∞/RMS quantities (e.g., ¯B∞,rms) is sufficient or necessary for stability. Other norms might capture different failure modes.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability of θ(p)≈1:** Based on a single 774M GPT-2 on FineWeb; may not hold for other architectures, datasets, or training regimes.
- **Exactness vs. causal relevance:** The exact softmax identity is proven, but its direct causal role in end-to-end stability relies on the geometric alignment argument (block-∞/RMS), which is asserted but not validated against alternative norms.
- **Causal isolation:** Experiments control architecture but do not isolate temperature scaling (τ) from learning rate warmup, leaving the primacy of “sensitivity throttling” vs. “gradient flow” partly entangled.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Reproduction notes list unspecified parameters (e.g., LayerNorm epsilon, embedding tie implementation, power iteration steps), introducing variance risk.

## Confidence
- **Exact Softmax Sensitivity Formula:** High – Theorem 4.2 is mathematically exact; verification shows negligible numerical error.
- **Pre-LN vs. Post-LN Gradient Flow Mechanism:** High – Jacobian structure derivation is clear; empirical gradient norm profiles strongly support the additive vs. multiplicative distinction.
- **DeepNorm N^{-1/4} Scaling Derivation:** Medium-High – Sound if quartic attention pathway is dominant; validated on 774M models, but not tested for m≠4.
- **θ(p) Persistence & Warmup Necessity:** Medium – Empirical finding solid for tested setup, but lacks breadth across scales/datasets; theoretical reason for persistence unknown.
