# ADAB: Arabic Dataset for Automated Politeness Benchmarking -- A Large-Scale Resource for Computational Sociopragmatics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13870
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13870
- **Reference Count:** 0
- **Primary Result:** Fine-tuned Arabic-specific transformer MARBERT achieved 91.19% accuracy and 85.82% macro-F1 on the new 10k-sample ADAB politeness benchmark.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ADAB, the first large-scale dataset for computational politeness detection in Arabic, addressing a critical gap in Arabic NLP resources. The dataset comprises 10,000 text samples annotated with three-way politeness labels (polite, impolite, neutral) across Modern Standard Arabic and four major dialects, sourced from social media, e-commerce, and customer service domains. Benchmarking 40 model configurations demonstrates that fine-tuned Arabic-specific transformers, particularly MARBERT, substantially outperform both traditional machine learning models and large language models. Error analysis reveals persistent challenges including a model bias toward the neutral class, difficulties with dialectal variation, and the interpretation of context-dependent religious expressions.

## Method Summary
ADAB was constructed by collecting text from three distinct domains (social media, e-commerce, customer service) to ensure topical diversity, covering Modern Standard Arabic and four prevalent dialects (Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, Maghrebi). Annotation followed guidelines rooted in Arabic linguistic and sociocultural traditions, with three annotators per sample and adjudication for disagreements, yielding substantial inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ = 0.703). For benchmarking, the authors evaluated a comprehensive suite of models, including traditional machine learning classifiers with TF-IDF features, multilingual transformers (e.g., mBERT, XLM-R), and Arabic-specific pre-trained models (e.g., MARBERT, JABER), all fine-tuned on the ADAB training set.

## Key Results
*   ADAB contains 10,000 meticulously annotated samples with a distribution of 33.5% polite, 33.8% impolite, and 32.7% neutral, providing a balanced foundation for politeness modeling.
*   The Arabic-specific model MARBERT achieved state-of-the-art performance on ADAB with 91.19% accuracy and 85.82% macro-F1-score, significantly outperforming other transformers and all traditional ML baselines.
*   Universal error analysis across models identified a strong bias toward neutral classifications and highlighted specific failure modes involving dialectal ambiguity, sarcasm, and culturally embedded expressions like religious greetings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of MARBERT and similar Arabic-specific models stems from their pre-training on extensive Arabic corpora, which captures deep linguistic patterns, dialectal variations, and socio-cultural nuances that generic or multilingual models lack. This domain-specific linguistic knowledge allows them to better model the subtle pragmatic markers and formulaic expressions that signal politeness in Arabic contexts.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Code-Switching & Dialect Identification:** Arabic text often mixes MSA with regional dialects. Models must recognize dialectal lexicon and grammar to correctly interpret politeness cues.
    *   *Quick Check:* Evaluate model confidence scores on known dialectal vs. MSA samples.
*   **Sociopragmatic Norms:** Politeness is defined by cultural conventions (e.g., use of religious invocations, honorifics, indirect requests) rather than just lexical polarity.
    *   *Quick Check:* Audit false positives/negatives for sentences containing common Arabic honorifics (e.g., "حضرة", "سيادة").
*   **Implicature and Sarcasm:** Politeness can be conveyed indirectly or through sarcasm, requiring discourse-level understanding beyond sentence boundaries.
    *   *Quick Check:* Test model performance on ADAB samples explicitly annotated for sarcasm.
*   **Domain-Specific Formality:** Customer service interactions have different politeness conventions than social media banter.
    *   *Quick Check:* Compare per-domain performance metrics; low variance suggests robust domain generalization.
*   **Label Granularity Trade-off:** The three-way (polite/impolite/neutral) scheme simplifies a continuous spectrum, helping model learning but potentially obscuring finer shades of meaning.
    *   *Quick Check:* Conduct a pilot study re-annotating a subset with a 5-point Likert scale to measure intra-class variance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Raw Text -> Dialect/Noise Normalization -> Arabic-Specific Transformer (MARBERT) -> [CLS] Token Representation -> Fully-Connected Classification Head -> 3-Way Politeness Label

**Critical Path:** The fine-tuning of the pre-trained MARBERT backbone on the ADAB training set is the single most determinant step, as it adapts the general Arabic linguistic knowledge to the specific politeness classification task.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Dataset Scope vs. Noise:** Including social media provides real-world language but introduces noise (slang, typos) that complicates learning core politeness signals.
*   **Label Simplicity vs. Fidelity:** A three-way scheme enables reliable annotation and model learning but forces discretization of inherently nuanced sociopragmatic states.
*   **Model Specificity vs. Generalizability:** Arabic-specific models (MARBERT) excel on ADAB but may underperform on mixed-language or highly specialized Arabic text not seen in pre-training.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Neutral Over-prediction:** Model consistently assigns "neutral" to samples where human annotators detected polite or impolite cues, especially when those cues are implicit or sarcastic.
*   **Dialect Mismatch:** Performance drops sharply on dialectal samples (e.g., Maghrebi) if the model's pre-training data was dominated by other dialects (e.g., Egyptian).
*   **Expression Literalism:** Misclassification of fixed expressions (e.g., "الله يباركلك", "تعpsychological Sally") by interpreting their literal meaning instead of their conventional politeness function.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Cross-Dialect Holdout Evaluation:** Train on combined data, test separately on each dialect's held-out subset to diagnose dialect-specific performance gaps.
2.  **Context Ablation Probe:** Train models with and without preceding conversational turns for samples where politeness is context-dependent, quantifying the impact of discourse context.
3.  **Adversarial Religious Expression Test:** Create a challenge set where a neutral statement is framed with a religious expression that flips its politeness polarity (e.g., polite request + "إن شاء الله"), and measure model accuracy.

## Limitations
*   Social media data predominance may limit generalizability to more formal or specialized Arabic text domains.
*   The three-way labeling schema may inadequately capture the continuous and context-sensitive nature of politeness, particularly for implicit expressions.
*   The observed neutral classification bias may partly stem from dataset distribution or annotation guidelines rather than a true prevalence of neutral utterances.

## Confidence
*   **Dataset creation & annotation (High):** Well-documented multi-source collection with substantial inter-annotator agreement (κ = 0.703).
*   **Benchmark results (Medium):** MARBERT's superiority is rigorously demonstrated on ADAB, but external validation on independent Arabic politeness corpora is absent.
*   **Error analysis insights (Medium):** Identified challenges (dialect, religious expressions) are plausible but may be specific to ADAB's sampling and annotation frame.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-dialect robustness test:** Evaluate top models (MARBERT, JABER) on held-out samples from each Arabic dialect separately to quantify performance disparities.
2.  **Contextual implicit politeness probe:** Manually curate a small set of ADAB samples where politeness is only inferable from broader discourse (e.g., sarcasm, cultural norms) and measure model accuracy vs. human annotators.
3.  **Human-model agreement audit:** Sample 500 model misclassifications (especially neutral→polite/impolite) for re-annotation by domain experts to assess whether errors reflect annotation limits or model failure.
