# Coronary Artery Segmentation and Vessel-Type Classification in X-Ray Angiography

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17429
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17429
- **Reference count**: 14
- **Primary result**: The best deep model (FPN+SE-ResNet18 at 756×756) trained with merged coronary+catheter supervision achieves internal validation Dice of 0.931±0.006; on external DCA1 test, Dice is 0.814 (vs. 0.798 for coronary-only) and recovers to ~0.882 with light fine-tuning; vessel-type classification attains >95% accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses robust coronary artery segmentation in X-ray angiography, a task complicated by low contrast, motion, and catheter artifacts. The authors enhance classical Hessian-based vesselness filters by automatically predicting per-image optimal hyperparameters via Support Vector Regression from inexpensive image descriptors. For deep learning, they benchmark U-Net, FPN, and Swin models under coronary-only, catheter-only, or merged supervision, finding that combining coronary and catheter labels improves segmentation stability and external generalisation. A second-stage network then classifies segmented vessels into LAD, LCX, and RCA types. Internal validation shows the high-resolution FPN with SE-ResNet18 and merged supervision reaches a Dice of 0.931. External evaluation on DCA1 reveals a domain shift (Dice ~0.80), but performance recovers with modest fine-tuning. Vessel-type classification achieves 95–98% accuracy. The work demonstrates that per-image adaptation—either through hyperparameter tuning or inclusive supervision—yields significant gains in this clinical imaging domain.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by selecting the optimal frame from each cine via a histogram-based peak detector, followed by resolution enhancement using a CNN. Segmentation is then performed either by a classical vesselness filter (Frangi, Meijering, or Sato) whose parameters are chosen per image by an SVR regressor trained on oracle-optimal values, or by a deep neural network. Among deep models, an FPN with an SE-ResNet18 encoder operating at 756×756 resolution performs best. Crucially, training the deep model with merged labels (coronary + catheter as foreground) improves robustness over coronary-only masks. A second-stage classifier takes the binary segmentation mask (concatenated with the image) and predicts pixel-wise LAD, LCX, and RCA labels via three binary classifiers sharing a backbone, followed by argmax and post-processing. Models are trained with Dice loss and 5-fold cross-validation; external adaptation is achieved through light fine-tuning on the target dataset.

## Key Results
- Classical vesselness: SVR-based per-image tuning improves mean Dice over global parameters (Frangi: 0.759 vs. 0.741).
- Deep segmentation: FPN+SE-ResNet18@756×756 with merged supervision yields internal Dice 0.931±0.006; external DCA1 Dice 0.814 (vs. 0.798 for coronary-only) and recovers to ~0.882 after fine-tuning.
- Vessel-type classification: RCA 98.5% accuracy (Dice 0.844), LAD 95.4% (0.786), LCX 96.2% (0.794).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Per-image hyperparameter tuning via SVR**  
   - Claim: Predicting optimal vesselness filter parameters per image using Support Vector Regression from inexpensive descriptors (140-D vector) improves performance without needing ground-truth masks at inference.  
   - Mechanism: SVR learns a mapping from image statistics (intensity histograms, Minkowski functionals, Betti curves) to oracle-optimal hyperparameters. At inference, parameters are tailored to the specific image’s contrast, noise, and structure.  
   - Break condition: If test images fall outside the training distribution of descriptors (e.g., new catheter type, acquisition protocol), the SVR may extrapolate poorly, degrading performance.

2. **Merged supervision (coronary+catheter)**  
   - Claim: Treating the catheter as part of the foreground during deep model training yields more robust segmentation by reducing boundary ambiguity and providing a consistent tubular structure prior.  
   - Mechanism: The model learns to segment all high-contrast linear structures, which includes both coronaries and the catheter. This prevents the network from suppressing catheter-adjacent coronary pixels due to confusing boundaries.  
   - Break condition: If the application requires explicit catheter removal (e.g., for device analysis), the merged mask must be split post-hoc, which may introduce errors; performance gains may vanish if catheter appearance varies significantly across domains.

3. **High-resolution multi-scale FPN architecture**  
   - Claim: Using an FPN with SE-ResNet18 backbone at 756×756 resolution optimally balances fine detail preservation and multi-scale contextual understanding for coronary angiography.  
   - Mechanism: The Feature Pyramid Network fuses high-resolution features from early encoder stages (preserving thin vessel edges) with semantically strong low-resolution features (providing context). Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks adaptively reweight channels to emphasize vascular signals and suppress background clutter.  
   - Break condition: Gains diminish under extreme noise or ambiguity; computational cost scales poorly with resolution, limiting real-time deployment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hessian-based vesselness filtering**  
  - *Why needed*: These classical methods provide an interpretable, parameterized response for tubular structures, forming the baseline and enabling the SVR-based tuning approach. Understanding eigenvalues and response functions is key to grasping the paper’s first mechanism.  
  - *Quick check*: How do the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix distinguish a vessel cross-section from a blob or noise, and how do the Frangi/Meijering/Sato response functions differ in using them?

- **Multi-scale feature fusion (Feature Pyramid Networks)**  
  - *Why needed*: Coronary arteries appear at vastly different scales within a single angiogram. FPN’s top-down pathway with lateral connections is the core architectural principle that allows the best deep model to capture both fine details and contextual information.  
  - *Quick check*: What is the purpose of the lateral 1×1 convolution and the top-down upsampling in an FPN, and how do they differ from a standard U-Net’s skip connections?

- **Per-image adaptation vs. global fixed parameters**  
  - *Why needed*: The paper’s central thesis is that domain shift across patients, machines, and contrast timing necessitates per-image adjustment. This concept underpins both the SVR tuning for classical filters and the observed benefits of merged supervision.  
  - *Quick check*: What are the two complementary strategies presented in the paper for achieving per-image adaptation, and what are their respective data and computational requirements?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Preprocessing (best-frame selection, enhancement) → Segmentation branch: (a) classical vesselness filter with SVR-predicted parameters OR (b) deep FPN (SE-ResNet18, 756×756) trained with merged supervision → Binary vessel mask. Second stage: mask + image → three binary classifiers (shared backbone) → per-pixel LAD/LCX/RCA labels → argmax + post-processing → final vessel-type map.

**Critical path**: For primary robust segmentation: Preprocessing → FPN with merged supervision → Binary mask.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Resolution vs. Compute*: 756×756 yields higher Dice than 384×384 but increases memory and slows inference by ~4×.
- *Supervision Granularity*: Merged supervision improves internal Dice and external stability but produces a single foreground mask, unsuitable if explicit catheter separation is needed; coronary-only is cleaner for vessel-type labeling but more sensitive to boundary ambiguities.
- *Architecture Complexity*: FPN outperforms U-Net and Swin at equivalent resolution; SE-ResNet18 offers best accuracy/efficiency trade-off, while larger backbones (ResNeXt50) give marginal gains at higher cost.

**Failure signatures**:
- Significant drop on external DCA1 without fine-tuning → indicates domain shift (acquisition protocol, labeling conventions).
- Catheter-only model performs poorly (Dice ~0.856) → confirms merged supervision’s benefit is non-trivial.
- SVR-tuned vesselness approaches oracle Dice only if 140-D descriptors capture relevant statistics; a large gap suggests descriptor limitations.
- Vessel-type confusion at bifurcations (e.g., LAD vs. LCX near left main) due to inherent 2D projection ambiguity.

**First experiments**:
1. Replicate classical baseline: Implement Frangi filter with global mean parameters and with oracle per-image tuning on a small internal subset; confirm the Dice gap (reported ~0.018 absolute improvement).
2. Ablate supervision design: Train FPN+SE-ResNet18@756×756 under three regimes (coronary-only, catheter-only, merged). Verify internal Dice ordering: merged > coronary-only > catheter-only, and inspect merged masks near catheter tips to confirm inclusion of the device.
3. Test external transfer & fine-tuning: Load the merged-supervision FPN checkpoint; evaluate on DCA1 test set (zero-shot). Then fine-tune on the DCA1 training set for 5-10 epochs and re-evaluate; expect Dice recovery from ~0.814 to ~0.882.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Temporal information**: How can temporal information from cine X-ray angiography be incorporated into segmentation pipelines to improve robustness against motion and contrast dynamics?
- **Topological constraints**: Can explicit topological constraints be integrated into deep segmentation networks to guarantee anatomically plausible coronary trees?
- **Cross-site generalisation**: How can segmentation models generalise across different angiography centres and acquisition protocols without requiring per-site fine-tuning?
- **Reducing annotation effort**: Can annotation effort be reduced while maintaining high segmentation accuracy via semi-supervised or self-supervised learning?

## Limitations
- External validation limited to a single dataset (DCA1); cross-site robustness not established.
- The 140-D descriptor for SVR may not generalise to unseen catheter types or contrast protocols, risking poor extrapolation.
- Merged supervision obscures catheter boundaries, hindering applications requiring explicit device removal.

## Confidence

- Merged supervision improves robustness: **Medium**
- High-resolution FPN+SE-ResNet18 is optimal: **Medium**
- Vessel-type classification accuracy: **Medium**

## Next Checks
- Independent SVR replication: Acquire a new angiography dataset from a different hospital; train SVR on oracle-tuned subset; evaluate whether SVR-tuned vesselness consistently outperforms global parameters on held-out images.
- Cross-architecture supervision ablation: Train U-Net, FPN, and Swin under coronary-only, catheter-only, and merged supervision; test all models on ≥2 external datasets without fine-tuning to confirm merged supervision’s universal robustness benefit.
- Fine-tuning efficiency and catheter separability: Fine-tune the merged-supervision FPN on increasing fractions (10%, 30%, 50%, 100%) of external data; plot Dice improvement. Then apply a simple catheter extraction step to the merged mask and measure change in coronary Dice to assess impact on downstream tasks.
