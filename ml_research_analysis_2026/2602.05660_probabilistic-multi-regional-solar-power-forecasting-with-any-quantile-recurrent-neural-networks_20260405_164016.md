# Probabilistic Multi-Regional Solar Power Forecasting with Any-Quantile Recurrent Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.05660  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05660  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Achieved state-of-the-art probabilistic forecasting performance (lowest CRPS, MARFE, and Winkler score) on 30 years of hourly data from 259 European regions.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles probabilistic solar power forecasting for high PV penetration power systems. It introduces the Any-Quantile Recurrent Neural Network (AQ-RNN), a novel dual-track architecture that processes both series-specific and cross-regional data. Key innovations include dilated recurrent cells, patch-based temporal modeling, dynamic ensembles, and overlapping quantile ranges. This allows a single trained model to generate calibrated forecasts for any quantile level without retraining. Evaluated on a large dataset of 259 European regions over 30 years, AQ-RNN significantly outperforms all baseline statistical and neural models on CRPS, MARFE, and Mean Winkler Score. An ensemble variant yields further improvements, with statistically significant CRPS reductions across nearly all regions.

## Method Summary
AQ-RNN is a probabilistic forecasting framework for multiple time series (solar PV capacity factors across 259 regions). It employs a dual-track recurrent architecture: a context RNN processes all series simultaneously to capture spatial correlations, while a primary RNN generates forecasts for each target series using a patch-based approach that respects daily cycles. The model is trained on all quantiles jointly by sampling quantile levels from a Beta distribution and minimizing the pinball loss. To enhance calibration, particularly in the distribution tails, it uses a team of RNNs for each overlapping quantile subrange; members are dynamically selected per batch based on a learned confidence score, and forecasts from overlapping teams are linearly combined. The model is trained on normalized hourly data (using a rolling mean) and includes calendar embeddings. Evaluation uses CRPS, MARFE, and Winkler score on a held-out test period.

## Key Results
- AQ-RNN achieves the lowest CRPS (6.17×10⁻²), MARFE (1.46×10⁻²), and Mean Winkler Score (3.96×10⁻¹) among all baselines.  
- The ensemble variant (AQ-RNNe) further improves performance, with statistically significant CRPS reductions against all competitors in nearly all regions.  
- The model provides well-calibrated probabilistic forecasts across the full quantile range without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Any-Quantile Training Paradigm
- **Claim**: Training across the full quantile range via Beta-sampled quantile levels enables a single model to produce calibrated forecasts for arbitrary quantiles without retraining, improving efficiency and flexibility.  
- **Mechanism**: During training, for each batch a quantile level \( q \) is randomly sampled from a Beta distribution with \( \alpha=\beta<1 \), oversampling extreme quantiles. The model receives \( q \) as input and is trained with the quantile (pinball) loss, exposing the network to the entire conditional distribution and learning a mapping from (inputs, \( q \)) to the \( q \)-quantile.  
- **Core assumption**: The conditional distribution can be represented by a single function linearly dependent on \( q \).  
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract, Section 3.5, and analogous any‑quantile load forecasting work [45].  
- **Break condition**: Insufficient model capacity or poorly tuned Beta sampling (e.g., \( \alpha=\beta \) close to 1) may degrade calibration for central quantiles.

### Dual‑Track Architecture with Cross‑Regional Context
- **Claim**: A dedicated context RNN processing all regions provides a contextual representation that, when adapted per series, improves forecast accuracy and calibration by exploiting spatial correlations.  
- **Mechanism**: The context RNN runs synchronously on a batch containing all \( L \) series and outputs a short vector for each series. These raw context vectors are transformed by two parallel embedding layers: a global linear layer (shared) and a per‑series linear layer (one per region). The resulting adapted context \( r' \) is fed into the primary RNN of the target series alongside series‑specific inputs.  
- **Core assumption**: PV generation in different regions is meaningfully correlated due to coherent weather patterns.  
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract, Section 3.2.2, and multi‑site PV forecasting literature.  
- **Break condition**: Weak spatial correlations or overly low context dimensionality may cause the context to become a bottleneck or introduce noise.

### Hierarchical Specialization via Overlapping Quantile Subranges and Dynamic Ensembles
- **Claim**: Partitioning the quantile range into overlapping intervals and training a dedicated team of RNNs per interval, combined with dynamic confidence‑driven member selection, enhances calibration (especially at distribution tails) and robustness.  
- **Mechanism**: The (0,1) quantile interval is split into overlapping subranges (e.g., (0,0.3), (0.1,0.7), (0.5,1)). For each subrange, a predictor is implemented as a team of \( T \) RNNs. Each member outputs a quantile forecast and a scalar confidence \( p \). During training, the loss combines pinball loss and a confidence loss that aligns the ranking of \( p \) with the ranking of pinball loss; only the top‑\( K \) confident members are used for back‑propagation. At inference, for a given \( q \) the forecasts from the relevant subrange teams are linearly combined in overlaps, and within a team the median of the top‑\( K \) confident members is taken.  
- **Core assumption**: Different quantile regions exhibit distinct error characteristics and can be better modeled by specialized predictors; additionally, model confidence can be learned and used to select the most reliable specialist.  
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract, Sections 3.4 and 3.6; ensemble methods are common but this specific combination lacks direct precedent.  
- **Break condition**: Poorly chosen subrange knots or inappropriate team size can prevent specialization; miscalibrated confidence scores lead to erroneous member selection.

## Foundational Learning

### Probabilistic Forecasting and Quantile Regression
- **Why needed here**: The core objective is to produce probabilistic forecasts via conditional quantiles. Understanding the pinball loss, calibration metrics (CRPS, MARFE, Winkler score), and the interpretation of quantiles is essential to grasp the training objective and evaluation.  
- **Quick check**: What does the pinball loss minimize, and how does its piecewise linear form ensure that the predicted \( q \)-quantile has approximately \( q \) probability of exceeding the true value?

### Dilated Recurrent Neural Networks
- **Why needed here**: The architecture relies on dilated recurrent cells to capture long‑range temporal dependencies without increasing depth. Understanding dilation in RNNs—and the separation of output into \( y_t \) and \( h_t \) streams—is key to how the model handles seasonality and long patterns.  
- **Quick check**: How does the dilated recurrent cell expand the receptive field compared to a standard RNN cell, and what is the purpose of splitting the output into a forecast‑relevant stream and a control stream?

### Context Adaptation in Multi‑Series Models
- **Why needed here**: The dual‑track architecture extracts context from all regions and adapts it per target series via embedding layers. This requires understanding how context vectors are generated, reduced, and injected into the primary RNN to leverage cross‑regional information.  
- **Quick check**: In the context adaptation mechanism, what is the functional difference between the global embedding and the per‑series embedding, and why might both be necessary?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Input processor (normalization, segmentation) → Context RNN (processes all series) → Context embedding (global + per‑series) → Adapted context \( r' \) → Auxiliary patch \( x_0 \) (includes \( q \), \( r' \), mean, date embedding, padding) + \( m \) daily patches → Each patch → Shared linear layer → Context vectors \( v_i \) → \( M \) layers of dRNN\(Cells\) → Final linear layer → (quantile forecast, confidence) → Denormalization → Output. Additionally: Team mechanism (T RNNs per subrange) and Overlap aggregation.

**Critical path** (for a target series and quantile \( q \)):  
1. Run context RNN over all series to obtain raw context vectors.  
2. Adapt the target’s context via global and per‑series embeddings → \( r' \).  
3. Build patches: auxiliary patch \( x_0 \) (concatenate \( q \), \( r' \), mean, date embedding, padding) and \( m \) daily patches from normalized history.  
4. Compute context vectors \( v_i \) from the patches (shared linear layer).  
5. Feed patches and \( v_i \) through \( M \) layers of dRNN\(Cells\).  
6. Apply final linear layer to get forecast \( y' \) and confidence \( p \).  
7. Denormalize: \( \hat{y} = y' \cdot \text{mean} \).  
8. If \( q \) falls in an overlap, combine forecasts from the two relevant teams using linear weights; otherwise use the single team’s median forecast.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Team size \( T \) vs. computation: larger \( T \) increases specialization potential but raises memory/time; optimal top‑\( K \) ratio (e.g., 3/4) is empirical.  
- Number of quantile subranges and overlap size: more subranges allow finer specialization but multiply model count; larger overlap yields smoother quantile curves but may dilute specialization.  
- Per‑series embedding parameters: many series lead to large per‑series matrices; they capture series‑specific traits but risk overfitting when series count is small.  
- Dilation schedule: determines maximum lookback without deep stacks; aggressive dilation may miss short‑term patterns.  
- Patch size: daily patches (24) capture diurnal cycles; finer patches could model intra‑day dynamics but increase complexity.

**Failure signatures**  
- Poor calibration (high MARFE, biased RFE): often due to insufficient oversampling of extreme quantiles or imbalanced loss weighting; check Beta shape parameters and confidence loss coefficients.  
- Unreliable confidence scores (low correlation between \( p \) and actual error): indicates confidence loss is not effective; adjust \( \gamma_1 \), \( \gamma_2 \) or team diversity.  
- Context overfitting: per‑series adapters too large relative to series count; monitor validation vs. training error.  
- Quantile discontinuities at subrange boundaries: inspect forecasts near knots; ensure correct overlap interpolation.  
- Training instability: dynamic member selection may cause gradient variance; verify selection probability settings.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Deterministic baseline: Train AQ‑RNN on a single region with fixed \( q=0.5 \). Compare MAE to persistence and exponential smoothing to verify basic temporal learning.  
2. Any‑quantile validation: Train one model across all quantiles using Beta sampling. Evaluate CRPS and RFE over a dense quantile grid. Confirm CRPS is competitive with training separate models for a few quantiles (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 0.9) while being far more efficient.  
3. Context ablation: Remove the context RNN and embedding branches (feed only series‑specific inputs). Compare CRPS, MARFE, and Winkler score to the full model to quantify the contribution of cross‑regional information.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified architectural details**: Precise implementation of dilated recurrent cell (dRNNCell) and padding vectors is not fully described, hindering exact replication.  
- **Weak corpus validation**: The hierarchical specialization mechanism (overlapping quantile subranges + dynamic confidence ensembles) lacks direct precedent and may not generalize to all spatial configurations.  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Effectiveness relies on tuned Beta shape parameters, subrange knots, team size, and loss weights; no sensitivity analysis is provided.
