# Brenier Isotonic Regression

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.10452
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10452
- References: 40
- Primary result: Achieves lower L1 calibration error for multiclass calibration (e.g., 0.061 vs 0.108 on balance-scale) compared to OvR binning, isotonic regression, and matrix scaling.

## Executive Summary
Brenier Isotonic Regression extends isotonic regression to multivariate responses by enforcing cyclic monotonicity—a property of gradients of convex functions—via a bi-level optimization framework. The inner loop solves a discrete optimal transport (Kantorovich) problem to obtain a cyclically monotone coupling between inputs and latent targets; the outer loop minimizes the squared regression error. Predictions for new inputs are generated using a Laguerre map derived from the semi‑discrete OT dual. Evaluated on multiclass probability calibration and single‑index models, Brenier IR achieves lower L1 calibration error than one‑versus‑rest binning, standard isotonic regression, matrix scaling, and calibrated least squares, with minimal hyperparameters and better scalability than iterative recursive partitioning for many classes.

## Method Summary
The method formulates the regression function as a cyclically monotone map T. For a fixed number k of latent target points U = {u_j} on the probability simplex, the inner problem computes a transport plan P between the uniform distribution on training inputs z_i and uniform on u_j under squared L2 cost. This yields an optimal coupling P* that is cyclically monotone. The outer objective minimizes the Frobenius norm between response matrix Y and the matrix nP*U, effectively fitting T via the barycentric map T_P*(z_i) = Σ_j nP*_{ij} u_j. For test inputs, a kernel density estimate êµ provides a continuous source measure; solving the semi‑discrete dual OT problem yields potentials g, and the Laguerre map T_g(z) = arg max_j ⟨z,u_j⟩ - ½‖u_j‖² + g_j assigns each new z to a latent target u_j, preserving cyclic monotonicity.

## Key Results
- Multiclass calibration: With k=30 bins, Brenier IR attains significantly lower L1 calibration error than OvR binning, isotonic regression, and matrix scaling (e.g., 0.061 vs 0.108 on balance‑scale, 0.040 vs 0.063 on car).
- Scalability: Outperforms iterative recursive partitioning (IRP) as the number of classes grows, with fewer bins needed.
- Single‑index models: Provides better‑calibrated probabilities than calibrated least squares and LegendreTron, while retaining non‑parametric flexibility and requiring minimal tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The cyclically monotone constraint on the multivariate regression function is enforced by reformulating it as a discrete optimal transport problem whose optimal coupling is guaranteed to be cyclically monotone.
- **Mechanism:** The bi‑level optimization (Eqs. 10–11) replaces direct constraints on {by_i} with an inner minimization over transport plans P between source inputs z_i and latent targets u_j. The squared L2 cost Kantorovich problem (Eq. 6) yields an optimal P* whose support is cyclically monotone (Prop. 3). The barycentric map T_P* then inherits this property (Thm. 2), satisfying the CMIR constraint.
- **Core assumption:** Squared L2 cost; source measure µ discrete uniform on {z_i}.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “leverage the fact that Kantorovich’s optimal transport (OT) always yields a cyclically monotone coupling.” Section 2, Prop. 3; Section 3, Eq. (10)-(11); Thm. 2.
- **Break condition:** If the cost function is not suitable (e.g., non‑convex in displacement) or if source/target measures are degenerate (e.g., µ singular without a density for Brenier’s theorem in test prediction), the cyclic monotonicity guarantee may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Predictions for new inputs are generated via the Laguerre map from a semi‑discrete OT dual problem, which is also cyclically monotone and extends the binning effect to the probability simplex.
- **Mechanism:** A kernel density estimate êµ provides a non‑singular source measure. Solving the semi‑discrete dual (Eq. 15) yields Kantorovich potentials g*. The Laguerre map partitions R^d into cells L_g*(u_j) each mapped to a target quantile u_j. This piecewise‑constant map is a measurable selection with a cyclically monotone graph (Thm. 3), serving as the test‑time calibration function.
- **Core assumption:** Source measure êµ absolutely continuous; cost is squared L2.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3: “To compute the Laguerre cells, we need to solve semi‑discrete OT (15).” Thm. 3; Section 4: “The Laguerre map naturally extends the idea of the binning estimator to the multinomial case.”
- **Break condition:** Kernel bandwidth h in êµ poorly chosen (too diffuse or too spiky) leads to unstable cell partitions; dual problem may lack a unique solution if ν overlaps with extremes of êµ’s support.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** In probability calibration for multiclass classifiers, the adaptive binning induced by the OT‑based approach captures inter‑class correlations and yields lower L1 calibration error than OvR‑based baselines.
- **Mechanism:** The transport plan P* effectively performs data‑driven binning of the probability simplex (via u_j as bin centers). Unlike OvR binning/IR which treat classes independently, the joint OT optimization couples all classes, allowing bin boundaries to adapt to the model’s joint error structure (Fig. 3). The number of bins k controls the bias‑variance tradeoff.
- **Core assumption:** True calibration map η(q) is piecewise‑constant or smooth enough to be approximated by a few quantiles u_j; base classifier errors are not too pathological.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Table 1; Fig. 3 (visualization of class‑adaptive binning).
- **Break condition:** Base model predictions extremely concentrated near a simplex corner, causing degenerate OT coupling; k too small (coarse) or too large (overfitting) as shown in Fig. 4 tradeoff.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Handler -> OT Inner Solver -> Bi‑level Optimizer -> Training Prediction Module -> Test Prediction Module

Critical path: Data -> Initialize U -> OT inner solve (compute P) -> Compute transport map (barycentric) -> Update U via gradient estimation -> Repeat until convergence -> For test: build êµ -> solve semi‑discrete dual -> build Laguerre map -> predict.

Design tradeoffs:
- k (bins) vs performance: small k (e.g., 15) faster, more global, higher bias; large k (e.g., 50) finer binning, lower bias, risk overfitting. Must be ≥ number of classes d.
- OT solver choice: Exact LP (ot.emd) O(n³) for inner; for large n, entropic regularization (Sinkhorn) is faster but may break cyclic monotonicity guarantee.
- Test prediction: Using primal ot.emd output as heuristic for dual avoids fresh sampling but may be approximate; exact semi‑discrete requires SGD on êµ samples (slower).

Failure signatures:
- Non‑differentiable bi‑level objective: Perturbing U may change active set of P*, causing gradient estimates to jump; SQP may stall.
- Singular source for test prediction: If êµ has zero‑density regions (bad bandwidth h), Laguerre cells may be ill‑defined; some z may have no active u_j.
- Degenerate transport: If k too small, P* may have many zeros, barycentric map becomes non‑injective; calibration too coarse.

First 3 experiments:
1. Univariate sanity check: Run BrenierIR on 1D data with increasing z_i. Compare by_i from T_P* to PAV output; they must match (Thm. 1).
2. Cyclic monotonicity verification: After training, sample a cyclic sequence (z_i, by_i) from training set; check inequality (7) holds for random quadruples.
3. Calibration benchmark: Recalibrate a poorly calibrated multiclass classifier (e.g., MLP with high temperature) on balance‑scale. Compute L1 calibration error on test set; expect BrenierIR(k=30) to beat OvR binning and standard isotonic regression.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the O(n³) computational bottleneck of the inner optimal transport subproblem be overcome without sacrificing the calibration accuracy of BrenierIR?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicit: “the computational cost of BrenierIR remains a bottleneck. Even the inner OT requires O(n³) time complexity, and the total time complexity of the bi‑level program is opaque.”
- **Why unresolved:** Exact solution of the discrete Kantorovich problem scales cubicly, hindering large datasets; no approximation or scaling strategies explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Approximate OT algorithm (e.g., entropic regularization, stochastic gradient, or sketching) with theoretical guarantees on calibration error; empirical demonstration on large‑scale benchmarks showing reduced runtime while maintaining/improving calibration.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the statistical guarantees (e.g., consistency, finite‑sample bounds) of BrenierIR for multiclass probability calibration?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicit: “generalization analysis of recalibration is still underrepresented (Fujisawa and Futami, 2025), and further analysis is left for future work.”
- **Why unresolved:** No theoretical error decomposition linking bi‑level optimization error, sample size, and calibration metrics; only empirical results.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Probabilistic bounds on L1 calibration error showing dependence on n, k (bins), d (dimension), and properties of the base classifier; asymptotic consistency proofs for the estimated calibration map.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How should the number of bins k be chosen in a data‑driven way to optimally trade off bias and variance for calibration?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: Paper discusses the bias‑variance tradeoff in k and notes that “the choice of k may also affect the bias‑variance tradeoff of BrenierIR,” but experiments use fixed values without guidance.
- **Why unresolved:** Relationship between k and generalization error not studied; no principled criteria to select k for new datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical study quantifying bias‑variance tradeoff across varying n, d; proposing a data‑driven selection method (e.g., cross‑validation, information criterion) validated on multiple benchmark datasets.

## Limitations
- **Theoretical assumptions:** Cyclic monotonicity via OT relies on squared L2 cost and a discrete uniform source; test‑time Laguerre map requires an absolutely continuous kernel density estimate, which can fail with poor bandwidth choice.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Bi‑level optimization uses finite‑difference gradients, which may be unstable when the active set of the transport plan changes. Dual potential extraction from the primal solution is heuristic and may break if the active bipartite graph is disconnected.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Unknown hyperparameters (initialization of latent targets U, number of bins k, base classifier architecture) and lack of regularization details could affect results. External validation is limited; the approach is nascent and not yet widely scrutinized.

## Confidence
- **Cyclic monotonicity enforcement (inner OT):** High confidence. Grounded in standard OT theory (Prop. 3, Thm. 2) and mathematically sound.
- **Test prediction via Laguerre map:** Medium confidence. Thm. 3 guarantees cyclic monotonicity under absolute continuity, but practical issues (bandwidth selection, heuristic dual extraction) introduce uncertainty.
- **Calibration performance gains:** Medium confidence. Empirical results are strong but depend on specific base models and hyperparameters; the paper acknowledges unknowns that could affect replication.
