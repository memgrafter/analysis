# FT-Dojo: Towards Autonomous LLM Fine-Tuning with Language Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01712
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01712
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FT-Agent outperforms baselines on 10 of 13 tasks, achieving a breakthrough 13.3% accuracy on AIME 2025 (0% for others) and a 50% relative gain in Table QA visualization (Pass@1 36% vs 24%).

## Executive Summary
FT-Dojo introduces the first interactive benchmark for autonomous end-to-end LLM fine-tuning, comprising 13 tasks across 5 domains. FT-Agent, a purpose-built framework, addresses this via structured iteration planning, fail-fast validation, and structured feedback analysis. Experiments show FT-Agent outperforms baselines on 10 of 13 tasks, achieving a breakthrough 13.3% accuracy on AIME 2025 (0% for others) and a 50% relative gain in Table QA visualization (Pass@1 36% vs 24%). Ablations confirm generalization to 3B models and reveal data scaling trade-offs and backbone sensitivity. Case studies demonstrate emergent cumulative learning from failures but expose fundamental limitations in causal reasoning for systematic diagnosis.

## Method Summary
The paper targets autonomous fine-tuning of LLMs without human intervention. The agent must select and process raw data from a repository, configure training hyperparameters, and iteratively refine based on evaluator feedback. FT-Agent implements a three-stage loop: (1) Strategy Proposal generates a unified hypothesis combining data strategy and training configuration, inheriting the best prior configuration and reviewing past failures; (2) Implementation & Fail-Fast Validation produces code and runs progressive checks (static schema, mini-run, runtime sanity) to catch errors before full training; (3) Feedback Analysis interprets structured diagnostics (metrics, loss curves, error cases) to diagnose capability gaps and update the best configuration. The agent operates within a sandboxed environment using tools like LlamaFactory for training and OpenCompass for evaluation, under a 12-hour compute budget. Baselines include an untuned base model, a manual SFT pipeline, and a tool-augmented OpenHands agent.

## Key Results
- FT-Agent surpasses baselines on 10 of 13 tasks, with notable breakthroughs: 13.3% accuracy on AIME 2025 (vs. 0% for others) and 36% Pass@1 on Table QA visualization (vs. 24%, a 50% relative gain).
- Ablation studies show the framework generalizes to smaller 3B models (e.g., +40% on Mol Edit) and reveals trade-offs: larger data budgets (5k samples) can hurt performance by limiting iteration count, and frontier backbones (GPT-5.2) are critical for procedural tasks.
- Case studies uncover emergent cumulative learning (e.g., adding RDKit for chemistry) but also fundamental limits: agents resort to "shotgun debugging" when causal reasoning is required (e.g., PI4PC overfitting), and can suffer data collapse from myopic filtering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Structured Iteration Planning
- **Claim:** Structured iteration planning prevents the agent from being overwhelmed by growing context and focuses it on high-level hypotheses rather than verbose logs.
- **Mechanism:** The agent creates a compact, unified hypothesis per iteration that describes a data strategy and a training configuration, inheriting the current best configuration and reviewing past failures to avoid repetition. This summary replaces raw, accumulating artifact logs.
- **Core assumption:** LLM backbones can reason effectively over structured summaries of past experience but struggle with raw, unstructured, and growing logs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "structured iteration planning that keeps growing context manageable and helps the agent focus on high-level hypotheses instead of verbose logs"
  - [section 3.2] "By enforcing high-level descriptions instead of accumulating low-level operational details, FT-Agent keeps iteration histories manageable."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like TrainerAgent (paper_id:247018) also propose structured planning for model training, suggesting this pattern is recognized, but direct evidence for *summary-based* context management is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the unified hypothesis format cannot capture the necessary nuance of a failed experiment's root cause, the agent may propose ineffective, high-level changes that do not address specific failure modes, leading to repeated failures.

### Mechanism 2: Fail-Fast Validation
- **Claim:** Fail-fast validation prevents wasted computational resources by catching configuration errors, data pipeline bugs, and incompatibilities before full-scale training begins.
- **Mechanism:** A three-stage progressive validation pipeline runs: (i) static/schema checks, (ii) data format checks and mini-run validation on a small sample, (iii) runtime sanity detection during a short mini-batch run. Failures at any stage provide targeted diagnostics for immediate correction.
- **Core assumption:** Most failure modes in autonomous fine-tuning pipelines are detectable with minimal, inexpensive compute (e.g., syntax errors, invalid data formats, shape mismatches) rather than requiring a full training run.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "fail-fast validation that prevents wasted computation by catching configuration and data problems early"
  - [section 3.2] "Configurations that fail any stage are rejected immediately, and the agent receives targeted diagnostic logs that encourage rapid correction."
  - [corpus] HackSynth (paper_id:188440) uses an iterative Planner/Summarizer loop with feedback, which shares a philosophy of reactive correction, but lacks the explicit progressive validation stages. Evidence is suggestive but not direct.
- **Break condition:** If a failure mode is only latent and detectable after significant training progress (e.g., a subtle learning rate schedule that causes divergence at epoch 50), the fail-fast stages may pass, leading to a wasted full run. The system assumes such deep failures are less common than early, obvious ones.

### Mechanism 3: Structured Feedback Analysis
- **Claim:** Structured feedback analysis enables the agent to interpret multifaceted evaluation signals (metrics, loss curves, error cases) to diagnose capability gaps and coordinate data and training refinements.
- **Mechanism:** The agent receives multi-level structured feedback from the evaluator (aggregate metrics, per-instance errors, loss trajectories, sampled failures) and performs an "Expert-Guided Diagnosis" to identify patterns like overfitting, noise sensitivity, or systematic reasoning errors, which then informs the next hypothesis.
- **Core assumption:** The backend LLM can generate actionable insights from structured diagnostic data (e.g., "loss diverges after 10 steps") more reliably than from unstructured logs or scalar metrics alone.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "structured feedback analysis that helps the agent interpret metrics, loss curves, and error cases to decide how to refine"
  - [section 3.2] "This diagnosis supports coordinated refinement: the agent can expand data to cover failed patterns while also adjusting training settings such as learning rate or training duration."
  - [corpus] AMOR (paper_id:120683) uses process feedback for adaptation, aligning with the concept of structured feedback, but its focus is on reasoning with knowledge bases, not training diagnostics. Direct evidence is limited.
- **Break condition:** If the failure patterns are subtle, correlated, or require causal inference (as shown in the PI4PC case where the agent engages in "shotgun debugging"), the structured analysis may misidentify the root cause, leading to ineffective or harmful refinements.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: Interactive Agent Environments for Evaluation
- **Why needed here:** FT-Dojo is not a static dataset but an interactive sandbox where agents must explore an open-ended decision space (data curation, training configs) over multiple episodes. Understanding this paradigm is prerequisite to grasping why traditional static benchmarks fail for this problem.
- **Quick check question:** What are the three core interfaces (Meta API, Data Repository, Evaluator) that define the agent's action space in FT-Dojo, and how do they differ from inputs in a standard classification benchmark?

### Concept 2: Tool-Augmented LLMs for Automated ML Pipelines
- **Why needed here:** FT-Agent does not "know" how to fine-tune innately; it relies on tools (LlamaFactory wrappers, OpenCompass, RDKit) to execute data processing and training. An engineer must understand how agents orchestrate external tools and handle their outputs/errors.
- **Quick check question:** In the Implementation phase, what are the two primary types of code the agent must generate, and what specific validation step applies uniquely to the training configuration?

### Concept 3: The Distinction Between Pattern-Matching and Causal Reasoning in Diagnostics
- **Why needed here:** The paper's key limitation finding is that agents can succeed via cumulative learning when effective interventions are in their training distribution (e.g., adding RDKit for chemistry) but fail when diagnosis requires inferring latent, causal mechanisms (e.g., PI4PC overfitting). This conceptual split explains the boundary of current autonomous systems.
- **Quick check question:** Contrast the successful intervention on Mol Edit (adding RDKit validation) with the failed interventions on PI4PC (NEFTune, filtering). What key difference in the *nature* of the bottleneck does each address?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (simplified flow):**  
Meta API (I) + Data Repository (D) -> Agent Core -> Strategy Proposal (θd, θt) -> Code Generation (data pipeline + training config) -> Fail-Fast Validation (schema, mini-run, runtime) -> if pass: Full Training (Tools + Sandbox) -> Evaluator (F) -> Structured Feedback -> Agent Core (Feedback Analysis) -> Update Best Config & Memory -> Loop; if fail: return to Strategy Proposal with diagnostics.

**Critical path:** The agent iterates through the three-stage loop (Strategy Proposal → Implementation & Fail-Fast Validation → Feedback Analysis), with validation gating whether a configuration proceeds to full training. Each iteration updates the best configuration and memory, guiding the next hypothesis. The process terminates after a 12-hour compute budget or convergence.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Exploration Depth vs. Compute Cost: Deeper iteration loops yield better performance but increase total cost; the 12-hour budget forces a tradeoff.
- Quality vs. Quantity in Data Scaling: More training samples (5k) can hurt performance by consuming the iteration budget, favoring a smaller, high-quality set (2k) that allows more strategic experimentation.
- Full Fine-Tuning vs. PEFT: For tasks with rigid output contracts (Table QA), full tuning is chosen despite higher cost because LoRA struggles with strict formatting.

**Failure signatures:**
- Data Collapse: Progressive filtering pipelines reduce training data to a tiny, non-representative subset (e.g., 1,800 -> 27 samples), causing catastrophic performance drop.
- Shotgun Debugging: Applying generic techniques (NEFTune, strict filters) without causal linkage to the diagnosed failure mode, leading to volatility and zero net gain (PI4PC trace).
- Context Overwhelm: Generalist agents (OpenHands) fail because they cannot distill growing logs into actionable hypotheses, getting stuck in debugging loops.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Scalability Test:** Run FT-Agent on the 3B model (Qwen2.5-3B) using the default 2k sample protocol. Expected outcome: significant gains (+40% on Mol Edit) proving model-agnostic framework, as shown in ablation (Fig 3c).
2. **Backbone Sensitivity Swap:** Replace the planning backbone (GPT-5.2) with GPT-4o while keeping all other components (data cleaning models, tools) identical. Expected outcome: significant performance drop on procedural tasks (e.g., -16% on Visualization), proving the necessity of frontier intelligence for high-level orchestration.
3. **Cross-Domain Transfer Probe:** Manually inspect the agent's data mixing strategy on Numerical Reasoning (TableBench). Expected finding: agent autonomously mixes in 10% out-of-domain math data (DeepScaleR) to address arithmetic bottlenecks, demonstrating emergent capability to identify and source orthogonal skills.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we design LLM-based agents that perform causal reasoning about training dynamics to diagnose root causes of failure (e.g., overfitting, data misalignment) and propose targeted interventions, rather than relying on pattern-matching or generic fixes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that agents "exhibit pattern-matching rather than causal reasoning when diagnosing training failures" and describe the "shotgun debugging" failure mode on PI4PC where interventions lacked causal grounding.
- **Why unresolved:** Agents currently infer correlations from feedback (using hedging language like "likely") without understanding underlying mechanisms, causing them to apply ineffective techniques repeatedly and fail to systematically improve.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of agents that, given historical experiment logs, can generate and test causal hypotheses (e.g., through controlled ablations) that lead to consistent performance gains across diverse fine-tuning tasks, particularly those requiring deep diagnostic insight.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can agents simulate the downstream effects of compound decisions—such as the interaction between data filtering pipelines and training configurations—to avoid myopic optimizations that cause data collapse or performance degradation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion identifies "myopic local optimization rather than coherent multi-step planning" as a key limitation, and cites the Visualization task where progressively stricter filters reduced data to 27 samples, collapsing Pass@1.
- **Why unresolved:** Agents currently optimize each step in isolation without a world model to predict long-term consequences, leading to catastrophic reductions in dataset size or coverage.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An agent that, before applying a series of transformations, can estimate their joint effect on data distribution and model performance, and successfully avoids such collapses while improving results.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What mechanisms can allow agents to maintain a structured, compressible representation of the iterative fine-tuning process (including logs, configurations, and outcomes) that scales to many loops while preserving critical diagnostic information for long-horizon planning?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper addresses Challenge 1 (long context) with structured iteration planning, but case studies show agents still struggle with long-horizon improvement (e.g., PI4PC with 7 loops and zero net gain). This suggests current summarization may lose actionable insights.
- **Why unresolved:** While FT-Agent uses high-level hypothesis summaries, it may fail to capture subtle interactions between experiments or recall relevant failure modes when planning future steps, limiting cumulative learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An agent that, over 10+ iterations across multiple tasks, shows monotonic or at least non-decreasing performance by effectively leveraging historical experience, with ablation studies showing that richer memory representations lead to better long-term outcomes.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can we develop predictive models of training outcomes from early validation signals (e.g., first few epochs, data characteristics) that agents can use to prune unpromising configurations before full training, thereby improving exploration efficiency within fixed compute budgets?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper's fail-fast validation catches configuration errors but not performance failures; agents still waste iterations on configurations that ultimately plateau or overfit. Table 6 shows OpenHands has fewer effective iterations partially due to such waste.
- **Why unresolved:** Current validation focuses on syntax and runtime, not on estimating final metric performance. Agents lack a way to predict whether a given data/training strategy will succeed without incurring the full training cost.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrating a lightweight predictor (e.g., a meta-model or heuristics) that agents consult after a short validation run, and that accurately forecasts final performance, leading to a higher ratio of successful experiments.

## Limitations
- Prompt & Schema Fidelity: Exact prompt templates and JSON schemas for each stage are unspecified; replication relies on engineering from high-level descriptions, risking behavioral drift.
- Validation Thresholds: Numerical thresholds for fail-fast stages (e.g., "unstable loss" definition, mini-run sample size) are absent; default choices may alter iteration efficiency and success rates.
- Memory & Summarization: The mechanism for summarizing past failures and experience retrieval is opaque; this is central to cumulative learning but could be a brittle heuristic.

## Confidence
- Performance gains (10/13 tasks, AIME 2025 breakthrough): Medium-High.
- Structured planning/feedback mechanisms (causal contribution): Medium.
- Fundamental causal reasoning limit (shotgun debugging): High.
- Generalization to 3B models: High.
