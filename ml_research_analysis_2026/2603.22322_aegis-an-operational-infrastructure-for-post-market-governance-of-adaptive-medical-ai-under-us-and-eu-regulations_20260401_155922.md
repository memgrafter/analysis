# AEGIS: An Operational Infrastructure for Post-Market Governance of Adaptive Medical AI Under US and EU Regulations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22322
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22322
- **Reference Count:** 32
- **Primary Result:** AEGIS operationalizes FDA PCCP and EU AI Act provisions for adaptive AI, using a fixed performance reference and decoupled monitoring to autonomously execute deployment decisions and post-market surveillance alarms across continuous learning iterations in sepsis prediction and brain tumor segmentation.

## Executive Summary
AEGIS is a configurable software infrastructure designed to bridge the regulatory gap for continuously learning medical AI systems under the FDA’s Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP) framework and the EU AI Act. It translates high-level governance requirements into an executable, automated lifecycle. The system continuously assimilates new clinical data, retrains models, monitors performance and data distribution against a fixed reference from the first approved model, and issues autonomous deployment decisions (APPROVE, CONDITIONAL APPROVAL, CLINICAL REVIEW, REJECT) alongside independent post-market surveillance (PMS) ALARM signals. Validation on 11 sepsis prediction iterations and a brain tumor segmentation case demonstrates its ability to detect critical degradation states, enforce safety thresholds, and provide a domain-agnostic operational layer for safe adaptive AI.

## Method Summary
AEGIS implements three core configurable modules: (1) **Dataset Assimilation and Retraining (DARM)** manages a three-way split of clinical data—static Golden (`D_G`), dynamic Drifting (`D_{D,k}`), and cumulative Training (`D_{T,k}`)—ensuring patient-level separation and versioning. (2) **Model Monitoring (MMM)** computes performance metrics (e.g., sensitivity, AUC), fairness scores, and a Bonferroni-corrected Kolmogorov-Smirnov test drift score (`S_D`) comparing `D_{D,k}` to historical distributions. (3) **Conditional Decision (CDM)** applies a priority-ordered Boolean logic (from Tables III/IV) to produce a four-category deployment decision for the new candidate model evaluated on `D_G`, and a separate PMS ALARM signal assessing the currently released model on `D_{D,k}`. A fixed performance reference (`P_ref`) is established from the first APPROVE model’s metrics on `D_G`, and all subsequent candidate models must maintain non-inferiority within tolerance `τ` to it. The configuration layer allows clinical and regulatory teams to set thresholds, drift ranges, and metric weights.

## Key Results
- In 11 sepsis prediction iterations, AEGIS exercised all four deployment categories: 8 APPROVE, 1 CONDITIONAL APPROVAL, 1 CLINICAL REVIEW, 1 REJECT.
- The system co-issued APPROVE + ALARM at iteration 8 (new model approved while released model showed major drift risk) and REJECT + ALARM at iteration 10 (critical state with no deployable model and released model failing).
- Drift detection via KS-test identified distributional shifts before observable performance degradation; AEGIS enforced a safety floor (e.g., sensitivity ≥ 0.65) and rejected iterations violating it.
- The same architecture successfully governed a high-dimensional brain tumor segmentation task, producing APPROVE, ALARM, and REJECT decisions in response to real cohort shifts, demonstrating domain agnosticism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Fixed Performance Reference as Invariant Safety Anchor
AEGIS prevents silent degradation by anchoring all candidate model evaluations to the validated performance of the first fully approved model (`P_ref`). The CDM stores the first APPROVE decision’s metrics as `P_ref`. Every subsequent candidate model (`P_G,k`) must not regress beyond a tolerance `τ` from this fixed reference (Priority P2 in Table IV). This implements the FDA’s Substantial Equivalence (SE) concept in an adaptive context, ensuring new iterations maintain equivalence to a known-safe baseline rather than merely meeting absolute thresholds. The mechanism fails if the initial `P_ref` model is overfitted, compromised, or clinically invalid.
- **Evidence:** Implements SE via fixed reference comparison ([section II-A], [section II-B4]). Corpus shows adjacent work on AI governance but not this specific anchoring.

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Deployment and PMS Monitoring
AEGIS separates the "Can we deploy?" (deployment decision for new model on static `D_G`) and "Is the fielded model still safe?" (PMS ALARM for released model on drifting `D_{D,k}`) questions. These use different datasets and reference points, enabling proactive risk detection without blocking conservative updates. This allows co-issuance like APPROVE+ALARM, where a new model passes checks while the released model shows risk.
- **Evidence:** Independent evaluation of two outputs ([section II-B3]), demonstrated by iteration 8 results ([section III-A]). Neighbors focus on policy enforcement but not dual-channel monitoring.

### Mechanism 3: Dynamic Dataset Stratification for Auditability and Drift Detection
The DARM’s three-split architecture ensures evaluation integrity and provides a dedicated drift detection channel. The Golden set (`D_G`) is static and never used for training, guaranteeing unbiased cross-iteration comparison. The Drifting set (`D_{D,k}`) tests the *current* model *before* its contents are assimilated into the next training pool, creating a temporal buffer where new data is characterized for drift prior to influencing models. The KS-test on `D_{D,k}` against historical baselines quantifies covariate shift.
- **Evidence:** Static Golden for unbiased evaluation; Drifting set used for pre-assimilation drift testing ([section II-B1], [section II-B2]). Iteration 8 shows drift score 1.000 while performance stable ([section III-A]).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Clinical data stream → DARM → MMM → CDM → {deployment decision, ALARM}. Configuration layer sits above all modules.
- **Critical path:** 1) Establish `D_G` and initial `D_T,0`. 2) Train first model → if metrics ≥ clinical thresholds → APPROVE → freeze `P_ref` and `P_rel^G`. 3) Each cycle: ingest new batch → form `D_{D,k}` → MMM computes metrics on `D_G` and `D_{D,k}` → CDM evaluates priority chain → update `D_T,k+1 = D_T,k ∪ D_{D,k}` regardless of decision. Decision governs model release, not data assimilation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Configurability vs. Complexity:* Same core logic supports sepsis and imaging via parameters, but each instantiation requires careful calibration; misconfigured thresholds or drift methods can cause unsafe or overly conservative behavior.
  - *Static Benchmark vs. Staleness:* `D_G` ensures fair comparison but risks becoming outdated; no automatic refresh mechanism.
  - *Aggregate Metrics vs. Local Failures:* Governance uses aggregate KPIs; a model could maintain aggregate performance while failing on critical subpopulations.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Silent degradation: Metrics stable but uncertainty increases or fairness worsens → ALARM not triggered if only clinical performance thresholds used.
  - Reference corruption: `P_ref` set from overfitted initial model → all subsequent approvals systematically biased.
  - Drift false negative: KS-test insensitive to certain shifts (e.g., label corruption preserving feature margins) → `S_D` low while performance collapses.
  - Decision category collision: Multiple category conditions met simultaneously → priority order (Table IV) determines outcome; misconfigured thresholds can cause instability.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Dataset Integrity Test:** Verify DARM separation; confirm no patient ID appears in >1 split. Intentionally leak a sample from `D_G` into `D_T,k` → should cause `P_G,k` inflation; CDM should route correctly but audit log must record contamination.
  2. **Drift Detection Boundary Calibration:** On stable data (iter. 0–5), run KS-test repeatedly; establish baseline `S_D` distribution (paper shows 0.000). Set `S_D^minor` lower bound > max baseline noise (paper uses 0.30). Validate minor drift triggers CONDITIONAL APPROVE, major triggers ALARM.
  3. **Priority Chain Enforcement:** Construct inputs satisfying multiple conditions: e.g., `P_G,k` < `P_fail` **and** `S_D` in minor drift range. CDM must route to REJECT (P1 priority). Log must show P1 triggered, P3 condition true but ignored. Test recovery: after REJECT, next iteration with clean data must be able to achieve APPROVE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What standardized methodology should manufacturers follow to derive clinically justified performance thresholds for AEGIS deployment decisions and PMS ALARM signals?
- **Basis:** Thresholds used are illustrative and must be derived through formal clinical risk assessment per ISO 14971:2019, but no concrete method is provided (Sections II-A, IV.D).
- **Why unresolved:** Without a standardized risk assessment process, threshold setting may be arbitrary, leading to inconsistent safety guarantees and regulatory challenges.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A validated framework mapping specific clinical hazards to quantitative metric thresholds, with documented traceability to risk acceptability criteria, tested across multiple medical AI domains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can concept drift (changes in the conditional relationship between features and outcomes) be reliably detected and incorporated into AEGIS governance decisions?
- **Basis:** Handling concept drift is identified as a future extension; current MMM primarily addresses covariate drift (Section IV.I).
- **Why unresolved:** Concept drift can cause degradation without feature distribution change, potentially escaping current detection.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation of a concept drift detection module (e.g., sequential prediction error monitoring) integrated into MMM, with evaluated sensitivity and specificity on datasets with induced concept shifts.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What monitoring strategies can effectively detect adversarial data poisoning attacks in the AEGIS post-market surveillance pipeline?
- **Basis:** Paper acknowledges aggregate KPIs may not detect data poisoning and proposes a temporal ensemble disagreement approach requiring validation (Section IV.H).
- **Why unresolved:** Data poisoning attacks preserving overall metrics yet corrupting behavior pose a serious threat; without robust detection, AEGIS could silently deploy compromised models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical evaluation of disagreement-based signals under realistic poisoning scenarios (e.g., targeted label flips) demonstrating timely ALARM triggers, with thresholds calibrated on benign model evolution data.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the natural characteristics and frequencies of distributional drift in real-world deployed medical AI systems, and how should these inform AEGIS configuration?
- **Basis:** Validation used synthetic perturbations; authors call for future work incorporating documented real-world drift events (Section IV.D, Limitation 1).
- **Why unresolved:** Synthetic perturbations may not represent actual drift patterns; without empirical data, setting appropriate drift thresholds, batch sizes, and monitoring frequencies is difficult.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Multi-center longitudinal studies collecting deployment data from operational medical AI systems, analyzing drift metrics over time to establish empirical distributions of covariate and potential concept drift under varying clinical conditions.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of synthetic perturbations for sepsis iterations 6–10 hinders exact replication.
- Threshold derivation (τ, P_fail) lacks detail, limiting generalizability to new clinical contexts.
- Static Golden dataset risks staleness, potentially invalidating fixed-reference comparisons over time.
- Aggregate metrics may miss subpopulation failures (e.g., rare tumor types).
- Concept drift (label–feature relationship shifts) is not detected by current KS-test–only monitoring.

## Confidence
- **Fixed Performance Reference:** Medium (validated in sepsis study but critically dependent on initial model validity).
- **Decoupled Monitoring:** High (empirically demonstrated via APPROVE+ALARM co-issuance).
- **Dynamic Stratification:** Medium (effective in experiments; staleness risk unmitigated).
- **Domain-Agnostic Claim:** Medium (supported by two domains; broader validation needed).
