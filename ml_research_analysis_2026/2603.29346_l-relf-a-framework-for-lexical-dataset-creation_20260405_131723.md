# L-ReLF: A Framework for Lexical Dataset Creation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29346  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29346  
- **Reference count:** 17  
- **Primary result:** Created a high-quality, Wikidata-compatible lexical dataset for Moroccan Darija with 100% transcription accuracy via a semi-automated pipeline combining OCR and rigorous human verification.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces L-ReLF, a reproducible framework for constructing structured lexical datasets from non-digital sources, addressing the scarcity of standardized resources for low-resource languages like Moroccan Darija. The pipeline leverages authoritative print materials, employs Google Drive OCR (noting a 17% character error rate with 59% of errors due to Modern Standard Arabic bias), and applies a mandatory dual-pass human verification to ensure accuracy. The resulting dataset integrates seamlessly with Wikidata Lexemes, providing a foundation for NLP applications and terminology development. The authors publicly release the framework to enable other language communities to build similar resources.

## Method Summary
The workflow begins with selecting authoritative academic books in the target language. Digitization proceeds via a bifurcated pathway: high-quality scans are processed with Google Drive OCR, while poor-quality sources undergo full manual transcription. Raw outputs then enter a dual-pass verification and standardization phase where native linguists correct OCR errors (particularly MSA bias) and map entries to a simplified tabular schema (word, lexical category, grammatical gender, etymology). Finally, custom scripts translate the standardized spreadsheet into Wikidata Lexeme properties for integration. The verification step consumes approximately 90% of the total preparation time but guarantees 100% accuracy relative to source materials.

## Key Results
- Achieved **100% transcription accuracy** relative to authoritative sources after human verification.
- Measured OCR **character error rate (CER) of 17%**, with **59% of errors attributed to Modern Standard Arabic bias** (systematic "correction" of Darija graphemes).
- Produced a **Wikidata-compatible lexical dataset** for Moroccan Darija, demonstrating the pipeline's end-to-end reproducibility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Verification Overcomes OCR Noise
**Claim:** A rigorous dual-pass human verification protocol can compensate for the inherent noise of MSA-biased OCR tools, delivering a dataset with 100% transcription accuracy.  
**Mechanism:** OCR output is treated as an initial draft. Native linguists perform a visual sanity check against physical sources to correct character-level errors (especially Darija-specific features misread as MSA) and enrich missing fields (e.g., grammatical gender). This shifts accuracy guarantees from the fallible algorithmic layer to deterministic human judgment.  
**Evidence:** Pre-verification CER was 17%; 59% of these errors stem from MSA bias. Manual verification accounted for ~90% of total preparation time, underscoring its central role.

### Mechanism 2: Universal Simplified Data Model Enables Interoperability
**Claim:** A minimal, standardized spreadsheet model (word, category, gender, etymology) allows consolidation of heterogeneous source materials and direct mapping to Wikidata Lexemes, ensuring downstream utility.  
**Mechanism:** Disparate academic books are normalized into a flat schema, forcing consistency and explicitly exposing missing data. The model's design mirrors Wikidata Lexeme properties, enabling automated translation via scripts.  
**Evidence:** The fields were selected for linguistic relevance and Wikidata compatibility; developed mapping scripts are language-agnostic and publicly released.

### Mechanism 3: Bifurcated Extraction Pathway Adapts to Source Quality
**Claim:** Conditionally routing source material to either OCR or manual transcription based on scan quality maintains feasibility despite variable print fidelity.  
**Mechanism:** Sources are inspected upfront; high-quality scans proceed through OCR, while degraded texts are manually transcribed. Both streams converge at the standardization step, with human verification applied to OCR outputs.  
**Evidence:** ~20% of the corpus required full manual transcription due to inherently poor print quality; the bifurcation prevented OCR failures from halting progress.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Source Inventory & Inspection → Digitization Engine (Google Drive OCR / Manual Transcription) → Verification & Standardization Workbench → Mapping Scripts → Integration Gateway (Wikidata sandbox/production)
- **Critical path:** Source Selection → Digitization (branch) → Raw Data Aggregation → Dual-Pass Verification & Standardization → Automated Mapping → Wikidata Upload. Verification is the central, non-skippable bottleneck.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *OCR tool:* Chose Google Drive (accessible, free) over specialized Arabic OCR (potentially more accurate but costly).  
  - *Schema granularity:* Opted for simple model (4 fields) over linguistically rich one to ensure volunteer completion and Wikidata compatibility.  
  - *Verification depth:* Dual-pass adds time but justified by high pre-verification CER and 100% accuracy goal.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High OCR CER (>25%) persisting after first verification pass indicates insufficient linguist capacity or scan quality.  
  - >15% of entries unmappable to Wikidata due to missing core data (e.g., lexical category).  
  - Verification phase time overrun exceeding 200% of estimates.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **OCR Benchmark:** Process a 100-word sample from a high-quality scan with Google Drive OCR. Calculate CER and classify error types (MSA bias vs. other). Decision: proceed with OCR or mark source for manual transcription?  
  2. **Schema Fit Test:** Take 50 fully transcribed entries. Attempt mapping all required fields; document fields with >15% missing/ambiguous data. Decision: adjust schema or seek supplemental sources?  
  3. **Dry-Run Upload:** Execute mapping script on 10 verified entries in a Wikidata sandbox. Validate correct population of Lexeme properties. Decision: debug mapping logic before bulk upload?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can specialized Darija OCR reduce the 17% CER and 59% MSA bias?** The paper identifies MSA bias as the primary technical constraint and notes the need for LRL-specific OCR tools. A fine-tuned model (e.g., Tesseract) trained on transcribed Darija could automate a major bottleneck, but training costs and accuracy gains are unknown. Resolution requires a comparative study using a held-out sample.
- **What morphological patterns enable systematic new term generation?** The paper promises a methodology for generating new Darija terms from the structured dataset's etymology and relationship fields, but does not perform the analysis. Identifying productive root/affix patterns and testing their generative acceptability with speakers would resolve this.
- **How might noisy digital sources (social media) augment the print-based dataset?** The authors avoided web-scraping due to unstandardized Latin script usage, prioritizing print for quality. A hybrid approach could expand coverage (e.g., for neologisms) but risks quality trade-offs. A pilot study comparing parallel samples from books and digital text, with noise-filtering, would quantify costs/benefits.
- **Is the framework transferable to typologically diverse LRLs?** Generalizability is claimed but only validated for Arabic-script Darija. Languages with different features (e.g., tone, logographic scripts) may challenge the fixed-field model and Wikidata mapping. Case studies on 2–3 diverse LRLs, using the same scripts and schema, would test required adaptations.

## Limitations
- **Script accessibility:** The publicly released Wikidata mapping scripts lack detailed documentation (repository link, dependencies, adaptation guide), blocking direct replication of the integration step.
- **Verification protocol specificity:** The "dual-pass" process lacks annotator training guidelines, dispute resolution mechanisms, and time-per-entry metrics, making exact replication and scalability assessment difficult.
- **Data model generalizability:** The simplified 4-field schema may not capture linguistically critical features for non-Afro-Asiatic or non-gendered languages, risking data loss and reduced utility for some LRLs.

## Confidence
- **HITL accuracy claim (100%):** High – directly supported by pre-verification CER analysis and post-verification assertion relative to sources.
- **Universal data model claim:** Medium – successful for Darija and rationally designed, but sufficiency across diverse languages remains untested.
- **Bifurcated pathway feasibility:** Medium – documented use for ~20% of sources, but scalability under high manual-transcription burden is not formally analyzed.
