# rePIRL: Learn PRM with Inverse RL for LLM Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07832
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07832
- **Reference Count:** 32
- **Primary Result:** Introduces an inverse RL framework for learning process reward models (PRMs) with minimal assumptions, achieving up to +4.3% accuracy gains on math and coding benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Process reward models (PRMs) are crucial for guiding LLM reasoning, but existing methods rely on strong expert assumptions or suffer from entropy collapse. rePIRL proposes an inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) inspired approach that learns a PRM by alternating policy and reward updates, using importance sampling to ensure tractability. It offers a theoretical unification of online and offline PRM learning. Experiments on Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen3-4B across math and coding tasks show state-of-the-art performance, with average accuracy improvements of up to +3.2% on math and +4.3% on coding. The resulting PRM also benefits test-time training, scaling, and accelerates learning on hard problems.

## Method Summary
rePIRL casts PRM learning as an IRL problem: the PRM is the reward function to be inferred from the policy's behavior. To avoid the need for expert demonstrations, it employs a dual optimization loop where the policy (LLM generating reasoning steps) is updated to maximize the cumulative PRM reward, while the PRM is updated to assign higher rewards to steps from high-quality (reference or self-generated) trajectories. Importance sampling corrects for distribution mismatch between the current policy and the reference data, making the method scalable to LLM action spaces. This alternating process continues until convergence, yielding a PRM that reflects step-level desirability without strong supervision.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy on math and coding benchmarks (using Qwen2.5-3B/4B), improving by up to +3.2% (math) and +4.3% (coding) over prior PRM methods.
- The learned PRM enhances test-time training, test-time scaling, and speeds up training on hard problems.
- Provides a unified theoretical perspective covering both online and offline PRM learning scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
rePIRL's success stems from its IRL-inspired dual update mechanism. By treating the PRM as a reward to be inferred from the policy's own rollouts, it avoids dependence on a fixed expert dataset, which can be biased or incomplete. Importance sampling reweights trajectories to correct for the divergence between the current policy and the reference distribution, stabilizing learning despite the large action space of LLMs. Alternating updates prevent the PRM from overfitting to a single policy snapshot, promoting a steady co-evolution that mitigates entropy collapse (where the policy converges to a deterministic, low-quality strategy).

## Foundational Learning
- **Process Reward Model (PRM):** Provides step-level feedback during reasoning; needed to guide policy beyond final-answer rewards. Quick check: Can a PRM distinguish correct vs. incorrect reasoning steps on a held-out set?
- **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL):** Infers reward functions from demonstrated behavior; rePIRL adapts IRL to learn PRMs without expert demonstrations. Quick check: Does the recovered reward align with ground-truth step values in a toy setting?
- **Importance Sampling:** Corrects distribution mismatch between training data and current policy; essential for unbiased gradient estimation. Quick check: Do importance weights remain well-behaved (non-exploding) across training iterations?
- **Dual Optimization:** Alternating updates of policy and PRM; maintains stability and avoids mutual collapse. Quick check: Does alternating yield monotonic improvement compared to joint training?
- **Entropy Collapse:** A common failure in RL where policy entropy vanishes; rePIRL's alternating updates and IRL objective maintain exploration. Quick check: Is the policy's token entropy maintained at healthy levels throughout training?
- **Test-time scaling:** Using more compute at inference (e.g., more rollouts) with a learned PRM; rePIRL's PRM is effective for this paradigm. Quick check: Does increasing the number of test-time rollouts with the PRM improve accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
LLM Policy generates reasoning steps → PRM scores each step → Trajectories are collected → Importance weights computed from reference data → Policy updated via PRM rewards → PRM updated via reweighted trajectories → loop repeats.

**Critical Path:** The core loop involves (1) generating rollouts from the current policy, (2) scoring steps with the current PRM, (3) updating the policy via gradient ascent on cumulative PRM reward, (4) updating the PRM by minimizing a reward regression loss on high-quality reference trajectories reweighted by importance sampling.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Importance sampling reduces bias but introduces high variance; the scheme uses a budgeted number of samples to control variance. Dual updates increase training time but improve stability. The approach assumes access to a reference dataset of high-quality trajectories (offline) or a way to generate them (online), trading off between data requirements and performance.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Diverging importance weights (e.g., extremely high variance) leading to unstable PRM updates.  
- Policy entropy rapidly decreasing, indicating collapse.  
- PRM scores stop improving on validation trajectories.  
- No gain over baselines on held-out tasks.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **PRM Quality Check:** Train rePIRL on a small, controlled dataset with known ground-truth step rewards; measure correlation between learned PRM scores and ground truth.  
2. **Ablation of Importance Sampling:** Compare performance with and without importance weighting, or with alternative weighting schemes (e.g., per-token vs. trajectory-level).  
3. **Entropy Monitoring:** Track policy entropy (e.g., average log probability variance) throughout training to verify that entropy collapse is mitigated relative to baselines.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited to the evaluated model families (Qwen2.5-3B/4B) and task domains (math/coding); extension to other architectures and reasoning types remains untested.
- The theoretical unification claim relies on the paper's own derivations and has not been independently verified by the community.
- Community validation is weak; the work has zero citations and narrow benchmark scope, making it hard to gauge long-term impact.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical unification of PRM methods:** Medium
- **Empirical SOTA gains on specified benchmarks:** Medium
- **PRM utility for test-time training/scaling:** Medium

## Next Checks
