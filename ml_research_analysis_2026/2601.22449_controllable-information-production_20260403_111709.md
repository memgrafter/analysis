# Controllable Information Production

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2601.22449
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22449
- **Reference count**: 33
- **Primary result**: Introduces Controllable Information Production (CIP), an intrinsic motivation principle derived from optimal control that maximizes the gap between open-loop and closed-loop Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy to drive exploration and regulation.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents Controllable Information Production (CIP) as a novel intrinsic motivation derived from optimal control theory. CIP is defined as the non-negative difference between the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy rates of the open-loop and closed-loop dynamical systems, mathematically emerging from the Hessian decomposition of the optimal value function. This formulation avoids the need for designer-specified information transmission variables, instead directly quantifying the agent's ability to regulate chaotic dynamics. Theoretically, CIP connects to the Riccati equation and Lyapunov exponents, providing a measure of controllable chaos. Empirically, the authors implement a model-predictive controller that maximizes CIP to solve pendulum swing-up and stabilization tasks, including the chaotic double pendulum. Results show that the controller induces behaviors that seek high open-loop entropy (exploration) while minimizing closed-loop entropy (exploitation/regulation), effectively navigating to the "edge of chaos" where dynamical complexity is highest.

## Method Summary
The core method is a model predictive control (MPC) loop that optimizes action sequences by maximizing the Controllable Information Production (CIP) objective. For each candidate action sequence, the dynamics are linearized along the simulated trajectory, and a finite-horizon linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) is solved to obtain feedback gains and closed-loop Jacobians. Two backward recursions compute the determinants of open-loop and closed-loop state transition Hessians, whose logarithms scaled by horizon length yield estimates of Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy rates; CIP is their difference. The MPC uses an improved cross-entropy method (iCEM) to sample and refine action distributions based on CIP scores. The system operates entirely on intrinsic motivation with no external reward, and is evaluated on MuJoCo-MJX simulated pendulum environments.

## Key Results
- CIP is mathematically defined as \(h_{ks}(f_{ol}) - h_{ks}(f_{cl}) \ge 0\), emerging from the Hessian decomposition of the optimal value function in optimal control.
- Theoretically, CIP links the Riccati equation and Lyapunov exponents, quantifying the controllable portion of a system's chaotic dynamics.
- Empirically, an MPC maximizing CIP successfully solves single pendulum, cart-pole, and chaotic double pendulum benchmarks, with highest CIP values on the double pendulum and behaviors consistent with operating at the “edge of chaos”.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The optimal policy for perturbation regulation decomposes into extrinsic (goal-directed drift) and intrinsic (goal-agnostic feedback) components, with the intrinsic part solely responsible for chaos regulation.
- **Mechanism**: Under linearized dynamics and quadratic costs, the policy gradient yields \(\pi(x_t) = \bar{u}_t + d_t + \pi_{x_t} \delta x_t\). The term \(\pi_{x_t} = -(c_{uu} + f_u^T V_{xx}^{t+1} f_u)^{-1} f_u^T V_{xx}^{t+1} f_x\) depends only on the Hessian of the value function, not on the cost gradient, making it intrinsic.
- **Core assumption**: Dynamics are linearized around a nominal trajectory; costs are twice differentiable and positive definite.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "CIP appears as the non-negative gap between open-loop and closed-loop Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy... mathematically emerging from the Hessian decomposition of the optimal value function."
  - [section 4.2]: Lemma 4.1 and Equations (3)-(4) explicitly state the decomposition.
  - [corpus] Evidence is weak or missing; neighboring papers do not cover this policy decomposition.
- **Break condition**: If linearization is inaccurate due to strong nonlinearities over long horizons, the decomposition may fail. Also, if cost Hessians are not positive definite, \(\pi_{x_t}\) might not stabilize perturbations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Hessian of the optimal value function simultaneously encodes open-loop and closed-loop Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy rates via two auxiliary backward recursions.
- **Mechanism**: Define recursions: \(Y_t = c_{xx} + f_x^T Y_{t+1} f_x\) (open-loop) and \(X_t = c_{xx} + (f_x + f_u \pi_t)^T X_{t+1} (f_x + f_u \pi_t)\) (closed-loop). Asymptotically, \((1/(2T)) \log \det Y_0 \to h_{ks}(f_{ol})\) and \((1/(2T)) \log \det X_0 \to h_{ks}(f_{cl})\). Their difference is CIP.
- **Core assumption**: Ergodicity of the dynamical system, existence of Lyapunov exponents, and convergence of state-dependent cost matrices to their averages.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "CIP appears as the gap between open-loop and closed-loop Kolmogorov-Sinai entropies, which simultaneously rewards the pursuit and regulation of chaos."
  - [section 4.3]: Lemma 4.3 and Definition 4.2 present the recursions and their limits.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; corpus neighbors discuss intrinsic motivation but not this specific entropy decomposition.
- **Break condition**: Non-ergodic systems or absence of Lyapunov exponents invalidates the limit. Numerical overflow can occur when computing determinants for high-dimensional or long-horizon systems.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Maximizing CIP drives the agent toward the "edge of chaos"—states with high open-loop entropy (rich, chaotic dynamics) but low closed-loop entropy (high controllability).
- **Mechanism**: CIP = \(h_{ks}(f_{ol}) - h_{ks}(f_{cl})\). Large values indicate that the environment produces much uncertainty without feedback, yet feedback can suppress it. Optimizing this gap encourages exploration of chaotic regions while seeking control influence.
- **Core assumption**: Open-loop entropy can be estimated accurately; closed-loop entropy reduction is feasible via feedback.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "inducing behaviors that seek high open-loop entropy (exploration) while minimizing closed-loop entropy (exploitation/regulation)... edge of chaos."
  - [section 5.2]: Empirical results show CIP values correlate with dynamical complexity; double pendulum has highest CIP and the agent stabilizes at slightly off-vertical angles, consistent with edge-of-chaos behavior.
  - [corpus] Indirect support: neighboring papers on intrinsic motivation explore related concepts like "edge of chaos" (e.g., Zhang et al. 2025, Tower 2024), but not specifically for CIP.
- **Break condition**: In deterministic systems, open-loop entropy may be zero, yielding no CIP signal. If the system is uncontrollable (e.g., perturbations orthogonal to control inputs), open-loop and closed-loop entropies coincide, giving low CIP regardless of state.

## Foundational Learning
### Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy
- **Why needed here**: CIP is defined as a difference of KSE rates; KSE quantifies the asymptotic information production rate of a dynamical system, foundational to understanding what CIP measures.
- **Quick check question**: What is Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy and how does Pesin's theorem relate it to Lyapunov exponents?

### Lyapunov exponents
- **Why needed here**: The proof that the recursions converge to KSE uses Oseledets' multiplicative ergodic theorem and Lyapunov exponents; they characterize the exponential divergence of trajectories that underlies entropy production.
- **Quick check question**: Why do only positive Lyapunov exponents contribute to the asymptotic log-determinant in the proof of Lemma 4.3?

### Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) and Riccati equation
- **Why needed here**: The derivation of CIP hinges on the Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation (DARE) that the value function Hessian satisfies; the policy decomposition and entropy extraction both rely on LQR theory.
- **Quick check question**: How does the Riccati equation enable the decomposition of the value Hessian into open-loop and closed-loop components?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Dynamics model -> Linearization module -> LQR solver -> Entropy estimator -> MPC optimizer (iCEM) -> Execution

### Critical Path
1. At current state \(x\), sample batch of action sequences from distribution \(D\).
2. For each sequence, simulate state trajectory, linearize dynamics at each step.
3. Solve finite-horizon LQR along that trajectory to obtain feedback gains \(\pi_t\) and closed-loop Jacobians.
4. Perform backward recursions to compute \(X_0\) (closed-loop) and \(Y_0\) (open-loop) determinants.
5. Compute CIP score for the sequence.
6. Update \(D\) to elite sequences (high CIP) via iCEM (fit Gaussian).
7. Execute first action of mean elite sequence; repeat.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Horizon length \(T\)**: longer gives better asymptotic approximation but increases computational load and may exacerbate numerical issues.
- **Cost matrices \(c_{xx}, c_{uu}\)**: should be positive definite; they influence the feedback gains and thus closed-loop entropy. Choice may affect behavior; paper uses arbitrary PD matrices.
- **Linearization accuracy**: using a single nominal trajectory per candidate may be insufficient for highly nonlinear systems; iterated LQR or more samples could improve robustness.
- **Entropy estimation**: direct log-det of large matrices is expensive; low-rank approximations or Lyapunov exponent sums may scale better but were not used in the paper.

### Failure Signatures
- **Numerical overflow/underflow in determinant calculations**, especially for open-loop entropy (\(Y_0\)). Mitigation: use log-space computations and regularization.
- **CIP values uniformly near zero**: may indicate either that the system is too controllable (closed-loop entropy close to open-loop) or too chaotic to control; check dynamics and cost scaling.
- **Failure to achieve swing-up in pendulum tasks**: could be due to insufficient exploration (open-loop entropy underestimated) or poor stabilization (closed-loop entropy not reduced). Monitor component entropy values.
- **Divergent behavior in double pendulum**: might arise from numerical instability in Riccati recursion for highly unstable open-loop systems; consider tightening control cost or using shorter horizons.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the numerical instability in computing open-loop Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy be mitigated for systems with high information production rates?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state: "One limitation of the current implementation lies in the numerical calculation of open-loop entropy. Our approach involves backward recursions which can develop extremely large values... This issue is most pronounced for open-loop entropy and can lead to numerical instability in systems with very high information production rates."
- **Why unresolved**: Current backward recursion becomes numerically unstable in highly chaotic regimes, limiting applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A revised computational scheme (e.g., via regularization, alternative formulations) that maintains theoretical guarantees while remaining stable and accurate.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can Controllable Information Production be integrated into scalable reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., policy gradient, actor-critic) for high-dimensional and long-horizon tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state: "A second limitation is the simplicity of the controller... it does not scale well to high-dimensional or long-horizon tasks. An important direction for future work is to integrate CIP into learning-based controllers, such as policy gradient or actor–critic methods."
- **Why unresolved**: The current random-shooting MPC approach is sample-inefficient and computationally burdensome for complex environments.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Successful implementation of CIP as an intrinsic objective in standard RL algorithms, demonstrating learning on high-dimensional benchmarks (e.g., robotic manipulation from pixels).

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the effect of combining Controllable Information Production with extrinsic reward signals on sample efficiency, robustness, and final performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state: "Finally, an exciting avenue for future research is the integration of CIP with extrinsic objectives... it provides a principled foundation for studying how intrinsic and extrinsic motivations interact."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only considers pure intrinsic motivation; the interplay with extrinsic goals remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic experiments comparing pure intrinsic, pure extrinsic, and combined objectives across diverse tasks, measuring sample efficiency, robustness to perturbations, and asymptotic performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the theoretical derivation of CIP extend to nonlinear, stochastic, or partially observable dynamical systems, or does it fundamentally rely on linearization and full state observability?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The derivation assumes linearized deterministic dynamics (Section 4.1) and uses Lyapunov exponents defined for such systems. The paper does not address how CIP would be defined or computed for general nonlinear, stochastic, or POMDP settings.
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world systems often involve nonlinearities, noise, and partial observability; the current theory may not directly apply.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A generalization of the CIP framework to broader dynamical system classes (e.g., via stochastic linearization, information-theoretic alternatives) with empirical validation.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Is CIP theoretically equivalent to, or does it subsume, existing information-theoretic intrinsic motivation objectives (e.g., Empowerment, Curiosity) under certain conditions?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors critique existing IM methods for requiring designer-specified variables for information transmission. They claim CIP avoids this by focusing on information production, but no formal comparison is provided. The relationship between CIP and mutual-information-based IM remains unclear.
- **Why unresolved**: Without such analysis, it is uncertain whether CIP is a special case, a generalization, or fundamentally distinct from prior IM objectives.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Mathematical analysis deriving conditions under which CIP reduces to known IM objectives, or proof of distinction; empirical comparisons on shared benchmarks.

## Limitations
- **Underspecified cost matrices**: The exact positive-definite state and control cost matrices used in the LQR recursions are not reported, which significantly affects feedback gains and entropy estimates.
- **Unstabilized open-loop entropy computation**: The numerical method for handling the open-loop recursion's instability in chaotic systems (critical for double pendulum experiments) is not described, risking overflow/underflow.

## Confidence
- **High**: Theoretical derivation (policy decomposition, entropy recursions) is mathematically grounded in LQR/Riccati theory with stated lemmas.
- **Medium**: Empirical success on benchmarks is claimed but relies on unreported hyperparameters; scalability to higher dimensions is untested.
- **Low**: Broader claims about "edge of chaos" as a universal principle have weak corpus support; applicability beyond deterministic, differentiable systems is unclear.

## Next Checks
