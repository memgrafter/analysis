# Detecting Overflow in Compressed Token Representations for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12235
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12235
- **Reference Count:** 12
- **Primary Result:** Overflow in compressed token representations (e.g., from xRAG) is detectable via lightweight probing of joint query-context embeddings immediately after projection, achieving ~0.72 ROC-AUC, whereas query-agnostic saturation statistics fail for this task.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses a critical failure mode in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that use soft compression architectures like xRAG to reduce context length. The authors formally define *token overflow* as the condition where a compressed token fails to contain sufficient query-relevant information, causing a correct answer in the uncompressed setting to become incorrect. They demonstrate that while query-agnostic saturation statistics (e.g., Hoyer’s sparsity, spectral entropy) reliably distinguish compressed tokens from uncompressed ones, these metrics are ineffective for predicting overflow. Instead, the paper introduces a practical detection methodology: training lightweight probing classifiers on joint query-context representations *immediately after* the compression projection, before LLM inference. This approach achieves strong detection performance (average 0.72 ROC-AUC) across three QA datasets, proving the overflow signal exists at the projection stage and enabling efficient pre-LLM gating. The findings shift the paradigm from query-independent diagnostics to query-aware detectors that model the alignment between the query and compressed context.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates overflow detection within the xRAG architecture, where a retriever (SFR-Embedding-Mistral) yields passage embeddings that are compressed by a lightweight projector into a fixed number of tokens. Overflow is operationally defined: an instance is labeled as overflow if the LLM’s answer is correct with the full (uncompressed) context but incorrect with the compressed context. The core detection method involves training linear or MLP probing classifiers on frozen embeddings from the projection stage. Key comparisons include probes using (a) only compressed token embeddings, (b) joint query-compressed token embeddings, and (c) saturation statistics computed from the embeddings. Performance is measured via ROC-AUC on overflow classification across HotpotQA, SQuADv2, and TriviaQA. The methodology confirms that the joint representation probe works *before* LLM processing and significantly outperforms context-only or query-agnostic baselines.

## Key Results
- Saturation statistics (Hoyer’s sparsity, spectral entropy, kurtosis) show large relative differences (7–87%) between compressed and uncompressed tokens and achieve >0.95 AUC for distinguishing token types, but perform near-random (~0.5 AUC) on overflow prediction.
- Lightweight probing classifiers on joint query-context representations from the projection stage achieve an average 0.72 ROC-AUC across datasets, matching the performance of probes applied to middle-layer LLM hidden states, thus detecting overflow pre-LLM inference.
- Incorporating query information into the probe is essential: context-only probes underperform joint probes by 5–8 AUC points, while saturation statistics combined with joint representations yield poor performance (0.58–0.63 AUC).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Saturation statistics reliably distinguish between compressed (xRAG) and uncompressed tokens because the compression projection fundamentally alters the activation geometry, producing a separable distributional shape (e.g., flatter, higher entropy).
**Evidence:** Consistent, large (>50%) differences in kurtosis and spectral entropy across layers; linear classifiers achieve >0.95 AUC for token-type separation (section 4.2.1, Tables 2,4,5).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Token overflow is detectable by modeling the alignment between joint query-context representations immediately after projection, as overflow arises from a mismatch between the information encoded in the compressed token and the information demanded by the query.
**Evidence:** Pre-inference (post-projection) probing matches post-inference performance (~0.72 AUC), proving the signal exists before LLM processing (section 4.2.2).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Query-agnostic metrics fail at overflow detection because overflow is a relational failure mode, not an absolute degradation. A compressed token may be geometrically similar to other compressed tokens yet be sufficient for some queries and insufficient for others.
**Evidence:** Saturation stats achieve near-perfect token-type classification but near-random overflow prediction; joint representations outperform context-only models by 5–8 points (section 4.2.3).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Retriever (SFR-Embedding-Mistral) -> Compressor/Projector (xRAG module) -> Frozen LLM (xRAG-7B). Overflow Probe (classifier) acts on the output of the Projector, alongside the query embedding, to gate whether to proceed with the LLM.
- **Critical path:** Input -> Retriever Embeddings -> **Projector** -> **Probe** (gating decision) -> LLM. The probe is positioned immediately after the projector.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Stage:* Projection-stage probing is efficient (no LLM forward pass) but may lack higher-order context; middle-layer probing is equally accurate but costly.  
  - *Features:* Joint query-context embeddings strongly outperform saturation statistics and context-only representations.  
  - *Complexity:* Linear probes nearly match more complex MLP/SCL probes, favoring simplicity for gating.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Saturation statistics yield ~0.5 AUC for overflow prediction despite >0.95 AUC for token-type separation.  
  - Context-complexity features (length, PPL, compressibility) show near-random performance.  
  - Context-only probes underperform joint probes by 5–8 AUC points.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate token-type separability: Train a linear classifier on saturation stats to distinguish xRAG vs. non-xRAG tokens; expect >0.95 AUC.  
  2. Validate pre-inference detection: Train a joint probe on post-projection embeddings and compare to a middle-layer probe; expect statistically equivalent performance (~0.72 AUC).  
  3. Ablate query information: Compare a probe using only compressed token embeddings vs. joint query-context embeddings; expect a significant drop (e.g., ~0.65 to ~0.72 AUC).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can reliable query-agnostic metrics for overflow be developed, given current saturation statistics only distinguish compressed tokens but fail to correlate with task performance loss?
- To what extent are the findings on pre-inference overflow detection generalizable to longer contexts, higher compression ratios, and non-extractive tasks?
- Is the reliance on joint query-context representations for overflow detection an artifact of xRAG’s projector design, or a fundamental requirement for all soft compression methods?
- Can overflow detection be improved beyond 0.72 AUC-ROC by incorporating richer architectural features of the compression module itself (e.g., projector weights, training objectives)?

## Limitations
- **Architectural generalizability:** Findings are tied to xRAG’s specific single-layer MLP projector; unknown if the signal persists for more aggressive or non-linear compressors (e.g., VAEs, auto-regressive encoders).
- **Probe generalizability & cost:** The probe is trained on task-specific QA data; its portability to unseen domains, query types, or LLM sizes without retraining is unprobed, and the cost of maintaining this additional classifier is not analyzed.
- **Signal mechanism:** The precise geometric or informational property in the projection space that correlates with overflow (e.g., subspace misalignment, norm collapse) is not dissected, limiting theoretical interpretation.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1:** Saturation stats reliably distinguish compressed vs. uncompressed tokens. **Confidence: High** (consistent >0.95 AUC across metrics and layers).
- **Claim 2:** Saturation stats fail at overflow prediction (near-random). **Confidence: High** (explicit results show ~0.5 AUC, contrasting sharply with Claim 1).
- **Claim 3:** Lightweight probing on joint query-context representations detects overflow pre-LLM inference (~0.72 ROC-AUC). **Confidence: Medium** (solid across three datasets, but generalizability to other models/compression schemes is unprobed).
- **Claim 4:** Overflow signal exists immediately after projection, prior to LLM processing. **Confidence: Medium** (pre-inference probe matches middle-layer probe, but reliant on xRAG-7B and may not hold if LLM processing fundamentally alters the signal).

## Next Checks
1. Test probe generalizability across compression architectures: Train and evaluate the overflow probe on datasets generated by alternative soft compressors (e.g., ICAE, Autoencoder-based context compressors) to check if the joint-query signal is specific to xRAG or universal.
2. Evaluate zero-shot transfer of the overflow probe: Apply a probe trained on HotpotQA/SQuAD/TriviaQA to a completely different retrieval-augmented task (e.g., technical QA, summarization) without retraining to assess whether the detector learns a task-agnostic overflow concept.
3. Analyze the projector’s output space geometrically: Perform representational similarity analysis (RSA) or canonical correlation analysis (CCA) between query embeddings, compressed token embeddings, and uncompressed context embeddings to identify the specific structural attribute (e.g., reduced rank, directional bias) leveraged by the probe.
