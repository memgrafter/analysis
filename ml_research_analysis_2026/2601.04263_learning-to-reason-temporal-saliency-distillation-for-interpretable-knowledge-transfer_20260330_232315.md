# Learning to Reason: Temporal Saliency Distillation for Interpretable Knowledge Transfer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04263
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04263
- **Reference count:** 39
- **Primary result:** Knowledge distillation for time series using computer vision techniques lacks temporal interpretability and transfers limited knowledge, causing student predictive distributions to diverge from teachers. We propose Temporal Saliency Distillation (TSD), which computes timestep importance via perturbation-based KL divergence on teacher logits and trains students to match this saliency, requiring no additional parameters. On 28 UCR datasets, TSD achieves the lowest average rank (2.18) in AUC-PRC among all KD methods, with 16 wins. It improves fidelity: higher test top-1 agreement and lower predictive KL than vanilla KD. Interpretability transfer is evidenced by reduced MSE in teacher-student saliency maps across multiple explanation methods. TSD is model-agnostic and robust to noise and data scarcity.

## Executive Summary
Knowledge distillation (KD) for time series classification typically transfers only predictive information, ignoring temporal interpretability. This paper addresses this limitation by proposing Temporal Saliency Distillation (TSD), which transfers both predictive knowledge and timestep-level importance information from teacher to student models. The key innovation is computing timestep importance via perturbation-based KL divergence on teacher logits and training students to match this temporal saliency, achieving superior performance and interpretability transfer without adding parameters.

## Method Summary
TSD computes timestep importance by measuring how much each timestep contributes to the teacher's predictive distribution through perturbation-based KL divergence. The method then trains students to both predict like the teacher and match the temporal saliency patterns. This dual objective transfers both predictive knowledge and interpretability from teacher to student, addressing the key limitation that standard KD methods focus only on predictions while ignoring which timesteps matter most for classification.

## Key Results
- TSD achieves lowest average rank (2.18) in AUC-PRC across 28 UCR datasets among all KD methods, winning on 16 datasets
- Improves fidelity with higher test top-1 agreement and lower predictive KL than vanilla KD
- Demonstrates interpretability transfer through reduced MSE in teacher-student saliency maps across multiple explanation methods
- Maintains model-agnosticism and robustness to noise and data scarcity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by leveraging perturbation-based saliency computation to identify which timesteps are most important for the teacher's predictions. By measuring KL divergence changes when individual timesteps are perturbed, the method creates a quantitative importance score for each timestep. Training students to match both the teacher's predictions and saliency patterns ensures that the student learns not just what to predict, but also which temporal patterns are most informative, leading to better generalization and interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Distillation:** Transfer learning technique where a smaller model (student) learns from a larger model (teacher). Needed because standard KD ignores temporal interpretability, causing predictive distributions to diverge. Quick check: Verify teacher-student performance gap on baseline KD.
- **Temporal Saliency:** Importance of individual timesteps in time series classification. Needed because time series data has inherent temporal structure that standard KD methods overlook. Quick check: Compare saliency maps between teacher and student models.
- **Perturbation-based Attribution:** Measuring input importance by observing prediction changes when inputs are modified. Needed to quantify timestep importance without requiring model access or gradients. Quick check: Validate saliency computation on simple interpretable datasets.
- **KL Divergence:** Information-theoretic measure of distribution difference. Needed to quantify how much each timestep contributes to predictive uncertainty. Quick check: Ensure KL divergence values are stable across perturbations.
- **AUC-PRC:** Area under Precision-Recall Curve. Needed because it's more informative than ROC for imbalanced datasets common in UCR benchmarks. Quick check: Verify PR curves are well-behaved before computing AUC.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Teacher model -> Saliency computation (perturbation + KL divergence) -> Student model training (KL loss + saliency matching loss)

### Critical Path
Perturbation of individual timesteps → KL divergence measurement → Saliency map generation → Student training with combined loss

### Design Tradeoffs
The method trades computational overhead (multiple forward passes for saliency) for improved interpretability transfer and predictive performance. No additional parameters are added, maintaining model efficiency, but training time increases due to saliency computation.

### Failure Signatures
- Saliency maps that are uniform across timesteps indicate perturbation computation issues
- High predictive KL between teacher and student suggests poor knowledge transfer
- Low agreement on saliency patterns indicates students aren't learning temporal reasoning

## Limitations
- Perturbation-based saliency computation requires multiple forward passes per timestep, potentially expensive for long sequences
- The "no additional parameters" claim may be misleading as method increases computational overhead during training
- Results are limited to UCR datasets, restricting generalizability to other time series domains
- Comparison is limited to KD methods, excluding other temporal interpretability techniques
- Fidelity improvement metrics use relative improvements without establishing statistical significance
