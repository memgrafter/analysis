# Enhancing Structural Mapping with LLM-derived Abstractions for Analogical Reasoning in Narratives

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29997
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29997
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** YARN improves narrative analogy performance by using LLMs to extract and abstract events across multiple levels before applying structural mapping, with notable gains on far analogies over end-to-end LLM baselines.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles analogical reasoning in narratives, a task where models typically rely on superficial lexical overlap rather than deeper structural relationships. The authors introduce YARN, a modular framework that first uses an LLM to identify key event phrases in stories, then abstracts these events across four conceptual levels (conceptual, evaluative, narrative arc, stage). These abstractions are fed into a structural mapping algorithm to align elements between a source and target narrative. Evaluated on the ARN and MCQ benchmarks, YARN achieves consistent improvements, particularly on far analogies where surface similarity is low and structural reasoning is essential. The results demonstrate that explicit abstraction can compensate for the lack of structured representations in standard LLMs.

## Method Summary
YARN operates in three modular stages. First, an LLM extracts atomic event phrases from narrative texts. Second, each event is abstracted along four dimensions: conceptual (generalized action/object), evaluative (valence/impact), narrative arc (story function), and stage (plot position). These abstractions transform surface details into higher-level representations. Third, a structural mapping algorithm (inspired by SME) computes alignments between the abstracted source and target events, selecting the best analogy based on mapping quality. The framework is evaluated on the Analogy Recall in Narratives (ARN) dataset and a multiple-choice MCQ benchmark, comparing against end-to-end LLM prompting (zero-shot, CoT) and prior methods.

## Key Results
- YARN with Qwen achieves 0.46 accuracy on MCQ, surpassing the LLM-CoT baseline (0.41) and approaching human performance.
- On ARN far analogies (low surface similarity), YARN matches or exceeds zero-shot LLM performance, while baselines drop significantly.
- The benefit of abstraction increases as surface similarity between narratives decreases; different abstraction levels show varying effectiveness depending on the analogy type.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LLM-derived abstractions act as a noise filter, reducing dependence on exact lexical matches and highlighting relational and thematic parallels. By separating extraction, abstraction, and mapping, YARN imposes a structured reasoning pipeline that mimics human analogical thinking: identify key elements, generalize their roles, then align structures. The multi-level abstraction allows the system to emphasize different aspects (e.g., evaluative tone vs. plot progression) that may be critical for specific analogies, especially when surface features are misleading. Structural mapping then operates on these cleaner, more relational signals, improving alignment accuracy for far analogies where end-to-end LLMs fail due to over-reliance on surface patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structural Mapping Theory (SME):** Why needed: Provides a formal framework for aligning relational structures between two domains, moving beyond feature matching. Quick check: Does the alignment preserve higher-order relations (e.g., cause-effect) rather than just object similarities?
- **Narrative Abstraction Granularity:** Why needed: Narratives contain implicit elements at multiple levels (events, emotions, plot); abstraction must capture the right granularity to support analogy. Quick check: Can the model generalize "saving a cat" to "helping someone in distress" without losing narrative coherence?
- **Surface vs. Structural Similarity:** Why needed: Far analogies rely on deep structural commonalities despite surface differences; models must learn to ignore spurious lexical overlaps. Quick check: Does performance degrade when surface features are artificially matched but structures misaligned?
- **LLM-based Event Extraction:** Why needed: Narratives are unstructured text; extracting discrete events is a prerequisite for systematic analysis. Quick check: Does the extraction cover all pivotal actions without significant omissions or hallucinations?
- **Evaluative Abstraction:** Why needed: Analogies often hinge on emotional or moral parallels (e.g., betrayal, sacrifice) that are not explicit in actions. Quick check: Can the abstraction layer capture the valence shift (e.g., from hope to despair) across a narrative arc?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Event Extraction (LLM) -> Multi-level Abstraction -> Structural Mapping -> Analogy Decision  
**Critical path:** Raw narrative text → LLM identifies event phrases → Each phrase is abstracted at four levels → Abstracted events from source and target are fed to mapping algorithm → Alignment score determines analogy strength.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Modularity improves interpretability and allows targeted debugging but introduces error propagation between stages; abstraction levels balance expressiveness vs. noise—too abstract loses narrative specificity, too concrete retains surface bias.  
**Failure signatures:** Extraction misses key events; abstraction produces incoherent or irrelevant tags; mapping returns high scores for semantically unrelated pairs; performance collapses on narratives with implicit causality or metaphor.  
**First experiments:** 1) Ablate each abstraction level individually to quantify its contribution. 2) Swap LLM backbones (e.g., GPT vs. Qwen) to gauge prompt sensitivity. 3) Test on a zero-shot cross-domain narrative (e.g., fables to news) to evaluate generalization.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to two English-language narrative datasets; generalizability to other genres, cultures, or languages remains unknown.
- LLM-generated abstractions lack human validation; it is unclear whether they align with human-derived narrative representations or analogical reasoning.
- No statistical significance testing or detailed implementation specifics; modest gains could be prompt-sensitive or dataset-specific.
- Abstraction granularity, handling of implicit causality, and brittleness in the mapping stage are acknowledged as unresolved challenges.

## Confidence
- **Performance improvement on benchmarks:** Medium (consistent across two datasets but gains modest, no significance tests)
- **Abstractions particularly aid far analogies:** Medium (supported by analysis but possible confounding from prompt engineering)
- **Abstraction effectiveness varies with surface similarity:** Low-Medium (correlational finding, not causally isolated)
- **Modular design enhances interpretability:** Low (no human evaluation of explanation quality)
