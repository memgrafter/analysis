# Conformalized Neural Networks for Federated Uncertainty Quantification under Dual Heterogeneity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23296
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23296
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** FedWQ-CP achieves nominal 95% coverage globally and per-agent under dual heterogeneity while producing the smallest prediction sets/intervals among baselines, using only one communication round and two scalars per agent.

## Executive Summary
Federated learning (FL) faces severe challenges in reliable uncertainty quantification due to joint data and model heterogeneity across agents, which can lead to silent failures—particularly for under-resourced (weak) agents. This paper introduces FedWQ-CP, a one-shot federated conformal predictor that remedies this. Each agent computes a local nonconformity score quantile from its calibration data and transmits it, along with its calibration size, to a server. The server aggregates these via a weighted average (weighted by local calibration size) to form a global threshold, which all agents then use to construct prediction sets. Evaluated on seven public benchmarks with six agents (three strong, three weak) under Dirichlet-induced calibration shifts, FedWQ-CP empirically maintains target coverage at both agent and global levels while achieving superior efficiency (smaller sets/intervals) compared to prior federated conformal methods. Ablation studies confirm that sample-size weighting is essential for weak-agent coverage.

## Method Summary
FedWQ-CP is a one-shot protocol for federated conformal prediction. Each of *K* agents first trains a local model potentially heterogeneous in architecture and training intensity (e.g., strong CNN vs. weak linear). Using its local calibration set *D*ₖᶜᵃˡ, an agent computes nonconformity scores (e.g., APS for classification, CQR for regression) for all calibration examples. From these scores, it extracts the (1−α) empirical quantile *q*ₖ = *V*ₖ,(*⌈(**n**ₖ+1)(1−α)⌉)*, where *n*ₖ is its calibration size. It then sends only the two scalars (*q*ₖ, *n*ₖ) to a central server. The server aggregates these into a global threshold via weighted averaging: $\hat{q} = \sum_{k=1}^K (n_k / N) q_k$, where $N = \sum n_k$. It broadcasts $\hat{q}$ back to all agents. Finally, each agent constructs its conformal prediction set for a new test point *x* as $C_k(x) = \{ y : V_k(x,y) \le \hat{q} \}$ for classification, or a calibrated interval $[q_{\text{low}}(x) - \hat{q},\; q_{\text{up}}(x) + \hat{q}]$ for regression. The entire process requires only a single round of communication and no sharing of raw scores or model updates.

## Key Results
- FedWQ-CP maintains both global and per-agent marginal coverage at the nominal 95% level across all seven datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, DermaMNIST, BloodMNIST, TissueMNIST, RetinaMNIST) under dual heterogeneity.
- It achieves the smallest average prediction set size (classification) and interval length (regression) among compared baselines (FCP, CPhet, DP-FedCP).
- Ablation shows that unweighted aggregation (FedAvgQ-CP) leads to significant under-coverage on weak agents, demonstrating the necessity of calibration-size weighting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Local Quantile Normalization
**Claim:** Local calibration per agent normalizes heterogeneous model scales via rank-based quantiles.  
**Mechanism:** Each agent computes its own nonconformity score quantile *q*ₖ from its calibration data, making the threshold invariant to monotonic transformations of the score distribution. This accounts for architecture-specific "temperature" and variance differences across heterogeneous local models.  
**Core assumption:** Heterogeneous architectures produce nonconformity scores with different distributions but preserve ordinal ranking within each agent's calibration set.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "each agent computes a local nonconformity score quantile threshold"; Section 3 notes "quantiles are invariant to monotone transformations of the scores within each agent"; prior work (e.g., FedCP-QQ) pools scores directly and fails under model heterogeneity.  
**Break condition:** If all agents use identical architectures and training, local quantization adds negligible benefit but still functions.

### Mechanism 2: Calibration-Size-Weighted Aggregation
**Claim:** Weighted aggregation stabilizes the global threshold against noisy quantiles from small, weak-agent calibration sets.  
**Mechanism:** The server computes $\hat{q} = \sum (n_k/N) q_k$, reducing the influence of agents with small *n*ₖ (typically weak agents) whose empirical quantiles have higher variance. The asymptotic variance of a sample quantile scales as *O*(1/*n*ₖ).  
**Core assumption:** Weak agents have smaller calibration sets and higher-variance score distributions, leading to unreliable quantile estimates; weighting by *n*ₖ down-weights this noise.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 mentions "statistical reliability (via the weight *n*ₖ/*N*)"; Section 6 ablation (FedAvgQ-CP) shows unweighted aggregation causes systematic under-coverage on weak agents; Section 3.1 cites the $O(1/n_k)$ variance scaling.  
**Break condition:** If all agents have equal *n*ₖ, weighting has no effect; if strong agents have very small *n*ₖ, they may be underweighted despite reliability.

### Mechanism 3: One-Shot, Scalar-Only Communication
**Claim:** One-shot communication (two scalars per agent) provides privacy-preserving, low-overhead calibration suitable for resource-constrained FL.  
**Mechanism:** Agents transmit only (*q*ₖ, *n*ₖ); server aggregates and broadcasts global $\hat{q}$ in a single round. No raw scores, model parameters, or iterative optimization are exchanged.  
**Core assumption:** Privacy regulations prohibit sharing raw data; communication efficiency is critical for edge devices.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Algorithm 1 show single communication round; Table 1 contrasts FedWQ-CP's "One-shot" and "No Iter. Opt." with DP-FedCP and CPhet.  
**Break condition:** If calibration distributions are extremely mismatched (e.g., severe covariate shift), one-shot aggregation may be insufficient; iterative refinement (as in FedCP-QQ) might help but at higher cost.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Agents (local model *f*ₖ, calibration set *D*ₖᶜᵃˡ, score function *V*, local quantile *q*ₖ, size *n*ₖ) -> Server (aggregation: $\hat{q} = \sum (n_k/N) q_k$, broadcaster) -> Communication (send (*q*ₖ, *n*ₖ); receive $\hat{q}$)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Agent computes scores *V*ₖ(*x,y*) on *D*ₖᶜᵃˡ.  
  2. Agent derives *q*ₖ = *V*ₖ,(*⌈(**n**ₖ+1)(1−α)⌉)*.  
  3. Agent sends (*q*ₖ, *n*ₖ) to server.  
  4. Server computes $\hat{q} = \sum (n_k/N) q_k$ and broadcasts.  
  5. Agent constructs set *C*ₖ(*x*) = { *y* : *V*ₖ(*x,y*) ≤ $\hat{q}$ }.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Simplicity & efficiency vs. theoretical guarantees: FedWQ-CP has no finite-sample coverage guarantee under heterogeneity (only asymptotic) but is highly communication-efficient.  
  - Weighted vs. unweighted: Weighting by *n*ₖ improves weak-agent coverage but assumes *n*ₖ correlates with quantile reliability.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Under-coverage on weak agents if unweighted (Fig 2).  
  - Overly conservative sets if $\hat{q}$ is too high due to outlier strong-agent quantiles.  
  - Violated coverage if Dirichlet *β* is very small and models are highly diverse.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Homogeneous model sanity check:** Use identical architectures for all agents, vary only Dirichlet *β*. Expect near-nominal coverage globally and per-agent.  
  2. **Weighting necessity test:** Compare FedWQ-CP vs. FedAvgQ-CP (unweighted) with heterogeneous models and *β*=0.3. Expect significant under-coverage on weak agents in unweighted.  
  3. **Baseline comparison:** Run FedWQ-CP against FCP, CPhet, DP-FedCP on CIFAR-10 and RetinaMNIST. Expect FedWQ-CP to achieve target coverage with smallest set sizes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Under what conditions does weighted quantile aggregation accurately approximate the pooled conformal quantile, and how does error scale with heterogeneity?  
  **Basis:** Theorem 2 requires |$\hat{q}$ − *q*ₘᵢₓ| → 0 for asymptotic coverage; Proposition 2 bounds error but assumes local densities bounded and local quantiles close.  
  **Why unresolved:** No finite-sample characterization; conditions for negligible error relative to other errors are not quantified.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Finite-sample bounds on |$\hat{q}$ − *q*ₘᵢₓ| in terms of heterogeneity metrics; empirical phase diagram of error vs. heterogeneity.
- **Question:** Is calibration-size weighting optimal, or could other weighting schemes improve coverage and efficiency?  
  **Basis:** Ablation (Fig 2) shows unweighted causes under-coverage on weak agents, demonstrating "necessity of sample-size-aware aggregation." No optimality proof or alternative weight comparisons.  
  **Why unresolved:** Sample-size weighting is motivated by variance reduction, but model strength also affects quantile accuracy; optimal trade-off unknown.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Systematic experiments comparing different weight functions; theoretical analysis linking weights to minimax quantile estimation risk under heterogeneity.
- **Question:** How does FedWQ-CP perform when training distributions are also heterogeneous, beyond isolated calibration heterogeneity?  
  **Basis:** Assumption 1 isolates calibration heterogeneity only; paper states this design "isolates calibration heterogeneity" and is not meant to model fully general cross-silo training heterogeneity.  
  **Why unresolved:** Real FL has both training and calibration heterogeneity; interaction effects are unknown.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Experiments with heterogeneous training splits; evaluation under combined shifts; theoretical extensions accounting for multiple distribution shifts.
- **Question:** What are finite-sample, high-probability coverage guarantees, and how do calibration size, number of agents, and heterogeneity affect them?  
  **Basis:** Theorem 2 is purely asymptotic; Remark 3 states it "does not imply finite-sample coverage guarantees under heterogeneous calibration splits." No finite-sample concentration analysis.  
  **Why unresolved:** Safety-critical applications need finite-sample bounds; worst-case behavior cannot be guaranteed without them.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Derive concentration inequalities for coverage error; validate via simulations at small *n*ₖ; establish required sample sizes for target coverage under heterogeneity.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees are asymptotic only; finite-sample coverage under extreme heterogeneity (tiny calibration sets, severe covariate shift) is unproven.
- Weighted quantile aggregation assumes calibration size (*n*ₖ) correlates with quantile reliability; if strong agents have small *n*ₖ, they may be underweighted, risking global coverage.
- Experiments isolate calibration heterogeneity; performance under combined training and calibration heterogeneity in fully general FL remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Coverage validity:** High – strong empirical support across 7 datasets, though finite-sample theory lacking.
- **Efficiency superiority:** Medium – baseline comparisons shown, but baseline implementations may vary.
- **Weighting necessity:** High – ablation clearly shows unweighted aggregation fails for weak agents.
- **One-shot sufficiency:** Medium – works in tested settings, but may break under extreme shifts.
