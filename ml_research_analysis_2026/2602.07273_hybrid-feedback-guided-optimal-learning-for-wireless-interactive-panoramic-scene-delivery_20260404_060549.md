# Hybrid Feedback-Guided Optimal Learning for Wireless Interactive Panoramic Scene Delivery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07273
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07273
- **Reference count:** 40
- **One-line primary result:** AdaPort achieves asymptotic optimality and reduces relative throughput degradation to 0.5–3.6% versus 3.2–16.1% for bandit-only baselines by leveraging hybrid full-information/bandit feedback.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing panoramic scene delivery for VR/AR streaming, where two distinct feedback signals exist: (1) prediction feedback (whether a transmitted portion covers the user’s viewport) and (2) transmission feedback (packet delivery success). The key insight is that prediction feedback is full-information (retrospectively computable for all portions) while transmission feedback is bandit, forming a novel hybrid 2/F/B feedback model. The proposed AdaPort algorithm uses empirical mean updates for prediction and Thompson sampling for transmission. Theoretically, it achieves an asymptotic regret upper bound that matches the derived instance-dependent lower bound, proving asymptotic optimality. Empirically, using real-world traces, AdaPort significantly reduces throughput degradation compared to state-of-the-art bandit-only baselines, especially under high network reliability.

## Method Summary
AdaPort frames portion selection as a multi-armed bandit problem with N arms (delivery portions). The reward for arm i is the product of two stochastic factors: αᵢ (probability the portion covers the viewport) and βᵢ (probability of successful transmission). The algorithm exploits the hybrid feedback structure: after each transmission, the server receives the user’s actual head pose, enabling backward computation of the coverage outcome Xᵢ(t) for *every* portion i (full-information). Only the transmission outcome Yᵢ₍ₜ₎(t) for the selected portion is observed (bandit). AdaPort maintains:
- An empirical mean α̂ᵢ(t) updated each round using the full vector X(t) (all arms).
- A Beta posterior (Sᵢ, Fᵢ) for βᵢ updated only with Yᵢ₍ₜ₎(t) for the selected arm.
Each round, it draws Thompson samples θᵢ ~ Beta(Sᵢ+1, Fᵢ+1) for all arms and selects i(t) = argmaxᵢ α̂ᵢ(t)·θᵢ.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical:** Proves an instance-dependent lower bound for the 2/F/B model; AdaPort’s asymptotic regret matches it, establishing optimality. The lower bound constant is strictly smaller than for 2/B/B or 1/B bandit models because arms with αᵢ ≤ αᵢ*βᵢ* contribute only constant (not logarithmic) regret.
- **Empirical:** On real head-motion and bandwidth traces, AdaPort reduces relative throughput degradation to 0.5–3.6% (at 100–150 Mbps), compared to 3.2–16.1% for bandit-only baselines. The advantage grows with network reliability (higher βᵢ).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Full-information prediction feedback:** Once the user’s actual head pose arrives in the ACK, the server can retrospectively determine coverage for *all* portions by comparing predicted vs. actual poses. This yields a binary vector X(t) = (Xᵢ(t))ᵢ∈[N] available at the next timeslot, with Xᵢ(t)=1 iff portion i would have covered the viewport.
- **Hybrid learning eliminates prediction exploration:** Traditional bandit models must explore to learn both αᵢ and βᵢ. Under 2/F/B, αᵢ is estimated precisely via the empirical mean of full-information Xᵢ(t), which concentrates rapidly (O(1/t)). The only remaining uncertainty is βᵢ, addressed via Thompson sampling. This avoids unnecessary pulls of arms with clearly insufficient coverage (αᵢ ≤ αᵢ*βᵢ*).
- **Instance-dependent lower bound improvement:** For arms where even perfect transmission (βᵢ=1) cannot overcome a coverage deficit (αᵢ ≤ αᵢ*βᵢ*), the 2/F/B lower bound contribution is zero after a constant number of plays. Bandit-only models force logarithmic plays for all suboptimal arms because neither αᵢ nor βᵢ is ever fully resolved, leading to a larger constant.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-armed bandits (MAB)**
  - *Why needed:* The portion selection maps to an MAB with unknown reward probabilities αᵢβᵢ. Understanding exploration–exploitation trade-offs and classic algorithms (UCB, Thompson Sampling) is essential.
  - *Quick check:* Given two arms with unknown success probabilities 0.7 and 0.8, what is the expected cumulative regret after 100 rounds if you always pick the arm with empirical mean 0.65? (Answer: Depends on how many times the suboptimal arm is pulled; regret = (0.8−0.7)·n₂(T).)
- **Thompson Sampling (TS)**
  - *Why needed:* AdaPort uses Beta-Bernoulli TS to handle bandit transmission feedback. The Bayesian update (Sᵢ, Fᵢ) and posterior concentration properties underpin the theoretical guarantees.
  - *Quick check:* If you pull an arm with prior Beta(1,1) and observe 3 successes and 1 failure, what is the posterior distribution and what value would you sample? (Answer: Posterior Beta(4,2); sample from that distribution.)
- **Full-information vs. bandit feedback**
  - *Why needed:* The core innovation hinges on recognizing prediction feedback as full-information. This distinction determines algorithmic design (empirical mean vs. TS) and regret scaling.
  - *Quick check:* If you observe all arm rewards every round, what’s the optimal regret rate in a stochastic setting? (Answer: O(∑ᵢ log T / Δᵢ), instance-dependent; or O(√(NT)) for adversarial.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Portion selector → Prediction rate estimator (α̂) → Transmission rate estimator (Beta posterior) → Feedback dispatcher → Regret calculator (evaluation). The Thompson sampler draws θ from the Beta posterior for the selector each round.
- **Critical path (per timeslot t):**
  1. Initialize (t=1): α̂ᵢ(1)=0, Sᵢ=Fᵢ=0.
  2. For each t: Draw θᵢ(t) ~ Beta(Sᵢ(t)+1, Fᵢ(t)+1) for all i.
  3. Select i(t) = argmaxᵢ α̂ᵢ(t)·θᵢ(t).
  4. Transmit portion i(t); receive ACK containing user’s actual head pose and transmission success Yᵢ₍ₜ₎(t).
  5. Compute full prediction vector X(t) from predicted vs. actual poses for all i.
  6. Update α̂ᵢ(t+1) = α̂ᵢ(t) + (1/t)(Xᵢ(t) − α̂ᵢ(t)) for all i.
  7. Update selected arm: Sᵢ₍ₜ₎ ← Sᵢ₍ₜ₎ + Yᵢ₍ₜ₎(t), Fᵢ₍ₜ₎ ← Fᵢ₍ₜ₎ + 1 − Yᵢ₍ₜ₎(t); other arms unchanged.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Thompson vs. UCB for bandit component: TS chosen for empirical robustness and Beta conjugacy; UCB would require confidence-bound tuning.
  - Full-information update cost: O(N) per round to update all α̂ᵢ; acceptable for small N (paper uses N=4) but may not scale.
  - Independence assumption: Algorithm assumes X and Y are independent; correlation could bias α̂ estimates.
  - Argmax decision rule: Theoretical bounds rely on pure argmax; practical thresholds might be needed for very low α̂ᵢθᵢ.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High variance in α̂ᵢ across arms after many rounds: indicates prediction failure or broken pose data.
  - All Thompson samples θᵢ converging to similar values: insufficient exploration or nearly identical βᵢ; algorithm cannot discriminate portions.
  - Skewed pull count nᵢ(T) for a non-optimal arm: check if α̂ᵢθᵢ spuriously exceeds optimal due to variance or correlation.
  - Regret degradation at high bandwidth: If AdaPort does not outperform bandit baselines when βᵢ≈1, there may be bugs in feedback separation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic Bernoulli verification:** Generate arms with known (α,β), run AdaPort for T=10⁵, compute regret; compare to theoretical bound ∑_{i≠i*} Δᵢ log T / d(βᵢ, αᵢ*βᵢ*/αᵢ). Constant should match.
  2. **Full-information ablation:** Implement a 2/B/B variant that only observes X for the selected arm. On the same trace, AdaPort should pull arms with αᵢ ≤ αᵢ*βᵢ* far less often.
  3. **Real-trace throughput:** Use provided (or similar) traces. Compute relative throughput degradation; AdaPort should achieve <4% at 100 Mbps and <2% at 150 Mbps, while baselines degrade more.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1:** How can the binary reward model be refined to account for partial delivery failures, and how would this impact learning efficiency and user experience? (Conclusion mentions over-penalizing minor packet losses.)
- **Q2:** Can AdaPort and its regret analysis be extended to nonstationary environments where prediction and transmission rates change over time? (Conclusion mentions exploring nonstationary bandits.)
- **Q3:** Do the lower bound and asymptotic optimality hold when X(t) and Y(t) exhibit temporal or mutual dependence? (Section C notes assumptions of independence and i.i.d. may not hold in practice.)
- **Q4:** How can the hybrid feedback framework be generalized to multi-user scenarios with shared bandwidth constraints? (Related work cites multi-user papers but this paper focuses on single-user.)

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Exact viewport dimensions, portion bit encoding, prediction model specifics, and raw traces are not provided, blocking exact replication and possibly affecting αᵢ, βᵢ estimates.
- **Theoretical assumptions:** Independence of X and Y, deterministic coverage mapping, and argmax robustness under finite samples are not fully validated; violations could weaken regret guarantees.
- **Scalability:** Empirical evaluation only for N=4 fixed portions; performance for larger N or different geometries is unproven.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid 2/F/B feedback model:** High (logical consequence of geometric determinism once poses are known).
- **Instance-dependent lower bound & asymptotic optimality:** Medium (proofs appear rigorous but rely on independence; no independent verification or finite-time bounds).
- **Empirical throughput advantage:** Medium (depends on unreleased traces and assumed parameters; synthetic ablation supports mechanism but real-world generalization uncertain).

## Next Checks

2. **Instance-dependent pull distribution:** On synthetic data with known (αᵢ, βᵢ), plot nᵢ(T). Arms with αᵢ ≤ αᵢ*βᵢ* should be pulled a constant number of times; others should grow logarithmically.
3. **Robustness to prediction degradation:** Inject Gaussian noise into predicted head poses; measure AdaPort’s regret increase relative to bandit-only baselines.
