# Quantifying Cross-Modal Interactions in Multimodal Glioma Survival Prediction via InterSHAP: Evidence for Additive Signal Integration
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29977
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29977
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Architectural improvements in multimodal glioma survival prediction yield higher discrimination (C-index up to 0.819) but exhibit lower measured cross-modal interaction (as low as 3.0%) compared to a simpler baseline (4.8%), indicating gains stem from additive signal aggregation rather than learned synergy.

## Executive Summary
This study adapts the Shapley interaction index (InterSHAP) to Cox survival models to directly test a core assumption in multimodal deep learning: that superior performance arises from learning synergistic cross-modal interactions. Using glioma data (n=575) with histopathology (WSI) and RNA-seq inputs, the authors compare four fusion architectures. Counter to expectation, models with better discrimination (higher C-index) showed lower measured interaction, with variance decomposition revealing predominantly additive contributions (WSI ~40%, RNA ~55%, interaction ~3–5%) across all architectures. The findings suggest that performance gains are driven by improved unimodal feature extraction and stable, linear aggregation, with implications for model simplicity, interpretability, and federated deployment strategies where modalities may be missing.

## Method Summary
The pipeline involves preprocessing TCGA glioma data (WSI patches → ResNet-50 features; RNA-seq → filtered, normalized genes → MLP features) to obtain fixed 2048-dimensional unimodal embeddings. Four fusion architectures (Early MLP, Cross-Attention, Bilinear, Gated) are trained with a Cox partial likelihood loss to predict a scalar log-hazard. After training, InterSHAP is computed on a held-out test set for each patient by evaluating the model's log-risk under four modality coalitions (neither, WSI only, RNA only, both) using mean-imputation for missing modalities. The global interaction percentage is derived from the sum of absolute interaction Shapley values divided by the sum of absolute total Shapley values. This quantifies the proportion of prediction variance attributable to synergistic cross-modal effects.

## Key Results
- **Inverse performance-interaction relationship:** The best-performing model (Bilinear, C-index 0.819) had lower InterSHAP (3.72%) than the simplest baseline (Early MLP, C-index 0.636, InterSHAP 4.82%).
- **Stable additive decomposition:** Across all fusion architectures, the variance decomposition was remarkably consistent: WSI contributed ~40%, RNA ~55%, and interaction ~3–5%.
- **Interaction stability despite design:** Even the Bilinear fusion, which by design forces multiplicative interactions, resulted in only 3.7% measured interaction, reinforcing the predominance of additive signal integration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Superior predictive performance arises from enhanced unimodal signal extraction and additive aggregation, not from increased learning of cross-modal synergy.
- **Mechanism:** Architectural innovations (attention, bilinear terms) improve the quality of modality-specific embeddings or their linear combination, but the final log-risk prediction remains the sum of largely independent unimodal contributions. InterSHAP, measuring the second discrete derivative of the model output with respect to modality coalitions, registers near-zero values for purely additive relationships.
- **Evidence anchors:** Tables showing inverse C-index vs. InterSHAP; Bilinear fusion, which explicitly models interactions, still shows low Interaction (3.7%).
- **Break condition:** An architecture exhibiting both a significantly higher C-index *and* a significantly higher InterSHAP would contradict the observed inverse pattern.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The relative unimodal contribution (WSI ~40%, RNA ~55%) is stable across fusion architectures, indicating reliance is determined by unimodal encoder information content, not the fusion mechanism.
- **Mechanism:** InterSHAP isolates each modality's average absolute contribution to the log-risk. Consistency across fusion modules (MLP, attention, bilinear, gated) implies the fusion layer primarily preserves and weights unimodal signals rather than generating novel cross-modal features.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 shows WSI contributions: 42.2±2.1% (MLP), 43.2±1.8% (Cross-Attention); RNA contributions: 53.0±2.3% across models; interaction ~4.8±0.8%.
- **Break condition:** A modality's contribution percentage varying widely (>15% SD) across architectures would indicate the fusion mechanism significantly redistributes predictive reliance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Patient-level InterSHAP values correlate with survival not due to an independent biological "interaction signal," but because they are deterministic functions of the model's own prognostic risk prediction.
- **Mechanism:** InterSHAP decomposes a prognostic model's output f(x). Any monotonic function of f(x)'s components (e.g., the ratio of interaction to main effects) will inherently correlate with the outcome. This is a form of target leakage within the model's internal explanation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states correlation "reflects model-internal structure rather than independent prognostic evidence"; Section 4.5 references Kaufman et al.'s critique that such correlation is expected.
- **Break condition:** If patient-level InterSHAP remained prognostic after adjusting for the model's raw predicted risk score, it might indicate an orthogonal signal. The paper does not show this adjustment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Shapley Value & Additivity Axiom**
  - **Why needed:** InterSHAP uses the Shapley Interaction Index (SII), which extends Shapley values to quantify pairwise interactions. The additivity axiom states that for a purely additive model, all interaction indices must be zero.
  - **Quick check:** If a model's prediction is exactly the sum of two feature outputs, what should the SII for those two features be? (Answer: Zero.)

- **Concept: Cox Proportional Hazards & Partial Likelihood**
  - **Why needed:** The paper adapts InterSHAP to survival analysis. The Cox model outputs a log-hazard ratio. Computing InterSHAP on this raw score (not its exponent) is crucial to avoid conflating multiplicative survival probabilities with learned interactions.
  - **Quick check:** Why might computing InterSHAP on `exp(log-hazard)` be problematic for measuring interaction? (Answer: The exponential is a nonlinear transformation that can create artificial interactions even if the underlying log-hazard is additive.)

- **Concept: Modality Fusion Strategies**
  - **Why needed:** The core experiment compares four fusion architectures (Early MLP, Cross-Attention, Bilinear, Gated). Understanding their operational differences—concatenation+MLP (implicit interactions), attention (explicit query-key-value), bilinear (forced outer product), gating (dynamic weighting)—is key to mapping the "component map."
  - **Quick check:** Which fusion mechanism *by design* cannot produce zero interaction if both inputs are non-zero? (Answer: Bilinear Fusion, as it explicitly multiplies inputs.)

- **Concept: InterSHAP Computation & Coalition Evaluation**
  - **Why needed:** InterSHAP requires evaluating the model with modalities "present" or "missing." The choice of imputation (mean, zero, shuffle) for missing modalities critically affects measured interaction.
  - **Quick check:** If you use zero-imputation for a missing modality in a ReLU-based network, how might that artificially inflate measured interaction? (Answer: Zero input can silence ReLU units, causing a nonlinear drop in activation that is not representative of the modality's true marginal contribution.)

- **Concept: Variance Decomposition in Model Explanations**
  - **Why needed:** The global InterSHAP percentage is a variance decomposition: total absolute Shapley value is partitioned into main effects (per modality) and interaction effects. This quantifies the proportion of prediction "energy" from synergy.
  - **Quick check:** If a model is purely additive, what should the sum of absolute interaction Shapley values be relative to the sum of absolute main effects? (Answer: Interaction sum should be zero; all variance is in main effects.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Unimodal Encoders (ResNet-50 for WSI, MLP for RNA) → Fusion Heads (Early MLP / Cross-Attention / Bilinear / Gated) → Output Layer (scalar log-hazard) → Loss (Cox partial likelihood) → *Post-training:* Auditing Module (InterSHAP computation on test set).

**Critical path:**  
1. Preprocess TCGA data to fixed 2048D embeddings.  
2. Train fusion head (and optionally encoders) with Cox loss on 80% training data, early stopping on validation C-index.  
3. Freeze model, compute patient-level InterSHAP on held-out 20% test set: evaluate log-risk for four coalitions (∅, {WSI}, {RNA}, {both}) using training-set mean imputation for missing modalities.  
4. Aggregate: Global InterSHAP % = Σ|ϕ_int| / (Σ|ϕ_WSI| + Σ|ϕ_RNA| + Σ|ϕ_int|) × 100%.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Param count vs. Interaction expressivity:* Bilinear (0.54M) forces multiplicative terms but yields low InterSHAP (3.7%), suggesting forced terms may be redundant or cancelled. Cross-Attention (1.8M) is most expressive but also low (3.0%).
- *Masking strategy:* Mean imputation is default. Zero imputation causes distribution shift → artificially high interaction (12.13%). Random shuffle is viable alternative.
- *Pre-training independence:* Encoders are pre-trained separately. If encoders were trained jointly with fusion, interaction might be absorbed earlier, lowering measured InterSHAP at the modality level.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Elevated InterSHAP (>10%) with zero-imputation:** Indicates model relies on input distribution statistics; not a true synergy.
2. **High variance in per-architecture InterSHAP (CV >20%):** Suggests unstable training or that interaction is dataset-seed dependent.
3. **One modality contribution <15%:** Risk of "modality collapse"; the model ignores one input.
4. **Replication gap between architectures but similar InterSHAP:** If two models have identical InterSHAP but different C-indices, the gain comes from unimodal signal quality, not fusion.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Replicate Table 1:** Train all four architectures on the provided 80/20 split (n=575) with 5 random seeds. Confirm the inverse pattern: best C-index (likely Bilinear or Cross-Attention) should have equal or lower InterSHAP than the MLP baseline.
2. **Synthetic Validation:** Generate synthetic survival data with a known interaction pattern (e.g., XOR-like synergy between two noise features). Apply InterSHAP to a simple model on this data. Expect ~100% measured interaction. This validates the metric's calibration before applying to real data.
3. **Masking Robustness Check:** For the best-performing model (e.g., Cross-Attention), compute InterSHAP using three masking strategies: mean imputation (default), random shuffle of WSI/RNA embeddings across patients, and zero-imputation. Expect mean and shuffle to be similar (~3%), zero to be inflated (>10%). Document the difference.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Does independent pre-training of unimodal encoders linearise cross-modal interactions?
- **Basis:** Explicitly stated as an open question in Section 5.1 (Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** The study did not isolate pre-training effects; encoders were either ImageNet-pretrained or randomly initialized, but not compared under controlled training regimes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare InterSHAP from models with encoders pre-trained on unimodal tasks and frozen during fusion training to models with encoders trained jointly. A significant reduction in interaction with frozen encoders would support linearisation.

### Does end-to-end joint encoder training increase measured cross-modal interaction compared to frozen-encoder fusion?
- **Basis:** Explicitly suggested for future work in the Conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** All architectures were trained with all parameters updated; no ablation with frozen encoders was performed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** For each fusion architecture, train two versions: (1) joint training of encoders and fusion, (2) encoders pre-trained on unimodal tasks and frozen during fusion training. Higher InterSHAP in joint training would indicate increased interaction due to co-adaptation.

### Do cross-modal interaction patterns differ by IDH mutation status within lower-grade gliomas?
- **Basis:** Explicitly suggested for future work in the Conclusion, noting the limitation of using histological diagnosis rather than molecular classification.
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis grouped all LGG together, ignoring IDH status which strongly influences prognosis and may affect modality interactions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Obtain IDH status for TCGA-LGG patients, split into IDH-mutant and IDH-wildtype, recompute InterSHAP variance decomposition and patient-level statistics for each subgroup. Significant differences in interaction percentages would indicate subtype-specific behavior.

## Limitations
- **Single-cancer, single-modality-combi study:** Findings are specific to glioma and WSI+RNA-seq; generalizability to other cancers or modality pairs (e.g., imaging + clinical) is unknown.
- **Frozen unimodal encoders:** Pre-trained and fixed ResNet-50 and RNA MLP may artificially constrain the model's ability to learn cross-modal interactions in early layers, potentially inflating the measured interaction at the fusion stage.
- **Imputation fragility:** Global InterSHAP is sensitive to the missing-modality imputation strategy; zero-imputation yields dramatically higher interaction (12% vs ~3% with mean), questioning robustness.

## Next Checks
1. **External validation:** Apply the same pipeline (data, models, InterSHAP) to an independent glioma cohort (e.g., a separate TCIA set) to test reproducibility of the inverse C-index vs. InterSHAP pattern and the stable additive decomposition.
2. **End-to-end training ablation:** Unfreeze the unimodal encoders and train the entire model (encoders + fusion) jointly. Measure whether InterSHAP interaction decreases further (consistent with linearisation) and if the inverse relationship between performance and interaction still holds.
3. **Imputation robustness quantification:** For the top-performing model (e.g., Cross-Attention), compute global InterSHAP under multiple imputation strategies: training-set mean, random shuffle across patients, zero, and a generative imputation (e.g., VAE). Quantify the variation in the interaction percentage and identify a robust default.
