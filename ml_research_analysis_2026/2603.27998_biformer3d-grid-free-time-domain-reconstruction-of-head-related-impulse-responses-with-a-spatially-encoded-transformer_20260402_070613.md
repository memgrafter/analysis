# BiFormer3D: Grid-Free Time-Domain Reconstruction of Head-Related Impulse Responses with a Spatially Encoded Transformer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27998
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27998
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** BiFormer3D achieves state-of-the-art ILD-E (1.14, 1.10, 0.95, 0.70 dB) and competitive ITD-E (18.5, 16.4, 15.4, 10.6 µs) for HRIR reconstruction from sparse directional measurements.

## Executive Summary
BiFormer3D introduces a grid-free transformer architecture for continuous head-related impulse response (HRIR) reconstruction from sparse directional measurements. The model takes a few measured HRIRs at arbitrary directions and predicts the full spherical set (793 directions) in the time domain. It employs sinusoidal spatial encodings to represent coordinates, a transformer encoder to capture global spatial dependencies, an MLP decoder, and a Conv1D temporal refinement module. Auxiliary heads predict interaural time difference (ITD) and interaural level difference (ILD) to enforce binaural cue consistency. Trained on the SONICOM dataset with losses combining time-domain reconstruction, complex HRTF, and ITD/ILD terms, BiFormer3D achieves state-of-the-art ILD error and competitive ITD error across various sparse input counts, without requiring minimum-phase preprocessing.

## Method Summary
The task is framed as masked inpainting over a set of \(L = M + N\) directions on a sphere. Inputs consist of spherical coordinates (azimuth \(\phi\), elevation \(\theta\), radius \(r\)) and binaural HRIRs (left and right, 256 samples at 48 kHz). For each subject, \(M\) directions are randomly selected as measured; the remaining \(N\) are to be predicted. The model processes all \(L\) directions in parallel. Spatial coordinates are encoded using sinusoidal positional encoding \(\gamma(x)\) with \(P=6\) frequencies. These encodings, combined with raw coordinates, are projected and added to features extracted from the measured HRIRs via a signal encoder (MLP with GELU and LayerNorm). A transformer encoder with multi-head self-attention and feed-forward networks propagates information across all directions, with attention masked to exclude unmeasured positions. A shared MLP decoder produces raw HRIR predictions for all directions. The measured HRIRs are then fused back via a masked operation: \(\hat{H}_{\text{fused}} = i \odot H_{\text{full}} + (1-i) \odot \hat{H}_{\text{raw}}\), where \(i\) is a binary mask. The fused outputs are reordered by descending elevation and ascending azimuth, then refined with a depthwise 1D convolution along the direction axis to improve temporal consistency. Auxiliary linear heads predict ITD and ILD from decoder features. The total loss combines \(L_{\text{rec}}\) (MSE on unmeasured directions), \(L_{\text{HRTF}}\) (orthonormal DFT magnitude and phase difference, \(\lambda=500\)), \(L_{\text{ITD}}\) and \(L_{\text{ILD}}\) (L1 losses, \(\lambda=0.05\)). Training uses AdamW (lr=3e-4, batch=8) for 500 epochs on NVIDIA A100.

## Key Results
- ILD-E: 1.14 dB (M=3), 1.10 dB (M=5), 0.95 dB (M=19), 0.70 dB (M=100), setting a new state-of-the-art.
- ITD-E: 18.5 µs (M=3), 16.4 µs (M=5), 15.4 µs (M=19), 10.6 µs (M=100), competitive with prior work.
- NMSE ranges from -6.90 to -10.20 dB and cosine distance improves from 0.233 to 0.102 as M increases.
- Ablations confirm: sinusoidal encoding is critical; Conv1D refinement reduces directional artifacts; the method works without minimum-phase preprocessing, unlike frequency-domain baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BiFormer3D's effectiveness stems from several synergistic design choices. The sinusoidal spatial encoding provides a smooth, continuous representation of spherical coordinates, enabling the model to generalize to arbitrary directions beyond a fixed grid. The transformer encoder captures long-range dependencies across all directions, allowing information from measured positions to inform unmeasured ones globally. The masked attention mechanism ensures that only measured HRIRs contribute to predictions, preventing leakage. The auxiliary ITD/ILD heads explicitly supervise binaural cues that are crucial for spatial hearing, steering the model toward perceptually relevant reconstructions. The Conv1D temporal refinement, applied after reordering directions by elevation and azimuth, enforces local consistency along the direction axis, eliminating vertical streaking artifacts seen in ablations. Finally, operating directly in the time domain avoids the phase distortions introduced by minimum-phase approximations, preserving the full temporal structure of HRIRs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sinusoidal Positional Encoding**: Converts continuous coordinates into a high-dimensional embedding using multiple frequencies, preserving neighborhood relationships. *Why needed*: To represent arbitrary spatial positions in a way that the model can interpolate and generalize. *Quick check*: Confirm that the encoding uses a set of frequencies (e.g., \(2^k\)) and that the output dimension matches the model's hidden size.
- **Masked Inpainting with Transformers**: A technique where certain tokens are masked and the model predicts them while attending only to unmasked tokens. *Why needed*: To reconstruct missing HRIRs without using information from other missing positions. *Quick check*: Verify that the attention mask sets masked positions to -inf before softmax, so they receive zero attention weight.
- **Binaural Cues (ITD/ILD)**: Interaural time and level differences are primary cues for sound localization. *Why needed*: Enforcing these cues ensures predicted HRIRs yield accurate spatial perception. *Quick check*: Compute ITD via cross-correlation peak lag (within ±1 ms) and ILD as dB difference in 1-4 kHz band; compare predicted vs. ground truth.
- **Time-Domain HRIR Processing**: Directly modeling impulse responses in the time domain. *Why needed*: Avoids the phase ambiguities and approximations of frequency-domain methods (e.g., minimum-phase). *Quick check*: Check that model outputs are physically plausible (causal, decaying) and that no minimum-phase conversion is applied.
- **Conv1D for Directional Smoothing**: Applying a 1D convolution along the direction axis after ordering directions meaningfully. *Why needed*: To reduce high-frequency artifacts along the direction dimension, improving visual and perceptual quality. *Quick check*: Reorder directions by elevation descending then azimuth ascending; apply depthwise Conv1D; inspect stacked HRIRs for reduced vertical streaks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input coordinates + measured HRIRs -> Sinusoidal Encoding (\(\gamma(x)\)) -> Geometric Embedding (raw coords + encoding) -> Signal Encoder (MLP) -> Feature Addition -> Transformer Encoder (T layers) -> MLP Decoder -> Raw HRIR predictions -> Masked Fusion (preserve measured rows) -> Reorder (elev desc, az asc) -> Conv1D Refinement -> Final HRIRs.  
Auxiliary paths: Decoder features -> ITD/ILD Heads.

**Critical path**: The main flow from input to output relies on accurate spatial encoding, transformer-based global reasoning, and masked fusion to inject ground-truth measurements. The Conv1D refinement is a post-processing step that corrects directional discontinuities.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Grid-free vs. grid-based*: Grid-free allows arbitrary direction sampling but requires robust spatial encoding; grid-based simplifies but limits flexibility.
- *Time-domain vs. frequency-domain*: Time-domain retains full phase but is harder to train; frequency-domain often uses minimum-phase simplification, which can degrade accuracy.
- *Auxiliary losses*: ITD/ILD supervision improves perceptual metrics but depends on correct ground-truth computation and adds training complexity.

**Failure signatures**:
- High NMSE if sinusoidal encoding scaling is off (e.g., coordinates in degrees instead of radians).
- Information leakage if masked attention is not properly applied (e.g., masked tokens still influence others).
- Directional artifacts (vertical streaks) if Conv1D reordering is incorrect or refinement omitted.
- Poor convergence if loss weights (e.g., \(\lambda_{\text{HRTF}}=500\)) are mis-set, causing spectral loss to dominate.

**First experiments**:
1. **Sinusoidal encoding sensitivity**: Train with different coordinate normalizations (e.g., [0,1] scaling, radians, meters) and track validation ILD-E/NMSE. Expect significant drop if encoding does not cover the spatial range adequately.
2. **Masked attention integrity test**: Create a batch where all HRIRs are masked (set to zero) and coordinates are arbitrary. The model's raw predictions (before fusion) should be near zero (or consistent with random initialization), confirming no attention to masked positions.
3. **Conv1D refinement ablation**: Train identical models with and without the Conv1D module. Visualize predicted HRIRs as a 2D plot (directions vs. time) to assess directional continuity; the model without refinement should exhibit vertical streaking.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does better objective performance (NMSE/ITD-E/ILD-E) imply improved perceptual binaural rendering? No subjective evaluation was conducted; objective gains may not translate to audible differences.
- Can BiFormer3D generalize to other HRIR databases or diverse listener groups without retraining? Only SONICOM was used; cross-dataset generalization is unknown.
- How does the spatial distribution of sparse measurements affect reconstruction accuracy? Experiments used fixed uniform sampling; non-uniform patterns (clustered, band-limited) were not explored.
- What spatial features or dependencies does the Transformer encoder learn to attend across directions? No analysis of attention weights or internal representations was provided.

## Limitations
- Key hyperparameters (number of transformer layers, heads, dropout, Conv1D kernel size, grouping) and coordinate normalization are unspecified, forcing assumptions that may affect replication.
- Ground-truth ITD/ILD computation methods (e.g., GCC-PHAT parameters, frequency bands) are not defined, creating ambiguity in auxiliary loss targets.
- Training regime (separate models per M vs. variable M per batch) and exact masked attention implementation are unclear, risking implementation drift.
