# Fire on Motion: Optimizing Video Pass-bands for Efficient Spiking Action Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.22675  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22675  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PBO yields over 10 percentage point improvement in top‑1 accuracy on UCF101 (e.g., +11.55% on Spikformer) and consistent gains across multiple video benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are energy‑efficient but underperform on video action recognition because the leaky integrate‑and‑fire (LIF) neuron acts as a temporal low‑pass filter, attenuating high‑frequency motion cues that are critical for discriminating dynamic actions. This work proposes the Pass‑Band Optimizer (PBO), a plug‑and‑play pre‑filter inserted before the first spiking membrane. PBO applies a learnable, time‑modulated difference operation with only two scalar parameters and a lightweight consistency loss to preserve semantic content. By reshaping the effective temporal pass‑band toward motion‑relevant frequencies, PBO delivers substantial accuracy improvements on UCF101, HMDB51, HARDVS, and RGB‑DVS settings, and reduces false alarm rates in video anomaly detection. The results establish pass‑band alignment as a key factor for efficient SNN‑based video understanding.

## Method Summary
PBO is a lightweight module that preprocesses an input video sequence \(X[t]\) by computing \(Y[t] = X[t] - \lambda[t] X[t-1]\), where \(\lambda[t] = \mu + A \sin(\omega t + \phi)\). Only \(\mu\) and \(\omega\) are learned; amplitude \(A=0.1\) and phase \(\phi=0\) are fixed. This time‑varying linear filter reshapes the frequency response of the subsequent LIF network, boosting mid‑band frequencies that carry motion. Training combines a standard classification loss with a consistency regularizer \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{int}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\). The intensity term \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}\) aligns \(Y\) to a convex combination of the original input (λ=0) and a differenced version (λ=1), while the gradient term \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\) preserves edge structure. The consistency loss ensures semantic fidelity without sacrificing pass‑band adaptation. PBO adds negligible parameters and computational overhead and can be inserted into any SNN backbone before the first spiking layer.

## Key Results
- On UCF101, PBO improves top‑1 accuracy by over 10 percentage points (e.g., +11.55% on Spikformer).  
- Gains are consistent across HMDB51, HARDVS, and RGB‑DVS multi‑modal benchmarks.  
- PBO reduces false alarm rates in weakly supervised video anomaly detection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

1. **LIF temporal low‑pass filtering attenuates motion.**  
   The discrete‑time LIF recursion \(U[t] = (1-\tau)V[t-1] + \tau X[t] + \text{const.}\) yields a frequency response that peaks at DC (\(\omega=0\)) and decays monotonically as \(|\omega|\) increases. Motion‑carrying mid‑to‑high frequencies are therefore suppressed, causing SNNs to underperform on dynamic tasks.

2. **PBO reshapes the pass‑band via time‑modulated difference.**  
   The filter \(Y[t] = X[t] - \lambda[t] X[t-1]\) with periodic \(\lambda[t]\) introduces harmonic sidebands when cascaded with LIF. This shifts low‑frequency energy into non‑zero bands, creating a mid‑band peak in the effective magnitude response and thus amplifying motion‑relevant frequencies.

3. **Consistency loss preserves semantics.**  
   \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}}\) penalizes deviations from two reference signals: the original input (λ=0) and the simple differenced signal (λ=1). The intensity term enforces a closed‑form interpolation between these endpoints after spatial demeaning, while the gradient term aligns edge structures. Together they prevent degenerate filtering that discards essential content.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input video \(X[t]\) → PBO module (\(Y[t] = X[t] - \lambda[t] X[t-1]\)) → SNN backbone (LIF‑based) → spiking activity → classification head → prediction.

- **Critical path:**  
  \(X[t] \xrightarrow{\text{PBO}} Y[t] \xrightarrow{\text{backbone}} \text{spikes} \xrightarrow{\text{head}} \text{prediction}\). The backbone parameters and PBO scalars (\(\mu\), \(\omega\)) are trained end‑to‑end.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • *Simplicity vs. expressivity:* PBO uses two scalars, minimizing overhead but cannot represent arbitrary temporal filters.  
  • *Semantic preservation vs. pass‑band shift:* Consistency loss weight \(\alpha\) balances fidelity and adaptation; too high restricts adaptation, too low risks semantic drift.  
  • *Period selection:* \(\omega\) is mapped via logistic to \((0,\pi]\); initialization targets roughly one period over clip length \(T\).  
  • *Energy‑accuracy:* PBO aims to increase sparsity by suppressing static content, but improper conditioning may increase spike rate.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  • \(\lambda[t]\) diverges beyond \([0,1]\) → instability/NaNs.  
  • PBO collapses to an endpoint (\(\lambda[t] \approx 0\) or \(1\)) → no adaptation (check \(\mu\) and \(A\)).  
  • Excessive spike rate → over‑suppression of low frequencies.  
  • Poor generalization on longer clips → learned \(\omega\) too high.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline replication: Train Spikformer‑2‑256 on UCF101‑CEP with \(T=10\); record top‑1 accuracy (~46%).  
  2. PBO insertion: Add PBO (\(\mu=0.5\), \(\omega=2\pi/(T-1)\), \(A=0.1\), \(\alpha=10^{-2}\)); expect >10% absolute gain.  
  3. Consistency loss ablation: Compare full loss vs. no loss vs. only intensity vs. only gradient; validate each term’s contribution (Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What alternative parameterizations for \(\lambda[t]\) can improve pass‑band shaping for different video dynamics beyond the fixed‑amplitude sinusoidal model?
2. How does inserting PBO at multiple layers or in a depth‑dependent manner influence performance compared to a single pre‑filter?
3. Can we develop a general, dataset‑independent supervision loss for PBO that maintains semantic fidelity without per‑dataset tuning of \(\alpha\)?
4. To what extent does the assumed low‑pass behavior of LIF hold across different SNN architectures and video content, and are there scenarios where motion semantics reside in low‑frequency bands?

## Limitations
- Initialization of \(\omega\) is described inconsistently (two different formulas), risking suboptimal training start.
- Consistency loss implementation details (Sobel gradient computation, spatial demeaning) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- The simple two‑parameter sinusoidal \(\lambda[t]\) may be insufficient for complex temporal modulations needed for varied motion patterns.

## Confidence
- LIF low‑pass filtering: **High**

- Consistency loss preserves semantics: **Medium**
