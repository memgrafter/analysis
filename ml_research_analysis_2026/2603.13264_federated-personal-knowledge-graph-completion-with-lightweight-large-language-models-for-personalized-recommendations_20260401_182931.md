# Federated Personal Knowledge Graph Completion with Lightweight Large Language Models for Personalized Recommendations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13264  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13264  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** FedTREK-LM achieves more than a 4× improvement in F1-score over state-of-the-art KG completion and federated recommendation baselines on movie and food benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
FedTREK-LM introduces a federated learning framework that combines lightweight large language models (LLMs) with personal knowledge graphs (PKGs) to enable privacy-preserving personalized recommendations. By fine-tuning local LLMs using Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) on user-specific feedback and aggregating model updates via federated averaging, the system captures cross-user patterns without exposing raw data. Evaluations on movie and food benchmarks show more than a 4× improvement in F1-score over state-of-the-art KG completion and federated recommendation baselines. Ablation studies reveal that real user interactions are critical, as synthetic data reduces performance by up to 46%. The federated approach maintains robustness with only a modest recall drop compared to centralized training, demonstrating effective decentralized personalization.

## Method Summary
FedTREK-LM operates on user-specific PKGs represented as JSON-LD. Each client device hosts a quantized lightweight LLM (Qwen3, 0.6B–4B parameters) and locally fine-tunes it using LoRA adapters. Training data consists of prompt-completion-label triplets derived from user-LLM interactions, where prompts include a relevant subgraph of the user's PKG. The KTO loss optimizes the model to prefer desirable completions (liked items) over undesirable ones. Federated learning is orchestrated via Flower: in each round, a subset of clients updates their local LoRA adapters, which are then aggregated on a central server using FedAvg and redistributed. This cycle enables collective learning while keeping raw PKG data on-device.

## Key Results
- FedTREK-LM achieves >4× higher F1-score compared to HAKE, KBGAT, and FedKGRec on movie and food benchmarks.
- Incorporating synthetic training data reduces F1-score by up to 46%, highlighting the importance of real user conversational feedback.
- Federated training yields performance close to centralized training with only a modest recall drop, enabling practical decentralized personalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Federated Learning for Cross-User Pattern Sharing
- **Claim:** Federated learning enables cross-user pattern sharing while preserving data locality, overcoming sparsity in individual PKGs.
- **Mechanism:** Locally fine-tuned model updates (via LoRA adapters) are aggregated by a central server using Federated Averaging, distilling collective preference patterns without accessing raw PKG data.
- **Core assumption:** User preference distributions share latent structure that can be captured through model weight averaging despite non-IID local data.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions federated learning for scalable decentralized personalization; Section 3.3 details FL rounds; empirical comparisons show FedTREK-LM maintains robustness where KG baselines degrade sharply (Table 2, Figure 5).
- **Break condition:** Extreme client heterogeneity where no shared latent patterns exist; aggregation fails, evidenced by local-only performance parity (Table 2 "Local-only" rows).

### Mechanism 2: Structured Prompting with PKGs for Context-Aware Reasoning
- **Claim:** Structurally prompting lightweight LLMs with user PKGs enables context-aware semantic reasoning without massive parameter overhead.
- **Mechanism:** The user's PKG is serialized into a structured prompt (system message) providing triple-based context for generating relevant entity completions.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's pretrained knowledge can be grounded to user-specific facts via prompt-based context injection, surviving quantization and LoRA fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states prompting with structured PKGs; Section 3.1 describes prompt construction; Section 4.2 uses 4-bit quantized Qwen3 models for device constraints.
- **Break condition:** PKG structure diverges from expected JSON-LD/schema.org format or contains insufficient triples, leading to hallucination or generic responses.

### Mechanism 3: KTO for Efficient On-Device Fine-Tuning from Implicit Feedback
- **Claim:** Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) enables efficient, label-light on-device fine-tuning from implicit user feedback, crucial for sparse, evolving PKGs.
- **Mechanism:** User-LLM interactions are converted into prompt–completion–label triplets; KTO loss fine-tunes the LLM to prefer "desired" over "undesired" completions, combined with LoRA to minimize memory and communication cost.
- **Core assumption:** User feedback provides a reliable binary signal for aligning the model's PKG completion behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions feedback-driven fine-tuning; Section 3.4 details triplet creation and LoRA use; Section 5.1 shows performance improvement from KTO fine-tuning (Figure 6).
- **Break condition:** Feedback becomes extremely sparse or ambiguous, or the reference model is mismatched, causing KTO misalignment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Federated Averaging (FedAvg)**: The core aggregation algorithm; understanding how local updates combine without data pooling is essential. Quick check: What is the typical impact of non-IID data on FedAvg's global model performance relative to centralized training?
- **Knowledge Graph Completion (Link Prediction)**: The task of predicting missing relations in a KG; PKG completion frames recommendation as link prediction. Quick check: How does predicting a missing "liked" triple in a PKG differ from predicting a generic relation in a static KG like FB15k-237?
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**: Updates only low-rank matrices to adapt large models with minimal memory; key for on-device training. Quick check: Why does using LoRA adapters instead of full-model gradients reduce the attack surface for model inversion attacks in FL?
- **Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO)**: A fine-tuning objective for binary feedback, preferred over DPO/RLHF for sparse settings. Quick check: What is the key difference between KTO's loss function and that of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
PKG Manager → constructs system prompt → LLM (base + local LoRA) generates recommendation; user feedback → KTO Trainer updates LoRA; Federated Coordinator selects clients; Aggregation Server applies FedAvg to LoRA updates; global LoRA broadcast to clients.

**Critical path:**  
1. User interaction updates local PKG.  
2. For inference: PKG Manager extracts relevant subPKG, builds system prompt, LLM generates item recommendations.  
3. User feedback (like/dislike) creates new triplet.  
4. On FL round trigger: KTO Trainer uses accumulated triplets to fine-tune local LoRA.  
5. Upload LoRA to server; server aggregates; download new global LoRA; repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Model size vs. feasibility: Smaller models enable on-device training but limit reasoning depth; 4B adapters are feasible but higher bandwidth (Table 1).  
- Synthetic data utility: Helps in centralized low-data regimes but harms federated performance (Figure 8), indicating poor capture of user-specific distributions.  
- Federated vs. centralized: FL incurs recall drop but gains precision (Table 2), suggesting more conservative, higher-quality predictions.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Near-zero metrics on Recipe PKG (Table 3): failure when training lacks real conversational feedback.  
- Performance collapse with synthetic data in FL (Figure 8): synthetic data introduces harmful distribution shifts.  
- Local-only models at chance level (Table 2): demonstrates necessity of FL for cross-user knowledge.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline Comparison: Evaluate TREK-LM against HAKE, KBGAT, FedKGRec on Movie PKG in centralized, federated, and local settings using F1, MRR, Hits@k.  
2. Synthetic Data Ablation: Train with and without synthetic KTO examples to measure impact across settings.  
3. Centralized vs. Federated Degradation: Compare TREK-LM's federated performance to centralized variant to quantify decentralization cost.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can prompt engineering and hyperparameter tuning be systematically optimized?**  
   *Basis:* Conclusion states potential gains through improved prompts and tuning. *Why unresolved:* No systematic exploration of prompt structure or KTO weights. *Evidence needed:* Ablation studies varying prompt templates and KTO weighting ratios across datasets, measuring F1, MRR, Hits@k.

2. **What are privacy-utility trade-offs with formal privacy (e.g., differential privacy)?**  
   *Basis:* Paper intentionally omitted DP to avoid confounding utility. *Why unresolved:* No empirical analysis of DP's impact on sparse PKG learning. *Evidence needed:* Experiments adding calibrated noise to LoRA updates, evaluating privacy leakage (e.g., membership inference) and metric changes.

3. **Why does synthetic data degrade performance up to 46%?**  
   *Basis:* Synthetic data lacks semantic/contextual fidelity. *Why unresolved:* No dissection of missing attributes (e.g., diversity, temporal evolution). *Evidence needed:* Comparative analysis of real vs. synthetic PKG graphs (density, entity novelty) and controlled injections of real-user properties.

4. **Is there a scaling law or optimal model size for lightweight LLMs in federated PKG completion?**  
   *Basis:* Only three model sizes tested; interaction between scale, LoRA efficiency, and FL costs not modeled. *Why unresolved:* No intermediate sizes (e.g., 2B, 3B) explored. *Evidence needed:* Scaling experiments with additional sizes, plotting performance vs. per-round communication and memory footprint.

5. **To what extent does FedTREK-LM generalize beyond movie/food domains?**  
   *Basis:* Conclusion claims natural generalization to healthcare/finance but no empirical tests. *Why unresolved:* Evaluation limited to two datasets; Recipe PKG results near-zero due to synthetic data, not domain mismatch. *Evidence needed:* Replication studies on real PKGs from healthcare or finance, measuring gains over baselines and need for domain-specific tuning.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed local training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs per round) hinder exact replication.
- Ambiguous mechanism for extracting "relevant subPKG" from full PKG may lead to inconsistent prompt construction.
- Evaluation confined to two datasets (movie conversations, recipe ratings); generalizability to other domains uncertain.

## Confidence
- >4× F1 improvement over baselines: Medium (strong empirical results but limited dataset diversity and potential baseline inconsistencies).
- Synthetic data reduces performance by up to 46%: High (consistent ablations across model sizes and settings).
- Federated robustness with modest recall drop: Medium (acceptable trade-offs in provided setup but scalability untested).
