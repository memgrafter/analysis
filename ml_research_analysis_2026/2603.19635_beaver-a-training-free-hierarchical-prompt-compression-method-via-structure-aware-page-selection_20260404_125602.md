# BEAVER: A Training-Free Hierarchical Prompt Compression Method via Structure-Aware Page Selection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19635
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19635
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** BEAVER achieves near-perfect retrieval fidelity on RULER (100% single-needle, 99% multi-needle), reduces inference latency by 26.4× on 128k-token contexts, and matches/exceeds state-of-the-art on LongBench and ZeroSCROLLS without any model training.

## Executive Summary
BEAVER introduces a training-free, hierarchical prompt compression framework that mitigates inference latency and semantic fragmentation in long-context large language models. Unlike token-level pruning, it structures the context into pages based on natural delimiters (e.g., newlines, headings) to preserve discourse integrity. The method employs a dual-path page encoder with in-context inverse term frequency (ITF) weighting to produce robust page representations, and a query-aware planner that fuses semantic and lexical relevance with structural priors (anchor, flow, flash), later smoothing selections to sentence boundaries. Empirical results on the RULER benchmark show near-perfect retrieval fidelity (100% single-needle, 99% multi-needle). Compared to prior work, BEAVER reduces latency by 26.4× on 128k-token contexts relative to LongLLMLingua, and matches or outperforms state-of-the-art on LongBench and ZeroSCROLLS without any model training. The approach demonstrates stable performance across model scales from 0.6B to 32B parameters.

## Method Summary
BEAVER’s pipeline consists of three sequential modules. The **Segmenter** partitions the raw context into semantic segments (e.g., paragraphs, code blocks) using natural delimiters such as newlines or markdown headers. Consecutive segments are then greedily packed into pages of fixed token capacity M (default 64), converting variable-length input into a dense 2D page tensor. The **PageEncoder** first embeds each token using a pretrained model (e.g., Qwen3-8B). For each page, tokens are reweighted by an in-context inverse term frequency (ITF) score to downweight frequent but less informative tokens. Two pooled representations are then computed: a mean of the weighted embeddings (capturing global semantics) and a max pooling (highlighting salient local features). These vectors are fused with a learnable weight γ (default 0.7) to form the final page vector. The **QueryPlanner** receives the page vectors and a query. It calculates a hybrid relevance score per page by linearly combining a semantic component (ITF‑weighted cosine similarity between page and query) and a lexical component (sum of ITF weights for tokens shared with the query), controlled by λ (default 0.7). Structural priors are then enforced: the first k_anc (default 4) pages are kept as anchors (global metadata), a contiguous window of w_flow (default 4) pages ending at the query is retained as flow (recent context), and the top‑k_flash scoring pages from the remainder are selected to fill the remaining budget. Finally, the chosen page intervals are expanded to full sentence boundaries via sentence smoothing, ensuring syntactic integrity before the compressed context is fed to the target LLM. No model parameters are updated; only the embedding model is used off‑the‑shelf.

- Achieves 100% single-needle and 99% multi-needle retrieval accuracy on RULER.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Structure-aware pagination preserves discourse boundaries and enables efficient hardware parallelism by mapping variable-length contexts into dense 2D page tensors.
- **Mechanism**: The Segmenter splits the context into logical segments using natural delimiters (e.g., newlines, headings) and greedily packs consecutive segments into pages of fixed capacity M, minimizing padding while maintaining local semantic cohesion. This transforms irregular token sequences into a structured matrix that standard tensor operations can process efficiently.
- **Core assumption**: Natural language delimiters align with semantic units (e.g., paragraphs, sentences), so preserving these boundaries prevents fragmentation that would disrupt coherence.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract]: "shifts compression from linear token removal to structure-aware hierarchical selection" and "mapping variable-length contexts into dense page-level tensors via dual-path pooling".
  - [Section 3.2]: Full description of delimiter-based segmentation and greedy pagination strategy.
  - [corpus]: Weak; related corpus entries discuss hierarchical reconstruction (HRDoc) but not BEAVER's specific pagination.
- **Break condition**: If the input context lacks clear delimiters (e.g., code snippets, continuous prose without paragraph breaks), pagination may create semantically incoherent pages, leading to information loss or noise.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Dual-path pooling with in-context inverse term frequency (ITF) down-weights redundant tokens and captures both global semantics and salient local features for robust page representations.
- **Mechanism**: For each page, token embeddings are weighted by an ITF score computed from token frequency within the context, reducing the influence of high-frequency tokens. Mean pooling aggregates weighted semantics globally, while max pooling extracts the most salient local activations (e.g., rare keywords). The two pooled vectors are fused to form the final page representation.
- **Core assumption**: Within a specific context, frequently occurring tokens are often redundant or less informative for the query, whereas rare tokens carry distinctive information. This context-aware weighting is more effective than corpus-level statistics.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract]: "dual-path pooling for page encoding" and "in-context ITF weighting".
  - [Section 3.3]: Equations (2)–(5) define ITF weighting, mean/max pooling, and fusion.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus contains unrelated token pruning methods.
- **Break condition**: In extremely short or homogeneous contexts, ITF may fail to distinguish informative tokens. If critical information uses high-frequency words (e.g., common verbs in a specialized domain), down-weighting could discard them.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Hybrid semantic-lexical scoring enhanced with structural priors (anchor, flow, flash) and sentence smoothing selects pages that maintain query relevance, discourse coherence, and syntactic integrity.
- **Mechanism**: The QueryPlanner computes per-page relevance via a linear fusion of semantic similarity (ITF-weighted cosine similarity between page and query vectors) and lexical overlap (sum of ITF weights for shared tokens). It then enforces three priors: anchor pages (first few) retain global metadata; flow pages (contiguous window before query) preserve recent context; flash pages (top-scoring from remaining) capture distant evidence. Finally, sentence smoothing extends selected spans to full sentence boundaries.
- **Core assumption**: Human-like reading leverages beginning anchors, working memory of recent flow, and targeted scanning (flash). Exact lexical matches are crucial for answer extraction (e.g., numbers, names). Sentence boundaries are natural units for syntactic completeness.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract]: "hybrid semantic-lexical planner with sentence smoothing".
  - [Section 3.4]: Detailed description of scoring, structural priors, and smoothing; Figure 3 illustrates smoothing.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence.
- **Break condition**: For multi-hop reasoning where evidence is distributed and lacks direct lexical/semantic overlap with the query, the scoring may omit necessary pages. Fixed-size anchor/flow windows might exclude important information if it lies outside these ranges.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: In-Context Information Theory
- **Why needed here**: BEAVER's ITF is the core unsupervised signal for token importance. Understanding its distinction from corpus-level TF-IDF explains why the method works without training.
- **Quick check question**: "What is the key difference between corpus-level IDF and BEAVER's in-context ITF, and why does this matter for prompt compression?"

### Concept: Document Structure and Discourse Coherence
- **Why needed here**: The Segmenter relies on natural delimiters to create pages, and sentence smoothing relies on sentence boundaries. Appreciating why structure matters clarifies BEAVER's advantage over unstructured token pruning.
- **Quick check question**: "Why might breaking a paragraph in the middle harm an LLM's understanding, even if the broken tokens are individually informative?"

### Concept: Dual-Path Pooling in Representation Learning
- **Why needed here**: The PageEncoder's mean+max pooling design balances global and local information, a pattern common in representation learning (e.g., NetVLAD). Knowing this helps understand the design choice.
- **Quick check question**: "What kind of information does max pooling capture that mean pooling might miss, in the context of page representations?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Segmenter → PageEncoder → QueryPlanner
- **Critical path:** Linear pipeline: Segmenter → PageEncoder → QueryPlanner. The query only interacts at the QueryPlanner stage; PageEncoder is query-agnostic (ITF uses only context).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Page size (M):** Smaller M increases granularity but fragments discourse; larger M reduces noise but may include irrelevant content. (Default: M=64.)
  - **Fusion weight (γ):** Balances mean vs. max pooling in PageEncoder. (Default: γ=0.7.)
  - **Score fusion (λ):** Balances semantic vs. lexical relevance. (Default: λ=0.7.)
  - **Structural prior sizes (k_anc, w_flow, k_flash):** Larger windows preserve more context but reduce space for flash pages. (Default: k_anc = w_flow = 4; k_flash adapts.)
  - **Embedding model:** Tradeoff between quality and speed. (Default: Qwen3-8B.)
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Fragmentation artifacts: Incomplete sentences or broken phrases indicate sentence smoothing failed or was bypassed.
  - Irrelevant retention: Many high-frequency stop words kept suggest ITF weighting ineffective.
  - Poor recall on distributed queries: On multi-needle tasks, missed needles imply lexical/semantic scoring or structural priors failed to select all necessary pages.
  - Latency spike: Excessively small M increases number of pages N, raising QueryPlanner cost.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation - Page Size:** Test M=32, 64, 128 on LongBench QA (2K budget). Expect performance dip at extremes; latency decreases with larger M but compression quality may suffer.
  2. **Ablation - Structural Priors:** Disable anchor, flow, or flash individually on RULER. Flash-only should fail on tasks needing recent context; anchor-only should fail on long-range retrieval.
  3. **Scalability Test:** Evaluate BEAVER with different backbone LLMs (0.6B, 8B, 32B) on RULER. Performance should remain stable across scales; large drops indicate dependency on model capacity for ITF or scoring.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can BEAVER improve page-level granularity to approach token-level precision without harming discourse integrity?  
**Basis in paper:** The limitations state that page-level granularity is “inherently less precise than fine-grained token pruning, which may occasionally result in the retention of minor redundancies within selected segments.”  
**Why unresolved:** The current design trades precision for efficiency; refining granularity could reduce retained noise but risks fragmentation or increased overhead.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic comparison of hybrid approaches (e.g., token-level refinement within pages) or adaptive segmentation strategies on benchmarks like RULER with token-level ground truth.

#### Open Question 2
**Question:** How can BEAVER be extended to handle deep multi-hop reasoning where supporting evidence shares little direct lexical/semantic overlap with the query?  
**Basis in paper:** The authors note that the retrieval mechanism “may face challenges in deep multi-hop reasoning scenarios where the supporting evidence shares little direct overlap with the query and requires intermediate deductive steps.”  
**Why unresolved:** Direct semantic/lexical similarity scores may fail to retrieve indirectly relevant information needed for multi-hop inference, limiting broader applicability.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate graph-based or iterative retrieval mechanisms; validate on multi-hop QA datasets (e.g., HotpotQA) within the LongBench framework.

#### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can BEAVER’s hyperparameters (e.g., weighting factors, structural priors) be automatically adapted to different domains without manual tuning?  
**Basis in paper:** The limitations acknowledge that “as a training‑free method, BEAVER depends on pre‑set hyperparameters that might require manual adjustment to achieve optimal results across drastically different domains.”  
**Why unresolved:** Manual tuning hinders the “plug‑and‑play” goal; automatic adaptation would improve robustness and ease of deployment across diverse tasks.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Develop unsupervised or meta‑learning based hyperparameter selection (e.g., using context statistics); demonstrate consistent performance on L‑Eval without per‑dataset tuning.

#### Open Question 4
**Question:** How can BEAVER mitigate fairness risks from embedding biases that might systematically deprioritize underrepresented dialects or cultural contexts?  
**Basis in paper:** The ethical considerations state: “Biases inherent in these pre‑trained models, such as the under‑representation of certain dialects or cultural contexts, may be propagated. Future work should investigate the fairness of relevance scoring across diverse linguistic demographics.”  
**Why unresolved:** Biased importance scoring could lead to inequitable compression and information loss for minority language content.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct fairness audits using diverse corpora (e.g., code‑switched, non‑standard dialects); apply debiasing techniques to the embedding‑based scoring and measure both performance and fairness metrics.

## Limitations
- Delimiter ambiguity: exact delimiter set and priority order for segmentation are unspecified, which may affect reproducibility and performance on texts with unconventional formatting.
- Sentence smoothing: The toolkit (e.g., spaCy vs. NLTK) and handling of edge cases (e.g., incomplete sentences) are not defined, leading to potential inconsistencies.
- Dynamic k_flash allocation: The rule for adapting k_flash to fill the remaining token budget is not detailed, making it hard to reproduce exact selection behavior.
