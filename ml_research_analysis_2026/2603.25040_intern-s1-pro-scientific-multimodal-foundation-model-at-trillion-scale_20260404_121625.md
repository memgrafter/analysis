# Intern-S1-Pro: Scientific Multimodal Foundation Model at Trillion Scale

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.25040  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25040  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** First trillion-parameter scientific multimodal foundation model achieving state-of-the-art performance on scientific benchmarks (SciReasoner 55.5, SmolInstruct 74.8, MatBench 72.8) and matching top open-source models on general benchmarks (MMLU‑Pro 86.6, AIME‑2025 93.1), with only ~20% efficiency loss at scale.

## Executive Summary
Intern-S1-Pro is the first trillion-parameter scientific multimodal foundation model, pushing the boundaries of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) scaling. It introduces two key algorithmic innovations—the Grouped Router, which guarantees absolute load balance across expert-parallel devices, and the Straight-Through Estimator (STE), which enables full gradient updates to the router during training. Coupled with a dedicated scientific caption pipeline that extracts 270 B high-quality image-text tokens from PDFs, and FP8 mixed-precision reinforcement learning with meticulous stability techniques, the model achieves state-of-the-art results on a suite of scientific benchmarks while remaining competitive on generalist tasks. This work demonstrates that trillion-scale models can be trained efficiently and applied to high-stakes scientific domains.

The model outperforms proprietary systems in scientific depth and specialized biology tasks while maintaining strong general capabilities. Infrastructure co-design (XTuner ↔ LMDeploy) ensures that efficiency loss stays under 20% despite a fourfold parameter increase from Intern‑S1, proving the viability of trillion-parameter MoE training.

## Method Summary
Intern-S1-Pro builds upon the Intern-S1 architecture by expanding to approximately one trillion parameters via expert proliferation in a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework. The Grouped Router partitions experts into groups aligned with device mapping, selecting the top-1 expert per group under EP=8 to ensure balanced activation. The Straight‑Through Estimator uses a temperature‑scaled softmax in the backward pass, allowing all experts to receive gradient signals despite sparse Top‑K forward selection. The model processes images, text, and time series through a Native ViT, a text encoder, and an adaptive time‑series encoder, respectively, and employs FoPE for positional encoding.

Training proceeds in two phases. First, continued pre‑training on 6 trillion tokens combines web‑scale image‑text data with a proprietary dataset of 270 B scientific image‑text tokens extracted from PDFs. To mitigate domain conflict, the pipeline applies structured transformation, diversification, and system prompt isolation. Second, post‑training uses reinforcement learning (RL) in FP8 mixed precision. Stability is achieved through operator‑level precision alignment between the XTuner training engine and the LMDeploy inference engine, rollout router replay to maintain routing consistency, and importance‑sampled REINFORCE loss. Sensitive operations (e.g., RMSNorm, router softmax, LM head) are kept in higher precision. This co‑design limits efficiency loss to ~20% even as the model scales fourfold from Intern‑S1.

## Key Results
- **Scientific benchmarks:** State-of-the-art on SciReasoner (55.5), SmolInstruct (74.8), and MatBench (72.8).
- **General benchmarks:** Competitive with top open‑source models on MMLU‑Pro (86.6) and AIME‑2025 (93.1).
- **Specialized performance and efficiency:** Outperforms proprietary models on biology tasks (average 52.45 vs. 39.24) while maintaining only ~20% efficiency loss despite a 4× parameter scale‑up.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Grouped Router Enables Stable Large-Scale MoE Training
- **Claim:** The Grouped Router may improve training stability and efficiency for trillion-parameter MoE models by enforcing absolute load balancing across expert-parallel devices.
- **Mechanism:**  
  The total expert pool (E) is partitioned into G disjoint groups aligned with device mapping. During routing, each group independently selects its top-(K/G) experts (e.g., Top-1 per group for K=8, EP=8). This guarantees exactly one expert is activated per device, eliminating cross-device load imbalance that causes memory spikes and OOM risks in standard Top-K routing.
- **Core assumption:** That expert load imbalance during post-training is a primary cause of inefficiency and instability in massive MoE models, and that device-aligned grouping can enforce balanced activation without sacrificing capacity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "overcoming scaling challenges via a Grouped Router for load-balanced MoE training"
  - [section 2.1] Detailed algorithmic formulation and Figure 3 showing absolute load balance.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Every Step Evolves" (Ring-1T) and "Every Activation Boosted" (Ling 2.0) address similar trillion-scale MoE training challenges, indicating load balancing is a recognized bottleneck in the field.
- **Break condition:** If the initial expert distribution is too homogeneous (as noted in the paper’s hypothesis), groups may fail to differentiate during early training, potentially degrading performance; the mechanism relies on sufficient training steps for experts to specialize.

### Mechanism 2: Straight-Through Estimator (STE) Enhances Router Optimization
- **Claim:** The Straight-Through Estimator may accelerate router convergence and improve load balancing by providing gradient updates to all experts, not just the selected Top-K.
- **Mechanism:**  
  In forward pass, standard sparse Top-K selection is preserved. In backward pass, gradients flow through the dense softmax distribution (temperature-scaled), not the sparse mask. This ensures every expert’s routing logit receives a meaningful gradient signal, allowing the router to better optimize allocation strategy over time.
- **Core assumption:** That gradient sparsity from discrete Top-K selection significantly impedes router learning in large MoE models, and that dense gradient estimation via STE can mitigate this without compromising inference sparsity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "a Straight-Through Estimator to enable full router gradient updates"
  - [section 2.2] Mathematical derivation showing gradient flow to all logits via Jacobian of softmax.
  - [corpus] The referenced work "DenseMixer" [46] and general MoE literature (e.g., "GRIN") also address router gradient sparsity, suggesting this is a known challenge; STE is a common solution in other domains (e.g., binary networks) but its application to MoE routing at this scale is novel.
- **Break condition:** If temperature τ is poorly tuned, gradient estimates may be too blunt (high τ) or too noisy (low τ), destabilizing routing; also, STE’s forward-backward discrepancy could introduce bias if the model is near convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Scientific Caption Pipeline Improves Multimodal Scientific Understanding
- **Claim:** The dedicated PDF-based caption pipeline may enhance the model’s scientific vision-language capabilities by providing high-density, aligned image-text pairs that are scarce in standard web corpora.
- **Mechanism:**  
  PDFs are mined for scientific figures using MinerU2.5, deduplicated via pHash, and routed by topic. Scientific images get dense captions from a strong base model (InternVL3.5-241B), while general images use CapRL-32B. Multi-template prompting and a quality discriminator ensure diversity and fidelity, yielding 270B tokens of domain-rich data.
- **Core assumption:** That existing web-crawled image-text data (alt-text) lacks the alignment, detail, and domain density required for scientific VLM training, and that PDFs are an underutilized source of high-quality scientific multimodal data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "A dedicated scientific caption pipeline extracted 270 B high-quality image-text tokens from PDFs"
  - [section 3.1] Full pipeline workflow and motivation (Figure 6,7).
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Innovator-VL" and "SciEvalKit" emphasize the need for specialized scientific data and evaluation, underscoring the domain gap. The lack of cited corpus on data pipelines suggests this is a differentiating factor.
- **Break condition:** If PDF extraction fails to accurately localize figures or if the captioning models (InternVL3.5, CapRL) lack sufficient scientific knowledge, the generated captions may contain hallucinations or low information density, degrading training signal.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Data Layer → Model Core → Training Stack → Evaluation

Data Layer: PDF extraction (MinerU2.5) → Deduplication (pHash) → Captioning (InternVL3.5/CapRL) → Filtering (0.5B discriminator) → 270B scientific caption tokens + general text data.  
Model Core: Trillion-parameter MoE backbone with Grouped Router (EP8, K=8) and STE; Vision encoder (Native ViT) + MLP projector; Time series module (adaptive subsampling + encoder); FoPE for positional encoding.  
Training Stack: XTuner (training) ↔ LMDeploy (inference) co-design; FP8 mixed-precision RL with operator-level alignment, rollout router replay, and importance sampling.  
Evaluation: OpenCompass, VLMEvalKit, AgentCompass for scientific and general benchmarks.

### Critical path
1. **Data preparation:** PDF → figure extraction → captioning → quality filtering → tokenized dataset.  
2. **Pre-training:** Continued pre-training on 6T tokens (scientific + general) with grouped routing and STE, maintaining system prompt isolation.  
3. **Post-training:** Reinforcement learning phase using FP8 with stabilization (rollout router replay, precision alignment) and importance-sampled REINFORCE objective.  
4. **Inference:** LMDeploy engine with grouped routing and FP8 support, consistent with training precision.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Stability vs. Capacity:** Grouped Router initializes groups with similar experts (some homogenization) to ensure stable start, sacrificing immediate expert diversity for training viability.  
- **Precision vs. Memory:** FP8 quantization for expert linear layers reduces memory but risks numerical instability; mitigated by keeping sensitive components (LM head, RMSNorm) in higher precision and operator-level alignment.  
- **Specialization vs. Generality:** Joint training on scientific and general data risks negative transfer; addressed by structured transformation, diversification, and system prompt isolation to maintain separate processing contexts.

### Failure signatures
- **Load imbalance:** GPU memory spikes or straggler delays during MoE training indicate grouped router not functioning (e.g., grouping misconfigured).  
- **RL divergence:** Validation accuracy dropping or KL divergence between train/rollout engines increasing signals precision misalignment or router replay failure.  
- **Poor scientific performance:** Low scores on SciReasoner, SmolInstruct, etc., despite good general performance, suggest caption pipeline quality issues or scientific data conflict not fully resolved.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Grouped router sanity check:** On a small MoE model (e.g., 30B), log expert activations per device during training with and without grouping. Expect balanced counts per device with grouping, imbalance without.  
2. **FP8 RL stability test:** Train a 7B model with RL in FP8 vs BF16 on a held-out set; compare validation accuracy and log-prob KL (should match BF16 as in Figure 8).  
3. **Caption quality validation:** Randomly sample 100 PDF-extracted figures and their generated captions. Have domain experts score alignment (1-5) and scientific detail (1-5). Expect average scores >4.0; low scores indicate pipeline failure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does joint training on general and scientific data yield superior specialized scientific performance compared to training exclusively on domain-specific data for trillion-scale models?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The introduction claims a "sufficiently large generalist model, when trained jointly, can achieve superior performance," but the comparison in Table 4 used the same underlying dataset with only text expression differences, not truly exclusive domain-specific training.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiment did not isolate data distribution effects; both models saw similar biology data. The advantage may stem from scale or text quality rather than joint training synergy.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train a specialist exclusively on biology data (no general data) with comparable capacity, vary the proportion of general data in the generalist, and compare on held-out biology benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the initial homogenization of expert activation in Grouped Router affect long-term expert specialization, load balancing, and final performance compared to alternative initialization schemes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Page 2 states: "While this approach results in some homogenization of expert activation during the initialization phase, the experts naturally differentiate after a few step training" and reports >20 point performance differences between two initialization methods.
- **Why unresolved:** They do not quantify specialization evolution or compare with more strategies over full training. The trade-off between initial stability and long-term expert diversity remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Track expert similarity (e.g., weight cosine similarity, activation patterns) throughout training for different initializations, and correlate with load balancing metrics and final accuracy across diverse benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal temperature schedule for the Straight-Through Estimator in trillion-scale MoE training to maximize router convergence and model performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Page 3 mentions temperature τ as a "continuous knob over gradient sharpness" but does not specify its value or schedule, a critical hyperparameter for STE.
- **Why unresolved:** Temperature balances gradient signal strength versus routing accuracy; a suboptimal choice may hinder router optimization. Its interaction with model scale and training dynamics is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate τ (constant, annealed, layer-wise) on a smaller trillion-parameter proxy model, measuring router load balancing, gradient norms, and downstream task performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the failure modes of the adaptive subsampling for extreme time series (length >10^6, irregular sampling, high dimensionality), and can performance be maintained without specialized time-series architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Page 5 claims the module handles "sequences from 100 to 10^6 time steps" but evaluation was only on a moderate-length subset of SciTS.
- **Why unresolved:** Real scientific time series often exceed these bounds (e.g., climate records, neural signals). The adaptivity mechanism's boundary conditions and limitations are untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark on synthetic and real-world extremes beyond 10^6 steps, with irregular rates and high dimensionality; compare with specialized time-series models and measure degradation points.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How transferable is the PDF-based caption pipeline to scientific images from non-PDF sources (e.g., astronomy FITS, medical DICOM), and what is the minimum PDF-derived data required for robust performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Pages 6–7 describe extracting 270B tokens from PDF corpora as the "primary carrier of scientific visual content" but do not discuss non-PDF sources or generalization.
- **Why unresolved:** Scientific images exist in many formats; PDF layout analysis (MinerU) may not generalize. Minimum data requirements for out-of-distribution robustness are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate on scientific VQA tasks using non-PDF images; conduct controlled experiments varying the proportion of PDF training data; measure performance drops and domain adaptation effectiveness.

## Limitations
- **Architecture hyperparameters undisclosed:** Exact configuration (layers, hidden sizes, expert counts, intermediate dimensions) is not disclosed, blocking precise scale replication and performance attribution.
- **Scientific caption pipeline opacity:** The scale, clustering logic, and training data for the quality discriminator are unknown, making it impossible to reconstruct the 270B token dataset or assess its generality.
- **Insufficient ablations and external validation:** The paper lacks controlled experiments isolating individual contributions, and benchmark results rely on internal suites without independent confirmation, limiting confidence in claimed improvements.

## Confidence
- **Grouped Router & STE Mechanisms:** Medium – Detailed algorithmic descriptions and internal evidence exist, but external validation and rigorous ablations are missing.
- **Scientific Caption Pipeline Impact:** Low – The pipeline's design is described, but its specific contribution to final model capability is inferred, not measured in isolation; dependence on proprietary caption models and an unreleased discriminator prevents independent verification.
- **Reported Performance Numbers:** Medium – Results on proprietary/less-common benchmarks are stated but not externally audited; scores on standard benchmarks are strong but from a single source, needing cross-validation.
- **FP8 RL Stability Claims:** Medium – Stabilization techniques are clearly specified and internal metrics are presented, but the efficiency loss claim depends on co-designed infrastructure not publicly available.
