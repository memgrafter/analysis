# When Shallow Wins: Silent Failures and the Depth-Accuracy Paradox in Latent Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03475
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03475
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Analysis of Qwen2.5-Math-7B on 500 GSM8K problems shows that only 18.4% of correct answers stem from stable faithful reasoning, while 81.6% are lucky guesses, and 8.8% are confident yet incorrect silent failures; deeper latent reasoning does not improve accuracy (depth-accuracy paradox).

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether high benchmark accuracy in mathematical reasoning large language models (LLMs) truly reflects genuine multi-step computation or merely shallow pattern-matching heuristics. Focusing on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the authors analyze a random subset of 500 GSM8K problems through a suite of novel faithfulness metrics designed to probe the stability and coherence of the model's latent (implicit) reasoning process.

Their findings reveal a stark picture: while the model achieves 61% overall accuracy, only 18.4% of the correct answers are grounded in stable, faithful reasoning; a dominant 81.6% of correct results emerge from computationally inconsistent "lucky guesses." Moreover, 8.8% of all outputs constitute silent failures—confident predictions that are internally consistent yet wrong. Notably, scaling from 1.5B to 7B parameters yields no accuracy improvement on this subset, exposing a depth-accuracy paradox. Finally, a mere ~20% of latent reasoning trajectories resemble compressed chain-of-thought, underscoring the prevalence of diverse and often unstable computational strategies. The study concludes that single-sample accuracy metrics conceal fundamental reliability problems and urges a shift toward evaluation frameworks that measure consistency and stability across runs.

## Method Summary
The methodology evaluates a pre-trained model without any additional training. The authors select Qwen2.5-Math-7B and a random sample of 500 test problems from the GSM8K dataset. For each problem, they generate two independent greedy-decoding responses and extract the full stack of layer activations. From these, they compute three faithfulness measures: activation stability (S), which quantifies the cosine similarity of activations across runs per layer with a penalty for high variance; reasoning-hop alignment (A), which detects sharp increases in activation magnitude as candidate reasoning hops and compares their count to the expected number of steps derived from the reference solution; and depth efficiency (E), which evaluates how well the model utilizes its layers relative to the problem's hop structure. A composite fidelity score F(q) is formed as a weighted combination of normalized S, A, and E. Predictions are classified as faithful if S≥0.65 and E≥0.6 (with F≥0.6). The analysis further includes causal interventions (injecting noise into intermediate layers) to test the importance of detected hops and compares implicit latent reasoning to explicit chain-of-thought by measuring trajectory similarity. This pipeline aims to distinguish genuine multi-hop reasoning from unstable pattern-matching and to identify high-confidence errors.

## Key Results
- Only 18.4% of correct answers exhibit stable, faithful reasoning; the vast majority (81.6%) result from unstable "lucky guesses".
- 8.8% of all predictions are silent failures—outputs that are confident (high activation stability) yet incorrect.
- Scaling from 1.5B to 7B parameters produces no accuracy improvement (both 61%), and only ~20% of latent reasoning patterns resemble compressed chain-of-thought, confirming a depth-accuracy paradox and the prevalence of non-CoT strategies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Faithfulness metrics expose unstable computation
The claim is that the proposed metrics can reliably separate stable, genuine latent computation from unstable, heuristic-driven guesses. Faithful reasoning is operationalized as computationally consistent across independent forward passes (high activation stability S), aligned with expected reasoning hops (high alignment A), and depth-efficient (high E). These metrics are aggregated into a composite fidelity score F(q). The core assumption holds that genuine multi-hop reasoning yields reproducible internal representations and proportional use of model depth, whereas shallow pattern-matching produces inconsistent or inefficient activation patterns. Evidence includes formal definitions (Section 3.2) and the finding that only a small fraction of correct answers achieve high fidelity. A break condition would be if the metrics fail to correlate with external ground-truth reasoning steps or do not generalize across models and tasks.

### Mechanism 2: Silent failures as confident errors
The claim is that "silent failures"—confident yet incorrect outputs—represent a significant deployment risk masked by accuracy. These are identified by combining correctness with a stability threshold (S≥0.65). The mechanism relies on activation stability as a proxy for model confidence in its internal computation, beyond surface-level token probabilities. Evidence: 8.8% of outputs are classified as silent failures (44/500). A break condition occurs if S does not correlate with calibrated confidence scores (e.g., entropy) or if errors are not harder to detect.

### Mechanism 3: Depth-accuracy paradox
The claim is that deeper latent reasoning does not guarantee better performance; scaling model parameters may not improve accuracy on certain subsets. The mechanism defines "depth" by reasoning-hop density (ρ_hop = |H|/L). The paradox is observed when accuracy does not increase with higher depth utilization. Evidence: a comparison between 1.5B and 7B models shows identical 61% accuracy despite the larger model exhibiting deeper reasoning (higher D) and lower entropy. The break condition: if the phenomenon does not replicate on the full GSM8K or across other model families, it may be subset-specific.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Model inference (multiple runs) → Activation extraction → Magnitude computation → Stability calculation (S) → Hop detection → Alignment (A) & Efficiency (E) → Composite fidelity (F) → Classification → (Optional) Noise intervention → Trajectory similarity

**Critical path**: The input problem passes through multiple forward passes to obtain layer activations; these are used to compute S, A, E; the three are combined into F; then thresholding classifies the prediction as faithful or not. Additional optional steps include injecting noise for causal analysis and comparing trajectories with explicit CoT.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Stability metric requires multiple runs per problem, increasing computational cost.
- Hop detection uses a fixed 75th percentile threshold (τ_p), which is arbitrarily chosen and may affect scores.
- Composite fidelity weighting (Eq. 9) is empirically set without theoretical justification.
- The analysis discards intermediate outputs, focusing solely on final activations, which may miss temporal aspects.

**Failure signatures**:
- High F but incorrect : potential silent failure (stable yet wrong).
- Low F but correct : likely a lucky guess (unstable reasoning).
- Low variance of S across problems indicates that some problems inherently produce more stable reasoning than others, independent of correctness.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Reproduce fidelity distributions: Run two independent forward passes per GSM8K problem, compute S, A, E, F, and verify the mean F≈0.671 and that about 20% of predictions are faithful.
2. Validate silent failure detection: For incorrect predictions with S≥0.65, manually inspect any generated reasoning traces to confirm that the model exhibited confidence in a wrong answer.
3. Test the depth-accuracy paradox: Compare the 1.5B and 7B Qwen2.5-Math models on the same 500-problem subset; check that overall accuracy is identical (61%) while depth metrics differ (e.g., higher D for the larger model).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1: Do the findings generalize beyond the evaluated subset?** The study used only 500 GSM8K problems (~6% of the benchmark). It remains unclear whether the observed proportions of faithful reasoning, lucky guesses, silent failures, and the depth-accuracy paradox hold on the full dataset and across other mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Full-dataset validation would be needed.
- **Q2: What is the theoretical basis of the faithfulness metrics?** The metrics are empirically motivated and lack formal guarantees. A theoretical framework linking activation patterns to computational steps (e.g., via a theory of transformers or information bottlenecks) is required to understand what the metrics truly capture and to ensure their robustness.
- **Q3: How does problem phrasing affect faithful prediction?** If semantically equivalent formulations of the same problem lead to significantly different faithfulness scores, it would indicate reliance on brittle heuristics. Testing multiple paraphrases of GSM8K items could reveal such sensitivity.
- **Q4: How do alternative latent reasoning designs compare?** The paper only examines standard transformer-based latent reasoning. Comparing with models that employ explicit thinking tokens or recurrent latent loops might yield more stable, interpretable computation and mitigate lucky guesses and silent failures.

## Limitations
- The evaluation covers only a small (≈6%) subset of the GSM8K test set, raising doubts about the representativeness of the reported distributions and the depth-accuracy paradox.
- The chosen thresholds (τ_p = 75% for hop detection, S ≥ 0.65 for stability, etc.) are ad hoc and their sensitivity is not thoroughly analyzed; small changes could alter the reported rates of faithful reasoning and silent failures substantially.
- All experiments are restricted to a single model family (Qwen2.5-Math-7B); without cross-architecture validation, the findings cannot be generalized to other LLMs, scales, or training methodologies.

## Confidence
- Faithfulness metrics distinguish stable reasoning: **Medium**
- High proportion of lucky guesses and silent failures: **Medium**
- Depth-accuracy paradox (no scaling gain): **Medium**
- Scarcity of CoT-like latent trajectories: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Full benchmark replication**: Run the evaluation on the complete GSM8K test set (and ideally additional math reasoning benchmarks) with multiple seeds to verify the stability of the metrics and the generalizability of the observed ratios.
2. **Confidence correlation analysis**: Correlate activation stability (S) with conventional confidence measures (e.g., output entropy, maximum token probability) and manually examine silent failure cases to assess whether S indeed reflects model confidence and error detectability.
3. **Cross-model extension**: Replicate the study on at least two other math-oriented LLMs (e.g., Llama-3-Math-8B, Mistral-7B-Math) to test whether the depth-accuracy paradox and the distribution of faithfulness hold across different architectures and parameter scales.
