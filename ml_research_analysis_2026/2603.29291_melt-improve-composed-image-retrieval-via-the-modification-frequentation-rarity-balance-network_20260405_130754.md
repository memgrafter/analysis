# MELT: Improve Composed Image Retrieval via the Modification Frequentation-Rarity Balance Network
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29291
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29291
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: State-of-the-art performance on FashionIQ (+3.07% avg R@10) and CIRR (+0.45% avg) over prior SOTA.

## Executive Summary
Composed image retrieval (CIR) aims to retrieve a target image given a reference image and a modification text. Existing approaches suffer from frequency bias (neglecting rare modification semantics) and interference from hard negative samples. MELT introduces two complementary modules: Rarity-Aware Token Refinement (RATR) amplifies rare semantics via text-guided attention and statistical rarity scoring; Diffusion-Based Similarity Denoising (DSD) iteratively refines the similarity matrix to suppress noise from hard negatives. Evaluated on FashionIQ and CIRR, MELT achieves significant gains, especially on FashionIQ where rare samples and hard negatives are prevalent, demonstrating the effectiveness of addressing both issues simultaneously.

## Method Summary
MELT builds on the BLIP-2 Q-Former backbone. It encodes the reference image, modification text, and target image into token features Fr, Fm, Ft. The RATR module computes attention between Fm and Fr, selects top-k reference tokens, and evaluates a rarity score R for the modification direction; if R exceeds a threshold, it adds a learned token refinement δ to the selected tokens. The refined reference tokens are fused with Fm by the Q-Former to produce the composed feature Fc. The initial similarity S between Fc and all target features Ft is computed. DSD then reorders S to place the positive target first and negatives in descending similarity, treats this vector as a noisy signal, and applies a diffusion process with a lightweight denoiser to produce a refined similarity Ŝ. The training loss combines a batch contrastive loss L_bbc, a knowledge distillation loss L_KD aligning Ŝ with S, and a KL divergence L_diff between the denoised and ground-truth similarity distributions. Hyperparameters: κ=0.3, λ=0.1, η=0.8, γ=2.2.

## Key Results
- On FashionIQ, MELT improves average R@10 by 3.07% and average R@50 by 2.27% over the strongest prior method.
- On CIRR, it delivers consistent improvements across R@k metrics, with overall average performance increasing by 0.45%.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of both RATR and DSD, with the fashion dataset showing larger relative gains due to its higher prevalence of rare samples and hard negatives.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: RATR amplifies rare modification semantics
**Claim:** RATR identifies and reinforces image tokens that are both highly relevant to the modification text and statistically rare.

**Mechanism:**  
1. Compute text-guided attention weights `w` between modification tokens and reference image tokens using a Transformer decoder.  
2. Select the top-k reference tokens with highest attention (`I`).  
3. Calculate a fitting score `R` = residual energy (Mahalanobis distance of modification direction) × (1 − cosine similarity). High `R` indicates a large and misaligned modification relative to frequent patterns.  
4. If `R > µ_R + γσ_R` (beyond two standard deviations), add a learned refinement `δ` to the selected tokens.

**Core assumption:** Rare modifications are spatially localized (captured by attention) and statistically deviant (captured by Mahalanobis distance and cosine similarity).

**Evidence:** The abstract mentions "asymmetric rare semantic localization"; Section 2.2 formalizes the equations; related works (FineCIR, ConText-CIR) address fine-grained modifications but not this rarity-aware approach.

**Break condition:** If attention focuses on frequent background objects or the rarity score fails to reflect human-perceived rarity, the refinement may reinforce wrong semantics.

### Mechanism 2: DSD robustly estimates similarity via iterative denoising
**Claim:** DSD treats interference from hard negatives as additive noise and uses a diffusion process to recover a cleaner similarity distribution.

**Mechanism:**  
1. For each query, reorder target features and similarity scores so the positive sample is first and negatives are sorted descending.  
2. Treat the reordered similarity vector as `x0`.  
3. Forward diffusion: `x_t = √ᾱ_t x0 + √(1−ᾱ_t) ε`.  
4. Train a lightweight denoiser to reverse this process via DDIM, conditioned on the composed feature `Fc` and target features `T`.  
5. Losses: `L_diff` (KL between denoised and `x0`) and `L_KD` (distillation aligning denoised scores with original scores).

**Core assumption:** Hard negatives create false peaks that can be modeled as Gaussian noise; a diffusion process can sequentially remove this noise while preserving true positive-negative separation.

**Evidence:** The abstract describes "Diffusion-Based Similarity Denoising"; Section 2.3 details the reordering, diffusion, and losses; neighboring methods (DQE-CIR, QuRe) tackle hard negatives via sampling, highlighting the problem but not this solution.

**Break condition:** If the noise is non-Gaussian or the denoiser oversmooths legitimate high-similarity negatives, the refined matrix may harm ranking.

### Mechanism 3: Joint loss balance synergizes objectives
**Claim:** The total loss balances contrastive learning, knowledge distillation, and denoising to prevent either RATR or DSD from dominating and hurting retrieval.

**Mechanism:** The total loss `L = L_bbc + κ L_KD + λ L_diff` combines:
- `L_bbc`: batch-based contrastive loss pulling composed and target features together.
- `L_KD`: knowledge distillation aligning denoised similarity scores with original scores to maintain consistency.
- `L_diff`: KL divergence enforcing agreement between denoised matrix and ground-truth labels.
Hyperparameters `κ` and `λ` weight the denoising components.

**Core assumption:** RATR (rare semantics) and DSD (denoising) have complementary but potentially conflicting objectives; a weighted sum with tuned coefficients allows synergistic optimization.

**Evidence:** Section 2.4 defines the loss; Section 3.3 ablations (D#5, D#6) show removing `L_diff` or `L_KD` hurts performance, confirming their necessity.

**Break condition:** If optimal `κ, λ` vary widely across datasets, the balance may be dataset-specific; over-weighting `L_diff` could cause overfitting to denoising at the expense of feature learning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (e.g., InfoNCE)**  
  Why needed here: The baseline `L_bbc` is a batch-based contrastive loss that forms the foundation for multimodal matching. Understanding negative sampling and temperature scaling is key to interpreting how MELT improves upon this baseline.  
  Quick check question: In `L_bbc`, what is the effect of the temperature parameter `τ` on the sharpness of the similarity distribution?

- **Concept: Diffusion Models (DDIM)**  
  Why needed here: DSD repurposes a diffusion process for denoising similarity matrices. Grasping the forward/reverse process, noise scheduling, and deterministic sampling (DDIM) is essential to understand how iterative refinement occurs.  
  Quick check question: How does DDIM differ from standard DDPM in terms of sampling speed and determinism?

- **Concept: Cross-Attention and Token Selection**  
  Why needed here: RATR relies on a decoder-based cross-attention to compute text-guided weights for image tokens. Understanding how attention sparsity (via top-k) affects localization is critical.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
BLIP-2 Q-Former encodes reference image (`Fr`), modification text (`Fm`), and target image (`Ft`) into token features. RATR takes `Fr` and `Fm`, computes attention, selects top-k tokens, calculates rarity, and refines tokens to produce `ˆFr'` and composed feature `Fc`. DSD receives initial similarity `S` (from `Fc`·`Ft`), reorders it and `Ft`, then iteratively denoises with a semantic denoiser to yield refined similarity `Ŝ`. Losses: `L_bbc` (contrastive), `L_KD` (distillation), `L_diff` (denoising KL).

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode `Fr`, `Fm`, `Ft` using BLIP-2 Q-Former.  
2. RATR: compute attention `w` → select top-k tokens → compute rarity `R` → if rare, add `δ` → obtain refined `Fr'` and compose `Fc`.  
3. Compute initial similarity matrix `S` between `Fc` and all `Ft`.  
4. DSD: reorder `S` and `Ft` (positive first, negatives descending) → add diffusion noise → denoise via DDIM steps → produce `Ŝ`.  
5. Compute total loss `L = L_bbc + κ L_KD + λ L_diff` using `S`, `Ŝ`, and ground-truth labels.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- RATR: `k` (top-k) trades off localization precision vs. noise inclusion; `γ` (rarity threshold) controls false positive/negative rates in rare detection; `η` (refinement intensity) balances amplification.  
- DSD: number of diffusion steps `n` vs. inference speed; denoiser capacity (layers, hidden dim) vs. overfitting; noise schedule `β` affects denoising behavior.  
- Loss weighting: `κ` (KD weight) and `λ` (diffusion KL weight) need dataset-specific tuning.

**Failure signatures:**  
- RATR failure: attention becomes uniform or focuses on background; rarity scores remain low for genuine rare samples; recall on rare queries drops.  
- DSD failure: refined similarity matrix shows reduced separation between positives and hard negatives; overall R@k degrades, especially on FashionIQ.  
- Systemic: Larger performance drop on FashionIQ than CIRR suggests overfitting to fashion-specific patterns.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train BLIP-2 with only `L_bbc` (no MELT modules) to establish the performance gap.  
2. **RATR-only ablation:** Enable RATR but disable DSD (`λ=0`); evaluate on rare query subsets to measure impact of rare semantics handling.  
3. **DSD-only ablation:** Disable RATR (no refinement) but keep DSD; assess on FashionIQ to isolate denoising benefits against hard negatives.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the performance of RATR vary with the rare sample classification threshold γ across different datasets, and does the optimal threshold correlate with dataset characteristics like label distribution entropy?  
2. Does the proposed fitting score based on residual energy and cosine similarity effectively capture semantically important rare modifications, as validated by human judgment?  
3. To what extent are the improvements from RATR and DSD complementary versus overlapping, particularly in datasets with varying ratios of rare samples and hard negatives?  
4. How does MELT's performance scale with increasing candidate pool size in retrieval scenarios, where hard negatives become more prevalent and the similarity matrix sparser?

## Limitations
- **Reproduction blockers:** The semantic denoiser's architecture (layers, hidden size) and exact diffusion hyperparameters (timesteps T, β-schedule) are unspecified; also missing EMA decay rates for rarity statistics, top-k value, and training schedule details.
- **Generalizability risk:** Hyperparameters (γ, η, κ, λ) appear tuned for FashionIQ; performance on CIRR is modest (+0.45%), suggesting potential overfitting to fashion-domain patterns.
- **Mechanism assumptions:** RATR assumes rare modifications are localized and statistically deviant; DSD assumes hard-negative noise is Gaussian. These assumptions may not hold universally, limiting applicability to other domains.

## Confidence
- **RATR mechanism:** Medium-High (supported by equations and ablation, but attention localization needs visual verification).  
- **DSD mechanism:** Medium (backed by diffusion formulation and gains, but denoiser details and noise-model validity are uncertain).  
- **Joint loss balance:** Medium-Low (ablations show both loss terms help, but dataset-specific tuning indicates fragile synergy).
