# Interpretable Graph-Level Anomaly Detection via Contrast with Normal Prototypes
## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID**: 2602.10708
* **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10708
* **Reference count**: 11
* **Primary result**: ProtoGLAD achieves the best average rank (1.25) in AUC among compared methods while providing direct, visual, contrastive explanations that highlight substructural deviations from specific normal prototypes.

## Executive Summary
ProtoGLAD introduces an interpretable framework for graph-level anomaly detection (GLAD) that addresses the black-box nature of existing methods. Instead of relying on abstract latent vectors, it discovers multiple normal clusters and their representative prototype graphs directly from the dataset using a point-set kernel based on Isolation Kernel and Weisfeiler-Lehman embeddings. Anomaly scores are derived from a graph's similarity to its nearest normal cluster. For each detected anomaly, the method provides a concrete explanation by contrasting it with its nearest normal prototype and highlighting nodes that deviate from normal substructure. On eight real-world datasets, ProtoGLAD matches or surpasses state-of-the-art detection performance while offering inherent interpretability.

## Method Summary
ProtoGLAD operates in four stages: 1) **Embedding**: Each graph is converted to a vector by first mapping node attributes via Isolation Kernel (IK) to create binary partition features, then applying Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) iterations to aggregate neighborhood information, and finally averaging node embeddings to obtain a graph-level vector. 2) **Clustering**: A point-set kernel `ˆK(x, C) = <Φ(x), mean_{y∈C} Φ(y)>` is used to iteratively discover dense normal clusters. Starting from a high-similarity prototype graph, a cluster grows by absorbing graphs with similarity above a decaying threshold `γ` until saturation. This repeats on remaining points until a minimum threshold `τ` is reached. 3) **Scoring**: A graph's anomaly score is its maximum similarity to any discovered normal cluster. 4) **Explanation**: For an anomalous graph, its nearest normal prototype is identified. Each node receives a normality score `c(u) = <φ_h(u), Φ(G*_p)>`; low-scoring nodes are highlighted as contributing to the anomaly.

## Key Results
* ProtoGLAD achieves the best average rank (1.25) in AUC across eight real-world datasets, matching or surpassing state-of-the-art methods.
* It provides direct, visual explanations by contrasting an anomalous graph with a concrete normal prototype from the dataset, highlighting specific deviant substructures.
* The use of Isolation Kernel with WL embeddings enhances sensitivity to local structural variations, improving detection performance especially in datasets with uneven graph distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
* **Claim**: ProtoGLAD discovers multiple normal clusters with representative prototype graphs from the dataset using a point-set kernel, enabling contrastive anomaly detection.
* **Mechanism**: The algorithm iteratively selects high-density graph embeddings as prototypes (via point-set similarity `ˆK`), then expands each cluster by absorbing graphs with similarity above a decaying threshold `γ`. Remaining points dissimilar to all clusters become anomalies.
* **Core assumption**: Normal graphs form dense, separable clusters in the embedding space, while anomalies are isolated.
* **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "employs a point-set kernel to iteratively discover multiple normal prototype graphs and their associated clusters"; [section 4.2] Full clustering algorithm (Algorithm 1) and point-set kernel definition `ˆK(x, C) = ⟨Φ(x), ˆΦ(C)⟩`.
* **Break condition**: If normal graphs do not form dense clusters (e.g., uniform distribution or multimodality with sparse regions), clustering may misidentify normal graphs as anomalies or produce non-representative prototypes.

### Mechanism 2
* **Claim**: Node-level normality scores highlight substructural deviations by measuring each node's similarity to the nearest normal prototype.
* **Mechanism**: For an anomalous graph `G_A`, its nearest prototype `G*_p` is identified. Each node `u` in `G_A` receives a score `c(u) = ⟨φ_h(u), Φ(G*_p)⟩`. Low scores indicate nodes that contribute to anomalousness.
* **Core assumption**: Node embeddings `φ_h(u)` preserve structural semantics such that similarity to a normal prototype's mean embedding indicates normality.
* **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "provides explanation for each detected anomaly by explicitly contrasting with its nearest normal prototype graph"; [section 4.3] Formal definition of node normality score and explanation process.
* **Break condition**: If node embeddings are not discriminative (e.g., all nodes yield similar scores), the explanation fails to pinpoint anomalous substructures.

### Mechanism 3
* **Claim**: The combination of Isolation Kernel (IK) and Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) embeddings produces a graph representation that enhances sensitivity to local structural variations, improving detection.
* **Mechanism**: IK maps node features into binary partition vectors that adapt to local data density. WL iteratively aggregates these IK-mapped features from neighborhoods. The mean of node embeddings yields a graph-level embedding where dot-product similarity reflects both structure and attributes.
* **Core assumption**: A data-dependent kernel (IK) better captures subtle deviations in graph distributions than fixed kernels (e.g., RBF), especially in sparse or irregular datasets.
* **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "point-set kernel based on Isolation Kernel and Weisfeiler-Lehman embeddings"; [section 4.1] IK-WL embedding process and graph similarity via dot product; [section 3.3/4.1] Cites [Xu et al., 2021] showing IK improves WL's discriminative power.
* **Break condition**: If graphs are structurally very simple (e.g., all trees of similar size), WL without IK might suffice; IK's density adaptation provides marginal gain, but computational overhead may not justify benefits.

## Foundational Learning
* **Concept**: Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) graph embeddings  
  *Why needed here*: Provides a fast, kernel-based method to convert attributed graphs into fixed-dimensional node and graph vectors without training.  
  *Quick check*: What does increasing WL iteration `h` capture in terms of graph structure?
* **Concept**: Point-set kernel `ˆK(x, C)`  
  *Why needed here*: Extends graph similarity from pairwise to graph-to-set, allowing a graph to be compared against a cluster of normal graphs (via mean embedding).  
  *Quick check*: How does `ˆK(x, C)` differ from the pairwise kernel `κ(x, y)`?
* **Concept**: Isolation Kernel (IK)  
  *Why needed here*: A data-dependent kernel that adapts to local density, making similarity measures more sensitive to anomalies in unevenly distributed graph embeddings.  
  *Quick check*: Why is IK considered "data-dependent" and how does this help anomaly detection?

## Architecture Onboarding
* **Component map**: Input Graphs -> IK-WL Embedding Module -> Point-Set Kernel Clustering Module -> Anomaly Scoring Module -> Explanation Module
* **Critical path**: Embeddings → Clustering → Scoring → Explanation. Errors in embedding propagate; clustering must complete before scoring.
* **Design tradeoffs**: 
  * Interpretability vs. expressiveness: Concrete prototypes offer direct contrast but may miss abstract normal patterns that learned latent vectors capture.
  * Non-parametric vs. scalability: IK-WL is fast and parameter-light but may struggle with very large graphs; GNN-based methods scale better but are black-box.
* **Failure signatures**:
  * Poor clustering: Many graphs remain unclustered or clusters are tiny/overly broad.
  * Unrepresentative prototypes: Selected `G*_p` are themselves anomalous (check visually or via low `ˆK` with other cluster members).
  * Uniform node scores: All `c(u)` similar → embeddings not capturing substructural variations.
* **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Synthetic validation**: Use dataset with known anomalous motifs (Table 2). Verify prototypes contain normal motifs and explanations highlight anomalous insertions.
  2. **AUC benchmarking**: Run 5-fold CV on 8 datasets (Table 1). Confirm ProtoGLAD achieves rank ≈1.25 and investigate failures (e.g., DHFR/NCI1).
  3. **Qualitative comparison**: On MUTAG, compare explanations with SIGNET (bottleneck subgraph, no normal reference) and GLADPro (latent prototypes). Check that ProtoGLAD’s prototypes are concrete normal graphs and node colors correctly indicate deviation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
* **Open Question 1**: Is the use of concrete normal prototypes from the dataset fundamentally necessary for effective and trustworthy interpretability in GLAD, or could well-regularized latent prototypes achieve comparable explanatory validity?
* **Open Question 2**: How should the number of normal clusters (k) be determined in an unsupervised manner, and what is the impact of over- or under-segmentation of normality on both detection accuracy and the fidelity of explanations?
* **Open Question 3**: Do the node-level normality scores (c(u)) computed as similarity to the prototype embedding accurately identify the substructural elements responsible for a graph's anomalousness, and are these explanations correlated with human judgment or ground-truth anomalous motifs?
* **Open Question 4**: How robust is the Isolation Kernel-enhanced Weisfeiler-Lehman (IK-WL) embedding to variations in graph properties (e.g., size, density, attribute distribution), and could its sensitivity to such variations introduce bias in the discovered normal prototypes and subsequent anomaly judgments?

## Limitations
* Hyperparameters undisclosed (`t`, `ψ`, `τ`, `ϱ`) prevent faithful reproduction and may hide dataset-specific tuning.
* No quantitative explanation evaluation (e.g., fidelity, sparsity) against ground-truth anomalous substructures.
* Scalability unaddressed; complexity likely superlinear in graph size, with no comparison to GNN-based methods on large graphs.

## Confidence
* **Interpretability**: High – Method explicitly contrasts with concrete prototypes; visual explanations provided.
* **AUC performance**: Medium – Strong reported rank (1.25) but missing hyperparameters hinders replication confidence.
* **Mechanism (IK-WL + clustering)**: Medium – Theoretically sound and validated on synthetic data, but break conditions threaten robustness; minimal corpus support.
