# Less is More for RAG: Information Gain Pruning for Generator-Aligned Reranking and Evidence Selection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17532
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17532
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** IGP improves the quality‑cost trade‑off in RAG, achieving a relative 12‑20% token‑level F1 increase while reducing final‑stage input tokens by 76‑79% compared to retriever‑only baselines, by pruning evidence based on generator‑perceived information gain.

## Executive Summary
Retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) systems often rely on retrieval relevance metrics (e.g., NDCG) to rerank passages under a fixed context budget. However, this approach correlates poorly with end‑to‑end QA quality when multiple passages are injected, as redundancy and mild conflicts destabilize generation. This paper introduces Information Gain Pruning (IGP), a plug‑and‑play module that replaces the reranking step. IGP scores each candidate passage by the reduction in the generator’s output uncertainty (information gain) when that passage is injected, using only step‑wise logits. It then prunes passages below a utility threshold before truncation, filtering out weak or harmful evidence while preserving the original budget interface. Across five open‑domain QA benchmarks, IGP consistently yields a better quality‑cost trade‑off, with up to 12‑20% relative F1 gains and 76‑79% fewer input tokens. The gains stem primarily from threshold‑based admission control rather than pure reordering, and the method proves robust across different retrievers and generator scales—even enabling a smaller model with IGP to outperform a larger one without it.

## Method Summary
IGP is a lightweight module that sits between the first‑stage retriever and the truncation stage. For a given query, it first computes a baseline "normalized uncertainty" (NU) by rolling out the generator’s greedy decoding without any retrieved passages. It then computes the NU for each candidate passage injected individually. The information gain (IG) for a passage is the difference between the baseline NU and the conditional NU. Passages are ranked by IG descending; a pruning threshold $T_p$ is applied to admit only passages with IG $\ge T_p$ into the candidate pool. The remaining truncation step (e.g., Top‑M) selects the final evidence set within the token budget. This process requires only access to the generator’s step‑wise logits and uses a fixed probing prompt to isolate the effect of evidence injection.

## Key Results
- IGP achieves a relative 12‑20% increase in average token‑level F1 while reducing final‑stage input tokens by 76‑79% compared to retriever‑only baselines in a multi‑evidence setting.
- The improvements are primarily driven by threshold‑based admission control (pruning) rather than reordering alone; IG‑only ranking without pruning yields minimal end‑to‑end gains.
- IGP is robust across different first‑stage retrievers (BM25, Contriever) and generator families/scales, and can enable a smaller model with IGP to outperform a larger model without it.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Offline Relevance Mismatch
- **Claim:** Offline retrieval relevance metrics (e.g., NDCG) are weakly correlated with—and can even be negatively correlated with—end‑to‑end QA quality (F1) under multi‑passage injection, due to generator‑side destabilization from redundant/conflicting evidence.
- **Mechanism:** When multiple passages are injected, even highly relevant ones may contain parallel claims, conditional constraints, or contradictions. This spreads the generator’s token‑level output probability mass, increases uncertainty on key tokens, and makes generation less stable and sometimes less accurate. Thus, optimizing for retrieval relevance does not optimize for the marginal utility of evidence under a fixed context budget.
- **Core assumption:** The generator’s output distribution concentration (or its inverse, uncertainty) is a more direct signal of evidence utility for generation than retrieval relevance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract states that retrieval relevance metrics correlate weakly with end‑to‑end QA quality and can become negatively correlated under multi‑passage injection due to destabilization.
  - Section 4.4.1 (Spearman correlation on NQ) shows weak/negative correlation between NDCG and F1 as evidence budget increases; Table 5 shows IGP achieving lower NDCG but higher F1.
  - Existing work on context pruning (e.g., “Provence”) supports the identified problem of noisy context harming generation.
- **Break condition:** If strong positive correlation between NDCG and end‑to‑end F1 is consistently found across multi‑evidence settings and diverse benchmarks, the claimed mismatch would be undermined.

### Mechanism 2: Generator‑Aligned Utility via Information Gain
- **Claim:** Information Gain Pruning (IGP) provides a generator‑aligned utility signal for evidence selection by measuring the reduction in the generator’s uncertainty (information gain) when a candidate passage is injected, using only step‑wise logits.
- **Mechanism:** IGP computes a normalized uncertainty (NU) proxy from TOPK‑truncated and renormalized token distributions under greedy decoding. NU is averaged across rollout steps. Information gain (IG) is defined as the difference between unconditional NU (no evidence) and conditional NU (with a single passage). A higher IG indicates the passage makes the generator more decisive (lowers entropy) on the generation trajectory. This signal is used to rerank and prune candidates before truncation.
- **Core assumption:** Under a fixed prompt and decoding protocol, the relative reduction in TOPK entropy across greedy rollout steps is a reproducible, low‑variance proxy for a passage’s marginal utility for the final generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: IGP scores passages by reduction in generator’s output uncertainty (information gain) using step‑wise logits.
  - Section 3.2 defines TOPK normalized uncertainty via renormalization over top‑K tokens and averaging across steps.
  - Section 3.3 defines IG and explains that useful evidence increases logit margins on key tokens, reducing TOPK entropy.
- **Break condition:** If controlled experiments show that IG scores do not correlate with human judgments of evidence helpfulness or with end‑to‑end F1 improvements after pruning, the generator‑aligned utility claim would be weakened. Also, if black‑box logit access becomes infeasible or prohibitively expensive in deployment, the practicality assumption fails.

### Mechanism 3: Pruning Threshold as the Primary Driver
- **Claim:** The primary gains of IGP come from threshold‑based admission control (pruning weak/negative‑utility passages) rather than reordering alone, especially under wide evidence budgets where truncation would otherwise admit many low‑utility passages.
- **Mechanism:** IGP applies a pruning threshold $T_p$ after IG‑based ranking: only passages with IG $\ge T_p$ enter the candidate list for truncation. This explicitly filters out evidence that introduces ambiguity, redundancy, or conflicts (negative IG) before the budget‑limited truncation step. Reordering without pruning (e.g., using IG as a score but $T_p = -\infty$) yields minimal end‑to‑end gains because truncation still admits the full budget of passages, including those that spread the distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Under a fixed TopM budget, filtering out passages that increase generator uncertainty (IG < 0) or provide negligible gain (IG ≈ 0) is more effective than merely changing their order, because truncation will still select the top‑M passages from the full candidate set.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: gains are primarily driven by threshold‑based admission control rather than reordering alone.
  - Section 4.2, Table 4: Comparing +IG (reordering only) vs. +IGP($T_p$=0.0) and +IGP($T_p$=0.05) shows substantial token reduction and F1 improvement only when pruning threshold is applied, especially for TopM=5.
  - Section 4.3.1, Figure 4: unimodal sensitivity of F1 to $T_p$, with optimal values around small positive thresholds, confirming the importance of the admission barrier.
- **Break condition:** If future work demonstrates a method that achieves comparable quality‑cost improvements using pure reordering (no thresholding) on the same benchmarks and budget interfaces, the pruning‑dominant claim would be challenged.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs**
  - *Why needed here:* IGP’s core signal (information gain) is defined as the reduction in a normalized uncertainty proxy computed from token logits. Understanding how entropy and probability concentration relate to model confidence is essential to grasp why NU serves as a utility proxy.
  - *Quick check question:* If a passage makes the generator’s next‑token distribution more peaked (lower entropy) on key steps, what does that imply about the passage’s likely utility for answering the query?

- **Concept: Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) Pipeline**
  - *Why needed here:* IGP is designed as a drop‑in replacement for the *rerank* stage in the standard retrieve→rerank→truncate pipeline. Knowing the pipeline structure, budget interfaces (TopM, token guard B), and the role of each stage is necessary to understand IGP’s placement and constraints.
  - *Quick check question:* In a budgeted RAG system, what is the functional difference between the *rerank* stage and the *truncate* stage? Why is it significant that IGP only replaces *rerank*?

- **Concept: Information Gain / Mutual Information Intuition**
  - *Why needed here:* IGP’s utility score is called “information gain” and is inspired by the reduction in uncertainty given evidence. While not an unbiased mutual information estimator, the conceptual link helps understand the objective: select evidence that most reduces the generator’s uncertainty about its own output.
  - *Quick check question:* If passage A reduces uncertainty more than passage B when injected alone, what might that say about their relative decisiveness for the query, assuming the generator is properly prompted?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Retriever → IGP Module → Truncate → Generator  
(Probing Orchestrator manages the N+1 rollout calls for IGP inside the IGP Module)

### Critical path
1. Receive query q and initial candidate set D from retriever.
2. Compute unconditional NU rollout ν₀ = ÛNU(q; φ, K) once.
3. For each passage dᵢ in parallel: compute conditional NU νᵢ = ÛNU(q|dᵢ; φ, K), then IG sᵢ = ν₀ − νᵢ.
4. Sort passages by sᵢ descending → initial list L.
5. Filter L to keep only passages with sᵢ ≥ Tₚ.
6. Pass L to existing truncate module to get final evidence set S (TopM and/or token guard B).
7. Final generation with S.

### Design tradeoffs
- **TOPK (K):** Smaller K increases sensitivity to local logit ties but reduces cost; larger K improves entropy resolution but increases cost. Choose moderate K (e.g., 128) after ensuring rollout length is sufficient.
- **Max rollout length (MT):** Shorter MT reduces probing cost but increases variance in IG rankings; longer MT stabilizes rankings but adds cost. Prioritize increasing MT until F1 plateaus, then tune K.
- **Pruning threshold (Tₚ):** Lower Tₚ increases coverage but risks including weak/negative-utility evidence; higher Tₚ strengthens denoising but may exclude necessary evidence. Start with Tₚ ≈ 0.05 and sweep ±0.05 based on validation set pass rate and F1.
- **Prompt design:** Probing prompt must be neutral and fixed across unconditional/conditional rollouts to isolate the effect of evidence injection. Changes alter the NU baseline and break comparability.

### Failure signatures
- **Very low pass rate** (fraction of passages with IG ≥ Tₚ): Tₚ likely too high, or retriever/corpus changed. Consider lowering Tₚ or reverting to relevance‑based reranking.
- **IG distribution drift over time** (mean IG shifts): Indicates retriever, corpus, or generator drift. Recalibrate Tₚ or temporarily disable pruning.
- **No improvement over baseline despite pruning:** Could be due to (a) too short MT causing noisy IG rankings; (b) probing prompt not aligned with final generation prompt; (c) generator’s uncertainty signal not predictive of utility for this task.
- **Large variance in F1 across threshold sweeps:** Suggests IG ranking is unstable; increase MT or adjust K.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Threshold Sensitivity Sweep:** Run IGP with Tₚ ∈ [−0.1, 0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2] under TopM=1 on a validation set. Plot F1 vs. Tₚ to locate the “sweet spot.” Also monitor the fraction of candidates passing threshold to ensure sufficient coverage.
2. **Pruning vs. Reordering Ablation:** Compare three configurations: (a) +IG (IG ranking but Tₚ = −∞, no pruning), (b) +IGP(Tₚ=0.0), (c) retriever‑only. Measure change in final‑stage input tokens and F1. Expect minimal change from (a), significant token reduction and F1 gain from (b).
3. **Retriever Robustness Check:** Repeat the main comparison (BM25 vs. Contriever as first‑stage retriever) with the same IGP hyperparameters (K, MT, Tₚ). If IGP’s Pareto shift persists, the gains are retriever‑agnostic. If gains vanish with one retriever, investigate whether candidate quality (e.g., redundancy level) differs.

## Limitations
- IGP’s core assumption—that generator uncertainty (via TOPK entropy) is a reliable utility proxy—depends on the generator’s calibration and the probing prompt’s neutrality. Its effectiveness may degrade if the generator’s distribution becomes miscalibrated or if the evidence requires multi‑hop reasoning not captured by single‑passage uncertainty.
- The pruning threshold (Tₚ) is dataset‑dependent and may need recalibration for new domains or model updates.
- IGP’s long‑term robustness across all generator families/scales and its generalizability to non‑QA tasks (e.g., summarization) remain uncertain; the paper tests several models but not the full spectrum.

## Confidence
- **High:** IGP improves quality‑cost trade‑offs in the tested open‑domain QA settings; the gains are empirically demonstrated across five benchmarks.
- **Medium:** The primary driver is threshold‑based pruning rather than reordering; this is supported by ablation studies but may vary with budget sizes and evidence redundancy patterns.
- **Low:** IGP’s generalizability to non‑QA tasks and its full spectrum of architectures; the paper tests several models but not the entire landscape.

## Next Checks
1. **Correlation Stress Test:** Compute Spearman correlation between NDCG@M and end‑to‑end F1 across *multiple* evidence budgets (M=1,5,10) on *all* five benchmarks. Verify whether the negative/weak correlation persists uniformly or is benchmark‑specific.
2. **Pruning Mechanism Isolation:** Compare three systems: (a) IGP with pruning (Tₚ>0), (b) IGP *without* pruning (ranking only, Tₚ=−∞), (c) retriever‑only. Measure (i) final token count and (ii) F1. The gap between (a) and (b) must be significant to confirm pruning is the dominant factor.
3. **Scalability & Cost Profile:** Benchmark IGP’s overhead (additional forward passes per candidate) against the final generation cost for varying candidate set sizes (e.g., 100 vs. 1000). Identify the maximum viable candidate pool before IGP’s probing cost outweighs generation savings from pruning.
