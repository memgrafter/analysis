# CLASE: A Hybrid Method for Chinese Legalese Stylistic Evaluation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.12639
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12639
*   **Reference Count:** 0
*   **Primary Result:** A hybrid method combining logistic regression on linguistic features and an LLM judge achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.8271 with human expert judgments on Chinese legal text style, outperforming ROUGE (0.4250) and pure LLM-as-judge (0.3049).

## Executive Summary
CLASE tackles the neglected problem of automatically evaluating the stylistic quality of LLM-generated Chinese legal text. Existing reference-based metrics like ROUGE ignore style, while LLM-as-judge methods are opaque and costly. CLASE’s core innovation is a hybrid, annotation-free framework that learns from contrastive pairs of authentic legal documents and their stylistically degraded or restored versions. It merges an objective score from a logistic regression model trained on 100 linguistic features with a subjective score from an LLM judge guided by a constructed "experience pool." On 200 Chinese civil judgments, CLASE-Mix demonstrates a strong correlation (0.8271) with human expert assessments, providing both high accuracy and interpretable, natural language feedback on stylistic flaws.

## Method Summary
The method operates without manual annotations by synthesizing training data. It generates contrastive pairs by applying style-preserving degradation (e.g., simplifying syntax, removing formulaic phrases) and restoration operations to authentic legal documents. From these pairs, 100 handcrafted linguistic features (e.g., sentence length, passive voice ratio, paragraph structure) are extracted. A logistic regression model is trained to predict whether a text is "authentic" or "degraded," producing an objective score. Separately, an experience pool of exemplar texts with high/low stylistic scores is constructed. An LLM judge (e.g., Qwen2.5) evaluates a target document by comparing it to this pool, yielding a subjective score. The final CLASE-Mix score is a weighted sum of the two, with weights guided by empirical performance on a validation set.

## Key Results
*   **High Correlation with Humans:** CLASE-Mix achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.8271 with human expert judgments on 200 Chinese civil judgment reasoning sections.
*   **Strong Superiority:** This substantially outperforms traditional reference-based metrics (ROUGE: 0.4250) and pure LLM-as-judge methods (Qwen2.5-72B-ref: 0.3049).
*   **Interpretable Feedback:** The framework generates natural language feedback pointing to specific stylistic deficiencies, such as "lack of rigorous logical connectors" or "inconsistent use of legal terminology."

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CLASE works by decoupling the style evaluation problem into an **objective, feature-driven component** and a **subjective, comparative component**, then fusing them. The contrastive learning setup (authentic vs. degraded) implicitly defines the target style without needing human labels, allowing the logistic regression to learn which linguistic patterns are hallmarks of professional legal writing. The LLM judge, primed with an experience pool of high/low-quality examples, captures nuanced, holistic stylistic preferences that may not be fully encoded in the 100 features. The hybrid fusion mitigates the brittleness of pure feature-based methods and the opacity/unpredictability of pure LLM judges, leading to robust performance and explainable outputs.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Contrastive Learning for Style:** Learns a stylistic representation by discriminating between authentic and synthetically degraded texts. *Why needed:* Provides a scalable, annotation-free training signal for "good" legal style. *Quick check:* Verify that the feature distribution of degraded texts is significantly shifted from authentic ones using a multivariate test (e.g., MANOVA).
*   **Linguistic Feature Engineering:** The manual design of 100 features (e.g., lexical density, modal verb frequency, clause chaining) to quantify legal text formality and structure. *Why needed:* Offers an interpretable, low-dimensional signal that complements the LLM. *Quick check:* Compute feature importance from the logistic regression; top features should align with known legal writing conventions (e.g., high use of "shall," low pronoun density).
*   **LLM-as-Judge with Experience Pool:** Using an LLM to score text by comparing it to a curated set of high- and low-quality exemplars. *Why needed:* Captures complex, expert-like holistic judgments beyond handcrafted features. *Quick check:* Measure LLM judge consistency by re-evaluating a sample with different seed prompts; high variance indicates poor prompt design.
*   **Hybrid Score Fusion:** Combining the objective (logistic) and subjective (LLM) scores via weighted summation. *Why needed:* Balances reliability (features) and nuance (LLM) to maximize human agreement. *Quick check:* Perform a sensitivity analysis on the fusion weights on a validation set; optimal weights should be stable across adjacent values.
*   **Synthetic Data Generation for Style:** Applying controlled perturbations (degradation) and corrections (restoration) to real legal documents. *Why needed:* Creates a large-scale, cost-free training corpus of known style quality pairs. *Quick check:* Human evaluation of a sample pair: the degraded version must be stylistically worse but semantically equivalent to the original.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Authentic Chinese Legal Documents -> Degradation/Restoration Models -> Contrastive Pair Dataset -> Feature Extractor (100 linguistic features) -> Logistic Regression Trainer -> Objective Score Module; Authentic Documents + Expert Intuition -> Experience Pool Construction (high/low exemplars) -> LLM Judge (Qwen2.5) -> Subjective Score Module; Objective Score + Subjective Score -> Weighted Sum -> CLASE-Mix Final Score + Feedback Generator

**Critical Path:** The construction of the **experience pool** is the critical path. It operationalizes expert stylistic knowledge and directly conditions the LLM judge's behavior. Without a representative, high-quality pool, the subjective score becomes unreliable.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
1.  **Accuracy vs. Cost:** CLASE's multi-stage pipeline (feature extraction + LLM inference) is computationally heavier than a single ROUGE call but yields far higher accuracy.
2.  **Generality vs. Specificity:** The feature set and experience pool are tailored to Chinese civil judgments, maximizing in-domain performance but limiting out-of-domain transfer.
3.  **Interpretability vs. Performance:** The logistic regression provides interpretable feature weights, but the LLM judge component remains a black box, reducing full system interpretability.

**Failure Signatures:**  
1.  **Low Human Correlation (Pearson < 0.6):** Indicates either flawed feature set (fails to capture style) or a misaligned LLM judge (experience pool unrepresentative).
2.  **Semantic Corruption in Synthetic Pairs:** If degradation models alter meaning (e.g., changing legal obligations), the logistic regression learns spurious correlations, hurting generalization.
3.  **High Variance in LLM Judge Scores:** For the same input, large score swings across random seeds suggest poor prompt design or an unstable experience pool.

**First Three Experiments to Run:**  
1.  **Feature Weight Validation:** Present the top 20 weighted features from the trained logistic regression to a legal expert. Ask: "Do these reflect core principles of good Chinese legal writing?" A low positive rating (<70% agreement) suggests feature design flaws.
2.  **LLM Judge Prompt Sensitivity Test:** Keep the experience pool fixed but vary the LLM judge's prompt phrasing (e.g., "rate 1-5" vs. "compare to examples"). Compute score correlation across prompt variants; high variance (>0.2 std dev) necessitates prompt stabilization.
3.  **Inference Latency & Memory Benchmark:** Run CLASE-Mix on a standardized hardware setup (e.g., A100 GPU) with a batch size of 1. Record end-to-end time and peak memory. Compare against ROUGE-L and a single LLM-as-judge baseline to quantify the hybrid method's overhead.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How well does CLASE generalize to languages other than Chinese and legal domains beyond civil judgments?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors state experiments are restricted to Chinese civil judgments and would require adaptation for other languages or legal domains, specifically domain-specific experience pool construction.
*   **Why unresolved:** CLASE has only been validated on one specific genre (reasoning sections of Chinese civil judgments). Its performance on statutes, contracts, criminal rulings, or non-Chinese legal systems is unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the full CLASE pipeline (including feature re-engineering and experience pool building) to multilingual legal corpora (e.g., English contracts, German statutes) and diverse document sections, then compute correlation with local expert judgments to measure transfer performance.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Do the synthetically generated contrastive pairs preserve semantics while altering style, and how does their quality affect CLASE's performance?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors acknowledge they did not perform a large-scale manual audit of the synthetic pairs and that the degradation step assumes style alteration without semantic corruption.
*   **Why unresolved:** The validity of the training data is assumed but not verified. If semantic content is unintentionally changed during degradation, the logistic regression could learn to predict semantic coherence rather than pure style.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a human evaluation of a stratified sample of contrastive pairs. Annotators rate (a) semantic equivalence and (b) stylistic difference between the authentic and degraded versions. Correlate these human ratings with CLASE's feature weight patterns and overall scores on a held-out set.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Can CLASE's computational overhead be reduced for low-latency use cases without sacrificing correlation with human judgments?
*   **Basis in paper:** The limitation section explicitly states that CLASE's multi-stage pipeline demands higher computational resources, potentially limiting adoption in low-latency applications.
*   **Why unresolved:** Inference speed and memory footprint are not quantified, and no optimization strategies (e.g., model distillation, caching) are explored.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark CLASE's end-to-end latency and memory usage on standardized hardware. Then, systematically trade components: (a) distill the LLM judge, (b) cache feature vectors for the experience pool, (c) replace the feature extractor with a lightweight neural model. Measure the correlation-human judgment trade-off curve for each optimization.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** What specific linguistic features drive CLASE's objective scoring, and how do they align with expert intuitions about legal style?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors admit the logistic regression's "knowing-what but not knowing-why" limitation and that the mechanisms by which weights capture professional preferences remain opaque.
*   **Why unresolved:** While the model uses 100 features, the interpretability of the logistic regression coefficients is limited. It's unclear which features are most critical and whether they match what legal professionals consciously value.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** (a) Analyze and rank features by absolute coefficient weight. (b) Present the top-weighted features and their expected direction (e.g., "higher passive voice = better score") to a panel of legal experts for validation. (c) Conduct an ablation study where groups of features (e.g., syntactic, lexical) are removed to quantify their isolated contribution to the final human correlation.

## Limitations
*   **Corpus Scale & Diversity:** Evaluation is on only 200 documents from a single sub-domain (Chinese civil judgments), limiting statistical power and generalizability claims.
*   **Unspecified Human Evaluation:** The protocol for collecting expert judgments is not detailed (number of experts, their qualifications, inter-rater reliability), making it hard to gauge the gold standard's robustness.
*   **Fixed Hybrid Weighting:** The fusion weight between the logistic and LLM scores is "experience-guided" but not systematically derived from a validation set or optimized via a held-out human evaluation, potentially missing an optimal balance.
*   **Domain-Tied Design:** The linguistic feature set and experience pool construction are deeply intertwined with conventions of Chinese legal writing, making direct application to other languages or formal domains (e.g., academic prose) non-trivial.

| Performance superiority on Chinese civil judgments (Pearson 0.8271) | High       |

*   **High:** The result is empirically strong on the tested corpus, with clear, large-margin improvements over baselines.
*   **Medium:** The feedback generation mechanism is described, but its practical utility for legal practitioners is unvalidated by user study.
*   **Low:** The framework's design is highly specific to the source domain; cross-domain transfer is hypothesized as requiring significant adaptation, not demonstrated.

## Next Checks
1.  **Scale and Diversify the Evaluation Corpus:** Replicate the study on a corpus of ≥1,000 Chinese legal documents spanning civil, criminal, and administrative law. Collect ratings from multiple qualified experts and report inter-rater reliability (ICC) to strengthen the gold standard and test performance stability.
2.  **Conduct a Full Component Ablation Study:** Systematically remove or modify key components: (a) use only logistic regression scores, (b) use only LLM judge scores, (c) remove subsets of linguistic features (e.g., all syntactic features), (d) randomize the experience pool. Quantify each component's contribution to the final Pearson correlation.
3.  **Test Cross-Lingual and Cross-Domain Transfer:** Apply CLASE's feature extractor and LLM judge (with minimal prompt adaptation) to English legal texts (e.g., US contracts) and other formal domains (e.g., academic paper abstracts). Measure the decay in correlation with local human judgments to empirically define the boundaries of transferability.
