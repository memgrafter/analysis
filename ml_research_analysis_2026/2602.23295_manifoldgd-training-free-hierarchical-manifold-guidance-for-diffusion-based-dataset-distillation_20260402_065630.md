# ManifoldGD: Training-Free Hierarchical Manifold Guidance for Diffusion-Based Dataset Distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23295
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23295
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ManifoldGD achieves state-of-the-art training-free dataset distillation, e.g., 64.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNette IPC=10 (ResNetAP-10) with improved FID, ℓ2, and MMD.

## Executive Summary
ManifoldGD introduces a training-free framework for dataset distillation that leverages diffusion models to generate high-quality synthetic datasets. The core innovation is hierarchical manifold guidance: it extracts representative centroids via divisive clustering on VAE latents, constructs timestep-aligned local manifold patches, and projects the mode guidance vector onto the tangent space of the estimated manifold during denoising. This prevents off-manifold drift while preserving semantic alignment, yielding samples that are both faithful and diverse. Extensive evaluation on ImageNette, ImageWoof, and ImageNet-100 demonstrates consistent improvements over existing training-free methods and even some training-based baselines, with notable gains in accuracy, FID, and diversity metrics.

## Method Summary
ManifoldGD proceeds as follows: First, a VAE encodes all real training images into a latent space. Second, for each class, a divisive hierarchical clustering algorithm builds a binary tree on the latents; by traversing from a chosen start depth, it selects IPC centroids and their static neighborhoods (all points within a radius). These centroids serve as anchors for synthetic samples. Third, during distillation, each synthetic sample is assigned a centroid and its neighborhood. For each diffusion timestep, the neighborhood is forward-diffused to form a local manifold patch consistent with the current noise level. The current latent's nearest neighbors on this patch are used to estimate the local tangent space via PCA. The mode guidance vector (computed via an RBF kernel between the latent and its centroid) is projected onto the tangent space, removing the normal component. This corrected guidance is added to the standard diffusion score to update the latent. After reversing the diffusion process, the VAE decoder produces the synthetic image. The method is training-free, requiring no retraining of the diffusion model, and relies on careful hyperparameter tuning (e.g., neighborhood radius, tangent dimension, start depth) per dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieves 64.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNette with IPC=10, surpassing MGD (61.9%).
- Demonstrates lower FID, ℓ2 distance, and MMD across ImageNette, ImageWoof, and ImageNet-100, indicating improved representativeness, diversity, and fidelity.
- Outperforms both training-free (e.g., MGD, DiT) and some training-based dataset distillation baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Hierarchical centroid selection**  
ManifoldGD uses divisive hierarchical clustering (bisecting k-means) on VAE latents per class to select IPC centroids. By traversing the clustering tree from a start depth, it picks centroids at various levels, capturing both coarse semantic modes and fine intra-class variability. This deterministic multi-scale coreset balances global and specific modes, improving representativeness and diversity. Core assumption: the VAE latent space has a hierarchical structure where splits correspond to semantically meaningful sub-modes. Evidence includes the abstract and Section 3.3, though direct corpus support is weak. Break condition: if latent clusters are entangled, chosen centroids may miss key modes, degrading performance.

**Mechanism 2: Timestep-aligned local manifold patches**  
To ensure denoising updates occur on a manifold consistent with the current noise level, ManifoldGD constructs a local manifold patch \(M_t\) for each centroid. A static neighbor set \(N_s\) (all latents within radius \(r\)) is forward-diffused with noise variance \((1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\) at step \(t\) to form \(M_t^{(s)}\). This approximates the conditional distribution \(p(x_t | x_0 \in N_s)\) and thus the local geometry of the diffusion manifold. Core assumption: forward diffusion is a Gaussian kernel, so applying it to a small neighborhood yields a valid approximation. Evidence: abstract mentions local manifold creation; Section 3.3 formalizes Equation (5). Related work on local manifold approximations provides partial support. Break condition: at high noise levels early in diffusion, added noise may overwhelm neighborhood structure, making tangent estimation unreliable if \(r\) is small.

**Mechanism 3: Tangent-space projection**  
To prevent off-manifold drift while preserving semantic alignment, ManifoldGD projects the mode-guidance vector onto the local tangent space of \(M_t\). At step \(t\), it finds \(K\) nearest neighbors of current \(x_t\) in \(M_t^{(s)}\), computes covariance, and extracts top-\(d\) eigenvectors as tangent basis. The normal projector \(P_N = I - P_T\) is used to compute corrected guidance: \(g_{\text{manifold}} = g_{\text{mode}} - P_N g_{\text{mode}}\). This constrains generation to be manifold-faithful. Core assumption: data manifold is locally flat, tangent space is a good linear approximation. Evidence: abstract and Section 3.2 define tangent score and Equation (4). Ablation in Section 4.1 shows adding manifold guidance boosts accuracy (63.3% → 64.5% for ResNetAP-10). Related work on manifold-preserving diffusion corroborates. Break condition: if tangent basis is poorly estimated (few neighbors or high curvature), projection may remove valid variation or retain off-manifold components, harming quality.

## Foundational Learning
Key concepts underlying ManifoldGD include:

- **Diffusion models and score decomposition**: The method modifies the conditional score function of a diffusion model. Understanding the forward/reverse process, noise schedule, and how guidance terms are added is essential.  
  **Quick check**: In the decomposition \(\nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t|c) = \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t) + \nabla_{x_t} \log p(c|x_t)\), which term is mode guidance and which is marginal denoising?

- **Manifold hypothesis and tangent spaces**: The core correction relies on locally approximating the data manifold by its tangent space and separating updates into tangent and normal components.  
  **Quick check**: If a point \(x_t\) lies on the data manifold \(M_t\), what is the relationship between its normal space and the gradient of any function constant on \(M_t\)?

- **Hierarchical (divisive) clustering for prototype selection**: The IPC centroids are obtained via divisive hierarchical clustering; the level-wise selection strategy directly affects coverage of semantic modes.  
  **Quick check**: Why might divisive clustering produce more central centroids compared to agglomerative clustering?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
VAE encoder → Divisive clustering module → (centroids & static neighborhoods) → Diffusion denoising loop (includes local manifold estimator, tangent projector, integration) → VAE decoder → synthetic images.

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode all real training images with VAE → latent set \(Z\).  
2. For each class, run divisive clustering on \(Z\) to get centroids \(\{c_s\}\) and static neighborhoods \(\{N_s\}\).  
3. During distillation, assign each synthetic sample an IPC centroid \(c_s\) (and its \(N_s\)).  
4. For diffusion timestep \(t = T\) down to \(1\):  
   - Forward-diffuse \(N_s\) to \(M_t^{(s)}\) using noise variance \((1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\).  
   - Find \(K\) nearest neighbors of current \(x_t\) in \(M_t^{(s)}\) → \(N_t\).  
   - Compute covariance of \(N_t\); top-\(d\) eigenvectors give \(P_T\); normal projector \(P_N = I - P_T\).  
   - Compute \(g_{\text{mode}}(x_t, c_s)\) (e.g., RBF kernel).  
   - Compute \(g_{\text{manifold}} = g_{\text{mode}} - P_N g_{\text{mode}}\).  
   - Update: \(x_{t-1} = x_t + \eta_t (s_t(x_t, t) + \lambda g_{\text{manifold}}) + \sqrt{\beta_t} \epsilon\).  
5. Decode final \(x_0\) with VAE decoder.  
6. Repeat for all synthetic samples.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Neighborhood radius \(r\): small → precise local geometry but risk insufficient neighbors; large → mixes modes, ambiguous tangents. Tuned per dataset.  
- Tangent dimension \(d\): too small discards variation; too large includes noise; paper finds \(d=3\) optimal.  
- Sample budget \(K_t\): larger improves covariance stability but increases compute.  
- Start level \(s_{\text{start}}\): deeper for fewer/well-separated classes; shallower for many/overlapping classes.  
- Guidance strength \(\lambda\): tradeoff between semantic alignment and manifold constraint.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Visual artifacts (blur, distortion) if tangent projection omitted or \(M_t\) poorly estimated.  
- Mode collapse (low diversity) if mode guidance too strong relative to tangent preservation or \(r\) too small.  
- Numerical instability (NaNs) from singular covariance; mitigated by ridge regularization (\(\gamma\)).  
- Degraded accuracy when \(s_{\text{start}}\) mismatched or VAE fails to separate classes.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablation: evaluate with/without \(g_{\text{manifold}}\) to quantify impact (Table 3).  
2. Parameter sweep: vary \(s_{\text{start}}\), \(r\), \(d\) to find dataset-specific optima (Figs. 10, 7; supplementary).  
3. Class-wise accuracy on challenging fine-grained ImageWoof to see where manifold guidance helps most (Table 6 and discussion).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Theoretical guarantee**: What is the formal theoretical guarantee on the projection error and manifold reconstruction fidelity introduced by the tangent-space correction term \(g_t^{\text{manifold}}\), especially under varying curvature and noise conditions? The paper acknowledges this as future work.
2. **Adaptive hyperparameters**: How sensitive is ManifoldGD's performance to the choice of tangent subspace dimension \(d\) and neighbor count \(K_t\) in the local covariance estimate, and can these be adaptively determined rather than fixed heuristically?
3. **Optimal centroid selection**: Is the proposed hierarchical divisive IPC selection strategy (\(s_{\text{start}}\) sweep) universally optimal, or can a more rigorous, data-driven criterion for selecting the level of granularity in the centroid coreset be developed?
4. **Diversity vs. constraint**: Does enforcing strict tangent-space projection at every timestep inadvertently limit diversity by over-constraining valid manifold directions, especially for manifolds with high curvature or non-linearities? The paper uses annealed neighborhood radius to trade off consistency vs. diversity but does not quantify the fundamental limitation.
5. **Scalability**: Can ManifoldGD's training-free paradigm be extended to generate synthetic datasets at higher resolutions (e.g., 512×512) or for extremely low IPC (e.g., IPC=1 per class) without significant degradation in fidelity, and what are the scaling bottlenecks (e.g., nearest-neighbor search, covariance estimation)?

## Limitations
- The approach assumes a hierarchical latent structure; entangled clusters may cause centroid selection to miss key modes, reducing representativeness.
- Tangent estimation can be unstable at high noise levels or with insufficient neighbors, potentially leading to artifacts or numerical issues.
- Hyperparameters (neighborhood radius \(r\), tangent dimension \(d\), start depth \(s_{\text{start}}\)) require manual tuning per dataset; no automatic selection method is provided.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority** (outperforms baselines): High

- **Timestep-aligned manifold patch validity**: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate hierarchical centroid selection**: Replace divisive clustering with random or standard k-means; measure changes in diversity (MMD) and accuracy to isolate the benefit of hierarchical mode coverage.
2. **Analyze manifold patch quality across timesteps**: Compute condition numbers of neighbor covariance matrices at each diffusion step; correlate with final sample quality (e.g., FID) to identify failure regions, especially at high noise.
3. **Test cross-modality generalization**: Apply ManifoldGD to a text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) using text embedding centroids; evaluate whether manifold guidance improves few-shot generation fidelity on textual prompts.
