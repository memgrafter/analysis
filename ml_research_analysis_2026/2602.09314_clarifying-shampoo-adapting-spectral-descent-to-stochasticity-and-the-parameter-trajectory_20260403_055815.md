# Clarifying Shampoo: Adapting Spectral Descent to Stochasticity and the Parameter Trajectory

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09314
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09314
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Shampoo achieves higher token efficiency than Muon, with up to 35% fewer tokens for equivalent validation perplexity across Llama models.

## Executive Summary
This paper clarifies the relationship between the matrix optimizer Shampoo and the recently proposed Muon, which parallels the Adam/Signum relationship in vector settings. The authors analytically decompose Shampoo's update for weight matrices into a Muon-like core modulated by left and right adaptation matrices. Through controlled experiments on language and vision models, they demonstrate that Shampoo's token-efficiency advantage is exclusive to weight matrices and stems from its two-sided preconditioning, which enforces time-averaged semi-orthogonality in expectation rather than instantaneous spectral descent. The findings highlight the importance of matrix geometry and adaptation to stochastic gradient noise.

## Method Summary
The authors implement Shampoo and Muon within the PyTorch Distributed Shampoo codebase, isolating core algorithmic differences by using exact SVD/eigendecomposition and per-iteration preconditioner updates. They train Llama 3 models (320M, 1.5B) on the C4 dataset, comparing validation perplexity over fixed token budgets. Ablations systematically vary parameter shapes (2-D weights vs. embeddings/1-D parameters), preconditioning types (one- vs. two-sided), and optimizer variants (standard vs. KL-Shampoo). Hyperparameters (learning rate, β₁, β₂, ε) are tuned within reported ranges, with weight decay 0.1 and gradient clipping ℓ₂ norm 1.0. Vision experiments reshape ConvNeXt kernels to test the effect of matrix geometry on performance.

## Key Results
- Shampoo achieves higher token efficiency than Muon, requiring up to 35% fewer tokens for equivalent validation perplexity on Llama models.
- Shampoo's gains are exclusive to weight matrices; applying it to embeddings or 1-D parameters degrades performance.
- Two-sided preconditioning (using both left and right factor matrices) consistently outperforms one-sided variants; KL-Shampoo and Shampoo 1/2 are the best-performing variants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Decomposition into Muon + Adaptation Matrices
**Claim**: Shampoo’s update for weight matrices decomposes into a Muon update modulated by left and right adaptation matrices.  
**Mechanism**: Equation (10) factorizes the preconditioned gradient as  
\(L_t^{-p} M_t R_t^{-p} = \big( L_t^{-p} (M_t M_t^\top)^{1/4} \big) \cdot (U_t V_t^\top) \cdot \big( (M_t^\top M_t)^{1/4} R_t^{-p} \big)\).  
The middle factor is Muon (the matrix sign/polar factor of the gradient EMA), while the outer factors adapt its scale and orientation.  
**Evidence**: Abstract and Equation (10) explicitly state this; neighbor papers discuss similar decompositions.  
**Break condition**: Relies on two-sided preconditioning and full-rank gradient EMA; one-sided variants do not yield a Muon-like core.

### Mechanism 2: Time-Averaged Semi-Orthogonality in Expectation
**Claim**: Shampoo enforces time-averaged semi-orthogonality in expectation rather than instantaneous semi-orthogonality.  
**Mechanism**: Definition 4.6 requires EMAs of second-moment matrices to satisfy \(\operatorname{EMAT}_{t=1}(\mathbb{E}[G_t G_t^\top]) = I_m\) and similarly for columns. KL-Shampoo (Equation 7) approximates this coupled optimality condition (Corollary 4.7) via recursive updates using current gradients and previous factors, relaxing strict orthogonality to account for stochasticity and parameter changes.  
**Evidence**: Abstract mentions this; Definition 4.6 and Corollary 4.7 formalize it; Proposition 4.8 shows KL-Shampoo recovers spectral descent in the limit.  
**Break condition**: Poor β₂ choices or rapid gradient shifts cause EMA tracking failure; stale preconditioners degrade the approximation.

### Mechanism 3: Exclusivity to Weight Matrices
**Claim**: Shampoo’s token-efficiency advantage is exclusive to weight matrices; applying it to other parameter types harms performance.  
**Mechanism**: Reshaping non-matrix parameters (e.g., 1-D embeddings) into 2-D matrices degenerates Shampoo into full-matrix Adam, which underperforms. Conversely, reshaping higher-order tensors (4-D conv kernels) into 2-D matrices outperforms tensor-wise preconditioning. The benefit is tied to the natural 2-D structure of linear layer weights.  
**Evidence**: Abstract and results in Figure 2 (vision) and Table 5 (Llama ablations) show worse performance when Shampoo is applied outside weight matrices.  
**Break condition**: May not hold if embeddings or other parameters exhibit similar 2-D statistical structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: System groups parameters by shape: 2-D weight matrices → Shampoo; 1-D/other → AdamW. Each Shampoo-treated layer maintains individual state: gradient EMA Mt, factor matrices Lt/Rt (or KL-coupled versions), inverse-root caches, and grafting statistics from AdamW. The update pipeline distinctively handles these layers versus standard AdamW paths.

Critical path (per weight matrix layer, per iteration):
1. Compute gradient Gt from backward pass.
2. Update gradient EMA Mt = β1 Mt₋₁ + (1‑β1) Gt.
3. Update factor matrices (standard: Lt/Rt from GtGt⊺; KL-Shampoo: coupled using previous factors).
4. Compute stabilized inverse roots \((L_t + ϵI)^{-p}, (R_t + ϵI)^{-p}\) via eigendecomposition or SVD.
5. Precondition: \(u_t = (L_t + ϵI)^{-p} M_t (R_t + ϵI)^{-p}\).
6. Graft magnitude from AdamW (or other base optimizer) to set the overall step size.
7. Apply update to weights with weight decay and learning rate.
8. (Optional) Update grafted optimizer state (e.g., Adam’s vt).

Design tradeoffs:
- Preconditioner update frequency: Updating every iteration yields best performance but doubles compute/memory vs. stale updates; stale updates require eigenvalue corrections or grafting to compensate.
- Matrix accuracy: Exact SVD/eigendecomposition vs. approximate Newton-Schulz iteration; exact is stable but slower, approximate is faster but introduces error that may require tuning coefficients.
- ϵ damping: Critical for numerical stability; optimal values differ between p and between standard vs. KL-Shampoo. Too large washes out signal, too small risks NaNs.
- Grafting source: AdamW is typical; grafting ties β₂(Adam) to scaling, decoupling β₂(Shampoo) from step size but potentially affecting optimal hyperparameters.

Failure signatures:
- NaNs or exploding norms: non-PD factor matrices due to insufficient ϵ or large β₂.
- Discrepancy between Shampoo and Muon performance: may indicate incorrect reshaping (applying Shampoo to non-2-D params), stale preconditioner updates, or un-tuned ϵ.
- High validation variance across seeds: unstable hyperparameter (learning rate, β, ϵ) choices; consider smaller LR or adjusting β₁/β₂.
- No improvement over AdamW: could be insufficient token budget, or batch size too small for matrix methods.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline replication**: Train Llama‑320M with Shampoo₁/₂ and Muon (exact SVD) using per‑iteration preconditioner updates and identical hyperparameter sweeps. Expect Shampoo to match or beat Muon’s perplexity within the same token budget.
2. **Parameter‑shape ablation**: On a vision transformer, compare three Shampoo configurations: reshape all params to 1‑D (full‑matrix Adam), keep native shapes (2‑D for linear, 4‑D for conv), and force all to 2‑D. Validate that native‑shape 2‑D for linear layers performs best, while reshaping others hurts.
3. **KL-Shampoo vs. standard**: Run both with p=1/2 on Llama‑320M, batch 256; observe whether KL‑Shampoo consistently matches or exceeds standard Shampoo, illustrating the effect of coupled factor updates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the effects of adaptation to stochasticity and to the parameter trajectory be theoretically separated in adaptive optimizers? Basis: The authors write: "disentangling the effects of this theoretically and empirically remains under-explored, even for Adam." Why unresolved: Current analyses treat the two adaptations jointly through the same EMAs. Evidence needed: A mathematical decomposition of the preconditioners and ablative experiments that remove one adaptation while preserving the other.
2. Can the concept of time-averaged semi-orthogonality in expectation be extended to Lp norms and incorporated into a unified modular duality theory for matrix optimization? Basis: The authors suggest: "one could explore using Lp norms and time-averaging to build towards an adapted modular duality theory." Why unresolved: No formal link exists between time-averaged orthogonality and Lp geometries or modular duality. Evidence needed: A rigorous extension of Definition 4.6 to Lp spaces, derivation of associated update rules, and empirical validation.
3. Why does optimization in the S∞ geometry (e.g., SpectralGD, Shampoo) consistently outperform ℓ∞ (SignGD) and ℓ2 (GD) geometries in deep learning, and does this advantage extend beyond heavy-tailed class imbalance? Basis: The authors ask: "why does optimization in the S∞ geometry (via SpectralGD) outperform ℓ∞ geometry (via SignGD) and ℓ2 geometry (via GD) in deep learning ... its benefits seem to extend beyond this setting." Why unresolved: Recent work attributes the benefit to robustness to heavy-tailed gradients, but evidence suggests additional factors. Evidence needed: Systematic experiments controlling gradient distribution, model architecture, and task type.
4. Does Shampoo suffer from the same large-scale stability issues reported for Muon, and what mechanisms cause any instabilities in matrix-based optimizers? Basis: The authors inquire: "does Shampoo suffer from the same large-scale stability issues as Muon?" Why unresolved: Muon's stability challenges have been observed at scale, but Shampoo's behavior under comparable conditions has not been directly compared. Evidence needed: Train very large language models (e.g., tens of billions of parameters) with both optimizers, monitoring training dynamics and failure modes.
5. How do matrix optimizers such as Shampoo and Muon influence performance in downstream tasks like continual learning and quantization compared to element-wise methods? Basis: The authors note: "How do Shampoo and Muon affect downstream applications such as continual learning and quantization?" Why unresolved: Most evaluations focus on validation perplexity; little is known about representation quality or memory-limited scenarios. Evidence needed: Benchmark suites measuring catastrophic forgetting, sample efficiency, and quantization error for models trained with different optimizers.

## Limitations
- Reliance on exact SVD/eigendecomposition and per-iteration preconditioner updates makes Shampoo computationally heavier; approximate methods may lose benefits.
- Validation is limited to Llama language models and a few vision transformers; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., RNNs, GNNs) is unknown.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (ε, β₂, grafting source) is high; tuned values may not transfer across datasets or scales; the exclusivity to weight matrices might not hold for all parameter types.

## Confidence
- Decomposition into Muon + adaptation matrices: High (analytic derivation and numerical verification)
- Exclusivity to weight matrices and two-sided advantage: High (consistent ablation results)
- Token-efficiency gains (up to 35% fewer tokens): Medium (empirical evidence, depends on hyperparameters and token budget)
- Time-averaged semi-orthogonality mechanism: Medium (supported by theory and experiments, relies on EMA approximations)
