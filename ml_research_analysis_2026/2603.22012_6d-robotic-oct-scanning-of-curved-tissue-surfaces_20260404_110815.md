# 6D Robotic OCT Scanning of Curved Tissue Surfaces
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22012  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22012  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** A custom ChArUco fiducial marker enables full 6D hand-eye calibration for robot-mounted OCT probes, achieving sub-millimeter calibration repeatability and reducing sphere radius error from 8.26 mm to 0.35 mm on curved surfaces.

## Executive Summary
Robotic optical coherence tomography (OCT) scanning of curved tissue surfaces is traditionally limited by translation-only motions or registration-based stitching, which introduce geometric errors and signal artifacts. This paper presents a method using a custom ChArUco fiducial marker to perform a full 6D hand-eye calibration in a single OCT volume acquisition, eliminating the need for multi-volume stitching during calibration. The calibrated system allows the robot to adjust the probe orientation during scanning, maintaining near-optimal incidence angles on curved surfaces. Results show highly repeatable calibration (max standard deviation 0.4 mm / 0.24°) and dramatically improved accuracy on a spherical phantom (radius error 0.35 mm vs. 8.26 mm for conventional 3D-translation scanning), with surface artifacts removed. Feasibility is demonstrated on a chicken tissue phantom where 6D scanning provides consistent reconstruction where 3D-translation fails.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is a ChArUco pattern—a checkerboard with embedded ArUco markers—designed to fit entirely within the OCT probe's limited field of view from a single robot pose. During calibration, the robot is moved to at least 25 diverse poses; for each, simultaneous OCT volumes and RGB-D images are captured. The 3D coordinates of ArUco corners are extracted from each OCT volume by detecting their 2D projections in an en-face view and assigning depth via maximum intensity along the corresponding A-scan. A Perspective-n-Point (PnP) solver then computes the transform from the OCT frame to the robot end-effector (Hog). Separately, the camera-to-robot transform (Hcg) is computed using the checkerboard. Hand-eye calibration (solving AX=XB) yields both transforms without stitching multiple OCT volumes. For scanning, the calibrated Hog converts planned robot poses into OCT frame poses, enabling the probe orientation to follow the local surface normal and avoid artifacts from oblique incidence.

## Key Results
- Calibration repeatability across multiple runs: standard deviation ≤ 0.4 mm in translation and ≤ 0.24° in orientation.
- On a styrofoam sphere phantom, 6D scanning achieved a fitted sphere radius error of 0.35 mm, whereas conventional 3D-translation scanning produced an error of 8.26 mm.
- On a chicken tissue phantom, 3D-translation scanning failed to capture larger curvatures due to signal drop-off, while 6D scanning provided a complete, artifact-free surface reconstruction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Single-volume 6D hand-eye calibration.** The ChArUco pattern is engineered to be fully visible within the OCT's field of view from a single pose. This permits detection of multiple ArUco markers' 3D positions in one OCT volume, enabling a PnP solver to compute the OCT-to-robot transform (Hog) directly. This bypasses the error-prone step of stitching multiple OCT volumes to view the entire pattern, which was a major source of prior inaccuracies. *Core assumption:* the pattern's geometry is precisely known and fully captured. *Break condition:* extreme surface curvature prevents the entire pattern from fitting within the OCT's field of view and depth-of-field from any single pose, making calibration data acquisition impossible.

**Mechanism 2: Orientation adaptation maintains optimal incidence.** With an accurate Hog, the robot trajectory is planned so the OCT probe's optical axis remains approximately normal to the local surface topography. This maximizes back-reflected signal and ensures the imaged volume accurately represents the surface geometry. In contrast, fixed-orientation (3D-translation) scanning causes the incidence angle to vary dramatically with curvature, leading to signal drop-off ("shark-fin" artifacts) and misalignment between adjacent volumes. *Core assumption:* the local surface normal can be estimated (e.g., from a pre-scanned RGB-D mesh) and the robot can execute the orientation changes without collisions. *Break condition:* high-frequency curvature variations exceed the mechanical compliance and positioning accuracy of the robot arm and probe mount, making normal incidence unachievable.

**Mechanism 3: Error non-accumulation via direct pose transformation.** The calibration yields a consistent Hog estimate (low standard deviation). When scanning, each volume's pose in the OCT frame is computed by transforming the robot's recorded pose using this fixed Hog. Since stitching relies on these directly computed poses—not on iterative inter-volume image registration—errors from one volume do not propagate to the next. The final composite surface error is thus bounded by the per-volume calibration repeatability, not by accumulated registration drift. *Core assumption:* the dominant error source is the uncertainty in Hog, not robot kinematic drift or dynamic tissue interaction. *Break condition:* significant non-repeatable backlash, thermal drift, or external disturbances in the robot over long scan durations (e.g., >1 hour) invalidate the fixed Hog transform.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hand-Eye Calibration (AX=XB problem):** Solves for the rigid transform between a robot's end-effector and a rigidly mounted sensor from paired robot motions and measured target poses. *Why needed:* It is the mathematical foundation for determining Hog and Hcg. *Quick check:* If you move a robot to several poses and for each know the transform from a calibration board to the robot base (via kinematics) and the transform from the board to the sensor (via PnP), what equation do you solve to find the sensor-to-robot transform?
- **OCT Imaging Geometry & FOV Constraints:** OCT provides a thin slab field of view (e.g., 10×10×2.66 mm) with high axial resolution but limited lateral extent. *Why needed:* This anisotropic FOV makes orientation critical—tilting the slab changes its intersection with a curved surface. *Quick check:* Given an OCT depth resolution of 5.2 µm and a depth range of 2.66 mm, how many discrete depth slices can be resolved? Why must the probe orientation be adapted when scanning a curved surface?
- **Fiducial Marker Detection in 3D Volumes:** Extracting precise 3D coordinates of known marker corners from noisy OCT volumetric data requires segmentation and depth assignment. *Why needed:* The 6D calibration depends on accurate 3D positions of the ArUco corners within the OCT frame. *Quick check:* If an en-face maximum intensity projection (MIP) of an OCT volume clearly shows an ArUco corner at pixel (x,y), what processing step is essential to recover its depth (z) in the original 3D volume?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Robot Arm (ABB IRB 120) -> End-Effector Adapter (rigid 3D-printed mount) -> Sensor Suite (co-located Thorlabs OCT scan head and Intel RealSense D405 RGB-D camera) -> Calibration Phantom (3D-printed ChArUco pattern). Processing Pipeline: (a) Camera Calibration (intrinsics for RGB-D); (b) Data Acquisition (synchronized robot poses, OCT volumes, RGB-D images); (c) Pose Estimation (camera: checkerboard PnP; OCT: 3D ArUco corner extraction and PnP); (d) Hand-Eye Solver (compute Hcg and Hog); (e) Scanning (use Hog to transform planned robot poses into OCT frame poses for volume acquisition and stitching).

**Critical path:** 1) Acquire ≥25 paired calibration datasets with the ChArUco fully visible in both sensors. 2) Compute Hcg and Hog from pose pairs. 3) Validate calibration via reprojection error on unseen markers (target <0.5 mm). 4) If validation passes, plan scan trajectory using a pre-operative surface model (e.g., from RGB-D) and Hog. 5) Execute scan, directly transforming each robot pose to an OCT pose using Hog for stitching.

**Design tradeoffs:** Marker size vs. FOV—larger ArUco markers improve detection in noisy OCT but require a larger ChArUco pattern, which may exceed the workspace. Number of calibration poses—more poses improve robustness but increase acquisition time; the paper shows error plateaus after ~25 volumes. Calibration target material—must provide high contrast in OCT; printed black/white is low-cost but alternative materials (e.g., metal) could improve SNR.

**Failure signatures:** Calibration failure: high reprojection error (>1 mm), especially for distant markers, indicating poor marker detection or insufficient pose diversity. Scanning failure: large gaps or "shark-fin" artifacts in the stitched point cloud where surface curvature is high, indicating either large Hog error or inadequate trajectory planning. OCT marker detection failure: inability to segment ArUco corners in en-face projections due to low signal (e.g., from highly scattering tissue or incorrect depth windowing).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Calibration repeatability test:** Perform the full calibration pipeline (data acquisition, transform computation) 5 times on a fixed ChArUco phantom without moving it. Compute standard deviation of the resulting Hog parameters. Target: σ_translation < 0.5 mm, σ_orientation < 0.3° (paper achieved ≤0.4 mm / 0.24°).  
2. **Spherical phantom accuracy benchmark:** Scan a known-radius styrofoam sphere using both 6D and 3D-translation trajectories. Fit a sphere to the stitched point clouds and compute mean absolute radius error. Expect ≈0.13±0.12 mm for 6D (paper) and ≈8 mm for 3D-translation.  
3. **Chicken tissue feasibility scan:** Place a raw chicken breast (curved surface) in the workspace. Acquire a 6D scan planned via a pre-scanned RGB-D mesh and a naive 3D-translation raster scan (65 and 32 volumes respectively). Compare surface completeness and presence of artifacts.

## Limitations
- Calibration algorithm details (specific hand-eye solver, outlier rejection, required pose count) are not fully specified.
- The OCT 3D corner extraction method (e.g., whether sub-pixel refinement or smoothing is used) is vague, likely relying on maximum intensity along A-scans.
- Statistical evaluation is weak: repeatability based on few runs and performance tested on single phantoms, limiting generalizability.
- The method requires the entire ChArUco pattern to fit within the OCT's FOV from a single pose, which may fail for very large curvatures or small probe FOVs.

## Confidence
- 6D hand-eye calibration is feasible with a custom fiducial: Medium (internal demonstration, no independent validation).
- Calibration repeatability (≤0.4 mm / 0.24°): Medium (few trials reported).
- Scanning accuracy improvement (0.35 mm vs 8.26 mm on sphere): Medium-High (large effect size, single-phantom test).
- Feasibility on biological tissue (chicken): Medium (single sample, qualitative comparison).

## Next Checks
1. **Pose set optimization:** Systematically vary the number and distribution (translation range, rotation axes) of calibration poses. Evaluate resulting reprojection error and Hog variance to determine a minimal robust set and identify diversity thresholds.
2. **Depth extraction method comparison:** Implement alternative depth assignment strategies for OCT corners (e.g., centroid of intensity profile, Gaussian fitting) and test on a phantom with precisely known 3D marker depths. Quantify bias and variance to choose an optimal method.
3. **Temporal stability test:** Perform an extended 6D scan (e.g., 1 hour) on a static phantom. Re-calibrate at intervals (e.g., every 15 minutes) and monitor drift in Hog parameters to determine re-calibration frequency requirements for long procedures.
