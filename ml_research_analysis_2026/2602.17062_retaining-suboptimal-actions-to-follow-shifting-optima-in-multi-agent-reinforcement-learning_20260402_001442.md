# Retaining Suboptimal Actions to Follow Shifting Optima in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.17062
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17062
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: S2Q learns multiple sub-value functions to retain alternative high-value actions, enabling rapid adaptation when optima shift and achieving state-of-the-art performance on SMAC-Hard+, GRF, and SMAC-Comm.

## Executive Summary
Value decomposition frameworks in cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) typically assume a stationary optimal joint action, which fails when the environment's optimal strategy moves during training. The Successive Sub-value Q-learning (S2Q) algorithm introduces a novel approach to preserve alternative high-value actions by learning a sequence of sub-value functions, each specializing on a distinct suboptimal joint action. A latent coordination mechanism via an encoder-decoder samples which sub-value function to use, encouraging persistent exploration across promising modes. This design allows agents to swiftly follow shifting optima, yielding higher win rates and faster convergence on challenging benchmarks compared to existing MARL baselines, with only moderate computational overhead.

## Method Summary
S2Q operates within the centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm. During training, it learns K+1 sub-value functions Q_sub^k (k=0..K) and an unrestricted Q^*. The base sub-value Q_sub^0 matches the traditional total Q-function. Each subsequent Q_sub^k is trained to approximate the k-th suboptimal joint action by suppressing the value of actions identified by previous sub-networks using a loss term with suppression factor α. Concurrently, an encoder processes all agents' local histories to produce a latent representation z_t, which a decoder translates into a categorical distribution \hat{P}_t over the sub-value indices. Agents synchronously sample a shared index k from this distribution (except with probability 0.5 they fall back to k=0) and execute actions via ϵ-greedy on Q_sub^k. The training objective combines losses for successive sub-values, Q^*, and the latent reconstruction (cross-entropy plus state MSE). At test time, communication ceases and agents act greedily according to Q_sub^0. The method can be combined with various value decomposition architectures (e.g., VDN, QPLEX) and extended to communication settings (S2Q-Comm) by feeding z_t to individual Q networks.

## Key Results
- S2Q significantly outperforms state-of-the-art MARL baselines on SMAC-Hard+, Google Research Football (GRF), and SMAC-Comm in terms of average win rates and convergence speed.
- The algorithm adapts rapidly when the optimal joint action shifts during training, a scenario where traditional value decomposition methods collapse.
- S2Q's design is compatible with multiple CTDE frameworks (VDN, QPLEX) and yields consistent improvements with moderate additional computational cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
S2Q retains suboptimal actions by explicitly learning a sequence of Q-functions, each specializing on a different high-value mode of the action space. The suppression mechanism ensures that Q_sub^k focuses on actions not already captured by earlier sub-values, preserving a spectrum of viable strategies. The encoder-decoder coordination provides a simple yet effective way to synchronize agents on which sub-value function to employ during training, yielding diverse exploration that is both focused and adaptable. When the environment's optimum moves, the prelearned sub-value functions that represent alternative strategies can be immediately activated, avoiding the costly process of rediscovering a new optimum from scratch. This persistent multi-modal exploration combined with fast switching explains the observed robustness to shifting optima.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Value Decomposition**: Decomposes a joint Q-function into per-agent Q-values while satisfying the Individual-Global Maximization (IGM) principle. *Why needed*: Provides the basis for learning decentralized policies with centralized training. *Quick check*: Does the mixing network ensure that the argmax of the joint Q corresponds to the combination of individual argmaxes?
2. **CTDE (Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution)**: Agents use global state information during training but act on local observations at execution. *Why needed*: Enables learning cooperative strategies while respecting decentralized execution constraints. *Quick check*: Are agents' policies trained with global context but evaluated without it?
3. **Sub-value Functions**: Learned approximations of selected suboptimal joint actions. *Why needed*: Preserve alternative high-value strategies for rapid adaptation. *Quick check*: Do successive Q-sub networks show distinct peaks in their action-value distributions?
4. **Suppression Mechanism**: A loss term that penalizes previously selected actions to force new sub-values onto different modes. *Why needed*: Avoids collapse of all sub-values onto the same optimum. *Quick check*: Is the suppression constant α sufficiently large to push Q-values of earlier actions down?
5. **Encoder-Decoder Coordination**: Converts collective histories into a latent code that determines which sub-value to use. *Why needed*: Synchronizes agents' exploration without explicit communication. *Quick check*: Is the cross-entropy loss low enough to indicate consistent k selection among agents?
6. **Softmax-Based Policy Selection**: Samples k from the decoder's categorical distribution. *Why needed*: Promotes stochastic switching among sub-values, sustaining exploration. *Quick check*: Does the temperature parameter T yield a balanced distribution across indices?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Local histories τ_i -> Individual GRU Q_i^k networks -> Monotonic mixing networks (one per Q_sub^k and Q^*) -> Q_sub^k totals. All τ_t -> Encoder RNN -> latent z_t -> Decoder -> \hat{P}_t (categorical over K+1) and reconstructed state. For S2Q-Comm: z_t concatenated to each τ_i as extra input.
- **Critical Path**: During training, trajectories are sampled from the replay buffer. The encoder processes all τ_t to produce z_t and \hat{P}_t. A shared k is sampled from \hat{P}_t (with fixed k=0 with probability 0.5). Each agent computes its Q_i^k and the mixing network yields Q_sub^k(s,·). Actions are chosen ϵ-greedily from Q_sub^k. Losses (L_successive, L_Q*, L_latent) are computed and gradients applied. Periodically target Q^* network is updated.
- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - *Number of sub-networks K*: Larger K preserves more modes but increases variance and computational cost.
  - *Suppression factor α*: Too small fails to separate sub-values; too large may force sub-values onto low-reward actions.
  - *Fixed k=0 proportion*: Higher proportion ensures baseline performance but may reduce exploration of sub-optimal modes.
  - *Decoder accuracy*: Higher capacity improves coordination but adds complexity; low accuracy causes desynchronization.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Diverging Q-values or vanishing rewards (indicative of unstable α or K).
  - High variance in selected k among agents within an episode (decoder mis-coordination).
  - Sudden drop in win rate after a certain number of steps (possible collapse to a single sub-value).
- **First Experiments**:
  1. Train S2Q on a simple SMAC scenario with known shifting optimum (e.g., 3m) and compare to a standard VDN baseline to verify adaptation.
  2. Perform a coarse hyperparameter sweep over K∈{1,2,3} and α∈{0.5,1.0,2.0} to identify stable settings.
  3. Ablate the fixed k=0 schedule (p∈{0,0.25,0.5,0.75}) to measure its impact on exploration and final performance.

## Limitations
- The appropriate value of the suppression constant α is task-dependent and lacks concrete tuning guidelines.
- The accuracy requirements for the encoder-decoder's \hat{P}_t are not quantified, risking performance degradation if coordination fails.
- The claimed moderate computational overhead is not substantiated with explicit memory or runtime measurements.

## Confidence
- **Empirical superiority on benchmarks (SMAC-Hard+, GRF, SMAC-Comm)**: High
- **Theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) under ideal α**: Medium
- **Generalization to unseen CTDE frameworks**: Low
