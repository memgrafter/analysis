# General Flexible $f$-divergence for Challenging Offline RL Datasets with Low Stochasticity and Diverse Behavior Policies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11087
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11087
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A flexible $f$-divergence with adaptive parameters α-, α+, β, integrated into IQL (Flex-f-Q) and OptiDICE (Flex-f-DICE), yields significant gains on low-diversity datasets (e.g., 99.0 vs. 77.9 on Hopper 4-p).

## Executive Summary
Offline reinforcement learning (RL) on datasets with low stochasticity and mixed expertise requires carefully balancing reward maximization with staying within the support of the behavior data. This paper addresses this challenge by first deriving a general linear programming (LP) formulation that connects $f$-divergence regularization to the minimization of Bellman error. Based on this, the authors propose a flexible $f$-divergence family with adaptive parameters (α-, α+, β) that can adjust the strength of the constraint during learning. The method is agnostic to the base algorithm and is instantiated by incorporating it into IQL (yielding Flex-f-Q) and OptiDICE (yielding Flex-f-DICE). Extensive experiments on MuJoCo, Fetch, and AdroitHand benchmarks show substantial improvements, particularly on challenging datasets with limited diversity, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed approach.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a unified LP perspective on $f$-divergence-regularized offline RL, showing that constraining the $f$-divergence between the learned policy and the behavior policy is equivalent to limiting the Bellman error in the LP objective. This insight leads to a flexible divergence measure where the $f$-function parameters (α-, α+, β) are adapted based on estimated state-action distribution statistics. The adaptation allows the algorithm to tighten or loosen the divergence constraint as needed, which is crucial for datasets with low coverage or heterogeneous behavior policies. Implementation-wise, the flexible constraint is integrated into existing offline RL algorithms by modifying their policy update or value learning steps. Specifically, the authors apply it to IQL, which uses expectile regression, and to OptiDICE, which solves a distribution matching problem. In both cases, the flexible divergence replaces a fixed divergence (e.g., KL) and leads to more robust performance on difficult datasets.

## Key Results
- Flex-f-DICE achieves a normalized return of 99.0 on Hopper 4-p, surpassing OptiDICE's 77.9; on Walker 4-p, it achieves 94.2 vs. 75.7.
- Flex-f-Q (integration into IQL) also shows significant gains over the baseline IQL on the same challenging datasets.
- Improvements are consistent across MuJoCo locomotion, Fetch robotics, and AdroitHand manipulation tasks, indicating broad applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The flexible $f$-divergence enables the algorithm to dynamically adjust the regularization strength based on the estimated data support. In low-diversity datasets, a tighter constraint prevents the policy from deviating into unsampled regions, while in mixed-expertise settings, a looser constraint allows the policy to surpass the average behavior in high-reward areas. By linking the divergence constraint to Bellman error minimization within an LP framework, the method maintains a theoretically sound trade-off between exploitation and conservatism. Moreover, the adaptive parameters (α-, α+, β) are derived from the empirical distribution, making the approach data-driven and thus better suited for heterogeneous datasets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning**: Learning a policy exclusively from a static dataset without environment interaction. Why needed: Enables RL in settings where online exploration is expensive or dangerous. Quick check: Does the method assume access to a behavior policy? Yes, it uses the dataset as a behavior constraint.
- **$f$-divergence**: A family of divergences (e.g., KL, total variation) that measure the discrepancy between two distributions. Why needed: Used to regularize the learned policy and keep it close to the data distribution. Quick check: Standard choices (KL) can be too restrictive or too permissive; a flexible $f$-divergence generalizes them.
- **Linear Programming Formulation**: RL can be posed as an LP over state-action visitation distributions, where the optimal policy satisfies Bellman constraints. Why needed: Provides a convex optimization perspective and facilitates incorporation of divergence constraints. Quick check: The Bellman equation appears as linear constraints in the LP.
- **Bellman Error**: The inconsistency in value estimates across time steps. Why needed: Minimizing Bellman error drives the policy towards optimality. Quick check: In the LP, the objective often involves minimizing a norm of the Bellman error.
- **Adaptive Regularization**: Adjusting regularization parameters based on data statistics. Why needed: Handles varying data coverage and policy quality, avoiding over- or under-constraining. Quick check: The method sets α-, α+, β from empirical distribution quantiles.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: The system comprises (1) a behavior dataset $\mathcal{D}$, (2) an empirical state-action distribution estimator, (3) a flexible $f$-divergence module that computes adaptive parameters (α-, α+, β), (4) an LP solver (or integration into a base algorithm like IQL or OptiDICE) that solves the constrained Bellman error minimization, and (5) a policy extractor. The data flow is: $\mathcal{D}$ → distribution estimate → parameter adaptation → constrained optimization → policy. When instantiated in IQL or OptiDICE, the flexible divergence replaces the fixed divergence in their respective objectives.

**Critical Path**: The core steps are:
1. Compute the empirical state-action distribution from $\mathcal{D}$.
2. Determine adaptive thresholds $\alpha^-, \alpha^+, \beta$ based on the distribution (e.g., using quantiles or support estimation).
3. Formulate the constrained optimization problem (LP or variant) that minimizes a Bellman error measure while constraining the $f$-divergence between the policy and the empirical distribution.
4. Solve the optimization to obtain a policy (or value functions), optionally iterating with fitted value iteration.
5. Deploy the learned policy.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The flexible parameters offer adaptability but introduce tuning complexity. A more expressive $f$-divergence family can better match dataset characteristics but may increase computational cost for parameter estimation and optimization. The LP approach ensures optimality under the constraint but may not scale well to high-dimensional continuous actions; integrating with IQL (which uses expectile regression) provides a scalable approximation. Balancing constraint tightness is critical: too tight yields overly conservative policies, too loose risks unsampled state-action pairs and unstable training.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- **Divergence estimate instability**: On sparse datasets, the empirical distribution may have high variance, causing erratic updates to α-, α+, β and leading to performance collapse.
- **Bound saturation**: If α- or α+ are set too extreme (e.g., near 0 or 1), the divergence constraint becomes meaningless or overly restrictive, causing either no improvement or no policy updates.
- **Optimization divergence**: The LP or its approximation may become infeasible if the adaptive parameters conflict with the empirical support, resulting in solver failures or non-convergence.

**First Experiments**:
1. Compare Flex-f-Q against vanilla IQL on low-diversity MuJoCo tasks (e.g., Hopper 4-p, Walker 4-p) to quantify improvement.
2. Ablate the adaptive mechanism by fixing the f-divergence to standard KL or χ² and compare with the flexible version to isolate the contribution of adaptability.
3. Evaluate robustness by training on datasets constructed from mixtures of policies with varying qualities (e.g., 50% medium + 50% random) and measuring normalized returns.

## Limitations
- Benchmarks limited to MuJoCo, Fetch, and AdroitHand; image-based and real-world tasks remain untested.
- Adaptive parameters (α-, α+, β) require tuning; the paper lacks a systematic sensitivity analysis.
- No code release, which hinders independent verification and reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **LP formulation linking $f$-divergence to Bellman error**: High.
- **Flexible $f$-divergence parameterization**: Medium.
- **Empirical gains on challenging datasets**: High.
