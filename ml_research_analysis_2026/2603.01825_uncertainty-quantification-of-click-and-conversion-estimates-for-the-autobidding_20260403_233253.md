# Uncertainty Quantification of Click and Conversion Estimates for the Autobidding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01825
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01825
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DenoiseBid achieves near-optimal conversion ratios (R/R* ≈ 1) under high noise while maintaining CPC compliance (CPC/CPC_camp ≤ 0.2), and on Criteo Attribution it yields statistically significant reductions in CPC deviation and conversion uplifts, especially under data scarcity or feature removal.

## Executive Summary
Accurate click-through rate (CTR) and conversion rate (CVR) estimates are critical for autobidding in second-price auctions, yet real-world predictors are noisy and this uncertainty degrades performance. DenoiseBid proposes a Bayesian framework that replaces deterministic predictions with posterior expectations derived from a recovered distribution of true logits. The prior is a Gaussian mixture learned from historical noisy observations via Extreme Deconvolution, accounting for heteroscedastic noise. The resulting stochastic optimization problem admits a closed-form bidding rule. Evaluated on synthetic data, iPinyYou, BAT (with injected noise), and Criteo Attribution (with empirical model uncertainty), DenoiseBid consistently achieves conversion ratios close to the offline optimum (R/R* ≈ 1) and respects the cost-per-click constraint, outperforming baselines that ignore or only partially address noise. The approach proves particularly robust under high noise, limited data, or aggressive feature removal.

## Method Summary
DenoiseBid models the true logit-CTR and logit-CVR as latent variables with a prior distribution assumed to be a Gaussian mixture. Observations are noisy predictions, modeled as additive Gaussian noise with known per-auction variances (e.g., from model ensembles). The prior parameters (mixing weights, means, variances) are recovered from a historical buffer using Extreme Deconvolution (XDGMM), which fits a GMM directly to the marginal distribution of noisy logits while incorporating their heteroscedastic noise levels. For each new auction, the algorithm computes the posterior distribution over true logits, which remains a Gaussian mixture. The denoised expectations E[CTR|O] and, for the joint CTR-CVR case, E[CTR·CVR|O] are then approximated: univariate case uses a probit approximation; bivariate case employs Gauss-Hermite quadrature (M=5) on each mixture component. These expectations replace point estimates in the linear bid formula derived from the dual of the stochastic linear program that maximizes conversions under a budget and an average CPC constraint. The bid is computed in closed form (Eq. 6) and applied to the second-price auction. The method is validated in controlled synthetic settings with known ground truth, on iPinyYou and BAT with synthetic noise sweeps, and on Criteo Attribution using CatBoost Virtual Ensembles and feature removal to simulate model degradation.

## Key Results
- Under synthetic Gaussian noise with standard deviation up to 10, DenoiseBid maintains conversion ratio R/R* ≈ 1 and CPC/CPC_camp ≤ 0.2, while NonRobustBid and RobustBid deteriorate substantially.
- On Criteo Attribution with model uncertainty induced by feature removal, DenoiseBid produces statistically significant (p < 0.05) reductions in CPC deviation and increases in conversions, particularly when data is sparse or many features are removed.
- A two‑component Gaussian mixture prior consistently outperforms a single-component prior in high‑noise and multimodal scenarios, confirming the benefit of capturing multimodality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Bayesian Posterior Expectations Replace Noisy Point Estimates
The deterministic autobidding LP bids linearly in CTR and CVR. Noise in these inputs directly translates into suboptimal performance. DenoiseBid reformulates the problem to maximize the *expected* number of conversions conditioned on the noisy observations. This stochastic program leads to a bid rule that uses the posterior expectations E[CTR|O] and E[CTR·CVR|O] instead of raw predictions. The derivation linearizes the CPC constraint via a first-order Taylor expansion, preserving linearity and enabling a closed-form solution. Evidence: Section 4 derives the stochastic program (3) → (5) → (6) and shows how posterior expectations replace point estimates. Break condition: If auction dynamics introduce strong non-linearities that invalidate the linear approximation, the bid rule may become inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2: Extreme Deconvolution Recovers the True Logit Prior from Noisy Data
True logits are unobservable; only noisy predictions with heteroscedastic variance are available. DenoiseBid assumes a Gaussian mixture prior in logit space and additive Gaussian noise. It recovers the prior from historical data by fitting a GMM to the marginal distribution of observed noisy logits using Extreme Deconvolution (XDGMM). XDGMM maximizes the marginal likelihood (Eq. 19) and explicitly incorporates each sample’s noise variance, thus disentangling the prior from the observation noise. Evidence: Section 4.1 presents the XDGMM formulation for both univariate and bivariate cases. Break condition: If the true prior cannot be well-approximated by a low-component GMM, or if the noise deviates from Gaussian (e.g., heavy‑tailed), the recovered prior will be biased, leading to inaccurate posteriors.

### Mechanism 3: Tractably Computing Joint Denoised Values via Gauss‑Hermite Quadrature
When CTR and CVR are correlated, the denoised value requires the expectation E[σ(ξ)σ(ζ)|O], where the posterior is a bivariate Gaussian mixture. No closed form exists. DenoiseBid employs Gauss‑Hermite quadrature to approximate the integral over each Gaussian component. By applying a Cholesky decomposition of the posterior covariance, standard quadrature nodes and weights are transformed to evaluate the product of sigmoids efficiently with a small grid (M=5). Evidence: Section 4.2 details the quadrature (Eqs. 54‑57) and argues that sigmoid smoothness justifies few nodes. Break condition: If the posterior covariance is near‑singular or the posterior distribution is highly non‑Gaussian, the quadrature may incur significant error, especially for tail auctions.

## Foundational Learning

**Bayesian Linearization of Stochastic Optimization**  
*Why needed:* The core innovation is recasting a deterministic LP with noisy parameters into a stochastic program whose solution retains a closed-form bid. This requires moving expectations inside constraints and justifying approximations (e.g., Taylor expansion for the CPC ratio).  
*Quick check:* Why is the first‑order Taylor approximation for the CPC constraint justified, and what property of the posterior expectations makes the resulting problem still an LP?

**Deconvolution with Heteroscedastic Noise (XDGMM)**  
*Why needed:* The prior must be estimated from noisy historical observations where each has a different variance. Standard GMM EM cannot handle heteroscedastic noise directly; XDGMM maximizes the marginal likelihood while incorporating per‑sample noise variances.  
*Quick check:* How does the XDGMM likelihood (Eq. 19) differ from standard GMM EM, and why does this difference matter for recovering the prior of true logits?

**Gauss‑Hermite Quadrature for Correlated Integrals**  
*Why needed:* The joint CTR·CVR expectation involves a product of sigmoids over a correlated bivariate Gaussian posterior. No closed form exists; Gauss‑Hermite quadrature provides an efficient numerical integration scheme.  
*Quick check:* Given a bivariate normal N(η|µ, Σ) and a function f(ξ,ζ)=σ(ξ)σ(ζ), how can one generate quadrature nodes η(i,j) to compute ∫ f(η) N(η|µ, Σ) dη efficiently using the Cholesky decomposition of Σ?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Historical Data Buffer → Prior Estimator (XDGMM) → Online Posterior Calculator → Expectation Approximator → Bid Solver. The Model Uncertainty Provider (an external ML model) supplies noisy predictions and their logit variances to the Online Posterior Calculator.

**Critical path**  
Offline: Prior estimation via XDGMM on historical data. Online (per auction): (1) Posterior update using prior and current observation; (2) Expectation computation (probit for univariate, Gauss‑Hermite quadrature for joint); (3) Bid output by solving the LP dual. The expectation step, especially quadrature for joint CTR‑CVR, dominates per‑auction computational cost.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Prior complexity:* One component (logit‑normal) is simpler and more robust with limited data; two components capture multimodality but risk overfitting and slower XDGMM.  
- *Quadrature grid size (M):* M=5 balances precision and cost for bivariate integration; larger grids increase compute with diminishing returns.  
- *Noise model:* Assuming Gaussian noise in logit space yields tractable posteriors, but may mis‑specify heavy‑tailed errors; using empirical variances from ensembles is practical but requires well‑calibrated uncertainties.

**Failure signatures**  
1. Prior mismatch: Data distribution shift (e.g., new ad format) makes the XDGMM prior stale; monitor log‑likelihood of new predictions under the prior—a sudden drop signals shift.  
2. Numerical instability: Posterior covariance near‑singular causes Cholesky failures; regularize with ε on the diagonal. Extreme posterior means may produce sigmoid inputs that yield probabilities outside [0,1]; clamp if necessary.  
3. Constraint violation: Consistently exceeding the CPC threshold indicates biased posterior expectations; compare E[CTR|O] vs. predicted CTR to detect systematic under‑estimation.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. *Synthetic Data Sanity Check:* Generate auctions with known true logits (GMM priors) and inject Gaussian noise (σ=0.5). Verify DenoiseBid achieves R/R* ≈ 1, matching the offline optimum.  
2. *iPinyYou with Synthetic Noise:* Run the pipeline on iPinyYou logs and sweep σ from 0.01 to 10. Confirm DenoiseBid maintains CPC/CPC_camp ≤ 0.2 and its R/R* decays slower than NonRobustBid and RobustBid.  
3. *Criteo Attribution Empirical Noise:* Train CatBoost with Virtual Ensembles on Criteo Attribution, obtain predictions and logit variances, then simulate noise via feature removal (e.g., remove bottom 8 features). Expect DenoiseBid to yield statistically significant conversion uplift and CPC reduction vs. baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1: Extension to First‑Price Auctions**  
*Basis:* The introduction and conclusion explicitly state that extending DenoiseBid to first‑price auctions is future work.  
*Why unresolved:* The current bid derivation relies on the linear structure of second‑price auctions; first‑price auctions involve a different optimization problem that may not admit a closed‑form Bayesian rule.  
*Resolving evidence:* Develop a Bayesian formulation for first‑price auctions, derive an implementable bidding policy (possibly via approximation), and validate it on simulated and real auction data.

**Open Question 2: Adaptation to Deep Learning Predictors**  
*Basis:* Section 6 acknowledges experiments only with GBDT models and expresses intent to adapt state‑of‑the‑art deep models.  
*Why unresolved:* Deep models produce high‑dimensional, non‑Gaussian posteriors; the XDGMM prior recovery may need fundamental modifications.  
*Resolving evidence:* Integrate uncertainty quantification from deep models (e.g., ensembles, Bayesian neural networks) into the DenoiseBid pipeline and demonstrate comparable or improved performance on benchmarks like Criteo Attribution.

**Open Question 3: Impact of the Number of Mixture Components**  
*Basis:* The method uses a Gaussian mixture prior but experiments only with one or two components; the effect of prior complexity is not studied.  
*Why unresolved:* Too few components may fail to capture multimodality; too many may overfit and increase computational cost.  
*Resolving evidence:* Conduct sensitivity analysis varying the number of mixture components on datasets with varying degrees of multimodality, measuring conversion ratio (R/R*), CPC compliance, and computational efficiency.

**Open Question 4: Robustness to Noise Model Misspecification**  
*Basis:* The framework assumes additive Gaussian noise in logit space; real‑world prediction errors may deviate.  
*Why unresolved:* Non‑Gaussian noise could bias posterior expectations, leading to suboptimal bids and constraint violations.  
*Resolving evidence:* Simulate non‑Gaussian noise models (Laplace, Student‑t) and compare DenoiseBid’s performance against an oracle that knows the true noise distribution, assessing sensitivity to noise shape and scale.

## Limitations
- Performance claims rely heavily on synthetic noise and a single empirical dataset (Criteo Attribution); generalization to production environments with natural model noise remains uncertain.
- The RobustBid baseline is referenced but not implemented or detailed in the paper; comparisons may be against a suboptimal or incorrectly specified version, potentially overstating DenoiseBid’s relative gains.
- The Gaussian mixture prior assumption may be too restrictive for complex, heavy‑tailed, or high‑modality logit distributions; prior misspecification could silently degrade denoising accuracy.
