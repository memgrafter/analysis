# Towards Latent Diffusion Suitable For Text
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.16220
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16220
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** NFDM achieves an ELBO of 3.12 bits‑per‑character (bpc) on ROCstories, nearly matching the autoregressive GPT‑J likelihood (3.05 bpc) and significantly outperforming Diffusion‑LM (5.94 bpc) and MuLAN (5.78 bpc), while maintaining comparable sample quality (PPL 26.44, MAUVE 0.43).

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models for text offer faster sampling than autoregressive language models but suffer from fixed noise schedules and a large likelihood gap. This work introduces Neural Flow Diffusion Models (NFDM) for text, extending the NFDM framework to learn a multivariate, data‑conditioned forward process in a continuous latent embedding space. The forward process automatically adapts per‑token noise schedules, and the reverse process is trained jointly via the NFDM diffusion loss, enabling generation through SDE‑based reverse sampling and nearest‑neighbor decoding.

On the ROCstories dataset, NFDM achieves an ELBO of 3.12 bpc, reducing the autoregressive likelihood gap to near equivalence (GPT‑J: 3.05) and far surpassing Diffusion‑LM (5.94) and MuLAN (5.78). Sample quality metrics—perplexity 26.44 and MAUVE 0.43—match prior diffusion baselines, demonstrating that NFDM can bridge the gap between likelihood and generation quality for text diffusion.

## Method Summary
NFDM operates on continuous latent embeddings of tokens. It learns a forward diffusion process conditioned on the input sequence and timestep:  
`z_t = µ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x), t) + σ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x), t)·ε`,  
where `µ_ϕ` and `σ_ϕ` are outputs of a small BERT with Adaptive LayerNorm that takes token embeddings and time as inputs, respecting boundary conditions `µ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x),0)=E_ϕ(x)`, `σ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x),0)=δ`, `µ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x),1)=0`, `σ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x),1)=1`. The reverse process is parameterized by a predictor `ˆE_θ(z_t, t)` (BERT‑base with Fourier time embeddings) that estimates the original token embeddings. The forward and reverse models are trained jointly using the NFDM diffusion loss, which aligns the reverse SDE drift with the conditional ODE of the forward process, optionally with auxiliary reconstruction and prior terms. At generation, start from `z_1 ~ N(0,I)` and apply iterative reverse steps to obtain `z_0`, which is decoded by assigning each latent vector to its nearest vocabulary embedding. Training uses Adam (for non‑transformer parameters) and Muon (for transformer weights), with gradient clipping and careful learning‑rate scheduling to maintain stability. Sampling can be fast DDIM‑style deterministic or stochastic generative SDE, controlled by `σ_s|t`.

## Key Results
- ELBO of 3.12 bits‑per‑character on ROCstories, nearly matching GPT‑J (3.05) and far better than Diffusion‑LM (5.94) and MuLAN (5.78).
- Sample quality comparable to baselines: perplexity 26.44 and MAUVE 0.43.
- Demonstrates that a learnable, data‑conditioned forward process can close the likelihood gap for text diffusion without sacrificing generation quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Learning a multivariate, input‑conditioned forward noise schedule reduces the likelihood gap between diffusion models and autoregressive models while adapting per‑token noising to linguistic structure.
- **Mechanism:** The forward process is parameterized as `F_ϕ(ε, t, E_ϕ(x)) = µ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x), t) + σ_ϕ(E_ϕ(x), t)ε`, where `µ_ϕ` and `σ_ϕ` (small BERT with Adaptive LayerNorm) condition on token embeddings and time. This yields a learned Gaussian `q_ϕ(z_t|x)`. The reverse predictor `ˆE_θ(z_t, t)` is trained via the NFDM diffusion loss (Eq. 8), which aligns the reverse SDE drift with the conditional ODE of the forward process.
- **Core assumption:** The data distribution can be effectively captured by a stochastic process whose noise schedule adapts to each input sequence, and that this process permits tractable ELBO optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “extending NFDM to learn a multivariate, data‑conditioned forward process … adapting per‑token noise schedules automatically.” Section 3.1: “NFDM learns a multivariate forward process from the data, ensuring that the forward process and generative trajectory are a good fit for language modeling.” The neighbor paper introducing NFDM (arXiv:2404.12940) establishes the theoretical framework.
- **Break condition:** If model capacity is insufficient or training unstable (e.g., rescaled loss without proper SNR constraints), the forward process may collapse or fail to learn token‑specific schedules, leading to high ELBO (MuLAN without rescaling yields 5.78 bpc vs NFDM 3.12).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Operating in a continuous latent embedding space bridges continuous diffusion models and discrete text, enabling SDE‑based generation while preserving discrete output via nearest‑neighbor decoding.
- **Mechanism:** Tokens are mapped to continuous embeddings `E_ϕ(x)`. The forward diffusion perturbs these embeddings with learned Gaussian noise. At generation, the reverse process produces `z_0`, which is decoded by assigning each latent vector to the nearest vocabulary embedding (dot‑product similarity). Diffusion thus occurs in a continuous space without discrete sampling.
- **Core assumption:** The embedding space is smooth and structured so that continuous diffusion trajectories converge to points that decode to valid tokens, and that nearest‑neighbor decoding introduces acceptable distortion.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “enables the straightforward application of continuous diffusion models to discrete state spaces.” Section 3.1: “We parameterize a continuous forward process by using an embedding matrix `E_ϕ(x)`… Once `z_0` is obtained, the decoder `p_ϕ(x|z_0;E_ϕ)` maps the embeddings back to discrete tokens by assigning each latent vector to its closest vocabulary embedding.” Neighbors such as Diffusion‑LM and TEncDM adopt similar latent diffusion.
- **Break condition:** If the learned embeddings drift away from the vocabulary space or the reverse process fails to land near valid token embeddings, decoding quality degrades, leading to high perplexity and low MAUVE.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Jointly training the forward process parameters `ϕ` and reverse predictor `θ` with the NFDM diffusion loss yields a tighter ELBO and improves likelihood compared to fixed forward processes.
- **Mechanism:** The diffusion loss `L_diff = E[ (1/(2g_ϕ(t)²)) || ˜fB_ϕ(z_t,t,x) - ˆfB_θ,ϕ(z_t,t) ||² ]` matches the reverse SDE drift to the conditional ODE derived from the forward process. This end‑to‑end training ensures consistency between learned forward trajectories and the reverse generative path.
- **Core assumption:** The parameterizations of `F_ϕ` and `ˆE_θ` are expressive enough to capture the data distribution, and the loss provides a valid variational bound optimizable via stochastic gradient descent.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1: “We can learn the parameters of this predictor `θ`, jointly with the parameters of the forward process using the NFDM diffusion loss in Equation 8.” Table 1 shows NFDM ELBO 3.12 vs Diffusion‑LM 5.94, demonstrating improvement from joint training. The original NFDM paper derives this joint training framework and confirms its benefit.
- **Break condition:** If the forward process becomes too flexible relative to the reverse predictor capacity, training may destabilize. The paper uses gradient clipping and a mix of Adam/Muon to mitigate; without such measures the loss may diverge (Appendix B.4 notes collapse risks with rescaled loss).

## Foundational Learning
### Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) in diffusion models
- **Why needed here:** NFDM formulates forward and backward processes as SDEs; understanding drift, diffusion coefficient, and score function is essential to grasp the training loss and sampling algorithms.
- **Quick check question:** How does the reverse SDE differ from the forward SDE, and what role does the learned score function play?

### Continuous latent space diffusion for discrete data
- **Why needed here:** The core innovation is applying continuous diffusion to text via embeddings; one must understand how discrete tokens are represented and decoded.
- **Quick check question:** Why is nearest‑neighbor decoding used, and what potential issues does it introduce?

### Transformer‑based neural networks for time‑conditioned predictions
- **Why needed here:** Both the forward process network (`F_ϕ`) and reverse predictor (`ˆE_θ`) are transformer‑based; time conditioning is implemented via Fourier embeddings or Adaptive LayerNorm.
- **Quick check question:** How is the timestep `t` incorporated into the transformer architecture, and why is Adaptive LayerNorm used for the forward process?

### Variational inference and ELBO for diffusion models
- **Why needed here:** The training objective is a variational bound on the log‑likelihood; the ELBO derivation for continuous diffusion ties into the loss used.
- **Quick check question:** What role does the KL divergence between the forward and reverse processes play in the ELBO?

### Nearest‑neighbor decoding and embedding alignment
- **Why needed here:** Decoding from continuous latent space requires mapping back to discrete tokens; understanding the alignment between latent vectors and embedding matrix is crucial.
- **Quick check question:** What are the trade‑offs of nearest‑neighbor decoding versus a learned soft assignment?

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
- **Embedding matrix `E_ϕ`**: Learns continuous token representations.
- **Forward process network (`F_ϕ`)**: Small BERT with Adaptive LayerNorm; outputs `µ_ϕ, σ_ϕ` to perturb embeddings.
- **Reverse predictor (`ˆE_θ`)**: BERT‑base with Fourier timestep embeddings; predicts token embeddings from `z_t`.
- **Decoder**: Nearest‑neighbor search between predicted `z_0` embeddings and vocabulary matrix.
- **Loss calculator**: Computes NFDM diffusion loss (Eq. 8) plus optional reconstruction/prior terms.

The data flow: Tokens → `E_ϕ(x)` → sample `t` and `ε` → `z_t = F_ϕ(ε, t, E_ϕ(x))` → `ˆE_θ(z_t, t)` → loss → backprop; generation: `z_1 ~ N(0,I)` → iterative reverse steps → `z_0` → nearest‑neighbor decoding → tokens.

#### Critical Path (Training)
1. Input tokens are embedded via `E_ϕ` to obtain continuous representation `h = E_ϕ(x)`.
2. Sample a timestep `t` uniformly and noise `ε ~ N(0,I)`.
3. Compute `z_t = µ_ϕ(h, t) + σ_ϕ(h, t)·ε`.
4. Predict original embeddings `ˆh = ˆE_θ(z_t, t)`.
5. Calculate diffusion loss `L_diff` (Eq. 8) which enforces consistency between reverse SDE drift and forward process.
6. Optionally add reconstruction and prior regularization terms.
7. Update `θ` (BERT‑base parameters) with Muon optimizer and `ϕ` (other parameters) with Adam; apply gradient clipping and learning‑rate schedule.
8. Repeat for many iterations until convergence.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Fixed vs learnable forward:** Fixed schedule (Diffusion‑LM) is simpler and stable; learnable (NFDM) adapts per‑token but risks instability and requires careful training.
- **Rescaled loss:** Using `L_x` (x‑prediction) stabilizes sharp schedules but biases toward high‑probability modes; original diffusion loss is theoretically sounder but may collapse.
- **Sampling method:** DDIM‑style (`σ_s|t=1`) is fast deterministic but high bias; generative SDE is slower stochastic but better coverage. Choice of `σ_s|t` governs this trade‑off.
- **Number of steps:** More steps improve quality; learned forward processes may need more steps (NFDM degrades at 32 steps vs Diffusion‑LM).
- **Model sizing:** Larger reverse predictor (BERT‑base) vs smaller forward network; capacity imbalance can affect training stability.

#### Failure Signatures
- **Training collapse:** ELBO spikes/NaN, often due to rescaled loss without SNR constraints.
- **Poor likelihood:** BPC > 4 indicates forward/reverse mismatch.
- **Low sample quality:** MAUVE < 0.2 or perplexity far above dataset suggests bad decoding or biased sampling.
- **Memorization near zero:** Mode dropping; diversity near zero: repetitive outputs.
- **Time‑conditioning failure:** Ablate to additive embeddings → perplexity rises, showing ineffective time conditioning.

2. **Time‑conditioning ablation:** Compare NFDM with additive timestep embeddings versus Adaptive LayerNorm (Table 3). Adaptive LayerNorm should yield lower perplexity, confirming effective time conditioning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the performance of NFDM scale to larger datasets, and does it maintain its reduced likelihood gap with autoregressive models?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "The main limitations of this work are the limited dataset scale [...] Future work should explore larger datasets."
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments were conducted only on ROCstories (~90k examples), which is relatively small; scaling to larger, more diverse corpora is necessary to evaluate robustness and generalizability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train NFDM on large‑scale corpora (e.g., C4, The Pile) and compare ELBO, perplexity, and sample quality metrics against strong autoregressive baselines of comparable size.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can NFDM be effectively extended to conditional generation tasks (e.g., summarization, translation) while preserving its likelihood advantage?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "Future work should extend the method to conditional generation tasks."
- **Why unresolved:** The current work focuses solely on unconditional generation; conditional settings introduce additional complexity such as conditioning on input contexts, which may require architectural modifications or new training objectives.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt NFDM to standard conditional benchmarks (e.g., CNN/DailyMail for summarization, WMT for translation) and report both likelihood (ELBO) and task‑specific metrics compared to existing diffusion and autoregressive models.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the fixed‑average SNR parameterization strictly necessary for stable training with rescaled losses, or can fully flexible learned forward processes be trained without collapse?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors constrain `γ_ϕ(t,c)` to maintain a global average SNR (Appendix B.4), noting that "in the general NFDM setting, such rescaling can cause training collapse."
- **Why unresolved:** This constraint limits the flexibility of the learned forward process and may prevent the model from adapting noise schedules optimally to each token or sequence.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose alternative training schemes (e.g., adaptive loss weighting, regularization terms) that stabilize training without fixing average SNR, and evaluate whether ELBO and sample quality improve.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why do models with learned forward processes (e.g., NFDM, MuLAN) degrade significantly when using few sampling steps, and how can this be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** Appendix F.2.3 shows NFDM's MAUVE drops to zero and perplexity soars at 32 steps, whereas Diffusion‑LM (static forward process) remains relatively robust.
- **Why unresolved:** Learned processes may produce less “straight” trajectories, making discrete‑step approximations inaccurate; this contradicts findings from prior work ([3]) where learned processes improved few‑step sampling.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement trajectory straightening techniques (e.g., from [3]) and measure performance at low step counts; analyze the smoothness of learned marginal distributions and their compatibility with discrete timestep grids.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Exact architectures for `µ_ϕ/σ_ϕ` and Adaptive LayerNorm conditioning are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Training instability:** Original diffusion loss risks collapse; even with Muon and gradient clipping, joint training requires careful hyper‑parameter tuning.
- **Sampling efficiency:** Quality degrades sharply at low function evaluations (≤ 64 steps), limiting practical speed advantages of diffusion over AR models.

## Confidence
- **Likelihood improvement (Mechanisms 1+3):** Medium – ELBO reduction is clear on ROCstories but joint training is unstable and details are not fully replicable.
- **Latent diffusion effectiveness (Mechanism 2):** High – Paradigm is established; NFDM’s matching sample quality supports it.
- **Overall claims:** High – Benchmark results (ELBO 3.12 vs GPT‑J 3.05) are concrete and compare to strong baselines.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of learnable forward:** Train NFDM with a fixed linear schedule (identical to Diffusion‑LM) while keeping reverse predictor and embeddings trainable. Expect ELBO increase, isolating the gain from per‑token adaptation.
2. **Cross‑dataset replication:** Run full training/evaluation on WikiText‑103. Consistent likelihood gap reduction and sample quality would confirm generalizability beyond ROCstories.
3. **Empirical schedule analysis:** For a trained model, compute average `σ_ϕ` across validation tokens binned by frequency. A systematic variation (e.g., rare tokens receiving higher noise) would directly evidence data‑conditioning.
