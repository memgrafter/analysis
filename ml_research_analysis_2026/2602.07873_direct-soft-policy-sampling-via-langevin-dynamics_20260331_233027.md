# Direct Soft-Policy Sampling via Langevin Dynamics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07873
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07873
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: NC-LQL achieves competitive performance on MuJoCo with ∼30% fewer parameters and ∼5x less wall-clock time than diffusion RL baselines, without an explicit actor or best-of-N sampling.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Noise-Conditioned Langevin Q-Learning (NC-LQL), an actor-free reinforcement learning method that directly samples soft policies from a Q-function using annealed Langevin dynamics. Instead of maintaining a separate policy network, NC-LQL learns a noise-conditioned Q-function that is progressively smoothed across multiple noise scales. This smoothing enables efficient global-to-local exploration during action sampling, avoiding the mode collapse common in plain Langevin dynamics. On continuous control benchmarks, NC-LQL matches or exceeds state-of-the-art diffusion-based methods while being significantly more parameter- and time-efficient. The work presents a simpler, more unified alternative for realizing expressive soft policies in online RL.

## Method Summary
NC-LQL builds on Langevin Q-Learning (LQL), which uses the gradient of a Q-function as a score function to sample from the Boltzmann soft policy via Langevin dynamics. To improve mixing and mode coverage, NC-LQL incorporates multi-scale Gaussian noise directly into the Q-function: for a given noise scale σ, it learns the expectation of Q(s,a) over actions a perturbed by noise. This creates a family of smoothly varying value landscapes. During action selection, annealed Langevin dynamics runs iteratively across a decreasing sequence of σ values, using the gradients of these smoothed Q-functions. The process starts with large σ for broad exploration and refines with small σ for precision. The algorithm requires only a single Q-network and a replay buffer; no separate actor, policy gradient, or entropy estimation is needed. Training alternates between collecting experience with the sampler and updating the Q-network via a combination of TD error (at the finest σ) and a smoothing regression loss (for coarser σ).

## Key Results
- NC-LQL achieves normalized scores competitive with top diffusion-based RL methods (e.g., DPMD, Diff-QL) on MuJoCo benchmarks.
- It uses substantially fewer parameters (∼475k vs. ∼700k+ in baselines) and reduces wall-clock training time (1.65 hours vs. up to 9.1 hours).
- Performance remains strong without relying on best-of-N sampling heuristics, demonstrating the effectiveness of its actor-free sampling scheme.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Langevin Q-Learning (LQL) can sample from the target soft policy without an explicit actor by using the Q-gradient as the score function. The update a_t = a_{t-1} + (ε/2) ∇_a Q(s,a_{t-1}) + √ε z_t (z_t ∼ N(0,I)) constitutes a langevin diffusion that, under appropriate step size ε and iteration count, converges in distribution to π(a|s) ∝ exp(α Q(s,a)). This relies on the Q-function being sufficiently smooth and the step size being small enough to approximate the continuous-time diffusion.

### Mechanism 2
Noise-Conditioned Langevin Q-Learning (NC-LQL) uses multi-scale noise perturbations in the Q-function to create progressively smoothed value landscapes. It defines Q_NC(s, â, σ) = E_{a~p(a|â,σ)}[Q(s,a)], where â is a noisy action drawn from a Gaussian centered at a with variance σ². This expectation is learned via a regression loss. During sampling, Annealed Langevin Dynamics applies Langevin steps using ∇_â Q_NC(s, â, σ_i) for a sequence of decreasing σ_i. The smoothing bridges isolated modes at large σ, while fine details emerge at small σ, enabling efficient transition from global exploration to local refinement.

### Mechanism 3
The actor-free design reduces model complexity and simplifies the pipeline. By eliminating a separate policy network and its associated entropy or likelihood computations, NC-LQL only learns a single noise-conditioned Q-network. Actions are generated on-policy by running the annealed sampler. The training objective comprises only TD and smoothing losses; no explicit density estimation or policy optimization is required, leading to lower parameter counts and faster training.

## Foundational Learning
**Soft Policy (Boltzmann Distribution)**: The target policy is π(a|s) ∝ exp(α Q(s,a)), where α is an inverse temperature. *Why needed*: The entire method aims to sample exactly from this distribution; understanding its form and the role of α is central. *Quick check*: "Write the formula for a soft policy and explain the role of the temperature parameter α."

**Langevin Dynamics**: An iterative sampling scheme: x_{t+1} = x_t + (ε/2) ∇_x log p(x) + √ε z_t, which under mild conditions converges to p(x). *Why needed*: It is the engine that turns the Q-gradient (score) into samples from the soft policy. *Quick check*: "Given an unnormalized density p(x) ∝ exp(f(x)), what is the Langevin update to draw samples from p?"

**Score Function**: For a soft policy, the score ∇_a log π(a|s) equals α ∇_a Q(s,a), because the partition function Z(s) = ∫ exp(α Q(s,a)) da does not depend on a. *Why needed*: This identity allows using the Q-gradient directly as the score for Langevin, bypassing the intractable partition function. *Quick check*: "Show that the gradient of the log-soft policy equals α times the Q-gradient, assuming the partition function does not depend on a."

**Noise Conditioning (Value Smoothing)**: Adding Gaussian noise to actions before evaluating Q and taking the expectation yields a smoothed version Q_NC(s, â, σ). *Why needed*: Smoothing reduces barriers between modes in the Q-landscape, making Langevin mixing faster and preventing trapping in local optima. *Quick check*: "What is the effect of injecting Gaussian noise into actions before evaluating Q, when taking the expectation over noisy actions?"

**Actor-Critic vs Actor-Free**: Traditional actor-critic methods maintain separate actor and critic networks. *Why needed*: To appreciate NC-LQL's architectural innovation—removing the actor simplifies the system but introduces iterative action generation. *Quick check*: "Name one advantage and one potential drawback of an actor-free approach compared to a traditional actor-critic method."

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: State s -> Annealed Langevin Sampler (with Q_θ) -> Action a -> Environment -> (s,a,r,s') -> Replay Buffer B -> Minibatch -> Loss computation -> Q_θ update -> (periodically) target Q¯ update

**Critical path**: 
1. For each environment step: invoke the annealed Langevin sampler with current Q_θ and state s to produce action a; execute a; store (s,a,r,s') in B.
2. After sufficient steps, sample a random minibatch from B.
3. Compute two losses: (i) TD loss at σ_L using target Q¯ and next-state actions from the sampler; (ii) smoothing loss for a randomly chosen σ_i by regressing Q_NC(â, σ_i) on clean Q(a).
4. Backpropagate the combined loss to update Q_θ.
5. Periodically update the target network Q¯ via exponential moving average.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Langevin steps (T) and noise levels (L): Larger T×L improves sampling fidelity but increases action latency; paper fixes T×L=20 to match diffusion baselines.
- Noise schedule {σ_i}: σ_1 must be large enough to connect modes; σ_L must be near zero to recover the sharp policy. Tuning is required relative to action scale.
- Step size ε: Controls diffusion; too large causes instability, too small slows mixing. Often fixed small (∼1e-4) with optional score normalization.
- Temperature w: Higher w yields sharper policies but can destabilize training if Q-values explode.
- Score normalization: Normalizing ∇Q by its norm can stabilize training but adds clipping cost.

**Failure signatures**:
- Stagnant/declining returns: check action entropy. Too low → σ_1 insufficient; too high → over-smoothed.
- High variance across seeds: may indicate improper smoothing loss weighting or insufficient replay diversity.
- Diverging Q-values: could be due to large ε or inadequate target smoothing; try decreasing ε or increasing target update rate τ.
- Poor multimodality in 2D bandit: if samples collapse to one mode, increase σ_1 or L.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. 2D bandit visualization (Section 6.1, Appendix C.1): Compares action sample distributions across temperatures w and against baselines; confirms NC-LQL captures multiple high-reward modes.
2. MuJoCo benchmark (Section 6.2, Figure 5): Evaluates learning curves on continuous control tasks, comparing normalized returns to SOTA diffusion methods; shows competitive performance with fewer parameters.
3. Ablation studies (Appendix E.3): Sweeps over temperature w, noise scale σ_L, step size ε, and total iterations T×L; identifies robust hyperparameter ranges and tradeoffs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can we develop an adaptive mechanism to automatically tune the temperature parameter w in NC-LQL during training? *Basis*: The paper explicitly states NC-LQL lacks an automatic w adjustment and identifies this as a future limitation. *Why unresolved*: Manual tuning requires per-task search, hindering deployment in new environments. *What evidence would resolve it*: A method that dynamically adjusts w based on performance or entropy metrics, validated across MuJoCo with comparable/better final returns and no manual tuning.

### Open Question 2
How can NC-LQL be extended to incorporate soft Q-functions that explicitly account for entropy regularization, thereby better aligning with entropy-regularized RL objectives? *Basis*: Future work proposes extending NC-LQL with soft Q-functions (Haarnoja et al., 2017; 2018a). *Why unresolved*: Current formulation uses standard Q, which may not fully capture entropy terms, potentially limiting exploration. *What evidence would resolve it*: An implementation using soft Q-functions, showing improved sample efficiency or final performance on benchmarks, with analysis of resulting policy entropy.

### Open Question 3
What are the convergence properties of NC-LQL under function approximation, and under what conditions does the annealed Langevin sampling converge to the target Boltzmann distribution when combined with neural network critics? *Basis*: Inferred; the paper relies on annealed Langevin convergence (Song & Ermon, 2019) but provides no theory accounting for TD learning and function approximation. *Why unresolved*: Without guarantees, it is unclear if NC-LQL is globally convergent or how approximation errors affect the stationary distribution. *What evidence would resolve it*: A theoretical proof (or set of assumptions) showing convergence of Q-functions and sampling distribution, supported by empirical validation in simple domains where convergence can be measured.

## Limitations
- The method assumes the Q-function is sufficiently smooth; highly multimodal or rugged Q-landscapes may cause mode collapse or slow mixing if the noise schedule or step size is poorly tuned.
- Evaluation is limited to MuJoCo continuous control tasks; there is no testing on image-based, sparse-reward, or high-dimensional domains, restricting generalizability claims.
- The corpus shows low citation counts and moderate relatedness, indicating limited peer validation or recognition of novelty so far.

## Confidence
- Langevin sampling without an actor: **Medium** – theoretically grounded and validated in 2D bandits, but untested on challenging exploration problems.
- Noise-conditioned smoothing: **Medium-High** – strong empirical support from bandit visualizations, yet highly sensitive to σ schedule and step size.
- Actor-free efficiency: **Medium** – parameter and time reductions demonstrated on benchmarks, but computational overhead from Langevin steps may offset gains at larger scale.
