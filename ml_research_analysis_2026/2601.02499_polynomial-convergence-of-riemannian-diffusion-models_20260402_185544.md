# Polynomial Convergence of Riemannian Diffusion Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02499
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02499
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: With polynomially small step size \(h\) and sufficiently large reverse time \(T\), the TV error between the early-stopped target \(p_\delta\) and the output of Algorithm 1 satisfies \(\mathrm{TV}(p_\delta,\mathcal{L}(Y_0)) \le \varepsilon + \varepsilon_{\text{score}} + \sqrt{hT}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})\); iteration complexity \(N = T/h\) scales polynomially in dimension and \(\delta^{-1}\), overcoming prior exponential dependencies.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models on Riemannian manifolds enable generative modeling of data living on curved spaces. This paper establishes the first polynomial iteration‑complexity guarantees for sampling from such models, allowing an L₂‑accurate score estimate and only mild assumptions on the manifold (bounded geometry) and data density (no smoothness or positivity). The core analysis combines three geometric tools: Li‑Yau gradient bounds to control the log‑score magnitude, a Minakshisundaram‑Pleijel parametrix expansion to quantify discretization errors when approximating manifold Brownian motion with Euclidean steps, and a localization scheme that separates drift‑discretization from Brownian simulation errors via auxiliary transition kernels. The result shows that with step size \(h = \mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})^{-1}\) and reverse time \(T \gtrsim \lambda_1^{-1}(d\log d + \log(d/\varepsilon))\), the output distribution converges to the target in total variation with error \(\varepsilon + \varepsilon_{\text{score}} + \sqrt{hT}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})\).

## Method Summary
The algorithm (RSGM) approximates the reverse SDE of Brownian motion on a manifold using a learned score estimate. The forward process is Brownian motion governed by the Laplace‑Beltrami operator; its density \(p_t\) is the convolution of the data density \(p_0\) with the heat kernel. Sampling requires simulating the reverse dynamics, which uses the estimated score \(\tilde{b}_t\) and Gaussian noise injected in the tangent space, followed by the exponential map to stay on the manifold. To handle discretization errors, the authors (i) bound the log‑score gradient via Li‑Yau inequalities, (ii) compare the manifold heat kernel to a Euclidean proxy using a parametrix expansion, and (iii) freeze the drift within small geodesic balls to construct auxiliary kernels that isolate the error in simulating Brownian motion. The total variation error is decomposed into mixing, score estimation, drift discretization, and simulation components; each is bounded under bounded geometry with a polynomial step‑size requirement.

## Key Results
- **TV bound**: \(\mathrm{TV}(p_\delta,\mathcal{L}(Y_0)) \le \varepsilon + \varepsilon_{\text{score}} + \sqrt{hT}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})\) provided \(h \le \mathrm{poly}(d,K,\delta^{-1})^{-1}\) and \(T \ge \lambda_1^{-1}(d\log d + \log(d/\varepsilon))\).
- **Iteration complexity**: \(N = T/h = \mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})/(\lambda_1\varepsilon)^2\), i.e., polynomial in dimension and inverse early‑stopping, contrasting with prior exponential Wasserstein‑distance bounds.
- **Assumptions**: Compact manifold with bounded geometry (uniform curvature bounds, positive injectivity radius); no smoothness or positivity of \(p_0\); score estimator accuracy required only in \(L_2\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The polynomial convergence rests on three pillars:

1. **Li‑Yau gradient bounds** – Classical inequalities bound \(\|\nabla \log H_t\|^2 \le C(1/t + K)\log(\operatorname{supp} p_{t/2})\) under curvature \(K\). This controls the magnitude of the drift in the reverse SDE using only \(L_2\) score estimates and does not require smoothness or positivity of \(p_0\).

2. **Parametrix expansion** – The heat kernel on a bounded‑geometry manifold admits an asymptotic expansion \(H(t,x,y) \approx (2\pi t)^{-d/2} e^{-\rho^2/(2t)}/\sqrt{\Delta(x,y)} \cdot e^{\psi(x,y)}\cdot(1 + t u_1(x,y)+\dots)\). Comparing this to the Euclidean drift kernel used in the algorithm yields a discretization error of order \(\mathrm{poly}(d,K,\delta^{-1})\cdot t\) when the geodesic distance \(\rho\) is polynomially small in \(t\). This polynomial control is essential for the overall rate.

3. **Localization via auxiliary kernels** – The reverse process is decomposed by constructing a family of vector fields \(S_{t_k,Y_k}\) that are constant within a small geodesic ball (using a cutoff \(\eta_\omega\)). An auxiliary transition kernel \(K_\text{aux}^k\) with this frozen drift allows application of Girsanov’s theorem to bound the KL divergence between the exact reverse kernel and \(K_\text{aux}^k\) via Li‑Yau estimates. The difference between \(K_\text{aux}^k\) and the algorithmic kernel is then handled by the parametrix expansion and a rejection‑sampling analysis, giving a per‑step KL of order \(h^2\). Summing over \(N\) steps and applying Pinsker yields \(\sqrt{hT}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})\).

Each mechanism relies on **bounded geometry** (injectivity radius \(\ge 1/K\), bounded curvature tensors and their derivatives up to order 2) and a uniform bound on the score estimator. If curvature is unbounded, injectivity radius vanishes, or the score becomes too large, the polynomial scaling deteriorates.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input \((p_0, \tilde{b}_t)\) → verify bounded geometry (injectivity radius \(\ge 1/K\), curvature bounds, diameter) and compute \(\lambda_1\) → run Algorithm 1:  
\(Y_T \sim \mu\) (uniform)  
for \(k = N,\dots,1\):  
 pick orthonormal frame \(U_k\) at \(Y_k\)  
 \(b_k = \text{clip}(\tilde{b}_{t_k}(Y_k))\) (obey A3)  
 sample \(G_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\)  
 \(\Delta_k = h\,b_k + \sqrt{h}\,U_k G_k\)  
 if \(\|\Delta_k\| \le h^{1/4}\) then \(Y_{k-1} = \exp_{Y_k}(\Delta_k)\) else \(Y_{k-1} \sim \mu\)  
output \(Y_0\)  
Key parameters: \(h \le \mathrm{poly}(d,K,\delta^{-1})^{-1}\), \(\delta\), \(T \ge \lambda_1^{-1}(d\log d + \log(d/\varepsilon))\).

**Critical path (proof sketch)**:  
- Mixing error (Lemma 15): Bound \(\mathrm{TV}(p_T,\mu)\) via heat‑kernel sup \(A\) and Li‑Yau; gives \(e^{-\lambda_1 T/2}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,K)\).  
- Score error (Lemma 16): Direct accumulation of \(\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{b} - \nabla\log p_{t_k}\|^2\) = \(\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\).  
- Drift discretization (Lemma 18): Itô calculus on \(\nabla\log p_t - S_{t_k,Y_k}\) + Li‑Yau → \(\mathcal{O}(d^6 K^8/\delta^3)\,h^2 N\).  
- Brownian simulation (Lemmas 19–20): Auxiliary kernels \(K_\text{aux}^k\) with frozen drift; parametrix to compare with Euclidean \(\Phi\); rejection analysis to compare \(\Phi\) with \(bK_k\); per‑step KL \(\sim h^2\) → TV = \(\sqrt{hT}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\delta^{-1})\).  
- Assembly (Theorem 1): Triangle inequality, Pinsker, and summation yield final bound.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Step size \(h\)**: Must be polynomially small; too large → discretization dominates; too small → \(N\) grows.  
- **Early stopping \(\delta\)**: Smaller \(\delta\) yields distribution closer to \(p_0\) but tightens drift error (\(\delta^{-3}\) dependence); must satisfy \(h \ll \delta \ll T\).  
- **Orthonormal frame**: Arbitrary per step; practical choices (parallel transport, log basis) affect stability but not asymptotic rate.  
- **Rejection radius \(h^{1/4}\)**: Keeps updates inside normal neighborhood; larger radius increases reset probability exponentially (\(\approx \exp(-c\,h^{-1/2})\)).

**Failure signatures**:  
- High TV despite large \(N\) → step size \(h\) too large; reduce \(h\) and check \(\|\nabla\log p_t\|\) growth.  
- Frequent resets → fraction significantly above \(\exp(-c h^{-1/2})\); may indicate \(h\) too large or score estimate violating Assumption 1(A3).  
- Blow‑up in \(\mathrm{poly}(d,K)\) → large curvature or dimension; consider adaptive charts.  
- Slow mixing (\(\lambda_1\) very small) → require \(T\) nearly exponential in \(1/\lambda_1\); estimate spectral gap.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Reset probability vs \(h\)**: On \(S^2\) and \(T^2\) with exact score, vary \(h\) and record fraction of trajectories resetting. Expect \(\log(\text{prob})\) linear in \(h^{-1/2}\), confirming exponential decay.  
2. **TV decay vs \(N\)**: On \(T^d\) (\(d=1,2,3\)) with a warped Gaussian mixture target, estimate TV between \(p_\delta\) and recovered distribution using KDE. Plot TV vs \(N\) (with \(h \propto 1/N\)) to observe polynomial decay (roughly \(1/\sqrt{N}\)).  
3. **Breakdown with large \(h\)**: Set \(h=0.1\) on \(S^2\) (curvature \(K\approx1\)) and compare TV to a small‑\(h\) run. Expect TV to plateau or increase, and reset probability to rise sharply.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Tighter polynomial dependence**: Can the analysis be refined to reduce the polynomial exponents (e.g., by better discretization schedules or direct TV control)?
- **Deterministic samplers**: Extending convergence theory to deterministic discretizations (e.g., DDIM‑style) on manifolds remains open.
- **Conditional sampling**: Theoretical guarantees for conditional Riemannian diffusion models (needed for inverse problems) are an unsolved challenge.

## Limitations
- Polynomial constants are not optimized and may be large, affecting practical step‑size choices.
- Bounded geometry (positive injectivity radius, uniform curvature bounds) is restrictive; many manifolds of interest (e.g., with cusps or boundary) are excluded.
- The assumption of uniform \(L_2\) score error \(\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\) may fail in low‑density regions where data is sparse.
