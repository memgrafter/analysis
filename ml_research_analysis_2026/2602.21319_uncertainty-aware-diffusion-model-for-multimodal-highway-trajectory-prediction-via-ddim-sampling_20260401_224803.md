# Uncertainty-Aware Diffusion Model for Multimodal Highway Trajectory Prediction via DDIM Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21319
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21319
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** cVMDx achieves mean ADE 1.37m and best-of-K ADE 1.33m on the highD dataset, with a 100× inference speedup via DDIM sampling, enabling efficient multi-sample generation for uncertainty-aware prediction.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces cVMDx, a framework for efficient, uncertainty-aware multimodal trajectory prediction in autonomous driving. It tackles the slow inference and limited diversity of prior diffusion models by integrating DDIM sampling for deterministic, fast generation (10 steps), a CVQ-VAE for robust scenario encoding that mitigates codebook collapse, velocity-based training for stability, and an adaptive guidance scheme that modulates conditioning strength based on scenario uncertainty. Trajectories are aggregated via a Gaussian Mixture Model to extract clear motion hypotheses. On the highD dataset, cVMDx demonstrates improved accuracy and drastically reduced inference time, making practical multi-sample generation feasible for uncertainty estimation.

## Method Summary
cVMDx comprises a pre-trained CVQ-VAE that encodes observed traffic scenarios into discrete tokens while estimating scenario-specific uncertainty via Mahalanobis distance in latent space. A diffusion model (VMD) is trained to predict vehicle control sequences (acceleration, yaw-rate) conditioned on these tokens, using a velocity-based loss for training stability. At inference, DDIM sampling generates multiple control sequences in 10 steps, with classifier-free guidance dynamically scaled by a cosine schedule and the estimated uncertainty—stronger conditioning for certain scenarios, relaxed for ambiguous ones. Finally, a vehicle motion model integrates control sequences into trajectories, and a GMM fits the samples to produce a set of distinct motion hypotheses.

## Key Results
- **100× speedup:** DDIM sampling reduces inference time from ~1000 DDPM steps to 10 steps while maintaining predictive fidelity.
- **Improved accuracy:** Achieves mean ADE 1.37m and best-of-K ADE 1.33m on the highD dataset, outperforming prior diffusion-based approaches.
- **Effective uncertainty handling:** Adaptive guidance and GMM-based aggregation yield diverse, physically plausible trajectories suitable for downstream planning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: DDIM for Efficient Sampling
- **Claim:** DDIM sampling enables a ~100× speedup in inference while maintaining predictive fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** Replaces stochastic DDPM reverse diffusion with a deterministic, non-Markovian update derived from the probability flow ODE, allowing generation with far fewer linearly spaced timesteps (S=10) without noise injection.
- **Core assumption:** The deterministic DDIM trajectory approximates the expected value of the stochastic DDPM path for the highway trajectory distribution; the velocity-parameterized model trained with DDPM is compatible with DDIM inference.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Through DDIM sampling, cVMDx achieves up to a 100× reduction in inference time... enabling practical multi-sample generation."  
  - [section IV.E] "DDIM interprets the diffusion process as the solution of an ordinary differential equation (ODE) rather than a stochastic process... the DDIM sampling trajectory can be discretized with fewer timesteps... S≪T."
- **Break condition:** If the motion distribution has high variance not captured by the deterministic path (e.g., highly non-Gaussian modes), DDIM may miss key modes, requiring more stochastic steps or a hybrid scheduler.

### Mechanism 2: CVQ-VAE for Robust Scenario Encoding
- **Claim:** CVQ-VAE produces more robust and balanced scenario embeddings than standard VQ-VAE, mitigating codebook collapse and preserving context diversity.
- **Mechanism:** Introduces an online, adaptive codebook update that redistributes codebook usage during training, preventing a small subset of vectors from dominating (codebook collapse), thus ensuring discrete token `q` better represents traffic scenario variety.
- **Core assumption:** Codebook collapse in standard VQ-VAE leads to a lossy scenario representation; adaptive updating directly addresses this in trajectory prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Integration of a CVQ-VAE to mitigate codebook collapse."  
  - [section IV.C] "CVQ-VAE extends VQ-VAE by adaptively updating codebook entries to prevent codebook collapse... This effect has been observed empirically... by Roßberg et al. [13]."  
  - [corpus] Table I shows marginal gains over VQ-VAE, suggesting limited practical benefit in this context.
- **Break condition:** If adaptive updates cause training instability or the scenario space is too large for the codebook size `Q`, representation quality degrades.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Guidance for Uncertainty-Aware Sampling
- **Claim:** Uncertainty-aware, cosine-scheduled CFG adapts conditioning strength to balance fidelity and multimodality, improving sample quality in both certain and uncertain scenarios.
- **Mechanism:** Guidance scale `w_t` is modulated by (1) a cosine schedule over diffusion timesteps `t` to soften conditioning later, and (2) scenario-specific uncertainty `δ_m` from Mahalanobis distance in the CVQ-VAE latent space. Lower `δ_m` allows higher `w_max`; higher `δ_m` relaxes it to preserve diversity.
- **Core assumption:** Mahalanobis distance `δ_m` in the discrete latent space is a calibrated estimate of epistemic uncertainty; modulating `w` based on it improves the fidelity-diversity trade-off.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "An adaptive guidance scheme."  
  - [section IV.E] Equations define `w(t,δ_m)` and `w_max(δ_m)`, explicitly linking conditioning strength to uncertainty.
- **Break condition:** If `δ_m` is miscalibrated (e.g., does not correlate with true multimodality), adaptive scaling could over-condition in ambiguous situations or under-condition in simple ones.

## Foundational Learning
- **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) & DDIM**  
  *Why needed:* The trajectory generator is a diffusion model; understanding the forward noising, reverse process, and the distinction between stochastic DDPM and deterministic DDIM is key to grasping the speed/diversity trade-off.  
  *Quick check:* What is the mathematical relationship between the DDIM update and DDPM's reverse step, and why does removing the stochastic term `σ_t η` allow fewer steps?
- **Vector Quantization (VQ) and Codebook Collapse**  
  *Why needed:* The context encoder compresses traffic scenarios into discrete tokens; codebook collapse risks a brittle conditioning signal. Understanding the VQ loss and straight-through estimator is essential.  
  *Quick check:* In the VQ-VAE loss `L = ||x - D(z_q)||^2 + ||sg[E(x)] - z_q||^2 + β||E(x) - sg[z_q]||^2`, what is the role of the `sg[]` operator, and how does CVQ-VAE modify updates to address collapse?
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and Guidance Scale**  
  *Why needed:* CFG steers the diffusion model toward the scenario token; adaptive scaling of `w` is a core contribution. One must understand how `˜ϵ = (1+w)ϵ_cond - wϵ_uncond` combines predictions.  
  *Quick check:* How does increasing the guidance scale `w` affect the trade-off between fidelity and diversity? What is the "over-conditioning" risk?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input `ξ` (observed vehicle states) -> CVQ-VAE (Encoder `E` -> continuous `z` -> quantize `z_q` -> index `q`; Decoder `D` reconstructs `ξ`) -> Scenario uncertainty `δ_m` (Mahalanobis distance in latent space) -> Trajectory Diffusion (U-Net denoiser `v_θ(xt, t, c=q)`, trained with velocity loss `L_vel`, supports CFG) -> Vehicle Motion Model (integrates predicted control `x_0` into trajectory `Y`) -> Post-processing (generate `N_samples=9` trajectories; PCA -> GMM fit -> BIC select `C` -> component means `¯Y^(C)`).
- **Critical path:** Pre-trained CVQ-VAE (fixed) -> scenario token `q` & uncertainty `δ` -> DDIM-sampled VMD with adaptive CFG -> VMM integration -> GMM clustering.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Speed vs. Diversity:** DDIM (S=10) is fast but deterministic per noise seed; more samples needed for distribution estimate vs. slower stochastic DDPM.  
  - **Codebook Capacity `Q` vs. Usage:** Larger `Q` (256) risks sparse clusters; smaller `Q` (60) may under-fit. Paper finds marginal gains from increasing `Q`.  
  - **Guidance Strength `w` vs. Realism:** High `w` forces adherence but causes over-conditioning; adaptive `w` based on `δ` attempts mitigation.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Scenario Encoder Failure:** Very high KL divergence for a codebook entry `q` (Fig. 3) indicates poor CVQ-VAE modeling of that scenario, harming conditioning.  
  - **Over-Conditioning:** Generated trajectories collapse to a single mode even for high-uncertainty (`δ` large) scenarios, suggesting `w_max` too high or `δ` miscalibrated.  
  - **Physical Implausibility:** Trajectories violating kinematic constraints (e.g., sudden jumps) indicate VMM integration issues or poor control prediction.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Train CVQ-VAE standalone: Verify reconstruction loss converges, codebook usage is balanced (no token >~5%), and maneuver classification accuracy `L_cl` is reasonable (>~70% on validation).  
  2. Train VMD with velocity loss: Monitor `L_vel` on held-out set; check that sampling with S=10 DDIM steps and fixed `w=1.0` produces physically plausible trajectories after VMM (smooth accelerations/yaw-rates).  
  3. Test adaptive guidance: For a batch, compute `δ_m`. Generate samples with (a) fixed `w=1.0`, (b) adaptive `w_t(δ_m)`. Qualitatively inspect: low-`δ` scenarios should be more focused with (b); high-`δ` should show more diversity. Quantitatively compare MinADE_k and mode counts from GMM.

## Limitations
- **DDIM deterministic approximation** may fail under highly multi-modal or non-Gaussian traffic distributions, risking loss of rare but critical modes.
- **CVQ-VAE's practical impact** is questionable; Table I shows marginal gains over standard VQ-VAE, suggesting adaptive codebook updates may not substantially improve scenario representation for this dataset.
- **Uncertainty metric δ_m** (Mahalanobis distance in latent space) lacks calibration validation; if miscalibrated, adaptive guidance could misbalance fidelity and diversity.

## Confidence
- **DDIM speedup & fidelity:** High (theoretically grounded, strong empirical 100× speedup reported).
- **CVQ-VAE robustness:** Medium (limited empirical benefit; relies on external work [13] for broader validation).
- **Adaptive guidance efficacy:** Low (no direct evidence linking δ_m to true epistemic uncertainty or demonstrating improved fidelity-diversity trade-off).

## Next Checks
1. **Stress-test DDIM vs. stochastic DDPM:** For high-entropy scenarios (measured by ground-truth trajectory spread), compare mode coverage and minADE using DDIM (S=10) against stochastic DDPM (T=100). Quantify missing modes.
2. **Validate δ_m calibration:** Correlate δ_m with human-annotated scenario ambiguity or alternative uncertainty estimates (e.g., ensemble variance from multiple context encoders) on a held-out set. Aim for Spearman ρ > 0.6.
3. **Ablate adaptive guidance across uncertainty strata:** Run experiments with fixed w ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} and compare to adaptive w(δ_m). Stratify results by δ_m deciles to measure differential impact on ADE and mode count; expect adaptive w to improve worst-case (high-δ) diversity without hurting low-δ accuracy.
