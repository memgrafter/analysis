# Constructing Composite Features for Interpretable Music-Tagging
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28644
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28644
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** GP-evolved composite features improved music tagging performance: +0.2-0.3% ROC-AUC on MTG-Jamendo and +3.5-5.0% accuracy on GTZAN while maintaining interpretability.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of interpretability in deep learning-based music tagging when fusing multiple audio features. The authors propose a Genetic Programming (GP) pipeline that automatically evolves mathematically interpretable composite features by combining base features and capturing synergistic interactions. On the MTG-Jamendo dataset, GP-augmented features yield a modest gain of 0.2–0.3% in ROC‑AUC (e.g., ALL62 from 0.727 to 0.730), while on GTZAN they achieve a larger improvement of 3.5–5.0% in accuracy (e.g., ALL62 from 0.765 to 0.805). Most of the performance increase occurs within the first few hundred GP evaluations, indicating effectiveness under moderate computational budgets. Analysis of the evolved expressions reveals interpretable feature interactions and transformations, providing insights that are unavailable from black‑box models.

## Method Summary
The approach uses Genetic Programming (GP) to automatically construct composite features from a predefined set of base audio features. GP individuals are tree‑structured expressions that combine base features via arithmetic operators and simple functions. Fitness evaluation balances predictive performance—assessed by training a gradient boosting classifier (XGBoost) on the composite features—and expression complexity through parsimony pressure. After evolution, the best composite features are selected and concatenated with the original base features to form an enhanced input for the final tagging model. This pipeline is designed to uncover nonlinear interactions and transformations that are both effective and human‑readable.

## Key Results
- On MTG‑Jamendo, GP‑augmented features improved baseline ROC‑AUC by 0.2–0.3% (e.g., ALL62 from 0.727 to 0.730).
- On GTZAN, accuracy gains were 3.5–5.0% (e.g., ALL62 from 0.765 to 0.805).
- Most performance gains were achieved within the first few hundred GP evaluations, indicating effectiveness under modest computational budgets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The GP pipeline captures synergistic interactions among base audio features by searching over a space of mathematical combinations that would be difficult to design manually. Parsimony pressure encourages the evolution of simple yet informative composite features, keeping them interpretable. The evolutionary process efficiently discovers transformations that enhance the discriminative power of the feature set, leading to improved tagging performance while retaining transparency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Genetic Programming (GP):** Population‑based evolutionary algorithm that evolves tree‑structured programs/expressions. Why needed: Automates discovery of nonlinear feature combinations. Quick check: Verify generated expressions are syntactically valid and population diversity is maintained.
- **Parsimony Pressure:** Fitness penalty applied to expression size or complexity. Why needed: Ensures evolved features remain interpretable (simple). Quick check: Monitor correlation between complexity and fitness; ideally low complexity achieves high performance.
- **Music Tagging:** Multi‑label classification task that predicts tags (e.g., genre, mood, instrument) for music tracks. Why needed: The target application; features must be predictive across many tags. Quick check: Evaluate tag‑wise ROC‑AUC to confirm broad improvements.
- **Interpretability:** Degree to which a model or feature can be understood by humans. Why needed: Provides transparency and insight beyond black‑box models. Quick check: Can domain experts decipher the meaning of the evolved expressions?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Base Features -> GP Feature Construction -> Composite Features -> XGBoost Classifier -> Predictions

**Critical path:** The GP evolution loop (selection, crossover, mutation) is central; it generates expressions, evaluates fitness by training XGBoost on composite features, and iteratively improves both performance and simplicity.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Expressiveness vs. interpretability: Deeper/complex expressions capture more interactions but become less readable; parsimony pressure balances this.
- Search thoroughness vs. computational budget: More generations or larger populations yield better results but increase runtime.
- Diversity vs. convergence: Maintaining population diversity aids exploration but may slow convergence to high‑fitness individuals.

**Failure signatures:**
- No improvement over baseline features (GP fails to find beneficial composites).
- Evolved expressions are excessively complex (large tree size/depth), violating interpretability goals.
- Poor generalization to unseen data or other datasets (overfitting to the training set).
- GP search stagnates early (fitness plateaus with no improvement over generations).

**First three experiments:**
1. Baseline comparison: Train XGBoost using only the original base features to establish a performance reference.
2. Early stopping analysis: Track best fitness over GP evaluations to confirm that most gains occur within the first few hundred iterations.
3. Parsimony pressure ablation: Vary the complexity penalty coefficient to study its effect on feature complexity and tagging performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the GP‑based composite feature construction generalize to other music tagging datasets, especially those representing diverse cultures or annotation schemes?
- What music‑theoretic or perceptual mechanisms explain the beneficial feature interactions and operator transformations repeatedly found by GP?
- How does the complexity of composite features trade off with human interpretability and tagging performance in real‑world applications?
- Can the GP feature construction framework be combined with deep learning models to enhance their performance while retaining interpretability?

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to two datasets (MTG‑Jamendo and GTZAN), limiting claims about generalizability.
- Performance gains on MTG‑Jamendo are very small (+0.2‑0.3% ROC‑AUC) and may not be statistically or practically significant.
- Interpretability of the evolved features is assumed but not empirically validated through user studies or expert assessment.

## Confidence

## Next Checks
1. Cross‑dataset test: Evaluate on at least two additional datasets (e.g., AudioSet, MSD) and report performance with confidence intervals.
