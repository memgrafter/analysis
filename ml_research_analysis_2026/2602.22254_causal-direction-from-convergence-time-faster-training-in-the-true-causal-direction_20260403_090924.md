# Causal Direction from Convergence Time: Faster Training in the True Causal Direction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22254
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22254
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Identifies causal direction from observational data by measuring neural network convergence speed in both predictive directions, under the additive noise model with nonlinear injective functions, achieving 96% accuracy on the Tübingen benchmark.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to causal direction inference by leveraging the asymmetry in training convergence times of neural networks. Under the additive noise model with nonlinear injective functions, the true causal direction (from cause to effect) is proven to converge in fewer gradient steps than the reverse, due to a harder optimization landscape in the latter. The method, implemented via Causal Convergence Asymmetry (CCA) for bivariate pairs and Causal Convergent Learning (CCL) for full graphs, demonstrates high empirical accuracy on synthetic and real-world data, while explicitly failing under linear Gaussian mechanisms, non-injective functions, and improper data normalization.

## Method Summary
For pairwise causal inference (CCA), two neural networks are trained to predict Y from X and X from Y, respectively. The number of gradient steps required to reach a predefined early-stopping threshold is recorded for each direction; the direction with fewer steps is inferred as causal. For multi-variable causal graphs (CCL), an iterative scheme uses a variational Conditional Independence Bayes (CIB) encoder to learn parent sets, refines mechanism networks, and updates the graph structure until convergence. The core assumption is an additive noise model with a nonlinear injective forward function.

## Key Results
- Achieved 26/30 correct identifications on synthetic data across six architectures, with perfect 30/30 on sine and exponential data-generating processes.
- Obtained 96% accuracy on the Tübingen real-world causal benchmark.
- Confirmed three boundary conditions: failure on linear Gaussian mechanisms, non‑injective functions (e.g., Y = X² + ε), and without proper data normalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Under the additive noise model Y = f(X) + ε with nonlinear injective f and independent ε, the forward loss landscape satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, yielding fast linear convergence. The reverse direction (predicting X from Y) involves learning a function that disentangles the cause from the noise-affected effect, creating a more complex, ill-conditioned landscape with slower convergence. This asymmetry in expected gradient steps is provable and serves as a consistent signal for causal inference.

## Foundational Learning
- **Additive Noise Model (ANM)**
  - *Why needed:* Structural assumption enabling identifiability via independence of cause and noise.
  - *Quick check:* Fit nonparametric regression, test residual-cause independence with HSIC.
- **Nonlinear Injective Function**
  - *Why needed:* Ensures E[X|Y] is single-valued; required for Lemma 4.1 linking convergence to direction.
  - *Quick check:* Sample inputs, verify distinct outputs for distinct inputs over a dense grid.
- **Convergence Time Asymmetry**
  - *Why needed:* Core metric distinguishing true from reverse direction based on gradient steps.
  - *Quick check:* Train forward/reverse models, compare epochs to early-stopping threshold.
- **Causal Convergence Asymmetry (CCA)**
  - *Why needed:* Algorithmic instantiation for pairwise causal inference using convergence speed.
  - *Quick check:* Apply to synthetic ANM data; expect correct direction identification >90%.
- **Causal Convergent Learning (CCL)**
  - *Why needed:* Scales CCA to full causal graphs via iterative parent set learning.
  - *Quick check:* Run on small known graphs (3–4 nodes); measure graph recovery accuracy.
- **Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) Condition**
  - *Why needed:* Theoretical guarantee for linear convergence rates; underpins asymmetry proof.
  - *Quick check:* Estimate PL constant during training via min(‖∇L‖² / (L−L∗)).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data → Neural Network Trainer (Optimizer) → Convergence Monitor → Direction Decider  
For CCL: Add CIB Encoder ↔ GraphLearner with loop: Encoder → GraphLearner → Mechanism Update → Encoder

**Critical path:**  
- CCA: (1) Train forward (X→Y) net, (2) Train reverse (Y→X) net, (3) Record convergence steps, (4) Output direction with fewer steps.  
- CCL: (1) Initialize random graph, (2) For each node, use CIB encoder to infer parents, (3) Update mechanism networks, (4) Update graph, (5) Repeat until graph stable.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Normalization sensitivity: Asymmetry breaks without standardization; requires preprocessing.
- Injectivity dependency: Non-injective f reverses signal; method fails.
- Computational cost: Multiple neural net trainings per pair/node; scales quadratically with variables.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Linear Gaussian: convergence times approximate equality → random guessing.
- Non-injective: reverse converges faster → systematic misidentification.
- Unnormalized data: loss landscapes distorted → unreliable step counts.

**First experiments:**  
1. Replicate sine and exponential DGPs from the paper; expect near-perfect identification.
2. Run Tübingen benchmark with standardized data; expect >95% accuracy.
3. Ablate network width/depth (e.g., 2 vs. 5 layers, 32 vs. 128 units) to confirm robustness within reasonable architecture choices.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the CCA convergence asymmetry hold for high-dimensional multivariate causal mechanisms?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 9.2(1) directly states: "Whether the convergence-time asymmetry extends to high-dimensional multivariate mechanisms is an open question."
- **Why unresolved:** The theory (Theorem 4.4) and experiments are restricted to bivariate scalar variables. In multivariate settings, interactions among multiple parents may violate the local PL condition or affect the residual dependence structure that underpins the asymmetry.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Prove the PL condition and Lemma 4.1 for multivariate additive noise models; conduct large-scale experiments with synthetic multivariate DGPs varying in dimensionality, parent count, and functional forms to measure the persistence of the convergence gap.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can CCA be adapted to correctly identify causal direction when the true mechanism is non‑injective (e.g., Y = X² + ε)?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 9.2(2) notes that handling non‑injective mechanisms is a practical requirement; Section 4.8(2) confirms the boundary condition experimentally, showing 10/10 seeds misidentify such edges.
- **Why unresolved:** Lemma 4.1 requires f to be injective; without it, the reverse regression target E[X|Y] collapses to a constant by symmetry, making the reverse problem trivially fast and reversing the CCA signal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a statistical test for approximate injectivity in finite samples; modify CCA (e.g., by incorporating curvature checks or alternative scoring) that correctly distinguishes causal direction on a suite of non‑injective DGPs; theoretically characterize identifiability conditions under approximate injectivity.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the finite‑sample convergence rate of the CIB encoder in CCL, and how does it impact overall graph consistency?
- **Basis in paper:** Remark 5.3 states: "The finite‑sample convergence rate is an acknowledged open problem." Theorem 5.2 only guarantees local convergence asymptotically.
- **Why unresolved:** The MDL consistency proof (Lemma 5.1) uses an asymptotic bound O(1/k); the rate at which the encoder error ηₖ shrinks with finite n and k remains unquantified, affecting how quickly the graph learning step receives accurate representations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derive explicit finite‑sample bounds for the variational CIB objective under realistic encoder families (e.g., neural networks); empirically measure representation fidelity vs. sample size and its effect on graph selection accuracy; integrate the rate into the CCL sample complexity bound (Theorem 5.6).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can CCL be extended to support counterfactual reasoning (Pearl’s Rung 3) while preserving provable guarantees?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 9.5 declares: "Extending CCL to Rung 3 via twin‑network abduction is the natural next theoretical step." The current framework stops at Rung 2 (interventions).
- **Why unresolved:** Counterfactuals require abducting exogenous noise variables, performing interventions, and predicting outcomes—functions not implemented in CCL+. The twin‑network method assumes a known SCM, but CCL learns graph and mechanisms jointly; integrating these steps without breaking consistency is non‑trivial.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Incorporate a twin‑network abduction module into the CCL loop; prove identifiability of counterfactual queries under the learned graph; validate on benchmarks with known counterfactual ground truth (e.g., synthetic SCMs with hidden confounding), showing accurate counterfactual prediction and robustness to model misspecification.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strict assumptions (additive noise, nonlinear injective functions); performance degrades on linear Gaussian and non‑injective cases, limiting real-world applicability.
- Empirical results are based on a narrow set of synthetic distributions (sine, exponential) and a single real-world benchmark (Tübingen); generalization to diverse scenarios remains uncertain.
- Method sensitivity to data normalization is noted but not fully explained; hyperparameter choices (network architecture, optimizer) may also influence convergence asymmetry and were not thoroughly ablated.

## Confidence
- **Core theoretical asymmetry (faster convergence in true direction under assumptions):** Medium. Proof exists but is conditional; independent verification needed.
- **Empirical effectiveness (high accuracy on synthetic/Tübingen):** Medium. Results are promising but limited in scope; replication on broader benchmarks required.
- **Reported failure modes (linear Gaussian, non‑injective, no normalization):** High. Directly tested and confirmed.
