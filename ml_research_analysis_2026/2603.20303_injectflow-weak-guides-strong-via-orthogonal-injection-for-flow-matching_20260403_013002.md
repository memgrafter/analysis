# InjectFlow: Weak Guides Strong via Orthogonaljection for Flow Matching

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20303
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20303
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Training-free inferencefix that corrects 75% of compositional generation failures on GenEval, with a 7.14% overall score improvement.

## Executive Summary
Flow Matching (FM) models suffer performance drops on compositional prompts due to *conditional expectation smoothing*, where the MMSE-trained vector field averages out high-frequency semantics, locking trajectories onto a low-rank bias manifold. InjectFlow intervenes at inference by injecting an orthogonal velocity component from a weaker companion model at the initial step, then switching to an SDE to explore the newly opened subspace while preserving data marginals. On GenEval, it achieves full correction on the Two Objects task and strong correction elsewhere with minimal added inference cost.

## Method Summary
InjectFlow requires no retraining. At the first denoising step (`t=start`), it computes the primary model’s velocity `vT`. A weaker companion model processes a masked version of the same prompt to produce `vS`. The orthogonal component `v⊥ = vS - proj_{vT}(vS)` is extracted and scaled by a factor `α` before being added to `vT`. Thereafter, the integration switches from a deterministic ODE to an SDE with a specific noise schedule `σ_t` and drift correction that mathematically preserves the original data distribution. The weak model’s masking strategy is designed to force it to capture residual semantics orthogonal to the strong model’s collapsed representation.

## Key Results
- Corrected 75% of prompts that standard flow matching failed on the GenEval benchmark.
- Achieved 100% correction rate on the "Two Objects" compositional task.
- Improved overall GenEval generation score by 7.14% with only a 3.7% increase in inference time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conditional Expectation Smoothing → Bias Manifold Formation
The FM MMSE objective learns `v*θ = E[q(x1|xt,c)][ut(...)]`. For complex, sparse posteriors `q(x1|c)`, this conditional expectation acts as a low-pass filter, regressing the velocity field into a low-rank subspace (`Mt`) defined by majority biases in the training data. Once the initial velocity lies in `Mt`, all subsequent ODE steps remain confined to that manifold, preventing generation of minority/compositional features.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonal Semantic Injection via Weak Model
A weaker companion model (trained on the same data) is prompted with a dynamically masked version of the original text. Its output `vS` contains semantic residuals (`vcomp`) that are not perfectly collinear with the strong model’s `vT`. The orthogonal component `v⊥` is computed via projection. Adding `α·v⊥` at `t=start` expands the tangent space rank (Theorem 2), forcing the trajectory out of the bias manifold’s basin of attraction.

### Mechanism 3: SDE-Driven Trajectory Fission for Marginal-Preserving Exploration
After injection, integration uses an SDE with drift `fSDE = vθ + (σ²_t/2) ∇logpt(xt)` and noise `σ_t dWt`. The Brownian motion explores the newly opened orthogonal directions, while the decaying noise schedule (`σ_t = a·√(t/(1-t))`) ensures the path asymptotically returns to the ODE near `t=0`, preserving sample quality. Lemma 3 allows approximating `∇logpt(xt)` from `vθ` without retraining.

## Architecture Onboarding
Primary FM Model (Strong) -> Weak Model (with Masked Prompt) -> Orthogonal Projection Unit -> Velocity Injector -> SDE Scheduler

*Critical path:* The intervention must occur at the very first step (`t=start=1`). If the initial tangent is locked into the bias manifold `Mt`, later steps only render details within it. The weak model’s forward pass and orthogonal computation must finish before the strong model’s first update.

*Design tradeoffs:*
- Weak model selection: Stronger weak models may collapse similarly; weaker ones may be too noisy. The 8B (strong) vs 2.5B (weak) pairing is empirical, not theoretically optimized.
- Injection strength `α`: Paper uses `α=1.0`. Too low: insufficient rank expansion. Too high: overshoot, losing fidelity.
- Masking strategy: "Dynamic prompt-masking" heuristic is underspecified (which tokens to mask?).
- SDE noise `a=0.7`: Balances exploration vs. quality; may need tuning per model/task.

*Failure signatures:*
1. Persistent bias (no GenEval correction) → `v⊥ ≈ 0` (weak model collapsed or masking ineffective).
2. No latent volume jump at `t=start` (see Fig 2) → orthogonal expansion failed.
3. High seed sensitivity → instability in `v⊥` or SDE noise.
4. Unrealistic images → `α` or `a` too high, or `∇logpt` approximation broke in OOD regions.

*First 3 experiments:*
1. **Replicate Latent Volume Tracking:** Implement the 8-trajectory parallelotope volume measurement (Eq 2) on a prone prompt (e.g., "red zebra"). Confirm baseline volume collapses early; post-injection shows discrete jump at `t=start`.
2. **Orthogonal Direction Sanity Check:** For a given prompt, compute `vT` and `vS` (with/without masking). Verify `⟨v⊥, vT⟩ ≈ 0` and `∥v⊥∥ > 0`.
3. **Ablation of `α` and `a`:** Sweep `α` in [0.5, 1.0, 1.5] and `a` in [0.3, 0.7, 1.0] on a small GenEval subset. Measure trade-off between correction rate and image quality (e.g., FID, user study).

## Limitations
- Relies on finding a "weak" companion model that retains useful orthogonal semantics without collapsing similarly—a fragile, empirical balance.
- Theoretical guarantees assume Gaussian/path-linearization approximations that may not hold in complex, high-dimensional latent spaces.
- Evaluation limited to GenEval and SD3.5 architecture; generalization to other models and benchmarks is untested.

## Confidence
- Bias manifold formation via smoothing: Medium (plausible derivation, indirect causal evidence).
- Orthogonal injection efficacy: Medium (theorem proves rank expansion, but empirical reliance on specific weak model pairing not theoretically guaranteed).
- SDE marginal preservation & correction: Medium-High (theorem under assumptions; GenEval results support, but quality trade-offs heuristic).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-benchmark stress test:** Apply InjectFlow to other biased generative benchmarks (e.g., FairFace, COCO-completion prompts) to measure consistency of correction rates and failure modes.
2. **Weak model ablation:** Systematically vary weak model size (e.g., SD3.5 Small, 2B, 8B) and training data distribution to isolate what property (capacity, data divergence) enables useful `v⊥`.
3. **Masking strategy dissection:** For a fixed weak model, compare random token masking vs. heuristic (e.g., masking adjectives/nouns) vs. no masking; quantify impact on `∥v⊥∥` and GenEval correction.
