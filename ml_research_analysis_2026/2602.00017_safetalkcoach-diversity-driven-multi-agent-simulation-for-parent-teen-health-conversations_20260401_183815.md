# SafeTalkCoach: Diversity-Driven Multi-Agent Simulation for Parent-Teen Health Conversations

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.00017
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00017
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A multi-agent framework that generates diverse, realistic, and guideline-adherent parent-teen conversations about sexual health, outperforming baselines on diversity, engagement, and communication quality.

## Executive Summary
SafeTalkCoach is a multi-agent framework designed to simulate authentic and diverse parent-teen conversations about sexual health. It addresses the scarcity of realistic dialogue data by generating synthetic interactions that are varied across contexts, behaviors, and utterances while adhering to evidence-based communication guidelines. The system employs hierarchical diversification at data, architectural, and prompting levels, an engagement score module to model child responsiveness, and a planning module to ensure coherent, guideline-grounded parent behavior. Evaluations show SafeTalkCoach significantly outperforms baseline methods on diversity, engagement, and communication quality, offering a valuable tool for training and research in sensitive parent-child communication.

## Method Summary
SafeTalkCoach uses a hybrid data pipeline combining crowd-sourced scenarios (from Reddit/StackExchange) and LLM-synthesized scenarios covering four teachable moment categories. It instantiates three specialized agents: a parent agent with a planning module that generates a conversation plan grounded in guidelines (Planned Parenthood, STATE framework); a child agent with an engagement score module that tracks a latent responsiveness state updated heuristically; and a moderator agent that determines termination. Hierarchical diversification controls diversity at the scenario selection level, through control module parameters, and via verbalized sampling in prompts. The system generates dialogues turn-by-turn until the child disengages or reflects without further questions.

## Key Results
- SafeTalkCoach achieves 87–100% win rates in pairwise diversity comparisons against Direct/Procedural/PSYDIAL and outperforms FCS on situational coverage.
- It attains the highest Engagement score (4.87) among frameworks, with parent utterances that adapt to child responsiveness.
- The system yields competitive scores on Warmth (4.88) and Information (4.81), demonstrating guideline adherence and communication quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Hierarchical Diversification**: The framework generates diverse conversations across contexts, behaviors, and utterances while maintaining focus and guideline adherence through three levels: (1) data-level scenario synthesis from crowd-sourced and synthetic sources covering teachable moments; (2) architectural control modules (parent planning, child engagement) that parameterize agent behaviors; (3) prompting-level verbalized sampling to reduce mode collapse. Evidence shows SafeTalkCoach wins 87–100% of pairwise comparisons on dialogue-level diversity versus baselines, and outperforms on situational coverage. Ablations reveal that removing scenario or planning modules reduces diversity win rates from 83–100% to 33–50%, confirming the necessity of hierarchical control.

**Engagement Score Module**: Child engagement is modeled as a continuous latent state updated each turn via a Gaussian process. The mean is adjusted based on heuristic ratings of the parent's last utterance (e.g., openness, caring, empathy increase engagement; hostility, taboo, poor communication decrease it), with a random upper bound per child persona to limit maximum engagement. This enables realistic, non-lecture-like interactions where the parent adapts to the child's responsiveness. SafeTalkCoach achieves the highest Engagement score (4.87) among frameworks; ablations show that removing the module slightly reduces the parent's ability to encourage engagement and makes dialogues less adaptive.

**Planning Module**: Before dialogue, an LLM generates a conversation plan reflecting the parent's specific concerns and approach, conditioned on persona, scenario, and topic. At each turn, the parent's utterance is generated conditioned on this plan, dialogue history, and child attributes, with explicit instructions to follow the STATE framework and Planned Parenthood/Crucial Conversations guidelines. This grounding improves consistency and adherence to best practices. SafeTalkCoach scores competitively on Warmth (4.88) and Information (4.81); removing the planning module reduces Information score from 4.71 to 4.65, indicating its contribution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Teachable Moments**: Scenarios are synthesized around four categories (Major Life Events, Social Media, Mass Media, Everyday Occurrences) to ensure authentic triggering events. *Why needed*: Without structured triggers, conversations may lack real-world context. *Quick check*: What are the four teachable moment categories?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Scenario Database -> Parent Agent (Planning Module) -> Child Agent (Engagement Score Module) -> Moderator Agent (Termination check) -> loop until termination. Hierarchical Diversification Controller configures diversity at data, architectural, and prompting levels.

**Critical path**:
1. Select scenario and personas from database.
2. Parent agent generates conversation plan.
3. For each turn:
   a. Parent generates utterance conditioned on plan, history, child attributes.
   b. Update child engagement score based on parent utterance.
   c. Child generates utterance conditioned on engagement, persona, history.
   d. Moderator checks termination conditions (disengagement or reflection with no questions).
4. Repeat until stop.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Diversity vs. Controllability: Accepts slightly lower strategy diversity than DiaSynth to avoid off-topic drift.
- Realism vs. Guideline Adherence: Strict guidelines may slightly reduce naturalness; FCS's verbosity harms realism.
- Multi-agent vs. Simplicity: Multi-agent introduces termination challenges but enables adaptive engagement modeling.

**Failure signatures**:
- Off-topic drift when scenario variety is limited or control modules disabled.
- Unrealistically consistent or oscillatory child behavior if engagement updates are too abrupt or random upper bound absent.
- Generic, less informative parent utterances if planning prompt is underspecified or guidelines omitted.

**First experiments**:
1. Ablate the scenario diversification module and measure diversity win rates.
2. Disable the planning module and evaluate information and warmth scores.
3. Remove the engagement module and assess parent adaptability and engagement scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization**: How can SafeTalkCoach be extended to cover more sexual health topics beyond safe sex, abstinence, and consent? The framework's design and dataset are specific to these three; generalizing requires adapting scenario synthesis, guidelines, and persona definitions for new topics.
- **Ecological validity**: How can the ecological validity of SafeTalkCoach dialogues be assessed through evaluations with domain experts and parents? Current evaluations rely on automated metrics and limited human annotation; expert and parent feedback is needed to ensure real-world applicability.
- **Evaluation bias**: How can evaluation of synthetic dialogues in sensitive domains be improved to mitigate biases inherent in LLM-as-a-judge methods? LLM judges may not align with human judgment, especially for nuanced, sensitive conversations; alternative or supplemental evaluation approaches are needed.

## Limitations
- The physiological plausibility of the continuous Gaussian engagement model for real teens is unvalidated.
- Generalization beyond the narrow sexual health topic domain is unknown.
- Long-context coherence and plan adherence in extended dialogues (>10 turns) are not evaluated.

## Confidence
- **High**: Hierarchical diversification's empirical effectiveness on measured diversity metrics (supported by ablations and pairwise wins).
- **Medium**: Planning module's contribution to guideline adherence and informativeness (supported by ablation scores but dependent on LLM prompt quality).
- **Low**: Engagement module's behavioral realism (heuristic ratings are proxies; no validation with actual parent-teen interaction data).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a within-subject user study comparing SafeTalkCoach-generated dialogues to human-authored ones for perceived teen authenticity and parental adaptability.
2. Perform a parameter sensitivity ablation on the Engagement Score Module's Gaussian α and upper bound to quantify impact on dialogue stability and "stubbornness."
3. Extend scenario coverage analysis to explicitly measure intersectional diversity (e.g., LGBTQ+ youth, rural families) against known population distributions.
