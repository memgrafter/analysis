# A Comprehensive Information-Decomposition Analysis of Large Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29676  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29676  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LVLMs exhibit two distinct information-use regimes (synergy‑driven vs. knowledge‑driven); visual instruction tuning is the critical stage for learning multimodal fusion, as quantified by a novel PID framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Partial Information Decomposition (PID) framework to dissect how large vision‑language models combine visual and linguistic information. By adapting the scalable BATCH estimator, the authors profile 26 LVLMs on four datasets across three dimensions: breadth (cross‑model & task), depth (layer‑wise), and training dynamics. Key findings reveal two task regimes (synergy‑driven vs. knowledge‑driven), persistent family‑level strategies (fusion‑centric vs. language‑centric), a consistent three‑phase layer‑wise information flow with synergy spiking at the final layer, and that visual instruction tuning is the critical stage where multimodal synergy emerges. The work provides an information‑theoretic lens that goes beyond accuracy‑only evaluation to understand multimodal fusion in LVLMs.

## Method Summary
The pipeline extracts vision and language embeddings from frozen LVLM checkpoints. Unimodal conditionals \(P(Y|X_1)\) and \(P(Y|X_2)\) are obtained by masking one modality with calibrated Gaussian noise (mean and variance computed per dataset). The multimodal conditional \(P(Y|X_1,X_2)\) comes from a standard forward pass. These predictive distributions, together with a softly aggregated marginal \(P(Y)\), are fed to the BATCH estimator. BATCH learns a joint distribution \(\tilde{Q}\) that matches the source‑target marginals via a Sinkhorn projection and optimizes an information‑theoretic objective; PID atoms (redundancy \(R\), visual uniqueness \(U_1\), language uniqueness \(U_2\), synergy \(S\)) are derived analytically from \(\tilde{Q}\) and the true marginals. For layer‑wise analysis, a logit lens projects each transformer block’s hidden states through the LM head. The method is model‑agnostic and relies only on embeddings and predictions without retraining.

## Key Results
- Two task regimes: on MMBench and POPE, accuracy strongly correlates with synergy (\(\rho \ge 0.74\)); on Reefknot and PMC‑VQA, language uniqueness dominates (\(\rho = 0.406\) on PMC‑VQA).  
- Family‑level strategies: fusion‑centric families (e.g., InternVL, Qwen2‑VL) emphasize synergy, while language‑centric families (e.g., Gemma) emphasize language uniqueness.  
- Layer‑wise information flow shows a consistent three‑phase pattern with synergy spiking at the final layer.  
- Visual instruction tuning (stage 2 of two‑stage training) is where multimodal synergy emerges dramatically; alignment pre‑training alone yields negligible fusion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adapted BATCH Estimator for LVLMs
- **Claim:** The adapted BATCH estimator can compute PID components from LVLM outputs without retraining, using only unimodal predictions and embeddings.  
- **Mechanism:** For each sample, vision and language embeddings (\(X_1, X_2\)) are extracted. Unimodal conditionals \(P(Y|X_1)\) and \(P(Y|X_2)\) are obtained by masking the other modality with calibrated noise drawn from the dataset‑wide mean and variance of that modality’s embeddings. The multimodal conditional \(P(Y|X_1,X_2)\) comes from a standard forward pass. Together with a softly aggregated marginal \(P(Y)\), these are fed to BATCH, which learns a joint \(\tilde{Q}\) that matches the source‑target marginals via Sinkhorn projection and optimizes an information‑theoretic objective. PID atoms are derived analytically from \(\tilde{Q}\) and the true marginals.  
- **Core assumption:** The calibrated noise approximates the true unimodal distribution while keeping embeddings in‑distribution, and BATCH converges to a joint satisfying marginal‑matching constraints.  
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract; section 3.2; corpus [109795] (provides context on language priors but not direct BATCH evidence).  
- **Break condition:** Severe distribution shift from masking or BATCH non‑convergence yields unreliable PID values.

### Mechanism 2: Two Task Regimes
- **Claim:** Datasets fall into two information‑use regimes—synergy‑driven (fusion essential) and knowledge‑driven (language priors dominate)—as shown by differential accuracy‑PID correlations.  
- **Mechanism:** For each dataset, Spearman’s \(\rho\) between accuracy and each PID component (over 26 models) is computed. On MMBench and POPE, synergy \(S\) shows strong positive correlation (\(\rho \approx 0.75\)) while language uniqueness \(U_2\) is weak; on Reefknot and PMC‑VQA, \(U_2\) is the stronger predictor (\(\rho = 0.406\) on PMC‑VQA). This indicates that on some tasks performance hinges on cross‑modal combination, whereas on others the bottleneck is internal language‑side knowledge.  
- **Core assumption:** Variation in accuracy among models is primarily driven by differences in multimodal information processing, and PID components capture these strategic differences.  
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract; section 4.1.1, Table 2, Figure 2; corpus [109795] supports prevalence of language priors.  
- **Break condition:** If correlation patterns change on a different model or dataset set, the regime classification may not be robust.

### Mechanism 3: Visual Instruction Tuning as Critical Stage
- **Claim:** Visual instruction tuning is the critical stage where multimodal synergy \(S\) emerges; alignment pre‑training alone does not generate significant fusion.  
- **Mechanism:** The two‑stage training of LLaVA‑1.5 is reproduced, with checkpoints saved at four points in each stage. PID is computed on MMBench and PMC‑VQA. During Stage 1 (projector‑only alignment), both \(S\) and \(U_2\) remain negligible. Once Stage 2 (instruction tuning of the LLM and projector) begins, \(S\) increases dramatically and continues to grow, while \(U_2\) also rises but more modestly. This demonstrates that fine‑tuning on multimodal instructions unlocks synergistic combination of modalities.  
- **Core assumption:** The LLaVA‑1.5 recipe is representative of many modern LVLM protocols, and the surge in \(S\) is causally due to the instruction‑tuning objective and data.  
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract; section 3.3.3, 4.3, Figure 5; corpus limited.  
- **Break condition:** If other LVLM families with different training recipes do not show a similar surge during a distinct second stage, the conclusion may be specific to the two‑stage paradigm.

## Foundational Learning
**Partial Information Decomposition (PID)**  
- *Why needed:* Provides the theoretical foundation for quantifying redundant, unique, and synergistic information in a three‑variable system. Without PID, we cannot separate contributions of vision vs. language or their interaction.  
- *Quick check:* What are the four non‑negative atoms in PID, and what algebraic relations among them follow from the definitions?

**Mutual Information (MI)**  
- *Why needed:* MI is the building block for PID atoms; total mutual information \(I(X_1,X_2;Y)\) equals \(R + U_1 + U_2 + S\). Understanding MI is essential to grasp what the decomposition measures.  
- *Quick check:* If \(I(X;Y)=2\) bits and redundancy \(R = 0.5\) bits, what is the maximum possible value of \(U_1+U_2+S\)?

**BATCH Estimator (Scalable PID)**  
- *Why needed:* The BATCH estimator makes it feasible to compute PID from high‑dimensional, continuous LVLM embeddings without discretization or retraining.  
- *Quick check:* What role does the Sinkhorn algorithm play in the BATCH framework?

**Layer‑wise Logit Lens**  
- *Why needed:* Applying the logit lens to each transformer block yields a layer‑specific prediction distribution, enabling tracking of information flow across depths.  
- *Quick check:* If the final layer shows a spike in synergy \(S\) while the penultimate layer has high \(U_2\), what does this pattern suggest about the reasoning process?

**Two‑Stage LVLM Training**  
- *Why needed:* The common pre‑training (alignment) + instruction‑tuning pipeline is the context in which the emergence of fusion is studied; understanding each stage’s purpose clarifies why synergy develops only later.  
- *Quick check:* What are the two stages of standard LVLM training (e.g., LLaVA), and what is trained in each?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input loader → Embedding extractor (vision encoder, text tokenizer + LLM embedding layer) → Masking module → Forward pass engine → Prediction transformer → BATCH estimator → Aggregator

### Critical Path
1. Load model and pre‑compute noise statistics (mean, variance) for each modality on the dataset.  
2. For each sample, run three forward passes (vision‑only, language‑only, multimodal) to obtain predictive distributions.  
3. Apply confidence threshold \(\tau=0.3\) and renormalise; accumulate soft predictions to estimate \(P(Y)\).  
4. Feed the distributions and raw embeddings to the trained BATCH neural networks to compute PID atoms.  
5. Average PID values across the test set.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Noise‑based masking vs. true unimodal inputs:** Noise is efficient and model‑agnostic but introduces a distributional approximation; real unimodal data is more realistic but requires separate pipelines.  
- **Discrete MC‑VQA vs. open‑ended generation:** Restricting \(Y\) to a small candidate set is necessary for PID but limits analysis to multiple‑choice; free‑form text would need clustering or projection heads, adding bias.  
- **BATCH hyperparameters:** Larger hidden dimensions or more epochs improve estimation quality but increase compute; a trade‑off exists.

### Failure Signatures
- Extremely low or zero synergy on all models even on fusion‑heavy tasks → likely bug in unimodal masking or BATCH loss.  
- High redundancy \(R\) across the board, especially when vision and language are unrelated → confidence threshold \(\tau\) may be too low, causing artificial concentration in \(P(Y)\).  
- Layer‑wise logit lens producing nonsensical distributions (e.g., uniform) for early layers → LM head may be uncalibrated for internal states; need alternative probing.  
- LLaVA training diverging or not showing \(S\) increase → check data splits, optimizer settings, or projector fine‑tuning stage.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Image‑removal intervention:** Remove the image input and recompute PID on the text‑only condition; verify that on fusion‑driven tasks, \(S\) correlates with the accuracy drop caused by image removal (expected \(\rho > 0.7\)).  
2. **Layer‑wise ablation on a new model family (e.g., InternVL3):** Apply the same logit‑lens analysis; check whether the three‑phase pattern holds; if not, investigate architectural differences.  
3. **Training‑dynamics replication with a different base LLM (e.g., Qwen2‑VL):** Follow the LLaVA two‑stage protocol but with the new backbone; examine whether visual instruction tuning still yields the largest increase in \(S\), and whether scale effects on \(U_2\) follow the same trend.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1: Extending PID to open‑ended generative settings**  
- *Basis:* The paper explicitly states PID estimation assumes a discrete target space, so fully open‑ended generation tasks are not covered.  
- *Why unresolved:* Existing PID estimators rely on a finite output set; extension would require new definitions/approximations for continuous or high‑cardinality spaces.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Development and validation of a scalable PID estimator that works with generative models (e.g., using token probability distributions or embedding‑based measures), demonstrated on open‑ended VQA or captioning benchmarks.

**Open Question 2: Causal vs. correlational interpretation of PID components**  
- *Basis:* The authors note PID is correlational; relationships to accuracy reflect associations, not full causal mechanisms.  
- *Why unresolved:* Causal claims require interventional evidence; changing one component directly causing performance improvement has not been demonstrated.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Systematic interventions on internal representations (e.g., patching or masking features associated with \(S\), \(U_1\), \(U_2\)) followed by measurement of downstream performance changes.

**Open Question 3: Using PID as a training‑time objective or diagnostic**  
- *Basis:* Inferred; analysis reveals stable family‑level strategies and scaling effects on synergy, suggesting PID captures meaningful aspects of model design.  
- *Why unresolved:* Unknown whether optimizing for a desired PID profile (e.g., higher \(S\)) yields gains or introduces trade‑offs.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Incorporate terms that directly maximize particular PID components as auxiliary losses during multimodal instruction tuning; compare resulting models to baselines on diverse benchmarks.

**Open Question 4: Designing benchmarks to isolate synergy or language priors**  
- *Basis:* Authors propose using PID‑based analyses to guide construction of benchmarks that explicitly require high synergy or isolate language priors.  
- *Why unresolved:* Many existing benchmarks show regime‑level patterns, but item‑level manipulation of required integration vs. knowledge is unclear.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Create new datasets where the need for cross‑modal integration versus language knowledge is manipulated at the item level (e.g., by varying visual ambiguity, language complexity). Perform PID analysis to validate that the benchmarks indeed elicit the intended information spectrum and correlate with targeted capabilities.

## Limitations
- The BATCH estimator’s unimodal masking may introduce a distribution shift that compromises the approximation of true unimodal conditionals.  
- PID values depend on hyperparameters such as the confidence threshold \(\tau = 0.3\) and the soft‑aggregated marginal \(P(Y)\); no ablation studies quantify their impact.  
- The two‑regime classification is based on only four datasets; its generalizability to other tasks and model families remains uncertain.

## Confidence

- **Two task regimes:** Medium
