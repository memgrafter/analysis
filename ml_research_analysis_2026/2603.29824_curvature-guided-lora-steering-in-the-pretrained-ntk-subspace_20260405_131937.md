# Curvature-Guided LoRA: Steering in the pretrained NTK subspace
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29824
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29824
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key result:** CG-LoRA improves accuracy and convergence on GLUE benchmarks while using less memory and faster initialization than LoRA-GA.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the performance gap between parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) with LoRA and full fine-tuning. Instead of aligning parameter updates, they propose to align the model outputs (predictors) of LoRA and full fine-tuning directly. This prediction alignment objective, under Lipschitz continuity of the loss and NTK linearization, yields a curvature-aware second-order formulation where optimal LoRA initialization corresponds to a Newton-like, curvature-whitened gradient. Their method, Curvature-Guided LoRA (CG-LoRA), approximates the required curvature using randomized sketching of the input and output covariances (K-FAC) without constructing large matrices.

Evaluations on GLUE benchmarks with RoBERTa-base and T5-base demonstrate CG-LoRA's effectiveness. On RoBERTa-base, CG-LoRA (no shift) achieves 80.53%±0.43 on CoLA and 82.27%±2.71 on MRPC, surpassing rsLoRA (79.16%±0.16, 78.34%±1.33), LoRA-GA (75.03%±4.18, 76.22%±3.81), and LoRA-One (79.02%±1.08, 76.71%±6.05). Further, CG-LoRA's initialization is nearly twice as fast as LoRA-GA and uses 60% less memory (509.95 MiB vs. 1.28 GiB), making it a practical and efficient improvement over existing PEFT methods.

## Method Summary
CG-LoRA initializes LoRA matrices $A_0, B_0$ using second-order curvature information from the pretrained model. It first collects layer-wise input activations $h$ and pre-activation derivatives $\delta$ on a small batch ($B_{\text{init}}=16$, accumulated to 512 samples). For each LoRA layer, it estimates the principal subspaces of the covariances $S = \mathbb{E}[hh^\top]$ and $T = \mathbb{E}[\delta\delta^\top]$ via randomized sketching (Algorithm 1). The loss gradient $\nabla_W L$ at the pretrained weights is whitened by these subspaces to form $F = D_T^{-1/2} U_T^\top (\nabla_W L) U_S D_S^{-1/2}$. The top-$r$ singular vectors of $F$ define target subspaces $L_r$ and $R_r$. A balanced realization solves a small linear system to compute $M = -\Pi_{L_r} \nabla_W L \Pi_{R_r}$; the SVD of an intermediate matrix $K$ yields the final $A_0$ and $B_0$ with scaling $\gamma=16$. The method optionally applies a shift, initializing from $W_0 + \eta B_0 A_0$ instead of $W_0$, with $\eta=2/\sqrt{r}$.

This initialization avoids explicit construction of the full NTK or Hessian, leading to significant memory and time savings. After initialization, standard LoRA fine-tuning proceeds with AdamW and a cosine learning rate schedule, following the same protocol as baseline LoRA methods.

## Key Results
- On RoBERTa-base, CG-LoRA (no shift) achieves CoLA: 80.53%±0.43 and MRPC: 82.27%±2.71, outperforming rsLoRA (79.16%±0.16, 78.34%±1.33), LoRA-GA (75.03%±4.18, 76.22%±3.81), and LoRA-One (79.02%±1.08, 76.71%±6.05).
- Initialization of CG-LoRA is nearly twice as fast as LoRA-GA while using 60% less memory (509.95 MiB vs. 1.28 GiB).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Directly aligning the predictor (model output) rather than parameter updates reduces the fine-tuning performance gap.
- **Mechanism:** Minimizes the squared norm of the difference between the full-fine-tuned predictor and the LoRA predictor at convergence; this norm upper-bounds the empirical loss gap when the loss is Lipschitz in predictions.
- **Core assumption:** The loss function is Lipschitz continuous in the model outputs; full and LoRA minimizers exist and are unique under NTK linearization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "introduce the prediction alignment problem, aiming to match the predictor obtained via PEFT to that of full fine-tuning at the level of outputs."
  - [section 3.2] Equation (3) defines the alignment objective; Remark 3.1 derives the loss upper bound from Lipschitz continuity.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Linearization Explains Fine-Tuning..." (arXiv:2602.08239) support the function-space alignment perspective.
- **Break condition:** If the loss is not Lipschitz (e.g., non-smooth), the bound fails and predictor alignment may not correlate with final loss.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Optimal LoRA initialization requires a curvature-whitened gradient, i.e., second-order information, not just the first-order gradient.
- **Mechanism:** Under NTK and K-FAC assumptions, the alignment problem reduces to placing A0⊤ in range(R_r Q_A) and B0 in range(L_r Q_B), where L_r, R_r are derived from the top-r SVD of the whitened gradient F = D_T^{-1/2} U_T^T ∇W L U_S D_S^{-1/2}; here S and T are Kronecker factors of the NTK matrix J^T J.
- **Core assumption:** The NTK regime holds (linear approximation f(W0+ΔW) ≈ f(W0)+JΔW is accurate) and the K-FAC factorization J^T J ≈ S⊗T is valid.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Definition 4.4 defines F; Proposition 4.5 shows the subspace conditions achieve the lower bound on alignment error.
  - [abstract] "curvature-aware, second-order formulation, where optimal low-rank updates correspond to a Newton-like, curvature-whitened gradient."
  - [corpus] The neighbor "LoRA Training in the NTK Regime has No Spurious Local Minima" (arXiv:2402.11867) explicitly studies LoRA under NTK, supporting this regime's relevance.
- **Break condition:** If the network is not wide enough for NTK to hold, or if J^T J deviates significantly from S⊗T (e.g., due to BatchNorm, weight sharing beyond K-FAC-reduce), the whitened gradient may misidentify important directions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CG-LoRA computes curvature-guided subspaces efficiently by avoiding explicit construction of large NTK or Hessian matrices, using randomized sketching instead.
- **Mechanism:** Algorithm 1 approximates the top singular subspaces of S and T via random projections (Ω), QR decompositions, and Rayleigh quotient iterations (power iters q). The whitened gradient F is assembled from these subspaces and the loss gradient (computed in one backward pass), then its top-r SVD yields the modes. The final A0, B0 are extracted via a balanced realization that solves a small Cholesky-based system, never forming full (d_out d_in)-size matrices.
- **Core assumption:** Randomized linear algebra with oversampling m and q power iterations accurately approximates the dominant eigenspaces of S and T.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.3] States: "none of these steps requires computing or storing any (d,d) matrix" and details the sketching procedure.
  - [Table 3] Shows CG-LoRA uses 509.95 MiB vs. LoRA-GA's 1.28 GiB and is ~2× faster, confirming memory/compute efficiency.
  - [corpus] "Randomized k-facs: Speeding up k-fac..." (International Conference on Intelligent Data Engineering and Automated Learning) shows randomized methods for K-FAC are viable.
- **Break condition:** If m is too small (e.g., m < r) or q=0 with poorly behaved S/T, the subspace estimates become noisy, degrading initialization quality. Also, extremely high-dimensional layers may require larger m to capture curvature.

## Foundational Learning
- **Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Regime**:
  - **Why needed here:** CG-LoRA's analysis hinges on the linearization f(W0+ΔW) ≈ f(W0) + JΔW to equate predictor alignment with a norm on the parameter mismatch projected onto the Jacobian's row space. Without this, the objective (3) would not simplify to a projection problem.
  - **Quick check question:** For your model layer, does the linearized prediction f(W0) + JΔW remain accurate for ΔW of the scale expected during full fine-tuning? (Test by comparing linear vs. actual forward on a small random ΔW on a few examples.)

- **Kronecker-Factored Curvature (K-FAC)**:
  - **Why needed here:** The NTK matrix J^T J is of size (d_out d_in)×(d_out d_in). CG-LoRA relies on the K-FAC factorization J^T J ≈ S⊗T (with S = avg(h h^T), T = avg(δ δ^T)) to efficiently compute the whitening transforms D_T^{-1/2} U_T^T and U_S D_S^{-1/2}. This avoids the cubic cost of full eigendecomposition.
  - **Quick check question:** On a mini-batch, compute exact J^T J (for small d_out,d_in) and compare to S⊗T. Is the relative Frobenius error acceptable (<5%)? If not, K-FAC may be a poor approximation for your layer.

- **LoRA Parameterization**:
  - **Why needed here:** CG-LoRA initializes the LoRA matrices A0, B0. Understanding that the effective update is ΔW = η(ΔB A0 + B0 ΔA) (or with shift W0+ηB0A0) is crucial to interpret the subspace constraints (A0⊤ ∈ range(R_r), B0 ∈ range(L_r)). The parameterization also introduces scaling factors (η=2/√r) and choices about shift.
  - **Quick check question:** Given A0 and B0 from CG-LoRA, does B0 A0 approximate the scaled first-step gradient direction M = -Π_{L_r} ∇W L Π_{R_r}? (Compute cosine similarity on a test batch.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - Hooks: Layer-wise capture of input activations h and pre-activation derivatives δ during forward/backward on an initial batch.
  - Subspace estimator (Algorithm 1): For each layer, computes US, Σ_S from h and UT, Σ_T from δ via randomized SVD with sketching matrix Ω, QR, and optional power iterations q.
  - Whitened gradient builder: Computes F = D_T^{-1/2} U_T^T (∇W L) U_S D_S^{-1/2} using stored ∇W L and the subspaces.
  - Top-r SVD: Gets UF,r, DF,r, VF,r → defines L_r = UT D_T^{-1/2} UF,r and R_r = US D_S^{-1/2} VF,r.
  - M reconstruction: Computes M = -Π_{L_r} ∇W L Π_{R_r} implicitly: forms JL = L_r^T L_r, JR = R_r^T R_r, solves Q⋆ = JL^{-1} DF,r JR^{-1} via Cholesky, constructs K = chol(JL)^T Q⋆ chol(JR), takes SVD K = UK DK VK^T.
  - Balanced initialization: Sets A0^T = (d_out^{1/4}/γ) ||M||_2^{1/2} VK DK^{1/2} (chol(JR))^{-T} and B0 = (d_out^{1/4}/γ) ||M||_2^{1/2} UK DK^{1/2} (chol(JL))^{-T}.
  - Scaling: Global factor γ=16 (default) to match LoRA-GA's stability.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Forward pass with batch size B_init (e.g., 16) to collect h^{(k)} for all LoRA layers k.
  2. Backward pass to collect δ^{(k)} and ∇W L^{(k)} for the same layers.
  3. For each LoRA layer:
     a. If tokenized inputs, pool h and δ over sequence length Ω.
     b. Run SUBSPACE(h, δ, m, q) → (US, Σ_S, UT, Σ_T).
     c. Assemble F (requires only vectorized ∇W L, not full matrix).
     d. Compute top-r SVD of F → UF,r, DF,r, VF,r.
     e. Form L_r, R_r and compute Cholesky of JL, JR.
     f. Solve for Q⋆ via two triangular solves, form K, SVD it.
     g. Extract A0, B0 with scaling; store and immediately delete intermediate tensors for this layer.
  4. After all layers, proceed to LoRA fine-tuning with the initialized (A0, B0).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Oversampling m: Controls accuracy of S, T estimation. Larger m → better approximation but O(d_in m) and O(d_out m) costs per layer. Typical m = r (tight) up to 4r (safe).
  - Power iterations q: q=1 (default) usually suffices; q=0 risks poor subspace if S/T are not exactly rank-r. Each q adds one extra backward-like matrix-vector product (Algorithm 1 line 7-8).
  - Shift vs. no-shift: CG-LoRA (no shift) initializes LoRA from W0; CG-LoRA (shift) starts from W0+ηB0A0. No-shift more robust to noisy pretrained gradients (observed on RoBERTa), but shift can give a head start when gradients are clean (e.g., T5). Controlled by a flag.
  - γ scaling: Balances initialization magnitude; γ=16 from LoRA-GA works well. Smaller γ → larger ∥A0∥, ∥B0∥ (faster initial step but risk instability).

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance across random seeds: Likely unstable subspace estimation → increase m or q, or ensure B_init is large enough for reliable gradient statistics.
  - Cholesky decomposition fails (non-positive-definite JL or JR): This indicates L_r or R_r has near-zero singular values beyond rank r. Remedies: increase r slightly, or add small ridge (εI) to JL, JR before Cholesky.
  - Initialization worse than random (LoRA baseline): Check that F's top-r singular values capture sufficient variance (>90% cumulative). If not, K-FAC assumption may be violated; consider fallback to LoRA-GA (SVD of gradient) for that layer.
  - Out-of-memory during initialization: Ensure per-layer cleanup; avoid storing full ∇W L across all layers simultaneously. Use gradient checkpointing if needed.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Curvature approximation validation**: For a representative layer, compute exact J^T J on a small batch (d_out,d_in < 256) and compare to K-FAC S⊗T. Report relative error to certify Assumption 4.3 holds for your architecture.
  2. **Whitened gradient spectrum**: Compute singular values of F on a batch. Plot cumulative squared singular values. Confirm that r chosen covers the bulk of the spectrum (e.g., >80%). If not, adjust r upward.
  3. **Baseline replication and ablation**: Reproduce Table 1 for RoBERTa-base on CoLA with fixed seeds (use provided hyperparams). Confirm CG-LoRA (no shift) beats rsLoRA/LoRA-GA/LoRA-One. Then systematically vary m∈{r,2r,4r} and q∈{0,1} to see impact on accuracy and initialization time; expect stable improvement up to m=2r.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does CG-LoRA perform on large language models (e.g., decoder-only architectures) for text generation tasks compared to full fine-tuning and other PEFT methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Conclusion states: "As future work, we plan to conduct more extensive experiments across a wider range of models and tasks, including large language models for language generation..."
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments are limited to encoder-only (RoBERTa) and encoder-decoder (T5) models on GLUE NLU tasks. No evaluation on generative tasks or scaling to larger models (e.g., LLaMA, GPT) is provided.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark results on generation tasks (e.g., summarization, translation) using larger pre-trained models, reporting metrics like BLEU or ROUGE alongside convergence speed and memory usage.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions (e.g., model depth, width, data distribution) does the curvature-guided initialization remain effective when the NTK linearization assumption is violated?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper derives CG-LoRA in the NTK regime (Sections 3.1 and 4) but acknowledges in the Conclusion that "our method demonstrates strong empirical performance in practical settings, highlighting the importance of second-order information... beyond the NTK regime." The theoretical justification beyond NTK is not provided.
- **Why unresolved:** The derivation relies on the NTK linearization (Eq. 3), which may not hold for deep, fine-tuned models. The empirical success is shown but not explained theoretically.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis extending the prediction alignment objective to non-NTK regimes (e.g., using probabilistic robustness bounds) or systematic experiments measuring NTK alignment (e.g., kernel eigenvalue spectra) vs. performance across model depths and datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How should the rank $r$ and scaling factor $\gamma$ be chosen optimally for different tasks and model layers, and what is the theoretical trade-off between parameter efficiency and alignment error?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method uses fixed $r=8$ and $\gamma=16$ in experiments (Section 6.1), but no analysis of sensitivity or optimal selection is provided. Theorem 5.3 gives an alignment error bound dependent on the singular values of $F$, which themselves depend on $r$.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not study how $r$ and $\gamma$ affect the lower bound $\left(\sum_{i\geq r+1} s_i^2(F)\right)^{1/2}$ or practical performance. The choice of $r$ is known to be task-dependent, but no guidance is given.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies varying $r$ and $\gamma$ across multiple datasets, plotting alignment error (e.g., prediction norm) vs. parameter count; theoretical characterization of how the bound in Proposition 4.5 degrades with $r$.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the impact of the sketching parameters (oversampling factor $m$, power iterations $q$) on the accuracy of the curvature subspaces and the resulting fine-tuning performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Algorithm 1 uses randomized sketching with $m$ and $q$ to approximate $U_S, \Sigma_S, U_T, \Sigma_T$. The paper sets $m=r$, $q=1$ for efficiency (Section 5.3, Table 3) but does not evaluate higher-fidelity approximations.
- **Why unresolved:** The quality of the K-FAC basis depends on the accuracy of the estimated singular subspaces. Using small $m$ and $q$ may introduce noise that harms the whitened gradient $F$, but this trade-off is not measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments increasing $m$ and $q$, measuring subspace approximation error (e.g., $\|S - \hat{S}\|_F$) and correlating with prediction alignment error and final task accuracy.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How robust is the K-FAC curvature approximation (Assumption 4.3) across different transformer architectures (e.g., with weight sharing, layer norms), and does its violation degrade CG-LoRA's gains?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method relies critically on the K-FAC assumption to factorize the Hessian as $S \otimes T$. The paper applies a variant (K-FAC-reduce) for weight sharing but does not validate the approximation or compare with alternative curvature estimates (e.g., exact diagonal, empirical Fisher).
- **Why unresolved:** The approximation may be poor for transformers with complex interactions (e.g., attention). The success of CG-LoRA might be despite K-FAC inaccuracies, not because of them.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare CG-LoRA initialized with K-FAC vs. other curvature estimates (e.g., using exponented moving averages of gradients) on the same tasks; measure the discrepancy between $J^\top J$ and $S \otimes T$ and correlate with performance drop.

## Limitations
- K-FAC approximation accuracy varies across layer types and may degrade with small batch sizes.
- Unclear whether main results used shift (W0+ηB0A0) or not, affecting reproducibility.
- NTK regime assumption may not hold for all models; theoretical justification limited to wide networks.

## Confidence
- **High:** Prediction alignment objective and Lipschitz-bound derivation; empirical memory/time savings vs. LoRA-GA.
- **Medium:** Second-order curvature-whitened gradient formulation (depends on NTK/K-FAC validity).
- **Low:** Generalization to larger ranks, deeper models, or non-transformer architectures.
