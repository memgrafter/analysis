# UpSkill: Mutual Information Skill Learning for Structured Response Diversity in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22296  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22296  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** UpSkill improves multi-attempt accuracy (pass@k) by ~3% on GSM8K for Llama 3.1-8B and Qwen 2.5-7B without hurting single-attempt correctness; up to 7% gains with combined rewards.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) often optimizes for single-attempt accuracy, which can suppress response diversity and degrade multi-attempt metrics like pass@k. UpSkill introduces a discrete latent skill variable and a token-level mutual information (MI) reward within the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework. By encouraging the model to produce diverse, strategy-specific trajectories under different skills, UpSkill effectively increases the number of independent attempts. On GSM8K, it yields mean gains of ~3% in pass@k for Llama 3.1-8B and Qwen 2.5-7B without reducing pass@1, with some configurations reaching up to 7% improvement when MI is combined with correctness rewards. In a toy arithmetic setting, UpSkill improves pass@5 by 10% and produces clearly distinct operator distributions across skills. The method also demonstrates that MI reward alone can boost pass@k, and a theoretical bound links the improvement to the mutual information objective. Results are positive for two of three tested open-weight models, indicating variability across model families.

## Method Summary
UpSkill addresses the diversity-accuracy trade-off in RLVR for reasoning tasks. It introduces a latent skill variable z ∈ {1,…,N} (default N=5) prepended to each prompt (e.g., “Strategy [z] |”). During training with GRPO, for each question x a random skill z is sampled, and C completions are generated from the policy π_θ(·|x,z). Each completion τ receives a composite reward:  

r(τ;x,z) = r_corr(τ) - β ΔKL(τ) + α₁ r_TMI(τ;x,z).  

The correctness reward r_corr is a binary verifier (e.g., exact match on GSM8K). The token-level MI reward r_TMI(τ;x,z) = Σ_t [log p(y_t|x,z,y<t) - log p(y_t|x,y<t>)] where p(y_t|x) is the uniform mixture over skills (1/N) Σ_{z'} p(y_t|x,z',y<t>). ΔKL is the KL divergence from a frozen reference policy. The rewards are grouped by (x,z) and normalized to compute advantages for GRPO updates, typically using LoRA adapters. At inference, the model samples k distinct skills and generates one completion per skill, aggregating results to compute pass@k.

## Key Results
- UpSkill yields mean gains of ~3% in pass@k on GSM8K for Llama 3.1-8B and Qwen 2.5-7B without degrading pass@1; combining MI with correctness rewards can achieve up to 7% improvement.
- In a controlled arithmetic environment, pass@5 improves by 10% and operator distributions become skill-specific, confirming the diversity effect.
- MI reward alone (without correctness) boosts pass@k, demonstrating that mutual information objectives can uncover latent solution diversity. Results hold for two of three open-weight models, with benefits varying across model families.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
UpSkill improves multi-attempt metrics through three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Token-level MI reward induces skill-specific trajectories**. The reward r_TMI(τ;x,z) encourages each skill z to produce token sequences that deviate from the uniform mixture over all skills. Maximizing this difference reduces conditional entropy H(τ|x,z) while maintaining marginal entropy H(τ|x), yielding distinct behavioral modes for each z. Evidence: Section 3.2 formally defines r_TMI and its Monte Carlo estimation of I(τ;z|x); related work on mutual information skill learning supports the principle.

2. **Diverse strategies reduce correlation between attempts**. Each skill z corresponds to a reproducible reasoning path (e.g., different arithmetic operators). Sampling distinct skills at inference generates less correlated attempts, increasing the effective number of independent trials: k_eff ≈ k/(1+(k-1)ρ). By lowering correlation ρ, UpSkill boosts pass@k even if per-attempt accuracy stays constant. Evidence: Section 2.1 derives the correlation effect; Lemma 1 shows pass@k improvement is bounded below by a function of I(τ;z|x).

3. **MI reward alone can increase pass@k**. Maximizing I(τ;z|x) pushes the policy to explore distinct modes under different z, covering a broader set of candidate solutions. Even if individual strategies are not correctness-optimized, the union of their outputs contains more correct answers on average. Evidence: Section 5.3 reports MI-only fine-tuning yields Δpass@k = +5.2% (Qwen) and +0.8% (Llama).

The approach fails if the latent z cannot control the output (p(τ|x,z) ≈ p(τ|x)), causing mutual information to remain near zero and no diversity to emerge.

## Foundational Learning
- **Token-level MDP formulation**: Needed to apply reinforcement learning to autoregressive LLMs, treating each token as an action in a Markov decision process. Quick check: How does token-level MDP differ from episode-level RL for text generation? It allows per-token rewards and tighter credit assignment.
- **Mutual information for skill discovery**: Needed to quantify how much a trajectory reveals the latent skill. Maximizing I(τ;z|x) encourages low conditional entropy (skill-specificity) while keeping marginal entropy high (coverage). Quick check: Why not simply maximize output entropy H(τ|x) to encourage diversity? That would not guarantee skill-specific behaviors; MI ensures distinct modes across skills.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: Needed as a stable RL algorithm for LLMs, using within-group baselines to reduce variance. Quick check: How does GRPO differ from PPO? It uses a relative baseline computed from a group of completions for the same (x,z), which stabilizes advantage estimates for reasoning tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Latent skill z → Policy π_θ generates τ → Token-level MI estimator (computes r_TMI) → Correctness verifier (r_corr) → KL penalty (ΔKL) → Combined reward → GRPO updater → Policy update.

**Critical path** (per training step):
1. Sample minibatch of questions {x}.
2. For each x, sample a skill z ~ Uniform(1..N), generate C completions τ_i ~ π_θ(·|x,z).
3. Compute r_TMI(τ_i;x,z) by contrasting log p(y_t|x,z,y<t) against mixture log p(y_t|x,y<t>).
4. Compute r_corr(τ_i) via answer extraction.
5. Compute ΔKL(τ_i) = KL(π_θ(·|x,z) || π_ref(·|x)).
6. Form total reward r_i = r_corr_i - β ΔKL_i + α₁ r_TMI_i.
7. Normalize r_i within the group of C completions to obtain advantages A_i.
8. Update π_θ using GRPO's clipped surrogate loss while keeping reference policy fixed.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- α₁ (MI coefficient): Higher values force stronger specialization but risk degenerate strategies. Typical range: 0.1-5 depending on task.
- β (KL penalty): Stabilizes training; positive β aligns with theory but may hinder exploration. Start with 0.05-0.1.
- N (number of skills): Should be ≤ intended inference-time attempts k; too large may fragment capacity (N=5 works for GSM8K).
- C (completions per group): Affects gradient variance; C=5-6 recommended.

**Failure signatures**:
- MI reward spikes then collapses: model finds trivial z-dependent patterns (e.g., repetition) that maximize MI but destroy correctness.
- Model-specific sensitivity: Smaller or heavily fine-tuned models (e.g., R1-Distilled) degrade unless β>0.
- pass@1 drop indicates over-diversification; monitor r_corr.
- No increase in r_TMI suggests latent z has no influence; verify prompt formatting and N.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Arithmetic toy environment**: Confirm MI prevents entropy collapse and yields operator-specialized skills (check per-z operator distributions).
2. **GSM8K with Qwen 2.5-7B**: Expect ~+3% pass@k gain without pass@1 loss.
3. **MI-only ablation**: Train with α₁>0 and no r_corr; verify pass@k improvement. If unstable, add KL penalty.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why does UpSkill’s efficacy vary across model families?** The paper observes gains for Llama/Qwen but decline for R1-Distilled. The cause is speculative (e.g., smaller capacity, heavier prior optimization) and requires systematic ablation.
2. **Can the theoretical bounds be strengthened?** The current bound linking I(τ;z|x) to pass@k assumes a “k-uniform mixture model” that is only softly enforced. Tighter, more practical guarantees are lacking, especially for non-uniform skill distributions.
3. **What causes training instability and strategy collapse?** Models sometimes learn degenerate modes (e.g., infinite repetition) to maximize MI. The root cause is not diagnosed, and reliable prevention methods are unknown.
4. **Why does token-level MI often fail to encourage semantic diversity?** Token-level differences tend to reflect formatting rather than distinct strategies. A semantic MI objective (e.g., based on embeddings) showed promise but was unstable due to estimator variance.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter reporting is ambiguous: best results come from many runs with post-hoc filtering, obscuring expected gains.
- Implementation details (e.g., exact MI computation, answer extraction) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- The method’s benefits do not transfer to all models; R1-Distilled-Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B showed degradation, indicating limited robustness.
- The core theoretical assumption of a uniform mixture over skills is not empirically validated and may be violated in practice.
- Evaluation is confined to GSM8K and a toy arithmetic task; generalizability to other domains is untested.

## Confidence
- MI induces distinct, skill-specific trajectories: **Medium**
- Diversity reduces correlation and improves pass@k: **Medium**
- MI reward alone can boost pass@k: **Medium**
- Overall ~3% gain on GSM8K without harming pass@1: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Direct correlation measurement**: For a test set, sample multiple z per problem and compute pairwise success agreement. A significant decrease compared to the base model would confirm reduced correlation ρ.
2. **Uniform mixture assumption test**: For diverse prompts, compute marginal token distribution p(y_t|x) and compare it to the average over skills (1/N) Σ_z p(y_t|x,z) using KL divergence. Low divergence would validate the assumption.
3. **Comprehensive hyperparameter sweep**: Exhaustively vary α₁, β, C, N on Llama 3.1-8B and Qwen 2.5-7B with multiple seeds. Report mean and variance of pass@k to assess typical effect size and identify stable configurations.
