# Beyond Outliers: A Data-Free Layer-wise Mixed-Precision Quantization Approach Driven by Numerical and Structural Dual-Sensitivity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17354  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17354  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: NSDS, a calibration-free layer-wise mixed-precision quantization framework, consistently outperforms state-of-the-art calibration-free and calibration-based baselines on Llama and Qwen models (7B–14B) across reasoning and language modeling tasks at low average bit budgets (e.g., 3-bit).

## Executive Summary
Layer-wise mixed-precision quantization (LMPQ) traditionally treats all weight modules within a transformer layer uniformly and relies on single numerical metrics for sensitivity estimation. This work introduces NSDS, a calibration-free LMPQ framework that addresses two key limitations: operational heterogeneity and incomplete sensitivity characterization. NSDS decomposes each layer into functional roles—Detectors (e.g., QK, input projections) and Writers (e.g., OV, output projections)—and evaluates sensitivity from two complementary perspectives: Numerical Vulnerability (NV), measured via excess kurtosis to capture outlier-driven instability, and Structural Expressiveness (SE), measured via SVD with role-aware reweighting to capture spectral capacity and functional alignment. Dual scores are normalized using a robust MAD‑Sigmoid and aggregated via a Soft‑OR operation to produce a per-layer ranking, which guides bit allocation (e.g., 4‑bit for sensitive layers, 2‑bit otherwise) without any calibration data. Evaluations on Llama and Qwen models (7B–14B) show NSDS achieves superior accuracy and perplexity across reasoning and language modeling benchmarks compared to both calibration-free (e.g., KurtBoost, ZD) and calibration-based (e.g., LIM, LLM‑MQ) methods, demonstrating robustness across bit budgets and model scales.

## Method Summary
NSDS operates in four stages. First, it decomposes each transformer layer’s weights into Detectors (W_QK, W_in, and W_gate if present) and Writers (W_OV, W_out). Second, for each component it computes two sensitivity scores: NV, the excess kurtosis of the flattened weight matrix, identifies numerical vulnerability to quantization; SE, derived from SVD (truncated to top‑90% variance), multiplies the sum of singular values by exponential spectral entropy, then reweights using a role‑specific factor (β_DS = kurtosis of left singular vectors for detectors; β_WD = L1 projection onto the unembedding matrix for writers). Third, for each component type (detector, writer) across all layers, raw NV and SE scores are normalized via MAD‑Sigmoid (robust scaling followed by sigmoid) to yield comparable probabilities. Per‑layer scores are obtained by applying Soft‑OR (fuzzy maximum) over the component probabilities for NV and separately for SE, then combining these two with Soft‑OR. Finally, given an average bit budget (e.g., 3‑bit), the number of 4‑bit layers is computed; layers are sorted by the final NSDS score and the top‑ranked receive 4‑bit precision while the rest receive 2‑bit, using a calibration‑free quantizer like HQQ or GPTQ.

## Key Results
- At a 3‑bit average budget on Qwen2.5‑7B, NSDS achieves +2.26% ARC‑C accuracy over the calibration‑free ZD baseline and –0.62 WikiText‑2 perplexity versus KurtBoost.
- NSDS consistently outperforms both calibration‑free (MSE, EWQ, ZD, KurtBoost) and calibration‑based (LIM, LSAQ, LLM‑MQ, LieQ) baselines across Llama‑3.1‑8B and Qwen2.5‑7B on reasoning (ARC‑C, HellaSwag, PIQA) and language modeling (WikiText‑2, C4) tasks.
- The method shows strong scalability on larger models (Llama‑2‑13B, Qwen2.5‑14B) and remains effective across a range of bit budgets without requiring calibration data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Role‑Aware Decomposition Captures Heterogeneous Sensitivity
- **Claim**: Splitting layers into Detectors and Writers exposes quantization sensitivity patterns missed by uniform treatment.
- **Mechanism**: In a transformer, QK and input projections (plus gate) act as pattern Detectors, while OV and output projections act as Writers that modify the residual stream. These roles imply different error propagation: quantizing detectors may distort attention patterns; quantizing writers may corrupt information injection.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] explicitly mentions decomposition; [section 2.1] details roles and equations; neighboring works like KVTuner do not incorporate such fine‑grained role distinctions.
- **Break condition**: If detectors and writers exhibit no statistically significant difference in quantization sensitivity, the decomposition provides no benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Dual Metrics Combine Numerical and Structural Views
- **Claim**: Numerical Vulnerability (outlier‑based) and Structural Expressiveness (SVD‑based with role‑aware reweighting) together provide a more complete sensitivity measure than either alone.
- **Mechanism**: NV uses excess kurtosis to detect heavy‑tailed weight distributions prone to clipping error. SE uses SVD to extract spectral capacity (sum of singular values) and entropy, then reweights by a role‑specific factor: for detectors, β_DS captures input sharpness via kurtosis of left singular vectors; for writers, β_WD captures output influence via L1 projection onto the unembedding matrix.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] highlights dual perspectives; [section 2.2] defines NV/SE and reweighting; KurtBoost (kurtosis only) and LieQ (representational compactness) are outperformed, suggesting complementarity.
- **Break condition**: High correlation between NV and SE would make the dual approach redundant; if role‑aware reweighting does not improve correlation with actual performance degradation, it may be ineffective.

### Mechanism 3: Robust Normalization and Soft‑OR Aggregation Ensure Reliable Layer Scores
- **Claim**: MAD‑Sigmoid normalization and Soft‑OR aggregation are essential to combine heterogeneous component scores into a valid ranking.
- **Mechanism**: For each component type, raw scores across layers are scaled by median absolute deviation (MAD) to achieve robustness, then passed through a sigmoid to map into (0,1). For a given layer, Soft‑OR (1 minus product of complements) over its component probabilities yields a fuzzy maximum for NV and similarly for SE; a final Soft‑OR combines these two. This prevents any single scale‑mismatched component from dominating and emphasizes the most sensitive part.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] mentions the normalization and aggregation; [section 2.3] provides equations and rationale; ablations (Fig. 4, 8) show large drops when these are removed.
- **Break condition**: If component scores are already comparable or a simpler max/mean works equally, the added complexity may be unnecessary.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Layer‑wise Mixed‑Precision Quantization (LMPQ)  
  - **Why needed here**: NSDS allocates bits per layer; understanding why uniform low‑bit quantization fails and how mixed precision mitigates layer‑specific error is foundational.  
  - **Quick check question**: Why might assigning 3‑bit uniformly to all layers cause more accuracy loss than a mixed‑precision scheme with the same average bit width?
- **Concept**: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)  
  - **Why needed here**: SE relies on SVD to compute base expressiveness (spectral magnitude and entropy) and to derive role‑specific reweighting factors.  
  - **Quick check question**: How does the product of singular‑value sum and exponential entropy reflect a weight matrix’s expressive capacity?
- **Concept**: Kurtosis  
  - **Why needed here**: NV uses excess kurtosis to detect heavy‑tailed weight distributions that are more sensitive to quantization clipping.  
  - **Quick check question**: Why would a weight matrix with high kurtosis (heavy tails) experience larger quantization error than a light‑tailed matrix with the same variance?
- **Concept**: Soft‑OR Aggregation  
  - **Why needed here**: NSDS combines per‑component sensitivity probabilities via a fuzzy maximum to avoid averaging across heterogeneous scales.  
  - **Quick check question**: How does Soft‑OR differ from the arithmetic mean, and why is a “fuzzy maximum” appropriate for sensitivity scoring?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Decomposer → NV estimator / SE estimator → Normalizer → Aggregator → Bit allocator  
  (Per component: Detector/Writer types processed independently then merged)
- **Critical path**: Model weights → decomposition into Detectors/Writers → per‑component NV/SE calculation → MAD‑Sigmoid normalization → layer‑wise Soft‑OR aggregation → final NSDS score ranking → bit assignment (4‑bit vs 2‑bit) → quantization via backend (HQQ/GPTQ).
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - **Calibration‑free** avoids data bias but ignores activation‑specific effects.  
  - **Role‑aware reweighting** adds SVD overhead but aligns metrics with functional roles.  
  - **MAD‑Sigmoid** is robust to score outliers but may under‑weight if score distributions are narrow.  
  - **Soft‑OR** emphasizes the most sensitive component but may be noisy; the framework is backend‑agnostic.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Incorrect role assignment (e.g., treating gate as writer).  
  - Over‑aggressive SVD truncation (top‑90% threshold) discarding critical low‑variance structure.  
  - Negative kurtosis yields β_DS = 0 after ReLU + log, zeroing detector SE.  
  - Zero MAD in normalization leads to degenerate scores; Soft‑OR saturation across many components may require geometric mean adjustment.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Main evaluation**: Compare NSDS against calibration‑free (MSE, EWQ, ZD, KurtBoost) and calibration‑based (LIM, LSAQ, LLM‑MQ, LieQ) baselines on Llama‑3.1‑8B and Qwen2.5‑7B at 3‑bit average; NSDS yields consistent gains in reasoning accuracy and perplexity.  
  2. **Ablation study**: Remove SE, NV, reweighting (β_DS/β_WD), and aggregation (MAD‑Sigmoid + Soft‑OR) to quantify contributions; full combination is essential, with aggregation having the largest impact.  
  3. **Scalability & orthogonality**: Test on larger models (Llama‑2‑13B, Qwen2.5‑14B); NSDS remains top‑performing against calibration‑based methods. Integration with GPTQ backend matches or exceeds SliM‑LLM, showing backend independence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does NSDS perform on LLMs beyond 14B (e.g., 30B, 70B), and do the identified sensitivity patterns remain consistent at such larger scales?**  
  *Basis*: The Limitations section explicitly notes experiments are limited to 7B–14B models due to computational constraints, and sensitivity patterns may differ for larger models. *Why unresolved*: No empirical evidence exists for much larger scales; internal representations could change significantly. *Resolution evidence*: Comprehensive experiments on 30B/70B models, correlation analysis of NSDS scores vs. quantization degradation across scales.
- **Is the Detector/Writer decomposition universally valid across all transformer‑based LLM architectures, or are there uncaught operational roles in alternative designs (e.g., MoE, different FFN structures)?**  
  *Basis*: Inferred—the decomposition is based on standard transformer layers and only evaluated on Llama/Qwen; architectures with MoE or custom norms might not fit. *Why unresolved*: Unexamined architectures may have hybrid or distinct roles that NSDS’s current role definitions misestimate. *Resolution evidence*: Apply NSDS to diverse families (Mixtral, Gemma, Phi‑3); analyze if current role scores correlate with performance; identify missing roles if needed.
- **Are the role‑aware reweighting factors β_DS (kurtosis of input singular vectors) and β_WD (L1 projection onto unembedding) optimal, or could alternative metrics improve sensitivity estimation?**  
  *Basis*: Inferred—the paper introduces these heuristics with theoretical motivation but no comparison to alternatives. *Why unresolved*: Their effectiveness is assumed but not validated against other interpretability‑based metrics. *Resolution evidence*: Extensive ablation with varied reweighting strategies (e.g., spectral entropy for detectors, gradient‑based saliency for writers); correlate each variant’s scores with actual quantization degradation.
- **Can NSDS be extended to joint weight and activation quantization in a calibration‑free manner, and how does this affect the accuracy‑efficiency trade‑off?**  
  *Basis*: Inferred—the paper focuses on weight‑only quantization; activation sensitivity likely differs and traditionally needs calibration. *Why unresolved*: Activation data‑free metrics are non‑trivial; extending NSDS may break its calibration‑free advantage. *Resolution evidence*: Develop weight‑derived activation proxies; implement a unified NSDS variant; compare weight‑only vs. joint quantization on benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Implementation details (SVD truncation stability, handling zero MAD, WU alignment) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Role assignment may not generalize to non‑standard architectures (e.g., shared QKV, alternative gates, MoE experts).
- Data‑free design ignores activation distributions, potentially missing error propagation effects.
- Scalability of SVD overhead for very large layers is not quantified.

## Confidence
- **Decomposition benefit**: High (ablation shows large drop without role‑aware reweighting).
- **Dual metrics complementarity**: Medium (each helps individually, but correlation and redundancy are not fully analyzed).
- **Aggregation necessity**: Medium (ablation supports improvement, but alternatives like geometric mean are not thoroughly tested).
- **Overall superiority**: High (consistent gains across multiple models and diverse baselines).
