# Towards Empowering Consumers through Sentence-level Readability Scoring in German ESG Reports

## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2603.29861
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29861
*   Reference count: 0
*   Primary result: A finetuned XLM-RoBERTa base model achieves the lowest prediction error (MSE 0.0295) for sentence-level readability scoring in German ESG reports.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the challenge of making German ESG reports more accessible to non-expert consumers by developing sentence-level readability scoring models. The authors extended an existing dataset with new crowd-sourced readability annotations and systematically evaluated a range of approaches, including traditional syntactic features, classic readability formulas, fine-tuned transformers (XLM-RoBERTa), and large language model (LLM) prompting. Results indicate notable subjectivity in human ratings, with native speakers often deeming sentences readable. Among automated methods, a fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa model delivered the lowest mean squared error, while the Qwen LLM demonstrated the strongest ability to rank sentences by difficulty (Kendall's τ). Model ensembles offer a marginal accuracy gain but with increased computational cost.

## Method Summary
The research methodology centered on extending a prior German ESG readability dataset via crowd-sourcing to obtain continuous readability scores for individual sentences. This new annotated corpus was used to train and evaluate several predictive modeling approaches. These included feature-based models using hand-crafted syntactic metrics, traditional readability indices (e.g., Flesch Reading Ease), the fine-tuning of a pre-trained multilingual transformer (XLM-RoBERTa) on the domain-specific task, and few-shot prompting of a large language model (Qwen). Performance was primarily measured using Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for absolute score prediction, and Kendall's τ for ranking capability.

## Key Results
*   The fine-tuned **XLM-RoBERTa base model** achieved the best absolute error performance (MSE: 0.0295, MAE: 0.1114).
*   The **Qwen-7B-Chat LLM**, via prompting, attained the highest rank correlation with human judgments (Kendall's τ: 0.2822).
*   A simple **ensemble combining syntax features and XLM-base predictions** slightly reduced error (MSE: 0.0264) but increased inference latency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Fine-tuning a pre-trained multilingual model like XLM-RoBERTa on the specific German ESG corpus works effectively because it leverages broad linguistic knowledge from its pre-training while adapting to the domain's unique vocabulary, syntactic structures, and semantic patterns. This allows it to capture subtle features influencing readability beyond what hand-crafted syntactic metrics can express. The Qwen LLM's strong ranking performance suggests that large-scale parameter knowledge and in-context learning can effectively model the relative ease of sentences, likely by assessing deeper semantic coherence and complexity, even without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Raw German ESG Sentences -> (Annotation Layer: Crowd-sourced Score Collection) -> Annotated Dataset -> (Modeling Branch A: Hand-crafted Feature Extraction) -> Syntax/Formala Model -> Evaluation
Annotated Dataset -> (Modeling Branch B: Transformer Fine-Tuning) -> XLM-RoBERTa -> (Optional: Ensemble with Branch A) -> Evaluation
Annotated Dataset -> (Modeling Branch C: LLM Prompting) -> Qwen (via API) -> Evaluation

**Critical Path:** The central path is `Annotated Dataset -> XLM-RoBERTa Fine-Tuning -> Evaluation`. This represents the highest-performing single-model pipeline and the core contribution of the work.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Accuracy vs. Inference Speed:** The ensemble (syntax + XLM) reduces error marginally but adds significant computation versus a single XLM-RoBERTa model.
2.  **Development Cost vs. Performance:** Fine-tuning requires dataset curation and compute resources, while LLM prompting is API-cost dependent but needs no training.
3.  **Interpretability vs. Performance:** Feature-based models offer transparent insights (e.g., "long sentences are hard") but are hand-crafted and underperform neural models.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **High Rater Disagreement:** A low inter-annotator agreement (IAA) score (e.g., Krippendorff's α < 0.6) would indicate the gold standard labels are noisy, limiting any model's upper bound.
*   **Poor Cross-Domain Rank Correlation:** A model with good MSE but low Kendall's τ predicts absolute scores poorly aligned with human ordinal judgments of ease.
*   **Catastrophic Forgetting in Fine-Tuning:** If XLM-RoBERTa's performance degrades on standard German NLP benchmarks after fine-tuning, its linguistic knowledge may have been overwritten.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Reproduce Annotation Consistency:** Compute the inter-annotator agreement (IAA) metric on the crowd-sourced dataset to quantify the inherent subjectivity and label noise.
2.  **Establish Neural Baseline:** Fine-tune XLM-RoBERTa on the provided training split, report validation MSE/MAE and Kendall's τ against the test set to validate the claimed results.
3.  **Prompting Sensitivity Analysis:** For the Qwen LLM, systematically vary the prompt template, temperature, and number of few-shot examples to measure their impact on Kendall's τ and understand the prompting sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions; however, its identified limitations and suggested next checks imply several open questions for future research:
*   What is the inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Krippendorff's α) on the crowd-sourced dataset, and how does label noise affect model performance ceilings?
*   How do the best-performing models (XLM-RoBERTa, Qwen) generalize to other languages (English, French) and domains (e.g., legal, medical texts) beyond German ESG reports?
*   What is the practical inference speed-accuracy trade-off when deploying the ensemble versus the single XLM-RoBERTa model on real-world hardware?
*   How would other large language models (e.g., GPT-4, Llama) compare to Qwen for readability ranking under similar prompting conditions?
*   What specific prompt engineering choices (e.g., template, temperature, few-shot examples) most influence Qwen's ranking performance, and how robust is it to these variations?

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Quality & Size:** The extended crowd-sourced annotations lack reported inter-annotator agreement (IAA) and quality controls, and the corpus size/sentence distribution are unspecified, risking unreliable benchmarks.
*   **LLM Evaluation Depth:** The Qwen prompting setup (prompt design, hyperparameters) is sparsely detailed, and no comparison to other major LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Llama) is provided.
*   **Generalizability:** Findings are confined to German ESG reports; transferability to other languages, domains (e.g., legal, medical), or non-corporate texts is untested.

## Confidence
*   **XLM-RoBERTa achieves lowest MSE (0.0295):** High – Clear numerical result from a standard evaluation protocol.
*   **Qwen achieves best Kendall's τ (0.2822):** Medium – Metric is provided but without baseline comparisons or significance testing against other LLMs.
*   **Ensemble reduces MSE to 0.0264:** High – Direct outcome of a stated combination experiment.
*   **Human rating exhibits subjectivity:** High – Explicitly noted as a observed characteristic in the study.
*   **Generalizability to other languages/domains:** Low – No empirical cross-lingual or cross-domain validation is presented.

## Next Checks
1.  **Quantify Annotation Noise:** Compute inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Krippendorff's α, intra-class correlation) on the crowd-sourced dataset to establish the reliability ceiling for models.
2.  **Test Cross-Domain & Cross-Lingual Transfer:** Evaluate the best-performing XLM-RoBERTa and Qwen models on English and French ESG reports, and on a non-ESG technical text corpus (e.g., scientific abstracts), to probe robustness.
3.  **Benchmark Inference Latency:** Measure wall-clock inference time per sentence for the single XLM-RoBERTa model versus the syntax+XLM ensemble on standardized hardware to quantify the practical speed-accuracy trade-off.
