# A Syllogistic Probe: Tracing the Evolution of Logic Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17426
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17426
- **Reference count**: 26
- **Primary result**: LLMs shift from Aristotelian to Boolean logic with model scaling and RL-trained reasoning, but show systematic failures on empty minor terms, mood-specific biases, and cross-lingual gaps.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) rely on Aristotelian logic, which assumes existential import for universal statements, or modern (Boolean) logic, which does not. The authors construct a comprehensive dataset of 9,600 syllogisms in Chinese and English, systematically varying whether the minor term has non-empty or empty reference. By evaluating dozens of pretrained and post‑trained models, they find that model scaling, reinforcement‑learning‑based “thinking” training, and the base model’s inductive bias all accelerate the transition to Boolean reasoning. However, persistent difficulties with empty‑term cases, certain mood forms, and cross‑lingual inconsistencies reveal limits in current LLMs’ logical capabilities.

## Method Summary
The dataset is created via a three‑stage pipeline: (1) **topic seeding** generates domain‑diverse concept seeds; (2) **concept validation** checks factual properties (e.g., non‑emptiness of the minor term) using external knowledge sources; (3) **triplet completion** maps validated concept triplets to all 24 syllogistic mood‑figure forms in both Chinese and English. Evaluation uses two prompt templates: a baseline prompt simply asks whether the syllogism is valid, while a “Prior‑check” prompt first inquires about the emptiness of the minor term. For each model, responses are parsed into binary decisions (valid/invalid) and compared against the known Aristotelian and Boolean truth tables to compute three metrics: traditional‑logic accuracy (Acct), modern‑logic accuracy (Accm), and consistency (Cons), which measures agreement between the two interpretations. No models are trained; the study compares existing checkpoints across a wide range of sizes, architectures, and training regimes.

## Key Results
- **Scaling promotes shift**: Larger models (e.g., GPT‑5, GPT‑o3) achieve near‑perfect modern‑logic accuracy (Accm ~100%) and consistency (Cons ~100%), while smaller models remain anchored in traditional logic.
- **RL‑trained thinking accelerates shift**: Qwen3‑8B‑Thinking matches the modern‑logic performance of much larger non‑thinking models, indicating that reasoning‑focused post‑training is more sample‑efficient than sheer scale.
- **Base model predisposition**: If a base model already exhibits modern‑logic signals, subsequent fine‑tuning yields stable transitions; otherwise, the shift is less reliable. Systematic failures remain with empty minor terms, certain moods (e.g., AAI‑4), and cross‑lingual gaps (Chinese vs. English).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The observed shift can be understood through three interacting factors. First, **increased model scale** provides greater capacity to abstract away from surface statistical regularities and capture the underlying logical form, allowing models to override heuristics tied to existential import. Second, **RL‑trained thinking models** are optimized to produce intermediate reasoning steps, which helps them handle counterintuitive cases (e.g., universal statements about empty sets) by explicitly considering emptiness conditions. Third, the **pre‑training corpus** likely contains examples that favor Boolean reasoning; models with a stronger prior exposure to such patterns require less post‑training to adopt modern logic stably. Together, these factors determine whether an LLM will treat universal premises as not carrying existential import, i.e., align with modern logic.

## Foundational Learning
- **Syllogism**: A deductive argument consisting of two premises and a conclusion. *Why needed*: Provides a well‑defined, mathematically tractable test bed for logical inference. *Quick check*: Does the model correctly judge “All mammals are animals; All dogs are mammals; therefore All dogs are animals” as valid?
- **Existential import**: The assumption that a universal statement (e.g., “All A are B”) implies that the subject class (A) is non‑empty. *Why needed*: It is the pivotal distinction between Aristotelian (with) and Boolean (without) logic. *Quick check*: Does the model treat “All unicorns are white” as true regardless of whether unicorns exist?
- **Mood and figure**: Classification of syllogisms based on the types of categorical statements (A: All, E: No, I: Some, O: Some not) and the position of the middle term. *Why needed*: Enables systematic coverage of all logical forms and isolates those where existential import matters. *Quick check*: Are there particular moods (e.g., AAI‑4) where accuracy drops sharply?
- **Metrics (Acct, Accm, Cons)**: Traditional‑logic accuracy (Acct), modern‑logic accuracy (Accm), and consistency (Cons) measuring agreement between the two interpretations. *Why needed*: Quantify the degree of adherence to each logic system and the stability of the model’s reasoning pattern. *Quick check*: Are Acct and Accm negatively correlated? Does Cons approach 100% for large models?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
The overall system comprises two pipelines:

**Data Generation**: Topic Seeding -> Concept Validation -> Triplet Completion -> Dataset  
**Evaluation**: Prompt Construction -> Model Inference -> Response Parsing -> Metric Calculation

Data flows from the generated dataset into the evaluation prompts, then through the model and parsing steps to produce metrics.

### Critical Path
1. Build a balanced dataset where Aristotelian and Boolean validity diverge specifically for empty‑minor‑term cases.
2. For each model, query with both baseline and Prior‑check prompts.
3. Compare model decisions to the two truth tables to compute Acct and Accm.
4. Correlate metrics with model properties (size, training, language) to answer the research question.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Mood coverage vs. dataset size**: Including all 24 mood‑figure forms twice (empty/non‑empty) yields a manageable 9,600 examples but may omit rare edge cases.
- **Automated concept validation**: Using Wikipedia/factual checks scales well but introduces noise for borderline or fictional concepts.
- **Bilingual design**: Increases generalizability but adds confounds from tokenizer differences and training‑data imbalances.
- **Prompt simplicity**: The baseline prompt is minimal to avoid cueing; the Prior‑check adds a direct emptiness question, potentially altering reasoning.
- **Model decoding**: Greedy decoding ensures reproducibility but may not reflect the model’s distributional robustness; temperature effects are unexplored.

### Failure Signatures
- **Parsing failures**: Model responses that are not unambiguously “valid” or “invalid” break the pipeline.
- **Dataset contamination**: If syllogisms appear in pre‑training corpora, measured accuracy may overestimate true logical competence.
- **Concept ambiguity**: The emptiness label can be debatable (e.g., “dragons” as fictional but culturally concrete).
- **Cross‑lingual variability**: Tokenization or training‑data skew might cause differences unrelated to logic.

### 3 First Experiments
1. **Sanity check**: Run a rule‑based syllogism solver on a small dataset slice to verify the evaluation metrics correctly reflect known logical status.
2. **Baseline replication**: Test a small open‑source model (e.g., Llama2‑7B) across all moods and emptiness conditions to confirm the expected gap between Acct and Accm.
3. **Empty‑term sensitivity**: Isolate the effect of emptiness by comparing model performance on non‑empty vs. empty minor‑term subsets; a significant accuracy drop signals Aristotelian bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Assumption: The paper does not explicitly list open questions in a dedicated subsection, but its discussion of limitations and future directions implies several:
- How does dataset construction (e.g., topic seeding, validation criteria) systematically bias results toward certain concepts or logical structures?
- To what extent do tokenizer differences and training-data imbalances explain cross-lingual performance gaps, independent of logical reasoning?
- Can the observed shift to Boolean logic be causally attributed to specific training data patterns versus architectural inductive biases?
- How robust are these findings to variations in prompting strategy, decoding parameters, and evaluation parsing heuristics?
- What is the minimal set of training experiences (e.g., specific RL thinking steps) required to achieve a stable transition to modern logic?

## Limitations
- Dataset generation hyperparameters (prompts, validation criteria, topic distribution) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Access to RL‑trained Thinking checkpoints and proprietary models (GPT‑5, GPT‑o3) is limited; inference settings (temperature, top‑p) are not fully reported.
- Potential training‑data contamination and cross‑lingual confounds (tokenizer imbalances) may affect conclusions.
- The evaluation relies on binary parsing of free‑form model outputs, which may not capture nuanced or self‑contradictory reasoning.
- The study focuses on a specific logical formalism (syllogisms); generalizability to other reasoning domains (e.g., propositional, first-order logic) is unknown.

## Confidence
- Larger models shift to Boolean logic: **High**
- RL‑trained thinking accelerates the shift: **Medium**
- Base model predisposition influences shift stability: **Medium**
- Persistent failures (empty minor terms, mood biases, cross‑lingual gaps): **High**
