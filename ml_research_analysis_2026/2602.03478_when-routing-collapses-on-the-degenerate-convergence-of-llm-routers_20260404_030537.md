# When Routing Collapses: On the Degenerate Convergence of LLM Routers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03478
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03478
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** EquiRouter reduces cost by ~17% at GPT-4-level performance while achieving the lowest Routing Collapse Index (RCI), mitigating routing collapse.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies "routing collapse," a phenomenon where LLM routers overuse the strongest, most expensive model as the budget increases, even when cheaper models suffice. The root cause is an objective-decision mismatch: routers are trained to predict scalar performance scores, but routing decisions depend on brittle discrete comparisons among models, especially in a prevalent small-margin regime where models are nearly tied. To address this, the authors propose EquiRouter, a decision-aware router that directly learns per-query model rankings using a pairwise ranking loss and query-conditioned model representations. Evaluated on RouterBench, EquiRouter achieves significant cost savings and the lowest Routing Collapse Index (RCI) among compared routers, demonstrating effective mitigation of collapse.

## Method Summary
EquiRouter comprises a shared trunk that maps query embeddings to a latent representation, which is modulated per model via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) using learnable model embeddings. The modulated features are combined with model embeddings through concatenation and element-wise operations to form joint features, scored by a linear head. A separate two-layer MLP predicts per-query model costs. The router is trained with a pairwise logistic ranking loss that compares model scores based on their true performance and cost, encouraging correct orderings even when performances are tied. At inference, the router filters models with predicted costs ≤ budget and selects the highest-scoring feasible model.

## Key Results
- EquiRouter reduces cost by ~17% at GPT-4-level performance on RouterBench compared to prior routers.
- Achieves the lowest Routing Collapse Index (RCI), indicating significantly better utilization of smaller models.
- Ablations confirm that the pairwise ranking loss is critical for collapse mitigation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core issue is an objective-decision mismatch: routers trained with regression losses (e.g., MSE) optimize for accurate scalar score prediction, but routing decisions rely on brittle pairwise comparisons among models. In the small-margin regime where many models have nearly equal performance, small prediction errors cause frequent ordering flips, leading routers to default to the strongest, most expensive model as budget increases (collapse). EquiRouter aligns training with decisions by directly optimizing a pairwise ranking loss that respects the true partial order induced by performance and cost. The FiLM-based query-conditioned model representations enhance discrimination in tie situations, while the cost predictor ensures feasible model selection.

## Foundational Learning
1. **LLM Routing**  
   - *Why needed:* To dynamically select among multiple models for cost-efficient inference under a budget.  
   - *Quick check:* Verify that the router selects different models for different queries when budget varies.

2. **Small-Margin Regime**  
   - *Why needed:* Collapse occurs when model performances are nearly tied, making decisions sensitive to prediction errors.  
   - *Quick check:* Compute distribution of performance differences between top models; high concentration near zero indicates this regime.

3. **Pairwise Ranking Loss**  
   - *Why needed:* Aligns training objective with discrete routing decisions by directly optimizing model orderings.  
   - *Quick check:* Ensure loss includes all pairs where one model is strictly better (higher performance, lower cost) or tied with lower cost.

4. **FiLM Modulation**  
   - *Why needed:* Enables query-specific adjustment of model representations to better discriminate in tie situations.  
   - *Quick check:* Ablate FiLM (use static model embeddings) and measure increase in RCI.

5. **Routing Collapse Index (RCI)**  
   - *Why needed:* Quantifies collapse severity by measuring dominated model selections.  
   - *Quick check:* Compute RCI as proportion of queries where a cheaper model with equal/better performance was available but not chosen.

6. **Cost-Aware Routing**  
   - *Why needed:* The goal is to maximize performance under a cost budget, not just maximize performance.  
   - *Quick check:* Evaluate router over a range of budgets; a good router shows increasing use of cheaper models as budget tightens.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Query Embedding -> Shared Trunk f -> FiLM Modulation (with Model Embeddings m_j) -> Joint Features (concatenation + element-wise ops) -> Scoring Head g_θ -> Scores s_j.  
Parallel: Model Embeddings m_j -> Cost Predictor -> Predicted Costs c_j.

**Critical Path:** For each query, compute trunk output; for each model, apply FiLM using its model embedding, form joint features, score, filter by predicted cost, and select highest-scoring feasible model.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- FiLM adds parameters/computation but enables query-specific weighting.  
- Pairwise ranking loss scales quadratically with model pool size; may require sampling for large pools.  
- Separate cost predictor decouples cost estimation but needs additional training data.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- High RCI: indicates collapse, router overusing strongest model.  
- Low nAUC: poor overall cost-efficiency.  
- Cost predictor high MSE: leads to incorrect feasible set filtering.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Ablate FiLM: Replace FiLM with static model embeddings; compare RCI and nAUC.  
2. Loss Ablation: Train with MSE loss instead of ranking loss; measure collapse (RCI) and cost savings.  
3. Cost Predictor Accuracy: Evaluate test MSE; if high, consider joint training or increased capacity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What is the theoretical threshold for the "small-margin regime" that triggers routing collapse, and can it be derived from the distribution of model performances on a given benchmark?
2. Is the pairwise ranking loss used in EquiRouter provably optimal for mitigating collapse in the small-margin regime, or could other ranking objectives yield better decision alignment?
3. How robust is routing collapse (and EquiRouter's mitigation) to distribution shifts beyond the train-test split considered, such as temporal drift, new model introductions, or entirely new task domains?
4. Does the Routing Collapse Index (RCI) fully capture the spectrum of collapse severity, or does it overlook partial collapse scenarios where routers frequently select mid-tier (but still unnecessarily expensive) models?
5. Can the objective-decision mismatch be resolved by calibrating scalar score predictions to better reflect ranking probabilities instead of adopting a ranking loss?

## Limitations
- Architecture and hyperparameters of EquiRouter are not fully specified, affecting reproducibility.
- Training details (learning rate schedule, handling of ties) are missing, introducing variability.
- Baseline implementations have ambiguous design choices, weakening comparative claims.
- Evaluation is restricted to two datasets; generalizability to other domains is unclear.
- Static cost model does not account for dynamic or usage-based pricing.

## Confidence
- Routing collapse exists: High
- EquiRouter achieves cost reduction: Medium
- Pairwise ranking loss is key: Medium
- Generalization to unseen setups: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset robustness:** Evaluate EquiRouter on at least three additional benchmarks (e.g., multilingual tasks, code generation) to verify consistent cost savings and collapse mitigation.
2. **Component-wise ablation:** Systematically remove FiLM modulation, joint features, and model embeddings while keeping other factors constant; measure changes in RCI and nAUC to isolate critical design elements.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Perform a range sweep around reported defaults (hidden size D, learning rate, batch size) and report performance variance; identify parameter regimes that yield stable improvements over baselines.
