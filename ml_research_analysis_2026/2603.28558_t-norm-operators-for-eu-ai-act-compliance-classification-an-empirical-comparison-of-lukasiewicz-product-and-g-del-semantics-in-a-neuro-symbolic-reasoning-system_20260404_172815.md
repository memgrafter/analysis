# T-Norm Operators for EU AI Act Compliance Classification: An Empirical Comparison of Lukasiewicz, Product, and Gödel Semantics in a Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning System

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2603.28558
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28558
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Gödel t-norm yields highest accuracy (84.5%) and borderline recall (85%) at cost of 0.8% false positives; Product and Łukasiewicz maintain zero false positives, with Product (81.2%) outperforming Łukasiewicz (78.5%).

## Executive Summary
This paper compares three triangular norm (t-norm) operators—Łukasiewicz, Product, and Gödel—within the LGGT+ neuro-symbolic reasoning system for classifying AI systems under the EU AI Act. Using a benchmark of 1035 annotated AI system descriptions, the study evaluates accuracy, false positive/negative rates, and borderline case performance. Gödel’s min-semantics achieves the highest overall accuracy (84.5%) and best recall on borderline cases (85%), but introduces a small false positive rate (0.8%). The other two operators avoid false positives entirely, with Product slightly more accurate than Łukasiewicz. All pairwise differences are statistically significant. The findings highlight a key trade-off between recall and precision and suggest that operator choice matters less than completeness of the formalized rule base.

## Method Summary
The evaluation is conducted on the LGGT+ system, which combines neural confidence extraction for 22 vocabulary terms with symbolic reasoning over 14 conjunctive rules derived from the EU AI Act. For each of the 1035 cases, the reasoning engine aggregates condition confidences using one of three t-norm operators (Łukasiewicz, Product, Gödel) and applies a fixed threshold θ=0.5. Rules are prioritized (prohibited > high_risk > limited_risk > minimal_risk), and the highest-priority firing rule determines the classification. No training occurs; the study performs deterministic inference on pre-scored data. Primary metrics include overall accuracy, per-case-type performance, false positive/negative rates, and McNemar’s test for pairwise significance. The benchmark spans four risk categories with label distribution: minimal_risk 32%, high_risk 28%, limited_risk 27%, prohibited 13%.

## Key Results
- Gödel t-norm delivers highest accuracy (84.5%) and borderline recall (85%) but incurs 8 false positives (0.8%).
- Product and Łukasiewicz achieve zero false positives; Product (81.2%) modestly outperforms Łukasiewicz (78.5%).
- All pairwise differences are statistically significant (McNemar p<0.001); rule base incompleteness remains the dominant error source for false negatives (14.7–21.5%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 (Bottleneck Conjunction):** Gödel’s min-semantics acts as a “weakest-link” aggregator: the rule fires if any condition exceeds θ=0.5, even if others are lower. This interpretation aligns with certain EU AI Act provisions where a single moderately satisfied condition may suffice, thereby capturing borderline cases that stricter operators miss. Evidence: Gödel correctly classifies 68 of 80 borderline cases (85%) vs. 25% (Łukasiewicz) and 35% (Product). Break condition: if legal experts later reclassify borderline labels, the recall advantage could vanish.

**Mechanism 2 (Cumulative Satisfaction):** Product and Łukasiewicz require multiple conditions to contribute jointly before exceeding θ. Product’s multiplicative smoothness provides higher accuracy (81.2% vs 78.5%) by better differentiating moderately strong combinations. Evidence: On cases with marginal scores (a+b+c ≤ 2.5), the operators disagree on 28 of 1035 instances (p<0.001). Break condition: changes in the distribution of condition scores could narrow or reverse the accuracy gap.

**Mechanism 3 (Rule Base Completeness Dominance):** The majority of false negatives (31–36%) arise from missing rule conditions (e.g., nuanced human-in-the-loop scenarios), not from the t-norm’s aggregation logic. All operators suffer similarly on under-specified cases, indicating that expanding the rule base would improve accuracy more than operator tuning. Evidence: Error analysis shows 23–26 FN cases involve classification to minimal_risk despite high_risk/limited_risk expert labels.

## Foundational Learning
- **Triangular Norms (t-norms):** Why needed: They mathematically define how to combine multiple uncertain condition confidences into a single conjunctive decision score. Quick check: What is the key semantic difference between Gödel (min) and Łukasiewicz (max(0, a+b-1)) when one condition is much lower than the others?
- **Neuro-Symbolic Hybrid Architecture:** Why needed: LGGT+ integrates neural perception (confidence scoring) with symbolic logic (rule-based proof trails) to produce auditable compliance decisions. Quick check: Why does LGGT+ use a deterministic t-norm at inference time, not as a trainable loss component?
- **Legal Conjunctive Standards:** Why needed: The EU AI Act’s “and” conditions can be interpreted as either strong (all must be definitively present) or bottleneck (weakest link governs) conjunction, directly mapping to t-norm semantics. Quick check: Which t-norm best matches a rule stating “system is high-risk if it operates in education *and* affects access,” where “affects access” might be satisfied by a moderate influence?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: L1 Logic (LukasiewiczLogic, LogFuzzyLogic, HypergraphEncoder, LogicAugmentedAttention, GumbelSoftmaxAnnealing) -> L2 Graph (KnowledgeGraph, TemporalKnowledgeNode, OntologyPatcher, AdaptiveTripleFilter) -> L3 Reasoning (ReasoningEngine, ProofTreeBuilder) -> L4 API (FastAPI, AnnexIVPDFGenerator)

Critical path: Condition confidence extraction (neural) → t-norm aggregation per rule → priority-ordered classification → proof trail generation.

Design tradeoffs:
- Rule completeness vs. operator sophistication: Adding missing conditions yields larger gains than fine-tuning t-norm.
- Auditability vs. flexibility: Deterministic t-norms enable explainable proof trails but cannot learn from data; future learnable components (LogicAugmentedAttention) may increase flexibility.
- Precision vs. recall: Gödel improves recall but risks false positives; Łukasiewicz/Product are strictly conservative.

Failure signatures:
- High false negatives: Likely due to rule base gaps (e.g., missing human oversight nuances).
- All operators agree on wrong prediction: Investigate rule condition definitions and confidence scoring pipeline.
- Gödel false positives: Occur on marginal cases where one condition barely exceeds θ=0.5 while others are high.

First 3 experiments:
1. Re-run the benchmark with the classification threshold θ varied across [0.25, 0.75] to map operating curves for each t-norm.
2. Conduct an expert annotation study: have two EU AI Act lawyers label the 80 borderline cases; compute Cohen’s κ and per-operator agreement.
3. Implement a mixed-semantics classifier: manually annotate each of the 14 rules as “strong” (→ Łukasiewicz/Product) or “bottleneck” (→ Gödel); evaluate against the full benchmark.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Mixed-semantics classifier:** Could assigning rule-specific t-norm operators based on legal annotations of conjunctive standard type (strong vs. bottleneck) outperform any single-operator system? The paper proposes this as a next step but provides no empirical validation.
2. **Inter-rater reliability among legal experts:** What is Cohen’s κ when independent EU AI Act lawyers label the benchmark, especially borderline cases? Without this, ground truth stability is unknown.
3. **Legal validation of Gödel false positives:** The 8 false positives from Gödel on borderline cases may be rightful overcautious classifications or labeling errors; independent review is needed to resolve.
4. **Formalizing proportionality reasoning:** Can extending the rule base to capture human-in-the-loop nuances (e.g., effective human oversight downgrading automated_decision confidence) reduce the dominant false negative rate (14.7–21.5%) in marginal cases?

## Limitations
- Rule base of 14 formalized rules is incomplete, omitting key provisions like Art. 5 exemptions and proportionality reasoning.
- Labels were assigned without independent legal review, threatening the validity of accuracy comparisons, particularly for borderline cases.
- The fixed decision threshold θ=0.5 may not reflect regulatory trade-offs; threshold sensitivity remains unknown.

## Confidence
- Gödel t-norm improves borderline recall via bottleneck conjunction: **Medium**
- Product and Łukasiewicz achieve zero false positives, with Product slightly more accurate: **High**
- Rule base completeness is primary error source; operator choice secondary: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Expert annotation study:** Have two EU AI Act lawyers independently label the 80 borderline cases; compute Cohen’s κ and per-operator agreement.
2. **Rule base expansion:** Add commonly missing conditions (e.g., human oversight effectiveness, Art. 5 exemptions) and re-evaluate all operators to measure impact on false negatives.
3. **Threshold sensitivity analysis:** Evaluate each operator across θ ∈ [0.25, 0.75] to generate precision-recall curves and identify optimal operating points for different compliance policies.
