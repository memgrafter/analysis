# Learning Adaptive LLM Decoding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09065  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09065  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Learns decoding adapters (sequence-level contextual bandit and token-level POMDP) that dynamically select sampling strategies based on compute budget and model state, achieving up to 10.2% relative Pass@1 improvement on MATH under fixed token budgets and 2–3% under fixed parallel sampling.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the inefficiency of fixed hyperparameters during large language model decoding by introducing lightweight adapters that dynamically adjust sampling strategies at two granularities: sequence-level (choosing a decoding configuration per prompt) and token-level (selecting an action at every generation step). Both adapters are trained with REINFORCE using verifiable task rewards without modifying the base model. On the MATH dataset, the token-level adapter yields substantial gains under token budgets, while the sequence-level adapter improves accuracy under parallel sampling constraints. Mixed-domain training shows complementary benefits, demonstrating the effectiveness of adaptive decoding across different compute regimes.

## Method Summary
The method attaches a small, trainable adapter (a multi-layer perceptron) to a frozen LLM. The adapter observes a context (prompt embedding for sequence-level; per-token hidden state for token-level) and the available compute budget (parallel samples *B* or remaining tokens *b_t*), then selects an action from a discrete set of decoding configurations (e.g., specific temperature/top-k combinations). For sequence-level, the chosen action applies uniformly across *B* parallel rollouts; for token-level, it applies at each step within a single trajectory. The policy is optimized with REINFORCE to maximize terminal task rewards (e.g., answer correctness), with variance reduction via baselines, entropy regularization, and token masking (token-level). Budget conditioning enables the policy to learn budget-dependent behaviors, such as more exploration when *B* is large or conservatism when *b_t* is low.

## Key Results
- Token-level adapter improves Pass@1 by up to 10.2% relative to the best static strategy on MATH under fixed token budgets.
- Sequence-level adapter gains 2–3% under fixed parallel sampling constraints.
- Mixed-domain training (MATH + CodeContests) yields smaller but positive improvements on both domains, showing complementary gains across adaptation granularities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A sequence-level adapter that selects a fixed decoding configuration per prompt (modeled as a contextual bandit) improves the accuracy-budget tradeoff under fixed parallel sampling constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The adapter observes a prompt embedding and a parallel sampling budget *B*, then selects an action *a* from a discrete set of decoding strategies (e.g., specific temperature/top-k combinations). This action is applied uniformly throughout the entire generation of a single trajectory. The policy is trained with REINFORCE to maximize the terminal task reward (e.g., Pass@*k*), where the reward for a given *B* reflects the success probability across *B* sampled rollouts. The adapter learns to associate prompt contexts and budget levels with strategies that maximize expected success, avoiding a one-size-fits-all approach.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal decoding strategy varies across prompts and parallel sampling budgets; a learned selector can generalize from training rewards to unseen prompts and budgets.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "sequence-level adapter gains 2–3% under fixed parallel sampling."
  - [section 2.2] Formal contextual bandit formulation where context *x* = [*e*; *B*], action set *S*, and objective *J*<sub>seq</sub>.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; nearest neighbors discuss adaptive decoding but not explicitly contextual bandits for per-prompt strategy selection.
- **Break condition:** If the selected discrete action space *S* fails to contain any strategy that can achieve high reward on a significant subset of prompts, coverage maximization (Eq. 3) will not yield a useful set, and the adapter cannot outperform the best static strategy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A token-level adapter that selects a decoding action at every generation step (modeled as a POMDP) substantially improves accuracy under a fixed per-sequence token budget by dynamically modulating stochasticity within a trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** At each token step *t*, the adapter observes a compact state *x<sub>t</sub>* (last hidden state *e<sub>t</sub>* and remaining token budget *b<sub>t</sub>*) and selects an action *a<sub>t</sub>* (e.g., a temperature value). This action determines the sampling distribution for the next token. The entire trajectory is rewarded only at the end. REINFORCE updates the policy to increase the probability of action sequences that lead to correct solutions. The policy learns to allocate higher stochasticity (higher temperature) at steps with high uncertainty (e.g., "forking" tokens) and lower stochasticity elsewhere, all while respecting the remaining budget.
- **Core assumption:** Token-level uncertainty and its impact on final outcome are predictable from internal model states and remaining budget; adapting sampling per token leads to better final solutions than a fixed strategy for the whole sequence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "token-level adapter improves Pass@1 accuracy by up to 10.2% relative to the best static strategy under a fixed token budget."
  - [section 2.3] POMDP formulation with state *x<sub>t</sub>* = [*e<sub>t</sub>*; *b<sub>t</sub>*], REINFORCE objective *J*<sub>tok</sub>, and stabilizations (masking high-confidence tokens).
  - [section 4] Main results Table 5 show ~9–10% absolute gains; entropy-only ablation fails to match full adapter.
- **Break condition:** If the gradient variance from token-level REINFORCE cannot be stabilized (e.g., via reward sparsity filtering and high-entropy token masking), training becomes unstable and the policy fails to learn meaningful per-token adaptations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Explicitly conditioning the decoding policy on the available compute budget (parallel *B* or token *b<sub>t</sub>*) during training is critical for achieving robust performance under fixed inference-time budgets.
- **Mechanism:** The budget is encoded (e.g., via a small MLP) and concatenated to the context features before the policy's MLP. This allows the policy to learn distinct behaviors for different budget levels—e.g., more exploratory strategies when *B* is larger (since failures can be averaged out) or more deterministic actions when *b<sub>t</sub>* is low (to avoid wasting remaining tokens on unproductive exploration).
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between optimal decoding strategy and compute budget is non-trivial and learnable; exposing the policy to varied budgets during training induces representations that generalize to evaluation at any fixed budget.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "improve the accuracy–budget tradeoff" and gains are observed under fixed budgets.
  - [section 3.2] "Conditioning the policy on the parallel sampling budget consistently improves performance relative to budget-agnostic training."
  - [section 2.1] Budget *B* and *b<sub>t</sub>* are included in the policy input *x*.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal decoding strategy is invariant to budget magnitude, then budget conditioning adds no signal and may even hurt by introducing a spurious feature that the policy overfits to during training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: REINFORCE (Policy Gradient)**  
  *Why needed here:* Both adapters are trained from scratch using sparse, binary terminal rewards (correct/incorrect). REINFORCE allows updating the policy based on the return of entire sequences without requiring a learned value or reward model.  
  *Quick check:* How does the REINFORCE update rule (∇θ J ≈ E[∇θ log πθ(a|x) R]) handle the high variance of returns in long generation trajectories, and what role does the baseline play?
- **Concept: Contextual Bandits**  
  *Why needed here:* The sequence-level adapter makes a single, irrevocable decision per prompt before any tokens are generated, receiving immediate reward based on the *B*-sample outcome. This is a classic contextual bandit (state *x*, action *a*, reward *r(x,a)*).  
  *Quick check:* What distinguishes a contextual bandit from a full reinforcement learning problem (MDP/POMDP) in terms of action impact and state evolution?
- **Concept: Decoding Strategies (Temperature, Top-k, Top-p, Min-p)**  
  *Why needed here:* The adapter's action space consists of specific instantiations of these common sampling transformations. Understanding how each parameter affects output distribution is key to interpreting what the policy learns.  
  *Quick check:* If you set temperature=0.0 while keeping top-k=50 active, what is the resulting sampling behavior compared to greedy decoding (temperature=0, no truncation)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
The system comprises: (1) a frozen LLM (f) that produces logits and hidden states; (2) a decoding adapter (π_θ) – a lightweight MLP – that takes context embeddings (or per-token hidden states) and budget encodings and outputs a distribution over discrete actions; (3) a finite action space *S* of decoding configurations (temperature, top-k, top-p, min-p values); (4) a transformation *T_a* that applies the selected configuration to the LLM's next-token distribution; (5) a task-specific reward function *r* that returns a binary correctness signal at sequence end; (6) a budget encoder (small MLP) that transforms scalar budgets into vectors.

**Critical path (Token-level):**  
1. Start with prompt *q*.  
2. For *t* from 1 to *b*:  
   a. Get LLM hidden state *e<sub>t</sub>* from prefix.  
   b. Form *x<sub>t</sub>* = [*e<sub>t</sub>*; *b<sub>t</sub>*].  
   c. Adapter π<sub>θ</sub>(*x<sub>t</sub>*) → distribution over actions → sample *a<sub>t</sub>* from *S*.  
   d. Apply *T<sub>a_t</sub>* to *p<sub>f</sub>(·|q, y<1:t-1)*, sample token *y<sub>t</sub>*.  
3. After full sequence *y* is generated, compute terminal reward *r*(*q, y*).  
4. Store trajectory (*x<sub>1:T</sub>*, *a<sub>1:T</sub>*, *r*).  
5. After *N* trajectories, compute REINFORCE loss with baseline, update θ.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Discrete vs. Continuous Actions:* Discrete *S* simplifies policy output and ensures interpretable strategies but limits expressivity. Continuous parameterization (e.g., directly outputting temperature) would require differentiable sampling tricks (Gumbel-Softmax) and may be less stable.  
- *Budget Granularity:* Condition on exact scalar *B*/*b<sub>t</sub>* vs. bucketized. Scalar allows fine-grained learning but may overfit to specific values in training; buckets improve generalization but lose resolution.  
- *State Representation:* Using last hidden state *e<sub>t</sub>* is simple but may not capture all uncertainty signals. More complex aggregations (e.g., attention-weighted states) could help but increase adapter size.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Sequence-level:** Policy collapses to a single action regardless of context/budget, identical to "BEST" baseline. Check action distribution entropy → near 0.  
- **Token-level:** Training loss diverges or validation reward oscillates wildly. Likely cause: insufficient gradient stabilization (missing token masking, sparse reward filtering).  
- **General:** No improvement over static "MIXED" baseline. Possible cause: action space *S* lacks any high-performing strategy (verify by best-of-|S| oracle on validation).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity check:** Train a sequence-level adapter on a single domain (e.g., MATH) with a small candidate action set (e.g., 4 strategies). Verify it learns to allocate probability mass to at least 2 distinct strategies and that its Pass@1 exceeds the best single static strategy at some budget.  
2. **Budget conditioning ablation:** Train two token-level adapters on MATH: one with remaining token budget *b<sub>t</sub>* in *x<sub>t</sub>*, one without. Evaluate both at fixed budgets (e.g., 512, 1024 tokens). Confirm the budget-conditioned version shows a consistent gap, proving budget information is utilized.  
3. **Mixed-domain robustness:** Train a single sequence-level adapter on a balanced mix of MATH and CodeContests data. Evaluate on each held-out test set. Expect smaller but positive gains over best static on both, showing the policy learns compromise behaviors without catastrophic forgetting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Would a continuously parameterized decoding policy (e.g., learning custom temperature functions or dynamic truncation thresholds) improve the accuracy-budget tradeoff beyond discrete action spaces?  
   *Basis in paper:* The "Future Directions" section states: "Moving to more expressive, continuously parameterized decoding transformations would fundamentally change the learning problem."  
   *Why unresolved:* The paper restricts actions to a small discrete set for training stability and interpretability, leaving open whether richer parameterizations yield further gains.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Train a token-level adapter with a continuous action head (e.g., predicting temperature values) and evaluate whether it surpasses discrete-tableau adapters on MATH/CodeContests under identical budgets.

2. Does filtering prompts with "extremely sparse or noisy reward signals" during token-level training introduce a bias that limits the adapter's robustness on out-of-distribution or ambiguous prompts?  
   *Basis in paper:* Section 2.3 mentions: "we filter the training distribution to exclude prompts that produce extremely sparse or noisy reward signals" for stability.  
   *Why unresolved:* The filter is a heuristic; its impact on generalization to diverse problem types (e.g., very hard or under-specified prompts) is not analyzed.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablate the filtering criterion and measure performance on a held-out set of high-uncertainty or low-signal prompts; compare failure modes with and without filtering.

3. Can the sequence-level adapter's strategy-selection policy generalize to completely unseen inference budgets (e.g., budgets outside the training range) without performance collapse?  
   *Basis in paper:* The adapter is trained "across a range of budgets" and conditions on B, but evaluation only reports fixed budgets from training.  
   *Why unresolved:* The paper does not test extrapolation to novel budget values, which is critical for real-world deployment where compute constraints vary.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate a budget-conditioned adapter trained on budgets {B1,...,Bn} on held-out budgets B* ∉ {B1,...,Bn} and report accuracy degradation.

4. Does joint training of the decoding adapter and the base language model (alternating updates) yield complementary gains, or does adapter learning become redundant if the model itself learns to output calibrated logits?  
   *Basis in paper:* "Future Directions" proposes: "Joint training with the base model... may reduce the burden on the decoding policy by shaping representations."  
   *Why unresolved:* The paper treats the LLM as frozen; interaction between adapter guidance and model fine-tuning is unexplored.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement alternating training rounds where the adapter is updated via REINFORCE (using task rewards) and the base model is fine-tuned via GRPO/RLVR; compare final accuracy-budget curves to the frozen-model baseline.

5. Are the gains from token-level adaptation primarily driven by allocating higher stochasticity to "forking tokens" (high-entropy, high-impact tokens) as postulated, or by non-local contextual patterns not captured by entropy alone?  
   *Basis in paper:* The paper cites "forking tokens" as motivation and shows an entropy-modulation trend in Figure 3, but the entropy-only ablation underperforms.  
   *Why unresolved:* The paper does not directly measure alignment between policy actions and identified forking tokens (e.g., via perturbation analysis), nor does it identify which contextual features beyond entropy matter.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Compute per-token "impact scores" (e.g., by masking tokens and measuring accuracy drop) on a validation set; correlate these scores with the adapter's chosen temperature to test whether high-impact tokens receive more stochasticity.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter and training details missing:** Exact MLP dimensions, learning rate schedule, entropy coefficient, number of training steps, and optimizer settings are unspecified.
- **Data and splits unclear:** Number of training prompts, validation/test split definitions, and prompt templates (e.g., CoT ratios) are not provided.
- **Reward aggregation ambiguity:** For sequence-level parallel sampling, it is unclear whether the terminal reward is Pass@1, best-of-B, or majority vote.
- **Action selection overlap:** The candidate set *S* is chosen via a greedy coverage procedure on a validation set; this may bias the action space toward that validation distribution and risk overfitting if the same set is used for evaluation.
- **Stability risks:** Token-level REINFORCE training is notoriously high-variance; the paper’s stabilizing tricks (reward filtering, high-entropy token masking) are mentioned but thresholds and exact implementations are not given.
- **Domain and model specificity:** Experiments are limited to MATH and CodeContests with a single model (Qwen3-4B). Generalization to other domains or architectures is unknown.
- **Discrete action space constraint:** The adapters select from a fixed set of decoding configurations; this limits expressivity compared to continuous parameter control.
- **Assumption of verifiable rewards:** The method requires a deterministic correctness checker, which may not exist for many tasks (e.g., open-ended generation).

## Confidence
- **Token-level adapter gains** (up to 10.2% relative improvement): **Medium** – Substantial improvement is reported, but training stability and missing hyperparameter details reduce certainty.
- **Sequence-level adapter gains** (2–3% under fixed parallel sampling): **Medium** – Positive results are shown, but the smaller effect size and reliance on action set coverage leave room for variance.
- **Budget conditioning importance**: **Medium** – The paper claims consistent improvement when conditioning on the budget, but the ablation details are thin and could be confounded.
- **Mixed-domain improvements** (smaller but positive): **Low** – Gains are marginal and the paper does not explore sensitivity to domain mixture ratios; results may not hold for more diverse mixtures.
