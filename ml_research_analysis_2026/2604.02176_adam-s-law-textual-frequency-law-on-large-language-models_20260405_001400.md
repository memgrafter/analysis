# Adam's Law: Textual Frequency Law on Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02176  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02176  
- **Reference count:** 31  
- **Primary result:** Preferring high-frequency paraphrases of semantically identical text improves LLM performance across tasks; e.g., math reasoning accuracy ↑~8% (63.55%→71.54% on DeepSeek-V3) and translation metrics ↑ for nearly all language pairs.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies textual frequency—the commonness of expression in a model’s training distribution—as a critical yet overlooked factor in LLM performance. It proposes the Textual Frequency Law (TFL): for inputs with identical meaning, high-frequency paraphrases should be used in both prompting and fine-tuning. Supporting methods include Textual Frequency Distillation (TFD) to refine frequency estimates from the target model’s own generations, and Curriculum Textual Frequency Training (CTFT) to fine-tune models in ascending order of sentence-level frequency. Experiments on a curated paired dataset (TFPD) across math, translation, reasoning, and tool calling show consistent gains, validating the law’s broad applicability.

## Method Summary
The approach centers on the Textual Frequency Paired Dataset (TFPD), which pairs high- and low-frequency paraphrases of identical-meaning sentences for multiple tasks. Frequency is estimated as the geometric mean of token marginal probabilities (from web corpora or model-generated “story completions” via TFD). For prompting, the high-frequency version is selected. For fine-tuning, CTFT sorts training data in ascending frequency order before each epoch. Evaluation compares task metrics (accuracy, BLEU, etc.) between frequency conditions. Key unknowns include exact paraphrase generation parameters, TFD weighting factors, and CTFT scheduling details (static vs. per-epoch re-sort).

## Key Results
- Prompting with high-frequency paraphrases boosted math accuracy by up to ~8% on DeepSeek-V3 (63.55%→71.54%).
- For machine translation, high-frequency prompts improved BLEU/chrF/COMET in 99–100 out of 100 language pairs on DeepSeek-V3.
- CTFT fine-tuning outperformed random, reverse-order, and difficulty-based curricula across all tested tasks and metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** High-frequency paraphrases yield better LLM performance (lower NLL loss, higher accuracy/BLEU) than low-frequency ones under semantic equivalence.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs approximate training data distribution via cross-entropy. Under Zipf’s law, high-frequency tokens have lower ideal NLL. If per-token log-probability error ε(r) is smaller for high-frequency tokens (due to more exposure), sentences composed of them incur lower average conditional loss (Theorem 4).
- **Core assumption:** Assumption 2: Token rank r correlates with error ε(r), with ε smaller for high-frequency tokens. Supported by empirical findings that LLMs encode frequency biases and Zipfian alignment correlates with performance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract claims consistent gains; Section 5.1 shows 8% math accuracy gain; neighbor papers on scaling laws and heavy-tailed distributions provide indirect support.
- **Break condition:** Semantic drift in paraphrasing (why human annotation was necessary for TFPD). If high-frequency versions subtly alter meaning, gains may not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Textual Frequency Distillation (TFD) improves frequency estimation for closed-source LLMs by simulating their training distribution via story completion.
- **Mechanism:** Query the target LLM to complete stories from seed sentences; the generated corpus D′ better reflects the model’s internal frequency biases than external web corpora. Final frequency F(x) blends original (F1) and distilled (F2) estimates, with a strengthening factor for OOV words.
- **Core assumption:** Model generations during story completion approximate its pre-training distribution. Assumes no major post-training distribution shift (e.g., from RLHF).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 describes TFD; Section 5.5 shows removing TFD harms performance (e.g., 100% of language pairs better with TFD on COMET for DeepSeek-V3).
- **Break condition:** If generation distribution is heavily shaped by instruction tuning/RLHF rather than pre-training likelihood, D′ may not reflect true pre-training frequency. Story completions may also introduce stylistic artifacts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Curriculum Textual Frequency Training (CTFT) fine-tunes in ascending frequency order, outperforming random, reverse-order, or difficulty-based curricula.
- **Mechanism:** Low-frequency expressions are more diverse/complex; training from low to high frequency leverages pre-existing high-frequency knowledge to stabilize fine-tuning and reduce forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** Frequency is the primary learnability factor for paraphrase pairs, independent of other difficulty metrics (syntactic complexity, reasoning depth).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 defines CTFT; Section 5.4 shows BLEU improvements (e.g., 3.7781→4.9102 on pag_Latn); Section 5.6, Table 5 shows weak correlation between textual complexity metrics and frequency, distinguishing TFL from traditional curricula.
- **Break condition:** If frequency ordering conflicts with a more fundamental curriculum (e.g., logical complexity in math), CTFT may harm performance. Baselines (easy-to-hard by dependency depth) underperformed but may not generalize.

## Foundational Learning
- **Zipf’s Law and Token Frequency:** Needed to understand why high-frequency tokens have lower loss and how sentence-level frequency is estimated (geometric mean of token marginals). Quick check: Why geometric mean instead of arithmetic mean? (Preserves product structure of sentence probability under independence assumption).
- **Autoregressive Loss Decomposition:** Needed to follow the proof linking frequency to loss via ideal marginal loss, approximation error δ, and contextual discrepancy η. Quick check: What does η capture and why is it generally negative for high-frequency sentences? (Contextual predictability; high-frequency sentences often have more predictable continuations).
- **Curriculum Learning:** Needed to see CTFT as a frequency-based instantiation of the easy-to-hard principle. Quick check: How do weak correlations between frequency and traditional complexity metrics challenge standard curriculum design? (Suggests frequency captures a distinct, overlooked dimension of learnability).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Frequency Estimator -> Paraphraser -> Curriculum Scheduler -> Fine-Tuning Engine
- **Critical path:**  
  *Prompting:* Input sentence -> estimate frequency (F1 or F2) -> select high-frequency paraphrase -> query LLM.  
  *Fine-tuning:* Build TFPD -> estimate frequencies -> apply CTFT schedule (sort ascending) -> fine-tune LLM (e.g., LoRA).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  TFD improves accuracy but is costly (LLM story completions). Frequency metric (unigram geometric mean) is simple but ignores syntax/length. Curriculum can be static (once) or dynamic (per-epoch), trading overhead for adaptability.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Semantic drift in paraphrases (diagnose via BERTScore <0.85).  
  - Frequency misestimation due to domain mismatch (compare F1 vs. F2 distributions).  
  - Curriculum conflict if frequency ordering opposes other difficulty measures (ablate with alternative orders).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Validate TFL: Use manually verified paraphrase pairs (BERTScore >0.9) from TFPD math subset; prompt a base LLM (e.g., Qwen-7B) with high/low versions; expect high-frequency accuracy > low-frequency.  
  2. Test TFD impact: For fixed sentences, compute F1 (web) and F2 (distilled) rankings; correlate with held-out NLL loss; expect F2 correlation > F1.  
  3. Run CTFT ablation: Fine-tune Qwen-1.5B on TFPD translation subset with schedules: random, high-to-low, low-to-high (CTFT), easy-to-hard (dependency depth). Evaluate on FLORES-200; expect CTFT best.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** What cognitive/architectural mechanisms cause LLMs to process high-frequency expressions more effectively?  
  **Basis:** Abstract and introduction call frequency an overlooked topic.  
  **Why unresolved:** Empirical law established but causal factors unexplored.  
  **Needed evidence:** Ablations isolating token predictability, training exposure, or internal representations; comparative analyses across architectures.
- **Question:** How can TFD’s computational overhead be reduced while maintaining accuracy?  
  **Basis:** Limitations section notes TFD’s cost from LLM queries.  
  **Why unresolved:** No alternatives explored; scalability bottleneck.  
  **Needed evidence:** Efficient distillation techniques (e.g., smaller models, synthetic data) that correlate highly with TFD estimates and downstream gains.
- **Question:** Does Assumption 2 (pointwise log-domain accuracy) hold across LLM families/scales, and how does ε(r) scale with rank?  
  **Basis:** Appendix notes Assumption 2 is empirical, not derived; no direct ε(r) measurement.  
  **Why unresolved:** Foundational to theory but unvalidated.  
  **Needed evidence:** Large-scale measurement of token-level errors across frequency ranks for multiple models; fit ε(r) vs. rank and correlate with task performance.

## Limitations
- Paraphrase generation parameters (temperature, top_p) unspecified, affecting frequency distribution and semantic equivalence.
- TFD weighting factors (α, β) and story completion details (prompt, token count) omitted, hindering replication.
- CTFT scheduling unclear (static vs. per-epoch re-sort); potential confound with length/simplicity despite weak correlation in Table 5.

## Confidence
- **Textual Frequency Law (TFL):** Medium. Strong empirical gains but dependent on careful paraphrase curation; risk of semantic drift.
- **Textual Frequency Distillation (TFD):** Medium. Ablation supports benefit, but assumption that LLM generations reflect pre-training distribution may not hold for instruction-tuned models.
- **Curriculum Textual Frequency Training (CTFT):** Medium. Outperforms baselines in experiments, but curriculum order might be task-specific; more baselines needed.

## Next Checks
1. Re-run prompting with fixed, manually verified paraphrase pairs (BERTScore >0.9) across multiple LLMs to isolate frequency effect.
2. Ablate TFD: compare F1-only vs. F1+F2 frequencies; measure correlation with held-out NLL loss to test estimation accuracy.
