# D-COT: Disciplined Chain-of-Thought Learning for Efficient Reasoning in Small Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21786
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21786
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: D-CoT improves GPQA-diamond accuracy by 9.9 percentage points to 52.93% and reduces average tokens by 64.7% for Qwen3-8B with 5,079 training samples.

## Executive Summary
D-CoT introduces a method to mitigate overthinking in small language models by enforcing structured reasoning during training via control tags (e.g., <TEMP_LOW>, <TEMP_HIGH>). The model internalizes this discipline, achieving simultaneous gains in accuracy and efficiency without needing the tags at inference. On Qwen3-8B, D-CoT yields a 9.9% absolute accuracy increase on GPQA-diamond and a 64.7% token reduction, demonstrating that disciplined reasoning can enhance both performance and computational efficiency in small models.

## Method Summary
D-CoT augments chain-of-thought training data with special control tags that mark transitions between low and high reasoning intensity. During fine-tuning, the small language model learns to generate reasoning steps that adhere to these tags, effectively learning to produce more concise and structured reasoning. The key innovation is that the model internalizes the discipline such that at test time, even without explicit tags, it generates efficient and accurate reasoning chains. The training set size is moderate (5,079 samples), making the approach data-efficient.

## Key Results
- GPQA-diamond accuracy improves by 9.9 percentage points to 52.93% with 64.7% fewer tokens (from 5,875 to 2,073).
- MMLU-Pro accuracy increases by 9.1 points to 64.73% with a 31.2% token reduction.
- The model achieves peak performance even when control tags are omitted during inference, confirming internalization of disciplined reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
D-CoT works by explicitly training the model to recognize and adhere to reasoning intensity boundaries via control tags. These tags act as training signals that shape the model's internal reasoning process, encouraging it to avoid unnecessary elaboration (overthinking) and instead produce concise, stepwise logic. The internalization occurs because the model learns to associate certain reasoning patterns with the tag-mandated structure, and this learned behavior persists even when the tags are removed. Essentially, the tags serve as a scaffold that, once learned, can be discarded, leading to efficient reasoning without external guidance.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**: CoT enables models to break down complex problems into intermediate steps, improving accuracy on reasoning tasks. *Why needed*: D-CoT builds upon CoT by adding discipline. *Quick check*: Does the model generate interpretable reasoning steps?
2. **Overthinking in Small Models**: Small LMs often generate verbose, inefficient reasoning chains that may harm performance. *Why needed*: D-CoT directly targets overthinking to improve efficiency. *Quick check*: Compare token counts of naive CoT vs. D-CoT outputs.
3. **Controlled Generation via Tags**: Using special tokens (tags) to steer model behavior during training. *Why needed*: Tags provide explicit structure for the model to learn. *Quick check*: Are tags consistently used in training data?
4. **Internalization of Learned Behaviors**: The phenomenon where models retain trained behaviors even after removal of scaffolding. *Why needed*: Allows tag-free inference. *Quick check*: Evaluate performance without tags at test time.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Control tag insertion -> Fine-tuning with tag-conditioned data -> Internalized disciplined reasoning -> Efficient tag-free inference

**Critical path**: The method relies on three sequential steps: (1) preprocessing training data to inject control tags at appropriate reasoning boundaries; (2) fine-tuning the target small LM on this tagged data; (3) leveraging the model's learned internalization to generate concise reasoning during inference without tags. Each step is essential; failure at any stage breaks the chain.

**Design tradeoffs**: The use of control tags simplifies the training signal but requires careful design of tag placement rules. Overly rigid tagging might constrain reasoning flexibility, while too lenient tagging may not enforce discipline. The approach also assumes the model has sufficient capacity to internalize the structure, which may vary with model size and architecture.

**Failure signatures**: 
- If the model does not reduce token count when tags are removed, internalization failed.
- If accuracy does not improve or degrades, the tagging scheme may be misaligned with the reasoning process.
- If the model generates incoherent reasoning, the tags may have introduced unwanted constraints.

**First three experiments**:
1. Replicate the main results on GPQA-diamond and MMLU-Pro with Qwen3-8B, measuring both accuracy and token count with and without tags.
2. Perform an ablation study removing each control tag individually to assess their individual contributions.
3. Test the approach on a different model architecture (e.g., Llama 2 7B) to evaluate generalizability.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to a single model (Qwen3-8B) and two benchmarks; generalizability is uncertain.
- No analysis of individual control tag semantics or ablation of specific tags.
- Training data scale is fixed; performance with different dataset sizes is unknown.
- Internalization test lacks out-of-distribution robustness and multi-seed verification.
- Potential overfitting due to absence of separate validation sets.

## Confidence
- Accuracy and efficiency gains on Qwen3-8B: Medium (strong empirical results but narrow evaluation).
- Internalization of disciplined reasoning: Low (tested only on same benchmarks without tags, no generalization).
- Generalizability to other model architectures: Low (no cross-architecture experiments).
- Simultaneous improvement in accuracy and efficiency: Medium (observed but not stress-tested across diverse tasks).

## Next Checks
1. Cross-architecture replication: Apply D-CoT to at least two other model families (e.g., Llama, Mistral) across sizes 1B–8B on the same and additional reasoning benchmarks.
2. Robust internalization test: Evaluate tag-free inference on a held-out reasoning suite (e.g., BBH) across 5 random seeds; measure accuracy and token count stability.
3. Tag ablation and data scaling: Train ablations removing each control tag, and with data sizes {1k,5k,10k} to isolate contributions and sample efficiency.
