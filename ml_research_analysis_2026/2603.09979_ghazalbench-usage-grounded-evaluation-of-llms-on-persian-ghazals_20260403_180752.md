# GhazalBench: Usage-Grounded Evaluation of LLMs on Persian Ghazals

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.09979
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09979
*   **Reference Count:** 34
*   **Primary Result:** LLMs demonstrate strong semantic understanding of Persian ghazals (via paraphrasing) but exhibit very low exact verse recall (<12%), revealing a gap between meaning and form memorization, especially for low-resource languages.

## Executive Summary
GhazalBench introduces a usage-grounded benchmark to evaluate large language models on culturally canonical Persian poetry (Hafez's ghazals). The benchmark isolates two capabilities: poem-to-prose paraphrasing (semantic understanding) and canonical verse recall under realistic cue conditions (completion and multiple-choice). Results show models achieve high-quality paraphrasing (e.g., BERTScore F1 ~0.93) but fail at free recall of exact verses, even with semantic or partial lexical cues. Recognition-based tasks (multiple-choice) yield much higher accuracy, indicating retrieval is cue-dependent and familiarity-driven. A cross-lingual comparison with Shakespeare’s sonnets shows markedly higher recall for English, suggesting the limitation stems from training data density rather than model architecture.

## Method Summary
The benchmark uses 50 ghazals (438 couplets) from the Divan of Hafez, each paired with a reference prose explanation generated by Gemini 2.5 Flash and verified by experts. Models are evaluated zero-shot on two task families: (1) **Paraphrasing**: generating prose from a couplet, assessed via LLM-as-Judge (Gemini 3 Pro) and automatic metrics (BERTScore, chrF++, BLEU); (2) **Verse Recall**: given a cue (poet name + first verse, plus optional prose/salient words/shuffled second verse), either complete the missing verse or select the correct second verse from multiple-choice options. Distractors are generated using semantic similarity and metrical matching. A parallel English evaluation uses 50 Shakespeare sonnets. Persian text undergoes normalization (Unicode unification, diacritic removal). Success for completion is defined as a character error rate (CER) ≤0.2.

## Key Results
*   **Semantic vs. Form Gap:** All models achieve strong paraphrasing quality (LLM-as-Judge scores >4.0/5.0) but very low free completion recall (≤11.9% even with prose cues).
*   **Recall-Recognition Disparity:** Multiple-choice recognition accuracy is substantially higher (up to 98.9% with prose cues), showing models rely on familiarity with options rather than generative recall.
*   **Cross-Lingual Disparity:** English sonnet recall exceeds 80% under similar cues, while Persian recall remains below 12%, implicating training data density as a primary factor.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupling of Semantic and Surface-Form Memory
*   **Claim:** Semantic understanding of canonical poetry is decoupled from exact surface-form recall in current LLMs.
*   **Mechanism:** Exposure to poetic text during training builds robust abstract semantic representations, enabling accurate paraphrasing. However, the low-resource, sparse nature of Persian literary corpora prevents stable verbatim parametric encoding of specific canonical verses.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] gap between semantic understanding and exact recall; [section 4.2] strong paraphrase vs. very low completion rates (Table 2).
*   **Break Condition:** If paraphrasing fidelity drops proportionally with recall decline across languages, the decoupling assumption fails.

### Mechanism 2: Recognition-Dominant Retrieval
*   **Claim:** Verse retrieval is dominated by recognition over recall, especially under semantic or partial-lexical cues.
*   **Mechanism:** Models use cue-dependent associative retrieval or familiarity-based pattern matching. Recognition tasks provide external candidates, allowing reliance on weak semantic or metrical signals rather than generative reconstruction from parametric memory.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] recognition-based tasks substantially reduce gap; [section 4.2] multiple-choice >> completion (Tables 2,3); errors favor same-ghazal distractors.
*   **Break Condition:** If multiple-choice gains disappear under paraphrased-prose or shuffled-verse cues, recognition relies on surface overlap, not semantic/structural cues.

### Mechanism 3: Training Data Density as Primary Limiter
*   **Claim:** Form-level recall performance is primarily limited by training data density, not architectural constraints.
*   **Mechanism:** Greater exposure to English sonnets in training corpora leads to more robust verbatim encoding of canonical lines. Underrepresented Persian ghazals lack sufficient repetition for stable surface-form memorization, even if semantically understood.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] English recall markedly higher than Persian; [section 4.3] >80% vs. <12% (Table 4).
*   **Break Condition:** If fine-tuning on Persian ghazals closes the recall gap without hurting paraphrasing, data density is confirmed as the key factor.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Recall vs. Recognition (Tulving, 1972)
    *   **Why needed here:** The benchmark contrasts free completion (recall) with multiple-choice selection (recognition) to diagnose retrieval modes and isolate memorization.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why might a model score 95% on multiple-choice but 5% on completion for the same couplet?
*   **Concept:** Usage-Grounded Evaluation
    *   **Why needed here:** Tasks mirror real-world Persian poetic interactions (explanation, cue-based quotation) rather than generative poetry composition.
    *   **Quick Check:** How does evaluating a model’s ability to *explain* a Hafez couplet differ from evaluating its ability to *complete* it from the first line?
*   **Concept:** Low-Resource Language Modeling Challenges
    *   **Why needed here:** Persian classical poetry is a low-resource domain within a mid-resource language; sparse training signal affects form memorization.
    *   **Quick Check:** If GPT-4 scores 90% on Shakespeare recall but 10% on Hafez under identical prompts, what does that imply about its training data?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Dataset (Hafez/Shakespeare + Prose) -> Task Pipelines (Paraphrase / Completion / Recognition) -> Evaluation Engine (Zero-shot Inference + Scoring) -> Analysis (Recall-Recognition Gap, Cross-lingual)
*   **Critical Path:** Data collection (couplet + prose) -> Question generation (cue variants) -> Model inference (zero-shot) -> Scoring (automatic + LLM-judge) -> Analysis.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** Zero-shot tests parametric knowledge vs. fine-tuning (which could conflate memorization with adaptation); LLM-as-Judge is costly but aligns with human criteria vs. automatic metrics; cue diversity isolates mechanisms but increases dataset size.
*   **Failure Signatures:** High paraphrase + low completion: classic decoupling; low performance with shuffled cues: no stored representation; paraphrase robustness drop: overfitting to lexical overlap.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline paraphrase:** Run all models; expect BERTScore >0.9, LLM-judge >4.0; Claude > open-weight.
    2.  **Canonical continuation (poet-name-only cue):** Expect all models ≤12% recall; Gemma near zero.
    3.  **Cue ablation:** Compare +Prose vs. +ParaphrasedProse vs. +Salient vs. +Shuffle in completion and multiple-choice. Expect Shuffle cue highest completion; multiple-choice > completion by >50% absolute.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **OQ1:** Does the recall-recognition dissociation generalize to other cultural poetic traditions (e.g., Arabic, Chinese) and non-poetic canonical texts (e.g., religious scriptures)?
*   **OQ2:** Can targeted model adaptation (e.g., fine-tuning, retrieval augmentation) improve completion-based recall for underrepresented cultural texts without increasing unintended verbatim memorization?
*   **OQ3:** How do different prompting strategies (e.g., zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought) affect the recall-recognition gap and overall performance?
*   **OQ4:** Is the observed gap primarily due to the specific poet (Hafez) or the ghazal form? Would other Persian poets/forms show similar patterns?
*   **OQ5:** What specific characteristics of training data (e.g., token frequency, domain diversity) explain the stark performance disparity between high-resource and low-resource languages in poetic recall?

## Limitations
*   Study focuses on a single poet (Hafez) and form (ghazal), limiting generalizability across Persian literature.
*   Reference prose generation and LLM-as-Judge rely on proprietary models (Gemini), hindering full reproduction.
*   Key implementation details (Persian normalization rules, distractor similarity thresholds) are underspecified.

## Confidence
*   **Decoupling (semantic vs. form recall):** Medium. Strong task disparity evidence, but relies on indirect metrics without human-grounded form-memorization baseline.
*   **Recognition > recall:** High. Clear empirical gap across cues; error analysis supports cue-dependent retrieval.
*   **Data density as primary limiter:** Medium. Cross-lingual contrast compelling but lacks controlled corpus statistics; fine-tuning ablation needed.

## Next Checks
1.  **Human paraphrase audit:** Sample 50 model outputs; measure human-judged semantic fidelity and correlate with LLM-as-Judge scores.
2.  **Fine-tuning ablation:** Fine-tune a mid-size model (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B) on Hafez’s divan; re-evaluate completion recall. If gap closes, confirms data density.
3.  **Token frequency analysis:** Compute unigram/bigram frequencies of Hafez couplets in Persian web corpora vs. Shakespeare in English; correlate with model recall per verse.
