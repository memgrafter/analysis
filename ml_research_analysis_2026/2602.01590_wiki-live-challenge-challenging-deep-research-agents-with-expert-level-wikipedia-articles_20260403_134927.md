# Wiki Live Challenge: Challenging Deep Research Agents with Expert-Level Wikipedia Articles

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.01590
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01590
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Current Deep Research Agents (DRAs) perform far below expert-level standards, with the best proprietary system achieving only 58.33% win rate on writing quality and 30.76% factual coverage against Wikipedia Good Articles.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Wiki Live Challenge (WLC), a novel live benchmark designed to evaluate Deep Research Agents (DRAs) by comparing their generated articles against recent, expert-reviewed Wikipedia Good Articles. It presents the Wiki Eval framework, which separates evaluation into two orthogonal dimensions: writing quality (adherence to 39 Wikipedia-style criteria) and factual verifiability (coverage of Wikipedia's facts and accuracy of self-citations). Experiments on 11 state-of-the-art DRAs reveal a substantial performance gap, with even the best systems falling dramatically short of the expert-level reference standard, highlighting major unsolved challenges in automated long-form research and synthesis.

## Method Summary
The WLC benchmark uses 100 newly created Wikipedia Good Articles (from a 6-month window) as static, high-quality reference targets. For each topic, a DRA is prompted to generate an article without accessing the original Wikipedia page. The Wiki Eval framework then evaluates the output. **Wiki Writing** uses an LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-pro) to perform pairwise comparisons against the reference across 39 fine-grained criteria derived from Wikipedia's Manual of Style. **Wiki Fact** first extracts atomic facts from the reference and `(statement, citation)` pairs from the DRA output. It then measures (1) **Cov. Wiki**: the consistency of DRA statements with reference facts via retrieval and fact-checking, and (2) **Ref. Acc.**: the proportion of DRA statements fully supported by their own cited sources, verified by fetching and checking each URL.

## Key Results
*   A significant performance gap exists; the top proprietary model (Gemini-3-pro Deep Research) achieved a Wiki Writing win rate of 58.33% and a Cov. Wiki score of 30.76%.
*   Open-source DRA frameworks lagged severely, with the best open-source writing score at just 2.28% (Deep Researcher).
*   All evaluated systems exhibited low factual coverage and incurred non-trivial conflict rates, demonstrating that current DRAs cannot reliably produce expert-level, verifiable research articles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Live, Expert-Verified Gold Standard
The use of recent Wikipedia Good Articles (GAs) as references eliminates benchmark contamination from training data. The GA process involves peer review against stringent criteria for neutrality, verifiability, and comprehensiveness, providing a stable, objective proxy for "expert-level" output. The 6-month rolling window ensures references post-date most model training cutoffs.
*   **Core Assumption:** The Wikipedia GA review process is a reliable indicator of high-quality, encyclopedic content.
*   **Break Condition:** Systematic bias or a fundamental shift in GA criteria would invalidate the gold standard.

### Mechanism 2: Rubric Grounded in Documented Human Standards
The 39 evaluation criteria are reverse-engineered directly from Wikipedia's official Good Article guidelines and Manual of Style (e.g., "Words to Watch," lead section requirements). The LLM judge is prompted to make a pairwise decision for each specific, documented rule between the GA reference and the DRA output, making assessments traceable.
*   **Core Assumption:** These explicit editorial rules can be consistently interpreted and applied by an LLM for comparative judgment.
*   **Break Condition:** A flawed mapping from high-level GA principles to the 39 discrete criteria would misalign evaluation with true expert intent.

### Mechanism 3: Orthogonal Evaluation of Writing and Factuality
Wiki Writing (stylistic/encyclopedic adherence) and Wiki Fact (factual retrievability and citation integrity) are measured as independent dimensions. This separation prevents confounding and reveals distinct failure modes (e.g., a system could be well-written but factually sparse, or factually dense but poorly cited).
*   **Core Assumption:** Writing quality and factual verifiability are separable competencies for a DRA.
*   **Break Condition:** If the two dimensions are perfectly correlated in practice for this task, separate metrics provide no diagnostic value.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Get GA Article (Scheduler + Wikipedia API + GA filter) -> Run DRA (Unified wrapper/API client) -> Post-Process Output (Parser for text & citations) -> PARALLEL: Run Wiki Writing Eval (Judge LLM per criterion) AND Run Wiki Fact Eval (Extractor LLM -> [Coverage Scorer (retrieval + fact-check LLM)] & [Reference Accuracy Scorer (URL fetch + fact-check LLM)]) -> Results Aggregator

**Critical path:** Task Ingestion -> DRA Execution -> Output Parsing -> **Parallel Evaluation Paths** -> Score Aggregation.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Judge Model:** Cost/performance trade-off (Gemini-2.5-pro vs. cheaper open-source judges).
*   **Fact Extraction:** Speed/cost (Gemini-2.5-flash) vs. potential completeness errors that bias coverage downward.
*   **Leakage Handling:** Strict filtering of target Wikipedia citations is clean but may penalize legitimate coincidental source reuse.
*   **Reference Fetching:** Using a reader API (Jina) is scalable but may fail on paywalled/JS-heavy sites, measuring "accessible groundability."

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Systemic Low Performance (<10% Writing, <15% Cov. Wiki):** Indicates fundamental retrieval/synthesis failure (e.g., Deep Researcher).
*   **High Leakage + Medium Performance:** Suggests shortcutting by copying/paraphrasing Wikipedia (e.g., Perplexity's 33.77%).
*   **Disjuncture between Metrics:** High Writing + low Cov. Wiki = "eloquent but empty"; low Writing + high Cov. Wiki = "fact-dump but poorly structured."
*   **High Wiki Conf. vs. Low Ref. Conf.:** Retrieves internally consistent but incorrect information from flawed search results.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline Sanity Check:** Run a non-agentic LLM (e.g., GPT-4.1) with a simple "write a Wikipedia article" prompt (no web search). Expect near-zero Cov. Wiki, validating the "Deep" in DRA.
2.  **Judge Consistency Validation:** On a sample, run the 39-criteria evaluation with multiple Judge models (Gemini-2.5-pro, GPT-5, Qwen3-235B). Compute Pairwise Agreement Rate (PAR) against the human gold set to validate judge selection.
3.  **Category Difficulty & Robustness Check:** Run a strong DRA on all 100 tasks. Compute its win rate per category and perform ANOVA on its relative performance deviation. A significant result flags a category (e.g., History) as an outlier needing investigation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How can the WLC benchmark be scaled to hundreds or thousands of tasks while maintaining the quality, recency, and expert verification of Wikipedia Good Articles, given the limitations imposed by model knowledge cutoffs and the manual effort required for GA selection?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Task Scale: Due to constraints imposed by model knowledge cutoff dates, the number of collected Good Articles meeting our strict criteria is limited to the hundreds scale."
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies scale as a key limitation but does not propose a concrete, feasible methodology for large-scale curation that preserves the benchmark's core value.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A detailed, reproducible pipeline for continuous, automated scraping and filtering of candidate GAs that balances volume with strict adherence to GA criteria, possibly incorporating crowdsourced or semi-automated expert review.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What are the primary architectural, training data, or algorithmic reasons behind the stark performance gap between open-source DRAs (e.g., Deep Researcher at 2.28% Wiki Writing) and top proprietary systems (e.g., Gemini-3-pro at 58.33%)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Notably, fully open-source DRA frameworks lag significantly behind proprietary models... This suggests that achieving the long-form report generation capabilities of proprietary models remains a challenge for smaller models trained via end-to-end RL."
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper presents the performance gap as a key empirical finding but does not analyze the specific components responsible for this disparity.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies comparing open-source vs. proprietary DRAs, isolating factors like backbone model size, retrieval tool robustness, planning algorithm sophistication, and reinforcement learning reward design.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** To what extent do the low fact coverage scores (max ~30%) and specific difficulty gradients stem from inherent limitations in web-based information retrieval (e.g., paywalls, dynamic content, fragmented sources) versus deficiencies in the agent's synthesis and reasoning capabilities?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The heatmap analysis shows agents cover broad concepts but fail on granular, quantitative, or domain-specific details. The authors cite "lack of transparency in internal search mechanisms" and "inaccessibility of some cited web pages" as limitations affecting Reference Accuracy.
*   **Why unresolved:** The Wiki Fact metric conflates retrieval success with synthesis correctness. The paper cannot disentangle whether missing facts are due to the agent failing to find them online or failing to incorporate them correctly from found sources.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A diagnostic benchmark where the exact set of source documents used by the DRA is known and controlled, allowing separate measurement of retrieval recall and synthesis fidelity.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Can LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations using models like Gemini-2.5-pro reliably and consistently assess nuanced, fine-grained criteria such as "Neutral point of view" and "Broad in its coverage" at scale, or do they introduce their own biases that diverge from human expert judgments on complex editorial standards?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper validates Judge LLMs via PAR with human annotators (Table 4), but the highest agreement is 83.59%, leaving ~16% of judgments inconsistent. The framework relies entirely on this LLM judge.
*   **Why unresolved:** While human validation is performed on a small sample (390 annotations), the long-term reliability and bias of the LLM judge across 100 diverse articles and 39 criteria are not fully established.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale, multi-round human evaluation of the LLM judge's outputs on a stratified sample, coupled with an analysis of failure modes where the LLM disagrees with human experts.

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** What is the root cause of the "conflict" rates (e.g., statements contradicting Wikipedia facts or an agent's own citations)? Are these primarily due to retrieval of unreliable sources, incorrect fact extraction from sources, reasoning errors during synthesis, or a misalignment between the agent's generation policy and the verifiability constraint?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 2 shows distinct patterns: e.g., LangChain (GPT-4.1) has high Wiki Conflict (24.69%) but low Ref. Conf. (2.94%), while Qwen-3-max has high Ref. Conf. (6.87%). This decoupling suggests different failure modes.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper measures conflict as an outcome but does not perform fine-grained error analysis to classify the underlying cause of each conflicting statement.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A manual audit of a sample of conflicting statements, tracing each back to the agent's retrieved sources and reasoning steps to categorize errors into source reliability, extraction error, reasoning flaw, or generation hallucination.

## Limitations
*   **Gold Standard Validity:** Benchmark fidelity hinges on Wikipedia's GA process; systematic biases or evolving standards could undermine the "expert-level" reference claim.
*   **Evaluation Generalizability:** Heavy reliance on a single judge model (Gemini-2.5-pro) introduces model-specific biases; PAR validation uses a small human sample.
*   **Reproducibility Gaps:** Critical extraction and retrieval heuristics are underspecified (e.g., exact "nearest citation" logic, similarity search method), creating implementation variability.

## Confidence
*   **Benchmark Design (Live, GA-based):** **High**. The methodology for creating a contamination-resistant dataset is meticulous and directly addresses a known flaw.
*   **Evaluation Framework (39 Criteria + Fact Metrics):** **Medium**. The decomposition is logical, but operationalization depends on unstated quality of the LLM judge and extraction heuristics.
*   **Results (DRAs Fall Far Short):** **Medium**. Results are consistent and striking, but confidence is tempered by single-judge dependency and the particular 100-article sample; exact performance magnitudes may vary.

## Next Checks
1.  **Judge Model Sensitivity Analysis:** Re-run Wiki Writing evaluation on a subset of 20 articles using three diverse judge models (e.g., GPT-4.1, Claude-3.7, Qwen3-235B). Measure PAR against the human gold set and inter-judge consistency to test if the "drastic failure" conclusion holds.
2.  **Good Article Process Audit:** Manually review GA review histories for a stratified sample (e.g., 5 per category). Flag articles with contentious histories or borderline passes. Re-compute scores after excluding potential "weak GA" articles to assess benchmark robustness.
3.  **Metric Orthogonality Stress Test:** For all 11 DRAs, compute the correlation between Wiki Writing win rate and Cov. Wiki. Then, perform a multiple regression predicting human-rated "helpfulness" using both metrics as predictors. Low inter-correlation and both contributing significant independent variance would confirm their orthogonal utility.
