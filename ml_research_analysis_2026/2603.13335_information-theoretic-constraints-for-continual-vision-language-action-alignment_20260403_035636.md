# Information-Theoretic Constraints for Continual Vision-Language-Action Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13335
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13335
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Info-VLA achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy on the LIBERO benchmark, improving by 6–10% over strong baselines (e.g., ER), reaching 78.7% on LIBERO-Long B5-5N1 and 73.3% on LIBERO-Goal B0-5N1.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models during continual learning. It identifies the degradation of cross-modal information structure—caused by diffusion of attention patterns—as the core issue. To mitigate this, the authors propose Info-VLA, which combines Replay Anchor Contrastive Learning (using frozen teacher models as stable representation anchors) and Cross-Modal Mutual Information Maximization (preserving statistical dependencies between visual and language representations). On the LIBERO benchmark, Info-VLA consistently outperforms strong baselines like Experience Replay, achieving absolute gains of 6–10% in average accuracy. Ablations confirm that both constraints are essential and complementary. This work provides an information‑theoretic framework for maintaining alignment in continual VLA adaptation, a step toward robust lifelong robotic learning.

## Method Summary
Info-VLA builds upon a pre‑trained vision‑language model (SigLip vision encoder + Gemma language model) equipped with a lightweight action head for flow‑matching. During continual learning, after finishing each task the current model is frozen as a teacher. For a new task, the student processes batches from the new data and a replay buffer (storing one trajectory per old task). The total loss combines: (1) the standard action prediction loss (L_CL); (2) Replay Anchor Contrastive loss (L_RAC) that attracts student representations for replay samples to their teacher anchors while repelling other samples; and (3) Cross‑Modal Mutual Information loss (L_CMI) that maximizes mutual information between teacher and student visual‑language embeddings using a learned discriminator, with an added marginal consistency term. Only the action head’s parameters are updated, leaving the backbone stable. This design preserves cross‑modal alignment and mitigates forgetting.

## Key Results
- LIBERO‑Long B5‑5N1: Info-VLA AA 78.7% vs. ER 72.0% (+6.7%).
- LIBERO‑Goal B0‑5N1: Info-VLA AA 73.3% vs. ER 67.2% (+6.1%).
- Ablation: Removing either RAC or CMI causes significant performance drop, confirming both are essential.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Degradation of Cross-Modal Information Structure
- **Claim**: Catastrophic forgetting in VLA models is driven by the progressive diffusion of cross‑modal attention patterns during continual adaptation, which erodes task‑relevant visual grounding.
- **Mechanism**: As the model learns new tasks, the conditional dependencies between visual observations and language instructions distort, causing attention to spread from target objects to irrelevant background and weakening responses on key areas.
- **Core assumption**: The cross-modal alignment formed during pre‑training is fragile and susceptible to interference from sequential task learning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we observe that this degradation is related to the deterioration of cross-modal information structure, where dependencies among visual observations, language instructions, and actions progressively diffuse during continual adaptation."
  - [section 4.1]: Fig. 1 and text show attention maps becoming increasingly diffused after Task 1 training compared to the original after Task 0.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor paper "GNSP: Gradient Null Space Projection for Preserving Cross‑Modal Alignment in VLMs Continual Learning" similarly identifies alignment degradation as central to forgetting in VLMs.
- **Break condition**: If attention patterns for old tasks remain consistently focused after new‑task training, the proposed link between diffusion and forgetting is falsified.

### Mechanism 2: Replay Anchor Contrastive Learning
- **Claim**: Frozen teacher models provide stable representation anchors that protect historical cross‑modal alignment from being overwritten during new‑task adaptation.
- **Mechanism**: After each task, the current model is frozen as a teacher. For replay samples, teacher embeddings serve as positive anchors; student embeddings for the same samples are attracted to them, while other samples (including new‑task) are repelled. This enforces task‑level separation and preserves alignment manifold.
- **Core assumption**: The teacher’s frozen parameters encode a stable cross‑modal alignment structure that can guide the student.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Replay Anchor Contrastive Learning constructs stable alignment anchors from a frozen teacher model, preserving cross‑modal alignment in the representation space."
  - [section 4.2]: Equations (1) and text describe the asymmetric contrastive loss with teacher anchors.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor "Continual Learning with Vision‑Language Models via Semantic‑Geometry Preservation" also uses distillation to maintain pretrained geometry.
- **Break condition**: If contrastive loss is removed while keeping the teacher frozen, performance on old tasks degrades significantly, indicating the mechanism’s necessity.

### Mechanism 3: Cross‑Modal Mutual Information Maximization
- **Claim**: Maximizing mutual information between teacher and student visual‑language representations preserves global statistical dependencies (e.g., similarities, decision boundaries) that pairwise contrastive learning misses.
- **Mechanism**: A KL‑divergence‑based estimator maximizes I(Z_new; Z_old) using an MLP on concatenated representations, with an additional marginal consistency term to avoid degenerate solutions. This transfers the teacher’s distributional structure to the student.
- **Core assumption**: The teacher’s representation distribution encodes rich cross‑modal semantic structures that should be maintained across task transitions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Cross‑Modal Mutual Information Maximization further preserves dependency structure between visual and language representations through mutual information constraints."
  - [section 4.3]: Equations (2)–(5) define the mutual information and marginal consistency losses.
  - [corpus]: Information‑theoretic continual learning is explored in other multimodal contexts (e.g., neighbor "MANTA: Cross‑Modal Semantic Alignment and Information‑Theoretic Optimization").
- **Break condition**: If ablating the CMI term while keeping RAC does not reduce cross‑modal structural metrics (e.g., attention coherence), the mechanism is not essential.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting**
  - **Why needed here**: The paper’s core goal is mitigating catastrophic forgetting in VLAs; understanding its typical form—performance collapse on old tasks after new‑task training—is prerequisite.
  - **Quick check question**: How does catastrophic forgetting in multimodal models differ from that in single‑modality classifiers?

- **Concept: Contrastive Learning**
  - **Why needed here**: RAC relies on contrastive objectives to align student representations with teacher anchors; knowing the basics of contrastive losses (e.g., InfoNCE) is necessary.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the role of the temperature parameter τ in contrastive loss, and how does it affect alignment?

- **Concept: Mutual Information**
  - **Why needed here**: CMI maximizes mutual information to preserve statistical dependencies; a basic grasp of information theory (e.g., KL divergence, joint vs. marginal distributions) is required.
  - **Quick check question**: Why might maximizing mutual information alone lead to degenerate solutions, and how does the marginal consistency term address this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**  
  Pre‑trained VLM backbone (SigLip vision encoder + Gemma language model) → Cross‑attention fusion layers → Action head. Teacher model (frozen copy after each task) and Replay buffer (stores one trajectory per old task) support training.

- **Critical path**  
  1. Train initial task T₀ with only L_CL; freeze model as teacher.  
  2. For each new task T_k (k>0): sample batch from D_k ∪ replay buffer M.  
  3. Compute total loss L = L_CL + λ₁ L_RAC + λ₂ L_CMI.  
  4. Update student parameters (only action head).  
  5. After training on T_k, freeze current student as new teacher and store one trajectory from T_k into M.

- **Design tradeoffs**  
  - Frozen teacher vs. updating: Freezing provides stable anchors but may become outdated; however, only action head updates, so backbone remains stable.  
  - Replay buffer size: One trajectory per task minimizes memory but may not capture full task distribution; larger buffers could improve retention at memory cost.  
  - Hyperparameters λ₁, λ₂: Set to 0.1; higher values strengthen preservation but may hinder adaptation to new tasks.

- **Failure signatures**  
  - L_RAC stagnant: Teacher and student representations not aligning—check teacher freezing, contrastive batch construction, or temperature τ.  
  - L_CMI NaN or exploding: Mutual information estimator unstable—ensure MLP output is properly normalized (softmax) and gradients are clipped.  
  - Old‑task success rate drops sharply: Forgetting not mitigated—verify replay buffer content, teacher freshness, and λ weights.

- **First 3 experiments**  
  1. Ablation study (Table 3): Removing either RAC or CMI reduces performance, confirming complementary roles.  
  2. Benchmark comparison (Tables 1,2): Info‑VLA outperforms ER, EWC, Sequential on LIBERO‑Long and LIBERO‑Goal.  
  3. Attention visualization (Fig. 4): Info‑VLA better preserves original cross‑modal attention patterns compared to ER.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does Info‑VLA's performance transfer to real robotic hardware?  
  - *Basis in paper*: The conclusion states they are exploring real robotic platforms, but no real‑world results are presented.  
  - *Why unresolved*: Simulation‑to‑real gaps may affect cross‑modal information structure and forgetting metrics.  
  - *Resolution evidence*: Deploy on physical robots with task sequences mirroring LIBERO; measure success rates and attention stability.

- **Open Question 2**: Is the frozen teacher anchor effective for long sequences (>10 tasks)?  
  - *Basis in paper*: Teachers are frozen after each task but experiments only cover up to 10 tasks.  
  - *Why unresolved*: Teacher representations may become misaligned over many tasks, weakening anchor stability.  
  - *Resolution evidence*: Extend to 20+ tasks, track teacher‑student alignment metrics, compare against refreshed teachers.

- **Open Question 3**: How sensitive is CMI to the choice of mutual information estimator?  
  - *Basis in paper*: Uses an MLP parameterization without comparing alternatives.  
  - *Why unresolved*: Different estimators have varying biases; unsuitable estimator may misrepresent dependencies.  
  - *Resolution evidence*: Replace estimator with MINE/CLUB, evaluate cross‑modal structure preservation.

- **Open Question 4**: Are λ₁, λ₂ robust across architectures and task distributions?  
  - *Basis in paper*: Ablation fixes λ₁=λ₂=0.1 from π₀.5 experiments; no robustness analysis.  
  - *Why unresolved*: Optimal weighting likely depends on model capacity, pretraining, and task relatedness.  
  - *Resolution evidence*: Hyperparameter sweeps across multiple VLA backbones and diverse sequences.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to simulated LIBERO tasks; real‑world robotic applicability remains unverified.
- Replay buffer uses only one trajectory per task, which may not adequately represent task distributions, potentially limiting retention for complex tasks.
- Hyperparameters λ₁, λ₂ and the mutual information estimator are fixed without extensive sensitivity analysis, raising questions about generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Claim**: Catastrophic forgetting in VLA models is primarily caused by degradation of cross‑modal information structure.  
  **Confidence**: High (supported by attention visualizations and ablation results).
- **Claim**: Replay Anchor Contrastive Learning with frozen teacher anchors preserves cross‑modal alignment and reduces forgetting.  
  **Confidence**: High (ablation shows performance drop without RAC).
- **Claim**: Cross‑Modal Mutual Information maximization preserves global dependencies beyond pairwise contrast.  
  **Confidence**: Medium (benefit shown in ablations, but estimator sensitivity not explored).
- **Claim**: Info‑VLA scales to long continual learning sequences (e.g., >10 tasks).  
  **Confidence**: Medium (tested up to 10 tasks; long‑term stability unproven).
- **Claim**: The method is architecture‑agnostic and will transfer to other VLA backbones.  
  **Confidence**: Low (only evaluated on π₀.5 backbone; no cross‑architecture experiments).
