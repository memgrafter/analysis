# Learning to Explore with Parameter-Space Noise: A Deep Dive into Parameter-Space Noise for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.02555  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02555  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: PSN-GRPO improves pass@k on math benchmarks (e.g., +8.9% on AIME24 at pass@256) and increases semantic/operation diversity, outperforming baselines and combining additively with other methods.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) often struggles to explore beyond existing reasoning strategies, particularly in long-horizon chain-of-thought (CoT) tasks. This paper introduces parameter-space noise for RLVR (PSN-RLVR), which injects Gaussian noise into model parameters before rollout generation to induce consistent, trajectory-level exploration. Off-policy mismatch from perturbed rollouts is mitigated via truncated importance sampling. A real-time adaptive noise scheduler—using semantic diversity and model self-certainty as proxies—replaces expensive KL divergence computation. Applied to the GRPO algorithm, PSN-GRPO yields substantial gains in pass@k across multiple math benchmarks and model families, and demonstrates additive improvements when combined with other exploration techniques.

## Method Summary
PSN-RLVR modifies the standard RLVR pipeline by first perturbing the model’s parameters with adaptive Gaussian noise. The perturbed model then generates rollouts, which are evaluated using verifiable rewards. To correct the distribution shift introduced by the perturbation, truncated importance sampling weights the rollouts before performing a GRPO policy gradient update. The noise magnitude is adaptively adjusted in real time based on two signals: semantic diversity (measured via embedding similarity of generated solutions) and model self‑certainty (e.g., output entropy). This avoids the computational cost of explicitly tracking KL divergence to a reference policy. The overall loop repeats, with the scheduler increasing or decreasing noise to maintain a balance between exploration and exploitation.

## Key Results
- PSN-GRPO consistently improves pass@k on large sampling budgets (e.g., pass@256) across multiple math benchmarks and model families, while also increasing semantic and operation diversity.
- It outperforms alternative exploration methods like pass@k training and action-space noise, and shows additive gains when combined.
- On AIME24, PSN-GRPO achieves +8.9% pass@256 over the best temperature‑scaling baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Injecting noise directly into parameters perturbs the entire policy uniformly across an entire rollout, which is more effective for long‑horizon CoT reasoning than action‑space noise that may accumulate or cancel. The adaptive scheduler ensures that exploration is intensified precisely when the model is overly confident or when solution diversity drops, avoiding manual tuning. Truncated importance sampling stabilizes learning by reducing the variance of off‑policy corrections, making the approach viable even with sparse, verifiable rewards.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Model Parameters -> Noise Injection -> Perturbed Parameters -> Rollout Generation -> Reward Evaluation -> Truncated Importance Sampling -> GRPO Update -> Adaptive Scheduler (Semantic Diversity & Self‑Certainty) -> Noise Scale Adjustment -> (loop back to Model Parameters)

**Critical path:**  
1. Sample noise from a zero‑mean Gaussian with current variance.  
2. Perturb model parameters.  
3. Generate CoT rollouts with perturbed model.  
4. Compute verifiable rewards for each rollout.  
5. Compute truncated importance sampling weights relative to the unperturbed policy.  
6. Perform GRPO policy gradient update using weighted rollouts.  
7. Measure semantic diversity (e.g., embedding clustering) and self‑certainty (e.g., average entropy) from recent rollouts.  
8. Update noise variance based on these signals (e.g., increase if diversity low or certainty high).  
9. Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Noise magnitude:* Too low yields insufficient exploration; too high harms stability and slows learning.  
- *Truncation threshold:* Lower variance vs. higher bias in importance weights.  
- *Scheduler proxies:* Semantic diversity and self‑certainty are cheap but may not perfectly track true KL divergence; could be task‑dependent.  
- *Overhead:* Additional metrics (diversity, certainty) increase per‑iteration compute but avoid expensive KL calculations.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Exploding gradients or diverging policy loss indicate excessive noise or unstable importance weights.  
- Plateauing diversity despite scheduler increases suggests the scheduler may be stuck or the diversity metric ineffective.  
- Poor performance on problems with extremely sparse rewards (e.g., only one correct solution) may indicate the method cannot discover novel solutions even with high noise.

**First experiments:**  
1. Compare PSN‑GRPO against standard GRPO on a math benchmark (e.g., MATH) with a fixed sampling budget; measure pass@k.  
2. Ablate the adaptive scheduler by replacing it with a fixed noise schedule or a true KL‑based scheduler; evaluate performance and stability.  
3. Quantify semantic and operation diversity of generated rollouts from PSN‑GRPO vs. baselines to verify exploration claims.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What theoretical exploration guarantees does PSN‑RLVR provide for RLVR with verifiable rewards?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper connects PSN to Thompson sampling but doesn't extend theoretical guarantees to RLVR with sparse rewards and long horizons. The adaptive scheduler is heuristic without formal justification.  
- Why unresolved: Without regret bounds or exploration metrics, we cannot confirm PSN achieves deep exploration versus superficial improvements.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Derive formal guarantees for PSN in RLVR; prove coverage of solution space; empirically correlate noise with exploration metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Are semantic diversity and self‑certainty optimal surrogate signals for real‑time adaptive noise control in RLVR?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Variant II uses these signals to avoid KL computation but the choice is empirical; other combinations may be more effective or robust across tasks.  
- Why unresolved: The surrogate design space remains unexplored; sensitivity to task characteristics is unknown.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation of surrogate combinations; correlation studies with true KL divergence; evaluation on diverse benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does PSN‑RLVR generalize to other verifiable reward domains beyond mathematical reasoning (e.g., code generation, fact verification)?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] All experiments are on math benchmarks. The method is claimed general for RLVR with verifiable rewards but hasn't been tested elsewhere.  
- Why unresolved: Different domains may require different noise characteristics; success in math doesn't guarantee transfer.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply PSN‑GRPO to code benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP) and fact‑checking datasets; measure pass@k and diversity gains.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does PSN‑RLVR scale to model sizes beyond 10B parameters in terms of exploration effectiveness and scheduler overhead?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Experiments use 4B–7B models only; scaling behavior to 70B+ models is unaddressed despite industry trend.  
- Why unresolved: Larger models may have different noise sensitivity; scheduler overhead may become prohibitive; real‑time adaptation could fail at scale.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Run experiments on 70B+ models; analyze noise magnitude scaling laws; measure wall‑clock overhead; adjust scheduler if needed.
