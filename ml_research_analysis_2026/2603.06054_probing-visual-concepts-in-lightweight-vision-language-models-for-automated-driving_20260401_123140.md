# Probing Visual Concepts in Lightweight Vision-Language Models for Automated Driving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06054
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06054
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Object presence is explicitly linearly encoded across VLM layers, but fine-grained spatial concepts like orientation are only implicit; failures split into perceptual (info not encoded) and cognitive (info present but misaligned), with the vision encoder as the primary bottleneck for long-range representations.

## Executive Summary
This study probes the internal representations of four sub-4B parameter vision-language models (VLMs) designed for automated driving. Using counterfactual traffic images generated in the CARLA simulator—varying only one visual concept at a time (presence, count, spatial relationship, orientation)—the authors train linear probes on activations from the vision encoder, projector, and LLM. They find that coarse concepts like object presence are explicitly and linearly encoded from mid-vision through the LLM, whereas fine-grained spatial concepts like orientation are only implicitly retained via spatial structure, exhibiting near-zero linear separability. Comparing probe accuracy to model performance reveals two distinct failure modes: perceptual failure (information not linearly encoded) and cognitive failure (information present but mismatched to language semantics). Distance is a critical degradation factor, with the vision encoder identified as the primary bottleneck for long-range representations.

## Method Summary
The authors create counterfactual image pairs in CARLA, each differing in exactly one visual concept at distances from 5 to 50 meters. Activations are extracted from every layer of four VLMs (Ovis2.5-2B, InternVL3.5-2B, VST-3B). For all concepts, activations are average-pooled across patch tokens. For spatial tasks (relationship, orientation), additional region-pooling is applied: the 2D patch grid is split into left/right halves, each pooled separately and concatenated. Linear classifiers are trained on these pooled vectors to assess linear separability. Model accuracy is measured via single-word VQA prompts. The gap between probe and model accuracies diagnoses perceptual (both low) versus cognitive (probe high, model low) failures. Distance ablation tracks how probe accuracy degrades across layers to locate bottlenecks.

## Key Results
- **Explicit vs. implicit encoding:** Object presence is linearly separable from mid-vision encoder through LLM; orientation remains near chance even with region-pooling, indicating implicit-only encoding via spatial layout.
- **Two failure modes:** Perceptual failures occur when probe accuracy is low (information never encoded). Cognitive failures show large accuracy gaps (up to ~50% for spatial tasks at short distances), where linearly encoded information is misaligned with output semantics.
- **Distance bottleneck:** Linear separability degrades rapidly with increasing object distance, with the sharpest drops occurring in vision encoder layers, identifying it as the primary bottleneck for long-range representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Visual concepts exhibit distinct linear encoding patterns across VLM layers. Coarse concepts (presence) are explicitly encoded; fine-grained spatial concepts (orientation) are only implicitly retained via spatial structure.  
**Mechanism:** Counterfactual images isolate single concepts. High linear probe accuracy on pooled activations indicates explicit, linearly accessible encoding. For orientation, region-pooling preserves minimal left/right structure; near-zero accuracy even then implies information is present only in non-linear spatial configurations, not as a linear direction.  
**Core assumption:** Linear probe accuracy directly reflects whether the concept is linearly separable in that layer’s representation space.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states presence is explicitly linear, orientation implicit with near-zero separability. Section 5.1 (Figures 4/5) shows high presence probe accuracy vs. near-zero orientation accuracy, even with region-pooling. Corpus validates linear probing for visual representations.  
**Break condition:** If region-pooled orientation probes exceed ~85% accuracy, the implicit-only claim is contradicted.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Model failures on simple visual questions fall into two modes: perceptual (information not linearly encoded) and cognitive (info encoded but misaligned with language output).  
**Mechanism:** Compare probe accuracy (what’s available in activations) to model accuracy (what’s correctly output). A gap (probe > model) indicates cognitive failure; both low indicates perceptual failure.  
**Core assumption:** Last-layer probe accuracy measures information accessible to the final linear layer; model accuracy measures successful mapping to output tokens. The gap isolates misalignment from missing information.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports accuracy gaps up to ~50% for spatial tasks. Section 5.3 & Figure 7 show cognitive gaps vary across models. Corpus shows VLMs often attend correctly but answer incorrectly.  
**Break condition:** Perfect correlation between probe and model accuracies across all tasks would collapse the two-mode distinction.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Object distance critically degrades linear separability, with the vision encoder as the primary bottleneck for long-range representations.  
**Mechanism:** Test counterfactuals at varying distances (5–50 m). Track probe accuracy degradation per layer. The component where degradation first appears and persists indicates the bottleneck.  
**Core assumption:** Distance-related accuracy drop reflects loss of visual detail; the layer with maximum degradation is where information is irretrievably lost.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states distance rapidly degrades linear separability, vision encoder is primary bottleneck. Section 5.1 shows large accuracy drops from 5 m to 50 m in vision encoder layers, smaller drops in LLM layers. Corpus lacks this distance-specific analysis.  
**Break condition:** Stable probe accuracy across distances would mean distance is not a factor, contradicting the finding.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Model Architecture:** Three-stage pipeline: vision encoder (image → patch tokens), projector (maps visual tokens to LLM embedding space), LLM (processes multimodal sequence). Crucial for interpreting where bottlenecks occur.  
  *Quick check:* Does the CLS token from the vision encoder typically reach the LLM? (Usually no; only patch tokens are projected.)
- **Linear Probing as Interpretability:** Train linear classifiers on frozen activations to test if concepts are linearly accessible. Standard tool for diagnosing representation quality.  
  *Quick check:* 95% probe accuracy on ImageNet from ResNet activations indicates what? (Activations contain linearly separable ImageNet-class information.)
- **Counterfactual Control:** Create image pairs differing in exactly one concept to isolate probe targets. Datasets like COCO vary many confounders (lighting, co-occurrence).  
  *Quick check:* Why not use COCO images directly for probing spatial orientation? (Scenes vary in layout, lighting, object pairs; impossible to isolate orientation as the sole varying factor.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Vision Encoder -> Projector -> LLM. Models: Ovis2.5-2B uses SigLIP2; InternVL3.5-2B uses InternViT with 8-tile processing; VST-3B uses QwenViT. All LLMs are Qwen variants (1.7B or 3B).
- **Critical path:** For each image: (1) extract every vision encoder block’s patch activations; (2) get projector output; (3) get every LLM layer’s activations (including visual tokens). Apply average-pooling globally; for spatial/orientation tasks, also apply region-pooling (split 2D patch grid left/right, pool separately, concatenate). Train a linear probe per (concept, distance, layer).
- **Design tradeoffs:** InternVL3.5’s tiling preserves high-resolution details but disrupts cross-tile spatial relationships. Ovis2.5’s massive projector (~400M parameters) may explain its relatively better spatial performance. VST’s spatial-pretraining did not overcome orientation limitations despite specialized data.
- **Failure signatures:** Perceptual: probe accuracy ≈ chance in early layers (e.g., <70% for orientation). Cognitive: probe >90% but model <70% (gap >20%). Distance degradation: >20% probe drop from 5 m to 50 m in vision encoder layers.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline evaluation:** Run all models on the full counterfactual dataset (all concepts, all distances) using fixed prompts (Table 2). Record VQA accuracy to establish failure modes and accuracy gaps.  
  2. **Activation extraction:** Implement exact pooling: average-pool all patch tokens; for spatial tasks, reconstruct 2D grid from token order, split left/right, pool each half, concatenate. For InternVL3.5, process first 8 tiles as described. Save compressed vectors per layer.  
  3. **Linear probing:** For each (concept, distance, layer), train 10 linear probes with different seeds. Use AdamW, grid-search learning rate [1e-4, 5e-1] based on validation performance. Report mean test accuracy ± standard deviation. Reproduces Figures 4/5.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling effects:** Do larger VLMs (>4B parameters) exhibit the same bottlenecks and failure modes, or do architectural scaling and training data quantity alter the flow of visual information?
2. **Real-world generalization:** How do findings based on synthetic CARLA data generalize to real-world traffic scenes with natural variation in lighting, weather, sensor noise, and scene complexity?
3. **Non-linear probing:** Do non-linear probing methods detect visually relevant information that linear probes miss, particularly for fine-grained spatial concepts like orientation?
4. **Mitigating perceptual failures:** What specific architectural or training interventions can mitigate perceptual failures, where visual information is not linearly encoded early in the model?
5. **Cognitive failure mechanisms:** What mechanisms underlie cognitive failure, where linearly encoded visual information is not used correctly by the LLM, and how can training align representations with language semantics?

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Exact CARLA scene parameters (spawn coordinates, weather) and the precise 2D patch-grid reconstruction—especially for InternVL3.5’s tiled inputs—are underspecified. Region-pooling assumptions may not match the authors’ implementation.
- **Probing sensitivity:** Linear probing may not fully capture implicit encodings; results for spatial concepts heavily depend on the pooling strategy (average vs. region), which is not exhaustively justified.
- **Generalizability:** Study restricted to four sub-4B VLMs and synthetic driving scenes. Findings may not transfer to larger models, different architectures (e.g., diffusion VLMs), or non-driving domains with varied visual distributions.

## Confidence
- **High:** Object presence is explicitly linearly encoded from mid-vision through LLM; perceptual vs. cognitive failure dichotomy; vision encoder as primary bottleneck for long-range representations (directly supported by multiple figures and consistent across models).
- **Medium:** Fine-grained spatial concepts (orientation) are *only* implicitly encoded (relies on region-pooled probe accuracy near zero; could be sensitive to alternative region definitions or pooling).
- **Low:** Cognitive failures are caused by misalignment between visual encoding and language semantics (correlational; no causal intervention definitively proves misalignment vs. other factors like LLM capacity).

## Next Checks
1. **Validate region-pooling for tiled inputs:** Implement the exact tile-stitching and patch-ordering logic used by InternVL3.5, then re-run orientation probes. If accuracy rises above 85%, the implicit-only claim is weakened.
2. **Isolate distance vs. resolution effects:** Render counterfactuals at varying distances while *holding object pixel size constant* by adjusting camera FOV. This decouples representational loss from mere resolution drop.
3. **Cross-model projector ablation:** Freeze a common vision encoder (e.g., SigLIP) and only vary projectors across models. Train probes on projector outputs to isolate the projector’s contribution to distance-related spatial degradation.
