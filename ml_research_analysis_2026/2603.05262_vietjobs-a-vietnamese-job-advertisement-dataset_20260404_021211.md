# VietJobs: A Vietnamese Job Advertisement Dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: `2603.05262`
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05262
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: A dataset of 48,092 Vietnamese job postings (15M words) enables LLM benchmarks: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct achieved top few-shot classification accuracy 0.47 (Macro F1 0.42) and Llama-SEA-LION fine-tuned on combined data reached salary estimation RMSE 12.40 (M VND) and R² 0.16.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces VietJobs, a large-scale Vietnamese job advertisement dataset collected from TopCV, covering all 34 provinces and 16 occupational domains. The dataset addresses the scarcity of Vietnamese NLP resources for labour market analysis. The authors benchmark ten large language models on two tasks: job category classification (16 classes) and salary estimation. Results show that few-shot prompting with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct yields the highest classification accuracy (0.47), while domain adaptation via fine-tuning Llama-SEA-LION on a combination of VietJobs and an external Vietnam Jobs dataset improves salary prediction (RMSE 12.40, R² 0.16). The work establishes a new benchmark for Vietnamese NLP and highlights the value of regional models and domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
Data collection spanned seven days using Crawl4AI, followed by LLM-assisted parsing (GPT-4o/Gemini 2.5) into structured JSON. Preprocessing normalized salaries to monthly million VND, consolidated 24 raw job categories into 16 standardized domains, and performed an 80/10/10 train/dev/test split. Models were evaluated under zero-shot, few-shot, and LoRA fine-tuning regimes. Fine-tuning used low-rank adaptation (rank 8, alpha 16, dropout 0.2) on attention projection layers, with AdamW (lr=5e-5), batch size 64, and 2 epochs. Classification metrics included accuracy and Macro F1; salary estimation used RMSE and R², with strict parsing of model outputs (e.g., "X triệu"). The experimental design demonstrates the efficacy of instruction-tuned and regionally pretrained LLMs for Vietnamese job analysis.

## Key Results
- Few-shot classification: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct achieved 0.47 accuracy and 0.42 Macro F1.
- Salary estimation: Llama-SEA-LION-v3-8B-IT fine-tuned on VietJobs + Vietnam Jobs reached RMSE 12.40 and R² 0.16 on the combined test set.
- Dataset scale and scope: 48,092 postings (15M words), covering all 34 Vietnamese provinces and 16 occupational domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Domain adaptation for salary estimation
Combining VietJobs with an additional Vietnamese job dataset (Vietnam Jobs) for fine-tuning exposes models to a broader distribution of salary contexts, job titles, and industry representations, improving predictive generalisation. Evidence: Fine-tuned Llama-SEA-LION achieved RMSE 12.40 and R² 0.16 on the combined test set, outperforming single-dataset training (Table 4). Break condition would be incompatible salary normalisation or occupational taxonomy mismatches between datasets.

### Mechanism 2: Few-shot prompting for classification
Instruction-tuned multilingual LLMs leverage in-context examples to capture salient lexical and structural cues (e.g., skill mentions, industry jargon) that map Vietnamese job descriptions to the predefined 16-category taxonomy, relying on pretrained cross-lingual knowledge. Evidence: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct few-shot accuracy (0.47) exceeded its zero-shot (0.31) and fine-tuned (0.34) scores (Table 3). Break condition could be extremely short descriptions or heavy code-switching outside example distribution.

### Mechanism 3: Regional LLM adaptation
Southeast Asia-focused models (e.g., Llama-SEA-LION) exhibit strong Vietnamese performance due to tokenisation aligned with regional linguistic features and pretraining on typologically related languages (Thai, Indonesian), reducing out-of-vocabulary issues for Vietnamese compounding and code-switching. Evidence: SEA-LION consistently ranked among the top performers in both few-shot classification and salary estimation (sections 5.1, 5.2). Break condition would be platform-specific jargon absent from regional pretraining.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vietnamese linguistic complexity**: Tonal, compounding morphology and English loanwords affect tokenisation and model understanding; quick check: *How might compounding in job titles (e.g., "kỹ sư phần mềm") impact subword tokenisation compared to English?*
- **Occupational taxonomy normalisation**: Merging 24 raw platform categories into 16 standard domains ensures consistent labels; quick check: *What ambiguities could arise when merging "Financial Analyst" and "Accountant" into a single "Finance" category?*
- **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)**: Enables adaptation of large models with limited compute; quick check: *Why might a low LoRA rank (r=8) be sufficient, and what risks does higher dropout (0.2) mitigate?*

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data ingestion (Crawl4AI scraper → LLM-assisted parser → structured JSON) → Preprocessing (salary normalisation, category consolidation, data split) → Modeling (zero/few-shot prompting OR LoRA fine-tuning) → Evaluation (Accuracy/Macro F1 for classification; RMSE/R² for salary).
Critical path: Collection (7 days) → Schema mapping → Split → Hyperparameter configuration → Training (5h per classification model, 1–2h per salary model) → Final test evaluation.
Design tradeoffs: Single-platform source (TopCV) vs. diversity; LoRA vs. full fine-tuning (compute vs. adaptation depth); strict salary format enforcement vs. parsing flexibility.
Failure signatures: Zero-shot outputs deviate from canonical labels; few-shot performance drops if examples underrepresent rare categories; fine-tuning shows overfitting (e.g., Ministral accuracy decline) or unstable negative R².

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does expanding VietJobs to include multiple recruitment platforms and longitudinal data affect the robustness and generalizability of NLP models for Vietnamese job advertisements?
2. What impact does incorporating multilingual and demographic annotations have on fairness-aware and cross-lingual analysis of Vietnamese job ads?
3. Can advanced modeling techniques like retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) or domain-adaptive pretraining significantly improve performance on Vietnamese job classification and salary estimation compared to standard fine-tuning?
4. How do traditional machine learning approaches (e.g., TF-IDF features with classifiers) compare to LLM-based methods in accuracy, efficiency, and resource requirements for Vietnamese job-related tasks?

## Limitations
- Few-shot protocol specifics (number of in-context examples, selection strategy) are unreported, impairing reproducibility of the classification benchmark. This omission makes it difficult to assess how the choice of few-shot examples influences performance and to compare fairly with other few-shot studies.
- Verbatim prompt templates for zero/few-shot and fine-tuning are not provided, risking output formatting and parsing inconsistencies. Without the exact prompts, it's unclear if the models were instructed to output in a specific format, which could lead to parsing errors and affect the reported metrics.
- The integration process (schema alignment, normalization) between VietJobs and the external Vietnam Jobs dataset is undocumented, blocking exact replication of combined experiments. The absence of this documentation prevents verification that the two datasets were merged consistently, which is critical for the validity of the combined training results.

## Confidence
- Domain adaptation for salary estimation (Mechanism 1): Medium – The paper presents comparative results (Table 4) indicating improvement from combining datasets, but without an ablation study that isolates the contribution of each dataset, confidence is moderate. Moreover, the lack of external validation on a truly independent dataset limits generalizability.
- Few-shot classification efficacy (Mechanism 2): Medium – The performance gap between few-shot and other settings is demonstrated, but the report does not investigate prompt variations or the representativeness of the in-context examples, leaving open the possibility that results are prompt-sensitive.
- Regional LLM robustness (Mechanism 3): Low-Medium – SEA-LION's strong performance is noted, but the evidence is correlational; without controlled experiments that disentangle regional pretraining from model size or architecture, the specific contribution of regional adaptation remains uncertain.
