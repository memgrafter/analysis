# CHiL(L)Grader: Calibrated Human-in-the-Loop Short-Answer Grading

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11957  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11957  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** Temperature scaling reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by 53–65%, selective prediction automates 35–65% of responses at expert-level quality (QWK ≥ 0.80), and continual learning improves QWK from 0.458 to 0.882 after one adaptation cycle.

## Executive Summary
CHiL(L)Grader is a human-in-the-loop framework for automated short-answer grading that tackles two key challenges in using large language models (LLMs): overconfidence and distribution shift. By integrating post-hoc temperature scaling for confidence calibration, confidence-based selective prediction to route uncertain cases to human graders, and continual learning with a replay buffer from human corrections, the system achieves reliable and adaptive grading. Temperature scaling reduces Expected Calibration Error by 53–65%, yielding well-calibrated confidence (ECE < 0.1). Selective prediction automates 35–65% of responses while maintaining expert-level quality (QWK ≥ 0.80), and the confidence gate effectively separates easy from hard cases (QWK gap +0.347). Moreover, each human feedback cycle yields substantial performance gains; on the DAMI dataset, QWK improves from 0.458 to 0.882 after one adaptation.

The approach uses an instruction-tuned base LLM (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B), fine-tuned with LoRA adapters to follow grading rubrics. During deployment, calibrated confidence scores determine which responses are auto-graded. Human corrections on the remaining responses are combined with a scale-aware replay buffer of historical examples to fine-tune the adapters, after which temperature is recalibrated. This closed loop enables the model to adapt to new exam questions and rubrics while avoiding catastrophic forgetting, demonstrating a practical path toward trustworthy LLM-assisted grading.

## Method Summary
CHiL(L)Grader begins by instruction-tuning a pretrained LLM on historical short-answer grading data, using prompts that ask the model to output a structured JSON grade respecting the maximum grade scale. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA adapters to preserve the base weights. Next, post-hoc temperature scaling is applied on a held-out calibration set to align the model’s confidence with its actual accuracy, minimizing Expected Calibration Error (ECE). During deployment on a new exam, the calibrated model predicts a grade and confidence for each response; only predictions with confidence above a threshold τ are auto-accepted, while the rest are deferred to human graders. The threshold τ is selected to meet a reliability target (e.g., QWK ≥ 0.80) on the calibration set while maximizing coverage.

After each exam, the collected human corrections form a feedback set. To prevent catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning on this new data, the system maintains a replay buffer containing a scale-aware sample of historical questions retrieved via Sentence-BERT similarity. The LoRA adapters are fine-tuned on the union of corrections and replay samples, after which temperature is recalibrated on the most recent accepted predictions. This cycle repeats across successive exam splits, allowing the model to adapt to shifting rubrics and question styles while maintaining confidence calibration.

## Key Results
- Temperature scaling reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by 53–65% on two datasets, achieving well-calibrated confidence (ECE < 0.1).
- Selective prediction automates 35–65% of responses with expert-level quality (QWK ≥ 0.80). The confidence gate yields a QWK gap of +0.347 between accepted and rejected predictions, confirming effective uncertainty routing.
- Continual learning from human corrections progressively improves performance: on DAMI, QWK increases from 0.458 to 0.882 after one adaptation cycle.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Post-Hoc Confidence Calibration via Temperature Scaling
- **Claim:** Temperature scaling reduces model overconfidence, yielding reliable confidence scores suitable for decision-making.
- **Mechanism:** A single scalar temperature *T* is fitted on a held-out calibration set to minimize Expected Calibration Error (ECE). At inference, logits *z* are rescaled as *softmax(z/T)*, preserving accuracy while aligning confidence with empirical accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** Model logit distributions contain sufficient signal such that a simple monotonic transformation can correct miscalibration.
- **Evidence anchors:**

- **Break condition:** If the calibration set is unrepresentative of deployment distribution, the learned *T* will not generalize, leaving confidence miscalibrated and selective prediction ineffective.

### Mechanism 2: Confidence-Based Selective Prediction with Explicit Coverage
- **Claim:** A calibrated confidence gate automates only high-trust predictions while routing uncertain cases to human review, balancing automation and reliability.
- **Mechanism:** After calibration, each prediction’s confidence *ĉ* is compared to a threshold *τ*. Predictions with *ĉ*≥*τ* are auto-accepted; others are rejected for human grading. *τ* is selected via a sweep on a held-out set to achieve a target reliability (e.g., QWK≥0.80) at maximal practical coverage.
- **Core assumption:** Post-calibration confidence is monotonically related to per-sample accuracy; thus, high-confidence predictions are more likely correct.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Selective prediction automates high-confidence grading, achieving expert-level quality (QWK ≥ 0.80) on 35–65% of responses. The confidence gate shows a QWK gap of +0.347 between accepted and rejected predictions."
  - [section 3.2] "We select the largest *τ* that satisfies a pre-specified reliability target on the accepted set."
  - [corpus] Neighbors explore uncertainty signals (e.g., semantic entropy) but do not detail this explicit threshold-based routing with coverage-quality tradeoff; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If calibration fails (ECE remains high), confidence no longer reflects accuracy, and the gate will mis-route—accepting low-quality predictions and/or rejecting high-quality ones.

### Mechanism 3: Continual Learning with Replay-Augmented Human Feedback
- **Claim:** A human-in-the-loop fine-tuning loop, using a scale-aware replay buffer, adapts the model to evolving rubrics and questions while mitigating catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** Human corrections (on rejected samples) are combined with a replay buffer of historical samples—stratified by maximum grade scale *G*—to fine-tune only LoRA adapters. This interleaves new supervision with diverse past examples, preserving performance across heterogeneous rubrics. Temperature is recalibrated post-fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** Human corrections provide high-quality, on-distribution supervision for the current deployment context, and replay samples anchor learning to prior tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Each correction cycle strengthens the model’s grading capability... QWK increases from 0.458 to 0.882 after one adaptation cycle."
  - [section 3.2] "The replay buffer is scale-aware... prevents overfitting to the dominant scales... preserves consistent performance."
  - [section 4.4] "Removing it reduces the QWK to 0.025 due to catastrophic forgetting."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like LLM-based Human-in-the-Loop exist but do not specify replay buffer design for heterogeneous scales; evidence is framework-specific.
- **Break condition:** If the replay buffer is not scale-aware or if corrections are biased/noisy, fine-tuning may overfit to current scale or incorporate errors, degrading performance on past question types.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Instruction-Tuned Base LLM:** Initial model fine-tuned on historical exam data to follow grading rubrics.
  2. **Calibration Module:** Post-hoc temperature scaling fitted on a held-out split to produce calibrated confidence.
  3. **Selective Prediction Gate:** Applies threshold *τ* to calibrated confidence to route predictions to auto-accept or human review.
  4. **Human Review Interface:** Collects corrected grades for rejected samples, forming the HiL set *H*.
  5. **Replay Buffer (*R*):** Scale-aware reservoir of historical samples stratified by *G*.
  6. **Fine-Tuning Engine:** Updates only LoRA adapters on *H* ∪ *R*, preserving base weights.
  7. **Recalibration Step:** Refits temperature *T* on newly accepted samples after adaptation.

- **Critical path:** Base model → calibration → selective prediction during deployment → collect human corrections → augment with replay → LoRA fine-tuning → recalibrate temperature → repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *τ* selection: Higher *τ* increases accepted-set QWK but reduces automation coverage.
  - Replay buffer size (*k*): Larger *k* better prevents forgetting but increases fine-tuning cost; *k*=3 was found sufficient.
  - LoRA vs full fine-tuning: Adapter-only updates balance adaptability with stability, avoiding destructive overwrites.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **High post-calibration ECE (≥0.1):** Calibration set unrepresentative or model capacity insufficient for calibration.
  2. **Accepted-set QWP dropping after HiL cycle:** Replay buffer contaminated or corrections noisy; check grade scale distribution in *H* ∪ *R*.
  3. **Coverage collapsing (<20%) after adaptation:** Temperature recalibration sharpened excessively; accepted samples may have become overly conservative.
  4. **Large val-test gap in baseline fine-tuning:** Indicates inherent brittleness; HiL should show progressive improvement on test.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline fine-tuning & calibration sweep:** Train initial model on *D*<sub>train</sub>, then grid-search *T* on *D*<sub>cal</sub> to establish baseline ECE and achievable calibration.
  2. **Selective prediction operating point analysis:** Sweep *τ* on *D*<sub>cal</sub> to plot coverage vs. accepted-set QWK, selecting *τ* that meets target QWK (e.g., ≥0.80) with maximal coverage.
  3. **Single HiL cycle simulation:** Using *D*<sub>2j</sub> (e.g., DAMI's *D*<sub>21</sub>), simulate human corrections, perform replay-augmented fine-tuning, then evaluate on *D*<sub>2(j+1)</sub> to measure adaptation gain (∆QWK).

## Limitations
- Prompt template and exact output formatting for instruction-tuning are not fully specified; the "basic" template is assumed but may not match the paper’s default.
- Replay buffer construction details (SBERT variant, similarity on question vs. question+scale, scale-aware sampling) are vague, risking implementation variance.
- Evaluation is restricted to three ASAG datasets with specific grade scales; generalizability to broader educational domains remains unverified.
- Human feedback quality is assumed high; noisy corrections could undermine continual learning gains.

## Confidence
- **Temperature scaling reduces ECE:** High – standard post-hoc calibration with consistent 53–65% ECE reduction across datasets.
- **Selective prediction achieves expert-level quality:** Medium – relies on well-calibrated confidence; QWK gap (+0.347) is promising but threshold sensitivity to distribution shift is a concern.
- **Continual learning improves performance:** Medium – replay buffer ablations confirm its role, but unspecified retrieval details and short evaluation horizon limit certainty.
