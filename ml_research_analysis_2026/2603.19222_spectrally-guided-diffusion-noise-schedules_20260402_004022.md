# Spectrally-Guided Diffusion Noise Schedules

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19222
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19222
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Spectrally-guided per-instance noise schedules achieve better sample efficiency (e.g., FID 1.32 with 256 steps vs SiD2's 1.37 with 512 steps on ImageNet 256x256) while enabling control over high-frequency detail.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models typically use fixed noise schedules that apply suboptimal noise levels across many steps, requiring manual tuning for different resolutions. This paper introduces spectrally-guided per-instance noise schedules for single-stage pixel diffusion. The core idea is to derive theoretical bounds for minimum and maximum noise levels based on an image's radially-averaged power spectral density (RAPSD). These bounds define a "tight" schedule that eliminates redundant denoising steps. During sampling, a lightweight network predicts the spectrum from the conditioning prompt. The method modifies the denoiser conditioning and uses a t-based guidance interval. On class-conditional ImageNet generation, it consistently improves metrics over the SiD2 baseline while using significantly fewer denoising steps, with the performance gap widening in the low-step regime. Additionally, manipulating the predicted spectrum allows controlling high-frequency detail in generations.

## Method Summary
The approach replaces the fixed noise schedule in diffusion models with a per-instance schedule derived from the image's RAPSD. For a clean image x₀, its RAPSD Ψₓ₀(k) is computed and approximated by a power law ˜Ψ(k)=βk^α. The noise schedule's minimum and maximum levels (κ_min, κ_max) are derived from the highest and lowest frequency powers of ˜Ψ, ensuring that noise is always effective relative to the signal. The schedule λ_M(t) interpolates between these bounds along a frequency axis that mixes frequency-focused and power-focused mappings. During training, the denoiser is conditioned on the current noise level λ_M(t) and its endpoints λ_M(0), λ_M(1), and classifier-free guidance is applied in a t-based interval. At inference, a learned RAPSD sampler S(y) maps the conditioning (e.g., class label) to a Gaussian Mixture Model that samples power-law parameters (α, β), which then generate the instance-specific schedule. This approach yields tighter schedules that adapt to each image's spectral characteristics, improving sample efficiency and enabling controllable detail via spectrum manipulation.

## Key Results
- Improves FID/IS over SiD2 baseline while using fewer denoising steps (e.g., heavy model: FID 1.32 with 256 steps vs SiD2's 1.37 with 512 steps at 256×256 resolution).
- Performance gap widens in the low-step regime (e.g., 32 steps), demonstrating superior sample efficiency.
- Allows controlling high-frequency detail in generations by scaling the predicted spectrum's high-frequency energy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Spectrally-derived theoretical bounds on minimum and maximum noise levels enable "tight" per-instance noise schedules that eliminate redundant denoising steps.
- **Mechanism:** For an image with power spectrum Ψx0(k), the noised signal's RAPSD follows Ψzq(k) = α²_q Ψx0(k) + σ²_q. By setting σ²_q proportional to the signal power at a frequency q (σ²_q = κ_q α²_q Ψx0(q)), the authors derive that the maximum noise (κ_max) is determined by the lowest frequency power (k=1) and the minimum noise (κ_min) by the highest frequency power (k=N_f). This ensures noise levels are always effective relative to the instance's signal structure.
- **Core assumption:** Natural images' RAPSD follows a power law (Ψx0(k) ≈ k^αβ) with high power at low frequencies and low power at high frequencies, allowing extrapolation of bounds from extremes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "derive theoretical bounds for minimum and maximum noise levels based on an image's power spectrum (RAPSD)"
  - [section 4.2] Full derivation from Eq. (9) to (13) linking κ_max/κ_min to extreme frequency powers.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related work on spectral analysis (Rissanen et al., 2023; Dieleman, 2024) but the specific bound derivation for per-instance schedules is novel here (weak direct corpus evidence).
- **Break condition:** If an image's RAPSD does not follow the assumed monotonic decrease (e.g., synthetic patterns with concentrated power at arbitrary frequencies), the derived bounds may not correspond to "effective" noise levels, leading to schedules that still include redundant steps.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Predicting the image's RAPSD from the conditioning prompt before sampling allows generation of the per-instance noise schedule at inference time without requiring the clean image.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight network (RAPSD sampler S) maps the conditioning y to parameters (α, β) of a power-law fit ˜Ψx0(k)=βk^α via a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). At sampling, sampled α, β define the spectrum, which in turn defines the tight noise schedule λ_M(t;x0) via Equations (17)-(21).
- **Core assumption:** The RAPSD of generated images can be effectively modeled by a two-parameter power law and sampled from a distribution conditioned on semantic information (class label), such that the diversity of spectra in the dataset is captured.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "During sampling, a lightweight network predicts the spectrum from the conditioning prompt."
  - [section 4.4] Describes the GMM-based sampler, training via log-likelihood, and the closed-form schedule computation.
  - [corpus] Corpus includes work on learned noise schedules (Sahoo et al., 2024) but focuses on per-pixel polynomials, not spectrum prediction from prompts (indirect support).
- **Break condition:** If the conditional distribution P(α,β|y) is poorly modeled (e.g., multimodal spectra for a single class not captured by the GMM), the sampled schedules may be misaligned with the eventual generated image's true spectrum, causing suboptimal noise application.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Modifying the denoiser's conditioning to include the instance-specific noise schedule bounds (λ_M(0), λ_M(1)) and using a t-based classifier-free guidance interval improves sample quality by making the model fully aware of the non-standard, per-image noise trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** The denoiser x_θ is conditioned on (y, λ_M(t), λ_M(0), λ_M(1)) instead of just (y, t). The guidance interval [t_min, t_max] is defined in normalized time t rather than logSNR λ, because λ varies per instance. This ensures guidance is applied only during the effective portion of the instance's schedule.
- **Core assumption:** The denoiser's performance is sensitive to precise knowledge of the noise level schedule it is effectively operating under; providing the schedule endpoints allows it to adjust its predictions for the specific noise distribution it will see.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The method modifies the denoiser conditioning and uses a t-based guidance interval."
  - [section 4.5] Explicitly states the conditioning change c = (y, λ_M(t), λ_M(0), λ_M(1)) and the switch to t-based intervals.
  - [table 2] Ablation shows "w/o minmax conditioning" and "logSNR intervals" both worsen FID/IS, supporting the claim.
  - [corpus] Kynkääniemi et al. (2024) proposed t-based intervals for standard schedules; this work adapts the principle to per-instance schedules (supporting context).
- **Break condition:** If the model architecture lacks capacity to effectively utilize the additional conditioning signals (λ_M(0), λ_M(1)), or if the t-based interval hyperparameters are not retuned for the new schedules, performance could degrade compared to baseline conditioning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Radially-Averaged Power Spectral Density (RAPSD)**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the core spectral statistic used to derive noise level bounds and define the schedule. Understanding how RAPSD Ψ(k) quantifies signal power at different frequencies is essential to grasp why different images need different noise schedules.
  - *Quick check question:* If an image has high RAPSD values at low k and very low values at high k, what does this imply about its content and how should its noise schedule be shaped relative to a white-noise image?
- **Concept: Diffusion Noise Schedules (λ(t), α_t, σ_t)**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire method revolves around designing and modifying the noise schedule. Prerequisite knowledge of common schedules (cosine, linear) and the logSNR parameterization λ(t) = log(α²_t/σ²_t) is necessary.
  - *Quick check question:* For a variance-preserving schedule, what is the relationship between α_t and σ_t? How does a "tight" schedule differ from a standard cosine schedule in its λ(t) curve?
- **Concept: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and Guidance Intervals**
  - *Why needed here:* The method adapts CFG by changing the conditioning and defining the guidance interval in t-space. Understanding standard CFG (scale w, null embedding) and why the interval matters (Kynkääniemi et al., 2024) is key.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the purpose of the guidance interval in diffusion sampling? Why might defining it in terms of t be more robust than in terms of λ when schedules vary per instance?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **RAPSD Computation Module:** Takes a clean image x0, computes its 2D DFT, averages power radially to get Ψx0(k) (Eqs. 6,7).
  2. **Power-Law Fitter:** Performs least-squares in log-space on Ψx0(k) to obtain parameters α (slope) and β (intercept) for ˜Ψx0(k)=βk^α.
  3. **RAPSD Sampler (S):** A simple linear layer mapping class one-hot to GMM parameters (w_c, μ_c, σ_c). During sampling, it samples (α,β) from the GMM.
  4. **Schedule Generator:** Uses sampled (α,β) in closed-form equations (B.2) to compute λ_M(t) for all t. Computes α_t, σ_t via Eq. (15).
  5. **Modified Denoiser (x_θ):** Standard U-ViT backbone with additional FiLM layers conditioning on (λ_M(t), λ_M(0), λ_M(1)) alongside (y,t).
  6. **Sampling Loop:** Uses standard ancestral sampler (Eqs. 3-5) but with instance-specific α_t, σ_t and the t-based guidance interval.
- **Critical path:**
  - *Training:* For each batch: Compute RAPSD → Fit power law → Generate instance schedule λ_M → Compute xt with schedule → Compute loss L_θ with conditioning on λ_M(t), λ_M(0), λ_M(1) → Update θ and S.
  - *Sampling:* Input y → S(y) samples (α,β) → Generate λ_M → Sample x1 → For each step: compute α_t,σ_t, apply guidance interval → denoise to xs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Spectral Approximation:* Using a 2-parameter power law (˜Ψ) is simple and ensures monotonicity but may not capture all spectral details (e.g., textural bumps). The GMM sampler adds minimal overhead but assumes a simple conditional distribution.
  - *Schedule Generators:* Frequency-focused (λ_F) biases towards high frequencies (details), power-focused (λ_P) towards low frequencies (structure). The paper's mixed schedule (1/2 average) is a heuristic balance; weighting could be tuned.
  - *Conditioning Overhead:* Adding λ_M(0), λ_M(1) to denoiser conditioning increases compute slightly (two extra FiLM signals) but is crucial for performance (Table 2 ablation).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *RAPSD Sampler Mismatch:* Generated (α,β) are far from the true spectrum of the eventual image → schedule is "untight" → FID increases, especially at low NFE.
  - *Incorrect κ_min/κ_max:* Values too extreme cause schedules that still include ineffective noise levels (too much noise destroys signal early, too little fails to decorrelate). See Table 3 for sensitivity.
  - *Guidance Interval Misalignment:* Using logSNR intervals or poorly tuned t-interval (Table 4) leads to over- or under-guidance, harming quality.
  - *Power-Law Violation:* For images with non-power-law spectra (e.g., strong periodic patterns), the fit ˜Ψ may be a poor proxy, derailing schedule generation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Oracle Spectrum vs. Predicted Spectrum:** Replace the RAPSD sampler S with the ground-truth spectrum computed from a real x0 during sampling. Measure FID/IS at multiple NFE. Expect minimal gap (Table 2 "GT spectrum" vs. "Ours"), validating that the sampler's approximation is sufficient.
  2. **Schedule Tightness Visualization:** For a set of ImageNet images, plot the per-instance λ_M(t) against the fixed cosine schedule. Compute the fraction of steps where the noise level σ_t is either below Ψx0(N_f) (ineffective) or above Ψx0(1) (excessive). Expect this fraction to be near zero for tight schedules, significant for cosine.
  3. **Ablation of Conditioning Signals:** Train/run models with conditioning: (a) only (y,t) [SiD2 baseline], (b) (y,t,λ_M(t)), (c) (y,t,λ_M(t),λ_M(0),λ_M(1)). Evaluate at optimal NFE. Expect (c) to be best, (b) intermediate, showing the value of schedule endpoints.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters (loss bias b, guidance exponent γ) and full U-ViT architecture specifications hinder exact replication.
- Power-law RAPSD approximation may fail for images with non-monotonic spectra (e.g., strong periodic patterns).
- GMM-based RAPSD sampler likely undersamples multimodal spectra per class, causing schedule misalignment.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (spectral bounds for tight schedules): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (RAPSD prediction from prompt): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (modified conditioning and t-based guidance): High
