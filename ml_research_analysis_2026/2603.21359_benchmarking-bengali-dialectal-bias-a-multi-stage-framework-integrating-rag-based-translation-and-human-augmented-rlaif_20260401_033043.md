# Benchmarking Bengali Dialectal Bias: A Multi-Stage Framework Integrating RAG-Based Translation and Human-Augmented RLAIF

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21359
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21359
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: A framework quantifying LLM bias against Bengali dialects reveals systematic performance gaps (e.g., Chittagong 5.44/10 vs. Tangail 7.68/10) and shows model scale does not consistently reduce bias.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of tools to measure LLM bias toward low-resource language dialects, using Bengali as a case study. It introduces a two-phase methodology: first, a RAG-based translation pipeline generates dialectal variants of 400 standard Bengali questions across nine dialects, yielding a 4,000-question benchmark; second, an RLAIF evaluation with multi-judge validation scores responses from 19 open-weight LLMs. Results demonstrate significant dialectal bias, with highly divergent dialects underperforming, and model size alone failing to close the gap. The work validates LLM-as-a-judge with Chain-of-Thought as a superior alternative to traditional metrics for agglutinative languages and proposes a Critical Bias Sensitivity (CBS) metric for safety-critical contexts.

## Method Summary
The method unfolds in two integrated stages. Initially, a RAG-enhanced translation system (Gemma-3-27B-IT) transforms standard Bengali questions into nine dialectal forms, producing a gold-labeled benchmark. Traditional metrics (BLEU, WER) proved ineffective due to Bengali’s agglutinative morphology and orthographic variation; thus, an LLM-as-a-judge approach with Chain-of-Thought reasoning was adopted and validated against human scores (Lin’s CCC=0.506). Subsequently, the benchmark evaluates 19 open-weight LLMs via an RLAIF framework that aggregates judgments from multiple AI evaluators, primarily Gemini 2.5 Flash, with secondary judges ensuring consensus. This pipeline generated 68,395 assessments, enabling dialect-specific performance scoring and CBS computation.

## Key Results
- Systematic bias observed: Chittagong dialect scored 5.44/10 on average, while proximate Tangail scored 7.68/10.
- Model scale did not reduce bias gaps; larger models did not consistently outperform smaller ones on divergent dialects.
- Multi-judge agreement between primary (Gemini 2.5 Flash) and secondary judges was strong (Lin’s CCC=0.861), confirming evaluation reliability.
- LLM-as-a-judge with Chain-of-Thought outperformed BLEU/WER, achieving a concordance of 0.506 with human scores, validating its use for agglutinative languages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework’s efficacy arises from synergistic design choices. The RAG-based translation enriches standard prompts with dialect-specific knowledge, generating authentic variants that preserve meaning. LLM-as-a-judge with Chain-of-Thought overcomes surface-form metric limitations by explicitly reasoning about semantic fidelity and dialectal appropriateness, aligning better with human judgment. RLAIF’s multi-judge validation mitigates individual biases, and high inter-rater agreement (CCC=0.861) confirms robustness. CBS combines performance disparity with confidence intervals, flagging dialects where models are both underperforming and uncertain—critical for safety-critical deployments.

## Foundational Learning
- **RAG-based Translation**: *Why needed?* Generates dialectal variants without massive parallel corpora by retrieving relevant knowledge during translation. *Quick check*: Does the system incorporate an external knowledge source (e.g., dialect lexicon) during generation?
- **LLM-as-a-Judge with Chain-of-Thought**: *Why needed?* Traditional metrics fail for agglutinative languages; CoT enables nuanced evaluation of meaning preservation. *Quick check*: Does the judge produce intermediate reasoning steps before scoring?
- **RLAIF (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback)**: *Why needed?* Scales evaluation across many models/dialects while maintaining consistency via AI-generated preferences. *Quick check*: Are model outputs ranked or scored by an AI judge to derive a reward model?
- **Agglutinative Language Challenges**: *Why needed?* Bengali’s morphology renders token-level metrics (BLEU) unreliable; understanding this guides metric selection. *Quick check*: Are assessments based on morpheme or meaning rather than exact word match?

## Architecture Onboarding
Standard Bengali Questions -> RAG Translation Pipeline (Gemma-3-27B-IT + Knowledge Base) -> Dialectal Variants (Benchmark) -> LLM Inference (19 models) -> Responses -> RLAIF Evaluation (Gemini 2.5 Flash primary, secondary judges) -> Bias Scores & CBS

Critical path: The RAG translation step is pivotal; inaccuracies propagate to all downstream evaluations. RLAIF reliability hinges on judge consistency; multi-judge validation ensures robustness.

Design tradeoffs: The framework prioritizes scalability over human parity—AI judges enable 68k evaluations but introduce potential biases. Single-model translation (Gemma) simplifies the pipeline but may limit dialectal diversity.

Failure signatures: (1) Low human-LLM judge agreement (CCC < 0.5) indicates unreliable evaluation. (2) High variance across secondary judges suggests RLAIF instability. (3) Disproportionate performance drops on agglutinative dialects not captured by BLEU may signal translation artifacts.

First three experiments:
1. Human evaluation of a stratified sample of dialectal translations to assess semantic equivalence and dialectal authenticity.
2. Correlation study between LLM-as-judge scores and human scores on a subset to validate judge reliability.
3. Pilot run of the full pipeline on 2–3 representative LLMs to confirm bias patterns (e.g., Chittagong vs. Tangail) emerge consistently.

## Limitations
- Dialect coverage limited to nine variants; rarer Bengali dialects may be underrepresented.
- LLM-as-judge generalizability untested beyond sampled dialects and the specific Gemma judge; hidden biases may persist.
- Gold-standard dialectal variants are generated by a single pipeline; lack of human-written parallel corpora hinders absolute verification of translation quality.

## Confidence
- **Framework Validity & Reproducibility**: High
- **Existence of Systematic Dialectal Bias**: Medium
- **LLM-as-Judge as Superior Metric**: Medium
- **CBS Metric for Safety-Critical Use**: Low

## Next Checks
1. Expand dialect and speaker validation: Conduct human evaluation with native speakers from each dialect to assess benchmark quality and bias scores.
2. Judge model sensitivity analysis: Re-run RLAIF using diverse judge models (e.g., different architectures) to ensure findings are not judge-specific.
3. Correlate CBS with real-world failures: Test in safety-critical domains (e.g., healthcare Q&A) whether higher CBS predicts more harmful or inconsistent outputs.
