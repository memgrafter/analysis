# Property-driven Protein Inverse Folding With Multi-Objective Preference Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06748
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06748
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ProtAlign fine-tunes ProteinMPNN into MoMPNN, improving developability (solubility up to 0.884, thermostability up to 0.963) without sacrificing designability (TM ~0.74) on CATH 4.3 and de novo backbones.

## Executive Summary
Protein sequence design requires balancing structural fidelity (designability) with developability properties like solubility and thermostability. This paper introduces ProtAlign, a framework that aligns inverse folding models with multiple objectives via semi-online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with an adaptive margin. Applied to ProteinMPNN to create MoMPNN, ProtAlign shows significant improvements in solubility and thermostability while maintaining high TM scores on standard benchmarks (CATH 4.3 and de novo backbones). For binder design, the soluble variant of MoMPNN matches or exceeds baselines in success rates and reduces evolutionary perplexity. The approach consistently outperforms subset-trained and guidance-based baselines, offering a generalizable solution for multi-objective protein design.

## Method Summary
ProtAlign fine-tunes an inverse folding model (ProteinMPNN) using a semi-online DPO procedure with an adaptive preference margin. Starting from a pretrained ProteinMPNN, the algorithm iteratively: (1) samples protein backbones; (2) generates multiple sequences per backbone at a rollout temperature; (3) annotates sequences with property predictors for designability (TM-score, pLDDT) and developability (solubility, thermostability, evolutionary perplexity); (4) constructs per-property preference pairs by ranking sequences and pairing top vs bottom performers when their score gap exceeds a threshold; (5) computes an adaptive margin for each pair that penalizes improvements on one property if they come at the cost of others; and (6) updates the policy via the multi-objective DPO loss, which incorporates the margin and a KL penalty relative to the reference model. The order-agnostic likelihood of ProteinMPNN is approximated by averaging over multiple random residue permutations, reducing variance. This process is repeated for 20 rounds, with property predictors called only during rollouts, not during backpropagation, making it computationally efficient.

## Key Results
- On CATH 4.3 test set, MoMPNN achieves solubility up to 0.884 and thermostability up to 0.963 while maintaining TM scores around 0.74.
- On de novo RFDiffusion backbones, MoMPNN attains TM scores up to 0.751.
- For binder design, its soluble variant matches or exceeds baselines in sequence and backbone success rates while lowering evolutionary perplexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adaptive Preference Margin
- **Claim**: The adaptive margin mitigates conflicts between multiple developability objectives and prevents over-optimization of a single property.
- **Mechanism**: For property \(k\), the DPO loss subtracts \(m(y_w,y_l)=\lambda \sum_{k' \neq k} w_{k'}(r_{k'}(y_w)-r_{k'}(y_l))\) from the reward difference, down-weighting updates where the winner on \(k\) underperforms on others.
- **Core assumption**: Developability properties often trade off; ignoring auxiliary objectives leads to imbalanced improvements.
- **Evidence**: Paper introduces flexible margin (section 4.2, Eq. 4); builds on multi-objective DPO theory (Zhou et al., 2023a).
- **Break condition**: \(\lambda\) too high suppresses learning; too low leaves conflicts. Requires accurate property predictors.

### Mechanism 2: Semi-online Rollout
- **Claim**: Decoupling rollout from training updates enables efficient offline-style learning while allowing exploration.
- **Mechanism**: Current policy generates sequences at higher temperature; property predictors annotate them to build preference datasets; then multiple DPO steps are performed before next rollout. Predictors not used during backprop.
- **Core assumption**: Running expensive predictors online at every step is computationally prohibitive and destabilizing.
- **Evidence**: Abstract notes cost reduction; section 4.3 describes Algorithm 1; inspired by semi-online DPO (Lanchantin et al., 2025).
- **Break condition**: Infrequent rollouts cause stale data; rollout temperature too high floods dataset with junk; too low limits exploration.

### Mechanism 3: Order-agnostic Probability Estimation
- **Claim**: Averaging sequence probability over several random residue orders provides a stable estimate of \(\log(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{ref}})\) for order-agnostic models.
- **Mechanism**: Approximate marginal likelihood by sampling \(K\) orders and computing \(\hat{p}_\theta(y|x)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_k \pi_\theta(y|x,\sigma_k)\) (same \(\sigma_k\) for both policies).
- **Core assumption**: Modest \(K\) suffices; shared orders cancel noise.
- **Evidence**: Section 4.2 describes approach inspired by discrete diffusion LLMs (Zhu et al., 2025).
- **Break condition**: Too few orders (\(K\)) yields high variance; non-uniform sampling biases estimate; stable for \(K \geq 5\).

## Foundational Learning

- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**
  - Why needed: Replaces RLHF (reward model + PPO) with a single loss on pairwise preferences, enabling multi-objective alignment.
  - Quick check: How does DPO derive a policy update from a Bradley‑Terry preference model and a KL‑regularized reward objective?

- **Protein inverse folding and designability**
  - Why needed: Baseline task; designability (e.g., TM-score) must be preserved while optimizing developability. High amino‑acid recovery does not guarantee good designability because many sequences can fold to the same backbone.
  - Quick check: Why is high AAR not a guarantee of good designability?

- **Protein developability properties**
  - Why needed: Solubility and thermostability are targeted but often conflict with structural stability. Understanding their prediction (Protein‑Sol, TemBERTure) is key.
  - Quick check: Why might a protein designed for high solubility accidentally lose structural stability?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Backbone (3D coordinates) -> ProteinMPNN (base inverse folding model) -> Property predictors (ESMFold & TMalign for TM/pLDDT, Protein‑Sol for solubility, TemBERTure for thermostability, ESM‑2 for evolutionary perplexity) -> ProtAlign controller (orchestrates rollout, preference pair construction, adaptive margin computation, DPO training loop using multiple‑order probability estimates)

**Critical path**
1. Sample a backbone \(x\) from CATH or de novo set.
2. Current policy \(\pi_\theta\) generates 8 sequences at rollout temperature \(\tau=1.0\).
3. Run all property predictors on each sequence to obtain scores \(\{r_k\}\).
4. For each property \(k\): sort sequences by score, pair the top half with bottom half if gap \(> \delta_k\); store pairs in \(D_k\).
5. Precompute adaptive margin \(m_k(y_w,y_l)\) for every pair in \(D_k\) using other property scores.
6. Estimate \(\log \pi_\theta(y|x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)\) by averaging over \(K=5\) random residue orders (same orders for both).
7. Compute multi‑objective DPO loss \(L_{\text{MO}}\) using Eq. (4) and update \(\theta\) (Adam, lr 5e‑6).
8. Repeat for \(T=20\) rounds, discarding old rollouts each round.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Semi‑online vs fully online: Semi‑online is more stable and computationally feasible but may suffer from stale data; fully online offers fresher gradients at higher cost.
- Adaptive margin: heavier weight on designability (\(w_{\text{IG}}=0.6\)) vs developability (0.4) maintains structural fidelity; \(\lambda\) balances conflict resolution.
- Property predictor choice: in silico scores are cheap but noisy; using multiple predictors acts as regularizer.
- Rollout temperature: high \(\tau\) encourages diversity but risks unrealistic sequences; too low limits exploration.

**Failure signatures**
- Drop in TM‑score/pLDDT while Sol/Thermo climb → \(\lambda\) too low, conflicts unaddressed.
- No improvement in developability after several rounds → \(\delta_k\) too high or rollout diversity insufficient.
- Sequences with very high evolutionary perplexity → EP not included or weight too low.
- Systematic length biases → check if property predictors correlate with length.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **CATH 4.3 redesign**: Validate MoMPNN maintains designability (TM ≈ 0.74) while improving Sol/Thermo over ProteinMPNN and subset‑trained baselines.
2. **De novo RFDiffusion backbones**: Test generalization; MoMPNN should retain high designability (TM ≈ 0.75) and superior developability on unseen structures.
3. **Real‑world binder design**: Evaluate on 6 challenging targets. Sequence success rate (pLDDT>80, PAE<10, RMSD<2Å) and backbone success should match ProteinMPNN, while EP and Sol improve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How do sequences designed by MoMPNN perform in wet‑lab assays for solubility and thermostability compared to baselines?  
   *Basis*: Conclusion explicitly states experimental verification is lacking. *Why unresolved*: All reported improvements are based on in silico predictors. *Evidence needed*: Experimental measurements (e.g., solubility yield, melting temperature) on MoMPNN‑designed sequences alongside controls.

2. Can ProtAlign optimize properties specific to multi‑chain complexes or membrane environments, beyond single‑chain monomer metrics?  
   *Basis*: Conclusion notes focus on monomer properties; binder design tests used monomeric targets. *Why unresolved*: Properties like binding affinity or membrane insertion involve additional, unmodeled factors. *Evidence needed*: Extend framework to design sequences for multi‑protein assemblies or transmembrane proteins and evaluate functional performance experimentally.

3. How accurate are the in silico property predictors (e.g., Protein‑Sol, TemBERTure) for sequences generated by MoMPNN, given distribution shift?  
   *Basis*: Method relies on these predictors as ground truth for preference pairs; their calibration on highly optimized or de novo sequences is not validated. *Why unresolved*: Predictors are trained on natural protein datasets; designed sequences may lie outside their training distribution. *Evidence needed*: Large‑scale correlation studies between predictor scores and experimental measurements on MoMPNN‑generated sequences across property extremes.

4. Does the semi‑online DPO approach with adaptive margins generalize to other inverse folding architectures (e.g., ESM‑IF, InstructPLM) as effectively as to ProteinMPNN?  
   *Basis*: All experiments use ProteinMPNN; no tests on alternative architectures are reported. *Why unresolved*: Different models vary in inductive biases and training data; alignment dynamics may differ. *Evidence needed*: Replicate ProtAlign training pipeline on multiple state‑of‑the‑art inverse folding models and compare multi‑objective performance on standard benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter gaps: exact thresholds \(\delta_k\), margin coefficient \(\lambda\), per‑iteration backbone count \(N\), and weight normalization are not reported, blocking faithful reproduction without extensive tuning.
- Property predictor dependence: performance relies on accuracy of offline predictors (Protein‑Sol, TemBERTure, etc.); robustness to predictor noise/bias is not analyzed.
- Generalization scope: evaluation on real‑world binder design uses only 6 targets; broader validation on a larger, more diverse benchmark is needed.

## Confidence
- Core claim that semi‑online adaptive‑margin DPO can balance developability and designability: **Medium** (supported by results but theoretical assumptions untested).
- Reproducibility of exact reported numbers: **Low** (critical hyperparameters missing).
- Real‑world applicability of designed sequences: **Low** (lack of experimental validation).
