# Distribution-Free Sequential Prediction with Abstentions
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17918
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17918
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A boosting framework (AbstainBoost) enables distribution-free sequential prediction with abstentions under adversarial corruptions, achieving O(T^{3α}) misclassification and Õ(d^2 T^{1-α}) abstention errors for VC classes, with a tunable trade-off parameter α ∈ [0,1/3].

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses sequential prediction where the learner may abstain, operating under adversarial corruptions without distributional assumptions. It introduces AbstainBoost, a boosting-based method that aggregates weak learners estimating shattering probabilities without knowledge of the underlying distribution μ. For oblivious adversaries, the algorithm guarantees O(T^{3α}) cumulative misclassification error and Õ(d^2 T^{1-α}) abstention error for any VC class of dimension d, with α ∈ [0,1/3]. For adaptive adversaries, analogous bounds hold for classes with finite reduction dimension (e.g., linear classifiers). A lower bound confirms the trade-off is necessary: Ω(T^{α}) misclassification versus Ω(T^{1-α}) abstention. The framework also extends to censored feedback, where labels are observed only when a prediction (not an abstention) is made. These results provide a rigorous foundation for robust, abstention-aware learning in sequential, adversarial environments.

## Method Summary
AbstainBoost combines weak hypotheses that estimate the probability a random sample is shattered by a candidate classifier; these estimates are obtained from the sequential data stream without requiring μ. The boosting process reweights examples based on past errors, constructing a final predictor that can output a label or an abstention. The theory covers two adversarial models: oblivious (corruptions fixed beforehand) and adaptive (corruptions chosen based on learner’s actions). The error bounds follow from controlling the shattering probability estimation errors and the boosting dynamics, yielding the stated trade-off between misclassification and abstention. The method also adapts to censored feedback, where true labels are revealed only after a non‑abstention prediction.

## Key Results
- For oblivious adversaries: O(T^{3α}) misclassification error and Õ(d^2 T^{1-α}) abstention error for any VC class of dimension d, α ∈ [0,1/3].
- For adaptive adversaries: analogous guarantees for function classes with finite reduction dimension (e.g., linear classifiers in ℝ^d).
- A lower bound proves any algorithm suffers at least Ω(T^{α}) misclassification and Ω(T^{1-α}) abstention, confirming the optimality of the trade‑off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AbstainBoost leverages the ability of weak learners to estimate shattering probabilities directly from the data stream, avoiding distributional assumptions. These estimates reflect hypothesis complexity and generalization, enabling the boosting process to combine weak rules into a strong predictor. The trade‑off parameter α governs the balance: smaller α reduces misclassification at the cost of more abstentions. Under adversarial corruptions, the analysis tracks how estimation errors propagate and derives bounds that hold even when the adversary reacts to the learner. The use of reduction dimension extends the results beyond VC classes to adaptive settings. The achieved rates match the lower bound, demonstrating near‑optimality.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data Stream -> Weak Learner Estimator -> AbstainBoost Aggregator -> Final Predictor (with abstain) -> Feedback (possibly censored)

### Critical Path
1. Weak learner estimator receives each instance (and possibly history) and outputs a hypothesis with an estimated shattering probability.
2. AbstainBoost combines these hypotheses using boosting weights to form a final prediction rule.
3. For each round, the predictor outputs a label or abstains based on the aggregated rule.
4. If a prediction is made, the true label is revealed (if not censored); otherwise no label is observed.
5. The process repeats with updated weights/estimates.

### Design Tradeoffs
- α balances misclassification vs abstention; smaller α increases accuracy but raises abstention rate.
- Weak learner richness vs sample complexity: more expressive classes yield better estimates but may need more data.
- Censored feedback: deciding when to abstain to gather information vs when to predict.
- Computation vs robustness: more boosting iterations may improve performance but also amplify adversarial influence.

### Failure Signatures
- **High abstention rate:** α too small or weak learners too weak.
- **Rising misclassification:** α too large or poor shattering probability estimates.
- **Instability under adaptive adversary:** high reduction dimension or flawed weak learners.
- **Censored feedback deadlock:** excessive abstentions starve the learner of labels.

### First Experiments
1. **Synthetic VC class validation:** Use threshold functions on [0,1] (VC dim 1); vary T, d, α; verify O(T^{3α}) and Õ(d^2 T^{1-α}) scaling.
2. **Oblivious adversary robustness:** Inject fixed label corruptions; compare observed errors to theoretical bounds.
3. **Adaptive adversary test:** Employ linear classifiers in ℝ^d under adaptive corruptions (flips based on past mistakes); check error rates against reduction‑dimension predictions.

## Limitations
- Practical construction of weak learners that estimate shattering probabilities without μ remains unclear.
- Theoretical bounds are asymptotic (big‑O, Õ) and lack explicit constants, limiting practical interpretability.
- No empirical validation; real‑world performance under adversarial corruptions is unknown.

- Adaptive adversary bounds: Medium
- Lower bound trade‑off: Medium
- Extension to censored feedback: Low
