# SPD-Faith Bench: Diagnosing and Improving Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought for Multimodal Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: `2602.07833`
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07833
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces SPD-Faith Bench and SAGE, improving key faithfulness metrics (Contradiction Rate +5.6%, Difference Reasoning Faithfulness +4.2%) in MLLMs.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles unfaithful Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), where generated reasoning traces do not accurately reflect visual evidence. The authors introduce SPD-Faith Bench, a diagnostic benchmark of 3,000 image pairs requiring fine-grained comparison, and six metrics spanning global perception, faithful perception, and faithful reasoning. Evaluation of 12 MLLMs uncovers two systematic failure modes: perceptual blindness (ignoring visual differences) and perception-reasoning dissociation (generating contradictory reasoning). To address these, they propose SAGE, a training-free framework that dynamically enhances visual attention, rectifies information flow via KL divergence suppression, and uses visual-anchored contrastive decoding. SAGE yields significant improvements on SPD-Faith Bench and also shows gains on MME and POPE benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The task is to diagnose and improve faithfulness in CoT reasoning for MLLMs. The SPD-Faith Benchmark consists of 3,000 image pairs derived from COCO with controlled fine-grained differences (color change, object removal, position change) across single-difference (easy/medium/hard) and multi-difference (2–5 changes) subsets. Each sample includes ground-truth difference descriptions and metadata. Faithfulness is evaluated using six metrics: Difference Sensitivity (DS) and Difference Quantity Recall (DQR) for global perception; Type-Level F1 (TF1) and Category-Level F1 (CF1) for faithful perception; Contradiction Rate (CR) and Difference Reasoning Faithfulness (DRF) for faithful reasoning. SAGE is a training-free inference framework with three stages: (1) Dynamic Visual Routing modulates attention weights based on decay, with initial weight α₀=0.1; (2) Information Flow Rectification computes KL divergence between multi-head attention (MHA) and feed-forward network (FFN) updates, suppressing FFN with β=0.9 when divergence exceeds threshold τ; (3) Visual-Anchored Generation uses contrastive decoding between original and masked image (top-k mask, size k) with η=0.5.

## Key Results
- Introduced SPD-Faith Bench: a benchmark of 3,000 image pairs requiring fine-grained comparison, with controlled single- and multi-difference subsets and ground-truth annotations.
- Evaluation of 12 MLLMs revealed two systematic failure modes: perceptual blindness (low DS/DQR, indicating missed visual differences) and perception-reasoning dissociation (high DS but low CR/DRF, indicating contradictory reasoning).
- SAGE, a training-free framework, improves faithfulness: on SPD-Faith Bench, Contradiction Rate increases by 5.6% and Difference Reasoning Faithfulness by 4.2% over baselines; also shows gains on MME and POPE benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies two root causes of unfaithful CoT in MLLMs: (1) **decaying visual attention** across transformer layers, where later layers under-attend to image patches containing critical differences (perceptual blindness); (2) **residual stream shifts** driven by FFN updates that diverge from MHA’s attention pattern, causing the model to generate reasoning not grounded in attended visual evidence (perception-reasoning dissociation). SAGE intervenes at each layer: Dynamic Visual Routing counters attention decay by reweighting patch tokens based on their relevance; KL-based Information Flow Rectification suppresses FFN updates when they deviate excessively from the attention distribution, preserving faithful representations; Visual-Anchored Generation contrasts logits from the original and a masked image (hiding difference regions) to amplify tokens that truly depend on the evidence. Together, these steps realign the model’s internal computations with the visual input.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**
   - *Why needed*: CoT enables models to generate intermediate reasoning steps, but faithfulness requires these steps to be grounded in the input modality (e.g., image).
   - *Quick check*: For a given sample, inspect the model’s generated reasoning and verify via human or automated judge whether each claim can be attributed to the image.

2. **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**
   - *Why needed*: MLLMs integrate visual encoders with LLMs, but the projection layer and autoregressive decoding can introduce misalignment between perception and language reasoning.
   - *Quick check*: Probe the model with symmetric queries (e.g., “What is different?” vs. “What is the same?”) and check for consistent answers.

3. **Attention Mechanism in Transformers**
   - *Why needed*: Attention weights determine which tokens (image patches or text) influence hidden states. Decay in visual attention across layers leads to lost information.
   - *Quick check*: Visualize attention maps for a difference-containing patch across layers; decreasing attention weight indicates decay.

4. **Contrastive Decoding**
   - *Why needed*: Contrastive decoding compares logits from original input vs. a perturbed input (e.g., masked image) to suppress tokens not supported by visual evidence.
   - *Quick check*: Generate responses with and without contrastive decoding; the latter should show fewer claims contradicted by the image.

5. **KL Divergence for Information Flow**
   - *Why needed*: KL divergence between MHA and FFN updates measures their alignment; high divergence indicates FFN may be introducing unfaithful shifts.
   - *Quick check*: Compute KL per layer; high KL layers should correlate with drops in faithfulness metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Image -> Visual Encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT) -> Patch Tokens -> LLM Transformer (MHA + FFN per layer) -> SAGE Intervention (Dynamic Routing & KL Suppression per layer) -> Visual-Anchored Generation (Contrastive Decoding with masked image) -> CoT Output

**Critical path**  
During autoregressive generation, the model processes image patches and previously generated tokens through each transformer layer. SAGE modulates the residual stream after MHA and FFN: Dynamic Routing reweights patch tokens based on a decay function; KL Suppression zeros out the FFN contribution when its update diverges from MHA beyond threshold τ. After the final layer, Visual-Anchored Generation computes logits for the next token using both original and masked image representations, contrasting them to favor evidence-grounded tokens.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Training-free vs. fine-tuning*: SAGE requires no additional training, avoiding catastrophic forgetting and data needs, but introduces inference overhead (extra forward passes for masked image and KL computations).
- *Parameter sensitivity*: Hyperparameters (α₀, β, τ, k) are fixed in the paper but may need per-model tuning; defaults might not generalize.
- *Generality*: Designed for autoregressive MLLMs; not directly applicable to encoder-decoder or non-autoregressive architectures.

**Failure signatures**  
- *Perceptual blindness*: Low DS/DQR scores; attention maps show uniform or irrelevant focus on difference regions; token activation maps (TAM) indicate minimal engagement with changed patches.
- *Perception-reasoning dissociation*: High DS (model notices difference) but low CR/DRF (reasoning contradicts ground truth); mismatch between attention maps and TAM for generated tokens.

**First experiments**  
1. Reproduce baseline: Evaluate Qwen2.5-VL-7B on SPD-Faith Bench without SAGE; confirm failure modes by reporting DS, DQR, CR, DRF.
2. Apply SAGE: Use default hyperparameters; measure improvements in CR and DRF. If gains are smaller than claimed, perform a grid search over τ and k on a held-out validation split.
3. Ablate components: Disable each SAGE stage (dynamic routing only, KL suppression only, contrastive decoding only) in isolation to quantify individual contribution and test additivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Open Question 1: Can the diagnostic insights from SPD-Faith Bench (perceptual blindness, perception-reasoning dissociation) be generalized to other multimodal reasoning tasks beyond difference captioning, such as visual question answering or complex scene reasoning?  
Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state in the Limitations: "First, our evaluation is restricted to image difference captioning... Future work will therefore extend our framework to broader multimodal reasoning tasks, e.g., visual question answering and multimodal decision-making."  
Why unresolved: The paper’s benchmark and failure mode analysis are tightly coupled with the fine-grained comparison task. It is unknown whether the same attention decay and residual stream shift mechanisms dominate in tasks requiring different reasoning patterns (e.g., counting, spatial reasoning, commonsense inference).  
What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the mechanistic analysis (attention visualization, residual similarity, neuron activation) on a diverse suite of multimodal benchmarks (e.g., VQA, MathVista, MME) while controlling for task structure. If the same failure patterns emerge, the claims generalize; if different patterns dominate, the findings are task-specific.

Open Question 2: What underlying architectural or training factors cause the progressive decay of visual attention and the FFN-driven representation shifts identified in MLLMs? Are these inevitable in current Transformer-based MLLM designs?  
Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper identifies decaying visual attention and residual stream representation shifts as root causes of unfaithfulness (Section 4.2, 4.3) but does not explain why these occur systematically. The analysis is observational rather than causal.  
Why unresolved: The paper traces symptoms to mechanisms (initial suppression, progressive decay, FFN divergence) but does not investigate whether these stem from the vision-language connector, the LLM backbone’s original training objectives, the autoregressive decoding process, or a combination. Without an ablation of architectural components or training data, the fundamental source remains unclear.  
What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments modifying one component at a time (e.g., freezing the LLM backbone, altering the vision-language projection layer, varying the amount of multimodal instruction tuning) and measuring the resulting attention decay curves and FFN/MHA divergence. Causal mediation analysis could identify which layers/modules are necessary and sufficient for the observed drift.

Open Question 3: Can the effectiveness of the training-free SAGE framework be matched or surpassed by fine-tuning-based methods that intrinsically align perception and reasoning, and what are the trade-offs in computational cost, generalizability, and catastrophic forgetting?  
Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge: "SAGE is designed as a training-free intervention... an important direction for future work is to investigate parameter-efficient training strategies that can achieve intrinsic faithfulness while maintaining practical computational costs." They cite FRIT as a unimodal example but note scaling to MLLMs is demanding.  
Why unresolved: SAGE applies dynamic post-hoc corrections but does not fix the underlying misalignment. It is unclear if intrinsic alignment via training (e.g., reinforcement learning with faithfulness rewards, contrastive pretraining on comparison tasks, or representation regularization) could produce more robust and general faithfulness without the inference-time overhead of SAGE’s multi-stage intervention.  
What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study training small-to-medium MLLMs with faithfulness-specific objectives (e.g., consistency regularization on symmetric queries, contrastive losses between visual attention maps and generated rationales) and evaluating them on SPD-Faith Bench, MME, and POPE. Compare performance, inference speed, and transfer to unseen multimodal tasks against SAGE-augmented baselines.

Open Question 4: How robust are the proposed faithfulness metrics (CR, DRF) to the choice of LLM-as-a-Judge (e.g., GPT-4o) and their inherent biases? Could a model’s “unfaithfulness” be partially an artifact of the judge’s limitations?  
Basis in paper: [inferred] The CR and DRF metrics rely on GPT-4o to judge semantic consistency and alignment between model outputs and ground truth. This introduces a dependency on another model’s own faithfulness and biases, which is not validated.  
Why unresolved: The paper assumes GPT-4o is a reliable oracle for judging multimodal reasoning faithfulness. However, GPT-4o itself may exhibit perceptual or reasoning errors on fine-grained differences, or it may have a bias toward certain explanation styles. This could lead to false positives/negatives in the faithfulness assessment of the evaluated MLLMs.  
What evidence would resolve it: A human evaluation subset where expert annotators independently score a sample of model responses for consistency and grounding, then correlate these scores with GPT-4o’s judgments. If low correlation is found, the automated metrics need recalibration or a more robust judging protocol (e.g., ensemble of smaller, validated models).

## Limitations
- Missing implementation details: Exact SAGE hyperparameters (layer boundary ℓₛ, KL threshold τ, top-k mask size k, decay function), prompt templates, and metric extraction code are not provided, hindering precise replication.
- Environment dependencies: No `requirements.txt` or Dockerfile; reproducibility across CUDA/library versions is uncertain.
- Generalization: Findings are based on 12 specific MLLMs; it is unclear if failure modes and SAGE gains extend to other architectures or newer models.
