# BayeSQP: Bayesian Optimization through Sequential Quadratic Programming
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03232
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03232
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** BayeSQP merges sequential quadratic programming with Bayesian optimization using GP surrogates to efficiently solve high-dimensional constrained black-box problems, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in both performance and speed.

## Executive Summary
BayeSQP is a novel algorithm for constrained black-box optimization that integrates classical sequential quadratic programming (SQP) with Bayesian optimization (BO). It builds Gaussian process (GP) surrogates that model the objective and all constraints, including their gradients and Hessians, using only zero-order evaluations. At each iteration, it formulates an uncertainty-aware subproblem (B-SUB) that incorporates posterior variances, solves this subproblem as a second-order cone program (SOCP) to obtain a search direction, and then performs a line search via constrained Thompson sampling to select the next evaluation point. Experiments on within-model and standard benchmark problems demonstrate that BayeSQP achieves superior feasible solutions in dimensions up to 96D, is orders of magnitude faster than TuRBO, and exhibits robustness to uncertainty parameters. The method provides a principled bridge between deterministic SQP and probabilistic BO.

## Method Summary
BayeSQP operates by maintaining a joint GP model over the objective and constraint functions, trained on all observed function values. The GP provides predictive means, variances, and analytically derived gradients and Hessians at any query point. Using these, the algorithm constructs a local quadratic approximation of the objective and linearized constraints, but unlike traditional SQP, it incorporates epistemic uncertainty via the GP posterior variances. This yields an uncertainty-aware subproblem (B-SUB) that balances exploitation of promising regions with exploration of uncertain ones. B-SUB is cast as an SOCP, enabling efficient computation of a globally optimal search direction within the conic framework. A line search based on constrained Thompson sampling then respects constraint boundaries by sampling from posterior distributions, effectively simulating a set of candidate constraint functions and choosing steps that maximize probability of feasibility. The new observation updates the GP, and the cycle repeats.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art constrained BO methods (logEI, TuRBO, SAASBO, MPD) in high-dimensional settings (up to 96D) and provides feasible solutions where competitors often fail.
- Achieves speedups of multiple orders of magnitude over TuRBO in wall-clock time.
- Demonstrates low sensitivity to uncertainty parameters (δf, δc) and reliably finds high-quality feasible solutions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BayeSQP succeeds by systematically combining the strengths of SQP and BO. The joint GP surrogate supplies accurate derivative information (gradients, Hessians) without extra function evaluations, enabling a local quadratic model akin to SQP. However, by augmenting the subproblem with posterior variances, the method directly accounts for model uncertainty, which prevents premature convergence and promotes global exploration. The SOCP formulation ensures that the resulting search direction is optimal for the approximated trust-region problem under the uncertainty model, while remaining computationally tractable even for nonconvex constraints due to conic duality. Finally, constrained Thompson sampling in the line search respects the unknown constraint boundaries by sampling from posterior distributions, effectively simulating a set of candidate constraint functions and choosing steps that maximize probability of feasibility. This layered integration yields an algorithm that is both sample-efficient and robust in high-dimensional, constrained black-box scenarios.

## Foundational Learning

- **Sequential Quadratic Programming:** Classic method for constrained nonlinear optimization. *Quick check:* Know how to form and solve quadratic subproblems with linearized constraints.
- **Gaussian Process Derivatives:** Provide gradient and curvature information from a GP without extra evaluations. *Quick check:* Derive mean and variance of GP gradients; understand how to compute them analytically.
- **Second-Order Cone Programming:** Convex optimization framework used to solve the uncertainty-aware subproblem. *Quick check:* Be familiar with cone constraints and standard solvers (e.g., ECOS, SCS).
- **Constrained Thompson Sampling:** Bayesian approach to handle constraints during decision-making. *Quick check:* Practice sampling from posterior distributions and applying feasibility rules.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Data (evaluations) -> GP Surrogate -> B-SUB Formulation -> SOCP Solver -> Search Direction -> Constrained Thompson Sampling Line Search -> Next Evaluation Point -> Update GP (loop)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Update GP with all observed data.  
2. Compute GP predictive mean, variance, gradients, and Hessians at a discrete set of points.  
3. Assemble the B-SUB SOCP using these statistics and uncertainty parameters (δf, δc).  
4. Solve SOCP to obtain the search direction.  
5. Perform line search via constrained Thompson sampling to pick step size and next point.  
6. Evaluate true objective/constraints at the new point.  
7. Repeat from step 1.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Joint vs. separate GP modeling: joint modeling captures correlations but increases computational cost; separate models reduce cost but ignore cross-correlation.  
- SOCP approximation: trades off exactness of the nonconvex SQP subproblem for global optimality of the direction and polynomial-time solvability.  
- Thompson sampling randomness: aids exploration but introduces variance in the optimization path; requires careful tuning of the number of samples.  
- High-dimensional GP scalability: full GP inference scales cubically; the paper hints at using a 96D GP without detailing approximations, which may limit applicability beyond ~100 dimensions.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Poor GP fit (e.g., due to insufficient data or mismatch of kernel) leading to inaccurate gradients/Hessians.  
- SOCP solver returning infeasible or degenerate directions (numerical instability).  
- Thompson sampling repeatedly proposing infeasible points, causing stagnation.  
- Line search failing to find an acceptable step (e.g., all candidates violate constraints or yield no improvement).  
- Excessive runtime in GP updating or SOCP solving as dimension or number of constraints grows.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Replicate the method on a set of low-dimensional constrained test functions (e.g., Branin-Hoo with linear constraint) and compare to known optima to sanity-check implementation.  
2. Run a comprehensive benchmark on high-dimensional constrained synthetic problems (e.g., Ackley, Rosenbrock with constraints) against baselines (logEI, TuRBO, SAASBO, MPD), measuring best feasible value and wall-clock time.  
3. Conduct a parameter sensitivity study varying δf and δc across several orders of magnitude on a subset of benchmark problems to confirm robustness and recommend default values.

## Limitations
- Independent validation is lacking: claims rely on authors' internal experiments; code and data are not publicly available for external scrutiny.
- Scalability of the GP and SOCP components in very high dimensions or with many constraints is not thoroughly analyzed, despite reported success up to 96D.
- Benchmark suite may not represent the full complexity of real-world constrained optimization tasks, such as non-smooth or combinatorial constraints.
