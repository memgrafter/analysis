# Decision-oriented benchmarking to transform AI weather forecast access: Application to the Indian monsoon

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.03767
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03767
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A decision-oriented benchmarking framework evaluating AI weather models on a tailored Indian monsoon onset index demonstrated that nearly all evaluated AI models outperformed climatology in the Core Monsoon Zone for 1-15 day leads, directly informing a 2025 government project delivering forecasts to 38 million farmers.

## Executive Summary
Current AI weather model benchmarks rely on aggregated meteorological metrics that do not align with the operational needs of local stakeholders, hindering adoption for climate adaptation in vulnerable regions. This study introduces a decision-oriented benchmarking framework tailored to agricultural decision-making for the Indian monsoon. By evaluating AI weather prediction (AIWP) models on a locally relevant onset index derived from rain-gauge data, the framework assesses both deterministic and probabilistic skill across multiple test periods. The approach successfully identified model skill, informed a multi-model blending strategy, and was deployed in 2025 to provide actionable forecasts to millions of farmers, including capturing an unusual monsoon pause weeks in advance.

## Method Summary
The framework modifies the Moron-Robertson monsoon onset index by anchoring local onset to the median climatological date of the India Meteorological Department’s monsoon onset over Kerala (MOK). This reduces false early starts without requiring a long post-onset dry spell, making it operationally relevant for planting decisions. Forecasts from several AIWP models and a NWP baseline are generated for twice-weekly initializations from early May. Deterministic (MAE, false alarm/miss rates) and probabilistic (Brier skill score, reliability) metrics are computed for defined forecast windows (1-15, 16-30 days). To address the small sample size (one onset per year), evaluation spans multiple periods: a recent test set (2019-2024), an extended historical set (1965-1978 + 2019-2024), and common years (2004-2021). This design leverages the computational efficiency of AI models to produce large hindcast ensembles for robust statistical assessment.

## Key Results
*   Nearly all evaluated AI models outperformed climatology in the Core Monsoon Zone for 1-15 day leads, achieving approximately a 2-day reduction in MAE.
*   Some models demonstrated skill extending to a 3-week lead time.
*   The customized onset index reduced false onsets by ~74% compared to the standard definition, directly enhancing decision utility for farmers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
A locally relevant, agriculturally tailored onset index makes AIWP forecasts actionable by filtering spurious early rains common in semi-arid regions, aligning with the high cost of false planting starts versus slight timing errors. The modification anchors onset to the regionally consistent Kerala onset (MOK), removing the need for a 30-day post-onset dry spell check.

### Mechanism 2
The computational efficiency of AIWP models enables rapid generation of decades of identical-version hindcasts. This creates a larger validation sample, including pre-satellite years (1965-1978), which reduces uncertainty in skill estimates for a rare annual event and provides a more robust evaluation despite small test sample sizes.

### Mechanism 3
Evaluating a suite of deterministic and probabilistic metrics reveals complementary model strengths and weaknesses. No single model dominates all metrics, informing a multi-model blending strategy that enhances operational reliability and allows users to adapt to their risk profiles (e.g., balancing false alarms vs. misses).

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Index Definition Module -> Hindcast Generation Engine -> Verification Suite -> Multi-period Orchestrator -> Blending/Calibration Interface
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Acquire/preprocess ground-truth rainfall (IMD 1°) and model initial conditions (ERA5).
    2.  Generate/retrieve hindcasts for all specified initialization dates across chosen periods.
    3.  Compute forecast onset dates within windows and compare to observed onset to accumulate metrics.
    4.  Aggregate metrics over the Core Monsoon Zone and compare to climatology.
    5.  Identify models with positive skill across key metrics and computational feasibility.
    6.  Design blending/calibration scheme using historical skill and reliability.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Index resolution: 4° grid reduces noise but may average local detail; finer grids increase sample but decrease gauge support.
    *   Hindcast period: Including pre-1979 boosts sample but risks initialization bias; excluding ensures consistency.
    *   Metric weighting: Must align with unknown user risk profiles; no universal solution.
    *   Computational cost: High inference cost for models like GenCast limits hindcasts, potentially biasing comparisons.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   High FAR despite low MAE → early bias, crop failure risk.
    *   Total miss years → unusable for planting.
    *   Overconfidence in reliability diagrams → uncalibrated ensembles mislead risk-averse users.
    *   Sharp skill drop in recent vs. extended period → possible overfitting to training-era climate.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Reproduce CMZ skill (2019-2024): Generate hindcasts for AIFS, NGCM, IFS. Verify most models beat climatology in 1-15 day MAE.
    2.  Extended period validation: Repeat Experiment 1 for 1965-1978 initializations. Check if skill improvement persists, noting any degradation in IFS/NGCM.
    3.  MOK filter sensitivity: Redefine onset using alternative cut-off dates (e.g., May 25, June 10). Re-compute metrics to assess robustness to filter choice.

## Limitations
*   MOK filter's reliance on Kerala onset may fail if climate change decouples the typical northward progression.
*   Pre-1979 initial conditions likely contain biases that could distort extended-period skill estimates.
*   Small sample size (one onset per year) persists despite extended record, restricting statistical power.

## Confidence
*   Framework adoption (delivered to 38M farmers): High
*   Model skill for 1-15 day leads: Medium
*   Skill to ~3 weeks: Medium
*   MOK index effectiveness (74% false reduction): High
*   Extended period robustness: Medium
*   Blending improves reliability: Medium

## Next Checks
1.  **MOK robustness:** Test alternative onset definitions (e.g., all-India rainfall, ±5 day MOK shifts) to verify skill persistence.
2.  **Blending generalization:** Perform leave-one-year-out cross-validation (2019-2024) to evaluate if blending weights generalize; compare Brier skill score and reliability against the best individual model.
3.  **Pre-1979 bias impact:** Compare skill metrics between 1965-1978 and 2019-2024; if older period underperforms, apply bias correction (e.g., quantile mapping) and reassess extended-period advantage.
