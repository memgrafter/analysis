# Who is the richest club in the championship? Detecting and Rewriting Underspecified Questions Improve QA Performance

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.11938  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11938  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** Detecting and rewriting underspecified questions improves QA performance by up to +20% F1, revealing that many benchmark errors stem from question ambiguity rather than model limitations.

## Executive Summary
Underspecified questions—those with implicit or missing constraints—are prevalent in QA benchmarks and cause modern models to fail. This paper introduces a two-stage LLM-based pipeline: first, a classifier detects underspecified queries; second, a rewriter transforms them into fully specified versions while preserving gold answers. Applied to Natural Questions, HotpotQA, TriviaQA, and FRAMES, the method finds 16–53% of questions underspecified and yields substantial QA gains after rewriting (e.g., GPT‑4o F1 on NQ improves by 20.3 points). The results highlight question ambiguity as a major confound in benchmark evaluation and demonstrate that careful question design can unlock a large fraction of existing “model failures”.

## Method Summary
The authors build an LLM classifier fine‑tuned or prompted on the UNDER dataset (855 examples from CLAMBER, IN3, CoCoNot). The classifier achieves 77% accuracy on an expert‑annotated gold subset. It is then applied to four QA benchmark datasets to split them into fully specified (FS) and underspecified (UND) subsets. For each UND question, a strong LLM rewriter (GPT‑4o or Gemini) generates a new question that fully specifies the query while ensuring the original gold answer remains correct. The rewriting uses the classifier’s reasoning as guidance. Finally, the authors evaluate target QA models (e.g., GPT‑4o, Gemini‑2.5‑Flash) on both original UND and rewritten questions in a cross‑assignment setup (different LLMs for detection, rewriting, and QA) and measure performance with F1 and answer accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- 16%–53% of questions across NQ, HotpotQA, TriviaQA, and FRAMES are underspecified.
- QA models perform significantly worse on underspecified subsets compared to fully specified ones.
- Rewriting underspecified questions yields consistent improvements: GPT‑4o F1 increases from 37.0% to 57.3% on NQ (+20.3%) and from 34.6% to 51.8% on HotpotQA (+17.2%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Underspecified questions introduce ambiguity that causes models to generate incorrect or incomplete answers. By resolving this ambiguity through rewriting—while holding the gold answer fixed—the pipeline eliminates a key source of error. The consistent performance gains across datasets and models confirm that many benchmark failures are not inherent limitations of the models but rather artifacts of poorly phrased questions. The approach effectively “pre‑processes” the benchmark to present clearer challenges, thereby allowing models to demonstrate their true capabilities.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** UNDER Classifier (Qwen3‑4B) → Rewriter (GPT‑4o/Gemini) → QA Model (GPT‑4o/Gemini) → Evaluation  
**Critical path:** 1) Classify benchmark questions into FS/UND. 2) Rewrite all UND questions with gold answers. 3) Run QA model on original and rewritten sets. 4) Compare metrics.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Classifier choice: smaller (Qwen3‑4B) balances accuracy and speed; using a larger LLM might improve detection but increase cost.  
- Gold‑answer conditioning in rewriting prevents answer drift but risks leakage if not enforced strictly.  
- Cross‑assignment avoids bias from a single model chain but introduces variability from different model architectures.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Classifier accuracy below expected 77% on gold subset.  
- Rewritten questions classified as FS at low rate (<64% reported).  
- Evidence of answer drift (gold answer no longer fits rewritten question).  
- QA gains disappear or reverse when using different rewriter models.  
**First experiments:**  
1. Run classifier on a small gold‑labeled set from the target dataset; confirm accuracy >70% before proceeding.  
2. Manually inspect 50 rewritten questions to verify the gold answer remains fully supported and the intent is preserved.  
3. Evaluate a single QA model (e.g., GPT‑4o) on both original UND and rewritten questions to establish the base improvement signal; ensure this improvement is statistically significant (paired t‑test).

## Limitations
- The UNDER training data (855 examples) and gold subset (150 examples) are not released, hindering exact replication and prevalence estimates.  
- The classifier’s 77% accuracy is imperfect; misclassifications likely contaminate the FS/UND split, especially on out‑of‑domain data.  
- Rewriting may inadvertently simplify questions or alter the original intent, potentially inflating QA gains; large‑scale human evaluation of rewritten questions is absent.

## Confidence
- **Underspecification prevalence (16–53% across datasets):** Medium  
- **Classifier accuracy (77% on gold):** High  
- **QA improvement after rewriting (up to +20% F1):** Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Manual annotation study:** Randomly sample 200 questions labeled UND by the classifier per dataset; recruit human annotators to verify true underspecification and estimate precision/recall.
2. **Answer preservation audit:** For 100 rewritten questions, use both automated answer‑validity checks (e.g., QA model on rewritten question) and human judgment to confirm the gold answer remains correct and the rewritten question is not trivially answered.
3. **Robustness across rewriters:** Re‑run the pipeline with two alternative rewriter LLMs (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Llama‑3‑70B) and measure variance in QA performance gains; low variance would strengthen the generalizability claim.
