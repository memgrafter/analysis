# Distribution-Free Robust Predict-Then-Optimize in Function Spaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08215
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08215
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Theoretically derive coverage and suboptimality bounds; empirically demonstrate calibrated coverage on Poisson, heat, and quantum wavefunction PDEs after spectral correction. Robust design solutions yield significant improvements over nominal and baseline schemes in collection and quantum state discrimination tasks.

## Executive Summary
Neural operators accelerate solving partial differential equations (PDEs) but lack accuracy guarantees, posing risks for downstream design optimization. This paper extends split conformal prediction to infinite-dimensional Sobolev spaces by defining score functions on spectral truncations with smoothness parameters, yielding marginal coverage guarantees for the full function. The resulting uncertainty sets are used in a min–max robust optimization framework, solved efficiently through a multi‑stage refinement algorithm that leverages nested truncation levels. The authors prove theoretical coverage and suboptimality bounds and validate the approach on Poisson, heat, and quantum wavefunction PDEs, as well as resource collection and quantum state discrimination tasks. Results show calibrated coverage after spectral margin correction and statistically significant improvements in robust decisions compared to nominal and baseline methods.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three key innovations. First, functional conformal prediction is achieved by defining scores based on truncated spectral representations in Sobolev spaces of smoothness $s-\tau$, where $\tau>0$ controls the margin decay $N^{-2\tau}$. A padding term derived from problem-specific output bounds $B(A)$ ensures that the conformal quantile, when applied to the untruncated function, provides marginal coverage. Second, robust optimization is formulated as a min–max problem over the resulting uncertainty sets. To solve it efficiently, a multi‑stage algorithm iteratively refines the solution over increasing spectral truncation levels $N_t$, warm‑starting each stage and exploiting the nesting of adversarial sets. Third, the authors derive concrete $B(A)$ bounds for several PDE families using regularity theory (e.g., elliptic regularity for Poisson, semigroup bounds for heat equations), making the coverage guarantee actionable. The pipeline trains a spectral neural operator on truncated PDE solutions, calibrates conformal scores on a held‑out set, constructs uncertainty sets, and then runs the multi‑stage robust optimization.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantee: For any smoothness parameter $\tau>0$ and truncation level $N$, the margin‑corrected conformal set $C_{N;\tau}^*(a)$ achieves at least $1-\alpha$ marginal coverage over the full infinite‑dimensional Sobolev function, under boundedness assumptions (Theorem 3.2).
- Efficient robust optimization: The multi‑stage refinement scheme reduces computational cost compared to a naive single‑stage high‑resolution approach, with cost bounded by $O(\sum_{t} N_t^d)$ and objective improvement scaling with the smoothness of the optimum w.r.t. $N$ (Theorem 3.10).
- Empirical validation: Calibration curves on Poisson, heat, and quantum PDEs confirm that the uncorrected sets under‑cover while the corrected sets meet or exceed the target coverage. In collection and quantum discrimination tasks, robust solutions significantly outperform nominal and baseline methods, with mutual information gains that are statistically significant across various spectral resolutions and smoothness settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Spectral truncation with margin correction yields infinite‑dimensional coverage
- **Claim:** The score $s_{N;\tau}(a,u) = \|G(a) - \Pi_N u\|_{s-\tau}^2$ combined with a padded quantile $bq_{N;\tau}^* = bq_{N;\tau} + B(A)N^{-2\tau}$ produces a prediction set $C_{N;\tau}^*(a)$ that covers the true untruncated function $U$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$.
- **Mechanism:** The margin $B(A)N^{-2\tau}$ accounts for the truncation error $\|U - \Pi_N U\|_{s-\tau}$, which under a Sobolev embedding and smoothness $\tau$ decays as $N^{-\tau}$. The bound $B(A)$ is a problem‑specific constant upper‑bounding $\|U\|_s^2$.
- **Core assumption:** There exists $B(A)$ such that $\|U\|_s^2 \le B(A)$ almost surely; the true function resides in a Sobolev space of smoothness $s$ (embedding into $s-\tau$). 
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 3.2 states the coverage guarantee; Corollaries 3.3 and 3.4 provide $B(A)$ for uniformly elliptic and parabolic PDEs via regularity theory; Lemma 5.1 gives $B(\psi_b)$ for quantum wavefunctions.

### Mechanism 2: Multi‑stage warm‑starting exploits nested uncertainty sets
- **Claim:** Solving the robust problem $\min_w \max_{v\in V_{N_t;\tau}(a)} J[w,v]$ iteratively over increasing $N_t$ yields substantial computational savings while converging to the high‑resolution optimum.
- **Mechanism:** The adversarial sets $V_{N;\tau}(a)$ are nested because a larger $N$ relaxes the spectral truncation constraint. Each stage $t$ uses the solution $w_{t-1}^*$ from the previous (coarser) stage as an initial point, reducing the number of gradient steps needed. The total cost is bounded by a sum of stage costs, each scaling with $N_t^d$.
- **Core assumption:** The objective $J[w,v]$ is $L$‑smooth and $\mu$‑strongly convex in $w$; the optimal $w_N^*$ varies smoothly with $N$ (Theorem 3.10).
- **Evidence anchors:** Algorithm 1 outlines the procedure; Theorem 3.10 provides a cost bound; Section 3.6.1 discusses the efficiency spectrum.

### Mechanism 3: PDE regularity theory provides computable $B(A)$
- **Claim:** For specific PDE families, the abstract bound $B(A)$ can be replaced by explicit expressions derived from solution regularity, making the coverage guarantee concrete and implementable.
- **Mechanism:** Elliptic regularity yields $\|u\|_s^2 \le C_{s,L} \|f\|_{s-2}^2$ for Poisson; parabolic semigroup theory gives $\|u(T)\|_{s+2\beta}^2 \le C_{s,\beta}(T^{-\beta}+1)^2 e^{2\omega_s T}\|u(0)\|_s^2$ for heat; for quantum wavefunctions a deterministic bound on the $L^2$ norm of eigenstates is used.
- **Core assumption:** The PDE coefficients and boundary conditions satisfy standard smoothness conditions; the solution belongs to the predicted Sobolev space.
- **Evidence anchors:** Corollary 3.3 (Poisson), Corollary 3.4 (heat), Lemma 5.1 (quantum); empirical calibration curves in Figures 1–4 confirm that using these $B(A)$ leads to correct coverage.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conformal Prediction:** Provides distribution‑free marginal coverage guarantees for black‑box predictors under exchangeability. *Why needed:* Neural operators lack theoretical error bounds, so conformal prediction yields reliable uncertainty sets. *Quick check:* Does your score function produce regions $C(X)$ such that $P(Y\in C(X))\ge 1-\alpha$ regardless of the joint distribution of $(X,Y)$?
- **Sobolev Spaces:** Define smoothness of infinite‑dimensional function outputs; the spectral truncation norms must align with the function space structure to propagate coverage from truncated to full functions. *Why needed:* The method’s coverage guarantee relies on Sobolev embeddings and norms. *Quick check:* Are your operators and score functions norm‑compatible with the Sobolev space containing the true PDE solutions?
- **Spectral Neural Operators:** Learn mappings between function spaces via spectral truncation; preserve discretization invariance and allow efficient computation across multiple resolutions. *Why needed:* The conformal score is defined on truncated spectra; spectral operators naturally interface with this representation. *Quick check:* Does your operator preserve a functional representation and allow efficient computation at varying truncation levels $N$?
- **Robust Optimization (Min‑Max):** Transforms uncertainty sets into decisions resilient to predictor misspecification. *Why needed:* Conformal sets correct for model error; robust optimization protects against worst‑case deviations within those sets. *Quick check:* Is your objective Lipschitz in the uncertain functional parameter to bound the suboptimality gap?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Module → Operator Learner → Conformal Calibrator → Uncertainty Constructor → Optimization Engine → Validator

**Critical path:** Generate PDE solution data → Train spectral neural operator $G$ on truncated outputs → Compute calibration scores and quantile $bq_{N;\tau}$ → Determine problem‑specific bound $B(A)$ → Construct uncertainty set $C_{N;\tau}^*(a)$ or its finite‑dimensional counterpart $V_{N;\tau}(a)$ → Run multi‑stage robust optimization (Algorithm 1) → Evaluate coverage and suboptimality on test set.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Smoothness $\tau$: Larger $\tau$ accelerates margin decay ($N^{-2\tau}$) but inflates $bq_{N;\tau}$ (conservative sets). Default $\tau = s$ if $B(A)$ is well‑behaved.
- Truncation $N$: Larger $N$ shrinks the margin but increases computational cost (dot products in $H^{s-\tau}$ scale with $N^d$). Choose $N$ based on smoothness of true functions; start with $N_1\approx 16$–32 for 2‑D problems.
- Number of stages $T$: More stages reduce early‑stage cost but may yield diminishing returns if $w_t^*$ already stabilizes. Balance against total runtime.
- Coverage level $\alpha$: Smaller $\alpha$ (e.g., 0.05) yields larger uncertainty sets, increasing robustness but potentially suboptimal decisions. Set according to safety requirements.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Undercoverage** (empirical residual $> $ margin): $B(A)$ too small or $N$ too low. Check $\|u\|_s$ range in calibration data; increase $\tau$ or $N$.
2. **Over‑conservative robust decisions** (gap $\Delta^*$ near bound): $bq_{N;\tau}$ inflated. Reduce $\tau$ or verify score norm choice.
3. **Optimization divergence** in Algorithm 1: Lipschitz constant $L$ unknown; use adaptive step size or line search. Ensure $V_{N_t;\tau}(a)$ fits in memory.
4. **Poor separation from nominal:** Uncertainty sets too tight. Check that $N^{-2\tau}$ margin is not negligible; increase $\alpha$ slightly or smooth GRF inputs more (higher $\rho$).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Calibration check:** For quantum Hamiltonians (Lemma 5.1), plot empirical coverage vs nominal level across $\rho$, $N$. Verify corrected curves lie above the diagonal; uncorrected fall below.
2. **PDE generality:** Repeat calibration for Poisson and heat equations (Figures 3, 4) to confirm Theorem 3.2/3.3 hold with Corollary bounds; compute $C_{s,L}$ correctly.
3. **Robust gain test:** On the collection problem, compute paired t‑test of $\Delta^* = J(w_{\text{rob}},u) - J(w_{\text{nom}},u)$. Expect significance for moderate $N$ and smooth $\rho$. Visualize collector placements (Figure 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Iterative design settings:** How to extend the framework to iterative PDE‑constrained design problems (e.g., shape optimization) where predictions are made repeatedly, leading to uncertainty propagation and accumulation.
- **Non‑spectral operators:** Adapting the functional conformal coverage approach to neural operators based on discretized domains (e.g., Fourier neural operators) requires new score functions and coverage proofs.
- **Robust PDE control:** Incorporating conformal uncertainty sets into robust control synthesis (e.g., model predictive control) and establishing stability guarantees remains an open challenge.
- **Tighter quantum Gram matrix bounds:** The current Wielandt–Hoffman–based radius for the Gram matrix uncertainty set may be loose; future work should characterize problem‑specific conditions under which a smaller radius suffices.

## Limitations
- **Training details unspecified** (learning rates, epochs, batch size) might affect neural operator accuracy and thus coverage.
- **Margin correction** relies on problem‑specific bounds $B(A)$ that can be loose or difficult to derive for complex PDEs, risking under‑coverage.
- **Smoothness assumptions** on Sobolev norms and boundedness may not hold for real‑world data with rough inputs or irregular boundaries.
- **Nested uncertainty sets** are required for multi‑stage efficiency; if adversarial sets are non‑nested, warm‑starting may fail.
- **Quantum experimental details** (state constellations, Hamiltonians) are underspecified, hindering full replication.

## Confidence
- **Coverage guarantees:** High (Strong theory + empirical validation)
- **Multi‑stage efficiency:** Medium (Cost bound proven, but practical performance depends on tuning)
- **Robust decision improvement:** Medium (Statistical significance shown on specific tasks, but generality limited)

## Next Checks

2. **Stress test multi‑stage scheme:** Use non‑nested truncation levels (e.g., $N_1=32, N_2=20, N_3=40$) or aggressive jumps in the collection problem; compare objective gap and runtime against the nested schedule.
