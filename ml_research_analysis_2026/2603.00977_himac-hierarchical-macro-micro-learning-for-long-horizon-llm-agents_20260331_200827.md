# HiMAC: Hierarchical Macro-Micro Learning for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.00977
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00977
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** HiMAC achieves state-of-the-art performance on long-horizon benchmarks, improving success rates by up to 16% over strong RL baselines like GiGPO while demonstrating superior sample efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Long-horizon tasks challenge LLM agents because errors in flat policies cascade across entire trajectories. HiMAC introduces a hierarchical solution that explicitly separates high-level planning from low-level execution. A Macro-Policy generates a structured blueprint of sub-goals, which a shared-backbone Micro-Policy executes sequentially, using a learned `<sub_done>` token to transition between goals. Training uses a critic-free, bi-level group-based reinforcement learning approach and an iterative co-evolution strategy that alternates between updating the planner and the executor. This design contains error propagation within sub-goal segments, stabilizes credit assignment, and mitigates non-stationarity, leading to significant gains on ALFWorld, WebShop, and Sokoban.

## Method Summary
HiMAC factors the policy into a Macro-Policy `π_macro` that produces a blueprint `z = (g1, ..., gK)` of sub-goals from the task instruction, and a Micro-Policy `π_micro` that executes atomic actions conditioned on the current active sub-goal `gk`. Execution advances to the next sub-goal only upon generating a special `<sub_done>` token. Training alternates between two phases: **Phase A (Macro-Exploration)** samples a group of blueprints, evaluates each with a frozen, greedy Micro-Policy to estimate their return, and updates only the blueprint tokens via a group-relative policy optimization loss. **Phase B (Micro-Adaptation)** selects the highest-return blueprint `z*`, samples execution trajectories conditioned on it, and updates only the atomic action tokens. This co-evolutionary alternation isolates each level's optimization to a stationary objective, avoiding destructive interference.

## Key Results
*   HiMAC sets new state-of-the-art success rates on ALFWorld, WebShop, and Sokoban, with improvements of up to 16% over prior RL-based LLM agent methods like GiGPO.
*   The approach demonstrates superior sample efficiency, reaching target performance with significantly fewer environment interactions than flat policy baselines.
*   Ablations confirm the importance of the `<sub_done>` token (3.9–4.3% drop without it) and the iterative co-evolution strategy (6.8–9.3% drop without it).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Hierarchical Decoupling Contains Error Propagation**
The Macro-Policy generates a blueprint, and the Micro-Policy executes each sub-goal autonomously until emitting `<sub_done>`. This rigid conditioning acts as a temporal attention mask, narrowing the agent's context window to the relevant task segment and preventing semantic drift from cascading across the full trajectory. Failures are contained within sub-goal segments. Ablation removing `<sub_done>` causes a 3.9–4.3% performance drop, confirming the mechanism's sensitivity to termination fidelity.

**Mechanism 2: Critic-Free Hierarchical Group-Based RL Improves Credit Assignment**
Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is applied bi-level-wise. Blueprints are grouped for Macro updates, isolating plan quality from execution variance. Execution trajectories conditioned on the *same* blueprint are grouped for Micro updates, isolating action quality. This clean separation provides level-specific advantage estimates with lower variance than flat GRPO, where a single group conflates plan and execution quality. The bi-level extension addresses a cited limitation of prior flat group-based methods.

**Mechanism 3: Iterative Co-Evolution Stabilizes Bi-Level Optimization**
Training alternates between Phase A (Macro update with frozen Micro) and Phase B (Micro update with frozen best blueprint). This converts the non-stationary problem of jointly optimizing two interdependent policies into a sequence of stationary single-level optimization problems. Without this alternation, the planner optimizes for an evolving executor and vice versa, causing destructive gradient interference. Ablation removing the alternation results in a 6.8–9.3% performance drop, validating the stability gain.

    *   **Why needed here:** HiMAC's entire critic-free optimization paradigm is an extension of GRPO to a bi-level structure. One must understand how group-relative advantages replace value-based targets and why this reduces variance in sparse-reward, high-dimensional language spaces.
    *   **Quick check:** Why is the group size *N* critical for stable advantage estimation in GRPO, and how might it interact with HiMAC's two-level grouping?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Task Instruction `x` -> Macro-Policy (`π_macro`) -> Blueprint `z` (sequence of sub-goals) -> Micro-Policy (`π_micro`, shared backbone) -> Atomic Action `a_t` or `<sub_done>` -> Environment

**Critical path:** For a given task `x`, **Phase A**: Sample blueprint group `{z^(i)}`; for each, run greedy rollout with frozen `π_micro` to get returns `R(z^(i))`; compute group-relative `Â_macro`; update **blueprint token parameters** via `J_macro`. Select `z^* = argmax R(z^(i))`. **Phase B**: Sample trajectory group `{τ^(j)}` conditioned on frozen `z^*`; compute `Â_micro`; update **action token parameters** via `J_micro`. Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Blueprint expressivity vs. executability:** Free-form natural language blueprints are flexible but risk incoherence or infeasibility for the current Micro-Policy. The `z^*` selection and co-evolution act as an implicit curriculum.
*   **Single shared backbone vs. separate models:** Sharing parameters (`π_θ`) enables knowledge transfer but com gradient interference. Phase alternation and token-masking (blueprint vs. action positions) mitigate this.
*   **Rigid `<sub_done>` vs. fixed step budget:** The learned termination is adaptive but can fail if the Micro-Policy is under-trained. A budget fallback is a safety net but reintroduces inefficiency.

**Failure signatures:**
1.  **Context drift persists:** Micro-Policy rarely emits `<sub_done>`, leading to long, erratic sub-goal executions. *Diagnostic:* Monitor `<sub_done>` frequency per sub-goal.
2.  **Planner proposes nonsense:** Blueprints become semantically incoherent or impossible. *Diagnostic:* Low `R(z^(i))` variance in Phase A group; planner isn't finding feasible plans.
3.  **Training instability/divergence:** Loss spikes in either phase. *Diagnostic:* Likely caused by violating stationary assumption (imbalanced phase counts or too small group sizes `G`/`M`).

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Blueprint Quality Sanity Check:** Run Phase A only (Macro updates) with a perfect frozen Micro-Policy. Track if blueprints become more efficient and coherent over iterations. Isolates Macro learning.
2.  **Ablation: Co-Evolution:** Implement a baseline where both levels update simultaneously in one combined loss. Compare stability (loss variance) and final performance to the alternating method. *Expected:* Simultaneous updates show higher loss variance and lower final success.
3.  **Ablation: `<sub_done>` Token:** Replace with a fixed step budget per sub-goal (e.g., `T_limit`). Compare total steps to completion and final success rate. *Expected:* Fixed budget reduces success rate and increases average episode length.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

*   **Open Question 1:** Can HiMAC's hierarchical framework scale to truly open-ended environments with unbounded action/observation spaces, or does it fail when tasks lack clear, decomposable semantics?
    *   **Basis:** Conclusion states future work will explore more open-ended environments.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Evaluations are on benchmarks with discretizable objectives. Open-endedness breaks assumptions about valid blueprints and sub-goal termination.

*   **Open Question 2:** To what extent are learned blueprints transferable across different tasks or environments? Can they accelerate learning on new problems, or are they overly specialized?
    *   **Basis:** Conclusion mentions investigating blueprint transferability.
    *   **Why unresolved:** All experiments train and test on the same benchmark suite. No cross-domain transfer experiments are conducted.

*   **Open Question 3:** What is the computational and sample efficiency trade-off of the hierarchical architecture? Does the overhead of evaluating multiple blueprints (Phase A) negate gains for shorter tasks?
    *   **Basis:** Inferred from the two-phase rollout process (G blueprints then M trajectories) not being cost-analyzed against flat baselines.
    *   **Why unresolved:** "Superior sample efficiency" is relative to flat RL baselines, but absolute cost (wall-clock, FLOPs) and performance on short-horizon tasks are not measured.

*   **Open Question 4:** How robust is the `<sub_done>` termination under highly stochastic or adversarial observations that might trigger premature or belated completion?
    *   **Basis:** Inferred from the critical role of `<sub_done>` shown in ablation, but no stress-testing under distribution shift or noise.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The token is learned by the Micro-Policy. Its reliability under perceptually challenging conditions, which would break hierarchical containment, is not evaluated.

*   **Open Question 5:** Does Iterative Co-Evolution guarantee convergence to a stable joint optimum, or can it cause harmful "lock-in" where the planner's blueprints become biased toward the executor's current limited capabilities?
    *   **Basis:** Inferred from the described emergent curriculum, but no analysis of convergence dynamics or blueprint complexity over time.
    *   **Why unresolved:** No ablation varying phase alternation frequency or analyzing the distribution of blueprint complexity (e.g., sub-goal count) to check for premature plateauing.

## Limitations
*   Key hyperparameters like exact group sizes (`G`, `M`) and rollout budgets are unspecified, making full reproduction and sensitivity analysis difficult.
*   Blueprint representation format and prompt engineering details are underspecified, creating implementation variance risk.
*   The `<sub_done>` token's training signal and the "lightweight budget-based fallback" mechanism are not fully described, potentially leading to unstable termination behavior.
*   The claim of improved credit assignment from bi-level grouping lacks a direct ablation isolating it from other co-occurring design choices (confidence: Low).

## Confidence

*   **Hierarchical decoupling contains error propagation:** Medium. Ablation shows degradation when `<sub_done>` is removed, but the causal chain (semantic drift) is not directly measured.
*   **Critic-free hierarchical group-based RL improves credit assignment:** Low. No direct ablation isolates the bi-level grouping; gains may stem from other factors.
*   **Iterative co-evolution stabilizes training:** High. Removing alternation causes a large performance drop (6.8–9.3%), confirming its stabilizing role.

## Next Checks

1.  **Group-size sensitivity study:** Train with varying `G` and `M` (e.g., {4, 8, 16}) to determine performance plateaus or degradation, testing robustness of group-relative advantage estimation.
2.  **Blueprint quality trajectory:** With an oracle (perfect) Micro-Policy, evaluate Macro-Policy blueprint coherence and feasibility over training iterations to confirm macro-learning progress.
3.  **Gradient leakage detection:** Instrument training to log parameter gradients during Phase A and B. Verify that only intended token positions (blueprint vs. action) receive updates and frozen levels' parameters remain unchanged.
