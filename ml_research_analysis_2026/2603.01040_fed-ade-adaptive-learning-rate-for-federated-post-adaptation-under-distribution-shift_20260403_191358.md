# Fed-ADE: Adaptive Learning Rate for Federated Post-adaptation under Distribution Shift

## Quick Facts

- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.01040  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01040  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Fed-ADE introduces an adaptive per-client learning rate computed from unsupervised uncertainty and representation dynamics estimators, achieving up to 6% accuracy gains over strong federated baselines and up to 24× wall‑time efficiency.

## Executive Summary

Federated learning after deployment faces non‑stationary data streams across heterogeneous clients without ground‑truth labels. Fixed learning rates often lead to underfitting or divergence under client‑specific, time‑varying distribution shifts. Fed-ADE addresses this by estimating per‑client, per‑timestep adaptive learning rates using two lightweight, unsupervised signals: uncertainty dynamics (changes in predictive uncertainty) and representation dynamics (feature drift). The approach splits each client model into shared and personalized layers, and uses a pre‑computed confusion matrix for unsupervised risk estimation. Theoretically, the method yields a dynamic regret bound of \(O(\bar{S}_c^{1/3} T^{2/3})\) and convergence guarantees under convex losses.

Empirical results on multiple datasets (Tiny ImageNet, CIFAR‑10/100, CIFAR‑10/100‑C, LAMA) demonstrate consistent improvements: average accuracy gains of 1‑2% over fixed learning rates and up to 6% over competing federated methods (FTH, ATLAS, Fed‑POE, FedCCFA), while being up to 24× more wall‑time efficient. The method also shows robustness to hyperparameter choices and to mismatches between pre‑training and deployment distributions.

## Method Summary

Fed-ADE operates in a federated setting where each client maintains a model split into shared layers \(\psi^c\) (communicated) and personalized layers \(\phi^c\) (local). At each timestep, a client draws an unlabeled batch, computes two summary statistics: the mean softmax vector \(q_t^c\) (uncertainty) and the mean ℓ2‑normalized feature vector \(z_t^c\) from the shared layers (representation). The cosine distances to the previous timestep yield \(S_{\text{unc}}^c\) and \(S_{\text{rep}}^c\); their average \(S_c^c\) estimates the local distribution shift magnitude. The per‑client learning rate is then set as \(\eta_t^c = \eta_{\min} + (\eta_{\max}-\eta_{\min}) S_c^c\), bounded within predefined limits.

To train without labels, Fed-ADE uses Black‑Box Shift Estimation (BBSE). A confusion matrix \(M\) computed from pre‑training data maps pseudo‑label distribution \(q_t^c\) to an estimated true label distribution \(w_t^c = M^{-1} q_t^c\) (projected onto the simplex). The unsupervised risk \(\hat{F}_t^c\) is a weighted sum of class‑wise risks initially measured on a small labeled dataset from the source distribution. Each communication round proceeds in two phases: (1) participating clients update both \(\psi^c\) and \(\phi^c\) using the adaptive rate and \(\hat{F}_t^c\); they upload \(\psi^c\) to the server; (2) the server aggregates \(\psi^c\) (weighted by local batch sizes) into \(\bar{\psi}\) and broadcasts it; all clients replace their \(\psi^c\) with \(\bar{\psi}\) and then perform an additional update solely on \(\phi^c\) using the same learning rate and (re‑computed) unsupervised risk. This protocol enables fast yet stable adaptation to non‑stationary, unlabeled client streams.

## Key Results

- **Accuracy gains**: 1‑2% higher than fixed‑learning‑rate baselines and up to 6% over strong federated methods (Fed‑POE, FedCCFA) across label and covariate shift benchmarks.
- **Efficiency**: Up to 24× reduction in wall‑clock time compared to prior FL approaches that require additional communication or complex computations.
- **Robustness**: Performance remains stable across a wide range of learning‑rate bounds and shows tolerance to pre‑training distribution mismatches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Uncertainty Dynamics
**Claim**: Temporal change in the batch predictive distribution (mean softmax) approximates the magnitude of label distribution shift.  
**Mechanism**: Compute \(q_t^c = \frac{1}{|x_t^c|}\sum_{x\in x_t^c} H(\theta^c;x)\). The cosine distance \(S_{\text{unc}}^c = 1-\cos(q_{t-1}^c,q_t^c)\) measures how the model’s average uncertainty has shifted.  
**Assumptions**: The model is sufficiently calibrated (predictive distribution close to true label distribution) and the batch summary is representative.  
**Evidence**: Analysis in Section 5.1 (Theorem 1) bounds the error of this estimator under \(\epsilon\)-calibration. The method is referenced in the abstract and defined in Section 4.2 (Eqs. 11, 12).  
**Break condition**: Miscalibration (e.g., overconfidence) or very small batches cause noise in \(q_t^c\), making \(S_{\text{unc}}^c\) unreliable.

### Representation Dynamics
**Claim**: Changes in feature representations capture covariate shift (feature distribution drift).  
**Mechanism**: Extract features \(h_{\psi^c}(x)\) from shared layers, compute ℓ2‑normalized batch mean \(z_t^c = \frac{1}{|x_t^c|}\sum \frac{h_{\psi^c}(x)}{\|h_{\psi^c}(x)\|_2}\). Then \(S_{\text{rep}}^c = \frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(z_{t-1}^c,z_t^c))\) measures directional drift.  
**Assumptions**: The feature extractor \(h_{\psi^c}\) is Lipschitz continuous and the batch feature mean concentrates around the population mean.  
**Evidence**: Section 5.1 (Theorem 2) provides an error bound under these conditions; Section 4.2 (Eqs. 13, 14) defines the estimator.  
**Break condition**: Non‑Lipschitz updates, extremely small batches, or shifts purely in label distribution (without feature change) can render \(S_{\text{rep}}^c\) uninformative.

### Adaptive Learning Rate via Combined Shift Signal
**Claim**: The per‑client, per‑timestep learning rate \(\eta_t^c = \eta_{\min} + (\eta_{\max}-\eta_{\min}) S_c^c\) with \(S_c^c = \frac{1}{2}(S_{\text{unc}}^c + S_{\text{rep}}^c)\) achieves dynamic regret \(O(\bar{S}_c^{1/3} T^{2/3})\) and stable convergence.  
**Mechanism**: The combined signal \(S_c^c\) estimates the local shift magnitude. Large \(S_c^c\) yields larger steps for rapid adaptation; small values yield conservative steps. The bounds \([\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max}]\) ensure stability even if the estimate is imperfect.  
**Assumptions**: The cumulative surrogate \(\bar{S}_c\) approximates the true total variation \(V_c\); the optimal learning rate \(\eta^* = \Theta(T^{-1/3}\bar{S}_c^{1/3})\) lies within the specified interval. The underlying loss is convex in the model parameters.  
**Evidence**: Section 4.1 (Eq. 9) defines the adaptive rate; Section 5.2 (Theorem 3) and Appendix E.2 derive the regret bound and show min‑max optimality.  
**Break condition**: If either estimator systematically under‑ or over‑estimates the true shift, \(S_c^c\) becomes inaccurate. If \(\eta^*\) falls outside \([\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max}]\), adaptation becomes sub‑optimal (underfitting or divergence). Non‑convex losses invalidate the theoretical guarantee.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: Partial Model Personalization in Federated Learning
- **Why needed**: In non‑stationary FL, clients experience distribution shifts that are unique and time‑varying. Sharing all parameters would force consensus and hinder adaptation; keeping some layers fully local allows personalization while still benefiting from global knowledge.  
- **Quick check**: After the server broadcasts the aggregated shared parameters \(\bar{\psi}\), what layers does each client update? (Answer: only the personalized layers \(\phi^c\) in the second phase.)

### Concept 2: Black‑Box Shift Estimation (BBSE) for Unsupervised Risk
- **Why needed**: Without ground‑truth labels, the expected empirical risk cannot be computed directly. BBSE uses a confusion matrix estimated from source (pre‑training) data to correct the pseudo‑label distribution obtained from model predictions, yielding an unbiased estimate of the true label distribution under covariate shift.  
- **Quick check**: What role does the confusion matrix \(M\) play in Fed‑ADE’s unsupervised risk estimator? (Answer: It maps the model’s average softmax output \(q_t^c\) to an estimated true label distribution \(w_t^c = M^{-1}q_t^c\).)

### Concept 3: Dynamic Regret in Non‑Stationary Online Learning
- **Why needed**: Standard regret assumes a stationary environment; in FL with continuous client drifts, performance must be measured against a sequence of changing optimal decisions. Dynamic regret captures the cumulative excess loss relative to these moving targets, providing a more appropriate performance measure.  
- **Quick check**: How does the dynamic regret bound \(O(\bar{S}_c^{1/3} T^{2/3})\) scale with the total variation \(\bar{S}_c\)? (Answer: As \(\bar{S}_c\) increases, the bound grows proportionally to \(\bar{S}_c^{1/3}\); it is sublinear in \(T\).)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
- **Server**: Maintains confusion matrix \(M\) (pre‑computed), aggregates uploaded shared parameters \(\psi^c\) via weighted averaging to produce \(\bar{\psi}\), and broadcasts \(\bar{\psi}\) to all clients.
- **Client**:
  - **Model**: \(\theta^c = (\psi^c, \phi^c)\); \(\psi^c\) (shared) is communicated, \(\phi^c\) (personalized) is local.
  - **Distribution estimators**: Uncertainty estimator computes batch mean softmax \(q_t^c\) and stores \(q_{t-1}^c\); Representation estimator computes batch mean normalized feature \(z_t^c\) and stores \(z_{t-1}^c\).
  - **Adaptive LR module**: Computes \(S_{\text{unc}}^c\), \(S_{\text{rep}}^c\), and \(S_c^c\), then sets \(\eta_t^c \in [\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max}]\).
  - **Unsupervised risk**: Uses pre‑computed class‑wise risks \(\hat{F}^{0,i}_c\) from the initial labeled data and the estimated label weights \(w_t^c = M^{-1}q_t^c\) (simplex‑projected) to form \(\hat{F}_t^c = \sum_i w_t^c[i] \cdot \hat{F}^{0,i}_c\).
  - **Updater**: First update: gradient step on both \(\psi^c\) and \(\phi^c\) using \(\eta_t^c\) and \(\nabla\hat{F}_t^c\); second update (after receiving \(\bar{\psi}\)): replace \(\psi^c \leftarrow \bar{\psi}\) and then update only \(\phi^c\) with the same rate (re‑computing \(\hat{F}_t^c\) if needed).
- **Pre‑training data**: Used solely to construct the confusion matrix \(M\) on the server.

### Critical Path
1. **Initialization**: Pre‑train a global model on source data; compute confusion matrix \(M\); initialize client models with global weights; each client obtains a small labeled dataset to compute \(\hat{F}^{0,i}_c\).
2. **Per communication round**:
   a. Server selects participating clients \(\mathcal{C}^{(r)}\).
   b. For each client \(c \in \mathcal{C}^{(r)}\):
      i. Draw unlabeled batch \(x_t^c\).
      ii. Compute current summaries \(q_t^c\) and \(z_t^c\).
      iii. Retrieve stored previous summaries \(q_{t-1}^c, z_{t-1}^c\) (initialized to zero at \(t=1\)).
      iv. Compute \(S_{\text{unc}}^c\), \(S_{\text{rep}}^c\), \(S_c^c\) and set \(\eta_t^c\).
      v. Estimate label distribution \(w_t^c\) via BBSE.
      vi. Assemble unsupervised loss \(\hat{F}_t^c\).
      vii. Compute gradient \(g = \nabla_{(\psi^c,\phi^c)} \hat{F}_t^c\).
      viii. Perform first update: \(\psi^c \leftarrow \psi^c - \eta_t^c g_\psi\), \(\phi^c \leftarrow \phi^c - \eta_t^c g_\phi\).
      ix. Upload \(\psi^c\) to server.
   c. Server aggregates received \(\psi^c\) (using batch‑size weights) into \(\bar{\psi}\).
   d. Server broadcasts \(\bar{\psi}\) to all clients.
   e. After receiving \(\bar{\psi}\), every client (including non‑participants):
      i. Replace \(\psi^c \leftarrow \bar{\psi}\).
      ii. Re‑compute (or adjust) \(\hat{F}_t^c\) with the updated shared parameters.
      iii. Compute gradient w.r.t. \(\phi^c\) only (freeze \(\psi^c\)).
      iv. Second update: \(\phi^c \leftarrow \phi^c - \eta_t^c \nabla_{\phi^c} \hat{F}_t^c\).
      v. Cache \(q_t^c, z_t^c\) for the next timestep.
3. Loop to step 2.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Memory vs. Accuracy**: Storing previous summaries requires \(O(|\mathcal{I}|+d)\) memory per client (negligible relative to model size). Larger memory could store longer histories for smoother estimates.
- **Computation vs. Robustness**: Estimators (cosine distances) are cheap, but BBSE involves solving a linear system and simplex projection; cost grows with number of classes \(|\mathcal{I}|\) but remains acceptable for moderate \(|\mathcal{I}|\). For thousands of classes, overhead may become significant.
- **Learning‑rate bounds**: The interval \([\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max}]\) must encompass the optimal rate \(\eta^*\). Too narrow a range can miss the optimum, degrading performance; too wide may risk instability. Fed‑ADE is empirically robust but still requires some tuning.
- **Two‑step update**: Using the same adaptive rate for both updates simplifies design but may not be optimal. Separate rates could further improve stability at the cost of additional hyperparameters.

### Failure Signatures
- **Erratic learning rates**: \(S_c^c\) oscillates between near‑0 and near‑1. Likely caused by noisy batch summaries (small batch size or miscalibration). Remedy: increase batch size, apply temperature scaling.
- **Divergence**: Accuracy collapses and loss explodes. Could indicate \(\eta_{\max}\) too large, ill‑conditioned confusion matrix, or corrupted risk estimates. Check gradient norms and condition number of \(M\).
- **Stagnation**: No improvement over fixed‑LR baseline. Possible uninformative estimators (e.g., label shift without uncertainty change) or biased initial class‑wise risk estimates. Verify that \(S_{\text{unc}}\) and \(S_{\text{rep}}\) respond to known shifts.
- **Communication overhead**: Although not increased by the algorithm, in large‑scale FL stragglers or client dropouts can delay aggregation; consider partial participation and client sampling strategies.
- **BBSE projection failures**: The solution \(w_t^c\) contains negative entries or fails to sum to 1. Regularize inversion of \(M\) (e.g., add \(\lambda I\)) or use a more robust solver.

### First Three Experiments (as in the paper)
1. **Comparison with Fixed Learning Rates and Other FL Baselines** – Evaluate Fed‑ADE against fixed‑LR variants and methods like Fed‑POE, FedCCFA on label shift (Tiny ImageNet, CIFAR‑10, LAMA) and covariate shift (CIFAR‑10‑C, CIFAR‑100‑C). Fed‑ADE consistently outperforms, gaining 1‑2% over FixLR and up to 6% over competitors, with similar or lower wall‑time.
2. **Ablation on Estimators** – Remove either \(S_{\text{unc}}\) or \(S_{\text{rep}}\) from the combined signal. Results (Table 4) confirm both components contribute; removing uncertainty hurts label shift most, removing representation slightly hurts covariate shift, and fixed LR performs worst.
3. **Sensitivity to Learning‑Rate Bounds** – Vary \(\eta_{\min}\) and \(\eta_{\max}\) while keeping the other fixed. Fed‑ADE’s accuracy remains stable across a broad range, whereas fixed‑LR performance collapses when the learning rate is not well‑tuned. Demonstrates robustness to hyperparameter choices.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Under what conditions do the uncertainty and representation dynamics estimators fail to capture true distribution shifts, and how can their combination be optimized for complex, multimodal shifts?
- How does Fed‑ADE scale to very large federated networks (e.g., thousands of clients) with highly heterogeneous, rapidly shifting distributions?
- Can the adaptive learning rate bounds \([\eta_{\min},\eta_{\max}]\) be set automatically, eliminating manual tuning?
- How does the choice of shared vs. personalized layers interact with the adaptive learning rate, and could different partitioning improve adaptation?

## Limitations

- **Theoretical assumptions**: The analysis assumes convex losses and ideal conditions (model calibration, Lipschitz features); deep non‑convex networks may violate these, weakening guarantees.
- **BBSE dependence**: The unsupervised risk relies on a pre‑computed confusion matrix \(M\); inaccurate \(M\) (due to small pre‑training data or distribution mismatch) can degrade adaptation.
- **Estimator instability**: Batch‑based estimators become noisy with small batch sizes or miscalibrated models, leading to erratic learning rates and possible divergence.
- **Simulated evaluation**: Shifts are generated via standard corruptions or Dirichlet label splits; real‑world non‑stationary streams may exhibit richer, client‑correlated dynamics not captured in experiments.

## Confidence

- **Adaptive LR improves adaptation**: High (consistent empirical gains across datasets and baselines).
- **Estimators capture true shift magnitude**: Medium (theoretically bounded but sensitive to calibration and Lipschitz assumptions).
- **Dynamic regret guarantee (\(O(\bar{S}_c^{1/3} T^{2/3})\))**: High within the convex setting (theorem proved).
- **Robustness to hyperparameters and pre‑training mismatch**: Medium (shown on simulated shifts; real‑world behavior uncertain).
