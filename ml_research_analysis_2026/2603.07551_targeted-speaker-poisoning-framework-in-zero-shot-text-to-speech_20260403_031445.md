# Targeted Speaker Poisoning Framework in Zero-Shot Text-to-Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07551
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07551
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Encoder-Guided Poisoning (EGP) achieves strong privacy-utility trade-off (AUC 0.79-0.95, low average FSSIM) for up to 15 forgotten speakers, but scalability degrades at 100 speakers (high Max-FSSIM ~0.91-0.95) due to latent crowding; triplet loss improves worst-case privacy with minor utility cost.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the failure of conventional machine unlearning to prevent zero-shot TTS models from cloning specific speaker identities, formalizing the problem as Speech Generation Speaker Poisoning (SGSP). The authors adapt Teacher-Guided Poisoning (TGP) and propose Encoder-Guided Poisoning (EGP) with optional triplet loss, modifying only the diffusion module of StyleTTS2 to remap forget-set speakers to retain-set identities. Evaluation using AUC and Forget Speaker Similarity (FSSIM) shows EGP consistently outperforms TGP, and triplet loss further improves privacy at a small cost to utility. However, scalability is limited: when forgetting 100 speakers, worst-case FSSIM remains high, indicating incomplete unlearning due to latent space crowding.

## Method Summary
The framework targets zero-shot TTS systems like StyleTTS2, which generate speech conditioned on a reference audio via a style encoder. The unlearning objective is to prevent the model from generating audio resembling any of a set of forgotten speakers while preserving performance on retained speakers. Two fine-tuning approaches are introduced: (1) Teacher-Guided Poisoning (TGP) uses a teacher model (same architecture, different random init) to generate mel-spectrograms conditioned on random retain-set speakers, and distills this into the student diffusion module when processing forget-set references. (2) Encoder-Guided Poisoning (EGP) bypasses the teacher by directly using the style encoder's output on the reference audio as the fine-tuning target, yielding a cleaner signal. Additionally, a triplet loss can be added to explicitly pull the diffusion output toward a retain-set anchor and away from a forget-set negative. Only the diffusion module's parameters are updated; all other components (text encoder, duration/prosody predictors, style encoder, vocoder) remain frozen. Training uses a mixture of forget-set and retain-set batches, with a scheduled probability to condition on forget-set samples.

## Key Results
- EGP consistently outperforms TGP in separating forget-set and retain-set speaker embeddings (higher AUC, lower average FSSIM).
- For up to 15 forgotten speakers, both methods achieve strong separation (AUC ~0.79-0.95) and low average FSSIM.
- At 100 forgotten speakers, Max-FSSIM remains high (~0.91-0.95), indicating severe latent space crowding and failure to unlearn some speakers.
- Adding triplet loss improves privacy metrics (higher AUC, lower FSSIM) but slightly degrades utility on forget-set samples (WER increases by ~0.4).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Teacher-Guided Poisoning (TGP)
**Claim:** Modifying the diffusion module using a teacher model's outputs can remap forget-set speaker embeddings toward retain-set identities.  
**Mechanism:** During fine-tuning, when a forget-set reference is provided (with probability p_forget), the student model is trained to match the teacher model's output, which was conditioned on a random retain-set speaker. This encourages the diffusion process to ignore the forget-set speaker's identity.  
**Core assumption:** The teacher model's output represents a "clean" retain-set speaker identity that the student can mimic, thereby overriding the forget-set conditioning.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "Teacher-Guided Poisoning (TGP) framework... modify trained models to prevent the generation of specific identities"; Section 3.3: "TGP follows a knowledge distillation paradigm... ground truths by conditioning a teacher model on... a randomly sampled speaker in the retain set".  
**Break condition:** If teacher and student capacities are identical, distillation provides a noisy signal, limiting effectiveness as shown by EGP's superior performance.

### Encoder-Guided Poisoning (EGP)
**Claim:** Directly targeting the style encoder's output, bypassing teacher generation, yields cleaner optimization and better speaker separation.  
**Mechanism:** Instead of using a teacher-generated mel-spectrogram as the fine-tuning target, EGP uses the ground-truth style encoder output from the reference audio. This removes generative noise from the teacher's synthesis process.  
**Core assumption:** The style encoder's output is a more stable and direct representation of speaker identity than a full synthetic utterance from the teacher.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "propose Encoder-Guided Poisoning (EGP)... directly learns speaker representations from ground-truth data"; Section 3.3: "EGP bypasses teacher-generated outputs and instead directly uses the output of the style encoder as the fine-tuning target"; Section 6.1: "EGP consistently outperforms TGP... EGP circumvents this by targeting the original style encoder representations, providing a cleaner target".  
**Break condition:** Relies on the quality and consistency of the frozen style encoder; if encoder embeddings are not discriminative or are noisy, the poisoning signal degrades.

### Triplet Loss Integration
**Claim:** Adding a triplet loss explicitly pushes diffusion outputs away from forget-set embeddings, improving worst-case privacy (Max-FSSIM).  
**Mechanism:** During training on forget-conditioned inputs, the loss minimizes distance between output and an anchor (retain-set identity) while maximizing distance to a negative (forget-set sample), with a margin β. This directly shapes the embedding space.  
**Core assumption:** The latent space structure allows explicit contrastive learning to separate forget-set clusters without severe penalty to retain-set utility.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "incorporate a contrastive objective to explicitly suppress forgotten identities"; Section 3.3: "We employ contrastive learning... triplet loss... pushes the diffusion output (x) away from a negative reference (n) sampled from F"; Section 6.1: "incorporating triplet loss shifts the forget-set distribution toward lower similarity... improving AUC".  
**Break condition:** In multi-speaker settings, many forget-set negatives exist; pushing away from one may inadvertently move toward another ("latent space crowding"), limiting scalability as seen in Max-FSSIM at 100 speakers.

## Foundational Learning
### Zero-Shot Text-to-Speech (TTS) Voice Cloning
**Why needed:** The entire problem exists because zero-shot TTS can synthesize novel speakers from short references, making simple data deletion insufficient for "unlearning."  
**Quick check:** If a model is trained on speakers A, B, C, can it convincingly synthesize speaker D (unseen during training) from a 3-second prompt? (Yes, in zero-shot systems like StyleTTS2.)

### Speaker Embeddings & Cosine Similarity
**Why needed:** Privacy (FSSIM) and utility (SSIM) metrics are defined via cosine similarity in a speaker embedding space (WavLM). Understanding this metric is key to interpreting results.  
**Quick check:** Does a cosine similarity of 0.9 between synthesized and reference speech indicate high or low speaker similarity? (High.)

### Diffusion Models for Speech Generation
**Why needed:** The attack only modifies the diffusion module of StyleTTS2. Knowing diffusion's role (generating mel-spectrograms from noise conditioned on style) explains why tuning it affects speaker identity.  
**Quick check:** In a diffusion-based TTS, which component most directly controls the speaker characteristics of the output? (The style encoder and diffusion module.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Text Encoder → Duration/Prosody Predictors → Style Encoder → Diffusion Sampler → Vocoder. Only the Diffusion module is fine-tuned; all others frozen.

**Critical path:**
1. Load pretrained StyleTTS2 checkpoint.
2. Freeze text encoder, duration/prosody predictors, and discriminators.
3. For each batch: randomly select transcript and reference audio.
4. With p_forget=0.5, if reference is from forget-set, replace it with forget-set sample; else use retain-set.
5. Compute target: for TGP use teacher's generated mel; for EGP use style encoder output of reference.
6. Compute diffusion loss (L2) between student's diffusion output and target.
7. For triplet variant, add L_triplet on student's diffusion output against anchor (retain-style encoder output) and negative (forget-set style encoder output).
8. Backpropagate, update only diffusion parameters.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Privacy vs. Utility: Triplet loss improves privacy but harms WER on forget-set.
- Scalability vs. Privacy: AUC remains high for 15 speakers, Max-FSSIM fails at 100.
- EGP vs. TGP: EGP more stable, no teacher dependency.
- Modifying only diffusion protects other capabilities but limits representational scope for unlearning.

**Failure signatures:**
1. High Max-FSSIM (>0.86 threshold) indicates some forget-set speaker is still being replicated.
2. Significant WER increase on retain-set (>~3.0 from Table 1) indicates utility collapse.
3. For triplet loss, FSSIM Avg drops but Max remains high in multi-speaker settings (Table 2), showing crowding.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Single-speaker sanity check: Train EGP (no triplet) for 1 forgotten speaker. Verify: FSSIM (forget) ~0.77, R-SSIM ~0.88, AUC ~0.79. Check distributions in Figure 2: blue curve shifted left relative to orange.
2. Multi-speaker scalability stress test: Train EGP for 100 forgotten speakers. Expected failure: Max-FSSIM ~0.94, distributions overlapping heavily (Figure 2, last row). Confirms latent crowding limit.
3. Ablation of triplet loss: Compare EGP vs. EGP+Triplet for 15 speakers. Expected: Triplet lowers FSSIM and raises AUC, but check WER on F—may slightly increase (Table 2, EGP 3.00 → EGP+Trip. 3.38), showing utility cost.

## Limitations
- **Scalability ceiling:** Performance degrades sharply at 100 forgotten speakers (Max-FSSIM ~0.91–0.95) due to latent space crowding.
- **Utility-privacy trade-off:** Triplet loss explicitly improves worst-case privacy but incurs a measurable WER penalty on forget-set samples (~0.4 WER increase).
- **Assumption reliance:** EGP’s superiority assumes the frozen style encoder produces stable, discriminative embeddings; no analysis on how encoder quality affects poisoning efficacy.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism claims (TGP/EGP/triplet loss):** High
- **Scalability limit at 100 speakers:** High
- **Break conditions (e.g., teacher-student capacity, encoder quality):** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Encoder robustness test:** Replicate EGP using alternative, more expressive speaker encoders (e.g., ECAPA-TDNN) to determine if improved embedding separability mitigates crowding at 100 speakers.
2. **Crowding root-cause analysis:** Visualize the diffusion latent space (e.g., via t-SNE) for 100 forget-set speakers to quantify overlap. Compare against a baseline where speaker embeddings are artificially decorrelated to isolate crowding from acoustic similarity.
3. **Non-speaker attribute retention audit:** Evaluate retain-set outputs for prosody naturalness (e.g., F0 correlation) and linguistic accuracy (e.g., phoneme error rate) to confirm that modifying only the diffusion module preserves non-identity attributes.
