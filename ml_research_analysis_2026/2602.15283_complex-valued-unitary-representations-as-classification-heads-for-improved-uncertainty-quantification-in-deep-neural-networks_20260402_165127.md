# Complex-Valued Unitary Representations as Classification Heads for Improved Uncertainty Quantification in Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15283
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15283
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** On CIFAR‑10, the unitary magnitude head achieves an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of 0.0146, a 2.4× improvement over a standard softmax head (0.0355) and 3.5× over temperature scaling (0.0510).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a quantum‑inspired classification head to improve uncertainty quantification in deep neural networks. The head projects features into a complex‑valued Hilbert space, normalises them to unit norm, and applies a learned unitary transformation parameterised by the Cayley map. The unitary evolution preserves the norm, which bounds the magnitudes of the subsequent logits, mitigating overconfidence and calibration errors. Two readout mechanisms are explored: the Born rule, which measures probabilities via squared inner products with learned measurement vectors, and a magnitude‑based approach that uses absolute values followed by a linear layer and softmax. Experiments on CIFAR‑10 show that the magnitude readout yields dramatically lower Expected Calibration Error (0.0146), while the Born rule aligns better with human perceptual uncertainty on CIFAR‑10H but suffers from poor absolute calibration due to an information bottleneck.

## Method Summary
The proposed architecture attaches a complex‑valued classification head to a frozen backbone. Backbone features are linearly mapped to a complex vector ψ₀ ∈ ℂᵈ via separate real and imaginary weight matrices. The vector is ℓ2‑normalised to unit norm. A learned skew‑symmetric matrix S (S = A − Aᵀ) is formed, and the Cayley transform U = (I−S)(I+S)⁻¹ yields an exactly unitary matrix. The state evolves as ψ′ = Uψ. Two readouts are possible: (i) Born rule: p(k) = |⟨mₖ|ψ′⟩|² normalised across classes; (ii) magnitude‑softmax: compute v = |ψ′|, produce logits via a linear layer W_c v + b_c, then apply softmax. Training optimises cross‑entropy with Adam. After a warmup period (no regularisation), a phase diversity regulariser λ·Var(∠ψ′) is added to encourage use of the complex phase. The hybrid design (frozen backbone) isolates head effects and simplifies ablation.

## Key Results
- The unitary magnitude head reduces ECE to 0.0146 on CIFAR‑10, improving 2.4× over a standard softmax head (0.0355) and 3.5× over temperature scaling (0.0510).
- The Born‑rule (WaveFunction) head achieves the lowest KL‑divergence (0.336) to human soft labels on CIFAR‑10H, suggesting better capture of perceptual ambiguity.
- Ablation experiments confirm that discarding phase information via the magnitude readout avoids the Born rule’s information bottleneck and preserves the calibration benefit of unitary dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Norm‑preserving unitary dynamics bound logit magnitudes
The head projects backbone features into a complex vector and ℓ2‑normalises it to unit norm. A learned unitary matrix U, parameterised via the Cayley transform (U = (I−S)(I+S)⁻¹ where S is skew‑symmetric), is applied. Unitarity guarantees that the state remains on the unit hypersphere for any input, regardless of feature scale. Consequently, any linear projection (logits) from this state is bounded by the Frobenius norm of the projection matrix (Theorem 1). Since softmax probabilities become increasingly peaked as logit magnitudes grow, this bound prevents systematic overconfidence and reduces Expected Calibration Error. The mechanism relies on exact Cayley‑parametrised unitarity (I+S invertible) and finite weight norms. If the Cayley inverse becomes numerically unstable or the ℓ2‑normalisation is omitted, the bound degrades.

### Born rule readout introduces an information bottleneck
The Born rule measures probabilities as p(k) = |⟨mₖ|ψ′⟩|² / ∑ⱼ|⟨mⱼ|ψ′⟩|². This mapping from the d‑dimensional complex state to C probabilities depends only on the projection onto the ≤ C‑dimensional subspace spanned by the measurement vectors {mₖ}. When d > C (e.g., 64 vs. 10 in the experiments), 2d−C real degrees of freedom are discarded (Proposition 2). Different states that differ only in the null space yield identical probabilities, limiting the classifier’s ability to express nuanced uncertainty. This information bottleneck often leads to over‑ or under‑confidence, explaining the high ECE (0.082) observed for the full WaveHead. The bottleneck severity depends on d relative to C; if the unitary evolution compresses all discriminative information into the measured subspace, the impact may be mitigated.

### Magnitude‑based readout avoids the bottleneck while retaining calibration gains
The magnitude‑based readout retains all d components |ψ′ᵢ|, forming a non‑negative vector in ℝᵈ. A linear layer then maps this to logits, followed by softmax. Because no information is discarded, the classifier maintains expressive capacity to adjust confidence levels. Combined with the bounded logit magnitudes from the unitary stage, this yields the lowest observed ECE (0.0146) while preserving accuracy. The magnitudes carry sufficient discriminative signal; phases, while useful for human alignment (CIFAR‑10H), are not required for calibration. If magnitudes become uninformative (e.g., phase collapse making all |ψ′ᵢ| similar) or the linear layer overfits, the advantage may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Backbone features → Complex projection (two linear maps) → ℓ2‑normalisation → Cayley unitary evolution → Readout (Born rule or magnitude‑softmax)

### Critical path (forward pass)
1. Compute ψ₀ = W_re f + i·W_im f from backbone features f.
2. Normalise: ψ ← ψ₀ / ‖ψ₀‖₂ (add small ε).
3. Form skew‑symmetric S = A − Aᵀ from learnable A.
4. Compute unitary U = (I−S)(I+S)⁻¹ (matrix inverse of I+S, possibly with jitter).
5. Evolve state: ψ′ = U ψ.
6a. (Born) For each class k, compute |⟨mₖ|ψ′⟩|², then normalise across classes to get probabilities.
6b. (Magnitude) Compute v = |ψ′| (element‑wise magnitudes); logits = W_c v + b_c; apply softmax.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Hilbert dimension d:** Larger d increases capacity and matrix inversion cost (O(d³)); default d=64 offers a good trade‑off.
- **Cayley vs other unitary parameterisations:** Cayley gives exact unitarity without iterative retraction but requires a matrix inverse; approximations trade precision for speed.
- **Readout choice:** Born rule yields better alignment with human uncertainty (low KL) but higher ECE; magnitude‑softmax gives best calibration. Choose based on application needs.
- **Phase regularisation:** Crucial to avoid collapse to real‑valued representations; linear ramp (λ to 1 over T_r epochs) balances phase usage without destabilising training.
- **Backbone freezing vs joint training:** Hybrid (frozen backbone) isolates head effects and eases ablation; end‑to‑end training may improve performance but introduces optimisation difficulties (convergence plateaus, higher asymptotic loss).

### Failure signatures
- **Numerical instability:** If I+S is near‑singular, its inverse becomes inaccurate, breaking unitarity and causing gradient explosions. Monitor condition number; add jitter to diagonal.
- **Zero‑norm projection:** If ψ₀ has near‑zero norm, ℓ2‑normalisation yields NaNs. Ensure ε > 0 in denominator and diagnose dead backbone units.
- **Phase collapse:** Without sufficient phase regularisation (or with too high λ), imaginary parts may vanish, losing complex‑representation benefits. Track phase variance across batch.
- **Born‑rule degeneracy:** If measurement vectors mₖ become nearly identical, probabilities approach uniform, harming accuracy and calibration.
- **OOD detection failure:** The method does not improve (and may worsen) out‑of‑distribution AUROC; do not rely on it for that purpose.
- **Convergence plateaus:** Complex‑valued models often converge slower and to a higher loss than real‑valued counterparts; use appropriate learning rates and warmup.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation of head variants:** Using a shared frozen backbone (ResNet‑18 pretrained on CIFAR‑10), train the six heads (including standard softmax, unitary magnitude, full WaveHead, etc.) and evaluate ECE, NLL, Brier, accuracy on CIFAR‑10 test set. Verify that the NoBorn (magnitude) head achieves the lowest ECE (~0.015) while matching standard softmax accuracy.
2. **Human‑uncertainty alignment:** With the same frozen backbone, evaluate all heads on the CIFAR‑10H test set (human soft labels). Compute KL‑divergence between model predictions and human distributions. Confirm that the WaveFunction (Born rule) head achieves the lowest KL (~0.336).
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Sweep Hilbert dimension d (16, 32, 64, 128) and warmup epochs T_w (0, 4, 8, 15) for the NoBorn head. Plot ECE versus these settings; validate robustness and the default choices (d=64, T_w=8) that yield the best calibration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does the calibration advantage persist on larger datasets like ImageNet? Unresolved; needs validation beyond CIFAR‑10.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the exact relationship between phase diversity encouraged by the regularizer and calibration performance? The role of phase is not isolated.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the Born rule’s superior human alignment be combined with the magnitude readout’s calibration via recalibration or a hybrid? The trade‑off remains open.
- **Open Question 4:** Why does the WaveTransformer fail on negation tasks, and does this generalize to other compositional semantics? The root cause (optimization vs fundamental mismatch) is not disentangled.
- **Open Question 5:** How does joint end‑to‑end training of backbone and head affect calibration compared to the hybrid design? This remains an open challenge due to optimisation difficulty.

## Limitations
- **Dataset scope:** Experiments only on CIFAR‑10; calibration advantage on larger/more complex datasets (e.g., ImageNet) remains unvalidated.
- **Reproducibility gap:** Phase regularisation hyperparameters (λ, T_r) are not fully specified; optimal values may vary with backbone/dataset.
- **No OOD benefit:** The approach does not improve (and may worsen) out‑of‑distribution detection, limiting its use in open‑set safety‑critical scenarios.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Unitary dynamics bound logit magnitudes and improve calibration. **Confidence:** High (supported by Theorem 1 and strong ablation results).
- **Claim:** Born rule introduces an information bottleneck that degrades calibration. **Confidence:** Medium (Proposition 2 and ablation show the effect, but bottleneck severity depends on d and may vary).
- **Claim:** Magnitude‑based readout avoids the bottleneck while preserving calibration gains. **Confidence:** High (direct ablation contrast validates).
- **Claim:** Born rule better aligns with human perceptual ambiguity. **Confidence:** Medium (single benchmark CIFAR‑10H shows lower KL, but needs replication).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑dataset validation:** Apply the unitary‑magnitude head to CIFAR‑100 and a subset of ImageNet; measure ECE, accuracy, and OOD AUROC to test robustness beyond CIFAR‑10.
2. **Phase regularisation sensitivity:** Systematically sweep λ (e.g., 0.05–0.2) and T_r (e.g., 5–15) with a frozen backbone; monitor phase diversity and resulting ECE to identify stable configurations.
3. **Numerical stability analysis:** Track condition number of I+S and deviation from orthogonality (UᵀU − I) during training; correlate with ECE to quantify the practical impact of Cayley inversion errors.
