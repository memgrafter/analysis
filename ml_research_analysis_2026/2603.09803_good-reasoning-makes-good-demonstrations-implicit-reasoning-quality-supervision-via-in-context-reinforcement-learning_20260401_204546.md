# Good Reasoning Makes Good Demonstrations: Implicit Reasoning Quality Supervision via In-Context Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2603.09803
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09803
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** IC-DAPO improves average accuracy by +2.5 points over DAPO at 1.5B/7B scales, with larger gains on competition math (+5.6 on AIME24, +5.8 on AIME25), using implicit reasoning-quality reweighting with <5% overhead.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a key limitation of standard Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR): it treats all correct solution traces equally, potentially reinforcing flawed reasoning. To address this, the authors introduce Evidence Gain, an intrinsic quality signal derived from the policy model's in-context learning ability. Evidence Gain measures how much a candidate reasoning trace improves the generation of high-quality reference solutions. By prepending demonstrations during training rollouts, the method implicitly reweights rewards by exp(Evidence Gain) without explicit computation. The resulting approach, In-Context RLVR (combined with DAPO as IC-DAPO), yields consistent accuracy improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including +2.5 average points across model scales and up to +5.8 on AIME competitions, while adding negligible computational overhead.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is Evidence Gain Δ(q,r) = E_e[log π_θ(er|q,r,eq) - log π_θ(er|eq)], where e=(eq,er) is a high-quality reference from a held-out set E, and (q,r) is a training query and reasoning trace. Δ captures how much trace r makes reference er more likely when provided as context, indicating transferable reasoning quality. The training objective samples a demonstration e ∈ E uniformly, prepends it to query q, and generates trace r from the conditioned policy π_θ(r|e,q). The KL-regularized reinforcement objective (e.g., DAPO) applied to these conditioned trajectories is mathematically equivalent to the standard objective augmented by reward reweighting w ∝ exp(Δ). Thus the implicit reweighting favors traces that are both correct and have high Evidence Gain, promoting better reasoning without extra supervision. The approach is evaluated on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen models (1.5B/7B) trained on KlearReasoner-MathSub-30K, using a demonstration set built from DeepSeek-R1-0528 outputs. Zero-shot accuracy is measured on AIME24/25, HMMT25, MATH500, AMC23, and OlympiadBench; reasoning quality is assessed by DeepSeek-V3.2 on eight dimensions.

## Key Results
- IC-DAPO improves average accuracy over DAPO by +2.5 points at both 1.5B and 7B scales.
- Larger gains on competition problems: +5.6 on AIME24 and +5.8 on AIME25 for the 1.5B model.
- Evidence Gain correlates stably with human-assessed reasoning quality (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.4) throughout training; the method adds less than 5% training overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The policy model's in-context learning (ICL) ability provides an intrinsic measure of reasoning quality (Demonstration Utility), quantified as Evidence Gain.
- **Mechanism:** Prepend a candidate reasoning trace `(q, r)` to a held-out high-quality reference `(eq, er)` and measure the increase in log-likelihood of generating `er`. Higher increase indicates `r` contains more transferable problem-solving patterns.
- **Core assumption:** High-quality reasoning traces serve as more effective in-context demonstrations for generating new high-quality solutions (i.e., `πθ(er|q,r,eq)` increases more with better `r`).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "the policy model’s own in-context learning ability provides an efficient way to measure it, yielding a quality signal termed Evidence Gain." [section 2] Formal definition `Δ(q, r) = E_e~E [logπθ(er|q,r,eq) − logπθ(er|eq)]` and validation showing positive correlation (ρ≈0.4) with human-assessed quality.
- **Break condition:** If the validation set `E` contains low-quality or non-transferable references, `Δ` may not reflect general reasoning quality. Also, if the model's ICL capability is too weak (e.g., small scale), `Δ` values may collapse.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Prepending demonstrations during training (In-Context RLVR) is mathematically equivalent to standard RLVR with rewards implicitly reweighted by `exp(Δ)`.
- **Mechanism:** A Bayesian identity shows `πθ(r|e,q) = πθ(r|q) * [πθ(er|q,r,eq)/πθ(er|eq)]`. Sampling from the conditioned policy `πθ(r|e,q)` therefore upweights traces `r` where the likelihood ratio (and thus `Δ`) is high.
- **Core assumption:** Question statement `eq` alone does not alter the distribution over `r` for a different query `q`, and vice versa. This makes the likelihood ratio depend mainly on the reasoning trace `er`.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3] Theorem E.3 derivation showing `J_IC(θ) = E_q,r~πθ(·|q)[R(q,r)·w(q,r)]` with `log w(q,r) ≈ Δ(q,r) + c`. [section 3] Empirical validation in Figure 7 showing strong linear correlation (ρ>0.82) between `log w` and `Δ` across model scales.
- **Break condition:** If Assumption E.1 fails—e.g., `eq` contains method-specific cues—the likelihood ratio may capture spurious correlations. Also, if `Var_e[Δ_e]` varies widely across `(q,r)`, the approximation weakens.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Implicit reweighting via In-Context RLVR improves reasoning quality more than standard RLVR, especially on challenging problems, because it amplifies gradients from high-quality traces while filtering incorrect ones.
- **Mechanism:** During each rollout, a demonstration `e` is prepended to the query. The policy samples from `πθ(r|e,q)`, which favors traces `r` with high `Δ`. Over many rollouts, this steers the policy toward traces that are both correct (`R=1`) and have high Demonstration Utility.
- **Core assumption:** The held-out validation set `E` is representative of high-quality reasoning patterns, and `Δ` computed on `E` remains stable as the policy evolves.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "IC-DAPO improves average accuracy by +2.5 points... with larger gains on competition problems (+5.6 on AIME24, +5.8 on AIME25)." [section 4.3] Figure 2 shows mean `Δ` and human-rated quality scores increase steadily under IC-DAPO, with Spearman correlation `ρ≈0.4` stable across training.
- **Break condition:** If `E` is too small or not diverse, `Δ` becomes noisy. If the policy drifts significantly, `Δ` may become misaligned, though the paper shows correlation remains stable.

## Foundational Learning
#### Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
- **Why needed here:** The paper builds on RLVR (e.g., GRPO, DAPO) as the base framework. Understanding outcome-based rewards, group normalization, and policy gradient updates is essential to see how In-Context RLVR modifies the input distribution rather than the reward function.
- **Quick check question:** How does DAPO's asymmetric clipping and dynamic filtering differ from GRPO, and why does RLVR treat all correct solutions equally?

#### Concept: In-Context Learning (ICL)
- **Why needed here:** Evidence Gain relies on the model's ability to extract patterns from a prepended demonstration and apply them to a new query. This is the core mechanism for measuring Demonstration Utility without external supervision.
- **Quick check question:** Why does prepending a high-quality reasoning trace `(eq, er)` to a query `q` increase the likelihood of generating a new high-quality solution for `q`?

#### Concept: Bayesian Reweighting in Policy Optimization
- **Why needed here:** The theoretical equivalence between conditioned policy `πθ(r|e,q)` and base policy `πθ(r|q)` reweighted by a likelihood ratio is the mathematical foundation for implicit reward modulation.
- **Quick check question:** Given `πθ(r|e,q) = πθ(r|q) * πθ(er|q,r,eq)/πθ(er|eq)`, what role does each term play, and why does the ratio act as a quality signal for `r`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Held-out validation set `E` (1,082 high-quality traces) -> Random sample `e` per rollout -> Prepend to query `q` -> Policy network `π_θ` (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen) generates trace `r` -> Verifier gives reward `R(q,r)` -> RLVR optimizer (DAPO) updates policy. (All components are identical to standard RLVR except the prepending step.)

- **Critical path (step-by-step):**  
  1. Prepare `E` with ~1k high-quality `(eq, er)` pairs from DeepSeek-R1.  
  2. For each training step: sample batch of queries `q`; for each `q`, sample a random `e ∈ E` and prepend to `q`.  
  3. Generate rollouts `r ∼ π_θ(·|e,q)`, compute binary reward `R(q,r)` via program verifier.  
  4. Apply RLVR update (DAPO) using `(e,q,r,R)` tuples.  
  5. Monitor: (a) Held-out `Δ` on separate set `E0` to confirm rising `Δ`; (b) Human/LLM quality scores; (c) Correlation `ρ(Δ, quality)` stability.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Validation set quality vs. cost:** `E` must contain truly high-quality references (best from strongest model), but gathering these requires inference from a capable model. Using a weaker model yields smaller gains.  
  - **Implicit vs. explicit reweighting:** Implicit method adds <5% overhead (prepending tokens) vs. explicit `Δ` computation which requires O(|E|) additional forward passes (~80 hours). Tradeoff: less control over exact reweighting weights.  
  - **Generalization risk:** `Δ` is computed on math subdomain; using `E` from a different domain may not transfer. Assumes reasoning patterns generalize.

- **Failure signatures (diagnostic checks):**  
  1. **Stable or decreasing `Δ`** while accuracy improves: Model learns to game the demonstration context without improving intrinsic reasoning. Monitor `Δ` on `E0`.  
  2. **Drop in correlation `ρ(Δ, quality)`** during training: ICL signal misaligned, likely due to small `E` or policy drift. Check correlation every ~100 steps.  
  3. **Entropy collapse** not observed in paper, but if using algorithms prone to collapse, add entropy regularization.  
  4. **No gain on non-math domains:** Method validated only on math; applying to other domains may fail if `E` not representative.

- **First 3 experiments to run:**  
  1. **Validation set sanity check:** Compute `Δ` for a small set of correct/incorrect and high/low-quality traces (using human or LLM evaluator) to confirm `Δ` distinguishes quality *within* correct traces.  
  2. **Implicit reweighting verification:** Run 100 steps of IC-DAPO and standard DAPO with same seed. Sample 100 queries, generate 8 rollouts each, compute `Δ` and quality scores. Expect IC-DAPO trajectories to have higher mean `Δ` and quality.  
  3. **Ablation on demonstration source:** Train with `E` built from R1 vs. a non-reasoning model (V3.1). Expect R1 to yield higher final accuracy, confirming demonstration quality matters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Question 1: Domain Generalization
- **Question:** Does In-Context RLVR generalize to non-mathematical reasoning domains such as scientific question answering or logical deduction?
- **Basis:** The limitations section explicitly states that generalization to other reasoning-intensive domains like STEM problem-solving remains an open question due to computational constraints.
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments were conducted solely on mathematical benchmarks; resource limitations prevented comprehensive evaluation on other domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across diverse reasoning tasks (e.g., physics, biology, commonsense QA) and multiple model scales to test transferability and consistent improvement.

#### Question 2: Reducing Dependency on Strong Models
- **Question:** Can the dependence on a strong external model for generating high-quality demonstration traces be eliminated or reduced?
- **Basis:** The paper notes that constructing the demonstration set requires access to a strong model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) and calls for alternative strategies that reduce this dependency.
- **Why unresolved:** The current method relies on proprietary or high-cost models for reference traces, limiting reproducibility and broad adoption.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and validation of techniques such as self-consistent generation, iterative self-improvement, or using weaker supervision to produce comparable demonstration quality without external strong models.

#### Question 3: Long-term Stability and Scaling
- **Question:** Is the correlation between Evidence Gain and human-assessed reasoning quality stable over extended training or with significantly larger models?
- **Basis:** The study reports a stable Spearman correlation (~0.4) during training up to ~1000 steps for 1.5B and 7B models, but does not explore longer training horizons or scales beyond 7B.
- **Why unresolved:** Potential degradation or saturation of the signal over longer training or with architectural changes remains unknown; the current evidence is limited to specific setups.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Long-duration training runs tracking the correlation, along with experiments at larger scales (e.g., 20B+, 70B) and on varied datasets to confirm robustness.

#### Question 4: Demonstration Set Sensitivity
- **Question:** How sensitive is In-Context RLVR to the size, diversity, and domain match of the demonstration set E?
- **Basis:** The method uniformly samples demonstrations from a fixed validation set E, but the paper does not analyze the impact of varying |E|, coverage of reasoning patterns, or distributional mismatch between E and training data.
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical equivalence assumes uniform sampling and a fixed E; practical performance may depend on these factors, yet no ablations are provided.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies manipulating demonstration set size, diversity, and domain similarity, measuring effects on training stability, accuracy, and reasoning quality.

## Limitations
- Evidence Gain signal: medium confidence due to moderate correlation (ρ≈0.4) on math only; domain generality untested and held-out set small (N=100).
- Implicit reweighting equivalence and accuracy gains: high confidence supported by theory and strong empirical results.
- Generalization to other reasoning domains and long-term stability of Evidence Gain remain open.

## Confidence
- Evidence Gain as a quality signal: **Medium** (moderate correlation on math; limited validation).
- Implicit reweighting equivalence: **High** (theorem + empirical >0.82 correlation).
- Accuracy improvements: **High** (consistent +2.5 avg, +5.8 on AIME, low overhead).
- Stability of correlation across training: **Medium** (stable over ~1000 steps, but longer horizon unknown).
