# Prophet as a Reproducible Forecasting Framework: A Methodological Guide for Business and Financial Analytics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05929
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05929
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Prophet reduces RMSE by 64.6-65.4% on seasonal retail data versus non-seasonal ARIMA, provides fully covered uncertainty intervals on volatile financial data, and improves reproducibility via a standardized, scriptable workflow.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the reproducibility crisis in business and financial forecasting, where traditional methods like ARIMA require manual tuning and machine learning approaches often lack interpretability. It evaluates Prophet, an additive forecasting framework, as a solution that balances standardization, interpretability, and accessibility. Using Tesla stock (highly volatile) and retail demand (strongly seasonal) datasets, Prophet is compared against multiple ARIMA specifications and a Random Forest model under a fully documented, controlled experimental design.

Key findings show that on the seasonal retail data, Prophet dramatically outperforms all benchmarks, achieving a 64.6-65.4% reduction in RMSE compared to non‑seasonal ARIMA models and yielding statistically significant accuracy gains (Diebold‑Mariano p < 0.01). On the volatile financial data, Prophet’s point forecasts are less accurate but produce conservative uncertainty intervals with 100% coverage, whereas benchmarks often fail to cover. A multi‑ARIMA comparison reveals severe specification‑induced variability, while Prophet’s standardized, scriptable workflow consistently reduces reproducibility barriers across data, computational, and analytical dimensions. The study concludes that reproducibility should be a primary evaluative criterion alongside accuracy, and that Prophet serves as a methodological building block for transparent, auditable forecasting pipelines.

## Method Summary
The authors do not provide explicit reproduction notes. The methodology centers on a controlled experimental comparison between Prophet and baseline models (various ARIMA specifications and Random Forest) using two real‑world datasets: Tesla stock prices (volatile) and retail demand (seasonal). Prophet’s additive model is applied with its default automated settings, while ARIMA models are manually configured across a range of seasonal and non‑seasonal orders to assess specification sensitivity. All code, data transformations, and evaluation metrics (RMSE, interval coverage, Diebold‑Mariano test) are fully documented to ensure reproducibility. The experimental protocol is designed to isolate the impact of model specification on forecast accuracy and uncertainty quantification.

## Key Results
- On the seasonal retail dataset, Prophet reduced RMSE by 64.6–65.4% compared to non‑seasonal ARIMA models and achieved statistically significant accuracy improvements (Diebold‑Mariano p < 0.01).
- On volatile Tesla stock data, Prophet’s point forecasts had higher RMSE than some benchmarks but provided 100% coverage of uncertainty intervals, demonstrating robust probabilistic calibration.
- Prophet’s standardized workflow eliminated the specification‑induced variability observed across multiple ARIMA configurations, thereby enhancing reproducibility across data, computational, and analytical dimensions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Prophet’s effectiveness stems from its additive decomposition of a time series into trend, seasonality, and holiday components, combined with automatic changepoint detection that adapts to structural breaks. This design yields interpretable model structures and dramatically reduces the need for manual tuning, which directly tackles the reproducibility barrier associated with traditional statistical methods like ARIMA. The Bayesian approach to parameter estimation provides well‑calibrated uncertainty intervals that reflect both model and data uncertainty, explaining the full coverage observed on volatile series despite conservative width. Moreover, the framework’s default parametrizations are deliberately robust across a wide range of domains, allowing practitioners to achieve strong performance with minimal intervention, thereby standardizing the forecasting pipeline. These mechanisms collectively enable Prophet to deliver superior accuracy on seasonal data and reliable probabilistic forecasts on noisy financial data while maintaining a transparent, scriptable workflow.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Data -> Preprocessing -> Trend Component -> Seasonality Component -> Holiday Effects -> Combined Forecast -> Uncertainty Quantification

**Critical Path:**  
1. Prepare time‑series data (timestamp, value, optional regressors).  
2. Define model: choose growth (linear/logistic), changepoint prior scale, seasonality modes, and holiday list.  
3. Fit the model.  
4. Create a future dataframe covering the forecast horizon.  
5. Generate predictions and extract uncertainty intervals.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Default vs. custom parameters:* Defaults offer ease of use and robustness; customization can capture domain specifics but introduces specification complexity and potential reproducibility loss.  
- *Coverage vs. sharpness:* Prophet’s intervals are designed to be conservative; increasing sharpness may sacrifice coverage, especially on volatile series.  
- *Interpretability vs. flexibility:* The additive form is highly interpretable; adding too many regressors or complex interactions can obscure insights and increase overfitting risk.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Residuals show significant autocorrelation, indicating unmodeled dynamics.  
- Uncertainty intervals consistently miss observed values (under‑coverage) or are excessively wide (over‑conservatism).  
- Forecasts exhibit abrupt, unrealistic jumps at changepoints that do not align with known events.  
- Model fails to converge or produces implausible parameter estimates when data contain extreme outliers or missing values without proper handling.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Replicate the retail demand forecasting experiment: Load the dataset, fit Prophet with default settings, compute RMSE, and compare against the reported 64.6‑65.4% reduction versus non‑seasonal ARIMA.  
2. Replicate the Tesla stock forecasting experiment: Fit Prophet to Tesla prices, evaluate point forecast error and uncertainty interval coverage (should be 100%), and contrast with Random Forest.  
3. Reproduce the ARIMA specification variability analysis: Fit multiple ARIMA models (e.g., (p,d,q) and seasonal orders) on both datasets, record RMSE distribution, and observe the high variability that Prophet avoids.

## Limitations
- The study uses only two datasets (one stock, one retail), limiting generalizability to other financial instruments or seasonal domains.
- Benchmarks exclude modern deep learning models (e.g., LSTMs, Transformers), so Prophet’s relative performance against state‑of‑the‑art forecasters remains unknown.
- The reproducibility evaluation focuses on technical aspects (scriptability, specification stability) and does not address institutional or human factors (e.g., analyst expertise, data governance).

## Confidence

  - Prophet outperforms ARIMA on seasonal retail data.  
  - Prophet provides fully covered uncertainty intervals.  
- **Medium:**

- **Low:**
