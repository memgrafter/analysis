# CITED: A Decision Boundary-Aware Signature for GNNs Towards Model Extraction Defense

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20418  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20418  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: CITED provides unified ownership verification for GNNs at embedding and label levels, significantly outperforming watermarking/fingerprinting baselines in ARUC (up to 59.29) while maintaining or improving downstream accuracy.

## Executive Summary
CITED addresses model extraction attacks (MEAs) on Graph Neural Networks by generating an ownership signature from subgraph nodes near the model’s decision boundary. It uses three complementary metrics—boundary margin, thickness, and heterogeneity—to select a node set that reflects model-specific uncertainty and is likely preserved during extraction. Verification employs the ARUC metric, comparing embeddings via Wasserstein distance and labels via accuracy. Evaluated on seven datasets and five GNN backbones, CITED achieves substantially higher ARUC than baselines (e.g., label ARUC up to 59.29 vs. <40) without harming utility, and with lower inference overhead than embedding-level methods like GrOVe.

## Method Summary
CITED first computes a boundary score for each node using logit margin and prediction entropy to identify nodes near the decision boundary. For non-boundary nodes, it derives three normalized metrics: margin (embedding distance to nearest same-class boundary node), thickness (softmax similarity to boundary nodes with a confidence gap), and heterogeneity (local label disagreement). These are aggregated with weights α₁ and α₂ to select the final signature set (e.g., bottom ρ% scores, union with boundary nodes). The target model may be fine-tuned on the original task to stabilize learning. During verification, a suspicious model is queried on the signature inputs; matching scores are computed (Wasserstein for embeddings, accuracy for labels), and ARUC is derived by plotting robustness vs. uniqueness across thresholds to distinguish surrogates from independently trained models.

## Key Results
- CITED achieves label ARUC up to 59.29, far exceeding watermarking/fingerprinting baselines (<40).
- Downstream task accuracy is maintained or improved relative to undefended models; watermarks often cause drops.
- Inference overhead is lower than embedding-level methods like GrOVe.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Boundary-Aware Signature Selection
CITED creates an ownership signature by selecting subgraph nodes near the target model’s decision boundary and summarizing them with three complementary metrics. For each node, a boundary score combines the ReLU of the logit margin between the top-2 classes and prediction entropy; the lowest-scoring nodes form the boundary set. For other nodes, the signature score aggregates normalized margin (embedding distance to nearest same-class boundary node), thickness (label distribution similarity with confidence gap), and heterogeneity (fraction of neighbors with different labels). The core assumption is that the decision boundary is unique to a training run and will be reproduced by surrogates that query near it. Evidence includes the paper’s equations (1)-(5) and experimental results. However, if an attacker deliberately avoids boundary queries (e.g., uses only high-confidence samples), the signature may not transfer.

### Mechanism 2: Unified ARUC Verification
Ownership is verified by comparing the suspicious model’s outputs on signature nodes against the target’s, using the ARUC metric at both embedding and label levels. For embeddings, the 2-Wasserstein distance between signature embedding distributions is computed; for labels, prediction accuracy on signature nodes is used. Varying a threshold yields robustness (TPR for surrogates) and uniqueness (TNR for independents) curves; the Area under the Min curve (ARUC) summarizes separation quality. The assumption is that surrogates will produce statistically closer outputs on the signature nodes than independent models because the signature lies on the decision boundary implicitly learned during extraction. Evidence includes the ARUC definition in Section 3.3 and consistent experimental superiority. A break condition occurs if post-extraction transformations (e.g., adversarial fine-tuning) alter the model’s representation significantly.

### Mechanism 3: Utility Preservation
CITED’s signature is derived from real graph nodes reflecting genuine model uncertainty, so embedding it does not degrade downstream performance—unlike watermarking which often harms accuracy. The signature nodes are not synthetic triggers; they are selected based on the model’s own predictions. After selection, the target model is optionally fine-tuned on the original task labels only, consolidating learning without introducing task-irrelevant patterns. This ensures the model’s utility remains intact. Evidence includes Table 1 showing CITED matches or improves accuracy across datasets, while watermarks degrade performance. Potential break conditions arise on extremely imbalanced or noisy graphs where selection might over-represent minority boundary regions, though robustness experiments show limited impact.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Trained Target GNN → Signature Generator → [Optional Fine-Tuner] → Deployed Target & Stored Signature → Ownership Verifier  
- **Critical path**: Train target GNN → Extract signature → (optional) Fine-tune target → Deploy target and store signature secretly → Upon suspicion, query suspicious model on signature inputs → Compute matching scores (Wasserstein or accuracy) → Compare to threshold or compute ARUC → Ownership decision.  
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Signature size (ρ%)*: Larger sets improve robustness but increase storage/verification cost; stable above ~30%.  
  - *α₁, α₂ weights*: Balance thickness and heterogeneity; ARUC is stable across moderate ranges but extreme values harm label-level performance.  
  - *Matching metric*: Embedding-level (Wasserstein) is more robust but computationally heavy; label-level (accuracy) is cheap but sensitive to boundary shifts.  
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - False negative: Attacker avoids boundary queries or uses a different architecture, weakening signature transfer.  
  - False positive: Independently trained model coincidentally aligns on signature nodes, especially with small signatures or homogeneous datasets.  
  - Degraded verification after removal attacks (pruning, fine-tuning, distribution shift); CITED still leads baselines but with performance drop.  
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Utility preservation: Compare task accuracy of CITED vs. watermark baselines to confirm no harm.  
  2. Verification effectiveness: Compute ARUC across datasets/backbones to demonstrate superior separation of surrogates from independents.  
  3. Ablation study: Remove individual signature score components (margin, thickness, heterogeneity) to validate their contributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can CITED be effectively extended to other graph learning tasks (e.g., link prediction, graph classification), and does the signature need task-specific redesign? The paper focuses on node classification; tasks with different output semantics may not align with the node-centric boundary metrics.
- How does CITED scale to massive graphs (e.g., millions of nodes) in terms of computational and memory requirements, and can approximate methods preserve verification effectiveness? Complexity is O(n·B·d + n·k); experiments are limited to ~34k nodes.
- Is CITED robust against adaptive adversaries who know the signature selection method and deliberately manipulate the surrogate model’s outputs on the signature nodes to evade detection? The paper evaluates generic removal attacks but not white-box adaptive attacks.

## Limitations
- Relies on the assumption that a GNN’s decision boundary is unique and preserved under extraction; attackers may avoid boundary queries, weakening the signature.
- Critical hyperparameters (λ, γ, α₁, α₂) are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Robustness evaluation focuses on standard removal attacks; adaptive adversaries with full signature knowledge (e.g., adversarial fine-tuning targeting signature nodes) remain unexplored.

## Confidence
- **High**: Verification via ARUC reliably distinguishes surrogates from independents (consistent superiority across 7 datasets, 5 backbones); CITED preserves or improves downstream accuracy (Table 1).
- **Medium**: Signature captures model-specific boundary behavior (empirical support but no formal guarantee; depends on attacker querying boundary).

## Next Checks
1. **Boundary uniqueness test**: Train surrogates with varied query distributions (e.g., high-confidence only, mixed) and measure signature matching scores to quantify preservation under different attack strategies.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Conduct a systematic grid search over λ, γ, α₁, α₂, reporting ARUC and accuracy heatmaps to establish robust default ranges.
3. **Extended removal robustness**: Simulate stronger post-extraction modifications (pruning, quantization, adversarial fine-tuning) and evaluate ARUC degradation; if significant, design adaptive signature re-generation.
