# Halfway to 3D: Ensembling 2.5D and 3D Models for Robust COVID-19 CT Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14832
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14832
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Ensemble achieves 94.48% accuracy and 0.9426 Macro F1 on PHAROS-AIF-MIH validation for binary COVID-19 detection, ranking runner-up in the official challenge.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses robust COVID-19 detection and multi-class lung disease classification from multi-source chest CT scans, tackling domain shifts and gender fairness concerns. The core method ensembles a 2.5D multi-view branch—processing axial, coronal, and sagittal slices with a DINOv3 vision transformer—with a 3D volumetric ResNet-18 branch pretrained via Variance Risk Extrapolation (VREx) and supervised contrastive learning. On the PHAROS-AIF-MIH benchmark validation set, the ensemble for binary detection achieved 94.48% accuracy and a 0.9426 Macro F1-score, outperforming individual branches. For multi-class classification, the 2.5D model alone was best. In the official challenge test, the approach ranked runner-up for binary detection and third for multi-class classification, with ensemble weighting improving gender fairness in the latter task.

## Method Summary
The proposed pipeline processes chest CT scans through two parallel branches that are ensembled at logit level. The 3D branch inputs a reconstructed 128³ volume into a ResNet-18, first pretrained with VREx to minimize loss variance across predefined hospital domains, then fine-tuned with a combination of cross-entropy, supervised contrastive loss, and MixUp augmentation. The 2.5D branch samples 8–12 slices per orthogonal plane (axial, coronal, sagittal), resizes them to 224², and feeds them individually to a DINOv3-pretrained vision transformer; the branch undergoes staged fine-tuning from frozen backbone to full unfreeze. Final predictions combine logits from both branches via averaging, with configurable weights to balance accuracy and fairness.

## Key Results
- Validation binary detection (Task 1): 94.48% accuracy, 0.9426 Macro F1.
- Validation multi-class (Task 2): 2.5D model best at 79.35% accuracy, 0.7497 Macro F1; ensemble did not surpass this.
- Official challenge: runner-up Task 1 (Macro F1 0.751), third Task 2 (Macro F1 0.633); weighted ensemble improved male F1 in Task 2.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: 2.5D Multi-View Branch
- **Claim:** The 2.5D branch captures high-resolution, slice-level anatomical patterns complementary to volumetric context, improving robustness to domain shifts.
- **Mechanism:** DINOv3 ViT processes orthogonal slices independently, leveraging large-scale natural image pretraining to extract fine-grained features (e.g., subtle opacities). Multi-view fusion aggregates these orthogonal cues, compensating for potential downsampling in 3D convolutions.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained 2D representations transfer effectively to medical slices; orthogonal views provide complementary pathology evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 describe the multi-view setup; Table 6 shows 2.5D top accuracy in multi-class Task 2.

### Mechanism 2: VREx and Contrastive 3D Branch
- **Claim:** VREx pretraining and supervised contrastive learning promote domain-invariant, discriminative volumetric features.
- **Mechanism:** VREx penalizes variance of classification loss across predefined domains, encouraging features that generalize across hospitals/scanners. Supervised contrastive learning then refines class separation by pulling same-class embeddings together and pushing different classes apart; MixUp smooths the feature space.
- **Core assumption:** Domain labels capture major acquisition biases; label-aware contrastive loss enhances separability after domain-invariant pretraining.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2.2 details the two-stage training; Table 3 shows VREx mitigates but does not eliminate domain shift (e.g., Source 2 remains poor).

### Mechanism 3: Logit-Level Ensemble
- **Claim:** Averaging logits from both branches combines global volumetric context and local high-resolution evidence, stabilizing predictions across domains and improving fairness.
- **Mechanism:** Errors of the two branches are partially independent; averaging reduces variance and mitigates individual weaknesses (e.g., 3D’s poor Source 2 performance, 2.5D’s gender gap). Weighting can be tuned to balance overall accuracy and subgroup fairness.
- **Core assumption:** Complementary evidence and uncorrelated errors across domains/demographics allow variance reduction.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions logit ensemble; Tables 7 and 12 show Macro F1 and fairness gains with weighted averaging (e.g., 0.7 weight improves male F1 in Task 2).

## Foundational Learning

### DINO Self-Supervised Learning
- **Why needed here:** Provides a powerful, general-purpose visual feature extractor for the 2.5D branch, avoiding training a large transformer from scratch on limited medical data.
- **Quick check:** How does the student-teacher network with sharpening in DINO encourage learning of semantically meaningful features without using labels?

### Variance Risk Extrapolation (VREx)
- **Why needed here:** Used in 3D branch pretraining to explicitly penalize performance variance across known domains (hospitals), incentivizing domain-invariant representations.
- **Quick check:** What is the mathematical form of the VREx penalty term, and how does it differ from simply minimizing the average loss across domains?

### Supervised Contrastive Learning
- **Why needed here:** Applied during 3D branch fine-tuning to enhance feature discrimination by contrasting samples of the same class against samples of different classes, complementing cross-entropy.
- **Quick check:** How does the supervised contrastive loss function differ from self-supervised losses like SimCLR, and why might it be beneficial after VREx pretraining?

## Architecture Onboarding

Input Slices -> 3D Reconstruction (128³) -> Parallel Processing:
  - 3D Branch: ResNet-18 -> VREx pretraining -> Fine-tuning (cross-entropy + supervised contrastive + MixUp)
  - 2.5D Branch: Multi-view slices (224²) -> DINOv3 ViT backbone -> Staged fine-tuning (frozen -> progressive unfreeze -> full)
Logit Averaging (configurable weights) -> Output

**Critical path:** Raw axial DICOM slices are reconstructed into 3D volumes; these are processed simultaneously by both branches; their logits are averaged to produce the final prediction. The multi-view extraction in the 2.5D branch is a key divergence from standard 3D pipelines.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- 2.5D uses more memory (multiple 2D images per volume) but benefits from powerful pretrained 2D backbones; 3D is computationally heavier for 128³ volumes but models full 3D context.
- Ensemble weighting requires validation-set tuning; heavier 2.5D weight for multi-class (Task 2), heavier 3D weight for fairness in Task 2 (0.7 improves male F1).
- VREx relies on reliable domain labels; performance sensitive to λ (set to 1.0) and domain definition.

**Failure signatures:**
- Task 1: Severely degraded F1 on Source 2 (all models ~0.48) indicates domain shift resistant to current methods.
- Task 2: Very low F1 for Squamous Cell Carcinoma (Class G) across all models (recall as low as 0.12), suggesting insufficient discriminative features for this rare class.
- Fairness: Significant gender performance gap (e.g., 2.5D Task 2: Male 0.402 vs Female 0.709), requiring ensemble weighting adjustments.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Reproduce preprocessing: Validate that reconstructed 128³ volumes from raw slices maintain correct orientation and intensity distribution; check slice sampling covers lung fields for all orthogonal views.
2. Benchmark individual branches: Train 3D (VREx + contrastive) and 2.5D (DINOv3) models without ensemble on validation set. Confirm 2.5D outperforms 3D on Task 1 and identify per-source/class weaknesses.
3. Test ensemble weighting grid: For Task 2, evaluate ensemble with 2.5D weights [0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0] on validation set. Confirm 0.7 weight improves male F1 and identify optimal weight for Macro F1 vs. fairness balance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Why does ensembling 2.5D and 3D models improve binary detection but not multi-class classification?**  
   - Basis: Ensemble outperforms individual models on Task 1 (Macro F1 0.9426 vs. 0.8221/0.7648) but does not exceed the best single model on Task 2 (Macro F1 0.7229 vs. 0.7230).  
   - Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze whether volumetric features provide complementary information for fine-grained classification or why they fail to enhance already strong slice-based features.  
   - Resolving evidence: Quantify feature complementarity (e.g., via CCA, mutual information) and test alternative fusion mechanisms (e.g., attention, learned weights) on both tasks.

2. **What factors cause the extremely low classification performance for Squamous Cell Carcinoma (Class G) and how can it be improved?**  
   - Basis: Class G consistently has worst F1-scores; recall as low as 0.12 (3D) and 0.36 (2.5D); training data highly imbalanced (84 G vs. 120–125 others).  
   - Why unresolved: Paper notes degradation but does not investigate causes (imbalance, visual similarity, annotation noise); no targeted remedies proposed.  
   - Resolving evidence: Train with class-balanced sampling, focal loss, or synthetic oversampling; perform error analysis to characterize misclassifications; evaluate label reliability.

3. **Can ensemble weighting be optimized to achieve gender fairness without sacrificing overall accuracy?**  
   - Basis: 0.7-weight favoring 3D improves male F1 to 0.557 (best Macro F1 0.633) but gap remains (female 0.709, male 0.557).  
   - Why unresolved: Weights manually tuned; no method to learn weights that directly optimize fairness metrics; unclear if gap can be closed.  
   - Resolving evidence: Optimize ensemble weights on validation under fairness constraints (e.g., minimize max gender disparity); systematically sweep weight space and analyze per-gender error breakdowns.

4. **Would pretraining the 2.5D vision transformer on medical CT images (instead of natural images) improve robustness to domain shifts?**  
   - Basis: DINOv3 pretrained on natural images; both 2.5D and 3D perform poorly on Source 2 (F1 ~0.48), indicating domain shift limitations.  
   - Why unresolved: No medical-pretrained baseline tested; potential to mitigate source-specific failures unknown.  
   - Resolving evidence: Pre-train a vision transformer on a large multi-source CT dataset via self-supervision, replace the DINOv3 backbone, and evaluate on PHAROS with per-source analysis.

## Limitations
- Exact DINOv3 model variant (e.g., `vit_small`, `vit_base`) and single-channel input adaptation (channel repeat vs. learned projection) unspecified.
- VREx implementation details vague: how domain labels are derived, exact variance penalty computation, whether domains are batched separately.
- 2.5D multi-view fusion strategy (feature-level fusion: average, concatenation, learned network?) unclear.
- Class imbalance handling for rare Squamous Cell Carcinoma (Class G) not described; likely unaddressed.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (2.5D multi-view):** Medium – indirect support via 2.5D top accuracy in multi-class; complementarity with 3D not explicitly shown.
- **Mechanism 2 (VREx + contrastive):** Medium – Table 3 shows partial domain mitigation but residual gaps (Source 2) remain.
- **Mechanism 3 (Logit averaging):** High – Tables 7 and 12 confirm Macro F1 and fairness gains with weighted ensemble.
