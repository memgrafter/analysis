# Evaluating the Impact of Post-Training Quantization on Reliable VQA with Multimodal LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13289
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13289
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Post-training quantization degrades VQA accuracy and reliability; a data-aware quantization method (MBQ) and an extrinsic confidence estimator (Selector) together recover most losses, with int4 MBQ + Selector matching bf16 performance at ~75% less memory.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) affects both task accuracy and reliability (confidence calibration) in multimodal Visual Question Answering models. Evaluating Qwen2-VL-7B and Idefics3-8B quantized to int8/int4/int3 via data-free (HQQ) and data-aware (MBQ) methods, the study finds that PTQ consistently degrades both metrics. MBQ mitigates these losses more effectively than HQQ at lower bit-widths. Furthermore, a trained extrinsic confidence estimator called the Selector substantially restores reliability for moderately quantized models, particularly when combined with int4 MBQ. The best efficiency-reliability trade-off achieves near-full-precision accuracy (~98% retention) with a 75% memory reduction, marking the first work linking quantization and multimodal reliability.

## Method Summary
The study quantizes two multimodal LLMs using weight-only PTQ (HQQ and MBQ) at int8, int4, and int3. MBQ uses 128 calibration samples from ShareGPT4V to adjust scaling per modality. Models are evaluated on VQAv2 (in-distribution), AdVQA (linguistic OOD), and VizWiz (multimodal OOD) using accuracy, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and selective prediction metrics (C@R, AUC, Φc). A separate two-layer MLP, the Selector, is trained on features from the bf16 model’s validation set to predict answer correctness. This trained Selector is then applied to confidence scoring for all quantized models, and its impact on reliability is compared against the intrinsic MaxProb baseline.

## Key Results
- PTQ degrades both accuracy and reliability, with losses proportional to bit-width reduction.
- Data-aware MBQ outperforms data-free HQQ in preserving accuracy and calibration, especially at int4.
- The extrinsic Selector estimator recovers most reliability lost to quantization for int4 models; its gains diminish at int3.
- The combination of int4 MBQ and Selector matches bf16 accuracy (~98% retention) while reducing memory by ~75%, offering the best Pareto front.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Quantization introduces numerical precision loss that distorts weight and activation distributions. This noise propagates through the transformer, corrupting both semantic representations for answer generation and internal signals (like token probabilities) used for confidence estimation. Degradation scales with lower bit-widths. MBQ uses calibration data to balance quantization scaling between vision and text pathways, preserving modality-specific dynamic ranges better than a uniform, data-free approach. The Selector, an extrinsic MLP trained on intermediate multimodal features (pooled embeddings, first output token, joint probability), learns a more robust mapping to correctness from bf16 data. These features retain sufficient signal under moderate quantization, allowing the Selector to recalibrate confidence without altering base model weights. At extreme quantization (int3), feature corruption becomes too severe for full recovery.

## Architecture Onboarding
Base Models (Qwen2-VL-7B, Idefics3-8B) -> Quantization Modules (HQQ/MBQ, weight-only int8/int4/int3) -> Confidence Estimators (MaxProb intrinsic, Selector extrinsic MLP) -> Evaluation Pipeline (VQAv2/AdVQA/VizWiz, metrics: Acc, ECE, C@R, AUC, Φc).  
**Critical path:** 1) Load bf16 checkpoint. 2) Apply PTQ (HQQ/MBQ). 3) For Selector: train MLP on bf16 validation features, then apply to all quantized models. 4) Generate answers/confidence scores on test sets; compute metrics.  
**Design tradeoffs:** MBQ needs calibration data but preserves accuracy/reliability better than HQQ; lower bits save memory but cause collapse; MaxProb is trivial but miscalibrated, while Selector requires an extra forward pass and training but yields strong reliability.  
**Failure signatures:** Severe ECE increase (>5%) at int4/int3; C@5% near 0% on OOD datasets; Selector performance converging to MaxProb at int3; MBQ underperforming HQQ on a specific model/dataset.  
**First 3 experiments:** 1) Establish bf16 baseline with MaxProb on all datasets. 2) Quantize via HQQ/MBQ at int8/int4/int3; evaluate with MaxProb; plot accuracy/ECE vs. bit-width. 3) Train and apply Selector to int4 MBQ; compare reliability against MaxProb and bf16 baseline; test on OOD datasets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can reliability be preserved more effectively through quantization-aware training (QAT) that explicitly optimizes for reliability metrics, compared to PTQ followed by a separate confidence estimator?
- Is the Selector’s effectiveness primarily due to stabilizing distorted activation patterns, or does it learn a fundamentally independent uncertainty signal?
- To what extent do the observed trade-offs generalize beyond VQA to other multimodal reasoning tasks (e.g., image captioning, visual entailment, multimodal dialogue)?
- Is the observed correlation between accuracy degradation and reliability degradation under PTQ causal, or are there latent factors (e.g., changes in output distribution entropy) that independently affect both?

## Limitations
- Incomplete reporting of MBQ calibration details (sample selection, preprocessing) and Selector training hyperparameters.
- Limited model/dataset coverage (two models, three VQA benchmarks) restricts generalizability.
- Weak external corroboration from neighboring papers (average near-zero citations) for the multimodal quantization-reliability link.

## Confidence
- PTQ degrades accuracy and reliability: High (consistent internal results across models and bit-widths).
- MBQ outperforms HQQ at low bit-widths: Medium (modest gains; may not generalize to all architectures).
- Selector recovers reliability for int4 models: Medium (works for int4 but fails at int3; relies on bf16-trained estimator).
