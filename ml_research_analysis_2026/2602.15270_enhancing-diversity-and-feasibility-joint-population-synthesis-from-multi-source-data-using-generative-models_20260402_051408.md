# Enhancing Diversity and Feasibility: Joint Population Synthesis from Multi-source Data Using Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15270
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15270
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Joint synthesis with WGAN-GP and inverse gradient penalty achieves a unified evaluation score of 88.1 (vs. 84.6 for sequential), improving recall by 10% and precision by 1% over the joint GAN alone.

## Executive Summary
Population synthesis for agent-based models often relies on fusing multiple data sources, but sequential fusion struggles with feature interplay and zeros, hurting diversity and feasibility. This paper proposes a joint integration approach using a Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty (WGAN-GP) and an inverse gradient penalty (IGP) regularization. The method simultaneously learns from census and travel survey data via a single generator and two critics, each evaluating a subset corresponding to one source. IGP encourages diverse outputs by enforcing a minimum Lipschitz constant. Experiments on Montreal datasets show the joint approach outperforms sequential baselines, with the full model reaching a unified score of 88.1. The study demonstrates improved handling of sampling zeros and structural zeros, offering a robust framework for multi-source population synthesis.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a joint generative model that integrates two heterogeneous datasets (census and travel survey) without a separate fusion step. A WGAN-GP is trained with two critic networks: one evaluates the subset of generated attributes corresponding to the first dataset, the other evaluates the second subset. The generator takes random noise and produces a combined sample; its loss includes the negative outputs of both critics plus an Inverse Gradient Penalty (IGP) term. IGP maximizes the ratio of output differences to input differences (capped at τ) to ensure latent perturbations yield diverse outputs. The model is trained with Adam (LR=5e-5), batch size 512, for 5001 epochs with 5 critic updates per generator step. Quality is assessed via a unified score aggregating five metrics: distributional similarity (SRMSE, JSD), correlation preservation, PMSE, support coverage (Scr), and machine learning efficacy (Sml). Recall (diversity) and precision (feasibility) are also measured by comparing unique attribute combinations.

## Key Results
- The joint WGAN-GP (without IGP) already beats the sequential fusion baseline: unified score 86.9 vs 84.6, recall +7%, precision +15%.
- Adding IGP yields further gains: recall increases by 10% (to ~50.4%) and precision by 1% (to ~81.3%), for a final unified score of 88.1.
- The unified score components all improve, especially machine learning efficacy (Sml), indicating the synthetic population is more useful for downstream tasks like classification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**1. Joint integration captures cross-source feature interplay.** By training a single generator with dual critics on the combined dataset, the model learns the true joint distribution across both feature sets in one pass, avoiding error propagation from a separate fusion step. Evidence: Direct comparison shows the joint approach (without IGP) outperforms sequential fusion on unified score (86.9 vs 84.6).

**2. Inverse Gradient Penalty (IGP) promotes diversity without sacrificing feasibility.** IGP adds a regularization term that maximizes the minimum Lipschitz constant of the generator, ensuring small latent changes produce sufficiently distinct outputs. This mitigates mode collapse, increasing population diversity (higher recall). The Wasserstein critics still penalize infeasible samples, preserving precision. Evidence: Adding IGP improves recall by 10% and precision by 1% (Table V).

**3. Unified evaluation score provides a balanced quality measure.** The composite metric equally weights five complementary aspects (distributional similarity, correlation, PMSE, support coverage, ML efficacy), preventing overfitting to any single statistic. This yields a more reliable indicator of overall synthetic population suitability for ABMs. Evidence: The best model achieves 88.1 and shows consistent gains across all five components.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Random noise z → Generator G (produces concatenated attributes for both datasets) → [D1 (critic for dataset P’s subset) + D2 (critic for dataset C’s subset)]

**Critical path:**  
1. Pre-process: align categorical values, one-hot encode, convert aggregate census to individual records (via an initial GAN or other method).  
2. Initialize G, D1, D2.  
3. For each iteration:  
   a. Sample real minibatches from P and C.  
   b. Sample noise minibatch z, generate fake samples G(z).  
   c. Update D1 and D2 using their respective subsets, applying WGAN-GP loss (Wasserstein distance + gradient penalty).  
   d. Update G using combined critic outputs plus λ·IGP term (sample two noise vectors per batch, compute min(∥G(z1)−G(z2)∥/∥z1−z2∥, τ), maximize).  
4. After training, generate final synthetic population by sampling noise through G.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Dual critics vs. single critic:* Dual critics focus on each dataset’s feature space, potentially improving fidelity but increasing model complexity and requiring balanced training.  
- *IGP strength (λ):* Too low yields mode collapse; too high forces unrealistic output jumps, harming feasibility.  
- *Generator branching:* Specialized output heads help but assume a fixed feature grouping; a fully shared final layer might capture more interactions but could be harder to train.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Critic loss becomes unstable or vanishes: indicates gradient penalty weight insufficient or N_critic too low.  
- Generator loss plateaus near zero: generator stuck, possibly due to over-regularized critics or excessive IGP.  
- Low precision (many infeasible combos): IGP λ too high or insufficient critic training; check for impossible attribute combinations (e.g., negative age).  
- Low recall (poor diversity): IGP λ too low; generated samples may cluster in few modes.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Implement a sequential fusion (e.g., via IPF) to create a combined dataset, then train a standard WGAN-GAN on it. Compute unified score and recall/precision. Expected: ~84.6.  
2. **Core method - Joint WGAN-GP (no IGP):** Train the dual-critic architecture without IGP. Compare to baseline; expect unified score ~86.9, recall +7%, precision +15%.  
3. **Full model - Add IGP:** Same as experiment 2 but include IGP in generator loss; tune λ. Expect unified score 88.1, recall +10% (vs. experiment 2), precision +1%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extending to more than two data sources.** The framework currently fuses two datasets; its scalability and architectural changes for three or more heterogeneous sources (e.g., adding smart card or social media data) are unexplored.  
2. **Comparative regularization techniques.** IGP is proposed but not compared against other diversity/feasibility regularizers; the trade-off curve between recall and precision for alternative methods is unknown.  
3. **Dual-critic vs. single-critic advantage.** The benefit of using two separate critics rather than one unified critic for preserving distinct statistical properties of each source dataset is not isolated experimentally.  
4. **Downstream ABM impact.** While proxy metrics (Sml, recall/precision) improve, it remains unproven that these improvements translate into more accurate Agent-Based Model simulation outcomes (e.g., traffic flows, mode shares).  
5. **Robustness of the unified evaluation score.** The weighting (equal 1/5) and choice of five sub-metrics are not validated across different urban contexts or synthesis tasks; the score may be overfitted to this study’s specific datasets.

## Limitations
- **IGP hyperparameters unspecified:** The truncation constant τ and regularization weight λ are not given, hindering exact replication of the reported recall/precision trade-off.
- **Census conversion method unclear:** The process for converting aggregate census data to individual-level records using a “GAN” is not described, a critical preprocessing step that could affect results.
- **Evaluation details ambiguous:** Definitions of “categories” for recall/precision and Scr, discretization for SRMSE/JSD, and computation of third‑order correlations on one‑hot data are not fully specified.
- **Architecture choices ambiguous:** The final generator architecture (from Table II options) and critic details (e.g., BatchNorm placement) are not stated.

## Confidence
- **Joint integration superiority:** High – direct experiment shows unified score 86.9 vs 84.6 for sequential.  
- **IGP efficacy for diversity:** Medium – ablation shows large recall gain with minimal precision loss, but sensitivity to unknown λ/τ parameters.  
- **Unified evaluation score validity:** Low – composite metric’s equal weighting is unvalidated; no evidence it predicts ABM utility better than alternatives.

## Next Checks
