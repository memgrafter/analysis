# Mitigating Translationese Bias in Multilingual LLM-as-a-Judge via Disentangled Information Bottleneck

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10351
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10351
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DIBJudge reduces translationese bias severity by 56–80% relative to vanilla SFT while achieving higher accuracy (91.37% on m-RewardBench vs. 88.58% for mR3-Qwen3-8B).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces DIBJudge, a framework to mitigate translationese bias in multilingual LLM-as-a-Judge systems. Translationese bias causes judges to prefer machine-translated text over human-authored references, especially in low-resource languages. DIBJudge uses a disentangled information bottleneck that learns a compressed robust representation for judgment-critical semantics while routing spurious factors (like English manifold alignment and cross-lingual predictability) into a separate bias branch. A cross-covariance penalty enforces independence between the two branches. Evaluations on multiple multilingual reward benchmarks and dedicated bias suites show DIBJudge reduces bias severity by 56–80% relative to vanilla supervised fine-tuning while achieving higher accuracy. The approach also generalizes to other biases like length and self-preference, maintaining a superior bias-utility Pareto frontier.

## Method Summary
DIBJudge builds on a frozen LLM judge (Qwen3 family) and adds a learnable encoder that processes inputs into two latent vectors: Z_r (robust semantics) and Z_b (bias-related factors). The training loss combines four terms: (1) L_task: standard language modeling loss on the judge’s output; (2) L_compress: KL divergence that regularizes Z_r toward a fixed prior; (3) L_bias: negative log-likelihood of spurious attributes S given Z_b, trained via two proxy tasks—cross-lingual alignment contrastive learning (using back-translated pairs) and log-probability bin classification; and (4) L_disc: Frobenius norm of the cross-covariance matrix between the mean representations of Z_r and Z_b to enforce independence. Training uses LoRA on the judge, a two-stage procedure (first train the projection and heads, then jointly train LoRA), Adam optimizer, cosine scheduler, 3 epochs, and a sequence length of 16384. The bias evaluation suite constructs translationese pairs by back-translating human references from BELEBELE, AYA, and XL-SUM using NLLB-200-3.3B, with position-swapping to avoid position bias.

## Key Results
- DIBJudge reduces translationese bias severity by 56–80% relative to vanilla SFT.
- Achieves 91.37% macro-average accuracy on m-RewardBench, surpassing mR3-Qwen3-8B (88.58%).
- Maintains superior bias-utility trade-off and generalizes to other biases (length, self-preference), preserving a Pareto frontier.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The disentangled information bottleneck explicitly separates the representation space into a robust branch that retains only task-relevant semantics and a bias branch that captures known spurious correlations. The cross-covariance penalty forces the two branches to be linearly independent, preventing leakage of bias information into the robust branch. By routing the bias signals (such as cross-lingual predictability and alignment to English) into Z_b, the judge's decision-making relies primarily on Z_r, which is compressed and thus resistant to translationese artifacts. The two proxy tasks for L_bisc effectively teach Z_b to encode the spurious attributes, while the compression term on Z_r ensures the robust representation is succinct and discards irrelevant variance. This structured disentanglement directly attacks the source of translationese preference, leading to significant bias reduction without sacrificing task accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Information Bottleneck**  
   - Why needed: Provides a principled way to compress input into a minimal representation that preserves relevant information for the task while discarding noise.  
   - Quick check: Verify that the KL divergence term in the loss regularizes the posterior of Z_r toward a fixed prior.

2. **Disentangled Representation Learning**  
   - Why needed: Allows isolating factors of variation (e.g., semantic content vs. stylistic artifacts) into separate latent variables, enabling targeted debiasing.  
   - Quick check: Ensure two distinct latent vectors (Z_r, Z_b) are produced and that downstream probes show different behaviors.

3. **Cross-Covariance Penalty**  
   - Why needed: Enforces statistical independence between the robust and bias branches, preventing overlap of encoded information.  
   - Quick check: Compute Frobenius norm of cross-covariance matrix; it should decrease during training.

4. **LLM-as-a-Judge**  
   - Why needed: Uses large language models to evaluate other models’ outputs, but these judges can inherit biases from training data and translation artifacts.  
   - Quick check: Confirm that the judge model (e.g., Qwen3) is frozen and that its outputs are supervised by the loss.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Input -> Encoder (Qwen3-0.6B-Embedding) -> [Projection -> Z_r branch; Projection -> Z_b branch] -> (Z_r used by frozen LLM judge; Z_b used by bias predictor heads) -> Judge output & bias predictions.

Critical path:
1. Encoder processes the concatenated chosen/rejected pair and produces initial embeddings.
2. Embeddings are projected to obtain mean vectors for Z_r and Z_b (variational distributions).
3. Samples from Z_r are fed to the frozen LLM judge; the judge’s output is compared to the preference label to compute L_task.
4. Z_b is used to predict spurious attributes (via CLA and LPBC) to compute L_bias.
5. The cross-covariance between the means of Z_r and Z_b contributes L_disc.
6. Total loss = L_task + βL_compress + γL_bias + λL_disc drives updates to encoder and LoRA parameters.

Design tradeoffs:
- Disentanglement strength vs. task utility: Too strong independence (high λ) may hurt Z_r’s expressiveness; too weak may leave bias in Z_r.
- Compression strength (β) vs. information retention: High β over-regularizes and may drop task-relevant cues.
- Two-stage training: First stabilizing projection heads, then joint fine-tuning of LoRA, prevents early collapse but requires careful scheduling.

Failure signatures:
- Linear probe on Z_r achieves high accuracy (>70%) in distinguishing human vs. machine text → insufficient disentanglement.
- Large drop in macro accuracy on m-RewardBench → over-compression or over-regularization.
- L_disc does not converge → λ too low or ineffective covariance penalty.

First experiments:
1. Train a baseline variant without L_disc to confirm the penalty’s effect on bias reduction.
2. Vary β (compression) and measure both bias severity and task accuracy to locate the sweet spot.
3. Extract Z_r and Z_b from a held-out set, train logistic regression probes to predict origin (human/machine) and evaluate probe performance; expect near-chance on Z_r and high on Z_b.

## Limitations
- Loss weighting coefficients (β, γ, λ) are not reported, hindering exact replication.
- Data preprocessing details for the mR3 corpus, especially generation of spurious attribute labels, are underspecified.
- The cross-covariance penalty only enforces linear independence; non-linear correlations could remain.

## Confidence
- Utility claim (bias reduction 56–80% + higher accuracy): Medium
- Generalization to other biases (length, self-preference): Low
- Method design (disentangled bottleneck + covariance penalty): High
