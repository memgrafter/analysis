# Segmentation and Processing of German Court Decisions from Open Legal Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.01449
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01449
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** A rule-based pipeline processes 251,038 decisions, achieving segmentation accuracy of 97.40% (±1.59%, 95% CI: 95.81–98.99%) and section coverage of 87.7% (Tenor), 65.4% (Tatbestand), and 95.1% (Entscheidungsgründe).

## Executive Summary
German court decisions in the Open Legal Data corpus are inconsistently formatted and unsegmented, hindering NLP applications. This work introduces a cleaned dataset of 251,038 decisions and a fully rule-based pipeline that reliably segments each decision into three canonical sections: Tenor (operative part), Tatbestand (facts), and Entscheidungsgründe (reasoning). The pipeline also normalizes metadata (court, state), extracts legal references, and isolates the procedural Rechtsmittelbelehrung. Segmentation accuracy is verified on a random sample, reaching 97.40%. The processed corpus is publicly released in JSONL format, providing a valuable resource for downstream legal text processing.

## Method Summary
The pipeline first parses raw HTML content using an HTML parser to extract visible text from `p`, `h1`–`h4`, `td`, and `rd` tags, then normalizes whitespace and removes empty/duplicate lines. Section detection relies on two regular expressions per section marker (compact and spaced-letter forms, e.g., `^\s*tenor\s*:*$` and `^\s*t e n o r\s*:*$`), which are case-insensitive and match entire lines. The active section defaults to Tenor and switches upon a pattern match. For *Beschlüsse* (decisions without a hearing), the *Gründe* section is subdivided by Roman numerals: *Gründe I* maps to *Tatbestand* and *Gründe II* to *Entscheidungsgründe*; if undivided, the whole *Gründe* becomes *Entscheidungsgründe*. The *Rechtsmittelbelehrung* is extracted separately via a pattern match. Metadata (court, state) is normalized by querying Open Legal Data APIs. An external tool then extracts categorized legal citations (statutes, cases). The final output is a JSON object per decision, written to a JSONL file. No training data is required.

## Key Results
- Segmentation accuracy: **97.40%** (95% confidence interval: 95.81%–98.99%) on a manually verified random sample of 384 decisions.
- Section coverage across the full dataset: **Tenor** 87.7%, **Tatbestand** 65.4%, **Entscheidungsgründe** 95.1%.
- The cleaned dataset comprises **251,038** decisions and is publicly available in JSONL format.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Fixed Header Patterns
Fixed, case-insensitive, full-line header patterns reliably separate the primary sections. The pipeline scans normalized text line-by-line, switching the active section buffer when a line matches either the compact (e.g., `^\s*tenor\s*:*$`) or spaced-letter (e.g., `^\s*t e n o r\s*:*$`) regex for markers (tenor, tatbestand, entscheidungsgründe, gründe). The core assumption is that most decisions start with a Tenor header and subsequent headers follow a standard vocabulary. Evidence includes the abstract’s manual verification claim, explicit regex details in section 3.1, and the high 97.40% verification rate. The mechanism fails when headers are absent, malformed (e.g., embedded in a paragraph), use non-standard vocabulary, or if the initial Tenor assumption is violated, causing misalignment from the start.

### Mechanism 2: Handling Beschlüsse via Gründe Subdivision
For *Beschlüsse* (decisions without a hearing), which often start with Tenor followed by a Gründe section, the pipeline checks for Roman numeral subdivisions (e.g., “Gründe I”, “Gründe II”). If present, “Gründe I” is remapped to *Tatbestand* and “Gründe II” to *Entscheidungsgründe*. If no subdivision exists, the entire *Gründe* becomes *Entscheidungsgründe*. The assumption is that Roman numerals reliably indicate the internal structure of *Beschlüsse*, mapping cleanly to the *Tatbestand*/*Entscheidungsgründe* dichotomy used for *Urteile*. Evidence is provided in section 3.1’s description and indirectly by the coverage statistics: lower *Tatbestand* coverage (65.4%) is attributed to *Beschlüsse* where the factual background is merged. The mechanism fails for *Beschlüsse* with non-standard or missing subdivisions when a *Tatbestand* section is actually present but unmarked, or if *Gründe* is used differently.

### Mechanism 3: Separating Rechtsmittelbelehrung
The *Rechtsmittelbelehrung* (appeal notice) is a formulaic procedural instruction, not substantive reasoning. The pipeline identifies lines matching its typical phrasing and extracts them into a dedicated JSON field, excluding them from the three primary section buffers. This preserves semantic purity of the core sections for downstream tasks. The assumption is that the text is consistently worded and appears as a distinct block. Evidence includes the abstract’s statement, section 3.1’s description, and the corpus showing the field appears in 8,335 decisions (3.32%). Failure occurs if the notice is absent, merged with other content, or phrased non-standardly, leading to omission or false inclusion.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
HTML Parser & Normalizer -> Metadata Resolver -> Section Detector -> Reference Extractor -> Schema Serializer

**Critical Path**  
HTML Parse -> Normalize -> Section Detection (with sub-logic) -> Reference Extraction -> JSON Output

**Design Tradeoffs**
- *Precision vs. Recall*: Fixed patterns maximize precision on common cases (97.4% accuracy) but sacrifice recall on rare/novel formats, explaining the 4–12% section non-coverage.
- *Simplicity vs. Adaptability*: Regex is simple, debuggable, and requires no training data, but is brittle to HTML structure changes or new phrasing.
- *Eager vs. Lazy Processing*: The one-pass pipeline is efficient but cannot easily recover from mid-stream boundary ambiguities; a multi-pass or state machine could be more robust.

**Failure Signatures**
- High `tenor` / low `tatbestand` ratio suggests *Beschlüsse* without *Gründe* subdivision were not properly handled.
- Non-empty `gründe` field but empty `entscheidungsgründe` indicates failed subdivision logic.
- Overlap between section texts in the JSON output means a header pattern was missed or triggered incorrectly inside a paragraph.
- A massive spike in `rechtsmittelbelehrung` field text length likely indicates a false positive where a long *Entscheidungsgründe* paragraph was misidentified.

**First 3 Experiments**
1. **Sanity Check**: Run the pipeline on 10 manually inspected decisions of known types (*Urteil*, *Beschluss*) and visually verify section boundaries and isolation of *Rechtsmittelbelehrung*.
2. **Pattern Stress Test**: Intentionally corrupt a held-out sample by removing line breaks from headers, adding prefix/suffix text, or using uppercase variants; measure the drop in segmentation accuracy to identify brittle regex components.
3. **API Failure Simulation**: Disable the metadata resolver API calls. Confirm all `court`/`state` fields become `Unspecified` and that this does not break downstream section detection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does segmentation into Tenor, Tatbestand, and Entscheidungsgründe improve downstream tasks?** The authors plan to evaluate a RAG system across subtasks but provide no experimental results; the hypothesis remains untested.
- **What specific formatting irregularities cause the 2.6% segmentation errors?** Errors are attributed to rare irregularities, but no detailed error analysis is given. Manual examination of the missegmented sample is needed.
- **What is the precision and recall of the legal reference extraction tool?** The dataset includes references extracted by an external tool, yet correctness is not evaluated. Annotating a sample for reference accuracy would resolve this.

## Limitations
- **Regex specification gaps**: Exact patterns for spaced-letter headers and *Rechtsmittelbelehrung* detection are not fully detailed, risking implementation drift and missed edge cases.
- **Underspecified Beschlüsse subdivision logic**: The Roman numeral mapping rules are ambiguous (e.g., numeral formats like “I.” vs. “1.”), likely explaining the lower *Tatbestand* coverage (65.4%) and causing failures on atypical prints.
- **Sample-based verification**: The claimed 97.40% accuracy relies on a single random sample of 384 decisions; this may not fully capture rare formatting variations across the full corpus of 251,038 decisions.

## Confidence
- **High**: Rule-based pipeline architecture and reported sample accuracy (±1.59% CI) are clearly described and verifiable from the released corpus.
- **Medium**: Section coverage statistics (Tenor 87.7%, Tatbestand 65.4%, Entscheidungsgründe 95.1%) are directly reported, but their causal link to specific failure modes (e.g., *Beschlüsse* without subdivisions) is inferred, not proven.
- **Low**: Generalizability to unseen legal formats or post‑2022 drafting changes; brittleness to minor header variations is not stress‑tested.

## Next Checks
1. **Regex robustness stress test**: Corrupt a held-out sample by altering header formats (e.g., adding punctuation, non-breaking spaces, or mixed‑case variants) to quantify accuracy drop and identify the most brittle patterns.
2. **Beschlüsse subdivision audit**: Manually review 200 *Beschlüsse* decisions to validate the Roman numeral mapping logic, measuring how often *Gründe I* correctly corresponds to *Tatbestand* and identifying common deviations.
3. **Rechtsmittelbelehrung extraction evaluation**: Create a gold‑standard subset (n≥100) labeling the presence/absence and bounds of *Rechtsmittelbelehrung* to compute precision/recall for this component, since its pattern is unspecified.
