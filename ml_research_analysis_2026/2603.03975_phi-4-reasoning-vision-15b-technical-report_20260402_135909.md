# Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B Technical Report

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.03975
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03975
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: Achieves competitive accuracy on key multimodal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, ScreenSpot-v2) with only 200B training tokens and up to 10× faster inference compared to larger models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a 15B-parameter multimodal reasoning model that balances high accuracy with low training and inference costs, challenging the trend toward ever-larger models. Its core architecture employs mid-fusion with a dynamic-resolution SigLIP-2 vision encoder and the Phi-4-Reasoning LLM backbone. The work emphasizes systematic data quality improvements (filtering, error correction, synthetic augmentation) as the primary performance lever, and uses a hybrid training mixture (20% reasoning with `<think>` tokens, 80% non-reasoning with `<nothink>` tokens) to enable the model to switch between direct answers and chain-of-thought reasoning based on task complexity.

Despite using only 200B training tokens—far fewer than models like Qwen3-VL (>1T tokens)—the model achieves competitive results on benchmarks such as MathVista and ScreenSpot-v2 and offers up to 10× faster inference. Ablations show that high-resolution dynamic encoding yields substantial gains (e.g., +17.5% on ScreenSpot-Pro) and that balancing math and computer-use data proportionally improves performance in both domains.

## Method Summary
The architecture is mid-fusion: images are processed by a SigLIP-2 NaFlex dynamic-resolution encoder producing up to 3600 visual tokens, which are mapped to the LLM dimension by a randomly initialized MLP projector. The system is built on the Phi-4-Reasoning (15B) LLM. Training follows three stages: (1) MLP-only pretraining (1.4B tokens) on image-text alignment; (2) full instruction tuning (188.5B tokens) on single-image tasks using a hybrid data mixture of ~20% reasoning (tagged with `<think>`) and ~80% non-reasoning (tagged with `<nothink>`), with max sequence length 8192; (3) long-context, multi-image, and responsible AI training (12B tokens) with max sequence length 16384. Default hyperparameters use AdamW, bf16, DeepSpeed ZeRO-1, and learning rates of 1e-3, 2e-5, and 7e-7 for the respective stages. The data mixture emphasizes both math/science reasoning and computer-use (CUA) grounding, with careful curation (filtering, error correction, synthetic augmentation) to maximize token quality.

## Key Results
- Achieves strong performance on reasoning and grounding benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, ScreenSpot-v2) while using only 200B training tokens—an order of magnitude less than larger models like Qwen3-VL—and with up to 10× faster inference.
- High-resolution dynamic visual encoding (up to 3600 tokens) delivers large gains: +17.5% absolute on ScreenSpot-Pro compared to a 2048-token baseline.
- A hybrid data mix (≈20% reasoning with `<think>`, 80% non-reasoning with `<nothink>`) ensures the model directly answers simple queries and performs chain-of-thought for complex ones, optimizing latency without sacrificing accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Systematic data quality improvements (filtering, error correction, synthetic augmentation) are the primary driver of performance, enabling competitive results with far less training data than larger models.
- **Mechanism**: The authors manually inspected datasets, categorized them by quality, regenerated incorrect answers using GPT-4o, fixed formatting errors, and used high-quality images as seeds to generate new text. This process reduces noise and increases the usefulness of each training token.
- **Core assumption**: Model performance is bottlenecked by the quality (correctness, relevance, formatting) of training data rather than its sheer volume; thus, investing in curation yields multiplicative gains.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The most substantial improvements come from systematic filtering, error correction, and synthetic augmentation—reinforcing that data quality remains the primary lever for model performance."
  - [section 3.1]: Detailed process of manual inspection, regeneration, and synthetic data generation; e.g., "We found that 5–10 minutes per dataset was enough to classify..." and descriptions of generation techniques.
  - [corpus]: The corpus shows many contemporary multimodal models (e.g., Qwen3-VL, Kimi-VL) use >1 trillion training tokens, while Phi-4-reasoning-vision uses only 200 billion, yet achieves competitive accuracy, suggesting data quality compensates for lower scale.
- **Break condition**: If models trained on curated data still underperform on diverse tasks, it may indicate that curation introduced bias or reduced diversity, or that the curation techniques are not generalizable across domains.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A dynamic-resolution vision encoder (SigLIP-2 NaFlex) with high token capacity (up to 3600 tokens) significantly improves performance on high-resolution reasoning tasks by capturing fine-grained visual details.
- **Mechanism**: The encoder adapts the number of patches based on image content, allocating more tokens to complex regions. Ablations show that increasing max tokens from 2048 to 3600 yields a +17.5% absolute gain on ScreenSpot-Pro, a high-resolution GUI grounding benchmark.
- **Core assumption**: Accurate visual perception is a prerequisite for high-quality reasoning; thus, enhancing the vision encoder directly benefits downstream tasks, especially those requiring precise localization (e.g., computer-use agents).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 2.2]: "Our primary finding is that dynamic resolution vision encoders with a large number of visual tokens perform uniformly well, and the best on high-resolution datasets. It is particularly interesting to compare dynamic resolution with 2048 vs. 3600 maximum tokens: the latter roughly corresponds to native HD 720p resolution and enjoys a substantial boost on high-resolution benchmarks, particularly ScreenSpot-Pro."
  - [table 1]: Shows results: dynamic resolution 3600 achieves 17.5% on ScreenSpot-Pro vs. 9.2% for 2048.
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes works like Gemma3 and NVILA that also explore high-resolution techniques, but this paper's systematic ablation provides clear evidence for dynamic resolution's effectiveness.
- **Break condition**: If the increase in visual tokens leads to prohibitive inference latency or memory usage, or if performance gains plateau beyond a certain resolution, then the trade-off may become unfavorable for deployment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Training a reasoning-capable LLM on a hybrid mixture of reasoning (20%) and non-reasoning (80%) data, with explicit <nothink> tokens for non-reasoning, enables the model to efficiently switch between direct answers for simple tasks and chain-of-thought for complex ones, optimizing latency without sacrificing accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: The model learns to condition on special tokens: <think> for reasoning traces and <nothink> for direct responses. For perception-focused tasks (captioning, OCR), <nothink> yields concise outputs; for math/science, <think> yields structured reasoning. This avoids unnecessary verbosity on easy tasks.
- **Core assumption**: Not all multimodal queries require multi-step reasoning; by learning a switch, the model can provide efficiency for simple queries while retaining deep reasoning when beneficial.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "A hybrid mix of 20% reasoning tokens) and 80% non-reasoning (with <nothink> tokens) data allows the single 15B-parameter model to produce direct answers for simple tasks and chain-of-thought for complex ones, optimizing for latency."
  - [section 4.2]: "Our model defaults to direct inference for perception-focused domains where reasoning adds latency without improving accuracy, avoiding unnecessary verbosity and reducing inference costs, and it invokes longer reasoning paths for domains, such as math and science, that benefit from structured multi-step reasoning."
  - [tables 5 and 6]: The model's default mixed behavior often matches or exceeds forced thinking/non-thinking accuracy, demonstrating effective switching in practice.
- **Break condition**: If the model frequently misclassifies task difficulty (e.g., generates reasoning for simple queries or direct answers for complex ones), then the data ratio or learning signal for mode switching may be insufficient, or the boundary between modes may be too ambiguous.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
The data flow: Image → SigLIP-2 (NaFlex dynamic-resolution encoder) → variable number of visual tokens → MLP projector → embedding tokens → interleaved with text tokens (including special mode tokens `<think>` or `<nothink>`) → Phi-4-Reasoning LLM (15B) → output.

### Critical path
1. Image input is processed by the SigLIP-2 encoder, which outputs a set of visual tokens. The number of tokens varies dynamically based on image resolution and content (more tokens for complex regions).
2. Visual tokens are passed through the MLP projector to match the LLM's embedding dimension.
3. Projected visual tokens are interleaved with text tokens (including special mode tokens) to form a unified sequence.
4. The sequence is fed into the Phi-4-Reasoning LLM, which generates output autoregressively. The presence of `<think>` or `<nothink>` at the start influences the generation style.
5. For multi-image or long-context tasks, Stage 3 training extends context length and teaches cross-image reasoning.

### Design tradeoffs
- Mid-fusion vs Early-fusion: Mid-fusion is chosen for efficiency; it leverages pretrained unimodal components and avoids the high compute of full cross-attention. Trade-off: potentially less tight visual-textual grounding.
- Dynamic resolution vs fixed: Dynamic resolution allocates tokens adaptively, improving performance on high-res tasks but increasing context length and quadratic attention cost. Fixed resolution (e.g., 384x384) is simpler but may lose details.
- Reasoning mix ratio: 20% reasoning data balances accuracy and latency. More reasoning data might improve math performance but increase latency on simple tasks; less might hurt reasoning ability.
- Data quality vs scale: Heavy curation yields high-quality tokens but may reduce diversity and require more effort. Scaling with low-quality data may lead to diminishing returns.

### Failure signatures
- Poor performance on high-resolution benchmarks (e.g., ScreenSpot-Pro < 50%) indicates vision encoder not capturing fine details; might need higher max tokens or better dynamic allocation.
- High latency on simple tasks (e.g., captioning) suggests the model is defaulting to reasoning; may need to adjust mode token usage or data ratio.
- Low math/science accuracy despite good perception suggests insufficient reasoning data or ineffective mode switching.
- Safety failures: generating harmful content, especially with visual prompts; indicates inadequate RAI training data or misalignment.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Vision encoder resolution ablation**: Train a small model with different max token settings (2048 vs 3600) and evaluate on ScreenSpot-Pro to confirm dynamic resolution benefits. Expected: higher tokens improve accuracy but increase latency.
2. **Reasoning mix ratio sweep**: Train small models with varying reasoning percentages (0%, 10%, 20%, 30%) and measure accuracy on math (MathVista) and perception (OCRBench) tasks, along with average output length. Expected: 20% gives best trade-off.
3. **Data quality intervention**: Take a baseline dataset with known errors (e.g., a math QA dataset with 20% wrong answers), apply the described regeneration pipeline (using GPT-4o), and compare model performance on a clean test set. Expected: regenerated data yields higher accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can text-conditioning be integrated into image tokenization to dynamically allocate tokens to query-relevant regions, improving efficiency for agentic tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "It is an open question how to leverage text-conditioning to most efficiently tokenize the image—for example, if a specific question is asked about a high-resolution scene, the background could be encoded in a lower resolution to save on tokens."
- **Why unresolved**: Current dynamic-resolution methods process all regions equally, ignoring query context and wasting tokens.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement a text-conditioned tokenizer (e.g., cross-attention patch selection) and evaluate token efficiency and accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro versus dynamic resolution baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How do math vs. computer-use data proportions affect cross-domain performance at larger scales and extreme ratios (<1%), and do trade-offs emerge?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "A clear open question is to study the effects of data proportion at a scale which challenges the edge of current models’ capabilities: do our insights about strong uniform performance hold, or do trade-offs between different reasoning tasks become more obvious at larger scales? ... understanding data dynamics at more extreme ratios (1% or less) is an open problem."
- **Why unresolved**: Their small-scale experiments with mild imbalance showed uniform gains; scaling may cause competition.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train trillion-token models with math:CUA ratios from 0.1% to 10%, evaluate on MathVista and ScreenSpot-v2 to map performance trade-offs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the optimal reasoning/non-reasoning data mix for mixed-mode models, and how can mode switching be made reliable?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "The 20/80 reasoning-to-non-reasoning data split may not be optimal... Determining the ideal data balance, and ensuring that the model switches appropriately between modes, remains an open research problem."
- **Why unresolved**: The split was ad-hoc; implicit switching from data can be imprecise.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablate reasoning percentages (0-50%), measure accuracy, token length, and latency on reasoning and perception benchmarks; analyze hidden states for switching cues.

## Limitations
- **Curation reproducibility**: Manual data inspection (5–10 minutes per dataset) is not fully documented, risking hidden bias and hindering exact replication.
- **Dynamic-resolution details**: The precise NaFlex algorithm (patch grid, token budget policy) is omitted, preventing accurate recreation of the encoder's adaptive token allocation.
- **Data mixture and prompting**: Exact dataset counts, the 80/20 split implementation, and prompt templates for mode token injection are unspecified, making it difficult to reproduce the hybrid training signal.

## Confidence
- **Data quality improvements**: High confidence. Strong ablations and contrast with much larger models.
- **Dynamic-resolution encoder**: Medium confidence. Clear ablation (+17.5% on ScreenSpot-Pro) but NaFlex specifics unknown.
- **Hybrid mix & <nothink> token**: Low confidence. Supported by tables but lacking full protocol details (prompt templates, Stage 3 mixing).
