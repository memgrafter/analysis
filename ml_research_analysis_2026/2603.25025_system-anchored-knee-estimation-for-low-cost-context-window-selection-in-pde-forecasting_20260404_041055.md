# System-Anchored Knee Estimation for Low-Cost Context Window Selection in PDE Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25025
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25025
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SAKE achieves strongest matched-budget performance: 67.8% exact knee recovery, 91.7% within-1, 6.1% mean regret, and 0.051 cost ratio (94.9% search-cost savings).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the under‑formalized problem of selecting an optimal context window length for autoregressive neural PDE simulators in a low‑cost manner. It introduces System‑Anchored Knee Estimation (SAKE), a two‑stage method that first extracts a small set of physically interpretable system anchors from clean dynamics to narrow the candidate set, then performs lightweight knee‑aware downstream selection within that region. Evaluated on eight PDEBench families under a shared \(L\in\{1,\dots,16\}\) protocol, SAKE reduces search cost to just 5.1% of an exhaustive sweep while maintaining high accuracy (67.8% exact knee recovery, 91.7% within‑1). This demonstrates that leveraging system‑level memory characteristics can dramatically accelerate hyperparameter selection for PDE forecasting.

## Method Summary
SAKE operates in two stages. Stage 1 (System Anchoring) uses clean‑system trajectories to compute a low‑dimensional state summary via randomized PCA (retaining 99% variance, up to 64 components). For each candidate window length \(L\), a ridge VAR model (\(\lambda=10^{-3}\)) is fit on the summary, producing a system risk curve \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\). From this curve, two anchors are extracted: \(L_{\text{core}}\) (smallest \(L\) where the residual gap to the longest window falls below a tolerance with an upper confidence bound) and \(L_{\text{plateau}}\) (first \(L\ge L_{\text{core}}\) where the relative gain drops below a threshold, or \(L_{\text{max}}\) if none). These anchors define a shortlist \(S_0\). Stage 2 (Downstream Selection) runs a coarse pilot on \(S_0\), ranks candidates, and expands the top‑2 by adding immediate neighbors and the boundary anchors to form \(S_1\) (capped at 6). A stronger pilot runs on \(S_1\), computing diagnostics—mean rollout error \(\tilde{m}(L)\), terminal error \(\tilde{u}(L)\), variability \(\tilde{v}(L)\), and an asymptotic proxy \(\tilde{a}(L)\) when available. These are normalized over \(S_1\) and combined into a composite score \(q(L)\) with fixed weights. The smallest candidate near the best score that lies on the saturation frontier (adjacent gain and remaining gap \(<0.15\)) and is within \(1.5\) standard errors of the best is selected. By concentrating evaluation on a tiny subset, SAKE achieves high oracle‑knee recovery at a fraction of the exhaustive cost.

## Key Results
- 67.8% exact knee recovery, 91.7% within‑1 accuracy, 6.1% mean regret@knee.
- Cost ratio 0.051, representing 94.9% normalized search‑cost savings compared to exhaustive validation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: System anchoring compresses the candidate space to a small, physically interpretable region likely containing the oracle knee, dramatically reducing search cost.
- **Mechanism**: From clean‑system trajectories, SAKE builds a low‑dimensional state summary via randomized PCA (retaining 99% variance, ≤64 components). For each candidate window \(L\), it fits a ridge VAR(\(L\)) (regularization \(10^{-3}\)) on this summary, producing a system risk curve \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\). The **core anchor** \(L_{\text{core}}\) is the smallest \(L\) where the residual gap to the longest window, \(T_{\text{sys}}(L)=R_{\text{sys}}(L)-R_{\text{sys}}(L_{\text{max}})\), falls below a tolerance \(\varepsilon_{\text{sys}}=\rho(R_{\text{sys}}(L_{\text{min}})-R_{\text{sys}}(L_{\text{max}}))\) with an upper confidence bound (UCB) to avoid early stopping. The **plateau anchor** \(L_{\text{plateau}}\) is the first \(L \ge L_{\text{core}}\) where the relative gain \(G_{\text{rel}}(L) = \frac{R_{\text{sys}}(L)-R_{\text{sys}}(L^+)}{\max(R_{\text{sys}}(L),10^{-12})}\) has UCB below \(\tau_{\text{pl}}\); if none exists, it defaults to \(L_{\text{max}}\). The initial shortlist is \(S_0 = \{L_{\text{min}}, L_{\text{core}}, L_{\text{plateau}}\}\).
- **Core assumption**: The system‑level memory characteristics captured by \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\) are aligned with the context window needed by the neural simulator for optimal rollout performance. That is, the knee in system risk correlates with the knee in downstream rollout error \(M(L)\).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "first extracts a small set of physically interpretable system anchors (\(L_{\text{core}}\), \(L_{\text{plateau}}\)) from clean‑system dynamics to define a plausible candidate region"
  - [section 3.2.2]: Provides the exact formulas for \(T_{\text{sys}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\text{sys}}\), \(G_{\text{rel}}\), and the UCB‑based definitions of \(L_{\text{core}}\) and \(L_{\text{plateau}}\).
  - [corpus]: The related corpus focuses on LLM context windows and shows limited direct overlap; this anchoring mechanism is a novel contribution for PDE simulators. Assumption: The lack of corpus evidence does not invalidate the mechanism but indicates its originality.
- **Break condition**: If \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\) decreases monotonically without a clear knee or plateau (e.g., in very simple systems), \(L_{\text{core}}\) may be \(L_{\text{min}}\) and \(L_{\text{plateau}}\) may default to \(L_{\text{max}}\), producing an uninformative \(S_0\). More critically, if the clean‑system dynamics are not representative of the simulator’s learned behavior (due to noise, partial observations, or architectural biases), the anchored region may exclude the oracle knee, as seen in the DiffReact band coverage of only 25% (Table 3).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A knee‑aware composite score derived from multiple rollout diagnostics reliably identifies the smallest near‑optimal window within the anchored candidate set.
- **Mechanism**: After refining the candidate set to \(S_1\) (via local expansion around top coarse‑pilot candidates), SAKE computes for each \(L \in S_1\):
  - mean rollout error \(\tilde{m}(L)\) (average over 4 rollout anchors),
  - terminal error \(\tilde{u}(L)\) (final rollout step),
  - variability \(\tilde{v}(L)\) (standard deviation across anchors),
  - asymptotic proxy \(\tilde{a}(L)\) (when available, e.g., from pilot trajectory fit).
  These are normalized over \(S_1\) and combined into a stage‑two score:
  \[
  q(L) = \alpha \bigl( w_{\text{mean}} \tilde{m}(L) + w_{\text{term}} \tilde{u}(L) + w_{\text{std}} \tilde{v}(L) \bigr) + (1-\alpha) \tilde{a}(L),
  \]
  where lower is better. The final window is the smallest candidate after the saturation frontier \(r\) (first index where adjacent gain and remaining gap to best score \(q^\star\) are both \(< 0.15\)) whose score is statistically indistinguishable: \(q(L) \le q^\star + \kappa \cdot \mathrm{SE}(q^\star)\) (\(\kappa=1.5\)).
- **Core assumption**: \(q(L)\) is strictly decreasing before the oracle knee and flattens beyond it, such that the saturation frontier \(r\) occurs near or after the knee. The statistical guard (\(\kappa\)) protects against pilot noise.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2.3]: Explicit definition of \(q(L)\) and the final selection rule, including the saturation thresholds (0.15) and \(\kappa\).
  - [abstract]: "knee‑aware downstream selection only within this anchored set"
  - [corpus]: No direct counterpart; generic baselines like ASHA use early‑stopping on a single metric, while SAKE’s multi‑diagnostic approach is tailored to PDE rollout brittleness.
- **Break condition**: If the pilot rollout length or number of anchors is insufficient, the diagnostics may not correlate with full‑protocol performance, causing misranking. Fixed thresholds (0.15, \(\kappa=1.5\)) assume a typical saturation pattern; atypical curves (e.g., continuous slow improvement) can lead to an overly large \(r\) (overshooting) or a too‑early frontier (underserving).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The two‑stage coupling of system anchoring and knee‑aware selection yields the strongest matched‑budget performance by focusing evaluation on a tiny, relevant subset of windows, achieving high oracle‑knee recovery with minimal search cost.
- **Mechanism**: Stage 1 provides a scaffold \(S_0\) that already contains the oracle knee with moderate probability (25–75% across datasets, Table 3). Stage 2 then:
  1. Runs a coarse pilot on \(S_0\) to rank candidates.
  2. Refines the candidate set by adding local grid neighbors (\(h=1\)) of the top‑\(k\) (\(k=2\)) while retaining boundary anchors, yielding \(S_1\) (capped at 6 windows).
  3. Runs a stronger pilot on \(S_1\) and applies the knee‑aware rule.
  Because \(S_1\) is tiny (mean 5.3 windows vs. a full grid of 16), the total selector cost is only 5.1% of an exhaustive sweep (cost ratio 0.051), while final coverage (\(L_{\text{knee}} \in S_1\)) reaches 95.8% on average.
- **Core assumption**: The oracle knee lies within a local neighborhood of the top‑ranked coarse‑pilot candidates. The refinement step expands just enough to correct for anchor errors without blowing up cost.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.2.2]: Table 3 shows initial coverage (in‑band) 25–75% but final coverage (in \(S_1\)) 95.8%.
  - [abstract]: "67.8% Exact, 91.7% Within‑1, 6.1% mean regret@knee, and a 0.051 cost ratio"
  - [corpus]: Generic low‑cost baselines (ASHA, Direct‑Shortlist) lack system priors and are outperformed, demonstrating the value of coupling.
- **Break condition**: If system anchors are severely mis‑specified (e.g., \(L_{\text{plateau}}\) too small), the coarse pilot may rank a wrong region highly, and refinement will not recover \(L_{\text{knee}}\). The paper’s hard case (RDB/ConvLSTM) shows all selectors failing, indicating some knees are intrinsically difficult to localize with short pilots.

## Architecture Onboarding

#### Component map
Clean‑system trajectories \(D_{\text{sys}}\) → Randomized PCA summary (99% variance, ≤64 components) → For each \(L\in\{1,\dots,16\}\): fit ridge VAR(\(L\)) (\(\lambda=10^{-3}\)) → System risk \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\) → Compute \(T_{\text{sys}}(L)=R_{\text{sys}}(L)-R_{\text{sys}}(L_{\text{max}})\) and \(\varepsilon_{\text{sys}}=\rho(R_{\text{sys}}(L_{\text{min}})-R_{\text{sys}}(L_{\text{max}}))\) → \(L_{\text{core}} = \min\{L:\text{UCB}(T_{\text{sys}}(L)) \le \varepsilon_{\text{sys}}\}\) → Compute \(G_{\text{rel}}(L) = (R_{\text{sys}}(L)-R_{\text{sys}}(L^+))/\max(R_{\text{sys}}(L),10^{-12})\) → \(L_{\text{plateau}} = \min\{L \ge L_{\text{core}}:\text{UCB}(G_{\text{rel}}(L)) \le \tau_{\text{pl}}\}\) else \(L_{\text{max}}\) → \(S_0 = \{L_{\text{min}}, L_{\text{core}}, L_{\text{plateau}}\}\) → Coarse pilot on \(S_0\) (cheap training) → coarse scores → top‑\(k=2\) → \(S_1 = (\bigcup_{L \in \text{top‑}k} N_h(L)) \cup \{L_{\text{min}}, L_{\text{plateau}}\}\), \(h=1\), cap 6 → Strong pilot on \(S_1\) (stronger training) → diagnostics: mean rollout error \(\tilde{m}(L)\), terminal error \(\tilde{u}(L)\), variability \(\tilde{v}(L)\), asymptotic proxy \(\tilde{a}(L)\) (if available) → Normalize each over \(S_1\) → Composite score \(q(L)=\alpha(0.75\tilde{m}+0.25\tilde{u}+0.20\tilde{v})+(1-\alpha)\tilde{a}\), \(\alpha=0.25\) → Saturation frontier \(r\) (adjacent gain and remaining gap <0.15) → Selection: smallest \(L \le r\) with \(q(L) \le q^\star + \kappa\cdot\mathrm{SE}(q^\star)\), \(\kappa=1.5\), else \(L_r\) → Output \(L_{\text{sel}}\).

#### Critical path
1. Clean trajectories → PCA → state summary.
2. For each \(L \in \mathcal{L}\): fit ridge VAR → \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\).
3. Compute \(L_{\text{core}}\) and \(L_{\text{plateau}}\) → \(S_0\).
4. Cheap pilots on \(S_0\) → coarse scores → top‑\(k\).
5. Expand to \(S_1\) (local neighbors + boundaries).
6. Strong pilots on \(S_1\) → diagnostics → \(q(L)\).
7. Find saturation frontier \(r\).
8. Apply statistical rule to select \(L_{\text{sel}}\).
Any break (e.g., ridge VAR failure, poor coarse ranking) propagates to final selection.

#### Design tradeoffs
- **Pilot budget vs. cost**: Coarse (2 epochs) and strong (6 epochs) budgets are fixed to match baselines; increasing them would improve reliability but reduce cost savings.
- **Anchor robustness**: Assumes clean, full‑state \(D_{\text{sys}}\). Appendices D/E show graceful degradation under noise/downsampling, but severe corruption breaks the alignment.
- **Generality vs. specificity**: Fixed thresholds (\(\rho=0.05\), \(\tau_{\text{pl}}=0.05\), \(\kappa=1.5\)) and score weights avoid per‑backbone tuning, enabling plug‑and‑play use but may not be optimal for all PDE families.
- **Refinement scope**: \(h=1\) and cap 6 keep \(S_1\) minimal. Larger \(h\) increases coverage risk but also cost; SAKE prioritizes minimalism.

#### Failure signatures
- **Low system coverage**: \(L_{\text{knee}} \notin [L_{\text{core}}, L_{\text{plateau}}]\) (e.g., DiffReact 25% in‑band, Table 3).
- **Coarse‑pilot misranking**: Top‑\(k\) from \(S_0\) exclude candidates near \(L_{\text{knee}}\), causing \(L_{\text{knee}} \notin S_1\).
- **No clear saturation**: \(q(L)\) curve drops continuously or is flat early; saturation frontier \(r\) becomes too large or too small.
- **High regret**: \(\text{Regret}_{\text{knee}} > 10\%\) indicates \(L_{\text{sel}}\) far from knee (Table 2 shows SAKE mean 6.1%).
- **Cost overrun**: \(S_1\) exceeds cap due to many neighbors; monitor unique evaluations.

1. **Anchor extraction validation**: Using DiffReact clean trajectories, implement PCA (randomized, 99% variance) and ridge VAR (\(\lambda=10^{-3}\)). Compute \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\) for \(L=1..16\). ## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can SAKE be extended to context‑window selection for neural PDE simulators with adaptive memory or internal state, rather than fixed‑window autoregressive models?**  
   *Basis:* The Discussion explicitly scopes the study to "fixed‑window one‑step autoregressive simulators" and notes SAKE is "complementary to architectural remedies that internalize memory."  
   *Why unresolved:* Adaptive‑memory architectures (e.g., recurrent or stateful models) handle temporal dependencies differently, so the discrete window grid and anchor‑based compression may not directly apply; new methods are needed to define and select context in such settings.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Empirical evaluation of modified SAKE variants on stateful backbones (e.g., Neural ODEs, memory‑augmented transformers), with comparisons against baselines; theoretical analysis of how memory mechanisms alter the context‑window optimization problem.

2. **What system‑theoretic properties of PDEs predict whether the anchor band (between \(L_{\text{core}}\) and \(L_{\text{plateau}}\)) will contain the oracle knee for a given simulator backbone?**  
   *Basis:* The paper reports variable knee‑in‑band coverage (e.g., 25% on DiffReact, 75% on RDB in Table 3) and persistent hard cases like RDB/ConvLSTM, but provides no theoretical characterization of when anchors succeed or fail.  
   *Why unresolved:* Anchor extraction relies on heuristics (PCA, ridge VAR) and fixed thresholds; without linking PDE characteristics (e.g., chaos, stiffness, dimensionality) to anchor reliability, practitioners cannot anticipate SAKE’s effectiveness a priori.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Correlate anchor coverage with quantitative PDE descriptors (Lyapunov exponents, spectral decay, characteristic timescales); derive analytical conditions under which the system risk curve \(R_{\text{sys}}(L)\) aligns with downstream rollout knees.

3. **Can the knee‑aware downstream selection heuristic in SAKE be replaced with an adaptive or learned mechanism that generalizes better across PDE families and model architectures?**  
   *Basis:* SAKE uses fixed saturation thresholds (0.15 fractions) and standard error multiplier (\(\kappa=1.5\)), along with constant score weights; while robust to moderate changes, this rule may not optimize for all performance curve shapes or pilot noise levels.  
   *Why unresolved:* The hand‑crafted rule is not data‑driven and may miss knees in scenarios with irregular pilot curves or high variability; an adaptive approach could better handle diverse downstream behaviors.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Develop and test machine learning models (e.g., small regressors) that predict the knee from pilot diagnostics (mean, terminal, variability, asymptotic scores); compare adaptive thresholding schemes against the fixed rule across a wider range of PDE benchmarks.

4. **How robust is SAKE to extreme observation noise, severe sensor sparsity, or significant model mismatch in real‑world PDE forecasting tasks?**  
   *Basis:* The robustness appendices (D, E) test noisy and partial observations, showing gradual degradation, but the main evaluation assumes clean‑system dynamics; the Discussion notes reliance on "usable full‑state or proxy state summary."  
   *Why unresolved:* Practical applications often involve harsh corruption or complex noise structures not fully captured by the studied perturbations (e.g., structured noise, non‑Gaussian errors); the failure thresholds and comparative performance under such conditions remain unclear.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Benchmark SAKE on real‑world datasets with natural noise and missing data, or simulate extreme corruption regimes (e.g., heavy‑tailed noise, extreme masking); identify where SAKE’s advantage disappears and compare to baselines that do not require clean anchors.

## Limitations
- **Representativeness assumption**: System anchoring uses clean trajectories to estimate memory; this may misalign with simulator behavior under noise/partial observability (e.g., DiffReact coverage 25%, all selectors fail on RDB/ConvLSTM).
- **Fixed heuristic thresholds**: Hyperparameters (\(\rho=0.05\), \(\tau_{\text{pl}}=0.05\), \(\kappa=1.5\), \(\alpha=0.25\)) are not tuned per PDE family; their sensitivity is unclear.
- **Evaluation scope**: Results cover only eight synthetic PDEBench families; performance on real‑world or more complex PDEs remains unknown.

- Knee‑aware selection (Stage 2): Medium
