# Bayesian Optimization with Gaussian Processes to Accelerate Stationary Point Searches

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2603.10992
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10992
* **Reference count:** 40
* **Primary result:** A unified Bayesian optimization framework using Gaussian Processes with inverse-distance kernels reduces oracle calls for finding stationary points (minima, saddles) and reaction paths by roughly an order of magnitude compared to classical methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a unified Bayesian optimization framework that uses Gaussian Process (GP) surrogates to drastically reduce the number of expensive electronic structure calculations required to locate stationary points and minimum energy paths on potential energy surfaces. The core innovation is a single six-step algorithmic loop applicable to local minimization, dimer-based saddle searches, and nudged elastic band (NEB) path optimizations. The GP surrogate is built with an invariant kernel that analytically incorporates energy and force observations, enabling accurate local modeling from sparse data. Several practical extensions—including farthest point sampling with Earth mover’s distance, MAP hyperparameter regularization, adaptive trust regions, and random Fourier features for scaling—are integrated to ensure robustness on real molecular systems. Benchmarks show reductions of ~10× in oracle calls, validating the approach on both toy surfaces and complex reactions like a 9-atom cycloaddition.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a local Gaussian Process surrogate model of the potential energy surface using a rotationally and translationally invariant kernel based on inverse interatomic distances. This kernel analytically incorporates both energy and gradient (force) observations, providing a cheap, differentiable model for guiding optimization. A unified outer loop iteratively: (1) trains the GP on accumulated data, (2) performs method-specific inner optimization (e.g., L-BFGS for minimization, dimer rotation for saddles, NEB relaxation for paths) on the surrogate, (3) applies an adaptive trust region to ensure safety, and (4) evaluates the true oracle at the proposed configuration(s) to update the training set. Active learning is achieved via implicit trust clipping for minimization/dimer and explicit Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) for NEB. Extensions like farthest point sampling for stable hyperparameter tuning, MAP regularization to prevent oscillations, and random Fourier features for linear-scaling prediction make the approach robust and scalable to larger molecules.

## Key Results
* GP-dimer converges to saddle points in ~30 oracle evaluations versus hundreds for the classical dimer method.
* GP-NEB with one-image evaluation (OIE) reduces oracle calls by 5–10× on benchmark surfaces and real molecular systems (e.g., 9-atom cycloaddition).
* The integrated Optimal Transport GP extensions lower failure rates from ~12% to ~2% across 500+ benchmark reactions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Invariant Kernel with Analytic Derivatives
A Gaussian Process surrogate with an inverse-distance kernel and analytically computed derivative blocks accurately models the potential energy surface near stationary points using only tens of expensive oracle evaluations. The inverse-distance feature map \(\phi_{ij} = 1/r_{ij}\) ensures rotational and translational invariance. This representation compresses repulsive regions and stretches van der Waals regions, yielding more uniform effective curvature for a stationary squared exponential kernel to interpolate. Derivative observations (forces) provide \(3N+1\) constraints per evaluation, densely covering the PES. The kernel blocks for energy–force and force–force covariances are derived via the chain rule and must be computed analytically to avoid numerical noise that would corrupt the Cholesky factorization. **Break condition:** Replacing analytic derivatives with automatic differentiation introduces numerical noise (\(\sim10^{-8}\)) that can cause the covariance matrix to lose positive definiteness after \(\sim\)10 training points, leading to Cholesky failure.

### Mechanism 2: Unified Bayesian Optimization Loop
All stationary point searches (local minimization, dimer saddle search, NEB path) are framed as the same Bayesian optimization outer loop, differing only in the inner-optimization target and acquisition criterion. The GP provides a cheap surrogate with posterior mean (for fast optimization) and posterior variance (for active learning). The loop iterates: train GP; run a method-specific optimizer (L-BFGS, CG+L-BFGS, NEB) on the surrogate; clip proposed steps to an adaptive trust region; evaluate the oracle; update data. Acquisition is implicit via trust clipping for minimization/dimer (the trust-clipped step is evaluated) and explicit via UCB for NEB-OIE. The inner optimizer uses relaxed gradient tolerances on the surrogate because the true PES is only queried upon trust violation or convergence. **Break condition:** If the surrogate becomes inaccurate outside the training region, the inner optimizer may converge to a spurious GP stationary point. The trust region mitigates this by forcing an oracle evaluation when a proposed step exceeds a geometrically defined threshold.

### Mechanism 3: Optimal Transport GP Extensions
The OT-GP extensions (farthest point sampling with Earth mover’s distance, MAP regularization, adaptive trust radius, random Fourier features) collectively ensure robustness and scalability. FPS with EMD selects a geometrically diverse subset for hyperparameter optimization, bounding cubic cost while keeping the matrix well-conditioned. MAP regularization (logarithmic barrier on \(\sigma_f^2\) and oscillation detection) prevents chaotic hyperparameter fluctuations. The adaptive trust radius grows with data but is capped by a system-size-dependent physical limit, balancing exploration and safety. Random Fourier features (RFF) decouple hyperparameter training from prediction, scaling prediction to linear cost in observations and enabling larger training sets. **Break condition:** If the FPS subset is too small (<10), the marginal likelihood landscape is flat and hyperparameters oscillate; although oscillation detection triggers subset growth, failure to find a suitable subset due to low diversity causes instability. If RFF features are too few, the kernel approximation error stalls convergence (evident on 9-atom cycloaddition when \(D_{\text{RFF}} < 300\)).

## Foundational Learning

### Gaussian Process Regression with Derivatives
**Why needed:** The surrogate must interpolate both energies and forces to guide optimization. Derivative observations increase information content per oracle call (\(1+3N\) constraints for \(N\) atoms). Understanding the block covariance structure and the necessity of analytic derivatives is essential for implementation.  
**Quick check:** Why can’t we use automatic differentiation to compute derivative kernel blocks, and what numerical issue does it introduce?

### Bayesian Optimization and Acquisition
**Why needed:** The active learning loop decides where to query the expensive oracle based on surrogate uncertainty. The acquisition criterion (e.g., UCB for NEB, implicit trust clipping) balances exploitation (following surrogate gradient) and exploration (reducing uncertainty). Without this, random/fixed sampling would not achieve the same reduction in oracle calls.  
**Quick check:** How does the GP’s posterior variance guide the selection of the next oracle evaluation in the NEB-OIE variant?

### Molecular Invariance and the Inverse-Distance Kernel
**Why needed:** Atomic coordinates are not intrinsically meaningful due to lab frame and atom permutation symmetries. The kernel must be invariant to rigid motions and permutations of identical atoms; otherwise, physically identical configurations appear dissimilar, destroying interpolation. The inverse-distance feature map achieves invariance automatically.  
**Quick check:** What would go wrong if the GP operated directly on Cartesian coordinates with a simple SE kernel?

## Architecture Onboarding
chemgp-core structure: kernel.rs -> train.rs/scg.rs -> (dimer.rs, neb.rs, min.rs) with sampling.rs/emd.rs and trust.rs support, rff.rs for scaling, oracle.rs trait.

Critical path for GP-NEB OIE: initialize path -> evaluate endpoints -> outer loop: FPS subset selection -> MAP hyperparameter optimization -> build GP (exact or RFF) -> inner L-BFGS relaxation on surrogate for all unevaluated images -> UCB acquisition picks one image -> oracle evaluation -> update training set -> check true force convergence.

Design tradeoffs: Exact GP (precise to ~50 points) vs RFF (linear prediction cost, approximation error; need \(D_{\text{RFF}} \gtrsim 10 N_{\text{pairs}}\)). Per-bead FPS for NEB vs global FPS (per-bead maintains region-specific length scales). Trust region uses EMD (rotation/permutation-invariant, system-size-scaled) vs fixed Euclidean. OIE (minimizes oracle calls) vs AIE (simpler, robust).

Failure signatures: Cholesky failure (analytic derivative missing or duplicate points). Hyperparameter oscillation (rapid \(\sigma_f\) or length scale sign changes; triggers FPS growth). Trust region violation (proposed step exceeds EMD threshold; surrogate extrapolation). Spurious GP stationary points (inner converges to zero surrogate gradient but large true gradient; caught by post-step oracle eval). Poor NEB tangent estimation at local extrema (improved energy-weighted tangent fixes).

First 3 experiments: 1) Müller-Brown surface with GP-dimer: visualize surrogate learning, show ~30 oracle calls vs hundreds. 2) LEPS surface with GP-NEB variants: OIE uses ~1/3 calls of standard NEB, verify barrier consistency. 3) 9-atom cycloaddition with PET-MAD: test RFF (\(D_{\text{RFF}} \approx 500\)) matching exact GP, demonstrate adaptive trust and per-bead FPS stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can the oscillations in Gaussian Process hyperparameters during online retraining be more fundamentally prevented, beyond the current MAP regularization with adaptive subset growth?  
**Basis:** Section 8.2 details pathologies (unbounded signal variance, oscillation) and fixes (log barrier, adaptive FPS growth). The marginal likelihood landscape is "flat or multimodal" with few points; current fixes are reactive heuristics, not guarantees.  
**Why unresolved:** No modification of the underlying objective; a hierarchical Bayesian prior or different surrogate model is not explored.  
**Resolution evidence:** Benchmarking showing alternative priors (horseshoe, hierarchical) or multi-task GPs reduce retraining/adaptation needs while maintaining accuracy/convergence speed.

### Open Question 2
Can a single, general acquisition criterion be derived that provably balances exploitation and exploration optimally for all three unified methods (minimization, dimer, NEB), replacing the current method-specific implicit/explicit choices?  
**Basis:** Section 4 and Table 2 differentiate strategies: implicit acquisition (trust-clipped step) for minimization/dimer, explicit UCB for NEB-OIE, exhaustive for NEB-AIE. No theoretical justification for UCB's optimality or universality.  
**Why unresolved:** Implicit acquisition for dimer/minimization is reactive (trust region), not a designed function. A universal criterion's theoretical derivation and empirical validation are absent.  
**Resolution evidence:** Theoretical analysis deriving a universal acquisition function for constrained optimization on derivative-observed GPs, validated across hundreds of benchmark reactions matching/exceeding current strategies.

### Open Question 3
What are the precise scaling limits and failure modes of the Random Fourier Features (RFF) approximation for inverse-distance kernels in high-DOF molecular saddle searches, and can adaptive feature selection improve robustness?  
**Basis:** Section 8.4 gives heuristic \(D_{\text{RFF}} \gtrsim 10 N_{\text{pairs}}\); for 9-atom system, \(D_{\text{RFF}} \sim 500\) needed, lower values stalled. No formal error bound for inverse-distance kernel's spectrum or active learning loop.  
**Why unresolved:** Rule is practical, not principled; no investigation of adaptive feature selection (e.g., weighting based on GP length scales) or differential impact on gradient uncertainty estimates.  
**Resolution evidence:** Scaling studies from 10 to 100+ atoms measuring convergence rate, failure rate vs. \(D_{\text{RFF}}\) and exact GP; testing adaptive RFF schemes allocating features based on per-pair length scales.

### Open Question 4
Under what conditions does the local surrogate approach fundamentally fail to converge to the true stationary point, and can these be predicted a priori from the potential energy surface's local topology?  
**Basis:** Section 5.2 and Figure 16 handle failures (trust violation, oscillation); OT-GP extensions reduced failure rate from ~12% to ~2%. Residual failures' PES characteristics are not analyzed.  
**Why unresolved:** No correlation of residual failures with geometric/topological PES metrics (Hessian condition number, barrier width/height, Morse parameters).  
**Resolution evidence:** Benchmark 500+ reactions, compute PES descriptors per system, fit statistical model (logistic regression) to predict failure likelihood a priori, leading to pre-screening or adaptive algorithm parameters.

### Open Question 5
How does the performance and reliability of the inverse-distance kernel compare to more sophisticated, permutation-invariant kernels (e.g., SOAP, ACSF) when the reaction involves significant changes in atom connectivity (e.g., bond breaking/forming beyond a local region)?  
**Basis:** Section 3.3.1 rejects high-dimensional descriptors (SOAP) for local surrogates due to overhead and "inverse problem" of recovering Cartesian coordinates; inverse-distance captures relevant variation with fixed dimensions. Validated on 500+ benchmarks.  
**Why unresolved:** Justification is computational/assumption-based; no quantitative comparison on reactions with large reactive regions or changing coordination. SOAP's higher-body correlations might improve surrogate accuracy and trust region validity.  
**Resolution evidence:** Controlled study on reactions with long-range rearrangements, comparing GP-dimer/NEB convergence (oracle calls, success rate, saddle Hessian quality) using inverse-distance vs. SOAP kernel, accounting for total wall-clock time including descriptor computation.

## Limitations
* Requires analytic gradients for oracle; noisy finite-difference gradients can cause Cholesky failures.
* Earth mover's distance (EMD) for trust regions scales cubically with atom count; overhead for >50 atoms is unbenchmarked.
* Local surrogate assumes smooth PES; may struggle on highly corrugated surfaces (e.g., discontinuous forces during bond breaking) or large intramolecular rotations where permutation handling is insufficient.

## Confidence
* **Invariant kernel with analytic derivatives (High):** Strong theoretical derivation, clear break condition matching GP best practices.
* **Unified Bayesian optimization loop (High):** Rigorous Algorithm 1, extensive benchmarks show consistent ~10× reduction.
* **Optimal Transport GP extensions (Medium):** Well-motivated, reduce failure rates, but independent validation sparse; scaling claims for >100 atoms extrapolated.
* **Random Fourier features scaling (Medium):** Heuristic rule validated on small systems; formal error bounds for active learning loop lacking.
* **Failure mode characterization (Low):** Residual failure causes not systematically analyzed; no predictive models from PES topology.
