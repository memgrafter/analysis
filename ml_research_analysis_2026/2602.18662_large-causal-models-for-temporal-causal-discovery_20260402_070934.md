# Large Causal Models for Temporal Causal Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18662
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18662
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LCMs scale to V<sub>max</sub>=12 variables, outperform classical baselines on multiple benchmarks, and provide fast, single-pass inference with runtime independent of input dimensionality.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Large Causal Models (LCMs) as foundation models for temporal causal discovery (CD), addressing the critical limitations of scaling and generalization in prior methods. LCMs use a transformer-based architecture trained on a diverse corpus of 275k synthetic and realistic time-series/graph pairs, augmented with correlation-based training aids. Key results demonstrate that LCMs scale effectively to 12 variables, consistently outperform classical baselines (PCMCI, VARLINGAM, DYNOTEARS) across synthetic, semi-synthetic, and realistic benchmarks, and achieve fast, single-pass inference with runtime independent of input dimensionality. A principled 80% synthetic / 20% realistic training mixture is shown to yield superior zero-shot generalization on out-of-distribution datasets.

## Method Summary
LCMs perform supervised temporal causal discovery by directly mapping a normalized, padded multivariate time series *X* (with fixed maximum dimensions *V*<sub>max</sub>=12, *L*<sub>max</sub>=500) to a lagged adjacency tensor *Â* in a single forward pass. Training employs a large curated corpus of 275k paired (*X*, *A*) samples: 230k from diverse synthetic generators (varying graph densities, linear/nonlinear functions) and 45k from realistic simulators built via the TCS pipeline on real-world datasets. The transformer-based encoder incorporates lagged cross-correlation features as training aids and is optimized with a composite loss balancing edge prediction and correlation regularization. The model is designed for zero-shot application, with a key hyperparameter choice being an 80/20% synthetic/realistic data mixture to maximize out-of-distribution robustness.

## Key Results
- LCMs successfully scale to *V*<sub>max</sub>=12 variables, overcoming prior scaling failures in neural temporal CD methods.
- They match or exceed the performance of classical baselines (PCMCI, VARLINGAM, DYNOTEARS) across synthetic, semi-synthetic (fMRI, Kuramoto), and realistic (Power, Climate) benchmarks, achieving AUCs up to 0.987 on fMRI<sub>5</sub>.
- Inference is fast and single-pass, with runtime effectively independent of the number of input variables (*V*), contrasting the superlinear scaling of traditional methods.
- Training on an 80% synthetic / 20% realistic mixture yields systematic and statistically significant improvements in zero-shot generalization on out-of-distribution datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Scaling via Training Corpus Diversity**  
Scaling to higher variable counts is primarily enabled by training on a large, diverse corpus that combines multiple synthetic generators and realistic datasets, rather than by architectural changes alone. This diversity prevents overfitting to narrow synthetic patterns and allows the fixed-capacity Transformer to learn a generalizable amortized mapping *X* → *A*. The underlying assumption is that causal mechanisms across domains share sufficient statistical commonalities. Evidence: The paper attributes prior scaling failures to training data limitations; the 275k corpus spans varied graph densities and functional forms. Break condition: Scaling degrades if the corpus lacks diversity (e.g., synthetic-only models underperform OOD).

**Mechanism 2: Zero-Shot Generalization via Data Mixture**  
Superior out-of-distribution generalization is achieved by intentionally mixing 80% synthetic and 20% realistic data during training. This balances the structural diversity of synthetic data with the real-world fidelity of TCS-generated data, acting as an implicit regularizer that prevents the model from latching onto artifacts unique to either distribution. The model learns abstract causal principles invariant across domains. Evidence: The 80/20 mix consistently outperforms pure synthetic or higher realistic ratios on OOD benchmarks (fMRI, Power, Climate). Break condition: Performance drops if the OOD target contains mechanisms absent from the mixture (e.g., contemporaneous effects, latent confounders).

**Mechanism 3: Fast Inference via Amortization**  
LCMs achieve fast, single-pass inference with runtime independent of input dimensionality by amortizing the causal search process into a fixed forward pass of a pre-trained network, replacing iterative, combinatorial optimization. Once trained, the computational graph is fixed; operations like self-attention have complexity constant with respect to *V* (for *V* ≤ *V*<sub>max</sub>). Evidence: Abstract and Section 7.4 confirm runtime independence; Figure 4 shows LCM runtimes clustered tightly vs. baselines' superlinear scaling. Break condition: The claim holds only for *V* ≤ *V*<sub>max</sub> (12); for *V* > *V*<sub>max</sub>, performance depends on padding/truncation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Temporal Structural Causal Models (TSCMs)**  
  *Why needed:* LCMs are trained to recover the graph structure of an underlying TSCM, which defines the exact causal target (lagged adjacency, additive noise, stationarity, no contemporaneous effects) and the assumptions under which predictions are valid.  
  *Quick check:* If the true process has a contemporaneous effect (*V*<sub>i,t</sub> → *V*<sub>j,t</sub>), can the LCM discover it? **No**—it is a hard model assumption.

- **Amortized Inference**  
  *Why needed:* This is the core paradigm shift: instead of fitting a new model for each dataset, LCMs amortize the inference cost by training once on a distribution of TSCMs to approximate *P*(*A*|*X*) for any *X* from that distribution, enabling fast zero-shot application.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key computational trade-off of amortization? **Potential lower accuracy on a specific dataset vs. massive speedup and no per-dataset optimization.**

- **Foundation Models for Causality**  
  *Why needed:* LCMs are positioned as *causal* foundation models—pre-trained on a broad, multi-source corpus (275k pairs) to develop general causal representations, then applied to new temporal datasets without fine-tuning. It is not just a large model but a *pre-trained* model for a downstream task (causal discovery).  
  *Quick check:* How does an LCM FM differ from a traditionally trained neural CD method? **Scale and diversity of pre-training data, aiming for broad generalization across datasets rather than optimizing for one.**

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw *X* → Normalization/Padding → Conv1D Embedding → Positional Encoding → Transformer Encoder (with distillation) → Correlation Injection → FFN Head → Sigmoid → *Â*
- **Critical path:** Input time series is min-max normalized per variable and padded/truncated to [*L*<sub>max</sub>, *V*<sub>max</sub>]. A convolutional embedding projects each variable’s time series to *d*<sub>model</sub>-dimensional tokens, augmented with sinusoidal positional encodings. The transformer encoder stack (with optional length-distilling layers) processes these tokens. Lagged cross-correlations computed from raw *X* are flattened and concatenated to the final encoder’s global summary token. A two-layer feedforward head with SwiGLU/LayerNorm outputs a flat vector reshaped to [*V*<sub>max</sub>, *V*<sub>max</sub>, ℓ<sub>max</sub>], passed through a sigmoid for edge probabilities.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Fixed V<sub>max</sub>/L<sub>max</sub> vs. Flexibility:* Simplicity and fast inference vs. inability to handle *V* > *V*<sub>max</sub> without architectural changes; requires truncation/padding.  
  - *Correlation Injection:* Adds strong inductive bias for temporal precedence but risks over-reliance on linear, lagged dependencies, potentially missing complex nonlinear or instantaneous relations.  
  - *Flattened Output Head:* Simple and scalable but not permutation-equivariant; changing variable order in input could change output indexing unless externally tracked.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Poor OOD performance:* If training corpus lacks dynamics similar to target (e.g., no oscillatory systems like Kuramoto), AUC drops significantly.  
  - *Dimensionality collapse:* For *V* close to *V*<sub>max</sub> (12), if model capacity is too low (e.g., 905K parameters), performance may degrade.  
  - *Edge sparsity/density bias:* If training data graphs have a fixed density range, model predictions may be biased towards that density on new graphs with very different edge counts.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation of Training Aids (Table 1):** Train LCM variants on synthetic data with/without correlation-based regularization (CI and CR terms). Adding CR (λ<sub>corr</sub>=0.75) yields statistically significant AUC gains.  
  2. **Mixture Ratio Study (Table 2):** Train LCMs on synthetic/realistic mixes (100/0% to 20/80%). Evaluate OOD on fMRI and Power/Climate. The 80/20 mix maximizes generalization, proving data mixture is critical.  
  3. **Scaling to V=12 & Baseline Comparison (Tables 3–5):** Train large LCMs (up to 24M params) on the full corpus. Evaluate on holdout synthetic (Synthetic_2), OOD semi-synthetic (fMRI, Kuramoto), and realistic (Power, Climate) sets. LCMs match or outperform PCMCI, VARLINGAM, DYNOTEARS across the board, especially on OOD, while scaling in *V*.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Latent confounders:** How can LCMs be extended to handle latent confounders and violations of causal sufficiency? The paper explicitly identifies relaxing causal sufficiency as a key future direction. Current assumptions limit applicability to real-world datasets with hidden variables.
2. **Scaling beyond V=12 and longer lags:** What architectural or training modifications are necessary for LCMs to scale effectively beyond *V*<sub>max</sub>=12 and longer temporal lags? Experiments are capped at *V*<sub>max</sub>=12 and ℓ<sub>max</sub>=3; scaling may require changes like sparse attention or hierarchical processing.
3. **Robustness to ℓ<sub>max</sub>/V<sub>max</sub> misspecification:** How robust are LCMs to misspecification of the maximum lag ℓ<sub>max</sub> or variable count *V*<sub>max</sub> during inference? The paper does not analyze sensitivity when test data exceed these bounds, risking missed effects or truncation artifacts.
4. **Correlation aid biases:** Does the correlation-based training aid (CR) introduce biases in non-linear or confounded causal settings, undermining zero-shot generalization? CR aligns predictions with linear lagged correlations, which may produce spurious associations in non-linear or confounded contexts; impact on OOD benchmarks like Kuramoto or fMRI is unexamined.

## Limitations
- The training corpus (275k pairs) may not fully cover the distribution of real-world TSCMs, especially regarding contemporaneous effects and latent confounders, which are explicitly excluded.
- Key hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate schedule, dropout rates) are not fully enumerated, hindering exact reproduction.
- Fixed architectural limits (*V*<sub>max</sub>=12, *L*<sub>max</sub>=500) require padding/truncation; performance for *V* > *V*<sub>max</sub> is unknown.
- All evaluations are on simulated benchmarks; no assessment on real data with ground truth is provided.
- The output head is not permutation-equivariant; variable ordering may affect predictions.

## Confidence
- **Scalability to V=12:** Medium. Empirical results support scaling up to 12 variables, but the claim relies on training data diversity and does not extend beyond 12.
- **Zero-shot OOD generalization:** Medium. The 80/20 mixture consistently improves OOD performance on tested simulated domains, but generalization to unseen real-world scenarios remains unverified.
- **Runtime independence:** High. Inference time is constant w.r.t. input *V* (for *V* ≤ *V*<sub>max</sub>) by design, and experiments confirm this contrast to baselines.

## Next Checks
1. **Independent replication:** Reimplement the LCM pipeline using the described architecture and training procedure, then reproduce Tables 2–5 (AUCs and runtimes) across synthetic, semi-synthetic, and realistic benchmarks. Systematically vary λ<sub>corr</sub> and mixture ratios to confirm optimal settings.
2. **Stress test scalability beyond V=12:** Adapt the model to accept variable counts >12 (e.g., via chunking or dynamic attention) and evaluate on synthetic graphs with *V*=15 and 20. This tests whether performance degradation is due to insufficient training diversity or architectural constraints.
3. **Real-world zero-shot evaluation:** Apply the pre-trained LCM (without fine-tuning) to established real-world causal discovery datasets with known ground truth (e.g., Sachs protein signaling, Dream challenges). Measure AUC and analyze failures, particularly regarding contemporaneous and latent confounding assumptions.
