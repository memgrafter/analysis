# Artificial Intelligence for Sentiment Analysis of Persian Poetry

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11254
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11254
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: GPT-4o achieves the best zero-shot alignment with human sentiment annotations (Quadratic Weighted Kappa ~0.60, accuracy 33%) for classical Persian poetry.

## Executive Summary
This study explores the application of large language models (LLMs) to sentiment analysis of classical Persian poetry, a domain with limited prior research. By analyzing poems from Rumi and Parvin E'tesami using zero-shot BERT and GPT-based models, the authors assess LLMs' ability to capture poetic sentiment on a 1–5 scale. Results indicate that GPT-4o aligns most closely with human judgments, though overall accuracy remains modest. Additionally, sentiment differences between the two poets and correlations with poetic meter diversity are examined, highlighting both the potential and challenges of using LLMs for digital humanities tasks involving archaic language.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a zero-shot evaluation of multiple LLMs, including BERT variants (mBERT, Pars-BERT) and GPT models (GPT-4o), on a corpus of classical Persian poems. Sentiment scores from 1 (negative) to 5 (positive) were generated for each poem without any fine-tuning on poetry data. These automated scores were compared against human annotations using metrics like Quadratic Weighted Kappa and accuracy. The study also computed entropy of poetic meters to explore relationships between metrical diversity and sentiment variability across the two poets.

## Key Results
- GPT-4o demonstrates the strongest zero-shot correlation with human sentiment annotations (Quadratic Weighted Kappa ~0.60, accuracy 33%), outperforming BERT-based models.
- Rumi's poems exhibit significantly more positive average sentiment scores compared to works by Parvin E'tesami.
- Pars-BERT, fine-tuned on modern Farsi, underperforms relative to multilingual BERT, indicating a domain shift challenge when applied to archaic poetic language.
- Entropy analysis reveals Rumi employs a more diverse range of meters to express varied sentiments, while Parvin E'tesami's work shows more consistent emotional tones and metrical patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The zero-shot approach leverages pre-trained LLMs' inherent knowledge of language and sentiment without requiring costly poet-specific fine-tuning. GPT-4o's superior performance may stem from its larger scale and more robust handling of contextual nuance, which is critical for interpreting metaphorical and culturally embedded expressions in classical poetry. The use of meter entropy as a proxy for structural diversity allows for quantifiable links between formal poetic elements and emotional content, tapping into LLMs' ability to recognize patterns across textual features.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Poems -> Text Normalization -> Model Inference (BERT/GPT) -> Sentiment Score Generation -> Entropy Calculation (Meter Detection) -> Statistical Comparison.

Critical path: Raw poems must be normalized (handling orthographic variants) before model inference; sentiment scores then feed into correlation with meter entropy.

Design tradeoffs: Zero-shot evaluation avoids fine-tuning costs but sacrifices potential accuracy gains; using a coarse 1–5 scale may lose emotional granularity; focusing on two poets limits breadth but allows deeper comparative analysis.

Failure signatures: Low accuracy (~33%) suggests models struggle with archaic metaphors and cultural references; Pars-BERT's poor performance indicates domain shift; high entropy in Rumi's meters may reflect genuine diversity or noise from inconsistent meter detection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions; however, the discussion implies several: How can LLMs be better adapted to archaic poetic language? Do meter-sentiment relationships hold across more poets and eras? Would finer-grained emotion labels improve analysis? These remain unaddressed and warrant further exploration.

## Limitations
- Corpus limited to two poets (Rumi and Parvin E'tesami), hindering generalizability across Persian poetic traditions.
- 1–5 sentiment scale may oversimplify nuanced affect; human annotation consistency (inter-rater reliability) is not reported.
- Zero-shot evaluation likely underestimates potential gains from domain-specific fine-tuning; no statistical tests applied to meter-sentiment correlations.

## Confidence
- GPT-4o outperforms BERT variants in zero-shot alignment: **Medium** (moderate κ, single dataset).
- Pars-BERT underperforms vs. multilingual models due to domain shift: **Medium** (consistent with literature but unreplicated).
- Rumi's poems are significantly happier than Parvin E'tesami's: **Low** (small n, no control for genre/theme).
- Meter diversity (entropy) correlates with sentiment variability: **Low** (descriptive, no inferential stats).

## Next Checks
