# Fine-tuning Small Language Models as Efficient Enterprise Search Relevance Labelers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03211
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03211
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Distilled SLM (Phi-3.5 Mini) matches/exceeds GPT-4o on human benchmark (NDCG 0.953 vs 0.944, Pairwise Accuracy 63.81% vs 62.58%) while improving throughput by 17× and cost-effectiveness by 19×.

## Executive Summary
Enterprise search relevance labeling suffers from a lack of high-quality, domain-specific training data due to privacy and cost constraints. This paper proposes a synthetic data pipeline that generates queries from enterprise documents, mines hard negatives using BM25, and labels pairs with a teacher LLM (GPT-4o). The resulting dataset is used to fine-tune a small language model (Phi-3.5 Mini) via multi-task and instruction tuning. On a human-annotated benchmark of 923 query-document pairs, the distilled model achieves statistically non-inferior performance to GPT-4o, with dramatic gains in inference speed and cost. The work demonstrates that specialized SLMs can replace large LLMs for scalable, accurate relevance labeling in enterprise settings.

## Method Summary
The authors first create a seed corpus of 1,500 internal enterprise documents with metadata. Using GPT-4o, they generate three diverse queries per document based on sampled query templates derived from search traffic. For each query, they retrieve four hard negative documents via BM25 from the full corpus. A teacher LLM (GPT-4o) then assigns graded relevance scores (0–4) to each query-document pair. This synthetic triplet dataset is combined with public IR datasets (INTERS, TREC-CAsT, MS MARCO) and used to fine-tune Phi-3.5 Mini Instruct in two stages: multi-task tuning on the public datasets, followed by instruction tuning on the combined data. The final model is evaluated against a human-annotated enterprise benchmark using NDCG and pairwise accuracy.

## Key Results
- The fine-tuned SLM attains NDCG 0.953 and pairwise accuracy 63.81%, matching or surpassing GPT-4o (NDCG 0.944, accuracy 62.58%).
- Inference throughput increases by a factor of 17 and cost-effectiveness improves by 19× relative to the teacher LLM.
- Ablation studies show that multi-task tuning with heterogeneous datasets yields a measurable NDCG improvement (0.946 → 0.953).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: High-Quality Synthetic Data Generation
The pipeline produces domain-specific training data by generating realistic enterprise queries from documents, mining lexical hard negatives via BM25, and labeling with GPT-4o. This exposes the SLM to nuanced distinctions (e.g., keyword vs. semantic matches, context-dependence) typical of enterprise search. The core assumption is that LLM-generated synthetic queries and labels align sufficiently with human judgment. Evidence includes the detailed pipeline description (Section 3.2) and the resulting high human benchmark scores. A break condition occurs if query generation fails to capture true enterprise ambiguity and metadata usage.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Task Transfer for Robustness
Incorporating heterogeneous datasets (INTERS, TREC-CAsT, MS MARCO) during fine-tuning acts as regularization, preventing overfitting to the synthetic enterprise data and encouraging a more fundamental understanding of relevance. The assumption is that relevance knowledge from web search and general IR is transferable. Evidence comes from ablation results (Table 4) showing an NDCG gain when multi-task tuning is added. Potential failure arises if auxiliary tasks are too domain-distant or introduce conflicting labeling criteria.

### Mechanism 3: Distillation into a Specialized SLM
Distilling GPT-4o’s judgments into Phi-3.5 Mini preserves labeling quality because the task (discrete relevance scoring) is well-defined and the synthetic data is high-quality and consistent. The SLM does not require the teacher’s broad knowledge; instead it learns an efficient mapping from query-document features to the teacher’s score distribution. Evidence includes direct performance comparisons, statistical non-inferiority tests, and large efficiency gains. Risks include teacher label inconsistency or biases, which would be inherited by the SLM.

## Foundational Learning
- **Synthetic Data Generation for Low-Resource Domains**  
  Why needed: No public enterprise search relevance datasets exist; human labeling is infeasible due to privacy, cost, and scale. Synthetic data is the only viable path to large, domain-specific training sets.  
  Quick check: Why is it insufficient to simply use a generic LLM prompt for labeling without first creating synthetic queries and mining hard negatives?

- **Hard Negative Mining**  
  Why needed: Enterprise search contains many partially relevant documents; a model trained only on clear positives/negatives cannot learn fine-grained distinctions. BM25 provides lexical hard negatives that simulate a real retrieval system’s output, teaching discriminative judgment.  
  Quick check: How would the model’s performance differ if negatives were randomly sampled from the corpus instead of retrieved by BM25?

- **Knowledge Distillation & Model Specialization**  
  Why needed: The goal is task-specific, high-volume labeling, not general reasoning. A large LLM is prohibitively slow and expensive. Distillation transfers the specific judgment capability into a compact model optimized for this single function, achieving necessary throughput.  
  Quick check: What are the risks of distilling from a teacher model that might have systematic biases (e.g., always preferring longer documents)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Seed Documents -> Synthetic Query Generator -> (Query, Doc) Pairs -> BM25 Negative Miner -> (Query, Candidate Doc) Pairs -> Teacher Labeler -> Labeled Triplets + Public Data -> SLM Trainer (Multi-task Tuning + Instruct Tuning) -> Fine-tuned SLM -> Evaluation Harness

**Critical path:** The sequence from seed documents to the final fine-tuned SLM as shown above, emphasizing the data generation and training stages.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Query Diversity vs. Realism: The revision prompt increases diversity but risks unnatural queries; the paper enforces strict rules to mitigate.  
- Data Size vs. Quality: Ablation shows minimal gain beyond 14K samples, indicating quality (query refinement) matters more than sheer volume.  
- Multi-task Benefit vs. Noise: Adding web search data improves robustness but assumes transferability; if enterprise queries are highly unique, this may introduce noise.  
- BM25 k-value: k=4 balances label distribution and labeling cost; higher k retrieves more negatives but increases labeling burden.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. High SLM-Vanilla, Low SLM-Human: Overfitting to synthetic patterns; check query diversity and human logic alignment.  
2. Imbalanced Relevance Distribution: Most scores are extreme; indicates BM25 not challenging or teacher LLM mis-calibrated.  
3. Poor Throughput Scaling: Lower-than-expected RPM; check sequence length, batch size, gradient accumulation.  
4. Query Generator Bias: Generated queries only plausible/positive; would cripple negative mining (addressed by switching to GPT-4o pipeline).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Pipeline Sanity Check:** Run the full synthetic pipeline on a small manually reviewed subset of 50 seed documents. Inspect query diversity/realism, BM25 negatives, and teacher label reasonableness.  
2. **Base SLM Capability Probe:** Evaluate vanilla Phi-3.5 Mini Instruct on the 923-pair human benchmark using the teacher’s prompt. Expect poor performance (NDCG ~0.82, Accuracy ~42%) to establish baseline.  
3. **Throughput-Cost Benchmark:** On target hardware (e.g., single A100), run teacher LLM (GPT-4o) and fine-tuned SLM on a fixed batch of 1000 pairs. Measure RPM and estimate cost; verify 17×/19× improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions, but its limitations and discussion suggest several directions for future research:
- Generalizability beyond proprietary enterprise data: Can the synthetic pipeline work with public documents or domains without internal metadata?
- Teacher model bias propagation: How do systematic biases in GPT-4o's relevance judgments affect the distilled SLM, and can they be mitigated?
- Multi-task transfer boundary: At what point does incorporating heterogeneous IR datasets cease to improve robustness and instead introduce noise for highly specialized enterprise tasks?
These questions emerge from the paper's own identified constraints and could shape subsequent work in this area.

## Limitations
- Proprietary data dependency: The pipeline relies on internal enterprise corpus and a secret query pattern table, hindering external reproducibility and limiting generalizability.
- Teacher LLM alignment: All training supervision comes from GPT-4o; any systematic biases or misalignment with human judgment in enterprise contexts will be inherited by the SLM.
- Multi-task transfer risk: Gains from web-search datasets assume transferable relevance concepts; if enterprise needs are highly idiosyncratic, auxiliary tasks may introduce noise.

- Multi-Task Tuning Benefit: High
- Distillation & Efficiency Gains: High
