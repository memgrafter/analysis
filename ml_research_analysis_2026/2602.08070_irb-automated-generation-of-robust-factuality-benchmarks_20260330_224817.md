# IRB: Automated Generation of Robust Factuality Benchmarks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08070
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08070
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: IRB1K benchmark shows retrieval improves RAG correctness, reasoning models are more reliable, and enhancing retrieval is more cost-effective than scaling generators.

## Executive Summary
IRB is an automated framework for generating robust factuality benchmarks to evaluate Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. It leverages structured generation with factual scaffolds derived from Wikipedia citations and algorithmic scaffolds based on knowledge graphs to produce controllable question-answer pairs. The resulting benchmark, IRB1K, comprising 1,000 questions, reveals that retrieval consistently improves correctness across diverse language models, reasoning-oriented models demonstrate higher reliability, and improving the retrieval component offers a more cost-effective path to higher RAG performance than scaling the generator. This work provides a scalable approach to benchmarking factuality in RAG and offers insights into the relative importance of retrieval versus generation.

## Method Summary
IRB constructs benchmarks through a two-scaffold structured generation pipeline. The factual scaffold extracts citing sentences from Wikipedia, decomposes them into keypoints, and verifies groundedness against the original source documents to ensure verifiability. The algorithmic scaffold transforms these validated facts into knowledge graphs, then systematically masks nodes to generate single-hop, multi-hop, and false-premise questions with controlled reasoning complexity. The pipeline produces QA pairs that are assembled into a benchmark with metadata for evaluation. The framework evaluates RAG systems using nDCG@5 for retrieval performance and an LLM-based metric for answer correctness. A minimum viable reproduction involves: extracting and validating citing sentences, performing groundedness checks, generating knowledge graphs, creating question variants, and running the evaluation pipeline.

## Key Results
- Retrieval significantly improves RAG system correctness across multiple large language models, acting as an "equalizer" that aligns their performance.
- Reasoning models (e.g., o1) exhibit higher reliability and robustness compared to non-reasoning models.
- Enhancing the retrieval component is more cost-effective than scaling the generator for improving overall RAG correctness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: IRB's structured generation pipeline improves benchmark robustness by grounding QA pairs in human-verified factual scaffolds.
- Mechanism: The pipeline extracts citing sentences from Wikipedia, splits them into keypoints, and validates that each keypoint is supported by its source document. This ensures that the generated questions and answers are factually accurate and traceable to verifiable evidence.
- Core assumption: Human-written citing sentences reliably contain factual claims that can be independently verified in the cited documents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Abstract: IRB employs a structured generation pipeline utilizing factual scaffold and algorithmic scaffold.
  - Section: The authors describe the factual scaffold extraction, keypoint decontextualization, and groundedness verification steps.
  - Corpus: The surrounding work provides context but no direct evidence; the validation rests on the pipeline's design.
- Break condition: If citing sentences are ambiguous, contain errors, or if source documents are unavailable or altered, the factual scaffold fails, compromising benchmark reliability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The algorithmic scaffold controls question complexity through knowledge graph masking and transformation.
- Mechanism: A knowledge graph is constructed from each validated fact. Nodes are systematically masked to create different reasoning hops; the masked graph is then transformed into natural language questions and answers. This allows predictable generation of single-hop, multi-hop, and false-premise question types.
- Core assumption: Knowledge graphs extracted from text can be reliably manipulated to produce questions with the desired reasoning characteristics without loss of answerability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Abstract: IRB uses an algorithmic scaffold and transforms source text into an intermediate knowledge graph.
  - Section: The paper details the knowledge graph-based question generation algorithm and its controllability.
  - Corpus: The related work discusses knowledge graph applications but does not directly validate the method.
- Break condition: If knowledge graph extraction misses relevant information or the masking creates ambiguous or unanswerable questions, the generated QA quality degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Providing retrieved documents as context to LLMs improves answer correctness by grounding responses in verifiable evidence.
- Mechanism: In RAG evaluation, the retriever supplies relevant documents, allowing the generator to base its answer on retrieved facts rather than parametric memory. IRB results show that retrieval consistently boosts correctness across models.
- Core assumption: The retrieved documents contain the necessary factual information and the generator can effectively integrate this context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Abstract: IRB poses a significant challenge for closed-book models and shows retrieval improves correctness; improving retrieval is more cost-effective.
  - Section: The analysis demonstrates retrieval acts as an "equalizer" and that better retrievers yield larger gains than larger generators.
  - Corpus: Related work supports the value of retrieval but does not provide the specific empirical evidence from IRB.
- Break condition: If the retriever fails to return relevant documents or the generator cannot utilize the context effectively, the performance benefit vanishes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge graph construction from unstructured text**  
  Why needed here: The algorithmic scaffold requires converting validated facts into structured representations that can be masked and transformed into questions.  
  Quick check: Given the sentence "Cristiano Ronaldo scored a penalty in a 3-2 home win," what are the head, relation, and tail in the knowledge graph? (Expected: head=Cristiano Ronaldo, relation=scored, tail=penalty; additional context may be included.)

- **Information retrieval and relevance ranking**  
  Why needed here: IRB evaluates retriever performance, requiring understanding of how retriever systems rank documents by relevance to a query.  
  Quick check: If a retriever returns 5 documents and the gold document is ranked 3rd, what is its rank position and how does this affect nDCG@5? (Rank position 3; nDCG@5 would discount the gain based on position.)

- **Evaluation metrics for RAG systems**  
  Why needed here: IRB uses nDCG@5 for retrieval and an LLM-based metric for answer correctness; understanding these metrics is essential for interpreting results.  
  Quick check: What is the difference between precision@k and nDCG@k, and when would you use each for RAG evaluation? (Precision@k measures the proportion of relevant documents in top k; nDCG@k accounts for graded relevance and ranking positions, useful when relevance degrees vary.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Wikipedia corpus → Citing sentence extraction → Keypoint decontextualization → Groundedness verification → Knowledge graph construction → Question generation → Benchmark assembly → Evaluation pipeline  
Supporting components: Retriever (e.g., text-embedding-3-small, BM25), Generators (LLMs), Evaluation LLM.

### Critical Path
1. Extract and validate factual scaffolds from Wikipedia.
2. Generate knowledge graphs from validated facts.
3. Transform knowledge graphs into question variants (single-hop, multi-hop, false-premise).
4. Generate natural language questions and answers.
5. Assemble benchmark with metadata and metadata (e.g., complexity labels).
6. Evaluate retrievers and generators using the benchmark.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Wikipedia dependency vs. domain specificity:** IRB leverages Wikipedia's scale and reliability at the cost of applicability to specialized domains.
- **Structured generation vs. flexibility:** The algorithmic scaffold provides precise control over question complexity but may limit the diversity of question styles.
- **Cost vs. quality:** Higher scaffold quality (e.g., more rigorous groundedness checks) increases computational costs due to LLM usage.

### Failure Signatures
- Low groundedness rates indicate insufficient source document availability or quality.
- High question answerability failures point to problems in knowledge graph extraction or masking logic.
- Uniformly low nDCG scores across all retrievers suggest the benchmark is too difficult or retrieval setup is flawed.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Run groundedness verification on 100 random Wikipedia citing sentences to establish baseline validation throughput.
2. Perform knowledge graph extraction on 50 facts to verify transformation accuracy and identify common failure modes.
3. Generate 20 questions from successful knowledge graphs to assess question quality, answerability, and complexity alignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal number of retrieval contexts (k) to maximize RAG system performance while minimizing noise and computational costs?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the discussion on trade-offs between increasing retrieval contexts and system performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies a trade-off but does not provide concrete guidance on the optimal k value.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing system performance, noise levels, and computational costs across different k values (e.g., k=5, 10, 15).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does IRB framework performance vary when applied to domains outside Wikipedia, such as scientific literature or legal case law?
- Basis in paper: Explicitly mentioned in the Limitations section; IRB is restricted to Wikipedia and would require substantial redesign for other domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the limitation but does not explore or test adaptation to other domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies applying IRB to different knowledge sources and measuring benchmark quality and generation performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the long-term effects of using reasoning models versus non-reasoning models in RAG systems as the technology continues to evolve?
- Basis in paper: Explicitly concluded that reasoning models are more reliable and should be prioritized based on current evidence.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides current evidence but does not explore how this balance might shift as models improve.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking model performance, cost-effectiveness, and reliability over time.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can the IRB framework be extended to evaluate long-form text generation capabilities while maintaining its core strengths?
- Basis in paper: Explicitly stated in Limitations; IRB is designed for short answers and cannot evaluate long-form generation.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this limitation but does not propose solutions for extending to long-form evaluation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Prototype implementations demonstrating IRB adaptation for long-form evaluation with maintained quality metrics.

## Limitations
- Factual scaffold robustness: The reliance on Wikipedia citing sentences may be brittle when citations change or source documents are removed, and the pipeline lacks stress-testing against such source volatility.
- Algorithmic scaffold control: Knowledge graph masking may generate questions with unanswerable premises or ambiguous reasoning steps; the paper does not sufficiently ablate failure modes.
- Generalizability: IRB is currently limited to Wikipedia; extending it to other domains would require substantial redesign, and its effectiveness outside this domain remains untested.

## Confidence
- Factual scaffold robustness: Medium
- Algorithmic scaffold control: Medium
- Retrieval as equalizer: High

## Next Checks
1. **Source dependency test:** Re-run IRB’s groundedness verification on a time-shifted Wikipedia dump (e.g., 2023 vs. 2024) to measure scaffold brittleness when citations change or documents are removed.
2. **Graph masking fidelity:** Manually audit 100 generated multi-hop questions to quantify the gap between intended and actual reasoning hops, using human annotators to label hop count and ambiguity.
3. **LLM-evaluator calibration:** Compare IRB’s LLM-based correctness scoring against 500 human judgments on the same QA pairs to measure prompt sensitivity and scorer bias.
