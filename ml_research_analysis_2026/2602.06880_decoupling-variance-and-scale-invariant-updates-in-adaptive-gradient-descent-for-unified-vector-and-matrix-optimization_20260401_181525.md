# Decoupling Variance and Scale-Invariant Updates in Adaptive Gradient Descent for Unified Vector and Matrix Optimization

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.06880
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06880
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** DeVAS8, the matrix-adapted algorithm, achieves lower validation loss than Muon and SOAP on NanoGPT (274M) and ViT-L/16, cutting token usage by approximately 6.6% on language modeling.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Decoupled Variance Adaptation (DeVA), a framework that reformulates adaptive gradient updates by separating them into a variance adaptation component and a scale-invariant directional component. This decoupling unifies the treatment of vector parameters (yielding DeVAℓ8, which recovers Adam) and matrix parameters (yielding DeVAS8, an adaptive spectral descent method). For matrix parameters, DeVAS8 efficiently computes a spectral adaptation via Kronecker-factored covariance estimates and a rotated gradient space. Theoretically, the variance adaptation reduces the effective blockwise smoothness constant, leading to faster convergence rates. Empirically, DeVAS8 demonstrates improved token efficiency on large-scale language modeling (NanoGPT) and faster initial convergence on vision tasks (ViT) compared to prior matrix optimizers like Muon and SOAP.

## Method Summary
DeVA starts from the AdaGrad update `Δx = -E[H]^{-1/2} g`, where `H` is the gradient covariance. It algebraically decomposes the preconditioner as `E[H]^{-1/2} H^{1/2} · H^{-1/2} g`. The first factor is the "decoupled variance adaptation" (SNR-like), and the second is a "scale-invariant update" (sign-based). For scalar/vector parameters (`H` diagonal), this yields an update proportional to `(E[g^2] / g^2)^{-1/2} * sign(g)` (DeVAℓ8). For matrix parameters, assuming `H` factorizes as a Kronecker product `L^{1/2} ⊗ R^{1/2}`, the method leverages the eigendecompositions of `L = GG^T` and `R = G^TG`. This defines a rotated gradient `G¹ = Q_L^T G Q_R`. The update becomes `ΔX = -Q_L (Ê^{-1/2} ⊙ msign(G¹)) Q_R^T`, where `Ê` is an element-wise matrix approximating `E[σ_i σ_j] / (σ_i σ_j)` estimated efficiently via EMAs of row/column norms in the rotated space. A RMS alignment factor `(0.2 / √(max(n,m)))` is applied to match the scale of Adam-style updates.

## Key Results
*   DeVAS8 achieves lower validation loss than Muon, SOAP, and Adamuon when training a 274M NanoGPT model on FineWeb-Edu, improving token efficiency by ~6.6%.
*   On ImageNet-1K with ViT-L/16, DeVAS8 shows faster initial convergence compared to other matrix-based optimizers.
*   Theoretical analysis shows the decoupled variance adaptation reduces the effective blockwise smoothness term in the convergence bound, providing faster rates under non-smooth (heterogeneous) conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Decoupling Variance and Scale-Invariant Updates
**Claim:** Adaptive gradient updates can be reformulated as a product of a variance adaptation factor (based on second-order moments) and a scale-invariant direction (e.g., sign or matrix-sign). This unifies vector and matrix adaptive updates.
**Mechanism:** The AdaGrad preconditioner `E[H]^{-1/2}` is decomposed into `E[H]^{-1/2} H^{1/2}` (variance adaptation) multiplied by `H^{-1/2} g` (scale-invariant direction). For vectors (`H = diag(gg^T)`), this yields `γ_t ∝ (E[g^2] / g^2)^{-1/2}` times `sign(g)` (DeVAℓ8). For matrices (`H = L^{1/2} ⊗ R^{1/2}`), it yields a spectral adaptation matrix times `msign(G)` (DeVAS8).
**Break Condition:** The decoupling assumes the gradient covariance `H` is well-approximated by its expectation and that eigenbases are stable (local stationarity). Rapidly shifting gradient directions (non-stationarity) can misalign the separate variance and direction tracking.

### Mechanism 2: Spectral Adaptation via Kronecker-Factored Covariance
**Claim:** For matrix parameters, the decoupled variance adaptation is computed efficiently in a rotated spectral space, avoiding explicit large Kronecker matrices.
**Mechanism:** The left/right covariances `L=GG^T` and `R=G^TG` are eigendecomposed (`L=Q_L Λ_L Q_L^T`, `R=Q_R Λ_R Q_R^T`). The gradient is rotated: `G¹ = Q_L^T G Q_R`. The singular values of `G` map to `σ_i = √λ_i`, `σ_j = √μ_j`. The variance adaptation term `E[σ_i σ_j] / (σ_i σ_j)` is estimated as a matrix `Ê` in this space via EMAs of row/column squared norms of `G¹`, enabling an element-wise update `ΔX = -Q_L (Ê^{-1/2} ⊙ msign(G¹)) Q_R^T`.
**Break Condition:** Requires stable eigenbases `Q_L`, `Q_R` and a separable covariance (`E[L⊗R] ≈ E[L]⊗E[R]`). Rapid eigenvector change between decompositions or non-separable true covariance degrades performance.

### Mechanism 3: Variance Adaptation Reduces Blockwise Smoothness
**Claim:** The variance adaptation term (`γ_t` or `Γ_t`) reduces the influence of high-curvature directions in the convergence bound, leading to faster rates under blockwise smoothness.
**Mechanism:** In the descent inequality, a term like `η^2/2 * L * γ^2` (vectors) or `η^2 r/2 * ||√L||_Γ,F^2` (matrices) appears. Since `γ` or `Γ` are inversely related to local curvature estimates, they become small in high-curvature blocks/directions, effectively "flattening" the smoothness landscape.
**Break Condition:** Most beneficial when smoothness is heterogeneous (different `Lv,i` or `L_m`). Provides no benefit and adds overhead if smoothness is homogeneous. Analysis also assumes fixed mini-batch size `n_t = T`, which may not hold with smaller batches.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Steepest Descent under Weighted Norms**
    *   **Why needed:** The framework relies on solving `argmin_{||Δ||_M ≤ 1} <∇f, Δ> = -M^{-1}∇f`. Understanding how norms (ℓ₈, S₈) lead to sign-based updates explains why `H^{-1/2}g` yields `sign(g)` or `msign(G)`.
    *   **Quick check:** Given a Mahalanobis norm `||x||_M = sqrt(x^T M x)`, what is the update `Δx` that minimizes `<∇f, Δx>` subject to `||Δx||_M ≤ 1`? How does this simplify if `M` is diagonal vs. a Kronecker product?
*   **Kronecker Product Approximation for Matrices**
    *   **Why needed:** The full gradient covariance `E[vec(G) vec(G)^T]` is `(nm)²`-sized. Kronecker factorization (`L ⊗ R`) reduces it to `O(n² + m²)` and enables efficient updates like `ΔX = -L^{-1/4} G R^{-1/4}`.
    *   **Quick check:** How does `(L^{1/2} ⊗ R^{1/2}) vec(G)` relate to `L^{1/4} G R^{-1/4}`? What identity connects Kronecker products to matrix multiplication on `vec(X)`?
*   **Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for Stochastic Estimates**
    *   **Why needed:** True expectations `E[·]` are unavailable. EMA (`m_t = β m_{t-1} + (1-β) g_t`) provides a low-variance online estimate for gradient mean, second moments, and covariance factors.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the EMA of `g_t^2` approximate `E[g^2]`? What is the effect of the decay factor `β` on the memory and responsiveness of this estimate?

## Architecture Onboarding
Gradient -> Covariance accumulation (L_t, R_t) -> Periodic eigendecomposition (Q_L, Q_R) -> Rotate gradient (G¹) -> First moment (M_t) -> Second moment (r_t, c_t from M_t) -> Build variance adaptation Γ_t -> Combine (Γ_t ⊙ msign(M_t)) -> Apply RMS alignment -> Parameter update.

**Critical Path:** The sequence from gradient computation to the final update. Covariance factors (`L_t`, `R_t`) are updated every step via EMA. Eigendecompositions occur every `freq` steps to refresh the spectral rotation. The first moment (`M_t`) and second-moment statistics (`r_t`, `c_t`, `V_r`, `V_c`) are computed in the rotated space. The final direction `D_t` combines the variance adaptation `Γ_t` with the stabilized matrix sign `msign(M_t)` and a fixed RMS scaling.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Eigendecomposition frequency (`freq`):** Higher frequency maintains spectral accuracy but increases compute cost; lower frequency amortizes cost but risks stale rotation. empirically robust to large intervals.
*   **Memory for second moments:** Naive `V_t` matrix is `O(nm)`. Memory-efficient variants use separate row/column EMAs (`O(n+m)`) with minor performance impact.
*   **Sign-stabilization in `msign`:** Adding a small positive regularization inside `msign` stabilizes training for vision tasks but may harm language modeling performance.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Exploding updates:** If `Γ_t` becomes too large due to near-zero variance estimates, steps diverge. Ensure sufficient `ε`.
*   **Stagnation:** If `freq` is too low, `Q_L`, `Q_R` become stale, misaligning `msign(G¹)`. Monitor `||G¹_t||_F` or validation loss.
*   **Memory overflow:** Full `V_t` matrix is `O(nm)`; for large layers (`n,m > 4096`), use separated row/column accumulation.
*   **Shape mismatches:** Incorrect handling of `r_t` (length `n`) and `c_t` (length `m`) dimensions in the outer product for `Γ_t`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Under what precise conditions does the variance adaptation term `Γ_t` optimally reduce the effective blockwise smoothness constant? The bound improvement is shown but the exact geometric conditions (e.g., on gradient covariance spectrum) for maximal benefit are not characterized.
*   **Open Question 2:** Why does the "sign-stabilization" trick improve ViT performance but degrade NanoGPT performance? The paper notes the discrepancy but does not hypothesize whether it stems from Hessian heterogeneity, noise statistics, or activation function differences.
*   **Open Question 3:** Does the DeVA framework theoretically extend to other matrix preconditioners like K-FAC or ASGO, which use different curvature approximations (e.g., block-diagonal vs. Kronecker), and what are the new convergence implications?
*   **Open Question 4:** What is the exact computational trade-off and approximation error of using the rotated gradient `G¹_t` and its EMA to estimate `E[σ_i σ_j]` versus using the full, explicit expectation of the gradient covariance?
*   **Open Question 5:** Is DeVAS8's superiority primarily due to decoupled variance adaptation or the specific RMS alignment scaling (`0.2/√(max(n,m))`)? The comparison is confounded as both Muon and DeVAS8 use this scaling, while SOAP does not.

## Limitations
*   **Implementation ambiguity:** Exact details of the `msign` sign-stabilization and the precise second-moment accumulation variant (instantaneous `G¹_t` vs. EMA `M_t`) used in main results are not fully specified in the text; reliance on official code is necessary.
*   **Code versioning:** The "May 27 snapshot" of `modded-nanogpt` lacks a specific commit hash, risking architectural drift affecting reproducibility.
*   **Theoretical assumptions:** Convergence analysis assumes stable eigenbases (local stationarity), homogeneous mini-batch size (`n_t = T`), and blockwise smoothness. These may not hold in early training or for non-stationary data.
