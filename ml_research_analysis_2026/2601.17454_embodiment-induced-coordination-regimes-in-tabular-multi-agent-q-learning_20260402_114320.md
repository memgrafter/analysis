# Embodiment-Induced Coordination Regimes in Tabular Multi-Agent Q-Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17454  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17454  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** Centralized value learning failed to provide consistent performance benefits; independent learning (IQL-IQL) significantly outperformed centralized learning (CQL-CQL) in a predator-prey gridworld with speed and stamina constraints, achieving shorter episodes and higher predator rewards across kinematic regimes.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how embodiment constraints—specifically speed and stamina limits—affect the trade-off between centralized and independent multi-agent reinforcement learning. Using a tabular predator-prey gridworld, the authors compare fully independent Q-learning (IQL) against centralized Q-learning (CQL) under various kinematic regimes. Results show that centralized coordination often harms performance: IQL agents exploit speed advantages more effectively, while centralized policies suffer from reduced flexibility. Mixed learning configurations (e.g., IQL predators with CQL prey) induce persistent coordination breakdowns rather than transient instability. The work isolates coordination structure as the primary driver, demonstrating that increased coordination can become a liability under embodied constraints.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates Independent Q-Learning (IQL) and Centralized Q-Learning (CQL) in an 8×8 gridworld with 2 predators and 2 prey. Each agent has a stamina budget (5 units) and a speed mode (base: 1 cell/timestep costing 1 stamina; fast: 2 cells/timestep costing 2 stamina). The state includes positions, stamina, speed, and team. A potential-based reward shaping function uses distances to opponents. Training uses tabular Q-learning (α=0.25, γ=0.90) with ϵ-greedy exploration decaying from 1.0 to 0.1 over 23,000 episodes, for 40,000 episodes. Execution is decentralized: CQL agents marginalize the joint Q-table. Three kinematic regimes are tested: equal base speed, predators double speed, and prey double speed. Metrics (episode length, rewards) are averaged over the final 10,000 episodes, with statistical comparisons via Wilcoxon signed-rank and Cliff’s δ.

## Key Results
- IQL–IQL consistently achieved shorter episode lengths and higher predator rewards than CQL–CQL across all regimes (e.g., base speed: 24.9 vs 48.6 episodes; predator reward -32.0 vs -159.3).
- Mixed configurations (IQL–CQL or CQL–IQL) caused severe, persistent performance degradation, with episode lengths often doubling and predator rewards collapsing.
- Decentralized predators exploited speed advantages more effectively (episode length 17.5 under predator speed advantage vs 40.2 for CQL), while prey benefited from predator discoordination.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Embodiment constraints (speed and stamina) disrupt the assumed benefits of centralized coordination by making rigid joint-action coupling a liability under kinematic pressure.
- **Mechanism:** Centralized Q-learning (CQL) optimizes a joint-action value function `Q_total(s, a)`, forcing all agents toward globally consistent policies. Under speed/stamina limits, this reduces strategic flexibility: agents cannot independently adjust movement timing to exploit momentary opportunities (e.g., a predator using double-speed bursts), as the joint policy must satisfy all agents' constraints simultaneously. Decentralized IQL allows each agent to adapt its `Q_i(s_i, a_i)` locally, preserving responsiveness to transient evasion or pursuit windows.
- **Core assumption:** Coordination structure (centralized vs. independent) interacts with action-timing constraints; rigid global policy extraction cannot flexibly accommodate heterogeneous speed/stamina trade-offs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "increased coordination can become a liability under embodiment constraints"
  - [section 5.1–5.3]: IQL–IQL consistently achieves shorter episode lengths (e.g., 24.9 vs 48.6 in base speed) and higher predator rewards (e.g., -32.0 vs -159.3) than CQL–CQL.
  - [corpus]: Corpus signals show related work on coordination emergence but do not directly address tabular embodiment constraints; evidence for this specific mechanism is primarily from the paper's controlled experiments.
- **Break condition:** When embodiment constraints are minimized (e.g., unlimited stamina, identical speeds), centralized coordination may not disrupt flexibility; the paper's equal-speed base regime still shows IQL advantage, suggesting the break condition may require complete removal of kinematic trade-offs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Asymmetric learning structures (e.g., IQL predators with CQL prey) induce persistent coordination breakdowns rather than transient instability, because mismatched credit assignment and policy extraction create irreconcilable response patterns.
- **Mechanism:** In mixed configurations, predators and prey use different value decomposition strategies. For example, IQL–CQL (decentralized predators, centralized prey) causes predators to treat prey as part of the environment (non-stationarity), while prey optimize joint capture avoidance. This leads to systematic miscoordination: predators overcommit to pursuit strategies that centralized prey collectively evade, creating stable but inefficient behavioral loops. The paper's tabular setting shows these are not learning transients but converged equilibria.
- **Core assumption:** Persistent failures arise from structural misalignment in value decomposition, not from optimization noise or partial observability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "asymmetric centralized–independent configurations induce persistent coordination breakdowns rather than transient learning instability"
  - [section 5.4]: Mixed configurations (especially IQL–CQL) show significantly longer episodes and higher prey rewards across regimes (p ≤ 0.00391, |δ| ≥ 0.8), with stable divergence in learning curves.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers study coordination in MARL but do not isolate asymmetric learning-structure failures under tabular embodiment; mechanism is primarily evidenced by paper's specific configuration comparisons.
- **Break condition:** When all agents use aligned learning structures (IQL–IQL or CQL–CQL), coordination either stabilizes efficiently (IQL–IQL) or fails uniformly (CQL–CQL), but not with persistent asymmetry-induced breakdowns.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The effectiveness of centralized learning is fundamentally role-dependent: decentralized predators exploit kinematic advantages more effectively, while prey benefit from predator coordination breakdowns.
- **Mechanism:** Predators, as pursuers, require rapid, localized decisions to intercept evasive prey—benefiting from IQL's adaptability. Centralized predator learning imposes joint-policy inertia that slows response to prey maneuvers. Prey, as evaders, succeed by exploiting any predator discoordination; thus prey performance improves when predators use IQL (causing discoordination) and declines when predators use CQL (tight coordination). This reverses expected advantages based on role.
- **Core assumption:** Asymmetric roles (pursuer vs. evader) have opposing incentives regarding coordination; centralized coordination helps pursuers only if it does not reduce adaptability, but always harms evaders by reducing predator discoordination opportunities.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Effectiveness was regime and role-dependent: decentralized predators exploited speed advantages more effectively, while prey benefited from predator coordination breakdowns."
  - [section 5.2–5.3]: Under predator speed advantage, IQL predators drastically outperform CQL predators (episode length 17.5 vs 40.2, predator reward 18.7 vs -101.4). Prey reward is highest when predators are IQL and prey are CQL (e.g., -81.6 in prey-speed regime).
  - [corpus]: Limited direct evidence; corpus shows related work on pursuit-evasion but not role-dependent learning structure interactions in tabular settings.
- **Break condition:** When kinematic asymmetry is removed (equal speeds), role-dependent effects persist but are less pronounced; the paper's base-speed regime still shows IQL advantage for predators, suggesting role-dependence is primary but modulated by speed regimes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Tabular Q-learning**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper uses exact tabular methods to isolate coordination structure from function approximation artifacts. Tabular representation ensures all agents have perfect knowledge of the joint state, removing representation learning as a confound.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does using tabular Q-learning (instead of deep Q-networks) allow the authors to attribute performance differences solely to coordination structure?
- **Concept: Independent vs. Centralized Q-Learning (IQL/CQL)**
  - *Why needed here:* These are the two learning structures being compared. IQL treats other agents as part of the environment (non-stationarity), while CQL optimizes joint actions (scalability issues but better credit assignment).
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference in how IQL and CQL update their value functions, and how does this affect credit assignment in multi-agent settings?
- **Concept: Embodiment Constraints (Speed/Stamina)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's core hypothesis is that speed and stamina limits interact with learning structure. Speed determines moves per timestep; stamina limits total moves, creating trade-offs that couple agent decisions temporally.
  - *Quick check question:* How does a stamina budget that depletes per movement create a strategic coupling between agents' action timing?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Environment:** 8×8 gridworld with obstacles, 2 predators, 2 prey. State includes positions, stamina, speed mode.
  - **Agents:** Each has speed (1 or 2 cells/timestep), stamina budget (5 units), action set {UP,DOWN,LEFT,RIGHT,STAY}.
  - **Learning modules:** IQL agents maintain individual `Q_i(s, a_i)` tables; CQL agent(s) maintain joint `Q_total(s, a)` table. Potential-based reward shaping uses distance-based potential `Φ(s)`.
  - **Execution:** Decentralized execution for all configs; CQL agents marginalize joint Q: `Q_marg(s, a_i) = E_{a_{-i}}[Q_total(s, a_i, a_{-i})]`.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize all Q-tables to zero.
  2. For each episode: reset agents, stamina.
  3. For each timestep: agents select actions (ϵ-greedy), execute micro-steps according to speed, update stamina, detect collisions/captures.
  4. Compute rewards (capture, step cost) and shaping bonus `F(s,s') = γΦ(s') - Φ(s)`.
  5. Update Q-tables: IQL uses `r_i' + γ max_{a_i'} Q_i(s',a_i')`; CQL uses summed `r_total' + γ max_a Q_total(s',a)`.
  6. After 40,000 episodes, average last 10,000 episodes' metrics (episode length, rewards).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Tabular vs. function approximation:* Tabular ensures exact value representation and no representational bias, but limits scalability. Tradeoff: interpretability and isolation of coordination effects vs. ecological validity.
  - *Full observability:* All agents and learners observe full joint state, removing partial observability confounds but making IQL non-stationarity purely due to learning dynamics.
  - *Speed/stamina parameterization:* Discrete speed modes (1x/2x) and fixed stamina create clear kinematic regimes but simplify continuous movement dynamics.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Mixed configurations (IQL–CQL or CQL–IQL):* Persistent high episode length variance, predator reward collapse (e.g., IQL–CQL base speed: episode length 113.2 vs IQL–IQL 24.9; predator reward -505.2 vs -32.0). Indicates stable discoordination.
  - *CQL–CQL under speed advantage:* Under predator speed advantage, CQL predators fail to exploit speed (episode length 40.2 vs IQL predators 17.5), showing centralized policy extraction inhibits rapid adaptation.
  - *Prey reward spikes in asymmetric predator learning:* When predators are IQL, prey rewards increase (e.g., -72.6 in IQL–CQL base speed vs -109.5 in CQL–CQL), signaling predator discoordination.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Base speed regime (equal speeds):** Run all 4 configs (IQL–IQL, IQL–CQL, CQL–IQL, CQL–CQL) to establish baseline coordination effects without kinematic asymmetry. Expect IQL–IQL shortest episodes, mixed configs longest.
  2. **Predator speed advantage:** Double predator speed only. Test if speed amplification helps IQL predators more (hypothesized). Expect IQL–IQL episode length to drop further, CQL–CQL to improve only slightly.
  3. **Prey speed advantage:** Double prey speed only. Test if prey mobility exacerbates mixed-configuration failures. Expect IQL–CQL episode length to increase dramatically, CQL–IQL to be less affected.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What specific mechanisms within the value function structure of CQL cause it to systematically underperform IQL under embodiment constraints, and why do these effects persist rather than resolve over time?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper states that "centralized learning fails to provide a consistent advantage" and that mixed configurations induce "persistent coordination breakdowns rather than transient learning instability." It isolates "coordination structure as the primary driver" but does not explain the *internal value-based mechanism* behind the failure.
- **Why unresolved:** The tabular setting provides exact Q-values, yet the analysis focuses on performance metrics (episode length, reward) without inspecting the learned Q-tables to contrast how centralized joint-action values misrepresent credit assignment or create destructive policy inertia compared to independent marginalization.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comparative analysis of the converged Q-tables for IQL and CQL, examining (a) differences in action-value distributions for states involving coordination, (b) stability of greedy policies during learning, and (c) whether CQL’s joint-action values overfit to specific partner policies that become brittle under embodiment constraints.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what precise conditions of speed-stamina parameterization does the advantage invert between IQL and CQL for each role, and can we derive a phase diagram predicting when increased coordination is beneficial versus detrimental?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper finds "effectiveness was regime and role-dependent" and that "increased coordination can become a liability under embodiment constraints." It tests three discrete speed regimes but does not interpolate or generalize the boundary.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments vary speed in discrete steps (base/double) for one role at a time. The continuous interaction space of speed ratios, stamina budgets, and grid sizes remains unexplored, preventing a formal characterization of the coordination liability threshold.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic parameter sweep across a continuous range of speed ratios (e.g., predator speed/prey speed = 0.5x to 2.0x) and stamina budgets, measuring performance reversals and fitting a model that predicts the coordination-benefit region as a function of kinematic constraints.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why do asymmetric learning configurations (e.g., IQL predators with CQL prey) induce *persistent* coordination failures, and is this due to fundamental misalignment in temporal discounting, policy extraction errors, or resonance with embodiment-induced constraints?
- **Paper text reference:** Mixed configurations are noted to cause "persistent coordination breakdowns rather than transient learning instability."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the phenomenon but does not diagnose its root cause. Possible factors include: (a) CQL’s decentralized execution via marginalization being incompatible with IQL’s non-stationary environment, (b) mismatched learning speeds, or (c) the stamina constraint locking agents into maladaptive rhythmic patterns that are stable but suboptimal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies that isolate policy extraction methods (e.g., comparing marginalization to other CTDE techniques), measure policy divergence over time between agents in mixed configurations, and track stamina usage patterns to see if persistent failures correlate with resource-lockstep dynamics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do the observed regime-dependent effects scale with population size (e.g., more predators or prey), or are they specific to the 2v2 asymmetric setup?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The study uses a fixed 2-predator, 2-prey setup. The limitations section notes "whether similar coordination pathologies arise under... larger agent populations" as future work. The interaction between coordination structure and embodiment may change with more agents due to increased joint-action space complexity or different scaling of speed/stamina constraints.
- **Why unresolved:** With more agents, the curse of dimensionality in CQL’s joint-action space grows, but so do opportunities for decentralized specialization. The paper’s finding that IQL exploits speed advantages may not hold if predator swarms require tighter synchronization.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the experimental design with varying numbers of predators (e.g., 1, 2, 4) and prey, keeping the state space manageable in tabular form (e.g., smaller grids), and measure whether the IQL advantage persists, diminishes, or reverses with increased population.

## Limitations
- **Potential function weights (`wij`):** Unspecified; these directly shape the reward landscape and could bias comparative performance.
- **Initial state distribution:** Not described; starting positions (e.g., clustered vs. dispersed) could fundamentally alter learning dynamics in a small gridworld.
- **Movement mechanics precision:** The "micro-stepping" for speed (e.g., can an agent change direction mid-timestep? Can it move through obstacles/other agents?) is ambiguous, creating implementation variability.

## Confidence
- **Core Finding (CQL underperformance):** High Confidence. Results are consistent across three kinematic regimes and multiple metrics, supported by controlled tabular experiments that isolate coordination structure.
- **Persistent Mixed-Config Failures:** Medium Confidence. Evidence is clear from the paper's comparisons, but the specific mechanism (structural misalignment vs. non-stationarity) is inferred; the tabular setting precludes deep credit assignment diagnostics.
- **Role-Dependent Effects:** Medium Confidence. Strong empirical patterns link predator/prey roles to performance inverses, but causal attribution to role *alone* (vs. speed/position confounds) is limited by the experimental design.
- **External Validity:** Low Confidence. The claim that "increased coordination can be a liability" is specific to this tabular embodiment; transfer to modern deep MARL with partial observability is highly uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce Base Regime with Known Seeds:** Obtain the exact `wij` coefficients and initial position distribution from the authors. Replicate the base-speed experiment with 10+ seeds. Success criterion: Replicate the reported IQL-IQL vs. CQL-CQL effect size (episode length ~24.9 vs ~48.6) with statistical significance (p<0.05, |δ|>0.8).
2. **Ablate Potential Shaping:** Run the base regime *without* any reward shaping. If the IQL advantage disappears or reverses, the core claim is contingent on the specific shaping function, not just embodiment.
3. **Controlled Speed Mechanics Test:** Implement two versions of double-speed movement: (a) single-direction multi-step, (b) two independent directional choices. Compare IQL-CQL performance under both. A large divergence would indicate the mechanical interpretation is a critical hidden variable.
