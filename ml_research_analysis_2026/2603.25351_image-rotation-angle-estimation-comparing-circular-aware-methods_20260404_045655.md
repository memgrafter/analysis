# Image Rotation Angle Estimation: Comparing Circular-Aware Methods
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25351
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25351
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Probabilistic circular Gaussian distribution (CGD) is the most robust method across 16 architectures; top configuration (CGD with MambaOut Base) reaches 1.24° MAE on DRC-D and achieves state-of-the-art transfer to COCO (3.71° MAE on COCO 2014, 2.84° on COCO 2017).

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates five circular-aware methods for predicting image rotation angles, addressing the fundamental discontinuity at the 0°/360° boundary. Using ImageNet-pretrained backbones and the DRC-D dataset, the study finds that probabilistic approaches—especially the circular Gaussian distribution (CGD)—provide the highest robustness across 16 diverse neural architectures. While fine-grained classification achieves the best single-configuration accuracy (1.23° MAE with EfficientViT-B3), it suffers from training instability on several backbones. The optimal CGD+MambaOut configuration not only excels on DRC-D (1.24° MAE) but also transfers strongly to COCO, setting new state-of-the-art results. The work highlights that method choice interacts critically with architectural inductive biases, and that smoothing the optimization landscape via probabilistic supervision is key for stable fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The task is to predict a continuous rotation angle θ∈[0°,360°) for an image, with synthetic rotations applied during both training and evaluation. Five circular-aware prediction heads are compared over 16 ImageNet-pretrained backbones (CNNs, ViTs, hybrids): Direct Angle (DA) with circular MAE loss, Classification (CLS, 360-bin cross-entropy), Unit Vector (UV, cos/sin → atan2), Phase-Shifting Coder (PSC, three cosine phases), and Circular Gaussian Distribution (CGD, 360-bin probabilistic output with KL divergence). Evaluation uses circular MAE, RMSE, and accuracy thresholds. Training uses AdamW, mixed precision, and early stopping without data augmentation. The best methods are transferred to COCO for broader validation.

## Key Results
- Probabilistic CGD shows highest robustness across all 16 backbones on DRC-D.
- Classification (CLS) achieves peak single-config accuracy (1.23° MAE with EfficientViT-B3) but exhibits high instability (failed runs) on several architectures.
- Direct regression (DA) fails consistently (MAE >7°) due to boundary gradient conflicts.
- Top configuration (CGD + MambaOut Base) reaches 1.24° MAE on DRC-D and, when transferred to COCO, attains SOTA: 3.71° MAE (COCO 2014) and 2.84° MAE (COCO 2017), substantially improving over prior work.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Probabilistic Smoothing via Circular Gaussian Distribution (CGD)
- **Claim:** CGD provides robust rotation estimation by modeling angular uncertainty and avoiding sharp, conflicting gradients at the 0°/360° boundary.
- **Mechanism:** The network outputs a probability distribution over 360 discretized bins. The target distribution is a Gaussian-shaped spread centered on the ground truth angle using circular distance. Training minimizes KL divergence between predicted and target distributions, smoothing the optimization landscape and preventing penalties for numerically distant but geometrically close predictions.
- **Core assumption:** Smooth, uncertainty-aware supervision stabilizes fine-tuning across diverse pretrained backbones, even when their initial features are poorly aligned with orientation cues.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Probabilistic methods, particularly the circular Gaussian distribution, demonstrate the highest robustness across all backbones."; [section 3.2.2] Describes CGD's target encoding and KL divergence loss.
- **Break condition:** If a backbone's pretrained features already encode strong orientation priors, the overhead of distribution modeling may not yield significant gains over simpler methods.

### Mechanism 2: Classification Compatibility with Classification-Oriented Backbones
- **Claim:** Classification (CLS) achieves peak accuracy on well-matched backbones because its discretization and cross-entropy loss are structurally compatible with ImageNet-pretrained classifiers.
- **Mechanism:** Angles are binned into 360 classes; the pretrained backbone's head is replaced with a linear classifier. Cross-entropy loss aligns with ImageNet's pretraining objective, allowing texture/shape features to be repurposed with minimal conflict.
- **Core assumption:** The backbone's feature hierarchy contains separable cues for orientation that are readily activated by a classification head.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Classification achieves the best single-configuration accuracy (1.23° MAE with EfficientViT-B3 on DRC-D)."; [section 6] "The classification objective is structurally identical to the ImageNet pretraining task... The predicted angle is decoded as the center of the highest-probability bin."
- **Break condition:** On backbones where pretrained features do not naturally surface orientation-relevant information early in fine-tuning, cross-entropy's aggressive probability concentration can trap optimization in poor local minima, causing training instability.

### Mechanism 3: Direct Regression Brittleness
- **Claim:** Direct angle regression with circular loss (DA) fails because smoothing the loss function alone cannot resolve fundamental gradient conflicts when predictions evolve across the circular boundary during training.
- **Mechanism:** The network predicts a single scalar angle. The circular MAE loss computes `min(|θ̂-θ|, 360°-|θ̂-θ|)`, which is non-differentiable at the boundary and produces conflicting gradient signs when a prediction's error is computed across the wrap-around point (e.g., 1° vs. 359°).
- **Core assumption:** A scalar output cannot represent the circular manifold without discontinuities in its gradient field, regardless of loss wrapping.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Direct-angle regression collapses across all backbones, confirming that circular loss alone does not remove boundary issues."; [section 3.1.1] discusses vulnerability to gradient conflicts; [section 5.1] DA shows highest MAE and instability.
- **Break condition:** This is presented as a confirmed failure mode; no break condition is suggested—it is fundamentally brittle.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Circular topology of angles.** 0° and 360° are identical but numerically distant. *Why needed:* Grasping all method designs requires understanding this discontinuity; without it, the problem seems trivial. *Quick check:* If true orientation is 1° and prediction is 359°, circular error is 2°, not 358°.
- **Concept: Transfer learning fine-tuning.** All experiments use ImageNet-pretrained backbones with replaced heads. *Why needed:* Success depends on how well the pretrained feature extractor can be redirected to orientation tasks. *Quick check:* Why might a larger, more accurate ImageNet model perform worse on rotation estimation? (Answer: Overfitting on small DRC-D, or stronger pretrained biases that are harder to override.)
- **Concept: Probabilistic vs. deterministic supervision.** Top methods differ: CLS outputs a point estimate via argmax; CGD outputs a distribution. *Why needed:* This explains their differing robustness profiles. *Quick check:* What is a key disadvantage of CGD's distribution output compared to CLS? (Answer: Increased output dimensionality and slightly more complex decoding, though it provides uncertainty estimates.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pretrained Backbone -> Task-Specific Head -> Circular Distance Metric (for evaluation).
- **Critical path:** For a new architecture, replicate the exact head structure from the paper's best-performing combination for that architecture family. Start with CGD for maximum robustness, or CLS if the backbone is classification-oriented (e.g., EfficientViT).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Accuracy vs. Robustness: CLS gives best peak accuracy on specific backbones but can fail to converge; CGD gives near-peak accuracy with guaranteed convergence.
  - Continuous vs. Discrete: Regression methods (DA, UV, PSC) provide continuous outputs but are less stable; classification methods (CLS, CGD) are discrete-in-training but decode continuously; CGD's probabilistic softening improves stability.
  - Computational cost: CGD and CLS have similar output sizes (360 logits vs. 360-bin distribution); regression heads (UV, PSC) are smaller (2–3 outputs) but may require more epochs.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - CLS: High variance across random seeds, or runs converging to ~90° MAE (random chance). Check training loss curve for plateauing before early stopping.
  - DA: MAE consistently above 10° regardless of backbone or epochs; loss may not decrease meaningfully.
  - UV/PSC: Instability on small backbones or when regularization weight (λ) is improperly tuned.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity Check:** Train CLS and CGD on the smallest backbone (ViT-Tiny) for 50 epochs. Expect CGD to converge reliably (~5–6° MAE on DRC-D) while CLS may show high variance.
  2. **Learning Rate Probe:** For CLS on an unstable backbone (e.g., MambaOut), try reducing the learning rate by 10× to mitigate early optimization traps.
  3. **Gradient Check:** For DA on any backbone, monitor gradient norms; expect conflicting signs near the circular boundary leading to stagnation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1  
- **Question:** Why does classification (CLS) exhibit high training instability on certain architectures like MambaOut and Swin Base despite identical ImageNet pretraining and training protocols?  
- **Basis in paper:** Table 1 shows CLS has high variance and multiple failed runs; the Analysis hypothesizes that cross-entropy loss may interact poorly with pretrained representations not initially encoding rotation cues.  
- **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis is not rigorously tested; no experiments analyze gradient dynamics or feature space alignment.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare gradient norms and loss curvature during early fine-tuning across stable vs. unstable pairs; measure alignment between ImageNet-pretrained features and rotation-relevant representations (e.g., via representational similarity analysis).

### Open Question 2  
- **Question:** What specific architectural properties (beyond model size) cause systematic compatibility patterns with different circular-aware methods (e.g., CLS excelling on EfficientViT-B3, CGD on MambaOut)?  
- **Basis in paper:** Results show scaling capacity does not consistently improve performance; inductive biases (attention mechanisms, convolutional priors) likely interact with method representations.  
- **Why unresolved:** The study compares many pairs but does not isolate architectural factors driving these interactions.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablations modifying architectural components (e.g., swapping attention for convolutions) while controlling capacity; statistical correlation of performance with architectural descriptors.

### Open Question 3  
- **Question:** To what extent are the performance rankings and stability observations from DRC-D generalizable to larger-scale datasets like full COCO or beyond?  
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations state: "our primary comparison uses DRC-D... it also means that method rankings may shift on larger and more diverse datasets... validating the full architecture-method grid on a larger dataset would strengthen the conclusions."  
- **Why unresolved:** Only top configurations were transferred to COCO; the full 16×5 grid was not re-evaluated at scale.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Repeat the comprehensive evaluation on a larger dataset (e.g., full COCO train set) to test if relative method advantages and architecture compatibilities hold.

### Open Question 4  
- **Question:** Why does OAD-360 (direct regression with circular loss) produce a bimodal error distribution (low P90 but extremely high P95) on COCO while probabilistic methods like CGD do not?  
- **Basis in paper:** Table 2 shows OAD-360's P95 is 88.18° vs. CGD's 16.15°; the Analysis attributes this to direct regression's brittleness, where "the model is accurate on most images but produces catastrophic errors on a small subset."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper suggests causes but does not analyze specific failure cases or quantitatively compare optimization dynamics.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Detailed error analysis identifying image characteristics behind catastrophic failures; compare gradient distributions and loss landscapes of OAD-360 vs. CGD during training.

## Limitations
- **Secrecy of random seeds:** Exact seeds for DRC-D experiments are withheld, preventing precise replication of reported variances.
- **Inconsistent COCO evaluation:** Prior comparisons use undefined rotation sampling; thus the SOTA claim may not be on a level playing field.
- **DRC-D scale and augmentation:** The dataset is small (~2k images) and trained without augmentation, possibly causing overfitting and limiting real-world applicability.

## Confidence
- **CGD robustness across backbones:** High – Consistent superiority across 16 diverse architectures.
- **Classification instability/peak accuracy:** High – Strong empirical evidence; both benefits and drawbacks clearly observed.
- **COCO SOTA transfer:** Medium – Gains are clear but reliance on non-standard test setups weakens certainty.

## Next Checks
