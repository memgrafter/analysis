# Quantifying Document Impact in RAG-LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05260
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05260
- **Reference count**: 8
- **Primary result**: The Influence Score (IS) metric effectively identifies document-level contributions in RAG systems, validated by poison attack detection (86% success) and ablation preference (91-97% from GPT-4).

## Executive Summary
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems combine retrieved documents with an LLM to produce answers, but lack metrics to quantify each document's influence on the final output. This paper introduces the Influence Score (IS), a metric based on Partial Information Decomposition (PID) that uses Semantic Entropy to measure a document's impact by comparing the LLM's response uncertainty with and without that document. The method is validated through two experiments: a document poison attack across three datasets where IS identified the malicious document as most influential in 86% of cases, and an ablation study where responses using only top-ranked IS documents were preferred over those using remaining documents, with GPT-4 preference rates of 91-97% and similar human judgments. The results demonstrate IS's efficacy in isolating and quantifying document influence, addressing a transparency gap in RAG.

## Method Summary
The Influence Score leverages Partial Information Decomposition to partition the predictive information about the LLM's response among the retrieved documents. It computes the Semantic Entropy of the LLM's output distribution given the full set of documents, then removes one document at a time and recomputes the entropy; the reduction in entropy (or change) attributable to that document is its Influence Score. The process involves generating multiple LLM samples to estimate semantic clusters, quantifying uncertainty via entropy. The metric can be applied post-hoc to any black-box RAG system without retraining. In the poison attack experiment, a single malicious document is inserted into retrieved results; IS rankings are analyzed to see if that document is flagged as most influential. In the ablation study, the top-ranked documents by IS are used to generate a new response, and similarity (by GPT-4 and humans) to the full-set response is compared against responses from the remaining documents.

## Key Results
- IS identified the malicious document as the most influential in 86% of cases across three QA datasets in a poison attack experiment.
- In an ablation study, responses generated using only the top-ranked IS documents were consistently more similar to the full-set response than those from the remaining documents, with GPT-4 preference rates of 91-97%.
- Human evaluations (n=50) largely aligned with GPT-4 judgments, supporting IS's ability to isolate influential documents.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Influence Score works by measuring how much a document reduces the LLM's uncertainty about the response. Using Partial Information Decomposition, it quantifies the unique contribution of a document beyond what other documents already provide. The core idea: if removing a document significantly increases the Semantic Entropy of the LLM's output (i.e., makes the answer more uncertain), that document had high influence. Conversely, minimal change indicates redundancy or low impact. This approach captures both additive and synergistic effects because PID decomposes information into redundant, unique, and synergistic components. By focusing on the unique information, IS effectively isolates each document's distinct contribution. The metric's use of semantic clustering (via LLM embeddings) ensures that entropy is computed over meaning rather than token variance, making it robust to surface-level variations in LLM outputs.

## Foundational Learning
- **RAG**: Combines retrieval with generation to ground answers in external knowledge; foundational because the paper's metric operates on RAG outputs. Quick check: Are retrieved documents included in the LLM prompt?
- **Partial Information Decomposition (PID)**: Decomposes information contributed by multiple sources into unique, redundant, and synergistic parts; needed for fair attribution. Quick check: Does the metric distinguish each document's unique contribution beyond others?
- **Semantic Entropy**: Measures uncertainty over semantic clusters instead of token distributions; ensures robustness to LLM sampling variation. Quick check: Are LLM responses grouped by semantic similarity before computing entropy?
- **Black-box Attribution**: Requires only input prompts and output texts, enabling use with any LLM including proprietary ones. Quick check: Can the method work without access to model gradients or weights?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Retriever → Retrieved Documents → IS Calculator (per-doc removal loop) → Influence Scores → (Optional) Filtering for Generation

**Critical path**  
1. Given query, retrieve top‑k documents.  
2. Compute baseline Semantic Entropy using all documents.  
3. For each document:  
   a. Remove the document.  
   b. Generate multiple LLM responses with remaining documents.  
   c. Compute Semantic Entropy of those responses.  
   d. Calculate IS for the removed document using a PID‑derived formula (e.g., unique information).  
4. Rank documents by IS.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Sampling vs. cost**: More LLM samples stabilize entropy estimates but increase inference cost.  
- **Clustering granularity**: Tighter semantic clusters yield finer entropy but may overfit to response noise.  
- **Model consistency**: Using the same LLM for generation and embedding ensures semantic alignment but is expensive; a smaller separate model reduces cost but may misalign.  
- **PID component**: Focusing on unique information simplifies interpretation but ignores synergistic effects where documents together have more impact.  
- **Black-box vs. white-box**: Avoids reliance on gradients, applicable to any LLM, but may be less precise than gradient‑based attribution.

**Failure signatures**  
- High variance in IS scores across random seeds (sample instability).  
- IS scores strongly correlated with document length or term frequency, indicating bias rather than true influence.  
- Malicious documents highly correlated with benign ones receive low IS (synergy masking).  
- Rankings sensitive to small changes in clustering threshold or number of samples.

**Three first experiments**  
1. **Poison Attack Detection**: Insert a single misleading document into retrieved set; verify IS ranks it as top influential in >80% of cases.  
2. **Ablation Consistency**: Compare responses from top‑IS documents vs. remaining documents using GPT‑4/human evaluation; expect strong preference for top‑IS responses.  
3. **Parameter Stability**: Vary number of LLM samples (5, 10, 20) and clustering thresholds; measure Spearman rank correlation of IS scores; expect >0.9 correlation.

## Limitations
- **Dataset & task dependency**: IS validated on three QA datasets; generalizability to other domains (e.g., summarization, code generation) and retrievers remains unknown.
- **Metric design choices**: Semantic Entropy depends on LLM sampling and clustering thresholds; sensitivity to these parameters is not fully explored.
- **Evaluation robustness**: Human evaluation was small‑scale (n=50) and GPT‑4 as judge may exhibit bias toward the full‑set response.

## Confidence

| IS‑ranked top documents yield responses preferred over remaining ones in ablation (91‑97% GPT‑4) | Medium |
| IS generally quantifies document‑level influence across RAG systems | Low |

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑dataset generalizability**: Test IS on at least two additional diverse datasets (e.g., conversational QA, technical document QA) and with different retrievers (BM25 vs. dense) to assess robustness.
2. **Ablation on metric components**: Systematically vary Semantic Entropy parameters (number of samples, temperature, clustering method) and compare IS against alternative influence methods (e.g., leave‑one‑out likelihood difference) to understand sensitivity.
3. **Interaction effects analysis**: Compute pairwise synergy/redundancy by measuring joint influence of document pairs versus sum of individual IS; test whether IS correctly handles overlapping information.
