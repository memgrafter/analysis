# Deriving Character Logic from Storyline as Codified Decision Trees

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.10080
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10080
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CDT achieves state-of-the-art NLI scores (77.71 on Bandori, 60.82 on Fandom) across 85 characters, surpassing human-written profiles and prior methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Codified Decision Trees (CDT), a framework to derive executable, situation-specific character profiles from narrative data, addressing the brittleness of existing role-playing agent behavior. CDT constructs a hierarchical tree of conditional rules by clustering similar scene-action pairs, prompting an LLM to hypothesize triggers, and rigorously validating them via NLI discrimination. This process yields a deterministic, context-aware grounding mechanism for action generation. Evaluated on fine-grained Fandom and Bandori benchmarks, CDT significantly outperforms baselines like ETA and RICL, with ablations confirming the critical roles of clustering, validation, and diversification. The approach scales favorably with more training data and supports goal-driven specialization for multi-character relation modeling.

## Method Summary
CDT induces a decision tree from a character’s narrative (scene, action) pairs. At each node, it first clusters semantically similar pairs using combined embeddings of scenes (instruction-following representations) and actions. For each cluster, an LLM generates candidate (question, statement) rule pairs. A validator (LLM or distilled model) then tests each hypothesis globally and on filtered subsets using NLI judgments. Based on accuracy thresholds (θ_accept, θ_reject), the system adds the statement globally, creates a leaf, recurses on the filtered subset to grow a child node, or abolishes the rule. Recursion proceeds until depth, data sparsity, or filtering limits are reached. At inference, the tree is traversed by checking if a new scene satisfies each node’s question; collected statements from visited nodes ground an LLM-based role-playing policy to generate actions.

## Key Results
- CDT sets new SOTA on Fandom (NLI 60.82) and Bandori (NLI 77.71), beating human-written codified profiles and prior methods (ETA, RICL).
- Ablations show removing clustering, validation, or diversification consistently degrades performance, confirming each component’s necessity.
- Performance scales with more training data; CDT handles relation modeling via goal-driven specialization.
- The distilled CDT-Lite variant maintains competitiveness while reducing validation cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Clustering Surfaces Candidate Regularities
**Claim:** Clustering similar (scene, action) pairs groups functionally equivalent narrative situations, allowing the LLM to infer cleaner conditional rules from coherent subsets.
**Mechanism:** K-Means is applied to concatenated embeddings of scenes (instruction-following) and actions. The LLM then hypothesizes "if-then" triggers from each cluster, where similar scenes lead to similar actions.
**Core assumption:** Clustered scenes are behaviorally equivalent for the character; grouping reduces heterogeneity for rule mining.
**Evidence:** Section §3.4 describes the clustering step. Ablation "w/o Clustering" in §6.1 shows consistent performance drops, confirming clustering’s contribution.
**Break condition:** If clusters mix scenes with different character impacts (similar surface semantics but divergent behavior), induced triggers will conflate logic, reducing specificity.

### Mechanism 2: Validation Filters Spurious Triggers
**Claim:** NLI-style validation against the full dataset ensures only statistically and semantically grounded rules are retained.
**Mechanism:** Each hypothesized (question, statement) pair is tested on all scene-action pairs. Accuracy on the subset satisfying the question determines whether the statement is added globally, a leaf is created, recursion occurs, or the rule is abolished.
**Core assumption:** A trigger with high entailment accuracy across its filtered subset represents a reliable conditional pattern.
**Evidence:** Abstract and §3.3 detail the accuracy-based decision matrix. Ablation "w/ Abolished Statements" (re-adding unvalidated hypotheses) performs worse in §6.1, proving the filter’s importance.
**Break condition:** Thresholds (θ_accept, θ_reject) too low admit noise; too high prunes valid nuanced patterns. Discriminator accuracy limits filter reliability.

### Mechanism 3: Recursive Specialization Builds Hierarchical Profiles
**Claim:** Hierarchical tree growth enables deterministic retrieval of situation-specific grounding statements.
**Mechanism:** Starting from the root (all data), validated triggers become internal nodes, partitioning data. Child nodes grow recursively on filtered subsets. Traversal follows satisfied questions, accumulating statements along the path.
**Core assumption:** Character logic is compositional and decomposable by situational predicates; deeper paths capture more specific constraints.
**Evidence:** Abstract and §3.2 describe recursive specialization. Depth ablations in §6.1 show improvement until saturation, supporting hierarchical benefit.
**Break condition:** If logic isn’t well-structured by a hierarchy of questions (e.g., many orthogonal dimensions), the tree may overfit or fail to capture interdependencies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Decision Tree Induction:** CDT adapts classic recursive partitioning to text, using LLM-hypothesized predicates as splits instead of feature thresholds. *Quick check:* How does CART choose a split, and how does CDT’s split selection differ in process and format?
- **Rule Mining from Text:** The framework hinges on extracting "if-A-then-B" rules from narrative pairs. *Quick check:* What risks arise when an LLM infers triggers from clustered data without validation (e.g., hallucination, overgeneralization)?
- **Instruction-Following Text Embeddings:** Scene embeddings must capture "what action might follow?" not just semantic similarity. *Quick check:* Why would a standard semantic embedding (e.g., SBERT) be inferior for clustering scene-action pairs in this profiling task?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Clustering Engine -> Hypothesis Generator -> Validator -> Tree Grower -> Traversal Engine -> RP Policy
- **Critical path:** Training Data -> Clustering -> LLM Hypothesis -> Discriminator Validation -> Recursive Node Creation -> Traversal (inference) -> RP Generation. Validation is the central gatekeeper.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Validator strength vs. cost (GPT-4.1-mini vs. distilled DeBERTa); tree depth vs. overfitting (controlled by |D|_min, θ_f); hypotheses per cluster vs. diversity (coverage vs. redundancy).
- **Failure signatures:** High node count but low test performance (overfitting); performance drop without clustering (embedding failure); most hypotheses abolished (poor LLM prompt or inconsistent behavior); traversal selects no statements (tree too specialized).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce validation efficacy by comparing CDT with and without the validation step (w/ Abl. Statements).
  2. Test clustering impact by comparing CDT against a "w/o Clustering" variant that hypothesizes on the entire dataset per node.
  3. Validate depth scaling by training CDTs with max depth dmax = 1, 2, 3, 4 and measuring NLI score changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can CDT be extended for continual, incremental updates as new narrative data arrives, avoiding full retraining? The current process is static.
- How robust is CDT to sparse or low-quality training data, and how does it handle genuinely inconsistent character behavior (e.g., character development)? Validation thresholds may over-prune on sparse data.
- To what extent do the choice of LLM for hypothesis generation and the design of instruction-following embeddings bias the CDT structure and content? Impacts on tree statistics and RP fidelity are not isolated.
- Can CDT effectively model multi-character interactive scenes where behavior depends on dynamic states of multiple agents? Current format lacks a mechanism for conjunctions of multiple character-specific predicates.
- What specific aspects of conditional logic are lost during the wikification pipeline that converts CDT into a linear textual profile, and can a hybrid representation preserve situational triggers without sacrificing readability? Wikification underperforms runtime-traversed CDT.

## Limitations
- Clustering algorithm specifics (K-Means parameters, per-node cluster selection) are unspecified, risking reproducibility.
- Prompt templates for hypothesis generation and NLI validation may require per-model tuning; failure modes for open-source variants are not detailed.
- Fixed validation thresholds (θ_accept/θ_reject, θ_f) may not generalize across data sparsity or behavioral consistency levels.
- The scalability trade-off (CDT-Lite’s distilled discriminator) likely incurs an accuracy gap whose impact on tree quality is not quantified.

## Confidence
- **Clustering:** Medium (ablation shows benefit, but corpus neighbors weak; embeddings may conflate distinct contexts).
- **Validation:** High (strong ablation evidence and neighbor support; central and empirically validated).
- **Hierarchical Specialization:** Medium (depth ablations show gains to saturation, but logic may not always be hierarchically decomposable).
