# Understanding Task Aggregation for Generalizable Ultrasound Foundation Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.18123
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18123
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: Task aggregation effectiveness for ultrasound foundation models highly depends on data scale; clinically-grouped training harms low-data tasks, while all-task unified training offers robust performance across scales.

## Executive Summary
This study explores the conditions under which heterogeneous ultrasound tasks can be jointly learned within a foundation model without performance degradation. The authors propose the M2DINO framework, which integrates a DINOv3 backbone with task-conditioned Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) blocks to adaptively allocate model capacity. Evaluated on 27 diverse tasks—including segmentation, classification, detection, and regression—the framework is tested under three aggregation strategies: task-specific training, grouping by clinical domain, and a single unified model for all tasks. Results reveal that the effectiveness of task aggregation is strongly tied to the scale of training data. Clinically-grouped training yields improvements in data-rich domains (e.g., obstetrics) but induces severe negative transfer in low-data groups, exemplified by a drop in breast lesion segmentation Dice score from 0.713 to 0.145. In contrast, all-task unified training demonstrates more consistent performance across varying data scales and proves less sensitive to clinical domain boundaries. The findings underscore that successful task aggregation requires joint consideration of data availability and task characteristics, not merely clinical taxonomy.

## Method Summary
The M2DINO framework is built upon the DINOv3 self-supervised learning backbone, which provides a powerful feature extractor pretrained on natural images. To accommodate multiple tasks, the model incorporates Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) blocks whose gating is conditioned on task embeddings, allowing the network to dynamically allocate sub-network capacity to each task. Three training paradigms are evaluated: (1) task-specific, where each task has its own model; (2) clinically-grouped, where tasks are aggregated by clinical specialty (e.g., obstetrics, breast); and (3) all-task unified, where all tasks are trained jointly in a single model. Performance is assessed on 27 ultrasound tasks covering segmentation, classification, detection, and regression. This design enables systematic investigation of when and how tasks can be aggregated without sacrificing individual task performance.

## Key Results
- Data-scale dependency: aggregation effectiveness correlates with training data volume; clinically-grouped training helps data-rich groups but causes catastrophic negative transfer in low-data groups (e.g., breast lesion segmentation DSC drops from 0.713 to 0.145).
- All-task unified training yields consistent performance across data scales, outperforming clinically-grouped aggregation in low-data settings.
- Segmentation tasks are the most sensitive to aggregation design, showing the largest performance swings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The task-conditioned MoE mechanism enables the model to share a common representation via the DINOv3 backbone while routing inputs to specialized experts based on the task identity. This dynamic routing reduces interference between dissimilar tasks, especially when tasks have sufficient data to train distinct expert pathways. However, when tasks are grouped solely by clinical domain, the gating may still direct inputs to a shared set of experts, leading to interference if the underlying perceptual objectives diverge. Moreover, low-data tasks lack enough examples to properly train their dedicated experts, making them vulnerable to negative transfer from high-data tasks sharing the same experts. The unified training setting exposes each task to the full diversity of data, encouraging the gating to learn more discriminative routing patterns and mitigating harmful interference.

## Foundational Learning
- **Foundation Models**:  
  *Why needed*: Provide a versatile, pretrained representation that can be adapted to many downstream tasks with minimal retraining.  
  *Quick check*: Can the model be fine-tuned on a new task with limited data without forgetting previous tasks?

- **Self-Supervised Learning (DINO)**:  
  *Why needed*: Learns powerful visual features from unlabeled data, essential for medical imaging where annotations are expensive.  
  *Quick check*: Does the pretrained backbone generalize to diverse ultrasound tasks?

- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**:  
  *Why needed*: Dynamically allocates model capacity to different inputs/tasks, enabling specialization while controlling parameter growth.  
  *Quick check*: Do different tasks activate distinct sets of experts, and is expert utilization balanced?

- **Task Aggregation / Multi-Task Learning**:  
  *Why needed*: Joint training can improve data efficiency and performance through shared representations, but risks negative transfer.  
  *Quick check*: Does combining tasks lead to overall gain or loss compared to training separately?

- **Negative Transfer**:  
  *Why needed*: Understanding when and why joint training harms performance is crucial for designing effective multi-task systems.  
  *Quick check*: Are there tasks that degrade when trained together, and what factors correlate with such degradation?

- **Data Scale Sensitivity**:  
  *Why needed*: The amount of data per task determines its resilience to interference and its ability to contribute to shared learning.  
  *Quick check*: Does increasing data for a task change its impact on other tasks in a multi-task setup?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Input Ultrasound Image → DINOv3 Backbone (Vision Transformer) → Task Embedding Lookup → Conditional MoE Blocks (stacked) → Task-Specific Output Heads → Predictions

**Critical Path**:  
1. Input image is split into patches and linearly embedded.  
2. DINOv3 backbone processes tokens through transformer layers, producing a feature map.  
3. A task-specific embedding (learned per task) is broadcast to each conditional MoE block.  
4. Within each MoE block, a gating network (conditioned on the task embedding and input features) selects a weighted combination of experts to process the features.  
5. The output of each MoE block feeds into the next, eventually reaching the final feature representation.  
6. Task-specific heads (e.g., segmentation decoder, classification linear layer) produce the final outputs.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- **Shared backbone vs. task-specific capacity**: The DINOv3 backbone offers strong pretrained features but may not be optimal for ultrasound; adding MoE introduces task-specific capacity at the cost of increased parameters and routing complexity.  
- **Routing granularity**: Conditioning MoE on task token enables specialization but requires a separate gating mechanism per task; too many tasks may lead to routing collisions.  
- **Computational overhead**: MoE introduces dynamic computation, but conditional routing based on task may reduce efficiency if not all experts are used.  
- **Aggregation strategy**: Clinically-grouped training reduces number of independent models but risks grouping dissimilar tasks; all-task unified maximizes data diversity but needs careful regularization to avoid interference.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- **Catastrophic drop in low-data tasks**: e.g., breast lesion segmentation DSC collapse when grouped.  
- **Segmentation tasks are disproportionately affected**: likely due to pixel-level prediction sensitivity.  
- **Routing collapse**: gating assigns high weight to a small subset of experts for most tasks, indicating underutilization.  
- **Training instability**: loss oscillations or slow convergence when aggregating many heterogeneous tasks.

**First Experiments**:  
1. **Baseline comparison**: Train M2DINO under the three aggregation strategies on all 27 tasks and report per-task performance to validate the reported scale-dependent effects.  
2. **Ablation of task conditioning**: Remove task tokens from MoE gating (use input-only gating) to quantify the benefit of explicit task conditioning for reducing interference.  
3. **Expert capacity exploration**: Vary the number of experts per MoE block (e.g., 2, 4, 8) and measure impact on low-data segmentation tasks to find the sweet spot between capacity and overfitting.

## Limitations
- Task grouping based solely on clinical taxonomy may not capture semantic or perceptual similarity, potentially misaligning aggregation.
- Use of DINOv3, pretrained on natural images, may introduce representation bias for ultrasound, confounding observed data-scale effects.
- No analysis of Mixture-of-Experts routing patterns or failure modes per task group, limiting mechanistic understanding.

## Confidence
- Data-scale dependency of aggregation effectiveness: **High**  
- Harm from clinically-grouped training in low-data regimes: **High**  
- All-task unified training consistency: **Medium**  
- Segmentation tasks' heightened sensitivity: **High**

## Next Checks
- Replicate M2DINO with a medically-pretrained backbone (e.g., MedCLIP) to isolate natural-image bias from data-scale effects.
- Analyze MoE expert activation statistics per task to quantify cross-task interference, focusing on segmentation vs. other task types.
- Add 5+ new low-data ultrasound tasks (e.g., musculoskeletal, ophthalmic) to test if unified training generalizes beyond the original 27-task suite.
