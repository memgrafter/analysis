# compar:IA: The French Government's LLM arena to collect French-language human prompts and preference data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06669  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06669  
- **Reference count:** 10  
- **Primary result:** Collected 600k+ French prompts and 250k+ preferences via blind pairwise comparisons, released open datasets (conversations, votes, reactions) under Etalab 2.0 to enable French LLM alignment, leaderboards, and educational integration.

## Executive Summary
compar:IA is a public platform launched by the French government to address the scarcity of high-quality French-language human preference data for large language model (LLM) alignment. Using a blind pairwise comparison interface, it collects unconstrained prompts and votes from a general audience without requiring accounts. As of early 2026, it has amassed over 600,000 prompts (89% French) and 250,000 votes. The project conservatively filters personal data via LLM-based full-conversation exclusion and openly releases three structured datasets under an Etalab 2.0 license. This has enabled a French model leaderboard, thematic usage analyses, and integration into national digital literacy programs reaching over 1.5 million students.

## Method Summary
The platform operates as a blind "arena": users submit a prompt and see anonymized responses from two randomly selected models side-by-side, then vote for the preferred output or react. A FastAPI backend orchestrates parallel inference calls to around 104 models via providers like OpenRouter. After collection, an LLM-based pipeline detects any personal data in full conversations; flagged dialogues are discarded entirely (~5% discard rate) to ensure GDPR-compliant public release. Clean data—including conversations, votes, and reactions—is published on Hugging Face and data.gouv.fr. Weekly Bradley–Terry aggregations update a public leaderboard. The design emphasizes low friction (no accounts, simple interface) and privacy preservation to maximize participation from the French-speaking public.

## Key Results
- **Scale & language:** >600,000 prompts and >250,000 votes, with 89% of prompts in French.
- **Open data release:** Three datasets (conversations, votes, reactions) published under permissive Etalab 2.0 licenses, enabling downstream reuse.
- **Ecosystem impact:** Supported creation of a French model leaderboard, thematic analyses (e.g., 31.95% technical/educational prompts), and integration into national education programs (1.5M+ students).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 – Low-friction, blind comparison drives engagement:** The accountless, accessible interface with blind model presentation reduces barriers and brand bias, encouraging spontaneous participation from a broad public. This yields a high volume of unconstrained, real-world French prompts. *Evidence:* 300k+ unique visitors without incentives; corpus similarity to established arenas (Chatbot Arena, COLE). *Break condition:* Increased UI complexity would likely reduce participation and French-language share.

**Mechanism 2 – Conservative full-conversation PII filtering enables open release:** Instead of risky span-level redaction, the system discards entire conversations flagged by an LLM as containing personal data. This simplifies GDPR compliance and minimizes re-identification risk, making public release viable despite a ~5% data loss. *Evidence:* Section 3.3 details the policy and discard rate; release under Etalab 2.0. *Break condition:* A privacy incident from a false negative could force more aggressive filtering, further reducing dataset size.

**Mechanism 3 – Open licensing creates a multiplier effect:** Permissive licensing on Hugging Face/data.gouv.fr allows unrestricted reuse (training, research, leaderboards), amplifying each collected prompt/vote. *Evidence:* 778 Hugging Face access requests, documented reuse by Bunka.ai and PEReN, and cited literature on open feedback ecosystems. *Break condition:* Restrictive license clauses or loss of model access would curb commercial reuse and ecosystem growth.

*Why needed:* Core method for unbiased preference collection; understanding its biases (e.g., length, context) is key to interpreting data.  
*Quick check:* How might removing model names change user judgment compared to seeing known brands like "GPT-4"?

*Why needed:* The project’s legal feasibility hinges on its conservative PII-filtering trade-off (privacy over volume).

*Why needed:* License terms dictate permissible reuse (research vs. commercial) and signal public-interest intent.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Frontend (SvelteKit) -> Backend (FastAPI) -> Inference Providers (OpenRouter, HF APIs)  
Backend -> Data Pipeline (filtering, tagging) -> Leaderboard Service & Hugging Face/data.gouv.fr release  

**Critical path:** User prompt submitted -> Backend enqueues prompt to all selected models -> Inference providers return responses -> Frontend displays side-by-side -> User votes/reacts -> Data logged -> Async filtering job -> Clean conversation published.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Privacy vs. Volume: Full-conversation exclusion (safe) vs. span-level redaction (higher utility but riskier).  
- Consistency vs. Flexibility: Standardized system prompts (fairer comparison) vs. respecting provider defaults (authentic behavior).  
- Cost vs. Coverage: Self-financed per-token API costs allow many models but limit inference volume; early credit-based testing was unsustainable.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- PII detection rate spikes (>10%), indicating filter overreach or prompt shifts.  
- Drop in conversation length or "continue" clicks, suggesting arena-specific test behavior.  
- High latency variance between models, biasing preferences toward faster responses.  

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Filter Precision Test: Manually annotate filtered-out conversations to estimate true PII prevalence and validate the ~5% discard rate.  
2. Vote Consistency Probe: Collect 10+ independent votes per prompt pair; measure inter-annotator agreement to gauge preference noise.  
3. System Prompt A/B Test: Compare open model win rates with/without standardized system prompts to quantify confound noted in limitations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How do socio-demographic factors (age, profession, education level) influence French-language model preference judgments?  
*Basis:* Platform avoids metadata collection to minimize friction, preventing user characterization.  
*Resolution:* Integrate optional, privacy-preserving demographic surveys and correlate attributes with voting patterns.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent does self-selection into compar:IA (e.g., through educational programs) skew preference data relative to the broader French-speaking population?  
*Basis:* Voluntary participation skews toward AI-interested individuals; no data from non-participants.  
*Resolution:* Parallel data collection via randomly sampled French internet users; compare topic/length/preference distributions.

**Open Question 3:** What is the minimum viable dataset size (in prompts and votes) required to train a reliable preference model in a new low-resource language?  
*Basis:* Target is "tens of thousands" per language, but no empirical scaling studies validate this.  
*Resolution:* Systematic experiments training DPO models on incrementally larger subsets for a new language, measuring alignment robustness.

**Open Question 4:** How does the arena’s blind pairwise comparison setup alter prompt distribution compared to naturalistic chatbot usage?  
*Basis:* Arena-style may encourage evaluative, shorter prompts; no concurrent natural usage logs.  
*Resolution:* Compare with anonymized interaction logs from French AI assistants (with consent) on length, complexity, topic diversity, and multi-turn rates.

## Limitations
- **Filter parameters undisclosed:** Exact LLM-based PII detection model, thresholds, and false negative risk are not specified, hindering replication and risk assessment.
- **Arena-induced biases:** Blind evaluation may alter prompt authenticity (e.g., more evaluative, shorter prompts); impact is not quantified.
- **Anecdotal downstream utility:** Evidence of dataset reuse for state-of-the-art model training remains illustrative, not systematic.

## Confidence
- **Platform efficacy (low-friction design):** High. Strong internal traffic evidence and alignment with established arena paradigms.
- **Privacy filtering strategy (full-conversation exclusion):** Medium. Clear policy description but lacking independent validation of discard rate or false negative risk.
- **Open-data multiplier effect:** High. Documented reuse cases (leaderboards, analyses) and permissive licensing provide concrete support.
