# Deep Learning-Based Metamodeling of Nonlinear Stochastic Dynamic Systems under Parametric and Predictive Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12012
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12012
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Three deep learning metamodels with uncertainty quantification achieved low prediction errors on 40-DOF and 777-DOF structural systems under stochastic seismic loading, with predictive variance moderately correlated with actual error (Pearson ρ ≈ 0.5–0.7).

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the computational challenge of modeling high-dimensional nonlinear structural systems under stochastic seismic loading while simultaneously accounting for parametric uncertainty in structural properties and predictive uncertainty from deep learning models. Three metamodeling frameworks are proposed, each coupling a feature extraction module (multi-layer perceptron, message-passing neural network, or autoencoder) with a long short-term memory network trained using Monte Carlo dropout and negative log-likelihood loss to quantify both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. The frameworks are validated on a 40-DOF Bouc-Wen system and a 777-DOF 37-story steel frame. All frameworks achieve low prediction errors; for the simpler Bouc-Wen system, the MLP-LSTM yields the lowest mean squared error (0.0090), while for the complex steel frame, the MPNN-LSTM achieves the lowest mean absolute error (0.0314). A consistent moderate positive correlation between predictive variance and actual error is observed across both case studies, confirming the suitability of the frameworks for assessing prediction confidence and enabling future active learning strategies.

## Method Summary
The method employs wavelet transforms (Daubechies-6, scale 3) to compress temporal sequences of seismic excitation and structural response, reducing computational burden for LSTM training. Three feature extraction modules process the compressed coefficients together with static structural parameters Γ: (1) an MLP that directly concatenates inputs, (2) an MPNN that constructs a graph from member properties, performs message-passing, and pools to a global embedding, and (3) an autoencoder pretrained to map inputs to a low-dimensional latent space. The LSTM, with Monte Carlo dropout active at inference, predicts wavelet-domain response coefficients. Training uses a negative log-likelihood loss with a Gaussian output (β=0.5) to model input-dependent aleatoric uncertainty. At inference, multiple stochastic forward passes yield epistemic uncertainty; total predictive variance combines both components. Predictions are inverted to the time domain via inverse wavelet transform (and decoder for the autoencoder variant).

## Key Results
- On the 40-DOF Bouc-Wen system, MLP-LSTM achieved the lowest normalized mean squared error of 0.0090.
- On the 777-DOF steel frame, MPNN-LSTM achieved the lowest normalized mean absolute error of 0.0314.
- Across both case studies, a moderate positive correlation (Pearson ρ ≈ 0.5–0.7) between sample-wise average peak predictive variance and average peak absolute error was observed, indicating reliable uncertainty estimates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Wavelet-based Temporal Compression**  
A sixth-order Daubechies wavelet transform with scale parameter 3 is applied to both excitation and response, retaining only the coarsest-level approximation coefficients. This drastically reduces sequence length, making LSTM training feasible while preserving dominant frequency content. The core assumption is that discarded detail coefficients contribute minimally to dynamic behavior. Evidence is given in the abstract and Section 3.1, though no systematic trade-off analysis is provided. Break condition: if the response is dominated by high-frequency content (e.g., near resonance with higher modes), the coarsest approximation may omit critical dynamics.

**Mechanism 2: Graph-based Dependency Modeling via Message Passing**  
The structure is represented as a graph with nodes as structural members (beams/columns) and edges connecting nodes at shared joints; a virtual node connected to all member-nodes enables global aggregation. Message-passing iteratively updates node embeddings by aggregating neighbor features; global mean pooling yields a fixed-size graph embedding Γ̂, which is fused with wavelet coefficients for LSTM input. This captures spatial dependencies and relational interactions among members. Evidence appears in the abstract and Section 3.2.2. Break conditions include inappropriate graph construction (e.g., mismatched parameterization), long-range dependencies beyond the number of hops M, or over-smoothing from excessive layers.

**Mechanism 3: Bayesian Uncertainty Quantification with MC-Dropout and NLL Loss**  
Monte Carlo dropout is applied to LSTM hidden layers; at inference, dropout masks are retained and multiple stochastic forward passes produce an output distribution. Epistemic uncertainty is the variance across predicted means. Aleatoric uncertainty is modeled by a Gaussian output with input-dependent variance; the negative log-likelihood loss (with β=0.5) forces the network to assign higher variance to regions with larger residuals. Total variance combines both components. Evidence is explicit in the abstract and Section 3.3. Break conditions: poor dropout approximation (e.g., uncalibrated dropout rates), violation of the Gaussian noise assumption (e.g., heavy-tailed noise), or numerical instability in the NLL loss.

## Foundational Learning
- **Wavelet Transform for Signal Compression**  
  *Why needed:* Reduces long time series (e.g., 12,000 steps) to a few hundred coefficients, enabling efficient LSTM training and prediction without prohibitive computational cost.  
  *Quick check:* What is the trade-off between wavelet scale (decomposition level) and information loss? How to select the appropriate scale for a new structural system?

- **Graph Neural Networks (Message Passing)**  
  *Why needed:* Encodes the structure's topology and member properties into a fixed-size embedding, crucial for high-dimensional systems where simple concatenation fails to capture spatial correlations.  
  *Quick check:* How does adding a virtual node improve global information aggregation? What might happen if the graph is poorly constructed (e.g., missing edges)?

- **Bayesian Uncertainty in Neural Networks**  
  *Why needed:* Provides confidence intervals for predictions, essential for risk-aware decision-making in seismic hazard. Distinguishing epistemic (reducible) from aleatoric (irreducible) uncertainty guides data collection and model improvement.  
  *Quick check:* What distinguishes epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty in this metamodel? How does the NLL loss shape aleatoric uncertainty estimates?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input handling (stochastic excitation time series, structural parameters Γ) -> Preprocessing (wavelet transform on excitation and response, standardize Γ) -> Feature extraction (three variants: MLP concatenates wavelet coefficients + Γ; MPNN builds graph from Γ, performs message-passing, pools to Γ̂, fuses with coefficients; AE pretrained encoder maps coefficients+Γ to latent sequence) -> LSTM with MC-dropout (predicts wavelet coefficients or latent sequence) -> Post-processing (inverse wavelet transform; for AE, decoder reconstruction; combine aleatoric and epistemic variances).

**Critical path:**  
*Training:* For each sample, compute wavelet coefficients -> feature extraction -> LSTM forward pass with dropout -> compute NLL loss -> backpropagate. MPNN graph construction is done once per sample (static Γ); AE is pretrained separately.  
*Inference:* Same forward pass with dropout active; perform H_mc stochastic passes to estimate predictive distribution; average means and compute variances. For AE, generate latent samples via reparameterization and decode to physical space.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MLP is simpler and faster but ignores graph structure; MPNN captures member interactions but adds computational overhead and requires careful graph construction.
- AE reduces output dimensionality, beneficial for high-DOF systems, but introduces autoencoder reconstruction error and requires pretraining.
- Wavelet compression reduces sequence length but may lose high-frequency details; scale must be chosen based on dominant frequencies.
- Dropout rate balances uncertainty estimation and overfitting; typically tuned on validation set.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High errors on specific DOFs or time periods: Check if those DOFs correspond to high-frequency responses (wavelet issue) or are isolated in the graph (MPNN issue).
- Poor uncertainty calibration (low variance-error correlation): Check dropout implementation, Gaussian assumption, or aleatoric variance saturation.
- AE reconstruction error high: Increase latent dimension or train autoencoder longer.
- MPNN over-smoothing: Reduce message-passing steps M or add residual connections.
- NLL training instability (NaNs): Add small epsilon to variance output; use softplus activation; apply gradient clipping.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline validation:** Train MLP-LSTM on Bouc-Wen system; report normalized MSE, RMSE, MAE; visualize response time histories and 95% prediction intervals; confirm intervals cover ground truth.  
2. **Ablation on wavelet scale:** For MLP-LSTM, vary wavelet scale (e.g., 2, 3, 4) and measure prediction error and training time; identify optimal compression trade-off.  
3. **Graph representation check:** For MPNN-LSTM on steel frame, compare member-node vs. joint-node graph constructions and vary message-passing steps M; evaluate impact on performance and embedding similarity to detect over-smoothing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Active learning with predictive uncertainty:** How to implement an iterative active learning loop that selects high-variance samples for new simulations? The paper suggests this based on observed variance-error correlation but provides no procedure.
- **Joint training of autoencoder and LSTM:** Does end-to-end training (allowing gradients through the autoencoder during LSTM training) improve prediction accuracy and uncertainty calibration compared to separate pretraining?
- **Robustness under measurement noise:** How well does the uncertainty decomposition perform when response data contain noise? The paper assumes noise-free data; real data often violate this.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters (Bouc-Wen hysteretic parameters, MPNN architecture details, LSTM settings) hinder exact replication of reported results.
- No analysis of wavelet scale trade-offs or systematic graph construction ablations; assumptions about compression and topology are not validated.
- Uncertainty quantification relies on Gaussian aleatoric noise and MC-dropout as a Bayesian approximation; calibration (e.g., CRPS, coverage) is not assessed.

## Confidence
- Wavelet compression enables feasible LSTM training – **Medium** (supported by reduced sequence lengths and successful training but no systematic study).
- MPNN captures spatial dependencies for complex systems – **Medium** (good performance on steel frame but missing architecture details and ablations).
- MC-dropout + NLL yields predictive variance correlated with error – **Medium** (moderate Pearson ρ reported but no calibration metrics).
- Overall low prediction errors on both case studies – **Medium** (numbers given but reproducibility uncertain due to missing hyperparameters).

## Next Checks
- **Wavelet Scale Sensitivity Study:** Retrain MLP-LSTM with Daubechies-6 scales J=2, 3, 4 on both datasets; record normalized errors, training time, and reconstruction fidelity to identify optimal compression and detect performance cliffs.
- **Graph Design Ablation:** For the steel frame, compare (a) member-node vs. joint-node graphs, (b) removal of virtual node, and (c) varying message-passing steps M=1,3,5; evaluate errors and average pairwise node embedding cosine similarity to detect over-smoothing.
- **Uncertainty Calibration Study:** Compute continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) and 95% prediction interval coverage on test sets for all frameworks; benchmark MC-dropout uncertainty against a deep ensemble (5 random seeds) to evaluate Bayesian approximation quality and Gaussian noise hypothesis.
