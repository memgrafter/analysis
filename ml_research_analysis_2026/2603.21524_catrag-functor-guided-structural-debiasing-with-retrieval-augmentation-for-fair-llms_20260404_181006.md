# CatRAG: Functor-Guided Structural Debiasing with Retrieval Augmentation for Fair LLMs
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.21524
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21524
*   **Reference count:** 38
*   **Primary result:** Reduces demographic bias scores from ~60% to near zero and improves accuracy by up to 40% over base models on the BBQ benchmark, achieving state-of-the-art fairness-utility trade-offs.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) exhibit harmful demographic biases, especially in high-stakes applications. CatRAG is a novel framework that combines two complementary techniques to mitigate this problem: (1) a functor-guided structural projection that removes bias-associated directions from the model's embedding space while preserving task-relevant information, and (2) a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system that grounds the model's responses in a curated, diverse, and counter-stereotypical evidence corpus. Evaluated on the BBQ bias benchmark across three open-source LLMs, CatRAG significantly outperforms both base models and prior debiasing methods, demonstrating a powerful synergy between altering model internals and conditioning on balanced external knowledge.

## Method Summary
CatRAG operates in two phases. First, a one-time **structural debiasing** step is applied to the target LLM. Using manually defined anchor sets for a sensitive demographic (e.g., `{man, woman}`) and relevant occupations (e.g., `{doctor, nurse}`), it computes scatter matrices from the model's token embeddings. Solving a generalized eigenvalue problem identifies a subspace that maximizes occupational variance relative to demographic variance. An orthogonal projector formed from the top eigenvectors is then applied to the model's embedding layer, suppressing bias-associated directions. Second, at inference time, a **diversity-aware RAG** pipeline retrieves a small number of factual, demographically balanced snippets from a manually curated corpus. These passages are formatted and prepended to the original query, conditioning the debiased LLM to ground its generation in this balanced context before producing an answer.

## Key Results
*   On the BBQ benchmark, CatRAG reduces bias scores from approximately 60% to near zero across evaluated models.
*   It improves accuracy by up to 40% over base (unmodified) LLMs and by over 10% compared to prior state-of-the-art debiasing methods.
*   Ablation studies confirm synergistic effects: the combination of functor projection and RAG yields a superior fairness-utility trade-off, with each component contributing uniquely beyond its individual effect.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** A functor-guided orthogonal projection suppresses bias-associated directions in the embedding space while preserving task-relevant semantic geometry.
*   **Mechanism:** Demographic (S_D) and occupational (S_O) scatter matrices are constructed from anchor token embeddings. A generalized eigenvalue problem (S_O u = γ(S_D + ϵI) u) is solved to find eigenvectors (U) that maximize the ratio of occupational to demographic variance. The projector P = UU^⊤ maps all embeddings into this unbiased subspace, directly altering representations used for attention and predictions.
*   **Core assumption:** Demographic bias occupies separable, linear subspaces in the embedding space, distinct from directions encoding task-relevant information for BBQ questions.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "functor-guided structural projection"; [section III.C] functor mapping collapsing demographics; [section IV.B] spectral objective and GEP solution.
*   **Break condition:** If demographic and occupational concepts are not approximately orthogonal, the projection may discard task-relevant information. Efficacy depends on anchor sets (D, O) correctly capturing primary bias axes.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with a diversity-aware, counter-stereotypical evidence corpus grounds the model's output in balanced external facts, reducing reliance on internally stored, skewed priors.
*   **Mechanism:** For a query, K short, factual snippets are retrieved from a manually curated, demographically balanced corpus K. These passages are prepended to the query, directly conditioning the LLM's generation on this external, balanced context.
*   **Core assumption:** The model's internal knowledge contains stereotypical associations that can be overridden by salient, contradictory evidence at generation time. The curated corpus accurately reflects balanced real-world distributions for BBQ scenarios.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "retrieval-augmented generation using diversity-aware evidence"; [section IV.C] construction of balanced corpus K; [section IV.D] prompt construction with evidence injection.
*   **Break condition:** If the corpus is incomplete, non-representative, or fails to cover the query context, retrieval may be irrelevant. The LLM may also ignore or misinterpret the evidence, especially if its internal representations remain strongly biased.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The synergy between structural debiasing (functor projection) and evidence grounding (RAG) yields a superior fairness-utility trade-off because each addresses a complementary source of bias: encoded shortcuts and incomplete/skewed internal knowledge.
*   **Mechanism:** The projection weakens the model's capacity to *use* demographic shortcuts. RAG mitigates the default *content* by supplying balanced facts. Jointly, the model operates in a space less prone to demographic steering and is prompted with evidence discouraging stereotypical inferences, leading to more robust responses.
*   **Core assumption:** The two bias sources (encoded geometry and prior knowledge) are at least partially separable and orthogonal in their mitigation requirements. Addressing only one leaves residual bias from the other.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "synergy between representation-level projection and evidence grounding"; [section VI] discussion of complementarity; [Table III] ablation showing `Full CatRAG` (81.2% acc, 0.01 BS) outperforms `Functor-only` (70.5% acc, 0.15 BS) and `RAG-only` (65.3% acc, 0.24 BS).
*   **Break condition:** If the projection is too aggressive (small d_u), it may damage task-relevant information needed to integrate RAG evidence. Conversely, poor retrieval evidence breaks the synergy, leaving the projection to bear the full mitigation burden.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Generalized Eigenvalue Problem (GEP)**
    *   **Why needed here:** The core functor projector is derived by solving S_O u = γ(S_D + ϵI) u. This finds directions (eigenvectors) maximizing the ratio of occupational to demographic scatter (γ), defining the unbiased subspace.
    *   **Quick check question:** Given S_O and S_D, what does a large generalized eigenvalue γ imply about the corresponding eigenvector direction?
*   **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**
    *   **Why needed here:** CatRAG's second prong is a RAG system with a fairness-aware corpus. Understanding standard RAG (query → retrieve → fuse → generate) is key to seeing where the "diversity-aware" modification intervenes.
    *   **Quick check question:** In a standard RAG pipeline, where does the retrieved evidence get incorporated into the LLM's input, and what is a potential failure mode if that evidence is biased?
*   **Concept: Category Theory Functor (Mapping)**
    *   **Why needed here:** The paper's "functor" conceptually maps a biased semantic space (C) to an unbiased one (U) by collapsing demographic objects (e.g., `man`, `woman` → `u_Person`) while preserving occupational distinctions (e.g., `doctor` → `u_doctor`). This justifies the projection's design goal.
    *   **Quick check question:** If the functor maps both 'man' and 'woman' to the same object in U, what does this imply for the relationship between their embeddings in the projected space?

## Architecture Onboarding
1. Anchor Definition -> 2. Scatter Computation -> 3. Projection Learning -> 4. Embedding Patch -> (Per-query) 5. Retrieval -> 6. Prompt Fusion -> 7. Generation.
*   **Critical path:** Steps 1-4 (functor projection) are a one-time setup per model. Steps 5-7 (RAG) are per-query during inference.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Projection Strength (d_u): Smaller d_u means stronger debiasing but higher risk of losing task-relevant distinctions. Requires tuning.
    *   Corpus Quality vs. Scope: A small, high-quality corpus is auditable but may lack coverage; a larger one risks introducing new biases.
    *   Retrieval Relevance vs. Diversity: Prioritizing only relevance may re-inforce stereotypes; simple diversity re-ranking (e.g., MMR) is a heuristic.
    *   Inference Overhead: RAG adds latency from retrieval and longer prompts.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   High Bias Score after projection: Demographic subspaces not collapsed. Check anchor definitions and scatter matrix estimation.
    *   Low Accuracy with strong projection (small d_u): Occupational distinctions being discarded. Increase d_u or verify S_O captures correct variance.
    *   Retrieval Amplifies Bias: If `RAG-only` or full CatRAG worsens BS, audit retrieved passages; corpus likely lacks counter-stereotypes.
    *   Model Ignores Evidence: If `RAG-only` shows no improvement, the LLM may be overwriting the context. Test prompt clarity and evidence placement.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1. Validate Projection Geometry: Compute pre-/post-projection pairwise cosine similarities for held-out anchor tokens. Expect: within-D similarity ↑ (collapse), within-O similarity stable/↑ slightly (preservation).
    2. Test Retrieval Diversity Impact: Run full CatRAG with K=3 using (a) vanilla TF-IDF top-K and (b) diversity-re-ranked top-K. Measure Bias Score difference.
    3. Component Ablation on Validation: Systematically evaluate `Base`, `Functor-only`, `RAG-only`, and `Full CatRAG` on a held-out BBQ subset. Confirm synergistic uplift and find optimal d_u.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  How does the choice and granularity of anchor sets (D and O) affect CatRAG's debiasing efficacy across diverse demographic attributes and cultural contexts?
2.  Does the linearity of the functor-guided projection limit CatRAG's ability to mitigate non-linear bias associations, and can non-linear projections yield better fairness-utility trade-offs?
3.  How vulnerable is CatRAG to biases in the retrieval corpus, and does the diversity-aware re-ranking fully prevent the introduction of new stereotypes during evidence grounding?
4.  Can CatRAG's dual-mechanism approach generalize to generation tasks beyond multiple-choice QA, such as open-ended text generation or dialogue, where bias evaluation is less structured?

## Limitations
*   Anchor set specification: Exact demographic and occupational token lists for all BBQ categories are not provided, which is critical for constructing valid scatter matrices.
*   RAG corpus details: The content, size, and precise curation process of the "balanced, counter-stereotypical" evidence corpus are unspecified, making performance highly sensitive to its quality.
*   Hyperparameter sensitivity: Choices like projection subspace dimension (d_u), regularization (ε), retrieval K, and prompt formatting are critical but lack full ablation or justification in the paper.

## Confidence
*   **Functor projection mechanism:** High. The mathematical formulation (GEP) is clear and testable, assuming correct anchor definitions.
*   **RAG grounding mechanism:** Medium. The approach is sound, but efficacy depends entirely on the underspecified corpus.
*   **Synergy claim (superior trade-off):** Medium-Low. Ablations support it, but reproducibility and generality are uncertain due to corpus and hyperparameter unknowns.
*   **Overall SOTA results:** High for the reported experiments, but Low for confident reproduction or generalization due to the above unknowns.

## Next Checks
1.  **Anchor geometry validation:** Using example anchor sets (e.g., gender: `{man, woman}`, occupations: `{doctor, nurse}`), compute pre- and post-projection pairwise cosine similarities. Confirm demographic embeddings collapse while occupational distinctions remain separable.
2.  **Corpus audit & retrieval bias test:** Inspect retrieved evidence for a diverse query sample. Compute the stereotype preference (BS) of the retrieved passages alone to quantify if the corpus itself is balanced.
3.  **Ablation with retrieval variations:** Replicate the `Functor-only` vs. `RAG-only` vs. `Full` ablation. Then, test `Full` with (a) vanilla top-K retrieval and (b) no evidence (empty context) to isolate the contribution of diversity-aware retrieval.
