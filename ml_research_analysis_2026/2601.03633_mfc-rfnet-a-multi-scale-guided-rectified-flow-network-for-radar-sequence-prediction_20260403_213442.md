# MFC-RFNet: A Multi-scale Guided Rectified Flow Network for Radar Sequence Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03633
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03633
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Consistently outperforms baselines on multiple datasets; on SEVIR achieves CSI-M 0.3552 (+9% vs AlphaPre), HSS 0.4576 (+11%), and CSI-219 0.1079 (+85% vs DiffCast), with slower skill decay beyond 60 minutes.

## Executive Summary
Precipitation nowcasting from radar sequences suffers from multi-scale evolution, inter-frame misalignment, and efficient long-range context modeling. MFC-RFNet introduces a rectified flow generator enhanced by three core modules: a Feature Communication Module (FCM) for adaptive cross-scale interaction, a Condition-Guided Spatial Transform Fusion (CGSTF) for early-stage spatial alignment, and a Wavelet-Guided Skip Connection (WGSC) for detail-preserving decoder fusion. Vision-RWKV blocks at deep stages capture long-range dependencies linearly. Evaluated on SEVIR, MeteoNet, Shanghai, and CIKM, the model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with a favorable efficiency trade-off (27M parameters, 28.6 GFLOPs, 5-step sampler).

## Method Summary
MFC-RFNet uses a conditional U-KAN backbone trained with rectified flow. A separate conditional encoder extracts multi-scale features from the input history. The Feature Communication Module (FCM) enables bidirectional cross-scale feature exchange via pixel-wise attention and gated residual fusion. At shallow stages (1–3) of the main encoder, Condition-Guided Spatial Transform Fusion (CGSTF) predicts bounded displacement fields to warp features, correcting misalignment before it propagates. Wavelet-Guided Skip Connections (WGSC) apply discrete wavelet transform to conditional features, decomposing them into frequency bands to adaptively modulate skip connections at deep stages (bottleneck, first decoder). Vision-RWKV blocks are inserted serially at the encoder tail, bottleneck, and first decoder layer to inject global context with linear complexity. Training employs the rectified flow loss with a fixed 5-step ODE sampler for inference.

## Key Results
- On SEVIR, MFC-RFNet achieves CSI-M 0.3552 (+9% vs AlphaPre), HSS 0.4576 (+11%), and CSI-219 0.1079 (+85% vs DiffCast).
- Lead-time curves show significantly slower skill decay beyond 60 minutes compared to baselines.
- The model uses 27M parameters and 28.6 GFLOPs, demonstrating an effective accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
- Consistent improvements are reported across SEVIR, MeteoNet, Shanghai, and CIKM datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cross-Scale Feature Communication (FCM)
**Claim:** FCM mitigates weak coupling between fine-grained local details and global context by establishing adaptive cross-scale pathways.  
**Mechanism:** It forms bidirectional fusion (top-down, bottom-up, lateral) across the feature pyramid, then uses a pixel-wise cross-scale attention head to compute per-location softmax weights, allowing each spatial position to selectively draw from the most relevant resolution. A gated residual enhancement stage injects this context back into native-scale features.  
**Core assumption:** Simple concatenation or summation across scales underutilizes complementary cues; explicit coordination via learned pathways and pixel-wise selection is necessary for coherent spatiotemporal evolution.  
**Evidence anchors:** Ablations in Table 3 show removing cross-attention or multi-fusion degrades high-threshold skill; the corpus shows related multi-scale work but FCM's pixel-wise selection is novel in radar nowcasting.

### Mechanism 2: Condition-Guided Spatial Transform Fusion (CGSTF)
**Claim:** CGSTF corrects early-stage spatial misalignment caused by non-rigid motion (e.g., splitting/merging cells).  
**Mechanism:** From the conditioning radar sequence, it predicts a bounded displacement field (via `tanh` activation) that warps the main backbone's shallow features via differentiable bilinear sampling (`GridSample`), aligning them with conditional features before fusion at Stages 1–3.  
**Core assumption:** Inter-frame displacement induces misalignment in early encoder features; explicit, learned warping conditioned on the history is superior to implicit alignment through end-to-end training.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 details the formulation and rationale: "to perform explicit alignment at these early stages... reducing error propagation." The corpus contains spatial transformer references, but CGSTF's use for radar sequence alignment is specific.

### Mechanism 3: Wavelet-Guided Skip Connection (WGSC)
**Claim:** WGSC preserves high-frequency detail (sharp edges, small cells) while maintaining global structural consistency during decoder reconstruction.  
**Mechanism:** It applies 2D DWT to conditional features, decomposing them into low-frequency (structure) and high-frequency (detail) sub-bands. Separate convolutional stems produce guidance maps for each band. Combined with spatial/channel attention from concatenated encoder-decoder features, these modulate encoder and decoder streams via learned gates, with an adaptive fusion block synthesizing the final skip output.  
**Core assumption:** Standard skip connections indiscriminately pass encoder details, which can include noise; frequency-domain decomposition of conditional features provides principled control over detail/structural balance.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 specifies the pipeline and rationale: "Wavelet methods... help preserve high-frequency components while maintaining large-scale consistency." The corpus shows wavelet use in segmentation/denoising, but WGSC's adaptive fusion in nowcasting is novel.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rectified Flow Training:** Needed to learn near-linear probability-flow trajectories, enabling high-fidelity generation with few deterministic ODE steps (5-step sampler), critical for operational latency. *Quick check:* How does RF's straight trajectory (Eq. 1–2) differ from diffusion's stochastic reverse process, and why does this lead to fewer sampling steps?
- **Multi-Scale U-Net with Skip Connections:** Needed to capture radar evolution spanning large-scale advection and small-scale growth/decay. The U-KAN backbone's pyramid captures both, while skips transmit fine details; naive fusion is insufficient, necessitating WGSC. *Quick check:* Why are skip connections at *deep* stages (bottleneck/first decoder) particularly in need of WGSC's modulation compared to shallower skips?
- **Vision-RWKV (VRWKV) for Efficient Long-Range Context:** Needed because quadratic self-attention over spatiotemporal tokens is prohibitive. VRWKV provides linear-complexity sequence mixing at deep stages (lowest resolution) to inject global context without blowing up compute. *Quick check:* Why is placing VRWKV at the *encoder tail, bottleneck, and first decoder layer* a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off than placing it everywhere or only at the bottleneck?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Conditional Encoder -> FCM -> Main Backbone Encoder (Stages 1–3 with CGSTF) -> Bottleneck (VRWKV) -> Decoder (Stage 1 with VRWKV & WGSC; subsequent stages) -> Output.  
**Critical path:** Input history -> separate conditional encoder extracts multi-scale features -> FCM produces enhanced cross-scale features -> main backbone encoder integrates conditional features via CGSTF at shallow stages -> bottleneck and first decoder layer enriched with VRWKV -> WGSC modulates deep skip connections -> RF generator decodes future frames, sampled via 5-step ODE.  
**Design tradeoffs:** FCM's pixel-wise cross-attention adds compute but is justified vs. simpler fusion; CGSTF's `tanh`-bounded offsets stabilize training but limit maximum warp; WGSC's fixed DWT (non-differentiable) stabilizes training but sacrifices adaptability; VRWKV serial (one block per deep stage) outperforms parallel placement.  
**Failure signatures:** Blurry high-threshold predictions suggest WGSC failure; spatial misalignment (duplicated cells) indicates CGSTF offset issues; rapid skill decay beyond 60 minutes points to insufficient VRWKV context; training instability/NaN often due to unbounded CGSTF offsets or aggressive wavelet gates.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablate FCM (use plain concatenation) and expect drop in CSI-M/HSS, especially at long lead times and high thresholds.  
2. Replace CGSTF at Stages 1–3 with naive fusion (`F_out = Conv(Concat(F_main, F_cond))`) and expect degradation on fast-moving, small-scale datasets and early lead-time skill.  
3. Disable wavelet processor in WGSC (remove DWT branch) and expect reduced CSI at high thresholds (e.g., SEVIR CSI-219, CIKM CSI-40) and more fragmented small cells.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Does end-to-end differentiable wavelet processing yield gains over fixed-transform WGSC, and could it introduce instability? The paper uses fixed DWT to stabilize training but does not evaluate learned alternatives.
**Open Question 2:** What makes serial, one-block-per-stage VRWKV placement optimal, and is this generalizable beyond U-KAN? The finding is empirical and based on one dataset; interaction with other modules is unanalyzed.
**Open Question 3:** To what extent do the modules mitigate error accumulation in RF's ODE over many steps, and are their principles transferable to other few-step generative frameworks? The synergy between RF's path linearity and the spatial/temporal alignment modules remains hypothetical.
**Open Question 4:** How robust is MFC-RFNet to domain shifts in radar characteristics (e.g., beam blocking, different wavelengths), and which module is most critical for generalization? No cross-dataset generalization tests are provided.
**Open Question 5:** Can CGSTF's learned displacement fields be interpreted as physically meaningful motion vectors, and do they correlate with optical flow or storm tracking? The predicted offsets are used but not analyzed for meteorological plausibility.

## Limitations
- **U-KAN bottleneck specifics are underspecified:** Tokenization and KAN operator details are omitted, forcing implementation guesses that may affect performance.
- **Channel dimensions & VRWKV depth unknown:** Exact layer sizes and block counts are not provided; parameter count (~27M) must be reverse-engineered.
- **Module interaction effects not deeply explored:** Ablations measure isolated contributions, but potential synergies/conflicts between FCM, CGSTF, and WGSC remain unquantified.

## Confidence
- **FCM (cross-scale coordination):** High – mechanism clear, ablations show consistent gains on high-threshold metrics.
- **CGSTF (spatial alignment):** Medium – logical for early misalignment, but offset stability under varying motion is unevaluated.
- **WGSC (frequency-aware detail):** Medium – wavelet decomposition well-defined, but adaptive fusion may suppress high-frequency content if guidance is noisy.
- **Efficiency/skill trade-off:** High – reported GFLOPs, 5-step sampling, and multi-dataset improvements are credible.
- **Major uncertainties:** U-KAN implementation flexibility, exact channel sizing, and module interplay effects.

## Next Checks
1. **U-KAN replacement sanity check:** Implement bottleneck with a standard Transformer block (same channels) and compare CSI-M on SEVIR; >5% drop would confirm U-KAN's specific contribution.
2. **CGSTF offset magnitude analysis:** Visualize predicted displacement fields on high-motion samples; compute per-stage offset L1-norm distributions. Excessive magnitudes (>10 pixels) or inconsistency indicate poor warping regularization.
3. **WGSC frequency gate ablation:** Remove the DWT branch entirely and retrain; evaluate CSI at high thresholds (e.g., SEVIR CSI-219, CIKM CSI-40). Significant drop (>3% absolute) confirms wavelet guidance is critical.
