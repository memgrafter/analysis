# Enhancing Authorship Attribution with Synthetic Paintings
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04343
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04343
- **Reference Count:** 25
- **Primary Result:** Hybrid training with DreamBooth-generated synthetic paintings consistently improves ROC-AUC and accuracy in authorship attribution for low-data artists, with denser patch sampling providing further gains.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of data scarcity in painting authorship attribution, where some artists have very few available works. The authors generate synthetic paintings for each artist by fine-tuning Stable Diffusion with DreamBooth, using a small set of real paintings. These synthetic images are then mixed with real paintings to train a hybrid model. The discriminative system uses a LightGBM classifier on fused embeddings from three vision transformers (MaxViT, BEiT v2, VOLO), extracted from image patches. Evaluated on seven British artists, the hybrid approach significantly outperforms real-only baselines, particularly for artists with fewer than 15 real paintings. However, synthetic-only models suffer from a domain gap when tested on real data, and generation quality varies across artists, limiting robustness.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by cropping each artist's real paintings into 512×512 patches (200 per artist) to fine-tune a Stable Diffusion v1.5 model via DreamBooth, learning a unique token for the artist. This personalized generator produces 100 synthetic images per artist using the prompt "A full [TOKEN] painting" with a negative prompt to avoid cropped figures. For classification, all real and synthetic images are split into 224×224 patches under two regimes: M1 (moderate overlap) and M2 (double density along both axes). Each patch is processed by three frozen pre-trained transformers (MaxViT, BEiT v2, VOLO), and their embeddings are concatenated. A separate binary LightGBM classifier is trained per artist (e.g., "is this patch by Artist X?") on the hybrid or real-only patch sets. At inference, a held-out real painting is patched, classified, and patch predictions are aggregated (e.g., majority vote) to produce a painting-level attribution.

## Key Results
- Hybrid training (real + synthetic) consistently yields higher ROC-AUC and accuracy than real-only baselines, with especially large gains for artists having few real paintings (e.g., GD: 0.8746 → 0.9756).
- Denser patch sampling (M2) further improves hybrid model performance over M1 for most artists, reinforcing the value of fine-grained local cues.
- Synthetic-only models achieve near-perfect scores on synthetic test data but exhibit a severe domain gap when evaluated on real patches, with ROC-AUC plummeting for some artists (e.g., TB: 0.9593 → 0.5354).
- Hybrid-M2 emerged as the most robust configuration overall, though performance for certain artists (e.g., JH, TB) remained limited, indicating variability in generative fidelity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Data Augmentation Mitigates Low-Data Overfitting
- **Claim:** Mixing synthetic and real images into a hybrid training set improves classifier generalization for authorship attribution, especially when real data is scarce.
- **Mechanism:** Synthetic images augment the training distribution by adding stylistic variants that expand the coverage of an artist's visual vocabulary without requiring more real paintings. This effectively regularizes the classifier and mitigates overfitting on small real datasets.
- **Core assumption:** The generated images capture salient, non-spurious stylistic features (e.g., brushwork, palette, composition) that are consistent with the target artist's real work, rather than memorizing specific instances or introducing artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "hybrid approach combines these synthetic images with real paintings to train LightGBM classifiers... Results demonstrate that hybrid training consistently improves ROC-AUC and accuracy over real-only baselines, with notable gains for artists having few real paintings."
  - [section V.B] "For those with fewer real samples, gains were substantial. For instance, GD (7 paintings) improved from 0.8746 in Real-Only to 0.9756 in Hybrid-M2 (ROC-AUC)... These results highlight synthetic augmentation as an effective few-shot amplifier."
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on general authorship attribution or other domains; no direct evidence for hybrid data efficacy in painting attribution. *Assumption:* The observed benefit is specific to the visual similarity and low-data regime of this study.
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic generation fails to capture the artist's core stylistic manifold (e.g., as seen for TB and JH), the hybrid model may degrade relative to a real-only baseline or inject noise.

### Mechanism 2: Denser Patch Sampling Extracts More Discriminative Local Cues
- **Claim:** Increasing patch sampling density (M2 vs. M1) when using hybrid data further boosts performance by extracting more local stylistic cues from each image.
- **Mechanism:** Denser sampling (doubling patches per axis) provides more training samples per image, which is particularly valuable when the total dataset size (real + synthetic) is modest. This increases the probability of capturing fine-grained, discriminative textural and compositional details that define an artist's hand.
- **Core assumption:** The most salient stylistic signals for distinguishing closely-related artists are present at a local patch level and can be sampled redundantly without major loss of information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Denser patch sampling (M2) in hybrid setups further boosted performance."
  - [section V.A] "The M2 configuration... led to performance improvements in most cases. For GD, the ROC-AUC increased from 0.9659 in Hybrid-M1 to 0.9756 in Hybrid-M2... These findings indicate that denser patch extraction strengthens generalization, particularly for artists with scarce training data."
  - [section IV.A] Describes M2 as "doubling the number of patches along both axes" to capture "finer stylistic cues at the cost of higher redundancy."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence from neighbors on patch density effects.
- **Break condition:** If the increased redundancy primarily captures repetitive background or noise, or if the synthetic images themselves lack fine detail, the M2 strategy yields diminishing or negative returns (e.g., TB saw a drop in ROC-AUC with M2).

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Embedding Fusion Creates Richer Feature Representations
- **Claim:** Fusing embeddings from multiple pre-trained vision transformers (MaxViT, BEiT v2, VOLO) creates a richer, more discriminative feature representation for the LightGBM classifier than any single model.
- **Mechanism:** Each transformer architecture encodes different inductive biases (e.g., MaxViT's multi-axis attention, BEiT's masked modeling, VOLO's outlooker attention). Their concatenated embeddings provide a multi-faceted view of the patch, capturing hierarchical, semantic, and spatial relationships that are more robust for distinguishing subtle artistic styles.
- **Core assumption:** The stylistic differences between the British artists are encoded in complementary ways across these models' representation spaces, and a linear classifier (LightGBM) can effectively combine them.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III.E] "Visual embeddings were extracted from each patch using three transformer-based models: MaxViT, BEiT v2, and VOLO. These embeddings were concatenated and used to train a binary classifier per artist using LightGBM."
  - [section IV.C] "Instead of training separate models for each embedding type, we integrated all three feature representations into a unified classification model per artist, leveraging a richer and more diversified feature space."
  - [section V] The consistently high ROC-AUC scores (often >0.95) in multiple experiments suggest the multi-embedding approach is effective in this domain.
  - [corpus] Neighbors do not address multi-embedding fusion for painting attribution.
- **Break condition:** If the embedding spaces are highly correlated or one model dominates, the fusion provides marginal gain and increases computational cost. Failure could also occur if the LightGBM classifier cannot effectively learn from the high-dimensional concatenated space.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Model Personalization (DreamBooth)**
  - *Why needed here:* To generate stylistic synthetic paintings for each artist from a very small set of real examples (7-25 images). DreamBooth fine-tunes a base text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion) by associating a unique token with the artist's visual concept.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the primary risk when fine-tuning a diffusion model on only 200 image crops, and how does the study attempt to mitigate it?

- **Concept: Patch-Based Classification for Style**
  - *Why needed here:* Artistic style is often expressed in local textures, brushstrokes, and compositional elements. Processing the whole painting as one image might miss these cues or be computationally inefficient. Patches provide numerous localized samples for the classifier to learn from.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might patch-based classification be more suitable for authorship attribution of similar artists than whole-image classification?

- **Concept: Gradient Boosting on Embeddings (LightGBM)**
  - *Why needed here:* The high-dimensional embeddings from transformers are tabular data. LightGBM is efficient, handles class imbalance (via weighting), and is less prone to overfitting on small datasets compared to training a large neural network from scratch.
  - *Quick check question:* What are two advantages of using a tree-based model like LightGBM on pre-extracted embeddings versus an end-to-end trained CNN for this task?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Real Paintings (train) -> Crop/Resize (512x512) -> DreamBooth Fine-Tune (per artist) -> Synthetic Image Generation (100 per artist) -> Patch Extraction (M1/M2, 224x224) -> Multi-Embedding Extraction (MaxViT, BEiT v2, VOLO) -> Concatenate Embeddings -> Train LightGBM (binary per artist) -> Evaluate on Held-Out Real Painting Patches -> Aggregate Predictions (painting-level)
- **Critical path:** The core workflow requires completing DreamBooth fine-tuning for all artists before generating the synthetic set, which is then combined with real training patches. These combined patches (real+synthetic) are used to extract embeddings and train the LightGBM models. The held-out real painting is processed separately at the end.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Synthetic quantity vs. quality:* A fixed 100 synthetic images per artist aims to balance coverage and computational cost, but quality varies (e.g., cropped outputs), potentially harming hybrid performance for some artists.
  - *Patch density (M1 vs. M2):* M2 provides more samples per image (beneficial for small datasets) but introduces redundancy and higher compute. Its benefit is artist-dependent, failing for those with poor synthetic quality.
  - *Multi-embedding cost:* Using three transformers increases feature dimensionality (concanenation) and extraction time. The paper justifies this by claimed complementarity, though an ablation is lacking.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Generative failure:* Synthetic images show persistent cropped/partial figures or visually deviate from the artist's true style (visible in Figure 3). This correlates with poor performance in Synthetic-Real and Hybrid settings for that artist (e.g., TB, JH).
  - *Domain gap failure:* A large performance drop from Synthetic-Only (e.g., >0.98 AUC) to Synthetic-Real (e.g., TB: 0.9593 -> 0.5354). Indicates synthetic distribution does not match real test distribution.
  - *Artist-specific failure:* Low scores for an artist in Hybrid-M2 despite overall gains (e.g., JH ROC-AUC 0.6627). Suggests that artist's style was not well captured by the generative model or that its discriminative cues are not well-represented in the embedding space.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Real-Only Baseline:** Train LightGBM on M1 patches from real paintings only. Establishes the performance ceiling achievable without augmentation on the available data.
  2.  **Synthetic-Only:** Train and test on synthetic patches (M1). Measures the upper-bound performance if synthetic data perfectly matched the real domain and reveals the intrinsic quality/separability of the generated sets.
  3.  **Synthetic-to-Real (Synthetic-Real):** Train on synthetic patches (M1), test on real patches. Directly probes the domain gap. A large drop from Experiment 2 indicates poor synthetic-to-real transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive Synthetic Sampling:** How should the number of synthetic images per artist be adaptively determined based on the size and characteristics of the available real dataset to optimize hybrid model performance? The current study uses a fixed number (100), which may not be optimal across varying real data sizes.
- **Improving Generative Fidelity:** What specific modifications to the DreamBooth fine-tuning process or training data curation can reduce the generation of cropped compositions and improve stylistic fidelity? Negative prompts were insufficient, and the root cause (training crop composition) is hypothesized but untested.
- **Domain Adaptation for Synthetic-Real Gap:** Which domain adaptation methods (e.g., adversarial training, style alignment) are most effective in closing the performance gap observed when models trained on synthetic data are evaluated on real paintings? The gap is demonstrated but not addressed.

## Limitations
- Generative fidelity varies across artists; for some (e.g., TB, JH), synthetic images fail to capture core stylistic manifolds, limiting hybrid gains and indicating the approach's brittleness.
- Key implementation details are unreported (exact DreamBooth hyperparameters, patch extraction strides, LightGBM settings beyond learning rate, held-out painting selection), hindering exact reproduction.
- The construction of the negative class (patches from other artists) for the binary classifiers is unspecified, a major confounder that could significantly affect measured performance.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid training improves attribution, especially with few real samples:** **High**. Supported by consistent, significant gains across multiple artists in the presented experiments.
- **Denser patch sampling (M2) provides further boost in hybrid settings:** **Medium**. Evidence is strong for some artists (e.g., GD) but mixed (drop for TB), suggesting benefit is conditional on generative quality and artist-specific traits.
- **Multi-embedding fusion (MaxViT + BEiT v2 + VOLO) is superior to single models:** **Medium**. The paper shows strong results from the concatenated approach but does not ablate to prove complementarity; gains could come from simply having more features.
- **Synthetic-only models suffer a severe domain gap:** **High**. The dramatic performance collapse in the Synthetic->Real experiment clearly demonstrates the distribution mismatch.
- **Core assumption (synthetics capture *true* stylistic features):** **Low**. This is inferred from final metrics but not directly tested. The domain gap and artist-specific failures suggest the synthetics may capture spurious correlations for some artists.

## Next Checks
1.  **Style Capture Audit:** Compute distributional similarity (e.g., using a separate artist-agnostic style embedding model) between synthetic patches and *held-out real patches* per artist. A low similarity for TB/JH should correlate with their poor hybrid performance, validating the "style capture" break condition.
2.  **Negative Class Robustness Test:** Re-run Hybrid-M1 experiments using two alternative negative class constructions: (a) patches from all *other* artists pooled, and (b) patches from a single, stylistically distant artist. Significant variance in ROC-AUC would highlight sensitivity to this unreported parameter.
3.  **Embedding Fusion Ablation:** Train LightGBM classifiers for each artist in the hybrid (M1) setup using only one of the three embeddings (MaxViT, BEiT v2, VOLO). Compare performance to the fused model. If one model consistently matches or exceeds the fused performance, the claimed benefit of multi-embedding fusion is weakened.
