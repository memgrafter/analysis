# EarthSpatialBench: Benchmarking Spatial Reasoning Capabilities of Multimodal LLMs on Earth Imagery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15918  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15918  
- **Reference count:** 9  
- **Primary result:** EarthSpatialBench introduces the first large-scale benchmark for quantitative spatial reasoning on georeferenced Earth imagery, revealing substantial performance gaps among state-of-the-art MLLMs and highlighting limitations in spatial grounding and geometry-aware reasoning.

## Executive Summary
EarthSpatialBench addresses the lack of benchmarks for quantitative spatial reasoning on Earth imagery by generating 325K QA pairs from SatlasPretrain using GIS computations and diverse templates. The benchmark systematically varies spatial relations (distance, direction, topology), geometry types (bounding boxes, polygons, polylines), object reference modalities (text, visual overlays, coordinates), and question formats. Evaluations of six MLLMs (GPT-5, Claude, Gemini, Qwen3-VL, InternVL) show consistent brittleness: models perform better with explicit coordinates than textual descriptions, find polyline queries easier than polygon-polyline interactions, and exhibit limited spatial grounding despite visual overlays. These findings underscore the need for improved geometry-aware multimodal models.

## Method Summary
The dataset is built from SatlasPretrain’s high-resolution RGB Earth imagery with vector annotations. Geometries are standardized (small polygons converted to bounding boxes), and pairwise spatial relations are computed: distance via Euclidean metrics, direction via azimuth discretized into 8 bins, and topology via DE-9IM. QA pairs are generated by combining these computed relations with curated linguistic templates covering different reference styles and question formats. A multi-stage quality control process—automated bounds checks and selective manual review of complex scenes—ensures geometric validity and semantic clarity. Models are evaluated using standard or chain-of-thought prompts, with metrics like MAE, F1, and IoU against GIS-computed ground truth.

## Key Results
- GPT-5 achieves the lowest distance MAE (93.9 pixels); Qwen3-VL-Thinking excels in direction MAE (55.0°).  
- Models perform significantly better with explicit coordinates than textual descriptions; polyline-based queries are easier than polygon-polyline interactions.  
- Visual overlays improve localization tasks, but overall spatial grounding and reasoning remain limited across all tested MLLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** EarthSpatialBench generates a large-scale, geometrically accurate spatial QA dataset by combining GIS computations with template-based question generation.  
**Mechanism:** Vector annotations from SatlasPretrain are processed (polygons to bboxes for small objects). For each geometry pair, spatial relations are computed (distance via Euclidean, direction via azimuth bins, topology via DE-9IM). These are paired with linguistic templates to produce diverse QA pairs. Quality control includes automated checks (bounds, area/length thresholds) and manual review of high-density or boundary-intersecting scenes.  
**Core assumption:** Automated GIS computations provide unambiguous ground truth for spatial relations, and templates cover plausible query spaces without harmful ambiguity.  
**Evidence:** Section 3.2 describes geometry processing and quality control; abstract mentions GIS computations and templates.  
**Break condition:** If GIS metrics misalign with human spatial intuition (e.g., boundary cases, direction bins) or templates produce semantically ambiguous questions, benchmark validity degrades.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** EarthSpatialBench systematically isolates orthogonal factors of spatial reasoning difficulty (relation type, geometry, representation, format) to diagnose model weaknesses.  
**Mechanism:** The benchmark organizes questions along four independent dimensions: (1) spatial relation (distance, direction, topology), (2) geometry type (bbox, polygon, polyline), (3) object reference modality (textual, visual overlay, coordinates), and (4) question format (choice, quantitative, localization). This factorial design enables controlled comparisons (e.g., holding geometry constant while varying representation).  
**Core assumption:** These dimensions are sufficiently independent and collectively capture key axes of geospatial reasoning difficulty for MLLMs.  
**Evidence:** Abstract lists the four dimensions; section 3.1 details each. Corroborated by similar spatial benchmarks (e.g., SpatialMM, ORIGAMISPACE).  
**Break condition:** If dimensions are not orthogonal (e.g., certain geometries only appear with specific relations) or critical factors (e.g., image resolution, sensor noise) are omitted, conclusions about weaknesses may be confounded.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** EarthSpatialBench reveals that current MLLMs' spatial reasoning is brittle, with performance heavily dependent on explicit geometric input and degrade with complex geometries or ambiguous references.  
**Mechanism:** Evaluating multiple MLLMs across all dimensions shows patterns: (a) explicit coordinates outperform textual descriptions, (b) polyline-based queries are easier than polygon-polyline, (c) visual overlays improve localization but not counting/regression, (d) proprietary models excel at classification but lag in precise grounding. These indicate models lack integrated geometry-aware reasoning and rely on superficial cues.  
**Core assumption:** Performance variations across controlled dimensions directly reflect inherent model capabilities rather than dataset artifacts.  
**Evidence:** Abstract states key performance patterns; section 4 results tables/figures quantify dependencies. Neighbor papers (e.g., Foundation Models for Geospatial Reasoning) report similar struggles.  
**Break condition:** If dataset biases (e.g., template overfitting, geometry distribution skew) artificially create these patterns, conclusions about model limitations would be invalid.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vector Geospatial Representation**  
  *Why needed:* EarthSpatialBench uses bounding boxes, polylines, and polygons as fundamental units; understanding their differences (e.g., polygons capture true shape, polylines represent linear features) explains why geometry type affects model performance.  
  *Quick check:* Why might polyline–polyline queries be easier than polygon–polyline queries for MLLMs, based on geometric complexity?

- **Topological Relations (DE-9IM)**  
  *Why needed:* The benchmark includes within/intersect relations computed via the Dimensionally Extended Nine-Intersection Model (DE-9IM). This formalism underlies topological QA generation and must be distinguished from proximity or directional reasoning.  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between "within" and "intersect" in the context of a building (bbox) and a park (polygon)?

- **Grounding vs. Reasoning Decoupling**  
  *Why needed:* The paper finds models can sometimes reason correctly when given perfect coordinates but fail to ground textual descriptions. This decoupling is a core insight about current MLLMs.  
  *Quick check:* In EarthSpatialBench, what does "grounding" refer to, and why is it a prerequisite for spatial reasoning?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
SatlasPretrain → filter images with ≥2 distinct object classes → extract/standardize geometries → compute pairwise spatial relations → apply QA templates → quality filtering → split into train/test → prompt MLLMs → score outputs

**Critical path:**  
SatlasPretrain provides imagery and vector annotations. Geometry processor standardizes shapes (e.g., small polygons to bboxes) and computes spatial relations (distance, direction, topology) for all valid geometry pairs. QA generator combines results with template bank to produce QA pairs, applying negative sampling and thresholding. Quality controller runs automated checks (bounds, area/length) and manual review for complex scenes. Evaluator prompts MLLMs (with/without CoT) and computes task-specific metrics (MAE, F1, IoU) against ground truth.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Bbox vs. polygon/polyline:* Bboxes simplify small objects but lose shape detail; polygons preserve true regions but are harder to perceive.  
- *Template-based vs. free-form QA:* Templates ensure coverage and unambiguous answers but sacrifice natural language diversity.  
- *Pixel-space vs. real-world units:* All computations use pixel coordinates (512×512, 1m resolution) to avoid geodetic complexities, limiting generalization to other resolutions.  
- *CoT prompting:* Improves classification/regression but often harms localization, suggesting reasoning traces do not enhance spatial grounding.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High distance/angle MAE → model cannot estimate continuous metrics, possibly relying on categorical guesses.  
- Low localization F1 → poor visual grounding; inability to map abstract constraints to image regions.  
- Performance drop from coordinates to textual references → failure in language-based grounding (e.g., resolving "northernmost building").  
- Polygon-polyline errors → difficulty with irregular region-curve interactions, indicating limited geometric reasoning.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run all models on the full test set with default CoT prompts. Report overall MAE/F1 per relation type and question format to establish performance gaps (Tables 3–5).  
2. **Representation sensitivity test:** For a fixed model (e.g., GPT-5), compare performance on identical spatial relations under three reference styles: (a) explicit coordinates, (b) visual overlays, (c) textual descriptions. Hypothesis: coordinates > visual overlays > textual.  
3. **Geometry type ablation:** For distance and topology tasks, hold relation and reference style constant, vary geometry pair: (a) bbox-bbox, (b) bbox-polyline, (c) polygon-polyline. Expect polyline-polyline to be easiest, polygon-polyline hardest (per Fig. 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-temporal and multi-modal extension:** How can EarthSpatialBench be extended to multi-temporal and multi-modal data (e.g., DEM, SAR, LiDAR) for spatiotemporal reasoning? The current benchmark uses only single-date optical RGB imagery.
- **Architectural improvements for textual/geometry handling:** Why do MLLMs struggle with textual object references and polygon/polyline geometries, and how can architectures be improved? These weaknesses hinder practical geospatial analysis.
- **CoT and localization dissociation:** What causes the dissociation between chain-of-thought prompting and localization performance, and how can prompting or model design bridge this gap? Localization is essential for grounding, yet CoT often fails to improve it.

## Limitations
- **Dataset validity:** GIS-computed ground truths may not align with human spatial intuition for boundary cases (e.g., direction bins near cardinal transitions); template-based QA could introduce semantic ambiguity despite checks.
- **Dimensional orthogonality:** The four evaluation axes (relation, geometry, reference, format) may not be fully independent in distribution; some geometry-relation combinations could be rare or confounded.
- **Pixel-space restriction:** All metrics use pixel coordinates (1m resolution), which may not generalize to real-world geodetic reasoning or varying sensor resolutions.

## Confidence
- **Dataset generation mechanism (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Strong internal validation (automated + manual checks) but lacks human-intuition alignment studies and cross-dataset consistency tests.
- **Diagnostic factorial design (Mechanism 2):** High. Design mirrors established spatial benchmarking approaches; minor risk of confounding from uneven geometry-relation sampling.
- **Model brittleness conclusions (Mechanism 3):** High. Findings are consistent across six MLLMs and corroborated by independent neighbor studies; residual risk from undiscovered dataset artifacts.

## Next Checks
1. **Human-space alignment audit:** Sample 500 QA pairs (stratified by relation/geometry) and have expert annotators judge whether the GIS-computed answer matches intuitive spatial understanding. Target >90% agreement.
2. **Dimension orthogonality test:** Compute mutual information and conditional distributions between the four benchmark axes in the test set. Flag pairs with dependence >0.3 for stratified error analysis.
3. **Real-world unit transfer:** Re-run top 3 models on a held-out subset where distances/angles are converted to meters/degrees (using known 1m/pixel resolution). Measure performance delta to assess pixel-space overfitting.
