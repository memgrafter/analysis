# From Global to Granular: Revealing IQA Model Performance via Correlation Surface
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.21738
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21738
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Proposed GMC metric uses a 3D correlation surface to reveal fine-grained IQA model performance variations masked by global metrics like PLCC/SRCC and provides a stable global score (GMC_g).

## Executive Summary
Standard global image quality assessment (IQA) metrics (e.g., PLCC, SRCC) compress performance into a single number, obscuring how models behave across different quality regions and distortion scenarios. This paper introduces Granularity-Modulated Correlation (GMC), a framework that constructs a 3D correlation surface by modulating granularity (quality level and perceptual difference) with a Gaussian kernel and stabilizing the estimate with a kernel-smoothed distribution regulator. Analysis on four benchmark datasets shows GMC uncovers complementary model strengths (e.g., CLIP-IQA vs. NIQE) invisible to scalar metrics. The derived global score, GMC_g, demonstrates lower variance under distribution shifts, enabling more robust and scenario-specific model selection.

## Method Summary
GMC extends traditional correlation analysis by incorporating perceptual granularity. It first defines a granularity modulator using a Gaussian kernel over Mean Opinion Score (MOS) and absolute MOS difference (|ΔMOS|) bins. This modulator weights local correlation contributions. To produce a stable estimate from potentially sparse data, a kernel-smoothed distribution regulator is applied, mitigating sampling bias. The result is a continuous 3D surface (granularity vs. correlation) that maps model performance across quality/difference regimes. A global GMC_g score is derived by integrating this surface, offering a distribution-robust alternative to SRCC/PLCC.

## Key Results
- Global scalar metrics (PLCC/SRCC) hide significant performance variations across MOS and |ΔMOS| ranges, leading to misleading model comparisons.
- The GMC 3D surface reveals distinct, complementary behavioral regimes: e.g., CLIP-IQA excels in high-MOS/small-difference regions, while NIQE performs better in low-MOS/large-difference regions.
- The integrated global score GMC_g exhibits substantially lower variance than SRCC under varied test set sampling distributions (e.g., stratified vs. random), enhancing evaluation reliability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Image Quality Assessment (IQA) Metrics**: Objective measures (e.g., PSNR, SSIM, learned methods) predicting human-perceived quality. *Why needed*: Foundation for evaluating no-reference IQA models. *Quick check*: Reproduce PLCC/SRCC on a standard dataset like LIVE.
- **Correlation Analysis (PLCC/SRCC)**: Standard statistical measures for IQA evaluation. *Why needed*: Baseline metric that GMC improves upon. *Quick check*: Compute SRCC between model scores and MOS on KADID-10k.
- **Distribution Shift & Sampling Bias**: Variation in test set composition affecting metric stability. *Why needed*: Core problem GMC_g addresses. *Quick check*: Compare SRCC variance under random vs. stratified sampling of a dataset.
- **Kernel Density/Smoothing**: Non-parametric techniques for probability density estimation. *Why needed*: crux of the distribution regulator in GMC. *Quick check*: Implement and visualize a Gaussian KDE on a 1D score distribution.
- **Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS)**: Statistical method for stratified sampling from multi-dimensional distributions. *Why needed*: Used in GMC for robust surface modeling with limited samples. *Quick check*: Generate LHS samples over a 2D (MOS, |ΔMOS|) space and compare coverage to random sampling.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Raw Image & Distorted Pairs -> IQA Model -> Predicted Quality Scores -> MOS & |ΔMOS| Binning -> Gaussian Granularity Modulator -> Kernel-Smoothed Distribution Regulator -> 3D Correlation Surface (GMC) -> Integrated Score (GMC_g)

**Critical Path:**  
1. IQA model inference on test set. 2. Pair predictions with ground-truth MOS to compute per-bin correlations. 3. Apply Gaussian granularity weights across (MOS, |ΔMOS|). 4. Smooth the resulting weighted correlation estimates via kernel density. 5. Integrate smoothed surface for GMC_g.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Granularity vs. Stability**: Finer (MOS, |ΔMOS|) bins capture more detail but risk sparsity; Gaussian kernel and smoothing trade off resolution for robustness.
- **Global vs. Local Insight**: 3D surface provides rich diagnostics but is less immediately interpretable than a single number; GMC_g sacrifices some granularity for ease of comparison.
- **Computational Cost**: Surface modeling and smoothing add overhead vs. simple SRCC calculation, though offline analysis is feasible.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Surface shows high variance/noise even after smoothing, indicating insufficient data per bin or overly aggressive Gaussian bandwidth.
- GMC_g fails to reduce variance compared to SRCC, suggesting poor regulator kernel choice or extreme, unbalanced distribution shift.
- Model comparisons flip dramatically with small hyperparameter changes (bandwidth, kernel width), signaling instability.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Reproduce the 3D correlation surface for a single model (e.g., NIQE) on KADID-10k. Visualize slices at constant MOS and constant |ΔMOS|.
2. Conduct an ablation: compare surface stability (variance across bootstrapped test sets) with/without the kernel-smoothed distribution regulator.
3. Validate the LHS sampling claim: train a simple model on a subset sampled via LHS vs. random, then compare GMC_g variance on a fixed held-out set.

## Limitations
- Evaluation confined to four IQA datasets (KADID-10k, PIPAL, LIVEC, SPAQ); generalization to other distortions, video IQA, or culturally diverse images is unproven.
- GMC introduces hyperparameters (Gaussian bandwidth, smoothing kernel, LHS sample size) whose sensitivity across datasets is not fully analyzed; optimal settings may require per-dataset tuning.
- The practical utility of the 3D surface is unclear without dedicated visualization/analysis tools, potentially hindering adoption by practitioners who prefer scalar metrics.
