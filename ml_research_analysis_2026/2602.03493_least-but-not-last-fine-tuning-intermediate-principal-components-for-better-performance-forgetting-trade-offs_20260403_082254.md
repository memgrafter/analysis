# Least but not Last: Fine-tuning Intermediate Principal Components for Better Performance-Forgetting Trade-Offs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03493
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03493
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Selecting intermediate principal components for LoRA initialization reduces forgetting and improves accuracy versus top/bottom component methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates the performance-forgetting trade-off in Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), noting inconsistent results from existing methods that use either the top (PiSSA) or bottom (MiLoRA) principal components of weight updates. Through empirical analysis, it discovers that forgetting follows a U-shaped curve across components—directions at the extremes cause more forgetting. By fine-tuning only intermediate components, the proposed approach achieves a superior balance, yielding accuracy gains of up to 1% on vision (ViT-B) and NLP (LLaMA-2) benchmarks, enhanced robustness to high learning rates, and strong continual learning performance. This provides a simple, practical initialization strategy for LoRA.

## Method Summary
The method begins by computing the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the weight update matrix ΔW from a pre-trained model. Instead of selecting the top (highest singular values) or bottom (lowest) singular vectors, as in PiSSA and MiLoRA respectively, it selects a subset of intermediate singular vectors. These intermediate vectors are used to initialize the LoRA matrices (A and B). The model is then fine-tuned on the target task. This selection is motivated by the observed U-shaped relationship between principal components and forgetting.

## Key Results
- Forgetting exhibits a U-shaped curve across principal components: components with the highest and lowest singular values induce greater forgetting.
- Intermediate components improve the sum of accuracies by up to 1% on ViT-B across 11 ImageNet1k datasets and on LLaMA-2 across 3 NLP tasks.
- The approach shows robustness to high learning rates and achieves state-of-the-art results in continual learning settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a theoretical explanation for the U-shaped forgetting curve; it is an empirical observation. The hypothesized reason intermediate components reduce forgetting is that extreme components (top and bottom) may encode directions strongly associated with the pre-training distribution that conflict with new tasks, leading to catastrophic interference. Intermediate components might represent subtler, less task-specific variations that are more adaptable. However, this mechanism remains speculative and unproven.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**  
   *Why needed:* To efficiently fine-tune large models by approximating weight updates with low-rank matrices, avoiding full parameter updates.  
   *Quick check:* LoRA decomposes an update ΔW into two low-rank matrices B (rank r × d) and A (r × k) such that ΔW ≈ BA, where r ≪ d, k.

2. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) / SVD**  
   *Why needed:* To identify orthogonal directions (principal components) of maximum variance in the weight update matrix, enabling selective tuning.  
   *Quick check:* SVD of ΔW = UΣVᵀ yields singular vectors (columns of U, V) and singular values (Σ); principal components correspond to top singular vectors.

3. **Catastrophic Forgetting**  
   *Why needed:* To understand how fine-tuning on new tasks erases previously learned knowledge, a core challenge in transfer and continual learning.  
   *Quick check:* Catastrophic forgetting occurs when learning a new task causes a neural network to dramatically lose performance on earlier tasks due to weight overwriting.

4. **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**  
   *Why needed:* The computational tool to extract principal components from ΔW, forming the basis for component selection.  
   *Quick check:* For a matrix M, SVD factorizes it into M = UΣVᵀ, where U and V are orthogonal matrices and Σ is diagonal with non-negative singular values.

5. **Continual Learning**  
   *Why needed:* The method’s strong performance in continual learning scenarios highlights its utility for sequential task adaptation without forgetting.  
   *Quick check:* Continual learning involves training on a sequence of tasks while retaining performance on all previous tasks, often using regularization or architectural methods.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Pre-trained model weight updates → SVD decomposition → Selection of intermediate singular vectors → Initialization of LoRA matrices (A, B) → Fine-tuning on target task

Critical path: The process depends on accurate SVD of ΔW and proper selection of intermediate components; any error in SVD computation or heuristic for "intermediate" (e.g., rank percentile) directly impacts trade-offs.

Design tradeoffs:  
- *Component fraction:* The proportion of intermediate components selected balances capacity (more components) against inclusion of forgetting-prone extremes.  
- *Computational cost:* SVD on large weight matrices is expensive; approximations or randomized methods may be needed for very large models.  
- *Task specificity:* Optimal intermediate range may vary by task and dataset, limiting universality.

Failure signatures:  
- Selecting too many components (including extremes) reverts to PiSSA/MiLoRA behavior, increasing forgetting.  
- Selecting too few components underfits, reducing accuracy on the target task.  
- Poor SVD approximation (e.g., due to numerical instability) yields incorrect component ordering, breaking the U-curve assumption.

First experiments:  
1. Ablate the fraction of intermediate components (e.g., 10%, 25%, 50% of total rank) on a single dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100 with ViT-B) to identify a robust default range.  
2. Apply the method to a convolutional architecture (e.g., ResNet-50) on a standard transfer benchmark (e.g., Pascal VOC) to test architectural generality beyond transformers.  
3. Vary the learning rate schedule (e.g., constant vs. cosine decay) and optimizer (AdamW vs. SGD) to verify robustness claims under different training setups.

## Limitations
- The U-shaped forgetting pattern is empirically observed but lacks theoretical justification.
- Evaluation is confined to ViT-B and LLaMA-2; generalization to other architectures (e.g., CNNs, GPT-style decoders) and modalities is unverified.
- Sensitivity to the exact fraction of intermediate components is not studied; optimal selection may be task-dependent.

## Confidence
- U-shaped forgetting pattern: Medium (consistent empirical evidence, no theory)
- Superiority of intermediate components: High (replicated across vision/NLP with significant gains)
- Practical benefits (accuracy, robustness, continual learning): Medium (modest gains, implementation-dependent)
