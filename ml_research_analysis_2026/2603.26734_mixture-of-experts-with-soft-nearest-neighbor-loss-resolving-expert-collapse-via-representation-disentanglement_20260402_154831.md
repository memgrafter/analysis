# Mixture of Experts with Soft Nearest Neighbor Loss: Resolving Expert Collapse via Representation Disentanglement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26734
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26734
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** Integrating a Soft Nearest Neighbor Loss‑optimized feature extractor before MoE routing resolves expert collapse, yielding statistically significant accuracy gains on FashionMNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100 while increasing expert orthogonality and routing entropy.

## Executive Summary
Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) models often suffer from *expert collapse*: overlapping input distributions cause experts to learn redundant representations and the gating network to rigidly route most inputs to a single expert. This paper introduces a simple yet effective modification: insert a feature extractor trained with Soft Nearest Neighbor Loss (SNNL) between the input and the MoE gating/experts. SNNL minimizes intra‑class distances and maximizes inter‑class separation in the latent space, thereby disentangling class clusters. Experiments on MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR‑10 and CIFAR‑100 show that the disentangled space forces experts to learn more orthogonal weights (lower similarity), encourages the gating network to distribute inputs more evenly (higher entropy), and achieves modest but statistically significant improvements in classification accuracy on the three non‑trivial datasets. Performance drops slightly on MNIST, suggesting over‑regularization when data is already separable.

## Method Summary
The proposed architecture adds a lightweight CNN feature extractor (two convolutional blocks) before the standard MoE components. During training, a composite loss is minimized: the usual MoE loss plus a weighted SNNL term computed on the extractor’s hidden representations. SNNL encourages points of the same class to cluster while pushing apart different classes, using a temperature‑controlled soft neighbor probability. The gating network and expert networks are trained jointly with this regularizer. At inference, the gating network selects a single expert per input. The method directly targets structural collapse by pre‑conditioning the input space, rather than modifying gating or expert loss functions.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy:** On FashionMNIST (91.61% vs 91.33%), CIFAR10 (71.23% vs 70.91%), and CIFAR100 (36.74% vs 35.75%), the SNNL‑augmented MoE outperforms the baseline with statistically significant gains (Wilcoxon signed‑rank test). Accuracy on MNIST drops slightly.
- **Expert orthogonality:** Pairwise Embedding Similarity (cosine similarity between first‑layer weights of experts) decreases on FashionMNIST and CIFAR100, indicating more diverse expert representations.
- **Routing flexibility:** Expert Specialization Entropy (entropy of per‑class routing distribution) increases on the three non‑trivial datasets, showing a softer, more balanced assignment strategy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent Disentanglement via SNNL
SNNL minimizes intra‑class distances and maximizes inter‑class separation in the feature extractor’s hidden layers. By pulling together same‑class points and pushing apart different classes, it creates compact, well‑separated class clusters in the latent space. This topological regularization ensures that the subsequent MoE components operate on a space where class identity is already broadly encoded by neighborhood structure, reducing the need for experts to learn overlapping features. Break conditions: temperature *T* too high blurs class boundaries; too low causes numerical instability; excessive SNNL weight *α* may overfit clustering at the expense of classification‑relevant features.

### Mechanism 2: Simplified Gating with Flexible Routing
The disentangled latent space simplifies the gating network’s task. Instead of simultaneously learning feature extraction and expert assignment, the gating network only needs to route inputs to experts based on already separated class clusters. This reduces pressure to hard‑route and allows a softer, higher‑entropy distribution. Break conditions: on extremely simple datasets (e.g., MNIST) where classes are linearly separable, the extra disentanglement is unnecessary and can force over‑regularization, reducing accuracy and entropy.

### Mechanism 3: Orthogonal Experts through Reduced Input Overlap
Because the gating network receives well‑separated clusters, each expert is allocated a distinct region of the latent space. To minimize the composite loss, each expert’s first‑layer weights adapt to features unique to its assigned region, leading to lower cosine similarity (i.e., higher orthogonality) between expert weight matrices. Break conditions: if SNNL is too weak, the latent space remains entangled and experts collapse; if too strong, the feature extractor may degrade classification features, indirectly harming orthogonality.

## Architecture Onboarding
The system is a pipeline: Input → CNN Feature Extractor (two conv‑bn‑relu‑pool blocks) → flattened feature vector. This vector feeds two branches: (1) a linear gating network that produces expert selection probabilities, and (2) *n* expert networks (single hidden layer, 128 ReLU units) that output class logits. Training uses composite loss `L = L_moe + α * min_i ℓ_snn(layer_i)`, where `ℓ_snn` is computed on the extractor’s hidden representations. The forward pass computes the feature vector, applies SNNL (training only), then obtains gating probabilities and expert outputs. The loss is backpropagated through all components. **Design tradeoffs:** SNNL weight *α* controls disentanglement strength (too high causes over‑regularization), temperature *T* influences cluster sharpness, batch size affects SNNL’s pairwise distance estimate (larger is better but more memory), and feature extractor capacity balances richness vs. overfitting. **Failure signatures:** accuracy drop on simple datasets indicates over‑regularization; high pairwise similarity and low entropy indicate failed disentanglement; NaN/Inf in SNNL suggests numerical instability or extreme temperature; UMAP still shows entangled clusters. **First 3 experiments:** (1) Compare baseline vs. SNNL‑augmented MoE on all datasets with accuracy, SIM, ENT, and statistical tests. (2) Ablate SNNL weight *α* on a CIFAR10 subset to observe correlation with pairwise similarity. (3) Visualize feature manifolds with UMAP to qualitatively confirm disentanglement.

3. **Alternative representation losses** – Are other contrastive or clustering losses (e.g., Supervised Contrastive Learning) equally effective for inducing expert diversity?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity:** Key parameters (*α*, *T*, number of experts) are not explicitly reported, hindering reproducibility.
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Experiments only cover four small image datasets; no evidence on large‑scale or diverse domains.
- **Sensitivity and validation:** Over‑regularization on MNIST indicates high sensitivity to *α*; secondary metrics (SIM, ENT) lack statistical significance testing; quadratic SNNL cost may restrict batch size and training dynamics.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1** (SNNL disentangles latent space): Medium – supported by UMAP visualizations and ablation, but no independent clustering metrics.
- **Claim 2** (Simplified gating enables flexible routing): Medium – higher ENT suggests more even routing, but per‑sample entropy not measured.
- **Claim 3** (Orthogonal experts and accuracy gains): Low‑Medium – gains are modest (≤1%) and dataset‑specific; regression on MNIST; hyperparameter sensitivity unassessed.

## Next Checks
