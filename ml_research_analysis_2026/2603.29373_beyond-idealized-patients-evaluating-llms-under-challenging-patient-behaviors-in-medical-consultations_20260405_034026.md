# Beyond Idealized Patients: Evaluating LLMs under Challenging Patient Behaviors in Medical Consultations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29373
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29373
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit high failure rates on contradictory and implausible patient statements, with multi-turn interactions rarely recovering from initial mistakes; simple prompting interventions often cause overcorrection on benign inputs.

## Executive Summary
Medical dialogue systems need to handle challenging patient behaviors (e.g., contradictory information, factual inaccuracies, self-diagnosis, care resistance) that deviate from idealized interactions. This paper introduces CPB-Bench, a bilingual benchmark of 692 multi-turn dialogues annotated with these behaviors, to evaluate large language models (LLMs) in realistic consultation scenarios. Testing multiple LLMs reveals consistent failure patterns, especially on information contradiction and medically implausible claims. Multi-turn follow-ups seldom corrected initial errors. Four intervention strategies (Chain-of-Thought, Oracle Instruction, Assessment, Self-Review) produced inconsistent improvements and frequently increased unnecessary corrections on normal inputs. Stress tests with abnormal clinical values (e.g., extreme blood sugar) showed systematic failures such as false reassurance and ignoring critical numbers. The authors release the dataset and code to support further research.

## Method Summary
The authors built CPB-Bench by filtering four existing medical dialogue datasets (MediTOD, SIMORD, MedDG, IMCS) with an LLM filter (GPT-4o) and then human adjudication (three reviewers) to label challenging patient turns. Evaluation uses an LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o) with behavior-specific failure criteria; flagged cases undergo human review to establish ground truth. The study assesses several LLMs on single-turn responses, measures multi-turn correction rates, tests four prompt-based interventions (CoT, Oracle, Assessment, Self-Review), and constructs stress tests by synthetically inserting abnormal clinical values or contradictions. Metrics include per-behavior failure counts, unnecessary correction rates on normal inputs, and pattern analysis of failure modes.

## Key Results
- LLMs fail most frequently on information contradiction (e.g., 68 failures for Llama-3.1-8B) and struggle with medically implausible claims.
- Multi-turn follow-ups rarely recover from initial failures, indicating poor state repair.
- Interventions generally do not reduce failures and often increase unnecessary corrections on normal inputs (e.g., Oracle Instruction reduced failures but caused 141 unnecessary corrections for Llama-3.1-8B).
- Stress tests expose systematic mishandling of abnormal values: models commonly provide false reassurance or ignore critical numbers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Inadequate Contradiction Handling
LLMs fail to safely manage consultations when patients present mutually contradictory information within or across turns. Models process each turn with limited context and without explicit consistency checks, so they adopt one side of a contradiction (often the most recent or salient claim) without flagging the inconsistency or seeking clarification, thereby propagating incorrect premises. Evidence: The paper reports that "models often proceed by adopting one of the conflicting patient statements without explicitly addressing the inconsistency" and shows high failure rates on contradiction cases. A successful model would consistently (>80%) identify conflicts and ask targeted clarifying questions before proceeding.

### Mechanism 2: Interventions Misaligned with Interactional Policy
Generic reasoning interventions (CoT, Self-Review) do not reliably mitigate failures and can degrade performance on normal inputs via overcorrection. Interventions that encourage deliberation may amplify sycophantic tendencies or cause the model to overfit to the intervention heuristic (e.g., "always correct patient"), leading to unnecessary corrections on benign statements. This suggests safety failures stem from a lack of appropriate interactional policy rather than a lack of step-by-step reasoning. Evidence: Table 3 shows interventions generally increase unnecessary corrections; Oracle Instruction and Patient Statement Assessment lead to substantial degradation.

### Mechanism 3: Deference to Patient Framing of Abnormal Values
LLMs exhibit systematic, pattern-specific failures when patients state clinically abnormal values coupled with false reassurance (e.g., "my blood sugar is 800, but it's under control"). Models appear to defer to patient self-assessment and fail to apply absolute clinical thresholds or trigger a value verification subroutine, likely because training objectives prioritize user alignment and fluency over critical evaluation of numeric claims. Evidence: Stress test results (Table 5) show dominant failure types "False Reassurance" and "Ignore & pivot" across a range of abnormal values, indicating no internal calibrated representation of clinical abnormality that overrides patient framing. A successful model would consistently (>90%) query the abnormal value or indicate it requires medical attention.

## Foundational Learning
- **Clinically-Grounded Taxonomy Construction**  
  *Why needed:* The benchmark requires precise, behavior-specific definitions of "challenging" that map real clinical communication problems (e.g., contradiction, resistance) to observable, annotatable utterances and failure criteria.  
  *Quick check:* For the "Care Resistance" category, what specific model response would constitute a failure, and what real-world clinical communication problem does it mirror?

- **Multi-Turn Dialogue State Tracking for Safety**  
  *Why needed:* Safety depends on the model's ability to maintain and correct its internal representation of patient facts across turns; the evaluation isolates the first response to a challenging turn and tests whether multi-turn follow-ups can repair failures.  
  *Quick check:* Why does the paper find that "multi-turn follow-ups rarely corrected initial failures," and what does this imply for deploying medical chat assistants?

- **Intervention Strategy Evaluation with Dual Metrics**  
  *Why needed:* Interventions must be evaluated not only for reducing failures on challenging inputs but also for preserving appropriate responses on normal inputs (avoiding unnecessary corrections). This dual-objective prevents brittle solutions.  
  *Quick check:* What is the risk of an intervention that reduces failures on "Self-diagnosis" cases but causes many "unnecessary corrections" on normal symptom descriptions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Source Data Pipeline (ingests 4 existing medical dialogue datasets) → Annotation Engine (LLM filter + human adjudication) → CPB-Bench. CPB-Bench → Evaluation Harness (may apply Intervention Applier before generating doctor response) → LLM-as-judge (with failure criteria) → Human review for flagged cases → Metrics Calculator (computes failure counts, multi-turn correction rates, unnecessary corrections). Stress Test Generator produces edited dialogues that bypass CPB-Bench and feed directly into Evaluation Harness.

- **Critical path:** Build CPB-Bench annotation → Establish baseline failure profiles → Run intervention suite → Construct & evaluate stress tests.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  1. *Scalability vs. Ground Truth:* LLM-as-judge enables large-scale assessment but relies on limited human review (positives/sampled negatives), risking missed subtle errors.  
  2. *Realism vs. Control:* Stress tests use minimal edits on real dialogues to preserve context, offering controlled isolation of failure modes at the cost of ecological validity.  
  3. *Breadth vs. Depth:* The benchmark covers four behaviors across 692 dialogues, with deeper analysis on contradiction and abnormal values, leaving self-diagnosis and care resistance less explored.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High-level: Failure rates spike for Information Contradiction; multi-turn recovery is lowest.  
  - Pattern-level: In abnormal-value tests, "False Reassurance" and "Ignore & Pivot" dominate failure types.  
  - Intervention-level: Oracle Instruction reduces failures but catastrophically increases unnecessary corrections (e.g., +141 for Llama-3.1-8B), showing overfitting to the explicit instruction.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Annotation replication:** Apply the provided LLM filter prompt (Prompt 0) to a subset of MedDG and have human annotators label behavior categories and failures; verify inter-annotator agreement >90% and consistent application of Table 1 criteria.  
  2. **Baseline profiling:** Run single-turn evaluation on GPT-4o-mini across the full CPB-Bench test set; reproduce the per-behavior failure distribution from Figure 2 (left) and inspect 5–10 failure cases per category to confirm alignment with definitions.  
  3. **Intervention stress test:** Apply Oracle Instruction and Chain-of-Thought prompts to GPT-4o-mini on all 692 cases; replicate Table 3’s pattern: Oracle reduces failures but raises unnecessary corrections on normal controls, while CoT shows inconsistent or negative effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What other challenging patient behaviors beyond the four studied (information contradiction, factual inaccuracy, self-diagnosis, care resistance) are clinically relevant?  
2. How can medical LLM evaluation incorporate clinician-in-the-loop protocols to improve reliability of safety assessment?  
3. To what extent do observed failure patterns generalize to real-world deployment settings with actual patients?  
4. Why do simple prompting interventions often fail to reduce failures and sometimes cause unnecessary corrections on normal inputs, and how can we design more effective mitigation strategies?

## Limitations
- Dataset provenance: Exact splits and licensing for source datasets (MediTOD, SIMORD, MedDG, IMCS) are unspecified; reproduction depends on the provided GitHub repository.
- Annotation ground truth: Human annotator qualifications and full adjudication process are not fully described, limiting independent verification of failure criteria application.
- Evaluation validity: Primary reliance on LLM-as-judge with limited human review may miss subtler errors or introduce annotator bias.
