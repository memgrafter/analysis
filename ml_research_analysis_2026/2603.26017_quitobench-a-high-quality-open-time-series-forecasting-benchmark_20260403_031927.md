# QuitoBench: A High-Quality Open Time Series Forecasting Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26017
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26017
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A regime-balanced benchmark from Alipay traffic reveals a context-length crossover (DL leads at short context, foundation models dominate at long context) and identifies forecastability as the strongest difficulty driver.

## Executive Summary
QuitoBench is a new, large-scale evaluation suite for time series forecasting constructed from a billion-token corpus of Alipay application traffic. It addresses flaws in prior benchmarks—such as data leakage, skewed distributions, and short series—by enforcing a leakage-free temporal split and stratifying series across eight trend×seasonality×forecastability (TSF) regimes. With 1,290 uniformly long test series, it enables long-context evaluation. Benchmarking ten models yields four major findings: deep learning models excel with short context (L=96), but pretrained foundation models pull ahead with long context (L≥576); forecastability is the dominant factor in forecasting difficulty; task-specific deep learning models match foundation models despite using 59× fewer parameters; and scaling training data provides larger gains than scaling model size.

## Method Summary
QuitoBench is derived from Alipay telemetry (Quito-Min at 10-minute frequency, Quito-Hour at hourly). A global temporal cutoff (2023-07-28) is fixed before any analysis to prevent leakage. For each series, trend and seasonality are computed via STL decomposition on the pre-cutoff training segment (first 80%), and forecastability is derived from spectral entropy (1−H) using Welch's method. Continuous TSF scores are binarized at τ=0.4 to assign each series to one of eight regime cells. Stratified sampling then selects 1,290 series for the test set, ensuring near-uniform representation (~12.5% per cell). Evaluation uses dense rolling windows (stride=1) over the contiguous post-cutoff test segment. For each of 18 configurations (context lengths L∈{96,576,1024}, horizons H∈{48,288,512}, modes {multivariate, univariate}), models are ranked per series by MAE, and results are aggregated via mean rank. Deep learning models are tuned and trained on the train/valid splits; foundation models are used zero-shot; statistical baselines are fit on train+valid.

## Key Results
*   **Context-length crossover:** Deep learning models lead at short context (L=96), but foundation models dominate at long context (L≥576).
*   **Forecastability gap:** The hardest regime (low forecastability) has a 3.64× higher MAE than the easiest, making forecastability the strongest difficulty driver.
*   **Parameter efficiency:** Task-specific deep learning models match or exceed foundation models despite using 59× fewer parameters.
*   **Data scaling:** Increasing training data yields larger performance gains than increasing model size for both model families.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Regime-balanced sampling across TSF cells prevents prevalence-driven conclusions by ensuring aggregate metrics reflect model capability, not data distribution skew.
- **Mechanism:** Stratified sampling enforces near-uniform representation (~12.5% per cell) across the eight trend×seasonality×forecastability regimes, enabling both micro-averaged (per-series) and macro-averaged (per-regime) aggregation that equally weights all operational dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** Intrinsic statistical properties (trend, seasonality, forecastability) are more principled predictors of forecasting difficulty than application-domain labels.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "regime-balanced benchmark... coverage across eight TSF regimes" ; [section 2.2] "apply stratified sampling over the eight TSF regime cells... near-uniform coverage"
- **Break condition:** If the TSF threshold (τ=0.4) yields degenerate cell occupancy (e.g., >80% in one cell), the balanced design fails and regime-level insights become unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The context-length crossover arises from differing inductive biases: task-specific architectures excel at local pattern fitting with short histories, while pre-trained models exploit reusable long-range structure.
- **Mechanism:** Foundation models, pre-trained on diverse corpora, improve 43–50% when context extends to L≥576 by capturing delayed dependencies and stable seasonal motifs; deep learning models, optimized for the task, gain only 7–12%, reaching local optima quickly.
- **Core assumption:** Pre-training provides a library of temporal motifs that require sufficient context to activate, whereas task-specific models are tuned to the local training distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "context-length crossover where deep learning models lead at short context (L=96) but foundation models dominate at long context (L≥576)" ; [section 3.2 Analysis II] Table 4 and surrounding text show MAE reversal; Figure 14 visualizes the crossover.
- **Break condition:** If evaluation stops at L<576, the crossover is never observed, leading to incorrect conclusions about model superiority.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Forecastability (spectral entropy-derived signal regularity) is the dominant difficulty driver, producing a 3.64× MAE gap between easiest and hardest regimes.
- **Mechanism:** Low forecastability (high spectral entropy) indicates noise-dominated, irregular dynamics that obscure trend and seasonal signals, raising error floors for all models; high forecastability series remain predictable even with weak trend/seasonality.
- **Core assumption:** Spectral entropy captures overall temporal regularity and predictability more directly than STL component strengths.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "forecastability is the strongest difficulty driver, producing a 3.64× MAE gap" ; [section 3.2 Analysis IV] Table 13 shows the 3.64× gap; Table 14 reveals foundation models' greater robustness to low forecastability.
- **Break condition:** If spectral entropy is miscalculated (e.g., wrong windowing or normalization), regime labels become meaningless and the forecastability dominance disappears.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Raw Collection (Alipay telemetry) -> Sanitization (deduplication, filtering) -> TSF Diagnostics (STL + Welch PSD) -> Leakage-Free Splitting (global cutoff) -> Balanced Sampling (stratified quota) -> QuitoBench test files

- **Critical path:**
  1. Establish global temporal cutoff **before** any regime computation to prevent leakage.
  2. Compute TSF diagnostics **only on training data** to avoid test-set information seepage.
  3. Sample balanced test set **after** regime labeling, targeting uniform cell occupancy.
  4. Validate regime balance (~12.5% per cell) and series length uniformity (5.9k–15.3k steps).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Regime balance vs. total series count: Uniform 1,290-series benchmark vs. larger but skewed uncurated corpus.
  - Dense rolling windows vs. compute cost: ~1.6×10⁷ predictions per model (stable MAE) vs. sparse non-overlapping windows (faster but noisy).
  - Single provenance vs. perceived diversity: Alipay traffic spans 9 verticals (higher TSF entropy than Timer) but may miss cloud/HPC patterns.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Regime collapse: If one TSF cell drops below ~5% of test items, macro-averaging becomes unreliable.
  - Series shortness: If >10% of series have <1,000 steps, long-context evaluation (L=1024) fails.
  - Leakage detected: Overlap between any series' train window and another's test window (check item-id and temporal boundaries).
  - Diagnostic inconsistency: Recomputing TSF on a held-out slice yields different regime labels for a series.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Verify TSF diagnostics: Pick 10 series, manually plot STL decomposition and periodogram, confirm τ=0.4 binarization matches intuitive structure.
  2. Check split integrity: For a random sample, ensure no test timestamp appears in any training series' timeline; confirm global cutoff is respected.
  3. Run smoke test: Evaluate CrossFormer (L=96, H=48, UV) on 10 QuitoBench series; expect MAE ~0.35 and rank ~3–4 against all 10 models. If MAE >1.0, pipeline likely broken.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **Probabilistic & Anomaly Tasks:** How do models perform on probabilistic forecasting and anomaly detection using QuitoBench's protocol? The authors plan these extensions but have not evaluated them. Evidence needed: Implement rolling-window evaluation for CRPS/quantile loss and anomaly metrics, then analyze if the same crossovers, regime specializations, and scaling laws hold.
2.  **Generalizability to Other Cloud Traces:** Do core findings (e.g., forecastability dominance) generalize to independent cloud operations datasets (Azure, Google Borg)? Limitations note single-provenance risk; future work explicitly calls for a direct TSF comparison. Evidence needed: Apply QuitoBench's diagnostic and sampling pipeline to public cloud traces, compute forecastability gaps and regime rankings, and check consistency with Alipay results.
3.  **Optimal TSF Threshold τ:** What is the optimal binarization threshold for TSF diagnostics to maximize predictive power for model selection? The paper uses τ=0.4 for balance and shows robustness to small changes, but optimality is unstudied. Evidence needed: Sweep τ across [0.1,0.9] on multiple corpora, measuring inter-regime error variance (ANOVA F-statistic) and intra-regime model agreement (rank correlation); select τ that maximizes these criteria.

## Limitations
- TSF regime labels are sensitive to implementation details (STL parameters, Welch PSD settings); deviations can break the balanced design and invalidate regime-level insights.
- Leakage prevention strictly depends on correct temporal splitting; any oversight (e.g., calculating diagnostics on test data) invalidates results.
- Single-provenance data (Alipay) may limit generalizability to other domains (e.g., HPC, CDNs).
- Foundation model inference details (tokenization, context handling) are not fully disclosed, complicating exact reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Context-length crossover (DL leads at L=96, foundation dominates at L≥576):** Medium – Supported by Table 4/Figure 14, but sensitive to long-context handling and model versions.
- **Forecastability as strongest driver (3.64× gap):** High – Consistently observed across macro-averaged tables and entropy correlation analysis.
- **DL models match foundation despite 59× fewer parameters:** Medium – Depends on exact tuning and random seeds; small variations could shift rankings.
- **Scaling data > scaling model size:** Medium – Shown via data ablation, but training details and corpus representativeness may affect conclusion.
