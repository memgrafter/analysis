# Free Lunch for Pass@$k$? Low Cost Diverse Sampling for Diffusion Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.04893  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04893  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Training-free orthogonal repulsion (ODD) boosts Pass@16 by ~8-9% on code/math tasks with <6% overhead.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion language models (DLMs) often suffer from mode collapse during multi-sample generation, producing redundant outputs that fail to improve Pass@k metrics critical for code generation and mathematical reasoning. The authors introduce ODD (Orthogonal Diverse Diffusion), a training-free inference-time technique that sequentially adjusts each generated sample to repel from the feature space of previous samples in a batch. By extracting quality-weighted features from logits via max-pooling and maintaining an orthogonal basis with Gram-Schmidt, ODD encourages diversity without additional training. Evaluated on HumanEval and GSM8K using LLaDA-8B-Instruct, ODD yields consistent Pass@16 improvements (e.g., HumanEval: 42.7% → 51.3%) with negligible computational overhead (~5.8% time, 3.9% VRAM), demonstrating a Pareto improvement for practical deployment.

## Method Summary
ODD operates during decoding without model modifications. For each new sample in a batch, it extracts a feature vector from the current logits via max-pooling across token dimensions, weighted by a quality estimate (e.g., per-sample confidence). It then maintains an orthogonal basis of feature vectors from previously generated samples using Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. The logits are updated to maximize the component of the new feature vector that is orthogonal to this subspace, effectively repelling the sample from predecessors in the represented feature space. This process repeats sequentially for all samples in the batch, requiring only forward passes and lightweight linear algebra.

## Key Results
- HumanEval Pass@16 improves from 42.7% (baseline) to 51.3% with ODD (α=8, temperature=1.0).
- GSM8K Pass@16 improves from 81.3% to 84.0% under identical settings.
- Overhead is minimal: ~5.8% increase in generation time and ~3.9% extra VRAM usage versus baseline sampling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ODD mitigates mode collapse by explicitly encouraging diversity in the feature space derived from logits, which correlates with semantic content. Orthogonal repulsion ensures each new sample occupies a direction not covered by previous ones, increasing solution coverage for Pass@k. The max-pooled logit features capture high-impact token-level decisions, and quality weighting prevents low-confidence samples from dominating the repulsion. Gram-Schmidt provides a mathematically stable way to compute the orthogonal subspace incrementally. The method works because diffusion decoding’s iterative denoising allows controlled logit perturbations without straying from valid text distributions, and the feature-space alignment with task success (code/math correctness) makes repulsion beneficial rather than random.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Feature Extraction (logits → max-pooled vector) → Orthogonal Basis Maintenance (Gram-Schmidt on previous features) → Logit Update (add orthogonal component) → New sample

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate initial sample via standard diffusion decoding.  
2. Extract feature vector from final logits.  
3. For each subsequent sample:  
   a. Compute orthogonal complement of all prior feature vectors.  
   b. Modify logits to amplify the orthogonal component of the new feature.  
   c. Continue decoding with updated logits.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Feature representation:* Max-pooling is simple but may miss sequential patterns; alternatives (mean-pool, hidden states) could affect diversity/quality balance.  
- *Orthogonalization:* Gram-Schmidt is intuitive but may become numerically unstable for large k; QR decomposition could be more robust but costlier.  
- *Quality weighting:* Adds robustness but requires a reliable quality estimator; poor estimates may bias repulsion away from correct solutions.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Degraded correctness without diversity gains (e.g., Pass@k unchanged or lower).  
- Exponential increase in time/VRAM with sample count (indicating orthogonalization bottleneck).  
- High variance across random seeds (suggesting instability in repulsion strength).

**First three experiments:**  
1. Ablate feature extraction: compare max-pool, mean-pool, and final hidden state features on HumanEval with fixed α.  
2. Replace Gram-Schmidt with incremental QR decomposition to assess stability and performance for k > 32.  
3. Test ODD on a different DLM (e.g., Diffusion-LM) and a non-code task (e.g., summarization) using both Pass@k and diversity metrics (e.g., self-BLEU).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly raised by the authors; the paper focuses on empirical validation rather than theoretical analysis or broader implications.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to a single model (LLaDA-8B-Instruct) and two tasks (HumanEval, GSM8K); generalization to other DLMs and domains is unknown.
- Feature extraction design (max-pool over logits) is heuristic and not compared to alternatives like hidden states or mean pooling.
- Overhead figures lack hardware/batch size context; scaling behavior for large k (e.g., Pass@100) and Gram-Schmidt stability are unexamined.

## Confidence
- **Pass@k boost:** Medium (consistent on two tasks but only one model)  
- **Minimal overhead:** Medium (reported but without detailed profiling across setups)  
- **Training-free nature:** High (by construction, no model finetuning)  
- **Orthogonal repulsion efficacy:** Medium (empirical support, limited theoretical justification)
