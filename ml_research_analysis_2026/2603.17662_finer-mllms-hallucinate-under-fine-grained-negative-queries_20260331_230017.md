# FINER: MLLMs Hallucinate under Fine-grained Negative Queries

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17662  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17662  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** FINER-Tuning yields up to 24.2% gain in paired accuracy on fine-grained negative queries while improving existing hallucination suites and preserving general multimodal capabilities.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) hallucinate when faced with fine-grained negative queries—questions that mix multiple correct visual elements with a single subtle mistake. To isolate and address this failure mode, the authors introduce two benchmarks, FINER-CompreCap and FINER-DOCCI, which construct multiple-choice questions across four settings (multiple objects, attributes, relations, and "what" questions). They propose FINER-Tuning, a method using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on data containing both fine-grained positive and negative queries. Fine-tuning four frontier MLLMs with FINER-Tuning achieved up to 24.2% improvement on the FINER benchmarks, simultaneously boosted performance on eight existing hallucination suites, and avoided alignment tax across six general multimodal benchmarks.

## Method Summary
FINER benchmarks are built from scene graphs or dense captions. Positive and negative variants are generated for objects, attributes, and relations by replacing one component with a semantically plausible but image-inconsistent alternative; for "what" questions, LLM-generated templates are used. Negative queries are filtered via entropy-based classification to ensure they are truly mismatched. Multiple-choice questions are then constructed with one correct option and four distractor negatives, and evaluated using paired accuracy (both positive and negative questions must be correct). FINER-Tuning applies DPO to preference tuples (image, query, chosen, rejected) derived from long captions (Pixmo-captions). Positive queries expect an affirming answer; negative queries expect denial. Models are fine-tuned with LoRA adapters on projection layers using a balanced dataset of such contrasts.

## Key Results
- Up to 24.2% gain in paired accuracy on FINER benchmarks (e.g., InternVL3.5-14B on Multi-rel).
- Consistent improvements on eight existing hallucination suites (DASH, POPE, AMBER, CRPE-R, HallusionBench, MMHal-Bench, HaloQuest, RePOPE).
- No alignment tax: general multimodal capabilities preserved or enhanced on MMStar, TextVQA, ChartQA, MMVP, NaturalBench, and V*.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MLLMs exhibit increased false-positive hallucination when negative queries are fine-grained, mixing multiple correct visual elements with a single subtle mistake.  
- **Mechanism:** The model's internal reasoning or attention fails to detect a single contradictory element within a broader context of verifiable facts, leading to acceptance of the entire composite query. This brittleness scales with granularity.  
- **Core assumption:** The model processes queries holistically rather than exhaustively verifying each constituent element against the image.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Motivational study shows accuracy dropping from ~80% to ~20% as query granularity increases (Fig. 1); related work on fine-grained hallucination supports this failure mode.  
- **Break condition:** Model accuracy does not degrade with increasing number of elements in negative queries.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** FINER benchmarks isolate this failure mode by constructing MCQs where correct answers require identifying a single incorrect element among otherwise correct ones.  
- **Mechａｎｉｓｍ:** Using scene graphs, four negative variants per element are generated. Rule-based MCQ creation pairs positive/negative questions, forcing models to discriminate fine-grained differences rather than rely on binary bias.  
- **Core assumption:** High-quality negative generation (LLM + MLLM filtering) creates semantically valid but image-inconsistent distractors, making the benchmark a probe for fine-grained visual-grounding errors.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Entropy-based filtering with Qwen2.5-VL-72B removes negatives that are actually present; paired evaluation prevents answer pattern bias.  
- **Break condition:** Negative generation consistently produces elements present in the image, degrading benchmark validity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** FINER-Tuning mitigates this by training on preference data containing both fine-grained positive and negative queries, teaching rejection of subtle errors without over-caution.  
- **Mechanism:** DPO optimizes the likelihood gap between chosen (correct) and rejected (incorrect) responses for both positive and negative queries. Dual exposure focuses learning on precise element-wise verification.  
- **Core assumption:** Training on such fine-grained contrastive data improves internal representations for binding objects, attributes, and relations.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Up to 24.2% gains on FINER; improvements on eight hallucination suites; no regression on general benchmarks.  
- **Break condition:** DPO leads to over-rejection (increased false negatives) or degrades general task performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Why needed: FINER-Tuning uses DPO to train on fine-grained query-answer preferences without a separate reward model. Quick check: How does the DPO loss ensure the model learns to prefer correct over incorrect answers for both positive and negative queries?  
- **Scene Graph (SG) Representation:** Why needed: Benchmarks are built from SGs encoding objects, attributes, and relations; training data extraction also uses SG-like structures. Quick check: How does a scene graph enable the construction of multi-attribute or multi-relation negative queries?  
- **Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) Design for Hallucination:** Why needed: FINER uses MCQs with one correct and four distractor options to force precise discrimination, avoiding simple “Yes/No” bias. Quick check: Why is paired accuracy a stricter metric than individual accuracy on positive or negative questions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Image -> Backbone MLLM (vision encoder + LLM) -> FINER-Tuning (LoRA on q_proj, v_proj) -> Evaluation Pipeline (VLMEvalKit)  
- **Critical path:** Problem identification (motivational study) → Benchmark construction (SG extraction, negative generation, MCQ assembly) → Training data pipeline (long captions → positive/negative phrases → query-answer tuples) → DPO fine-tuning → Multi-benchmark evaluation.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - MCQ vs. binary: MCQ reduces polarity bias but may reduce naturalness; binary is simpler but easier to game.  
  - Negative generation scale vs. quality: LLM-based generation scales but needs MLLM+human filtering; fully human curation is gold-standard but not scalable.  
  - Training data source: Pixmo-captions provide scale but include non-natural images; filtering to natural images showed minimal impact, so full diversity retained.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Positional bias: Models perform worse when negated entity is in certain positions (e.g., last); FINER-Tuning reduces but does not eliminate it.  
  - Over-rejection: Model may refuse valid positive queries after tuning, monitored via general benchmarks.  
  - Wh-question brittleness: All models, including Gemini-2.5-Flash, struggle on Wh-subsets (max ~50% Acc_paired).  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate motivational study: Test baseline model (e.g., InternVL3.5-14B) on increasing granularity levels (L1-L7) with binary “Yes/No” to confirm hallucination increase.  
  2. Run FINER benchmarks: Evaluate base vs. tuned models on FINER-CompreCap and FINER-DOCCI MCQ pairs; compute Acc_paired.  
  3. Cross-benchmark validation: Test tuned models on established hallucination suites (POPE, AMBER, MMHal-Bench) and general benchmarks (MMStar, TextVQA) to verify transfer and check alignment tax.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can we systematically improve MLLM performance on high-granularity fine-grained negative queries and especially “what”-questions, which remain notably challenging even after FINER-Tuning?  
2. How can we construct fully human-validated, noise-free FINER benchmarks at scale without introducing subjective ambiguities?  
3. Does the rule-based MCQ template and the assumption of independent component replacement in negative query generation artificially constrain model robustness, and how would more natural, holistic negation strategies affect performance?  
4. What are the underlying reasons that FINER-Tuning avoids the typical “alignment tax” while other hallucination methods sometimes show degraded performance on general benchmarks?  
5. Can the findings from negative query analysis be extended beyond existing image domains (COCO, DOCCI) to open-world or specialized domains (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery) where fine-grained errors are critical?

## Limitations
- Exact prompts for negative generation with Gemini-2.0-Flash and Qwen3-14B are undisclosed, preventing precise replication of training data distribution.  
- Entropy threshold calibration (θ values) lacks open-sourced human-in-loop process, risking benchmark composition drift.  
- Unspecified Pixmo-caption shard splits and MCQ shuffling rules introduce uncontrollable variation in reproduced experiments.  
- Benchmarks are not fully human-curated; rule-based MCQ construction may reduce question naturalness and introduce ambiguity.

## Confidence
- **Fine-grained brittleness (Mechanism 1):** High – Strongly supported by granularity study and established literature.  
- **Benchmark isolation (Mechanism 2):** Medium – Construction logic clear, but unknown negative-generation details may affect distractor validity.  
- **FINER-Tuning efficacy (Mechanism 3):** Medium – Cross-benchmark gains compelling, but positional bias and over-rejection risks partially persist.
