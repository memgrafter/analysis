# An Approach to Simultaneous Acquisition of Real-Time MRI Video, EEG, and Surface EMG for Articulatory, Brain, and Muscle Activity During Speech Production

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04840
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04840
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: First simultaneous acquisition of real-time MRI, EEG, and surface EMG during speech production with effective artifact suppression, enabling integrated measurement of articulatory, brain, and muscle activity.

## Executive Summary
This study pioneers a multimodal protocol to concurrently capture real-time MRI (rtMRI) video, electroencephalography (EEG), and surface electromyography (EMG) during speech production. The core innovation is a robust multi-stage artifact suppression pipeline that removes the dominant electromagnetic and myogenic contaminants, allowing high-fidelity neural and muscular signals alongside clear articulatory kinematics. Validation in a single subject demonstrates sub-10 ms temporal alignment, preserved MRI quality, and strong correlation between inside- and outside-scanner EEG. The approach even reveals micro-articulatory movements during imagined speech, opening new avenues for investigating the speech production chain from intention to articulation.

## Method Summary
The experimental setup employs a 0.55 T MRI scanner equipped with a custom upper-airway coil and a spiral bSSFP sequence (TR = 5.05 ms, 99 fps). A MR-compatible BrainVision amplifier records 16 channels (9 EEG, 2 EOG, 3 EMG, 1 ECG) at 5 kHz, synchronized via a fiber-optic trigger from the MRI clock. Artifact removal follows three stages: (1) gradient artifact subtraction by template averaging using the MRI cycle periodicity, (2) ballistocardiogram correction via ECG-aligned averaging, and (3) reference Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) with EMG/EOG channels to eliminate residual myogenic and ocular noise. The pipeline’s performance is evaluated through temporal alignment checks, tongue region signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) measurements, EEG correlation with outside-scanner reference, and frontal peak amplitude attenuation.

## Key Results
- Temporal alignment: mean offset 8.3 ms/s within 10.1 ms frame size; MRI quality preserved (tongue SNR 10.148 ± 0.575).
- EEG artifact correction effective: correlation with outside-scanner reference up to 0.82 in language regions; frontal myogenic peaks reduced from ~60 µV to ~20 µV.
- Imagined speech: micro-articulatory movements detectable in rtMRI.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Multi-stage artifact suppression pipeline**  
- **Claim:** Simultaneous acquisition is feasible because a carefully designed pipeline mitigates dominant electromagnetic and myogenic contaminants.  
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline sequentially applies (1) template-based subtraction for gradient-switching artifacts using the MRI cycle periodicity, (2) ECG-informed template subtraction for ballistocardiogram artifacts, and (3) reference-based Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) using EMG/EOG channels to remove residual myogenic and ocular noise. Each stage targets a distinct, well‑characterized noise source.  
- **Core assumption:** Artifacts are linearly superimposable on the signals of interest and possess sufficiently distinct and repeatable temporal or reference signatures.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sections 2.3/2.4 describe the three‑stage correction; neighbor corpora provide indirect support.  
- **Break condition:** Non‑linear interactions (e.g., speech‑induced motion distorting templates) or contaminated reference signals could cause residual artifacts.

**Mechanism 2: Precise temporal alignment**  
- **Claim:** Sub‑10 ms alignment between rtMRI frames and electrophysiology is achieved via a common optical trigger synchronized to the MRI scanner clock.  
- **Mechanism:** All bio‑signals are sampled by a single MR‑compatible system receiving a fiber‑optic trigger directly from the MRI clock; alignment is verified by comparing total recording durations.  
- **Core assumption:** The trigger propagation delay is constant/negligible or calibratable; the MRI and EEG/EMG clocks share a stable relationship.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1 (5 kHz synced via fiber‑optic); Section 3.1 (8.3 ms/s offset).  
- **Break condition:** Trigger drift/jitter or unmodeled latencies could accumulate misalignment.

**Mechanism 3: Preservation of signal quality**  
- **Claim:** rtMRI video quality and electrophysiological SNR are preserved, enabling observation of micro‑articulatory movements even during imagined speech.  
- **Mechanism:** Use of MR‑compatible, non‑ferromagnetic electrodes and cabling minimizes field perturbations; the 0.55 T field reduces gradient‑induced voltages; the artifact pipeline maintains articulatory kinematics.  
- **Core assumption:** Electrodes do not significantly distort B0 or RF coils; EMG still contains signal during imagery.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1 (MR‑compatible system); Section 3.2 (tongue SNR 10.148 ± 0.575); Section 3.5 (micro‑movements).  
- **Break condition:** Poor electrode fit causing motion artifacts or field distortion; complete suppression of articulatory movement leaving EMG devoid of relevant activity.

## Foundational Learning
- **MRI Physics & Gradient Switching**  
  - *Why needed:* The gradient artifact arises from Faraday’s law; understanding its timing (TR) and periodicity is essential for template subtraction.  
  - *Quick check:* Why is the gradient artifact periodic and how does its frequency relate to the MRI sequence repetition time (TR)?

- **Ballistocardiogram (BCG) Artifact Mechanism**  
  - *Why needed:* BCG results from cardiac‑induced body motion in the static field, requiring ECG‑aligned averaging.  
  - *Quick check:* How does the BCG artifact differ in origin and temporal pattern from the gradient artifact, and why does it require an ECG channel for its correction?

- **Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) for Artifact Removal**  
  - *Why needed:* Reference‑based CCA isolates EEG components maximally correlated with EMG/EOG to reject myogenic/ocular noise.  
  - *Quick check:* What is the key mathematical principle behind CCA, and why is using EMG/EOG channels as references more effective for myogenic/ocular artifact removal than independent component analysis (ICA) alone?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
0.55T MRI scanner (spiral bSSFP) -> fiber‑optic trigger -> MR‑compatible BrainVision recorder (EEG/EMG/ECG/EOG) -> BrainVision Analyzer (gradient & BCG correction) -> External CCA (using EMG/EOG references, ρ > 0.4) -> Aligned rtMRI video + cleaned signals -> Validation & analysis

### Critical path
1. MRI trigger generation.  
2. Fiber‑optic synchronization to BrainVision system.  
3. Simultaneous acquisition: rtMRI (99 fps) + bio‑signals (5 kHz).  
4. Gradient artifact subtraction (template‑based).  
5. BCG artifact subtraction (ECG‑aligned averaging).  
6. Reference CCA (remove components with ρ > 0.4).  
7. Temporal alignment verification and SNR/metric computation.

### Design tradeoffs
- *Hardware:* Lower field (0.55 T) eases EEG compatibility but reduces MRI SNR/resolution; passive electrodes are safer/cheaper but introduce more noise than active ones.  
- *Template subtraction:* Sliding window adapts to slow drift but may under‑subtract abrupt motion.  
- *CCA:* Threshold (ρ > 0.4) is empirical; aggressive removal risks losing correlated neural activity.  
- *Electrode placement:* Fixed montage limits EMG coverage; frontal channels are most contaminated by jaw/head motion.

### Failure signatures
- Uncorrected gradient artifact: high‑amplitude (~200 µV), TR‑locked spikes.  
- Residual BCG artifact: slow (50‑100 µV) pulse‑synchronized wave in posterior channels.  
- Incomplete myogenic removal: frontal bursts remain >30 µV, indicating CCA threshold too low or insufficient references.  
- MRI degradation: shading or dropout in tongue ROI near electrodes.  
- Misalignment: systematic offset >10 ms between EEG events and rtMRI frames.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Temporal Alignment Validation:** Record 60 s of resting data; compare total duration from EEG triggers and MRI frame count; mean offset should be < frame duration (~10 ms).  
2. **Gradient+BCG Correction Efficacy:** Acquire EEG inside and outside scanner during same rest task; compute power spectral density (PSD) and variance; success: removal of harmonics at k·(1/TR) Hz and PSD resembling outside reference above 1 Hz.  
3. **Myogenic/CCA Efficacy & Imagined Speech Detection:** Compare phonated vs. imagined trials. (a) Check frontal ERP amplitude reduction and left‑lateralized topography preservation post‑CCA. (b) Inspect rtMRI frame‑by‑frame for velum/tongue root micro‑movements during imagery; success: ~70% frontal peak reduction and detectable <1 mm displacements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can imagined speech micro‑articulation be voluntarily inhibited?** The study observed micro‑movements in one subject without training; it remains unclear if these are intrinsic or can be suppressed, which would impact BCI decoding.  
2. **Does the protocol generalize to larger, diverse populations?** The pilot involved a single male subject; scalability across demographics, field strengths, and speech tasks is untested.  
3. **Would high‑density EEG with optimized placement improve neural signal detection?** Current cap has poor spatial resolution and suboptimal coverage of speech regions; a comparative study is needed.  
4. **Can residual frontal EEG artifacts from head motion be further reduced?** A localized artifact persists at FPz, possibly due to limited EMG references; alternative methods (e.g., more sensors, deep learning) should be explored.

## Limitations
- **Replication blockers:** Exact parameters for gradient artifact subtraction (thresholds, sliding window) and CCA component selection beyond ρ > 0.4 are not specified.  
- **Generalizability gaps:** Results obtained from one subject at 0.55 T; unknown robustness to higher fields (e.g., 3 T), diverse populations, or varied speech tasks.  
- **Assumption risks:** The pipeline assumes linear superposition of artifacts; non‑linear interactions (e.g., speech‑induced motion) may compromise correction.

## Confidence
- Feasibility of Simultaneous Acquisition: **Medium**  
- Temporal Alignment Precision: **High**  
- Signal Preservation & Micro‑Movement Detection: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Parameter Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary gradient artifact sliding window and thresholds to identify robust settings minimizing residual artifact power.  
2. **CCA Threshold Optimization:** Perform grid search on canonical correlation threshold (e.g., 0.3–0.6) to balance frontal peak reduction (target ~60 µV → 20 µV) against preservation of left‑hemisphere ERP topography.  
3. **Cross‑Validation Across Conditions:** Test the pipeline on new subjects (n ≥ 5) and, if possible, at 3 T to evaluate generalizability, electrode impact on MRI SNR, and artifact correction consistency.
