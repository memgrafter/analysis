# Measuring and Eliminating Refusals in Military Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10012
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10012
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Military-aligned LLMs refuse up to 98.2% of legitimate warfighter queries; abliteration can increase answer rates by 66.5 points but incurs 2–30% performance regression on other military tasks, arguing that zero refusals require building specialized models from the ground up.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical failure of safety-aligned large language models (LLMs) in military contexts: they frequently refuse legitimate warfighter queries involving violence, weapons, or tactical operations. The authors create the first military-specific refusal benchmarks, including a gold-standard set of 221 queries curated by U.S. Army veterans and two synthetic bronze sets. Evaluating 31 public and 3 military models reveals extremely high refusal and deflection rates. To mitigate this, they apply directional activation ablation (abliteration) to a military-tuned model, significantly improving answer rates but at a substantial cost to performance on other military tasks. The core conclusion is that fully eliminating refusals while preserving all capabilities is only achievable by training specialized military LLMs without any safety alignment data from the outset.

## Method Summary
The research methodology comprises three main phases: 1) **Benchmark construction**: A gold-standard dataset of 221 realistic military queries was generated by U.S. Army veterans. Two additional synthetic datasets (Bronze-Alpha, Bronze-Bravo) were created via LLM prompting and variation. 2) **Evaluation**: Models are assessed on these benchmarks using the Inspect AI framework with a two-stage pipeline: a refusal keyword filter followed by categorization (answer, refusal, deflection, lacks info, invalid) by an LLM judge (Atla Selene 1). 3) **Intervention**: The Heretic library performs abliteration on a military-tuned model (EdgeRunner 20B). This involves computing a difference-in-means direction vector between harmful and harmless calibration datasets and applying it to the model’s `W_out` matrices. The ablated model is re-evaluated on refusal benchmarks and a suite of military/general tasks to measure trade-offs.

## Key Results
- Refusal rates on the gold benchmark reached up to 98.2%, with deflection rates between 0% and 21.3% across evaluated models.
- Abliteration on EdgeRunner 20B increased the answer rate on the gold set by 66.5 points, achieving up to 93% answer rate.
- This improvement came with an average of 2% regression on other military tasks at moderate ablation strength; achieving near-zero refusals required accepting 10–30% performance degradation.
- Synthetic bronze benchmarks correlated well with the gold set for answer/refusal/deflection rates but poorly for "lacks info," indicating quality issues in synthetic answerability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Military-Specific Benchmarking Enables Accurate Measurement
- **Claim:** Creating military-specific refusal benchmarks enables measurement of refusal behaviors on legitimate warfighter queries that general safety benchmarks miss.
- **Mechanism:** Subject Matter Experts (U.S. Army veterans) directly generate a gold-standard dataset (`MIL-DEFLECT-GOLD-ALPHA`) of 221 queries characterized as realistic, common, and likely to trigger refusals. Two synthetic datasets are derived from it, showing high correlation for core categories.
- **Core assumption:** Standard safety benchmarks do not capture the unique query distribution of military domains, where legitimate queries often involve violence, terrorism, or military technology.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the gold benchmark as the first of its kind developed by veterans. [section 2.2] details the SME-based creation process emphasizing realism and refusal likelihood.
- **Break condition:** If the veteran-generated queries do not represent the true operational distribution or inadvertently include illegitimate requests, benchmark measurements become invalid.

### Mechanism 2: Abliteration Targets a Separable Refusal Direction
- **Claim:** Directional activation ablation (abliteration) can significantly reduce refusal rates on military queries but incurs performance regression on other tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Using the Heretic library, a difference-in-means vector is computed between harmful and harmless calibration datasets. This vector is used to modify the model’s `W_out` matrices, steering activations away from refusal-related directions. Applied to `EdgeRunner 20B`, it boosts answer rates by 66.5 points.
- **Core assumption:** Refusal behavior is mediated by a low-dimensional, linear direction in the model’s activation space that can be targeted without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] presents trade-off results in Tables 4 and 5; e.g., 93% answer rate requires ~14% regression on military tasks. Regression varies by task (e.g., `C130-BRONZE-ALPHA` -16.2% vs. `COMBAT-MEDIC-SILVER-ALPHA` ~0%).
- **Break condition:** If refusal mechanisms are not linearly separable or are deeply entangled with general knowledge representations, abliteration causes disproportionate capability collapse, as evidenced by the 10–30% degradation needed for near-zero refusals.

### Mechanism 3: Full Elimination Requires Ground-Up Specialization
- **Claim:** Full elimination of military refusals with maximum task performance requires building specialized models from the ground up, avoiding safety data during post-training.
- **Mechanism:** The paper argues that since abliteration imposes a performance tax, the only path to both zero refusals and high accuracy is to train a model on military data without ever introducing general safety alignment data (no safety SFT/DPO).
- **Core assumption:** Safety guardrails are "baked in" during post-training on curated safety datasets and cannot be completely removed later without residual effects or performance loss.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] and [section 5] explicitly argue for deeper specialization and excluding safety data from post-training. Neighbors like "Jailbroken..." support the persistence of safety training effects.
- **Break condition:** If military queries still require some subset of safety behaviors (e.g., preventing civilian harm), a model with zero refusals may be unacceptable or illegal under international law.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: LLM Safety Alignment (SFT/DPO on safety datasets)**
  - **Why needed:** The paper's core problem stems from standard safety training causing refusals on legitimate military queries. Understanding how alignment introduces refusal behaviors is prerequisite.
  - **Quick check:** How might a model trained with RLHF for "helpfulness" and "harmlessness" develop refusal behaviors for queries about explosive devices?
- **Concept: Refusal Categorization (hard refusal vs. soft deflection vs. lack of info)**
  - **Why needed:** The benchmark distinguishes between a flat "I can't" (refusal), a vague generic answer (deflection), and an answer indicating lack of knowledge. This nuance is critical for measuring the failure mode accurately.
  - **Quick check:** Why is it insufficient to simply count responses containing the phrase "I'm sorry"?
- **Concept: Abliteration (directional activation ablation)**
  - **Why needed:** It is the primary technical intervention explored to reduce refusals. Understanding its mechanism—modifying `W_out` matrices via a computed direction vector—is key to interpreting the trade-off results.
  - **Quick check:** What is the role of the `D_harmful` and `D_harmless` calibration datasets in the abliteration formula?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Gold/Bronze benchmarks -> Inspect AI pipeline (refusal marker + LLM judge) -> Metrics (answer/refusal/deflection rates). Heretic engine + base model (EdgeRunner 20B) -> Ablated model -> Reevaluation on military/general task suites.

**Critical path:** 1) Acquire/curate military queries → 2) Run models through benchmark pipeline → 3) Categorize outputs → 4) For ablation: define calibration sets → 5) Run Heretic to compute ablation parameters → 6) Apply modifications to model weights → 7) Re-evaluate on military and general tasks → 8) Analyze trade-off curves.

**Design tradeoffs:** Benchmark prioritizes realism and refusal likelihood over scale (Gold only 221 samples). Two-stage judgment (string match then LLM judge) balances precision/recall but introduces dependency on a judge model's biases. Abliteration optimizes for minimal KL divergence on a calibration set, not for preservation of downstream task performance, causing uneven regression.

**Failure signatures:** 1) High "invalid" rate (blank outputs) indicates runtime guardrails not accounted for in model weights. 2) Disproportionate regression on specific military tasks (e.g., `C130-BRONZE-ALPHA` -16.2% vs. `COMBAT-MEDIC` ~0%) suggests the ablation direction interferes with task-specific knowledge entangled with the safety mechanism. 3) Low correlation between Bronze-Alpha's "lacks info" and Gold indicates dataset quality issues (unanswerable synthetic questions).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Benchmark Sanity Check:** Run `MIL-DEFLECT-GOLD-ALPHA` on a known high-refusal model (e.g., `gpt-oss-20b`) and a low-refusal model (e.g., `Deepseek R1`). Verify the answer/refusal/deflection splits match Table 2. Check judge agreement by running a subset through multiple judges.
2. **Abliteration Parameter Sweep:** Use Heretic on `EdgeRunner 20B` with varying `calibration refusal rate` targets (e.g., 95%, 80%, 50%, 20%). Plot the trade-off curve between gold-set answer rate and average military task regression. Identify the "knee" point.
3. **Task-Specific Regression Analysis:** After a heavy ablation (e.g., target 20% calibration refusal), compare performance on individual military test sets. Identify which domains (e.g., technical maintenance vs. medical) degrade most. Manually inspect failing examples to see if reasoning is corrupted or if knowledge was lost.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How accurate are automated LLM judges in categorizing model responses into refusal, deflection, lacks-info, and answer?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 3.3 states: "As future work we would like to human-annotate a dataset to directly assess judge accuracy."
- **Why unresolved:** Current judge agreement metrics (98.6–99.4%) do not measure correctness against human labels; judge models may have inherent biases or safety triggers that misclassify certain responses.
- **Resolution evidence:** Human annotation of a diverse set of model outputs across the MIL‑DEFLECT benchmarks, followed by calculation of per‑category precision, recall, and F1 for each judge model.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What factors determine whether a model refuses a military query due to safety policies versus due to lack of knowledge?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 3.1 notes: "We leave further analyses of this behavior to future work," referring to the "lacks info" category and its low correlation with the gold set for MIL‑DEFLECT‑BRONZE‑ALPHA.
- **Why unresolved:** Distinguishing these causes is critical for targeted refusal reduction; safety‑related refusals require different mitigation (e.g., ablation) whereas knowledge gaps may need additional training.
- **Resolution evidence:** Controlled experiments with answerable vs. unanswerable military queries, measuring refusal rates and analyzing model confidence, calibration, or internal representations to identify the refusal trigger.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does abliteration cause variable performance degradation across different military tasks, and can we predict which tasks are most vulnerable?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Section 4 reports differential regression (e.g., COMBAT‑MEDIC‑SILVER‑ALPHA regressed minimally while C130‑BRONZE‑ALPHA regressed 16.2% at 49% calibration refusal). The authors suspect "semantic distance from the calibration datasets to our selected tasks" limits interpretability.
- **Why unresolved:** If ablation is used operationally, developers need to anticipate which capabilities will be harmed; understanding the drivers of vulnerability could guide calibration data design or ablation parameters.
- **Resolution evidence:** Systematic analysis of feature overlap between calibration and task datasets, or probing which model components (layers, heads) are modified during ablation and correlating changes with task‑specific activations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What training pipeline (data composition, mid‑training, post‑training) yields a military LLM with near‑zero refusals on legitimate queries while preserving other safety behaviors?
- **Basis:** [inferred] The conclusion argues for "deeper specialization, including with mid‑training and end‑to‑end post‑training, to achieve zero refusals" and a "tailored approach" that excludes only mission‑preventing safety behaviors. Abliteration showed unacceptable trade‑offs (10–30% degradation for near‑zero refusals).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the problem and a stopgap (abliteration with costs) but does not provide a recipe for building a specialized model from scratch that balances refusal elimination with safety retention.
- **Resolution evidence:** Comparative training runs varying the inclusion/exclusion of safety data, the use of mid‑training on military corpora, and post‑training alignment methods; evaluation should measure refusal rates on MIL‑DEFLECT alongside performance on standard safety benchmarks (e.g., truthfulqa, harmbench) and military task accuracy.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark validity:** The gold set lacks real-world operational validation; SME selection bias is possible. Synthetic sets show low correlation on "lacks info" category.
- **Abliteration trade-offs:** Strong empirical support, but regression is uneven across tasks; calibration dataset dependency is not fully characterized.
- **Specialized model claim:** Logical but untested; ignores potential legal requirements for safety constraints even in military contexts.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark validity:** Medium. Gold set is SME-generated but not operationally validated; synthetic set correlations are mixed.
- **Abliteration trade-offs:** High. Strong empirical results across multiple models and tasks, supported by neighboring research on refusal directions.
- **Specialized model claim:** Medium. The argument is logical and consistent with findings, but it remains an untested proposal without a demonstrated training recipe.

## Next Checks
1. **Judge model robustness:** Re-evaluate 10% of the gold set with 3+ alternative judges (e.g., unaligned base model, different API) to quantify disagreement beyond the reported 98.6% agreement.
2. **Operational SME audit:** Recruit 5+ active-duty warfighters to rate gold set queries for realism/legitimacy; compute acceptance rate and revise benchmark if <90% consensus.
3. **Directional knowledge probing:** Use activation patching on ablated vs. base model to test if the removed refusal direction correlates with domain-specific facts (e.g., explosive mechanisms) in layer-wise probes.
