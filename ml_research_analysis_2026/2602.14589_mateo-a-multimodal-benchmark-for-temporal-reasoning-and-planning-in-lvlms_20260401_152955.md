# MATEO: A Multimodal Benchmark for Temporal Reasoning and Planning in LVLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14589  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14589  
- **Reference count:** 21  
- **Primary result:** GPT-5.1 achieved up to 0.74 accuracy (multimodal, self-reflection, evaluated on 20% test set); best open-source model, fine-tuned InternVL3.5-8B, reached 0.69 accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MATEO, a benchmark for evaluating temporal reasoning and planning in large vision-language models (LVLMs). MATEO comprises 300 professionally curated Italian recipes, each step paired with an image and annotated with a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of execution dependencies via crowdsourcing. The task—Temporal Execution Order (TEO)—requires models to classify the relationship between two steps (before, after, independent) using text, images, or both. Six LVLMs were evaluated across prompting strategies (baseline, instructions, in-context learning, chain-of-thought, self-reflection) and fine-tuning. Results show GPT-5.1 reaches 0.74 accuracy with self-reflection, while fine-tuned InternVL3.5-8B achieves 0.69, the highest among open-source models. All models exhibit a bias toward sequential relations, struggle with reversed ordering and independent steps, and smaller models fail to leverage multimodal inputs effectively.

## Method Summary
The Temporal Execution Order (TEO) task is defined as a 3-way classification problem: given two recipe steps (with optional images), predict whether step A must execute before step B, after step B, or if they are independent (parallelizable). Predictions must be consistent when step order is swapped. The MATEO benchmark includes 300 recipes split into 210 training, 30 validation, and 60 test recipes. Each recipe is annotated with a DAG of step dependencies via crowdsourcing (54 annotators, MASI agreement 0.85). From these DAGs, the test set comprises 933 “before”, 933 “after” (swapped inverses), and 1573 “independent” step-pair examples. Models are evaluated across text-only, image-only, and multimodal inputs using prompting strategies: baseline (no instructions), instructions, in-context learning (ICL) with text-only examples, chain-of-thought (CoT), and self-reflection. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) on attention matrices (Q/K/V/O) with balanced step-pair triples (before, after, independent) for 1 epoch at learning rate 1e-5.

## Key Results
- GPT-5.1 achieves the highest accuracy (0.74) using multimodal inputs and self-reflection, evaluated on a 20% stratified test subset.
- Fine-tuned InternVL3.5-8B reaches 0.69 accuracy, the best among open-source models, with improved consistency on before–after pairs.
- All models show a strong bias toward sequential (“before”/“after”) relations, near-zero performance on “independent” steps in zero-shot settings, and poorer reasoning on reversed (“after”) dependencies. Smaller models (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-7B) fail to benefit from multimodal inputs, and self-reflection often harms smaller models or fails outright.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Multimodal dependency alignment**  
- **Claim:** LVLMs infer TEO by aligning and cross-referencing semantic features from textual descriptions and visual depictions of actions to compare pre- and post-conditions.  
- **Mechanism:** The model encodes each step’s image and text into a joint representation, then assesses whether the object state in Step B depends on an outcome produced in Step A. Success requires resolving conflicts or redundancies between modalities (e.g., text mentioning an ingredient not shown in the image).  
- **Core assumption:** Professionally curated dataset provides semantically aligned image-text pairs where visuals reliably ground action effects, enabling models to learn dependency patterns from either modality.  
- **Evidence anchors:** The dataset ensures “quasi-perfect semantic alignment” with images depicting outcomes or actions in progress; results show image-only fine-tuning sometimes outperforms text-only.  
- **Break condition:** Semantic misalignment (e.g., image shows mise en place while text describes a cooking action) creates ambiguous joint representations, leading to incorrect dependencies.

**Mechanism 2: Structured prompting as cognitive scaffold**  
- **Claim:** Prompting strategies (instructions, ICL, CoT, self-reflection) explicitly decompose the TEO task into sub-processes (identifying pre/post-conditions, reasoning about dependencies), improving performance over zero-shot baselines.  
- **Mechanism:** Prompts provide templates for stepwise reasoning; ICL demonstrates correct analysis; CoT forces articulation of “Step A produces…” and “Step B requires…” before prediction; self-reflection adds a verification loop. This structure mitigates pre-training biases toward linear sequencing.  
- **Core assumption:** LVLMs possess latent knowledge of procedural dependencies but require explicit reasoning chains to apply it consistently, especially for non-linear or reversed relations.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Prompt templates explicitly query “Dependency analysis:”; Table 3 shows accuracy gains from baseline to ICL/CoT for most models; self-reflection helps larger models (GPT-5.1: 0.73→0.74) but hurts smaller ones.  
- **Break condition:** Models may ignore or misinterpret complex instructions, especially smaller ones with limited instruction-following capacity. Failure rates for self-reflection reach 81.6% for LLaVA-OneVision-7B.

**Mechanism 3: Fine-tuning adapts representations to dependency patterns**  
- **Claim:** Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) updates the model’s internal representations to better capture temporal dependencies, improving consistency and accuracy by reducing reliance on spurious textual sequencing cues.  
- **Mechanism:** Supervised fine-tuning on annotated TEO examples (before/after/independent triples) strengthens connections between multimodal features and the correct dependency class, overcoming biases from pre-training where “before” correlates with narrative markers like “first”.  
- **Core assumption:** TEO patterns are learnable via fine-tuning on sufficiently diverse recipe step pairs, and the base architecture can adapt without catastrophic forgetting.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Training set provides 3,499 examples for before/after and 4,969 for independent; fine-tuned InternVL3.5-8B achieves 76% consistency for before–after pairs and 0.69 accuracy.  
- **Break condition:** Insufficient or modality-biased fine-tuning data may limit generalization. Image-only fine-tuning outperformed text-only, suggesting potential under-utilization of textual nuance.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) for partial ordering**  
- *Why needed here:* The TEO task predicts edges in a DAG where nodes are steps and edges encode “must-execute-before” dependencies. Understanding DAGs (acyclicity, transitive closure) explains why “independent” means no path exists between nodes.  
- *Quick check:* If Step A → Step B and Step B → Step C, what is the relation between Step A and Step C? How would you represent “Step X can be done in parallel with Step Y” in the DAG?  

**Concept: Allen’s Interval Algebra & Temporal Reasoning**  
- *Why needed here:* TEO classes (before, after, independent) map to Allen’s relations (before, after, during/simultaneous). Reasoning is based on logical dependencies (pre/post-conditions), not explicit time expressions.  
- *Quick check:* If Step A’s output is an ingredient used in Step B, but Step B’s text says “add X”, what Allen relation holds? Does it change if the image for Step B shows the ingredient already present?  

**Concept: Multimodal Fusion in LVLMs**  
- *Why needed here:* Models must integrate visual and textual streams to resolve dependencies. Common fusion strategies (early/late fusion, cross-attention) help interpret why some models fail to leverage both modalities effectively.  
- *Quick check:* In a recipe step, the text says “chop onions” but the image shows a blender. What might a multimodal model need to do to correctly infer dependencies with other steps? What failure mode could occur?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Encoders (vision encoder, text tokenizer) → Fusion Module (cross-attention/concatenation) → Reasoning Head (MLP/decoder for classification or CoT generation) → Prompt Parser (template filler for ICL/CoT)

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode Step A image → visual features; encode Step A text → textual features.  
2. Fuse Step A’s multimodal features into a joint vector.  
3. Repeat for Step B.  
4. Concatenate/fuse Step A and Step B joint vectors.  
5. Pass through classification head or generate CoT text.  
6. For self-reflection, loop: generate initial prediction → critique → re-generate final.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Modality weighting:* Equal vs. learned attention; smaller models may fail to attend to both modalities effectively.  
- *Prompt length vs. ICL examples:* More examples improve ICL but risk exceeding context window; paper limited ICL to text-only examples.  
- *Fine-tuning granularity:* Full fine-tuning vs. LoRA; paper used LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) to preserve base knowledge while adapting to TEO.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Sequential bias:* High “before”/“after” accuracy but near-zero “independent” in zero-shot.  
- *Order inconsistency:* Predictions change when steps are swapped (low consistency scores).  
- *Modality neglect:* Multimodal inputs degrade performance for smaller models vs. single-modality.  
- *Self-reflection failure:* Model produces incomplete reflections or fails to output final answer.  
- *Poor “after” generalization:* Lower F1 for “after” class even when “before” is high.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Zero-shot baseline:* Feed two step pairs (image+text) with only the three TEO questions, no instructions. Measure accuracy and bias toward sequential classes. Expected: Low accuracy, high before/after, low independent.  
2. *Multimodal ICL vs. Text-only ICL:* Provide three in-context examples (text-only) but test with image+text inputs. Compare to text-only test. Expected: Larger models gain from multimodal ICL; smaller models may not.  
3. *Swapped order consistency:* For a subset, run inference twice: original order (A,B) and swapped (B,A). Check consistency (before↔after, independent↔independent). Expected: Larger models show higher consistency; fine-tuned models improve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why do LVLMs exhibit a strong bias toward sequential (linear) temporal relations over parallel ones, and how can this be mitigated?  
  *Basis:* Models struggle with the ‘independent’ class under zero-shot and exhibit a bias toward before/after. Unresolved root cause (architectural, data, objective) hampers planning improvement. *Evidence needed:* Ablations on training data distribution, architectural interventions for DAG modeling, probing representations for dependency vs. sequential cues.

- **Open Question 2:** How can prompting strategies be standardized to enable fair, comparable evaluation across diverse LVLM architectures?  
  *Basis:* Models often interpret instructions differently, requiring model-specific adjustments that prevent uniform comparison. Unresolved standardization confounds performance differences with prompt engineering. *Evidence needed:* A validated prompt suite with automatic adaptation rules per model family, or a meta-evaluation measuring prompt sensitivity.

- **Open Question 3:** Why do smaller LVLMs fail to effectively leverage multimodal inputs for TEO, and what architectural modifications are needed?  
  *Basis:* Smaller models show degraded multimodal performance; image-only fine-tuning outperforms text-only. Suggests modality fusion bottleneck in compact models. *Evidence needed:* Controlled experiments varying fusion module design, multimodal attention analysis, or data efficiency studies for small models.

- **Open Question 4:** What causes the significant drop in performance for the ‘after’ class, especially under CoT prompting, and how can reasoning under reversed temporal order be improved?  
  *Basis:* Models struggle with ‘after’ predictions (lower F1 than ‘before’), indicating possible reliance on lexical cues or lack of symmetric dependency understanding. *Evidence needed:* Error analysis on ‘after’ failures (confusion with ‘before’/‘independent’?), or training with symmetrically swapped step pairs to enforce relational symmetry.

- **Open Question 5:** Can multimodal in-context learning (ICL) with image-text examples improve TEO performance beyond text-only ICL, and what is the optimal example composition?  
  *Basis:* Multimodal ICL was unexplored due to resource constraints, leaving its potential unknown despite multimodal inputs helping some large models zero-shot. *Evidence needed:* Systematic experiments varying ICL example modality (image-only, text-only, multimodal) and analyzing gains/failures, especially for models benefiting from multimodal inputs.

## Limitations
- Exact test pair selection method (e.g., whether all possible pairs or a subset are used) is unspecified, hindering precise test set reconstruction.  
- Model-specific prompt adjustments (e.g., removing `<assistant>` tokens for InternVL3.5-8B, explicit instructions for GPT-5.1) are not fully documented, risking result deviations.  
- Image preprocessing details (resolution, normalization, token configuration) are missing, affecting multimodal input handling across architectures.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark validity:** High (professional curation, high annotator agreement, clear task definition).  
- **Prompting benefits:** Medium (gains shown but depend on model size and undocumented tweaks).  
- **Fine-tuning gains:** Medium (improvement clear but LoRA configuration ambiguous).  
- **Observed LVLM limitations:** High (consistent biases and failures across models).
