# Ailed: A Psyche-Driven Chess Engine with Dynamic Emotional Modulation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05352
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05352
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: An audio-inspired signal chain applied to move probabilities produces a monotonic behavioral gradient from stress to overconfidence, with top-move agreement spreads of 19.9–24.8 percentage points and win rates ranging from 30.1% to 50.8% against Maia2-1100.

## Executive Summary
Conventional chess engines produce deterministic or temperature-perturbed moves but lack the human-like variability seen under emotional states like stress or overconfidence. This paper introduces Ailed, a chess engine that decouples static personality from dynamic psyche. A scalar psyche state ψ_t evolves from five board features and modulates an audio-inspired signal chain (gate, dynamics, EQ, saturation) that reshapes the move probability distribution from any underlying engine. Tested across 12,414 games against Maia2-1100 using both a weak model (Ailed-60k) and a strong model (Maia2), Ailed exhibits a consistent monotonic gradient in top-move agreement and win rates across ψ_t conditions. Ablation studies show the dynamics stage is the dominant mechanism. The architecture-agnostic design allows any probability-based engine to inherit these behavioral dynamics.

## Method Summary
Ailed is trained on filtered Lichess games (ELO 1025–1175) using a decoder-only transformer (23.7M parameters) for next-move prediction. The psyche calculator updates a bounded scalar ψ_t after each move from five weighted positional factors (material, king safety, mobility, center control, vulnerability) with phase-dependent reactivity and overnight decay. The signal chain consumes the move predictor’s legal move logits and a personality preset (six hand-tuned analogies) and applies: legal‑move extraction, softmax, noise gate, dynamics (power‑law), five‑band equalizer, saturation limiter, renormalization, and sampling. ψ_t linearly interpolates each stage between stress, neutral, and overconfident anchor parameters. Evaluation runs full‑game matches against Maia2‑1100 under three fixed ψ_t initial conditions (−80, 0, +80) to measure top‑move agreement, win rates, and distribution entropy. Cross‑engine tests with Maia2 itself confirm gradient consistency.

## Key Results
- Top‑move agreement spread of 19.9 pp (Ailed‑60k) and 24.8 pp (Maia2+Psyche) between stress and overconfidence conditions against Maia2‑1100.
- Overconfidence yields 66% agreement with vanilla Maia2 and a 50.8% win rate; stress reduces win rate to 30.1%.
- Stage‑wise ablation: removing the dynamics stage collapses the agreement spread from 34.7 pp to 14.3 pp; removing both gate and dynamics yields a near‑flat 2.4 pp spread.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper proposes three interrelated mechanisms:

### Mechanism 1: Audio‑Inspired Signal Chain
**Claim:** The signal chain (gate, dynamics, EQ, saturation) is the causal mechanism transforming a static move probability distribution into a psyche‑modulated one, producing behavioral gradients.  
**Mechanism:** A scalar ψ_t parameterizes each stage via linear interpolation among three anchor values (stress, neutral, overconfident). The dynamics stage (power‑law transform) dominates: flattening (α<1) under stress increases exploration, sharpening (α>1) under overconfidence increases exploitation. Gate and EQ amplify the effect; saturation caps extremeness.  
**Core assumption:** ψ_t correlates with human‑like emotional states, and these specific audio‑mapped transforms yield perceptibly human‑like play patterns.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] monotonic gradient in top‑move agreement; [section 7.4] ablation shows dynamics removal drastically reduces spread; [corpus] novelty supported by empirical isolation.

### Mechanism 2: Psyche State ψ_t Dynamics
**Claim:** The bounded scalar ψ_t evolves from five board‑based positional factors to capture emotional reaction to position changes.  
**Mechanism:** After each move, factors (material, king safety, mobility, center control, vulnerability) are weighted, summed, plus a check penalty, tanh‑compressed to target ˆψ_t, and blended toward that target using reactivity ρ_t varying by game phase. Overnight decay ψ_{t+1} = (1−λ)ψ_t provides longer‑term arc.  
**Core assumption:** These five factors, derived from chess heuristics and affect theory, suffice to approximate human‑like emotional valence without multidimensional modeling.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] ψ_t recomputed from five factors; [section 4.1] update equations and default weights; [corpus] no direct support; neighboring works use evaluations for quality, not emotion.

### Mechanism 3: Personality × Psyche Decomposition
**Claim:** The static personality preset paired with dynamic ψ_t yields architecture‑agnostic behavioral variation.  
**Mechanism:** Personality presets fix anchor parameters for each signal stage; ψ_t sets interpolation point. The signal chain operates only on move probabilities, independent of how they were generated.  
**Core assumption:** Any engine outputting move probabilities can be wrapped by this middleware to inherit behavioral dynamics.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] architecture‑agnostic middleware; cross‑engine tests with Ailed‑60k and Maia2 both show monotonic gradient despite large training‑data differences; [section 7.2.5] cross‑engine comparison.

## Foundational Learning
Key concepts underlying the approach:

- **Probability distribution shaping (signal processing analogy)**  
  *Why needed:* The behavioral mechanism is a sequence of probability transforms; understanding how each stage (e.g., dynamics exponent) reshapes probability mass is essential.  
  *Quick check:* If ψ_t is overconfident (high), which stages narrow the distribution, and what parameter values (gate τ_g, dynamics α, saturation σ) correspond?

- **Bounded rationality and exploration–exploitation trade‑offs**  
  *Why needed:* The system deliberately degrades optimality to simulate human variability; dynamics exponent α<1 under stress encourages exploration, α>1 under overconfidence encourages exploitation, mirroring prospect theory.  
  *Quick check:* How does increasing α affect output distribution entropy, and what human behavior might that parallel?

- **Centipawn loss vs. selection‑layer metrics**  
  *Why needed:* The paper argues centipawn loss is a position‑difficulty confounder, not a psyche measure. Selection‑layer metrics (agreement, entropy) are more appropriate.  
  *Quick check:* Why does CPL correlate with ψ_t zones even without signal chain modulation? What metric better isolates the signal chain’s impact?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Move Predictor (f_θ) → Psyche Calculator (ψ_t) → Signal Chain (Algorithm 1) → move selection.

**Critical path:**  
1. Board state → Psyche Calculator updates ψ_t.  
2. Move Predictor produces logits z_t over legal moves.  
3. Signal Chain consumes z_t, ψ_t, and personality preset Π, outputs move m*.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Hand‑tuned personality presets offer interpretability but lack personalization; learned presets could be future work.  
- No search by design sacrifices tactical strength for behavioral realism and low latency.  
- Scalar ψ_t simplifies debugging but discards multidimensional affect.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Weak model convergence:** ψ_t trajectories collapse to ψ_min (e.g., Ailed‑60k), erasing behavioral separation.  
- **Flat gradient:** Controls (flat preset, temperature‑only) show uniform agreement/score across ψ_t zones.  
- **Stage dominance missing:** Removing dynamics should shrink agreement spread; if spread remains large, dynamics implementation is likely incorrect.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Gradient existence test:** Play 3×667 games (stress/neutral/overconfident) against a deterministic opponent using full signal chain. Verify monotonic top‑move agreement increase (~20 pp spread).  
2. **Stage ablation (Dynamics check):** Repeat experiment 1 with dynamics stage disabled. Expect agreement spread to shrink >50% (≈34.7 pp → 14.3 pp).  
3. **Cross‑model consistency:** Test same three conditions with a different move predictor (e.g., stronger model). Expect similar gradient direction despite absolute agreement differences.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does Ailed's psyche modulation produce behavioral patterns that human players perceive as realistic representations of tilt and overconfidence?**  
   - *Basis:* No human‑subject validation was performed.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Human‑likeness inferred only from agreement with Maia2, which itself approximates human play.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Blind experiments where human players rate opponent realism, engagement, and difficulty; compare Ailed's moves to human move distributions in analogous zones.

2. **Can the audio‑inspired signal chain be effectively transferred to other sequential decision‑making games (e.g., Go, poker)?**  
   - *Basis:* Authors suggest future work; no implementation or results provided.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Chess‑specific design may not generalize due to different action‑space structures or human behavioral norms.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Implement same chain in target games, train domain‑specific predictors, and test for consistent behavioral gradients; cross‑validate with any available human behavioral data.

3. **Are hand‑tuned personality presets optimal, or can learned presets from human game data better capture individual stylistic variation?**  
   - *Basis:* Presets are hand‑tuned by music‑genre analogy; authors mention prototyping learned presets but present no data.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Presets not evaluated against human stylistic diversity; unknown if data‑driven approaches are superior.  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Train a model to predict signal chain parameters from individual player histories, compare behavioral metrics and human perception of stylistic distinctiveness.

4. **What is the practical impact of the thinking mode and study mode modules—which integrate the psyche state—on competitive performance and behavioral realism?**  
   - *Basis:* These modules are described but lack experimental validation.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Unclear if they affect outcomes or enhance human‑like patterns (e.g., plan fragility, post‑loss adaptation).  
   - *Resolving evidence:* Analyze Lichess bot logs to quantify thinking mode activation, disruption frequencies, study triggers; correlate with results and behavioral metrics.

## Limitations
- No human‑subject validation; human‑likeness is inferred indirectly.
- The psyche state ψ_t is a scalar derived from heuristically weighted chess features, lacking psychological grounding.
- Personality presets are hand‑tuned analogies, not learned from data.

## Confidence
- Signal chain mechanism: **High** (strong ablation evidence, gradient consistency).
- ψ_t factor model: **Low** (unvalidated weights, scalar simplification).
- Architecture‑agnosticism: **Medium** (cross‑model test but narrow model diversity).
- Human‑likeness claim: **Low** (indirect metric, no human validation).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct human‑play experiments: recruit players to rate move human‑likeness or compare Ailed's moves under different ψ_t to a human game corpus beyond Maia2.
2. Test opponent robustness: run the same ψ_t conditions against varied opponents (Stockfish levels, other Maia versions, humans) to measure gradient stability.
3. Validate ψ_t factors: correlate ψ_t trajectories with human emotional data (e.g., heart rate, self‑reports) or perform feature ablation/learning to identify a more predictive factor set.
