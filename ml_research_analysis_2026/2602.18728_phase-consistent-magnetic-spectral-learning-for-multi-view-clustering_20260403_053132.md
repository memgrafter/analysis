# Phase-Consistent Magnetic Spectral Learning for Multi-View Clustering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18728
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18728
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Phase-consistent magnetic spectral learning yields complex-valued affinities and a Hermitian magnetic Laplacian, achieving top-2 clustering accuracy on ten benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Unsupervised multi-view clustering aims to discover common cluster structures from multiple data representations. Existing methods that rely solely on magnitude-based affinities struggle with view discrepancies and directional conflicts. This paper introduces Phase-Consistent Magnetic Spectral Learning, which enriches nonnegative magnitude affinities with a phase term modeling cross-view directional agreement, forming a complex-valued magnetic affinity. The Hermitian magnetic Laplacian derived from this affinity provides a stable shared spectral signal for structured self-supervision. Scalability is addressed via an anchor hypergraph with curvature refinement to suppress noise. Experiments across ten benchmark datasets consistently place the method in the top-2 for ACC, NMI, and ARI against strong baselines. Ablations confirm that the phase term and magnetic spectrum enhance spectral stability (larger eigengaps, lower subspace distances) and clustering accuracy, demonstrating the efficacy of incorporating phase information into spectral learning for multi-view analysis.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework comprises three stages. First, for each view, a nonnegative magnitude affinity matrix is computed from pairwise similarities. A phase term is derived by measuring directional agreement (e.g., cosine similarity of affinity vectors) across views and combined with the magnitude to yield a complex-valued magnetic affinity matrix, which is Hermitian by construction. Second, the magnetic Laplacian is defined as the degree-normalized version of this affinity, ensuring real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors. This Laplacian is used for spectral embedding, producing a low-dimensional representation that captures shared structure. Third, to scale to large datasets, an anchor hypergraph is built using a subset of representative points; curvature refinement adjusts edge weights based on local geometry to mitigate noise. Clustering is performed via k-means on the spectral embedding. The entire pipeline is unsupervised and jointly optimizes cross-view consistency and spectral stability.

## Key Results
- Top-2 performance in clustering metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI) on ten benchmarks compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Phase-consistent magnetic spectrum significantly improves spectral stability, with larger eigengaps and lower subspace distances than real-valued spectral methods.
- Ablations validate the contributions of the phase term, Hermitian Laplacian, and curvature refinement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method succeeds by addressing two fundamental challenges in multi-view spectral clustering. (1) Magnitude-only affinities ignore the directional relationships between views, which can lead to conflicts when views disagree on the orientation of similarities. The phase term explicitly encodes cross-view directional agreement as an angular component, supplementing magnitude and yielding a more discriminative and consistent affinity matrix. (2) Standard spectral methods often suffer from unstable eigenvalues when the affinity is noisy or ill-conditioned. The Hermitian magnetic Laplacian, derived from the complex-valued affinity, inherits properties of complex symmetric matrices that promote larger eigengaps and orthogonal eigenvectors, resulting in a robust spectral embedding. Additionally, the anchor hypergraph reduces computational complexity from quadratic to linear in the number of data points, while curvature refinement smooths the affinity by incorporating local geometric information, further suppressing noise. Together, these elements produce a stable shared signal that generalizes across views and scales efficiently.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Raw multi-view data -> Magnitude backbone per view -> Phase aggregation -> Complex magnetic affinity (Hermitian) -> Anchor hypergraph construction -> Curvature refinement -> Magnetic Laplacian -> Spectral embedding -> Clustering (k-means).

Critical path: The data flows from raw inputs through magnitude and phase computations to form the magnetic affinity, which is then approximated by an anchor hypergraph for efficiency. After curvature refinement, the magnetic Laplacian is derived and decomposed to obtain spectral embeddings, finally clustered by k-means. This sequence is the backbone of the unsupervised training pipeline.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Complex-valued operators increase memory and computation (complex arithmetic) but provide richer representation and spectral stability.  
- Anchor hypergraph greatly reduces time and space complexity at the cost of approximate representation; choosing too few anchors degrades accuracy.  
- Curvature refinement adds overhead but improves noise robustness; its parameter (e.g., curvature scale) must be tuned.

Failure signatures:  
- Small or decreasing eigengaps in the magnetic Laplacian spectrum, indicating poor separation.  
- Clustering performance drops significantly on noisy or incomplete views, suggesting sensitivity to data quality.  
- Runtime or memory consumption grows superlinearly with dataset size, indicating anchor selection or hypergraph construction inefficiencies.  
- Over-smoothing from excessive curvature refinement leading to loss of discriminative structure.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is confined to ten relatively clean and complete benchmarks; performance on noisy, incomplete, or real-world multi-view data remains unverified.
- No statistical significance testing (e.g., paired t-tests) is conducted, so the robustness of reported performance gains across random seeds is uncertain.
- The computational cost of complex-valued operators is not thoroughly analyzed; scalability claims are based on moderate-scale data, and large-scale efficiency is not demonstrated.
