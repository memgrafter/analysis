# OmniCompliance-100K: A Multi-Domain, Rule-Grounded, Real-World Safety Compliance Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13933
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13933
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A web-agent pipeline collected 106,009 real-world compliance cases aligned with 12,985 manually curated rules across 74 regulations, enabling benchmarking that reveals specific LLM failure modes (e.g., poor on platform policies, bias, guardrails).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OmniCompliance-100K, a large-scale dataset of real-world safety compliance cases grounded in atomic rules derived from 74 authoritative regulations and policies across nine domains (AI law, data privacy, finance, etc.). A scalable web-searching agent pipeline automatically retrieves and summarizes cases from authoritative online sources, with subsequent validation confirming strong rule-case alignment (91–96%). Benchmarking diverse LLMs on a 2-way permitted/prohibited classification uncovers nuanced patterns: models struggle more with dynamic platform policies than formal laws, find bias/discrimination scenarios most challenging, perform well on financial regulations, and even small 3B models remain competitive. Notably, dedicated safety guardrail models perform poorly, highlighting a gap between narrow safety alignment and broad compliance reasoning.

## Method Summary
The construction process begins with manual expert curation of 74 regulations/policies into hierarchical trees, yielding 12,985 atomic rules. An LLM-powered agent (Grok-4.1) then processes each rule: it generates multiple search queries, invokes a web search API restricted to authoritative domains (e.g., .gov, official company sites), scrapes results, and filters/summarizes content into structured JSON cases (background, outcome, rule reference). The resulting cases are validated via LLM judges and human experts using a 1–3 relevance rubric on a 2,220-sample subset. Finally, a knowledge graph links rules and cases, and a benchmark harness evaluates target LLMs by comparing their permitted/prohibited predictions to ground truth, computing macro-F1 scores.

## Key Results
- The agent pipeline collects 106,009 cases with high rule-case alignment (91.32–95.90% across LLM judges; 91.77% human).
- LLMs perform worse on platform policies than formal laws and struggle most with bias/discrimination cases, while excelling in financial regulations.
- Small 3B models achieve competitive macro-F1 scores; dedicated safety guardrails (Llama-Guard, WildGuard) perform poorly (<40%), indicating limited generalization from synthetic safety data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The web-searching agent pipeline enables scalable collection of real-world, rule-grounded compliance cases from scattered online sources.
- **Mechanism:** The agent automates query planning, web retrieval, and filtering/summarization, overcoming manual barriers like varied website structures.
- **Core assumption:** Advanced LLMs can reliably interpret rules, generate effective queries, and distill relevant details from unstructured web content.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the agent "allows us to efficiently acquire high-quality, rule-grounded real cases"; Section 3.2 details the workflow; corpus shows related work but weak direct evidence.
- **Break condition:** If query generation or filtering fails to capture authoritative sources or introduces selection bias, rule-case coverage and authenticity degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Manual curation of a hierarchical rule set combined with LLM/human alignment validation ensures strong rule-case grounding.
- **Mechanism:** Experts manually structure regulations into atomic rules; retrieval ties cases explicitly to these rules, with alignment scores confirming correspondence.
- **Core assumption:** Expert curation produces a comprehensive, non-redundant rule enumeration faithful to source regulations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "manually curated rules" and strong alignment; Section 3.1 details the month-long manual process; Section 4.3 reports alignment scores (91–96%).
- **Break condition:** If manual rule enumeration misses nuanced provisions or introduces interpretative drift, cases may misalign with the regulation's true scope.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Benchmarking on this diverse dataset reveals specific capability ceilings and domain-dependent performance gaps in LLM compliance reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Testing across 74 regulations with 2-way classification surfaces patterns (e.g., poor on policies, bias) that synthetic datasets miss.
- **Core assumption:** Real-world cases require nuanced, context-sensitive judgment differing from simpler safety evaluation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract lists key findings; Section 4.2 presents per-domain macro-F1 scores; corpus includes related compliance-focused work.
- **Break condition:** If case sampling bias (e.g., over-represented enforcement actions) distorts domain difficulty, benchmark comparisons may not reflect true capability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Regulatory Compliance Structure:** Understanding hierarchical organization (acts → chapters → articles) is essential to grasp rule enumeration and dataset granularity. *Quick check:* Why might GDPR need thousands of atomic rules rather than article-level evaluation?
- **LLM Safety vs. Compliance Distinction:** "Compliance" is presented as a systematic, rule-grounded alternative to ad-hoc safety taxonomies; this shift underpins the dataset's design. *Quick check:* How does evaluating against a specific legal article (e.g., GDPR Art. 46) differ from evaluating against a generic safety principle like "avoid harmful content"?
- **Agentic Information Retrieval & Filtering:** The dataset construction relies on an LLM agent performing multi-step web search and summarization; its design explains scalability. *Quick check:* What are two distinct failure modes (e.g., query mis-generation, summary hallucination) that could corrupt the case collection pipeline?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Rule Module -> Agent Search Pipeline -> Validation Module -> Knowledge Graph Builder -> Benchmark Harness.
- **Critical path:** Rule enumeration -> Agent search (per rule) -> Case storage -> Alignment validation -> Knowledge graph construction -> Benchmark dataset finalization. The agent search loop is the runtime bottleneck.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Scalability vs. coverage (agent allows scale but may miss niche sources); Automation vs. quality (LLM summarization reduces cost but risks omitting subtle details); Breadth vs. depth (covers 74 regulations but may lack deep multi-article reasoning cases).
- **Failure signatures:** Low alignment scores (<85%) indicate weak rule-case ties; Systematically low scores per domain suggest inadequate sources or vague rules; High variance in per-category F1 for capable models points to domain-specific gaps; Guardrail model collapse (<40% F1) indicates overfitting to narrow synthetic data.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Replicate alignment validation on a fresh random sample (n≥500) to confirm 91–96% scores. 2) Conduct source ablation: restrict agent search for a subset (e.g., GDPR) to only official regulatory domains vs. full authoritative set; measure impact on diversity/alignment. 3) Test knowledge graph utility: retrieve multi-hop neighboring rules/cases for GDPR; have experts judge contextual meaningfulness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Why do LLMs systematically underperform on platform policy compliance cases compared to formal legal regulations?
- What specific characteristics of bias/discrimination cases make them the most challenging category for LLMs to judge correctly?
- Why do dedicated safety guardrail models (e.g., Llama-Guard, WildGuard) perform significantly worse than general-purpose LLMs on rule-based compliance tasks?
- How reliable and scalable is the web-search agent pipeline for collecting rule-grounded cases across diverse, unstructured regulatory sources?
- What actionable insights do the correlations in the rule-case knowledge graph provide for improving automated compliance verification systems?

## Limitations
- Agent pipeline fidelity is uncertain due to undisclosed prompts, search parameters, and filtering logic, risking hidden biases in source selection or summarization.
- Rule-coverage completeness may be compromised if manual extraction from 74 regulations misses nuanced provisions or creates artificial boundaries.
- Benchmark representativeness is limited as cases derive from the agent's source pool, which may over-represent enforcement actions or specific jurisdictions.

## Confidence
- Strong rule-case alignment (91–96% scores): **High** – Supported by multi-LLM/human validation on 2,220 samples with clear rubric.
- Agent scalability & quality: **Medium** – Output scale and authority filtering are demonstrated, but lack of prompt ablation/error analysis leaves efficacy unverified.
- Benchmark findings (e.g., model struggles on bias, platform policies): **Medium** – Patterns are empirically shown, but confounding factors (e.g., case distribution skew) cannot be ruled out.

## Next Checks
1. **Alignment replication:** Independently evaluate rule-case alignment on a fresh random sample (n≥500) using the same rubric to confirm stability of the 91–96% score range.
2. **Source diversity ablation:** For a subset of rules (e.g., GDPR), compare cases retrieved when restricting sources to only official regulatory domains (e.g., .gov, .eu) versus the full authoritative set; measure changes in alignment and topic coverage.
3. **Rule-enumeration audit:** Engage domain legal experts to review the atomic rule set for a complex regulation (e.g., GDPR), assessing whether the 12,985 rules holistically and accurately capture the source laws’ obligations without over- or under-generation.
