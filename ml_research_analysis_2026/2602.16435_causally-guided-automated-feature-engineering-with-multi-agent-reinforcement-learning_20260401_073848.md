# Causally-Guided Automated Feature Engineering with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16435
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16435
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CAFE achieves up to 7% improvement in predictive metrics (macro-F1 for classification, 1-RAE for regression) over strong baselines across 15 public benchmarks, and reduces performance drop under covariate shifts by approximately 4× compared to non-causal multi-agent methods.

## Executive Summary
Existing automated feature engineering (AFE) methods rely on statistical correlations, which often produce features that are brittle under distribution shifts. This paper introduces CAFE, a causally-guided AFE framework that reformulates feature generation as a sequential decision process. CAFE first learns a sparse causal directed acyclic graph (DAG) to group features by their influence on the target (direct, indirect, other). It then uses a cascading multi-agent deep Q-network with causally-informed priors and hierarchical reward shaping to generate robust transformations. Evaluated on 15 benchmarks, CAFE not only improves predictive performance but also significantly enhances robustness under covariate shifts, yielding more compact and stable feature sets.

## Method Summary
CAFE operates in two phases. Phase I applies NOTEARS-Lasso to learn a sparse causal graph over features and the target, then groups features into direct, indirect, and other causal roles based on their directed path relationships to the target. Phase II employs a multi-agent reinforcement learning system where three specialized agents sequentially select: (1) a causal group, (2) a transformation operator, and (3) an optional partner group for binary operations. The agents operate on state representations built from nested statistical summaries and are guided by a reward function that combines predictive improvement, causal alignment bonuses, diversity promotion, and complexity penalties. This design factorizes the large joint action space, reduces value-estimate variance, and steers the policy toward causally plausible features while controlling complexity.

## Key Results
- Across 15 public benchmarks, CAFE achieves up to 7% improvement in predictive metrics (macro-F1 for classification, 1-RAE for regression) over strong baselines.
- Under covariate shifts, CAFE reduces performance degradation by approximately 4× (7.1% vs. 28.1%) compared to a non-causal multi-agent method.
- Generated feature sets are more compact and exhibit 58.6% more stable SHAP attributions under perturbations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Learning a sparse causal DAG in Phase I groups features by estimated causal influence (direct, indirect, other), providing a soft inductive bias that guides RL agents toward transformations likely to remain predictive under distribution shift. NOTEARS-Lasso optimizes a continuous objective balancing reconstruction error and L1 sparsity under an acyclicity constraint. The resulting adjacency matrix defines a DAG; features are categorized based on their directed path to the target. This grouping acts as a probabilistic prior influencing action sampling and reward shaping. Core assumption: true data-generating process has a sparse causal structure approximable from observational data under linear additive noise and causal sufficiency. Evidence: [abstract] describes grouping; [section 3.1-3.2] details NOTEARS formulation and grouping rule; neighbor papers on causal stability corroborate principle. Break condition: if graph quality is poor (high SHD), misalignment may occur, but CAFE degrades gracefully due to soft-prior design and adaptive MI fallback.

### Mechanism 2
A cascading multi-agent DQN architecture with decision factorization reduces the combinatorial action space and stabilizes learning. Three agents sequentially select: (1) a causal group, (2) a transformation operator, (3) a partner group (for binary ops). Each agent observes a state built from nested summaries and prior decisions. Factorization yields lower-dimensional Q-functions, reduces TD update variance, and allows group-specific exploration. Core assumption: value function decomposes as Q(s,a₁,a₂,a₃) ≈ Q₁(s,a₁) + Qₒ(s,a₁,aₒ) + Q₂(s,a₁,aₒ,a₂); causal grouping provides meaningful decomposition. Evidence: [abstract] mentions cascading multi-agent DQN; [section 3.3] explains decision factorization; [section C.1] gives state constructions. Break condition: if causal groups are uninformative, factorization may force suboptimal decompositions, but system still functions, converging slower but not failing.

### Mechanism 3
A hierarchically shaped reward combines predictive improvement, causal alignment bonuses, diversity, and complexity penalties to steer policy toward accurate, causally plausible, simple features. Reward Rₜ = R_perf,t · (1 + α·Ψ_causal,t) + λ_div·H(πₜ) - λ_comp·C(Fᵍₜ) has: (1) Δ validation score, (2) weighted usage bonus for direct/indirect groups, (3) entropy for exploration, (4) complexity penalty. Multiplicative causal bonus scales performance reward, ensuring small improvements with high causal alignment yield greater net reward. Core assumption: causal alignment correlates with robustness; bonus α biases without overwhelming performance signal. Evidence: [abstract] mentions hierarchical reward; [section 3.3, D.1] gives specification and weights (w_direct=1.0, w_indirect=0.6, w_other=0.2, α=0.5); [section 4.2.2] ablation shows 1.5-2.8% drop when removing Rc. Break condition: erroneous group assignment may penalize useful transforms, but soft nature and R_perf dominance prevent catastrophe.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Causal Models (SCMs) & DAGs**  
  Why needed: CAFE’s premise rests on a stable causal graph approximated from data. Understanding X_j := f_j(PA_j, ε_j), d-separation, and causal roles is essential to grasp why grouping by directed path to Y matters for robustness.  
  Quick check: If feature A is a parent of B, and B is a parent of Y, how would you categorize A in CAFE’s grouping, and why might a transformation involving A be more robust under covariate shift than one involving a feature with no directed path to Y?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Q-Networks (DQN)**  
  Why needed: The multi-agent system is built on DQN. Understanding experience replay, target networks, ε-greedy, and TD learning is necessary to interpret how the policy learns from structured state and reward.  
  Quick check: How does factorization of actions into three agents affect the shape of replay buffer transitions (s, a₁, aₒ, a₂, r, s') compared to a single-agent system?

- **Concept: Automated Feature Engineering (AFE) & Transformation Spaces**  
  Why needed: CAFE solves the combinatorial problem of O(|O|·|F|²) transformations. Knowing expansion-pruning paradigms (e.g., AutoFeat) and their limitations (correlation-based brittleness) establishes the baseline CAFE improves upon.  
  Quick check: What is the computational complexity of naively enumerating all binary interactions between 100 features using 10 operators, and why is this intractable without a guided search strategy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: causal_discovery.py -> grouping.py -> agents/ (GroupAgent, OperatorAgent, PartnerAgent) -> state_builder.py -> reward_shaper.py -> feature_generator.py -> pruning.py; exploration_strategy.py interacts throughout.
- **Critical path**: 1) Run causal_discovery and grouping (Phase I). 2) Initialize agents. 3) For each episode: state_builder constructs states -> agent cascade selects group, op, partner -> feature_generator applies operators with safety guards -> pruning (variance then MI) -> reward_shaper computes Rₜ -> agents update via TD loss. 4) Track best feature set.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Causal prior strength (α) trades bias vs. missing spurious features; group size k_g trades signal preservation for speed; operator library size affects expressivity vs. stability.
- **Failure signatures**: Phase I failure if NOTEARS doesn’t converge or graph too sparse (fallback to correlation/PC); Phase II stuck if validation plateaus but feature count grows (increase λ_comp); memory explosion if |F_current| exceeds cap despite pruning (raise variance threshold).
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Sanity check Phase I on synthetic data to verify grouping matches known causal roles. 2) Compare training curves of CAFE vs. single-agent DQN on medium dataset to validate factorization benefits. 3) Ablate causal bonus on shifted data to confirm its role in robustness (replicate Fig. 3 partially).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can CAFE be extended to handle temporal or streaming data with time-varying causal structures?
- Basis: The conclusion identifies static causal graph assumption as limitation; future work calls for temporal extension.
- Why unresolved: Current design relies on static DAG learned in Phase I, unsuitable for evolving relationships (e.g., drift, markets).
- Resolution evidence: Develop temporal CAFE with incremental graph updates (sliding windows/recurrent models), evaluated on time-series with drift vs. static CAFE and baselines.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What robust causal discovery methods can mitigate bias from unobserved confounders in CAFE's feature engineering?
- Basis: Conclusion notes unobserved confounders may bias; limitations criticize causal sufficiency assumption.
- Why unresolved: NOTEARS-Lasso assumes no latent confounders; violations misguide grouping.
- Resolution evidence: Integrate confounder-aware methods (e.g., FCI) into Phase I, test on synthetic data with hidden confounders, measure stability/accuracy vs. standard CAFE.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can CAFE's computational complexity be reduced to scale to high-dimensional spaces (d > 200)?
- Basis: Limitations highlight Phase I O(d³) cost; future directions suggest distributed computing/meta-learning.
- Why unresolved: Cubic scaling makes CAFE impractical for hundreds of features.
- Resolution evidence: Implement scalable alternatives (sparse NOTEARS, subsampling, neural discovery), benchmark on high-D data, report runtime/memory/performance trade-offs.

## Limitations
- Assumes static causal structure and no latent confounders; violations can lead to inaccurate feature groupings.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (causal bonus weight α, complexity penalty λ_comp) may require dataset-specific tuning.
- High computational complexity from causal discovery (O(d³)) and multi-agent RL limits scalability beyond ~1000 features.

## Confidence
- Causal grouping improves robustness: Medium (ablations show benefit, but depends on approximate graph quality).
- Multi-agent factorization stabilizes learning: Medium (training curves indicate lower variance, but systematic comparison limited).
- Hierarchical reward yields accurate & parsimonious features: Medium-High (ablation causes 1.5-2.8% drop; complexity penalty effective).
- Overall CAFE outperforms baselines: High (consistent superiority across 15 benchmarks and ~4× shift robustness improvement).

## Next Checks
1. Graph quality sensitivity: Intentionally corrupt learned DAG (edge flips, noise) at varying levels, rerun CAFE, measure performance degradation under shift; verify adaptive MI fallback maintains robustness with poor graphs.
2. Full hyperparameter sweep: Systematically vary α (0-1), λ_comp, group-size rule across multiple datasets; record trade-offs between validation performance, feature compactness, and shift robustness.
3. Real-world shift benchmark suite: Curate datasets with natural covariate shifts (e.g., temporal sales splits, geographic healthcare splits); replicate shift experiments measuring predictive drop and SHAP stability beyond synthetic shifts.
