# Chain-of-Goals Hierarchical Policy for Long-Horizon Offline Goal-Conditioned RL

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03389  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03389  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: CoGHP achieves state-of-the-art performance on OGBench long-horizon tasks, with success rates up to 97% on cube-single and 79% on giant pointmaze, significantly surpassing baselines like HIQL and GCIQL.

## Executive Summary
Offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning faces challenges in long-horizon tasks due to suboptimal hierarchical methods that often generate only a single subgoal, lose final-goal awareness, and suffer from fragmented optimization across separate networks. This paper presents Chain-of-Goals Hierarchical Policy (CoGHP), a novel approach that recasts hierarchical decision-making as autoregressive sequence generation within a unified model. CoGHP processes a state and a goal, then sequentially emits latent subgoals from the farthest to the nearest, followed by a primitive action. Its backbone is an MLP-Mixer augmented with a causal token-mixer to respect autoregressive dependencies while enabling cross-token communication. Training leverages a shared goal-conditioned value function to compute advantages for all sequence elements, enabling end-to-end advantage-weighted updates. Experiments on the OGBench benchmark across navigation and manipulation tasks show substantial improvements over prior methods, with success rates up to 79% on giant pointmaze and 97% on cube-single. Ablations validate the contributions of the MLP-Mixer and causal mixer, especially on complex tasks.

## Method Summary
CoGHP treats hierarchical policy learning as an autoregressive generation problem. The policy takes a state s and goal g, embeds them into e_o = ϕ_o(s) and e_g = ϕ_g(g), and then generates a sequence of H latent subgoals z_1 (farthest) through z_H (nearest) and finally a primitive action a. The architecture employs an MLP-Mixer backbone: token-mixing (here a causal lower-triangular linear transformation) aggregates information across tokens in a causally masked manner, followed by a channel-mixing MLP that processes each token’s features. This stack of blocks preserves the autoregressive order while allowing each token to incorporate all preceding context. A shared goal-conditioned value function V_ψ(s, e_g) is trained via Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) with an expectile loss. Policy training uses advantage-weighted regression: for subgoals, the advantage Â_h = V(s_i, e_g) - V(s, e_g) is discounted by γ_h^{i-1}; for the action, Â_ℓ = γV(s', z_1) - V(s, z_1). The total loss combines weighted negative log-likelihoods under the policy’s distributions with temperature β=3. Teacher forcing with ground-truth subgoals (derived from the dataset by sampling future states at fixed intervals) stabilizes training. Optimization uses Adam (lr=3e-4) for about one million gradient steps.

## Key Results
- Giant pointmaze: 79% success (vs HIQL 46%)
- Antmaze-giant: 78% success (vs HIQL 65%)
- Cube-single: 97% success (vs HIQL 41%)
- Scene: 78% success (vs HIQL 38%)
- Ablations show that both the MLP-Mixer backbone and the causal token-mixer contribute significantly to performance, with larger effects on more complex tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CoGHP’s unification eliminates the optimization misalignment that plagues hierarchical methods with separate networks; all components are trained with a consistent value-based objective. Autoregressive generation from farthest to nearest subgoals preserves final-goal awareness throughout the sequence, ensuring that intermediate subgoals remain aligned with the ultimate objective. The causal token-mixer provides each subgoal with access to all preceding tokens while preventing leakage from future ones, maintaining a valid training-inference correspondence. Advantage-weighted updates focus learning on high-value state-goal regions, which is crucial under offline data limitations. Additionally, the shared value function acts as a unified critic that evaluates both subgoals and actions with respect to the same long-horizon return, avoiding the feedback loops that can arise from bootstrapping on separately trained models. These mechanisms collectively yield robust long-horizon performance.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Offline Goal-Conditioned RL**  
   - *Why needed*: Enables learning from static datasets and generalized goal reaching without online interaction.  
   - *Quick check*: Can the policy achieve goals from the same distribution as the dataset without further environment interaction?

2. **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning**  
   - *Why needed*: Breaks long-horizon tasks into manageable subproblems via temporally extended subgoals.  
   - *Quick check*: Does a two-level hierarchy improve sample efficiency and success over a flat policy for tasks requiring >100 steps?

3. **Goal-Conditioned Value Function**  
   - *Why needed*: Estimates expected return from any state to a given goal, serving as a common training signal.  
   - *Quick check*: Is V_ψ well-calibrated (e.g., low TD error) across state-goal pairs in the dataset?

4. **Advantage-Weighted Regression / Implicit Q-Learning**  
   - *Why needed*: Allows policy improvement without explicit policy gradients, robust to distribution shift in offline RL.  
   - *Quick check*: Does updating the policy to increase probability of high-advantage actions improve returns in a validation set?

5. **MLP-Mixer Architecture**  
   - *Why needed*: Provides an efficient backbone for sequence modeling with separate token and channel mixing.  
   - *Quick check*: Does replacing MLP-Mixer with a transformer of similar size yield comparable performance?

6. **Causal Token-Mixer**  
   - *Why needed*: Enforces autoregressive dependencies during training, ensuring the model cannot peek at future tokens.  
   - *Quick check*: Does removing causality (full attention) degrade performance due to training-inference mismatch?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**  
State s and goal g -> encoders ϕ_o and ϕ_g -> embeddings e_o, e_g -> token sequence: [e_o, e_g, placeholder for z_1, ..., placeholder for z_H, placeholder for a] -> MLP-Mixer blocks (causal token-mixing + channel-mixing) -> token-wise linear heads for subgoals (z_i) and action (a). Value network: s (or e_o) + e_g -> MLP -> scalar V(s,g).

**Critical Path**  
- *Training*: For each batch, extract (s, a, s', g) and ground-truth subgoals s_i (future states). Encode subgoals as target subgoal embeddings (likely e_g(s_i)). Construct token sequence with ground-truth subgoal embeddings and action. Pass through MLP-Mixer (causal masking applied). Compute V(s,g) and V(s_i,g) for advantages. Compute loss and backpropagate.  
- *Inference*: Start with state and goal tokens. Sample z_1 from first subgoal head conditioned on tokens up to position 2. Append z_1 and sample z_2, repeat until z_H. Finally sample action a from last head.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Autoregressive subgoal generation allows sequential reasoning but may accumulate errors; simultaneous prediction could be faster but requires handling dependencies.
- Unified model simplifies optimization but introduces interference: subgoal and action predictions share representations, which could lead to suboptimal specialization.
- Causal token-mixer respects generation order but limits parallelization within training; full attention would be faster but introduces training-inference mismatch.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Value network underestimates returns: check if V predictions are too low or have high variance; may indicate insufficient training or distribution shift.
- Large performance drop between teacher-forced evaluation (using ground-truth subgoals) and free generation: indicates error accumulation in autoregressive loop.
- Causal mixer weights collapse to zero or identity: monitor weight norms; could signal uninformative mixing.
- Hyperparameter (λ_h, β, γ_h) sensitivity: success rate varies widely with small changes; may indicate poor conditioning.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Baseline replication**: Train CoGHP on the OGBench pointmaze-medium-navigate-v0 dataset with default hyperparameters (H, k, λ_h, λ_ℓ, β). Verify success rate matches reported ~79% on giant pointmaze after scaling.  
2. **Causal mixer ablation**: Replace the causal token-mixer with a non-causal (full attention) token-mixing layer while keeping everything else identical. Compare performance on complex tasks (antmaze-giant, cube-single) to confirm causality’s role.  
3. **Subgoal spacing sweep**: Vary the subgoal sampling interval k (e.g., 5, 10, 20, 50, 100) across pointmaze and manipulation datasets. Plot success rate vs. k to identify robust range and potential under/over-decomposition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adaptive subgoal composition** – The current fixed number H and spacing k may not suit all tasks. A method to dynamically adjust subgoal granularity would improve robustness.  
2. **Abstract subgoal representations** – Using encoded future states as subgoals may be limiting. Exploring skills, language, or other latent spaces could enhance expressiveness and generalization.  
3. **Generalization to unseen tasks** – All evaluations are on OGBench; it is unknown if CoGHP transfers to new domains, shifted dynamics, or tasks with different state/goal distributions.  
4. **Bidirectional or diffusion-based planning** – Autoregressive generation risks error propagation. Extensions that refine subgoal sequences globally (e.g., diffusion models) might yield more stable and optimal plans.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps**: Critical details (target subgoal encoding, causal mixer parameterization, value network input, policy distribution) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- **Benchmark scope**: Results are confined to OGBench; performance on other offline goal-conditioned benchmarks (e.g., Meta-World, D4RL) remains unknown.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method’s dependence on λ_h, β, γ_h, and subgoal spacing k is not thoroughly analyzed; optimal values likely vary with task complexity.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority**: Medium – Large empirical gains are reported, but missing implementation details and limited benchmark coverage reduce certainty.
- **Architectural contribution (MLP-Mixer + causal mixer)**: Medium – Ablations are positive but the exact causal mixer operation is ambiguous, making it hard to isolate its effect.
- **Training procedure (shared value + advantage weighting)**: Medium – The loss form is clear, but choices for the policy distribution and value network preprocessing remain unclear.
