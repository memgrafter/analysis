# Additive Control Variates Dominate Self-Normalisation in Off-Policy Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.14914  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14914  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** The β★-IPS estimator, using an optimal additive baseline, asymptotically dominates self-normalized IPS (SNIPS) in mean squared error for off-policy evaluation.

## Executive Summary
Off-policy evaluation (OPE) is essential for assessing ranking and recommendation systems using logged interaction data. The widely used self-normalized inverse propensity scoring (SNIPS) reduces variance through a multiplicative control variate, but its variance properties are not optimal. This paper introduces β★-IPS, an estimator that employs an optimal additive baseline. The authors prove that, asymptotically, β★-IPS achieves lower mean squared error (MSE) than SNIPS. They analytically decompose the variance gap, showing SNIPS is equivalent to an additive estimator with baseline V(π), which is generally suboptimal. The exact variance gap is Δ = [V(π)σ²_w - σ_w,wr]² / (nσ²_w) ≥ 0. Under the Item-Position Model for rankings, β★⊥⊥-IPM attains position-wise dominance over SNIPM. These theoretical results suggest that replacing self-normalization with optimal additive baseline corrections can improve OPE accuracy.

## Method Summary
The paper compares two variance reduction techniques for OPE: the standard multiplicative self-normalization (as in SNIPS) and additive control variates. It defines the general class of β-IPS estimators, which add a baseline β to the IPS weight. The optimal baseline β★ minimizes the asymptotic variance and is expressed in terms of conditional variances and covariances of the reward and weight. The authors demonstrate that SNIPS corresponds to a specific choice β = V(π), the conditional variance of the reward given context under the target policy. Since V(π) is not generally the optimal baseline, SNIPS incurs an avoidable variance penalty. The analysis is extended to the Item-Position Model (IPM) for ranking, yielding β★⊥⊥-IPM, which dominates SNIPM position-wise. The results are derived under standard assumptions (e.g., known nuisance parameters, sufficient sample size).

## Key Results
- β★-IPS, with an optimal additive baseline, asymptotically lowers MSE compared to SNIPS.
- The variance gap Δ = [V(π)σ²_w - σ_w,wr]² / (nσ²_w) quantifies SNIPS’s suboptimality and is strictly positive unless specific conditions hold.
- In the Item-Position Model, β★⊥⊥-IPM achieves position-wise dominance over SNIPM, meaning it reduces variance for every individual position.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SNIPS uses a multiplicative normalizing factor that implicitly acts as an additive baseline equal to the conditional reward variance V(π). However, the optimal additive baseline for minimizing variance is β★ = V(π) - σ_w,wr / σ²_w. The discrepancy between V(π) and β★ generates the variance gap Δ. By explicitly adding β★, β★-IPS removes this gap and attains the minimum asymptotic variance among additive baseline estimators. The proof relies on a delta-method expansion and careful variance decomposition, showing that the O(1/n) variance of SNIPS has a larger constant than that of β★-IPS. Under IPM, the optimal baseline further leverages position-specific independence properties, yielding position-wise gains.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Logged data → Estimate propensities/rewards → Compute nuisance parameters (V(π), σ²_w, σ_w,wr) → Derive optimal baseline β★ → Calculate β★-IPS estimate

**Critical Path:**  
Accurate estimation of the nuisance parameters (especially conditional variances/covariances) is essential because β★ depends on them; errors directly affect the final estimator’s variance. If any step is inaccurate, the theoretical advantage may vanish or even reverse in finite samples.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Optimality vs practicality:* True β★ requires knowledge of population quantities; estimating them introduces additional variance. SNIPS avoids estimation but yields suboptimal variance.  
- *Assumption dependence:* The theoretical guarantees rely on the IPM for rankings; more complex user behavior models may break position-wise dominance.  
- *Focus on MSE:* The analysis centers on MSE; other criteria like confidence interval coverage or robustness to model misspecification are not considered.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Estimated β★ has high variance, causing β★-IPS to underperform SNIPS in finite samples.  
- Significant deviation from IPM assumptions (e.g., position interaction effects) leads to loss of position-wise dominance.  
- The derived variance gap Δ becomes negligible in practice due to estimation noise, making the theoretical gains irrelevant.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Reproduce the analytical variance gap Δ on synthetic data where V(π), σ²_w, and σ_w,wr are known.  
2. Conduct a controlled simulation under IPM: compare true β★-IPM and SNIPM across varying sample sizes to observe the asymptotic MSE crossover.  
3. Implement β★-IPM with estimated nuisance parameters (e.g., via cross-fitting) and evaluate its relative MSE compared to SNIPM on the same data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, the theoretical focus leaves several practical questions unaddressed, such as finite-sample performance with estimated baselines, extensions beyond the Item-Position Model, and the interplay with confidence interval construction.

## Limitations
- Asymptotic results rely on known nuisance parameters; estimation errors in finite samples could erase theoretical gains.  
- Computing β★ requires conditional second moments that are typically unknown and challenging to estimate accurately.  
- The ranking evaluation assumes the simplistic Item-Position Model; real systems may not satisfy its assumptions, and the lack of empirical validation means the practical benefits are uncertain.
