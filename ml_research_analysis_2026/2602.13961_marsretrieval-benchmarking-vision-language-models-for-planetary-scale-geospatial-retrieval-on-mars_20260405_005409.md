# MarsRetrieval: Benchmarking Vision-Language Models for Planetary-Scale Geospatial Retrieval on Mars

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13961
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13961
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Domain-specific fine-tuning (MarScope) outperforms general VLMs by ~50% in macro mAP on landform retrieval, proving planetary adaptation is critical for geospatial discovery.

## Executive Summary
MarsRetrieval introduces the first benchmark for evaluating vision-language models on text-guided, planetary-scale geospatial discovery on Mars, moving beyond closed-set supervised tasks. It defines three retrieval tasks: (1) paired image-text retrieval across spatial scales, (2) landform retrieval across 48 geomorphic categories, and (3) global geo-localization within a 1.4 million tile mosaic. A unified retrieval-centric protocol enables fair comparison of dual-encoder and MLLM-based VLMs. Results show the benchmark is highly challenging: general foundation models (e.g., CLIP, Qwen3-VL) significantly underperform a domain-specific fine-tuned model (MarScope), with a 50% gap in macro mAP on landform retrieval. Caption refinement and prompt ensembling provide gains, but domain-specific post-training is indispensable for generalizable geospatial discovery.

## Method Summary
MarsRetrieval employs a retrieval-based evaluation where images and texts are encoded into a shared, L2-normalized embedding space; candidates are ranked by cosine similarity. Task 1 uses 2,287 curated Martian image-text pairs from DataComp‑1B/Relation‑2B after rigorous filtering and MLLM‑based caption refinement. Task 2 evaluates retrieval over 1,185 CTX/HiRISE patches spanning 48 landform subclasses. Task 3 performs global geo‑localization against a 1.4 M tile equirectangular mosaic using catalog coordinates for five landforms. Metrics include R@K/MRR for paired retrieval, macro mAP/nDCG@10 for landform retrieval, and AUPRC/F1@K* for geo‑localization with a spatial tolerance radius *r*=0.5°.

## Key Results
- MarScope achieves 71.89% macro mAP on landform retrieval vs. 20.93% for the best general model—a 50% gap.
- In global geo‑localization, MarScope attains top AUPRC (e.g., 83.24% on Pitted Cones with text queries).
- Caption refinement and prompt ensembling improve all models, but domain-specific fine-tuning remains essential for high performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** A unified retrieval-centric protocol enables fair, zero‑shot comparison across diverse VLM architectures.  
**Mechanism:** Images and texts are encoded into a shared embedding space; candidates are ranked by cosine similarity. This removes task‑specific heads and classification biases, focusing purely on cross‑modal alignment.  
**Core assumption:** All models produce meaningful, normalised embeddings for both modalities.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "We propose a unified retrieval‑centric protocol to benchmark representative architectures for multimodal embedding..."  
- [section 3] "MarsRetrieval adopts a retrieval‑based evaluation protocol... can objectively measure zero‑shot understanding of VLMs..."  
**Break condition:** If the data distribution (spatial scales, landform diversity) is too narrow or biased, the protocol will not generalise to real planetary discovery scenarios.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Domain‑specific fine‑tuning is essential for Martian geospatial discovery because general VLMs lack specialised geomorphic knowledge.  
**Mechanism:** Fine‑tuning (e.g., MarScope) aligns the embedding space with subtle Martian landform distinctions via contrastive learning on curated planet imagery and text.  
**Core assumption:** There exists a representational gap between generic pre‑training data and domain‑specific planetary features that can be closed by adaptation.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "domain‑specific fine‑tuning is critical for generalizable geospatial discovery in planetary settings."  
- [section 7.3] "Domain‑specific fine‑tuning is essential... MarScope achieves dominant performance, with a 50% gap in macro mAP on landform retrieval..."  
**Break condition:** If the fine‑tuning data does not cover the morphological and geographic diversity of Mars (e.g., missing rare classes, illumination conditions), the model will overfit to a subset and fail to generalise.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** High‑quality caption refinement significantly improves vision‑language alignment for planetary data.  
**Mechanism:** A multi‑stage filtering (CLIPScore, LLM relevance, MLLM consistency) followed by MLLM‑based rewriting produces precise, visually‑grounded descriptions that reduce noise (IDs, coordinates, fluff) and better align with images.  
**Core assumption:** Raw web‑scale captions contain substantial domain‑irrelevant noise that harms cross‑modal representation learning.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "Caption refinement and prompt ensembling provide gains..."  
- [section 7.2] "Caption refinement consistently improves retrieval quality... all evaluated models yield consistent MRR gains..."  
**Break condition:** If the refinement process introduces hallucinations or over‑generalises beyond the visible evidence, alignment quality degrades and retrieval performance drops.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw Martian imagery/text corpora (DataComp‑1B, Relation‑2B, CTX/HiRISE) -> Filtering & refinement (CLIPScore, LLM/MLLM confidence) -> Caption refinement (MLLM rewriting) -> Expert validation -> Embedding extraction (model encoders) -> FAISS indexing -> Query encoding & cosine similarity search -> Metric computation (R@K, mAP, AUPRC) -> Model zoo (CLIP, SigLIP, PE‑Core, Qwen3‑VL, E5‑V, DINOv3).

**Critical path:**  
Raw data → Filtering → Caption refinement → Expert validation → Embedding extraction → FAISS indexing → Query encoding → Similarity search → Metric computation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Dual‑encoder vs MLLM:* Dual‑encoders are efficient and yield strong alignment; MLLMs offer richer semantics but are heavier and require pooling strategies.  
- *Caption refinement cost:* Automated MLLM rewriting adds computational overhead but improves performance; trade‑off between data quality and pipeline speed.  
- *Global mosaic tiling:* Overlapping tiles (0.2° resolution) provide planetary coverage but increase index size; non‑overlapping grids could reduce noise but miss features near tile borders.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Severe underperformance on long‑tail landform categories → indicates poor concept‑to‑instance generalisation.  
- Large gap between text‑ and image‑based queries in global geo‑localization → suggests insufficient vision‑language alignment or over‑reliance on visual similarity.  
- Low macro‑mAP despite high per‑class recall on common classes → reveals bias toward frequent landforms; model fails on rare geomorphic types.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can vision‑language models be effectively fine‑tuned for new planetary domains (e.g., Moon, Venus) with limited domain‑specific annotation, avoiding the need for large‑scale curated retrieval datasets like those used for MarScope?
2. What architectural modifications to vision‑language models can improve their ability to handle the extreme intra‑class morphological diversity of landforms (e.g., "pitted cones" at different erosion stages, scales, and latitudes) without relying on prompt ensembling or domain‑specific training?
3. For planetary‑scale geo‑localization, what is the principled method for setting the spatial tolerance radius (*r*) for different landform types and imaging resolutions, and how does this choice quantitatively affect the reliability and scientific utility of the retrieved distribution maps?
4. To what extent do the performance gains from caption refinement (using Qwen3‑VL) in Task 1 generalize beyond the specific Martian corpus used, and can this refinement process be automated or scaled for other scientific domains without human expert validation?
5. Do the retrieval tasks in MarsRetrieval (especially Global Geo‑Localization) measure *scientific discovery* capability or merely the alignment of model embeddings with existing catalog terminology? Can a model that scores highly on the benchmark actually lead to the identification of *new, previously uncatalogued* instances of these landforms?

## Limitations
- Dataset bias: curated Mars images may not cover all landform types; rare classes under‑represented.
- Caption refinement risks: MLLM rewriting may hallucinate details, harming alignment.
- Protocol scope: retrieval‑focused evaluation may miss other geospatial reasoning aspects.

## Confidence
- Unified retrieval protocol: **High** – consistently applied, cross‑architecture ablations confirm fairness.
- Domain fine‑tuning necessity: **High** – 50% macro mAP gap for MarScope; strong empirical evidence.
- Caption refinement gains: **Medium** – improvements shown but limited external validation; hallucination risk not measured.
