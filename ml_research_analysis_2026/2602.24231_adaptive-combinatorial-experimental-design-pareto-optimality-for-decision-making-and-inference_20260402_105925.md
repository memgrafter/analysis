# Adaptive Combinatorial Experimental Design: Pareto Optimality for Decision-Making and Inference
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.24231
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24231
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces two algorithms (MixCombKL and MixCombUCB) that achieve Pareto optimality between cumulative regret and reward gap estimation in combinatorial multi‑armed bandits.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies adaptive combinatorial experimental design within the combinatorial multi‑armed bandit (CMAB) framework, focusing on the trade‑off between cumulative regret and the accuracy of estimating reward gaps. It proposes MixCombKL for full‑bandit feedback (using KL‑divergence mirror descent and mixture sampling) and MixCombUCB for semi‑bandit feedback (using UCB with forced uniform exploration). Theoretical analysis provides finite‑time bounds for both regret and estimation error, and establishes a necessary and sufficient Pareto optimality condition: the product of the maximum expected gap error and the square root of regret must be bounded by a constant. The exploration parameter α governs the trade‑off; semi‑bandit feedback enjoys a tighter Pareto frontier by approximately a factor √(d/m). Simulations on small instances illustrate the theory.

## Method Summary
The problem involves selecting super arms (subsets of base arms) over n rounds to minimize cumulative regret while ensuring accurate estimation of reward gaps. Inputs are a set A of d base arms, a family M of super arms, a horizon n, and reward distributions with mean vector µ. Objectives are to minimize cumulative regret Rν(n,π) and the maximum expected absolute error in estimating any reward gap. MixCombKL maintains a distribution q_t over base arms and constructs a sampling distribution p_t over super arms such that the expected arm indicator equals m q_t. It performs a KL projection to keep q_t within a simplex capped at 1/m, then samples either uniformly (forced exploration) or from p_t. MixCombUCB begins with a greedy covering phase to identify a set S of special arms. In each round it computes UCBs for base arms, picks the top m arms, and samples either a special arm with probability α_t or the selected super arm with the remaining probability. The exploration parameter α ∈ [0,½] for full‑bandit and α ∈ [0,1] for semi‑bandit (with constant gaps) balances regret and estimation error.

## Key Results
- Pareto optimality condition: max_ν max_{i<j} E[|Δ̂-Δ|] · √{regret} = O(1) is necessary and sufficient.
- MixCombKL regret: O(√(m³ d n) + n^{1-α}); gap error: O(√(m³ d / n^{1-α})).
- MixCombUCB regret: O(m d log n + n^{1-α}) under constant gaps (otherwise O(√(m d n log n) + n^{1-α})); error: O(m d √(1/n^{1-α})).
- Semi‑bandit feedback improves the Pareto frontier by a factor ≈ √(d/m).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MixCombKL combines mirror descent with KL divergence and mixture sampling. Mirror descent updates the base‑arm distribution q_t based on observed rewards, encouraging concentration on high‑mean arms while the KL projection enforces the cap q_i ≤ 1/m. The mixture sampling guarantees a minimum level of uniform exploration (controlled by α), which bounds the estimation error at O(√(1/n^α)). MixCombUCB uses classic UCB bonuses to form optimistic super‑arm estimates and injects exploration by occasionally selecting special arms that cover all base arms. The forced exploration probability α_t = 1/(m₀ t^α) ensures each base arm is sampled Θ(n^α) times, yielding estimation error O(1/√{n^α}). The regret cost of this forced exploration appears as an additive n^{1-α} term. Theoretical analysis shows that for any algorithm satisfying the Pareto condition, the product of the maximum gap error and √(regret) is constant, and the proposed algorithms attain this bound up to problem‑dependent constants.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: MixCombKL: (observed rewards) → KL mirror descent update → q_t → (construct p_t via LP) → sample M_t → (full‑bandit feedback) → update all R_t(M). MixCombUCB: (greedy covering phase) → special set S → (per round: UCB computation → top‑m selection) → mixture distribution (α_t) → sample M_t → (semi‑bandit feedback) → update per‑arm estimates.

Critical path: MixCombKL: initialise q uniformly; each round: compute ˜w_t, form ˜q_t, project to q_t, solve LP for p_t, sample M_t, observe rewards, update R_t for all super arms. MixCombUCB: run covering to get S; then for t=1..n: compute U_t(e), pick top m ˜M, form π_t, sample M_t, observe, update.

Design tradeoffs: The exploration parameter α directly trades off regret (increasing α raises the additive n^{1-α} term) versus estimation error (decreasing α slows convergence). Full‑bandit feedback requires solving an LP each round (computational overhead) but yields a coarser Pareto frontier; semi‑bandit avoids LPs and enjoys tighter bounds due to direct per‑arm feedback.

Failure signatures:
- LP infeasible if q'_i > 1/m or if the convex hull of θ_S does not contain m q'; check projection and feasibility.
- Zero exploration probability if special arms S do not cover all base arms; some P(e∈M(t)) may become zero causing division‑by‑zero.
- Numerical instability in KL projection due to clipping; verify that projected q sums to 1 and respects caps.
- Σ_{t-1} singular or ill‑conditioned; add regularisation or ensure θ_S are linearly independent.
- Using α beyond theoretical limits (α>0.5 for full‑bandit, α>1 for semi‑bandit without large gaps) may break the Pareto guarantee; monitor empirical product.

3. Empirical Pareto sweep: vary α within theoretical ranges ([0.1,0.5] for MixCombKL, [0.1,1] for MixCombUCB), compute the Pareto metric, fit the frontier, and verify the predicted semi‑bandit improvement factor √(d/m).

## Limitations
- MixCombKL’s decomposition via a linear program to find p_t with prescribed first moment lacks algorithmic detail; feasibility and efficiency for large/complex families M are unclear.
- MixCombUCB initialization is ambiguously described; the pseudocode likely ignores initial rewards, potentially biasing UCB estimates.
- Experiments use α values (e.g., α=1) outside the theoretically guaranteed range for semi‑bandit without large gaps, casting doubt on the validity of the reported Pareto frontier.
- Theoretical bounds depend on problem‑dependent constants (λ_min, ρ_min) that may be much smaller for non‑uniform super‑arm families, weakening the stated rates.
- Scalability is questionable as both algorithms require operations over all super arms each round, contrary to the claimed oracle efficiency.

## Confidence
- MixCombKL theoretical bounds: Medium (decomposition feasibility not fully established)
- MixCombUCB theoretical bounds: Medium (initialization ambiguity and α range issues)
- Pareto optimality condition: Medium (derived under restrictive assumptions)
- Experimental validation: Low (insufficient detail for replication)
