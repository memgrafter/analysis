# GeoChallenge: A Multi-Answer Multiple-Choice Benchmark for Geometric Reasoning with Diagrams

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19252
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19252
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** GeoChallenge reveals a significant human-model gap in geometric reasoning (94.74% vs 75.89% exact match), with current LLMs failing due to inconsistency, weak diagram grounding, and non-convergent reasoning.

## Executive Summary
GeoChallenge is a large-scale (90K) automatically generated benchmark for diagram-grounded geometric reasoning. It features multi-answer multiple-choice questions with aligned text and diagrams, fine-grained difficulty ratings, and formal language annotations. The core method uses a symbolic generation pipeline with forward-chaining deduction and difficulty-weighted option sampling. Evaluation shows a large gap between humans (94.74% exact match) and the best LLM (GPT-5-nano, 75.89%). Analysis identifies three primary failure patterns: poor exact-match under strict no-guess multi-answer, weak and inconsistent reliance on visual diagrams, and frequent non-convergent reasoning leading to out-of-length outputs. The benchmark demonstrates that current LLMs struggle with long-step, globally consistent geometric proofs, especially as complexity increases.

## Method Summary
GeoChallenge is generated via a fully automated symbolic pipeline. Premises are sampled using clause templates and annealing-based construction composition up to depth 8. For each premise, the AlphaGeometry forward-chaining engine enumerates all provable conclusions within a depth limit. A difficulty scorer combines five features (description length, premise length, number of points, proof-search depth, proof length) with undisclosed weights to assign prior difficulty. The four highest-scoring conclusions become candidate answers; distractors are created via equivalence-preserving rewrites, relation negation, or ratio perturbation, ensuring at least one correct option. The premise and proofs are refined to include only used elements, translated to bilingual natural language, and rendered into a diagram. Finally, manual verification ensures readability, validity, and alignment, yielding 90,279 problems. The exact difficulty weights and distractor generation details are not disclosed, hindering full replication.

## Key Results
- **Human-model gap:** The best model (GPT-5-nano) achieves 75.89% exact match versus 94.74% for humans.
- **Failure patterns:** Models exhibit (1) inconsistency in exact-match under no-guess multi-answer (often over-selecting options), (2) weak and inconsistent diagram grounding (humans drop ~50% without diagrams, models show marginal change), and (3) non-convergent reasoning leading to OUT_OF_LENGTH errors.
- **Complexity sensitivity:** Model performance degrades more steeply than humans as problem difficulty increases, especially on long-step proofs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Inconsistency in Exact-Match**  
The strict no-guess, multi-answer multiple-choice protocol exposes that models' primary failure is inconsistency in producing the exact correct option set, not an inability to identify relevant geometric relations. The binary correct/incorrect judgment on the entire selected subset forces global constraint satisfaction. Models often recover parts of the correct set but fail to converge on the precise combination, evidenced by WRONG_ANSWER errors dominating (~75%) and average selected options (~1.66) exceeding the true average, indicating over-selection. Break condition: model exact match approaches human levels (>90%) with stable average selection count (~1.66).

**Mechanism 2: Weak Visual Grounding**  
Unlike humans, current LLMs fail to reliably ground their geometric reasoning in the provided diagram. Humans show a dramatic accuracy drop (51.88% exact match) when diagrams are removed from text-dense problems, proving heavy reliance on visual spatial information. Models show only marginal degradation or inconsistent improvement with images, suggesting they primarily rely on textual patterns and do not consistently extract or integrate visual evidence (e.g., point positions, line intersections, angle magnitudes) into their deductive process. Break condition: model performance degradation from text+image to text-only matches or exceeds the human degradation (~50% relative drop) across difficulty splits.

**Mechanism 3: Non-Convergent Reasoning**  
A significant fraction of model failures stem from non-convergent reasoning—generating excessively long, meandering, or repetitive deduction chains that never commit to a verifiable final answer set within the decoding budget. The long-step nature of the problems (average 16.72 steps) combined with greedy decoding and strict output format triggers failure modes where the model continues expanding or revising thoughts without reaching a concluding selection, recorded as OUT_OF_LENGTH and directly causing exact-match failure. Break condition: OUT_OF_LENGTH error rate drops below 5% for hard problems, and proof length distribution aligns with ground-truth.

## Foundational Learning
**Symbolic Geometry Representation**  
*Why needed here:* The entire dataset and evaluation are built on a formal language (clauses, constructions, premises) that defines geometric states. Understanding how points, lines, circles, and relations are axiomatically composed is prerequisite to interpreting problems, reasoning about them, and diagnosing model errors.  
*Quick check question:* Given the premise `a b c = right_triangle; d = midpoint a b;`, what are the defined points and what fundamental relations (e.g., collinearity, equality, perpendicularity) are implicitly or explicitly created?

**Multi-Answer Multiple-Choice Evaluation**  
*Why needed here:* The core innovation and diagnostic power of GeoChallenge lies in its multi-answer format. This changes the reasoning objective from "find one correct answer" to "verify all options and output the exact correct subset," which is critical for exposing inconsistency and over-selection.  
*Quick check question:* Why does a model that selects two correct options and one incorrect option receive a zero exact-match score, whereas in a single-answer setting it might receive partial credit?

**Diagram-Text Alignment and Grounding**  
*Why needed here:* Every problem has a rendered diagram that is semantically aligned with the formal premise and textual description. The key analysis compares performance with and without diagrams, making it essential to understand what constitutes valid grounding (using visual evidence to confirm or derive a textual claim) versus mere pattern matching on text.  
*Quick check question:* For a given problem, what specific visual evidence (e.g., a right angle symbol, a midpoint mark, parallel lines) would a human solver use to quickly verify option B, and how might a model miss or misinterpret that same evidence?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map (Critical Path):**  
Premise Sampling → Conclusion Search (Forward Chaining) → Top-K Conclusion Selection → Distractor Generation → Text/Diagram Rendering → Manual Viz QC → Dataset Finalization

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Scale vs. Tractability:* The annealing sampler explores broadly early but prunes aggressively to avoid combinatorial explosion, potentially missing some rare but valid complex configurations.  
- *Challenge vs. Solvability:* Option sampling selects the most difficult provable conclusions, maximizing diagnostic power but risking problems where the true solution is borderline-unprovable or highly non-intuitive, possibly conflating reasoning hardness with solvability ambiguity.  
- *Automation vs. Quality:* Full automation enables scale, but manual visualization QC is a necessary bottleneck to catch rendering failures (misaligned labels, invalid drawings) that symbolic validity alone cannot prevent.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- WRONG_ANSWER: Model produces a definite but incorrect option set. Indicates a reasoning error or flawed verification.  
- NO_ANSWER: Model fails to output any valid option letter(s). Indicates uncertainty, refusal, or format adherence failure.  
- OUT_OF_LENGTH: Generation truncates before final answer. Indicates non-convergent, verbose reasoning without termination.  
- Diagram Degradation Gap: The performance delta (text-only vs. text+image) is much smaller for models than for humans.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Modality Ablation:** Run the full evaluation suite in text-only mode for all models and compare EMA, option-level metrics, and Avg #Sel against the text+image results. Quantify the "diagram grounding gap" per model.  
2. **Difficulty Stratification Analysis:** Plot EMA vs. Prior/Posterior Difficulty bins for each model family. Verify that general-purpose models degrade more steeply than reasoning-oriented ones and humans as difficulty increases.  
3. **Error-Type Composition Analysis:** Categorize all model failures (on a held-out sample) into WRONG_ANSWER, NO_ANSWER, OUT_OF_LENGTH. Correlate each error type with problem features (proof length, element count, specific geometry relations) to identify triggering conditions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automatic error localization in long-step proofs:** How can we automatically localize errors in long-step geometric proofs to specific intermediate reasoning steps or proof-state transitions? The paper acknowledges they do not conduct fine-grained step-by-step analyses; current outcome-level scores cannot distinguish deduction, parsing, planning, or convergence failures. Resolution would require step-level annotations or symbolic debugging.
2. **Causal factors of weak diagram grounding:** What are the primary causal factors behind weak diagram grounding—is it failure to extract visual features, inability to align textual and visual modalities, or a lack of training on diagram-dependent reasoning? The paper diagnoses weak reliance via ablations but does not causally identify which visual cues or operations are the primary error sources. Resolution would need targeted perturbation studies (e.g., masking diagram elements).
3. **Evaluation protocol sensitivity:** How can evaluation protocols for multi-answer MCQ geometry be designed to be less sensitive to prompting and decoding choices that induce non-convergent reasoning (OUT_OF_LENGTH) or abstention (NO_ANSWER)? The paper notes these error types are sensitive to such choices, confounding capability assessment. Resolution would require systematic evaluation across varied prompting styles and decoding strategies.

## Limitations
- **Dataset reproduction:** Exact difficulty-scoring weights and distractor generation algorithms are undisclosed, hindering faithful recreation of the 90K benchmark’s difficulty distribution and option challenge.
- **Generalizability:** Findings rely on a symbolic generation pipeline; performance gaps on human-created or real-world geometry diagrams may differ.
- **Model scope:** Evaluated LLMs are from mid-2024–early-2025; rapid progress may narrow observed gaps, though core reasoning bottlenecks likely persist.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (No-guess protocol exposes inconsistency):** High
- **Mechanism 2 (Weak visual grounding):** High
- **Mechanism 3 (Non-convergent reasoning):** Medium
