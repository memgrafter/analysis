# AgentHER: Hindsight Experience Replay for LLM Agent Trajectory Relabeling

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.21357
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21357
*   **Reference Count:** 15
*   **Primary Result:** AgentHER converts failed LLM agent trajectories into valid training data, improving success rates by +7.1 to +11.7 percentage points across multiple model scales and achieving 2× data efficiency.

## Executive Summary
LLM agents frequently fail on complex tasks, and these failed trajectories are typically discarded, wasting valuable training data. AgentHER adapts the Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) principle to natural-language agent trajectories. It uses a four-stage pipeline—failure classification, outcome extraction, LLM-guided prompt relabeling with confidence gating, and data packaging—to synthesize alternative achievable goals from failures. This turns discarded failures into high-quality demonstrations for supervised fine-tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), or ShareGPT-style training. Evaluated on WebArena and ToolBench, AgentHER substantially improves over training on successes-only baselines, with gains holding across model scales from 1.5B to 72B parameters and compounding over iterative training rounds. Human evaluation confirms high relabeling precision (97.7%), demonstrating the fidelity of the converted data.

## Method Summary
AgentHER processes failed agent trajectories through four sequential stages. First, a **Failure Detector** classifies the failure type and assigns a severity weight *w*; trajectories with *w* < 0.3 (irrecoverable) are discarded. Second, an **Outcome Extractor** produces a factual list of achievements and observations strictly supported by the trajectory's output history. Third, a **Prompt Relabeler** uses an LLM to synthesize a new user prompt `ĝ` such that the original trajectory becomes a correct demonstration for `ĝ`, guided by constraints on naturalness, factuality, and complexity; a confidence threshold θ=0.5 and a multi-judge verification step ensure quality. Finally, a **Data Augmenter** packages accepted pairs into SFT, DPO (chosen=`(ĝ,τ)`, rejected=`(g_orig,τ)`), or ShareGPT formats, with severity weight *w* scaling the loss contribution. The pipeline uses a hybrid of rule-based components (for zero-cost, deterministic steps) and LLM calls (for nuanced synthesis and verification).

## Key Results
*   AgentHER improves success rates by **+7.1 to +11.7 percentage points** over SFT-on-successes baselines on WebArena and ToolBench across GPT-4o, Qwen2.5 (7B/72B), and LLaMA-3.1-8B.
*   It achieves **2× data efficiency**, matching the performance of SFT-on-successes using only 50% as many successful demonstrations.
*   Gains generalize in **zero-shot cross-benchmark transfer** (WebArena → ToolBench, +9.5 pp) and **compound over iterative rounds** (+2.1 pp additional gain).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Recoverable Failure Identification
A core claim is that only a subset of failures are "recoverable" and can be converted into valid training data; discarding irrecoverable ones prevents label noise. The Failure Detector classifies failure types (e.g., constraint violation, hallucination) and assigns a severity weight *w*∈[0.3,1.0]. Runs with *w* < δ (δ=0.3) are discarded, while others pass with a weight that modulates the training loss gradient. The underlying assumption is that the failure taxonomy correlates with the potential for faithful hindsight relabeling. Evidence includes the paper's stated goal to "reduce label noise" and the ablation showing that removing severity weighting harms performance.

### Mechanism 2: Factual Outcome Extraction
Accurate hindsight goals require a grounded summary of what the trajectory actually achieved, preventing hallucinated prompts. The Outcome Extractor produces a REPLAY_OUTCOME list strictly from non-error observations in the trajectory. It uses rule-based extraction (lexicons) for explicit results and an LLM for implicit achievements. The core assumption is that trajectory observations contain sufficient evidence for at least one alternative, factually correct goal. Evidence anchors are the abstract's mention of preventing "hallucinated hindsight prompts" and Section 3.4's "strict factuality" requirement.

### Mechanism 3: Valid Hindsight Goal Synthesis
An LLM can synthesize a natural-language prompt `ĝ` for which the original trajectory is a correct demonstration, given the extracted factual outcome. The Prompt Relabeler generates `ĝ` under four constraints: (1) natural user request, (2) all assertions satisfied by observations, (3) no reference to the original failed goal, (4) matched complexity. A confidence score *c* ≥ θ (θ=0.5) and multi-judge verification (Stage 3 repeat) ensure quality. The assumption is that capable LLMs (e.g., gpt-4o-mini) can perform this reverse-engineering with high precision. The 97.7% human-verified relabeling precision provides strong empirical evidence for this mechanism.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Hindsight Experience Replay (HER)**
    *   **Why needed:** AgentHER's central insight—that a failed trajectory for goal *A* may succeed for a hindsight goal *B*—is directly adapted from RL HER. Without HER's goal-relabeling principle, the pipeline's purpose is unclear.
    *   **Quick check:** In RL, if an agent fails to reach goal *G* but reaches state *S*, how does HER create a valid training sample?
*   **Concept: Goal-Conditioned Policies**
    *   **Why needed:** The pipeline treats the agent's policy as implicitly goal-conditioned (`π(a|o,g)`). A valid hindsight goal `ĝ` must be one that the oracle policy would have achieved with the observed trajectory (Definition 3.1).
    *   **Quick check:** What two conditions make `ĝ` a "valid hindsight goal" for trajectory `τ`?
*   **Concept: Failure Taxonomy & Severity Analysis**
    *   **Why needed:** Stage 1's discard/weight decisions rely on distinguishing failure types (e.g., INCOMPLETE, CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION) and assigning severity scores. This filters noise and modulates DPO gradients.
    *   **Quick check:** Which failure type yields the largest gains (+11.2 pp) and why? Which yields the least (+2.1 pp) and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Failure Detector -> Outcome Extractor -> Prompt Relabeler -> Data Augmenter

**Critical path:** A trajectory must survive Stage 1 (recoverable & severity >δ) → Stage 2 produces a non-empty factual outcome → Stage 3 generates ≥1 `ĝ` with `c≥θ` passing multi-judge verification → Stage 4 packages it. Failure at any stage discards the trajectory.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   Rule-based vs. LLM in Stages 1–2: Rules are zero-cost but brittle; LLM is more accurate but expensive.
*   Single-judge vs. Multi-judge (Stage 3): Single-judge accepts more (78.0% vs 73.2%) but has higher noise (5.9% vs 2.3%); multi-judge adds cost but boosts downstream gain (+0.8 pp).
*   Severity weighting: Scales DPO gradients by *w* to down-weight borderline relabelings; threshold δ=0.3 tuned via ablation.
*   Confidence threshold θ: Tuned to θ*=0.5; lower θ increases acceptance but noise, higher θ is stricter.

**Failure signatures:**
*   Low acceptance rate (<70%): Overly strict θ or poor outcome extraction.
*   High noise (>5%): Missing multi-judge or severity weighting.
*   No gain over SFT-Random: Unfaithful `(ĝ,τ)` pairs; check Stage 3 constraints.
*   Diminishing iterative returns: Model's failures shift to harder, less-relabelable modes (e.g., tool errors).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** To what extent does task-set leakage from using the same WebArena environments for failure collection and evaluation inflate reported performance gains?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors acknowledge that "training and evaluation draw from the same set of 812 WebArena task environments" and this "may expose fine-tuned models to page-level priors... which may inflate WebArena numbers" (Section 6(i)).
*   **Why unresolved:** While cross-benchmark transfer (+9.5 pp on ToolBench) suggests generalization, a strictly held-out test partition on WebArena is lacking.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-running experiments with a strict train/test split of distinct WebArena tasks (e.g., 600 for training, 212 for held-out evaluation).

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What is the optimal ratio of successful demonstrations to relabeled failures for maximizing agent performance, and does this ratio vary with model scale or task complexity?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors note "the data-volume asymmetry between AgentHER and SFT-Success is controlled by SFT-Random, which still underperforms by ∼9 pp, but a fully matched oracle comparison remains as future work" (Section 6(ii)).
*   **Why unresolved:** The 2× data efficiency finding shows relabeling helps, but the ideal combination given a fixed total data volume is unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation varying the proportion of successes and relabeled failures in the training mix across model scales and benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Can the 38.7% of borderline trajectory-goal pairs rejected at θ=0.5 be effectively utilized via active learning or threshold adjustment to further improve performance?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors state that "active learning over these pairs is a natural extension" (Section 6(iii)), noting that at θ=0.5, a sample showed 38.7% of filtered pairs were rated valid.
*   **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether targeted annotation or learning on this set would yield net gains beyond the current precision-recall trade-off.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** An experiment where borderline pairs (c ∈ [0.4θ, θ]) are annotated/self-corrected and added to the training set, comparing performance against the standard pipeline.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** How does judge imperfection (noise) in the multi-judge verification stage quantitatively affect the downstream performance bound, and can the theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3.1) be tightened under a realistic noisy-oracle model?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors note "the theoretical guarantee assumes a perfect judge; tightening the bound under a noisy-oracle model would close the theory–experiment gap" (Section 6(iv)).
*   **Why unresolved:** The corollary provides a lower bound dependent on precision *p*, but the empirical setup involves an imperfect multi-judge system (97.7% precision). A formal model relating judge error to expected performance degradation is missing.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Deriving a new bound incorporating a stochastic judge model and validating it via controlled introduction of label noise.

## Limitations
*   **Unspecified rule-based components:** Exact lexicons for Stage 1 failure classification and Stage 2 extraction logic are not provided, blocking exact replication.
*   **Ambiguous multi-judge protocol:** Details on whether judges use different models/prompts and the aggregation logic (e.g., majority vote) are vague.
*   **Potential task-set leakage:** The use of the same WebArena task environments for failure collection and evaluation may inflate scores, as acknowledged by the authors.
