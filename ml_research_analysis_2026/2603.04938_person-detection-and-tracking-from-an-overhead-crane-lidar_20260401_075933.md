# Person Detection and Tracking from an Overhead Crane LiDAR
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04938
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: Adapted detectors achieve AP up to 0.84 (5 m radius) and tracking MOTA 0.70, with real‑time CPU inference.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses person detection and tracking in an industrial crane workspace using an overhead‑mounted LiDAR. The domain shift from standard vehicle‑centric datasets motivates the creation of a site‑specific annotated dataset. The authors fine‑tune several 3D LiDAR detectors (e.g., VoxelNeXt, SECOND) via transfer learning and integrate lightweight tracking‑by‑detection algorithms (AB3DMOT, SimpleTrack). The best detector reaches average precision of 0.97 at 1.0 m and 0.84 at 5.0 m radius, while the combined detection‑tracking system yields a MOTA of 0.70. Latency measurements confirm real‑time feasibility on a CPU, and the work releases its dataset and code publicly.

## Method Summary
The authors collect a point‑cloud dataset of workers in a crane environment and manually annotate person instances. They fine‑tune detectors pretrained on large vehicle datasets to adapt them to the overhead perspective and the person class. For tracking, they employ tracking‑by‑detection frameworks that associate 3D detections across frames using motion models and heuristic matching. The pipeline runs in real time on a standard CPU.

## Key Results
- VoxelNeXt fine‑tuned on the overhead dataset achieves AP 0.97 within 1.0 m and 0.84 within 5.0 m radius.
- VoxelNeXt + AB3DMOT obtains MOTA 0.70, demonstrating effective multi‑object tracking.
- Inference latency meets real‑time requirements on the target CPU hardware.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Transfer learning enables leveraging rich spatial features from vehicle pretraining and specializing them for overhead person detection with limited site data. The top‑down mounting provides an unobstructed view, reducing occlusions and simplifying localization. Tracking‑by‑detection separates detection and tracking, allowing robust detection to feed a lightweight motion‑based association (e.g., Kalman filtering) that maintains identities smoothly during typical crane motions. The overall design balances accuracy and computational efficiency for on‑board deployment.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw point cloud -> Detector -> 3D bounding boxes -> Tracker -> Tracklets

Critical path: Data acquisition -> Manual annotation -> Detector fine‑tuning -> Tracker integration -> Real‑time inference.

Design tradeoffs:
- Detector selection: VoxelNeXt offers higher AP but may be slower; SECOND provides faster inference with modest accuracy drop.
- Tracker complexity: AB3DMOT uses global Hungarian matching on IoU, while SimpleTrack employs greedy matching; trade‑off between MOTA and computational overhead.
- Anchor design: Overhead viewpoint changes point distribution density, requiring adjusted anchor scales and rotations compared to vehicle detectors.

Failure signatures:
- Missed detections in sparse regions (e.g., distant persons) lead to track fragmentation.
- ID switches during abrupt motions or temporary occlusions increase IDSW metric.
- CPU inference exceeding frame rate causes backlog and degraded tracking latency.

First experiments:
1. Evaluate fine‑tuned detectors on a held‑out validation split to select the optimal AP‑latency trade‑off.

3. Profile inference time on the target CPU under varying point‑cloud sizes to confirm real‑time operation.

## Limitations
- Single‑site dataset; findings may not generalize to other crane environments or sensor configurations.
- Evaluation uses a radius‑based AP metric, deviating from standard BEV/3D AP and limiting comparability.
- Real‑time CPU claim lacks specific hardware details and stress‑testing under challenging conditions.
