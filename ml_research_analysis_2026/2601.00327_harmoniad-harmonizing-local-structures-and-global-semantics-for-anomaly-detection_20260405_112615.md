# HarmoniAD: Harmonizing Local Structures and Global Semantics for Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.00327
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00327
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** Achieves SOTA on MVTec-AD (+9.3 percentage points P-PR), VisA, and BTAD; shows robust zero‑shot generalization with low inference overhead.

## Executive Summary
Industrial anomaly detection (AD) faces a fundamental trade‑off: models need fine‑grained structural sensitivity to catch subtle defects (e.g., scratches) while preserving global semantic consistency to avoid false positives on normal variations. HarmoniAD introduces a frequency‑guided dual‑branch framework built on frozen CLIP image features. High‑frequency components are routed to a Fine‑grained Structural Attention Module (FSAM) to amplify textures and edges, while low‑frequency components feed a Global Structural Context Module (GSCM) with a Dynamic Modeling Unit to capture long‑range dependencies and enforce coherence. An adaptive Soft Gate computes an input‑conditional cutoff in the frequency domain, enabling flexible separation of structural and semantic information. Trained with multi‑class joint losses, the method sets new records on MVTec‑AD, VisA, and BTAD, and generalizes to unseen domains with minimal latency.

## Method Summary
Given a 224×224 RGB image, a frozen CLIP ViT‑B/16 encoder produces a spatial feature grid. A 2D Fast Fourier Transform decomposes the features into magnitude and phase. An adaptive Soft Gate determines a data‑dependent frequency radius `c` (as the expected value over candidate radii weighted by a Gibbs distribution) and separates high‑ and low‑frequency bands. The high‑frequency branch processes its components through FSAM: Frequency‑to‑Spatial (F2S) attention introduces a learned 4‑D relative position bias to focus on local patterns, and an amplitude modulation mask amplifies texture‑related frequency energy before inverse FFT. The low‑frequency branch employs GSCM, which computes dynamic token affinities with relative bias and applies per‑token linear transformations; a Dynamic Modeling Unit multiplicatively gates this stream with the upper branch’s signal to integrate local evidence into global context. The two branch outputs are fused via learned weights (P​h, P​l) to reconstruct a feature map `X_recon`. A composite loss (cosine reconstruction, contrastive, triplet, and push‑away terms) is computed for normal and abnormal regions, and multi‑class joint training is applied.

## Key Results
- **P‑PR 58.3** on MVTec‑AD, 9.3 percentage points above the previous best.
- **Best scores on BTAD:** P‑ROC 98.9, I‑ROC 94.4, P‑PR 60.9, I‑PR 98.8.
- Consistent SOTA on VisA; ablations validate the contributions of the adaptive Soft Gate, FSAM, GSCM, and F2S attention.
- Demonstrated cross‑domain zero‑shot robustness and inference delay comparable to lightweight baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Adaptive, Input‑Conditional Frequency Splitting
**Claim:** The Soft Gate optimally decouples high‑ and low‑frequency components from CLIP features based on each input’s spectral content.
**Mechanism:** After FFT, the magnitude spectrum is partitioned using a circular mask whose radius `c` is computed as the expectation over a set of candidate radii `r_m`: `c = Σ r_m · p_m`, with `p_m = Softmax(κ·J(r_m))`. This differentiable, data‑adaptive threshold avoids a fixed split.
**Why it helps:** Structural details (edges, textures) and semantic context reside in different frequency bands; an input‑specific cutoff ensures each sample’s features are routed to the appropriate branch.
**Evidence:** Table II shows that replacing the adaptive gate with a manually tuned fixed threshold degrades performance on BTAD, confirming the benefit of adaptivity.

### Fine‑Grained Structural Attention (FSAM)
**Claim:** FSAM enhances sensitivity to fine textures by injecting frequency‑derived priors into spatial attention.
**Mechanism:** (1) F2S Attention adds a 4‑D relative position bias `β_e(Δp_ij)` to the attention logits, biasing toward local neighborhoods. (2) Amplitude modulation applies a learned mask `m` to the Fourier amplitude `A` (via `bA = m ⊙ σ(A)`) before inverse FFT, non‑linearly boosting energy in bands associated with high‑frequency details.
**Why it helps:** Subtle anomalies like scratches or discolorations primarily alter high‑frequency content; FSAM amplifies these cues while preserving spatial coherence.
**Evidence:** Ablation in Table III (FSAM‑only) improves pixel‑level ROC but hurts image‑level ROC, indicating FSAM’s specialization for local detection. Visual examples (Appendix) show sharper heatmaps for small defects when FSAM is active.

### Global Structural Context (GSCM with DMU)
**Claim:** GSCM captures long‑range dependencies and enforces global semantic consistency, suppressing spurious activations and integrating local evidence coherently.
**Mechanism:** Low‑frequency tokens are processed with dynamic affinity `S` (including relative bias), per‑token dynamic transforms (`Π_t = diag(ξ(W^π x_t))`, `Γ_t = W^γ x_t`), and a Dynamic Modeling Unit that multiplicatively gates the upper branch: `x_t = W^o(u_t ⊙ SiLU(v_t + m_t))`.
**Why it helps:** Normal objects exhibit globally coherent semantics; deviations (e.g., missing components) become clearer when evaluated in the context of the whole. DMU allows the semantic stream to modulate the structural stream, reducing false positives from irrelevant local textures.
**Evidence:** Removing GSCM (Table III, GSCM‑only) lowers image‑level metrics, confirming its role in global anomaly detection. Heatmaps without GSCM tend to be fragmented and less focused.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Frequency Domain Representation (FFT/iFFT)**
- **Why needed here:** The entire framework splits CLIP features by frequency. FFT decomposes a spatial map into amplitude (magnitude of frequency components) and phase; high amplitudes in high‑frequency bands represent edges/textures, low amplitudes in low‑frequency bands represent global shape.
- **Quick check:** Zeroing out high‑frequency coefficients before iFFT removes fine details and sharp edges, leaving a blurred, low‑frequency version of the original.

**Concept: Attention Mechanisms & Relative Positional Bias**
- **Why needed here:** Both FSAM and GSCM are attention‑based. Understanding scaled dot‑product attention and the addition of a learned bias matrix `B` (based on coordinate differences) is key to their structure‑aware designs.
- **Quick check:** In `Attn = Softmax(QK^T/√d + B)`, `B` injects prior knowledge about spatial relationships (e.g., “nearby pixels should attend more”), biasing the attention pattern.

**Concept: Pretrained Vision‑Language Models (CLIP) as Frozen Feature Extractors**
- **Why needed here:** CLIP’s ViT backbone provides rich, semantically aligned features without task‑specific training; HarmoniAD innovates by post‑processing these frozen features.
- **Quick check:** Freezing CLIP avoids overfitting on limited anomaly data, preserves generalizable knowledge, and isolates the benefits of the proposed modules.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Input (224×224 RGB) → CLIP ViT‑B/16 (frozen) → feature grid → 2D FFT → magnitude A & phase P → Soft Gate (adaptive radius c) → split:  
 High‑freq (A_h, P_h) → FSAM (F2S Attn + amplitude modulation) → iFFT → F_high  
 Low‑freq (A_l, P_l) → GSCM + DMU → F_low  
Fusion: X_recon = P_h·F_high + P_l·F_low (learned weights) → Loss (6 terms: cosine normal/abnormal, contrastive, cosine abnormal, push‑away, triplet, L2) → Anomaly score (patch‑level cosine distance).

**Critical path:** Image → CLIP → FFT → Soft Gate split → (FSAM | GSCM+DMU) → iFFT → Fusion → Loss → Scoring.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Frozen vs. Fine‑tuned CLIP:* Frozen reduces cost and preserves general semantics; fine‑tuning may raise ceiling but risks overfitting.
- *Dual‑branch complexity:* Adds parameters/FLOPs vs. single path but improves robustness to structure/semantics trade‑off.
- *Soft Gate vs. Fixed Threshold:* Adaptive gate handles diverse frequency distributions but requires learning `κ`; fixed threshold is simpler but less flexible.
- *FSAM complexity:* F2S Attn (4D bias) adds overhead for local structural boost.

**Failure signatures:**
- *High false positives:* Mis‑splitting (too much high‑freq to low branch), FSAM over‑amplifying normal textures, GSCM failing to suppress noise.
- *High false negatives:* Too conservative splitting, FSAM not sensitive enough, GSCM over‑suppressing valid local anomalies.
- *Blurry/broad heatmaps:* GSCM dominates, merging nearby defects.
- *Hollow/ring‑like responses:* FSAM over‑emphasizes edges without global context.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline sanity check:** Implement HarmoniAD without the dual‑branch split (use full CLIP features or a single branch). Expect large drop in P‑PR.
2. **Ablation of Soft Gate:** Replace adaptive Soft Gate with a fixed manual threshold (e.g., `t=0.5`). Expect consistent degradation across metrics (as in Table II).
3. **Ablation of branches:** Train with `FSAM‑only`, `GSCM‑only`, and `both`. Expect `FSAM‑only` to boost P‑ROC but hurt I‑ROC; `GSCM‑only` to preserve I‑ROC but hurt P‑PR; `both` to achieve best overall.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extension to video:** How to incorporate temporal modeling for video anomaly detection? (Conclusion & Appendix C note it’s future work.)
- **Quantitative cross‑domain zero‑shot generalization:** Systematic evaluation on out‑of‑domain datasets (medical, satellite, retail) is missing; only qualitative examples are provided.
- **Impact of CLIP fine‑tuning:** Does adaptively updating the backbone improve performance, and does it alter the frequency decomposition’s behavior? (Authors froze CLIP to reduce cost and avoid overfitting.)
- **Branch contribution analysis:** How do FSAM and GSCM contribute to anomalies of different sizes, textures, or semantic types? (Ablations show complementarity but lack per‑category or per‑anomaly‑characteristic quantification.)

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Critical details (Soft Gate’s `J(r_m)`, FSAM’s mask network, GSCM’s reduction ratio `r`, exact loss formulations) are not fully specified, blocking faithful reproduction.
- **Weak external grounding:** The three core mechanisms lack prior‑art corroboration; corpus analysis shows low relatedness and no direct citations.
- **Domain dependence:** Reliance on frozen CLIP features may limit performance on domains where CLIP’s semantic knowledge is weak (e.g., non‑iconic textures), and zero‑shot claims need more rigorous validation.
