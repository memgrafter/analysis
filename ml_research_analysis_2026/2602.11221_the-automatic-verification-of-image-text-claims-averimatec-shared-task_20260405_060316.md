# The Automatic Verification of Image-Text Claims (AVerImaTeC) Shared Task

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11221
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11221
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: Six teams submitted systems outperforming the baseline (0.1136); the winner, HUMANE, achieved an AVerImaTeC score of 0.5455.

## Executive Summary
The AVerImaTeC shared task tackles automatic verification of real-world claims that combine text and images. Participants must retrieve evidence from a provided multimodal knowledge store or via web search and predict verdicts (Supported, Refuted, Not Enough Evidence, Claim-Centric) with justifications. Systems typically used multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for question generation, evidence retrieval (sparse/dense and multimodal), and verdict prediction. Performance is measured by the AVerImaTeC score, which only counts a correct verdict if the accompanying evidence score exceeds a 0.3 threshold. All six submissions beat the baseline; the top system (HUMANE) improved evidence coverage through advanced scraping and multimodal retrieval. The task highlights the benefits of dense retrieval and iterative reasoning, while also revealing low alignment between automatic evidence evaluation and human judgments (Spearman ~0.3), underscoring challenges in assessing multimodal support.

## Method Summary
The dataset comprises 1,297 claims (train/dev/test) each containing one or more images, textual claim, and metadata (date, source). A knowledge store accompanies the dataset, containing pre-scraped textual evidence (from claim source URLs) and image evidence (base64-encoded). Participants may additionally perform web searches. The baseline system (Gemini-2.0-Flash) follows a four-stage pipeline: (1) generate 5 information-seeking questions per claim via few-shot prompting from similar training claims (retrieved by BM25); (2) classify questions as text- or image-focused—text questions retrieve top-30 textual evidence (BM25), image questions either apply VQA to the claim image or retrieve top-30 text and top-2 images (CLIP) and answer; (3) aggregate all QA pairs and prompt the MLLM to predict a verdict; (4) generate a natural language justification. Submissions may retrieve up to 10 evidence pieces. The primary metric is the AVerImaTeC score, computed as verdict accuracy conditioned on an automatically assessed evidence score > 0.3. Evidence score combines textual match (LLM judge) and visual similarity (VQA rating ≥ 8 on a 0–10 scale).

## Key Results
- All six team submissions exceeded the baseline AVerImaTeC score of 0.1136.
- The winning system (HUMANE) achieved 0.5455 by augmenting the knowledge store with Playwright-based scraping (24.4% more textual evidence) and employing a multimodal retriever (ColPali).
- Top-performing systems relied on closed-source MLLMs (Gemini, GPT); only one team explored fine-tuning open-weight models.
- Dense retrieval and iterative question-answer generation correlated with higher scores, but automatic evidence evaluation exhibited low human alignment (Spearman ~0.3), indicating difficulties in judging multimodal relevance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Enhanced scraping and multimodal retrieval improve evidence quality
- **Claim**: Enriching the knowledge store with robust scraping (e.g., Playwright) and using multimodal retrievers (e.g., ColPali) yields higher evidence scores, thereby boosting overall AVerImaTeC performance.
- **Mechanism**: Advanced scraping circumvents website restrictions and loads dynamic content, expanding textual evidence coverage. Multimodal retrieval aligns text and image representations, retrieving cross-modal evidence more effectively.
- **Core assumption**: Higher-quality, comprehensive evidence directly enables more accurate verdict prediction by providing stronger multimodal support.
- **Evidence**: HUMANE increased textual evidence by 24.4% via Playwright and used ColPali for retrieval, leading to the top score (0.5455) [section 3].
- **Break condition**: If scraping encounters irreversible blocks or multimodal retrievers fail to capture cross-modal semantics, evidence quality degrades despite enhancements.

### Mechanism 2: Iterative question-answer generation captures reasoning dependencies
- **Claim**: Iteratively generating QA pairs, with later questions conditioned on prior evidence, improves verification on complex claims requiring multi-step reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: By maintaining a QA history, systems model sequential information needs and avoid overlooking dependencies between sub-questions.
- **Core assumption**: Many image-text claims involve interdependent sub-questions; iteration explicitly tracks these dependencies.
- **Evidence**: HUMANE and REVEAL employed iterative QA generation; the paper notes this design but did not isolate its impact [section 3, 1].
- **Break condition**: Error propagation in early QA steps or claims with independent sub-questions can render iteration ineffective or harmful.

### Mechanism 3: Closed-source MLLMs provide superior multimodal reasoning
- **Claim**: Top systems rely on closed-source MLLMs (e.g., Gemini) for all stages, leveraging their scale and training for robust multimodal understanding.
- **Mechanism**: Proprietary models inherently handle complex image-text integration and reasoning better than open-weight alternatives available at the time.
- **Core assumption**: Closed-source models have architectural and data advantages that translate to higher accuracy in this task.
- **Evidence**: Table 4 shows HUMANE, ADA-AGGR, and AIC CTU used Gemini or GPT; only ADA-AGGR explored fine-tuning open models. The paper explicitly observes top teams' reliance on closed-source models [section 5].
- **Break condition**: If open-weight models with task-specific fine-tuning and high-quality data close the gap, dependency on closed APIs may diminish.

## Foundational Learning
### Multimodal Evidence Integration
- **Why needed**: The task requires verifying claims using both text and images; systems must process and reason over heterogeneous evidence formats.
- **Quick check**: How are image components encoded in evidence statements (e.g., base64 or tokens), and how does the verifier combine them with text?

### Dense Retrieval for Semantic Matching
- **Why needed**: Sparse retrieval (e.g., BM25) misses paraphrased or contextually similar evidence; dense embeddings capture semantic relevance crucial for multimodal claims.
- **Quick check**: What embedding models (e.g., mxbai-embed-largev1) were used for dense retrieval, and how do they differ from sparse methods?

### Conditional Accuracy Metric (AVerImaTeC Score)
- **Why needed**: Ensures verdicts are only credited if backed by sufficient evidence (score >0.3), preventing lucky guesses and promoting reliable evidence-based verification.
- **Quick check**: Why is the evidence score threshold empirically set at 0.3, and how does it affect system design priorities?

### Iterative Reasoning vs. One-Shot Processing
- **Why needed**: Claims may have dependent sub-questions; iteration allows evidence from earlier steps to inform later queries, but adds complexity.
- **Quick check**: What performance difference did teams like REVEAL observe between iterative and non-iterative QA generation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input (claim with text, image, metadata) → Question Generator (MLLM) → Retriever (sparse/dense + multimodal) → Answer Generator (VQA/text-based) → Verifier (MLLM for verdict) → Justification Generator (MLLM for explanation) → Output (verdict, justification, evidence). Some systems combine question generation and answering.

**Critical path**: Claim → generate questions → retrieve evidence (text/images) → answer questions → aggregate evidence → predict verdict → generate justification.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Knowledge store vs. web search: Store lowers cost/reproducibility but may lack freshness; web search offers coverage but introduces API costs and noise.
- Closed vs. open models: Closed models yield higher accuracy but are opaque/expensive; open models allow fine-tuning but require more data/effort.
- Evidence quantity vs. quality: Up to 10 evidence pieces allowed; submitting more can hurt if low-relevance, but too few may miss key support.

**Failure signatures**:
- AVerImaTeC score near zero due to evidence score <0.3.
- Poor performance on rare verdicts (NEE, CE-C) or claim types (causal, numerical).
- Low human alignment in evidence evaluation (Spearman ~0.3), indicating automatic评估 misjudges multimodal relevance.
- Performance drop on post-knowledge-cutoff claims if models over-rely on parametric knowledge.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline replication: Run the provided baseline on dev set to establish component-wise benchmarks (e.g., evidence recall ~0.17).
2. Retrieval ablation: Compare BM25-only vs. hybrid sparse-dense retrieval (e.g., mxbai-embed-largev1) on evidence recall and AVerImaTeC score.
3. Iteration test: Implement iterative QA (conditioned on history) vs. one-shot question generation, measuring impact on claims with multiple dependencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can automatic evidence evaluation for multimodal image-text claim verification be improved to achieve higher alignment with human judgments? The current Ev2R evaluation treats multimodal evidence as separable text and image components, leading to poor alignment (Spearman ~0.31) especially when system output modality differs from reference. The root causes (e.g., how to weight modalities, handle missing images) are not fully diagnosed. Resolving this requires detailed error analysis across modality mismatches and human subject studies to establish ground-truth criteria for partial correctness and alternative representations.

### Open Question 2
Can high-performing, cost-effective fact-checking systems be built using open-weight models instead of closed-source models like Gemini? While top systems used closed APIs, only one team explored fine-tuning open-weight models. No controlled comparison isolates model architecture from data scale or fine-tuning strategies, leaving the performance ceiling for open models unclear. A systematic ablation fine-tuning open-weight MLLMs (e.g., Llama, Gemma) on the full training set and comparing to identical pipelines with closed models would address this.

### Open Question 3
Is the generation of information-seeking questions necessary and beneficial for evidence retrieval? Some teams generated questions but did not use them for retrieval, and the baseline used questions while another team used claim-only queries. No controlled experiment varied query generation while keeping the retriever constant, so the impact of incorporating generated questions into retrieval remains unclear. An ablation study comparing claim-only, claim+gold-questions, and claim+model-generated queries on retrieval metrics and final accuracy would resolve this.

### Open Question 4
Does iterative, multi-step question generation and answering provide a significant benefit over one-shot parallel generation? Two teams used iterative QA, but no controlled experiment compared iterative versus one-shot generation. The performance gains cannot be isolated to iteration alone. A controlled experiment with fixed base model and retriever, comparing iterative (history-conditioned) vs. parallel generation, especially on multi-hop claims, is needed.

### Open Question 5
What is the optimal way to represent and utilize the image component of a claim during retrieval and verdict prediction? The task assumes evidence must explicitly represent images using [IMG] tokens, but human evaluation found that text-only evidence sometimes suffices. The system design space (retrieve images, describe them textually, or ignore if textual context resolves) is not explored. A redesigned evaluation where humans determine per claim whether explicit visual evidence is necessary would guide optimal representation.

## Limitations
- Automatic evidence evaluation shows low human alignment (Spearman ~0.3), questioning metric validity for multimodal support.
- Baseline implementation details (prompts, heuristics, parameters) are underspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- Knowledge store incompleteness and reliance on closed-source models introduce biases and reproducibility challenges.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Enhanced scraping & multimodal retrieval)**: High – direct evidence from winning team (24.4% evidence gain) and clear quantitative improvement.
- **Mechanism 2 (Iterative QA reasoning)**: Medium – only two teams employed iteration; no controlled ablation isolating its effect was reported.
- **Mechanism 3 (Closed-source MLLMs superiority)**: Medium – top systems used closed models, but no apples-to-apples comparison with equally resourced open models exists.

## Next Checks
1. **Scraping impact ablation**: Re-run the HUMANE pipeline with and without Playwright scraping on the same claim set; compare evidence recall and AVerImaTeC scores.
2. **Iterative reasoning test**: Fix retriever and verifier; implement both iterative (history‑conditioned) and one‑shot QA generation; evaluate performance on claims with varying numbers of dependent sub‑questions.
3. **Open vs. closed fairness**: Fine‑tune an open-weight MLLM (e.g., Qwen‑VL) on the full training data and evaluate against Gemini using identical evidence and prompts; report the AVerImaTeC score gap.
