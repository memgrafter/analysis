# AG-VAS: Anchor-Guided Zero-Shot Visual Anomaly Segmentation with Large Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01305
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01305
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves SOTA zero-shot anomaly segmentation on six benchmarks, notably 51.0 AP on MVTec-AD with LLaVA-OneVision-7B.

## Executive Summary
Zero-shot visual anomaly segmentation (ZSAS) faces two key challenges: the abstract, context-dependent nature of anomalies makes defining stable prototypes difficult, and large multimodal models (LMMs) exhibit weak alignment between high-level semantics and pixel-level features. AG-VAS addresses these by introducing three learnable semantic anchor tokens—[SEG], [NOR], and [ANO]—that bridge abstract defect concepts to spatial grounding. A Semantic-Pixel Alignment Module (SPAM) explicitly aligns LMM semantic embeddings with fine-grained pixel features, and an Anchor-Guided Mask Decoder (AGMD) produces anomaly masks conditioned on these anchors. Trained on a new 20K instruction dataset (Anomaly-Instruct20K), AG-VAS sets new zero-shot records on industrial and medical benchmarks while demonstrating strong normal-sample rejection.

## Method Summary
AG-VAS builds on LLaVA-OneVision-7B, freezing its SigLIP semantic encoder and a SAM-ViT-H pixel encoder. Three special tokens ([SEG], [NOR], [ANO]) are added to the LLM's vocabulary. During training, an image and a segmentation instruction are processed: semantic tokens (from SigLIP) and pixel features (from SAM) are projected to a shared dimension. SPAM performs multi-head cross-attention (queries from semantic tokens, keys/values from pixel tokens) to produce aligned semantic features, which are fused with pixel features and fed to the LLM. The LLM (tuned with LoRA) is trained to generate responses containing the three anchor tokens. Their final-layer embeddings are extracted, refined via a linear Token Refiner, and fed to AGMD—a bidirectional cross-attention decoder that updates anchor-conditioned queries using pixel features. AGMD outputs three masks (P_seg, P_nor, P_ano) fused as P = 0.5·P_seg + 0.5·P_ano and thresholded at 0.5. Training uses a mixed dataset (general segmentation, anomaly segmentation, anomaly instructions, VQA) with weighted sampling and a combined language + segmentation loss (BCE + Dice). At inference, the model generates until the anchor tokens appear, then runs AGMD to produce the final mask.

## Key Results
- **SOTA zero-shot performance:** On MVTec-AD with LLaVA-OneVision-7B: AP 51.0, F1-Max 52.7, IoUano 44.8.
- **Strong normal rejection:** IoU_nor 87.7%, significantly outperforming CLIP-based and other LMM methods.
- **Robust across domains:** Consistently top performance on six industrial/medical benchmarks (MVTec-AD, KSDD2, RSDD, ISIC, ColonDB, ClinicDB).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Learnable Semantic Anchor Tokens
- **Claim:** The three special tokens ([SEG], [NOR], [ANO]) bridge abstract anomaly semantics and spatially grounded segmentation.
- **Mechanism:** [SEG] acts as an absolute anchor encoding world knowledge about defects (e.g., "holes", "scratches"), while [NOR] and [ANO] are relative anchors capturing normal-abnormal contextual contrast. These tokens are inserted into the LLM’s output sequence during training; their final-layer embeddings are extracted to condition the mask decoder.
- **Core Assumption:** Anomaly localization requires both explicit defect concept grounding ([SEG]) and relational context modeling ([NOR]/[ANO]) to handle abstract, context-dependent anomalies.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Paper Sec 3.1 defines tokens' roles; Table 3 ablation shows dropping [SEG] or [NOR][ANO] harms performance (IoU_ano and IoU_nor drop).
- **Break Condition:** If the LLM fails to internalize anchor semantics during tuning (e.g., anchors not consistently placed in responses), conditioning fails. Evidence: Ablation in Table 3 confirms necessity.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic-Pixel Alignment Module (SPAM)
- **Claim:** SPAM aligns high-level LMM embeddings with fine-grained pixel features to enable anchor-guided localization.
- **Mechanism:** SPAM uses multi-head cross-attention where LMM semantic features (from semantic encoder) act as queries attending to pixel encoder features (keys/values). The aligned features are fused with pixel features before LLM input.
- **Core Assumption:** The LMM's text-aligned visual tokens and the segmenter's pixel features exist in misaligned spaces; explicit alignment is necessary for anchors to meaningfully guide mask decoding.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Sec 3.1 describes SPAM as aligning language-level semantics with high-resolution features; equation (4) defines falign = MHCA(Q=fs, K=fp, V=fp).
- **Break Condition:** If pixel encoders are overly fine-tuned, pre-aligned features degrade, causing SPAM to fail. Evidence: Table 6 shows frozen SAM outperforms SAM-FT.

### Mechanism 3: Anchor-Guided Mask Decoder (AGMD)
- **Claim:** AGMD fuses absolute and relative anchor embeddings via bidirectional cross-attention to produce accurate, rejectable binary masks.
- **Mechanism:** AGMD takes refined anchor embeddings (h_seg, h_nor, h_ano) concatenated with learnable queries, performs bidirectional cross-attention with pixel features, and outputs three masks (P_seg, P_nor, P_ano). Final anomaly map P = α·P_seg + (1‑α)·P_ano (α=0.5).
- **Core Assumption:** Separately modeling absolute anomaly presence ([SEG]) and relative normal-abnormal contrast ([NOR]/[ANO]) yields more precise masks and enables normal rejection than a single binary output.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Sec 3.1 details AGMD; equations (6)-(8) define outputs and fusion. High IoU_nor (87.7%) demonstrates effective normal rejection, credited to relative anchors.
- **Break Condition:** For images with multiple disparate anomaly types, AGMD may under-segment (see Appendix failure cases). Single-fusion design may not handle diverse anomaly mixtures well.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) as Foundation**
  - *Why needed:* AG-VAS builds on LLaVA-OneVision for high-level semantic reasoning and instruction following. Understanding LMM tokenization, autoregressive decoding, and LoRA fine-tuning is essential to grasp how anchor tokens are integrated and trained.
  - *Quick check:* How does adding special tokens to an LMM’s vocabulary affect its output distribution during autoregressive generation?

- **Concept: Zero-Shot Anomaly Segmentation (ZSAS) Challenges**
  - *Why needed:* The paper targets ZSAS—localizing anomalies in unseen categories without retraining. Key challenges: abstract anomaly definitions (no stable prototypes) and weak vision-language-pixel alignment. Appreciating these explains the anchor design.
  - *Quick check:* Why do CLIP-based ZSAS methods struggle with pixel-level precision compared to LMM-based approaches?

- **Concept: Segmentation-as-Mask-Decoding Paradigm**
  - *Why needed:* AG-VAS generates masks by decoding from LLM token embeddings (like LISA). Understanding the "embedding-as-mask" trick—using token embeddings as queries to a mask decoder—is central to the architecture.
  - *Quick check:* In the embedding-as-mask paradigm, what is the role of the learnable query tokens (t_nor, t_ano, t_seg) in AGMD?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (image + text) → Semantic Encoder (SigLIP, frozen) → f_s; Pixel Encoder (SAM, frozen) → f_p; Linear projectors → f_s', f_p'; SPAM (MHCA: Q=f_s', K/V=f_p') → f_align; LLM (LoRA-tuned) processes [f_s', f_align, f_txt] → output with [NOR][ANO][SEG]; Token Refiner → h_nor, h_ano, h_seg; AGMD (bidirectional cross-attention with f_p'') → P_nor, P_ano, P_seg; Fusion (P=0.5·P_seg+0.5·P_ano) → thresholded binary mask.
- **Critical path:** Pixel features (F_p) → AGMD ← Anchor embeddings (from LLM via SPAM). The SPAM-LLM-AGMD chain is the novel alignment path; F_s provides context but is not directly used in mask decoding.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Anchor expressivity vs. inference cost: Adding special tokens and AGMD increases parameters and latency modestly (Table 7) but gains ~10 AP over LISA.
  - Dataset diversity vs. training stability: Joint training on 4 datasets with sampling ratios 0.4/0.25/0.25/0.1 balances general segmentation, anomaly knowledge, and efficiency but requires careful tuning.
  - Frozen vs. fine-tuned pixel encoder: Table 6 shows frozen SAM works best; fine-tuning (SAM-FT) harms generalization, likely due to catastrophic forgetting of pre-aligned features.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Over-conservative masks: Predicted masks smaller than GT on ill-defined boundaries (Appendix Fig. 8).
  - Multi-anomaly misses: Struggles when multiple anomaly types coexist (e.g., hole + crack on hazelnut).
  - Normal rejection failure: Without [NOR]/[ANO], IoU_nor drops significantly (Table 3), leading to false positives.
  - Textual noise: Describe-then-Segment modes slightly reduce AP vs. Direct Segmentation (Table 4) due to intermediate generation errors.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate anchor necessity: Ablate [SEG] and [NOR][ANO] separately (Table 3 row 1-2). Expect significant drop in IoU_ano and IoU_nor.
  2. Test SPAM impact: Remove SPAM (use only f_s or f_p in LLM input). Expect degraded alignment, measured by lower AP/F1.
  3. Swap pixel encoders: Replace SAM with CLIP, DINOv2, DINOv3 (Table 6). Expect performance scales with encoder quality; frozen SAM remains strong for its mask design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can AG-VAS be extended to segment multiple anomalous regions in a single image, particularly when they differ in type?  
  *Basis:* "In future work, we will further improve the model’s ability to simultaneously segment multiple anomalous regions." (Section E, Limitations)  
  *Why unresolved:* Current predictions are conservative and often miss secondary anomalies, as shown in failure cases.
- **Open Question 2:** How can inference speed be improved for LMM-based zero-shot anomaly segmentation to enable real-time industrial use?  
  *Basis:* "LMM-based methods inherently suffer from slower inference speed, which constrains their applicability in real-time industrial environments." (Conclusion, Limitations)  
  *Why unresolved:* Current architecture involves large LMs and multiple modules, leading to high computational cost.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the explicit reasoning modes (describe-then-segment, segment-then-explain) be modified to enhance segmentation accuracy rather than act as a distraction?  
  *Basis:* "The extra textual generation tends to divert the model’s focus from the segmentation objective" and "they do not yield additional performance gains without more expert priors." (Conclusion, Limitations)  
  *Why unresolved:* These modes produce text that does not contribute to mask quality; the model mainly uses implicit reasoning.
- **Open Question 4:** Is a fixed fusion weight (α=0.5) between absolute and relative masks optimal, or can an adaptive scheme improve performance across anomaly types?  
  *Basis:* Fusion weight α is fixed at 0.5 without analysis (Eq. 8).  
  *Why unresolved:* Different anomalies may rely more on absolute appearance cues or relative contrast; a single weight may not generalize.

## Limitations
- **Dataset unavailability:** Anomaly-Instruct20K is not released, preventing exact replication of training conditions.
- **Multi-anomaly handling:** Struggles when multiple disparate anomalies coexist in one image (Appendix).
- **Inference cost:** LMM autoregressive generation and extra modules introduce latency unoptimized for real-time use.

## Confidence
- **Anchor tokens bridge semantics to pixels:** Medium. Ablations show benefit, but token internalization mechanism remains unverified.
- **SPAM aligns LMM and pixel spaces:** Medium. Alignment is assumed; no quantitative feature similarity metric provided.
- **AGMD produces accurate masks and normal rejection:** Medium. High IoU_nor supports it, but failure cases indicate limits in complex scenes.
- **SOTA zero-shot performance:** High. Consistent gains across six benchmarks and strong baselines support claim.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate ablations:** Train AG-VAS without SPAM and without anchor tokens (separately) to confirm performance drops reported in Table 3.
2. **Multi-anomaly evaluation:** Build a test set with multiple defect types per image (e.g., from MVTec-AD) and measure segmentation completeness (e.g., number of predicted components vs. GT).
3. **Alignment quantification:** Compute cosine similarity between anchor token embeddings (from LLM last layer) and pixel features within GT anomaly regions; compare with and without SPAM.
