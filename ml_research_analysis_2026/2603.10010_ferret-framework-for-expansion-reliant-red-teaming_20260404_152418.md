# FERRET: Framework for Expansion Reliant Red Teaming
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.10010
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10010
*   **Reference Count:** 4
*   **Primary Result:** FERRET achieves higher Attack Success Rate (ASR) and competitive diversity against state-of-the-art automated red teaming baselines on major vision-language models.

## Executive Summary
FERRET addresses key limitations in existing automated red teaming for large vision-language models. Prior single-turn methods fail to exploit multi-turn vulnerabilities, multi-turn methods rely on predefined goals, and most lack integrated multimodal (text-image) attack capabilities. FERRET introduces a framework with three synergistic expansions: **horizontal** (self-improving generation of initial conversation starters from policy logs), **vertical** (expanding starters into multi-turn, multi-modal conversations), and **meta** (discovering novel attack strategies mid-conversation). A transformation toolkit enables fused text-image attacks via structured XML formatting. Evaluations on Llama Maverick, Claude Haiku, and GPT-4o show FERRET surpasses FLIRT and GOAT baselines in ASR while maintaining high attack diversity.

## Method Summary
FERRET is an inference-only framework that orchestrates an attacker LLM to generate adversarial multimodal conversations. For a given policy, **Horizontal Expansion** uses a log of prior starters labeled as successful/unsuccessful (by a judge) to steer the attacker toward generating more effective initial prompts via in-context learning. These starters include XML tags specifying potential image components. **Vertical Expansion** then iteratively expands a starter into a full multi-turn conversation: at each turn, the attacker selects an attack strategy (or invents a new one) and generates the next user prompt with XML tags. A **Transformation Toolkit** parses these tags to produce actual images (e.g., via text-to-image generation or OCR rendering), which are combined with text to form the multimodal prompt sent to the target model. The target's response is judged, and the loop continues for a fixed number of turns. **Meta Expansion** occurs within vertical turns, encouraging the attacker to define and immediately use novel attack strategies not present in the seed taxonomy, which are then added to the strategy pool for future use. The entire process is logged to feed back into horizontal expansion.

## Key Results
*   FERRET significantly outperforms FLIRT and GOAT in Attack Success Rate (ASR) across target models (e.g., 21.7% vs. 12.8% FLIRT and 18.1% GOAT on Llama Maverick).
*   It achieves higher diversity of attacks than GOAT while remaining competitive with FLIRT.
*   Ablations show that sampling only effective (successful) examples in horizontal expansion yields the highest ASR (33.0% on Llama Maverick).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Horizontal Expansion (Self-Improving Starter Generation)
*   **Claim:** The horizontal expansion feedback loop generates more effective initial conversation starters by learning from historical attack outcomes.
*   **Mechanism:** The attacker model is prompted with the policy description, attack strategy examples, few-shot samples, and a "horizontal memory" log containing prior starters labeled as successful or unsuccessful by a judge. Using a sampling strategy (e.g., positive-only), it performs in-context learning to generate a new, optimized starter formatted with XML tags for image components.
*   **Core Assumption:** A binary success/failure signal from a reliable judge can be used by the attacker model to discern patterns in effective vs. ineffective starters, leading to improved generation.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] describes shaping conversations via self-improvement; [section 2.2] details log-based sampling and XML formatting. The corpus lacks direct evidence for this specific feedback mechanism, making it a novel contribution.
*   **Break Condition:** If the horizontal memory is poisoned (e.g., by a biased judge) or dominated by failures, or if an ineffective sampling strategy (e.g., negative-only) is used, performance degrades, as seen in the ablation showing the lowest ASR for "non-effective samples only."

### Mechanism 2: Vertical Expansion (Multi-Turn, Multi-Modal Conversation Building)
*   **Claim:** Vertical expansion creates more successful attacks by developing a starter into a coordinated, multi-turn sequence that integrates text and image attacks over time.
*   **Mechanism:** Starting with the horizontal output, the attacker model, given the full conversation history and strategy examples, selects a strategy (or invents one) and generates the next prompt with XML. The transformation toolkit renders XML-specified images. This prompt is sent to the target, judged, and the turn is logged; the loop repeats.
*   **Core Assumption:** Model vulnerabilities can be more reliably exploited through a temporally extended, coherent sequence of multimodal attacks than via a single optimal prompt.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] states the goal of expanding starters into effective multi-modal conversations; [section 2.3] describes the iterative turn-generation, strategy selection, toolkit use, and logging. The corpus contains multi-turn red teaming work, but the specific fusion of text-image within each turn via a toolkit is distinctive to FERRET.
*   **Break Condition:** Toolkit failures (irrelevant or unreadable images) or model incoherence over turns can cause the conversation to terminate early without violation.

### Mechanism 3: Meta Expansion (In-Conversation Strategy Discovery)
*   **Claim:** Meta expansion may improve efficacy by having the attacker invent novel, out-of-taxonomy attack strategies during a conversation, potentially bypassing defenses tuned to known patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** During vertical turns, the attacker model is instructed to create a new attack strategy, defining its required XML formatting. This strategy is immediately used to construct the next prompt and is added to the pool for possible future use.
*   **Core Assumption:** An LLM-based attacker can on-the-fly generate *effective* novel jailbreak strategies that are qualitatively new and correctly formatted for the toolkit.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] mentions discovering strategies during a conversation; [section 2.4] explicitly encourages the model to come up with its own new strategy and formatting. The corpus includes work on generating new attacks, but on-the-fly invention within a conversation is a high-assumption, novel claim.
*   **Break Condition:** Novelty may compromise effectiveness (incoherence, easy filtering) or yield an XML format incompatible with the toolkit. The paper does not quantify the isolated contribution of truly *novel* strategies.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: In-Context Learning with Binary Feedback**
    *   **Why needed here:** Horizontal expansion relies on the attacker model interpreting a contrastive log (success/failure labels) to generate better starters.
    *   **Quick check:** How would you prompt the attacker model for "effective samples only" vs. "non-effective samples only" to maximize the learning signal from the memory log?
*   **Concept: Multimodal Transformation via Structured Prompting (XML)**
    *   **Why needed here:** The framework requires a deterministic bridge from a textual strategy description (in XML) to an actual image-based attack component.
    *   **Quick check:** If the XML tag `<text_in_image>` specifies "a STOP sign with the text 'GO' written on it," what are potential failure modes in the transformation toolkit (e.g., image generation, OCR) that could break this attack?
*   **Concept: Adversarial Conversation Trajectory Optimization**
    *   **Why needed here:** Vertical expansion is a sequential decision problem where each attack turn must consider full history to maintain coherence and progress toward violation.
    *   **Quick check:** Given a history where the target model has become resistant, what might be a valid next attack strategy from GOAT's taxonomy, and how would you instruct the attacker model to choose it?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Inputs (Policy Descriptions, Attack Taxonomy, Few-Shot Examples) -> Attacker Model -> Horizontal Expansion Module (with Horizontal Memory Log) -> Starter Prompt (XML) -> Transformation Toolkit -> Vertical Expansion Module (with Vertical Memory) -> (Multi-turn, Multi-Modal) Conversation -> Judge Model (LlamaGuard) -> Logging (Conversation Log). Supporting: Sampling Strategy Selector, Strategy Selector.
*   **Critical Path:** 1) For a policy, run horizontal expansion to generate N starters. 2) For each starter, run the vertical expansion loop (turn-by-turn generation, transformation, judgment) for T turns, invoking meta expansion to potentially invent new strategies. 3) Log full conversation. 4) Loop back to step 1.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** Coverage vs. Cost (more samples/turns increase success chance but scale compute); Strategy Diversity vs. Coherence (meta encourages novelty but risks incoherence); Modality Fusion Complexity (toolkit adds overhead/failure points but enables multimodal attacks).
*   **Failure Signatures:** Horizontal: stagnant ASR, repetitive starters. Vertical: early termination, toolkit errors (missing/irrelevant images). Meta: judge labels "safe" for novel-strategy conversations. Systemic: judge model bias poisoning the horizontal feedback loop.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Ablate Horizontal Expansion:** Compare FERRET against a variant where starters are generated randomly or from a fixed pool (no self-improvement). Measure drop in ASR and diversity.
    2.  **Ablate Vertical Expansion Depth:** Run FERRET with max_turns=1 vs. max_turns=3 using same horizontal outputs. Measure ASR gain from additional turns.
    3.  **Probe Meta Expansion:** Manually analyze conversations labeled as using "meta" strategies. Categorize strategies as *truly novel*, *paraphrased*, or *incoherent*; correlate with turn-level success.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   How can multi-agent FERRET improve attack success and diversity over single-agent versions?
*   What new expansion types (e.g., cross-policy) can be added to FERRET and how do they affect vulnerability discovery?
*   Can advanced self-learning (e.g., RL) improve horizontal expansion's sample efficiency and ASR?
*   How can meta-expansion's novel attack strategies be validated and integrated into the taxonomy for reuse?

## Limitations
*   The exact instruction prompts for horizontal/vertical/meta expansions (including memory formatting and meta-incentivization) and the precise XML schema and image-handling details of the transformation toolkit are underspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
*   Potential judge model (LlamaGuard) bias could poison the horizontal feedback loop and distort ASR measurements.
*   The paper notes a naming collision with prior "Ferret" red-teaming work and has zero citations, raising concerns about novelty positioning and background integration.

## Confidence
*   **FERRET's reported ASR/diversity gains vs. FLIRT/GOAT:** High (empirical results provided, though reproducibility limited by unknowns).
*   **Novelty of the three-expansion framework (horizontal/vertical/meta):** Medium (adjacent multi-turn concepts exist in corpus; meta's on-the-fly strategy invention lacks external corroboration).
*   **Transformation toolkit's necessity and implementation details for multimodal success:** Low (implementation opaque; failure modes untested in paper).

## Next Checks
1.  **Toolkit Fidelity Test:** Reimplement XML handling per the paper's descriptions; generate images from `<text_in_image>` tags (e.g., "STOP sign with 'GO'") and measure OCR readability/target model confusion rate.
2.  **Meta-Novelty Quantification:** Manually annotate 100 "meta" conversations—categorize strategies as *truly novel*, *paraphrased*, or *incoherent*—and correlate with turn-level success to isolate meta's effective contribution.
3.  **Judge Bias Isolation:** Rerun horizontal expansion with human-verified success labels (bypassing LlamaGuard) on a subset; compare ASR trajectories to detect feedback loop poisoning.
