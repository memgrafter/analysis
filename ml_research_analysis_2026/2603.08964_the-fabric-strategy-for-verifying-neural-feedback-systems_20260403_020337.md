# The FABRIC Strategy for Verifying Neural Feedback Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.08964
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08964
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The FABRIC strategy, with DRIPY (outer) and CRISP (inner), reduces backward reachable set volume by up to 2900× and speeds up avoid-property verification by up to 7× compared to prior state-of-the-art.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of verifying safety in nonlinear neural feedback systems, where traditional reachability methods struggle with scalability. It introduces FABRIC, a strategy that combines forward and backward reachability analyses. For backward analysis, the authors propose new algorithms for sound over- and under-approximations of backward reachable sets, extending domain refinement to nonlinear dynamics and developing three inner-set methods: SHARP, CRISP, and CLEAN. These are integrated with a forward analysis module.

Evaluation on three benchmarks—TORA, Unicycle, and Attitude—shows substantial gains. The outer-approximation algorithm DRIPY (with refinement) reduces set volume by as much as 2900× relative to the prior state-of-the-art while running faster. For inner approximations, CRISP consistently outperforms BURNS, with margins increasing in higher-dimensional scenarios. The combined FABRIC pipeline yields speedups of up to 7× for avoid properties on the Unicycle system, demonstrating improved verification efficiency.

## Method Summary
FABRIC’s backward reachability core extends domain refinement—a technique that partitions the state space—to nonlinear dynamics. It generates a sound over-approximation of the backward reachable set using DRIPY, which iteratively refines regions to tighten bounds. For under-approximations, three methods are offered: SHARP, CRISP, and CLEAN; CRISP proved most effective in experiments. The backward module operates on a neural network controller embedded within nonlinear dynamics, relying on sound set propagation.

A forward analysis module runs in parallel, computing an under-approximation of states reachable from the initial set within the time horizon. The two modules intersect: a non-empty intersection between the forward under-approximation and the backward over-approximation immediately yields an unsafe witness, enabling early termination. If no intersection is found, the system is either safe or the approximations are too coarse; further refinement of the backward region continues until a conclusion can be drawn. This integration prunes unnecessary exploration and focuses computation where it matters, producing the observed speedups.

## Key Results
- DRIPY achieved up to **2900× volume reduction** compared to prior outer-approximation methods while being **faster**.
- The **CRISP** inner-approximation method consistently outperformed BURNS, with larger gains in higher dimensions.
- The full **FABRIC** strategy (forward + backward) delivered up to **7× speedup** on avoid properties for the Unicycle benchmark.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on complementary strengths: backward reachability with domain refinement yields sound over-approximations that converge to the true backward set, while forward analysis provides a tractable under-approximation of reachable states. By checking for intersection between the forward under- and backward over-approximations, the algorithm can quickly confirm unsafety without fully constructing either set. When disjoint, the backward under-approximations (from SHARP/CRISP/CLEAN) can be used to bound the search space and guide refinement, ensuring progress toward a safe verdict. The refinement process tightens approximations locally, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of global refinement. This synergy reduces both the volume of sets computed and the number of expensive set operations, explaining the empirical efficiency gains.

## Foundational Learning
**Backward Reachability**  
*Why needed*: To determine all states that can reach an unsafe region within a given time horizon, enabling safety proofs.  
*Quick check*: Does the algorithm maintain soundness when propagating sets through nonlinear dynamics?

**Domain Refinement**  
*Why needed*: Nonlinear dynamics can warp sets into complex shapes; partitioning the space allows tighter polyhedral or zonotopic representations.  
*Quick check*: Does further refinement monotonically improve approximation tightness?

**Sound Over-approximation**  
*Why needed*: Guarantees that no unsafe state is overlooked; essential for conservative safety verification.  
*Quick check*: Is the over-approximation mathematically proven to contain the exact reachable set?

**Sound Under-approximation**  
*Why needed*: Provides a subset of reachable states, useful for quick falsification and for proving safety when disjoint from the initial set.  
*Quick check*: Can every point in the under-approximation be traced back to an actual trajectory?

**Integration of Forward and Backward Analysis**  
*Why needed*: Forward analysis can eliminate states that cannot lead to the unsafe region, focusing backward refinement on promising areas.  
*Quick check*: Does the integration reduce the number of refinement steps compared to backward-only?

**Reachability for Neural Controllers**  
*Why needed*: The controller is a neural network; its piecewise-linear behavior must be soundly captured within set propagation.  
*Quick check*: Are all activation patterns considered without exponential blowup?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Initial Set → Forward Analyzer → Forward Under‑approximation (F_U)  
Unsafe Set → Backward Analyzer → Backward Over‑approximation (B_O, via DRIPY) and Backward Under‑approximations (B_U, via CRISP/SHARP/CLEAN)  
F_U & B_O → Intersection Check → [UNSAFE if intersect]  
F_U & B_U → Disjointness Check → [SAFE if disjoint and bounds tight]  
Otherwise → Domain Refinement Loop → update B_O/B_U → repeat

### Critical Path
1. Load system model (dynamics, neural controller) and specify sets (initial, unsafe, time horizon).  
2. Run forward analysis to compute F_U (under‑approx of reachable states).  
3. Run backward analysis to compute B_O (over‑approx of states that can reach unsafe) and optionally B_U (under‑approx).  
4. Check intersection F_U ∩ B_O: if non‑empty, declare unsafe and terminate.  
5. If F_U and B_U are disjoint and B_U sufficiently tight, declare safe and terminate.  
6. Else, refine the domain partition in the backward region, recompute B_O/B_U, and return to step 4.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Precision vs. Cost**: Finer domain refinement yields tighter approximations but increases computational overhead.
- **Under‑ vs. Over‑approximation**: Under‑approximations enable fast falsification but may miss counterexamples; over‑approximations guarantee soundness but can be overly conservative.
- **Choice of Inner‑set Method**: CRISP, SHARP, and CLEAN trade off tightness and computational effort; CRISP was best in experiments.
- **Forward‑Backward Balance**: Running forward analysis can prune the backward search, but the forward computation itself adds overhead; the optimal balance depends on system dimensionality and nonlinearity.

### Failure Signatures
- Inability to reach a decision within resource limits (time/memory) indicates insufficient refinement or poor approximation.
- Persistent false positives (disjointness declared unsafe) suggest over‑conservative over‑approximations.
- Missed counterexamples (later found by simulation) indicate under‑approximations that are too coarse or incomplete refinement.
- Large disparity between DRIPY volume and prior methods without corresponding speedup may signal implementation issues.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Replicate DRIPY on TORA**: Run the outer‑approximation algorithm on the TORA benchmark and verify volume reduction and runtime against the reported 2900× improvement.
2. **Benchmark CRISP vs. BURNS on Unicycle**: Execute the CRISP inner‑set method on the Unicycle system and compare the produced under‑approximation volume and computation time with the BURNS baseline.
3. **Validate FABRIC Integration on Attitude**: Run the full forward‑backward pipeline on the Attitude benchmark for an avoid property and confirm the reported speedup (up to 7×) relative to backward‑only analysis.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark scope** is limited to three low-dimensional systems; generalizability to higher dimensions or complex neural architectures is untested.
- **Comparative analysis** is narrow (primarily vs. BURNS); performance relative to other reachability tools remains unknown.
- **Hardware sensitivity** results lack standardized computational metrics, making reproducibility and fair comparison difficult.

## Confidence
- **High**: The FABRIC components achieve superior measured performance on the three specific benchmarks compared to prior state-of-the-art.
- **Medium**: The core algorithms are sound in principle but lack validation across a broader range of dynamics and dimensions.
- **Low**: Broad claims about scalability to general neural feedback systems or real‑world deployment are not supported by current evidence.

## Next Checks
