# PrivAct: Internalizing Contextual Privacy Preservation via Multi-Agent Preference Training
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.13840
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13840
*   **Reference count:** 24
*   **Primary result:** A multi-agent preference training framework that reduces contextual privacy leakage by up to 12.32% across multiple LLM backbones while maintaining comparable helpfulness, and generalizes zero-shot to a probing benchmark.

## Executive Summary
PrivAct addresses the challenge of contextual privacy leakage in LLM agents, where models inadvertently reveal sensitive context-dependent information. Unlike brittle inference-time interventions, it internalizes privacy preservation directly into generation behavior through a multi-agent system and preference learning. Its core innovation is a leakage-conditioned asymmetric reward shaping (LC-ARS) that imposes severe penalties for any privacy violation while rewarding helpfulness only under perfect compliance. Evaluated on the PrivacyLens benchmark, PrivAct consistently improves the privacy-helpfulness trade-off frontier across four model architectures and demonstrates robust zero-shot transfer and generalization across diverse multi-agent topologies.

## Method Summary
PrivAct employs a chain of three specialized agents—Generator, Verifier, and Refiner—operating in a tree-structured generation process. For each input (context, instruction, sensitive items), the Generator produces multiple candidate responses. Each candidate is critiqued by the Verifier, and each (generator, verifier) pair is refined by the Refiner, yielding a tree of leaf responses. A leaf reward `R(L, H)` is computed using LC-ARS, which combines a leakage score `L` and helpfulness score `H` with asymmetric penalties. This reward is propagated backward through the tree (via child-averaging) to assign value estimates to every intermediate node. These estimates are then thresholded to construct agent-specific preference pairs (chosen vs. rejected responses). Each agent is fine-tuned independently using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on its respective pairs, embedding privacy-aware behavior directly into its parameters.

## Key Results
*   Reduces average contextual privacy leakage by up to 12.32% on PrivacyLens compared to baseline models, while maintaining helpfulness scores.
*   Achieves strong zero-shot transfer to the ConfAIde probing benchmark, demonstrating learned privacy norms apply to novel scenarios.
*   Generalizes its effectiveness across varied multi-agent topologies (linear, branched, etc.), indicating robust internalization of preferences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Multi-Agent Preference Learning for Internalization.** PrivAct decomposes privacy reasoning across a generator-verifier-refiner chain. By constructing a tree of responses and back-propagating final rewards (privacy + helpfulness) to assign value estimates to every intermediate node, it creates dense, agent-specific preference pairs for DPO. This process embeds privacy-rewarded behavior directly into each agent's weights, moving beyond prompt-based or inference-time guards. The core assumption is that reward propagation accurately isolates each agent's contribution to compliant outcomes. Breakage occurs if propagation is noisy due to complex agent interactions, leading to misaligned preferences.

**Mechanism 2: Leakage-Conditioned Asymmetric Reward Shaping (LC-ARS).** The reward function `R(L, H)` uses two distinct regimes. If any leakage (`L>0`) is detected, a harsh, compounding penalty (`-min(L^α + H^β + b1, 1.0)`) is applied to close privacy-utility shortcuts. Only when leakage is zero (`L=0`) is helpfulness rewarded (`b2 + (1-b2)H^β`). This decoupling shifts the Pareto frontier by making privacy a strict prerequisite. The core assumption is that this strictness prevents optimization for minor leaks. Breakage risks include over-conservatism (low `b2`) or insufficient deterrence (low `b1` or `α`) if hyperparameters are poorly tuned.

**Mechanism 3: Topology-Agnostic Internalization.** Because privacy preferences are internalized at the individual agent level via DPO—not at the system architecture level—fine-tuned agents (e.g., verifier, refiner) maintain privacy-aware behavior regardless of how they are subsequently arranged (chain, branched, etc.). This is evidenced by consistent performance across topology experiments. The core assumption is that learned preferences are robust to structural changes. Breakage could occur if agents overfit to specific interaction patterns from training or if topology changes disrupt communication flow.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (c, u, S) -> Tree Generation (Generator -> Verifier -> Refiner) -> Leaf Reward Calculation (LC-ARS with judges) -> Reward Propagation (child-average up tree) -> Preference Pair Construction (thresholding at τ₂, τ₃) -> Per-Agent DPO Training.

**Critical path:** The fidelity of the entire system hinges on the accurate propagation of leaf rewards (`Vi(ri) = 1/Bi+1 Σ Vi+1(ri+1)`) to create meaningful value estimates for non-leaf nodes, which then inform the binary splitting into preference pairs. A failure in propagation corrupts the training signal for all upstream agents.

**Design tradeoffs:** Branching factor `B` explores more candidate responses but increases compute cost. LC-ARS hyperparameters (`α, β, b₁, b₂`) directly control the privacy-helpfulness trade-off. The roles and prompts for the Verifier and Refiner (Appendix B.1) are critical; vague prompts lead to ineffective critique or refinement.

**Failure signatures:** 1) High average leakage or leak@K on PrivacyLens (worse than vanilla baseline). 2) Significant drop in helpfulness binary score. 3) Poor zero-shot transfer on ConfAIde (especially Tiers 3/4). 4) Performance is highly sensitive to changes in multi-agent topology (Section 4.5), indicating lack of robust internalization.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Main Evaluation on PrivacyLens:** Compare privacy-helpfulness frontiers of PrivAct against Vanilla, PPE, and AIFC baselines across Llama-3.1-8B/1B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen-4B (Section 4.2, Table 5).
2.  **Zero-Shot Transfer to ConfAIde:** Evaluate the fine-tuned Refiner and Verifier agents on the ConfAIde probing benchmark's Tiers 3 & 4 without any in-domain training (Section 4.3, Table 2).
3.  **Ablation of Multi-Agent Components:** Compare the full system (Generator+Verifier+Refiner) against partial systems (e.g., Verifier-only, Refiner-only) to justify the contribution of joint multi-agent preference training (Section 4.4, Figure 3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, based on the limitations and mechanisms discussed, potential open research directions include:
*   Improving reward propagation fidelity to more accurately assign credit to each agent in the multi-agent system.
*   Developing adaptive methods for setting LC-ARS hyperparameters to reduce sensitivity.
*   Enhancing evaluation reproducibility by releasing full judge prompts and tool environments.

## Limitations
*   **Reward propagation fidelity:** The assumption that backward propagation via child-averaging accurately estimates each agent's contribution to privacy compliance is not directly validated and could be noisy.
*   **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** The LC-ARS parameters (`α, β, b₁, b₂`) require careful tuning to balance strict privacy with utility; inappropriate tuning leads to over-conservative or leaky models.
*   **Evaluation reproducibility:** Critical details (full LLM judge prompts, exact tool environment specification, precise tree traversal logic) are partially redacted or omitted, hindering exact replication and potentially introducing variance.

## Confidence
*   **Internalization via multi-agent preference learning:** Medium (mechanism is clear but propagation fidelity is an assumption).
*   **LC-ARS asymmetric reward shaping for trade-off:** Medium (theoretical soundness is strong, but hyperparameter sensitivity is a practical limitation).
*   **Generalization across multi-agent topologies:** Medium (empirical evidence is provided, but long-term robustness to novel topologies is not proven).
*   **Zero-shot transfer to ConfAIde:** High (results are clear and the evaluation setting is standard).
