# Ergodicity in reinforcement learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10895
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10895
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposed methods enable positive long-term growth in non-ergodic RL environments where standard RL fails.

## Executive Summary
Standard reinforcement learning algorithms maximize expected cumulative reward. However, this objective fails in non-ergodic reward processes where the long-term performance of a single agent (i.e., its time-average growth) is not guaranteed by optimizing the expectation. This paper identifies this mismatch and introduces three complementary solution approaches to align learning with per-trajectory growth.

The methods are: (1) ergodicity transformation learning, which modifies the reward signal to produce an ergodic observable; (2) a modified objective that regularizes training with a geometric mean estimator of the growth rate; and (3) temporal training that incorporates path dependence by conditioning on repeated actions or time-dependent states. Empirical evaluations on a coin-toss example, CartPole, and LunarLander show that standard RL algorithms yield near-zero or negative returns, while all three proposed approaches achieve consistent positive growth. This confirms the effectiveness of optimizing for individual trajectory performance under non-ergodic dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In non-ergodic processes, the time-average of a single trajectory can differ significantly from the ensemble average. Maximizing expected return (ensemble average) does not guarantee that the agent's own long-term growth is high; it may lead to policies that perform well on average across many possible runs but poorly on any given run. The proposed methods address this by either transforming the reward to enforce ergodicity, directly optimizing the geometric mean of returns (a consistent estimator of growth rate), or incorporating temporal context to capture path dependence. These adjustments shift the optimization focus to per-trajectory growth, aligning the agent's objective with its actual long-term performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Ergodicity**: The property that time averages converge to ensemble averages. *Why needed:* Ensures that optimizing expected return aligns with long-term performance on a single trajectory. *Quick check:* Verify that the process is mixing and that the chain is irreducible and aperiodic.
- **Geometric Mean (Growth Rate)**: A metric representing the multiplicative growth of returns over time. *Why needed:* Captures the long-term performance of a single trajectory more accurately than the arithmetic mean in non-ergodic settings. *Quick check:* Compute the product of returns and take the nth root.
- **Temporal Dependence**: The phenomenon where the reward distribution changes over time or depends on the sequence of actions. *Why needed:* Non-ergodic processes exhibit path dependence, so the agent must consider history to make optimal decisions. *Quick check:* Check autocorrelation of rewards or test for stationarity.
- **Reinforcement Learning (MDPs)**: The standard framework for sequential decision-making. *Why needed:* The paper builds upon RL; understanding MDPs is essential to grasp how non-ergodic reward processes deviate from standard assumptions. *Quick check:* Define states, actions, transitions, rewards, and discount factor.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Standard RL pipeline (Environment -> Agent -> Action) with three optional modules: (1) Ergodicity Transformer inserted between Environment and Agent to modify rewards; (2) Geometric Mean Regularizer added to Agent's loss; (3) Time Encoding module feeding into Agent to capture temporal context.  
Mapping: Environment -> (Reward Transformation) -> Agent (with Geometric Mean Loss & Temporal Encoding) -> Action.

**Critical Path:**  
The training loop follows: interact with environment (potentially transformed), store transitions, compute geometric mean estimator from returns, apply regularization to policy gradient or Q-learning update, and update agent parameters. Temporal training may involve recurrent networks or concatenating time information to states.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Ergodicity transformation simplifies the problem by changing the MDP but may distort original reward semantics.  
- Geometric mean regularization introduces an additional estimator that can be biased/variance-sensitive, requiring careful tuning.  
- Temporal training increases model complexity (e.g., recurrent connections) and computational cost, and may suffer from vanishing gradients over long horizons.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- If the ergodicity transformation fails, the agent may still experience non-stationary returns and exhibit unstable learning.  
- An inaccurate geometric mean estimator can lead to negative growth rates, causing the agent to avoid beneficial but risky actions.  
- Temporal models may overfit to spurious temporal patterns or fail to capture relevant long-term dependencies, resulting in suboptimal policies.
