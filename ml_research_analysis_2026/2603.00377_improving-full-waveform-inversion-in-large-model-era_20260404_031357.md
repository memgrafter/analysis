# Improving Full Waveform Inversion in Large Model Era

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00377
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00377
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A coordinated scaling recipe enables a billion-parameter FWI model to generalize to challenging unseen geology, reducing MAE from 199.20 to 139.81, RMSE from 312.15 to 241.78, and improving SSIM from 0.5844 to 0.7669 across six benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the poor generalization of data-driven Full Waveform Inversion (FWI) models to complex, unseen geological structures. The authors introduce a comprehensive scaling recipe for large models, combining a non-causal transformer backbone for global context, massive data augmentation via latent diffusion (increasing training pairs tenfold), and post-training enhancements such as reinforcement learning fine-tuning and physics-based latent gradient refinement. Evaluated on six demanding benchmarks (Marmousi, Salt, etc.), the approach achieves state-of-the-art results, significantly lowering error metrics and boosting structural similarity. The work demonstrates that with appropriate architectural choices, data scaling, and physical alignment, billion-parameter networks can effectively invert seismic waveforms for realistic Earth models.

## Method Summary
The proposed FWI system follows a multi-stage pipeline. First, a latent diffusion model trained on the OpenFWI dataset synthesizes new velocity maps; each synthetic map is paired with forward-modeled seismic data using an acoustic wave equation solver, expanding the training corpus tenfold. A ViT-VQGAN tokenizer then maps both seismic data and velocity maps into discrete token sequences. A billion-parameter non-causal transformer backbone is trained to predict velocity token indices from seismic tokens in parallel, leveraging bidirectional attention to capture global relationships. After supervised pre-training, the model undergoes reinforcement learning fine-tuning (using a GRPO-style algorithm) to optimize a map-level reward (negative L1 loss), encouraging holistic geological coherence. Finally, a physics-based refinement step performs gradient descent in the continuous latent space of the VQGAN decoder to minimize the mismatch between forward-modeled seismic data and the observed data. Inference uses stochastic sampling and latent refinement ensemble.

## Key Results
- MAE reduced from 199.20 to 139.81 (approx. 30% drop) on six zero-shot benchmarks.
- RMSE reduced from 312.15 to 241.78.
- SSIM improved from 0.5844 to 0.7669.
- The method sets a new state-of-the-art, particularly excelling on complex structures like salt bodies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Non-Causal Transformer for Global Context
**Claim:** Non-causal, parallel decoding with a billion-parameter transformer captures global seismic-to-velocity relationships more effectively than sequential (causal) generation.  
**Mechanism:** The model treats velocity map prediction as a non-sequence task; all velocity token positions can attend bidirectionally to every seismic token and to each other simultaneously. This avoids error propagation and limited context of autoregressive decoding.  
**Evidence anchors:** The paper's abstract and section 2.2 explicitly contrast non-causal with causal decoding. Table 1 shows a clear MAE drop (0.0463 vs 0.0330) when switching to non-causal with the same data.  
**Break condition:** If the underlying physics imposes a strict sequential dependency (e.g., wavefront propagation directionality is the only valid prior), non-causal attention might introduce physically inconsistent spatial correlations.

### Mechanism 2: Massive Data Augmentation via Latent Diffusion
**Claim:** Latent diffusion mitigates overfitting and increases geological diversity, enabling stable training of a large model.  
**Mechanism:** A diffusion model trained on ~408k OpenFWI samples generates new velocity maps in latent space; each synthetic map is paired with forward-modeled seismic data, creating a dataset 10× larger. This exposes the model to a broader distribution of geological structures, including hybrid feature combinations.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 2.2 describe the two-stage pipeline. Section 3.2/Table 1 shows progressive MAE improvements with more data (0.0770 → 0.0463 with 2× data → 0.0368 with 10× data). FID of 260.33 indicates synthetic data quality.  
**Break condition:** If the diffusion model's latent distribution omits critical physical modes (e.g., cannot generate valid salt bodies), augmentation could harm generalization.

### Mechanism 3: Post-Training Alignment via RL and Latent Gradient Refinement
**Claim:** Post-hoc RL fine-tuning and physics-based latent gradient refinement move predictions toward physically plausible and geologically coherent solutions, addressing residual ill-posedness.  
**Mechanism:** (1) RL fine-tuning treats the frozen transformer as a policy sampling token sequences; it optimizes a map-level reward (negative L1/L2 loss) via GRPO, encouraging sequences that decode to better maps. (2) Latent GD refinement: after predicting tokens, the corresponding continuous latent vectors (pre-quantization in VQGAN) are iteratively updated to minimize reconstruction loss on forward-modeled seismic data from the decoder's output.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract lists both enhancements. Section 2.3 details both mechanisms. Table 1 shows both RL (0.0167 → 0.0159) and latent GD (0.0159 → 0.0136) reductions in MAE. Table 3 shows RL gains across model strengths.  
**Break conditions:** RL: mis-specified reward might optimize a proxy harming perceptual quality. Latent GD: if VQGAN latent space is not smooth, gradient descent could lead to degenerate maps.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Transformer Non-Causal Attention
**Why needed:** To replace slow, error-prone autoregressive decoding with efficient, globally informed parallel prediction for a spatial inverse problem.  
**Quick check:** What is the key difference in the attention mask between causal and non-causal decoding for this task, and why does that matter for reconstructing disconnected geological features like salt bodies?

### Concept: Latent Diffusion Models
**Why needed:** To synthesize a massive volume of diverse, physically consistent training pairs (velocity map + seismic data) from a limited real dataset, enabling large-model training.  
**Quick check:** Why is it critical to generate the seismic data via forward modeling from the synthetic velocity map, rather than trying to generate paired (seismic, velocity) data directly?

### Concept: Vector-Quantized GAN (VQGAN) for High-Fidelity Tokenization
**Why needed:** To create a discrete token sequence for the transformer while preserving fine-scale geological details that would be lost with heavy downsampling.  
**Quick check:** How does removing the compression bottleneck (going to a wider latent grid) help with a scientific inversion task where spatial fidelity of boundaries is crucial?

### Concept: Policy Optimization (RL) for Holistic Output Alignment
**Why needed:** To fine-tune the model beyond token-level cross-entropy loss, using a downstream, map-level metric as a reward to improve overall structural coherence.  
**Quick check:** Why might minimizing cross-entropy on individual velocity tokens not lead to a globally accurate or geologically plausible velocity map when decoded?

### Concept: Physics-Guided Optimization in a Manifold
**Why needed:** To enforce consistency with the acoustic wave equation after the data-driven prediction, without destabilizing high-frequency details by optimizing directly in pixel space.  
**Quick check:** What is the advantage of performing gradient descent on the continuous latent vectors of the VQGAN decoder rather than on the final velocity pixel values?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Seismic data → Seismic tokenizer → seismic tokens `m`  
Velocity map → ViT-VQGAN encoder (frozen during main training) → quantized token indices `t`  
Sequence builder: special tokens + `m` + placeholder velocity tokens `p_vel` (training uses ground-truth `t`)  
Non-causal transformer backbone (1B params) → predicts velocity token logits → token indices `y'`  
`y'` → VQGAN decoder → continuous velocity map `v_hat`  
Post-training: RL fine-tuner updates backbone; Latent GD refiner updates continuous latent `z_pred` from VQGAN pre-quantization space.

**Critical path:**  
1. Train latent diffusion model on OpenFWI.  
2. Generate ~5M synthetic velocity maps, compute forward-modeled seismic data → augmented dataset.  
3. Train ViT-VQGAN on augmented data (later frozen).  
4. Train 1B non-causal transformer on augmented data using cross-entropy on velocity tokens.  
5. Apply RL fine-tuning to transformer.  
6. Inference: run transformer → decode tokens → apply latent GD refinement → ensemble predictions.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Model scale (1B) vs. data volume: Requires aggressive augmentation to avoid overfitting.  
- Non-causal vs. causal: Non-causal yields higher accuracy but more memory.  
- Discrete tokens vs. continuous latent: Tokens enable large-model training; latent GD adds inference cost but improves physical fidelity.  
- Synthetic diversity vs. fidelity: Augmentation introduces hybrid structures beneficial for generalization but risks unrealistic velocity maps if diffusion is biased.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training: loss plateaus high, outputs oversmoothed on validation → likely insufficient data or model too large.  
- Validation on OpenFWI: high MAE but high SSIM → mean-field smooth outputs; check per-token accuracy.  
- Zero-shot (e.g., Salt): still oversmoothed, missing salt bodies → augmentation didn't cover heterogeneity modes.  
- Latent GD: divergence or blurry maps → learning rate `η` too high or latent space not smooth.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablate non-causal modeling: Train identical 1B transformers (causal vs. non-causal) with same data; compare MAE on OpenFWI validation. Expect non-causal to be lower.  
2. Impact of data scaling: Train non-causal model on original OpenFWI, 2× augmented, and 10× augmented data; plot MAE vs. dataset size. Expect decreasing trend.  
3. Qualitative check on zero-shot Salt: Run final model (RL+GD) on Salt benchmark; inspect if salt bodies are delineated sharply (as in Fig. 4) vs. smooth gradients.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to 3D elastic physics and variable acquisition geometries:** The current work is limited to 2D acoustic waves. Scaling to 3D introduces computational complexity and elastic physics changes the inversion problem.  
2. **Generalization to real field data:** The model is trained and evaluated solely on simulated synthetic data. Robustness to real-world noise, acquisition artifacts, and unmodeled Earth complexities remains unknown.  
3. **Limits of latent gradient refinement:** The post-hoc physics-based optimization operates in a fixed VQGAN latent space. It is unclear whether this discrete tokenizer bottleneck constrains the achievable physical fidelity compared to end-to-end differentiable approaches.  
4. **Coverage of geological diversity by diffusion augmentation:** The diffusion model is trained only on OpenFWI. Rare but critical geological structures (e.g., complex volcanic intrusions, multi-stage salt tectonics) might be underrepresented, limiting true extrapolation.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing:** Exact ViT-VQGAN architecture, RL hyperparameters, and latent GD settings (learning rate, steps) are not provided, hindering exact reproduction.  
- **Synthetic data fidelity:** The diffusion-generated dataset has an FID of 260.33, indicating a domain gap. It is uncertain whether the augmentation fully covers critical geological modes needed for robust zero-shot generalization.  
- **Black-box reasoning:** While ablations show benefits, the internal decision-making of the non-causal transformer—whether it truly captures wave physics or exploits spurious correlations—is not analyzed.

## Confidence
- **Non-causal transformer improves performance:** High (direct ablation evidence).  
- **10× data augmentation is essential for 1B model:** Medium (correlation between more data and lower MAE, but diversity vs. volume not isolated).  
- **RL and latent GD enhance physical consistency:** Medium (incremental metric improvements, but qualitative geological interpretability not deeply analyzed).  
- **Generalization to unseen benchmarks:** Medium (strong results on six synthetic benchmarks, but real-world field data remains untested).  
- **Diffusion augmentation covers critical geological modes:** Low (no systematic analysis of latent space coverage or rare mode representation).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate data diversity:** Train the non-causal transformer with the same 10× data volume but using a simpler augmentation (e.g., elastic deformations) instead of latent diffusion. Compare zero-shot performance on Salt/Marmousi to isolate the value of diffusion-generated diversity.  
2. **Probe VQGAN latent smoothness:** For predicted token sequences, perform latent GD with varying learning rates (e.g., 1e-4 to 1e-2). Monitor reconstruction loss and SSIM; degradation with higher rates would indicate a non-smooth manifold.  
3. **Zone-wise evaluation on Salt:** Segment the Salt benchmark into zones: inside salt bodies, sediment-salt boundaries, and background sediment. Compute MAE/SSIM per zone to verify improvement particularly at sharp boundaries, not just smooth regions.
