# Large-Scale Dataset and Benchmark for Skin Tone Classification in the Wild
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02475
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02475
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduced STW dataset (42,313 images, 10-tone MST), benchmarked models showing ViT-based SkinToneNet achieves human-level performance (OOAcc 0.901 on STW-test, 0.853 on MSTE) and exposes systemic underrepresentation of darker skin tones.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the lack of granular skin tone datasets by introducing STW, a large-scale dataset with 42,313 images labeled on the 10-tone MST scale. Through careful annotation with high inter-annotator agreement (ICC 0.939, off-by-one 88%), they benchmark classic and deep learning methods. Key findings: classic models fail (near-random) due to identity leakage, while deep learning approaches, particularly a fine-tuned Vision Transformer (SkinToneNet), approach annotator accuracy and generalize to out-of-domain data. The use of individual-based (IND) splits prevents identity leakage, enabling reliable fairness audits that reveal systemic underrepresentation of darker skin tones in public facial datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors present two main pipelines: (1) **SkinToneCCV** for classic computer vision, which uses Mediapipe for face and skin segmentation, extracts color descriptors (histograms, moments, CCV, BIC), reduces dimensions, and classifies with models like random forests or SVMs via grid search. (2) **SkinToneNet** for deep learning: a Vision Transformer (ViT-Small) pre-trained on ImageNet, fine-tuned on the STW dataset using full-face cropped images (no segmentation), cross-entropy loss, and strict individual-based (IND) splits to avoid identity leakage. Training employs class balancing (max 2 images per person) and data augmentation (flips, color jitter, blur, etc.). Evaluation focuses on balanced accuracy (bAcc) and off-by-one accuracy (OOAcc), with out-of-domain tests on MSTE and CCv2 to assess generalization. The IND split is crucial: it ensures all images of a person are confined to one split, forcing the model to learn skin tone features rather than identity.

## Key Results
- STW dataset: 42,313 images, 3,564 individuals, labeled on 10-tone MST scale; high inter-annotator agreement (ICC 0.939, off-by-one 88%).
- Classic models (e.g., random forest) perform near-random on IND splits, highlighting identity leakage issue.
- SkinToneNet (ViT-Small) achieves state-of-the-art: on STW-test (IND), balanced accuracy 0.449, off-by-one accuracy 0.901; on out-of-domain MSTE, accuracy 0.413, off-by-one 0.853.
- Fairness auditing of public facial datasets reveals systemic underrepresentation of darker skin tones (MST 1, 9, 10) and mid-tones (3–6).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reliable Labels via MST Scale and Hierarchical Annotation
The claim is that the MST scale combined with a hierarchical annotation protocol yields reliable, fine-grained skin tone labels suitable for deep learning. The mechanism involves a primary expert annotator labeling all images for consistency, with a stratified subset labeled by two independent annotators to measure agreement. A specialized interface with gold-standard examples minimizes subjective bias. High off-by-one accuracy (88%) and ICC (0.939) indicate disagreements are typically between adjacent tones, not random, making labels suitable for an ordinal classifier. The core assumption is that human perception of skin tone is subjective but exhibits a structured, ordinal pattern capturable with a robust labeling protocol. Evidence includes the abstract's mention of inter-annotator agreement metrics, section 3 details of ICC and OOAcc, and corroboration from related work [25,26] that also uses similar metrics. If the protocol is not adhered to (e.g., split interface or strict instructions violated), inter-annotator agreement would drop, degrading label quality.

### Mechanism 2: Preventing Identity Leakage with Individual-Based Splits
The claim is that an individual-based (IND) train/test split prevents identity leakage, which causes near-random performance in classic models and artificially high accuracy in deep learning models on standard splits. The mechanism ensures all images of a single person reside exclusively in one split, preventing the model from memorizing identities or person-specific conditions (lighting, makeup). This forces learning of skin tone features. Evidence: classic models collapse on IND splits, while deep learning models show moderate performance drop from IMG to IND splits, indicating prior overfitting. The core assumption is that performance degradation on IND vs. IMG splits is diagnostic of identity leakage rather than genuine feature learning. Evidence anchors: abstract notes classic models near-random and deep learning reaches annotator accuracy; section 6 describes IMG vs. IND splits; section 7 (Table I) shows steep decline for RF and DenseNet121 on IND, while DenseNet121's wOOAcc remains stable but lower. If data sources have multiple images per person and splits are not rigorously person-wise, identity leakage persists.

### Mechanism 3: Vision Transformers Leverage Holistic Context for Generalization
The claim is that Vision Transformers (ViTs), especially when fine-tuned on full-face images (not just skin segments), generalize better to out-of-domain data because their self-attention mechanism leverages holistic facial context (shape, texture, geometry) to disambiguate skin tone under varying conditions. Classic CV relies on handcrafted color descriptors from segmented skin, which are sensitive to lighting, makeup, beards. ViTs process the full image and learn to weight contextual cues (hair, eyes, face shape) that correlate with skin tone but are robust to noise. Grad-CAM in Figure 7 shows ViT-Small sometimes attends to hair, supporting this. The core assumption is that skin tone perception is not purely colorimetric; it involves integrating multiple visual cues beyond pure skin pixels. Evidence: abstract states SkinToneNet (ViT) achieves SOTA generalization; section 5 notes segmentation-based models were outperformed by cropped input; section 7 (Table 4) shows ViTs outperform CNNs and segmentation-based LabNet on out-of-domain datasets; Figure 7 shows attention on hair. If training data lacks diversity in facial context or the task requires isolated skin patches (e.g., medical devices), ViT's context reliance becomes a liability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Monk Skin Tone (MST) Scale vs. Fitzpatrick Scale**  
  Why needed: The paper's contribution hinges on using the more granular, perceptually representative 10-tone MST scale instead of the medical Fitzpatrick scale (6-tone). Understanding this distinction is crucial for interpreting dataset value and model complexity.  
  Quick check: Why is the MST scale considered more representative for fairness auditing than the Fitzpatrick scale?

- **Concept: Identity Leakage in Dataset Splits**  
  Why needed: The paper's central methodological contribution is exposing and fixing data leakage through IND splits. This is a fundamental ML hygiene concept critical for any generalization claim.  
  Quick check: What performance differential between IMG and IND splits indicates severe identity leakage, based on Table 1?

- **Concept: Off-by-One Accuracy (OOAcc) as an Ordinal Metric**  
  Why needed: For a 10-class ordinal task like skin tone, exact accuracy is too harsh. OOAcc measures if prediction is within one tone, aligning with human perception (88% annotator OOAcc). It is the primary metric for judging model closeness to human performance.  
  Quick check: Why is an OOAcc of 0.90 considered strong for this 10-class problem, whereas a 0.45 exact accuracy might be expected?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (full-face cropped image) -> Preprocessing (face detection, resizing, augmentation) -> Backbone (ViT-Small/Base or CNN) -> Head (linear classification layer) -> Output (MST class 1-10)

**Critical path:**  
1. Secure STW dataset and construct strict IND splits (no person in multiple splits). Use provided 80/20 holdout and 5-fold CV for model selection. Reserve 10 individuals per class for custom test to check leakage.  
2. Preprocess: detect and crop faces (Mediapipe), resize to model input (e.g., 224x224). For ViT models, use full image without skin segmentation. Apply augmentation (flips, color jitter, blur, noise, grid shuffle dropout).  
3. Load ViT-Small pretrained on ImageNet. Replace classification head for 10 classes.  
4. Fine-tune entire backbone on balanced STW training set (max 2 images/person) using cross-entropy loss. Use AdamW/SGD with LR scheduler, early stop on validation OOAcc.  
5. Evaluate final model on STW-test (IND), MSTE, CCv2 using bAcc and OOAcc. Primary success: OOAcc >0.85 on out-of-domain (e.g., MSTE).  
6. Generate Grad-CAM to verify attention on facial skin regions.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- ViT vs. CNN: ViT generally outperforms CNNs on out-of-domain due to global context but requires more data/compute.  
- Full image vs. segmented skin: Full image yields better generalization; segmentation removes useful context but may improve background robustness.  
- Full fine-tuning vs. partial: Full backbone fine-tuning works best for ViT; partial tuning may underfit.  
- Loss function: Cross-entropy outperformed ordinal losses in experiments; ordinal loss may help with extreme imbalance but wasn't beneficial here.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Collapse to majority classes (tones 2 and 7): model not learning discriminative features, possibly due to poor split (IMG), insufficient data, or weak capacity.  
2. High STW-test accuracy but near-random on MSTE/CCv2: overfitting to dataset-specific artifacts; check for IMG split usage.  
3. Grad-CAM highlights hair/background: model using non-skin context; may need better cropping or segmentation.  
4. Performance drop from balanced to imbalanced training: model not robust to skew; requires stronger regularization or class-weighted loss.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate IMG vs. IND leakage diagnostic: Train a random forest on pixel averages using an IMG split, evaluate on an IND test set. Expect drastic wOOAcc drop vs. IMG test (per Table 1), confirming leakage.  
2. Benchmark ViT-Small on STW with IND split: Follow SkinToneNet recipe (FI input, full fine-tune, cross-entropy). Record bAcc and OOAcc on STW-test to establish baseline.  
3. Test generalization to MSTE: Evaluate model from Exp 2 on MSTE without retraining. Report Acc and OOAcc. Target OOAcc >0.85 (paper reports 0.853).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How does the Colorimetric Skin Tone (CST) scale compare to the MST scale for in-the-wild skin tone classification, particularly regarding perceptual uniformity and bias mitigation?  
**Basis in paper:** Explicitly mentioned as future work; MST may not be fully decoupled from CIELab color space.  
**Why unresolved:** Paper adopts MST without validating its perceptual uniformity or comparing to CST on diverse data; CST's advantages remain speculative.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic human studies and model evaluations comparing MST and CST annotations across demographic groups and lighting conditions, measuring consistency and fairness metrics.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What architectural properties of Vision Transformers (ViTs) enable superior generalization in skin tone classification compared to CNNs like DenseNet, and can these be isolated?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred from Table 4 showing ViTs outperform CNNs on out-of-domain datasets; authors hypothesize global context via attention.  
**Why unresolved:** Lacks ablation studies separating attention, patch embedding, or training dynamics; unclear if success is due to architecture, pretraining, or both.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments with matched CNN/ViT backbones, gradient-based attribution across architectures, and feature space analyses on STW and out-of-domain data.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can the severe underrepresentation of extreme MST tones (1, 9, 10) and mid-tones (3–6) in existing facial datasets be mitigated without extensive new data collection?  
**Basis in paper:** Explicitly notes data scarcity for tones 1,9,10 due to "logistics challenge" and imbalance for 3–6; auditing shows systemic absence.  
**Why unresolved:** Authors propose adding more data but do not explore synthetic data generation, few-shot learning, or active learning; scalability unaddressed.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments with generative models (e.g., diffusion) to synthesize rare tones, evaluated via model performance and human validation on out-of-domain datasets; studies on sampling strategies for balanced training.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** To what extent does inherent subjectivity in human skin tone perception, reflected in low exact annotator agreement (38.8%), limit the validity of any discrete scale (MST/FST) for training objective models?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred from inter-annotator agreement (38.8% exact, 88% off-by-one); paper acknowledges subjectivity but doesn't question if 10-tone granularity is perceptually meaningful or if models learn annotator biases.  
**Why unresolved:** Paper treats MST as ground truth without probing if scale boundaries correspond to distinguishable cues; models may fit noise rather than true gradients.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Psychophysical experiments testing human discrimination thresholds for MST tones; analysis of model confidence vs. annotator disagreement; evaluation on continuous skin reflectance measurements.

## Limitations
- **Dataset access unknown:** STW dataset is not yet released ("available soon"), blocking replication of core experiments.
- **Critical hyperparameters missing:** SkinToneNet training details (exact learning rate, weight decay, epoch count, augmentation probabilities) are unreported.
- **Out-of-domain dataset specs unclear:** Composition and preprocessing of MSTE and CCv2 are not fully specified, affecting verification of generalization claims.

## Confidence
- **MST Scale & Annotation Quality:** Medium (high agreement metrics depend on unreleased protocol).
- **Identity Leakage via IND Split:** High (own experiments show classic models collapse and DL degrades on IND vs. IMG splits).
- **ViT Generalization via Full-Face Context:** Medium (superior out-of-domain performance demonstrated, but causal role of context inferred, not proven).
