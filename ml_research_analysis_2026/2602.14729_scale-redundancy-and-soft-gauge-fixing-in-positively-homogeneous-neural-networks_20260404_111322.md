# Scale redundancy and soft gauge fixing in positively homogeneous neural networks

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.14729  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14729  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** A soft norm‑balancing functional penalizing relative scale imbalance per neuron acts as a gauge‑fixing term, improving optimization stability for ReLU networks while preserving the represented function.

## Executive Summary
Neural networks with ReLU activations exhibit an exact neuron‑wise rescaling symmetry, creating flat directions in the loss landscape that can cause internal scale drift and optimization instability. The authors interpret this symmetry as a gauge redundancy and introduce a soft gauge‑fixing functional that penalizes the squared difference between the logarithms of incoming and outgoing weight norms for each hidden neuron. This term is invariant under the rescaling symmetry and adds a damping effect on the redundant scale‑imbalance coordinate. In a minimal regression experiment, weak gauge fixing (λ=0.05) preserves validation performance, while stronger fixing (λ=0.2) expands the stable learning‑rate regime, preventing divergence at a learning rate where the baseline fails. The work establishes a direct link between gauge‑orbit geometry and the conditioning of optimization.

## Method Summary
The study focuses on a single‑hidden‑layer ReLU network \(f(x)=W_2\sigma(W_1x+b_1)+b_2\) with \(H\) hidden units. For each neuron \(i\), define the norms \(n_{1,i}=\|W_{1,i}\|_2\) (incoming weight row) and \(n_{2,i}=\|W_{2,:,i}\|_2\) (outgoing weight column), and the logarithmic coordinates \(\alpha_i=\log(n_{1,i}+\varepsilon)\), \(\beta_i=\log(n_{2,i}+\varepsilon)\) with a small constant \(\varepsilon>0\). The gauge‑fixing functional is \(G(\theta)=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{i=1}^{H}(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2\), which vanishes when the norms are balanced and is constant along the continuous rescaling orbits generated by the symmetry. The total loss is \(L_{\text{total}}=L_{\text{MSE}}+\lambda G\). In gauge‑coordinates \(u_i=\alpha_i+\beta_i\) (gauge‑invariant) and \(v_i=\alpha_i-\beta_i\) (redundant), the gradient flow induced by \(L_{\text{total}}\) yields \(\dot{v}_i = -(F_i^{(1)}-F_i^{(2)}) - \kappa_i v_i\) where \(\kappa_i>0\) depends on \(\lambda\) and the norms. The additional \(-\kappa_i v_i\) term provides dissipative relaxation of the imbalance mode \(v_i\) toward zero without affecting the function represented by the network.

## Key Results
- **Exact functional invariance:** Random neuron‑wise rescalings change the network output by only \(\sim 10^{-17}\), confirming the symmetry.
- **Performance preservation:** Weak gauge fixing (\(\lambda=0.05\)) yields validation MSE \(0.5156\pm0.0044\) vs. baseline \(0.5169\pm0.0038\) (no significant degradation).
- **Optimization stability:** Moderate fixing (\(\lambda=0.2\)) stabilizes training at learning rate \(2\times10^{-2}\) where the baseline diverges, expanding the stable learning‑rate window.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Neuronal rescaling symmetry induces flat directions in the loss landscape, enabling internal scale drift without functional change.  
- **Mechanism:** For a ReLU network, positive homogeneity (\(\sigma(\alpha z)=\alpha\sigma(z)\)) allows neuron‑wise transformations: \((W_1,b_1)\rightarrow D(W_1,b_1)\), \(W_2\rightarrow W_2D^{-1}\) with a positive diagonal matrix \(D=\text{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_H)\). This leaves \(f(x)\) exactly invariant and generates continuous gauge orbits in parameter space along which the task loss is constant.  
- **Core assumption:** Activation is positively homogeneous (ReLU); weight norms are strictly positive (\(n_{1,i},n_{2,i}>0\)) to define the transformation group.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “Neural networks with positively homogeneous activations exhibit an exact continuous reparametrization symmetry... along which the input–output function is invariant.”; [section II] Formal derivation of transformation (3) and Fig. 1 illustrating orbits.  
- **Break condition:** If any weight norm becomes exactly zero, the rescaling group action is no longer well‑defined (\(s_i>0\) required); the paper introduces \(\varepsilon>0\) to avoid numerical pathology but does not guarantee positivity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The norm‑balancing functional \(G(\theta)=\frac{1}{H}\sum_i[\log(n_{1,i}+\varepsilon)-\log(n_{2,i}+\varepsilon)]^2\) softly fixes the gauge by penalizing relative scale imbalance.  
- **Mechanism:** \(G\) acts only on the gauge coordinate \(v_i=\alpha_i-\beta_i\). It vanishes at balanced representatives (\(n_{1,i}\approx n_{2,i}\)) and is invariant under simultaneous rescaling because \(v_i\) shifts by \(2\log s_i\) while \(G\) depends on \(v_i^2\).  
- **Core assumption:** Each orbit admits a balanced representative via choosing \(s_i=\sqrt{n_{2,i}/n_{1,i}}\) (for \(\varepsilon=0\)); with \(\varepsilon>0\) this holds approximately.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “We introduce gauge‑adapted coordinates… norm‑balancing functional acting only on redundant scale coordinates.”; [section III] Definition (9) and proof of existence for balanced representatives.  
- **Break condition:** If the task loss strongly favors extreme imbalance (e.g., \(F_i^{(1)}-F_i^{(2)}\) large and persistent), strong \(\lambda\) may interfere with optimization as seen in \(\lambda=0.50\) degradation; also if norms collapse to zero, the log terms become unstable despite \(\varepsilon\).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The gauge‑fixing term induces dissipative relaxation of imbalance modes via explicit damping in \(v_i\) dynamics, suppressing scale drift while preserving function.  
- **Mechanism:** Gradient flow on \(L_{\text{total}}=L_{\text{task}}+\lambda G\) yields \(\dot{v}_i = -(F_i^{(1)}-F_i^{(2)}) - \kappa_i v_i\), where \(\kappa_i = \frac{2\lambda}{H}\bigl[\frac{1}{(n_{1,i}+\varepsilon)^2}+\frac{1}{(n_{2,i}+\varepsilon)^2}\bigr] > 0\). The \(\kappa_i v_i\) term exponentially damps \(v_i\) toward zero without affecting \(u_i=\alpha_i+\beta_i\) (gauge‑invariant scale).  
- **Core assumption:** The task‑induced radial forces \(F_i^{(1)},F_i^{(2)}\) are bounded; damping dominates only if \(\lambda>0\) and norms are not vanishing.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [section IV] Derivation of Eq. (20) and dissipation rate Eq. (21); \(\dot{v}_i|_g = -\kappa_i v_i\). [section V] Experiment: \(\lambda=0.2\) stabilizes training at lr=\(2\times10^{-2}\) where baseline fails, consistent with damping preventing large \(v_i\) excursions.  
- **Break condition:** If \(\lambda=0\), \(\kappa_i=0\) and no dissipation; if task forcing \((F_i^{(1)}-F_i^{(2)})\) is perpetually non‑zero and aligned with \(v_i\), damping may be overcome, causing sustained imbalance. Also if norms become very large, \(\kappa_i\to0\) and damping weakens.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Positive homogeneity**  
  *Why needed here:* It is the mathematical property that exactly generates the rescaling symmetry; without it (e.g., with sigmoid), gauge redundancy does not exist.  
  *Quick check question:* For a ReLU neuron with weights \(w_i\) and \(a_i\), if you multiply \(w_i\) by \(s>0\) and divide \(a_i\) by \(s\), what happens to the neuron’s output and to the product \(n_{1,i}\cdot n_{2,i}\)?

- **Concept: Gauge redundancy**  
  *Why needed here:* It frames the symmetry as an equivalence relation on parameter space (orbits), distinguishing physical (function) from unphysical (scale‑imbalance) directions.  
  *Quick check question:* In a gauge theory, what is the role of a gauge‑fixing condition? How does that analogy translate to the network’s loss landscape?

- **Concept: Gauge coordinates (u_i, v_i)**  
  *Why needed here:* They separate invariant (\(u_i=\alpha_i+\beta_i\), related to total scale) from redundant (\(v_i=\alpha_i-\beta_i\), imbalance) directions, making the dynamics decomposable.  
  *Quick check question:* Under a neuron‑wise rescaling, how do \(u_i\) and \(v_i\) transform? Which is gauge‑invariant?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** For each hidden neuron \(i\), identify incoming weight row \(w_i\) (from \(W_1\)), outgoing weight column \(a_i\) (from \(W_2\)). Compute norms \(n_{1,i}=\|w_i\|_2\), \(n_{2,i}=\|a_i\|_2\). Define \(\alpha_i=\log(n_{1,i}+\varepsilon)\), \(\beta_i=\log(n_{2,i}+\varepsilon)\), then \(v_i=\alpha_i-\beta_i\).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. During training, at each step compute all \(v_i\).  
  2. Form \(G(\theta)=\frac{1}{H}\sum_i v_i^2\).  
  3. Add \(\lambda G\) to the task loss (MSE).  
  4. Backpropagate total loss; the gradient of \(G\) will be purely radial (Eq. A11–A12).  
  5. Monitor mean \(|v_i|\) to gauge imbalance drift.

- **Design tradeoffs:** \(\lambda\) controls strength: small \(\lambda\) (e.g., 0.05) modestly conditions without harming performance; larger \(\lambda\) (e.g., 0.2) expands stable learning‑rate regime but risks overconstraining; very large \(\lambda\) (0.5+) degrades validation error. \(\varepsilon\) is a small stability constant (paper uses default \(\sim10^{-8}\)).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. \(\lambda=0\): \(v_i\) drift large, training unstable at high learning rates.  
  2. \(\lambda\) too high: validation error increases, variance across seeds grows (Table I, \(\lambda=0.50\)).  
  3. \(\varepsilon\) too small: numerical issues if any \(n_{1,i}\) or \(n_{2,i}\) approach zero during training.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Functional invariance check:** After training, apply random \(s_i>0\) via Eq. (3) and compute \(\Delta_{\text{inv}}=\langle|f_\theta(x)-f_{\theta'}(x)|\rangle_x\). Expect \(\sim10^{-17}\) (double precision). Confirms gauge symmetry exactness.  
  2. **Scale drift measurement:** Track average \(|v_i|\) over training steps. Baseline should show drift; gauge‑fixed should show suppression/convergence to near zero.  
  3. **Learning‑rate stress test:** For fixed \(\lambda\) (e.g., 0.2), sweep learning rate (e.g., \(5\!\times\!10^{-3},10^{-2},2\!\times\!10^{-2}\)). Compare stability (whether loss diverges) and final validation MSE against baseline (\(\lambda=0\)). Expect gauge‑fixed stable at higher lr where baseline fails (Table II).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does soft gauge fixing affect optimization stability, scale drift, and performance in deep neural networks with many hidden layers?**  
   *Basis:* The paper explicitly calls for extending the perspective to deeper architectures, noting that symmetries propagate across layers, leading to richer orbit structures.  
   *Why unresolved:* Experiments are limited to a single hidden layer; deeper networks introduce inter‑layer symmetry interactions that could alter benefits or drawbacks.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Train deep ReLU networks (e.g., 5–100 layers) on standard benchmarks (CIFAR‑10, ImageNet) with and without gauge fixing. Measure per‑layer scale drift, stability across learning rates, and final test accuracy.

2. **Can the gauge‑fixing principle be embedded into a stochastic field‑theoretic framework (e.g., effective action, Lindblad dynamics) to model neural network training under SGD noise?**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion proposes embedding the construction into a stochastic QFT‑style effective action and promoting architectures to slow variables evolving under Markovian dynamics in function space.  
   *Why unresolved:* The present work treats deterministic gradient flow only; stochasticity from minibatch sampling is fundamental to deep learning and its interplay with gauge redundancy is unknown.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Develop a stochastic effective action that includes the gauge‑fixing functional. Derive a corresponding Lindblad or Fokker‑Planck equation. Verify new constraints (e.g., Ward identities) against simulations of wide networks trained with SGD.

3. **For which positively homogeneous activations beyond ReLU (e.g., leaky ReLU, ELU, maxout) does the proposed norm‑balancing functional remain effective, numerically stable, and compatible with the exact rescaling invariance?**  
   *Basis:* The theory is derived for any positively homogeneous activation, but experiments are exclusively on ReLU.  
   *Why unresolved:* Other activations may behave differently due to non‑differentiability, zero‑norm issues, or different curvature of the task‑forcing term.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Replicate the minimal regression experiment with leaky ReLU, ELU, and maxout. Check functional invariance under random rescalings, measure scale drift, stability across learning rates, and validation performance for various \(\lambda\). Investigate if adjustments to \(\varepsilon\) or the balancing condition are needed.

4. **How does soft gauge fixing interact with modern deep learning optimizers (e.g., SGD with momentum, Adam) and normalization layers (e.g., batch norm) when applied to homogeneous networks?**  
   *Basis:* The paper uses only plain gradient descent and contrasts gauge fixing with batch norm and weight decay but does not evaluate combinations.  
   *Why unresolved:* Momentum integrates past gradients, potentially changing damping of imbalance; Adam’s per‑parameter learning rates could distort the relative effect of \(\lambda\); batch norm already partially controls scale drift. Synergies or conflicts are unknown.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Train models with SGD+momentum (0.9), Adam, and with batch normalization layers inserted, both with and without gauge fixing. Across learning rates, compare stability, convergence speed, and final accuracy; tune \(\lambda\) per optimizer; monitor evolution of \(v_i\) to assess added benefit.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameter details (initialization, dataset sizes, number of training steps, batch size) hinder exact replication of the experiments.  
- The experimental validation is restricted to a shallow (one‑hidden‑layer, width 20) synthetic regression task; generalizability to deeper/wider architectures and real‑world datasets is unproven.  
- The analysis assumes strictly positive weight norms; while \(\varepsilon\) mitigates numerical issues, no sensitivity analysis is provided, and there is no comparison with other regularizers (e.g., weight decay, batch norm) or optimizers (Adam, momentum).

- **Gauge functional invariance & performance preservation with moderate \(\lambda\):** High  
- **Optimization benefit (expanded stable learning‑rate regime):** Medium
