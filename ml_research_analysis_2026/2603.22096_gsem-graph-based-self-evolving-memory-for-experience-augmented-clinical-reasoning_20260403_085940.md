# GSEM: Graph-based Self-Evolving Memory for Experience Augmented Clinical Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22096
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22096
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Achieves highest average accuracy on MedR-Bench (70.90%) and MedAgentsBench (69.24%) with DeepSeek-V3.2 and Qwen3.5-35B, with especially strong gains on treatment planning and overall reasoning quality.

## Executive Summary
GSEM addresses the core problem of ineffective experience reuse in clinical decision agents, where prior methods store experiences as independent records leading to noisy retrieval and unreliable application. It introduces a dual-layer memory graph: an entity layer encodes the internal decision structure of each experience (conditions, constraints, actions, rationales, outcomes) while an experience layer models relational dependencies between experiences with quality scores and edge weights. Retrieval combines entity-based and embedding-based seed recall with an LLM-guided multi-seed graph traversal to ensure boundary-aware applicability and coherent multi-experience composition. After each task, the graph self-evolves by updating node quality and edge weights from outcome feedback, refining reliability without altering content. Evaluated on MedR-Bench and MedAgentsBench, GSEM outperforms all baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of its structured, evolving memory for complex clinical reasoning.

## Method Summary
GSEM constructs a memory graph from logged clinical experiences. Each experience is decomposed into a typed entity graph (`Gent`) capturing its internal reasoning flow (conditions, constraints, actions, rationales, outcomes). Separately, an experience graph (`Gexp`) is built where nodes are whole experiences and edges encode relational similarity (entity/structure/synergy/task) with initial weights `W^(0)`. For a new clinical query, the system first retrieves a seed set of experiences via a hybrid of sparse (BM25 on extracted entities) and dense (embedding similarity) recall. It then performs a guided traversal over `Gexp`, with an LLM policy deciding actions (COLLECT, EXPLORE, BACKTRACK, STOP) at each step to gather a coherent set of relevant experiences while respecting the query's boundary conditions. Candidate experiences for exploration are ranked by a composite score blending edge weight and node quality. After the task is completed, a scalar feedback signal (e.g., accuracy) is used to update the quality score `Q` of each activated experience and the edge weights `W` between co-activated pairs via conservative, additive updates that preserve the initial structural prior. This closed loop of construction, retrieval, generation, evaluation, and evolution enables the memory to self-refine toward more reliable and relationally accurate knowledge.

## Key Results
- GSEM achieves the highest average accuracy among all baselines on both MedR-Bench (70.90%) and MedAgentsBench (69.24%).
- It shows particularly strong gains on treatment planning tasks and in overall reasoning quality metrics.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of the dual-layer structure, hybrid seed recall, and multi-seed graph traversal components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Layer Graph for Structured Recall
Claim: Organizing experiences into a dual-layer graph with entity decomposition mitigates noisy retrieval and incoherent composition by making applicability boundaries explicit and modeling inter-experience relations. Mechanism: The entity layer (`Gent`) breaks each experience into typed decision units and their internal flow. The experience layer (`Gexp`) connects experiences via edges weighted by entity/structure/synergy/task similarity. This allows retrieval to anchor on precise condition matches (boundary awareness) and traverse relationally meaningful connections (collaboration value), avoiding the "partial match" and "conflicting snippet" failures of flat memories. Core assumption: Clinical decision patterns are composable from reusable, structured knowledge units whose validity depends on specific context boundaries and whose utility emerges from meaningful combination with other patterns. Evidence: [abstract] explicitly states the dual-layer captures both decision structure and relational dependencies to address noisy retrieval; [section 3.3] defines `Gent` and `Gexp`; [section 4.2] details retrieval using entity recall and graph traversal; corpus support from "Graph-based Agent Memory" (FMR 0.578).

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Seed Recall with LLM-Guided Traversal
Claim: Hybrid seed recall (entity + embedding) followed by LLM-guided multi-seed graph traversal enables applicability-aware retrieval that respects both surface-level semantic matching and deeper reasoning-structure alignment. Mechanism: Initial seeds are selected via a union of sparse (BM25 on extracted entities) and dense (embedding similarity) retrieval, then reranked. Traversal proceeds simultaneously from all seeds, with the agent choosing actions (COLLECT, EXPLORE, BACKTRACK, STOP) at each step. Candidate experiences for EXPLORE are ranked by a composite score `(W_hj + Q_j)/2`, blending inter-experience association strength (`W`) with experience reliability (`Q`). This allows the system to start from contextually relevant points and expand along high-weight, high-quality relational paths while maintaining awareness of the original query context. Core assumption: A small set of high-recall seeds can anchor a search that, when combined with a structured, relevance-guided walk over a reliability-calibrated graph, will surface a coherent, applicable combination of experiences more effectively than a single-pass top-k retrieval. Evidence: [abstract] mentions the combined approach; [section 4.2] details the hybrid seed generation and multi-seed traversal algorithm; [section 6.1] ablation shows removing either entity_recall or embedding_recall or multi_seed_retrieval degrades performance; corpus: "SLEA-RL" (FMR 0.650) uses experience augmentation for RL, related but distinct.

### Mechanism 3: Online Calibration via Feedback-Driven Updates
Claim: Online calibration of node quality (`Q`) and edge weights (`W`) via outcome feedback, without altering experience content, progressively refines memory reliability and relational structure toward a performance-informed topology. Mechanism: After each task, scalar feedback `Δ_t` is distributed over the activated experience set (`A_t`) using rank-based credit assignment (`a_i`) and pairwise edge credit (`b_ij`). `Q_i` and the accumulated edge term `ϕ_ij` are updated with learning rates (`η_Q`, `η_W`), then `W_ij` is updated as `W_ij + ϕ_ij` and clipped. This preserves the initial similarity-based structure (`W^(0)`) while allowing co-activated experience pairs to be strengthened or weakened based on historical task success, thus refining the graph's connectivity. Core assumption: The utility of an experience (its `Q`) and the joint value of co-using two experiences (their `W`) can be inferred from task-level outcome signals, and a conservative, additive update rule that respects the initial structural prior will lead to stable improvement without catastrophic forgetting of valid relational knowledge. Evidence: [abstract] states the graph self-evolves by updating from outcome feedback; [section 4.3] provides the full update equations and rationale; [section 5.3] shows evolution effectiveness and polarization of `Q`/`W` distributions; corpus: "Live-Evo" (FMR 0.569) and "ReasoningBank" (FMR 0.522) address online memory evolution.

Understanding why a graph is used and what each layer represents is essential. - **Feedback-Driven Calibration (Non-Parametric Updates):** Why needed: GSEM evolves by updating `Q` and `W` based on outcomes, not by editing text or retraining. This conservative, performance-based structural refinement is key to its "self-evolving" claim. ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Experience Constructor -> Hybrid Retriever -> Graph Traversal Engine -> Evolution Manager

**Critical path:** Construction (build initial `G`) -> Retrieval (get `R_t` for task `x_t`) -> Generation (LLM produces answer using `R_t`) -> Evaluation (get `Δ_t`) -> Evolution (update `G`)

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Graph Construction Cost vs. Retrieval Precision: Parsing entities/role-edges and computing all-pairs `W` is O(n²). A pruning threshold `θ_edge` trades potential recall for scalability.
- LLM-guided Traversal vs. Deterministic Algorithm: Using an LLM as the traversal policy enables flexible, context-aware decisions but introduces non-determinism and prompt sensitivity.
- Conservative `W` Updates vs. Responsiveness: The additive `ϕ_ij` update preserves the initial similarity prior (`W^(0)`), preventing quick forgetting of valid relations but potentially slowing adaptation to new, strong negative feedback.

**Failure signatures:**
- Degraded retrieval: Frequent COLLECT of low-quality experiences (`Q` near 0) or traversal stuck in low-`W` loops.
- Stagnant evolution: Distributions of `Q` and `W` remain unimodal/unchanged after many updates, suggesting feedback signal `Δ_t` is too noisy or updates too small.
- Boundary violations re-emerge: Increased rate of "partial condition match" failures in case studies, indicating entity extraction or seed recall is failing to anchor on precise constraints.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Ablation of Hybrid Seeds: Run with only entity-based seeds, only embedding-based seeds, and combined. Expect combined best, especially on condition-intensive MedBullets.
2. Evolution Rate Sensitivity: Vary `η_Q` and `η_W` (e.g., 0.05, 0.1, 0.2); measure convergence speed and final accuracy on held-out set. Watch for overshooting (`Q`/`W` saturating) or no improvement.
3. Edge Threshold (`θ_edge`) Sweep: Test `θ_edge` values (0.2, 0.35, 0.5). Lower values create denser graph (more traversal options, higher latency); higher values sparser (faster, but risk missing connections). Measure accuracy vs. average traversal steps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Beyond accuracy, what additional feedback signals (e.g., safety metrics, expert evaluations) can improve GSEM's memory evolution and how do they compare?** Basis: Future work includes incorporating richer feedback signals; accuracy is coarse-grained and may not capture safety-critical nuances. Why unresolved: Current evolution relies on scalar accuracy, which may not reflect clinical reasoning quality or safety, risking unreliable calibration. Evidence needed: Controlled experiments comparing multiple feedback sources on memory drift, retrieval relevance, and accuracy across diverse clinical cases.
- **What mechanisms can provide robust attribution and safety monitoring for GSEM's self-evolving memory in clinical deployment to prevent harmful drifts?** Basis: Strengthening safety and attribution under clinical constraints is future work; concerns about unsafe drift with accuracy-based updates. Why unresolved: Online updates without interpretability or oversight risk propagating errors or unsafe patterns; GSEM lacks built-in safeguards. Evidence needed: Development and evaluation of attribution methods (provenance tracking, counterfactuals) and safety layers (veto, consistency checks) in simulated deployments, measuring reduction in harmful recommendations.
- **How does GSEM's performance and memory structure evolve in longer-horizon, interactive clinical workflows involving longitudinal patient follow-up and dynamic information updates?** Basis: Evaluating memory evolution in longer-horizon real-world workflows is future work; evaluation limited to public benchmarks. Why unresolved: Benchmarks use static cases; real workflows entail sequential decisions, information accrual, and outcome tracking over time, challenging graph stability and retrieval. Evidence needed: Longitudinal studies in simulations or pilot deployments, tracking graph changes, retrieval consistency, and accuracy across multi-step patient journeys.
- **What are the computational costs (time, memory) of GSEM's graph traversal as memory size increases, and what optimizations maintain efficiency without sacrificing retrieval quality?** Basis: Inferred from limitations noting latency and computation as graph grows. Why unresolved: Scalability for real-world clinical memory unknown; optimizations may trade off precision or coherence. Evidence needed: Empirical scaling experiments measuring latency, memory footprint, and accuracy across varying graph sizes, with tests of optimizations (neighbor sampling, hierarchical caching).
- **How sensitive is GSEM's retrieval effectiveness to variations in LLM prompting for traversal actions, and can learned retrieval policies reduce this non-determinism while maintaining interpretability?** Basis: Limitations state retriever sensitive to prompting and non-deterministic. Why unresolved: LLM-guided discrete actions depend on prompt design, leading to inconsistent retrieval paths that undermine reliability in safety-critical settings. Evidence needed: Sensitivity analysis across diverse prompts and LLM versions, measuring variance in retrieved sets and outcomes; exploration of learned policies (e.g., RL) with performance and interpretability comparisons.

## Limitations
- **Entity Extraction Brittleness:** GSEM's `Gent` relies on LLM parsing to decompose experiences into fixed roles. Clinical narratives often contain nuanced, implicit, or contradictory information; the assumption that all critical knowledge fits this schema without loss is a significant unvalidated risk.
- **Feedback Signal Fidelity:** The self-evolution mechanism updates `Q` and `W` using the coarse task accuracy signal `Δ_t`. Noisy or misaligned feedback could corrupt the graph's reliability metrics over time, especially for safety-critical nuances.
- **Scalability & Cost:** Building and updating the all-pairs experience layer (`Gexp`) is O(n²). While a pruning threshold is used, the long-term computational burden for a growing, real-world clinical memory is unknown and likely high.

## Confidence
- **Dual-Layer Graph Structure Effectiveness:** High (strongly supported by ablations showing degradation when either layer or integration is removed)
- **Hybrid Retrieval with LLM-Guided Traversal:** Medium-High (ablations support hybrid seeds and multi-seed design, but isolating the specific benefit of LLM-guided actions versus a deterministic heuristic is unclear)
- **Online Evolution via Feedback Calibration:** Medium (proof-of-concept via distribution shifts and improved performance, but confidence tempered by reliance on a single, potentially noisy scalar feedback and lack of long-term stability analysis)

## Next Checks
1. **Robustness of Entity Parsing:** Systematically inject clinical cases with subtle contraindications, temporal dependencies, or mixed polarity into the experience constructor. Measure fidelity of extracted `Gent` (e.g., via expert annotation) and downstream retrieval accuracy degradation compared to oracle parses.
2. **Feedback Signal Sensitivity Analysis:** Replace binary task accuracy `Δ_t` with a noisier or multi-dimensional signal (e.g., incorporating a safety violation penalty, or adding random wrong/right flips). Track stability and polarized calibration of `Q` and `W` distributions over 100+ update steps to identify susceptibility to reward hacking or corruption.
3. **Scalability Stress Test:** Construct `Gexp` with a large corpus (e.g., 5k+ experiences from MedAgentsBench trajectories) and measure: graph density after pruning at various `θ_edge`; average traversal steps and latency per query; accuracy decay when limiting traversal to a fixed budget (e.g., max 10 steps). This tests the practical boundary of the "coherent composition" promise.
