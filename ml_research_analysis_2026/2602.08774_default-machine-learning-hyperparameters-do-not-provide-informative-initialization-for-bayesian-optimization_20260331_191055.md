# Default Machine Learning Hyperparameters Do Not Provide Informative Initialization for Bayesian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08774
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08774
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Default hyperparameter initialization shows no statistically significant advantage over random initialization for Bayesian Optimization across multiple back-ends, models, and datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether default hyperparameters from machine learning libraries can serve as informative starting points for Bayesian Optimization (BO). Through extensive experiments using three BO back-ends (BoTorch, Optuna, Scikit-Optimize), three model families (Random Forests, SVMs, MLPs), and five datasets, the authors compare BO initialized with points drawn from truncated Gaussians centered at library defaults against standard uniform random initialization. The results show no statistically significant improvement in convergence speed or final predictive performance for default-informed initialization, with binomial p-values ranging from 0.141 to 0.908. A sensitivity analysis reveals that while tighter concentration around defaults yields transient early benefits, these vanish as optimization progresses, leaving final outcomes unchanged. The study concludes that library defaults do not encode useful directional information for BO.

## Method Summary
The experimental design is factorial, varying BO back-end, model family, dataset, and initialization strategy. Initialization strategies include uniform random sampling and truncated Gaussian sampling centered at library defaults with a concentration parameter λ. Each BO run uses Expected Improvement (EI) as the acquisition function with a Gaussian Process (GP) surrogate, evaluated via 3-fold cross-validation on subsampled datasets (≤1000 instances). The total budget includes initial points plus BO iterations (e.g., 3 initial + 30 iterations or 1 default + 15 iterations). Performance is measured by running-best trajectories, final best metrics, and convergence indices. Statistical significance is assessed via one-sided binomial tests comparing win counts between strategies across multiple repetitions.

## Key Results
- Default-centered initialization shows no statistically significant advantage over random initialization in final performance or convergence speed (binomial p-values: 0.141–0.908).
- Tighter concentration around defaults yields transient early improvements in mean performance, but this benefit disappears as BO progresses.
- Sensitivity analysis confirms that final outcomes remain unchanged regardless of the degree of default-centering (λ variation).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies three mechanisms explaining the lack of advantage for default-centered initialization:

1. Defaults are not optimized for any specific dataset, so they may lie far from the dataset-specific optimum. Library defaults are chosen for broad applicability, representing safe, moderate-performing regions. For a given dataset, the true optimum could be anywhere, so starting near defaults does not provide directional guidance toward the optimum.

2. Clustering initial evaluations near the default causes the GP surrogate to become overconfident locally and uncertain globally, potentially suppressing exploration of distant promising regions. The GP posterior variance is inversely related to the density of observed points. A cluster near the default yields low variance in that region and high variance elsewhere. Acquisition functions like Expected Improvement may then favor exploitation near the cluster and under-explore far regions where the optimum may lie.

3. Random initialization provides broader coverage of the search space, leading to a more globally informative surrogate early on, which compensates for any initial deficit and yields convergence comparable to default-centered initialization. Uniformly spread initial points allow the GP to model the objective surface more accurately across the entire domain from the start, guiding the acquisition function to promising regions more efficiently after a few iterations.

Why needed: EI's behavior depends on the GP's mean and variance, which are influenced by initialization. ## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Do meta-learned priors from historical optimization traces provide a statistically significant advantage over default-centered initialization for Bayesian optimization? Basis: Explicitly proposed in Section 7. Why unresolved: Only static defaults evaluated; not compared to data-driven priors. Evidence needed: Large-scale empirical study comparing meta-learned initialization against random and default-centered baselines using same binomial testing framework.
- **Question:** Does the non-informativeness of default hyperparameter initialization persist when applying Bayesian optimization to tune deep learning models (e.g., large pre-trained transformers)? Basis: Identified as key extension. Why unresolved: Limited to classical ML models; high-dimensional, high-cost deep learning may alter value. Evidence needed: Replicate protocol on deep learning benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, BERT fine-tuning) with same metrics and tests.
- **Question:** How does the interaction between initialization strategy (default vs. random) and acquisition function (e.g., Knowledge Gradient, Thompson Sampling) affect Bayesian optimization performance? Basis: Suggested as future work. Why unresolved: Only EI used; other acquisition functions may have different sensitivities. Evidence needed: Controlled experiment varying both initialization and acquisition function, analyze via binomial test.
- **Question:** Is the transient early benefit of default-centered initialization replicable across other high-sensitivity datasets beyond Letter? Basis: Sensitivity analysis only on Letter dataset. Why unresolved: Need to verify across diverse loss landscapes. Evidence needed: Re-run λ-sensitivity sweep on other high-spread datasets (BikeSharing, Higgs) and check consistency.
- **Question:** Can multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization methods reduce tuning cost more effectively than any initialization strategy, including default-centered? Basis: Proposed as alternative direction. Why unresolved: Paper compares single-fidelity strategies; multi-fidelity changes cost structure. Evidence needed: Comparative study holding total budget constant but allocating to cheaper evaluations, compare final performance and wall-clock time.

## Limitations
- GP kernel hyperparameter optimization details (length-scale, variance) and settings are not described.
- Handling of integer and categorical hyperparameters in truncated Gaussian sampling (e.g., rounding) is unspecified.
- Number of BO repetitions per condition is not stated, hindering reproducibility.
- Evaluation is limited to 3-fold cross-validation, subsampled data (≤1000 instances), and Expected Improvement acquisition, limiting external validity.

## Confidence
- Primary claim (defaults provide no BO advantage): High
- Mechanism 1 (defaults not dataset-optimized): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (clustering causes GP overconfidence): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (random gives broader coverage): High
