# Synthetic Data Augmentation for Medical Audio Classification: A Preliminary Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02955
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02955
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** A CNN baseline on respiratory sounds achieved macro F1 0.645; ensemble of models trained with synthetic augmentation (VAE, GAN, diffusion) yielded a modest improvement to 0.664 F1, while individual synthetic strategies were neutral or degraded performance.

## Executive Summary
This study investigated whether synthetic data generated by VAEs, GANs, and diffusion models could improve classification of COVID-19 cough sounds in a moderately imbalanced dataset. Training a CNN from scratch on real data augmented with synthetic minority samples did not yield consistent benefits. Individual augmentation strategies either had no effect (VAE, diffusion) or significantly harmed performance (GAN). Only an ensemble combining predictions from models trained with different synthetic data showed a modest gain. The findings suggest that direct augmentation with current generative models is unreliable for this medical audio task, and that benefits may depend on factors like data characteristics, model compatibility, and training regime.

## Method Summary
The authors used the Project Coswara dataset, comprising cough recordings labeled as healthy or COVID-19 infected. After resampling to 16 kHz mono and cropping to 3 seconds, audio was converted to mel-spectrograms (128 bins, 0–8 kHz) and normalized. A CNN with four convolutional blocks was trained from scratch on the real training set (70% split, stratified). To address the 3.4:1 class imbalance, three generative models (VAE, WGAN-GP, diffusion U-Net) were trained exclusively on real minority-class spectrograms. Each generated 558 synthetic samples to augment the minority class by 50%. The CNN was then retrained from scratch on each augmented set. Finally, an ensemble was formed by averaging the predicted probabilities of the baseline model and the three augmented models. Performance was evaluated on a held-out real test set using macro F1-score and AUROC.

## Key Results
- Baseline CNN macro F1-score: **0.645** (AUROC 0.745).
- Individual synthetic augmentation: neutral for VAE (0.646) and diffusion (0.644); degraded for GAN (0.609).
- **Ensemble** of all four models achieved best performance: macro F1 **0.664**, AUROC **0.761**.
- AUROC results followed the same pattern, with the ensemble outperforming all individual models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Synthetic Data Quality Mismatch Degrades Individual Model Performance
**Claim:** Incorporating synthetic audio samples generated by VAEs, GANs, or diffusion models into the training set does not improve and can harm the performance of a standard CNN classifier on a real-world test set.  
**Mechanism:** The generated spectrograms fail to capture subtle, discriminative pathological features present in real minority-class audio (COVID-19 coughs). Instead, they may replicate dominant noise patterns or average characteristics from the limited, noisy source data. The classifier, trained on this synthetic-real mix, learns representations that do not transfer effectively to the distribution of real test samples.  
**Core assumption:** Downstream classification performance is highly sensitive to distributional fidelity; synthetic data must preserve the signal of interest (pathological acoustics) and not just the broad data distribution.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "...performance gains were not observed, with several configurations demonstrating neutral or degraded classification performance." (GAN: F1 0.609 vs baseline 0.645).  
- [section 2.4] Generative models were trained exclusively on the minority class (1,116 samples), a small and noisy dataset.  
**Break condition:** If synthetic samples achieve high perceptual or feature-level fidelity quantified on a validation set of real minority-class samples, individual model performance may improve. The break condition is not met here.

### Mechanism 2: Ensemble Mitigates Individual Degradation via Error Diversity
**Claim:** An ensemble of models, each trained with a different synthetic augmentation strategy, yields a modest improvement over the baseline by combining partially uncorrelated error patterns.  
**Mechanism:** While each individual augmented model underperforms (or performs neutrally) relative to the baseline, their errors are not identical. The ensemble averages these predictions, effectively canceling out some model-specific mistakes rooted in the unique distortions or omissions of each generative model's output.  
**Core assumption:** The failure modes of models trained with different synthetic data distributions are sufficiently diverse to be complementary when averaged.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "Only an ensemble of augmented models yielded a modest improvement... ensemble also performed best (AUROC 0.761)."  
- [section 4] "...different augmentation strategies may induce distinct and partially uncorrelated error patterns, which can be exploited through ensembling."  
**Break condition:** If all augmentation strategies induce highly correlated errors (e.g., all fail on the same pathological feature), the ensemble gain would vanish.

### Mechanism 3: Direct Augmentation Benefit is Conditional on Pretraining/Architecture Compatibility
**Claim:** The lack of consistent improvement suggests synthetic data augmentation's value is not general but depends on specific interactions between the generative model, the classifier's training regime (e.g., from scratch vs. pretrained), and the task's data characteristics.  
**Mechanism:** Training a CNN from random initialization on synthetic-real mixes may force it to learn from noisy or misaligned features early in training, creating a detrimental initialization. A pretrained backbone (on a large, diverse audio corpus) might use synthetic data more effectively for fine-tuning, resisting distribution shifts.  
**Core assumption:** Learned representations from synthetic data are only beneficial if the classifier has prior knowledge to anchor them or if the augmentation strategy is carefully aligned with the model's inductive biases.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "...synthetic augmentation may not consistently enhance performance when applied to a standard CNN classifier."  
- [section 2.3 & 2.5] "The classifiers were trained from scratch to isolate the effect of synthetic data... Ensemble approach... required no additional training."  
- [section 4] "Future work should focus on delineating task-specific data characteristics, model–augmentation compatibility..."  
**Break condition:** Testing the same synthetic data with a CNN pretrained on a large general audio dataset (e.g., AudioSet) might show consistent gains, indicating the break condition is the absence of robust pretraining.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Class Imbalance & Minority-Class Synthesis
**Why needed here:** The core motivation for synthetic augmentation is the 3.4:1 imbalance (73% healthy, 27% COVID+). Understanding how imbalance affects loss functions (e.g., cross-entropy bias) and metrics (macro F1) is prerequisite to judging if augmentation resolved it.  
**Quick check question:** Why might generating more minority samples not fix a model's imbalance problem if the generated samples are not truly representative of the minority class's discriminative features?

### Concept: Generative Model Architectures (VAE, GAN, Diffusion)
**Why needed here:** Each model has a distinct training objective (reconstruction+KL, adversarial game, denoising) that influences sample diversity and fidelity. This underpins why their augmentation effects differed (GAN degraded most).  
**Quick check question:** How does the mode collapse tendency of a GAN versus the smoothed latent space of a VAE likely affect the diversity of generated pathological cough sounds?

### Concept: Spectrogram-Based Audio Classification
**Why needed here:** Raw waveforms are converted to mel-spectrograms (128 bins, 0-8kHz), which are the actual input to both the CNN classifiers and the generative models. This preprocessing step defines the feature space all models operate in.  
**Quick check question:** If the STFT parameters (hop length, window) are not optimal for capturing the transient features of coughs, how would that affect both the baseline CNN's performance and the quality of synthetic spectrograms?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Data Pipeline → Generative Models (train only) → Augmentation Module → Classifier → Ensemble Module → Evaluation

**Critical path:** Dataset loading → Preprocessing → (A) Train Generator on minority class → (B) Generate synthetic samples → (C) Augment training set → (D) Train CNN on augmented set → (E) Evaluate on real test set. Paths (A-D) are repeated for each generator. Ensemble requires paths (A-E) run four times in parallel.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Computational Cost vs. Gain:* Diffusion models required 400 epochs and 50-step sampling (slowest), yet yielded nearly neutral results (F1 0.644). GAN was fastest (300 epochs) but most harmful. Ensemble gave the only gain (+0.019 F1) but quadrupled training/inference cost.  
- *Simplicity vs. Control:* The ensemble uses equal-weight averaging without meta-learning. A weighted ensemble or stacking might extract more signal but adds complexity.  
- *Augmentation Ratio:* Fixed at 50% minority oversampling. Higher ratios might introduce more noise; lower might be ineffective. This hyperparameter is unexplored.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **GAN model:** Significant drop in macro F1 (0.609) and AUROC (0.726) vs. baseline. Likely indicates severe mode collapse or artifact generation in cough spectrograms.  
- **Individual VAE/Diffusion:** ~0.0 change in F1. Signals that synthetic data is "neutral" – neither harmful nor beneficial, suggesting distributional proximity to real data but lack of added *discriminative* value.  
- **Baseline vs. Ensemble:** Small but consistent gain across metrics (F1 +0.019, AUROC +0.016). The ensemble's value is in error correction, not stronger individual learners.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Establish Baseline:** Train the CNN on the *real-only* training set and evaluate on the real test set. Record macro F1 and AUROC.  
2. **Test Individual Generators:** For each generator (VAE, GAN, Diffusion): train on the augmented set (real + 50% synthetic minority), save the best model by validation macro F1, and evaluate on the same real test set. Compare each to baseline.  
3. **Form and Evaluate Ensemble:** Load all four trained models (baseline + 3 augmented). Average their predicted COVID+ probabilities for each test sample, then threshold to assign a label. Evaluate ensemble performance. If all individual models degraded, this tests the hypothesis of uncorrelated errors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
What specific data characteristics (e.g., class imbalance ratio, signal-to-noise ratio, intra-class variability) most strongly influence the effectiveness of synthetic augmentation for medical audio classification tasks?  
*Basis in paper:* The abstract states: "Future work should focus on delineating task-specific data characteristics... necessary for synthetic augmentation to be effective."  
*Why unresolved:* The study observed inconsistent performance gains across augmentation methods without isolating which data properties drove these outcomes.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments varying data characteristics (e.g., imbalance levels, noise injection) across multiple medical audio datasets, measuring augmentation impact on downstream performance.

### Open Question 2
How does the compatibility between synthetic augmentation techniques (VAEs, GANs, diffusion models) and classifier architectures (e.g., CNNs, transformers) affect classification accuracy and robustness?  
*Basis in paper:* The abstract calls for investigating "model-augmentation compatibility" as a key factor for effectiveness.  
*Why unresolved:* The study used only a single CNN architecture; interactions with other models or pretraining schemes remain unexplored.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative studies training diverse classifiers (e.g., transformers, residual networks) with each augmentation method, evaluating performance consistency and error patterns.

### Open Question 3
What standardized evaluation frameworks (including metrics, cross-validation protocols, and statistical testing) are required to reliably measure synthetic augmentation benefits in medical audio?  
*Basis in paper:* The abstract emphasizes the need for "evaluation frameworks necessary for synthetic augmentation to be effective."  
*Why unresolved:* The study relied on single-run macro F1 and AUROC without repeated trials or inferential statistics, limiting conclusions about augmentation reliability.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Establishment of benchmark protocols with multiple random seeds, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing across datasets to assess augmentation significance.

### Open Question 4
Can synthetic medical audio data be more effectively leveraged in indirect roles—such as pretraining, self-supervised learning, or adversarial robustness enhancement—rather than direct training set augmentation?  
*Basis in paper:* The discussion suggests exploring "alternative roles for synthetic data beyond direct training set augmentation" and examining interactions with adversarial robustness.  
*Why unresolved:* The study only tested direct augmentation; other paradigms might mitigate distributional mismatch or improve feature learning.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation studies comparing direct augmentation with pretraining on synthetic data, contrastive learning setups, and robustness tests under noisy or adversarial inputs.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Random seeds for data splits, model initialization, and training are unreported; key generative model hyperparameters (e.g., WGAN-GP architecture, diffusion steps) are incompletely specified. The minority-class training set (1,116 samples) is small and noisy, limiting generative model fidelity.
- **Generalizability:** All classifiers trained from scratch on a single dataset (Coswara); findings may not transfer to pretrained models or other medical audio tasks. The fixed 50% augmentation ratio is unoptimized.
- **Corpus context:** Neighboring papers report mixed synthetic augmentation results in medical audio/image domains, suggesting this challenge is not unique but still poorly understood.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Synthetic quality mismatch degrades individual models): High** – Strong evidence: GAN significantly harms performance; VAE/diffusion are neutral. Corroborated by domain literature on generative model limitations in small-data regimes.
- **Mechanism 2 (Ensemble mitigates via error diversity): Medium** – Modest ensemble gain is observed, but error correlation was not measured; the assumed diversity is plausible but unverified.
- **Mechanism 3 (Compatibility with pretraining/architecture): Low** – Speculative; no experiments with pretrained backbones were conducted to test this break condition.

## Next Checks
1. **Reproducibility audit:** Re-run all experiments with fixed random seeds and full hyperparameter disclosure (generator architectures, CNN learning rate schedule). Report variance across seeds.
2. **Error correlation analysis:** Compute pairwise prediction probability correlations on the validation set among the four models (baseline + three augmented). Low correlation would support Mechanism 2; high correlation would refute it.
3. **Pretrained backbone test:** Replace the from-scratch CNN with a standard audio pretrained model (e.g., AudioSet-pretrained CNN). Retest the same augmentation strategies. If gains emerge, it validates Mechanism 3's break condition.
