# Breaking the Assistant Mold: Modeling Behavioral Variation in LLM Based Procedural Character Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.03396
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03396
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Primary result**: PersonaWeaver disentangles world-building from behavioral-building using external banks (e.g., Moral Foundations Theory), producing LLM-simulated characters with significantly more diverse moral stances, reaction types (including refusals/deflections), and second-order stylistic variation compared to prior methods.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) used for procedural character generation exhibit alignment-induced biases: they create characters that uniformly agree on normative statements and invariably comply with questions. This homogenization stems from conflation of a character's world role (e.g., occupation) with stereotypical behaviors. The paper introduces PersonaWeaver, a framework that systematically separates the generation of world-building attributes (non-behavioral roles, demographics) from behavioral attributes (moral positions, interactional styles). By sampling these independently from predefined banks and then randomly mixing them, PersonaWeaver forces unlikely pairings that break stereotypes. Evaluations across 10 settings with three frontier models (GPT-4o, LLaMA 4, Qwen 3) show that PersonaWeaver yields a more balanced distribution of moral positions, dramatically increases reaction diversity (including refusals and deflections), and induces secondary stylistic variation (e.g., answer length, sentiment) compared to prior work like WorldWeaver and PersonaHub.

## Method Summary
PersonaWeaver is a multi-stage framework. First, a **World-Building Module** generates a setting-specific bank of non-behavioral attributes (axes like occupation, affiliation) with explicit instructions to avoid behavioral descriptors. Separately, a **Behavioral Module** contains two fixed-size banks: a **Moral Bank** with 8 positions inspired by Moral Foundations Theory and an **Interactional Bank** with 8 reaction categories (e.g., refusal, deflection). For each character, the **Sample & Mix** module randomly selects one moral position, one interactional category, and a set of world attributes. These are combined into a character profile; an LLM then flags implausible pairings (e.g., "medieval knight software engineer") and minimally revises them for coherence without altering the assigned behavioral traits. The final profile conditions the LLM simulator when generating responses to evaluation probes. This disentanglement and forced mixing prevent the LLM from defaulting to its alignment-trained "helpful, agreeable assistant" persona.

## Key Results
- Generated characters exhibit a significantly more balanced and entropic distribution of moral positions on Social Chemistry probes, overcoming the uniform agreement bias.
- Conversational responses to ConvAI2 prompts show a much more diverse reaction profile, with substantial increases in refusals, deflections, and other non-compliant behaviors compared to baselines.
- Characters demonstrate second-order stylistic variation (e.g., variance in response length, punctuation, filler words, sentiment) consistent with their assigned behavioral traits.
- These effects replicate across three different frontier models (GPT-4o, LLaMA 4, Qwen 3) and ten diverse settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Disentanglement of World-Building and Behavioral Attributes
**Claim**: Separating the generation of world-building attributes (roles, demographics) from behavioral attributes (moral stances, interactional styles) prevents behavioral leakage into world attributes and enables explicit control over behavioral variation.
**Mechanism**: The framework first generates a set of world-building axes tailored to the setting, with explicit instructions to exclude behavioral traits. These axes are expanded into a bank of non-behavioral attributes. Separately, behavioral attributes are sampled from external banks (moral positions, reaction categories). The two sets are then randomly combined in a Sample & Mix step, producing character profiles that link a world role with an independent behavioral specification.
**Core assumption**: LLMs tend to conflate world roles with stereotypical behaviors. By forcing independent sampling, the framework breaks these associations and allows the LLM to assign diverse behaviors to any role.
**Break condition**: If world-building generation inadvertently includes behavioral cues or the mixing step is not fully random, stereotypical associations may persist.

### Mechanism 2: External Behavioral Banks Override Default Biases
**Claim**: Providing a predefined set of moral positions and interactional reaction categories forces the LLM to adopt diverse stances, overcoming its default alignment-induced biases toward universal agreement and compliance.
**Mechanism**: Instead of relying on the LLM to spontaneously generate varied behaviors (which collapses to agreeable/compliant), PersonaWeaver attaches to each character a specific moral position (from 8 options) and an interactional reaction category (from 8 options). The LLM then generates character profiles and later responses conditioned on these specifications.
**Core assumption**: LLMs can accurately follow instructions to embody a given behavioral specification, and the specified spectrum is sufficiently broad to produce measurable diversity.
**Break condition**: If the LLM fails to adhere to the assigned behavioral bank and defaults to its trained biases, the mitigation fails.

### Mechanism 3: Random Mixing and Plausibility Filtering Prevent Archetype Collapse
**Claim**: Randomly combining world and behavioral attributes and then minimally revising implausible pairings creates unlikely character combinations that break stereotypes and prevent collapse into a narrow set of archetypes.
**Mechanism**: For each character, PersonaWeaver randomly samples world-building and behavioral attributes independently. The combination may be implausible. An LLM then flags such cases and revises them minimally for coherence, but without altering the assigned behavioral traits. This yields characters that are both diverse and plausibly coherent.
**Core assumption**: LLMs can maintain specified behavioral traits even when the world role is unusual, and minimal revision preserves the intended variation while ensuring internal consistency.
**Break condition**: If the revision step is too aggressive and changes behavioral attributes to match world stereotypes, the intended diversity is lost.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  World-Building Module -> (World Attribute Bank) & Behavioral Module -> (Moral Bank, Interactional Bank) -> Sample & Mix Module -> (Character Profile) -> LLM Simulator -> (Responses) -> Evaluation Suite
- **Critical path**:
  1. Input setting description.
  2. Run World-Building Module to produce world attribute bank.
  3. For each character: Sample moral position and interactional reaction from Behavioral Module; sample world attributes from world bank; combine via Sample & Mix; optionally revise implausible pairs.
  4. Output character profile (text).
  5. For evaluation: Use profile to condition LLM on probes; collect responses; analyze distributions.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Separation vs. coherence: Disentanglement increases diversity but may produce incoherent characters; minimal revision trades off diversity for plausibility.
  - Bank size vs. control: Fixed-size banks (8x8) ensure coverage and evaluation simplicity but may not capture all possible behaviors; larger banks risk LLM ignoring some categories.
  - Random mixing vs. realism: Random mixing creates unrealistic pairings that test LLM compliance but may require revision; overly aggressive revision could homogenize outputs.
  - External banks vs. open-endedness: Banks provide systematic variation but limit discovery of novel behaviors not in the banks.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Moral distribution remains peaked at agreement → LLM ignoring moral bank.
  - Reaction distribution remains dominated by compliance → Interactional bank not effective.
  - High rate of revisions changing behavioral attributes → Revision step too aggressive.
  - Low variance in stylistic features → Behavioral variation not inducing second-order effects.
  - Characters repeatedly share the same world-behavior combinations → Random mixing not sufficiently diverse or bank sampling not independent.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. *Moral stance diversity*: Generate 1,000 characters using PersonaWeaver and prior methods; probe with 10 Social Chemistry statements; compare entropy of agreement/disagreement distributions.
  2. *Reaction diversity*: For same characters, present ConvAI2 questions; classify responses into 8 reaction categories; compare category proportions and refusal/deflection rates.
  3. *Cross-model replication*: Repeat experiments 1 and 2 on LLaMA 4 and Qwen 3 to verify that bias mitigation generalizes beyond GPT-4o.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the PersonaWeaver framework be extended to model other fundamental behavioral axes beyond moral stances and interactional styles, such as emotional regulation, personality traits (e.g., Big Five), or cognitive styles?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Limitations section states: "Extending this framework to other behavioral axes—such as emotional regulation—remains an open direction and can be easily investigated with our framework."
- Why unresolved: The paper successfully demonstrates disentanglement for two behavioral banks but does not implement or evaluate additional banks. The feasibility, design, and impact of new behavioral dimensions on diversity and character believability are unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: A follow-up study implementing and evaluating at least one new behavioral bank (e.g., emotional volatility) across the same experimental settings, measuring its effect on reaction and moral diversity, as well as second-order stylistic variation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent do the observed moral and interactional distributions from PersonaWeaver reflect genuine, context-sensitive moral reasoning versus superficial adherence to a sampled bank label?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Limitations section notes: "our moral evaluation relies on multiple-choice agreement tasks... Such format may underrepresent nuanced or context-dependent reasoning that could emerge in richer, free-form moral dialogue."
- Why unresolved: The evaluation uses controlled, low-context prompts (Social Chemistry statements). This format may not capture how characters *reason* about complex, scenario-based moral dilemmas where multiple foundations conflict, potentially conflating label adherence with authentic disposition.
- What evidence would resolve it: An evaluation where characters are presented with detailed hypothetical moral dilemmas (e.g., trolley problem variants) and their open-ended justifications are analyzed for consistency with their assigned moral position, depth of reasoning, and contextual flexibility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How reliable is the automatic LLM-based classifier used to categorize conversational reactions (refusal, deflection, compliance, etc.) in ConvAI2 prompts, and could classifier bias be masking or inflating the true diversity of behaviors?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The methodology (Sec 2.1, Appendix B) describes using "an auxiliary LLM" to classify open-ended replies. The paper does not report inter-annotator agreement, error analysis, or potential biases of this classifier.
- Why unresolved: The core finding of increased reaction diversity depends on this automated classification. An unreliable or biased classifier could systematically over-estimate rare behaviors for PersonaWeaver characters or under-estimate them for baselines, threatening the validity of the key bias mitigation claim.
- What evidence would resolve it: A human evaluation study where a subset of generated responses is independently labeled by multiple human judges, comparing agreement with the auxiliary LLM classifier and recalculating the reaction distribution metrics based on human labels.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the causal role of the "Sample and Mix" module's "implausible pairings" revision step? Does it preserve behavioral diversity or inadvertently reintroduce alignment-induced uniformity by smoothing out edges?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The "Sample and Mix" step includes a mechanism to "flag implausible pairings and minimally revise them for coherence." The paper does not analyze the frequency of revisions, the nature of the revisions, or whether this step disproportionately removes or altered characters with extreme behavioral traits.
- Why unresolved: This step is a potential point where the system's attempt to maintain world-coherence could conflict with its goal of behavioral diversity. If revisions systematically "sanitize" implausible but behaviorally interesting combinations, the reported diversity gains might be smaller than claimed.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation experiment comparing diversity metrics between PersonaWeaver with and without the revision step, along with a qualitative analysis of what kinds of behavioral traits are most frequently revised away.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Are the observed second-order stylistic variations (filler words, punctuation, length, sentiment) causally induced by the specified behavioral traits, or are they merely correlated because both stem from underlying model prompts that vary in tone and instruction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 2.3.1 claims these stylistic effects are "emergent." However, the generation prompts for different behavioral banks likely vary in phrasing, which could directly instruct stylistic changes rather than the character's latent "behavior" causing them.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not perform a controlled experiment to isolate the effect of the behavioral trait label from the specific wording of the prompt used to instill it. The stylistic variation might be a direct consequence of prompt engineering rather than an emergent property of a consistent character persona.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled prompt experiment where the *exact same* character description is paired with different behavioral bank labels, but the *instructional phrasing* used to apply the label is held constant. Measuring stylistic variation across labels under fixed phrasing would test if the behavior label itself induces the style.

## Limitations
- Automatic evaluation (probe classification) may not capture nuanced behavioral diversity; human validation is needed.
- Fixed-size external banks (8 moral positions, 8 reaction categories) limit the spectrum of variation and may miss relevant behaviors.
- The minimal revision step’s impact on behavioral attributes is unquantified; it could inadvertently reintroduce stereotypical associations.

## Confidence
- Disentanglement of world-building and behavioral attributes enables fine-grained control: **High** (supported by experimental comparisons, but direct verification of zero behavioral leakage in world-building is absent).
- External behavioral banks override alignment-induced biases: **High** (consistent distributional shifts across models, though classification noise is a minor concern).
- Random mixing prevents archetype collapse and induces second-order variation: **Medium** (diversity metrics support the claim, but human evaluation and revision analysis would strengthen confidence).
