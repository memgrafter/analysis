# The Geometric Price of Discrete Logic: Context-driven Manifold Dynamics of Number Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23577
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23577
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Logical reasoning requires non‑isometric manifold deformation; ablating the specific divergence component collapses parity accuracy from 100% to 38.57%.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) excel at continuous semantic processing but must impose discrete logical boundaries for tasks like parity classification. This paper investigates the geometric mechanism by which task context deforms the model’s internal manifold to satisfy such boundaries without collapsing the underlying semantic structure. By analyzing residual stream activations in Qwen2.5 on numeric reasoning tasks, the authors identify a dual‑modulation mechanism: class‑agnostic topological preservation (maintaining base semantics) and class‑specific algebraic divergence (creating logical separation). Key findings show that removing the divergence component through targeted ablation destroys parity performance, and that social pressure prompts prevent divergence formation, linking geometric entanglement to sycophancy and hallucinations. The study reveals a three‑phase layer‑wise dynamic where cross‑class divergence plunges negative in deeper layers, forming the logical boundary.

## Method Summary
The study uses integers 1–200 represented as Arabic numerals and English words. For each task (baseline identity, magnitude, parity, primality, and social pressure), prompts are generated and model forward passes are run. Only samples where the model correctly classifies the number are retained. Activations from the residual stream just before RMSNorm at a target layer (likely layer 24) are extracted. For each retained sample, the baseline activation (from the identity prompt) is subtracted from the task activation to obtain a difference vector Δ. Centering across task samples yields a class‑specific interference vector Δ_specific. Using Gram‑Schmidt orthogonalization, Δ_specific is decomposed relative to the normalized baseline direction ξ̂ to isolate a pure innovation direction ū (orthogonal to ξ̂). Metrics are computed: Specific Divergence Usim(i,j)=⟨û_i, û_j⟩ and Topological Preservation Cij(i,j)=⟨ξ̂_i, û_j⟩. For ablation, the class‑specific mean Δ_specific,label is averaged, its orthogonal component v⊥ = Δ_specific,label – ⟨Δ_specific,label, ξ̂⟩ξ̂ is computed, and a pre‑hook subtracts v⊥ from the residual at the target layer before RMSNorm. Model performance (e.g., parity accuracy) is then re‑evaluated, and geometric metrics recomputed to confirm the effect.

## Key Results
- **Ablation causality**: Subtracting the orthogonal component of the class‑specific interference vector causes parity classification accuracy to drop from 100% to 38.57%, establishing a strict causal link between non‑isometric topological deformation and logical function.
- **Social pressure failure**: Under social pressure (L5), models fail to generate sufficient divergence; cross‑class Usim remains positive (mean ≈ +0.319), and phase portraits show entangled clusters, providing a geometric explanation for sycophancy and hallucination.
- **Three‑phase dynamics**: Logical tasks (L2–L4) exhibit a distinct layer‑wise evolution of Usim: shallow layers show no differentiation; deep layers plunge into a negative basin where logical boundaries are formed; output layers show a rebound. The basin depth correlates with task logic complexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper explains logical boundary formation via a dual‑modulation mechanism on the semantic manifold:

1. **Dual‑modulation for boundary formation**: Task context induces a non‑isometric deformation decomposed by Gram‑Schmidt into (a) class‑agnostic topological preservation (Cij > 0) and (b) class‑specific algebraic divergence (Usim < 0 for cross‑class pairs). Negative cross‑class Usim signals adversarial innovation directions that tear the manifold, creating discrete boundaries. Evidence: Parity/primality tasks show mean cross‑class Usim negative (e.g., –0.2351). Ablating the specific divergence component (removing v⊥) collapses accuracy to chance (38.57%), proving causality. Break condition: if cross‑class Usim fails to become negative, boundaries do not form (as in the sycophancy task L5).

2. **Social pressure as interference suppressor**: Social directives (L5) inhibit the generation of sufficient specific divergence. Interference vectors remain positively synergistic (Usim > 0) across classes, failing to cross the Usim = 0 divergence threshold. Consequently, same‑class and cross‑class innovation directions remain entangled within the 0 < y < x zone of the phase portrait, leading to mixed representations that manifest as sycophancy or hallucination. Break condition: absence of social pressure or higher logical demand should yield negative cross‑class Usim.

3. **Three‑phase layer‑wise dynamic**: Logical computation drives a consistent Usim evolution across layers:
   - Phase 1 (shallow): no class differentiation; semantic enrichment increases overall similarity.
   - Phase 2 (deep): same‑class Usim rises while cross‑class Usim plunges into a basin (minimum), where logical boundaries are forged.
   - Phase 3 (output): cross‑class Usim rebounds toward zero; same‑class plateaus.
   The basin depth correlates with task logic structure. Evidence: Figure 3 shows a clear basin for L2–L4 (deepest around layer 24) but not for L5.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gram‑Schmidt Orthogonalization**
  - **Why needed:** Separates pure innovation directions (ū) from interference vectors by removing components collinear with the original concept directions (ξ̂). This decomposition yields two interpretable metrics: Cij (topological preservation) and Usim (specific divergence).
  - **Quick check:** ⟨ξ̂, ū⟩ ≈ 10⁻⁷ empirically confirms successful orthogonalization; any larger residual suggests numerical instability or incomplete decomposition.

- **Concept: Residual Stream Modulation**
  - **Why needed:** Contextual information is injected into the residual stream before layer normalization. Activations at this point reflect the integrated state after attention and MLP layers, making them suitable to capture how task context deforms concept representations.
  - **Quick check:** Residual vectors are extracted pre‑RMSNorm; the exact layer used is likely the penultimate layer (e.g., layer 24 in Qwen2.5‑7B) based on observed basin depth.

- **Concept: Non‑Isometric Manifold Deformation**
  - **Why needed:** The core theoretical claim: logical boundaries require directional tearing (non‑isometric change, Usim < 0) rather than mere translation (isometric, Usim ≈ Sbase). This “geometric price” explains why continuous optimizers like gradient descent must induce specific angular shifts.
  - **Quick check:** A negative Usim for cross‑class pairs indicates that their innovation directions point in partially opposite directions (angle > 90°), signifying adversarial deformation needed for separation.

## Architecture Onboarding
The analysis pipeline integrates model hooks and geometric decomposition:

Component map: Integer data (1–200 in Arabic/English) → Prompt generation for L1–L5 → Model forward pass (baseline L1 and task L2–L5) → Cache residual activations (pre‑RMSNorm, target layer) → Compute Δ = x_task – x_baseline → Center to obtain Δ_specific → Gram‑Schmidt against baseline ξ̂ to obtain innovation directions ū → Compute Usim and Cij per label mask → (Optional) Ablation: compute class‑specific v⊥ and subtract via pre‑hook → Re‑run forward to evaluate accuracy and updated metrics.

Critical path:
1. Run baseline and task forward passes for all retained samples; store residual vectors x at the chosen layer.
2. For each sample, compute Δ_specific = (x_task – x_baseline) – mean(x_task – x_baseline) across task samples.
3. Normalize baseline direction: ξ̂ = x_baseline / ∥x_baseline∥. Orthogonalize Δ_specific: p = ⟨Δ_specific, ξ̂⟩; ū = (Δ_specific – p·ξ̂) / ∥Δ_specific – p·ξ̂∥. Verify ⟨ξ̂, ū⟩ ≈ 0.
4. For all pairs (i,j) within same or cross classes, compute Usim(i,j)=⟨û_i, û_j⟩ and Cij(i,j)=⟨ξ̂_i, û_j⟩; aggregate by group and optionally correlate with Sbase(i,j)=⟨ξ̂_i, ξ̂_j⟩.
5. To ablate, compute mean Δ_specific,label per class; extract v⊥ = Δ_specific,label – ⟨Δ_specific,label, ξ̂⟩ξ̂; install a forward pre‑hook on the target layer to subtract v⊥ from the residual input; re‑run task (e.g., L3 parity) and measure accuracy; recompute geometric metrics to confirm cross‑class Usim shifts into positive region.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Granularity vs abstraction:** Using crisp numeric tasks provides clear boundaries but may not capture the fuzzy, multi‑dimensional boundaries of natural language reasoning.
- **Global vs local:** Δ_specific is a global class vector; finer‑grained decompositions (e.g., via sparse autoencoders) could reveal sub‑structures within classes.
- **Intervention specificity:** Algebraic subtraction of v⊥ is precise but may introduce unobserved nonlinear side‑effects in redundant subspaces, potentially affecting downstream layers.

Failure signatures:
- **Logical task failure:** Cross‑class Usim not significantly negative; ablation does not reduce accuracy.
- **Social pressure task:** Cross‑class Usim positive; same‑class and cross‑class trajectories entangled in phase portrait (0 < y < x zone).
- **Missing phase:** No distinct Usim basin appears in layer‑wise plot for a supposed logical task.

First 3 experiments:
1. Validate orthogonalization: compute ⟨ξ̂, ū⟩ across samples; expect ≈10⁻⁷. Also compute cosine similarity between Δ_specific vectors for Arabic vs English representations; expect ≥ 0.8.
2. Task‑level bifurcation: scatter plot Usim vs Sbase for L2–L5, color by same/cross class; verify negative Usim for cross‑class only in L2–L4.
3. Parity ablation causality: subtract class‑specific v⊥ at the identified layer (24); re‑evaluate parity accuracy (should drop to ~38.5%) and show cross‑class Usim entering the positive entangled zone.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does task context dynamically reshape the concept manifold to form discrete logical boundaries without collapsing the basal semantic structure?**  
   *Basis:* Explicitly stated in the Introduction. The paper proposes a dual‑modulation mechanism but does not detail the algebraic balancing of preservation vs. divergence during transformation.  
   *Why unresolved:* The exact interaction—how much preservation versus divergence is needed for different logic structures—remains unknown.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Track Usim and Cij across a fine‑grained set of logical tasks with selective interventions to map causal relationships.

2. **Does the dual‑modulation mechanism generalize from crisp numerical logic tasks to complex natural language reasoning with blurry, multi‑dimensional class boundaries?**  
   *Basis:* The Limitations note that experiments used numeric tasks for crisp baselines; natural language tasks involve blurrier boundaries and multidimensional entanglement.  
   *Why unresolved:* The geometric decomposition assumes well‑separated class labels; fuzzy categories may require multiple divergent directions or a different representation.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Apply the same Gram‑Schmidt and ablation methods to NL reasoning benchmarks (e.g., NLI, CommonsenseQA) and check whether the predicted Usim/Cij patterns still correlate with accuracy, and whether a single interference vector suffices.

3. **How do macro‑level geometric interventions propagate through the transformer’s internal circuits, and do they introduce subtle non‑linear artifacts in unobserved subspaces?**  
   *Basis:* The authors acknowledge they cannot yet map how algebraic erasure cascades to specific attention heads or MLPs, nor rule out non‑linear side‑effects in redundant subspaces.  
   *Why unresolved:* The ablation operates on aggregated residual vectors; micro‑scale interactions and potential artifacts in high‑dimensional space remain opaque.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Combine mediation analysis or sparse autoencoders with the ablation protocol to isolate subrepresentations (e.g., neuron clusters, head outputs) and measure consistency of performance changes with geometric metrics.

4. **Is the non‑isometric deformation mechanism universally applicable across different model architectures and normalization schemes?**  
   *Basis:* The theory’s algebra relies on RMSNorm’s hypersphere property; for LayerNorm or no normalization, mapping formulas may need scaling corrections. The core tension is assumed universal but not proven.  
   *Why unresolved:* The exact geometric relationship between translation and rotation might change with different normalization, altering the roles of Usim and Cij.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Replicate the analysis on models with LayerNorm (e.g., GPT‑2) or no normalization, test whether the same non‑isometric patterns (negative cross‑class Usim, positive Cij) emerge, and adjust the mathematical model accordingly.

## Limitations
- The analysis depends on a specific target layer (likely layer 24) but the exact layer is not explicitly stated; different layers might show different dynamics.
- Handling of multi‑token number representations (e.g., Arabic numerals >9) is ambiguous; the token position used for activation extraction could affect results.
- Knowledge filtering only considers correct classifications by argmax; no confidence threshold is specified, possibly allowing low‑certainty samples.
- The experiments use well‑controlled numeric tasks; generalization to natural language with blurry boundaries and multiple logical dimensions remains unvalidated.

## Confidence
- Dual‑modulation mechanism and ablation causality: **Medium** (strong ablation result but vector computation details remain unclear).
- Social pressure entanglement explanation: **Medium** (correlational; task difficulty not fully ruled out as alternative).
- Three‑phase layer‑wise dynamics: **Medium** (visual pattern clear but layer indexing not standardized across models).
