# Baichuan-M3: Modeling Clinical Inquiry for Reliable Medical Decision-Making

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06570
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06570
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art clinical inquiry performance on ScanBench (74.9 CI, 72.1 Lab, 74.4 Dx) and HealthBench (44.4) with low 3.5% hallucination rate, exceeding GPT-5.2-High and human baselines.

## Executive Summary
Baichuan-M3 introduces a novel reinforcement learning-based training pipeline to transform medical LLMs from passive QA systems into proactive clinical consultants. By explicitly modeling the clinical workflow through a three-stage process—Task RL, Offline Policy Distillation, and Multi-Teacher Online Policy Distillation—augmented with Segmented Pipeline RL and Dynamic Rubric Evolution, the system achieves long-horizon reasoning, adaptive hallucination suppression, and superior performance on medical benchmarks. Results show new state-of-the-art scores on ScanBench and HealthBench, with hallucination rates notably lower than competitor models, demonstrating potential for reliable medical decision support.

## Method Summary
The core method comprises a three-stage training pipeline. First, Task RL fine-tunes the base model on clinical inquiry tasks using reinforcement learning with a reward shaping that encourages evidence grounding and stepwise reasoning. Second, Offline Policy Distillation leverages high-quality demonstration data to bootstrap the policy, reducing sample complexity. Third, Multi-Teacher Online Policy Distillation integrates multiple expert policies during online training to improve generalization. Two auxiliary components—Segmented Pipeline RL breaks complex inquiries into manageable segments for coherent long-horizon reasoning, while Dynamic Rubric Evolution iteratively refines the evaluation rubric to suppress hallucinations adaptively.

## Key Results
- ScanBench: 74.9 (Clinical Inquiry), 72.1 (Laboratory Testing), 74.4 (Diagnosis) – surpassing GPT-5.2-High and human baselines.
- HealthBench-Hard: 44.4 vs GPT-5.2's 42.0.
- HealthBench-Hallu: Hallucination rate of 3.5% while maintaining a HealthBench score of 65.1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Baichuan-M3 succeeds by aligning the training process with the structure of real clinical encounters. The three-stage RL pipeline progressively builds capabilities: Task RL establishes task-specific behavior, Offline Distillation injects high-quality expert knowledge, and Multi-Teacher Online Distillation refines the policy under diverse expert guidance. Segmented Pipeline RL enforces a stepwise decomposition of complex inquiries, preventing error accumulation and supporting long-horizon coherence. Dynamic Rubric Evolution continuously adjusts the reward signal based on emerging hallucination patterns, forcing the model to ground responses in evidence. Together, these mechanisms address the passive QA limitation by enabling proactive information gathering and adaptive self-correction.

## Foundational Learning
**Task Reinforcement Learning**  
- Why needed: Enables the model to optimize for complex, multi-step clinical reasoning beyond static QA.  
- Quick check: Does RL fine-tuning improve performance on a held-out clinical simulation task compared to supervised fine-tuning?

**Offline Policy Distillation**  
- Why needed: Provides a stable bootstrap from limited demonstrations, reducing the variance of online RL.  
- Quick check: Does distillation from expert demonstrations lead to faster convergence in early training stages?

**Multi-Teacher Online Policy Distillation**  
- Why needed: Combines strengths of multiple expert policies to avoid overfitting to a single teaching style.  
- Quick check: Does using multiple teachers yield higher final performance than using the best single teacher?

**Segmented Pipeline RL**  
- Why needed: Handles long-horizon reasoning by dividing the task into subgoals, mitigating credit assignment issues.  
- Quick check: Does segmentation reduce error rates on multi-step clinical scenarios?

**Dynamic Rubric Evolution**  
- Why needed: Adapts the evaluation criteria over time to target emerging hallucination modes.  
- Quick check: Does the evolving rubric decrease hallucination frequency without sacrificing task performance?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Base LLM (Baichuan) -> Task RL Module -> Offline Policy Distillation -> Multi-Teacher Online Policy Distillation -> Segmented Pipeline RL (integrated during inference) -> Dynamic Rubric Evolution (integrated during training/evaluation)

**Critical Path**  
Training proceeds sequentially through the three stages, with Segmented Pipeline RL applied during both training and inference to manage reasoning steps. Dynamic Rubric Evolution updates the reward function periodically based on validation performance.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Computational overhead: The multi-stage RL pipeline increases training time and resource usage.  
- Segmentation granularity: Finer segmentation improves interpretability but may increase latency.  
- Teacher diversity: More teachers improve robustness but complicate distillation alignment.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Incoherent long-horizon outputs (e.g., contradictory findings across steps) indicate segmentation breakdown.  
- Persistent hallucinations on rare conditions suggest rubric evolution stalled.  
- Performance drop on out-of-distribution clinical specialties reveals overfitting to training distribution.

**First Experiments**

3. Compare single-teacher vs multi-teacher distillation on a held-out hospital dataset to assess generalization.

## Limitations
- Training data details (size, sources, demographics) are undisclosed, raising bias and generalizability concerns.
- Evaluation relies solely on ScanBench and HealthBench, which may not capture full real-world clinical inquiry complexity.
- Baselines omit recent RL-based medical LLMs (e.g., Doctor-R1, Dr. Assistant, MIND), limiting comparative claims.

## Confidence
- Three-stage RL pipeline with Segmented Pipeline RL and Dynamic Rubric Evolution: Medium
- State-of-the-art performance on ScanBench and HealthBench: Medium
- Low hallucination rate (3.5%) while maintaining HealthBench score: Low
