# Can machines be uncertain?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02365
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02365
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** AI systems can realize fundamentally interrogative uncertainty (question-based), not solely probabilistic propositional attitudes, but only if manifested at the whole-system behavioral level.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether artificial intelligence systems can possess genuine subjective uncertainty, distinguishing this from mere epistemic uncertainty about data. Adopting a functionalist and behavioral perspective, it analyzes how different AI architectures—symbolic, connectionist, and hybrid—might realize uncertainty. The core thesis argues that some forms of uncertainty are interrogative in nature, meaning they take questions (e.g., "?D(a)") rather than propositions as their content. The analysis concludes that for uncertainty to be genuinely subjective, it must be embedded in a system where the uncertain state causally influences overall behavior; outputs from ignored subsystems do not suffice. The contribution is primarily a conceptual taxonomy and set of criteria for ascribing uncertainty to AI, with no empirical validation.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a purely conceptual, philosophical methodology grounded in functionalism. It synthesizes existing descriptions of symbolic and connectionist architectures to theorize how uncertainty might be realized in each, without empirical testing or quantitative metrics. The analysis is deductive, deriving conditions for subjective uncertainty from behavioral role definitions and examining architectural implications.

## Key Results
- Symbolic systems can realize uncertainty either probabilistically (symbolic probability assignments) or categorically via stored interrogative formulas like "?D(a)".
- Connectionist systems may exhibit uncertainty distributively through network weights/thresholds (disposing the system to hesitate) or point-wise via output vectors encoding probabilities or questions, but point-wise realizations only constitute subjective uncertainty if causally integrated into system-level behavior.
- Genuine subjective uncertainty must be manifest at the whole-system level; ascribing it to subsystems whose outputs are ignored by the overarching system (the "First Solution") is rejected.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not specify a technical implementation mechanism. Instead, it argues philosophically that uncertainty is realized through the functional role of interrogative content in guiding behavior. For an AI system to be uncertain, the state must play a causal role in producing hesitancy, inquiry, or non-commitment at the behavioral level, not merely encode numeric probabilities internally.

## Foundational Learning
- **Functionalism:** Mental states are defined by their causal roles, not internal makeup. *Why needed:* Provides the behavioral criterion for ascribing uncertainty. *Quick check:* Does the system's "uncertainty" cause distinct behavioral dispositions (e.g., deferring, seeking information)?
- **Interrogative attitude:** An intentional state directed at a question (e.g., "whether P?"). *Why needed:* Distinguishes uncertainty from credence. *Quick check:* Is the content a question mark syntax or explicit query representation?
- **Propositional attitude:** State like belief or desire with propositional content ("P is true"). *Why needed:* Contrast with interrogative. *Quick check:* Does the system assign truth values or probabilities to declaratives?
- **Symbolic architecture:** Rule-based systems manipulating explicit symbols. *Why needed:* One paradigm for interrogative realization. *Quick check:* Can the system store and reason with formulas like "?D(a)"?
- **Connectionist architecture:** Neural networks with distributed weights. *Why needed:* Alternative paradigm for uncertainty. *Quick check:* Do activation patterns or output vectors represent hesitation or multiple hypotheses?
- **Hybrid system:** Integration of symbolic and connectionist components. *Why needed:* Reflects modern AI trends. *Quick check:* Do symbolic interrogatives and neural uncertainty signals interact cohesively?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Symbolic Component (Rules/Interrogative Store) -> Integration Layer -> Whole-System Behavioral Output  
Connectionist Component (Network Weights/Output Vectors) -> Integration Layer -> Whole-System Behavioral Output  
Hybrid: Both components feed into Integration Layer.

**Critical path:** The representation of uncertainty (interrogative formula or network disposition) must propagate through the integration layer to influence final behavioral outputs (e.g., action selection, information-seeking). If the path is broken at any point (e.g., ignored subsystem), subjective uncertainty fails to be realized.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Symbolic:* Clear interrogative forms and explicit reasoning but lacks learning adaptability and may require hand-crafted rules.  
- *Connectionist:* Continuous, learned uncertainty representations but may not explicitly distinguish questions from probabilistic beliefs.  
- *Hybrid:* Potentially combines strengths but faces integration complexity and risk of level-splits if components are not cohesively coupled.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Level-split: Subsystem (e.g., a neural layer) encodes uncertainty but its output is overridden or unused by the decision module.  
- Purely probabilistic output without interrogative role: System outputs confidence scores but behaves decisively regardless.  
- Disconnected architecture: Symbolic and neural components operate in parallel without mutual influence on behavior.

**First experiments:**  
1. Build a symbolic agent that stores and processes interrogative formulas (e.g., "?is-a(cat, animal)") and test whether it exhibits behavioral hesitation or active querying in uncertain domains.  
2. Implement a simple ANN with an output layer designed to emit question-like vectors (e.g., [?, target, property]) and measure whether this leads to deferred actions or exploration behaviors in a decision task.  
3. Construct a minimal hybrid where symbolic interrogatives trigger modifications to neural network thresholds, and assess if the integrated system shows unified uncertain behavior (e.g., consistent deferral) rather than component-level dissociation.

## Limitations
- The analysis is purely conceptual, lacking empirical validation or metrics to test the proposed criteria.
- The functionalist definition of uncertainty may not align with practical computational realizability in complex AI systems.
- The rejection of subsystem ascription assumes perfect integration, which is often absent in modular or legacy AI architectures.

## Confidence
- Symbolic and connectionist realization categories: Medium  
- Embedding requirement for point-wise uncertainty: High  
- Whole-system level ascription (rejection of First Solution): High  
- Hybrid system combinations: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Empirical test of symbolic interrogative uncertainty:** Implement a symbolic AI system that stores and processes interrogative formulas like "?D(a)" and evaluate its behavioral dispositions (e.g., hesitation, information-seeking) in scenarios with incomplete knowledge.
2. **Case study on hybrid architectures:** Select existing neuro-symbolic AI systems (e.g., DeepProbabilistic Logic Networks) and apply the paper's whole-system criterion to determine whether they achieve genuine subjective uncertainty.
3. **Theoretical challenge from alternative perspectives:** Compare the functionalist approach with information-theoretic (e.g., uncertainty as entropy) or control-theoretic (e.g., uncertainty as policy variance) accounts to identify weaknesses and refine the taxonomy.
