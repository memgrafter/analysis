# CoV: Chain-of-View Prompting for Spatial Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05172
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05172
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** CoV yields an average +11.56% improvement in LLM-Match across four VLMs on OpenEQA, with state-of-the-art zero-shot results on ScanQA and SQA3D.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with Embodied Question Answering (EQA) because they process fixed, passive video inputs, limiting their ability to reason about spatially distributed or occluded elements in 3D scenes. This paper introduces Chain-of-View (CoV) prompting, a training-free, test-time framework that overcomes this by treating the scene as an actionable 3D environment. CoV first uses a View Selection Agent to filter redundant initial frames and identify question-relevant anchor views. A CoV Agent then iteratively predicts discrete camera actions (e.g., rotation, movement) to actively explore the scene, gather missing contextual evidence, and refine its answer. This active perception loop enables VLMs to resolve spatial ambiguities that are impossible from static views.

## Method Summary
CoV operates on a 3D scene mesh/grid and an initial sequence of video frames. In Stage 1, a VLM-based View Selection Agent scores all initial frames against the question and selects a small subset of "anchor" views. In Stage 2, a CoV Agent is initialized with the question and these anchor views. At each step, the agent reasons over the accumulated view history and predicts a discrete camera action (translation, rotation, or view switch). This action is translated into an SE(3) transformation, applied to the underlying 3D scene representation to render a new, previously unobserved viewpoint. The new observation is appended to the context. The loop repeats for a fixed step budget or until the agent declares completion, then outputs the final answer. The process is entirely prompt-based, requiring no model training.

## Key Results
- Average improvement of +11.56% in LLM-Match on OpenEQA across four VLMs, with a peak gain of +13.62% on Qwen3-VL-Flash.
- State-of-the-art zero-shot results on ScanQA (116 CIDEr, 31.9 EM@1) and SQA3D (51.1 EM@1).
- Test-time scaling: increasing the action step budget yields an additional average improvement of +2.51%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Coarse-Grained View Selection Filters Redundancy.** The View Selection Agent uses the VLM to score initial video frames against the question, selecting a small subset of candidate views (V'). This reduces input dimensionality and eliminates distractors, providing a cleaner starting context and improving the signal-to-noise ratio for subsequent reasoning. An ablation study shows that removing this stage degrades performance by an average of 4.59%. If the agent fails to retain any ground-truth relevant views, the subsequent active search starts from a false premise and cannot recover.

**Mechanism 2: Iterative Action-Reasoning Gathers Distributed Spatial Evidence.** The CoV Agent interleaves reasoning with discrete camera actions. At each step, it predicts an action (e.g., `forward-movement+2`), which is converted into an SE(3) transformation applied to the 3D scene mesh to generate a new viewpoint. This allows the VLM to accumulate evidence across distinct, previously unobserved vantage points, resolving ambiguities like "What is between X and Y?" that require multi-view context. The core assumption is that VLMs can learn to propose effective navigation actions at test-time. If the required viewpoint lies outside the reachable set within the step budget, or if the VLM hallucinates invalid actions, the agent will fail.

**Mechanism 3: Test-Time Scaling Enables Thorough Exploration.** A minimum step budget is enforced in the CoV agent's prompt. For questions genuinely requiring multi-step look-around, a larger budget permits longer action sequences to gather distributed evidence, perform verification movements, and refine hypotheses. The quality of the answer for complex questions is a non-decreasing function of the number of informative actions taken, up to a point. Evidence shows a clear upward trend in scores for questions requiring more steps, with an average +2.51% gain from increased budgets. However, excessive steps can introduce noise or hallucination, as noted in the paper's limitations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Embodied Question Answering (EQA)**  
  *Why needed:* CoV redefines EQA from passive video understanding to active scene exploration. The agent must move to gather evidence, not just summarize visible objects.  
  *Quick check:* Given a 3D mesh and a question, what is the difference between passively summarizing visible objects and actively moving to disambiguate "What is behind the sofa?"

- **Concept: 3D Scene Representation as an Actionable Environment**  
  *Why needed:* CoV's core operation (`vt+1_i = Transform(v_i; a_t, S)`) requires the scene `S` to be a geometry (mesh/point cloud) that can be re-rendered from arbitrary camera poses, bridging language to embodied action.  
  *Quick check:* If you have only 5 fixed RGB images from a room, can you implement the `Transform` function for CoV? Why or why not?

- **Concept: Discrete Action Space for Viewpoint Control**  
  *Why needed:* The CoV agent's policy is defined over symbolic, executable actions (translations, rotations, view switches) with semantic effects on the viewpoint (e.g., `forward-movement+N` moves N meters along the local forward axis).  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between an action like `left-rotation+15` and `switch to view V7` in terms of how the new observation is generated?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Processor -> View Selection Agent -> CoV Agent (Policy Loop) -> Action Executor / Scene Renderer -> Termination Controller -> Answer Generation.

**Critical path:** Input (question Q, initial frames V, scene S) are processed. The View Selection Agent outputs a filtered set of anchor views V'. The CoV Agent initializes with Q and V'. At each step t: it consumes context Ct (history of views and reasoning) and emits an action at. The Action Executor translates at into an SE(3) transformation, queries the 3D scene S to render a new view vt+1, and appends it to Ct+1. The loop repeats until termination (max steps or self-decision), then outputs answer A.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Step budget vs. latency/compute: Larger budgets improve accuracy on hard questions but linearly increase inference time and rendering calls.
- Action granularity: Finer-grained actions (e.g., `+1` degree rotation) allow precise control but increase required steps and demand more precise VLM reasoning.
- View selection sparsity: Selecting too few anchors may omit a critical starting point; too many reintroduces redundancy, diminishing the coarse stage's benefit.

**Failure signatures:**
- Stuck/repeating actions (e.g., `left-rotation+15` then `right-rotation+15`), indicating failure to form a coherent navigation plan.
- Irrelevant view selection where V' contains no views with objects mentioned in Q, dooming active search from the start.
- Premature termination where the agent outputs "done" after 1-2 steps on a known multi-view question.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Unit Test View Selection:** On a small, annotated subset, evaluate if V' contains at least one ground-truth relevant view for 90%+ of questions. (Validates Mechanism 1's prerequisite.)
2. **Action Executor Sanity Check:** For a fixed V' and a known required viewpoint (from ground-truth), can the Transform function, given a correct action sequence from an oracle, render a view that contains the answer? (Validates the environment interface.)
3. **End-to-End with Forced Oracle Anchor:** Replace V' with a guaranteed-relevant ground-truth anchor view. Run CoV with a small step budget (e.g., 3). Measure improvement over baseline. (Isolates value of the action-reasoning loop from view selection quality.)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1: Dynamic Environments.** How can CoV be extended to 3D environments where objects move? The current pipeline assumes a static scene and precomputed reconstructions; no temporal modeling is present. The paper notes it "may struggle in highly dynamic or cluttered environments where rapid context shifts occur." Resolution would require testing on datasets with moving objects or integrating time-aware features.

**Open Question 2: Adaptive Stopping Criterion.** What adaptive stopping criterion balances information gain and noise from long trajectories? The paper uses fixed step budgets and warns that "excessive exploration may also introduce noise or hallucination" but does not address when to stop. Evidence for resolution would involve confidence or uncertainty estimators from the VLM's outputs, evaluating policies that decide termination based on information gain versus cost.

**Open Question 3: Robust View Selection.** Can the coarse view selection be made more robust without large-scale training? The paper acknowledges "suboptimal view selection can impair reasoning accuracy" and calls for "advanced view selection algorithms." Current single-step LLM prompting lacks iterative refinement and performance feedback. Resolution might involve iterative re-ranking of views after initial exploration or training a lightweight RL policy using QA rewards.

## Limitations
- Requires a full 3D scene mesh for rendering; not all datasets provide this.
- Action semantics (e.g., local forward direction, rotation axis, units) are not fully detailed, risking implementation errors.
- Context length handling for long action histories is unspecified; large step budgets may exceed VLM context limits.
- Discrete, limited action space may not reach necessary viewpoints within the step budget.
- Potential for noise/hallucination with excessive exploration steps.

## Confidence
- **CoV gains (+11.56% LLM-Match):** Medium (strong results but not independently verified).
- **View selection filtering (−4.59% ablation impact):** Medium (ablation shows effect, but anchor recall unknown).
- **Iterative action-reasoning value:** Medium (mechanism sound; break conditions not fully tested empirically).
- **Test-time scaling (+2.51%):** Medium (trend present, but noise and cost concerns limit practical utility).
- **SOTA on ScanQA/SQA3D:** Medium (zero-shot claims depend on dataset specifics and evaluation setup).

## Next Checks
1. **Anchor recall test:** On a subset, evaluate if the View Selection Agent's output V' contains ≥1 ground-truth relevant view for the question.
2. **Oracle action sequence:** Feed a known correct action sequence (from human or ground-truth annotations) to reach a required viewpoint; verify if the answer becomes correct, isolating the action policy's value.
3. **Budget scaling curve:** Vary the step budget (e.g., 1–9) on the hardest questions; plot performance per step to confirm monotonicity, identify diminishing returns, and detect over-exploration noise.
