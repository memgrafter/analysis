# High-Fidelity Text-to-Image Generation from Pre-Trained Vision-Language Models via Distribution-Conditioned Diffusion Decoding

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.13389  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13389  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** Reduces ImageNet-1K reconstruction FID from 2.85 to 0.75 and MJHQ-30K generation FID from 14.29 to 9.42 (Janus‑Pro 7B), achieving 34% FID improvement for 31% end‑to‑end latency increase.

## Executive Summary
Pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) generate images by predicting discrete tokens from a codebook, but quantization during decoding introduces severe visual artifacts. This paper introduces a diffusion‑based decoding framework that leaves the VLM unchanged. It converts the VLM’s image‑token logits into continuous, distribution‑weighted code vectors and uncertainty features via a Logit‑to‑Code Distributional Mapping (LCDM). A lightweight Logit Calibration (LC) aligns training‑time proxy logits with inference‑time VLM logits, bridging the train‑inference gap. The continuous representations condition a diffusion decoder (DCDD) for high‑fidelity reconstruction. Trained only on ImageNet‑1K, the method improves reconstruction and generation fidelity significantly while incurring moderate latency overhead.

## Method Summary
The framework preserves the original VLM. Given text input, the VLM outputs a sequence of image‑token logits. These logits are processed by LCDM, which aggregates the VQ‑VAE codebook into a continuous mixture weighted by the logits’ probability distribution and derives uncertainty features. LC then calibrates these features to match the distribution encountered during training, mitigating the mismatch between VQ‑VAE encoder proxy logits and VLM inference logits. Finally, a diffusion decoder iteratively denoises a random noise map conditioned on the calibrated features to produce a high‑quality image. Only the LCDM, LC, and diffusion decoder are trained; the VLM remains frozen. Training uses a reconstruction loss (LPIPS) and an adversarial loss for realism.

## Key Results
- **Reconstruction:** ImageNet‑1K FID lowered from 2.85 (VQ‑VAE baseline) to **0.75**.
- **Generation:** MJHQ‑30K FID improved from 14.29 to **9.42** using Janus‑Pro 7B.
- **Compositional correctness:** GenEval score increased modestly from 77.24 to **78.31**.
- **Efficiency:** 34% FID improvement achieved with a **31%** end‑to‑end latency increase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LCDM transforms discrete probability distributions over the codebook into a continuous weighted sum of code vectors, preserving information that would be lost by a single‑token argmax selection. The uncertainty features provide additional signals about the VLM’s confidence. LC aligns the training‑time proxy logits (from the VQ‑VAE encoder) with the VLM’s inference‑time logits, reducing the train‑inference distribution gap. This calibration ensures that the continuous conditioning accurately reflects the VLM’s semantic intent. The diffusion decoder, being a powerful continuous generator, can then produce high‑fidelity images with fine details, explaining the significant FID reductions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision‑Language Models (VLMs)**: Provide text‑conditioned image token prediction; needed because they encode semantic alignment between text and visual content. *Quick check*: Verify that the VLM produces coherent image tokens for diverse prompts.
- **Vector Quantization (VQ) & Codebooks**: Enable compact discrete image representations; decoding via nearest‑neighbor quantization introduces information loss. *Quick check*: Reconstruct an image from VQ‑VAE indices and measure distortion.
- **Diffusion Models**: Iterative denoising process excels at high‑fidelity continuous generation. *Quick check*: Train a diffusion model on a natural image dataset and sample quality.
- **Logit‑to‑Code Distributional Mapping (LCDM)**: Converts logits into a weighted mixture of code vectors and uncertainty features, avoiding hard token selection. *Quick check*: Compare reconstruction fidelity using argmax vs. LCDM on fixed logits.
- **Logit Calibration (LC)**: Aligns proxy logits from VQ‑VAE encoder with VLM inference logits, bridging train‑inference mismatch. *Quick check*: Measure KL divergence between logit distributions before and after LC.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: VLM → LCDM+LC → Diffusion Decoder → Image  
- **Critical path**: Text prompt → VLM (predicts image‑token logits) → LCDM (computes weighted code vectors + uncertainty) → LC (calibrates features) → Diffusion Decoder (conditions on features, denoises noise → image).  
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Fidelity vs. latency*: Diffusion decoding improves quality but adds iterative steps, increasing latency by 31%.  
  - *Training data*: Using only ImageNet‑1K simplifies training but may limit generalization to unseen domains.  
  - *Frozen VLM*: Retains pretrained knowledge but its logit quality is fixed; LC adapts it with minimal overhead.  
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Mis‑calibrated LC leading to poor conditioning and visual artifacts.  
  - Overfitting to ImageNet‑1K, causing performance drop on out‑of‑distribution data (e.g., faces, complex scenes).  
  - Excessive latency rendering the system impractical for real‑time use.  
- **First experiments**:  
  1. Ablate the LC module to quantify its impact on FID and GenEval.  
  2. Evaluate the framework on COCO (text‑to‑image) and FFHQ (reconstruction) to test generalization.  
  3. Measure the trade‑off between diffusion decoder steps and quality‑latency Pareto frontier.

## Limitations
- Limited training data diversity (ImageNet‑1K only) may hinder generalization to broader domains.  
- Modest GenEval improvement suggests gains are primarily in perceptual fidelity, not compositional correctness.  
- 31% latency increase could impede real‑time deployment.
