# Functionality-Oriented LLM Merging on the Fisher--Rao Manifold

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04972
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04972
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Karcher merging achieves average 0.610 across five benchmarks for Qwen2.5-14B merging 5 models, far exceeding TIES (0.253) and Multi-SLERP (0.239), while preserving activation variance and effective rank to mitigate representation collapse.

## Executive Summary
Existing weight-space merging methods (e.g., linear interpolation, TIES) rely on Euclidean heuristics that cause representation collapse—shrinking activation variance and degrading effective rank—and scale poorly with heterogeneous models. This paper reformulates merging as computing a weighted Karcher mean on the Fisher–Rao manifold, which is locally equivalent to minimizing a KL divergence–based function distance between predictive distributions. To make this tractable, the authors introduce a lightweight spherical proxy that separates directional alignment (handled via geodesic averaging on the unit sphere) from magnitude preservation (via norm rescaling). On Qwen2.5-14B models, the method merges up to 11 experts with stable performance and significantly reduces collapse diagnostics compared to Euclidean baselines.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to merge models by minimizing the sum of squared geodesic distances on the Fisher–Rao manifold, targeting functional similarity rather than parameter proximity. Exact computation is intractable, so the method approximates the geometry using a spherical proxy: each parameter block is normalized to the unit sphere, a weighted Karcher mean is computed via fixed-point iteration (logarithmic map to experts, average, exponential map update), and the result is finally rescaled by the mean source norm per block. This decouples curvature-aware direction merging from norm preservation, generalizing SLERP to N>2 models. The pipeline, built on MergeKit, optionally incorporates per-block Fisher information preconditioning to weight sensitive subspaces.

## Key Results
- For merging 5 Qwen2.5-14B models, Karcher yields an average normalized score of 0.610 versus 0.253 for TIES and 0.239 for Multi-SLERP.
- Performance remains stable when scaling to 11 merged models, while Euclidean baselines degrade around m≈5.
- Layerwise activation variance and effective rank are preserved near base-model levels, demonstrating strong mitigation of representation collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fisher–Rao distance approximates functional divergence
- **Claim:** Minimizing the weighted Karcher mean on the Fisher–Rao manifold approximately minimizes a KL-based function distance between predictive distributions.
- **Mechanism:** The Fisher–Rao metric \(d_{FR}^2(\theta, \theta') \approx (\theta - \theta')^\top F(\theta) (\theta - \theta') \approx 2\, KL(p_\theta \| p_{\theta'})\) locally, where \(F(\theta)\) is the Fisher information matrix. Optimizing \(\theta^* = \arg\min_\theta \sum_i \alpha(i) d_{FR}^2(\theta, \theta^{(i)})\) thus aligns with reducing expected negative log-likelihood.
- **Core assumption:** The local linearization between FR distance and KL divergence holds across the neighborhoods of fine-tuned checkpoints.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 derive the connection via expected NLL decomposition; neighboring work (e.g., CAMEx) also critiques Euclidean merging but does not anchor the FR–KL link for LLMs.
- **Break condition:** When models are far apart (large task vectors), the second-order approximation may degrade, making the Karcher objective less faithful to true function distance.

### Mechanism 2: Geodesic averaging avoids chordal shortcuts and collapse
- **Claim:** Following geodesic paths in the fixed-point iteration keeps the merged model on or near the high-performing manifold that connects experts, avoiding norm shrinkage and drift caused by Euclidean chords.
- **Mechanism:** The iteration \(v^{(t)} = \sum_i \alpha(i) \text{Log}_{\theta^{(t)}}(\theta^{(i)})}\), \(\theta^{(t+1)} = \text{Exp}_{\theta^{(t)}}(\eta v^{(t)})\) averages tangent vectors and moves along geodesics. Euclidean averaging takes shortcuts through curved space, shrinking norms and leaving the high-loss valley, which correlates with activation variance loss and effective-rank degradation.
- **Core assumption:** Fine-tuned checkpoints lie on a connected, low-loss "valley" or shell in parameter space, and the geodesics between them stay within this region.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes Euclidean blends shrink norms and drift off the manifold; Section 4.2 (Figure 2) shows Karcher preserves layerwise activation variance and EffRank significantly better than Lerp/Ties.
- **Break condition:** If experts lie on disconnected or highly irregular low-loss regions (e.g., severe optimization divergence), geodesic averaging may not find a feasible barycenter or may still suffer interference.

### Mechanism 3: Spherical proxy enables norm-preserving, scalable merging
- **Claim:** Normalizing weight blocks to the unit sphere, computing the spherical Karcher mean, and rescaling by representative norms provides a tractable, norm-preserving approximation of FR geometry that generalizes SLERP to N>2 and mitigates collapse with minimal overhead.
- **Mechanism:** Directional curvature is captured by spherical log/exp operations; magnitude is preserved separately via per-block mean norms. This decoupling addresses two key collapse drivers: norm shrinkage and misalignment.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant effect of FR geometry is captured by directional alignment on the sphere, and per-block norms are approximately stable across experts (or their mean is a good proxy).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 describes the proxy; Section 4.1 (Table 1) shows Karcher (0.610) vastly outperforms (Multi-)SLERP (0.239) and TIES (0.253) for m=5; Figure 1 shows stability from m=2 to 11.
- **Break condition:** If Fisher information varies strongly within a block, the isotropic spherical proxy may misweight sensitive subspaces; performance could degrade without Fisher preconditioning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fisher Information Matrix (FIM)**
  - *Why needed:* Defines the Fisher–Rao Riemannian metric; quantifies sensitivity of the predictive distribution to parameter changes. High Fisher information indicates functionally important subspaces.
  - *Quick check:* If the FIM is approximately diagonal and isotropic for a layer, the loss landscape curvature is uniform across directions in that layer’s parameter space.
- **Karcher/Fréchet Mean**
  - *Why needed:* Generalization of the Euclidean mean to Riemannian manifolds; the point minimizing the sum of squared geodesic distances to a set of points. Used here as the optimality criterion for merging under FR geometry.
  - *Quick check:* For two points on a sphere with equal weights, the Karcher mean is the midpoint along the great circle (on the sphere), whereas the Euclidean mean lies inside the sphere.
- **Representation Collapse**
  - *Why needed:* The paper identifies collapse (shrinking activation variance, loss of effective rank) as the primary failure mode of Euclidean merges, directly harming model capability. Mitigating collapse is a key empirical validation.
  - *Quick check:* If a merged model’s layerwise activation covariance becomes near-singular (low numerical/effective rank), the model likely loses expressive capacity for fine-grained reasoning or discrimination.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Models (base + experts + weights) -> Block-wise Processor (split tensors) -> Spherical Proxy (normalize vectors) -> Fixed-point Iteration (log/exp cycles) -> Norm Rescaling (multiply by mean expert norm) -> Reassembly (final checkpoint) -> Optional Fisher Preconditioning (weight log vectors)
- **Critical path:** Initialize θ⁽⁰⁾ (e.g., base model). Repeat until convergence: for each block, compute v⁽ᵗ⁾ = Σ αᵢ Log_{θ⁽ᵗ⁾}(θ⁽ⁱ⁾) on sphere; update θ⁽ᵗ⁺¹⁾ = Exp_{θ⁽ᵗ⁾}(η v⁽ᵗ⁾); optionally precondition log vectors with blockwise Fisher. After global iteration, rescale each block by mean expert norm. The loop runs synchronously across all blocks.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Approximation fidelity vs. speed: Spherical proxy is fast but may misrepresent curvature if norms differ or FIM is anisotropic.
  - Block size: Finer blocks (per-layer) capture layer-specific geometry but increase cost; coarser blocks (per-tensor) are cheaper but may average out important directional info.
  - Step size η & convergence: Smaller η is stable but slow; larger η risks overshoot. No theoretical global convergence guarantees.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Diverging or oscillating v⁽ᵗ⁾ norms and objective values in iterations.
  - Performance collapse similar to Euclidean baselines when merging >2 heterogeneous models.
  - Layerwise activation variance or EffRank dropping below base-model levels (Table 5).
  - Inconsistent gains across blocks when Fisher information is highly variable (if not preconditioned).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Pairwise baseline replication:** Merge two related Qwen2.5 checkpoints (e.g., base + instruct) across scales (135M, 360M, 1.7B); verify Karcher matches/exceeds SLERP/LERP (Table 2, Table 4).
  2. **Scaling test:** Progressively merge m=2 to 11 diverse 14B experts; plot average performance vs. m; confirm Karcher remains stable while baselines degrade (Figure 1, Table 3).
  3. **Collapse diagnostics:** For m=5 merge, compute layerwise activation variance and EffRank on held-out dataset; confirm Karcher preserves these metrics near base model levels, while Lerp/Ties show severe degradation (Figure 2, Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the spherical proxy approximation error impact merging performance for models with large task vectors, and does this error correlate with collapse?
- Does the fixed-point iteration converge globally for arbitrary expert sets, and how sensitive are results to initialization and step size?
- What is the separate contribution of Fisher preconditioning within the spherical proxy to the overall performance gains?
- Does Karcher merging generalize to non-transformer architectures or multimodal models, and does the spherical proxy remain effective?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter ambiguity: Exact step size η, convergence criteria, max iterations, and block partitioning are not specified for reported experiments.
- Spherical proxy assumption: Treating weight blocks as isotropic on the sphere ignores potential per-direction Fisher anisotropy; may break for highly variable losses.
- Model selection bias: For progressive merges (m=2–11), the ordering of the 11 checkpoints is unclear, risking selection effects on reported stability.

## Confidence
- **FR–KL equivalence:** Medium – Theoretical derivation is sound but local linearization may fail for heterogeneous models; limited direct empirical validation.
- **Geodesic averaging mitigates collapse:** High – Strong evidence from activation variance and effective-rank diagnostics across layers (Fig. 2, Table 5) consistently shows reduced collapse.
- **Spherical proxy generalizes SLERP:** Medium – Clear gains for N>2 merging (Table 1), but the isolated contribution of norm-preservation vs. directional curvature remains unablated.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter ablation:** Vary η (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 1.0), max iterations (50, 100, 200), and block granularity (per-layer vs. per-tensor) on a fixed 5-model subset; measure accuracy and collapse metrics to identify stable settings.
2. **Fisher preconditioning test:** Estimate per-block diagonal Fisher information for each expert; rerun Karcher with and without preconditioning; compare results across model heterogeneities to quantify Fisher weighting impact.
3. **Cross-architecture transfer:** Apply merging pipeline to models from other families (e.g., Llama 3.1, Mistral) using the same benchmarks; assess whether performance gains and collapse mitigation transfer beyond Qwen2.5 transformers.
