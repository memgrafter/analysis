# Gender Disambiguation in Machine Translation: Diagnostic Evaluation in Decoder-Only Architectures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17952
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17952
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Instruction-tuned decoder-only models (TowerInstruct) significantly improve contextual gender cue use (MPA +24.4 pp) and reduce masculine default bias (-10.4 pp), yet still underperform encoder-decoder baselines.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates gender bias in machine translation (MT) focusing on decoder-only large language models (LLMs). It extends a diagnostic framework by introducing “Prior Bias” to quantify default gender assumptions on a neutralized WinoMT dataset and uses Minimal Pair Accuracy (MPA) to assess contextual disambiguation. Evaluation on English→Italian translation with Llama2, TowerBase, and TowerInstruct reveals that base decoder models exhibit strong masculine bias and poor contextual sensitivity, while instruction tuning markedly improves MPA (from 12.3% to 36.7%) and reduces Prior Bias (from 85.6% to 75.2% masculine). Attention analysis shows increased focus on feminine pronouns after tuning, correlating with better performance.

The findings suggest that instruction tuning aligns decoder-only models to leverage contextual cues for gender agreement, though they still lag behind encoder-decoder architectures. The study also highlights methodological limitations, including reliance on binary gender categories and potential noise in automatic evaluation pipelines. Overall, it provides a diagnostic lens for understanding gender bias in decoder-only MT and points to instruction tuning as a partial remedy.

## Method Summary
The study uses the WinoMT benchmark, which contains English source sentences and Italian reference translations with gendered professions. The dataset includes a regular version and a neutralized version where gendered pronouns are replaced by “they/them” to measure Prior Bias. Three decoder-only LLMs (Llama2-7B, TowerBase-7B, TowerInstruct-7B) are evaluated in a zero-shot translation setting using model-specific prompts. Model outputs are aligned to the source with `fast_align`, and the gender of translated profession nouns (and articles) is extracted using the Italian `spaCy` morphological analyzer. Key metrics are: Standard Gender Accuracy (overall and by gender), Minimal Pair Accuracy (MPA) requiring correct and gender‑differentiated translations for sentence pairs that differ only in the source pronoun, and Prior Bias (the proportion of masculine vs. feminine forms on the neutral set). Attention weights are extracted from the decoder during generation of the target profession noun, averaged over accurate minimal pairs to probe cue integration. The reproduction involves: (1) preparing data (downloading WinoMT, constructing neutral set), (2) running inference with appropriate prompts, (3) computing metrics via the pipeline, and (4) analyzing attention with sanity checks (e.g., prompt attention consistency, low attention to secondary entities).

## Key Results
- Base decoder-only models (Llama2, TowerBase) show high masculine default bias (~85% on Neutral Set) and low contextual disambiguation (MPA ~12.3% for TowerBase).
- Instruction tuning (TowerInstruct) improves MPA to 36.7% and reduces masculine Prior Bias to 75.2%, indicating better utilization of gender cues.
- Attention analysis reveals that TowerInstruct assigns higher weight to feminine pronouns in specific heads (e.g., layer 15 head 10), correlating with accurate minimal pair translations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoder-only LLMs exhibit a persistent masculine default gender bias when translating into morphologically gendered languages from a neutral source, which is explicitly quantified by the novel Prior Bias metric on a neutralized dataset.
- **Mechanism:** In the absence of explicit gender cues (e.g., “they/them” instead of “he/she”), the model’s output distribution for profession nouns defaults to the statistically more frequent masculine forms learned during pretraining. This reflects a reliance on prior probabilities rather than contextual disambiguation.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s internal probability distribution over target vocabulary is skewed toward masculine lemmas due to corpus frequency imbalances, and this skew persists unless explicitly countered by strong contextual signals or post‑training.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “we introduce a novel measure coined ‘Prior Bias’, capturing a model’s default gender assumptions”.  
  [section 4.3] Table 3 shows Llama2 and TowerBase produce masculine forms ~85% of the time on the Neutral Set.  
  [corpus] The Neutral Set construction replaces gendered pronouns with “they/them”; the resulting gender distribution in outputs is the direct measure of Prior Bias.
- **Break condition:** If, on the Neutral Set, the model outputs a near‑even distribution of masculine/feminine forms (e.g., 50/50), or if the gap between masculine and feminine Prior Bias closes significantly without corresponding MPA gains.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Instruction tuning (post‑training on translation‑specific tasks) improves a decoder‑only model’s ability to integrate available contextual gender cues, as measured by Minimal Pair Accuracy (MPA), and concurrently reduces—but does not eliminate—its masculine Prior Bias.
- **Mechanism:** Instruction tuning aligns the model’s decoding process to follow explicit translation prompts and likely reinforces patterns where correct translations depend on source‑context agreement. This strengthens the model’s sensitivity to the gendered pronoun cue, allowing it to override the default masculine prior more frequently.
- **Core assumption:** The base model (Llama2/TowerBase) has latent knowledge of gender agreement from pretraining, but instruction tuning acts as a “catalyst” that amplifies the use of this knowledge for the specific MT task, shifting the output policy.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “instruction tuning… significantly improves contextual awareness: MPA rises from 12.3% (TowerBase) to 36.7%”.  
  [section 4.2] Table 2 shows TowerInstruct’s MPA jump and its balanced Pro‑F/Pro‑M split (50.4%/49.6%), contrasting with the skewed baselines.  
  [section 4.3] Table 3 shows TowerInstruct’s Prior Bias decreases from ~85% to 75.2% masculine.  
  [section 5.2] Attention to feminine cues increases in specific heads (e.g., L15 H10) from Llama2 to TowerInstruct.
- **Break condition:** If instruction tuning on a comparable task does *not* increase MPA or reduce Prior Bias relative to its untuned counterpart, or if attention patterns to contextual cues do not strengthen.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Self‑attention weights on the gender cue **correlate** with successful contextual disambiguation, but are not a direct causal explanation for the output; they serve as a diagnostic proxy for cue integration.
- **Mechanism:** When the model correctly generates gendered forms in both members of a minimal pair, the attention distribution shows increased focus on the source‑side gendered pronoun (especially feminine, due to its lower prior frequency). This indicates the pronoun’s representation is being integrated into the decoder’s state when generating the target profession.
- **Core assumption:** Attention weights reflect the model’s internal relevance assignment. Higher attention to a cue when it is *necessary* for correct output suggests the cue is being used. The correlation is validated by restricting analysis to *accurate* minimal pairs.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 5.1] Analysis is restricted to accurate minimal pairs (n=195) to isolate where attention is present when disambiguation succeeds.  
  [section 5.2] “feminine cues often eliciting stronger activations, likely compensating for their lower frequency”; Figure 5 shows increased attention in specific heads for TowerInstruct.  
  [section 5.2.1] Sanity checks: prompt attention is consistent (~0.77–0.78%) across models; attention to secondary (non‑coreferent) entities is low (≤0.04%), supporting selective focus.
- **Break condition:** If no systematic difference in attention to the gender cue is found between accurate and inaccurate minimal pairs, or if attention to the cue is high even when the model ignores it (e.g., produces same gendered output for both pronouns in a pair).

## Foundational Learning

**Cross‑linguistic gender marking and agreement**  
- *Why needed:* The entire bias problem stems from translating from a language with sparse grammatical gender (English) to one with rich gender agreement (Italian). Understanding which target words (nouns, articles, adjectives) must inflect for gender is essential to construct the evaluation (WinoMT) and interpret errors.  
- *Quick check:* When translating “The doctor visited the nurse because she was tired” from English to Italian, which words in the output must change if “she” becomes “he”?

**Minimal Pair Evaluation (MPA)**  
- *Why needed:* Standard accuracy is insufficient because a correct translation can be correct for the *wrong reason* (e.g., defaulting to a stereotype). MPA forces the model to change its output based *only* on the swapped gender cue, measuring true contextual sensitivity.  
- *Quick check:* If a model translates both “The librarian… she” and “The librarian… he” as “la bibliotecaria” (fem.), is this considered accurate by MPA? Why or why not?

**Transformer self‑attention as a diagnostic tool**  
- *Why needed:* The paper uses attention weights to peek inside the black box and see *where* in the network the gender cue is being processed during generation, linking internal mechanics to the external MPA/Prior Bias metrics.  
- *Quick check:* If attention to the feminine pronoun “she” is high in layer 15 head 10 only for accurate translations, what does this tentatively suggest about that head’s role?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Data (WinoMT Regular & Neutral) → Model Inference (zero‑shot prompts for Llama2/TowerBase/TowerInstruct) → Alignment & Analysis (`fast_align` for source‑target alignment; `spaCy` for gender extraction) → Metric Computation (Standard Accuracy, MPA, Prior Bias) → Attention Extraction (averaging self‑attention from target token to source pronoun on accurate minimal pairs)

**Critical path:**  
1. *Prepare dataset:* download WinoMT and construct Neutral Set by replacing gendered pronouns with “they/them” and adjusting verb/adjective agreement.  
2. *Run inference:* generate translations for both regular and neutral sets using model‑specific templates.  
3. *Compute output metrics:* align outputs, extract gender, compute Standard Accuracy, MPA, and Prior Bias.  
4. *Extract & analyze attention:* for accurate minimal pairs, fetch attention weights at the generation step of the target profession; average across layers/heads.  
5. *Perform sanity checks:* verify prompt attention consistency and low attention to secondary entities.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Automatic pipeline vs. manual validation:* Using `fast_align` and `spaCy` is scalable but introduces noise; conservative “Unknown” tagging mitigates skew but some errors persist.  
- *Binary gender limitation:* The framework inherits WinoMT’s binary masc/fem focus, excluding non‑binary/neutral forms, limiting scope.  
- *Attention as proxy:* Attention is correlational; causal methods (e.g., ablation) needed for stronger claims.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High “Unknown” rate (>15%) indicating pipeline failures or garbled outputs.  
- No MPA gain after instruction tuning relative to baseline, contradicting core findings.  
- Attention to the gender cue does not increase for the model with improved MPA, weakening the internal signal link.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Reproduce Prior Bias:* run all models on the Neutral Set; compute the percentage of masculine vs. feminine outputs.  
2. *Compute MPA for Minimal Pairs:* for every profession pair (he/she), score a pair as correct only if both translations are correct and have different genders.  
3. *Extract Attention for Feminine Cues (Accurate Pairs):* for layer 15, plot attention heatmap for heads 10 & 25; verify increased attention to “she” and pattern across models; also run sanity checks on prompt and secondary entity attention.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can evaluation frameworks for gender bias in MT be extended to include non‑binary and gender‑neutral alternatives beyond the current binary masculine/feminine paradigm? (Limitations section: binary focus)
2. To what extent do errors in the automatic evaluation pipeline (alignment with `fast_align`, morphological analysis with `spaCy`) confound the measurement of gender bias, and how can these be mitigated? (Limitations: “Unknown” cases)
3. Which specific components of instruction tuning (e.g., training data composition, prompt templates, training objectives) are responsible for the observed reduction in masculine Prior Bias and improvement in contextual cue integration? (Conclusion: unclear which aspects drive improvement)
4. Can causal interpretability methods (e.g., attention intervention, counterfactual perturbation) establish a direct causal link between models’ attention to gender cues and their gender disambiguation decisions in translation, moving beyond current correlational analysis? (Limitations: correlational attention)

## Limitations
- **Pipeline noise:** Alignment (`fast_align`) and morphological analysis (`spaCy`) errors introduce measurement uncertainty, evidenced by up to 12.8% “Unknown” gender tags.
- **Correlational attention analysis:** Attention weights are used as a diagnostic proxy but do not establish causal necessity of cue integration.
- **Binary and limited context:** Evaluation is restricted to binary masculine/feminine categories and single‑sentence pronoun resolution, excluding non‑binary/neutral forms and richer discourse contexts.

1. **Parameter & Prompt Fidelity Audit:** Inspect the official codebase to confirm exact decoding settings (e.g., temperature=0, max_new_tokens) and prompt templates; re‑run a subset with variations to ensure output stability.
2. **Pipeline Error Quantification:** Manually annotate gender for a stratified random sample of model outputs (n≈200) and compare against automated (`spaCy`/`fast_align`) labels to quantify error rates and misclassification patterns.
3. **Attention Causality Probe:** Perform a targeted intervention (e.g., zeroing out attention to the feminine pronoun in identified critical heads) on a sample of accurate minimal pairs and measure change in MPA to test necessity.
