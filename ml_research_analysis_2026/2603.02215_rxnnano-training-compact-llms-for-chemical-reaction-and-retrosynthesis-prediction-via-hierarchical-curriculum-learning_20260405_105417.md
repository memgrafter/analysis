# RxnNano:Training Compact LLMs for Chemical Reaction and Retrosynthesis Prediction via Hierarchical Curriculum Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02215
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02215
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A 0.5B parameter model achieves 75.1% Top-1 accuracy on USPTO-50k (with AAM), a 23.5% improvement over prior state-of-the-art, and outperforms larger (>7B) fine-tuned LLMs.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of embedding deep chemical understanding—such as reaction common sense and atom-mapping topology—into compact language models for reaction prediction, countering the trend of scaling up parameters and datasets. The proposed RxnNano framework uses a 0.5B parameter model trained with three core innovations: a Latent Chemical Consistency objective for reversible transformations, a Hierarchical Cognitive Curriculum that progresses from syntax to semantics, and Atom-Map Permutation Invariance (AMPI) to learn relational topology without relying on specific atom indices. A structured plan-based reasoning mechanism is also incorporated. On standard benchmarks, RxnNano achieves state-of-the-art accuracy without test-time augmentation and demonstrates strong scalability.

## Method Summary
RxnNano employs a parameter-efficient 0.5B LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B) fine-tuned with LoRA adapters. Training follows a strict three-stage **Hierarchical Cognitive Curriculum**: (1) a Syntactic Phase learns SMILES grammar via standard language modeling; (2) a Denoising Phase trains reconstruction from corrupted inputs to build robustness; (3) a Semantic Phase uses atom-mapped reactions with **Atom-Map Permutation Invariance (AMPI)**, where mapping indices are randomly permuted in both input and target to force learning of relational topology. The model is also regularized by a **Latent Chemical Consistency** objective that enforces cycle consistency between forward and retrosynthesis predictions (`T_r(T_f(R)) ≈ R`), and is trained to generate an intermediate, rule-extracted **plan** of mechanistic steps before the final product or reactant SMILES. The curriculum stages are trained sequentially, with the final stage combining the semantic loss and a plan prediction loss.

## Key Results
- On USPTO-50k, RxnNano achieves **75.1% Top-1 accuracy** (with AAM) and **69.8% without AAM**, representing a **23.5% relative improvement** over the previous state-of-the-art.
- It significantly outperforms larger (>7B parameter) fine-tuned LLMs.
- The model scales effectively, achieving **62.1% Top-1 on USPTO-full** and **94.2% on USPTO-480k** for forward prediction, all without test-time augmentation.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of each curriculum stage and AMPI, with AMPI being critical for generalization in the absence of AAM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Latent Chemical Consistency
- **Claim:** The model learns chemically reversible transformations rather than arbitrary token mappings.
- **Mechanism:** A Latent Chemical Consistency objective enforces that the forward prediction function `T_f` and retrosynthesis function `T_r` form an approximate cycle: `T_r(T_f(R)) ≈ R` and `T_f(T_r(P)) ≈ P`. This is implemented as a consistency loss that aligns latent representations or predictions across the forward-retrosynthesis cycle, encouraging the model to treat reactions as movements on a continuous chemical manifold where transformations are physically plausible in both directions.
- **Core assumption:** Chemical reactions are fundamentally reversible processes, and modeling this reversibility imposes a strong structural prior that constrains the hypothesis space to chemically valid transformations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Latent Chemical Consistency objective that models reactions as movements on a continuous chemical manifold, ensuring reversible and physically plausible transformations."
  - [section 3.2] Formal definition of Chemical Cycle-Consistency (Definition 3.2) and the constraint `f^(r)_θ ◦ f^(f)_θ (P) ≈ P`.
- **Break condition:** If the model achieves high one-way accuracy but fails the cycle consistency check (e.g., `T_r(T_f(R))` deviates significantly from `R`), it indicates learning of direction-specific shortcuts rather than true bidirectional chemical understanding.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Cognitive Curriculum
- **Claim:** A progressive, structured curriculum builds chemical intuition by sequentially mastering syntax, robustness, and semantics.
- **Mechanism:** The curriculum consists of three stages: (1) **Syntactic Phase**: Standard language modeling on canonical SMILES to learn grammar and common token patterns. (2) **Denoising Phase**: Input reconstruction from corrupted SMILES (masking/deletion) to develop invariant representations and error recovery. (3) **Semantic Phase**: Training on atom-mapped reactions with AMPI to learn relational topology. This staged approach prevents catastrophic interference and builds increasingly abstract chemical knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** Chemical reasoning ability develops hierarchically—mastery of surface syntax and robustness to noise is a prerequisite for learning deep semantic relationships like atom mapping.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Hierarchical Cognitive Curriculum that trains the model through progressive stages, from syntax mastery to semantic reasoning, building robust chemical intuition."
  - [section 3.4] Detailed description of each stage, objectives `L_syn`, `L_denoise`, `L_sem`, and the rationale for "From Grammar to Logic" progression.
- **Break condition:** Ablation studies (Table 5) show removing any stage degrades performance, especially the semantic stage. If skipping directly to semantic training yields poor results, it confirms the prerequisite nature of earlier stages.

### Mechanism 3: Atom-Map Permutation Invariance (AMPI)
- **Claim:** AMPI forces the model to learn relational atom correspondences rather than memorizing specific index numbers.
- **Mechanism:** During training on AAM-annotated SMILES, the model is presented with randomly permuted mapping indices (e.g., atom `[C:6]` becomes `[C:3]` consistently across input and output). The loss is computed against the inversely permuted ground truth. This ensures the model's predictions depend only on *which* atoms correspond, not *what number* they bear.
- **Core assumption:** Atom-atom mapping information is valuable for learning reaction mechanisms, but its numerical assignments are arbitrary; generalization requires learning permutation-invariant relational topology.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Atom-Map Permutation Invariance (AMPI), which force the model to learn invariant relational topology and balance multi-task learning."
  - [section 3.4.3] Formal definition of AMPI transformation and objective `L_sem` with permutation `π`.
  - [section 4.5 Ablation] Removing AMPI in the w/o AAM setting causes catastrophic drop (34.5% → 69.8% Top-1), proving its necessity for generalization when explicit indices are absent.
- **Break condition:** Performance on unmapped test data (`D_unmapped_test`) collapses if AMPI is removed, while mapped performance may remain high, indicating over-reliance on index shortcuts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chemical Manifold & Reversibility**
  - **Why needed here:** The Latent Chemical Consistency mechanism only makes sense if the model operates in a latent space where forward and reverse transformations are symmetric. Understanding this requires grasping that molecules exist in a continuous, differentiable space of feasible structures.
  - **Quick check question:** If a model predicts `Reactant A → Product B` with high confidence, should it also predict `Product B → Reactant A` with high confidence? Why or why not?

- **Concept: SMILES Syntax and Fragility**
  - **Why needed here:** The Syntactic Phase assumes the model must first conquer the brittle, linearized representation. SMILES strings are not canonical; the same molecule can have many valid strings. The Denoising Phase specifically targets this fragility.
  - **Quick check question:** Give two different valid SMILES strings for ethanol. What makes a SMILES string "valid" versus "invalid" from a chemical grammar perspective?

- **Concept: Relational Topology vs. Absolute Indexing**
  - **Why needed here:** AMPI's core innovation is distinguishing *correspondence* (atom X in reactant becomes atom Y in product) from *labeling* (the number assigned to that pair). Learning the former is essential; the latter is a dataset-specific artifact.
  - **Quick check question:** In a reaction with mapped SMILES, if you randomly relabel all atom-map numbers (e.g., change every `:5` to `:12` consistently), should a correctly learned model's prediction change? Explain.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Qwen2.5-0.5B Base LLM -> LoRA Adapters (on attention & FFN) -> Curriculum Scheduler (Stage 1: Syntactic -> Stage 2: Denoising -> Stage 3: Semantic+Plan) -> Cycle-Consistency Loss (auxiliary) -> AMPI Transform (on-the-fly permutation) -> Plan Token Injector (prepends `<plan>` sequence)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize Qwen2.5-0.5B with LoRA (r=64, α=128).
  2. Stage 1: Train on clean canonical SMILES pairs with `L_syn` until syntax plateaus.
  3. Stage 2: Continue training, apply corruption (masking/deletion) to inputs, optimize `L_denoise`.
  4. Stage 3: Switch to AAM-annotated data. For each batch, sample random permutation π. Apply AMPI (permute indices in input & target). Compute `L_sem` on permuted sequences and `L_plan` on ground-truth plan tokens. Update with weighted loss `L_sem + λ_plan * L_plan`.
  5. Final evaluation: For AAM test, use clean mapping. For w/o AAM test, strip mapping numbers. No test-time augmentation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LoRA vs. Full Fine-tuning:** LoRA (~0.2% added params) prevents catastrophic forgetting and fits 24GB GPUs, but may limit peak performance compared to full fine-tuning.
  - **Curriculum vs. Mixed Training:** Sequential stages build stable representations but use more epochs; mixed training might converge faster but risks interference.
  - **Plan Token Fixed vs. Learned:** Fixed, rule-extracted plans reduce sample complexity and avoid needing annotated rationales, but may be less flexible than a latent-variable planner.
  - **AMPI Computational Overhead:** On-the-fly permutation adds negligible cost but requires consistent, reversible implementation.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Syntactic Phase failure:** High rate of invalid SMILES strings in outputs.
  - **Denoising Phase failure:** Sharp performance drop on test inputs with minor canonicalization differences.
  - **Semantic/AMPI failure:** Large performance gap (>20%) between AAM and w/o AAM evaluation. Permuted AAM training yields high loss.
  - **Cycle-Consistency failure:** Low round-trip accuracy (`T_r(T_f(R))` match) despite high one-way accuracy.
  - **Plan token misuse:** Model generates invalid plan formats or ignores plan tokens.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Stage-wise Learning Curve:** Train each curriculum stage in isolation and measure Top-1 accuracy on a held-out set. Expect rapid rise then plateau for Stage 1, improvement for Stage 2, and significant jump for Stage 3.
  2. **AMPI Permutation Sensitivity:** After Stage 3 training, evaluate on test sets with randomly shuffled atom-map labels. A successful AMPI implementation shows <1% accuracy drop.
  3. **Cycle-Consistency Evaluation:** For a subset of USPTO-50k, compute forward prediction then retrosynthesis on the result. Measure exact match against original reactants (and vice-versa). Report round-trip accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the hierarchical cognitive curriculum be extended to handle multi-step retrosynthesis, and what new objectives are needed to model sequential reaction planning?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5 states: "we trained the base model over single step chemical reaction prediction. For complex multi-step reactions need further study."
  - **Why unresolved:** Multi-step planning involves chaining single-step predictions with intermediate compounds and conditions, which the current single-step focused curriculum does not address.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Construct multi-step training data (e.g., from USPTO routes), design curriculum stages for intermediate reasoning, and evaluate route-level metrics (e.g., route accuracy, step count) against baselines.

- **Open Question 2:** Do the proposed innovations (AMPI, cycle consistency, curriculum) remain effective when scaling model size and training on more diverse reaction datasets?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5: "it is still valuable to train a larger model with more diverse data."
  - **Why unresolved:** The current work shows advantages for a 0.5B model on USPTO; scaling may change the balance between inductive bias and capacity, and diverse data may introduce new distributions that challenge current components.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Train models of varying sizes (0.5B, 1B, 2B, 7B) with identical curricula on USPTO and additional sources (e.g., Reaxys), and perform ablations to measure contribution of each component at different scales.

- **Open Question 3:** How can practical synthesis constraints (feasibility, cost, safety) be formally incorporated into reaction prediction models without sacrificing chemical accuracy?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5: "Incorporation of practical constraints (feasibility, cost, safety) represents another important direction."
  - **Why unresolved:** Current models predict chemically valid reactions but ignore real-world constraints, limiting deployment in actual synthesis planning.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate constraint predictors (e.g., cost estimators, safety classifiers) into the training objective or decoding process; evaluate on a curated set of synthesis problems with expert annotations on feasibility and cost.

## Limitations
- The formulation and implementation details of the Latent Chemical Consistency loss (e.g., exact loss function, weight λ, training phase) are not specified.
- The method for automatically generating the ground-truth "plan" tokens (`z*`) from reaction mechanisms is not described, making Stage 3 training reproduction unclear.
- Key curriculum hyperparameters (epochs per stage, learning rate schedules, noise corruption settings) are undisclosed.
- It is ambiguous whether cycle consistency is an auxiliary training loss or a post-hoc inference constraint.

## Confidence
- **Overall performance claim (75.1% Top-1):** Medium – strong results but reproduction hindered by missing core implementation details.
- **AMPI effectiveness:** High – well-defined mechanism with robust ablation evidence.
- **Hierarchical Curriculum design:** Medium – clear conceptual design but quantitative stage implementation details are lacking.
- **Latent Chemical Consistency mechanism:** Low – concept presented without mathematical formulation or isolated evaluation.
- **Plan-based reasoning contribution:** Low – plan generation method is ambiguous, and its isolated contribution is unverified.

## Next Checks
1. **AMPI Permutation Invariance Test:** After training, evaluate on a test set where all atom-map indices are randomly shuffled. A correctly implemented AMPI model should show negligible (<1%) accuracy drop compared to performance on the clean test set.
2. **Cycle-Consistency Ablation Study:** Implement a plausible cycle-consistency loss (e.g., a contrastive or reconstruction loss between `T_r(T_f(R))` and `R`). Train an otherwise identical model with and without this loss and measure the difference in Top-1 accuracy on USPTO-50k.
3. **Plan Token Substitution Analysis:** Replace the proposed automated plan extraction with a heuristic rule-based generator (e.g., using reaction SMARTS to identify bond changes). Retrain the model with these substitute plans and compare performance to the reported results, analyzing failure cases where plan quality impacts prediction.
