# Coarse-Guided Visual Generation via Weighted h-Transform Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12057  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12057  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Training-free coarse-guided generation using weighted h-transform sampling improves FID and LPIPS on image restoration and camera-controlled video without requiring knowledge of the forward degradation operator.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of generating high-quality visual content from degraded reference inputs, such as low-resolution images or blurred frames, without any task-specific training. Existing training-free methods either require explicit knowledge of the forward degradation process or fail to balance adherence to the coarse guidance with output realism. The authors introduce Weighted h-Transform Sampling, a training-free approach that leverages Doob’s h-transform to modify the diffusion sampling process. The ideal drift term is approximated from the coarse input, and a noise-level-aware weight schedule gradually reduces its influence as sampling progresses to mitigate approximation errors. Experiments on image restoration (super-resolution, deblurring, inpainting) and camera-controlled video generation show consistent improvements over baselines like SDEdit, with lower FID and LPIPS, while being compatible with both score-based and flow-based pretrained models.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the reverse diffusion process (SDE/ODE) by adding a drift term derived from Doob’s h-transform, which conditions the stochastic process on ending at a desired fine sample. Because the ideal h-function is intractable, it is approximated using the coarse input ê:  
\[
h(x_0=y) \approx h(x_0=\hat{e}) = \frac{\alpha_t \hat{e} - x_t}{\sigma_t^2} - s_\theta(x_t,t).
\]  
A noise-level-dependent weight \(\lambda(\sigma_t) = \sigma_t^\alpha\) (with tuned \(\alpha\)) is applied to this drift to suppress approximation errors as noise decreases. The formulation adapts to both score-based (ϵ-prediction) and flow-based (v-prediction) models. At inference, one initializes \(x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\) and iteratively updates \(x_t\) using an Euler solver with the guided drift, requiring no additional training or knowledge of the forward operator.

## Key Results
- Outperforms training-free baselines (e.g., SDEdit) on image restoration tasks with lower FID and LPIPS.
- Demonstrates compatibility with both score-based (DDPM) and flow-based (CogVideoX, Wan2.2) pretrained models.
- Achieves competitive results on camera-controlled video generation, reducing FVD and improving CLIP consistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Weighted h-transform modifies the diffusion sampling drift to steer generation toward the ideal fine sample.
- **Mechanism:** A drift term \(g^2(t) \cdot h(x_0=y)\) is subtracted from the reverse SDE's drift (Eq. 6), where \(h(x_0=y)=\nabla_x \log p_t(x_0=y|x_t)\) ensures the process ends at the desired fine sample \(y\). This is derived from Doob's h-transform, which conditions a stochastic process on a fixed endpoint.
- **Core assumption:** The ideal fine sample \(y\) exists and the h-function correctly biases transition probabilities toward it.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "modify the transition probability at each sampling timestep by adding... a drift function h"  
  - [section 3.1] "by minusing \(g^2(t)h_{x_0=y}\) from the original drifting term... the endpoint \(x_0\) must be \(y\)"  
  - [corpus] Related papers (e.g., DEFT) use h-transform for conditioning, but the exact drift formulation here is paper-specific; corpus provides contextual support for h-transform utility in diffusion.
- **Break condition:** If the h-function is miscalculated or the diffusion model's score is inaccurate, the guidance may push toward incorrect samples.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The intractable h-function is approximated using the coarse sample \(\hat{e}\), making the method training-free and operator-agnostic.
- **Mechanism:** \(h(x_0=y) \approx h(x_0=\hat{e}) = (\alpha_t\hat{e} - x_t)/\sigma_t^2 - s_\theta\) (Eq. 12), derived via Bayes' rule and the known conditional distribution \(p_t(x_t|x_0)\). This uses only the coarse input \(\hat{e}\) and the pretrained score predictor.
- **Core assumption:** The coarse sample \(\hat{e}\) is a degraded version of \(y\) such that \(\nabla \log p(x_t|x_0=\hat{e})\) approximates \(\nabla \log p(x_t|x_0=y)\).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.2] Full derivation from Eq. (8) to (12); "we can leverage another tractable \(h_{x_0=\hat{e}}\) to approximate it"  
  - [section 3.3] Error analysis \(J = \|(\alpha_t/\sigma_t^2)(\hat{e}-y)\|^2\), showing dependence on degradation.  
  - [corpus] Papers like "Training-Free Adaptation via Doob's h-Transform" use similar approximations but not for coarse guidance; evidence is conceptual.
- **Break condition:** If \(\hat{e}\) is too dissimilar from \(y\) (e.g., extreme degradation), the approximation error \(J\) becomes large, leading to poor guidance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A noise-level-aware weight schedule \(\lambda(\sigma_t)\) mitigates approximation error by reducing the influence of the approximate h-term as noise decreases.
- **Mechanism:** Set \(\lambda(\sigma_t)\) (e.g., \(\sigma_t^\alpha\)) so that \(\lambda \to 1\) when \(\sigma_t \to 1\) (high noise, low error) and \(\lambda \to 0\) when \(\sigma_t \to 0\) (low noise, high error). This gradually fades the approximation as error grows (Eq. 15).
- **Core assumption:** Approximation error \(J\) is inversely correlated with noise level \(\sigma_t\) (Eq. 14), so weighting by \(\sigma_t\) controls error impact.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.3] "the approximation error will gradually become unbearable... use a noise-level-aware schedule"  
  - [section 3.4] "\(\lambda_\sigma\) is a function of \(\sigma_t\)... (3) \(\lambda_\sigma \to 0\) when \(J \to \infty\)"  
  - [section 5.3] Ablation in Figure 6 shows performance peaks at \(\alpha \approx 5\), confirming schedule importance.  
  - [corpus] Limited direct corpus support; weight scheduling is a novel contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If \(\lambda(\sigma_t)\) is mis-scheduled (too aggressive/lenient), either guidance is lost early or error dominates late, harming quality.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** (1) Pretrained diffusion/flow model (\(s_\theta\) or \(v_\theta\)), (2) Coarse input \(\hat{e}\), (3) Noise schedules \(\alpha_t, \sigma_t\) (from VP-SDE or OT-FM), (4) Weight function \(\lambda(\sigma_t)\), (5) ODE/SDE solver (Euler).
- **Critical path:** Initialize \(x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\); for \(t=T\to0\): compute \(h_{approx} = (\alpha_t\hat{e} - x_t)/\sigma_t^2 - s_\theta\); weight via \(\lambda(\sigma_t) \cdot h_{approx}\); update \(x_{t-1}\) using modified drift from Eq. (15) or Appendix A.3.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - \(\lambda(\sigma_t)\) schedule: Too small → weak guidance; too large → error accumulation.  
  - Diffusion schedule choice: VP-SDE (ϵ-prediction) vs OT-FM (v-prediction) changes guidance term form (Appendix A.3).  
  - Model compatibility: Works with both score-based and flow-based models but requires correct schedule alignment.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Structural deviation from coarse input (if \(\lambda\) too low).  
  - Blurry or unrealistic outputs (if \(\lambda\) too high or schedule flawed).  
  - In video generation, temporal inconsistency or misaligned motion.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Image restoration** on FFHQ: Compare FID/LPIPS against SDEdit and inverse-problem baselines (Table 1); verify guidance without known operator.  
  2. **Camera-controlled video generation** on DL3DV: Compare MSE/LPIPS/FVD against GWTF/TTM; test video model compatibility (Table 2, Figure 5).  
  3. **Ablation on \(\alpha\)** in \(\lambda=\sigma_t^\alpha\): Validate weight schedule necessity (Figure 6, Table A3); observe performance peak at balanced \(\alpha\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the h-transform framework be generalized to a wider range of conditional generation tasks beyond coarse-guided synthesis, such as text-to-image with complex layouts or multi-modal conditions?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "In the future, probability transition modification mechanisms (represented by h-transform) can be further explored in more general conditional generation applications."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only demonstrates on image restoration and camera-controlled video, leaving other applications untested and the extension non-trivial due to differing conditional structures.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments on diverse tasks (e.g., text-to-image with spatial control, audio-driven generation) showing comparable or superior performance to task-specific methods, with analysis of adaptation requirements.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal design for the noise-level-aware weight schedule \(\lambda_\sigma\), and can it be learned or adapted per sample rather than using a fixed power function like \(\sigma^\alpha\)?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses \(\lambda_\sigma = \sigma^\alpha\) with \(\alpha\) manually tuned per task (e.g., \(\alpha=5\) for super-resolution), and ablations show sensitivity to \(\alpha\), indicating that the schedule is critical but heuristic.  
- **Why unresolved:** Manual tuning is inefficient and may not generalize across datasets or models; an adaptive schedule could improve robustness and reduce hyperparameter dependence.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a learning-based weight scheduler that predicts \(\lambda_\sigma\) from input features or error estimates, validated on multiple tasks with consistent gains over fixed schedules.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How robust is the method to real-world degradations where the forward operator is unknown, non-linear, or mismatched with the approximation \(h_{x_0=\hat{e}}\)?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The approximation assumes \(\hat{e}\) is a degraded version of \(y\) under a known process (e.g., bicubic downsampling), but experiments use synthetic degradations; real-world cases (e.g., compression artifacts, sensor noise) may violate this.  
- **Why unresolved:** The error analysis focuses on noise level \(\sigma_t\), not degradation model mismatch, so performance on arbitrary, unknown degradations is unclear.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on real-world degraded datasets (e.g., for super-resolution with camera noise) without assuming a forward operator, comparing with methods that handle unknown degradations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the h-transform approach maintain its benefits in latent diffusion models, which operate in compressed spaces with different noise schedules, or does it require significant modifications?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper claims compatibility with score-based and flow-based models but tests only on pixel-space or specific models (CogVideoX, Wan2.2); latent spaces (e.g., Stable Diffusion) have altered dynamics not covered in derivations.  
- **Why unresolved:** The provided derivations assume pixel-space SDEs; latent diffusion changes the noise schedule and semantics, potentially affecting the h-transform's drift term and error behavior.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation in latent space (e.g., with Stable Diffusion) for image restoration tasks, measuring performance against pixel-space baselines and analyzing any necessary adjustments.

## Limitations
- Exact noise schedules (\(\alpha_t, \sigma_t\)) for VP-SDE/OT-FM and sampling hyperparameters (step count M, \(\Delta t\)) are not disclosed, hindering precise replication.
- The video coarse-generation pipeline details (depth estimation, resolution, Z-buffering, hole-filling) are underspecified; small variations may affect results.
- The weight schedule \(\lambda(\sigma_t)=\sigma^\alpha\) requires per-task manual tuning (e.g., \(\alpha\) ≈5–7 for images, ≈4/8 for video), and the optimal choice lacks theoretical grounding.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (h-transform drift modification): **High** (direct application of Doob’s theorem)
- Mechanism 2 (coarse approximation for tractability): **Medium** (validated empirically but error scales with degradation)
- Mechanism 3 (noise-level-aware weighting mitigates error): **Medium** (heuristic schedule; not proven optimal)
