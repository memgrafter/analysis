# Global Convergence of Average Reward Constrained MDPs with Neural Critic and General Policy Parameterization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07698
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07698
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proves global convergence and cumulative constraint violation rates of $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ for infinite‑horizon average‑reward CMDPs with general policy parameterizations and neural critics, using a primal‑dual natural actor‑critic algorithm that removes the need for mixing‑time oracles.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles a theoretical gap in constrained reinforcement learning for infinite‑horizon average‑reward CMDPs when deep neural network critics and general policies are used. Existing analyses are limited to tabular or linear critics and often require knowledge of mixing times. The authors introduce PDNAC‑NC, a primal‑dual natural actor‑critic method that integrates a multi‑layer neural critic (analyzed via the Neural Tangent Kernel) and a Multi‑Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator for unbiased gradient estimation under Markovian sampling. MLMC eliminates the mixing‑time oracle. The algorithm achieves global convergence and cumulative constraint violation bounded by $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ (up to approximation errors), with step sizes $\alpha=\beta=O(T^{-1/4})$, outer iterations $K=O(T^{1/2})$, and inner iterations $H=O(T^{1/2})$. This provides the first such guarantees for average‑reward CMDPs with neural critics and general policies.

## Method Summary
PDNAC‑NC follows a nested loop structure. An outer loop (executed $K$ times) stores the policy parameters $\theta$ and dual variable $\lambda$. Inside each outer iteration, two inner loops (each $H$ steps) run:

1. **Critic inner loop:** Estimates the average reward/cost $\eta$ and the $Q$‑function using a multi‑layer neural network. The network’s weights $\zeta$ are projected onto a ball $S_R$ around the initialization to remain within the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime, where linearization holds. Multi‑Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) – sampling rollout lengths from a geometric distribution and aggregating across levels – yields an unbiased estimate of the infinite‑horizon return without discarding data or requiring mixing‑time knowledge.

2. **Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) inner loop:** With the current $Q$ estimates, computes the natural gradient direction $\omega$ by minimizing a quadratic objective; the gradient and advantage terms are also estimated via MLMC.

Finally, the outer loop updates: $\theta \gets \theta + \alpha (\omega_r + \lambda \omega_c)$ and $\lambda \gets \Pi_{[0,2/\delta]}(\lambda - \beta \eta_c)$. The theoretical analysis chooses $\alpha=\beta=O(T^{-1/4})$, $K=H=O(T^{1/2})$, MLMC truncation $T_{\text{max}}=T$, and projection radius $R=O(\log T)$.

## Key Results
- **Global convergence:** The optimality gap $J_r(\theta) - J_r(\theta^*)$ decays at a rate $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$.
- **Constraint violation:** Cumulative constraint violation $\sum_{t=1}^T \bigl(-J_c(\theta_t)\bigr)_+$ is bounded by $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$.
- **First of its kind:** Provides the first convergence guarantees for average‑reward CMDPs with neural critics and general (non‑linear) policy parameterizations, while avoiding assumptions about mixing times.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: MLMC eliminates mixing‑time oracle
- **Claim:** The Multi‑Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator provides unbiased gradient estimates under Markovian sampling without discarding data, thus removing the need for mixing‑time oracles.
- **Mechanism:** Trajectory lengths are sampled from a geometric distribution; the multi‑level aggregation cancels correlation bias while using only $O(\log T_{\text{max}})$ samples on average. The correction term $2^Q (g_Q - g_{Q-1})$ systematically offsets bias from Markovian rollouts. Bounded variance of per‑step gradients (Assumptions 5, 8) and ergodicity (Assumption 1) ensure validity.
- **Break condition:** If the MDP is not ergodic or per‑step gradients have unbounded variance, the MLMC bias/variance bounds collapse, and the convergence guarantee fails.

### Mechanism 2: NTK projection stabilizes neural critic
- **Claim:** Projecting the neural critic parameters onto a ball around initialization keeps the network within the NTK regime, where the linearized model accurately approximates the true $Q$‑function with error $O(m^{-1/2})$.
- **Mechanism:** After each critic update, weights are projected onto $S_R := \|\zeta - \zeta_0\|_2 \le R$. Within this ball, the NTK governs the network’s behavior, making the first‑order Taylor expansion accurate. The critic update effectively solves a linear system in the NTK feature space. The projection radius $R$ is chosen as $O(\log T)$ to balance stability and expressivity.
- **Break condition:** If the weights exit $S_R$, the linearization error grows, critic estimates become biased, and the NPG direction degrades, potentially causing divergence.

### Mechanism 3: Nested primal‑dual loop yields global convergence
- **Claim:** The outer primal‑dual updates combined with inner MLMC‑corrected NPG steps achieve global convergence despite the non‑contractive Bellman operator and coupled constraints.
- **Mechanism:** For each outer iteration $k$, two inner loops run: (i) the critic loop produces estimates of the average reward/cost $\eta_g$ and the $Q$‑function $\zeta_g$ using MLMC; (ii) the NPG loop solves $\min_{\omega} \frac{1}{2} \omega^\top F(\theta) \omega - \omega^\top \nabla_\theta L(\theta,\lambda)$ with MLMC gradient estimates. Error propagation from critic and NPG estimation is bounded via contraction arguments; dual stability relies on Slater’s condition (Assumption 3), ensuring a strictly feasible policy.
- **Break condition:** Violating Slater’s condition or having a near‑singular Fisher matrix can destabilize the dual updates or amplify noise in the NPG step, leading to high constraint violation or divergence.

## Foundational Learning

### Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Theory
- **Why needed:** Allows analysis of deep neural networks by linearizing around initialization; the complex critic behaves like a linear model with a fixed kernel, enabling control of approximation error.
- **Quick check:** Under NTK, the linearization error decays as $O(m^{-1/2})$ as network width $m$ increases.

### Multi‑Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) Estimation
- **Why needed:** Provides unbiased estimates of infinite‑horizon returns under Markovian sampling without discarding transitions, thus removing the mixing‑time oracle.
- **Quick check:** Sampling rollout lengths from a geometric distribution yields an unbiased estimator because the expected sample count per level is finite and bias cancels across levels; mathematically $\mathbb{E}[\text{MLMC estimate}] = \mathbb{E}[G_\infty]$.

### Natural Policy Gradient (NPG)
- **Why needed:** Preconditions the policy update with the Fisher information matrix, leading to better conditioning and faster convergence than vanilla gradient, particularly under function approximation.
- **Quick check:** The Fisher matrix $F(\theta)$ rescales the gradient to follow the natural Riemannian metric of the policy manifold; the update $\theta \gets \theta + \alpha F(\theta)^{-1} \nabla L$ tends to be more stable and less sensitive to scaling.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Outer storage (θ, λ) -> Critic inner loop (η_g, ζ_g) -> NPG inner loop (ω_g) -> Primal‑dual update -> updated (θ, λ)

**Critical path**
1. Initialize neural critic weights randomly.
2. For each outer iteration $k = 1$ to $K$:
   - Run $H$ steps of critic inner loop: sample geometric rollout lengths, collect trajectories, compute MLMC estimates for $\eta_r$, $\eta_c$, and update neural $\zeta$ with projection onto $S_R$. Output $\eta_g$, $\zeta_g$.
   - Run $H$ steps of NPG inner loop: using $\zeta_g$, compute advantage estimates via MLMC, solve the quadratic for $\omega$ using stochastic gradient descent. Output $\omega_g$.
   - Update $\theta \gets \theta + \alpha (\omega_r + \lambda \omega_c)$ and $\lambda \gets \Pi_{[0,2/\delta]}(\lambda - \beta \eta_c)$.

**Design tradeoffs**
- NTK projection radius $R$: Larger $R$ increases expressivity but risks leaving the linear regime; paper sets $R = O(\log T)$.
- MLMC truncation $T_{\text{max}}$: Larger $T_{\text{max}}$ reduces bias but increases per‑iteration cost; analysis uses $T_{\text{max}} = T$ (total steps), which must be chosen a priori.
- Network width $m$: Approximation error scales as $O(m^{-1/4})$; to reach $\varepsilon$‑optimality, $m = \Omega(T)$ may be needed, which is computationally heavy.
- Inner loop length $H$ vs outer $K$: Both set to $\Theta(T^{1/2})$ to balance inner‑loop optimization error and number of outer updates.

**Failure signatures**
- Constraint violation remains high: $\beta$ too small or critic bias too large.
- Policy oscillates or diffuses: NPG estimation error too high; increase $H$ or check MLMC variance.
- Neural weights explode: projection radius $R$ too small or critic step size $\gamma_\xi$ too large.
- Dual $\lambda$ stuck at upper bound: Slater condition may be violated or $\beta$ too large.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **MLMC bias validation:** On a simple tabular CMDP, compare bias of average reward estimates from MLMC vs. ordinary averages as $T_{\text{max}}$ varies; expect $O(T_{\text{max}}^{-1})$ decay.
2. **NTK regime stability:** During training, monitor $\|\zeta - \zeta_0\|$ and compare critic predictions with the linearized model; deviation should be $O(m^{-1/2})$.
3. **Primal‑dual convergence on toy CMDP:** Use a small state space with tabular softmax policy; sweep $\alpha$, $\beta$, $K$, $H$. Measure optimality gap and constraint violation; they should follow the $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ trend.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Improving the convergence rate:** Can the $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ rate be improved to an order‑optimal $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/2})$? The current analysis uses a mean squared error bound for the critic; a sharper squared bias bound would be needed.
- **Beyond the NTK regime:** Can the analysis be extended to capture feature learning in deep neural networks beyond lazy training? This requires non‑linear frameworks like mean‑field theory.
- **Relaxing ergodicity:** Can the uniform ergodicity assumption be weakened to allow non‑ergodic or unichain MDPs (e.g., with absorbing states)? The present analysis requires every policy to induce an ergodic Markov chain.

## Limitations
- **Strong MDP assumptions:** Ergodicity, Slater’s condition, bounded Fisher information, and finite mixing time may not hold in many realistic environments.
- **NTK regime constraints:** Requires very wide networks and strict projection to stay near initialization; finite‑width effects are not quantified and the required width may be impractically large.
- **Hidden logarithmic factors:** The $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ rate hides poly‑logarithmic factors that are not explicitly given, making precise performance prediction hard.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Step sizes $\alpha$, $\beta$, inner loop lengths $H$, $K$, projection radius $R$, and truncation $T_{\text{max}}$ need careful tuning with little guidance.

## Confidence
- **$\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate:** Medium (depends on strong assumptions; empirical evidence limited).
- **MLMC unbiasedness / removal of mixing‑time oracle:** High (well‑established technique).
- **NTK critic error control:** Medium (asymptotically valid; finite‑width effects uncertain).
- **Practical feasibility:** Low (many interdependent hyperparameters and stringent assumptions).

## Next Checks
1. **MLMC bias verification:** Empirically confirm $O(T_{\text{max}}^{-1})$ bias decay on a simple CMDP.
2. **NTK stability monitoring:** Track $\|\zeta - \zeta_0\|$ and linearization error; verify they remain within theoretical envelopes.
3. **Scaling experiment:** Run PDNAC‑NC with increasing total steps $T$ on a test CMDP; plot optimality gap and constraint violation on log‑log axes; slopes should approach $-1/4$.
