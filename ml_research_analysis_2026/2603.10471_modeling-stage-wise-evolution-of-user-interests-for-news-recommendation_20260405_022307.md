# Modeling Stage-wise Evolution of User Interests for News Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10471  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10471  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Outperforms CROWN on Adressa (AUC 0.7993 vs 0.6553, MRR 0.5223 vs 0.3915) and MIND (AUC 0.5804 vs 0.5501, MRR 0.4368 vs 0.3431).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the limitation of static interaction graphs in modeling time-sensitive user interests for news recommendation. It proposes a unified framework that jointly learns global long‑term preferences via LightGCN on the full user‑news interaction graph and local stage‑wise preferences by partitioning user history into temporal subgraphs. Within each temporal window, a local GCN refines embeddings, and two branches—LSTM for short‑term sequential dynamics and self‑attention for long‑range aggregation over cumulative prefixes—capture interest evolution. The model fuses global, short‑term, and long‑range representations, regularized to maintain consistency across temporal scales and smooth transitions between windows. Experiments on Adressa and MIND show substantial improvements over prior state‑of‑the‑art, demonstrating the effectiveness of explicitly modeling stage‑wise interest dynamics.

## Method Summary
The approach comprises three components: (1) a global preference module builds a bipartite user‑news graph from all interactions and applies LightGCN to generate stable long‑term embeddings; (2) a local preference module splits each user’s click history into consecutive temporal windows (e.g., 2 weeks on Adressa), constructs a subgraph per window, runs a local GCN (initialized with global embeddings) to obtain window‑specific embeddings, and then processes these with an LSTM (short‑term branch) and a self‑attention mechanism over cumulative clicked items (long‑range branch); (3) a fusion step sums the global, short‑term, and long‑range embeddings for both user and item, followed by a dot product and sigmoid to predict click probability. Training minimizes binary cross‑entropy per segment, augmented with consistency regularization (aligning local and global embeddings), smoothness regularization (encouraging gradual changes between windows), and ℓ2 weight decay, using Adam with a low learning rate (5e‑6) and negative sampling (1:4).

## Key Results
- On Adressa, the model achieves AUC 0.7993 (vs. CROWN 0.6553), MRR 0.5223 (vs. 0.3915), and nDCG@10 0.5327 (vs. 0.5283).  
- On MIND, it attains AUC 0.5804 (vs. 0.5501) and MRR 0.4368 (vs. 0.3431), confirming consistent and significant gains.  
- Ablation studies show that the local modeling (especially the LSTM branch) contributes the majority of the performance boost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model’s effectiveness stems from its explicit separation of temporal scales. The global LightGCN captures stable, long‑term preferences that persist across the entire interaction history, providing a robust baseline. The local modeling acknowledges that user interests evolve in stages—short‑term fluctuations within a window are captured by the LSTM, while medium‑term shifts across windows are modeled by the self‑attention branch that aggregates cumulative history. The fusion of all three sources allows the model to weight each according to the current context. Moreover, consistency regularization prevents local branches from overfitting to noisy short‑term data, and smoothness regularization enforces gradual transitions between windows, aligning with typical interest persistence. This design directly counters the key limitation of static graphs by introducing temporal granularity while maintaining stability.

## Foundational Learning
- **LightGCN**: Why needed: Efficiently propagates information across the bipartite user‑news graph to learn global collaborative signals. Quick check: Train on a tiny graph; verify that connected user‑news pairs have higher cosine similarity than unconnected ones.  
- **LSTM**: Why needed: Models sequential dependencies within a temporal window, capturing how recent clicks influence current interest. Quick check: Fit an LSTM to predict the next token in a short sequence; ensure loss decreases and hidden state changes over time.  
- **Self‑Attention**: Why needed: Aggregates all previously clicked items in a cumulative prefix without recurrence, weighting items by relevance to capture long‑range influence. Quick check: Apply a self‑attention layer to a sequence of random vectors; confirm attention weights sum to 1 and output is a weighted sum.  
- **Temporal Subgraph Partitioning**: Why needed: Isolates interactions occurring in specific time windows to model stage‑wise evolution and avoid temporal leakage. Quick check: For a user with timestamped clicks, split into non‑overlapping windows; ensure no edge appears in multiple subgraphs.  
- **Consistency Regularization**: Why needed: Aligns local (window‑specific) user embeddings with the global user embedding, preventing overfitting to short‑term noise. Quick check: During training, compute MSE between global and local user embeddings; verify it decreases and remains small.  
- **Smoothness Regularization**: Why needed: Encourages gradual changes between consecutive windows, reflecting natural interest evolution. Quick check: For a user’s embeddings in adjacent windows, compute L2 distance; confirm it is smaller than for random user‑window pairs.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
User History -> Global LightGCN -> Global Embeddings -> Temporal Splitting -> Local GCN (per window) -> LSTM & Self‑Attention Branches -> Fusion (sum with global) -> Prediction (dot product + sigmoid)

**Critical Path**:  
1. Build full bipartite graph from all training interactions.  
2. Run LightGCN to obtain global user and news embeddings.  
3. For each user, partition click history into fixed‑size temporal windows.  
4. For each window, construct subgraph of interactions within that window; apply local GCN (initialized with global embeddings) to get window‑specific embeddings.  
5. For the user, feed the sequence of window‑specific user embeddings into LSTM to get short‑term representation; for each window, take cumulative prefix of clicked item embeddings, apply self‑attention to get long‑range representation.  
6. Fuse global, short‑term, and long‑range embeddings for user; fuse global and short‑term for item.  
7. Compute dot product between final user and item embeddings, apply sigmoid to predict click probability.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Fixed window size is simple but ignores individual variation in interest change frequency; adaptive segmentation could help but increases complexity.  
- Dual branches (LSTM vs. self‑attention) balance local sequential modeling and long‑range aggregation, but increase computational cost.  
- Summation fusion is lightweight but may not optimally weight contributions; learned gating could improve performance.  
- Regularizations (consistency, smoothness) stabilize training but may dampen abrupt interest shifts (e.g., breaking news).  
- Graph‑based approach benefits from collaborative signal but suffers from sparsity in short windows.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Global‑only model performs similarly to full model → local branches not properly integrated.  
- Training loss decreases while validation AUC stays low → likely temporal leakage or incorrect window splitting.  
- Embeddings collapse to near‑identical vectors → regularization too strong or poor initialization.  
- Performance highly sensitive to window size → indicates lack of robustness; consider adaptive windows.  
- Inference time excessive → self‑attention over long prefixes may need limiting or approximation.

**Exactly 3 First Experiments**:  
1. **Baseline sanity check**: Train global LightGCN alone; verify it achieves reasonable AUC (≈0.6 on Adressa) and improves over random.  
2. **Local branch isolation**: Freeze global embeddings; train only local module (LSTM + self‑attention) on a single window; confirm adding local branches yields >0.01 AUC gain over global‑only.  
3. **Regularization ablation**: Train full model without consistency and smoothness losses; compare performance to full model to verify their contribution; also monitor the regularization loss values for sanity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive temporal segmentation**: How can time intervals be personalized per user to better match individual interest change patterns? Fixed windows may misalign with actual shift points.  
- **Cold‑start users**: How does the model handle new or sparsely active users where both global and local graphs are sparse? The paper does not address this practical scenario.  
- **Generalizability to other domains**: Can the global‑local balance extend beyond news to domains with longer item lifespans or slower interest evolution (e.g., e‑commerce, movies)? The focus is exclusively on news datasets.  
- **Capturing abrupt shifts**: Does the combination of fixed windows and smoothness regularization hinder detection of sudden interest changes (e.g., breaking news)? The model may smooth out such dynamics.

## Limitations
- Local modeling details are ambiguous: unclear whether GCN parameters are shared across windows and the exact LSTM/self‑attention configurations (layers, heads, positional encoding).  
- MIND validation split is poorly defined due to lack of timestamps; the pseudo‑period construction is ad‑hoc and may undermine reproducibility.  
- Negative sampling strategy during training is vague (uniform global vs. time‑aware), which could affect training dynamics and results.

## Confidence
- Global modeling (LightGCN) & fusion mechanism: **High**  
- Local modeling design (temporal subgraphs + dual branches): **Medium**  
- Performance claims (AUC/MRR lifts): **Low**
