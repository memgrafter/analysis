# GPT-4o Lacks Core Features of Theory of Mind

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12150
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12150
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: GPT-4o fails to demonstrate a coherent, abstract, and consistent Theory of Mind, despite surface-level social proficiency.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether GPT-4o possesses a genuine Theory of Mind (ToM)—a unified causal model linking mental states to behavior—rather than merely mimicking human social judgments. Using two logically equivalent paradigms (ContainerWorld and MovieWorld), the authors evaluate three core ToM features: coherence (alignment with a human-like model), abstractness (generalization across paradigms), and consistency (reciprocal relationship between forward action prediction and inverse mental-state inference). Results show that while GPT-4o exhibits within-domain coherence, its predictions do not generalize across paradigms and its forward and inverse models are inconsistent, indicating an absence of a true ToM. The findings suggest that current LLMs, despite impressive social capabilities, lack the abstract causal reasoning that underlies human mentalizing.

## Method Summary
The study employs two fully enumerable, text-based paradigms: **ContainerWorld**, where an agent chooses among containers based on beliefs about contents, desires for specific items, and physical costs; and **MovieWorld**, which is logically equivalent but uses genres, screening times, and temporal costs. For each paradigm, the model (GPT-4o) is queried to produce distributions over actions given mental states (forward model, ℱ︀) and over mental states given actions (inverse model, ℐ︀). These outputs are compared against a normative HumanToM model (incorporating beliefs, desires, and costs) and several ablations (e.g., ignoring costs or beliefs). Coherence is measured by agreement percentages; abstractness by Pearson correlation of predictions/inferences across the two paradigms; consistency by Bayesian inversion (correlating ℱ︀-inferred ℐ︀ with actual ℐ︀) and validity (using inferred mental states in ℱ︀ to recover the original action). The LLM is accessed via OpenAI API with temperature=1, top_p=1, and forced JSON responses.

## Key Results
- **Coherence**: Within ContainerWorld, GPT-4o's action predictions aligned strongly with HumanToM (87.24% agreement), and with ablations that omitted cost or belief information, indicating some coherent pattern-matching.
- **Abstractness**: Cross-paradigm correlations were low: desire inference *r* = 0.18, joint inference *r* = 0.39, and action predictions *r* = 0.48, showing that mental-state representations did not generalize to the logically equivalent MovieWorld.
- **Consistency**: Forward and inverse distributions were not reciprocally related; Bayesian inversion correlations were near zero or negative (e.g., belief inference in ContainerWorld), and validity agreements were well below ceiling (e.g., 55.56% for joint inference), violating constraints of a unified causal model.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper attributes GPT-4o's surface-level ToM performance to three distinct failure mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Statistical Pattern-Matching for Within-Domain Coherence**
- **Claim**: GPT-4o demonstrates coherence in ContainerWorld by pattern-matching on surface correlations between mental-state descriptors and actions, not by deploying a causal model.
- **Mechanism**: The LLM leverages statistical regularities in its training data to generate token sequences that align with human judgments when prompts explicitly enumerate beliefs, desires, and states, effectively simulating a forward model (ℱ︀) without internal causal reasoning.
- **Core assumption**: Human-level performance on simple ToM tasks can emerge from next-token prediction alone, bypassing the need for a unified theory.
- **Evidence**: In Study 1, GPT-4o's action predictions agreed with HumanToM at 87.24%.
- **Break condition**: When superficial features shift (e.g., physical cost in ContainerWorld replaced by temporal cost in MovieWorld), agreement attenuates, exposing reliance on domain-specific cues.

**Mechanism 2: Paradigm-Bound Heuristics Prevent Abstraction**
- **Claim**: GPT-4o fails to form abstract, domain-general mental-state representations; its inferences are tied to the lexical and narrative context of each paradigm.
- **Mechanism**: The model encodes context-sensitive heuristics that do not transfer across logically equivalent scenarios, preventing the emergence of a unified causal principle.
- **Core assumption**: True ToM requires abstraction across varied contexts—a capability not induced by standard language modeling objectives.
- **Evidence**: Study 2 reports low cross-paradigm correlations: desire inference *r* = 0.18, joint inference *r* = 0.39, indicating weak abstraction.
- **Break condition**: Cross-paradigm correlations for desire and joint inferences fall below *r* = 0.4.

**Mechanism 3: Disjoint Forward and Inverse Models**
- **Claim**: GPT-4o's forward action predictions (ℱ︀) and inverse mental-state inferences (ℐ︀) are inconsistent, violating the reciprocal constraints of a causal model.
- **Mechanism**: Separate, unintegrated processes govern prediction and inference—likely due to disparate training signals—resulting in distributions that do not satisfy Bayesian inversion or validity checks.
- **Core assumption**: A ToM must be bidirectional; the same causal graph should underlie both ℱ︀ and ℐ︀.
- **Evidence**: Study 3 shows Bayesian inversion correlations near zero or negative (e.g., belief inference in ContainerWorld) and validity agreements below ceiling (e.g., 55.56% for joint inference).
- **Break condition**: Bayesian correlation and validity agreement are significantly below theoretical ceiling (e.g., <80%).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: LLM (GPT-4o via API) → ContainerWorld / MovieWorld → Action predictions (ℱ︀) & Mental-state inferences (ℐ︀) → Comparison to HumanToM & ablations → Evaluation metrics (coherence agreement %, cross-paradigm correlations, Bayesian inversion correlation, validity %).

**Critical path**:
1. Design paradigms with parametrically varying beliefs, desires, and states.
2. Craft system and user prompts with explicit JSON response schema.
3. Query GPT-4o for all tuples of each paradigm, extracting probability distributions over actions or mental states.
4. Compare the model’s distributions to the candidate normative models (HumanToM and ablations) to assess coherence.
5. Test abstractness by correlating outputs between ContainerWorld and MovieWorld.
6. Evaluate consistency via Bayesian inversion (correlate ℱ︀-inferred ℐ︀ with actual ℐ︀) and validity checks (use inferred mental states in ℱ︀ to recover original action).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Chose simplified, fully enumerable paradigms to enable exhaustive coverage of the state space, sacrificing ecological validity for control.
- Fixed sampling parameters (temperature=1, top_p=1) to balance determinism and natural variability, while using JSON output to ensure structured parsing.
- Focused on a single LLM (GPT-4o) to allow deep analysis, limiting generalizability across models.
- Used explicit enumeration of mental-state variables in prompts to directly probe the model’s internal representations.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low cross-paradigm correlation (*r* < 0.5 for desire and joint inferences) indicating poor abstraction.
- Bayesian inversion correlations near zero or negative, and validity agreement percentages well below the theoretical ceiling (<80%).
- Drop in HumanToM agreement when moving from ContainerWorld to MovieWorld, suggesting reliance on superficial cues.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Replicate Study 1 (Coherence in ContainerWorld)**: Implement ContainerWorld with all 243 (9 beliefs × 3 desires × 9 states) tuples. Query GPT-4o for action predictions. Compute agreement percentages with HumanToM and each ablation.
2. **Replicate Study 2 (Abstractness across paradigms)**: Implement MovieWorld with the same number of tuples, using a 1:1 mapping of variables. For each output type (action predictions, desire inference, joint inference), compute Pearson correlation between ContainerWorld and MovieWorld results.
3. **Replicate Study 3 (Consistency)**: For each paradigm, compute Bayesian inversion by feeding the ℱ︀ outputs into the inverse calculation to derive an expected ℐ︀ distribution, then correlate with the actual ℐ︀. Also perform validity checks: use the inferred belief-desire pair from ℐ︀ in the forward model ℱ︀ and compare the predicted action to the original action; record agreement percentage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability to other LLMs**: The study only tested GPT-4o; it is unknown whether other models exhibit the same ToM deficiencies. Resolving this requires replicating the three core studies (coherence, abstractness, consistency) across a diverse set of LLMs (e.g., Claude, Gemini, Llama).
2. **Scaling and training objectives**: As models grow larger or are trained differently, will they develop a genuine ToM or merely improve surface-level social skills? This could be addressed by systematically evaluating next-generation LLMs using the same framework combined with mechanistic analysis to detect emergent causal representations.
3. **Framework generalizability**: Can the coherence-abstractness-consistency evaluation be applied to other folk theories (e.g., intuitive physics, economics)? Future work should construct logically equivalent paradigms for these domains and test them across LLMs.
4. **Prompt engineering effects**: To what extent do prompt wording, response format, and sampling parameters affect the observed ToM failures? A systematic manipulation of these factors (e.g., natural language vs. structured prompts, varying temperature, multi-turn dialogs) is needed to determine if abstractness or consistency can be improved.

## Limitations
- Prompt fidelity: Exact system/user prompts and JSON schemas were reconstructed; minor variations could alter outputs.
- Candidate model logic: The normative HumanToM and ablation specifications (e.g., cost scaling, belief integration) are based on descriptions; precise utility calculations are unclear.
- Cross-paradigm mapping: The assumed 1:1 equivalence between ContainerWorld and MovieWorld variables (e.g., physical vs. temporal cost) was not explicitly validated, possibly affecting abstraction measurements.
