# CWM: Contrastive World Models for Action Feasibility Learning in Embodied Agent Pipelines

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22452
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22452
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** CWM improves Precision@1 on minimal-edit negatives by +6.76 pp (93.2% vs. 86.5%) and AUC-ROC (0.929 vs. 0.906) over SFT; better OOD safety margin (-2.39 vs. -3.96).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a key bottleneck in embodied AI: training an action feasibility scorer that reliably distinguishes physically valid actions from subtle invalid ones. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of large language models (LLMs) fails on hard negatives. The proposed Contrastive World Model (CWM) fine-tunes an LLM with an InfoNCE contrastive objective, using hard-mined negative examples to explicitly rank valid actions above invalid ones. On the ScienceWorld benchmark, CWM achieves significant gains on the hardest test category (minimal-edit negatives) and demonstrates superior robustness in out-of-distribution stress tests. This work highlights the effectiveness of contrastive learning for capturing precise physical feasibility constraints in embodied agent pipelines.

## Method Summary
CWM extends a pretrained LLM with a contrastive fine-tuning stage. Given a state description and multiple candidate actions (one valid, others invalid), the model produces embeddings for each. The InfoNCE loss encourages the embedding of the valid action to be more similar to the state embedding than any invalid action embedding. The invalid actions are specifically hard-mined via minimal edits to the valid action, creating challenging negatives that SFT cannot reliably discriminate. This direct optimization for ranking forces the model to learn nuanced feasibility distinctions. At inference, the model scores actions by their similarity to the state embedding, enabling a feasible action ranking.

## Key Results
- On ScienceWorld's minimal-edit negative test set, CWM achieves Precision@1 of 93.2% vs. SFT's 86.5% (+6.76 pp).
- CWM attains a higher AUC-ROC (0.929) compared to SFT (0.906).
- In an out-of-distribution live filter scenario, CWM maintains a better safety margin (-2.39) than SFT (-3.96), indicating more robust ranking under stress.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SFT learns to generate valid actions from positive examples but does not explicitly train the model to reject invalid ones, particularly those that are only slightly altered. The contrastive objective with hard negatives directly optimizes for a ranking where valid actions are preferred over invalid ones. By mining negatives that are deliberately challenging (minimal edits), the model learns fine-grained constraints of action feasibility. This explicit discrimination leads to better generalization on hard negatives and improved robustness when faced with out-of-distribution actions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Learning**: Learns representations by pulling similar samples together and pushing dissimilar ones apart. *Why needed*: Enables explicit ranking of valid vs. invalid actions. *Quick check*: Does the model assign higher similarity scores to valid actions than to invalid ones?
- **InfoNCE Loss**: A contrastive loss that maximizes mutual information estimation. *Why needed*: Provides a differentiable objective for ranking. *Quick check*: Is the loss decreasing during training, indicating better separation?
- **Hard Negative Mining**: Selecting negative examples that are challenging for the model. *Why needed*: Forces learning of subtle features, prevents trivial solutions. *Quick check*: Are the mined negatives indeed hard (low initial model scores)?
- **World Models**: Internal models that predict environment dynamics. *Why needed*: Action feasibility requires understanding state transitions. *Quick check*: Does the model implicitly capture physical consequences of actions?
- **Action Feasibility**: Determining whether an action can be executed in a given state. *Why needed*: Critical for embodied agents to avoid invalid plans. *Quick check*: Can the model correctly judge feasibility of novel actions?
- **Embodied Agents**: Agents that interact with physical or simulated environments. *Why needed*: The problem domain. *Quick check*: Does the feasibility scorer improve downstream task success?

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map*: Pretrained LLM (shared parameters for state and action encoding) -> Embedding vectors for state and each candidate action -> Projection head (optional) -> Similarity scoring (dot product) -> InfoNCE loss over a batch.

*Critical path*: 1) Collect environment trajectories and mine hard negatives via minimal edits. 2) Fine-tune LLM with contrastive objective on batches of (state, valid_action, hard_negatives). 3) At deployment, encode state and candidate actions, compute similarity scores, rank actions.

*Design tradeoffs*: Hard negative mining increases computational cost but yields harder discriminative tasks; using a frozen LLM vs. fine-tuning all parameters trades off stability vs. adaptability; the size of the negative pool per batch affects gradient signal strength.

*Failure signatures*: Model assigns high scores to invalid actions with superficial similarity (e.g., actions that differ only in object type); performance degrades on actions with large deviations from the mined negative distribution; overfitting to specific hard-negative patterns seen during training.

*First three experiments*: 1) Reproduce SFT baseline: train with cross-entropy on positive actions only. 2) CWM with random negatives: replace hard-mined negatives with random invalid actions. 3) CWM with hard-mined negatives: full method. Compare performance on minimal-edit negatives and OOD tests.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, its *Limitations* section implies several key directions for future research: (1) generalizability beyond the ScienceWorld benchmark to other embodied environments (e.g., ALFRED, VirtualHome); (2) extension to multimodal inputs (vision-language-action pipelines); (3) deeper analysis of how negative mining strategies affect feature learning and bias.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark Dependence:** Results are only on ScienceWorld; generalizability to other embodied environments is untested.
- **Modality Scope:** The model is text-only; its applicability to vision-language-action pipelines is unknown.
- **Training Data Opacity:** The hard-mined negatives' source and diversity are not detailed, raising concerns about biases and coverage.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** CWM significantly outperforms SFT on the minimal-edit negative category (direct measured result).
- **Medium Confidence:** CWM's superior OOD safety margin indicates robust ranking under stress (based on a single stress test scenario).
- **Medium Confidence:** The InfoNCE contrastive objective with hard negatives is the primary cause (ablation likely supportive but confounding factors remain).

## Next Checks
1. Cross-benchmark replication: Reimplement CWM and evaluate on at least two additional embodied AI benchmarks (e.g., ALFRED, VirtualHome).
2. Multimodal extension test: Integrate a vision encoder (e.g., CLIP) to create a vision-language CWM and evaluate on a visually-grounded action feasibility dataset.
3. Negative mining ablation study: Isolate the effect of negative hardness by comparing random negatives vs. hard-mined negatives under the same InfoNCE objective.
