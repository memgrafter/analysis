# Late-to-Early Training: LET LLMs Learn Earlier, So Faster and Better

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05393
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05393
- Reference count: 40
- Up to 1.6× speedup in convergence and 5% improvement in downstream task accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LET (Late-to-Early Training), a novel pretraining paradigm that accelerates LLM training by leveraging representations from smaller, pretrained models. LET enables early layers of the target model to learn from late-layer representations of a smaller pretrained model during initial training steps. The approach uses two complementary mechanisms: late-to-early-step learning and late-to-early-layer learning. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in both convergence speed and final performance across multiple model sizes.

## Method Summary
LET introduces a two-pronged approach to accelerate LLM pretraining by transferring knowledge from smaller pretrained models. The first mechanism, late-to-early-step learning, applies during early training steps where the target model's early layers are aligned with late layers of a pretrained model. The second mechanism, late-to-early-layer learning, maintains this alignment throughout training with a decaying loss weight. The method adds a cosine similarity-based alignment loss to the standard causal language modeling objective, with the alignment weight decaying linearly to zero. Experiments use The Pile dataset and evaluate on nine downstream tasks including HellaSwag, Winogrande, and ARC.

## Key Results
- Achieved up to 1.6× speedup in convergence compared to standard training
- Improved downstream task accuracy by up to 5% across nine evaluation tasks
- Demonstrated effectiveness even when pretrained model has 10× fewer parameters than target model
- Showed LET performs comparably to continued pretraining of the pretrained model as initialization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LET works by providing informative gradients to early layers of the target model through alignment with mature representations from a smaller pretrained model. During early training steps, when the target model's layers are poorly initialized and would otherwise learn slowly, the alignment loss guides them toward representations that have already been optimized in the pretrained model. As training progresses, the alignment weight decays, allowing the target model to gradually develop its own representations while having already established a strong foundation. This accelerates the learning process and helps avoid poor local minima.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cosine similarity loss**: Used to measure alignment between representations - needed for computing the alignment objective; check by verifying values between -1 and 1
- **Layer-wise knowledge transfer**: Concept of transferring information from specific layers - needed to understand which layers to align; check by examining layer activation distributions
- **Decaying loss weight schedule**: Linear decay from initial value to zero - needed to gradually remove alignment guidance; check by plotting λ(t) over training steps
- **Projection mechanisms**: Handling mismatched hidden dimensions between models - needed when aligning layers with different sizes; check by verifying projection matrix shapes
- **Pretrained model selection**: Choosing appropriate smaller models - needed to ensure sufficient representational capacity; check by comparing model sizes and architectures

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Smaller pretrained model -> projection layers -> alignment loss -> target model early layers -> combined with standard loss
- **Critical path**: Input tokens → target model processing → early layer representations → alignment with pretrained model late layers → cosine similarity loss → gradient update
- **Design tradeoffs**: Alignment strength vs. model autonomy - higher λ provides more guidance but may restrict learning; layer selection - aligning final pretrained layer with early target layer (L2E) vs. other strategies
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance if λ too high (over-constrained learning) or too low (insufficient guidance); suboptimal results if layer alignment not chosen appropriately
- **First experiments**: 1) Verify cosine similarity loss computes correctly between mismatched dimension layers, 2) Test alignment with varying λ values to find optimal schedule, 3) Compare different layer alignment strategies (L2E vs. alternatives)

## Limitations
- Experiments limited to 1.4B and 7B parameter models, leaving scalability to larger models uncertain
- No analysis of computational overhead introduced by the alignment mechanism during training
- Potential overfitting concerns when pretrained model is trained on same data distribution as target model

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Core concept and mechanisms clearly defined with mathematical formulation
- **Medium Confidence**: Empirical results show statistical rigor but require parameter tuning
- **Low Confidence**: Generalizability to larger models and training stability impacts remain unclear

## Next Checks

3. Conduct ablation studies with pretrained model trained on subset of data to assess knowledge transfer vs. overfitting
