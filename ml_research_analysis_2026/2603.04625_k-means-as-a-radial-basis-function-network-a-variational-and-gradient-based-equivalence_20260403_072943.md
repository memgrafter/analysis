# K-Means as a Radial Basis function Network: a Variational and Gradient-based Equivalence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04625
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04625
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Establishes a rigorous variational and gradient-based equivalence between K-Means and differentiable RBF networks via Γ-convergence and Entmax-1.5 stabilization.

## Executive Summary
The paper bridges the discrete-continuous divide between K-Means clustering and radial basis function (RBF) networks. It proves that the K-Means distortion functional is the Γ-limit of a smooth RBF loss with entropic regularization as the temperature σ → 0. Consequently, gradient descent on RBF centers with appropriately scaled steps recovers exact K-Means centroid updates in the limit. To avoid numerical instability at low σ, the authors replace the Softmax assignment with Entmax-1.5, which ensures polynomial O(σ) convergence while preserving Voronoi partitions. This equivalence enables end-to-end joint optimization of representations and clusters in deep architectures, bypassing alternating optimization. Theoretical results include Γ-convergence proofs, convergence rates (exponential for Softmax, linear for Entmax under separation), and empirical validation on synthetic datasets showing monotonic collapse of soft centroids to K-Means fixed points.

## Method Summary
The work aims to empirically validate the theoretical equivalence between K-Means and a differentiable RBF network. Using four synthetic 2D datasets (Gaussian blobs, two moons, spiral, circles) with k=3 clusters, the authors measure centroid discrepancy R(σ) between K-Means solutions and those obtained by minimizing a smooth RBF loss with soft assignments. The RBF network computes activations based on squared Euclidean distances to centroids, and assignments are produced either by Softmax or Entmax-1.5 over normalized negative distances. Centroid updates follow a closed-form gradient step η_j = 1/(2∑i R_ij). Experiments compare two initialization schemes (fixed vs. resampled) across a logarithmic grid of σ values, and convergence exponents m are estimated via linear regression on log-log plots. The implementation relies on standard libraries for K-Means and the entmax transformation; centroid matching uses the Hungarian algorithm to handle label permutations.

## Key Results
- The RBF loss Γ-converges to the K-Means objective as σ→0, and gradient updates on its centers recover exact K-Means centroid updates in the limit.
- Replacing Softmax with Entmax-1.5 ensures polynomial O(σ) convergence and numerical stability while preserving Voronoi partitions.
- Empirical results on four synthetic datasets confirm monotonic collapse of soft centroids to K-Means fixed points; Softmax shows super-polynomial decay, Entmax-1.5 yields linear O(σ) convergence (exponent m≈1).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 (Γ-convergence):** By reparameterizing K-Means with an entropic regularizer 2σ²∑_ij r_ij log r_ij, the optimal soft responsibilities become r_ij(σ)=Softmax(-||x_i-μ_j||²/(2σ²)). The resulting smooth objective L_σ(μ) Γ-converges to the hard-assignment K-Means functional J(μ) as σ→0 because the Softmax concentrates on the nearest centroid. This requires a compact centroid space to apply Γ-convergence theorems.

**Mechanism 2 (Gradient equivalence & Entmax stabilization):** The gradient ∇_μ_j L_σ simplifies to 2(∑_i r_ij μ_j - ∑_i r_ij x_i). For fixed responsibilities, the optimum μ_j* is the weighted mean. With σ→0, r_ij→hard assignments, and choosing η_j=1/(2∑_i r_ij) yields the exact K-Means update μ'_j = (1/|S_j|)∑_{x∈S_j} x. Entmax-1.5 replaces Softmax to maintain polynomial O(σ) deviation of soft assignments from hard ones, ensuring stable gradients without altering the zero-temperature limit.

**Mechanism 3 (End-to-end deep integration):** The smooth RBF loss L_kmeans(θ,μ) = ∑_ij r_ij(θ)||x_i-μ_j||² is differentiable in both centroids μ and upstream parameters θ (e.g., neural network weights). This allows joint minimization with a task loss: min_{θ,μ} L_task(θ) + λ L_kmeans(θ,μ), replacing alternating optimization. However, the model retains a Euclidean Voronoi geometry, which may distort nonlinear manifolds.

## Foundational Learning
- **Γ-convergence:** Why needed: Provides a rigorous justification that minimizers of L_σ approach minimizers of J as σ→0. Quick check: Does Γ-convergence guarantee convergence of minimizers, or only of clusterings?
- **Entmax-α transformation:** Why needed: Offers a sparse, numerically stable alternative to Softmax at low temperatures, ensuring polynomial convergence. Quick check: How does Entmax-1.5’s sparsity differ from Softmax in the σ→0 limit and why does this matter for gradient flow?
- **Voronoi partition:** Why needed: K-Means solutions are defined by nearest-centroid assignments; the RBF limit must reproduce this discrete geometric structure. Quick check: In a tie (two centroids equidistant), what does K-Means prescribe and how does Entmax-1.5 behave as σ→0?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** RBF Layer (computes exp(-||x-μ_j||²/(2σ²)) -> Assignment Head (Entmax-1.5 over -||x-μ_j||²/(2σ²)) -> Centroid Update Module (closed-form step μ_j ← (∑_i R_ij x_i)/(∑_i R_ij)) -> Loss Aggregator (combines L_kmeans with task loss). Deep extension: upstream network f_θ(x) feeds into RBF layer.

**Critical path:** Initialize centroids μ; for t=1..T: compute distances, apply Entmax-1.5 to get R; compute r_j = Σ_i R_ij and weighted sums w_j = Σ_i R_ij x_i; update μ_j = w_j / r_j; optionally anneal σ.

**Design tradeoffs:** σ schedule (logarithmic annealing balances speed/stability); Entmax vs. Softmax (Entmax adds O(k log k) sorting but avoids underflow); geometry vs. differentiability (Euclidean Voronoi may not capture manifold structure).

**Failure signatures:** Numerical underflow in Softmax for σ<~0.1 (fix: use Entmax); cluster collapse (uniform assignments due to high σ; monitor margin γ_min); manifold mismatch (nonlinear data forces latent isotropy, harming expressiveness).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Γ-convergence sanity check: Run SoftRBF on blobs across σ, plot log R(σ) vs log σ; expect steep slope (super-polynomial).
2. Entmax stability test: Compare Softmax and Entmax-1.5 at very low σ (e.g., 1e-4); Entmax should maintain finite gradients without collapse.
3. Deep integration probe: Add L_kmeans to an autoencoder loss on circles/moons; visualize latent space; check if it becomes Euclidean/Voronoi-friendly but possibly loses curvature.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions; future work may explore scaling to large datasets, integration with modern deep architectures, and performance on real-world high-dimensional data.

## Limitations
- Exact Entmax-1.5 implementation (root-finding for τ) and its behavior at extreme σ are not detailed.
- Reported convergence exponents depend on choices of centroid matching (Hungarian vs. greedy) and initialization (random vs. k-means++); the paper’s specific settings are not fully specified.
- Theoretical assumptions (compact centroid space, bounded data, positive separation margin) are not extensively validated empirically.
- The enforced Euclidean Voronoi geometry may distort representations on nonlinear manifolds, limiting applicability to deep clustering tasks.

## Confidence
- Γ-convergence of RBF loss to K-Means: High
- Gradient equivalence and Entmax-1.5 stability: Medium
- Practical utility for end-to-end deep clustering: Low
