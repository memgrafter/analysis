# CURE: A Multimodal Benchmark for Clinical Understanding and Retrieval Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19274
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19274
- **Reference Count:** 21
- **Primary Result:** Diagnostic accuracy hinges on retrieved literature quality; with oracle references models reach 73.4% accuracy, but with independent retrieval it falls to 25.4% or lower, identifying retrieval as the key bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
CURE is a new benchmark designed to separate the challenges of evidence retrieval from multimodal reasoning in clinical diagnostics. It provides 500 cases, each with a clinical history, medical images, and physician-cited PubMed references. Testing state-of-the-art multimodal models reveals a stark performance gap: when given perfect, curated references, models achieve high accuracy (up to 73.4%). However, when using their own retrieval mechanisms, accuracy plummets (to ~25%), demonstrating that the failure to retrieve precise biomedical literature is the primary limiting factor, not the models' reasoning ability. Furthermore, textual radiology findings provide a much stronger signal for diagnosis than raw images, highlighting a current gap in visual evidence utilization.

## Method Summary
The benchmark evaluates models on 500 zero-shot cases from 2025 (post-training cut-off) to prevent data contamination. Each case includes a clinical history text, a set of images, and physician-selected PubMed references (PMIDs). Models are tested under four evidence conditions: 1) **Base** (history + images only), 2) **Physician Reference (PR)** (oracle abstracts provided), 3) **RAG** (dense retrieval using `bge-large-en-v1.5` and `MedCPT`), and 4) **Agent Retrieval (AR)** (`MiroFlow` agent). For closed-ended tasks, accuracy on multiple-choice differential diagnosis is measured. For open-ended diagnosis, Hit@1/Hit@3 are computed via an LLM-as-a-judge (`DeepSeek-V3.1`). Analysis also categorizes outcomes as "evidence-based correct," "evidence-misled," or "hallucination" based on groundedness.

## Key Results
- With oracle physician references, models achieve up to 73.4% Hit@1, establishing a high ceiling for reasoning given perfect evidence.
- With independent retrieval (RAG/AR), accuracy collapses to 25.4% or lower, showing retrieval quality is the dominant bottleneck.
- Adding textual "Findings" (structured radiology report) yields large, consistent gains, while adding raw images provides only modest and variable improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Diagnostic accuracy is primarily contingent on the quality and coverage of retrieved biomedical literature, not the model's intrinsic multimodal reasoning capability. When provided with physician-curated reference abstracts (high coverage, 92.1%), models achieve significantly higher accuracy (up to 73.4% Hit@1). The key differentiator is the lexical and semantic alignment between the retrieved documents and the decisive evidence cited by human experts. Poor retrieval (low coverage, ~23% in RAG) yields irrelevant or misleading context, causing models to generate plausible but incorrect, evidence-grounded diagnoses ("evidence-misled"). The model's reasoning process is sufficiently faithful to the provided context; thus, output correctness closely mirrors input evidence quality. If retrieved documents have low TF-IDF cosine similarity to the physician-annotated references (<0.17), correctness sharply declines regardless of model scale.

### Mechanism 2
High faithfulness to retrieved context is a double-edged sword: it maximizes performance with high-quality evidence but increases vulnerability to noise. Models exhibit low rates of "knowledge-based correct" outcomes (<6% in all settings), indicating they rarely override retrieved documents with internal knowledge. Consequently, when retrieval is accurate (PR), high groundedness translates to correct answers. When retrieval is noisy (RAG), the same faithfulness causes models to reason from incorrect premises, leading to "hallucination" (31.6%) or "evidence-misled" outputs. Models do not possess or apply a robust metacognitive filter to assess the quality or relevance of retrieved documents before incorporating them. If a model demonstrates >20% "hallucination" rate under a retrieval condition, it suggests the retrieved noise exceeds the model's capacity to reconcile contradictions or ignore irrelevant content.

### Mechanism 3
Textual radiology findings provide a stronger and more consistent signal for diagnosis than raw imaging data, revealing a gap in visual evidence utilization. Adding structured "Findings" text yields large, consistent improvements in Hit@k and MCQ across all models. In contrast, adding raw images alone provides modest, variable gains and can even degrade MCQ performance for some models. This suggests current MLLMs can more reliably integrate narrative, attribute-based descriptions than perform fine-grained visual pattern recognition required for diagnostic differentiation. The "Findings" text represents a curated, high-signal summary of visual observations; model performance gains reflect their ability to use this textual proxy rather than directly interpreting the image pixels. If a model's performance with "w/ Image" does not show a statistically significant improvement over "History only" on Hit@1 or Hit@3, its visual encoder is not effectively extracting diagnostic features for this task.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Fusion for Diagnostic Reasoning**
  - **Why needed:** CURE evaluates the integration of *clinical text*, *medical images*, and *external literature*. Understanding how these modalities contribute differently (e.g., history vs. findings vs. images) is core to interpreting the benchmark results.
  - **Quick check:** Why does adding "Findings" text improve performance more than adding raw images, according to the paper's analysis?

- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Agentic Retrieval**
  - **Why needed:** CURE's primary innovation is disentangling retrieval from reasoning. The experiments test three paradigms: Oracle PR, standard dense RAG, and agentic (MiroFlow) retrieval. One must understand the operational difference between these to interpret the performance gaps.
  - **Quick check:** What is the key operational difference between the "RAG" and "Agent Retrieval (AR)" paradigms in CURE's evaluation?

- **Concept: Zero-Shot Evaluation and Data Contamination**
  - **Why needed:** All 500 test cases are from 2025, post-dating the knowledge cut-off of all evaluated MLLMs. This is a critical design choice to ensure performance reflects *on-the-fly* retrieval and reasoning, not memorization of training data.
  - **Quick check:** How does using 2025 Eurorad cases mitigate the risk of data contamination in the evaluation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Processor -> Modality-Specific Encoders (Text, Vision) -> Retrieval Module (Oracle / RAG / Agent) -> Fusion & Reasoning Core (MLLM) -> Output Parser -> Evaluator (LLM-as-a-judge)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Receive input `Q = (T, V)`.
  2. If retrieval is enabled, pass `T` (and possibly `V` embeddings) to the retrieval module to obtain evidence set `E`.
  3. Construct prompt: `T` + (optional `V` representations) + `E` + task instruction.
  4. MLLM processes prompt and generates output.
  5. For open-ended, pass output & ground truth to evaluator for alignment scoring.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Oracle vs. Noisy Retrieval:** The stark performance gap shows that the *design of the retrieval system* is the current bottleneck. An engineering team must prioritize building a precise, low-noise biomedical retriever over scaling the MLLM itself.
  - **Agentic vs. Dense RAG:** Agents (MiroFlow) recover some performance over standard RAG, suggesting iterative search and refinement help filter noise. However, the persistent gap to PR indicates agent planning and query formulation for niche radiological features remain underdeveloped.
  - **Modality Inclusion:** Adding images increases computational cost with uncertain benefit. A pragmatic system might initially rely on `History + Findings + Retrieval`, using images only for verification or in a secondary pipeline.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Low PR Groundedness + High Hallucination:** Model is ignoring retrieval or hallucinating; check prompt formatting and context window management.
  - **High "Evidence-Misled" in RAG/AR:** Retrieval noise is the issue. Diagnose by checking TF-IDF similarity of retrieved docs to physician references (should be >0.25 for correct cases).
  - **Poor Modality Synergy:** If "w/ Both" does not significantly outperform "w/ Findings", the vision encoder or multimodal fusion mechanism is not extracting complementary signal from images.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Sanity Check:** Run a strong model (GPT-5.2) on `History only` and `History + Images` (Base). Expect low-mid single-digit Hit@1. Confirms model has minimal zero-shot clinical reasoning on these novel cases.
  2. **Oracle Upper Bound Test:** Run same model with `Physician Reference` context. Expect drastic jump (~70% Hit@1). This establishes the theoretical ceiling and confirms the model's reasoning *capacity* when given perfect evidence.
  3. **Retrieval Paradigm Comparison:** Run the model under `RAG` and `Agent Retrieval` configurations. Quantify the performance delta from the Oracle ceiling. This directly measures the current retrieval bottleneck. Use the TF-IDF similarity analysis (as in Fig 3) to correlate retrieval quality with outcomes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can retrieval systems be enhanced to consistently match the quality and diagnostic relevance of physician-selected PubMed references in multimodal clinical settings?
- **Basis in paper:** The study shows a stark performance drop when models rely on independent retrieval (RAG: as low as 25.4% Hit@1 for GPT-5.2; Agent Retrieval: 25.5%) versus physician references (73.4%), with low coverage of physician-cited abstracts (RAG: 23.0%). This identifies retrieval quality as the core bottleneck.
- **Why unresolved:** Current RAG and agent pipelines fail to surface decisive literature, often retrieving irrelevant or redundant content that misleads models. The paper demonstrates the problem but does not investigate architectural or training improvements for retrieval in this domain.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of retrieval models fine-tuned on CURE's query-evidence pairs, evaluated by (1) increased coverage of physician-referenced PMIDs and (2) reduced gap in diagnostic accuracy between physician-reference and independent retrieval conditions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why do medical images contribute only modest and inconsistent gains to diagnostic accuracy compared to textual findings, and what improvements in multimodal alignment are needed?
- **Basis in paper:** Table 2 shows that adding images alone yields small, variable improvements in open-ended metrics (Hit@1/3) and no consistent gain in MCQ accuracy across models, whereas adding textual findings produces large, consistent improvements.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper notes that current MLLMs do not reliably convert visual cues into decision-level benefits, suggesting deficiencies in lesion localization, attribute extraction, or vision-language alignment. The specific limitations in visual representation learning or fusion mechanisms remain unexamined.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies varying visual encoder architectures, attention patterns, and training objectives on CURE; clinician validation of model-generated visual explanations to identify missing or misinterpreted imaging features.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can models develop calibrated trust in retrieved evidence to avoid both hallucination (when unfaithful) and evidence-misled reasoning (when overly faithful)?
- **Basis in paper:** Figure 2 reveals that high groundedness correlates with accuracy under physician references but increases evidence-misled outcomes under noisy retrieval (57.3% for RAG). Models are excessively faithful to retrieved content, rarely overriding it with internal knowledge.
- **Why unresolved:** The trade-off between faithfulness and robustness is highlighted, but no methods for evidence critique or selection are explored. Current systems lack mechanisms to assess the reliability or relevance of retrieved documents.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrating techniques for evidence reliability estimation (e.g., cross-source consistency checks, confidence scoring) into MLLMs, with evaluation measuring shifts in the accuracy×groundedness quadrant distribution toward higher evidence-based correctness and lower evidence-misled rates under noisy retrieval.

## Limitations
- **Retrieval implementation specifics:** Exact RAG ensemble scoring, de-duplication logic, and MiroFlow agent query strategies are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Evaluation prompts & judge reliability:** Precise prompt templates for MLLMs and the LLM-as-judge rubric are not detailed, leaving scoring vulnerable to prompt engineering variance.
- **Generalizability beyond radiology:** Findings are derived from Eurorad cases (radiology). Performance in other clinical domains (e.g., pathology, EHR notes) remains unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **"Retrieval, not reasoning, is the primary bottleneck":** High. Strongly supported by the Oracle (73.4%) vs. RAG (≤25.4%) accuracy gap and evidence-grounding analysis.
- **"Faithfulness is a double-edged sword":** High. Consistently observed: high groundedness with PR, but dominant "evidence-misled" failure under noisy retrieval.
- **"Textual findings > raw images":** High. Replicated across all models in Table 2; the delta between `+Image` and `+Findings` is substantial and clear.
