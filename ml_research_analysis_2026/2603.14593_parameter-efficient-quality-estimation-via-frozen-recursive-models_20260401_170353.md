# Parameter-Efficient Quality Estimation via Frozen Recursive Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14593
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14593
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Frozen TRM-QE (7M trainable parameters) achieves Spearman's correlation of 0.370 on cross-lingual quality estimation, matching a fully fine-tuned 262M-parameter variant and outperforming an equivalent-depth standard transformer (0.336).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates applying Tiny Recursive Models (TRM) to cross-lingual Quality Estimation (QE) for low-resource languages. The core method uses a weight-shared TRM head over frozen XLM-R embeddings, decoupling representation learning from task-specific reasoning. Results on 8 language pairs show that external iteration harms performance, while internal recursion offers only narrow benefits. Crucially, representation quality dominates architectural choices: the frozen TRM-QE model (7M parameters) matches the performance of a fully fine-tuned 262M variant (0.370 vs 0.369 Spearman) and surpasses MonoTransQuest (560M parameters) on Hindi and Tamil with 80× fewer trainable parameters, demonstrating extreme parameter efficiency for QE.

## Method Summary
The approach freezes a pretrained multilingual encoder (XLM-R base) to generate contextual embeddings for source–translation sentence pairs. A lightweight TRM head—a weight-shared transformer block applied for L cycles—iteratively refines these embeddings. The final state of a special token is passed through a single-layer regression head to predict a continuous quality score. Only the TRM head (≈7M parameters) is trained, while the encoder remains fixed, drastically reducing trainable parameters and avoiding overfitting on small low-resource datasets. Optimal configuration uses L=4 internal cycles, a single external step (no re-encoding), and a projection from 1024D to 512D. Training minimizes mean squared error against human Direct Assessment scores (z-normalized and sigmoid-scaled).

## Key Results
- Frozen TRM-QE (7M trainable parameters) achieves Spearman's correlation of 0.370, statistically matching a fully fine-tuned 262M-parameter variant (0.369) and outperforming an equivalent-depth standard transformer (0.336).
- External iteration (multi-step re-encoding) harms performance, while internal recursion (L cycles) provides only narrow benefits, peaking at L=4 for frozen XLM-R.
- On Hindi and Tamil, frozen TRM-QE surpasses MonoTransQuest (560M parameters) with 80× fewer trainable parameters, demonstrating extreme parameter efficiency for low-resource QE.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Frozen embeddings match fine-tuned performance
Freezing XLM-R embeddings provides high-quality cross-lingual representations; a small TRM head learns to refine them for QE, decoupling representation learning from task-specific reasoning. This avoids overfitting on small datasets. Evidence: frozen 7M TRM matches fine-tuned 262M (0.370 vs 0.369 Spearman). Breaks when embeddings are intrinsically poor (e.g., Telugu 0.164) or when adaptation beyond frozen capacity is needed.

### Mechanism 2: Weight-shared internal recursion outperforms external iteration
The TRM head repeatedly applies the same transformer block (L cycles) to iteratively align source and translation representations. Weight sharing keeps parameters minimal (7M). External iteration—re-encoding from TRM output—adds unnecessary steps that degrade quality. Evidence: external steps increase degrades (Spearman 0.324→0.238 for 2 steps); internal L=4 optimal (0.333). Without weight sharing, standard transformer with frozen embeddings achieves only 0.290 vs TRM's 0.370.

### Mechanism 3: Representation quality dominates architectural choices
The encoder's semantic fidelity sets an upper bound on QE performance; once high-quality representations are available, even a simple head suffices, with recursion providing only marginal gains. Evidence: XLM-R embeddings (1024→512) yield 0.387 Pearson vs mT5-small's 0.294; frozen XLM-R (0.370) surpasses frozen mT5-small (0.297). Breaks when representation quality is irredeemably low (e.g., typologically distant languages with sparse pretraining data).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (source + translation concatenated) -> XLM-R Encoder (frozen) -> Projection (1024→512) -> TRM Head (L cycles, weight-shared 2-layer transformer) -> Output Head (single-layer regression) -> predicted score

**Critical path:**  
1. Tokenize and concatenate source and translation with special tokens; feed through XLM-R to get initial hidden states H₀.  
2. For l=1 to L: Hₗ = TransformerBlock(Hₗ₋₁) using the same block each cycle.  
3. Take first token's final state h_L; apply output layer to predict normalized score.  
4. Train with MSE loss against Direct Assessment scores (z-normalized, sigmoid-scaled).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Frozen vs fine-tuned encoder: frozen reduces parameters 37× and avoids overfitting but locks representation quality; fine-tuning adapts to task but risks overfitting on small data.  
- Recursion depth L: More cycles allow refinement but degrade beyond optimal (L=4 for frozen XLM-R); too shallow (L=1) underfits.  
- External iteration: Harmful; adding steps that re-encode from TRM output introduces noise.  
- Weight sharing: Critical for frozen models; without it, performance drops significantly (0.290 vs 0.370).  
- Embedding choice: XLM-R outperforms mT5; representation quality is paramount.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Poor performance on specific languages (e.g., Telugu, Sinhala) indicates biased or weak frozen representations.  
- Performance drop when external steps >1.  
- Degradation when L exceeds optimal (e.g., L=6 with frozen XLM-R).  
- Large gap between TRM and standard transformer with frozen embeddings (>0.07 Spearman) suggests weight sharing failure.  
- Fine-tuned model substantially outperforming frozen indicates need for adaptation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Recursion ablation:** Fix embeddings (e.g., fine-tuned mT5-small), vary external steps (1–16) and internal cycles (L=1,2,4,6) to identify optimal L and confirm external iteration harm.  
2. **Embedding comparison:** Freeze architecture (L=2, 1-step) and compare different frozen encoders (mT5-small, mT5-base, XLM-R) to quantify representation quality impact.  
3. **Frozen vs fine-tuned:** Train TRM head on frozen XLM-R (7M) vs full fine-tuning (262M); also test standard 8-layer transformer with same frozen embeddings to validate weight sharing necessity and parameter efficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why do Hindi and Tamil achieve significantly higher QE performance than Telugu and Sinhala when using frozen XLM-R embeddings?** The paper tentatively links this to XLM-R's pretraining data distribution but lacks causal evidence; it remains unclear whether the gap stems from representation quality, task difficulty, or other factors.
2. **To what extent do other recursive or iterative architectures (e.g., Universal Transformers, PonderNet) suffer from the same "external iteration hurts" phenomenon observed with TRM in QE?** Only TRM was evaluated; the generality of this finding to other frameworks is untested.
3. **Does the "frozen efficiency" phenomenon generalize to other cross-lingual tasks beyond sentence-level QE, such as word-level QE, document-level QE, or other semantic textual similarity tasks?** The study is limited to sentence-level QE; different tasks may have different demands on representation adaptation versus iterative reasoning.
4. **What is the precise mechanistic role of internal recursion within the frozen embedding setup?** Is it primarily performing deep refinement of cross-lingual alignment, or is it acting as a cheap form of increased model depth without meaningful iterative computation? Performance curves are non-monotonic, but interpretability analysis is lacking.
5. **Can the parameter efficiency of frozen TRM-QE be further improved by using even lighter-weight recursive heads?** The minimal required complexity of the recursive head is unknown; the architecture search was limited (fixed 2 layers per cycle, varying L only).

## Limitations
- Training hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate, optimizer, epochs) are incompletely reported, hindering exact replication.
- Target preprocessing is ambiguous: the order of z-normalization and sigmoid scaling is unclear and may affect outcomes.
- Evaluation is limited to 8 English-centric language pairs; poor Telugu/Sinhala results indicate representation quality limits for some languages.
- No comparison to other parameter-efficient methods (e.g., LoRA, adapters) for frozen embeddings.

## Confidence
- **Frozen embeddings efficiency:** High (direct evidence: 7M matches 262M fine-tuned, 0.370 vs 0.369 Spearman).
- **Recursion design:** High (ablation confirms external iteration harm, internal L optimality, weight sharing necessity).
- **Representation dominance:** Medium (XLM-R vs mT5 shows large gap, but generalizability beyond studied pairs is untested).
