# Almost Sure Convergence of Differential Temporal Difference Learning for Average Reward Markov Decision Processes
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16629  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16629  
- **Reference count:** 8  
- **Primary result:** Differential TD converges almost surely for average reward MDPs with standard diminishing stepsizes and without a local clock; off‑policy convergence is guaranteed under three distinct sufficient conditions involving the stepsize multiplier η.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes almost sure convergence of differential temporal difference (TD) learning for average‑reward Markov decision processes (MDPs) using only standard diminishing step sizes, eliminating the need for a state‑dependent local clock. The authors introduce an n‑step differential TD algorithm and analyze it via stochastic approximation with Markovian noise. The associated ordinary differential equation (ODE) is shown to be globally asymptotically stable when a certain matrix A is strictly positive stable. For on‑policy updates, this holds for any stepsize multiplier η>0. For off‑policy learning, three sufficient conditions are provided: (i) existence of some η₀>0 (not explicitly given), (ii) if the n‑step target policy transition matrix is strictly positive then η ≤ 2 min Pⁿ_π(i,j) guarantees convergence, and (iii) if that matrix is doubly stochastic then any η>0 works. Empirical results in a gridworld demonstrate convergence even when the theoretical bounds are violated. The analysis relies on D‑stability theory for rank‑one perturbations of M‑matrices.

## Method Summary
The algorithm corresponds to an off‑policy n‑step differential TD update. The state includes a buffer of the last n transitions; the update uses the sum of rewards over those n steps, scaled by the average‑reward estimate J, and incorporates importance sampling ratios to correct for the difference between behavior and target policies. Formally, after the buffer is full, for the earliest experience (s_start, a_start) the TD error is δ = R_sum – η·Σ + v(s_next) – v(s_start), where Σ is the running sum of the differential value estimates and η is a positive multiplier. The update is v(s_start) ← v(s_start) + α_t ρ δ, Σ ← Σ + α_t ρ δ, and J ← J + (η/n) α_t ρ δ, with ρ the product of importance sampling ratios over the n steps. Under standard diminishing stepsizes α_t (∑α_t=∞, α_t→0, α_t−α_{t+1}=O(α_t)) and assumptions of finite irreducible aperiodic MDPs with coverage, the iterates converge almost surely to the correct differential value function when the matrix A = D_μ (I – Pⁿ_π + η e e⊤) is strictly positive stable.

## Key Results
- On‑policy n‑step differential TD converges almost surely for any n≥1 and any η>0 with standard diminishing stepsizes, without a local clock.
- Off‑policy convergence is guaranteed under three sufficient conditions: existence of some η₀>0, η ≤ 2 min_{i,j} Pⁿ_π(i,j) when Pⁿ_π>0, or any η>0 when Pⁿ_π is doubly stochastic.
- Empirical evidence shows convergence even when the theoretical bounds are violated; the analysis leverages D‑stability theory for rank‑one perturbations of M‑matrices and the Borkar‑Meyn theorem for SA with Markovian noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: ODE Global Asymptotic Stability
The algorithm is recast as a stochastic approximation process with Markovian noise using an augmented state Y_t. The expected update operator yields the ODE dv/dt = –A v(t), where A = D_μ (I – Pⁿ_π + η e e⊤). According to the Borkar‑Meyn theorem (extended to Markovian noise), if this ODE is globally asymptotically stable (i.e., A is strictly positive stable), then the SA iterates converge almost surely to its equilibrium. The key assumptions are finite irreducible aperiodic MDPs with coverage and standard diminishing stepsizes (Assumption 4.2). Thus, convergence hinges on proving strict positive stability of A.

### Mechanism 2: D‑Stability via Rank‑One Perturbation
The matrix A is expressed as B + η d_μ e⊤, where B = I – (I + D_μ (Pⁿ_π – I)) is a singular M‑matrix having a simple zero eigenvalue due to ergodicity of Pⁿ_π. Lemma 4.3 (Bierkens & Ran, 2014) provides conditions under which adding a nonnegative rank‑one perturbation to an M‑matrix preserves strict positive stability. The authors verify these conditions: B, v=η d_μ, w=e satisfy Conditions 1‑3 (positivity, irreducibility, and singular M‑matrix structure). For on‑policy (μ=π), Condition 4 (wᵀ B = 0) holds, ensuring stability; for off‑policy, Condition 5 (2K_{i,j} ≥ v_i w_j for all i,j) is used under additional structural assumptions on Pⁿ_π (strict positivity or doubly stochastic). This linear‑algebraic argument replaces more restrictive local‑clock techniques.

### Mechanism 3: Three Off‑Policy Sufficient Conditions
From the general D‑stability framework, three distinct sets of assumptions guarantee A is strictly positive stable:
(i) Existence of some η₀>0 such that for all η∈(0,η₀] stability holds; this follows from continuity of eigenvalues and the fact that A is strictly positive stable at η=0 (B has a simple zero eigenvalue).
(ii) If Pⁿ_π is strictly positive (all entries >0), then Condition 5 reduces to η ≤ 2 min_{i,j} Pⁿ_π(i,j), providing an explicit bound.
(iii) If Pⁿ_π is doubly stochastic, choosing Lyapunov matrix M = D_μ⁻¹ yields AᵀM + MA ≻ 0 for any η>0, so no bound on η is needed.
These conditions are sufficient but not necessary; empirically convergence occurs even when they are violated.

## Foundational Learning
### Stochastic Approximation (SA) and the ODE Method
**Why needed:** The differential TD algorithm is analyzed as SA with Markovian noise; the ODE method links the discrete iterates to the stability of a continuous‑time system, enabling almost‑sure convergence proofs.
**Quick check:** What are the key assumptions (e.g., diminishing stepsizes, ergodicity) for the Borkar‑Meyn theorem to yield almost‑sure convergence with Markovian noise?

### M‑matrix and Positive Stability
**Why needed:** The matrix A governing the ODE must be strictly positive stable (all eigenvalues have positive real parts) for the ODE to be globally asymptotically stable; analyzing A as a perturbation of a singular M‑matrix allows the use of D‑stability theory.
**Quick check:** Define an M‑matrix and explain why a nonnegative rank‑one perturbation can preserve positive stability under Condition 5 (2K_{i,j} ≥ v_i w_j).

### Average‑Reward MDPs and Differential TD
**Why needed:** For continuing tasks with average reward criterion, the Bellman equation differs from the discounted case; differential TD estimates the differential value function v_π that satisfies the Poisson equation, while simultaneously learning the average reward estimate J.
**Quick check:** Write the average‑reward Bellman equation and contrast it with the discounted Bellman equation. How does differential TD incorporate the average reward?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Behavior policy μ → transitions/rewards → n‑step buffer → TD error δ → updates to v, Σ, J (η scaling); importance‑sampling ratios ρ; augmented state Y_t.

**Critical path:**
1. Derive expected update operator h(v)=E[H(v,Y)] → ODE: dv/dt = –A v with A = D_μ (I – Pⁿ_π + η e e⊤).
2. Prove A is strictly positive stable using Lemma 4.3 (D‑stability) under specific conditions (on‑policy or off‑policy with structural assumptions on Pⁿ_π and η).
3. Apply Borkar‑Meyn theorem (Liu et al., 2025a) to conclude almost‑sure convergence of iterates to the equilibrium set of the ODE (the correct v_π up to an additive constant).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Larger n reduces bias but increases memory (buffer size n) and variance due to product of importance‑sampling ratios.
- Too large η may violate D‑stability off‑policy; provided bounds (e.g., η ≤ 2 min Pⁿ_π) may be conservative.
- Step size schedule must satisfy Assumption 4.2; constant or decaying too slowly can harm finite‑sample behavior even if asymptotic convergence holds.
- Eliminating the local clock simplifies implementation and enables potential extensions to function approximation, but requires more delicate stability analysis.

**Failure signatures:**
- Value estimates diverge or oscillate, typically when η is too large off‑policy or when Pⁿ_π has a near‑zero eigenvalue and Condition 5 fails.
- Convergence stalls at a suboptimal solution, possibly due to poor coverage (μ does not cover π) leading to incorrect importance‑sampling weighting.
- Violation of ergodicity (e.g., periodic MDP) breaks the stationary distribution arguments, causing instability.
- Numerical instability in the product of importance‑sampling ratios (underflow/overflow) for long n.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Gridworld varying η** (n=3): Tests robustness to the stepsize multiplier; convergence observed even for η values that violate the theoretical bound η ≤ 2 min Pⁿ_π (which is 0 in this MDP due to deterministic transitions).
2. **Gridworld varying n** (η=0.1): Demonstrates stability across multi‑step depths (n=1..4) despite the transition matrix not being strictly positive.
3. **Empirical evaluation of theoretical bounds:** Compares convergence when the condition η ≤ 2 min Pⁿ_π holds (e.g., if the MDP were stochastic) vs. when it fails, illustrating that the sufficient condition is conservative and convergence may still occur.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Necessary and sufficient conditions for D‑stability:** What are necessary and sufficient conditions for the matrix A = B + η d^μ e⊤ to be strictly positive stable? The linear algebra community still lacks a tight upper bound on admissible η and a characterization for general triples (B, v, w). Closing this gap would yield sharper convergence guarantees.
2. **Universal η off‑policy:** Does off‑policy n‑step differential TD converge almost surely for any η>0 without a local clock under standard assumptions? Current results require η ≤ η₀ (unknown), η ≤ 2 min Pⁿ_π, or doubly stochastic Pⁿ_π. Empirical results suggest convergence even when η₀=0.
3. **Extension to function approximation:** Can the convergence analysis be extended to function approximation settings (linear or nonlinear) without requiring a local clock? The current tabular analysis uses explicit state visits; with features, defining a local clock is problematic.
4. **Other average‑reward algorithms:** Can the D‑stability approach prove almost sure convergence of differential Q‑learning and RVI Q‑learning without a local clock? The authors expect this to be possible but it remains open.

## Limitations
- The off‑policy sufficient conditions are not necessary; empirical evidence of convergence beyond the bounds is limited to a single gridworld, so generalizability is unknown.
- Analysis assumes finite, irreducible, aperiodic MDPs and full coverage; these may not hold in large or continuous‑state settings, especially with function approximation.
- The paper does not address finite‑sample performance, variance reduction, or computational overhead of the n‑step buffer.
- Details of the target ε‑greedy policy and the exact RMSVE(TVR) computation are underspecified in the paper, which may affect reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **On‑policy a.s. convergence (any η>0):** High (rigorous ODE method and D‑stability proof).
- **Off‑policy convergence under the three conditions:** Medium (proofs are sound but conditions are sufficient, not necessary).
- **Empirical robustness beyond theoretical bounds:** Low–Medium (based on one MDP; generalizability uncertain).
- **Extension to function approximation:** Low (no analysis presented).
