# Semantic Search At LinkedIn

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07309
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07309
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Deployed a 0.6B SLM-based semantic ranking system that improved NDCG@10 by >7% while achieving a 75x+ throughput increase under a 500ms latency budget.

## Executive Summary
LinkedIn deployed an end-to-end large language model (LLM) based semantic search system for Job and People Search to overcome the scalability limitations of previous cross-encoders under a strict 500ms latency budget. The system uses an 8B LLM as an oracle relevance judge to generate training data and for evaluation. This data trains a GPU-accelerated bi-encoder retriever and a compact 0.6B Small Language Model (SLM) ranker. The ranker is trained via multi-teacher distillation to jointly optimize for relevance and engagement. Key to meeting latency goals is a suite of inference optimizations co-designed with the model, including prefill-only execution, offline document summarization, and a text-embedding hybrid interaction (MixLM) that drastically reduces the context length processed by the ranker.

## Method Summary
The system construction follows a multi-stage pipeline. First, an 8B LLM oracle judge provides fine-grained (0-4) relevance scores and rationales for query-document pairs, establishing a consistent policy. This labeled data is used to train a bi-encoder retriever with contrastive loss on policy-bucketed hard negatives, augmented with personalization features. Concurrently, two teacher models are trained: a relevance teacher via soft-label supervised fine-tuning on the oracle scores, and an engagement teacher via multi-task learning on user actions. A 0.6B student SLM is then trained via multi-teacher distillation, initialized from the relevance teacher and trained with a weighted KL loss from both teachers, combined with task-specific loss masking. Before deployment, the student model is pruned (50% MLP neurons, final 8 layers removed) and fine-tuned. At inference, the system employs several optimizations: offline RL-trained summarization compresses candidate documents, MixLM replaces most text with cached embedding tokens, and the serving stack uses scoring-only prefill with shared-prefix amortization and CUDA graphs to maximize throughput.

## Key Results
- Job Search: +7.73% NDCG@10 and -46.88% Poor Match Rate@10.
- People Search: >10% NDCG@10 improvement.
- Business Impact: +1.2% lift in daily active users post-launch.
- Efficiency: >75x increase in ranking throughput (from 290 to 22,000 items/s/GPU) while meeting a strict p99 latency budget of 500ms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system succeeds by co-optimizing model design, training data, and inference infrastructure for a fixed latency envelope. The 8B oracle judge ensures a consistent, high-quality relevance signal for training. The multi-teacher distillation transfers both relevance and engagement knowledge into a tiny student model. Critically, aggressive context length reduction is achieved by removing text via offline summarization and replacing residual text with embeddings (MixLM), which allows the lightweight SLM to score tens of thousands of candidates per second. System-level serving optimizations (prefill-only, shared prefixes, CUDA graphs) then maximize hardware utilization, turning the efficient model into a scalable service.

## Foundational Learning
*Multi-Teacher Distillation*  
- Why needed: To compress the capabilities of large teacher models (relevance and engagement) into a single, tiny student SLM without catastrophic forgetting.  
- Quick check: Train a small model via KL divergence from two larger models' logits; measure performance relative to each teacher on held-out data.

*Structured Pruning (OSSCAR)*  
- Why needed: To reduce the FLOPs and memory footprint of the student SLM to meet throughput targets with minimal relevance loss.  
- Quick check: Apply structured pruning (e.g., removing entire neurons or layers) to a dense model and fine-tune; compare NDCG loss vs. speedup.

*Text-Embedding Hybrid Interaction (MixLM)*  
- Why needed: To emulate the quality of full-text cross-attention while avoiding the O(N) context length cost per candidate.  
- Quick check: Replace token embeddings in a cross-encoder input with frozen average-pooled embeddings from a separate encoder; measure ranking metric delta vs. full-text baseline.

*Inference Prefill Optimizations*  
- Why needed: To eliminate sampling overhead and amortize prompt processing costs across many candidates.  
- Quick check: Implement a "scoring-only" prefill mode in a decoder model's serving stack; profile per-token latency reduction.

*Policy-Bucketed Hard Negative Sampling*  
- Why needed: To construct high-quality contrastive training data that reflects the system's ranking policy and prevents the retriever from retrieving semantically similar but irrelevant items.  
- Quick check: Group negatives by inferred policy bucket (e.g., skill-mismatch, experience-level mismatch); sample hard negatives proportionally and train bi-encoder; measure recall@k.

*Offline RL Document Summarization*  
- Why needed: To minimize information loss from document truncation by learning a compression policy optimized for downstream engagement.  
- Quick check: Train a summarization model with RL where reward is an engagement predictor's score on the summary; evaluate summary fidelity and ranking performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**
Query & Searcher Context -> Bi-encoder Retriever -> Top-K Candidates -> Offline Summaries + MixLM Tokens -> Student SLM Ranker -> Ranked List
Oracle Judge (8B) -> Labels for Retriever & Teachers
Engagement Teacher (multi-task) -> Distillation Targets
Relevance Teacher (soft SFT) -> Distillation Targets & Warm Start

**Critical Path**
1.  Oracle judge generates relevance labels from logs.
2.  Bi-encoder trained with contrastive loss on retrieved candidates.
3.  Teacher models (relevance & engagement) are trained independently.
4.  Student SLM initialized from relevance teacher and distilled on both teachers' outputs.
5.  Student is pruned and fine-tuned.
6.  At query time, candidate documents are pre-summarized, encoded into MixLM tokens, and scored by the pruned SLM in a scoring-only prefill pass.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- *Quality vs. Latency*: MixLM and summarization risk information loss for complex queries but are necessary to meet 500ms budget. The tradeoff is managed by RL training for summarization and distillation for MixLM.
- *Model Size vs. Throughput*: A 0.6B SLM is large enough for strong distillation but small enough for high throughput after pruning and inference optimizations. A larger model would exceed latency limits.
- *Generality vs. Efficiency*: Hard negative sampling based on a fixed policy bucket is efficient but may not cover all failure modes of the deployed ranker.

**Failure Signatures**
- Student model NDCG drops significantly (>4%) from its teachers, indicating inadequate distillation or data scaling.
- Throughput falls below 1000 items/s/GPU at p99<500ms, suggesting prefill, tokenization, or CUDA graph bottlenecks.
- Pruning causes a sharp NDCG decline (>2%), pointing to over-pruning or insufficient fine-tuning.

**First Three Experiments**
1.  Implement the oracle judge prompt and validate its kappa agreement on a human-annotated sample set.
2.  Train the bi-encoder with the specified contrastive loss on a sampled log dataset and evaluate retrieval recall@100.
3.  Train a minimal student SLM (e.g., 100M params) via single-teacher distillation from the relevance teacher to establish a baseline quality and compute cost before scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can longer-term user context (e.g., historical search behavior, career trajectory) be effectively integrated into the semantic ranking model without violating latency constraints or introducing historical bias?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Future Work" section states a focus on "deeper personalization using longer-term user context." The current system uses summarized profiles and recent activity but does not detail modeling of extended temporal dynamics.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not specify architectural or training modifications needed to incorporate multi-session context at scale. Longer histories increase context length, conflicting with the strict 500ms budget, and may amplify feedback loops or privacy risks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies comparing ranker quality (NDCG, engagement) with varying historical context windows (e.g., 1 day vs. 90 days), under controlled latency budgets. Cost-benefit analysis of context compression techniques (e.g., hierarchical summarization) and bias audits on demographic subgroups.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the failure modes of the MixLM architecture when queries or candidates require nuanced, fine-grained semantic interactions that may not be captured by a small set of cached embedding tokens?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] MixLM replaces most item text with cached embedding tokens from an encoder (§2.3.3), claiming preserved cross-encoder quality. However, the paper only reports aggregate NDCG lifts; it does not analyze performance on queries requiring complex reasoning (e.g., "jobs requiring hybrid skills matching my varied experience") or on candidates with subtle textual distinctions.
- **Why unresolved:** The distillation-based training (§2.1.3) may not fully transfer the full-text teacher's capacity to handle intricate query-document interactions when input is reduced to a fixed-size embedding. The limits of this compression are unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Error analysis categorizing query types (e.g., multi-intent, attribute-heavy) and candidate types (e.g., long-form descriptions). Controlled A/B tests pitting MixLM against full-text SLM on subsets known to require deep reading, measuring delta in relevance/engagement.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How robust and unbiased is the SAGE LLM-based evaluation framework when used as the primary governance signal for launch decisions, given potential biases in the teacher LLM or human precedent data?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] SAGE is described as operationalizing relevance policy using an 8B LLM judge trained on human precedent data (§1). It reports alignment metrics (linear kappa 0.77 with humans, 0.81 with its teacher).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not address whether the LLM judge's judgments exhibit systematic bias across protected attributes (e.g., gender, ethnicity in profiles) or query categories. Governance relying on a single model risks amplifying embedded societal biases, especially if human precedent data reflects historical inequities.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Differential performance audits of SAGE scores across demographic slices. Sensitivity analysis to prompt variations or teacher model updates. Comparison of launch decisions (e.g., which model variants passed) if alternative, unbiased evaluation metrics were used.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent does the offline document summarization step (for context compression) introduce representational loss that disproportionately harms specific document types or query intents, and can this be mitigated without regressing throughput gains?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Summarization uses RL-trained 1.7B models (§2.3.1, [3]) to reduce document length "with negligible quality loss." However, the paper admits "naive summarization degrades performance" and the RL reward is based on the engagement teacher predicting a single next action. This may not preserve information crucial for other relevance aspects (e.g., required skills, company culture).
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation focuses on aggregate ranking metrics (NDCG) and throughput. There is no breakdown by document complexity (e.g., very long job descriptions vs. concise ones) or query type (e.g., exploratory vs. specific). The RL training objective may optimize for engagement signals at the expense of completeness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Human evaluation of summaries for fidelity across document categories. Correlation analysis between summary quality metrics (e.g., factual consistency, key-point retention) and downstream ranking performance for hard queries. Exploration of query-aware or adaptive summarization lengths.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can the proposed inference optimizations (e.g., shared-prefix amortization, CUDA graphs) and model pruning be generalized to other model architectures (e.g., MoE models) or hardware backends (e.g., older GPU generations, CPUs) without substantial quality or throughput degradation?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The system is co-designed for H100 GPUs and a specific 0.6B decoder-based SLM (Table 10, §2.5). Techniques like piecewise CUDA graphs and in-batch prefix caching exploit deterministic prefill patterns. The paper does not discuss portability.
- **Why unresolved:** Production environments are heterogeneous. Pruning patterns (OSSCAR [25]) and prefill-only execution may not apply efficiently to Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, which are gaining traction. The throughput gains (75x) are reported on a fixed H100 setup; other infrastructures may see different scaling.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication studies on different GPU architectures (e.g., A100, H200) and model types (e.g., 1B MoE). Characterization of which optimizations are hardware-agnostic (e.g., loss masking, distillation) vs. hardware-specific (e.g., CUDA graph shape stability). Formal modeling of latency and throughput as a function of model topology and batch size.

## Limitations
- **Critical details withheld:** Exact architecture of the 0.6B student SLM and the precise OSSCAR pruning mask (which layers/neurons) are not disclosed, blocking exact replication of the efficiency-quality trade-off.
- **Oracle judge opacity:** The specific prompt template, grading rubric, and few-shot examples for the 8B relevance judge are unknown, making label distribution and quality difficult to reproduce.
- **Algorithmic specifics missing:** The policy-bucketed hard negative sampling procedure (computation of `P_i`, `G_i`) and the MixLM training schedule (embedding token count, text replacement strategy, distillation temperature) are not fully specified.

## Confidence
- **Architecture & System Design (High):** The high-level component flow and co-design rationale are clearly described.
- **Online Metric Improvements (Medium):** Specific lifts are reported (e.g., +7.73% NDCG@10), but without ablations isolating individual component contributions.
- **Efficiency Gains (Medium):** The 75x throughput claim is precise but confidence is medium due to the opaque serving stack optimizations and hardware baseline.
- **Distillation & Pruning Efficacy (Low):** Claims of "near-teacher-level NDCG" lack detailed ablation numbers (e.g., exact pre/post pruning NDCG gaps), making performance verification difficult.
