# Neuro-Symbolic Process Anomaly Detection
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.26461
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26461
*   **Reference count:** 24
*   **Primary result:** A neuro-symbolic autoencoder that integrates Declare constraints via Logic Tensor Networks (LTN) improves F1 scores in process anomaly detection by correctly classifying rare but conformant traces, with gains evident from as few as 10 such traces, though performance heavily depends on the selected constraint's quality.

## Executive Summary
Process anomaly detection using autoencoders often misclassifies rare but valid process executions as anomalies due to their statistical infrequency. This paper proposes a neuro-symbolic autoencoder that injects domain knowledge in the form of Declare constraints. These constraints are transformed into differentiable, soft logical objectives using Logic Tensor Networks (LTN) and added as an auxiliary loss during model fine-tuning. Evaluations on synthetic and real-world event logs show consistent F1 score improvements over a standard autoencoder baseline. The method requires very few examples of rare conformant behavior (as few as 10 traces) to be effective. However, the performance gain is highly sensitive to the specific Declare constraint chosen, demonstrating that the quality of the human-provided domain knowledge is a critical factor.

## Method Summary
The method follows a two-phase training procedure. First, a standard denoising autoencoder is pretrained on all conformant traces to learn a general reconstruction model. Second, domain knowledge in the form of Declare constraints is mined from the event log and filtered for patterns with low support (rare antecedent) and high confidence (high satisfaction rate). For each selected constraint, the autoencoder is wrapped in a Logic Tensor Network (LTN) layer. This layer defines predicates for activity occurrences at specific positions in a trace and composes them according to the constraint's First-Order Logic (FOL) semantics. The model is then fine-tuned using a combined loss: the standard reconstruction loss plus a satisfiability loss that encourages the model to satisfy the constraint on a set of rare conformant traces (`t+`) and violate it on a set of anomalous traces (`t-`). After training, anomaly detection proceeds by computing the reconstruction error for each trace and thresholding based on a heuristic.

## Key Results
*   LTN fine-tuning consistently improves F1 scores over a baseline autoencoder on synthetic logs and the BPIC12 and BPIC13 real-world logs.
*   Performance gains are achieved with very few examples of rare conformant traces (as few as 10).
*   The effectiveness varies dramatically with the specific Declare constraint type selected (e.g., `Response` vs. `Succession`), confirming dependency on knowledge quality.
*   The method fails to improve performance on more complex logs (Wide, BPIC17), highlighting limitations in the autoencoder's capacity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The neuro-symbolic autoencoder reduces misclassification of rare but conformant traces by integrating Declare constraints as soft logical objectives during training.
*   **Mechanism:** Logic Tensor Networks (LTN) convert Declare constraints (expressed in First-Order Logic) into differentiable, real-valued functions that are added as auxiliary loss terms. This guides the autoencoder to reconstruct traces satisfying selected constraints more accurately, even if they are statistically rare.
*   **Core assumption:** That Declare constraints with high confidence and low support legitimately capture rare but valid process behavior, and that enforcing their satisfiability in the latent space improves anomaly discrimination.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...encode Declare constraints as soft logical guiderails within the learning process to distinguish between anomalous and rare but conformant behavior."
    *   [section 3.3] Details the LTN loss composition: `LLT N = λrec·Lreconstruction + λsat·(∀t+ : Φ(t+) ∧ ∀t− : ¬Φ(t−))`
    *   [corpus] Weak direct evidence; related work on neuro-symbolic anomaly detection (e.g., RPG-AE, xSemAD) shows similar patterns of using symbolic patterns to counter rarity, but no direct citation.
*   **Break condition:** If the selected Declare constraints poorly represent true rare conformant behavior (e.g., low confidence constraints), the soft objective misguides learning, potentially degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Performance hinges on selecting Declare constraints that correctly identify rare but conformant traces via a support-confidence filter.
*   **Mechanism:** Constraints are mined from the event log and filtered for `low support` (rare antecedent occurrence) and `high confidence` (high satisfaction rate when antecedent occurs). This statistical profile, combined with human domain knowledge, aims to isolate legitimate rare executions for the `t+` set in LTN training.
*   **Core assumption:** That the `low support, high confidence` pattern reliably separates rare-conformant from anomalous traces without manual labeling.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "...effectiveness is highly dependent on the specific Declare constraint selected, demonstrating that human domain knowledge quality directly influences performance."
    *   [section 3.2] "We focus on constraints with low support and high confidence. This combination indicates that while the constraint’s antecedent rarely occurs...it is almost always satisfied."
    *   [section 4.4] Ablation study shows F1 gains vary drastically by constraint type (e.g., `Response` vs. `Succession`), confirming selection sensitivity.
*   **Break condition:** If the process lacks clear, high-confidence Declare constraints for rare-but-valid patterns, the filtered knowledge base may be empty or misleading, providing no useful signal to LTN.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The pretrained autoencoder provides a stable reconstruction foundation; LTN fine-tuning then biases it toward preserving patterns in `t+` without catastrophic forgetting of common patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** Two-phase training: 1) Pretrain autoencoder on all traces (including rare ones) to learn general reconstruction. 2) Fine-tune with LTN loss that `∀t+ : Φ(t+)` (preserve rare-conformant) and `∀t− : ¬Φ(t−)` (amplify anomaly error), using a small `λ_sat` weight to avoid overwriting the pretrained representation.
*   **Core assumption:** That the autoencoder's initial weights encode sufficient general process structure such that constraint-based fine-tuning adjusts only necessary parameters, improving `t+` reconstruction while maintaining baseline performance on common traces.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.1] "We pretrain it on the event log to establish a baseline understanding...we enrich this pretrained model with domain knowledge."
    *   [section 3.3] `LLT N` combines reconstruction and satisfiability losses; `λ` hyperparameters balance them.
    *   [section 4.2] Improvements visible with as few as 10 `t+` traces, suggesting fine-tuning requires little data to adjust the pretrained model.
*   **Break condition:** If the initial autoencoder is undercapacity or the process is too complex (e.g., `Wide`, `BPIC17` logs), fine-tuning may cause overfitting to `t+` patterns, harming generalization and nullifying gains.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Declare Constraint Mining & Semantics**
    *   **Why needed here:** The entire knowledge injection depends on correctly extracting and interpreting Declare constraints (e.g., `Response`, `Chain Response`) from event logs to represent domain rules.
    *   **Quick check question:** Given a mined `Response(A, B)` constraint with confidence 0.95 and support 0.02, what does this imply about the frequency of `A` and the likelihood of `B` following `A` in traces?
*   **Concept: Logic Tensor Networks (LTN)**
    *   **Why needed here:** LTN is the mechanism for differentiable symbolic integration; understanding fuzzy logic operators (`∃` as `ApM`, `∀` as `ApME`) is key to grasping how constraint satisfiability is computed and optimized.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why is `ApME` used for the universal quantifier (`∀`) in the LTN loss for `t+` traces, and what would happen if `ApM` were used instead?
*   **Concept: Autoencoder-Based Anomaly Detection**
    *   **Why needed here:** The baseline and enhanced models are autoencoders; anomaly scoring relies on reconstruction error. Understanding how trace encoding/decoding works and why rare traces yield high error is fundamental.
    *   **Quick check question:** In a denoising autoencoder for process traces, why might a rare but fully conformant trace still produce a high reconstruction error during evaluation?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Trace Input -> One-Hot Encoding -> Trace Encoder -> Latent Vector -> Trace Decoder -> Reconstructed Trace -> LTN Layer (with Declare Constraint FOL & Predicates) -> Combined Loss (Reconstruction + Satisfiability) -> Anomaly Scorer (Reconstruction Error)
*   **Critical path:** Event Log -> Preprocessing -> (1) AE Pretraining -> (2) Declare Mining -> (3) Constraint Selection -> (4) LTN Fine-tuning (using `t+`/`t−` split) -> (5) Anomaly Detection.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Hard vs. Soft Constraints:* LTN uses soft (differentiable) constraints, avoiding binary satisfiability but requiring careful `λ` tuning to balance with reconstruction loss.
    *   *Constraint Generality vs. Specificity:* More constraints inject more knowledge but increase risk of conflicting objectives or overfitting; ablation studies needed to pick 1–2 high-impact constraints.
    *   *Autoencoder Capacity:* Simpler AE (as in paper) trains faster but may lack expressiveness for complex processes (`Wide`, `BPIC17`), limiting LTN gains.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Degraded performance on `Wide`/`BPIC17` logs** (Tab. 2,3): Indicates autoencoder capacity insufficient to generalize even before LTN; fine-tuning harms performance.
    *   **Low constraint satisfiability after training** (Fig. 6,7 `Succession`): LTN cannot satisfy constraint due to low confidence or conflict with common patterns; indicates poor constraint choice or `λ_sat` too high.
    *   **No improvement with >~150 rare traces** (Tab. 2): Suggests fine-tuning already saturated; more `t+` data doesn't help if AE capacity is maxed.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline replication:** Train standard denoising autoencoder on `Paper` synthetic log (all traces). Compute F1 vs. baseline in Tab. 2. Verify reconstruction error distribution matches [11] heuristics for thresholding.
    2.  **LTN injection with minimal data:** Using `Response(DevMethod, FinalDecision)` from Fig. 3, fine-tune pretrained AE with 10 `t+` traces (rare-conformant) and all anomalous `t−`. Expect F1 improvement over baseline (Tab. 2 ~0.58 vs 0.38).
    3.  **Constraint ablation:** On `BPIC12`, repeat experiment 2 for 7 constraint types (Fig. 7). Identify which constraints yield highest F1 gain; verify those also show higher final satisfiability scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can Declare constraint selection be automated for LTN-based anomaly detection?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that constraint choice significantly influences performance and relies on human domain knowledge.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Manual selection is subjective, time-consuming, and does not scale to large processes.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** An automated method (e.g., using constraint support, confidence, and diversity metrics) evaluated on multiple event logs, showing comparable or better F1 scores than manual selection with reduced expert effort.
*   **Open Question 2:** What specific aspects of process complexity cause the failure of LTN enhancements, and how can the model architecture be adapted?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The approach did not improve on Wide and BPIC17 logs because those logs were "too complex for the autoencoder to generalize effectively."
    *   **Why unresolved:** Without identifying the complexity factors and corresponding adaptations, the method cannot be applied to realistic, complex processes.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments on synthetic logs with varying complexity (activity count, trace length, variant diversity) combined with architectural modifications (e.g., hierarchical encoders, attention) that restore performance gains.
*   **Open Question 3:** Do more expressive neural architectures (e.g., LSTMs, graph neural networks) improve LTN-enhanced anomaly detection compared to simple autoencoders?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors suggest that LTN performance depends on the underlying network's generalization, and "more complex architectures, such as graph-based or LSTM models" are needed.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The baseline autoencoder may not capture temporal dependencies, limiting the benefit of symbolic constraints.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement the LTN layer with LSTM, Transformer, or GNN base models, train on the same datasets, and statistically compare F1 scores and training efficiency.
*   **Open Question 4:** How can the computational overhead of LTN training be reduced while preserving its benefits?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note that "real-valued logic does not allow for computational shortcuts like short-circuiting boolean expressions," and call for more efficient evaluation strategies.
    *   **Why unresolved:** High training cost hinders scalability to large event logs and longer traces, restricting practical use.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose optimized implementations (e.g., using sparse tensors, constraint batching, or approximate aggregators) and evaluate training time and memory usage against the standard LTN approach with comparable F1 performance.

## Limitations
*   **Architectural Unspecification:** Exact autoencoder architecture (layer types/sizes), LTN hyperparameters (`p` for aggregators, `λ` weights), and precise FOL grounding for each constraint template are not detailed, blocking direct replication.
*   **Constraint Selection Subjectivity:** Effectiveness relies on human expert selection of `low support, high confidence` Declare constraints, which is non-scalable and introduces subjectivity. Automated selection methods are not provided.
*   **Generalizability Failure:** The approach shows no improvement on more complex, realistic logs (Wide, BPIC17), indicating limited applicability and a dependency on simple underlying process structures.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Claim that LTN integrates Declare as soft constraints improving rare-conformant trace discrimination (directly described and evaluated).
*   **High:** Claim that performance is constraint-selection dependent (explicitly stated, supported by ablation).
*   **Medium:** Claim that pretraining provides stable foundation for fine-tuning (inferred from two-phase design, supported by minimal-data gains).
*   **Low:** Claim of generalizability to any process; evidence shows failure on complex logs.
