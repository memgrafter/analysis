# Toward Reliable Evaluation of LLM-Based Financial Multi-Agent Systems: Taxonomy, Coordination Primacy, and Cost Awareness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27539
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27539
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Proposes a four-dimensional taxonomy, identifies five pervasive evaluation failures that can invert performance, formulates the Coordination Primacy Hypothesis (coordination protocol > model scaling), and introduces the Coordination Breakeven Spread metric; no surveyed system meets all minimum evaluation standards.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the crisis of unreliable cross-system evaluation in LLM-based financial multi-agent systems. By analyzing 12 systems, it establishes a taxonomy across architecture, coordination, memory, and tools, and exposes five evaluation failures—look-ahead, survivorship, overfitting, cost neglect, and regime-blindness—that systematically inflate returns and can even reverse performance signs. The authors hypothesize that coordination protocol design is the primary driver of decision quality (Coordination Primacy Hypothesis), surpassing model scaling, and offer the Coordination Breakeven Spread to assess economic viability after costs. However, the survey finds no system satisfies all five minimum evaluation standards, highlighting urgent need for methodological rigor.

## Method Summary
The study conducts a qualitative literature review of 14 primary papers (12 multi-agent systems and 2 single-agent baselines) from 2023–2026. Researchers extract architectural details to classify systems along a four-dimensional taxonomy and audit evaluation methodologies against five minimum reliability standards (contamination control, point-in-time universe, rolling windows, net-of-cost returns, regime coverage). No new model training or trading simulations are performed; analysis relies solely on published content. Evidence for the Coordination Primacy Hypothesis is synthesized from reported ablations and theoretical arguments, but direct experimental validation is absent due to inadequate evaluation practices in the literature.

## Key Results
- Five evaluation failures are widespread, causing return inflation and sign reversals in systems like FinMem and TradingAgents.
- Coordination Primacy Hypothesis (CPH): Coordination protocol design is posited as the most consequential factor for decision quality, with marginal impact exceeding model scaling, yet remains untested.
- Coordination Breakeven Spread (CBS) metric defined as CBS(d) = Δp(d)/2 to determine if coordination's benefits exceed transaction costs and latency.
- All surveyed systems score below 5/5 on minimum evaluation standards; none meet all criteria.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mechanism 1: Coordination Primacy Hypothesis. The inter-agent coordination protocol (D2) structures information exchange and aggregation, directly shaping decision accuracy by mitigating individual errors through structured verification. Core assumption: D2's design has larger marginal impact than LLM scaling in decomposed financial tasks. Evidence: Adjacent systems innovate in coordination; theoretical support from structural patterns. Break condition: A factorial experiment shows model scaling improvements exceed coordination effects on net-of-cost, regime-robust metrics.

Mechanism 2: Coordination Breakeven Spread (CBS). CBS translates coordination-driven Sharpe gains into a basis-point threshold (Δp(d)/2), requiring an asset's typical bid-ask spread to be below this for coordination to be economically viable. Core assumption: Transaction costs and coordination-induced latency are primary fates that eliminate nominal gains. Evidence: CBS directly addresses pervasive cost neglect; no prior system computes it. Break condition: Empirical Δp(d) across assets/regimes consistently falls below observed spreads, invalidating CBS.

Mechanism 3: Pervasive Evaluation Failures. Look-ahead, survivorship, overfitting, cost neglect, and regime-blindness confound performance metrics, making any observed advantage possibly an artifact. Core assumption: Current evaluation is so compromised that rankings are untrustworthy, blocking CPH validation. Evidence: Documented reversals in multiple systems; survey shows most systems satisfy ≤2 standards. Break condition: Community benchmark enforcing all standards adopted, with stable rankings post-adjustment.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Four independent but interacting dimensions: D1 (Architecture Pattern) sets agent roles and flow; D2 (Coordination Mechanism) is the core interaction protocol; D3 (Memory Architecture) manages historical context; D4 (Tool Integration) controls data/execution access. CPH implies D2 is the highest-leverage knob; choices must be compatible (e.g., debate requires non-pipeline D1).

Critical path: Begin by selecting and refining D2 (Coordination), as it most impacts decision quality. Then choose D1 (Architecture) compatible with D2 (e.g., debate supports multiple agents). D3 (Memory) and D4 (Tools) follow, tuned to D2's latency and information demands.

Design tradeoffs:
- Cost vs. Signal: More agents/rounds boost signal but increase inference and coordination costs quadratically.
- Debate vs. Latency: Each debate round adds 1–3 seconds, risking slippage that negates gains in high-frequency trading.
- Memory Depth vs. Regime Drift: Richer memory risks anchoring during structural breaks; requires regime-change detection triggers.
- Planner-Executor Depth: Minimal 3-stage pipeline (signal, risk, execution) often suffices; extra stages yield diminishing returns.

Failure signatures:
- Evaluation: Low Evaluation Quality score (<3/5), especially missing cost/regime standards.
- Performance: Extreme Sharpe (>5) over <6 months in a single bull market.
- Replication: Performance sign reversal under slightly different windows or with costs added.
- Live vs. Backtest: Large gap indicating unaccounted costs or look-ahead.

First 3 experiments:
1. CPH Ablation: Run identical agents/tools with swapped coordination protocols (e.g., hierarchical vs. debate); measure net-of-cost, rolling-window Sharpe to isolate D2 effect.
2. CBS Calibration: Simulate coordinated vs. solo strategies across liquidity tiers; compute Δp(d) and compare CBS to typical spreads (e.g., large-cap vs. small-cap).
3. Memory Under Stress: Evaluate memory-equipped agent (e.g., FinMem-style) on a regime-shift benchmark (e.g., bull-to-bear); compare with/without regime-change detection triggers, measuring drawdown and reversal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we construct a financially multi-agent system that satisfies all five minimum evaluation standards (contamination control, point-in-time universe, rolling windows, net-of-cost returns, regime coverage) to produce trustworthy benchmarks?
- Under rigorous evaluation, does the Coordination Primacy Hypothesis hold across diverse coordination protocols, or are there regimes where model scaling or other factors dominate?
- What are the empirical values of Δp(d) for different coordination designs and asset classes, and how does the Coordination Breakeakeven Spread compare to realistic transaction costs and latency constraints?
- How do the five evaluation failures interact, and which mitigation strategies (e.g., cross-validation, synthetic regimes) most effectively restore validity?
- Can a community-driven benchmark be established that enforces these standards and yields stable, reproducible performance rankings?

## Limitations
- Absence of raw trade logs and code for most systems blocks exact replication and direct verification of reported returns.
- CBS metric lacks empirical Δp(d) calibration; current formulation is theoretical and illustrative.
- Hyperparameter details insufficient for quantitative overfitting analysis; CPH remains untested due to lack of controlled coordination-only experiments.

## Confidence
- Four-dimensional taxonomy utility: Medium
- Five evaluation failures pervasive: High
- Coordination Primacy Hypothesis (CPH): Low
- Coordination Breakeven Spread (CBS): Medium
- Audit showing no system meets standards: High

## Next Checks
1. Factorial ablation: Vary only coordination protocol in a fixed system, measuring net Sharpe with costs and regime segmentation.
2. CBS calibration: Estimate Δp(d) via controlled A/B tests across asset liquidity tiers, compare to observed transaction spreads.
3. Full replication: Re-run an open system (e.g., FinCon) under all five evaluation standards to quantify deviations from published metrics.
