# Federated Distributional Reinforcement Learning with Distributional Critic Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17820
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17820
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** TR-FedDistRL mitigates mean-smearing in federated distributional RL by anchoring client updates to a CVaR‑weighted Wasserstein barycenter, reducing output drift and safety incidents while maintaining returns.

## Executive Summary
Federated averaging of distributional critics in heterogeneous environments can distort return distributions, obscuring tail risks critical for safety. This paper introduces FedDistRL and its extension TR-FedDistRL, which uses a per‑client, CVaR‑weighted Wasserstein barycenter of recent critic outputs as a trust region to regularize updates. Across bandit, gridworld, and highway tasks, the method reduces mean‑smearing, lowers critic output drift, and improves safety metrics (e.g., lower catastrophe and accident rates) compared to standard federated averaging, with theoretical guarantees of contractiveness in a Wasserstein‑based metric.

## Method Summary
The approach federates quantile‑based distributional critics. Clients locally perform PPO‑style updates with a CVaR‑scaled advantage and quantile regression critic. After standard FedAvg parameter aggregation, each client constructs a risk‑aware reference distribution: a CVaR‑weighted Wasserstein‑1 barycenter over a buffer of its recent critic outputs. The client then applies a “shrink‑squash” trust‑region update to the aggregated critic, interpolating toward the reference and clipping quantiles within asymmetric tubes to constrain deviation. This preserves tail structure despite heterogeneous client environments.

## Key Results
- Reduces mean‑smearing: FedAvg’s parameter averaging smooths multimodality, while the barycenter‑based approach preserves distributional structure.
- Lowers critic output drift: e.g., gridworld drift reduced from 159 (FedAvg) to 98 (TR‑FedDistRL).
- Improves safety: catastrophe rate 0.017 vs. 0.032; highway accident rate 0.653 vs. 0.811.
- Maintains competitive returns: safety gains come without significant performance loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Standard federated parameter averaging of distributional critics induces “mean‑smearing.” When clients with heterogeneous return distributions average quantile‑based critic parameters, the arithmetic mean in weight space does not correspond to averaging in distribution space. Instead, the aggregated critic’s output approximates an arithmetic mean of individual clients’ predicted quantiles, which mixes distinct modes and shrinks extreme quantiles toward the mean. This collapses multimodality and attenuates tail behavior, degrading risk estimates.

### Mechanism 2
TR‑FedDistRL anchors each client’s critic update to a local, risk‑aware reference. Each client maintains a buffer of its recent critic outputs (quantile vectors). It computes a CVaR‑weighted Wasserstein‑1 barycenter over this buffer, giving higher weight to distributions with worse conditional Value‑at‑Risk. This barycenter serves as reference Z̄_i. After server aggregation, the client applies a shrink‑squash step: interpolate the aggregated critic toward Z̄_i, then clip each quantile to stay within an asymmetric tube (δ⁻, δ⁺) around Z̄_i’s quantiles. This constrains the update to a neighborhood that preserves downside risk.

### Mechanism 3
The constrained critic update is contractive in a Wasserstein‑1‑based metric. The client’s critic iterate q_i^n and reference q̄_i^n satisfy a linear recursion for the tracking error e_n = d(q_i^n, q̄_i^n). Under assumptions that the projected evaluation operator is γ‑contractive and the squash operator is Lipschitz, the error converges geometrically if α β_max γ < 1. This provides a theoretical stability guarantee that prevents unbounded drift away from the moving risk‑aware reference.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map** (plain text flow):
Client experience D_i^t -> Local PPO update (actor + CVaR‑scaled advantage) -> Critic update (quantile regression) -> Upload θ_i to server -> Server FedAvg -> Broadcast θ_avg -> Client buffer B_i (stores recent q_θ_i outputs) -> Barycenter module (compute Z̄_i via CVaR‑weighted W1) -> Trust region module (shrink + squash on θ_avg) -> New critic q_i -> Loop.

**Critical path:**
1. Client collects local trajectories.
2. Updates local policy and quantile critic.
3. Uploads critic parameters.
4. Server aggregates parameters via weighted average.
5. Client downloads aggregated parameters.
6. Constructs risk‑aware reference Z̄_i from buffer using CVaR weights.
7. Applies shrink (interpolation) and squash (tube clipping) to aggregated critic relative to Z̄_i.
8. Repeats.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Barycenter computation cost vs. buffer size and probe‑set coverage.
- Tube widths (δ⁻, δ⁺): too tight stalls learning; too loose fails to preserve tails.
- CVaR level α_cvar: smaller focuses on extreme tail but high variance; larger approaches mean.
- Communication frequency: more rounds reduce drift but increase overhead.

**Failure signatures:**
- Rising critic output drift (L1 on probe set) despite TR → tube too wide or reference outdated.
- Increasing catastrophe/accident rate → buffer lacks tail events or CVaR weighting mis‑identifies risk.
- Return collapse in sparse‑reward tasks → trust region too restrictive.
- One client diverges significantly → its heterogeneity not tracked by barycenter (λ_r may need tuning).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Bandit visualization:** Two‑armed bandit with clients having differing Gaussian rewards. Compare FedAvg’s mean‑smearing vs. barycenter aggregation; validate CVaR barycenter shifts toward heavier tails.
2. **Gridworld safety:** 10×10 warehouse with hazard zones. Measure catastrophe rate and output drift for Local, FedAvg, FedAvg(CVaR), FedAvg(CVaR+TR). Verify TR reduces drift and catastrophes, possibly with some return cost.
3. **Highway continuous control:** highway‑env with 5 heterogeneous clients (varying traffic). Track accident rate and policy KL drift. Confirm FedAvg(CVaR+TR) achieves lowest accident rate with competitive return and reduced cKL vs. FedAvg(CVaR).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extend stability theory to the full control setting.** The current analysis covers only fixed‑policy evaluation; incorporating policy updates could break contraction.
- **Improve reference construction under nonstationarity.** The buffer of recent critics may become outdated as the policy changes, degrading the barycentric reference.
- **Support heterogeneous critic architectures.** Current parameter averaging assumes identical architectures; real deployments may have varied model structures.
- **Scale to richer safety constraints and larger‑scale deployments.** Evaluation is limited to small simulated benchmarks; generalization to high‑dimensional domains with dense constraints remains open.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (shrink α, tube radii δ⁻/δ⁺, buffer B, CVaR α_cvar) are not fully disclosed; performance may be sensitive to these.
- Theoretical contraction relies on bounded errors and a fixed probe set, which may not hold under deep function approximation and non‑stationarity.
- Per‑client barycenter computation each round could be costly; scalability to large state‑action spaces is unclear.
- Experiments confined to simulated benchmarks; real‑world safety performance is untested.

## Confidence
- **Mean‑smearing from FedAvg:** High – bandit visuals and quantile arithmetic support the claim.
- **TR‑FedDistRL tail preservation:** Medium – empirical drift/safety gains are solid, but hyperparameter sensitivity and CVaR weighting need further validation.
- **Contractive critic update:** Medium – theoretical contraction assumes strong conditions; empirical tracking error is bounded but generalization is uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate CVaR weighting:** Run highway experiments with uniform (non‑risk‑weighted) barycenter weights; compare accident rates to isolate downside‑risk benefit.
2. **Empirically test contraction:** Measure d(q_i^n, q̄_i^n) across rounds with varying tube widths; verify geometric decay and the α β_max γ factor.
3. **Profile scalability:** Benchmark barycenter computation time vs. buffer size B and number of probe states; identify thresholds for high‑dimensional use cases.
