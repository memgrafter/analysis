# How Much Reasoning Do Retrieval-Augmented Models Add beyond LLMs? A Benchmarking Framework for Multi-Hop Inference over Hybrid Knowledge
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10210
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10210
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLM-only performance remains low (23–40%) on contamination-free questions; adding retrieval yields large gains (7–29 absolute points), and hybrid KG-RAG methods consistently outperform text-only RAG.
## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical issue of benchmark contamination in evaluating Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models, where test data overlaps with LLM pretraining, obscuring true retrieval and reasoning abilities. It introduces HybridRAG-Bench, a framework that automatically builds contamination-aware benchmarks by sourcing knowledge from recent arXiv papers, aligning unstructured text with knowledge graphs, and generating questions that require multi-hop integration of both modalities. Experiments demonstrate that even large LLMs fail on these temporally isolated questions, while hybrid KG-RAG approaches show significant and consistent improvements, validating the framework's ability to isolate retrieval-intensive reasoning from parametric recall.
## Method Summary
HybridRAG-Bench constructs benchmarks by first collecting a corpus of scientific papers from arXiv within a user-specified, post-cutoff time window. An automated pipeline (EvoKG) extracts entities and relations from each paper to build a temporal knowledge graph, then aligns facts across documents. QA pairs are generated by sampling explicit multi-hop reasoning paths from this graph, retrieving supporting text spans from the corpus, and prompting an LLM to formulate a question that necessitates integrating both the graph path and textual evidence. A quality control step filters invalid or ambiguous questions, yielding a final test set where all answers are temporally out-of-distribution for the model.
## Key Results
- LLM-only performance is low (23–40%) even for state-of-the-art models, confirming the benchmark's difficulty and effective contamination avoidance.
- Adding external retrieval provides substantial gains (7–29 absolute points) over LLM-only, demonstrating the value of retrieval-augmentation.
- Hybrid KG-RAG methods that jointly leverage structured graphs and unstructured text consistently outperform text-only RAG baselines.
- Performance varies significantly by question type; counterfactual queries are especially discriminative for distinguishing reasoning strategies.
## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Temporal Isolation Prevents Memorization
- Claim: The benchmark avoids parametric memorization contamination by sourcing all knowledge from post-pretraining scientific literature.
- Mechanism: The framework collects documents from arXiv within user-specified, recent time windows (e.g., after an LLM's cutoff date). Questions and answers are derived exclusively from these documents and the induced knowledge graph, ensuring no correct answer exists in the model's pretraining data.
- Core assumption: Models cannot answer questions correctly without retrieving the freshly provided external knowledge, as the factual content is temporally out-of-distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "collects time-framed scientific corpora from user-specified topics on arXiv, reducing overlap with LLM pretraining data."
  - [section 3.2]: "We assume that all documents, knowledge graph facts, and answers are strictly newer than the pretraining cutoff... correct answers cannot be recovered via parametric memorization alone."
- Break condition: If the "recent" corpora still contain facts that were widely disseminated in pretraining data (e.g., via web archives or earlier preprints), the contamination barrier fails.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Grounding Enforces Multi-Modal Integration
- Claim: Questions require genuine multi-hop integration of unstructured text and structured knowledge graphs, not just single-source retrieval.
- Mechanism: QA pairs are grounded in explicit reasoning paths sampled from the knowledge graph. For each path, supporting text spans from the corpus are retrieved. The generated question implicitly requires chaining these graph relations while verifying constraints from the text, making both modalities necessary.
- Core assumption: A model that only retrieves text or only queries the KG will fail on questions where evidence is split or needs relational composition across both sources.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3]: "constructs benchmark questions by grounding them in both structured reasoning paths from the knowledge graph and supporting unstructured textual evidence."
  - [section 5.3 (RQ3)]: "Hybrid KG-RAG methods that jointly leverage structured graph information and unstructured text consistently outperform text-only RAG baselines."
- Break condition: If the KG construction misses critical relations or the text alignment is poor, questions might become answerable from one source alone, weakening the diagnostic value.

### Mechanism 3: Fine-Grained Question Types Diagnose Strategy Gaps
- Claim: The benchmark discriminates between retrieval/reasoning strategies via diverse, fine-grained question types that stress different capabilities.
- Mechanism: Six question types (e.g., counterfactual, hard multi-hop) are generated by programmatically perturbing or sampling KG paths. Each type targets a specific failure mode (e.g., counterfactuals test causal reasoning beyond fact lookup).
- Core assumption: Different RAG/KG-RAG algorithms have distinct strengths/weaknesses across these reasoning regimes, so overall accuracy masks important behavioral differences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3]: Lists question types and their generation logic (e.g., "Counterfactual questions are constructed by minimally perturbing a relation...").
  - [section 5.2 (RQ4)]: "HybridRAG-Bench reveals substantial and consistent performance gaps among KG-RAG baselines... especially pronounced when performance is analyzed by question type."
- Break condition: If question type generation becomes noisy or categories overlap semantically, the diagnostic resolution degrades.
## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Pretraining Data Contamination**
  - Why needed here: The core problem addressed is that existing benchmarks overlap with LLM training data, conflating memorization with reasoning. Understanding this concept is essential to grasp HybridRAG-Bench's purpose.
  - Quick check question: Why does a model's performance on a benchmark created after its training cutoff provide a cleaner signal of retrieval ability?
- **Concept: Knowledge Graph (KG) Construction from Text**
  - Why needed here: The hybrid benchmark relies on automatically extracted KGs from scientific papers. One must understand entity/relation extraction, alignment, and temporal evolution to trust the "ground truth" paths.
  - Quick check question: What are the risks of using an LLM to extract and align entities across documents (e.g., synonym handling, fragmentation)?
- **Concept: Multi-Hop Reasoning vs. Single-Hop Retrieval**
  - Why needed here: The benchmark explicitly tests the ability to chain multiple facts. Distinguishing this from simple document retrieval is key to interpreting why some methods (e.g., ToG2.0) outperform others.
  - Quick check question: How does a "hard multi-hop" question (via high-degree entities) increase the difficulty beyond a regular multi-hop question?
## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Corpus Collector -> KG Builder (EvoKG) -> QA Generator -> Quality Controller -> Evaluation Harness
- **Critical path:** Corpus Collection → KG Construction → QA Generation → Quality Control → Benchmark Release. A failure in KG construction (e.g., poor fact recovery) cascades to invalid questions.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Automation vs. Flexibility: Fully automated pipeline enables rapid regeneration for new domains/times but may generate lower-quality or less creative questions than manual curation.
  - KG Complexity vs. Scalability: Rich, evolving KGs capture more relations but increase retrieval/search space; the framework balances this via time-windowed corpora.
  - LLM Reliance: All LLM-based components (extraction, generation, judging) introduce cost, variability, and potential leakage; the framework mitigates via time-framing but cannot eliminate LLM dependency.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High LLM-only accuracy on supposedly contamination-free questions → temporal window misconfiguration or KG/text extraction missed key facts.
  - KG-based methods (1-hop KG) underperforming LLM-only → KG is too noisy or disconnected, providing misleading evidence.
  - Counterfactual questions receiving near-zero scores across all models → generation process created unanswerable or ambiguous hypotheticals.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Contamination Sanity Check**: Run a state-of-the-art LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.2) with zero retrieval on the benchmark. Expected accuracy should be low (23–40% per paper). If higher, verify document dates and KG extraction coverage.
  2. **Hybrid Grounding Validation**: Compare RAG (text-only) vs. RAG+1-hop-KG on single-hop and multi-hop subsets. Expect hybrid to win on multi-hop; if not, inspect KG connectivity and text-KG alignment for sampled paths.
  3. **Question-Type Stress Test**: Evaluate a KG-RAG method (e.g., EvoReasoner) on counterfactual vs. open-ended questions. Expect strong relative performance on counterfactuals; if not, analyze failure modes (e.g., over-cautiousness, hallucination).
## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly stated by the paper. The limitations and next steps imply potential research directions, such as mitigating residual contamination, reducing KG extraction noise, and decreasing reliance on LLMs for generation and judging, but these are not framed as open questions by the authors.
## Limitations
- Residual contamination: facts might have appeared in preprints or web archives before the cutoff.
- KG noise: automated extraction may yield incomplete relations, weakening multi-hop requirement.
- LLM reliance: introduces variability and potential leakage in generation/judging.
## Confidence
- Temporal isolation prevents memorization: Medium (depends on strict date adherence and no earlier leaks).
- Questions require true text+KG multi-hop: Medium (assumes high-quality KG alignment; could be circumvented).
- Question types discriminate strategies: Medium (programmatic perturbation may be noisy).
- Hybrid KG-RAG outperforms text-only RAG: Medium‑High (consistent empirical gains across baselines, but limited method variety).
## Next Checks
1. Contamination audit: sample 100 QA pairs; verify answer facts are absent from pretraining corpora and pre‑cutoff sources.
2. Hybrid necessity ablation: disable either KG or text retrieval on a subset; expect significant performance drop for all methods.
3. Cross-domain replication: generate benchmark for a non-scientific domain (e.g., news) and re-evaluate key baselines.
