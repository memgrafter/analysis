# Early Quantization Shrinks Codebook: A Simple Fix for Diversity-Preserving Tokenization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17052
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17052
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Deferred Quantization mitigates token shrinkage by pretraining a continuous autoencoder before codebook initialization, leading to improved reconstruction (lower r-FID, MSE) and generative diversity (higher LPIPS Diversity, Pixel Distance, lower g-FID); e.g., on ImageNet-100 with VAR, g-FID improves from 12.88 to 10.44 and Pixel Distance from 70.69 to 75.75.

## Executive Summary
Token representation shrinkage—where codebook vectors collapse into a narrow latent region during early training—limits the expressive capacity of vector-quantized tokenizers and harms downstream generative diversity. This paper identifies the root cause as the timing of quantization: applying VQ too early, combined with initializing codebooks from untrained encoder outputs that are already concentrated, biases tokens toward high-density areas. The proposed solution, Deferred Quantization, introduces a two-stage protocol: first pretrain a continuous autoencoder to learn a dispersed latent representation, then initialize the codebook from these embeddings before enabling quantization. This simple change consistently reduces shrinkage metrics (higher perplexity, larger Euclidean codebook distance) and improves reconstruction and diversity across CIFAR-10, ImageNet-100, and medical datasets, outperforming prior collapse-fixing methods.

## Method Summary
Deferred Quantization separates tokenizer training into a continuous phase and a discretization phase. In the first phase, an autoencoder (encoder + decoder) is trained without vector quantization using standard reconstruction losses (e.g., MSE, perceptual). This allows the encoder to produce a well-distributed continuous latent space that reflects the data manifold. In the second phase, the VQ codebook is initialized by collecting many embeddings from the pretrained encoder and applying K-means clustering. The full VQ-VAE (with quantization and commitment losses) is then finetuned from this dispersed starting point. The approach requires no architectural changes and is compatible with common token-based generators like MaskGIT and VAR. Critical hyperparameters include the embedding-to-token ratio (e.g., 10:1 for CIFAR-10), pretraining duration, and codebook size.

## Key Results
- **Mitigated shrinkage:** Higher codebook Euclidean distance and perplexity on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-100, indicating better token spread.
- **Improved reconstruction:** Lower r-FID, MSE, and LPIPS for the tokenizer across multiple datasets.
- **Enhanced generative diversity:** Higher LPIPS Diversity and Pixel Distance, and lower g-FID for downstream models; e.g., on ImageNet-100 with VAR, g-FID drops from 12.88 to 10.44 and Pixel Distance rises from 70.69 to 75.75.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Early quantization with untrained encoders causes shrinkage.** A randomly initialized encoder produces embeddings clustered in a compact region. Initializing the codebook from these concentrated vectors biases tokens toward that narrow area. When nearest-center quantization is applied early, the codebook is pulled toward high-density latent zones, preventing it from covering the full data manifold. This is distinct from dead-token collapse; it affects active tokens by homogenizing their spread.

**Mechanism 2: Pretraining establishes a dispersed continuous manifold before discretization.** The continuous autoencoder phase allows the encoder to learn a representation where samples are semantically distributed. Initializing the codebook from embeddings of this pretrained encoder provides broad initial coverage. Since quantization begins from a dispersed state, tokens resist collapsing and better anchor to the mature latent landscape.

**Mechanism 3: Shrinkage directly limits generative diversity.** Concentrated tokens reduce token usage entropy (perplexity ↓) and codebook spread (Euclidean distance ↓). A generator sampling from this restricted token set has diminished output support, increasing quantization distortion and lowering mode entropy. Empirically, shrunk tokenizers yield lower LPIPS Diversity and Pixel Distance in generated samples, confirming the diversity bottleneck.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Encoder (E_θ) -> Codebook (T) -> Decoder (D_θ) -> Generator (e.g., MaskGIT/VAR)

**Critical path:**  
1. Pretrain encoder-decoder as a standard autoencoder (MSE + perceptual losses) without VQ.  
2. Collect encoder embeddings from many training batches and initialize codebook via K-means.  
3. Finetune the full VQ-VAE/VQGAN with quantization enabled, using the pretrained encoder/decoder weights and the new codebook.  
4. Train a transformer generator (MaskGIT/VAR) on discrete tokens extracted from the finalized tokenizer.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Codebook size:* Larger token sets increase shrinkage risk; deferred quantization helps but may need more pretraining data.  
- *Embedding-to-token ratio:* Higher ratio (e.g., 10:1 on CIFAR-10) improves coverage but uses more memory.  
- *Pretraining epochs:* More epochs yield a more dispersed latent space but add compute; insufficient pretraining limits benefits.  
- *Loss weighting:* Commitment loss coefficient (β) must be tuned; too high causes instability, too low may not prevent shrinkage.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low perplexity (e.g., <1000 for 16k codebook) → many unused tokens, poor spread.  
- Low average pairwise Euclidean distance among codebook vectors → tokens too similar.  
- Reconstruction quality stagnating or worsening as codebook size grows.  
- Generator diversity metrics (Pixel Distance, LPIPS Diversity) dropping relative to pretrained baseline.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Train VQ-VAE on a synthetic 10-mode Gaussian mixture; visualize token distribution vs. ground truth with/without deferred quantization to directly observe shrinkage mitigation.  
2. On CIFAR-10, vary codebook size (2⁴ to 2¹⁶) and record r-FID, perplexity, Euclidean distance; verify deferred quantization maintains improvement as size increases.  
3. On ImageNet-100, compare tokenizer shrinkage metrics for MaskGIT and VAR baselines; then train downstream transformers and measure g-FID and diversity to confirm consistent gains across generators.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Performance drop at small codebook sizes:** Deferred Quantization underperforms standard VQ-VAE when token counts are small (e.g., <2¹³ on CIFAR-10). Why? Pretraining creates a dispersed continuous space that may not align well with a limited discrete codebook. Resolving this requires techniques like gradual quantization or adaptive initialization to bridge the continuous-discrete gap.
2. **GAN loss interaction with shrinkage:** GAN-based losses (e.g., in VQGAN) may mask shrinkage effects by improving decoder expressiveness, leading to misleading FID scores while diversity still suffers. A systematic study varying GAN weight and decoder capacity is needed to disentangle these effects.
3. **Comprehensive theoretical framework:** Current theory assumes ideal conditions (identity encoder/decoder, perfect transformer). Real-world training dynamics, model capacity limits, and optimization noise are ignored. A richer model linking codebook spread to diversity metrics under realistic conditions is required.
4. **Generality to other tokenization schemes and modalities:** The method is evaluated only on standard VQ-VAE/VQGAN for images. It may not extend to residual quantization, finite scalar quantization, or non-image data (audio, video) without adaptation.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Exact training details (learning rates, EMA decay, precise epochs) are not fully specified; the appendix config is unavailable, hindering faithful replication.
- **GAN confounding:** Using GAN losses in tokenizers (e.g., VQGAN) can obscure shrinkage effects, making it hard to isolate the true impact on diversity.
- **Architecture scope:** Evidence is limited to CNN-based tokenizers; transfer to transformer-based or other architectures remains unvalidated.
