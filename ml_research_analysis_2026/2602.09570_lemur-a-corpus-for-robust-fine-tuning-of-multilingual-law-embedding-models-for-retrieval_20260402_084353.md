# LEMUR: A Corpus for Robust Fine-Tuning of Multilingual Law Embedding Models for Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09570
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09570
- **Reference Count:** 26
- **Primary Result:** Fine-tuning on LEMUR yields consistent Top-k retrieval accuracy gains across 5 evaluated languages, with pronounced improvements for low-resource languages and successful cross-lingual transfer.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of unreliable multilingual legal retrieval, hindered by noisy PDF-to-text conversion and a lack of domain-adapted open embedding models. It introduces LEMUR, a curated corpus of 24,953 EU environmental legislative PDFs in 25 languages, converted with olmOCR and filtered using a Lexical Content Score (LCS) to ensure high-fidelity text. Three multilingual embedding models are fine-tuned using contrastive objectives on metadata-to-document retrieval pairs, in both monolingual and bilingual settings. Results demonstrate reliable accuracy improvements on five test languages, particularly for low-resource ones, and show that fine-tuning enhances language-independent legal semantics, enabling transfer to unseen languages.

## Method Summary
The core method revolves around fine-tuning multilingual embedding models on the LEMUR corpus via contrastive learning. The corpus is split per language (60/20/20 train/val/test) with aligned document IDs across translations. Each document is paired with its metadata as a query-document pair. Training employs a Multiple Negatives Ranking (MNR) loss; for bilingual settings, aligned translations of the same document serve as multiple positives for one query. Models (E5-Multilingual, Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-4B) are trained with bfloat16, gradient checkpointing, and early stopping. Documents are truncated to sequence limits using an unspecified strategy. Evaluation embeds test documents, indexes them, and retrieves top-k results for metadata queries, computing accuracy against the ground-truth document.

## Key Results
- E5's Top-1 accuracy improved from 81.06% to 89.43% on English after fine-tuning.
- Qwen3-0.6B's Top-1 accuracy on Latvian rose significantly from 60.41% to 74.47%.
- Gains were most pronounced for low-resource languages, and cross-lingual evaluations showed transfer to unseen languages, indicating improved language-agnostic legal representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The performance gains stem from two synergistic factors. First, the LEMUR corpus provides high-quality, aligned multilingual legal text; the olmOCR pipeline combined with LCS validation (average ~0.94) filters out the most egregious conversion errors, ensuring the model learns from relatively clean text. Second, the contrastive fine-tuning objective forces the model to align metadata queries with their correct documents while pushing apart incorrect ones. In the bilingual setting, using translations of the same document as multiple positives encourages the model to abstract away from language-specific surface forms and focus on shared legal semantics, which explains the cross-lingual transfer to unseen languages.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
LEMUR Corpus (PDFs) -> olmOCR -> Raw Text -> LCS Filtering -> Clean JSONL -> Data Splitting (Metadata/Doc Pairs) -> Model (E5/Qwen) -> Fine-tuning (MNR Loss) -> Embedding Generator -> Vector Database (Index) -> Retrieval (Cosine Similarity) -> Accuracy Metrics (Acc@k)

**Critical Path:** Data preparation (OCR + LCS + split) -> Model fine-tuning -> Evaluation indexing -> Retrieval and scoring.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- Corpus specificity vs. generalization: LEMUR's focus on environmental law ensures quality but may limit transfer to other legal domains.
- Truncation vs. information loss: Sequence length limits force truncation of 8–15% of documents, potentially removing critical clauses.
- Bilingual training complexity: Combining languages introduces sampling and loss weighting challenges, as seen in inconsistent Qwen results.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Low average LCS (<0.90) indicates systemic OCR issues, likely harming model learning.
- Performance drop on truncated documents vs. full-length ones in validation suggests truncation is destructive.
- Bilingual training yields no gain or degrades high-resource language performance, pointing to implementation mismatches in multi-positive loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Domain Generalizability:** How can LEMUR be expanded beyond environmental legislation to cover more EUR-Lex categories, improving generalizability across distinct legal styles and terminologies? *Basis:* Explicitly noted in Limitations; current scope limits topical diversity.
2. **Optimal Multilingual Training:** What are the best strategies and language combinations for bilingual/multilingual fine-tuning across different resource levels to maximize cross-lingual transfer without negative interference? *Basis:* Explicitly noted; evaluated only on EN–LV with mixed results.
3. **LCS Validation Efficacy:** To what extent does LCS correlate with human-perceived semantic fidelity or downstream retrieval performance, especially for older documents and low-resource languages? *Basis:* Inferred; LCS measures lexical overlap, not semantic correctness, and HTML may not reflect PDF layout perfectly.
4. **Semantics vs. Shallow Cues:** Are cross-lingual improvements driven by enhanced language-agnostic legal semantics or by unintended language-specific shortcuts (e.g., citation formats)? *Basis:* Explicit claim not directly tested; conclusion inferred from transfer results.

## Limitations
- Critical training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, temperature) and exact truncation strategy are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Bilingual training design (sampling, loss weighting) is vague, possibly explaining inconsistent gains for Qwen models.
- The Lexical Content Score computation details and normalization steps are incomplete, limiting independent quality verification of the corpus.

## Confidence
- **Core claim of performance gains:** Medium (reproduction feasible but hyperparameter gaps create uncertainty).
- **Cross-lingual transfer assertion:** Medium (based on limited language pairs; broader validation needed).
- **Corpus quality (LCS ~0.94):** Medium (metric definition and normalization unclear).
