# Towards Simulating Social Media Users with LLMs: Evaluating the Operational Validity of Conditioned Comment Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22752
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22752
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** Supervised fine-tuning improves lexical similarity (e.g., length, syntax) but can degrade semantic alignment (embedding distance), especially in low-resource Luxembourgish; after fine-tuning, explicit biographies become redundant as models infer persona directly from behavioral history.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Conditioned Comment Prediction (CCP) as a task to evaluate whether large language models (LLMs) can simulate authentic social media user behavior. By conditioning on either explicit biographies or implicit behavioral histories, the study compares several 8B parameter models across English, German, and Luxembourgish. Results show that supervised fine-tuning (SFT) enhances surface-level lexical features but may harm semantic fidelity, with a notable form‑content decoupling in the low‑resource Luxembourgish setting. Moreover, after SFT the explicit biography input becomes unnecessary; the model can reliably infer the user persona from behavioral history alone. The findings challenge simplistic prompting strategies and emphasize the importance of using real user interaction traces for valid simulation.

## Method Summary
The authors frame CCP as: given a social media stimulus (e.g., a post) and a target user’s prior interactions (up to 30 stimulus-response pairs), predict that user’s reply. Conditioning strategies include: (1) no conditioning (control), (2) biography‑only (a generated 4‑dimensional profile from history), (3) history‑only, and (4) both. Datasets are collected from political discourse on X (English, German) and news comments in Luxembourgish, yielding ~7.79M, ~3.38M, and ~1.02M interactions respectively, with user‑level train/test splits (3,800/650 per language). Evaluation mixes automated metrics: lexical alignment (BLEU, ROUGE‑1), semantic alignment (cosine distance of embeddings using Qwen3‑Embedding‑8B), and output length ratio. Experiments run on three 8B instruction‑tuned models (Llama‑3.1‑8B‑Instruct, Qwen3‑8B, Ministral‑8B‑Instruct‑2410) under zero‑shot prompting and after SFT. SFT trains on full sequences (max 4500 tokens) for one epoch with 8‑bit AdamW and TRL defaults on a single NVIDIA L40S. Generation uses temperature 0.75 and max_new_tokens=500, with results averaged over five seeds.

## Key Results
- SFT boosts lexical metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) and output length alignment across all languages, indicating better surface‑level text structure.
- Semantic alignment (embedding cosine distance) worsens after SFT for Luxembourgish, suggesting a disconnect between form and content in low‑resource settings; English and German show more stable or slightly improved semantic scores.
- After fine‑tuning, biography‑only conditioning yields lower fidelity than history‑only; the explicit biography becomes redundant as the model learns to extract persona cues directly from behavioral history.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper hypothesises that instruction‑tuned models initially rely on stereotypical associations from pre‑training, producing generic replies. Conditioning on behavioral history grounds the generation in the target user’s actual language patterns. SFT on CCP teaches the model to map historical interactions to the user’s future responses, improving lexical mimicry. However, in low‑resource languages the model may overfit to surface patterns without capturing deeper semantic relationships, leading to the observed form‑content decoupling. The redundancy of biographies after SFT suggests that the model learns to reconstruct the user’s latent persona implicitly from the interaction history, making explicit descriptions unnecessary.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditioned Comment Prediction (CCP):** Task of predicting a specific user’s reply to a stimulus given their past behavior. *Why needed:* Provides a rigorous test of operational validity for user simulation. *Quick check:* Can the model generate replies that match the user’s authentic response style and semantics?
- **Supervised Fine‑Tuning (SFT):** Adapts a pre‑trained model on task‑specific input‑output pairs. *Why needed:* Aligns model behaviour with the CCP objective. *Quick check:* Does fine‑tuning improve both lexical and semantic metrics compared to zero‑shot?
- **Semantic vs. Lexical Alignment:** Lexical metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) measure surface overlap; semantic metrics (embedding distance) capture meaning similarity. *Why needed:* Ensures simulation is not just copying words but preserving intent. *Quick check:* Do improvements in BLEU correlate with improved embedding similarity?
- **Explicit vs. Implicit Conditioning:** Explicit biography provides a descriptive profile; implicit history uses raw interaction traces. *Why needed:* Tests whether summarised personas are sufficient or raw history is essential. *Quick check:* Which conditioning yields higher fidelity after SFT?
- **Multilingual Evaluation:** Evaluating across languages with different resource levels (English high, German medium, Luxembourgish low). *Why needed:* Reveals how data scarcity affects simulation fidelity. *Quick check:* Is there a performance hierarchy aligned with data size?
- **8‑bit Quantisation:** Reduces memory footprint by storing model weights in 8‑bit precision. *Why needed:* Enables fine‑tuning large models on a single 48 GB GPU. *Quick check:* Does quantisation degrade final metrics compared to full precision?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
User Raw Data → Preprocessing → Split (train/test) → Biography Generator (Qwen‑3‑235B) → Conditioning Assembler → LLM (8B) → Generated Comment

**Critical Path**  
1. Preprocess raw social media posts into user‑wise chronological stimulus‑response pairs.  
2. For each user, cap history at 30 pairs and generate a 4‑dim biography if needed.  
3. Assemble model input in chat format: system prompt + biography (optional) + history (optional) + current stimulus.  
4. Train model via SFT on training inputs paired with authentic replies.  
5. Generate predictions for test stimuli under each conditioning strategy.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Explicit vs. Implicit Conditioning:** Biographies offer compactness but may lose nuance; history provides full context but increases sequence length.  
- **Model Scale:** 8B models are affordable but may lack capacity for nuanced semantic capture, especially in low‑resource languages.  
- **Quantisation:** 8‑bit enables training on limited hardware but might affect representation quality.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Out‑of‑memory crashes (OOM) during SFT if batch size or sequence length too high.  
- Data leakage: any user appearing in both train and test splits.  
- Drastic length inflation (e.g., biography‑only outputs 4.9× longer) indicating prompt mis‑configuration.  
- High variance across random seeds; unstable metrics.

**First Three Experiments**  
1. **Zero‑shot baseline:** Run each model on test set with history‑only conditioning, no fine‑tuning, compute lexical/semantic metrics.  
2. **SFT validation:** Fine‑tune on English training data, evaluate on English test under all conditioning; verify improvement over zero‑shot.  
3. **Cross‑language check:** Fine‑tune on Luxembourgish, evaluate with history‑only; check for semantic degradation relative to lexical gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How effectively can instruction‑tuned LLMs predict authentic user comments across varying linguistic resource tiers? The performance hierarchy (EN > DE > LB) is observed, but it is unclear whether this reflects language‑specific limitations or dataset unpredictability. Resolution requires matched corpora or intrinsic evaluation of representations.  
2. Does SFT universally improve prediction fidelity, or is its effectiveness constrained by model capabilities in the target language? SFT improves lexical alignment but degrades semantic grounding in low‑resource Luxembourgish, possibly due to model scale or data scarcity. Scaling studies with larger models and analysis of fine‑tuning dynamics could clarify.  
3. Can latent inference from behavioral history alone sustain persona consistency across multiple turns, or does it require explicit biographies for long‑term coherence? The paper evaluates only single‑turn prediction; biography redundancy in one step does not guarantee stability over extended interactions. Multi‑turn simulation benchmarks are needed.  
4. To what extent do automated embedding‑based metrics align with human perceptions of simulation fidelity, especially for subtle failures like tonal or ideological drift? Reliance on automated metrics may miss nuanced persona mismatches. Human evaluation studies correlated with metric scores are required.  
5. For low‑resource languages, does joint multilingual training induce beneficial cross‑lingual transfer or primarily cause interference? Mixed training showed no improvement for Luxembourgish; the hypothesis that languages occupy orthogonal subspaces remains untested at larger scales. Experiments with larger multilingual models and embedding‑space analyses are needed.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed random seed for user‑level splits and unspecified SFT hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, batch size) hinder exact replication.  
- Biography generation parameters for Qwen‑3‑235B are unreported, potentially affecting conditioning quality.  
- Findings are restricted to 8B models evaluated on three languages, limiting generalisability to other scales or language families.

## Confidence
- SFT improves lexical alignment: **Medium** (empirically observed but hyperparameter‑sensitive).  
- SFT degrades semantic alignment in Luxembourgish: **Low** (small data, high variance).  
- Biography redundancy after SFT: **Medium‑Low** (absolute scores unstable across seeds).  
- Cross‑language performance hierarchy: **Medium** (consistent pattern but limited language set).  
- Overall validity of LLM‑based user simulation: **Medium** (promising but reproducibility issues).

## Next Checks
1. Audit user split integrity: confirm no user appears in both train and test; retrain with candidate seeds to quantify score variance.
