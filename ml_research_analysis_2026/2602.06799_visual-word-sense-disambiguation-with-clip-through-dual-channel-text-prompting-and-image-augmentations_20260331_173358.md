# Visual Word Sense Disambiguation with CLIP through Dual-Channel Text Prompting and Image Augmentations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06799
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06799
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Dual-channel prompt ensemble and image augmentations with CLIP achieve test MRR 0.7590 (+0.0363) and Hit Rate 0.6220 (+0.0410) on SemEval-2023 VWSD; aggressive image augmentation yields marginal gains; external lexical signals degrade performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles Visual Word Sense Disambiguation (VWSD) by leveraging CLIP's vision-language embedding space without fine-tuning. The authors propose a dual-channel text enrichment strategy: semantic prompts reframe the ambiguous target in relation to context, while photo-style prompts describe the phrase as an image. WordNet synonyms expand lexical coverage. At test time, each candidate image is augmented through multiple geometric and photometric transformations, and their embeddings are aggregated. On SemEval-2023 VWSD, the enriched embeddings significantly boost MRR and Hit Rate over a vanilla CLIP baseline. Ablations reveal that dual-channel prompting delivers strong, low-latency improvements, whereas aggressive image augmentation provides only marginal gains at high computational cost. Surprisingly, adding external signals such as multilingual translations or WordNet definitions harms performance, underscoring the importance of CLIP-aligned prompt design.

## Method Summary
The method uses a pre-trained CLIP model (ViT-B/32) as a frozen feature extractor. Textual input consists of an ambiguous target word and a context phrase. Two sets of prompts are generated: semantic prompts (e.g., “bank related to erosion”) and photo prompts (e.g., “a photo of bank erosion”), optionally enriched with WordNet synonyms of the target and context. Each prompt is encoded by CLIP’s text encoder; mean embeddings for each channel are fused via a weighted sum (β_p, β_s) and L2-normalized to produce a refined text embedding. For each of the 10 candidate images, 28 test-time augmentations (flips, rotations, crops, color jitter, grid patches) are applied; all views are encoded by CLIP’s vision encoder, averaged, temperature-scaled (τ), and L2-normalized to yield a robust image embedding. Final ranking is by cosine similarity between the text embedding and each image embedding. Hyperparameters (β_p, β_s, τ) are tuned via Bayesian optimization on a validation split; no model weights are updated.

## Key Results
- Dual-channel prompting yields substantial gains: test MRR increases from 0.7227 (vanilla CLIP) to 0.7590, and Hit Rate from 0.5810 to 0.6220.
- Adding test-time image augmentations (28 views) produces negligible MRR improvement (+0.0002) but increases per-query latency ~10× (from ~63ms to ~710ms).
- Incorporating external signals—multilingual prompts or raw WordNet definitions—degrades performance (MRR drops up to 0.115), indicating that prompt lexicality must closely match CLIP’s training distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dual-Channel Prompting with WordNet Synonyms
- **Claim**: Dual-channel prompting (semantic and photo) with WordNet synonyms yields strong, low-latency improvements in VWSD accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: The system generates two prompt ensembles: (1) semantic prompts that rephrase the ambiguous target in relation to the context (e.g., “bank related to erosion”), and (2) photo prompts that cast the phrase as a visual descriptor (e.g., “a photo of bank erosion”). WordNet synonyms of the target and context are added to the photo prompts to increase lexical diversity. Each prompt is encoded by CLIP’s text encoder; channel-wise mean embeddings (h_s, h_p) are fused via weighted sum (β_p, β_s) and L2-normalized, producing a refined textual embedding that captures both abstract relations and concrete visual properties.
- **Core assumption**: CLIP’s text encoder is most effective with short, photo-like prompts that resemble its training distribution; aggregating multiple such prompts stabilizes the representation and reduces sense ambiguity.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - The abstract states that “dual-channel prompting provides strong, low-latency gains” and that enriching embeddings increased MRR from 0.7227 to 0.7590 and Hit Rate from 0.5810 to 0.6220.
  - Section III-B1 details the prompt generation, encoding, mean pooling, and fusion equations.
  - Neighboring work such as “Augmenters at SemEval-2023 Task 1: Enhancing CLIP in Handling Compositionality and Ambiguity for Zero-Shot Visual WSD through Prompt Augmentation” also employs prompt augmentation, indicating community recognition of its utility.
- **Break condition**: If prompts become too lengthy, abstract, or semantically distant from CLIP’s training data (e.g., full WordNet definitions), the benefit may diminish or reverse, as seen in the WordNet ablation where high proportions degraded performance.

### Mechanism 2: Test-Time Image Augmentation
- **Claim**: Test-time image augmentations (multiple views) make image embeddings more robust but deliver only marginal gains relative to their high computational cost.
- **Mechanism**: For each candidate image, a suite of 28 deterministic and stochastic augmentations (flips, rotations, crops, color jitter, grid patches, quadrant crops) is applied. All augmented views are encoded independently by CLIP’s vision encoder; the mean embedding is taken, scaled by a learned temperature τ, and L2-normalized. This aggregation approximates an invariant representation across transformations, reducing sensitivity to photographic variations.
- **Core assumption**: The visual concept of a word sense is largely invariant to geometric and photometric distortions; averaging embeddings over such views yields a more semantically complete representation.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - The abstract reports that “aggressive image augmentation yields only marginal improvements.”
  - Section III-B3 enumerates the six augmentation strategies and the aggregation pipeline (including temperature scaling).
  - While the retrieved corpus highlights multimodal retrieval, explicit test-time augmentation is less prominent; the small MRR gain observed (0.7227→0.7225 in Table V) aligns with broader experience that TTA often yields limited returns in vision-language models.
- **Break condition**: Under latency constraints (e.g., real-time applications), the ~10x increase in per-query time is not justified by the negligible MRR improvement; also, if images are already standardized or capture the target centrally, augmentations may add noise rather than robustness.

### Mechanism 3: External Lexical Signals Degrade Performance
- **Claim**: Injecting external lexical resources (WordNet definitions, multilingual translations) without ensuring CLIP-alignment degrades performance by diluting semantic specificity.
- **Mechanism**: Two external signal experiments are conducted: (a) mixing the original contextual embedding with a weighted WordNet definition embedding (α h_d* + (1-α) h_t), where h_d* is the definition most similar to h_t; (b) translating prompts into Spanish, French, and German via MarianMT, then averaging the CLIP text embeddings across languages. Both strategies aim to enrich semantics but introduce out-of-distribution features.
- **Core assumption**: CLIP’s joint space is optimized for naturalistic, photo-like language descriptions; text that deviates from this (e.g., dictionary definitions or translated phrases) drifts away from the visual manifold, weakening cross-modal alignment.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - The abstract explicitly states that “Experiments adding external signals (multilingual prompts, WordNet definitions) degraded performance.”
  - Section IV.F presents quantitative results: full WordNet (α=1.0) drops MRR to 0.6068; German translation yields MRR 0.6729; the multilingual ensemble (ES+FR+DE) yields MRR 0.6756, all below the English-only baseline 0.7217.
  - Recent VWSD submissions incorporate LLM-generated context; this paper’s negative finding suggests such external knowledge requires careful CLIP-aligned processing to be beneficial.
- **Break condition**: If external signals are filtered (e.g., selecting only CLIP-like definitions) or integrated via a learned alignment module, dilution could be avoided; however, the simple averaging used here is harmful.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Contrastive Language–Image Pretraining (CLIP)**
- **Why needed**: CLIP provides the shared embedding space that enables direct comparison between text and images via cosine similarity. All model components (prompt encoders, vision encoder) are instances of CLIP’s pre-trained networks, and the contrastive objective during pre-training informs what linguistic patterns are aligned with visual content.
- **Quick check**: What is the purpose of the contrastive loss during CLIP training, and how does it allow zero-shot image–text matching?

**Concept: Visual Word Sense Disambiguation (VWSD)**
- **Why needed**: The paper addresses a specific task where an ambiguous word must be resolved by selecting the most appropriate image from a set. Understanding the task’s input format (target word, context phrase, 10 candidate images) and metrics (MRR, Hit Rate) is essential to interpret experimental results and contributions.
- **Quick check**: How does the presence of candidate images change the nature of word sense disambiguation compared to purely textual WSD?

**Concept: Test-time Augmentation (TTA)**
- **Why needed**: TTA is a core technique used to stabilize image embeddings against variations in viewpoint, lighting, and cropping. Without grasping why multiple views are aggregated at inference, the design of the image enrichment module and its computational trade-offs cannot be appreciated.
- **Quick check**: In typical classification models, TTA averages predictions over transformed inputs. How does this paper’s use of TTA differ (embedding-level vs. prediction-level), and what additional hyperparameter (τ) is introduced?

## Architecture Onboarding
The system architecture consists of the following components:

**Component map**:
Input Preprocessing: CLIP BPE tokenizer for text (max 77 tokens); image resizing to 224×224 and normalization.
Text Enrichment Module: Prompt Generator (creates semantic and photo prompts, possibly with WordNet synonyms); CLIP Text Encoder (encodes prompts); Channel Pooling (mean of embeddings within each channel → h_s, h_p); Fusion Layer (weighted sum β_p h_p + β_s h_s, then L2-normalization → final text embedding ˜h_t).
Image Enrichment Module: Augmentation Engine (applies 6 groups of transformations to each candidate image, yielding ~28 views); CLIP Vision Encoder (encodes each view); View Pooling (mean of view embeddings → h_xj; temperature scaling (τ); L2-normalization → final image embedding ˜h_xj).
Ranking Engine: computes cosine similarity between ˜h_t and each ˜h_xj; selects image with highest score.

**Critical path**:
1. Receive (target word, context phrase, 10 candidate images).
2. Generate prompt strings for semantic and photo channels (using context and optional WordNet synonyms).
3. Encode prompts via CLIP text encoder → channel means → fuse → normalized text embedding.
4. For each candidate image: apply augmentations → encode each view → average → temperature scale → normalize → image embedding.
5. Compute 10 cosine similarities and pick argmax.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Prompting vs. Augmentation: Prompting adds negligible latency (~63ms) and gives ~0.03 MRR gain; augmentation adds ~650ms and yields at most ~0.01 MRR gain (and sometimes none). The combination does not double the gain due to diminishing returns.
- Complexity vs. Interpretability: The model remains simple (pure CLIP, no fine-tuning) and fully interpretable (weights β, τ are explicit), but sacrifices the ~22% absolute Hit Rate gap to top SemEval systems that use large external retrieval and learned fusion.
- Overfitting risk: Using validation data to choose augmentation strength or β/τ can lead to overfitting; Table VII shows a large train/test performance drop when augmentations are used.

**Failure signatures**:
- Sudden MRR drop when α (WordNet mix) exceeds 0.15–0.25.
- Large validation–test gap (>0.12 MRR) when using augmentations; indicates poor generalization.
- If β_p or β_s are set to 0 (dropping one channel), performance regresses toward baseline, confirming the necessity of both channels.
- High per-query latency (>700ms) in deployment may indicate excessive augmentations.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Vanilla CLIP baseline: encode the raw context phrase (“target context”) directly, no augmentations. Expected: MRR ≈ 0.7227, Hit Rate ≈ 0.5810 on test. Validates environment and metric.
2. Prompt-only configuration: Implement dual-channel prompts (with fixed β_p=0.6, β_s=0.4) and no augmentations. Expected: MRR ≈ 0.7506, Hit@1 ≈ 0.625, latency ~63ms per query. Confirms benefit and low cost of prompting.
3. Augmentation-only configuration: Keep text embedding as vanilla or simple prompt; apply full 28-view augmentation pipeline. Expected: MRR ≈ 0.7225, Hit@1 ≈ 0.5745, latency ~710ms. Reveals that visual augmentation alone does not beat prompting and is computationally heavy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What specific characteristics of external lexical resources cause them to introduce noisy signals that dilute semantic specificity in CLIP-based VWSD?**  
   The paper observed degradation from WordNet definitions and multilingual prompts but did not analyze the mechanistic reason (e.g., definitional granularity mismatch, translation errors, embedding misalignment). Resolving this would require fine-grained analysis of embedding distributions of successful vs. failed examples.

2. **Can the performance gap between the proposed model and state-of-the-art VWSD systems be closed while maintaining low latency and simplicity?**  
   The paper acknowledges a notable gap to top SemEval systems but does not explore if a middle ground exists (e.g., lightweight learned fusion). Evidence would come from systematic experiments with minimal, interpretable parameterized fusion modules.

3. **Under what conditions does test-time image augmentation provide meaningful gains versus diminishing returns or overfitting?**  
   Ablations show that adding aggressive augmentation to prompting reduces test performance despite high validation scores, suggesting overfitting. The paper does not characterize which ambiguity types or image characteristics might benefit. Error analysis stratified by ambiguity type and image properties could resolve this.

4. **Are the optimal dual-channel prompt weights (β_p, β_s) and temperature scaling (τ) dataset- or ambiguity-type-specific?**  
   Hyperparameters were tuned globally on the SemEval training set; the paper does not investigate if different target words or contexts require different balances. Analysis across semantic categories or a meta-network to predict per-instance parameters would provide evidence.

## Limitations
- Exact replication is hindered by unspecified details: prompt templates, augmentation parameters, WordNet synonym selection heuristics, optimal view count, and temperature scaling order.
- The approach trades off a large absolute performance gap (~22% in Hit Rate) to top SemEval systems for interpretability and low latency, limiting its practical competitiveness.

## Confidence
- Dual-channel prompting benefit: **High**
- Image augmentation marginality: **Medium**
- External signal degradation: **High**

## Next Checks

2. **Augmentation ablation**: Systematically remove augmentation groups (geometric, photometric, crops) on the validation set to isolate which transforms contribute net gain vs. noise, measuring MRR change and latency.
