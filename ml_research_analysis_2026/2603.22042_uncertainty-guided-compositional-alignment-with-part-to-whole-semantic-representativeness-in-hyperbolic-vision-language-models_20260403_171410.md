# Uncertainty-guided Compositional Alignment with Part-to-Whole Semantic Representativeness in Hyperbolic Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.22042
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22042
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: UNCHA achieves SOTA zero-shot classification (ImageNet ViT-B: 48.8% vs. HyCoCLIP 45.8%), retrieval (COCO text→image R@1: 72.7% vs. 72.0%), and multi-label classification (COCO mAP: 59.43% vs. 58.12%).

## Executive Summary
Existing hyperbolic vision-language models (VLMs) struggle to capture the varying semantic representativeness of parts to the whole in multi-object scenes, limiting their ability to achieve hierarchical and compositional understanding. UNCHA addresses this by introducing hyperbolic uncertainty derived from the embedding radius: parts that are more representative of the whole have lower uncertainty. This uncertainty guides adaptive weighting in contrastive losses and calibrates entailment losses, with an entropy-based regularizer to stabilize learning.

In extensive evaluations, UNCHA sets new state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks: zero-shot image classification on ImageNet (ViT-B: 48.8% vs. HyCoCLIP 45.8%), zero-shot retrieval on COCO (text→image R@1: 72.7% vs. 72.0%), and multi-label classification on COCO (mAP: 59.43% vs. 58.12%). These gains demonstrate UNCHA's superior ability to align parts and wholes compositionally while preserving hierarchical structures.

## Method Summary
UNCHA builds upon a CLIP-style architecture with a Vision Transformer (ViT) and a text Transformer, projecting their Euclidean embeddings into hyperbolic space using the Lorentz model with learnable curvature. The model is trained on the GRIT dataset, which provides 20.5M image-text pairs with 35.9M part-level annotations. The core innovation lies in the hyperbolic uncertainty module: for each part embedding, uncertainty is computed as $u(x)=\log(1+\exp(-||x||^2))$, exploiting the monotonic relationship between Euclidean norm and hyperbolic radius. Lower uncertainty indicates a part is more representative of the whole scene.

This uncertainty drives two main mechanisms: (1) In the global-local contrastive loss, uncertainty modulates the temperature $\tau_{\text{un}} = \exp(u(\text{part})/2) \cdot \tau_{\text{gl}}$, thereby down-weighting less representative parts during alignment. (2) For the entailment loss that enforces part-whole hierarchical ordering, the loss is calibrated with an uncertainty term and an entropy regularizer $H(\tilde{u})$ to maintain diverse uncertainty levels and prevent embedding collapse. The total objective combines these with weights $\lambda_1=0.5$ and $\lambda_2=10.0$. Training uses a batch size of 768 for 500K steps with AdamW (lr $5\times10^{-4}$, cosine decay, 4k warm-up) on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hyperbolic Uncertainty as Semantic Representativeness
- **Claim**: UNCHA assigns lower hyperbolic uncertainty to part images that are more semantically representative of the whole scene, and higher uncertainty to less representative ones.
- **Mechanism**: The Euclidean norm of an embedding in the Lorentz model is monotonic with the hyperbolic radius (distance from the origin). The method defines uncertainty as $u(x) = \log(1 + \exp(-||x||^2))$. Since points nearer the origin are more abstract/uncertain, this inverse relationship means parts closer to the origin (higher radius, lower uncertainty) are treated as more representative.
- **Core assumption**: The geometric property that hyperbolic radius correlates with concept specificity/abstractness holds for part-whole relationships in vision-language tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "...assigning lower uncertainty to more representative parts and higher uncertainty to less representative ones for the whole scene."
  - [section 3.2.1]: Formal derivation linking Euclidean norm to hyperbolic radius and definition of $u(x)$.
  - [corpus]: Corpus work on hyperbolic representations supports radius as an uncertainty proxy (papers 2, 3, 4, 6), but direct application to part-whole representativeness is novel to this paper.
- **Break condition**: If part-whole hierarchies are not naturally tree-like or if Euclidean norm fails to maintain monotonic ordering with hyperbolic radius for very large norms (logarithmic regime), this uncertainty proxy may misrepresent true semantic specificity.

### Mechanism 2: Uncertainty-Guided Contrastive Alignment
- **Claim**: Modulating the temperature of the global-local contrastive loss using part-specific uncertainty weights the contribution of each part's alignment with the whole, preventing equal repulsion of all parts.
- **Mechanism**: For the global-local contrastive loss between part images/texts and whole scenes, UNCHA sets part-dependent temperatures $\tau_{\text{un}} = \exp(u(\text{part})/2) \cdot \tau_{\text{gl}}$. Higher uncertainty (less representative parts) leads to larger temperature, reducing their influence. This adaptively emphasizes representative parts during alignment.
- **Core assumption**: Standard contrastive losses with fixed temperature treat all negative (non-matching) parts equally, which disrupts semantic structure when parts have different relevance to the whole.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "...incorporate uncertainty as part-to-whole semantic representativeness into both contrastive and entailment loss... modulating the strength of each part’s alignment with the whole."
  - [section 3.2.2]: Precise equations for $\tau_{\text{un}}$ and integration into $L_{\text{un}}^{\text{con}}$.
- **Break condition**: If uncertainty estimates become degenerate (e.g., all parts assigned similar uncertainty), the weighting loses its adaptive effect and reverts to uniform treatment.

### Mechanism 3: Entailment Loss with Uncertainty Calibration and Entropy Regularization
- **Claim**: Calibrating part-to-whole entailment losses with uncertainty and an entropy term stabilizes learning, promotes diverse uncertainty levels, and prevents embedding collapse.
- **Mechanism**: The entailment loss $L^{*}_{\text{ent}}(p,q)$ is modified to include a small linear term $\alpha \phi(p,q)$ for fine-grained gradient even within the cone. The calibration loss $L_{\text{cal}}^{\text{ent}} = \lfloor L^{*}_{\text{ent}} \rfloor \cdot e^{-u(p)} + u(p) + H(\tilde{u}(p))$ has three parts: (1) $e^{-u(p)}$ encourages higher uncertainty for weak entailment, (2) $u(p)$ penalizes trivial high uncertainty, (3) entropy $H$ on normalized uncertainties across a batch promotes diversity and prevents uniform collapse.
- **Core assumption**: Hyperbolic embeddings often collapse into narrow regions; explicit regularization of uncertainty distribution maintains a healthy spread of part/whole radii.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "...uncertainty is further calibrated with an entailment loss regularized by entropy-based term."
  - [section 3.2.3]: Full formulation and explanation of each term's role.
  - [section 4.3 & Fig. 5]: Empirical visualization showing UNCHA's embeddings are more dispersed than HyCoCLIP's.
- **Break condition**: If entropy regularization is too strong, it may force artificial uncertainty diversity inconsistent with true representativeness, harming semantic ordering.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hyperbolic Geometry (Lorentz Model)**
  - *Why needed here*: All embeddings live in hyperbolic space; understanding geodesic distance, the origin as abstract, and entailment cones is essential to grasp how uncertainty relates to radius and how part-whole ordering is modeled.
  - *Quick check question*: "Given two embeddings in hyperbolic space, which is more abstract: one with larger or smaller geodesic distance from the origin?"
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning in VLMs**
  - *Why needed here*: UNCHA modifies the standard CLIP-style contrastive objective. The engineer must understand the global-local contrastive framework (Eq. 9 in paper) to see where uncertainty weighting is injected.
  - *Quick check question*: "In a standard image-text contrastive loss, what is the role of the temperature parameter τ?"
- **Concept: Entailment as Hierarchical Relationship**
  - *Why needed here*: UNCHA uses entailment loss to model that a part should lie within the cone of its whole (more abstract). This is the hyperbolic mechanism for part-whole ordering.
  - *Quick check question*: "In hyperbolic entailment, if image embedding *i_part* is more specific than *i_whole*, should *i_part* lie inside or outside the entailment cone centered at *i_whole*?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Encoders → Projectors → Uncertainty Module → LossEngine (Contrastive, Entailment+Calibration) → Entropy Regulator
- **Critical path**: Input → ViT/text encoders → Linear projectors (with scaling) → compute uncertainty from projected embedding’s Euclidean norm → part-dependent temperature τ_un for global-local contrastive → compute L_un_con, L_un_ent, L_cal_ent → total loss L = L_un_con + λ_ent L_un_ent. Inference uses only encoders+projectors.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Hyperbolic vs. Euclidean*: Hyperbolic space naturally represents hierarchies but introduces computational complexity (Lorentzian distance, log/exp maps). UNCHA's use of Euclidean norm as proxy avoids heavy hyperbolic ops during uncertainty computation.
  - *Part-Whole Asymmetry*: Treating all parts equally vs. uncertainty-weighted. The tradeoff is model simplicity vs. capturing varying representativeness.
  - *Entropy Regularization Strength*: Too weak → embeddings collapse; too strong → forced diversity harms semantic structure. λ₂=10.0 was chosen.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Embedding collapse (all parts/wholes cluster tightly near origin or a point) → indicates entropy regularization failed or uncertainty calibration not working.
  - No separation between part and whole radii distributions → uncertainty not being learned or used effectively.
  - Degraded performance on multi-object datasets (ComCo, SimCo) while single-object tasks remain stable → suggests part-whole modeling is broken.
  - Loss components with opposing gradients not stabilizing training → monitor gradient cosine similarity like in Fig. S.8(a).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Reproduce Baseline Metrics**: Evaluate zero-shot ImageNet (ViT-B) and COCO retrieval (R@1) against HyCoCLIP. Expect >45.8% and >72.0% respectively.
  2. **Visualize Embedding Radii**: Plot histogram of $||x||^2$ for 10k whole images and their random crops (like Fig. 5). Verify clear separation: whole > part radii.
  3. **Ablation Check**: Train ablations (w/o uncertainty guidance, w/o entropy reg) and confirm performance drop >1% on average classification/retrieval (Tab. 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How universally does hyperbolic radius correlate with semantic representativeness across different hierarchical structures beyond part-whole relationships?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method defines uncertainty via hyperbolic radius (or its Euclidean norm proxy), assuming it reflects semantic representativeness. This is validated empirically on part-whole data, but not on other hierarchies.
- **Why unresolved**: The correlation might be specific to visual part-whole relationships; other hierarchies (e.g., taxonomic, functional) could have different geometric properties.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Apply UNCHA's uncertainty estimation to diverse hierarchical datasets (e.g., WordNet, scientific taxonomies) and measure correlation between radius and ground-truth representativeness scores.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the optimal strategy for regularizing part-to-whole uncertainty estimates to maximize downstream task performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] “H(ũ(p)) regularizes the uncertainty distribution to remain diverse and informative, avoiding a collapse toward uniform or constant uncertainty.” However, the entropy-based form is presented without alternatives.
- **Why unresolved**: The entropy regularizer is one of many possible approaches; its impact on the balance between uncertainty diversity and alignment quality is not fully analyzed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct systematic ablation comparing entropy against other regularizers (e.g., variance constraints, distribution matching) and evaluate the trade-off between embedding dispersion and task accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the proposed uncertainty-guided compositional alignment be effectively applied to pre-trained hyperbolic VLMs without full retraining?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] UNCHA is trained from scratch with specialized losses. The paper does not address whether a pre-trained model (e.g., HyCoCLIP) can be adapted post-hoc using the uncertainty estimates.
- **Why unresolved**: Practical adoption would benefit from a lighter-weight adaptation; it's unclear if the uncertainties can be computed from frozen embeddings and used to re-weight losses without updating the model.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Take a pre-trained hyperbolic VLM, freeze its weights, compute part uncertainties, and apply uncertainty-guided weighting to a frozen contrastive loss; compare performance against full retraining.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How does the uncertainty-aware contrastive loss influence the preservation of arbitrary hierarchical structures beyond part-whole separation?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] “With the proposed losses, UNCHA learns hyperbolic embeddings with more accurate part-whole ordering.” Visualizations show improved part-whole separation, but not other hierarchical properties.
- **Why unresolved**: The method might overfit to the specific part-whole pattern, potentially distorting other hierarchies (e.g., sibling relationships, depth).
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluate embeddings on hierarchical classification benchmarks with diverse tree structures (varying depth, branching factor) and compare preservation metrics (e.g., tree distortion, LCA errors) against baselines.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter tuning**: Exact search ranges/validation protocols for λ₁, λ₂, α, η, K are unspecified, risking non-reproducibility of optimal settings.
- **Entropy regularizer details**: Implementation nuances (per-batch vs. per-modality, numerical stability ε) are ambiguous, potentially affecting uncertainty diversity.
- **Surrogate assumption**: Euclidean norm as a proxy for hyperbolic radius may break for extreme norms or non-tree-like part-whole hierarchies, undermining uncertainty validity.

## Confidence
- *Mechanism 1 (uncertainty as representativeness)*: **Medium**. Theory is grounded but hinges on the radius-specificity correlation holding for part-whole data; break conditions exist.
- *Mechanism 2 (contrastive weighting)*: **Medium**. Depends entirely on Mechanism 1’s uncertainty estimates; empirical evidence (Fig. 4b) supports but does not prove causality.
- *Mechanism 3 (entailment calibration)*: **Medium**. Ablations (Tab. 4) and visualizations (Fig. 5) show benefit, but entropy regularization strength is brittle.

## Next Checks
