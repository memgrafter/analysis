# Panoramic Affordance Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15558
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15558
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves 71.56% gIoU and 62.30% cIoU on the PAP-12K benchmark, significantly outperforming the best baseline (A4-Agent) by 9.01% and 12.33% respectively.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a key limitation in embodied AI affordance prediction: conventional pinhole cameras have narrow fields of view, missing holistic environmental context. The authors introduce panoramic affordance prediction using 360-degree imagery and present PAP-12K, a new benchmark with over 1,000 ultra-high-resolution (12k) panoramic images and more than 12,000 reasoning-based QA pairs annotated with precise affordance masks. They propose the Panoramic Affordance Prediction (PAP) framework, a training-free pipeline inspired by human foveal vision. PAP uses recursive visual routing for coarse target localization, an adaptive gaze mechanism to rectify geometric distortions, and a cascaded grounding pipeline to extract instance-level affordance masks. Evaluated on PAP-12K, PAP substantially surpasses state-of-the-art baselines, achieving a gIoU of 71.56% and cIoU of 62.30% compared to 62.55% and 49.97% for the best baseline. The results demonstrate robust handling of panoramic challenges such as distortion and extreme scale variations.

## Method Summary
PAP processes an equirectangular projection (ERP) panorama and a natural language task description through three stages. First, **Recursive Visual Routing** overlays a 4×3 numeric grid on a downsampled ERP image and prompts a vision-language model (VLM) to select grid indices containing the target object derived from the task. If the object is judged small (sub-scale) and occupies a single grid, the region is recursively cropped and re‑queried with higher resolution until the target spans multiple grids, progressively refining localization while controlling token load. Second, **Adaptive Gaze** computes the spherical coordinates and field‑of‑view (FoV) of the selected grid region, then projects that spherical patch onto a tangent plane to generate a perspective viewport. This rectification eliminates ERP distortion and normalizes scale, making the patch suitable for standard 2D models. Third, **Cascaded Affordance Grounding** uses the object name from the VLM to prompt an open‑vocabulary detector (Rex‑Omni) on the perspective patch, obtaining a bounding box and keypoints. These spatial prompts drive SAM to segment the affordance region, and finally an inverse spherical projection maps the mask back to the original ERP coordinates. The pipeline is training‑free and leverages large foundation models (Qwen3‑VL, Rex‑Omni, SAM2). Initial downsampling to 2000×1000 ensures efficiency, with recursion increasing resolution as needed.

## Key Results
- PAP achieves **gIoU 71.56%** and **cIoU 62.30%** on PAP‑12K, exceeding the best baseline (A4‑Agent) by 9.01% (gIoU) and 12.33% (cIoU).
- The method shows exceptional robustness to panoramic distortions and extreme scale variations; on the Hard subset (objects near boundaries, very small/large), the performance gap over baselines widens.
- The introduced PAP‑12K dataset (1,003 ultra‑high‑resolution panoramas, 13,493 QA pairs with segmentation masks) establishes a challenging new benchmark for panoramic affordance prediction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Recursive Visual Routing
Overlaying a coarse grid (e.g., 4×3) on a downsampled ERP allows a VLM to efficiently localize the target region using semantic reasoning. The recursive cropping strategy adapts the resolution: initially low resolution for speed, then progressively higher resolution focused on the relevant area when the object is sub‑scale. This multi‑scale approach handles the extreme scale variations inherent in panoramas (objects spanning from 0.1% to 30% of the image). The core assumption is that VLMs can reliably map natural language descriptions to grid locations when provided explicit visual anchors, and that recursive refinement balances detail with computational cost. Evidence: the design mirrors multi‑scale strategies in panoramic perception (e.g., PanoAffordanceNet) and the paper's ablation shows the grid mechanism is critical. Potential failure: VLM misselection of grids (e.g., due to distortion or ambiguity) leads to losing the target.

### Adaptive Gaze
After RVR identifies a grid region, the system transforms that spherical patch into a perspective viewport. By computing the region’s center spherical coordinates and an adaptive FoV that fully encloses it (with a small margin), the patch is projected via an inverse spherical‑to‑rectilinear mapping. This effectively removes ERP distortion and mitigates scale variations because the region appears as if viewed from a conventional perspective camera. The assumption is that local spherical patches are well‑approximated by tangent plane projections, and that foundation models trained on perspective data (e.g., SAM) generalize to these rectified views. Evidence: Section 4.3 details the projection and its benefits; an ablation (Table 5) shows a 7.70% gIoU drop when Adaptive Gaze is removed, confirming its role. Failure conditions: FoV too tight clips the target; FoV too wide re‑introduces distortion or includes distracting background.

### Cascaded Affordance Grounding
The rectified perspective patch is then processed by an open‑vocabulary detector (Rex‑Omni) using the object name extracted by the VLM. The detector provides a bounding box and keypoints, which prompt SAM to produce a precise affordance mask. Finally, the mask is inversely projected back to the ERP. Decoupling reasoning (VLM) from detection/segmentation (specialized models) leverages the strengths of each: VLM provides semantic focus, OVD narrows the spatial search, and SAM delivers high‑quality masks. The assumption is that object‑level names simplify detection and that SAM’s segmentation quality is preserved on distortion‑free patches. Evidence: The pipeline yields precise instance‑level masks; performance on the Hard subset (where distortion and scale are extreme) indicates robustness. However, errors may cascade: an incorrect object name from RVR, a missed detection by OVD, fragmented SAM output, or misalignment during remapping can all degrade the final result.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (ERP image + natural language task) → Recursive Visual Routing (VLM‑based grid selection & recursion) → Adaptive Gaze (spherical projection to perspective patch) → Cascaded Affordance Grounding (OVD detector → SAM segmentation) → Output (ERP affordance mask)

**Critical path**:  
1. VLM in RVR must correctly identify the target object and its grid locations.  
2. Adaptive Gaze must compute a viewpoint and FoV that fully captures the target without excessive distortion.  
3. OVD must detect the intended object using the name from RVR on the perspective patch.  
4. SAM must produce an accurate mask given the OVD prompts.  
5. Inverse spherical projection must align the mask precisely with ERP coordinates.  
Any break halts successful prediction; RVR is the earliest and most influential step.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Training‑free vs. accuracy*: PAP avoids training but depends on external model capabilities; may lag behind end‑to‑end trained models on new distributions.  
- *Resolution*: RVR uses aggressive downsampling initially for speed; recursion increases resolution to capture detail. Too little resolution loses small objects; too many tokens slows inference.  
- *Grid granularity*: Coarse grids (e.g., 2×2) provide weak spatial guidance; dense grids (e.g., 10×10) overload the VLM; 4×3 is a sweet spot.  
- *VLM backbone*: Larger VLMs (Gemini‑3‑Flash) boost RVR accuracy at cost of latency; lighter models (Qwen3‑VL‑4B) reduce speed but may degrade grid selection.  
- *Adaptive Gaze padding*: A small margin (~10°) prevents boundary cuts but may include background that confuses SAM; too much margin reduces effective resolution.

**Failure signatures**:  
- *RVR output*: invalid grid indices, missing object, or wrong object name; detectable by visualizing grid overlays on sample panoramas.  
- *Adaptive Gaze*: perspective viewport shows partial target (FoV too tight) or strong edge distortion (FoV too wide); inspect visually.  
- *OVD*: no bounding box, or box on wrong object; often due to ambiguous object name or occlusion.  
- *SAM*: fragmented or over‑merged mask; check against perspective patch.  
- *Inverse mapping*: mask appears shifted or wrapped at ERP boundaries; compare overlay on ERP.  
- *Cascading*: final mask irrelevant; verify intermediate outputs (grid, object name, perspective patch, box) to localize failure point.

**First three experiments**:  
1. *Isolated RVR validation*: Build a test set with known object grid locations across scales and distortions. Measure grid selection accuracy (correct indices included) for various VLM backbones and grid resolutions. Expect recursive zoom to improve identification of sub‑scale objects.  
2. *Adaptive Gaze ablation*: For a set of objects with ground truth masks, compare performance when masks are extracted from raw ERP crops versus perspective patches produced by AG. Expect a significant IoU gain from AG due to distortion removal; also test sensitivity to FoV margin.  
3. *End‑to‑End Hard subset evaluation*: Run full PAP on PAP‑12K’s Hard split (extreme scale, boundary, distortion). Compute gIoU/cIoU and compare against baselines (Table 3). Success criteria: PAP’s advantage on Hard should exceed that on Normal, confirming robustness to panoramic challenges.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Outdoor generalization**: Does PAP generalize to outdoor panoramas? The PAP‑12K dataset contains only indoor scenes; no outdoor data.
- **Real‑time inference**: Can PAP achieve real‑time (<1 s) on embedded hardware? Reported latency is ~10 s; accuracy‑latency trade‑offs on edge devices are unknown.
- **Supervised fine‑tuning**: Would fine‑tuning on PAP‑12K improve accuracy over zero‑shot? The method is training‑free; supervised training could adapt models to panoramic distortions.
- **End‑to‑end trainable model**: Can a single integrated network match or surpass PAP’s performance while reducing inference cost? A decoupled pipeline may accumulate errors; using PAP to auto‑label data could train an end‑to‑end model.

## Limitations
- Exact VLM prompt engineering and sampling parameters (beyond the provided system prompt) are not fully specified, affecting reproducibility of grid selection.
- The quantitative definition of “small” object that triggers recursive zoom is vague; the recursion threshold is not explicitly given.
- Detailed boundary‑handling logic for mask remapping across the ERP seam (e.g., when objects cross the ±180° meridian) is underspecified, risking misalignment.
