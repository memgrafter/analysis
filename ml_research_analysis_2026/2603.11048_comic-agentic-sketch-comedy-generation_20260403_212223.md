# COMIC: Agentic Sketch Comedy Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11048
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11048
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Outperforms frontier video models (Sora 2, Veo 3.1) in human evaluation and approaches professionally produced sketch quality.

## Executive Summary
Automating sketch comedy video generation is exceptionally difficult due to humor's subjective nature and the challenge of producing long-form, coherent video. COMIC introduces a multi-agent framework where specialized agents (writers, critics, directors) iteratively compete and refine scripts and videos across isolated "islands." A key innovation is training LLM critics on YouTube engagement data to align automated humor evaluation with real viewer preferences, enabling scalable, quality-driven search without a fixed reward. In human evaluation, COMIC significantly outperforms frontier models across funniness, watch intent, and narrative quality, approaching professional standards.

## Method Summary
COMIC uses a multi-agent system with two main loops: script evolution and video rendering. For script generation, the system maintains multiple isolated "islands" each with a distinct committee of LLM critics selected from a large pool. These critics are aligned with human humor preferences by validating their pairwise judgments against YouTube engagement scores (normalized view counts). Scripts within an island compete via round-robin tournaments; losers are refined using winner feedback. After evolution, top scripts proceed to video rendering. A scene director structures the script into shot specifications. Each shot is iteratively refined using script-conditioned critics, with the best version selected via a single-elimination tournament across the refinement history. Finally, multiple scene realizations compete to select the final video. This design allows test-time scaling via more islands, deeper shot refinement, and broader scene exploration.

## Key Results
- **Human evaluation (7-point scale):** COMIC achieves Funniness 3.45 vs. 2.73 for frontier models (Sora 2, Veo 3.1), Watch Intent 3.09 vs. 2.73, and higher Script/Narrative quality. Ratings approach those of professional sketches ("Slightly Less Funny" to "Comparable").
- **Automated pairwise win rate:** COMIC wins 44% against middle-tier professional sketches, substantially higher than baselines (~1–18%), while maintaining high output diversity.
- **Ablation evidence:** Multi-island evolution increases both win rate and intra-style diversity compared to single-island setups, validating the island mechanism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Critic alignment with engagement data:** By validating diverse LLM critic personas against YouTube engagement scores, COMIC selects a committee whose judgments correlate with real viewer preferences. This provides a scalable, reward-free evaluation signal for humor. Evidence: [abstract] mentions training critics on engagement; [section 3.3] details the logistic growth model and critic selection improving discrimination accuracy. Break condition: If engagement is driven by non-humor factors (e.g., clickbait), critics will optimize for virality, not comedic merit.
2. **Island-based diversity emergence:** Isolating script populations under distinct critic committees creates divergent fitness landscapes, encouraging niche specialization (e.g., dry vs. surreal humor). This yields a Pareto front of styles rather than a single convergent optimum. Evidence: [abstract] describes "isolated islands"; [section 3.4] formalizes the mechanism; Fig. 8 shows multi-island improves win rate and intra-diversity. Break condition: Insufficiently distinct committees cause island collapse; overly divergent committees optimize for non-human-appealing niches.
3. **Iterative refinement with tournaments:** Shot-level refinement conditioned on script and prior shots, combined with history tournaments at shot and scene levels, improves visual coherence and comedic impact. This provides orthogonal test-time scaling: deeper refinement per shot and broader scene exploration. Evidence: [abstract] notes "long-form, coherent video"; [section 3.5] details the storyboarding and tournament process; Fig. 7 shows concrete fixes. Break condition: Inconsistent critic feedback causes cycling; poor conditioning fails to maintain continuity.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (character specs, backgrounds) -> Writers -> Critic Pool (aligned committee) -> Islands (isolated script populations) -> Script Evolution (round-robin + refinement) -> Cross-Island League Tournament -> Top Scripts -> Scene Director -> Memory Bank -> Rendering Loop (shot refinement + tournaments) -> Output (final video)
- **Critical path:** Character/Background Input -> Island-based Script Evolution (G generations) -> Cross-Island League Tournament -> Selected Scripts -> Scene Direction Generation -> Shot-wise Iterative Refinement (history tournament) -> Scene-level Tournament -> Final Video.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Island count (K) vs. compute:* More islands increase diversity but linearly increase round-robin evaluation cost.  
  *Rendering critics (|C_render|) vs. refinement depth:* More critics allow more iterations but each is costly; history tournament mitigates over-refinement.  
  *Fixed personas vs. dynamic critics:* Critics are sampled once; dynamic evolution could prevent stagnation but adds complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Critic misalignment:* High inter-diversity but low human funniness; win rate vs. random high but vs. humans low.  
  *Island collapse:* Very low inter-diversity across islands; all converge to same style.  
  *Video incoherence:* High intra-diversity in shot evaluation but low visual consistency scores; continuity errors persist.  
  *Stagnant generation:* Win rate plateaus early; indicates insufficient mutation, narrow critic committee, or small population.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validate critic alignment:** Run critic selection on held-out YouTube engagement data. Expect >70% accuracy on Top-vs-Bottom pairwise comparisons (per Table 2).  
  2. **Test island necessity:** Compare K=1 (single-island) vs. K=3 (base) with identical total script count and critic budget. Expect multi-island to show higher intra-diversity and win rate (per Fig. 8).  
  3. **Probe tournament benefit:** For a fixed script, run rendering loop with vs. without history tournament (taking final refinement). Expect history tournament to reduce over-refinement artifacts and improve consistency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can we mitigate the influence of non-humor-related factors (e.g., clickbait, algorithmic promotion) on YouTube engagement metrics when using them as a proxy for humor quality to train LLM critics?** Basis: Conclusion acknowledges noise from clickbait and promotion. Why unresolved: Critic alignment relies on these scores; misalignment could optimize for virality over humor. What evidence: Collect human humor ratings for many YouTube videos, compare engagement proxies, train critics on each, and evaluate outputs via human studies.
- **How can audio elements beyond dialogue (e.g., sound effects, music) be integrated into the automated comedy generation pipeline to enhance comedic timing, emotional impact, and overall production quality?** Basis: Conclusion identifies sound effects as future work. Why unresolved: Current output lacks sound effects, limiting polish. What evidence: Extend scene director to generate sound specs; integrate synthesis/retrieval; A/B test with/without sound effects.
- **How can automated pipelines be developed to attribute generated comedic content (scripts, jokes, visual motifs) to source material and quantify its originality, addressing ethical and legal concerns around copyright and derivative work?** Basis: Conclusion calls for attribution and originality quantification. Why unresolved: COMIC evolves from existing YouTube sketches; without attribution, it risks reproducing protected material. What evidence: Build retrieval-augmented system to return similar training segments; define originality metrics validated against human judgments of copying vs. inspiration.
- **To what extent can the COMIC framework—with its multi-island competition and human-aligned critic mechanism—be generalized to other creative domains (e.g., short story writing, music composition, game design), and what core components versus domain-specific adaptations are necessary for success?** Basis: Conclusion suggests future research in other domains. Why unresolved: COMIC is tightly coupled to video comedy with visual continuity and humor-specific critics; other domains have different constraints. What evidence: Implement the paradigm in two new domains (e.g., stories, music) using domain-specific engagement signals and compare against baselines.

## Limitations
- **Engagement proxy fragility:** YouTube view counts as a humor quality proxy may be confounded by clickbait, algorithmic promotion, or creator popularity, potentially misaligning critics.
- **Dataset narrowness:** Critic selection is validated on only 5 comedy channels; overfitting may limit generalization to other styles or cultures.
- **Reproduction barriers:** Exact prompt templates, logistic growth model fitting details, and full model specifications are missing, hindering faithful replication.
- **Compute intensity:** Performance depends on multiple scaling axes (islands, refinements, scene directions), leading to high inference costs and unclear optimal trade-offs.

## Confidence
- **Critic Alignment:** Medium (validation on fixed channel set shows correlation, but proxy assumption is fragile).
- **Island-based Diversity:** High (ablations in Fig. 8 demonstrate clear gains in inter-diversity and win rate).
- **Iterative Refinement for Coherence:** Medium (strong script/narrative scores, but visual continuity risks remain).
- **Overall SOTA Claim:** High (consistent human-eval superiority across multiple metrics against frontier models).

## Next Checks
1. **Critic alignment robustness:** Test the aligned critic committee on an out-of-distribution YouTube corpus (different channels/cultures). Measure classification accuracy on held-out engagement tiers to validate generalization.
2. **Island count vs. diversity-compute trade-off:** Vary the number of islands K while fixing total script evaluations. Plot win rate and inter-diversity against compute to identify optimal K and collapse thresholds.
3. **Continuity error analysis:** Conduct a fine-grained human study quantifying continuity errors (character appearance, background, props). Correlate error rates with rendering iteration depth to evaluate the history tournament's effectiveness.
