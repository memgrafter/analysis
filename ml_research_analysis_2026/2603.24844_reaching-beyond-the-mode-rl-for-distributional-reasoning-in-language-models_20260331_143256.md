# Reaching Beyond the Mode: RL for Distributional Reasoning in Language Models

## Quick Facts

- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.24844
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24844
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Multi-Answer RL significantly improves coverage, diversity, and token efficiency across medical, QA, and coding benchmarks, with up to 37.8% coverage gain and 54% token reduction on MBPP.

## Executive Summary

Standard reinforcement learning for language models optimizes a single answer mode, which is ill-suited for tasks with multiple valid outputs or inherent uncertainty. This paper introduces Multi-Answer RL, which modifies the RL objective to train models to generate a set of diverse candidate answers (with verbalized confidences) in one forward pass. The method uses set-level rewards that count correct answers, and optionally incorporates proper scoring rules (e.g., Brier score) to improve calibration (RLCR Multi). Evaluated on DDXPlus (medical diagnosis), HotPotQA-Modified (ambiguous QA), and MBPP (coding), Multi-Answer RL yields substantial gains: on MBPP, coverage rises 37.8% (1.35 vs 0.98), uniqueness 42.6% (2.98 vs 2.09), token usage drops 54% (235.5 vs 511.7), and top‑1 accuracy improves 69% (0.49 vs 0.29). The results demonstrate that set‑level training is effective for distributional reasoning.

## Method Summary

The approach fine‑tunes a pretrained language model (policy πθ) via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. Instead of producing a single answer, the model outputs K distinct answers along with confidence scores, all within one generation using structured `<answer{i}>` and `<confidence{i}>` tags. The base reward \(R_{\text{multi\_RLVR}}\) equals the number of correct answers in the set; a format reward ensures tag compliance, and a uniqueness penalty zeroes the reward if any normalized answers collide. The RLCR variant subtracts a set‑level Brier score (based on confidence‑correctness alignment) to improve calibration. Policy gradient updates are computed with the GRPO algorithm (using BNPO aggregation) on token‑level advantages derived from reward minus baseline. Training samples multiple responses per prompt at temperature 0.7 for exploration. Experiments span three datasets: DDXPlus (multi‑diagnosis), HotPotQA‑Modified (ambiguous QA), and MBPP (multiple valid implementations), allowing comparison of coverage, diversity, token efficiency, and calibration.

## Key Results

- Coverage ↑ 37.8% on MBPP (1.35 vs 0.98), uniqueness ↑ 42.6% (2.98 vs 2.09), token usage ↓ 54% (235.5 vs 511.7), top‑1 accuracy ↑ 69% (0.49 vs 0.29) versus repeated sampling from single‑answer RL models.
- Simply prompting single‑answer models for multiple answers performs worse, highlighting the need for explicit multi‑answer training.
- RLCR Multi enhances set‑level calibration, reducing Brier score and expected calibration error (ECE).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Diversity & Coverage**  
The set‑level reward \(R_{\text{multi\_RLVR}}(A,Y^*) = \sum_{i=1}^{K} \mathbf{1}[a_i \in Y^*]\) directly incentivizes the model to include as many valid answers as possible in a single generation, rather than collapsing to a single mode. Under policy gradient, this signal shapes the distribution to allocate probability mass to multiple correct answers. Evidence includes equation (4) in Section 3.1 and consistent coverage gains across datasets (Table 1). Break condition: if the model cannot produce distinct answers (due to capacity or parsing failures), duplicates may be rewarded, halting coverage improvements.

**Token Efficiency**  
Generating all candidates in one forward pass reuses a chain‑of‑thought that supports multiple hypotheses, avoiding redundant computation of shared intermediate steps. This amortization drastically reduces total tokens—by up to 54% on MBPP—while preserving quality. Evidence: abstract, Table 1 (token counts), Figure 5 (reduced n‑gram overlap). Break condition: if the shared reasoning becomes overly compressed to accommodate many answers, individual answer quality may suffer.

**Calibration (RLCR Multi)**  
RLCR adds a Brier‑score penalty \(R_{\text{multi\_Brier}}\) to align verbalized confidence with correctness. Because answers are produced jointly, the model can reason about relative support, improving set‑level calibration (lower ECE/Brier). Evidence: abstract, Section 3.2, Table 2. However, on single‑ground‑truth ambiguous tasks (e.g., HotPotQA‑Modified), the model’s prior to sum confidences to 1 conflicts with the need for total confidence < 1, causing RLCR Multi to underperform RLCR Single.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Data Loader → Policy (πθ) → Generated Response (with `<answer{i}>` and `<confidence{i}>` tags) → Reward Calculator (Correctness verifier, Uniqueness validator, Format checker, [Calibration scorer for RLCR]) → Aggregated reward → Advantage computation → Policy Optimizer (GRPO with BNPO) → Updated πθ.

**Critical path:**  
1. Sample a batch of prompts; for each, generate one response containing K answers (and confidences) in a single forward pass.  
2. Parse the response to extract the set A (and confidences Q).  
3. Compute per‑answer correctness; sum to obtain \(R_{\text{multi\_RLVR}}\).  
4. Apply uniqueness and format penalties (usually zeroing reward if violated).  
5. For RLCR, subtract \(R_{\text{multi\_Brier}}\) from the set reward.  
6. Compute advantages (e.g., reward minus baseline) and perform a policy gradient update on tokens within the generated sequence.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Choice of K:* Larger K increases potential coverage but risks filler answers and longer sequences.  
- *Uniqueness enforcement:* Hard zero‑reward versus soft penalty; strictness directly affects diversity and training stability.  
- *Reward weighting:* Balancing correctness, calibration, and auxiliary losses; over‑emphasizing calibration can reduce top‑1 accuracy.  
- *Format rigidity:* Strict tags simplify extraction but may limit natural expression; flexible parsing increases complexity and failure modes.  
- *Exploration:* Entropy regularization is not used; lack of exploration may contribute to collapse.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Duplicate answers appear despite format reward (parser bug or model ignoring constraints).  
- Confidences sum to > 1 in RLCR Multi (format reward should catch; indicates model not following instructions).  
- Top‑1 accuracy drops sharply when increasing K (over‑optimizing set coverage at expense of quality).  
- Token count does not decrease with Multi‑Answer training (model not sharing reasoning; possibly due to CoT length constraints).  
- Calibration degrades on single‑label ambiguous tasks (e.g., HotPotQA‑Modified) due to summation prior.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline replication: Train RLVR Single on a dataset (e.g., MBPP); evaluate pass@1 and pass@K via repeated sampling; confirm mode collapse (low uniqueness, high token use).  
2. Multi‑answer RLVR sweep: Train RLVR Multi with K = 2, 3, 4, 5; measure coverage, uniqueness, and token efficiency; verify that coverage rises and token usage falls as K increases.  
3. Multi‑answer RLCR calibration: Train RLCR Multi; compute Brier score and ECE (set‑level and top‑1); compare against RLVR Multi and RLCR Single; inspect confidence histograms to ensure they are neither over‑ nor under‑confident.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Overcoming confidence summation prior** – On HotPotQA‑Modified, RLCR Multi underperforms RLCR Single because the model adheres to summing confidences to 1, which is inappropriate for single‑answer tasks. The authors leave improved exploration strategies for future work. Resolution evidence: Ablate entropy bonuses or reward reshaping that penalizes confident summation, then measure Set ECE and total confidence distribution.

2. **Application to non‑QA tasks** – Experiments are confined to QA. The transfer of coverage, diversity, and token efficiency gains to open‑ended generation, dialogue, or summarization (with noisier reward signals and freer‑form outputs) remains unknown. Resolution evidence: Implement Multi‑Answer RL on at least one non‑QA benchmark, design set‑level rewards, and evaluate against single‑answer baselines.

3. **Scalability with respect to K** – The paper fixes K=3 but shows coverage improves with larger K up to 5 on DDXPlus. It is unclear how models trained with one K generalize to a different K at inference, or how large K affects training stability. Resolution evidence: Train models with varying K (e.g., 2, 5, 10) and evaluate both same‑K zero‑shot generalization; track loss curves, gradient norms, and performance.

4. **Impact on internal reasoning** – While surface‑level diversity and token overlap improve, it is unknown whether the model’s internal representations and reasoning pathways become semantically more diverse, or whether format enforcement alone explains the gains. Resolution evidence: Probe last‑layer hidden states of single‑answer vs. multi‑answer models, compute representation entropy/clustering, and measure semantic similarity of reasoning chains.

## Limitations

- **GRPO implementation details unspecified:** The paper references BNPO and the removal of standard deviation normalization but does not provide full algorithmic details, hindering exact reproducibility.
- **Uniqueness enforcement ambiguous:** The normalization (lowercasing, punctuation stripping) and AST‑based equivalence for code are not precisely defined, risking inconsistent duplicate detection and inflated diversity metrics.
- **Calibration on ambiguous tasks:** Multi‑answer RLCR can degrade calibration on single‑ground‑truth ambiguous datasets due to the model’s strong prior to sum verbalized confidences to 1, leading to overconfident incorrect sets.
