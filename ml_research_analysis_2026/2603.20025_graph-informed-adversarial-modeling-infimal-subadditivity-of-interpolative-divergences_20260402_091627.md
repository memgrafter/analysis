# Graph-Informed Adversarial Modeling: Infimal Subadditivity of Interpolative Divergences
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20025  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20025  
- **Reference count:** 28  
- **Primary result:** Proves an infimal subadditivity theorem for interpolative divergences, enabling graph-informed GANs that replace a monolithic discriminator with localized family-level discriminators, yielding improved stability and structural recovery.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a theoretical foundation for graph-informed adversarial training when the target distribution factorizes according to a known Bayesian network. It proves that the global variational infimum of a broad class of interpolative divergences—including (f,Γ)-divergences, IPMs, and proximal optimal transport—is bounded above by an average of local discrepancies computed over the network’s node-parent families. This “infimal subadditivity” result justifies decomposing the discriminator into multiple localized networks, each operating on a child-parent family. Experiments on synthetic structured Gaussians, a ball-throwing trajectory model, and real discrete Bayesian networks (CHILD, EARTHQUAKE) confirm that the proposed approach achieves superior training stability, lower run-to-run variance, and more accurate recovery of conditional structure (e.g., parent coefficients, conditional probability tables) compared to graph-agnostic GAN baselines.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a generator \(P_\theta\) (unconstrained neural network) and, for each node \(i\) in a given DAG, a local discriminator \(\gamma_i\) acting on the family \(\{i\} \cup \text{Pa}(i)\). Training minimizes a weighted sum of local objectives, each being a variational representation of an \((f,\Gamma)\)-divergence (e.g., DV-KL for continuous data, JS-Wasserstein for discrete). Lipschitz constraints via spectral normalization are applied to discriminators to ensure the divergences are well-behaved. The global objective is an upper bound on the true divergence between the generator’s distribution and the target factorization, with equality under additive conditions. This decomposes a high-dimensional discrimination problem into smaller, more stable subproblems without requiring the generator to respect the graph structure.

## Key Results
- Theoretical: Infimal subadditivity theorem (Theorem 5.4) shows global \((f,\Gamma)\)-divergence ≤ average local family-level divergences under condition (C).
- Empirical: Graph-informed GANs achieve lower energy distance, higher log-likelihood, and better node-wise total variation than graph-agnostic GANs across all tested Bayesian networks.
- Practical: Localized discriminators reduce run-to-run variance and improve recovery of conditional parameters (parent coefficients, CPTs), with stable training under Lipschitz-regularized KL-type objectives.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The global interpolative divergence can be upper-bounded by an average of family-level divergences aligned with the Bayesian network structure, enabling a surrogate objective.
- **Mechanism:** The proof uses conditional expectation operators to project a global discriminator onto local node-parent families. Under the condition \(I_{i,\text{Pa}(i)}[\Gamma^L] \subset \Gamma_i^{L_i}\) with \(L_i \leq L\), the variational representation of \((f,\Gamma)\)-divergences satisfies infimal subadditivity (Theorem 5.4).
- **Core assumption:** The conditional expectations of the discriminator class remain in a discriminable class on each local subspace (condition (C)).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "proves an infimal subadditivity theorem... the global variational infimum is bounded by an average of local discrepancies"; [section 5] Theorem 5.4 and proof via data-processing inequalities.
- **Break condition:** Condition (C) fails; e.g., if the target’s conditional structure induces a different Lipschitz constant than allowed by \(\Gamma_i^{L_i}\), the inequality may not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Graph-informed adversarial training replaces a monolithic discriminator with multiple localized ones, improving stability and structural recovery without requiring the generator to respect the graph.
- **Mechanism:** Training minimizes a weighted sum of local objectives (23), each acting on a child-parent family. This decomposes a high-dimensional discrimination task into lower-dimensional subproblems that are easier to optimize and diagnose.
- **Core assumption:** The target distribution \(Q\) factorizes exactly according to a known DAG; the local families cover all conditional dependencies.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "replacing a graph-agnostic GAN... by a graph-informed GAN with localized family-level discriminators"; [section 6] Experiments showing lower variance and better recovery of parent coefficients/CPTs.
- **Break condition:** The generator’s implicit distribution \(P_\theta\) cannot match the target’s conditional independencies; the surrogate bound may be loose if families are misaligned.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Lipschitz-regularized discriminators (via spectral normalization) enable stable training with interpolative divergences, especially for KL-type objectives.
- **Mechanism:** Spectral normalization enforces \(\gamma \in \text{Lip}^L_b(\mathcal{X})\), ensuring the discriminator class corresponds to a well-behaved interpolative divergence. This prevents the unboundedness of the convex conjugate \(f^*\) (e.g., \(f^*_{\text{KL}}(s)=e^s-1\)) from causing gradient pathologies.
- **Core assumption:** Discriminators are neural networks with Lipschitz constraints enforceable via weight normalization; the data space is continuous.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4] Discussion of \(f^*\) growth; [section 6.2] Fig 5a,b showing KL instability without Lipschitz constraints, stability with them.
- **Break condition:** Data is discrete and Lipschitz constraints are unnatural; or the Lipschitz constant is improperly tuned.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian networks:**  
  Provide the factorization structure \(P(dx_1,\dots,dx_n)=\prod_{i=1}^n P_{i|\text{Pa}(i)}(dx_i|X_{\text{Pa}(i)}=x_{\text{Pa}(i)})\) that dictates the local family set \(\{i\}\cup\text{Pa}(i)\).  
  **Quick check:** Given a DAG with parent sets \(\text{Pa}(i)\), what are the five families for nodes with indices 1–4?
- **Variational divergences (f-divergences, IPMs, (f,Γ)-divergences):**  
  Define the training objective via discriminator suprema; the subadditivity results are proven for these classes.  
  **Quick check:** How does the primal representation (5) of an \((f,\Gamma)\)-divergence mix an IPM and an f-divergence?
- **Lipschitz function classes and Wasserstein metric:**  
  Allow interpolative divergences that constrain discriminators, providing stability; condition (C) requires discriminators’ conditional expectations to stay in a Lipschitz class.  
  **Quick check:** Why might a 1-Lipschitz discriminator avoid the exponential growth issues of the KL conjugate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Known DAG \(G\) → local discriminators \(\gamma_i: \mathcal{X}_{\{i\}\cup\text{Pa}(i)} \to \mathbb{R}\) (spectral norm) + generator \(g_\theta: \mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{X}\) → loss \(\mathcal{J}(\theta,\{\eta_i\}) = \frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{i\in V} w_i J_i\) (DV-KL or f-GAN) → Adam updates.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Define families \(\{i\}\cup\text{Pa}(i)\) from DAG.  
  2. Build discriminators with matching input dims and Lipschitz constraints.  
  3. Sample mini-batch of real data, generate fake batch.  
  4. Compute local objectives per family; sum; update discriminators; then update generator.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Family size:** Smaller families emphasize local conditional fidelity but may sacrifice global energy distance; larger ancestor sets tighten the surrogate but increase cost.  
  - **Lipschitz constant \(L\):** Smaller \(L\) yields faster decay but may underfit; larger \(L\) tightens the bound but can destabilize training.  
  - **Objective choice (JS vs KL):** JS’s bounded conjugate yields more stable discrete training; KL with Lipschitz regularization works for continuous but is sensitive to \(L\).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Unconstrained KL: exploding gradients, loss oscillates.  
  - Misaligned families: good global energy distance but poor parent-coefficient recovery or node-wise TV.  
  - Overly large families: may approach graph-agnostic behavior, losing localization benefits.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Structured linear-Gaussian Hasse network (Section 6.2.1):** Controlled test with known parent coefficients; use DV-KL (21), spectral norm \(L=1\); compare graph-agnostic, graph-informed (child-parent), and misaligned multi-discriminator; evaluate parent-coefficient \(L^2\) error and energy distance.  
  2. **Ball-throwing trajectory (Section 6.2.2):** Continuous time-series; use DV-KL with/without spectral norm; families are triples \((y_{t-2},y_{t-1},y_t)\); diagnose physics estimates (\(\hat{g},\hat{v}_0\)).  
  3. **CHILD discrete Bayesian network (Section 6.3.2):** Use JS-Wasserstein with Gumbel-softmax; compare graph-agnostic, graph-informed child-parent, and depth-3 ancestor families; evaluate node-wise total variation and average log-likelihood under ground-truth CPTs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does combining graph-informed discriminators with generators that respect the Bayesian network factorization affect theoretical guarantees, sample efficiency, and uncertainty calibration?
2. More broadly, it would be interesting to better understand how the choice of neighborhoods, discrepancy class, and discriminator regularity should be adapted to the scientific or decision-making task at hand.
3. Does the infimal subadditivity principle extend to other interpolative divergences beyond (f,Γ)-divergences, IPMs, and proximal optimal transport (e.g., Stein divergences, Sinkhorn distances)?

## Limitations
- The exact DAG structure and edge coefficients for the Hasse network (Fig. 1) are not explicitly tabulated, requiring manual reconstruction.
- Parent–child relationships for CHILD and EARTHQUAKE networks are only schematically shown; exact families must be derived from external sources.
- Critical hyperparameters (Adam β₁/β₂, Gumbel-softmax temperature τ, spectral normalization power iterations, early stopping frequency) are not reported, potentially affecting training stability and results.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical infimal subadditivity (Theorem 5.4):** High – proof is rigorous under stated assumptions.
- **Graph-informed GANs outperform graph-agnostic baselines:** Medium – experiments show consistent improvements, but missing implementation details and hyperparameter sensitivities introduce uncertainty.
- **Lipschitz-regularized discriminators stabilize KL-type objectives:** Medium – empirical evidence supports the claim, but the precise role of the Lipschitz constant \(L\) and its enforcement remain under-explored.

## Next Checks
