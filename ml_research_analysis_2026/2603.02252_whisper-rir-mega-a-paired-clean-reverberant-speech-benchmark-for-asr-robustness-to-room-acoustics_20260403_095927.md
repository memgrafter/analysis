# Whisper-RIR-Mega: A Paired Clean-Reverberant Speech Benchmark for ASR Robustness to Room Acoustics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02252  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02252  
- **Reference count**: 6  
- **Primary result**: Reverberation increases WER for all Whisper models, with reverb penalty ranging from 2.31 pp (large‑v3) to 15.50 pp (tiny); larger models exhibit greater robustness.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Whisper‑RIR‑Mega, a benchmark for evaluating automatic speech recognition (ASR) robustness to room reverberation. The benchmark pairs clean speech from LibriSpeech test‑clean with synthetic reverberant audio generated by convolving with room impulse responses (RIRs) from the RIR‑Mega dataset. The RIRs are stratified by acoustic parameters (RT₆₀ and DRR) to cover a range of room conditions. Five Whisper model variants (tiny to large‑v3) are evaluated on 1,600 samples. Results show that reverberation degrades performance for all models, but the degradation is significantly smaller for larger models, indicating that model scale improves robustness to acoustic variability.

## Method Summary
The methodology creates a controlled paired test set: each clean LibriSpeech utterance is convolved with a selected RIR from RIR‑Mega to produce a reverberant counterpart. RIR selection is stratified by RT₆₀ (and DRR when available) to ensure diverse acoustic conditions. Prior to convolution, RIR energy is normalized (method unspecified) and the resulting reverberant audio is peak‑normalized. Speech transcripts are obtained using five Whisper models via beam search (beam size 5, best_of 5, temperature 0, language “en”). Word error rate (WER) and character error rate (CER) are computed with the `jiwer` library after standard text normalization (lower‑casing, punctuation removal, whitespace collapse). The reverb penalty is defined as the difference between reverberant and clean metrics.

## Key Results
- Reverberation degrades ASR performance across all Whisper model sizes.
- Reverb penalty (WER increase) ranges from 2.31 percentage points (large‑v3) to 15.50 percentage points (tiny).
- Larger Whisper models demonstrate measurably greater robustness to reverberation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paired benchmark isolates the acoustic effect of reverberation by holding lexical content constant—clean and reverberant versions share the same transcript. Convolving with diverse RIRs, stratified by RT₆₀ and DRR, simulates real‑world room acoustics across a spectrum of decay times and clarity. This design ensures that performance differences reflect only the impact of reverberation. The observed trend of larger models being more robust likely stems from their greater capacity and exposure to heterogeneous acoustic conditions during pre‑training, enabling them to learn representations that are less sensitive to convolutional distortions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Room Impulse Response (RIR)**: The acoustic signature of a room; convolving with clean speech introduces reverberation. *Why needed*: To synthesize realistic reverberant audio. *Quick check*: Can you generate a synthetic RIR using a decaying exponential model?
- **RT₆₀ (Reverberation Time)**: Time required for sound energy to decay by 60 dB; characterizes room size and absorption. *Why needed*: Stratifies RIRs into bins of varying room acoustics. *Quick check*: Given an RIR, can you compute RT₆₀ via backward integration of energy decay?
- **DRR (Direct‑to‑Reverberant Ratio)**: Ratio of early (direct) to late (reverberant) sound energy; indicates clarity. *Why needed*: Provides an additional dimension for acoustic diversity. *Quick check*: Can you estimate DRR from an RIR by separating energy before and after a chosen cutoff?
- **Whisper ASR**: A sequence‑to‑sequence speech‑to‑text model trained on massive multilingual data. *Why needed*: The target model whose robustness is evaluated. *Quick check*: Can you load a Whisper model via `transformers` and transcribe a short audio clip?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text):
Clean Speech (LibriSpeech) + RIR (RIR‑Mega) → Convolution & Normalization → Reverberant Speech → Whisper Encoder‑Decoder → Transcript → jiwer → WER/CER

**Critical path**:
1. Acquire Whisper‑RIR‑Mega test pairs and RIR‑Mega train RIRs.  
2. For each pair: load clean audio, normalize RIR (energy), convolve, peak‑normalize reverberant output.  
3. Run Whisper inference with specified beam search parameters.  
4. Compute WER/CER against reference transcript using `jiwer` with standard normalization.  
5. Aggregate metrics over the test set and compute reverb penalties.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Beam size / best_of: larger beams improve accuracy but increase latency.  
- Normalization: choice of RIR energy norm (L2 vs. max‑abs) and peak target (1.0 vs. 0.9) affects audio scale and thus WER.  
- Chunking: Whisper’s internal 30‑second windowing may handle short LibriSpeech utterances differently if padding is applied.  
- Test set size: 1,600 samples balances evaluation cost and acoustic coverage.

**Failure signatures**:
- Clean WER unexpectedly high → model loading or inference misconfiguration.  
- Large variance in reverb penalties across RIR bins → inconsistent RIR assignment or normalization.  
- Reproducibility issues across runs → non‑determinism in beam search or audio resampling.

**First three experiments**:
1. Validate clean baselines: run Whisper models on original LibriSpeech test‑clean and compare WERs to published numbers.  
2. Single‑RIR sanity check: convolve one clean sample with one RIR and verify that the pipeline produces a plausible degradation.  
3. Normalization robustness: re‑run evaluation with alternative RIR normalization (e.g., max‑abs) and peak‑normalization targets to gauge sensitivity.

## Limitations
- Test set size (1,600 samples) may be insufficient to fully capture the acoustic diversity of RIR‑Mega.  
- Preprocessing details (exact RIR energy normalization, peak‑normalization factor, and stratification algorithm) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.  
- Potential influence of Whisper’s internal chunking on short‑utterance processing was not addressed.

## Confidence
- Reverberation degrades ASR performance: **High** (consistent with established literature).  
- Exact reverb penalty values: **Medium** (sensitive to preprocessing choices and normalization details).  
- Larger Whisper models show greater robustness: **Medium** (based on five model variants; possible confounders from architecture and training data).

## Next Checks
1. Clarify the complete preprocessing pipeline with the authors (RIR energy normalization method, reverberant peak‑normalization target, stratification protocol including number of RT₆₀ bins, deterministic assignment rule, and random seed).  
2. Conduct a normalization sensitivity analysis: re‑run the evaluation using alternative RIR normalization (e.g., L2 vs. max‑abs) and peak‑normalization targets (e.g., 0.9 vs. 1.0) to assess robustness of the reverb‑penalty conclusions.  
3. Verify audio I/O and chunking: inspect native sample rates of all RIR‑Mega files and resample to 16 kHz if necessary; compare WERs with Whisper’s default chunking disabled (e.g., `return_timestamps=False`, padding to 30‑second multiples) to rule out chunking artifacts.
