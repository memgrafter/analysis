# Learning to Compose for Cross-domain Agentic Workflow Generation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11114
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11114
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CapFlow achieves higher solve rates (e.g., 74.19% overall) than iterative refinement baselines while substantially reducing inference latency and cost.

## Executive Summary
Agentic workflow generation traditionally relies on costly and unstable iterative refinement, especially under domain shifts. CapFlow introduces a decompose-recompose-decide framework that learns a compact set of reusable workflow capability bases and uses a task-conditioned sparse composer with counterfactual attribution to generate task-specific workflows in a single pass. This approach yields higher solve rates (74.19% on average) compared to state-of-the-art iterative baselines (e.g., AFlow 72.93%, ScoreFlow 71.22%) across multi-domain, cross-domain, and unseen-domain evaluations, while dramatically cutting inference latency and cost. Ablations confirm the contribution of each component, and the method demonstrates strong generalization.

## Method Summary
CapFlow augments a frozen large language model (LLM) with a small set of learnable low-rank capability bases that capture reusable workflow factors. For a given task query \(q\), a task encoder produces an embedding \(z(q)\); a composer network then maps this to routing weights \(\alpha(q)\) and an adaptive temperature. Only the top-\(m\) bases are activated globally, and the generator constructs adapted model weights \(M(q) = M + \sum_{k \in \text{active}} \alpha_k \Delta B_k\) to produce the workflow code in a single forward pass. Training combines three objectives: multi-reference likelihood \(L_{MR}\) to fit diverse successful workflows, listwise NCE contrastive loss \(L_{NCE}\) to improve discrimination, and counterfactual capability attribution \(L_{CCA}\) to align the composer with causal contributions. Bases and composer are updated with a two-timescale optimizer, supported by orthogonal and entropy regularizations to promote diverse and specialized bases.

## Key Results
- CapFlow achieves 74.19% overall solve rate, surpassing AFlow (72.93%) and ScoreFlow (71.22%).
- It maintains strong performance gains in cross-domain and unseen-domain settings.
- Inference is significantly faster and cheaper than multi-iteration refinement approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Workflow Capability Decomposition
- **Claim:** Learning a compact set of reusable capability bases enables cross-domain transfer by capturing latent workflow factors.
- **Mechanism:** The model augments a frozen LLM with a small set of low-rank basis updates \(\Delta B_k\) (Eq. 5) that represent reusable workflow capability factors.
- **Core assumption:** Successful workflows across domains share common latent factors that can be disentangled into a compact basis set.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "to decompose, we learn a compact set of reusable workflow capabilities across diverse domains."
  - [section 4.1] "We operationalize the findings... by parameterizing these factors as a compact set of reusable capability bases."
  - [corpus] The corpus reveals limited direct evidence for this mechanism; neighboring papers focus on iterative refinement or performance prediction, underscoring the novelty of explicit capability decomposition.
- **Break condition:** If the number of bases \(K\) is too small, the model underfits diverse workflow factors; if too large, attribution becomes noisy and transfer degrades (ablation Tab. 2).

### Mechanism 2: Task-Conditioned Sparse Composition
- **Claim:** Mapping each task to a sparse combination of capability bases allows controllable, single-pass generation of task-specific workflows.
- **Mechanism:** A composer network \(g_\psi\) takes a task embedding \(z(q)\) and outputs routing weights \(\alpha(q)\) with adaptive temperature; only the top-\(m\) bases are globally activated (Eq. 7–9).
- **Core assumption:** Workflow generation can be steered by a global task-level composition that coherently shapes the entire generator.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "to recompose, we map each input task to a sparse composition over these bases to generate a task-specific workflow in a single pass."
  - [section 4.2] "We model this as a task-conditioned capability composer... Eq. (7)–(8)."
  - [section 5.3] "Composer activates multiple bases rather than collapsing to a single one, while several bases are repeatedly used across datasets."
- **Break condition:** Dense mixing blurs capability factors and increases shift variance; overly sparse composition lacks expressivity and hurts solve rate (Tab. 3).

### Mechanism 3: Counterfactual Capability Attribution
- **Claim:** Counterfactual attribution of workflow success to capability bases aligns the composer with causal drivers, improving transfer under domain shift.
- **Mechanism:** For each workflow, compute marginal contribution \(\Delta_k\) by ablating basis \(k\) and measuring likelihood difference (Eq. 14). Composer updates via policy gradient to increase probability for bases with positive \(\Delta_k\) on successful workflows (Eq. 15).
- **Core assumption:** The marginal effect of a capability basis on the likelihood of generating a successful workflow indicates its causal contribution to task success.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "to decide, we attribute the success or failure of workflow generation to counterfactual contributions... capturing which capabilities actually drive success by their marginal effects."
  - [section 4.5] "We optimize: \(L_{CCA} = -\mathbb{E}[\sum \text{sgn}(s) \cdot \Delta_k \cdot \log \alpha_k] + \lambda_{dead} L_{dead}\)."
  - [section 5.4] "CCA significantly improves the solve rate across all datasets... structured credit assignment is crucial."
- **Break condition:** Noisy or uninformative \(\Delta_k\) (e.g., near zero for many bases) degrades composer training; dead-basis penalty mitigates but does not eliminate risk.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Low-rank adaptation (LoRA-family)
  - **Why needed here:** Enables parameter-efficient learning of capability bases without modifying the entire frozen LLM.
  - **Quick check:** In Eq. (5), each basis update \(\Delta B_k = c_k U_k V_k^\top\) is rank-\(r\). What happens if \(r\) is too large?
- **Concept:** Sparse mixture of experts (MoE) routing
  - **Why needed here:** Provides controllable recomposition by activating only a few bases per task, preventing signal dilution.
  - **Quick check:** Why is top-\(m\) sparse activation preferred over dense mixing? (see Tab. 3 ablation.)
- **Concept:** Counterfactual reasoning for credit assignment
  - **Why needed here:** Aligns composer decisions with causal contributions rather than mere correlation, crucial for generalization.
  - **Quick check:** How is \(\Delta_k\) computed and why might it approximate a causal effect? (Eq. 14.)
- **Concept:** Multi-reference likelihood training
  - **Why needed here:** Tasks admit multiple successful workflows; we must avoid overfitting to a single reference pattern.
  - **Quick check:** What is the difference between \(L_{MR}\) (Eq. 11) and standard imitation? How does \(L_{NCE}\) augment it?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  - **Task encoder:** Embeds input query \( q \) to \( z(q) \).  
  - **Composer \(g_\psi\):** Maps \( z(q) \) to routing weights \( \alpha(q) \) and temperature \( T(q) \).  
  - **Capability bases \(\{\Delta B_k\}\):** Low-rank updates applied to selected linear layers of a frozen LLM.  
  - **Sparse activator:** Selects top-\(m\) bases from \( \alpha(q) \) and broadcasts them globally.  
  - **Generator:** The adapted LLM with weights \( M(q) = M + \sum_{k \in \text{active}} \alpha_k \Delta B_k \) outputs workflow code.  
  - **Attribution module (training):** Computes counterfactual \( \Delta_k \) and updates composer via \( L_{CCA} \).
- **Critical path (inference):**  
  1. Encode task \(q\) → \(z(q)\).  
  2. Compute \(\alpha(q) = \text{Softmax}(u(q)/T(q))\).  
  3. Activate top-\(m\) bases, construct \(M(q)\).  
  4. Generate workflow code in one pass.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - \(K\) (bases count): Fewer bases limit expressivity; more bases increase attribution difficulty and risk of underused bases.  
  - \(m\) (sparsity): Smaller \(m\) enforces specialization; larger \(m\) improves coverage but may reduce interpretability and transfer.  
  - Attribution interval \(E\): More frequent counterfactual updates improve alignment but increase compute; less frequent speeds up training but risks drift.  
  - Temperature adjustment: Adaptive \(T(q)\) allows per-task entropy control; extreme values can cause collapse or excessive randomness.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Basis collapse: Routing entropy \(H(\alpha(q))\) near zero; composer relies on one basis → poor transfer.  
  - Non-sparse activation: Average active bases >> \(m\) indicates composer uncertainty or uninformative task embeddings.  
  - Attribution misalignment: High \(\Delta_k\) for bases with low \(\alpha_k\) across successful tasks suggests noisy credit assignment.  
  - Cross-domain performance drop: Sharp decrease on unseen domains points to insufficiently reusable bases.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Decomposition capacity: Vary \(K\) and \(m\) (Tab. 2, 3) to identify effective basis library size and sparsity level.  
  2. Decide component impact: Ablate counterfactual attribution (CCA) and contrastive loss (NCE) (Tab. 4) to quantify contributions.  
  3. Cross-domain transfer: Compare single-domain vs. multi-domain training and evaluate on held-out datasets (Tab. 1) to confirm compositionality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can CapFlow be extended to handle evolving operator libraries and tool availability?  
  *Basis:* Future work includes extending to evolving operator libraries and tool availability.  
  *Why unresolved:* CapFlow assumes a fixed operator set during training; new operators lack corresponding capability bases, potentially causing failures.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Test tasks requiring previously unseen operators; integrate mechanisms like online basis addition or meta-adaptation; compare solve rates to static baselines.
- **Open Question 2:** How can CapFlow incorporate continual adaptation from execution feedback to improve over time?  
  *Basis:* Incorporating continual adaptation from execution feedback.  
  *Why unresolved:* CapFlow is trained offline and does not use outcomes of generated workflows when deployed, missing opportunities to adapt to distribution shifts.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Deploy in an online setting with feedback; update bases/composer via rehearsal or elastic weight consolidation; show sustained performance gains across sequential tasks.
- **Open Question 3:** What diagnostics and theoretical foundations are needed to generalize learned capability factors to longer-horizon, more complex workflow topologies?  
  *Basis:* Developing stronger diagnostics and theory for generalizing the learned capability factors to longer-horizon, more complex workflow topologies.  
  *Why unresolved:* Evaluations used moderate-length workflows; it's unclear if bases compose effectively for significantly longer or highly branched workflows, and no theory bounds performance.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Build benchmarks with extended depth/branching; analyze basis utilization and degradation; develop formal models linking basis count, sparsity, and workflow classes; empirically verify predictions.

## Limitations
- Operator library definitions and prompt templates are unspecified.
- Initial workflow curation procedure is vague.
- Hyperparameters, layer selection strategy, and training logistics (batch size, optimizer) are missing.

## Confidence
- Decomposition into reusable bases: Medium (ablation supports compact basis necessity but latent factors remain abstract).
- Sparse task-conditioned composition: Medium (sparsity helps but global steering assumption untested).
- Counterfactual attribution: Low (performance gain shown but causal interpretation unverified).
- Overall cross-domain performance: Medium (only one LLM size tested; domain generalizability uncertain).
