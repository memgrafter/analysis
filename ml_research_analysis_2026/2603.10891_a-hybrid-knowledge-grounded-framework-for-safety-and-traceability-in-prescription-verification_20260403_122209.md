# A Hybrid Knowledge-Grounded Framework for Safety and Traceability in Prescription Verification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10891
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10891
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Achieves 72.2% F1-score in prescription error detection (+13.4% over a traditional CDSS), with high precision (74.3%) and recall (70.3%), while reducing alert fatigue.

## Executive Summary
Medication errors are a critical patient safety issue, but existing safeguards are inadequate. Pharmacist verification is burdened by manual review, and direct application of large language models (LLMs) is prone to hallucinations and lacks auditability. This paper introduces **PharmGraph-Auditor**, a framework that uses a Hybrid Pharmaceutical Knowledge Base (HPKB) combining a relational store for numerical constraints and a graph store for semantic topology. A KB-grounded Chain of Verification (CoV) decomposes auditing into a deterministic pipeline of planning, querying, evidence curation, and synthesis. On real-world prescriptions, the system outperforms both human experts and a traditional rule-based Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) in F1-score, demonstrating a viable path toward safe, traceable AI for high-stakes clinical decision support.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in two phases: **Knowledge Base Construction** and **Auditing**. Construction uses an **Iterative Schema Refinement (ISR)** algorithm where an LLM proposes schema gaps from pharmaceutical documents, and a human expert abstracts these into a stable hybrid schema (relational tables for constraints, graph nodes/edges for semantics). A section-aware multi-agent extractor populates the HPKB with provenance. For auditing, the **KB-grounded Chain of Verification** takes a prescription and patient profile, decomposes it into verification tasks via an LLM, generates deterministic hybrid queries (SQL/Cypher), curates the single most relevant rule per task using a Patient Profile-Driven Evidence Selection Tree (P-EST), and finally synthesizes a report with explicit evidence citations and uncertainty flags for missing data.

## Key Results
-   **Overall Performance:** 74.3% precision, 70.3% recall, 72.2% F1-score on a real-world prescription benchmark.
-   **Comparison:** Outperforms human expert recall (45.9%) and a traditional CDSS (52.1% precision, 58.8% F1), achieving a +13.4% F1 improvement over the CDSS.
-   **Efficiency & Safety:** Reduces alert fatigue (higher precision) while maintaining high recall, and provides full traceability from finding to source document.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Knowledge Storage for Algorithmic Efficiency
The HPKB assigns knowledge to a relational (R) or graph (G) store based on logical operation type. R handles set constraint satisfaction (e.g., dosage thresholds) with efficient indexed scans (O(log N)). G handles topological traversal (e.g., drug-allergy hierarchies) with index-free adjacency (O(1) per hop). A mapping layer (φ) unifies them, allowing each sub-task in a complex query to be routed to the optimal store. The core assumption is that pharmaceutical auditing tasks are algorithmically distinct: numerical checks are best served by relational algebra, while semantic pathfinding is best served by graph traversal. A break condition would be a query requiring a deep graph traversal followed by a complex numerical filter on the intermediate set, where R-G coordination overhead could negate theoretical gains.

### Mechanism 2: Iterative Schema Refinement (ISR) for Robust Coverage
ISR evolves a stable hybrid schema (S_final) via a human-AI loop. Starting from a seed schema, an LLM agent identifies "schema gaps" (facts not fitting current schema) from sampled documents, generating proposals. A human expert reviews, abstracting specific proposals (e.g., separate tables for renal/hepatic adjustment) into generalized structures (e.g., a generic `Constraint` node). Proposals are classified into R or G. Iteration stabilizes when N consecutive documents yield no valid changes. The core assumption is that LLMs are effective "gap detectors" but prone to over-specific "schema fragmentation"; human experts are necessary for "semantic abstraction" to maintain a compact, generalizable schema. A break condition is an impoverished seed schema causing overwhelming proposals and expert fatigue, leading to a brittle or over-complex S_final.

### Mechanism 3: KB-Grounded Chain of Verification (CoV) for Safety & Traceability
The CoV framework prevents LLM hallucination by never asking the LLM for factual answers. Stage 1: A Decomposition Agent creates a task plan. Stage 2: A rule-based Query Generator converts tasks into exact hybrid queries (SQL for R, Cypher for G). Stage 3: P-EST applies exact-match then hierarchical fallback to select one rule per task. Stage 4: A Synthesis Agent uses only the curated evidence to generate the final report, explicitly flagging `INFO_GAPS` for missing patient data. The core assumption is an LLM's reasoning is reliable only when its inputs are verifiable, minimal, and sourced from a trustworthy KB; its strength is orchestration and synthesis, not fact retrieval. A break condition is an incomplete Verification Plan from Stage 1, which would omit a critical task and cause a silent failure.

-   **Concept: Chain-of-Verification (CoV)**
    *Why needed here:* This is the core inference paradigm replacing monolithic LLM prompting. It structures the LLM as a planner/synthesizer, not a fact generator, explaining the system's avoidance of hallucination.
    *Quick check:* How does CoV differ from standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in managing evidence?
-   **Concept: Knowledge Stratification**
    *Why needed here:* This theoretical principle mandates assigning data to R or G based on its logical operation (constraint vs. traversal). It is the "why" for the HPKB's hybrid design.
    *Quick check:* For the clinical rule "reduce dose if CrCl < 30," justify its placement in the Relational Component using the stratification framework.

## Architecture Onboarding
-   **Component map:** HPKB Construction (ISR Algorithm -> Section-Aware Multi-Agent Extraction) -> Hybrid Persistence (R & G Stores with φ) -> Auditing Engine (Decomposition Agent -> Query Generator -> P-EST Curator -> Synthesis Agent)
-   **Critical path:** The schema must be built and populated (ISR + Extraction) before any auditing can occur. The CoV stages are strictly linear; Stage 2's raw query results are mandatory input for Stage 3's curation.
-   **Design tradeoffs:**
    -   *Hybrid Storage vs. Simplicity:* Dual R/G stores with mapping increase complexity but are necessary for efficiency and correctness.
    -   *ISR Iteration vs. Speed:* The schema refinement loop is slow and requires expert time but is argued essential for coverage and abstraction.
    -   *CoV Stages vs. Latency:* The 4-stage pipeline adds latency versus a single-shot LLM call but trades speed for safety, traceability, and reduced hallucination.
-   **Failure signatures:**
    -   *ISR Failure:* Schema never stabilizes or stabilizes with poor extraction recall → indicates flawed gap detection/abstraction.
    -   *P-EST Failure:* Curated evidence is irrelevant/misses correct rule → leads to false negatives/positives.
    -   *CoV Incompleteness:* Missing `INFO_GAP` flags for absent patient data → system hallucinates a verdict.
-   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Knowledge Population Quality (RQ1):** Compare extraction F1 against Zero-shot OpenIE and One-shot Schema-guided baselines to validate the multi-agent framework.
    2.  **Prescription Auditing Benchmark (RQ2):** Compare Precision/Recall/F1 against Human Experience (memory-only) and a rule-based CDSS on real prescriptions.
    3.  **Ablation Study:** Ablate CoV components (w/o CoV, w/o Knowledge) on a synthetic "red-team" dataset to quantify the contribution of the staged query/curation process.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can Real-World Evidence (RWE) from clinical workflows (e.g., hospital SOPs, provider notes) be systematically integrated into the HPKB to resolve the "clinical context" gap, particularly for indications where procedural use is not formally listed in pharmaceutical documents?
*Basis:* The Conclusion states future work will integrate RWE to bridge the gap between rigid pharmaceutical definitions and flexible workflows. Supported by the case study showing low Indication precision (e.g., saline flagged as non-indicated).
*Why unresolved:* The current HPKB is built only from standardized documents (package inserts), lacking tacit, context-specific knowledge.
*What evidence would resolve it:* Augment the HPKB with a curated hospital-specific corpus (guidelines, notes) and re-evaluate performance on the Indication error type.

### Open Question 2
To what extent is the HPKB architecture and CoV framework generalizable to other high-stakes domains requiring both rigorous constraint satisfaction and complex semantic reasoning (e.g., legal compliance, financial auditing)?
*Basis:* The Abstract claims the hybrid architecture is adaptable to other complex domains.
*Why unresolved:* The implementation, ISR, and query generation are heavily tailored to pharmaceutical logic. Generalizability to non-medical domains is unevidenced.
*What evidence would resolve it:* A replication study applying the full pipeline to a different domain (e.g., financial regulation), measuring F1 improvements over domain baselines.

### Open Question 3
What is the optimal operational configuration of the Patient Profile-Driven Evidence Selection Tree (P-EST) to maximize the trade-off between computational cost (token usage, query latency) and auditing precision, especially for rare or complex patient profiles?
*Basis:* Inferred from the Ablation Study showing cost differences and the P-EST's hierarchical fallback logic being heuristics-based without formal tuning.
*Why unresolved:* The paper does not study how P-EST pruning aggressiveness affects cost and accuracy for edge-case patients.
*What evidence would resolve it:* A controlled A/B test varying P-EST parameters (fallback depth, confidence thresholds) on a held-out set of complex prescriptions, measuring multi-objective metrics (F1, tokens, latency).

### Open Question 4
How does the performance of the Iterative Schema Refinement (ISR) algorithm scale with initial seed schema diversity and source document heterogeneity? Is there a quantifiable, early-stopping metric for "rapid stabilization" less subjective than N_stable consecutive documents?
*Basis:* Inferred from ISR's stopping condition ("N_stable consecutive documents pass") being unspecified and human-dependent.
*Why unresolved:* The lack of a formal metric makes the process hard to automate, reproduce, and scale. Schema evolution curves across different seeds/orders are unanalyzed.
*What evidence would resolve it:* An ablation study with different seeds/orders, plotting cumulative schema growth, and proposing/validating a formal metric (e.g., rate of novel predicate introduction below ε).

### Open Question 5
Can the HPKB construction pipeline be made fully automated without compromising verifiability, by replacing/augmenting the human expert's role in "Semantic Abstraction" with a more capable LLM or a validated domain ontology?
*Basis:* ISR explicitly describes a "Human-AI Synergy" with the LLM as "Gap Detector" and human as "Architect" to mitigate "Schema Fragmentation."
*Why unresolved:* The human-in-the-loop is a scalability bottleneck. The paper asserts synergy is necessary but doesn't explore if advanced LLMs or ontologies (SNOMED CT, RxNorm) could reduce this dependency.
*What evidence would resolve it:* An experiment comparing schemas from (a) full human-in-the-loop ISR, (b) ISR with an LLM "Architect" agent, and (c) ISR initialized with ontology mappings, comparing schema compactness, coverage, and expert coherence.

## Limitations
-   **ISR Scalability & Subjectivity:** The human-AI loop's feasibility at scale, including expert burden and the unevidenced "semantic abstraction" step, is a major uncertainty.
-   **P-EST Specification Gap:** The rule hierarchy encoding and conflict resolution logic for the P-EST curator are underspecified, posing implementation risks.
-   **Hybrid Query Coordination Cost:** The theoretical overhead of routing complex, interleaved queries between R and G stores via the mapping layer (φ) is not empirically measured.
