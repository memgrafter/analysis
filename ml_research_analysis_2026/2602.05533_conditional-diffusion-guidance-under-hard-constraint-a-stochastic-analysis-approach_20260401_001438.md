# Conditional Diffusion Guidance under Hard Constraint: A Stochastic Analysis Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05533
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05533
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Off-policy algorithms (CDG-ML and CDG-MCL) using Doob’s h-transform steer pretrained diffusion models to satisfy hard constraints, with theoretical error bounds and empirical validation.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses conditional generation from diffusion models under hard constraints, requiring generated samples to satisfy an event with probability one. The core method employs Doob’s h-transform to modify the dynamics of a pretrained diffusion model by adding a drift correction derived from the logarithmic gradient of a conditioning function h(t,y), which represents the conditional probability of satisfying the constraint at the terminal time. This approach leaves the pretrained score network unchanged and operates off-policy, using only trajectories from the original model. Two learning algorithms are introduced: CDG-ML estimates h directly via a martingale loss; CDG-MCL estimates the gradient ∇h through a martingale-covariation loss. Theoretical analysis yields non-asymptotic total variation and Wasserstein-2 bounds that separate score approximation error from guidance estimation error. Empirical evaluations on synthetic Gaussian distributions, finance stress-testing portfolios, and supply-chain simulations demonstrate effective constraint enforcement and accurate rare-event generation.

## Method Summary
Given a pretrained score-based diffusion model defined by forward SDE: dY_t = f(t,Y_t) dt + g(t) dB_t, with score network sθ*(t,y) estimating ∇log p_t(y) of the data marginal, the objective is to sample from p_data(y | Z∈S) where Z = F(Y_T) for some constraint set S. The guided SDE derived from Doob’s h-transform is:
dY_t^S = [f(t,Y_t^S) + g(t)^2 ∇log h(t,Y_t^S)] dt + g(t) dB_t,
with h(t,y)=P(Y_T∈S | Y_t=y). Since h is unknown, the authors propose two off-policy methods:

- **CDG-ML**: Train a neural network h_ϕ(t,y) to approximate h by minimizing the martingale loss L_ML(ϕ)=E[∫_0^T (h_ϕ(t,Y_t) - 1(Y_T∈S))^2 dt] using trajectories from the pretrained model. After training, the guided drift uses g(t)^2 ∇log h_ϕ.
- **CDG-MCL**: First obtain h_ϕ* via CDG-ML. Then train a network q_ψ(t,y) to estimate the covariation term q(t,y)=d[h,Y]_t/dt via loss L_MCL(ψ)=E[∫_0^T (1/g(t)^2 d[h_ϕ*,Y]_t/dt - q_ψ(t,Y_t))^2 dt]. The guided drift becomes g(t)^2 * (q_ψ(t,y)/h_ϕ*(t,y)).

Both methods require only data from the fixed pretrained SDE, avoiding on-policy interactions.

## Key Results
- Theoretically, non-asymptotic total variation and Wasserstein-2 bounds are established, explicitly decomposing errors from the pretrained score network and from the estimated guidance.
- Empirically, on synthetic Gaussian examples, CDG achieves Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics of 0.0694 (CDG-ML) and 0.0437 (CDG-MCL); on finance stress-testing portfolios, guided samples correctly match real quantiles under constraints; on supply-chain simulations, the method successfully controls queue lengths under rare-event conditioning.
- Both algorithms enforce hard constraints with high reliability, and CDG-MCL tends to yield more accurate gradients at the cost of additional estimation noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Doob’s h-transform is a classical result in stochastic processes: for a Markov process Y, the conditional law of Y given the future event {Y_T∈S} is again Markov with generator modified by the drift g^2 ∇log h, where h(t,y) is the conditional probability of the event. This transformation does not alter the marginal score of the unconditioned process, so the pretrained score network remains valid. The key is that h(t,y) can be learned from data because h(t,Y_t) is a martingale under the original dynamics. The martingale loss exploited in CDG-ML directly targets this property, making the learning off-policy. CDG-MCL goes further by estimating the gradient of h via the covariation between h and Y, which appears in the product rule for martingales. The theoretical bounds follow from measuring the discrepancy between the guided SDE and the ideal conditional diffusion in terms of errors in score and guidance approximations, using tools like Girsanov’s theorem and Wasserstein distance coupling.

## Foundational Learning
**Diffusion models**  
*Why needed*: Provides the pretrained base distribution to be conditioned.  
*Quick check*: Verify low denoising score matching loss on held-out data.

**Doob’s h-transform**  
*Why needed*: Gives the exact dynamics for the conditional distribution without altering the original score.  
*Quick check*: For a tractable case (e.g., Gaussian with linear constraint), simulate the guided SDE and compare terminal distribution to the known conditional.

**Martingale loss**  
*Why needed*: Enables off-policy estimation of h using only samples from the pretrained process.  
*Quick check*: After training, evaluate the loss on a validation set; it should be small and h_ϕ values should stay in [0,1].

**Covariation estimation**  
*Why needed*: CDG-MCL uses it to learn ∇h directly, avoiding differentiation of h_ϕ.  
*Quick check*: Compare q_ψ predictions against finite-difference estimates of the covariation on out-of-sample trajectories.

**Off-policy learning**  
*Why needed*: Avoids costly re-simulation; leverages existing pretrained trajectories.  
*Quick check*: Ensure the empirical distribution of (t,Y_t) in training data matches that of the pretrained SDE (e.g., via marginal statistics).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Pretrained diffusion model (forward SDE, score network) → generates trajectories → h_ϕ network (martingale loss) → (CDG-ML) guided SDE; or h_ϕ → q_ψ network (covariation loss) → (CDG-MCL) guided SDE.

**Critical path**: (1) Generate a large corpus of trajectories from the pretrained SDE. (2) Train h_ϕ to minimize martingale loss. (3) For CDG-MCL, freeze h_ϕ and train q_ψ to predict the covariation term along the same trajectories. (4) Simulate the guided SDE either with drift g^2 ∇log h_ϕ (CDG-ML) or g^2 (q_ψ/h_ϕ) (CDG-MCL).

**Design tradeoffs**: CDG-ML is simpler and more stable but may suffer from high variance in the gradient of h_ϕ; CDG-MCL provides a direct estimate of the gradient ∇h, potentially reducing bias, but introduces additional estimation error via the noisy covariation term and is more sensitive to errors in h_ϕ. Larger networks improve approximation but increase overfitting risk and computational cost. The choice of time discretization for the guided SDE also trades off accuracy and simulation speed.

**Failure signatures**: 
- h_ϕ producing values outside [0,1] or rapidly changing signs → constraint violation or numerical instability.
- High martingale loss on validation data → failure to learn correct h.
- Guided samples not satisfying the constraint → drift correction is inaccurate (e.g., due to poor h_ϕ or q_ψ).
- Large discrepancy between empirical and theoretical TV/W2 → guidance scale η mis-scaled or insufficient model capacity.

**First experiments**:
1. **Synthetic Gaussian half-space**: Use a VE SDE with a standard normal data distribution and constraint {x₁>0}. Train CDG-ML and CDG-MCL with a 2-layer MLP (64 units, SiLU). With 50k pretrained samples, enforce 100% constraint satisfaction and achieve KS ≤0.07 on the marginal of x₁.
2. **Martingale property check**: On held-out pretrained trajectories, compute the drift of the process h_ϕ(t,Y_t) via finite differences. The average drift should be close to zero (e.g., <1e-3). Also ensure h_ϕ remains in [0,1] throughout.
3. **Covariation estimator variance analysis**: For CDG-MCL, estimate d[h_ϕ*,Y]_t/dt using high-resolution steps (Δt=1e-4) and compute the standard deviation of this estimator across trajectories. If the standard deviation exceeds 0.1, the estimator is too noisy, indicating the need for bias correction or longer trajectories.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Approximation guarantees for on-policy control methods**: Can we derive non-asymptotic error bounds (e.g., TV, Wasserstein) for conditional diffusion methods that learn on-policy? *Status*: No such theory exists; needed to understand convergence and accuracy.
2. **Convergence rate of quadratic variation term**: What is the explicit rate at which d[hϕ,Y]_t/dt converges to d[h,Y]_t/dt under neural network approximation of h? *Status*: Considered challenging; existing results (e.g., Jacod–Shiryaev) do not yield explicit rates in high dimensions.
3. **Sample complexity of covariation estimation**: What is the finite-sample error when estimating d[hϕ,Y]_t/dt from a finite set of pretrained trajectories? *Status*: Ignored in current analysis but can be significant, especially for rare events.
4. **Theoretical selection of guidance scale η**: How to choose η to optimally balance constraint satisfaction and sample diversity? *Status*: Heuristic tuning; lacking a principled trade‑off derived from theory (e.g., via Girsanov’s theorem).

## Limitations
- Critical architectural and hyperparameter details for h_ϕ and q_ψ are omitted, preventing exact replication of the experiments.
- The procedure for estimating the covariation term in CDG-MCL is not fully specified, which may lead to implementation-dependent biases.
- Experimental pipelines for the finance and supply-chain case studies (constraint definition, preprocessing, hyperparameters) are insufficiently described, making those results hard to verify.
- Theoretical bounds rely on assumptions (e.g., bounded score error, smoothness of h) that are not empirically validated.

## Confidence
- Method correctness (Doob h-transform implementation): Medium
- Non-asymptotic error bounds: Medium
- Empirical effectiveness (synthetic, finance, supply-chain): Low
