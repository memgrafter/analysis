# PRAC: Principal-Random Subspace for LLM Activation Compression and Memory-Efficient Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23111
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23111
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PRAC reduces activation memory by up to 36% with negligible performance drop and enables 25% training speedup via larger batches.

## Executive Summary
Activation memory dominates the memory footprint during large-batch LLM training, limiting batch size and thus training throughput. Existing compression methods either introduce bias (by discarding tail singular values) or suffer high variance (by using random projections alone). PRAC addresses this by decomposing activations into a principal subspace (capturing dominant singular vectors via SVD) and a random subspace sampled uniformly from the orthogonal complement. A precise scaling factor, derived from the subspace dimensions, guarantees an unbiased reconstruction. Under the Activation Degenerate Condition—which assumes a few large singular values and a bounded tail—PRAC achieves the minimum possible gradient variance among unbiased linear compressors. Practical implementations incorporate lazy subspace updates (reducing overhead), sharing subspaces across layers, and layer-wise rank allocation. Extensive experiments on pre-training LLaMA models up to 1B parameters and fine-tuning RoBERTa demonstrate substantial memory savings (up to 38%) and speedups (25%) with negligible accuracy loss. Combining PRAC with optimizers like Muon yields further memory reductions (~30%), highlighting its orthogonality and synergistic potential.

## Method Summary
PRAC compresses activation tensors by projecting them onto a hybrid subspace. Given an activation matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×d} (n tokens, d features), it computes a principal subspace Q₁ ∈ ℝ^{d×r₁} containing the top r₁ right singular vectors (via SVD) and a random subspace Q₂ ∈ ℝ^{d×r₂} sampled uniformly from the orthogonal complement of Q₁. The compressed representation stores the low-dimensional projections XQ₁ and k·XQ₂, where the scaling factor k = (n - r₁)/r₂ ensures that the reconstructed matrix ˜X = (XQ₁)Q₁ᵀ + (k·XQ₂)Q₂ᵀ is unbiased (E[˜X] = X). To amortize the computational cost of SVD and random subspace generation, PRAC updates the subspaces lazily: Q₁ every T₁ steps and Q₂ every T₂ steps. Additionally, it employs layer-wise rank allocation (higher ranks for linear layers, lower for normalization/non-linearities) and shares the same Q₁ and Q₂ across multiple projections (e.g., Q/K/V) within a layer. These design choices maintain the theoretical benefits while reducing overhead, making PRAC practical for large-scale training.

## Key Results
- Pre-training LLaMA models (up to 1B parameters) with PRAC achieves up to 36% reduction in total memory usage with negligible increase in perplexity.
- The memory savings enable a 25% training speedup by allowing larger batch sizes.
- When combined with optimizers like Muon, PRAC yields an additional ~30% memory saving; fine-tuning RoBERTa on GLUE with rank 4 reduces activation memory by 38% and matches or exceeds full fine-tuning performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Unbiased, Low-Variance Gradient Estimation is Necessary for Convergence
- **Claim:** Activation compression must produce an *unbiased* gradient estimator with *low variance* to guarantee fast stochastic optimization convergence.
- **Mechanism:** Training is framed as stochastic optimization. A constant bias in the gradient estimator (e.g., from principal-only projection) causes certain parameter dimensions to never be updated (Proposition 1), while high variance (e.g., from random-only projection) slows convergence (Proposition 2). Thus, effective compression requires both unbiasedness and minimal variance.
- **Core assumption:** Standard SGD assumptions (L-smoothness, bounded objective) hold; compression error directly bounds gradient variance (Lemma 3).
- **Evidence anchors:** Proposition 1 and 2 in Section 3.1; the paper contrasts PAC and RAC and shows PRAC satisfies both criteria.
- **Break condition:** If the activation spectral structure violates the (s,q)-degenerate condition (Assumption 4), the minimum variance guarantee may not hold.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Principal-Random Subspace Exploits Spectral Structure
- **Claim:** Projecting activations onto a *principal subspace* (top singular vectors) plus a *random subspace* (orthogonal complement) optimally balances bias and variance under the Activation Degenerate Condition.
- **Mechanism:** Activations exhibit a low-rank spectral structure: a few large singular values (principal) and a bounded, roughly uniform tail. Principal-only compression (PAC) has low variance but discards the tail (bias). Random-only compression (RAC) is unbiased but has high variance because random projections poorly capture dominant directions. PRAC uses SVD for Q₁ (exact capture of principal component) and samples Q₂ uniformly from Q₁⊥ (minimum variance for the tail under the degenerate condition). Theorem 7 proves that under the condition, PRAC achieves the lower bound variance.
- **Core assumption:** Activations satisfy (s,q)-D: only top s singular values are large; ∑_{i>s} σᵢ² ≤ q.
- **Evidence anchors:** Figure 2 shows singular value spectra of LLaMA activations; Section 4.2 contrasts PAC and RAC; Theorem 7.
- **Break condition:** If the tail singular values are highly non-uniform, a tailored distribution might achieve lower variance than PRAC's uniform random tail.

### Mechanism 3: Precise Scaling Factor Ensures Unbiasedness
- **Claim:** Scaling the random subspace component by **k = (n - r₁) / r₂** exactly corrects the expectation of the random projection, yielding an unbiased overall reconstruction.
- **Mechanism:** The principal component reconstruction is unbiased: E[XQ₁Q₁ᵀ] = X_proj_principal. For the random component, if unscaled, E[XQ₂Q₂ᵀ] = (r₂/(n−r₁)) * X_tail (Lemma 12). Multiplying Q₂ by √k scales this expectation to (k * r₂/(n−r₁)) * X_tail = X_tail. Thus, the sum ˜X = XQ₁Q₁ᵀ + (√k·XQ₂)(√k·Q₂)ᵀ is unbiased. This scaling is critical; without it, RAC is biased; with it, PRAC becomes unbiased while retaining minimum variance.
- **Core assumption:** Q₂ is sampled uniformly from the Stiefel manifold within the orthogonal complement of Q₁, ensuring E[Q₂Q₂ᵀ] ∝ I_{n−r₁} (Lemma 12).
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 6 derives k; Theorem 7 uses it for the variance bound; Figure 7 ablation shows deviating from k₀ harms convergence.
- **Break condition:** The derivation assumes X is fixed per step. If activation statistics drift rapidly between subspace updates, the expectation over the sampling period may deviate, introducing small bias.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1. Subspace Extractor: Computes top-r₁ right singular vectors Q₁ of activation X via SVD; samples random matrix S ~ N(0,I), projects onto Q₁⊥ via (I−Q₁Q₁ᵀ)S, QR-decomposes to get Q₂.
2. Scaler: Applies factor √k to Q₂, with k=(n−r₁)/r₂.
3. Compressor/Decompressor: Forward: cache (XQ₁, k·XQ₂). Backward: reconstruct ˜X = (XQ₁)Q₁ᵀ + (k·XQ₂)Q₂ᵀ.
4. Lazy Scheduler: Updates Q₁ every T₁ steps, Q₂ every T₂ steps (Algorithm 1).
5. Layer-Wise Policy: Assigns higher ranks (⌊0.3n⌋) to linear layers (MLP, attn projections), lower (⌊0.2n⌋) to non-linear layers (RMSNorm, GeLU); shares Q₁,Q₂ across Q/K/V projections in same layer.

**Critical path:**
1. Forward pass of a PRAC-targeted layer (e.g., GeLU down-projection).
2. Call `Compress(X, r₁, r₂, T₁, T₂, t)` → returns cached (Q₁, Q₂, XQ₁, kXQ₂).
3. Store only low-dimensional projections (memory saving).
4. Backward pass receives ∇_Y L.
5. Reconstruct ˜X from cache.
6. Compute ∇_W L = ˜Xᵀ ∇_Y L and ∇_X L = W ∇_Y L (normal autograd using ˜X).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Ranks (r₁, r₂):** Higher ranks improve accuracy but reduce memory savings and increase SVD/QR cost. r₁ should capture the "large" singular values (s from Assumption 4). Total rank r₁+r₂ typically ≤0.6n.
- **Update Intervals (T₁, T₂):** Larger T reduces amortized cost but risks stale subspaces if activation spectra change quickly. Paper uses T₁=T₂=500; warm-up may need more frequent updates.
- **Subspace Sharing:** Sharing Q₁,Q₂ across Q/K/V reduces memory and stabilizes gradients but prevents layer-specific adaptation.
- **Layer-Wise Allocation:** Assigning higher rank to linear layers is empirical; theory only covers linear layers. Over-compressing non-linear layers might destabilize normalization statistics.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Divergence or NaNs:** Check if scaling factor k is correctly applied to Q₂ (and thus to XQ₂). Wrong k breaks unbiasedness.
- **Slow initial convergence:** If random subspace Q₂ is stale or r₂ too low, early tail approximation is poor. Verify update intervals not too large during warm-up.
- **Plateau in later training:** If principal subspace Q₁ is stale and r₁ too small, dominant information is lost. Check SVD update frequency.
- **Memory not reduced as expected:** Verify ranks are applied to all targeted linear/non-linear layers. Check that Flash Attention ops are excluded.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Unbiasedness Check:** For a small linear layer, compute empirical mean of reconstruction error E[˜X−X] over many random seeds for a fixed X. Should be near zero (test with correct k, wrong k=0.5k₀ shows bias).
2. **Scaling Factor Ablation:** Pre-train LLaMA-130M with k ∈ {k₀, 0.5k₀, 0.2k₀, 1.2k₀} on linear and norm layers (replicate Figure 7). Expect k₀ to converge fastest/lowest loss.
3. **Memory-Speed Tradeoff:** Run LLaMA-1B with batch size 64 (baseline may OOM). Measure peak memory and tokens/second. Then increase batch size to 96 for PRAC (as in Table 4) to confirm speedup from memory headroom.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to non-linear operations:** Can PRAC be extended to compress activations in non-linear operations such as Flash Attention, which currently bypass compression due to structural constraints? (The paper excludes Flash Attention; adapting PRAC to such coupled operations is open.)
2. **Universality of Activation Degenerate Condition:** How universally does the (s,q)-degenerate condition hold across different architectures (transformers vs. CNNs), layers (early vs. late), and training stages? The condition is verified only on a single layer of LLaMA-130M at 10% training; its generality is unknown.
3. **Adaptive lazy update schedule:** Can the fixed update intervals (T₁, T₂) be improved by an adaptive strategy that detects when the activation subspace changes significantly, reducing unnecessary recomputation without sacrificing convergence?
4. **Combination with other memory-efficient techniques:** To what extent can PRAC be combined with activation checkpointing, gradient compression, or optimizer state compression before encountering diminishing returns or numerical instability? Interactions may cause bias amplification or variance increase.

## Limitations
- Scalability beyond 1B-parameter models not experimentally validated; overhead of SVD/QR at massive scale is uncertain.
- Theoretical guarantees assume linear layers and a static spectral structure; compression of non-linear layers is heuristic and not covered by the optimality proof.
- The (s,q)-degenerate condition, under which PRAC achieves minimum variance, may not hold universally; violations could degrade performance.

## Confidence
- **High:** Theoretical optimality (Theorem 7) and unbiasedness proof under exact assumptions.
- **Medium:** Empirical validation on small models (≤1B) for memory/accuracy trade-offs.
- **Low:** Generalization to decoder-only >10B models and claimed ~30% saving when combined with optimizers like Muon (less thorough evaluation).
