# ALERT: Zero-shot LLM Jailbreak Detection via Internal Discrepancy Amplification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03600
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03600
- **Reference Count:** 32
- **Primary Result:** Achieves >90% accuracy and F1-score across multiple datasets and models, outperforming the strongest baseline by at least 10% (up to 40%) in zero-shot jailbreak detection.

## Executive Summary
Zero-shot jailbreak detection—identifying malicious prompts without prior exposure to attack templates—remains a critical unsolved challenge for LLM safety. ALERT introduces a method that amplifies safety-relevant internal discrepancies across three granularities: layer-wise (selecting the 4th layer for maximal divergence), module-wise (using decoupled gating and context features from feed-forward networks), and token-wise (weighting tokens by proximity to benign/harmful prototypes). Two lightweight Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) classifiers are trained on these amplified representations. Evaluated on three datasets against three attacks on three LLMs, ALERT consistently ranks top-2, demonstrating strong generalization while adhering to practical constraints of efficiency and innocuousness.

## Method Summary
ALERT’s training uses only benign and harmful (non-jailbreak) prompts. For each prompt, it extracts token-level gating and context features from the 4th layer of a target LLM. Prototype vectors for benign and harmful classes are computed by averaging these features over the training set. Each token is then weighted by a softmax of its negative L2 distance to the prototypes, and a weighted average produces a prompt-level amplified feature vector. Two separate VIB classifiers (one for gating, one for context features) are trained on these vectors. During zero-shot evaluation on test prompts (including jailbreak attacks), the same training prototypes are used to weight tokens and compute amplified features. The two classifier outputs are averaged to produce the final label.

## Key Results
- Achieves >90% accuracy and F1-score on AdvBench, XSTest, and StrongREJECT benchmarks.
- Outperforms the strongest baseline by an absolute improvement of 10–40% in both accuracy and F1.
- Consistently ranks among the top-2 methods across all evaluated LLMs (Llama-3, Mistral, Vicuna) and jailbreak attacks (AutoDAN, Adaptive, CodeChameleon).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ALERT works by systematically amplifying the subtle internal discrepancies between benign and jailbreak prompts. First, layer-wise analysis identifies shallow layers (specifically the 4th) where the distributional divergence is maximized, capturing early safety-relevant signals before they might be obscured by later layers. Second, module-wise extraction targets gating and context features within the FFN, which decouple contextual information from transformation effects, preserving stronger zero-shot discriminative power than standard hidden states. Third, token-wise weighting down-weights noisy or manipulative tokens common in jailbreak templates by favoring tokens closer to the benign prototype and penalizing those near the harmful prototype. This multi-stage amplification yields representations where benign and jailbreak prompts are more separable for the final classifiers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Zero-Shot Jailbreak Detection:** The task of detecting malicious prompts without any examples of specific jailbreak attacks during training. *Why needed:* Ensures adaptability to novel, unseen attacks. *Quick check:* Verify the training set contains only benign and harmful (non-jailbreak) prompts.
- **Gated Feed-Forward Networks (FFN):** LLM components (e.g., in Llama, Mistral) that use a gating mechanism (e.g., `gate_proj`, `up_proj`) to modulate information flow. *Why needed:* Their decoupled gating and context features retain cleaner safety signals. *Quick check:* Confirm extraction from the correct sub-projections (not the final FFN output).
- **Prototype Vectors:** Class-level averages of token features computed from the training set. *Why needed:* Provide a reference distribution for weighting token relevance. *Quick check:* Ensure prototypes are computed only from training data, not test data.
- **Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB):** A classifier that learns representations by maximizing information about the label while minimizing information about the input, acting as a regularizer. *Why needed:* Improves generalization in zero-shot settings. *Quick check:* Check classifier architecture (typically two hidden layers) and β hyperparameter tuning.
- **KL Divergence Estimation:** Used in layer-wise analysis to quantify distributional differences between benign and jailbreak prompts at each layer. *Why needed:* Identifies the optimal layer for feature extraction (the 4th). *Quick check:* Replicate Figure 3: divergence should peak at shallow layers.
- **Token-Level Feature Aggregation:** Pooling token features into a single prompt representation. *Why needed:* Creates a fixed-size input for the classifier. *Quick check:* Verify weighted average (Eq.5) uses token weights from Eq.6.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Prompt -> Extract Token Features (4th Layer: Gating & Context) -> Compute Token Weights (using Benign/Harmful Prototypes) -> Weighted Aggregation -> Amplified Prompt Feature -> VIB Classifier (Gating) & VIB Classifier (Context) -> Average Outputs -> Binary Label

**Critical Path:**  
Feature extraction (4th layer, correct sub-modules) → Token weighting (prototype-based) → VIB classification (two separate models) → Output averaging.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Layer Depth:* Shallow layers (4th) capture early, more universal safety signals but may lack high-level semantics; deeper layers might be more task-specific but noisier for zero-shot detection.
- *Feature Type:* Gating/context features decouple transformation from context, but require precise extraction; hidden states are easier to access but contain entangled information.
- *Prototype Dependence:* Token weighting relies on stable prototypes; if training distribution is narrow, weighting may fail on diverse test prompts.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low accuracy/F1 on jailbreak prompts: likely incorrect layer or feature extraction, unstable prototypes, or misconfigured token weighting.
- High false positive rate on benign prompts: prototypes poorly represent natural benign distribution, or weighting over-penalizes common benign tokens.
- Poor cross-model generalizability: feature extraction method is model-specific; failure to adapt for different architectures.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Feature Extraction Validation:** On a fixed set of 10 prompts, extract all sub-component outputs from the 4th layer of Llama-3-8B. Confirm shapes and value ranges for gating (e.g., `gate_proj` output) and context (e.g., `up_proj` output) features match expectations (e.g., dimensionality, activation patterns).
2. **Prototype Stability Analysis:** Train ALERT with 5 different random training seeds. Compute cosine similarity between resulting `vf_B` and `vf_H` prototypes across seeds. Report mean similarity; values below 0.9 indicate instability that could degrade performance.
3. **False Positive Rate on Natural Queries:** Evaluate a trained detector on 500 naturally occurring, non-adversarial user queries (e.g., from OpenAssistant). Compute false positive rate. A rate significantly above 5–10% would challenge the innocuousness claim.

## Limitations
- **Benign Prompt Generation Pipeline:** Relies on GPT-5 to generate benign counterparts for harmful prompts, which is not publicly accessible; substitutions (e.g., GPT-4) may alter distributions and affect prototype quality.
- **Feature Extraction Specificity:** Exact implementation details for extracting "gating" and "context" features from various LLM architectures (e.g., which projections correspond to each) are not fully elaborated, risking misimplementation.
- **Innocuousness Operationalization:** The claim of "no prompt modification" is clear, but quantitative analysis of false positive rates on diverse, natural benign queries is lacking; usability under real-world traffic is unknown.
- **Efficiency Metrics:** "Lightweight" and "single-pass" are qualitative; actual latency, memory overhead, and FLOPs compared to simpler baselines (e.g., linear probe on hidden states) are unreported.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The β parameter for VIB classifiers is tuned via Optuna on an unspecified validation set; its sensitivity under zero-shot conditions and the temperature in token-weighting softmax are unreported.

## Confidence
- **Core Methodological Claims (Layer/Module/Token amplification):** High Confidence. Algorithmic steps are mathematically specified, and layer selection is empirically justified.
- **Superior Zero-Shot Performance (SOTA beating by ≥10%):** Medium Confidence. Strong cross-dataset/model/attack evidence exists, but reproducibility hinges on unverified benign prompt generation and feature extraction details.
- **Generalizability to Unseen Attacks & Models:** Medium Confidence. Supported by cross-evaluation, but contingent on quality of training benign/harmful distributions.
- **Efficiency & Early Detection:** Low Confidence. Lacks quantitative benchmarks (latency, FLOPs, parameter counts).
- **Innocuousness (Low False Positive Rate):** Low Confidence. No rigorous analysis on natural, diverse benign queries; false positive rates on realistic data are unknown.
