# SENSE: Efficient EEG-to-Text via Privacy-Preserving Semantic Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17109
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17109
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Achieves ROUGE-1 of 31.5% using Focal Loss and Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, outperforming the fine-tuned Thought2Text baseline (30.0%) while eliminating LLM fine-tuning and keeping raw EEG local.

## Executive Summary
SENSE tackles the challenge of decoding EEG signals into natural language text. Traditional methods require fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on raw neural data, which is computationally expensive and poses privacy risks. SENSE introduces a decoupled two-stage framework: a lightweight on-device module maps EEG to a discrete Bag-of-Words (BoW) by aligning neural embeddings with a CLIP-text space, and this BoW then conditions a frozen off-the-shelf LLM via zero-shot prompting. Evaluated on a 128-channel EEG dataset across six subjects, SENSE achieves comparable or superior generative quality to fully fine-tuned baselines while substantially reducing computational overhead and ensuring raw neural signals remain on the device.

## Method Summary
SENSE operates in two disjoint stages. **Stage 1 (on-device)** uses a frozen pretrained ChannelNet encoder to convert multi-channel EEG into a 512-dimensional vector. A trainable "Similarity Refiner" (a small MLP) transforms this vector into a latent `z`. This `z` is compared via cosine similarity against a fixed vocabulary matrix of CLIP text embeddings (1210 words). The top-k (k=15) most similar words form a Bag-of-Words (BoW). The system also predicts an object label from the EEG. **Stage 2 (off-device)** constructs a structured prompt containing the object label, confidence, and BoW tokens with scores. This prompt is fed to a frozen, off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., Gemini, GPT-4o-mini) in a zero-shot manner to generate a fluent caption. Only the ~1M-parameter Similarity Refiner is trained, using losses like Focal Loss to handle extreme label sparsity (~0.4% positives).

## Key Results
- SENSE (Focal Loss + Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite) achieves a **ROUGE-1 of 31.5%**, surpassing the fine-tuned Thought2Text (LLaMA-3-8B) baseline at 30.0%.
- The framework eliminates the need for LLM fine-tuning, drastically reducing computational cost and memory footprint.
- The system demonstrates **robust cross-subject generalization** without requiring per-subject calibration, as evaluated on data from six subjects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Privacy-Preserving Decoupling
**Claim:** Separating neural decoding (on-device) from language generation (off-the-shelf LLM) prevents raw EEG exposure and eliminates LLM fine-tuning costs.
**Mechanism:** A lightweight on-device module maps EEG to a discrete BoW. This BoW, not raw signals, conditions a frozen LLM via zero-shot prompting. The LLM generates text without gradient updates.
**Core assumption:** A sparse, high-level semantic summary (BoW) extracted from EEG is sufficient to guide a pre-trained LLM, and this abstraction neutralizes raw signal privacy risks.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...ensuring raw neural signals remain local while only abstract semantic cues interact with language models."; [section 3.4] "This modular design ensures that raw neural data remain local..."; Neighbors (SEE, ETS, Thought2Text) require fine-tuning on EEG, highlighting SENSE's distinct approach.

### Mechanism 2: CLIP-Space Alignment for Semantic Retrieval
**Claim:** Projecting EEG embeddings into the frozen CLIP text embedding space enables effective, vocabulary-constrained keyword retrieval.
**Mechanism:** A "Similarity Refiner" transforms EEG features `x` into a refined latent `z`. `z` is compared against a fixed vocabulary's CLIP text embeddings `E`. Top-k cosine similarities yield the BoW, framing decoding as retrieval.
**Core assumption:** The CLIP text space provides a shared, semantically structured manifold where perceptually relevant words are geometrically accessible from aligned EEG features.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...maps EEG to a discrete Bag-of-Words (BoW) by aligning neural embeddings with a CLIP-text space..."; [section 3.2] "We enforce semantic alignment between z and the frozen Vocabulary Matrix E using cosine similarity."; [section 5.1] t-SNE shows Focal Loss latents form "distinct semantic clusters" in CLIP space.

### Mechanism 3: Focal Loss for Extreme Multi-Label Sparsity
**Claim:** Using Focal Loss (γ=2.0, α=0.25) effectively handles the ~99.6% negative-class imbalance in the 1210-token vocabulary target space.
**Mechanism:** The loss down-weights well-classified easy negatives and focuses learning on rare positives and hard examples, preventing collapse to a trivial "predict all zeros" solution.
**Core assumption:** Standard losses (BCE) are dominated by the negative class; modulating loss weight based on prediction confidence is necessary for a meaningful decision boundary.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...using Focal Loss ... it attains a ROUGE-1 score of 31.5%..."; [section 3.3] Describes applying Focal Loss to "mitigate the overwhelming background of absent concepts."; [section 5.1 & Figure 3] t-SNE shows Focal Loss produces "more defined structure" versus BCE/Contrastive.

- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (Cross-Modal Alignment)**
  - **Why needed:** The core task is aligning EEG-derived vectors with CLIP text embeddings in a shared space. Understanding InfoNCE-style losses is key to grasping geometric similarity enforcement.

- **Concept: Extreme Multi-Label Classification**
  - **Why needed:** The output space is 1210-dimensional binary with ~0.4% positives. Standard classification assumptions fail; principles for handling massive negative spaces are fundamental.
  - **Quick check:** Why is standard accuracy meaningless for this target vector, and what does the model's "degenerate solution" predict?

- **Concept: Zero-Shot Prompting & In-Context Learning**
  - **Why needed:** The final stage relies entirely on an LLM's ability to generate from structured, noisy cues without gradient-based adaptation. Understanding prompt engineering for grounding and noise suppression is critical.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input (EEG) -> ChannelNet (x) -> Similarity Refiner (z) -> Vocabulary Retriever (logits ŷ) -> Token Selector (BoW) -> Prompt Builder -> LLM -> Caption

**Critical path:** `EEG → ChannelNet(x) → Refiner(z) → (z·Eᵀ)=logits(ŷ) → top-15(BoW) → Prompt → LLM → Caption`. Only the `Refiner` is trained.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **BoW Sparsity (k=15) vs. Semantic Coverage:** Small k filters noise but risks missing concepts; large k adds noise. Fixed k is a simplicity tradeoff vs. adaptive thresholding.
- **On-Device (Refiner) vs. External (LLM):** Full privacy/compute on-device for Stage 1, but final quality depends on third-party LLM capability and prompt fidelity.
- **Fixed Vocabulary (1210 words) vs. Open Vocabulary:** Vocabulary limits lexical diversity but makes retrieval tractable. Open vocab would require a different architecture (e.g., generative tokenization).

**Failure signatures:**
- **Primary:** `o_pred` is incorrect (e.g., "mushroom" for "flower"). This anchors the LLM, causing hallucination toward the wrong object class.
- **Secondary:** BoW contains only low-similarity or nonsensical tokens. LLM ignores weak cues and generates a generic, ungrounded description based on its prior.
- **Tertiary:** Consistent poor performance on specific subjects, indicating the subject-agnostic `Refiner` fails to generalize to that neural phenotype.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Validation:** Implement the "Naive Baseline" (`x·Eᵀ` without Refiner). Expect poor performance (ROUGE-1 ~25.8%) confirming the Refiner's necessity.
2. **Component Ablation:** Compare `WITHOBJ` vs `WITHOUTOBJ` prompts. Expect significant drop (e.g., Gemini Focal: 31.5% → 30.5% ROUGE-1), proving the object label's anchoring value.
3. **Loss Function Comparison:** Train three `Refiner` models with BCE, Contrastive ML, and Focal Loss. Validate on test set. Expect Focal Loss to achieve best t-SNE separation and highest NLG metrics, confirming its role in managing sparsity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Dynamic thresholding:** How can dynamic confidence thresholding replace static top‑k selection in Bag‑of‑Words extraction to improve semantic retrieval?
- **Syntactic priors:** How can syntactic priors be incorporated during concept selection to improve the grammatical coherence of generated captions?
- **Open vocabulary:** How can the fixed 1210‑word vocabulary be expanded to support open‑vocabulary EEG‑to‑text decoding without sacrificing accuracy?
- **Visual context:** How can cross‑modal alignment with visual context (i.e., the viewed image) be strengthened to improve EEG‑to‑text retrieval?

## Limitations
- The exact weights and preprocessing for the ChannelNet encoder (from Mishra et al., 2025) are unreported, blocking precise replication.
- The fixed 1210-word vocabulary limits lexical diversity and may miss domain-specific terms; CLIP alignment for rare/unseen concepts is unvalidated.
- Performance is heavily reliant on off-the-shelf LLM prompt fidelity; zero-shot results may vary across providers without robustness guarantees.
- Cross-subject generalization claims are based on only six subjects from one dataset; neural phenotype diversity may limit real-world applicability.

## Confidence
- **Privacy-preserving decoupling:** High (architecture explicitly isolates raw EEG; no raw data leaves device).
- **CLIP-space retrieval efficacy:** Medium (t-SNE clusters support alignment, but CLIP's semantic coverage for EEG-derived concepts not fully stress-tested).
- **Focal Loss for extreme sparsity:** High (clear ablation gains and visual separation evidence).
- **Calibration-free cross-subject decoding:** Medium (6-subject evaluation positive but lacks external validation; subject variability risks understated).
