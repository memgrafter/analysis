# Sabiá-4 Technical Report

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10213
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10213
- **Reference Count:** 30
- **Primary Result:** Sabiá-4 and Sabiazinho-4 achieve state-of-the-art cost-performance for Portuguese LLMs, especially in Brazilian legal tasks, via a four-stage pipeline (continued pre-training, long-context extension, multi-domain SFT, alignment).

## Executive Summary
We developed Sabiá-4 and Sabiázinho-4, high-performance Portuguese language models optimized for Brazilian legal applications. Through a four-stage training pipeline—continued pre-training on Portuguese and Brazilian legal corpora, long-context extension to 128K tokens, broad supervised fine-tuning across chat/code/legal/function calling, and preference alignment—the models achieve superior results on six benchmark categories. Sabiá-4 shows marked improvements over Sabiá-3.1 across legal drafting, Brazilian law knowledge, agentic tasks, exams, instruction following, and conversation. Sabiázinho-4 offers comparable performance at lower cost, making it efficient for production.

## Method Summary
The training pipeline starts from a general-purpose base model (architecture and size undisclosed). First, continued pre-training adapts the model to Portuguese and Brazilian legal domains using curated corpora. Second, long-context training extends the effective context window to 128K tokens by exposing the model to naturally long Portuguese documents. Third, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) covers multiple domains—chat, code, legal drafting, and function calling—to build broad instruction-following and agentic capabilities. Finally, preference alignment refines outputs for style, nuance, and strict adherence to constraints. This staged approach aims to balance domain specialization, long-context reasoning, multi-domain utility, and output quality.

## Key Results
*   Sabiá-4 substantially outperforms Sabiá-3.1: OAB legal drafting score increases from 7.21 to 7.49, Brazilian laws knowledge from 77.8% to 97.4%, agentic task success from 43.1% to 72.2%, exam accuracy from 82.4% to 86.6%, instruction following (Multi-IF PT) from 80.7% to 82.0%, and conversational win rate (BRACEval) from 44.6% to 53.8%.
*   Sabiázinho-4 provides comparable performance at a fraction of the inference cost, positioning it for cost-sensitive deployment.
*   Models achieve a superior cost-performance trade-off, occupying the upper-left region of pricing-accuracy charts across benchmark categories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Domain Specialization via Continued Pre-training
**Claim:** Continued pre-training on Portuguese and Brazilian legal corpora shifts the model’s internal representations toward domain-specific syntax, terminology, and reasoning without full retraining.
**Mechanism:** Starting from a generalist base, supervised learning on a high-quality, filtered corpus of general Portuguese and Brazilian legal documents adapts linguistic and domain priors while retaining general knowledge.
**Break condition:** Catastrophic forgetting or misalignment from low-quality/non-representative legal data could degrade non-target performance.

### Mechanism 2: Long-Context Extension via Native Documents
**Claim:** Training on naturally long Portuguese documents extends the model’s effective context window to 128K tokens, enabling processing of lengthy legal documents.
**Mechanism:** Exposure to genuinely long-form content trains attention and positional embeddings to maintain coherence and retrieval over extended sequences, rather than relying on architectural interpolation alone.
**Break condition:** Lack of diversity in document structure or topic may limit generalization to novel long-context scenarios or harm short-context performance.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Domain SFT + Preference Alignment
**Claim:** Broad SFT followed by targeted preference alignment produces a model that excels at multi-domain instruction following, function calling, and nuanced output generation.
**Mechanism:** SFT teaches mapping from prompts to structured outputs, tool use, and multi-turn dialogue. Alignment (e.g., DPO) then refines style, nuance, and constraint adherence using ranked response pairs.
**Break condition:** Poor SFT mixture balance could cause capability interference; noisy or sparse preference data may overfit to superficial style at the expense of accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Continued Pre-training (Domain Adaptation)**
  - **Why needed here:** To imbue a generalist model with deep Brazilian Portuguese linguistic patterns and legal domain knowledge without the cost of training from scratch.
  - **Quick check:** What are the risks of "catastrophic forgetting" in continued pre-training, and how might data curation mitigate them?
*   **Concept: Long-Context Training via Native Documents**
  - **Why needed here:** To enable processing and reasoning over lengthy Brazilian legal documents (e.g., full codes, case files) that exceed standard context windows.
  - **Quick check:** Why might training on "naturally long documents" be more effective for 128K context than concatenating shorter documents?
*   **Concept: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Multi-Domain Instruction**
  - **Why needed here:** To transform a knowledgeable model into an assistant that follows diverse prompts (chat, code, legal drafting, tool use) and maintains state in multi-turn interactions.
  - **Quick check:** How does the need for "function calling" data in SFT differ from standard conversational instruction data?
*   **Concept: Preference Alignment**
  - **Why needed here:** To refine outputs beyond correctness, focusing on Brazilian Portuguese stylistic nuance, precise constraint adherence, and reliable formatting—critical for professional legal use.
  - **Quick check:** What is the difference between training a model to "follow instructions" (SFT) and to produce "preferred outputs" (alignment)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Base Model -> Continued Pre-training (Portuguese General + Brazilian Legal) -> Long-Context Extension (Long Portuguese Corpus) -> SFT (Chat, Code, Legal, Function Calling) -> Preference Alignment -> Final Models (Sabiá-4, Sabiázinho-4)

**Critical path:**
Base Model → Continued Pre-training → Long-Context Extension → SFT → Preference Alignment

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Cost vs. Capability:** Sabiázinho-4 trades some absolute accuracy for inference efficiency (distillation/size reduction).
*   **Breadth vs. Depth in SFT:** Including code and general chat alongside legal tasks risks diluting specialization but is necessary for a generalist Portuguese model; mixing ratios manage this.
*   **Alignment Scope:** Focusing on style and fine details improves benchmarks like Multi-IF but requires high-quality, nuanced preference data.

**Failure signatures:**
*   Catastrophic forgetting: Sudden drop in general knowledge (exam scores) or conversational ability after pre-training/SFT.
*   Long-context degradation: Performance collapse on MRCR beyond 32K tokens or increased perplexity at long contexts.
*   Instruction misgeneralization: High single-turn scores but failure on Multi-IF (accumulating constraints), indicating poor stateful constraint management.
*   Agentic failure: Low `Success@1` on CLIMB/MARCA/Ticket-Bench, suggesting insufficient function-calling SFT data or inability to chain tool calls.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  Track training/validation perplexity on held-out sets from each pre-training corpus (general Portuguese, legal, long documents) to confirm learning without overfitting.
2.  Evaluate model checkpoints after pre-training, after long-context training, and after full pipeline on MRCR at 128K to isolate the contribution of explicit long-context training.
3.  Train minimal models with SFT data subsets (Legal-only, Chat-only, Mixed) and evaluate on OAB-Bench (legal) vs. BRACEval (chat) to diagnose capability interference and identify optimal mixing ratios.

## Limitations
*   **Undisclosed Base Model & Scale:** Exact architecture and parameter count of the initial model are not specified, hindering scaling analysis and precise performance attribution.
*   **Missing Data & Hyperparameter Details:** Specific corpus compositions (sizes, sources, filtering), SFT mixture ratios, learning rates, alignment algorithm (DPO vs. PPO), and context extension technique are not provided.
*   **Evaluation Integrity Risks:** Some benchmarks are noted as "will be published soon," and prompt templates/sampling parameters are not standardized, risking benchmark misalignment.

## Confidence
*   **Pipeline Structure & Mechanism:** High. Clearly articulated in abstract and methodology.
*   **Metric Improvements vs. Sabiá-3.1:** High. Specific numerical gains on defined benchmarks are reported.
*   **Superior Cost-Performance Trade-off:** Medium. Central claim but lacks explicit pricing/inference cost comparisons.
*   **"Naturally Long Documents" Efficacy:** Medium. Mechanism stated and MRCR results supportive, but correlation without controlled ablation is indirect.
