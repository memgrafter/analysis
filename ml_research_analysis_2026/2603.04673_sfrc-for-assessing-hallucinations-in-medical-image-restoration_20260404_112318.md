# sFRC for assessing hallucinations in medical image restoration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04673
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04673
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** sFRC is a patch-based Fourier Ring Correlation method that successfully detects imperceptible hallucinations in DL-restored medical images by analyzing mid-frequency discrepancies, outperforming standard metrics like PSNR/SSIM.

## Executive Summary
Deep learning (DL) models for medical image restoration can generate highly realistic but anatomically false structures, termed hallucinations, which are often undetectable by conventional quality metrics or human readers. This paper introduces scanning Fourier Ring Correlation (sFRC), an objective, automatic method to quantify and localize such hallucinations. sFRC compares a DL-restored image to a reference (e.g., a fully-sampled reconstruction) on a patch-by-patch basis using Fourier Ring Correlation, focusing on mid-spatial-frequency bands where hallucinations typically manifest. The method sets a hallucination threshold either through expert annotation of known false structures or via imaging theory (e.g., based on undersampling rates). Validated across CT super-resolution, CT sparse-view, and MRI subsampled restoration tasks, sFRC reveals hallucination patterns that standard metrics miss, providing a crucial tool for safety evaluation of AI-based medical imaging.

## Method Summary
sFRC is a patch-based analysis framework. It scans a DL-restored image and its reference with a fixed-size window. For each aligned patch pair, it computes the Fourier Ring Correlation (FRC) curve—the correlation coefficient averaged over concentric rings in frequency space. A key parameter, `x_ct`, is defined as the spatial frequency where the FRC curve drops below a preset threshold `Y`. Hallucinated patches exhibit a lower `x_ct` (i.e., correlation degrades at lower frequencies) compared to faithfully restored patches. A hallucination threshold `x_ht` is established during a tuning phase using a small set of patches with known hallucinations (via expert masks or theory-generated maps). In the application phase, any patch with `x_ct` ≤ `x_ht` is flagged as hallucinated. The overall hallucination rate is the proportion of flagged patches.

## Key Results
- sFRC detected significantly higher hallucination rates in out-of-distribution data and with increased undersampling, even when PSNR/SSIM indicated high fidelity (e.g., SRGAN: 0.065 vs. 0.037 on sharp vs. smooth tests; U-Net 3x MRI: 0.219 vs. iFFT 1x at 0.010).
- The method’s localized hallucination maps aligned qualitatively with independent, theory-based hallucination maps for MRI.
- Whole-image FRC failed to detect hallucinations, demonstrating the necessity of the patch-based approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Mid-Frequency Signature
Hallucinations in DL-restored medical images primarily manifest as structured discrepancies in mid-spatial-frequency bands relative to a reference. Very low frequencies (overall intensity, coarse anatomy) are often preserved, and very high frequencies are noise-dominated. sFRC’s patch-wise FRC analysis isolates correlation across frequency rings. Faithful patches maintain correlation up to a frequency dictated by the information preserved by the acquisition (e.g., the Nyquist limit corresponding to the downsampling factor). Hallucinated patches show a sharp drop in FRC within the mid-frequency band (e.g., between 0.25·r and 0.5·r for 4x CT downsampling), causing their `x_ct` (the frequency where FRC crosses threshold `Y`) to shift left. The hallucination threshold `x_ht` is set to the maximum `x_ct` observed across known hallucinated examples; patches with `x_ct` ≤ `x_ht` are flagged.
*Evidence:* Paper’s focus on mid-frequencies [abstract, section III] and bandpass visualizations (Fig 2). Break condition: global failures or extreme ill-posedness may involve low-frequencies.

### Mechanism 2: Patch-Based Local Analysis
Whole-image metrics (PSNR, SSIM) average errors over the entire image, diluting the signal from small, localized hallucinations. sFRC’s patch-based design ensures that if a hallucination is captured within a patch, its frequency-domain signature is analyzed in isolation, not averaged out by large artifact-free regions. This directly assesses local structural fidelity at a specific scale.
*Evidence:* Paper defines hallucinations as limited to small ROIs [section I]. Full-image FRC fails while patch-based succeeds [Figs. S14, S15]. Break condition: pervasive, uniform hallucinations would not benefit from patching.

### Mechanism 3: Threshold Setting via Prior Knowledge
The hallucination threshold `x_ht` is set objectively using prior knowledge, not statistical heuristics. During tuning, FRC analysis is run on a small set of patches with *a priori* known hallucination status (from expert annotation or imaging theory maps). The maximum `x_ct` across these positive examples defines `x_ht`. This transfers the knowledge of a "discrepancy in recoverable information up to a certain frequency" to new data.
*Evidence:* Abstract and sections IV-B4, IV-C2 describe setting `x_ht` from annotated plaques or theory-based maps. Break condition: unrepresentative tuning data (e.g., tuning on plaque-like artifacts but testing on structural distortions) leads to mis-calibration, as shown by some false negatives (Fig 9).

## Architecture Onboarding

Tuning Module -> Patch Scanning Engine -> Patch-wise FRC Calculator -> Thresholding & Aggregation -> Reporting Module

**Critical Path:** The performance chain is: **Choice of Tuning Set Annotations → Tuned Parameters (`x_ht`, `Y`, `patch_size`) → sFRC's Sensitivity/Specificity on Test Set.** The most critical step is obtaining representative known hallucinations for tuning.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **`patch_size`:** Small patches increase sensitivity to tiny hallucinations but raise false positives from noise/texture. Large patches dilute local errors. Tuning required (paper: 64x64 for CT SR, 48x48 for MRI).
- **`FRC Threshold (Y)`:** Controls the analyzed frequency band. `Y` near 1 focuses on very low-freq (blur); `Y` near 0 focuses on high-freq (edges/noise). Must be set based on modality and expected hallucination band (e.g., 0.5 for CT mid-freq, 0.75 for MRI).
- **`x_ht`:** The core sensitivity knob, set from tuning data. A low `x_ht` (relaxed) misses hallucinations; a high `x_ht` (aggressive) flags many non-hallucinated patches. Its physical meaning is "up to which frequency must correlation be maintained for a patch to be considered faithful."

**Failure Signatures:**
- **All patches flagged:** `x_ht` too high, `Y` too low, or test images have severe global conventional artifacts.
- **No patches flagged:** `x_ht` too low, DL model is truly faithful, or hallucinations lie outside the targeted frequency band (`Y`).
- **High false positive rate on simple undersampled reconstructions (e.g., iFFT):** This is expected behavior; sFRC is designed for *non-linear* methods that might hide such structured errors.
- **Inability to detect a known hallucination type:** The hallucination's frequency signature is outside the band targeted by `Y` or its `x_ct` exceeds `x_ht`. Requires re-tuning with examples of the new type.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Replicate CT Super-Resolution Tuning:** Use the LDGC dataset. Generate low-res CT pairs. Manually annotate 3-5 hallucination types (plaques, indentations) in SRGAN outputs. Run tuning to derive `x_ht`, `Y=0.5`, `patch_size=64x64`. Validate on sharp test set: recover ~6.5% hallucination rate? Check visual agreement with Fig 7/8.
2.  **Validate Generalization Across Architectures:** Apply the *exact same tuned parameters* from Exp 1 to SR-WGAN outputs (same task/data). Per Table II, hallucination rate should increase (~7.5%) but trend similarly. This tests parameter transferability across architectures.
3.  **Test Theory-Based Tuning (MRI):** Use fastMRI U-Net demo data. Generate Bhadra et al.'s hallucination maps for one OOD slice. Instead of expert annotation, set `x_ht` as the max `x_ct` from patches overlapping the theory's hallucination mask. Use `Y=0.75`, `patch_size=48x48`. Apply to other slices. Do sFRC bounding boxes overlap with Bhadra's maps as in Fig 5/9? This tests the "imaging theory" parameter setting route.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  How can the appropriate spatial frequency bands for sFRC analysis be systematically determined for novel imaging problems without relying on extensive expert annotation of hallucinated features?
2.  Can sFRC be extended to detect hallucinations in unconditional generative models or in scenarios where a reference image is unavailable?
3.  What is the clinical impact of hallucinations detected by sFRC on actual diagnostic performance and patient outcomes?
4.  How do sFRC parameters (patch size, FRC threshold, hallucination threshold) generalize across different DL architectures, training datasets, and institutional scanner variations?
5.  Can the Hallucination Operating Characteristic (HOC) curve and its area under the curve (AU-HOC) predict downstream clinical task performance (e.g., lesion detection, segmentation accuracy) for AI-restored images?

## Limitations
- Detailed FRC ring binning and patch extraction are unspecified; implementation must infer from general FRC literature.
- No official code release; reproducibility depends on independent reimplementation.
- Threshold `x_ht` relies on tuning data; transferability across hallucination types is not fully validated.
- Method assumes hallucinations are localized and affect mainly mid-frequencies; may fail for global failures or artifacts spanning all frequencies.
- Evaluation is limited to three tasks; broader generalizability across modalities and reconstruction methods remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Mid‑frequency hallucination signature:** Medium. Supported by internal bandpass visualizations but lacks independent verification.
- **Patch‑based local analysis advantage:** High. Strong experimental evidence showing full‑image metrics miss localized hallucinations.
- **Parameterization via prior knowledge:** Medium. Demonstrated with expert annotation and theory, but tuning representativeness is uncertain.
- **Overall effectiveness across tasks:** Medium. Results are promising but depend on correct implementation and proper threshold selection.
