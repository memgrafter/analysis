# Probing Length Generalization in Mamba via Image Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12499
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12499
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Mamba fails to generalize to sequences longer than ~4× its training length in an image reconstruction task, while Transformers succeed; the model adapts its processing speed (via Δt) to the training length, causing catastrophic collapse beyond that threshold.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates why the Mamba architecture struggles with length generalization. Using an image reconstruction task—processing random 4x4 patches of an Omniglot image and then reconstructing patches at query locations—the authors probe Mamba's internal dynamics. They find that Mamba's performance collapses sharply when the number of image tokens during evaluation exceeds about four times the maximum training length. In contrast, a Transformer baseline generalizes well. Analysis of the Δt parameter reveals that Mamba implicitly adapts its processing speed to the training sequence length: shorter-trained models update information quickly but forget on longer sequences, while longer-trained models integrate slowly but preserve information. A variant that explicitly prepends the sequence length improves performance within the training range but harms generalization.

## Method Summary
The authors train Mamba models on a reconstruction task. Input consists of a random sequence of 4x4 image patches from augmented Omniglot characters. Each patch is flattened to 14 dimensions and concatenated with normalized (x,y) coordinates, yielding 16D tokens. Sequences are structured as: V_I image tokens, a learnable separator token, and T_Q query tokens (each a linear projection of normalized target coordinates). The model outputs a reconstruction token per query; the loss is mean squared error (MSE) against the ground‑truth patch. A Mamba backbone (8 blocks, model dimension 16, state size 8) processes the full sequence, with the depthwise convolution removed from each block. During training, V_I is sampled randomly between 1 and a maximum T_I (default 1024) while T_Q is fixed (1024). Evaluation varies V_I (and sometimes V_Q) to measure generalization. The same setup is used for a Transformer baseline with a ViT‑style encoder and cross‑attention decoder.

## Key Results
- Mamba's reconstruction MSE remains low for V_I up to roughly 4× the training maximum (T_I), then collapses catastrophically. Transformer baselines show no such collapse.
- Query token processing degrades much later (~16× T_Q), indicating asymmetry: image token intake strains state updates, while query tokens mainly rely on readout.
- Models trained with larger T_I exhibit slower Δt dynamics (smaller Δt norms), integrating information gradually; this adaptation improves performance on long training contexts but harms performance on shorter ones and does not prevent collapse beyond 4T_I.
- Prepending a token encoding the sequence length yields length‑adaptive Δt dynamics, improving MSE within the training range but reducing generalization beyond it.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Mamba implicitly learns a sequence‑length‑dependent processing speed via its input‑dependent Δt parameter, creating a trade‑off across sequence lengths.  
**Mechanism:** Δt controls the step size of the hidden state's continuous‑time evolution. The model optimizes an average Δt distribution over the training length distribution. Shorter training contexts lead to larger Δt norms (faster updates) that cause instability or forgetting on longer sequences; longer contexts lead to smaller Δt norms (slower, stable updates) that are sluggish on short sequences.  
**Core assumption:** The model does not learn a length‑agnostic algorithm but rather one tailored to the typical training lengths.  
**Evidence:** Abstract and sections 4.2/4.3 show that Δt norms differ across models trained with different T_I and that default Mamba exhibits invariant Δt dynamics across evaluation lengths, whereas a prepended variant adapts. Neighboring work (DeciMamba, LongMamba) also shows a performance trade‑off consistent with learned Δt adaptation.  
**Break condition:** When evaluation length V_I >> T_I, the learned Δt distribution becomes out‑of‑distribution, leading to exponential state decay/growth and reconstruction collapse (observed around V_I ≈ 4T_I).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The spatial encoding scheme (coordinate concatenation vs. sinusoidal positional encoding) influences length generalization by affecting the input distribution that Δt must handle.  
**Mechanism:** Concatenating normalized (x,y) coordinates yields a dense, unscaled spatial signal that may help Mamba associate content with location. However, this encoding ties the state dynamics to the specific coordinate distribution seen during training. When V_I increases, the effective input distribution shifts, making the Δt parameters OOD.  
**Core assumption:** The encoding interacts with Mamba’s state update; a different encoding could alter the OOD threshold.  
**Evidence:** The paper reports that an alternative sinusoidal positional encoding performed slightly worse, indicating the encoding choice is not neutral. The collapse threshold (4T_I) is established with coordinate concatenation; it may shift with other encodings. Direct evidence is limited, as the paper does not systematically vary encoding.  
**Break condition:** Using an encoding that broadens or rescales the input distribution might shift the Δt OOD threshold, affecting generalization.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Image tokens and query tokens engage Mamba’s state mechanism differently, leading to asymmetric length generalization: image context length V_I generalizes poorly beyond ~4T_I, whereas query length V_Q generalizes up to ~16T_Q.  
**Mechanism:** Image tokens introduce new, information‑rich content, requiring frequent state updates (Δt‑driven). Query tokens are probes that only read the current state without adding new image information; they rely mainly on the output projection. Therefore, instability in Δt‑driven updates for long V_I does not affect query tokens as severely.  
**Core assumption:** The computational burden for length generalization falls primarily on the state‑update mechanism (image tokens), not on the readout (query tokens).  
**Evidence:** Abstract and section 4.1 note the asymmetric degradation points and hypothesize that query tokens do not introduce new information. Section 6 reinforces that Mamba does not learn a length‑agnostic algorithm, with the asymmetry highlighting the specificity to integration tasks.  
**Break condition:** If query tokens required significant state updates (e.g., complex functions of past context), the asymmetry would diminish.

## Foundational Learning
- **Selective SSMs & the Δt parameter**: Mamba is a selective state‑space model where Δt is an input‑dependent gate that modulates the continuous‑time state transition. Understanding Δt is essential because it directly controls the “processing speed” of the hidden state. *Quick check:* If a model is trained only on short sequences, what magnitude of Δt would you expect, and how would that affect performance on a very long sequence?
- **Length Generalization vs. Positional Encoding**: The paper’s Transformer baseline generalizes well, contrasting typical NLP failures. The inferred reason is that spatial query positions are densely covered during training, so positional encodings are never OOD; the failure in Mamba is instead due to state dynamics becoming OOD. *Quick check:* Why does the Transformer baseline not show the same collapse as Mamba, and what does that suggest about Mamba’s failure?
- **Reconstruction‑as‑Probe Methodology**: The image reconstruction task is used as an interpretable probe of internal state fidelity. ## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input Embedding (flattened 4x4 patch → linear 14D + concatenated normalized (x,y) 2D → 16D) -> Separator Token (learnable 16D) -> Query Tokens (linear projection of normalized (x,y) → 16D) -> Mamba Backbone (8 blocks, each: no depthwise conv -> Mamba core with Δt -> MLP; state size 8, model dim 16) -> Output Head (linear per token) -> Reconstruction tokens (only query token outputs used). Loss: MSE between reconstruction and ground‑truth patches.

### Critical Path
[Random Patches + Coords] -> [Embedding] -> [Mamba Sequence Processing] -> [Separator] -> [Query Tokens] -> [Reconstruction Tokens] -> [MSE Loss vs. Ground Truth]

### Design Tradeoffs
- Spatial encoding: coordinate concatenation (used) vs. sinusoidal addition (slightly worse). Tradeoff: directness vs. potential for broader input distribution.
- T_I vs. T_Q: T_I is primary bottleneck; training with large T_I hurts short‑seq performance. T_Q generalizes more robustly (~16T_Q).
- Model capacity: depth improves absolute performance but does not shift the 4T_I collapse point; state size has minor impact.
- Prepended Mamba: Adding a token that encodes T_I yields length‑adaptive Δt dynamics, improving within‑range performance but reducing generalization beyond it.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Catastrophic collapse:** MSE spikes sharply when V_I > ~4·T_I; reconstructions become noise.
2. **Slow integration:** Models trained with large T_I show very gradual improvement in reconstruction quality for already‑seen patches as more tokens are processed, indicating Δt norms are too small.
3. **Quadrant sensitivity:** Reconstruction quality depends on the order in which spatial quadrants are processed; models with small T_I forget earlier quadrants faster.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What specific architectural mechanisms in Mamba (e.g., Δt modulation, state transition dynamics) cause the model to adapt its processing speed to the training sequence length, leading to length generalization failures?** The paper correlates Δt norms with performance but does not establish causality. Resolving this would require ablations where Δt is manipulated independently or detailed state trajectory probing.
2. **Can we design Mamba variants that mitigate the performance trade‑off across sequence lengths without requiring explicit sequence length information?** Prepended Mamba still needs the length token and fails to generalize beyond training lengths. An ideal solution would dynamically adapt based on input statistics alone.
3. **Is the length generalization trade‑off observed in the image reconstruction task universal across different tasks and modalities (e.g., language modeling, time‑series forecasting)?** The paper compares to NLP studies but only evaluates on Omniglot images; the phenomenon might be task‑specific.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, augmentation ranges) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- The role of spatial encoding is inferred but not isolated; the collapse threshold may depend on the chosen coordinate concatenation.
- Results may not generalize beyond the synthetic image reconstruction task to natural language or other modalities.

## Confidence
- Δt‑driven processing speed adaptation: **High** (supported by Δt norm measurements and cross‑study consistency).
- Spatial encoding influence: **Medium** (comparative experiment shows difference, but causal effect on OOD threshold not proven).
- Image/query token asymmetry: **High** (directly observed and theoretically motivated).
