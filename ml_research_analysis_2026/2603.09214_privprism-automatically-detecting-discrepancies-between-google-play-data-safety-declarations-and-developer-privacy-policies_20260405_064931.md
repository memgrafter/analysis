# PrivPRISM: Automatically Detecting Discrepancies Between Google Play Data Safety Declarations and Developer Privacy Policies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09214
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09214
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Discrepancies found in 53–61% of 9,481 analyzed apps, with PrivPRISM improving data practice classification precision by 6 percentage points over GPT baselines.

## Executive Summary
Google Play requires developers to submit simplified data safety declarations, but these often conflict with the detailed privacy policies users are directed to. This misalignment undermines transparency and regulatory compliance. The paper introduces PrivPRISM, an automated framework that uses encoder and decoder language models to extract fine-grained data practices from privacy policies, align them with declared items, and apply self-supervised verifiers to reduce mapping errors. Evaluated on 7,770 mobile games and 1,711 generic apps, PrivPRISM uncovered widespread discrepancies and significantly outperformed baseline models in accurately matching declared and disclosed practices.

## Method Summary
PrivPRISM processes two inputs: the Google Play data safety declaration and the linked full privacy policy. First, an encoder language model extracts structured data practices (what data is collected and for what purpose) from the privacy policy text. A decoder model then attempts to align each declared practice from the data safety section with an extracted practice from the policy. A self-supervised verifier, trained without ground-truth alignments, filters out low-confidence mappings to reduce false positives. This pipeline enables scalable, automated detection of misalignments across thousands of apps without manual annotation.

## Key Results
- **High Discrepancy Rate:** 53% of games and 61% of non-games exhibited at least one mismatch between declared and disclosed practices.
- **Incomplete Disclosure:** Privacy policies disclosed only 66.8% of potential sensitive data accesses (e.g., location, financial), while data safety declarations covered just 36.4% of such accesses.
- **Improved Accuracy:** PrivPRISM achieved a 6 percentage point precision gain over GPT-based baselines in classifying data practices and reduced alignment mapping errors by 22.3 percentage points using its verifier.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide an explicit theoretical "why" beyond describing the pipeline. The mechanism's effectiveness stems from separating extraction (via an encoder) from alignment (via a decoder) and introducing a verifier that learns to reject mappings likely to be incorrect based on consistency signals, thereby reducing error propagation without requiring large labeled datasets.

- **Encoder Language Models:** Transform text into structured representations. *Why needed:* To automatically extract practices from unstructured policy text. *Quick check:* How would you extract "location data collected for ads" from a paragraph?
- **Decoder Language Models:** Generate or align outputs given inputs. *Why needed:* To map declared items to extracted policy practices. *Quick check:* What does it mean to "align" two sets of practices?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Privacy Policy Text -> Encoder LM -> Extracted Practices (items, purposes) -> Decoder LM + Data Safety Declaration -> Candidate Alignments -> Self-Supervised Verifier -> Final Mapped Practices (with confidence scores)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Extract all practices from the privacy policy.  
2. For each declared practice, find the best matching extracted practice.  
3. Apply the verifier to discard low-confidence matches.  
4. Report remaining aligned pairs as verified matches; unaligned declarations or extractions indicate potential discrepancies.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Accuracy vs. Scalability:** The verifier trades some recall for higher precision, avoiding the cost of full manual review.  
- **Generality vs. Specificity:** Using general-purpose LMs (e.g., T5) avoids domain-specific training but may miss nuanced legal phrasing.  
- **Automation vs. Explainability:** Pipeline decisions are based on neural scores; pinpointing *why* a specific mapping was rejected is non-trivial.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- High false negative rate when policies use unconventional wording not seen during verifier training.  
- Mismatches due to entity resolution errors (e.g., "device ID" vs. "unique identifier").  
- Verifier overconfidence on syntactically similar but semantically different purposes.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Replication:** Run PrivPRISM on a 100-app subset and compare alignment accuracy against a manually created gold standard.  
2. **Verifier Ablation:** Disable the verifier and measure the change in mapping error rate to quantify its contribution.  
3. **Cross-Model Generalization:** Replace the encoder/decoder with a different LM (e.g., BERT) and assess precision/recall drop to evaluate model dependence.

## Limitations
- **Sample Scope:** Analysis focused on popular games (7,770) and generic apps (1,711); findings may not generalize to less popular or niche categories.
- **Verifier Robustness:** The self-supervised verifier’s error reduction lacks stress-testing on edge cases like ambiguous language or non-standard formatting.
- **Manual Audit Scale:** Qualitative findings on policy reuse and vagueness derive from a small, unreported audit subset, raising concerns about coder bias and reliability.

## Confidence
- **High:** Discrepancy prevalence (53–61% of apps) is supported by large-scale automated analysis with clear metrics.
- **Medium:** PrivPRISM’s precision gain (+6 pp over GPT) is plausible but lacks statistical significance tests and broader baseline comparison.
- **Low:** Causal claims about policy reuse/vagueness driving discrepancies are speculative without controlled experiments or larger qualitative coding.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Category Replication:** Run PrivPRISM on a stratified sample including utilities, social, and finance apps to test generalizability.
2. **Adversarial Verifier Testing:** Generate/pollute policies with synthetic contradictions to quantify verifier failure modes and upper-bound accuracy.
3. **Independent Audit Replication:** Recruit two new coders to re-analyze 500 randomly selected policies, reporting Cohen’s κ for policy reuse/vagueness labels.
