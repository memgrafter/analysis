# A Step Toward Federated Pretraining of Multimodal Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.26786
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26786
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes Fed-CMP, a federated learning framework for multimodal LLM pre-training that significantly outperforms existing methods on seven benchmarks (e.g., MM-Vet 32.5, MMBench 32.7) by addressing parameter interference and gradient oscillations.

## Executive Summary
Federated pre-training of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is hindered by data privacy constraints and the need to handle heterogeneous client data. This paper introduces Fed-MA, a paradigm that freezes the vision encoder and LLM while collaboratively training only the cross-modal projector, thereby reducing communication overhead. To tackle two key challenges—parameter interference from heterogeneous data and gradient oscillations due to one-pass training—the authors propose Fed-CMP, combining Canonical Reliability-Aware Aggregation (CRA) and Orthogonality-Preserved Momentum (OPM). CRA decomposes client projectors into a shared alignment basis and client-specific coefficients, fusing the latter with reliability weighting to mitigate interference. OPM stabilizes the shared basis updates via momentum while preserving orthogonality. Evaluated on four federated pre-training splits of CC12M, Fed-CMP achieves state-of-the-art results on seven multimodal benchmarks, surpassing both FL and merging baselines by large margins. Ablations confirm the contributions of CRA and OPM, and downstream instruction tuning yields further gains.

## Method Summary
The paper studies federated MLLM alignment (Fed-MA) under a constrained setup: a frozen vision encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14) and LLM (Vicuna-1.5-7B) with a trainable 2-layer MLP projector. The goal is to align visual features to the LLM's textual embedding space via an autoregressive loss, using only local client data without sharing raw images. The proposed Fed-CMP framework operates over multiple rounds. Each client performs one epoch of local training on its subset, computing an alignment error dₖ. The server aggregates the uploaded projectors by concatenating them into ΔW and applying SVD to obtain a shared basis UΣ and client coefficient matrices Vᵏ. Reliability weights wₖ are derived from update magnitude and dₖ, and a fused coefficient V_fused is computed as a weighted average. The shared basis U is updated via OPM: the new basis Û is sign-aligned with the previous U, then combined with momentum and projected back to orthogonality via polar decomposition. The global projector is reconstructed as UΣV_fusedᵀ and broadcast. This process mitigates parameter interference through the shared subspace and stabilizes training via momentum on the orthonormal basis.

## Key Results
- Fed-CMP achieves substantial improvements over existing federated learning methods (FedAvg, FedAdam) and model merging baselines across seven multimodal benchmarks under all four federated pre-training splits (MI-I, MT-T, MJoint, MCross). For example, under the high-heterogeneity MI-I split, scores include MM-Vet 32.5, MMBench 32.7, SEED 30.5, LLaVA-Bench 46.6, POPE 74.5, MME 1143.2, MMVP 33.0.
- Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of Canonical Reliability-Aware Aggregation (CRA) and Orthogonality-Preserved Momentum (OPM), with each component providing measurable gains.
- When the federally pre-trained projector is used for downstream instruction tuning, performance further improves, e.g., GQA accuracy reaches 54.3.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Canonical Reliability-Aware Aggregation (CRA)
- **Claim**: Decomposing client projectors into a shared alignment basis and client-specific coefficients via SVD, then fusing coefficients with reliability weighting, suppresses destructive parameter interference from heterogeneous data.
- **How**: The server concatenates all client projectors ΔW and performs SVD: ΔW = UΣ[V¹, ..., Vᴷ]ᵀ. UΣ captures the common cross-modal subspace; each client's projector is expressed as Wᵏ = UΣ(Vᵏ)ᵀ. Aggregation occurs on the coefficient matrices {Vᵏ} using weights wₖ based on update magnitude and alignment error dₖ, producing V_fused = Σ wₖVₖ. The global projector is reconstructed as W_global = UΣ(V_fused)ᵀ.
- **Core assumption**: A shared alignment subspace exists across clients despite data heterogeneity, and client-specific deviations are captured in {Vᵏ}.
- **Evidence**: Section 3.2.1; ablations show CRA is critical for performance.
- **Break condition**: If the singular value spectrum of ΔW is flat (no dominant shared basis), the reconstruction may be incoherent.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonality-Preserved Momentum (OPM)
- **Claim**: Applying momentum to the shared alignment basis U via two-step orthogonal projection (align signs, polar decomposition) accumulates historical optimization directions while preserving geometric structure, stabilizing one-pass federated training.
- **How**: Let Uᵣ be the current basis and Ûᵣ₊₁ the newly aggregated basis. Compute M = βUᵣ + (1-β)Align(Ûᵣ₊₁, Uᵣ), where Align flips signs of Ûᵣ₊₁ columns with negative inner product vs. Uᵣ. Then Ur₊₁ = Orth(M) = PQᵀ from the polar decomposition of M, ensuring orthonormality. Singular values Σ are updated with standard momentum.
- **Core assumption**: The shared basis U evolves smoothly across rounds and is less sensitive to transient data shifts than raw projector parameters.
- **Evidence**: Section 3.2.2; OPM ablation yields significant stability gains.
- **Break condition**: Catastrophic client drift causing fundamental basis changes may make momentum pull away from the true consensus, requiring adaptive β.

### Mechanism 3: Fed-MA Task Formulation
- **Claim**: Freezing the vision encoder and LLM while federated training only the cross-modal projector reduces communication/computation overhead sufficiently to make MLLM pre-training feasible under FL constraints.
- **How**: Each client trains a lightweight projector ψ on local image-text pairs via the autoregressive loss (Eq. 2). Only ψ parameters (few million) are communicated per round, not the full MLLM (billions of parameters).
- **Core assumption**: A randomly initialized projector can be effectively trained to align vision and language spaces without tuning the massive encoder/LLM, and this alignment is robust to federated aggregation.
- **Evidence**: Section 3.1; strong empirical results despite frozen backbones.
- **Break condition**: Incompatible embedding spaces or insufficient projector capacity would prevent effective alignment, necessitating partial tuning of larger components.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Server holds global projector W and maintains shared basis U and singular values Σ. Each client possesses a frozen vision encoder f, frozen LLM L, and a trainable projector ψ (Wᵏ). Key per-round modules: CRA (server-side SVD and reliability weighting), OPM (server-side momentum update on U and Σ), and LocalTrain (client-side SGD on ψ).

**Critical path**:
1. Server broadcasts global projector Wᵣ₋₁ to all clients.
2. Each client performs one epoch of LocalTrain on its disjoint data subset, computes alignment error dₖ.
3. Clients upload updated projector Wᵏ and dₖ.
4. Server concatenates {Wᵏ} into ΔW and runs SVD, obtaining U, Σ, and individual Vᵏ.
5. Compute reliability weights wₖ from update magnitude ∥Wᵏ−Wᵣ₋₁∥ and dₖ; fuse coefficients V_fused = Σ wₖVᵏ.
6. Update shared basis U via OPM: Ur ← OrthMomentum(Ûᵣ, Ur₋₁, β). Update Σ with momentum.
7. Reconstruct global projector Wᵣ = UΣ(V_fused)ᵀ and broadcast for the next round.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Communication vs. Stability**: Transmitting full projectors every round is costly but required for SVD-based CRA; compressing Vᵏ could reduce bandwidth.
- **Canonical Space Cost**: Server-side SVD on ΔW (size I×(O·K)) has O(I·O·K²) complexity, potentially bottlenecking large K or high-dimensional projectors.
- **Momentum Sensitivity**: Adaptive β (Eq. 8) introduces hyperparameter λ; mis-calibration may over-smooth or under-stabilize updates.

**Failure signatures**:
- **CRA failure**: V_fused collapses to near-zero if reliability weights wₖ vanish (e.g., all dₖ large). Check α and normalization.
- **OPM failure**: Ur loses orthogonality (UᵀU ≠ I) due to incomplete polar decomposition or sign misalignment in Align; verify inner products.
- **Fed-MA failure**: No improvement over local training suggests projector capacity insufficiency or misaligned frozen backbones.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Ablate each component (CRA, reliability weighting, OPM) individually to measure isolated impact on benchmark scores.
2. Analyze performance across heterogeneity splits (MI-I vs. MT-T) to assess sensitivity to data distribution shift.
3. Vary the reliability weighting coefficient α to study trade-off between magnitude and alignment error; identify robust range.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extension to larger components**: How can Fed-CMP support federated fine-tuning of the visual encoder and LLM beyond the cross-modal projector? Freezing these components limits adaptability; systematic experiments on partial fine-tuning are needed, considering communication overhead and convergence.
- **Scalability of canonical space construction**: The SVD in CRA incurs high computational cost on the server, especially with many clients or high-dimensional projectors. Approximate or distributed SVD methods could improve scalability but require validation.
- **Robustness to real-world FL dynamics**: The current framework assumes synchronized rounds with full client participation. Deployments must handle heterogeneous devices, asynchronous communication, and client dropout; asynchronous variants and straggler mitigation need investigation.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete methodological details**: Exact computation of alignment error dₖ (per-sample vs. batch), local optimizer settings, SVD reshape scheme, and adaptive β formula (Eq. 8) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- **Computational scalability concerns**: The server-side SVD’s cubic complexity may become prohibitive as the number of clients or projector dimensions grows; no analysis or mitigation is provided.
- **Limited scope and evaluation**: Freezing the vision encoder and LLM may restrict the model’s expressive power; experiments use synthetic partitions, leaving real-world privacy and heterogeneity patterns unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Canonical Reliability-Aware Aggregation (CRA)**: Medium confidence; theoretical design and ablations are promising, but the break condition (flat singular spectrum) remains unverified.
- **Orthogonality-Preserved Momentum (OPM)**: Medium confidence; mechanism is sound, but sensitivity to β and risk of drift under severe heterogeneity are not fully characterized.
- **Fed-MA feasibility**: High confidence; strong empirical gains across benchmarks demonstrate that projector-only federated pre-training is effective and communication-efficient.
- **Overall superiority of Fed-CMP**: High confidence; consistent improvements across four splits and seven benchmarks validate the framework.
