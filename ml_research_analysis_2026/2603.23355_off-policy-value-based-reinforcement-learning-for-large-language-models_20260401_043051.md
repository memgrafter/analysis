# Off-Policy Value-Based Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.23355
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23355
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: ReVal achieves 2.7% absolute improvement on AIME24 and 4.5% on GPQA over GRPO, with 18% training time reduction.

## Executive Summary
ReVal is a novel off-policy value-based reinforcement learning framework designed to improve the sample efficiency of LLM post-training. It addresses the inefficiency of on-policy methods (e.g., GRPO) by reusing trajectories via a replay buffer and interpreting the model's logits as Q-values. The method combines stepwise consistency signals with trajectory-level verification using a shaped reward and a reference policy to stabilize training. Experiments on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, GPQA) with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B demonstrate that ReVal converges faster, achieves higher accuracy (up to 4.8% gain under limited rollouts), and reduces total training time by 18% compared to GRPO.

## Method Summary
ReVal formulates LLM post-training as a value-based RL problem. The token prediction logits of the LLM are treated as Q-values, i.e., the estimated value of selecting a particular token in a given context. This allows the use of standard Q-learning updates with a replay buffer, enabling data reuse across multiple gradient steps. Trajectories are generated with the current policy, and a shaped reward—combining stepwise consistency (e.g., intermediate correctness) and a final verification signal—is assigned to each trajectory. The tuples (state, action, reward, next_state) are stored in a replay buffer. During training, minibatches are sampled from the buffer to minimize the Bellman residual, augmented with a KL regularization term that keeps the learned policy close to a reference policy (typically the initial pretrained model). The framework uses a single model (no separate value network) and periodically updates the reference policy to maintain stability.

## Key Results
- ReVal outperforms GRPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B: absolute gains of 2.7% on AIME24 and 4.5% on GPQA.
- Under limited rollouts (N=1), improvements increase to 4.8% on AIME24 and 4.6% on GPQA.
- Total training time reduced by 18% due to fewer generation rounds enabled by effective replay.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ReVal's effectiveness stems from three key factors: (1) Off-policy data reuse via a replay buffer dramatically improves sample efficiency by allowing trajectories to be trained on multiple times. (2) Interpreting LLM logits as Q-values leverages the pretrained model's knowledge as a warm start for the Q-function and enables direct Bellman updates without additional value heads. (3) The combination of a shaped reward (providing dense supervision for reasoning steps) and a KL-regularized reference policy mitigates the instability common in off-policy RL for LLMs, ensuring that the policy improves without catastrophic divergence. These elements together yield faster convergence and better final performance than on-policy alternatives.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pretrained LLM → Trajectory Generation → Replay Buffer → Minibatch Sampling → Q-Update (Bellman Residual + KL Penalty) → Updated LLM (and periodic Reference Policy Update).
- **Critical path**: Generate trajectories → Compute shaped rewards → Store in buffer → Sample minibatch → Compute Bellman loss + KL loss → Perform gradient step → (Periodically) sync reference policy.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Off-policy reuse vs. stability: More reuse improves efficiency but risks distribution shift; mitigated by reference policy.
  - Reward shaping detail: Richer shaping gives better guidance but may handcraft biases.
  - Buffer size: Larger buffers preserve older experiences but increase memory and may become stale; FIFO is a simple compromise.
  - Reference policy update frequency: Infrequent updates maintain a stable anchor but may not reflect recent improvements; too frequent updates reduce regularization effect.
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - Diverging loss or KL explosion.
  - Degradation on held-out validation sets.
  - Overfitting to buffer contents (e.g., repeated memorization of specific trajectories).
  - Reward hacking where shaped reward is optimized without genuine reasoning.
- **First three experiments**:
  1. Sanity check on a small synthetic reasoning task: verify that ReVal reduces Bellman error and improves accuracy compared to on-policy baseline.
  2. Ablate the reference policy (remove KL term) to observe training instability or performance drop.
  3. Vary replay buffer size and sampling strategy (FIFO vs. random) to measure impact on sample efficiency and final performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can replay buffer sampling strategies be improved to further increase data efficiency in off-policy RL for LLMs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 4.3: "We leave the exploration of more efficient sampling strategies, such as prioritized experience replay (Schaul et al., 2015), to future work." and Conclusion: "plan to explore more advanced buffer sampling strategies, such as prioritized experience replay."
- Why unresolved: ReVal currently uses a simple FIFO replay buffer, which discards old trajectories without considering their potential learning value. More sophisticated sampling (e.g., prioritized by TD error, novelty, or difficulty) could further accelerate convergence and reduce total training cost.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic empirical evaluations of prioritized experience replay and other adaptive sampling methods on standard RLVR benchmarks, measuring both sample efficiency and wall-clock time. Theoretical analysis connecting sample priorities to variance reduction would also clarify benefits.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What explains the varying number of gradient updates needed per trajectory, and how can we adaptively determine the optimal update count for different samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Conclusion: "investigate the underlying mechanisms governing the varying update requirements of different data samples in value-based RL for LLM training."
- Why unresolved: ReVal currently applies a fixed number of updates per trajectory (K updates). However, some trajectories may be more informative (e.g., contain rare reasoning patterns) and could benefit from more updates, while others may be less useful. Understanding per-sample update requirements could enhance efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of gradient norms, loss reduction, and policy changes across multiple updates per trajectory, correlated with trajectory characteristics (e.g., length, correctness, diversity). Development of adaptive schedules that allocate updates based on online metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Under what conditions (e.g., model size, task domain, response length) do techniques like reward normalization and periodic reference policy updates become necessary for stable off-policy value-based RL?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 5.5.2: Experiments with Qwen2.5-Math-7B required both reward normalization and periodic reference resets to maintain stable training, while DPSK-R1-Distill-1.5B did not. The paper does not explain why these modifications were needed for one model but not the other.
- Why unresolved: Without a clear theoretical or empirical understanding of when these stabilizers are required, practitioners must rely on trial-and-error, hindering the application of ReVal to diverse LLMs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled studies varying model architecture, pretraining data, and task type, measuring training stability metrics (gradient norms, KL divergence). A theoretical analysis of KL regularization growth and its interaction with off-policy updates could also provide guidance.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How sensitive is the performance of ReVal to the quality of the pretrained LLM’s implicit Q-function initialization, and can we improve this initialization beyond standard pretrained logits?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 4.1: ReVal relies on the theoretical result that pretrained LLM logits correspond to a soft Q-function of an endogenous reward. However, the paper does not investigate how the strength of this initialization (e.g., model size, domain mismatch) affects final RL performance or whether a simple fine-tuning step could yield a better starting point.
- Why unresolved: If the pretrained model’s logits are poorly calibrated or not aligned with the target task, the off-policy updates might suffer from bias or slow convergence. Understanding this sensitivity could lead to robust initialization procedures for value-based LLM training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments initializing ReVal with models of varying pretraining quality or domain, possibly with additional calibration steps.Measuring convergence speed and final performance would reveal the relationship between initialization quality and training outcomes. A theoretical study of the error propagation from an imperfect Q-function in the Bellman residual minimization could also guide improvements.

## Limitations
- Generalization limited to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B on math reasoning benchmarks; performance on larger models or other domains (e.g., code, dialogue) remains unknown.
- Method details for reward shaping and reference policy updates are insufficient for full reproducibility.
- Off-policy training risks distribution shift from the replay buffer; the effectiveness of mitigations (e.g., KL regularization) needs independent validation.
