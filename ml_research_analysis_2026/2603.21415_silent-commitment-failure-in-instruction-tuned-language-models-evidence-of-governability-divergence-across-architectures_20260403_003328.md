# Silent Commitment Failure in Instruction-Tuned Language Models: Evidence of Governability Divergence Across Architectures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21415
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21415
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: Instruction-tuned models show divergent governability: Mistral-7B and Llama-3.2 exhibit silent commitment failure (zero pre-commitment warning), while Phi-3-mini emits detectable conflict signals; governability is architecture-determined and immutable to post-hoc adaptation.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces "governability" as a measurable property of whether errors in autonomous LLM agents produce a detectable warning before output commitment and are correctable upon detection. Through analysis of hidden-state trajectory dynamics, it reveals that some models (Phi-3-mini) generate an "authority band" spike indicating internal conflict, while others (Mistral-7B, Llama-3.2) commit to errors silently. A controlled 2×2 experiment demonstrates that this detectability is largely fixed at pretraining, as LoRA fine-tuning alters output policy but not the core conflict signal. The resulting Detection & Correction Matrix classifies model-task combinations into four deployment regimes, exposing a critical security assumption violation in current instruction-tuned models.

## Method Summary
Governability is quantified via two metrics: (1) **Conflict detectability**—the presence of a pre-commitment spike in hidden-state trajectory tension (measured by the ρ metric, a relative acceleration in latent space) and the warning margin (tokens between spike onset and commitment); (2) **Correction capacity**—success rate of structured interventions (e.g., verification directives) injected after detection but before finalization. Specialized probes (e.g., `diag_15` sequential dependency task, order-of-operands misalignment "2+3×6") induce aligned and misaligned outputs across twelve reasoning domains. Hidden states are captured during greedy decoding (primary) and temperature sampling (T=0.7). A 2×2 experiment varies architecture (Phi-3 vs. Mistral) and training adaptation (baseline vs. LoRA fine-tuning) to test whether governability is alterable post-pretraining.

## Key Results
- **Silent failure prevalence**: Mistral-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct show zero pre-commitment warning tokens across tested domains, while Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct produces a detectable signal 57 tokens before commitment.
- **Architectural divergence**: The order-of-operations probe reveals a 52× difference in conflict signal strength (spike ratio) between Phi-3 (~67×) and Mistral (~1×); LoRA fine-tuning on Mistral alters spike ratio by only ±0.32×.
- **Immutable geometry**: Governability is fixed at pretraining; post-hoc adaptation (LoRA) changes output policy but not the fundamental detectability of conflict as captured by the authority band.
- **Classification framework**: The Detection & Correction Matrix categorizes model-task combinations into four regimes: Governable, Monitor Only, Steer Blind, Ungovernable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on hidden-state trajectory dynamics. When a model faces a reasoning conflict (e.g., due to a misaligned scaffold), its internal representations accelerate through latent space, creating a measurable "authority band" spike just before committing to an answer. The ρ metric quantifies this relative acceleration. A spike occurring *before* the commitment token indicates the error is detectable; if the spike is absent or post-commitment, failure is silent. Correction capacity depends entirely on this detectability—without a timely warning, interventions cannot be applied. Crucially, the formation of such spikes appears baked into the model's pretrained geometry, rendering governability largely immutable to later fine-tuning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Governability**: Whether LLM errors emit detectable warnings before action commitment and are correctable upon detection. *Why needed*: Essential for safe autonomous agent deployment. *Quick check*: Does the model ever output an incorrect answer with no prior divergence in hidden-state tension?
- **Silent Commitment Failure**: Incorrect outputs with zero pre-commitment signal. *Why needed*: Identifies a critical security gap where errors go unnoticed until after action. *Quick check*: Compare ρ trajectories for aligned vs. misaligned conditions; if divergence occurs only at/after the final answer token, failure is silent.
- **Authority Band**: The spike in hidden-state trajectory tension signalling internal conflict. *Why needed*: Serves as the detectable precursor to error. *Quick check*: Is there a sharp increase in ρ (e.g., ≥4× baseline) in the tokens leading up to the commitment point?
- **Trajectory Tension (ρ)**: Metric computing relative acceleration between consecutive hidden states. *Why needed*: Quantifies conflict intensity. *Quick check*: Without the exact formula, infer by ratio of max ρ in misaligned vs. aligned conditions (spike ratio).
- **Detection & Correction Matrix**: 2×2 classification by detection (Yes/No) and correction (Yes/No) capacity. *Why needed*: Maps deployment risk per model-task pair. *Quick check*: For a given scenario, can you both detect errors before final output and successfully redirect with interventions?
- **Pretraining Determinism**: Core model geometry (e.g., authority band formation) is set during pretraining and resistant to post-hoc adaptation. *Why needed*: Suggests safety properties must be evaluated at pretrain time. *Quick check*: Does LoRA fine-tuning significantly change spike ratio? Stability implies pretraining fixation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Probe Generator → Model (with hidden state extraction) → Trajectory Calculator (ρ) → Spike Detector → Classifier (Governability Matrix) → Intervention Tester  
**Critical path**: Design probe → Generate aligned/misaligned outputs → Extract per-token hidden states → Compute ρ trajectories → Identify commitment token → Detect pre-commitment spike (threshold ≥4× baseline) → Quantify spike ratio/warning margin → Apply correction scaffolds if spike detected → Measure correction success.  
**Design tradeoffs**: Greedy decoding maximizes signal clarity but may not reflect typical usage; temperature sampling reduces detection rates. Probe design balances conflict induction—too subtle yields no spike, too obvious alters behavior unnaturally. The ≥4× spike threshold is heuristic, trading false positives/negatives.  
**Failure signatures**: (1) No pre-commitment spike (silent failure), (2) Spike occurs after commitment token (undetectable in time), (3) Correction scaffolds fail despite detection, (4) LoRA changes output correctness but not spike ratio (geometry/policy separation).  
**First experiments**:  
1. Replicate `diag_15` probe on Phi-3 and Mistral: compute ρ trajectories; confirm Phi-3 shows spike ~57 tokens before commitment while Mistral shows none.  
2. Run order-of-operations probe across all four 2×2 conditions: verify Phi-3 spike ratio ~67×, Mistral variants ~1×, LoRA variation within ±0.3×.  
3. Test correction on Phi-3 with "Verify your step count" scaffold immediately after spike detection; measure shift to correct answer and success rate across trials.

## Limitations
- **Metric opaqueness**: Exact ρ formula and baseline normalization are undisclosed, preventing independent computation and threshold validation.
- **Probe incompleteness**: Full prompt templates, gold answers, and misalignment scaffolds for all twelve domains are unavailable, blocking systematic matrix population.
- **Intervention specification gap**: Precise correction scaffold prompts and format compliance rules used in correction tests are not detailed.
- **Fine-tuning details**: LoRA hyperparameters (rank, alpha, learning rate, dataset preprocessing) for Mistral adaptations are missing.

## Confidence
- **High**: Observation of silent commitment failure in Mistral and Llama vs. detectability in Phi-3; 52× difference in conflict signal strength between architectures.
- **Medium**: Claim that governability is fixed at pretraining based on minimal LoRA-induced spike ratio change; inference assumes tested LoRA setups are representative.
- **Medium**: Utility of Detection & Correction Matrix classification depends on unverified ρ metric and unspecified intervention protocols.
