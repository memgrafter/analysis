# Span-Level Machine Translation Meta-Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19921
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19921
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed MPP with micro-averaging as robust meta-evaluation; LLM auto-evaluators still lag human annotators in span-level F-score on MQM benchmarks, with parity only in select language directions.

## Executive Summary
Machine translation (MT) error detection systems output character-level error spans with categories and severities, but there is no reliable way to meta-evaluate them. This paper investigates span-level precision, recall, and F-score implementations, exposing biases in common strategies: partial overlap matching (MP) favors longer spans, while macro-averaging favors low-recall systems. The authors propose "match with partial overlap and partial credit" (MPP) combined with micro-averaging as a robust solution, validated via sentinel auto-evaluators that simulate imprecise or sparse predictions. Empirical results on MQM and ESA benchmarks show that state-of-the-art LLM-based evaluators still underperform humans in span-level F-score, and rankings shift dramatically under alternative metrics, underscoring the need for the recommended approach.

## Method Summary
The meta-evaluation framework compares three matching strategies—Exact Match (EM), Partial Overlap (MP, τ=1), and Partial Overlap with Partial credit (MPP)—across two averaging schemes: micro (aggregate over all spans) and macro (average per sentence). MPP uses a one-to-one bipartite matching that maximizes corpus-level F-score, then computes per-match precision and recall as overlap ratios averaged over all matched spans. To diagnose biases, the authors construct sentinel auto-evaluators by extending ground-truth spans (to test length bias) and by randomly deleting predicted errors (to test sparsity bias). They evaluate real auto-evaluators, including LLMs and WMT25 submissions, on MQM and ESA datasets, measuring the stability of rankings across metric variants. The official implementation, data, and prompts are provided in a public repository.

## Key Results
- MP systematically overestimates performance as predicted spans lengthen, revealing a bias toward longer error spans.
- Macro-averaging inflates scores for low-recall systems, especially in sparse error settings; micro-averaging correctly tracks decreasing recall.
- MPP with micro-averaging resists both biases and yields rankings where LLM-based evaluators still trail human annotators on MQM, with parity only in some language pairs; metric choice substantially alters system rankings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Partial Overlap (MP) matching introduces a systematic bias that favors auto-evaluators producing longer error spans.
- **Mechanism:** MP declares a match if two spans overlap by at least τ characters. Longer spans have a larger surface area, increasing the probability of overlapping a ground-truth span and inflating true positives without requiring accurate localization.
- **Core assumption:** Error detection quality should be invariant to span length if the erroneous content is captured; MQM/ESA assign severity per discrete error, not per character.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states MP favors longer spans; Section 5.1 shows MP performance rises as span length increases; longer spans are more likely to match, inflating precision, recall, and F-score.
- **Break condition:** The bias disappears if all systems produce spans of similar average length, or if the objective explicitly rewards broader coverage over precision.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Macro-averaging across samples introduces a systematic bias that favors low-recall auto-evaluators in sparse error-detection tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Macro-averaging computes metrics per sample then averages them. In samples with no ground-truth errors (common in MQM), a system predicting no errors achieves perfect precision, recall, and F=1. Systems that under-predict errors accumulate more such “easy” samples, disproportionately boosting their average.
- **Core assumption:** Corpus-level performance should reflect aggregate detection capability across all errors, not be gamed by exploiting sample-level sparsity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions macro-averaging favors low-recall systems; Section 5.2 shows macro F-score increases as errors are deleted; Section 3.2 hypothesizes that macro favors systems predicting fewer errors to maximize F=1 on zero-error samples.
- **Break condition:** Bias is mitigated if the test set has few zero-error samples or if micro-averaging is used.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** "Match with Partial Overlap and Partial credit" (MPP) combined with micro-averaging provides a robust meta-evaluation strategy resistant to span-length and sparsity biases.
- **Mechanism:** MPP computes a one-to-one matching between predicted and ground-truth spans that maximizes corpus-level F-score. For each matched pair, precision = overlap/|predicted|, recall = overlap/|truth|. These per-match scores are then averaged across all matched spans (micro-averaging), giving each error equal weight regardless of length, while partial credit handles imperfect alignment.
- **Core assumption:** The fundamental evaluation unit is the individual error instance; each error should contribute equally to the final metric.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract proposes MPP+micro; Section 2.3 states averaging across error spans ensures equal weight; Section 5.1 shows MPP performance appropriately decreases as span length grows; Section 5.2 demonstrates micro-averaging robustness—F-score decreases as error removal probability increases.
- **Break condition:** Robustness holds as long as the optimal one-to-one matching and micro-averaging are used; severity weighting would require recalibration.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Span Matching Engine → Aggregation Module → Evaluation Pipeline  
  (Sentinel Generator creates perturbed spans for testing)

- **Critical path:** The one-to-one matching algorithm in the Span Matching Engine that maximizes global F-score; a greedy approach may not find the optimum.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • MPP (per-span averaging) vs. W25 (character-count): MPP treats each error equally but requires O(n³) bipartite matching; W25 is simpler but biases toward longer spans.  
  • Micro vs. Macro averaging: Micro is robust to sparsity but weights samples by error count; Macro treats all samples equally but is vulnerable to zero‑error samples.  
  • Computational cost: Hungarian algorithm for optimal matching scales as O(n³); corpus size and average errors per sample affect feasibility.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Rankings invert when switching from micro to macro averaging → sparsity bias.  
  2. System performance rises as its predicted spans are artificially lengthened → MP bias.  
  3. A system achieves perfect F-score on a sample by predicting a single long span covering all errors → over‑generous matching.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate Sentinel Tests:** Extend predicted spans by *n* characters (n = 0,10,…,100). Plot F-score vs. *n* under EM, MP, MPP (micro & macro). Expect MP F-score to increase, others to decrease.  
  2. **Replicate Low‑Recall Sentinel Tests:** Randomly delete predicted errors with probability *p* (p = 0,0.2,…,0.8). Compare micro vs. macro F-score trends. Expect macro to increase/plateau, micro to decrease.  
  3. **Ranking Stability Check:** Compute rankings of multiple evaluators using all four (M, avg) combinations. Calculate Spearman’s ρ. Expect high correlation among micro‑averaged variants, low correlation involving MP and macro.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can span-level meta-evaluation metrics incorporate error severity weighting?** The paper notes severity is orthogonal to detection measures; future work could design graduated penalties based on predicted vs. ground‑truth severity and validate on benchmarks.
- **Does the choice of unit (character, word, token) for span overlap significantly impact outcomes?** The authors used characters for tokenization‑agnosticism but did not test alternatives; comparative experiments across languages are needed.
- **Does span-level meta-evaluation provide higher resolution than score‑level evaluation?** The paper hypothesizes span‑level captures error location more precisely, but a direct controlled comparison on identical data is required to confirm.
- **What causes substantial differences between MQM and ESA ground‑truth protocols?** The paper observes ranking and score shifts but does not investigate whether they stem from annotation guidelines, annotator behavior, or test‑set properties; analyzing overlapping annotations could clarify.

## Limitations
- Data acquisition details (exact WMT file formats, character offset conventions) are ambiguous, risking preprocessing errors.
- LLM sampling parameters (temperature, top_p) and Bedrock defaults are unspecified, potentially affecting output consistency.
- Human MQM annotations used as ground truth are not publicly released, preventing full replication of the human baseline.
- The exact bipartite matching algorithm for MPP is not described in prose; code inspection is necessary for precise replication.
- Findings may not generalize beyond WMT’s annotated language pairs and genres.

## Confidence
- MP bias favors longer spans: **High**
- Macro-averaging favors low-recall systems: **High**
- MPP+micro-averaging robustness: **Medium**
- LLM evaluators lag humans on span-level F-score: **Medium**
