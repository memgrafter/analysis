# A CDF-First Framework for Free-Form Density Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25204
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25204
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** On synthetic tasks with complex topologies, the method achieves significantly lower sum-of-squared error (SSE) than all baselines; on 5 of 7 UCI datasets, it lowers negative log-likelihood (NLL) by up to 22.3%.

## Executive Summary
Direct conditional probability density function (PDF) estimation is mathematically ill-posed because differentiation amplifies finite-sample noise. This paper proposes a CDF-first framework that instead models the stable conditional cumulative distribution function (CDF) and recovers the PDF via finite differences. The CDF is parameterized by a Smooth Min-Max (SMM) network, which enforces monotonicity and boundary conditions by construction. An autoregressive decomposition handles multivariate outputs. Experiments on synthetic tasks with disconnected modes and rings show dramatic improvements in capturing sharp boundaries and topological holes. On UCI datasets, the method often lowers NLL and improves probabilistic calibration.

## Method Summary
The framework factorizes the conditional joint density into a product of univariate conditional densities via autoregression: \(p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}) = \prod_i p(y_i | \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}_{<i})\). For each factor, the conditional CDF \(F_i(y_i | \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}_{<i})\) is modeled directly by a masked Smooth Min-Max (SMM) network. The SMM uses smooth approximations of min/max operations and strictly positive weights to guarantee monotonicity in \(y_i\). Boundary normalization linearly rescales the raw output to ensure \(F_i(-1)=0\) and \(F_i(1)=1\). The conditional PDF is then approximated by central finite differences with a small step \(\delta\). During training, Gaussian noise is injected on both \(\mathbf{x}\) and normalized \(\mathbf{y}\) to regularize the CDF estimate and prevent overfitting to discrete samples. The final density in the original space is obtained via a change-of-variables adjustment from the normalization. The loss combines the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of the derived densities with a KL regularization on the noise scales.

## Key Results
- Synthetic tasks (Disconnected Squares, Elastic Ring): Achieves significantly lower sum-of-squared error (SSE) than all baseline methods, preserving sharp boundaries and topological holes that other approaches (MDNs, flows) miss.
- UCI datasets (7 tasks): Lowers NLL on 5 datasets, with relative improvements up to 22.3% over the strongest baselines.
- Calibration: Demonstrates lower Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on Concrete and Fish datasets, indicating better probabilistic calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Direct PDF estimation is ill-posed because differentiation is an unbounded operator that amplifies high-frequency noise in the empirical distribution. Finite-sample fluctuations in the empirical measure \(\mu_N\) cause the Radon-Nikodym derivative \(p = d\mu/dy\) to become unstable, forcing existing methods to impose restrictive architectural biases that limit expressivity on complex topologies.

### Mechanism 2
Conditional CDF estimation is well-posed: the mapping from the probability measure \(\mu\) to its CDF \(F(y|x) = \mu((-\infty,y]|x)\) is continuous under weak convergence. Integration acts as a low-pass filter, making \(F\) robust to finite-sample noise. The PDF can then be recovered stably by differentiating the smooth learned CDF.

### Mechanism 3
The Smooth Min-Max (SMM) network parameterization enforces valid CDF properties (monotonicity, boundary values) by construction. Smooth soft-min/soft-max layers with positive weights guarantee strict monotonicity in the input \(y_i\), and boundary normalization exacty sets \(F(-1)=0, F(1)=1\). This yields an infinitely differentiable, bounded CDF from which finite-difference differentiation produces a stable, non-negative PDF.

## Foundational Learning
- **Radon-Nikodym derivative and ill-posed inverse problems:** PDF estimation is a derivative of a measure; differentiation is unbounded under weak convergence, unlike integration. *Quick check: Why does integration stabilize while differentiation destabilizes finite-sample estimates?*
- **Autoregressive factorization:** Decomposes joint density into sequential univariate conditionals, simplifying constraints while preserving full dependency structure. *Quick check: How does autoregression avoid modeling a high-dimensional CDF directly?*
- **Finite-difference approximation:** Numerical derivative of the learned smooth CDF; trade-off between truncation error (large \(\delta\)) and floating-point noise (small \(\delta\)). *Quick check: What factors determine the optimal finite-difference step size?*

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input normalization → Noise injection → Autoregressive masked SMM → Boundary normalization → Finite differences → Change-of-variables → Loss

Critical path: For each batch, inject synchronized noise into normalized \(\mathbf{x}\) and \(\mathbf{y}\). For autoregressive dimension \(i\), compute \(F_i(\hat{y}_i | \hat{\mathbf{x}}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{<i})\) using a masked SMM that attends only to previous \(y_j\). Approximate \(\hat{p}_i\) via central finite difference at \(\hat{y}_i\). Transform \(\hat{p}_i\) back to original space using the normalization Jacobian. Sum \(-\log \hat{p}_i\) across dimensions and add KL regularization on noise scales \(\alpha_x, \alpha_y\).

Design tradeoffs:
- SMM smoothness (via SoftMin/SoftMax) ensures differentiability but adds computational cost vs. hard min-max.
- Autoregressive decomposition guarantees validity but forces sequential sampling, limiting parallelization.
- Noise injection strength (\(\alpha\)) regularizes but excessive smoothing washes out density features; tuned via KL penalty.

Failure signatures:
- Non-monotonic CDF outputs: Check SMM weight positivity (exp transform) and network capacity.
- Boundary violations: Verify rescaling logic and SMM output range.
- High SSE on synthetic topologies: Likely insufficient smoothing, noise scale mis-set, or SMM expressivity too low.
- Poor NLL/ECE on real data: Inspect autoregressive masking implementation and Jacobian determinant computation.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Toy task visualization:** Train on Disconnected Squares/Elastic Ring, plot conditional densities at selected \(\mathbf{x}\). Confirm preservation of disconnected modes and ring holes, contrasting with MDN/flow failures.
2. **Ablation study:** On Elastic Ring, remove noise injection, replace SMM with hard min-max, or use a monotonic MLP. Measure SSE to validate both SMM and noise regularization as necessary.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Vary finite-difference step \(\delta\) on Elastic Ring; expect optimal performance near \(\delta=5\times10^{-6}\), with larger \(\delta\) causing oversmoothing and smaller \(\delta\) amplifying numerical noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Parallel sampling:** Can a non-autoregressive CDF-first method enable fully parallel generation while preserving validity and expressive power? The current autoregressive factorization is the main scalability bottleneck.
- **High-dimensional outputs:** How to extend the framework efficiently to very high-dimensional \(\mathbf{y}\) (e.g., images) without sequential cost or representational degradation?
- **Finite-sample theory:** What are the statistical error bounds for the CDF-first estimator, especially regarding finite-difference error, network capacity, and noise injection? The well-posedness argument lacks finite-sample rates.
- **Mixed discrete-continuous variables:** Can the framework handle conditional distributions with both discrete and continuous components? The differentiability assumption for PDF recovery precludes direct application to mixed-type responses.

## Limitations
- The ill-posedness argument, while sound, may not fully explain practical PDF estimation failures; implicit regularization in other methods could mitigate noise amplification.
- The method assumes absolute continuity of the true conditional distribution; discrete or singular components break differentiability and invalidate finite-difference PDF recovery.
- Autoregressive sequential sampling scales poorly to high dimensions due to quadratic evaluation cost.
- Training hyperparameters (learning rate, optimizer, early stopping) are incompletely specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- The fixed finite-difference step \(\delta=5\times10^{-6}\) may not be optimal across all datasets; sensitivity is only partially explored.

## Confidence
- **CDF-first superiority over baselines:** Medium (strong synthetic results, mixed UCI performance).
- **SMM guarantees valid CDFs:** High (monotonicity/boundaries mathematically enforced).
- **State-of-the-art performance:** Medium (competitive on UCI but not tested on larger benchmarks).
- **Improved probabilistic calibration:** Low (ECE evidence limited to two datasets without statistical significance).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate SMM:** Replace SMM with a standard monotonic network (e.g., input-convex with non-negative weights) while keeping the CDF-first pipeline. Compare SSE/NLL on synthetic tasks to isolate SMM's contribution.
2. **Finite-difference robustness:** Compute PDFs via automatic differentiation of the learned CDF (bypassing finite differences) and evaluate metrics across a sweep of \(\delta\) values (e.g., \(10^{-7}\) to \(10^{-4}\)). Validate that results are stable near the chosen \(\delta=5\times10^{-6}\).
3. **Scaling study:** Train the method on a high-dimensional conditional density benchmark (e.g., CIFAR-10 or ImageNet-32 conditioned on labels). Compare NLL, sample quality, and wall-clock time against autoregressive flows and diffusion models to assess practical scalability beyond moderate-dimensional UCI tasks.
