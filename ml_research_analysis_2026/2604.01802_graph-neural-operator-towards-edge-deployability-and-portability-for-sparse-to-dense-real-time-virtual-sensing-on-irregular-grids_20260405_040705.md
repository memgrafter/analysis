# Graph Neural Operator Towards Edge Deployability and Portability for Sparse-to-Dense, Real-Time Virtual Sensing on Irregular Grids

## Quick Facts
* arXiv ID: 2604.01802
* Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01802
* Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
Virtual sensing of full multiphysics fields from sparse boundary measurements on irregular geometries is essential for real‑time monitoring but is traditionally limited by the computational cost of physics solvers and the deployability of neural operators. VIRSO introduces a graph neural operator with a spectral‑spatial collaborative architecture and a mesh‑informed Variable‑KNN (V‑KNN) graph construction that adapts connectivity to local geometric complexity. Evaluated on nuclear thermal‑hydraulic benchmarks, it reconstructs fields at compression ratios as high as 156:1 with mean relative L₂ errors under 1%, while using fewer parameters than prior baselines. On a high‑end NVIDIA H200, the full 10‑layer configuration lowers the energy‑delay product from ~206 to 10.1 J·ms. On an embedded Jetson Orin Nano, all configurations operate below 10 W and deliver sub‑second inference (down to 58.8 ms), proving edge feasibility without retraining or quantization. This work thus bridges high‑fidelity virtual sensing with practical edge computing.

## Method Summary
VIRSO processes unstructured meshes through a graph neural operator that alternates spectral and spatial convolutions. The graph is built using Variable‑KNN, where the number of neighbors for each node is determined by the local geometric complexity extracted from the mesh; this dynamic connectivity replaces fixed‑K strategies and better adapts to irregular domains. Spectral graph convolutions operate in the Laplacian eigenbasis to capture global dependencies efficiently, while spatial message passing aggregates information over local neighborhoods to preserve fine‑scale structure. The model is trained end‑to‑end to map sparse boundary sensor readings to dense field predictions on the entire mesh. The hybrid design yields high accuracy with a reduced parameter count compared to standard graph operators, enabling real‑time inference on edge hardware.

## Key Results
* Mean relative L₂ error below 1% on nuclear thermal‑hydraulic benchmarks with sparse‑to‑dense reconstruction ratios up to 156:1.
* On NVIDIA H200, full 10‑layer VIRSO reduces energy‑delay product from ~206 to 10.1 J·ms versus a standard graph operator baseline.
* On an embedded NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, all configurations consume sub‑10 W power and achieve sub‑second latency (minimum 58.8 ms for the spectral‑only variant) without retraining or quantization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VIRSO combines two key innovations that jointly address accuracy and efficiency. The V‑KNN graph construction tailors connectivity to local geometry: complex regions automatically receive more neighbors for detailed representation, while smoother areas use fewer edges, reducing unnecessary computation. The spectral‑spatial collaborative architecture interleaves global spectral convolutions (which have a large effective receptive field and few parameters) with local spatial convolutions (which respect the mesh geometry and enable equivariance). This synergy captures both long‑range dependencies and fine‑grained variations, overcoming the limitations of purely spectral or purely spatial graph operators. The resulting parameter efficiency and adaptive graph structure make the model lightweight enough for edge deployment while maintaining reconstruction fidelity even at high compression ratios.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Sparse boundary measurements + Mesh geometry → V‑KNN Graph Builder → Spectral‑Spatial Collaborative Operator (L stacked layers) → Dense field prediction on all mesh nodes.

**Critical path:**  
1. V‑KNN neighborhood search (per‑node degree computation).  
2. For each layer: spectral convolution (multiply by precomputed Laplacian eigenvectors), spatial message passing (aggregate neighbor features), nonlinear activation, and linear transform.  
3. Final linear projection to output channels.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *V‑KNN adaptivity*: improves accuracy on complex regions but adds per‑node degree selection cost.  
- *Spectral vs spatial*: spectral offers global context with fewer parameters but requires eigendecomposition (precomputed), spatial provides local equivariance but needs many layers for long‑range coupling.  
- *Model depth*: deeper networks increase accuracy but raise energy‑delay and latency; VIRSO’s 10‑layer version may exceed the tightest edge budgets.  
- *Full‑precision inference*: avoids quantization overhead but needs sufficient memory; the model’s compact size enables this on mid‑range edge GPUs but not on ultra‑low‑power devices.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Accuracy collapse on coarse or low‑quality meshes, indicating sensitivity to input mesh fineness.  
- Inability to meet sub‑second latency or sub‑10 W power on devices weaker than Jetson Orin Nano.  
- Excessive memory consumption if certain mesh elements induce very high V‑KNN degrees.  
- Poor generalization to other multiphysics couplings due to training on a single domain.

**First three experiments:**  
1. *Graph connectivity ablation*: Compare V‑KNN against fixed‑KNN (various K) for accuracy, runtime, and memory across mesh densities.  
2. *Component isolation*: Evaluate spectral‑only, spatial‑only, and full collaborative variants to quantify each branch’s contribution.  
3. *Hardware scaling*: Deploy the same model on a spectrum of devices (e.g., desktop GPU, Jetson Orin Nano, Raspberry Pi 5) measuring power, latency, and memory, including tests with INT8 quantization.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to a single application domain (nuclear thermal‑hydraulics); generalization to other multiphysics problems or highly irregular 3D geometries is unproven.  
- Hardware testing limited to two GPUs (NVIDIA H200, Jetson Orin Nano); results may not hold for other edge processors, CPUs, or memory‑constrained devices.  
- The V‑KNN method relies on high‑quality mesh data; its robustness to coarse or noisy meshes is not reported.
