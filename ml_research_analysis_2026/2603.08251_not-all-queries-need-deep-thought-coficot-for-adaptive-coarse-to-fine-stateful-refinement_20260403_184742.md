# Not All Queries Need Deep Thought: CoFiCot for Adaptive Coarse-to-fine Stateful Refinement

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.08251
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08251
*   **Reference Count:** 34
*   **Primary Result:** CoFiCot achieves superior accuracy (e.g., +4.0% on Llama-3-8B over Best-of-k, +6.5% on MATH) while consuming fewer tokens than 120-way self-consistency, demonstrating a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

## Executive Summary
CoFiCot introduces an adaptive coarse-to-fine framework that addresses the uniform computation paradox in LLM reasoning. It first generates a small set of diverse chain-of-thought (CoT) solutions. A multi-metric classifier then triages each query as Easy, Medium, or Hard. Simple problems are answered efficiently via weighted aggregation of the initial samples. Complex problems enter a stateful sequential correction loop, where a Process Reward Model (PRM) localizes errors and a correction function repairs them while preserving verified historical context. Across seven benchmarks, this differentiated approach yields significant accuracy gains and better token efficiency compared to uniform compute allocation strategies like Best-of-k and self-consistency.

## Method Summary
CoFiCot operates in two stages. First, a base LLM generates `k=40` diverse CoT solutions for a query. Stage 1 computes three metrics from this set: (1) **semantic entropy** of answer clusters (low entropy indicates consensus), (2) **consensus reliability** (Z-score of the majority answer's reward model score), and (3) **predicted reasoning depth** (from an LLM step-count estimate). These are synthesized into a difficulty label (`Easy`, `Medium`, `Hard`). `Easy` problems skip to final weighted voting (ORM-weighted). `Medium`/`Hard` problems enter Stage 2, an iterative loop: a PRM scores every step, the first erroneous step (score < `τ_step`) is identified, and a stateful corrector `Φ` generates a new step conditioned on the original query, the erroneous step, PRM feedback, and the *frozen, verified history* of prior correct steps; all subsequent steps are regenerated. The expanded solution pool is trimmed via ORM-based top-k selection, and the classifier re-evaluates difficulty for potential early exit (max iterations: 2 for Medium, 3 for Hard). The final answer aggregates the terminal solution set.

## Key Results
*   Achieves **+4.0%** absolute accuracy on Llama-3-8B over Best-of-k and **+6.5%** on the MATH benchmark.
*   Uses **fewer total tokens** than 120-way self-consistency while achieving higher accuracy.
*   Starting from only `k=40` samples, CoFiCot already surpasses the performance of 120-way SC and Best-of-k, highlighting the efficiency of adaptive compute.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adaptive Triage via Multi-Metric Classifier
**Claim:** CoFiCot avoids wasteful uniform computation by triaging query difficulty early, routing simple problems to cheap aggregation and complex ones to expensive iterative correction.
**How:** From an initial sample set, the classifier computes: (1) **Semantic entropy** of final answer clusters (low entropy = high confidence), (2) **Consensus reliability** (Z-normalized ORM score of the majority cluster), and (3) **Predicted reasoning depth** (LLM-estimated step count). Thresholds on these scores yield a difficulty label.
**Core assumption:** Difficulty correlates with the stochastic properties of a small, initial solution set and a lightweight complexity prediction, without requiring full solution.
**Evidence:** Ablations removing the coarse stage drop accuracy on simple datasets (e.g., GSM8K), confirming that over-correction harms easy problems. The paper's sensitivity analysis shows <0.5% variance from uniform metric weighting, supporting robustness.
**Break condition:** Misclassifying a hard problem as `Easy` skips needed refinement, causing failure. Over-classifying easy problems as `Hard` wastes tokens.

### Mechanism 2: Stateful Sequential Correction for Coherent Repair
**Claim:** Corrections must be stateful—conditioned on the verified history of prior steps—to maintain global logical coherence and avoid context fragmentation.
**How:** For a flawed solution, the PRM identifies the first erroneous step. The corrector `Φ` then generates a replacement step using: the original query `Q`, the erroneous step, PRM feedback, and the frozen, verified history `H` of all steps *before* the error. All downstream steps are subsequently regenerated from this new state, cascade-updating dependent logic.
**Core assumption:** Localized error correction must propagate downstream; stateless full-chain regeneration breaks causal dependencies between steps.
**Evidence:** The paper contrasts this with stateless methods (e.g., "fix the error" prompts), which struggle with coherence. Ablations removing the fine stage (stateful correction) show a significant drop on complex tasks like MATH, indicating the value of preserving history.
**Break condition:** A mislocalizing PRM (flagging a correct step or missing an error) causes the correction to target the wrong point, introducing new inconsistencies or failing to fix the root cause.

### Mechanism 3: Differentiated Refinement for Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off
**Claim:** The combination of (a) efficient aggregation for `Easy` problems and (b) targeted, PRM-guided stateful correction for `Medium`/`Hard` problems yields a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off versus uniform allocation.
**How:** `Easy` problems bypass the iterative loop entirely, using weighted voting on the initial `R0`. `Medium`/`Hard` problems enter the refinement loop where the stateful corrector expands the candidate pool, and an ORM selects top-k solutions for the next iteration. The loop has dynamic early exit: if re-classification yields `Easy`, it terminates immediately, avoiding further tokens.
**Core assumption:** The high cost of iterative correction is only justified for problems where the initial solution set shows low confidence/consensus and high predicted complexity.
**Evidence:** Figure 3 shows CoFiCot (`k=40`) already outperforms 120-way SC and Best-of-k (`k=120`) on several benchmarks. Total token counts are lower because `Easy` problems (which dominate many datasets) are solved cheaply without refinement loops.
**Break condition:** An overly aggressive early exit (misclassifying a still-unstable `Medium` problem as `Easy`) selects from an under-refined set, harming accuracy. Conversely, a too-conservative exit wastes tokens on already-converged problems.

## Foundational Learning

*   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** CoFiCot operates on and refines intermediate reasoning steps. *Why needed:* Understanding that LLMs generate step-by-step traces is fundamental to seeing why error localization and sequential correction are possible. *Quick check:* If a model generates a correct final answer via CoT but with a flawed intermediate step, what risk does this pose for self-correction methods?
*   **Process Reward Models (PRMs) vs. Outcome Reward Models (ORMs):** PRMs provide step-level feedback for error localization, while ORMs score full solutions for selection. *Why needed:* Distinguishing their roles is key: PRMs guide fine-grained repair; ORMs judge holistic quality for final voting. *Quick check:* Why would using only a PRM (by aggregating step scores) for final answer selection be suboptimal compared to using an ORM?
*   **Semantic Entropy & Consensus-Based Uncertainty:** The classifier uses the entropy of clustered final answers to measure predictive uncertainty. *Why needed:* This cheap signal from the initial sample set is a core input for triaging difficulty. *Quick check:* A problem yields 40 solutions: 38 give the same wrong answer, and 2 give two different wrong answers. What is the semantic entropy, and what difficulty label is likely?
*   **Stateful vs. Stateless Iterative Refinement:** Stateful methods preserve verified context across corrections; stateless methods regenerate entire chains from scratch. *Why needed:* Understanding the context fragmentation failure of stateless methods highlights why stateful propagation is necessary for coherent error repair. *Quick check:* In a stateless "regenerate the whole chain" loop, what happens to the logically correct steps that preceded the identified error?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
1.  **Stage 0 (Sampling):** Base LLM + high temperature → generates `k=40` diverse CoT traces (`R0`).
2.  **Stage 1 (Classifier):** Three parallel analyzers → (a) Clusterizer + Entropy Calculator → Confidence score, (b) Consensus Analyzer + Z-scorer → Reliability score, (c) Complexity Predictor (LLM prompt) → `N_steps`. Synthesizer (weighted average) → `D_final`.
3.  **Stage 2 (Refinement Loop for Medium/Hard):** PRM Scorer (scores every step) → Error Localizer (finds first step < `τ_step`) → Context-Aware Corrector `Φ` (prompts LLM with: `Q`, erroneous step, PRM feedback, **frozen verified history `H`**) → generates corrected step and re-generates remainder. ORM Scorer (scores full refined solutions) → Selector (merges old `R_curr` and new `R_refined`, picks top-k by ORM) → Re-evaluator (runs Stage 1 classifier on new `R_curr` for early exit).
4.  **Final Aggregation:** Weighted Self-Consistency on final `R_final` (ORM weights).

**Critical Path:**  
Query → `R0` (Stage 0) → Classify (Stage 1) → **IF `Easy`: Direct to Final Aggregation. ELSE: Enter Stage2 Loop** → Correction (PRM+`Φ`) → Selection (ORM) → Re-classify → (Loop until `Easy` or `Iter_max`) → Final Aggregation → Answer.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Latency vs. Efficiency:** Sequential correction (Stage 2) has higher per-iteration latency than parallel sampling (SC), but total token consumption is lower by avoiding refinement on `Easy` problems.
*   **Metric Weighting:** Uniform weights (`w_i=1/3`) are robust but not dataset-optimal; tuning could improve accuracy but harms generality.
*   **PRM Quality:** Correction quality hinges entirely on PRM step-level scoring; a weak PRM mislocalizes errors and corrupts the chain.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Misclassification (Stage 1):** Hard→`Easy` skips refinement; Simple→`Hard` wastes tokens and risks over-correction.
*   **PRM Failure:** High score on an erroneous step or low score on a correct step → correction targets wrong point → corrupts solution.
*   **Early Exit Misfire:** Exits too early on a still-unstable `Medium` problem → answer from under-refined set.
*   **History Corruption:** The "frozen history" `H` contains an undetected earlier error → correction builds on a flawed foundation, propagating inconsistency.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Ablation: Remove Coarse Stage.** Route *all* problems through Stage 2. Expect accuracy drop on simple datasets (GSM8K) due to over-correction, confirming triage necessity.
2.  **Ablation: Remove Statefulness.** Implement stateless refinement (regenerate entire chain with feedback). Expect drop on complex datasets (MATH) due to context fragmentation, demonstrating statefulness value.
3.  **Token-Efficiency Curve:** Plot accuracy vs. total tokens for CoFiCot vs. `k`-SC (`k=40,120`). Expect CoFiCot to achieve higher accuracy at equal or lower token counts, especially on harder benchmarks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  How robust is CoFiCot's error correction mechanism when the PRM provides inaccurate step-level feedback, such as mislocalizing errors or assigning misleading scores?
2.  Can the coarse-grained difficulty classifier's thresholds (e.g., confidence >0.6 for Easy) be optimized or learned per model/dataset rather than using fixed tertiles and heuristic weights?
3.  Does the stateful sequential correction mechanism inadvertently amplify initial correction errors, and how does this affect performance on problems with high uncertainty?
4.  How does CoFiCot generalize to domains lacking clear step-level ground truth or where "correctness" is subjective (e.g., creative writing, open-ended reasoning), given its reliance on PRMs and ORMs?

## Limitations
*   Underspecified clustering method for semantic answer grouping and missing implementation details for the stateful corrector `Φ` (prompt composition, history injection) hinder faithful reproduction.
*   Fixed thresholds (e.g., for difficulty classification, error detection) may be brittle across datasets and model scales; generalizability is未经充分验证.
*   The framework's effectiveness heavily depends on the quality of external PRMs and ORMs; mislocalization or inaccurate scoring can lead to incorrect corrections or suboptimal final selection.

## Confidence
*   **Coarse triage mechanism:** Medium. Ablations confirm its necessity, but validation of the specific three-metric synergy is weak (neighbor papers do not establish this combination).
*   **Stateful correction mechanism:** Medium. Conceptual rationale is solid and ablations support the fine stage, but isolating the incremental benefit of statefulness over stateless refinement remains unproven.
*   **Differentiated refinement (trade-off):** High. Empirical gains across seven benchmarks strongly demonstrate the accuracy-efficiency improvement, with clear ablations and scaling curves.
