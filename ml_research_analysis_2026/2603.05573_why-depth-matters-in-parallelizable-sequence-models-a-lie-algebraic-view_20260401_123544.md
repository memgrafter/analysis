# Why Depth Matters in Parallelizable Sequence Models: A Lie Algebraic View

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05573
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05573
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Depth exponentially reduces approximation error in parallelizable sequence models; for bounded word problems, the required depth scales as O(log T) (theoretically ⌈log₂ T⌉+1).

## Executive Summary
Parallelizable sequence models such as Transformers and state-space models (SSMs) offer computational efficiency but often at the cost of expressivity, particularly for order-sensitive tasks. This paper leverages Lie theory to analyze how increasing depth mitigates this trade-off. The authors model depth as a tower of Lie algebra extensions, quantify approximation error via commutator mass in the Magnus expansion, and prove that error decays exponentially with depth. For word problems of non-solvable groups, the required depth grows only logarithmically with sequence length, a bound confirmed through experiments on symbolic tasks (e.g., the alternating group A5) and continuous 3D rotations. Despite theoretical exponential benefits, practical training of deep parallelizable models remains challenging due to optimization instabilities.

## Method Summary
The study combines theoretical Lie algebraic analysis with empirical evaluation. Theoretically, restricted parallelizable SSMs (those with abelian single-layer generators) are shown to have limited expressivity, incurring unavoidable error on order-sensitive tasks. By stacking such layers, the model realizes solvable Lie algebras, with depth determining the derived length and thus the complexity of representable flows. The error scaling is derived from truncating the Magnus expansion, linking depth to nilpotency class. Empirically, the authors train various architectures (Transformer, GLA, signed Mamba, AUSSM, DeltaProduct) on word problems derived from groups of different algebraic classes (abelian, nilpotent, solvable, non-solvable) and on a continuous rotation prediction task. They measure sequence-level accuracy and mean squared error, respectively, to assess how depth affects performance across sequence lengths.

## Key Results
- Approximation error decreases exponentially as model depth increases.
- For word problems of non-solvable groups, the minimal depth required to achieve high accuracy scales as O(log T) (theoretically ⌈log₂ T⌉+1).
- Experiments confirm systematic improvement with depth for both symbolic (A5) and continuous rotation tasks, although deep models sometimes suffer from optimization instability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Single-layer restricted SSMs (e.g., transformers, diagonal SSMs) have an abelian Lie algebra, limiting expressivity to order-symmetric tasks and incurring inherent approximation error on order-sensitive tasks.
**Mechanism:** Permutation-equivariant operations (self-attention) or diagonal state transitions commute, forming an abelian algebra. This implies flows are order-invariant, mismatching tasks with non-abelian structure. The error manifests as non-zero commutator mass in the Magnus expansion, which measures order sensitivity.
**Core assumption:** The model’s generator set `{A(x)}` is closed under permutation or diagonal structure, forcing `Lie({A(x)})` to be abelian.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "Order-symmetric models incur an unavoidable approximation error when the task is order sensitive. Increasing depth extends expressivity."
- [section 2.2]: "We say `S_g` is restricted if `Lie({A(x)})` is abelian."
- [corpus]: Weak direct corpus support; however, cited works (Merrill et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023) align with abelian limitation in parallel models.
**Break condition:** The mechanism fails if the single-layer model uses non-commutative generators (e.g., general SSMs like DeltaProduct). Then `Lie({A(x)})` can be non-abelian and exact simulation possible.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Deep restricted SSMs realize solvable Lie algebras via a tower of abelian extensions. The required depth scales logarithmically with sequence length for bounded word problems.
**Mechanism:** Each layer contributes an abelian algebra; cascading them builds solvable algebras with derived length proportional to depth. For word problems of non-solvable groups (e.g., A5), the Chen-Fox-Lyndon factorization reduces the problem to a bounded number of bracket operations. Theorem 3.4 shows an abelian k-layer SSM simulates any solvable algebra of derived length k.
**Core assumption:** Tasks can be framed as Lie group actions (e.g., word problems) and the lifting construction faithfully decomposes flows into cascaded abelian systems.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "Theory formulates correspondence between depth and tower of Lie algebra extensions."
- [section 3.2]: Proposition 3.3: "Restricted k-layer SSMs are solvable with at most 2k derived length." Theorem 3.4: "Abelian k-layer SSM simulates `S_g` with derived length k."
- [section 4.2]: Figure 2 shows depth-generalization trends for A5 follow `⌈log₂ T⌉+1`.
**Break condition:** Training dynamics may prevent reaching the theoretical construct; deeper models sometimes underperform (Section 4) due to optimization barriers, not theory limits.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The approximation error decays exponentially with depth. Specifically, for a non-solvable target SSM, the local simulation error of an abelian k-layer SSM scales as `O(ϵ^{2^{k-1}+1})`, where `ϵ` is the generator mass.
**Mechanism:** The leading error term arises from the highest Magnus term not captured by the simulator. Depth increases the nilpotency class the simulator can represent, truncating the Magnus series at higher order. Nilpotentization error then scales exponentially with depth because each layer squares the effective `ϵ`-factor.
**Core assumption:** The generator mass `ϵ = ∫||A(x(t))|| dt` is small enough for the Magnus expansion to converge locally. Long horizons are handled by partitioning (Equation 7).
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "Error diminishes exponentially as the depth increases."
- [section 3]: Corollary 3.6: "Error scales with `O(ϵ^{2^{k-1}+1})`."
- [section 4.3]: Figure 3 shows continuous rotation task error decreasing with depth for transformer, GLA, Signed Mamba.
- [corpus]: Weak direct corpus support; the paper builds on prior works (e.g., Blanes et al., 2009 on Magnus series truncation) which anchor the exponential scaling.
**Break condition:** Finite-precision arithmetic or optimization failures can cause error to plateau or increase after certain depth (observed in Figure 3 deep models), deviating from the asymptotic exponential decay.

## Foundational Learning
- **Lie groups, Lie algebras, and solvability metrics** (derived series, nilpotency class). *Why needed:* The entire theory maps model depth to algebraic properties (solvable, nilpotent). Understanding these classes is essential to grasp expressivity bounds and depth requirements. *Quick check:* "If a SSM’s generator Lie algebra has derived length 3, what is the minimum number of abelian layers needed to simulate its flows according to Theorem 3.4?"
- **Magnus expansion and commutator mass**. *Why needed:* Magnus expansion quantifies order-relevant deviation from abelian behavior. The second-order commutator mass directly measures local error for single-layer restricted models; higher-order terms matter for deeper simulators. *Quick check:* "Why does the Magnus expansion’s second-order term vanish for abelian Lie algebras? What does that imply about their order sensitivity?"
- **Word problems and Chen-Fox-Lyndon factorization**. *Why needed:* Word problems define symbolic state-tracking tasks with well-defined algebraic complexity. The proof of Proposition 3.7 uses Lyndon words to show bounded-length word problems require depth only logarithmic in T. *Quick check:* "How does the canonical factorization of words into Lyndon words reduce the effective ‘bracket depth’ needed to simulate a long word?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Depth (L) ↔ Lie algebra extension tower height/derived length; Each layer (restricted SSM) ↔ abelian subalgebra extension; State space dimension ↔ dimension of the Lie group representation (may expand exponentially); Generator mass ϵ ↔ accumulated norm of state transition matrices over a window.

**Critical path:**  
1. Classify target task’s algebraic class (e.g., via its group/monoid).  
2. Determine minimal derived length or nilpotency class needed (from word problem or state transition).  
3. Use Proposition 3.7 or Corollary 3.6 to set depth: `L ≥ ⌈log₂ T⌉+1` for bounded T, or `L ≥ k` for derived length k.  
4. Check required state dimension: `n` may need to scale exponentially with T if using worst-case construction.  
5. Validate empirically that training converges; if not, consider architectural modifications (e.g., better initialization, normalization) or hybrid model (e.g., DeltaProduct).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Depth vs. state size: Depth reduces error with logarithmic growth, but worst-case width grows exponentially in T. In practice, structured tasks may need much less width.  
- Restricted vs. general: Restricted (parallelizable) models require depth for expressivity; general SSMs (e.g., DeltaProduct) may solve tasks with constant depth but lose parallelizability.  
- Trainability: Deep restricted models often show optimization instability. Hyperparameter tuning (learning rate, depth schedule) may be needed; consider residual connections or normalization.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No improvement beyond certain depth: might indicate optimization failure (e.g., vanishing gradients) rather than theoretical limit.  
- Poor length generalization despite adequate depth: could be from insufficient state dimension (width) or non-ideal initialization.  
- Training loss does not decrease for deep models on simple tasks: check implementation, ensure proper normalization and stable discretization.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Symbolic word problems (Table 2):** Train on length 128, test length 256. Compare 1- vs 2-layer models across abelian (C2, C3), nilpotent (D8, H3), solvable (S3, S4), non-solvable (A5). Expect: abelian tasks solved at L=1 by some models; non-abelian require more layers or fail within tested L.

Expect: deeper models handle longer sequences, following roughly logarithmic curve; deep models sometimes degrade due to optimization.  
3. **Continuous rotation prediction (Figure 3):** Predict rotated vectors after sequences of A5 elements. Measure MSE vs. sequence length for different L. Expect: error decreases with L on both train and test; but deep models may saturate or become unstable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do different forms of positional encoding, particularly multiplicative PE, enhance the expressivity of parallelizable sequence models within the Lie‑algebraic framework? (Section 5.1)
- **Open Question 2:** How does finite‑precision arithmetic interact with the exponential error‑reduction predicted by depth, and does it impair the model’s ability to exploit depth on order‑sensitive tasks? (Section 5.1)
- **Open Question 3:** What training‑algorithm modifications (e.g., initialization, optimizer choices, regularisation) can improve learnability of deep parallelizable sequence models so they reach theoretical depth‑dependent error bounds? (Section 5.1)
- **Open Question 4:** What factors fundamentally hinder the performance of deeper parallelizable sequence models on order‑sensitive tasks, despite the theoretical exponential mitigation of error with depth? (Section 4.1)
- **Open Question 5:** Does the conjectured limitation hold: for solvable tasks, restricted layers (e.g., signed diagonals) offer no expressivity advantage over abelian layers beyond a constant depth factor? (Remark 3.9)

## Limitations
- Data generation details (random seeds, element distribution) missing, hindering exact replication.  
- Optimal hyperparameters per model/depth unreported, so best achievable performance is uncertain.  
- Deep models frequently encounter optimization barriers (gradient issues), indicating a gap between theoretical expressivity and trainability.  
- AUSSM instability root causes and complete fixes are unclear.  
- DeltaProduct’s fixed number of Householder products lacks per-group theoretical justification.  
- Worst‑case state dimension grows exponentially with sequence length, limiting scalability.

- Depth‑solvability correspondence & O(log T) scaling: **Medium**  
- Exponential error decay with depth: **Medium**
