# Tackling Over-smoothing on Hypergraphs: A Ricci Flow-guided Neural Diffusion Approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15696
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15696
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: RFHND achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy, ranking first on average across benchmarks, and effectively mitigates over-smoothing in hypergraph neural networks through Ricci flow-guided diffusion.

## Executive Summary
Hypergraph neural networks (HGNNs) suffer from over-smoothing as network depth increases, limiting their ability to capture higher-order structural relationships. This paper introduces discrete Ricci flow from differential geometry to regulate feature evolution on hypergraphs. The proposed method, RFHND, frames node feature updates as a continuous partial differential equation where hyperedge weights adapt to local curvature, controlling information diffusion and preventing feature homogenization. Theoretical analysis provides guarantees of bounded Dirichlet energy and exponential convergence of curvature. Extensive experiments on academic and real-world hypergraph benchmarks demonstrate that RFHND achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy, maintains stable performance and Dirichlet energy even with many layers, exhibits robustness to feature and structural noise, and outperforms baselines on synthetic heterophilic hypergraphs. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both cosine-based modulation and learned curvature, offering a geometric foundation for mitigating over-smoothing in hypergraph representation learning.

## Method Summary
RFHND formulates node feature updates as a continuous PDE derived from discrete Ricci flow. Hyperedge weights are dynamically modulated by the cosine similarity of node features within each hyperedge, making aggregation adapt to semantic coherence. A hypernetwork (HyperNet) approximates a curvature term κ′ₑ(t) from aggregated hyperedge features, which then scales the feature update direction. The update rule projects feature differences onto tangent spaces, allowing positive curvature to smooth and negative curvature to sharpen features, preserving node distinguishability. The system is integrated via explicit Euler with a step size τ that satisfies a stability condition (τ ≤ 1/max row sum of diffusion matrix). This geometric regulation ensures that the Dirichlet energy remains bounded and curvature converges exponentially, theoretically preventing over-smoothing.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy, ranking first on average across academic (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, Cora-CA, DBLP-CA) and real-world (Zoo, NTU2012, ModelNet40, Walmart, Senate, House) hypergraph benchmarks.
- Maintains stable Dirichlet energy and classification performance with increased network depth (e.g., up to 40 layers), demonstrating effective mitigation of over-smoothing.
- Exhibits robustness to feature and structural noise; ablation studies confirm the necessity of both cosine-based weight modulation and learned curvature from HyperNet.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Adaptive weight modulation via cosine similarity**  
Hyperedge weights we(t) are defined as we(t) = (1/αₑ)[(1/|e|) Σ_{i,j∈e} cos(xᵢ(t),xⱼ(t)) √(dᵢdⱼ)] + 1 + ϵ, where αₑ normalizes and ϵ is small. This makes weights reflect feature coherence: high cosine similarity yields higher weight, amplifying contributions from semantically consistent hyperedges. The core assumption is that node features are normalized (|xᵢ(t)|=1) and weights evolve smoothly. Evidence includes the abstract’s claim that “hyperedge weights adapt to local curvature” and Section IV’s Equation (6) proving non-negativity and boundedness. The mechanism breaks if features become orthogonal (cosine ≈ 0), collapsing weights to near 1+ϵ, or if normalization αₑ is poorly estimated.

**Mechanism 2: Curvature-guided feature updates**  
The update ∂xᵢ/∂t aggregates (xⱼ – cos(xᵢ,xⱼ)xᵢ) scaled by κ′ₑ(t) for all j in hyperedge e incident to i. The term (xⱼ – cos(xᵢ,xⱼ)xᵢ) captures feature dissimilarity along the tangent direction. Positive curvature (homophilic) reduces dissimilarity (smoothing), while negative curvature (heterophilic) amplifies it (sharpening). The core assumption is that curvature is computed continuously from current features and the scaling ensures symmetric influence. Evidence includes the abstract’s statement on “adaptively regulates the rate of information diffusion at the geometric level” and Section V.A’s Equation (18) explaining bidirectional regulation. The mechanism fails if curvature estimates become zero (flat geometry), reverting to standard averaging and causing over-smoothing, or if curvature signs are misestimated due to noise.

**Mechanism 3: Theoretical boundedness and convergence**  
The continuous PDE system with discrete Ricci flow guarantees a strictly positive lower bound on average Dirichlet energy over any interval (Theorem 1), ensuring features never become completely uniform. Theorem 2 shows curvature converges exponentially to zero, meaning the system stabilizes quickly. The core assumptions are that hyperedge weights we(t) are monotonic on intervals, node features are unit-norm, and curvature satisfies a Lipschitz condition. Evidence is provided in Section IV.A (Theorem 1) and Section IV.B (Theorem 2). The break condition occurs if monotonicity of we(t) fails (e.g., due to oscillations in feature space), invalidating the energy bound, or if step size τ exceeds the stability threshold (Theorem 6), causing numerical divergence.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (node features F ∈ ℝ^{n×dᵢₙ}, incidence matrix H) -> Initial linear projection X(0) -> T iterations (time steps): scatter features to hyperedges, compute curvature κ′ₑ(t) via HyperNet (MLPθ₂), build diffusion matrix S and cosine matrix C, update X(t+1) = X(t) – τ F(X(t))X(t) -> Final linear layer -> Predictions. The critical path emphasizes curvature computation as central: HyperNet takes aggregated hyperedge features (optionally transformed by MLPθ₁) to output κ′ₑ(t), which modulates the diffusion operator. Design tradeoffs include: computational cost (scatter and MLP per hyperedge scale with m and r²), choice of curvature measure (Forman simpler, Ollivier more nuanced), step size τ tuning (stability vs convergence), and HyperNet capacity (expressiveness vs overfitting). Failure signatures: (1) numerical instability if τ > 1/max row sum of S (Theorem 6); (2) vanishing modulation if κ′ₑ(t) → 0, reducing to simple averaging; (3) numerical issues if features drift from unit norm, breaking weight bounds. First 3 experiments: (1) Over-smoothing analysis: train with varying depths (2–40) on Cora, plot accuracy and Dirichlet energy vs depth; (2) Ablation study: remove cosine modulation (w/o COS) and/or HyperNet (w/o HyperNet); (3) Robustness test: add feature noise (Gaussian, uniform, mask) and structure noise (edge deletion/addition) to Citeseer/Cora-CA, measure accuracy drop.

## Limitations
- HyperNet architecture (number of layers, hidden units) is unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Practical details of curvature computation for hyperedges (Forman vs. Ollivier) are unclear, affecting theoretical alignment.
- Hyperparameter tuning relies on broad search ranges; optimal configurations per dataset remain unknown, making precise reproduction difficult.
