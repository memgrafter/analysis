# Directional Reasoning Trajectory Change (DRTC): Identifying Critical Trace Segments in Reasoning Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15332
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15332
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DRTC isolates concentrated causal influences in reasoning traces; top 5% of chunks account for ~0.23-0.28 of total influence, and learned pivots significantly outperform random baselines (e.g., median δ = 0.409 on 500 MATH problems).

## Executive Summary
Understanding long-horizon reasoning in LLMs requires pinpointing when crucial reasoning turns happen and what earlier context causally contributed. Existing interpretability methods only highlight tokens correlated with the answer, not true causal drivers. The Directional Reasoning Trajectory Change (DRTC) method addresses this by first detecting pivot decision points via uncertainty (entropy) and distribution-shift (Jensen-Shannon divergence) signals. It then applies receiver-side attention masking at those pivots to block information from selected earlier chunks while preserving the rest of the rollout. DRTC measures the directional change in the log-probability trajectory relative to the overall rollout direction, yielding signed per-chunk attributions. An additional curvature diagnostic provides a geometric perspective on reasoning bends. Across four reasoning models, DRTC attributions are highly concentrated, learned pivots produce stronger intervention effects than random spans, and the method demonstrates moderate agreement with gradient- and perturbation-based baselines while showing superior outcome linkage under controlled interventions. These results suggest DRTC effectively identifies critical reasoning segments and their causal roles.

## Method Summary
DRTC operates on a fixed on-policy autoregressive rollout of a model solving a reasoning problem, segmented into equal-length token chunks. The core pipeline is:
1. **Pivot discovery**: For every token position, compute a score combining the token's prediction entropy, the margin between the top-2 probabilities (suggesting uncertainty), and the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the distributions of the 5 tokens before and after the position. The top K (default 8) pivots are selected with a minimum spacing of 4 tokens, and their weights are obtained via softmax over scores.
2. **Intervention**: At each pivot, the model's attention mechanism is masked so that the pivot cannot attend to any token in a given chunk. This masking is applied across all layers and query heads, effectively isolating the chunk's contribution to the pivot's logits.
3. **Screening**: After masking, the change in the pivot's log-likelihood for the teacher-forced target token is measured. This local effect is robustly scaled and passed through a sigmoid gate to downweight noise.
4. **Attribution**: The directional effect is computed as the dot product between the difference in the pivot's hidden states (with and without the chunk) and a global direction vector derived from the first and last pivots' states. The per-chunk DRTC score aggregates these signed directional effects across all pivots, weighted by pivot importance.
5. **Curvature diagnostic**: The turning angle in logit space between consecutive pivots is measured to quantify reasoning bends; chunk-level curvature impact is defined similarly but used only as a diagnostic.

The method relies on teacher-forced probing for stable estimates and uses the same rollout throughout, avoiding the complications of free generation.

## Key Results
- **Concentration**: DRTC attributions are sparse, with median Gini coefficients 0.50–0.58 and the top 5% of chunks capturing 0.23–0.28 of total influence across models.
- **Intervention strength**: Learned pivot-masking yields significantly larger changes in pivot likelihoods compared to masking random spans of the same length. Median gaps range from +0.039 to +0.178; in a scaling study on 500 MATH problems, median δ = 0.409 with 355/500 positive effects (p = 2.3×10⁻²¹).
- **Baseline comparison & outcome linkage**: DRTC shows moderate rank correlation with gradient- and perturbation-based attribution methods. Under graded embedding-interpolation interventions, top-ranked DRTC chunks degrade teacher-forced gold-answer log-probability more than matched random chunks on a stability-filtered subset (median paired difference 0.34, sign fraction 74%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DRTC's efficacy stems from three design choices:
- **Pivot selection** via entropy and JS divergence pinpoints tokens where the model's internal distribution shifts, which often correspond to key reasoning steps (e.g., after a calculation or conclusion). Using both uncertainty and distribution shift reduces noise from ordinary tokens.
- **Receiver-side attention masking** ensures that the pivot cannot incorporate information from a targeted chunk, providing a clean counterfactual to isolate the chunk's causal influence on that specific decision.
- **Directional alignment** with the global rollout trajectory (from first to last pivot) assigns a sign to each chunk's effect: chunks that push the model toward the final answer contribute positively, while those that push away contribute negatively. This yields interpretable, signed attributions rather than mere importance scores.
Additionally, the **screening gate** based on local likelihood change downweights chunks whose effect is within the noise floor, sharpening the signal. The resulting attributions naturally concentrate because only a few early chunks meaningfully alter the reasoning direction; the rest have minor or cancelling effects.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Attention Masking**
  - Why needed: To create a controlled counterfactual where a specific earlier context is inaccessible to a later decision, enabling causal attribution.
  - Quick check: What would be the consequence of applying masking only to a subset of attention heads? (Answer: The pivot might still receive the information through un-masked heads, weakening the intervention and contaminating attribution.)
- **Concept: Teacher-Forced Probing**
  - Why needed: Provides a deterministic way to compute logits at arbitrary positions using the ground-truth context, avoiding stochastic generation that would introduce variance and break isolation.
  - Quick check: Why can't we simply let the model generate freely after masking? (Answer: Free generation could diverge from the original rollout, making it impossible to isolate the chunk's effect on the specific pivot token.)
- **Concept: Jensen-Shannon Divergence**
  - Why needed: Measures the distributional change between the context before and after a token, serving as a robust signal for potential pivots where reasoning direction shifts.
  - Quick check: How does using a windowed JS divergence differ from computing divergence at a single token? (Answer: Windows smooth noise and capture broader context shifts, reducing false positives from token-level fluctuations.)
- **Concept: Global Directional Vector**
  - Why needed: To consistently assign polarity (helpful/harmful) to chunk effects across all pivots, using the net reasoning direction as a reference.
  - Quick check: Why define direction using the first and last pivots rather than all pivots? (Answer: It provides a stable, low-variance estimate of the overall trajectory; using all could be noisy and might not reflect the net shift.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw Rollout -> Chunk Splitter -> Pivot Scorer -> Pivot Selector -> Attention Masking Engine -> Logit Differencer -> Screening Gate -> Aggregation Module -> DRTC Scores; Parallel: Logits -> Turning Angle Calculator -> CurvImpact.

**Critical path**: The success of DRTC hinges on (1) accurate pivot discovery (correct hyperparameters and signal combination), (2) faithful implementation of all-layer, all-head attention masking (ensuring the pivot truly cannot attend to the chunk), (3) correct computation of the global direction from the first and last pivots, and (4) proper robust scaling of the screening gate. A deviation in any of these can severely distort attributions.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Chunk size*: Smaller chunks give finer-grained attributions but increase variance; larger chunks smooth effects but may obscure which tokens within the chunk are responsible.
- *Number of pivots (K)*: Larger K captures more decision points but increases computational cost and may include low-quality pivots. The weighting via softmax helps but does not eliminate noise.
- *Masking scope*: Masking all layers/heads is thorough but computationally heavy; selective masking (e.g., only certain layers) could reduce cost but risks missing indirect pathways.
- *Screening gate*: The sigmoid gate with robust scaling aims to filter out noise, yet introduces hyperparameters (β_k) that require careful calibration; too aggressive filtering may discard real effects, too lenient retains noise.

**Failure signatures**:
- Pivot weight concentration is low (e.g., max u_k < 0.1), indicating pivot scores are near-random.
- The C0 vs. C8 invariance test fails (DRTC scores change when curvature logging is toggled), suggesting a bug in masking or aggregation.
- Intervention magnitude (mean |δ|) for learned pivots is not significantly greater than for random spans, implying pivots are not informative or masking is broken.
- High variance in DRTC scores across different random seeds or rollout slices.

**First three experiments**:
1. **Pivot sanity check**: Run pivot discovery on a few examples and manually inspect whether selected pivots align with intuitive reasoning milestones (e.g., after a key equation, before a conclusion).
2. **Masking integrity test**: For a single pivot and chunk, apply the mask and verify that the pivot's attention scores to the chunk are indeed set to -∞ across all layers/heads, and that the pivot's logits change significantly relative to an unmasked baseline.
3. **Distribution comparison**: Compute Gini coefficients and top-5% mass on a small validation set; they should fall within the reported ranges (0.50–0.58 Gini, 0.23–0.28 top-5% mass). Additionally, compare DRTC scores against a random-span control (C9) to ensure a clear gap.

## Limitations
- Exact indices and tokenization of the evaluation rollouts (24-example slice, 500 MATH subset) are unreported, preventing exact replication of experiments.
- The robust scaling procedure for the screening gate (β_k determination, clipping thresholds) is described qualitatively, leaving room for implementation variation.
- The evaluation relies on fixed on-policy rollouts and teacher-forced probing; it remains unclear how DRTC performs on free-generation or diverse model architectures.
