# Elenchus: Generating Knowledge Bases from Prover-Skeptic Dialogues

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06974
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06974
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: End-to-end integration from natural language dialogue to formal, traceable knowledge base with provable logical properties.

## Executive Summary
Elenchus tackles the knowledge engineering bottleneck by treating formalization as explicitation via prover-skeptic dialogue. An LLM opponent acts as a defeasible derivability oracle, proposing tensions in a human expert's bilateral position (commitments/denials). The expert resolves these through retraction, refinement, or contestation, with accepted tensions forming material implications. The resulting dialectical state maps to a material base in Non-Monotonic, Non-Transitive Logic (NMMS), satisfying the Containment norm. A case study on the W3C PROV-O specification produced a material base with 19 commitments and 9 accepted implications from 7 challenges. Verification using pyNMMS confirmed all base consequences derivable and exhibited nontransitivity, nonmonotonicity, and pairwise independence—properties aligning with documented PROV design rationales.

## Method Summary
The Elenchus protocol orchestrates a structured dialogue between a human expert and an LLM opponent. The expert articulates commitments and denials derived from a natural language domain specification. The LLM identifies candidate tensions—incoherences between subsets of commitments and denials—and proposes them as sequents (Γ | ∼ ∆). The expert resolves each tension, and accepted ones are added to the set of material implications (I). The final dialectical state S = <[C:D], T, I> maps to a material base B_S = <L_BS, | ∼_BS>, where L_BS = C ∪ D and | ∼_BS = I ∪ Cont (with Cont encoding the Containment norm). The pyNMMS reasoner then verifies that all implications in I are derivable and checks structural properties (nontransitivity, nonmonotonicity, independence). Implementation uses GitHub issues for persistent state storage and Claude Code as the LLM agent, with Python scripts for formal mapping.

## Key Results
- PROV-O case study: 19 commitments, 9 accepted material implications from 7 challenge-driven tensions.
- pyNMMS verification: all base consequences derivable; structural properties (nontransitivity, nonmonotonicity, pairwise independence) confirmed.
- Structural properties correspond to independently documented PROV design rationales (Moreau et al. 2015).
- Demonstrated end-to-end pipeline from natural language dialogue to formal, traceable knowledge base.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Explicitation through dialectic
**Claim:** Knowledge engineering succeeds by re-conceiving it as explicitation through dialectical examination, not extraction of pre-formed content.
**Mechanism:** The prover-skeptic protocol forces the expert to articulate commitments and denials ([C:D]). The LLM opponent proposes tensions (Γ| ∼∆)—claims that subsets of commitments and denials are jointly incoherent. The expert must resolve each tension by retracting, refining, or contesting. Accepted tensions become material implications (I), structuring the expert's knowledge by exposing inferential connections and incoherences that were implicit in practice.
**Core assumption:** Experts possess tacit inferential commitments that can be made explicit through adversarial coherence-checking dialogue.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "knowledge engineering is re-conceived as explicitation rather than extraction"; [section 1] "knowledge is not determinate prior to articulation"; [section 3] full protocol specification; [corpus] weak signal from neighboring dialogue systems not addressing this specific mechanism.
**Break condition:** If the expert consistently fails to resolve tensions productively (e.g., always contesting without refinement), the dialogue may not produce a coherent material base, indicating insufficiently articulable expertise or lack of structured inferential norms.

### Mechanism 2: LLM as defeasible derivability oracle
**Claim:** The LLM opponent functions effectively as a defeasible derivability oracle despite its unreliability, because its errors are structurally contained by human authority.
**Mechanism:** The LLM generates candidate tensions based on its assessment of incoherence in [C:D]. These are proposals, not impositions. The human accepts or contests each. False positives (spurious tensions) are filtered via contestation, costing only a dialogue turn. False negatives (missed tensions) remain a limitation but do not corrupt the base. The base consequence relation | ∼_BS is built only from accepted tensions (I) plus the Containment norm (Cont).
**Core assumption:** LLMs can surface plausible inferential connections with sufficient frequency to be useful as a dialectical stimulator, and humans can reliably judge their validity.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "LLM opponent acting as a defeasible derivability oracle"; [section 3.2] "LLMs can function as classifiers-as-intensions... The key is defeasibility."; [section 10.3] acknowledges systematic false negatives as a limitation; [corpus] weak signal: neighboring LLM-for-knowledge papers treat LLMs as generators, not dialectical oracles.
**Break condition:** If the LLM's proposal rate of genuine tensions (those the expert accepts) drops below a threshold needed to structure the domain, the dialogue yields a sparse or trivial base. Conversely, if the expert accepts too many spurious tensions, the base becomes polluted.

### Mechanism 3: Mapping to NMMS material base
**Claim:** The mapping from dialectical state S = <[C:D], T, I> to a material base B_S = <L_BS, | ∼_BS> satisfies Containment and preserves the substructural (nonmonotonic, nontransitive) character of expert knowledge.
**Mechanism:** L_BS = C ∪ D. | ∼_BS = I ∪ Cont, where Cont = {<Γ,∆> | Γ,∆ ⊆ L_BS and Γ∩∆≠∅}. I contains discovered incoherences (accepted tensions with Γ∩∆=∅). Cont encodes the presupposed pragmatic norm of bilateral consistency. Their union ensures Containment (Γ| ∼_BS ∆ if Γ∩∆≠∅). The base is inherently nonmonotonic (adding premises can defeat an inference) and nontransitive because I consists of specific, contextually accepted pairs without closure properties.
**Core assumption:** The incoherences explicitly negotiated in dialogue (I) and the minimal coherence norm (Cont) are sufficient to constitute a well-formed material base reflecting the expert's position.
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] Definition 7, Proposition 1, Remark 1; [section 4.3] traceability of every implication to a dialogue move; [section 8] empirical verification of nontransitivity, nonmonotonicity, independence in PROV-O case; [corpus] no direct evidence; this is a novel formal contribution.
**Break condition:** If the dialogue yields I with highly interdependent implications (e.g., a transitive chain), it may indicate the domain is being forced into a classical pattern. More critically, if resolutions consistently violate Containment (e.g., accepts a tension where Γ∩∆=∅ but later asserts both an element of Γ and its denial), the base becomes inconsistent.

## Foundational Learning
### Inferentialist Semantics (Brandom 1994)
**Why needed:** Provides the philosophical foundation: meaning is inferential role. A knowledge base is not a list of facts but a structure of commitments and their inferential consequences. This flips the knowledge engineering problem from "what is true?" to "what follows from what?"
**Quick check:** If "A" means "it is raining," what are three things it inferentially commits you to (e.g., "the streets are wet") and three things it is incompatible with (e.g., "the streets are dry")?

### Material Inference vs. Formal Inference
**Why needed:** Expert knowledge is largely material (content-dependent, defeasible). Classical logic's formal inference is inadequate. Elenchus captures material inferences directly (Γ| ∼∆) without forcing them into a formal mold too early. The NMMS logical vocabulary later makes explicit these material relations.
**Quick check:** Give an example of a good material inference (e.g., "x is a bird, so x can fly") and explain why a formal, logic-book rule of inference (like Modus Ponens) doesn't capture its goodness.

### Bilateral Position [C:D] (Restall 2005)
**Why needed:** Knowledge involves both what we assert (commitments) and what we deny. Tensions arise from cross-side incoherences (commitment incompatible with denial) and same-side ones (contrariety, subcontrariety). This bilateral structure is essential for the dialectic.
**Quick check:** If your commitments are {A, B} and your denials are {¬C}, what are two different types of tensions (sequent forms) the opponent might propose?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** LLM Agent (Claude Code) -> Dialectical State Machine -> Persistent Store (GitHub Issues); Formal Mapper -> pyNMMS Reasoner

**Critical path:**
1. Initialization: Load state from GitHub issues labeled commitment, denial, tension, implication.
2. Input: Respondent provides speech act (new commitment/denial, or response to tension).
3. State Update: Update [C:D]. Run coherence check: if C∩D≠∅ (Containment violation) or if LLM identifies candidate Γ| ∼∆ with Γ⊆C, ∆⊆D.
4. Tension Handling: New candidate tension added to T. If respondent accepts (retraction/refinement), move sequent from T to I. If contestation, remove from T.
5. Persistence: Record all changes as new/updated GitHub issues.
6. Query (optional): Operator or agent can invoke pyNMMS to check derivability of sequents over current I.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- LLM Authority vs. Human Authority: Human is ultimate arbiter; sacrifices automation/scale but ensures validity.
- Granularity of Atoms: Too coarse yields few tensions; too fine overwhelms dialogue. Requires tuning.
- Context Window vs. Dialogue Length: GitHub-backed asynchronous design mitigates context limits but breaks conversational flow.
- Oracle Prompt Sensitivity: LLM's tension proposals depend heavily on system prompt; requires careful engineering.

**Failure signatures:**
- Stalled Dialogue: T accumulates many unresolved tensions; respondent overwhelmed. Action: prune/refactor T, introduce Socratic challenges.
- Trivial Base: All accepted tensions are of form A| ∼A (pure Containment); I empty or negligible. Action: dialectic failed to surface substantive inferences.
- Polluted Base: Respondent accepts many spurious tensions (LLM hallucinations). Action: requires external review of traceability log.
- Context Collapse: LLM forgets earlier state, proposing already resolved/contradicted tensions. Action: improve state-loading fidelity.
- Non-Independence: Many cross-chain queries become derivable, indicating decisions overly interdependent. Action: revisit challenge design.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Toy Domain Smoke Test: Run Elenchus on a 3-sentence spec (e.g., Bird, Fly, Penguin). Goal: base with 2-3 commitments and 1-2 accepted material implications; verify nontransitivity.
2. Oracle Calibration: For a fixed short PROV-O snippet, run 10 trials with different LLM temperatures/prompts. Measure: (a) valid tensions proposed (expert acceptance rate), (b) novel tensions (not initially explicit). Target: high validity (>80%) with meaningful novelty (>30%).
3. Design Tension Isolation: Apply Elenchus to a domain with two orthogonal design choices (e.g., access control and versioning). Goal: produce two separate challenge/response chains with pairwise-independent material implications (cross-chain queries all false).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can false negatives in the LLM opponent's identification of tensions be systematically addressed?
**Basis in paper:** Section 10.3 identifies false negatives as a limitation not addressed by the protocol.
**Why unresolved:** Current design only filters false positives via contestation; false negatives may cause systematic omissions.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Quantification of false negative rates in diverse domains and development of mitigation strategies (e.g., multi-LLM prompting, external knowledge bases).

#### Open Question 2
**Question:** What are the scalability limits of Elenchus regarding domain size and expert cognitive load?
**Basis in paper:** Section 10.4 notes the need to manage cognitive load and LLM context limits for larger domains.
**Why unresolved:** Case study covers only 350 words; performance on larger specifications unknown.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies with larger ontologies, measuring dialogue length, expert fatigue, and LLM context usage.

#### Open Question 3
**Question:** How can NMMS reasoning be integrated into the Elenchus agent loop to dynamically guide challenge selection?
**Basis in paper:** Section 10.1 proposes integrating pyNMMS to inform opponent's challenge selection during dialectic.
**Why unresolved:** pyNMMS currently used only for post-hoc verification; real-time reasoning could improve tension identification.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation of an interactive reasoner that suggests next challenges and evaluation of its effect on dialogue efficiency and base quality.

#### Open Question 4
**Question:** How does Elenchus compare to traditional knowledge acquisition methods, and can it facilitate discovery of novel design tensions?
**Basis in paper:** Section 10.5 calls for comparison with alternatives; Section 7 notes that testing discovery requires different experimental design.
**Why unresolved:** Presented case study is illustrative, not evaluative; no systematic comparison or discovery assessment exists.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments comparing Elenchus with methods like CommonKADS, inter-expert consistency studies, and coverage against gold-standard formalizations.

## Limitations
- The full system prompt (`CLAUDE.md`) is not public, critical for LLM behavior consistency.
- The human expert's tacit inferential stance and judgment are irreproducible, limiting generalizability.
- The LLM's tension generation lacks a formal specification—its reliability is pragmatically contained but not formally bounded.
