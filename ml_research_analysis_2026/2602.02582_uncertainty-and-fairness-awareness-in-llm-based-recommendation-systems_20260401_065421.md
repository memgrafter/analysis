# Uncertainty and Fairness Awareness in LLM-Based Recommendation Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02582
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02582
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** An uncertainty-aware evaluation framework for RecLLMs reveals systemic unfairness in Gemini 1.5 Flash across demographic and personality axes, with predictive entropy correlating with recommendation reliability.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles reliability and equity challenges in LLM-based recommendation systems (RecLLMs) by proposing an evaluation framework that integrates uncertainty quantification and fairness auditing. Focusing on Google Gemini 1.5 Flash, the authors curate a benchmark of 1,000 movies and music items annotated with eight demographic attributes. Using identity- and personality-conditioned prompts, they generate recommendations and measure fairness via SNSR, SNSV, and a novel Personality-Aware Fairness Score (PAFS). Empirical results show significant disparities—particularly for religion, race, and continent—that persist under linguistic perturbations. Higher predictive entropy is linked to lower recommendation quality, and personality prompts expose additional fairness-utility trade-offs. The work advances more transparent and equitable RecLLM evaluation.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a dual-domain benchmark (1,000 movies, 1,000 music items) with demographic annotations (8 attributes, 31 values). They design prompt templates: neutral (“I am a fan of [item]”), demographic-conditioned (“I am a [attribute] fan of [item]”), and personality-conditioned (using Big Five traits). These prompts are sent to Gemini 1.5 Flash (greedy decode) to retrieve top-25 recommendations. Similarity between neutral and sensitive-group outputs is computed using Jaccard, SERP*, and PRAG*. From these, fairness metrics SNSR (max-min gap) and SNSV (variance) are derived per attribute. Predictive entropy, calculated from token-level probabilities, measures output uncertainty. Robustness tests include typo and multilingual (French) prompt variations. The PAFS metric evaluates recommendation stability across personality prompts.

## Key Results
- Systematic unfairness is observed: music SNSR=0.1363, SNSV=0.0507; movies show SNSR up to 0.2599 (religion) and SNSV up to 0.1209, with disadvantaged groups (e.g., “African” under continent).
- Disparities persist under prompt perturbations (typos, French translations), indicating robustness of bias.
- Higher predictive entropy correlates with lower recommendation reliability; personality prompts reveal new bias patterns and trade-offs with personalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by exploiting three interconnected mechanisms. First, **predictive entropy**—computed from the token probability distribution—serves as a proxy for recommendation reliability. High entropy indicates model uncertainty across plausible tokens, leading to less coherent or off-target lists, which correlates with lower quality. Second, **demographic prompt perturbation** triggers systemic bias because demographic tokens activate stereotypical associations from training data, shifting the probability distribution over recommended items and creating measurable similarity gaps (SNSR/SNSV) between groups. Third, **personality-aware prompting** exposes additional fairness dimensions: personality traits, often correlated with demographic stereotypes in the corpus, can compound or counteract demographic effects. The PAFS metric captures stability across personality variations, highlighting fairness-utility trade-offs inherent in personalization.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Curator -> Prompt Generator -> LLM Inference Client -> Metrics Engine & Uncertainty Module -> Robustness Tester.  
**Critical path:** Curation → Prompt Generation (neutral/sensitive) → Inference → Similarity Computation → Fairness Metrics → Uncertainty Analysis → Robustness Checks.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Greedy decoding ensures reproducibility but may mask stochasticity; Jaccard is interpretable but ignores rank; eight attributes provide breadth but miss intersectionality.  
**Failure signatures:** Near-zero entropy → no token probability access; SNSR≈0 → prompt template failure or safety overrides; high metric variance → uncontrolled nondeterminism.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Neutral vs. Single-Attribute:** For each attribute value, compute SNSR/SNSV; expect religion/race to show highest gaps.  
2. **Perturbation Robustness:** Introduce typos into disadvantaged attribute values; measure change in SNSV (anticipate exacerbation).  
3. **Entropy-Reliability Correlation:** For a subset, compute entropy and manually judge list quality; expect negative correlation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do uncertainty quantification and fairness metrics generalize across diverse LLM architectures beyond Gemini 1.5 Flash?
- Can standardized personality models (e.g., Big Five) systematically characterize and mitigate personality-linked biases in RecLLM recommendations?
- What optimization techniques can balance fairness and personalization in LLM-based recommenders without severely degrading recommendation quality?
- How does uncertainty propagate through multi-step RecLLM agent pipelines, and does this amplify or mitigate biases?
- Can uncertainty-aware calibration (e.g., temperature scaling, ensembles) be adapted for list-wise LLM recommendations to improve both reliability and fairness?

## Limitations
- Exact benchmark items and full demographic mappings are not provided; replication may yield different fairness numbers.
- Personality prompt templates and specific traits (e.g., Big Five dimensions) are underspecified, hindering PAFS reproduction.
- Entropy computation method and Gemini API’s support for token logits are unclear; SERP* and PRAG* definitions are missing.
