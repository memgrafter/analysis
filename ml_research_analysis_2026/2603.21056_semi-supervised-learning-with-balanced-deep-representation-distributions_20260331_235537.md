# Semi-Supervised Learning with Balanced Deep Representation Distributions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21056
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21056
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Proposes S2TC-BDD to fix margin bias in semi-supervised text classification by balancing angular variances, achieving new SOTA (e.g., Macro-F1 0.876 on AG News with 100 labels vs. 0.867 best baseline).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the margin bias problem in semi-supervised text classification (SSTC), where imbalanced intra-class angular variances in deep representations lead to inaccurate pseudo-labels during self-training. The proposed S2TC-BDD method introduces a Balanced Deep representation Distribution (BDD) loss that applies class-specific Gaussian linear transformations to angular margins, equalizing these variances. This repositions decision boundaries to be more optimal. Evaluated on multi-class and multi-label benchmarks, S2TC-BDD consistently outperforms prior SSTC methods, particularly under low-label regimes. Ablations confirm the BDD component's critical role and show higher pseudo-label accuracy when used.

## Method Summary
S2TC-BDD is a self-training framework that modifies the standard Angular Margin (AM) loss. In each training epoch, it estimates per-class label prototypes and angle statistics (mean, variance) from both labeled and high-confidence pseudo-labeled texts. A target common variance is computed, and for each class, a linear transformation (scaling and shifting) is derived to map its angular distribution to this common variance while preserving inter-class separation. This transformation is applied to the angles before computing the AM loss, forming the BDD loss. The total training objective combines supervised loss, unsupervised loss (with pseudo-labels), and task-specific regularization (entropy for multi-class, low-rank for multi-label). Pseudo-labels are generated via sharpening (multi-class) or class-distribution-aware thresholding (CAP for multi-label).

## Key Results
- S2TC-BDD consistently outperforms state-of-the-art SSTC methods across multi-class and multi-label benchmarks, with significant gains when labeled data is scarce (e.g., AG News, 100 labels: Macro-F1 0.876 vs. 0.867).
- Ablation studies show removing the BDD component causes the largest performance drop, confirming its centrality.
- Pseudo-label accuracy (Macro/Micro-F1 on unlabeled data) is consistently higher when training with the BDD loss compared to standard AM loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Inaccurate pseudo-labels in SSTC self-training arise from a margin bias problem caused by imbalanced label angle variances (large differences in variance of angles between representations and label prototypes across classes).
- **Mechanism:** The angular margin (AM) loss decision boundary is suboptimal when intra-class angular variances differ significantly. This leads to misclassification of unlabeled points near boundaries, especially for classes with larger variance.
- **Core assumption:** Label angles for each class follow a Gaussian distribution *N(µ_k, σ²_k)*.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "margin bias problem caused by the large difference between representation distributions of labels in SSTC... the variance of label angles of texts within the same label."
  - [section I, Fig. 1 & 2] Empirically shows Avg.DLAV is larger in semi-supervised vs. supervised settings; illustrates toy example where unbalanced variances cause misclassification.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers address pseudo-label bias via different mechanisms (e.g., confidence thresholds, consistency), but do not explicitly target angular variance imbalance, suggesting this is a distinct identified gap.
- **Break condition:** If class angular distributions are not Gaussian or are multi-modal, the linear transformation balancing may misestimate required adjustments.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Applying class-specific Gaussian linear transformations to angles balances the intra-class variances (σ²_k → σ̄²), which repositions the AM loss decision boundary to a more optimal location, improving pseudo-label accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** For each class *k*, transform angles θ_ik via ψ_k(θ) = a_kθ + b_k where a_k = σ̄/σ_k, b_k = (1-a_k)µ_k. This rescales and shifts each class's angular distribution to have common variance σ̄² while preserving inter-class angular separation (µ_k).
- **Core assumption:** Balanced angular variances eliminate margin bias; the optimal boundary for classification under Gaussian class-conditional densities is determined by equalized variances.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III.A, Eq. 2 & 3] Derivation of BDD loss as AM loss applied to transformed angles ψ_k(θ_ik).
  - [section IV.E, Fig. 4] Pseudo-label accuracy (Macro/Micro-F1 on unlabeled data) is consistently higher when BDD loss is used vs. standard AM loss during training.
  - [section IV.D, Table VIII] Ablation: Removing BDD ("-BDD") causes significant performance drop, more than removing regularization.
- **Break condition:** If the common target variance σ̄² is poorly estimated (e.g., from noisy early pseudo-labels), transformations could distort discriminative angular structure.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Estimating label prototypes (c_k) and angle statistics (µ_k, σ²_k) from the combined pool of labeled and high-confidence pseudo-labeled texts during each self-training epoch provides more robust and stable estimates than using labeled data alone, further improving boundary adjustment.
- **Mechanism:** Label prototypes c_k are moving averages of representations weighted by (pseudo-)labels. Angles β_ik = arccos(f_i⊤c_k / (||f_i||c_k||)) are computed, and µ_k, σ²_k are estimated from these angles over all (pseudo-)labeled examples in the epoch. These estimates are updated via exponential moving average.
- **Core assumption:** As training progresses, pseudo-labels become sufficiently accurate to contribute meaningful statistics for variance estimation without overwhelming noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III.B, Eq. 6-8] Details the per-epoch estimation and moving average update for c_k, µ_k, σ²_k using both labeled and pseudo-labeled data.
  - [section IV.D, Table VIII] Removing unlabeled texts from the estimation pool ("-unlabeled texts") causes a major performance drop, confirming their contribution.
  - [section V.B] Paper notes this addresses the limitation of approximating distributions with only scarce labeled data.
- **Break condition:** Early in training, pseudo-labels are noisy; incorporating them too early could bias variance estimates. The paper does not specify a warm-up period for this estimation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Angular Margin (AM) Loss**
  - *Why needed here:* BDD loss is a direct modification of AM loss. Understanding AM's role in maximizing inter-class variance and minimizing intra-class variance via angular margins is prerequisite to seeing how BDD alters the intra-class variance term.
  - *Quick check question:* How does ℓ₂-normalizing features and weights in AM loss make the decision boundary depend on angles rather than magnitudes?
- **Concept: Self-Training Pseudo-Labeling**
  - *Why needed here:* S2TC-BDD is a self-training method. The cycle of: (1) train on labeled, (2) predict unlabeled, (3) retrain on labeled+pseudo-labeled is fundamental. The BDD loss operates within this loop.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the core risk of self-training that BDD aims to mitigate?
- **Concept: Gaussian Distribution Properties**
  - *Why needed here:* The linear transformation ψ_k(·) to balance variances relies on properties of the Gaussian (linear transformation of a Gaussian is Gaussian; variance scales by square of slope). The entire balancing mechanism assumes Gaussianity of class angles.
  - *Quick check question:* If θ ~ N(µ, σ²), what is the distribution of aθ + b? How would you compute its variance?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  BERT Encoder -> Classifier Head -> BDD Transformation Module -> Statistics Estimator -> Pseudo-Label Generator -> Regularization Module
- **Critical path:**
  *Initialize:* Train BERT+AM on labeled set D_l only for several epochs.
  *Self-training loop per epoch:*
  - Forward pass all (labeled + unlabeled) through model → get f_i, θ_ik, ψ_k(θ_ik), predictions p(k|x_i).
  - **Estimate statistics:** Compute c_k via weighted avg of f_i (current labels/pseudo-labels). Compute β_ik, then µ_k, σ²_k. Update moving averages.
  - **Compute BDD parameters:** Compute target variance σ̄² = mean(σ²_k). Compute a_k = σ̄/σ_k, b_k = (1-a_k)µ_k.
  - **Generate pseudo-labels:** Apply thresholding tricks to p(k|x_u) → y_u.
  - **Backward pass:** Compute total loss = L_l (D_l) + λ₁ L_u (D_u, y_u) + λ_reg (regularization). Update BERT & classifier parameters via SGD.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Computational overhead:* Estimating µ_k, σ²_k, c_k per-epoch requires full forward pass and aggregation, increasing memory/compute vs. standard self-training.
  - *Gaussian assumption risk:* If class angular distributions are skewed or multi-modal, linear balancing may not align with true Bayes optimal boundary.
  - *Pseudo-label noise sensitivity:* Variance estimates rely on pseudo-labels; high noise early on could destabilize statistics. Warm-up period and EMA help mitigate.
  - *Multi-label vs. Multi-class:* Different pseudo-labeling (CAP vs. sharpening) and regularization (low-rank vs. entropy) required; increases implementation complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Stagnant or decreasing pseudo-label accuracy over epochs (monitor using a small clean validation subset of unlabeled data).
  - Extremely imbalanced estimated σ²_k values that do not converge; indicates poor pseudo-labels or non-Gaussian distributions.
  - Degradation when λ₁ (unsupervised loss weight) is increased; suggests BDD adjustments are amplifying noise.
  - In multi-label, low-rank constraint causing underfitting (check nuclear norm vs. task performance).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *Ablation of BDD:* Train S2TC-BDD with L_bdd replaced by standard L_am. Expect significant drop in performance, confirming BDD's role.
  2. *Variance Balance Check:* Plot per-class σ²_k over epochs with/without BDD. Expect BDD to drive all σ²_k toward common σ̄².
  3. *Sensitivity to σ̄² estimation:* Try forcing σ̄² = 1 (fixed) vs. learned dynamic σ̄². Performance drop would confirm the benefit of dynamic estimation from current data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the Gaussian assumption for label angle distributions be relaxed to handle multi-modal or heavy-tailed distributions in real-world text data?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in Section V.B that "there are many complex scenarios, such as multi-model distributions for each class... The truncated Gaussian distribution, mixture-of-Gaussian distribution, or the widely-used von Mises-Fisher distribution could handle these cases, and we will exploit them in future work."
- **Why unresolved:** The current BDD loss specifically models label angles with Gaussian distributions and applies linear transformations to balance variances. This may not capture complex, non-Gaussian structures in text representations, potentially limiting effectiveness on datasets with irregular class distributions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical evaluation on synthetic or real datasets with known multi-modal label angle distributions, comparing BDD against extensions using mixture models or von Mises-Fisher distributions. Theoretical analysis of how non-Gaussian assumptions affect the margin bias problem would also clarify trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does the BDD method remain effective under class imbalance and long-tail label distributions, where some classes have very few labeled examples?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] In Section VI, the authors list "imbalanced and long-tailed semi-supervised text classification" as a specific limitation, noting that "balancing representation distributions for imbalanced, long-tailed classes... may cause an underestimate or overestimate of their variances."
- **Why unresolved:** The method estimates label angle variances using both labeled and pseudo-labeled data, but rare classes may have insufficient samples for accurate variance estimation. Balancing variances across all classes could distort representations for minority classes, degrading performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments on artificially imbalanced versions of standard benchmarks, measuring performance per class (e.g., per-class F1) with varying imbalance ratios. Analysis of how variance estimates for tail classes evolve during self-training, and whether adaptive balancing (e.g., class-weighted transformations) mitigates issues.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How robust is S²TC-BDD to open-world semi-supervised learning scenarios where unlabeled data contains out-of-distribution (OOD) samples not belonging to any known class?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors identify "open-set/open-world semi-supervised learning where unlabeled data includes some out-of-distribution samples" as a limitation in Section VI, stating that OOD samples "will decrease the approximate accuracy of representation distributions and introduce additional noise into pseudo-labels."
- **Why unresolved:** The BDD loss estimates label angle variances from all pseudo-labeled data, assuming all unlabeled texts belong to known classes. OOD samples may have representations far from any class prototype, distorting variance estimates and leading to incorrect pseudo-labels that harm model training.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments with benchmark datasets augmented with explicit OOD text (e.g., from other domains or with random labels), evaluating S²TC-BDD's performance drop compared to methods with OOD detection or rejection mechanisms. Measuring the impact of OOD contamination on estimated label angle variances would provide diagnostic evidence.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the BDD loss provide consistent benefits across different pseudo-labeling strategies (e.g., sharpening vs. CAP) and model architectures beyond BERT?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper implements separate multi-class (with sharpening) and multi-label (with CAP) versions, but does not ablate the interaction between BDD and these tricks. It also uses only BERT-based-uncased as the text encoder. The relative contribution of BDD versus pseudo-labeling design is unclear.
- **Why unresolved:** The ablation study (Table VIII) removes the entire BDD component but not individual pseudo-labeling tricks. Thus, it is unknown whether BDD's gains are orthogonal to or synergistic with specific pseudo-labeling methods, and whether the approach generalizes to other encoder architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, DeBERTa) or non-transformer models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation swapping pseudo-labeling strategies (sharpening, CAP, confidence thresholding) within S²TC-BDD for both multi-class and multi-label tasks, measuring performance changes. Additionally, replicating experiments with alternative pre-trained models (e.g., RoBERTa) to test architectural dependence of BDD's effectiveness.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can the theoretical insight of balancing label angle variances be extended to other angular margin-based losses (e.g., ArcFace, SphereFace) or non-angular contrastive objectives?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper builds BDD specifically on the CosFace/AM loss framework, applying Gaussian transformations to the angular space. The margin bias problem is framed generally in terms of label angle variances, suggesting the idea might apply beyond the specific AM loss used.
- **Why unresolved:** The derivation of BDD is tightly coupled with the AM loss formulation (Eq. 1). Other angular losses (e.g., ArcFace with additive angular margin) have different decision boundary properties. It is unclear if the same linear transformation approach balances variances effectively for those losses, or if alternative transformations are needed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Reformulating BDD for ArcFace or SphereFace losses, deriving corresponding transformations to balance label angle variances, and evaluating on the same benchmarks. Comparative analysis of how different angular margins interact with the balancing constraint would test the generality of the approach.

## Limitations
- **Gaussian Assumption Robustness:** The core mechanism assumes class angular distributions are Gaussian. Real-world text representations may be multi-modal or skewed, making linear balancing suboptimal.
- **Pseudo-Label Noise in Statistics Estimation:** Using pseudo-labeled data to compute class statistics risks bias from early noisy labels; the paper does not specify a warm-up period or quantify sensitivity.
- **Implementation Ambiguity:** Critical details are underspecified, including exact minibatch aggregation for statistics, CAP threshold solver, and ADMM hyperparameters, hindering exact reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Margin Bias Problem Identification:** Medium-High. Strong empirical correlation (Fig. 1, 2, ablation) but causality inferred, not proven.
- **BDD Loss Mechanism & Efficacy:** Medium-High. Sound derivation and ablations confirm role, but confidence tempered by Gaussian assumption and variance estimation dependencies.
- **Pseudo-Label-Driven Statistics Estimation:** Medium. Ablation shows benefit, but robustness under varying noise levels or alternative estimation schemes is unexamined.
