# Static and Dynamic Approaches to Computing Barycenters of Probability Measures on Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26940
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26940
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The paper introduces a dynamic, intrinsic gradient descent method for barycenter computation on graphs using a continuity equation and Riemannian structure, avoiding the degeneracy of classical Wasserstein metrics and the need for manually chosen ground metrics.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses computing barycenters (weighted averages) of probability measures on graphs, where the standard Wasserstein metric fails due to degeneracy. It proposes a dynamic approach based on a Riemannian structure derived from a continuity equation, using the Chambolle-Pock algorithm to approximate geodesics and their momenta. Barycenters are synthesized via intrinsic gradient descent: the gradient of the variance functional is obtained by summing initial momenta of geodesics to reference measures; the measure is updated using a discretized continuity equation. Analysis (recovering barycentric coordinates) is performed by forming a Gram matrix from these momenta and solving a quadratic program. Numerical experiments on various graphs (hypercube, grid, US states) validate the method, showing accurate barycenter synthesis and coordinate recovery, robustness to initialization, and avoidance of arbitrary ground metric choices. The dynamic method is contrasted with a static entropic regularization approach that relies on a hand‑picked ground metric.

## Method Summary
Two complementary methods are detailed. The dynamic synthesis algorithm iteratively updates the barycenter estimate ν by descending the variance functional J(ν)=∑ λ_i W²(ν, ν_i). The gradient is computed as the negative sum of initial momenta m_{ν,ν_i}(0) from geodesics connecting ν to each reference ν_i; the update is ν ← ν + ε div_X(∑ λ_i m_{ν,ν_i}(0)), with convergence assessed via ||ν_{k+1}−ν_k||_π. Geodesics are approximated using a Galerkin discretization and the Chambolle-Pock proximal splitting scheme. For analysis, given a target barycenter ν, geodesics to each reference are computed, their initial momenta are used to build a Gram matrix A_{ij}=⟨∇φ_i, ∇φ_j⟩_ν, and the barycentric weights λ̂ are recovered by solving the quadratic program λ̂ = argmin_{λ∈Δ} λᵀAλ via JuMP.

The static approach uses an entropic regularized optimal transport cost OT_{ε_reg} with a manually chosen ground metric (shortest‑path or diffusion distance). Synthesis minimizes ∑ λ_i OT_{ε_reg}(ν, ν_i) by gradient descent; analysis constructs transport maps T_i from ν to ν_i via Sinkhorn, forms A_{ij}=⟨T_i−Id, T_j−Id⟩_ν, and solves the same QP. Both frameworks rely on a provided graph Markov kernel (transition matrix Q and stationary distribution π) and probability measures as non‑negative vectors summing to one.

## Key Results
- Dynamic barycenters align with known geodesics for two‑measure cases, confirming correctness of the synthesis.
- The analysis step accurately recovers the original barycentric coordinates from the dynamic barycenter, demonstrating the consistency of the Gram matrix construction.
- The dynamic method does not require a manually chosen ground metric, unlike static entropic approaches that depend on shortest‑path or diffusion distances.
- Convergence of the dynamic algorithm is robust to initialization and exhibits empirical linear rates.
- The framework is validated on several graphs, including a hypercube, a grid, and a spatial geography of US states.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dynamic approach is grounded in a Riemannian geometry on the probability simplex induced by the continuity equation. The variance functional J(ν) is convex in the Euclidean sense, and its Riemannian gradient is given by the sum of initial momenta along geodesics to references. The continuity equation provides a canonical way to move mass along the graph without introducing an external ground metric. By discretizing the geodesic ODEs with Galerkin methods and solving the resulting saddle‑point problem via Chambolle‑Pock, the algorithm approximates the true geodesics and their momenta. The update step uses the divergence operator to translate momentum into a change in the probability density while preserving total mass (with respect to π). The analysis leverages the fact that the initial momenta encode the directions to references; their pairwise inner products at the barycenter yield a Gram matrix whose minimizer over the simplex recovers the weights. This intrinsic construction avoids the metric degeneracies that plague classical Wasserstein distances on discrete spaces.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Continuity Equation on Graphs** – Describes mass transport via a time‑dependent velocity field m_t; needed to model geodesics as curves (ρ,m) satisfying ∂_t ρ + div_X m = 0.  
   *Quick check:* Verify that for a constant field along a geodesic, the equation reduces to advection of mass along edges.

2. **Riemannian Gradient Descent** – The algorithm performs descent in the Riemannian metric associated with the optimal transport problem. One must compute the Riemannian gradient (initial momentum) and use the exponential map (here through the continuity equation) to update.  
   *Quick check:* Ensure the update direction is indeed the negative gradient and that step size ε scales correctly with the metric.

3. **Chambolle‑Pock Algorithm** – A proximal splitting method for convex‑concave saddle point problems; used to solve the dynamical optimal transport discretization. Understanding the primal‑dual updates and the choice of τ,σ is crucial for convergence.  
   *Quick check:* Run the algorithm on a simple 2‑node graph and check that the residual decreases.

4. **Stationary Distribution π** – The reference measure for the L²‑type inner products and the preservation of total mass; the divergence operator is defined w.r.t. π.  
   *Quick check:* Confirm that for any divergence field, the integral of divergence against π vanishes (integration by parts).

5. **Entropic Optimal Transport** – The static approach uses Sinkhorn iterations to approximate OT costs with entropy regularization. Understanding the role of ε_reg and its impact on sparsity vs. smoothing is key.  
   *Quick check:* Compare Sinkhorn OT costs with exact costs on a small graph as ε_reg varies.

6. **Quadratic Programming on the Simplex** – Analysis requires solving min_{λ∈Δ} λᵀAλ. The solution lies in the simplex and can be obtained via active‑set methods or via JuMP.  
   *Quick check:* For a random PSD A, solve the QP and verify λ sums to 1 and entries non‑negative.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**  
  Graph representation (Q,π) → Geodesic solver (Chambolle‑Pock) → Initial momenta m(0) → Gradient computation (sum weighted m(0)) → Continuity equation update → New ν.  
  Analysis: ν + geodesic solves → momenta → Gram matrix A → QP solver → λ̂.  
  Static: Ground metric C → Sinkhorn OT → gradient descent for synthesis; transport maps → A → QP for analysis.

- **Critical Path**  
  1. Construct graph (Markov kernel) and probability measures.  
  2. For synthesis: repeatedly compute geodesics to references (via Chambolle‑Pock) to obtain m(0).  
  3. Update ν via divergence of weighted sum of m(0).  
  4. Check convergence (ν change).  
  5. For analysis: compute geodesics from barycenter to references, build Gram matrix, solve QP.

- **Design Tradeoffs**  
  *Dynamic vs. Static:* Dynamic avoids arbitrary ground metric but requires solving time‑discretized PDEs (cost O(N) per geodesic). Static uses Sinkhorn (fast per iteration) but ground metric choice strongly influences results.  
  *Time steps N:* Larger N improves geodesic accuracy but increases computational cost.  
  *Step size ε:* Too large causes negativity; too small slows convergence.  
  *Entropic regularization ε_reg:* Smaller values give sharper results but may hinder Sinkhorn convergence.

- **Failure Signatures**  
  - Negative entries in ν after update → ε too large or poor momentum approximation.  
  - Chambolle‑Pock residual stalls above δ_g → τ,σ not satisfying τσ||L||²<1 or insufficient iterations.  
  - Analysis QP returns λ̂ outside simplex or ill‑conditioned → A near singular; add diagonal jitter.  
  - Static barycenters overly diffuse or weights unrecovered → ε_reg too large or ground metric poorly scaled.

- **First Three Experiments**  
  1. **Geodesic sanity check:** On a 3‑node line graph, compute geodesic between two point masses; verify that the path follows the graph edges and that m(0) points correctly.  
  2. **Synthesis convergence:** For a hypercube (16 nodes) with two reference Diracs, run dynamic synthesis; confirm that the barycenter lies on the known geodesic and J(ν) decreases monotonically.  
  3. **Analysis consistency:** Using the barycenter from Exp 2, compute analysis Gram matrix and recover λ̂; check that ∥λ̂−λ∥/∥λ∥ < 1e‑6 for equidistant weights.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under what conditions (e.g., graph structure, step size ε, reference measures) does the intrinsic gradient descent algorithm guarantee that iterates remain non‑negative probability measures throughout? (Lemma 1 only preserves total mass, not non‑negativity.)

2. Is the variance functional J(ν) = Σ_i λ_i W²(ν, ν_i) geodesically convex on the discrete Wasserstein space? Euclidean convexity yields existence, but geodesic convexity is needed to ensure Riemannian gradient descent finds a global minimizer.

3. Can an entropically regularized version of the discrete dynamic optimal transport metric be defined that preserves the non‑degeneracy and canonical nature of the Riemannian structure while enabling fast Sinkhorn‑type iterations?

4. For reference measures all supported on a subset A⊂X, under what conditions is the barycenter also supported entirely on A? (Discrete geodesics can be non‑local, so support may expand.)

## Limitations
- The Chambolle‑Pock implementation details (proximal operators, relaxation parameters, slack handling) are not fully described; deviations could affect geodesic accuracy.
- Diffusion distance computation lacks specifics (spectral truncation, handling of non‑integer diffusion time, scaling), leading to variability in the static approach.
- The US states adjacency graph is not explicitly sourced; reproduction of the geography‑based figure may be hindered.
- Random seeds for reference measure generation are not reported, making exact numerical reproduction difficult.
- The dynamic update may produce negative probabilities if the step size ε is too large; the paper does not provide a projection scheme.
