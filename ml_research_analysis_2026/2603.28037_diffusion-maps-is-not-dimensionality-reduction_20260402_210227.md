# Diffusion Maps is not Dimensionality Reduction
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28037
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28037
- **Reference Count:** 9
- **Primary Result:** Diffusion maps provide a spectral basis that spans the correct low-dimensional chart, but standard embeddings (using leading eigenvectors) are suboptimal; an oracle affine combination of many eigenvectors surpasses Isomap and UMAP in reconstruction accuracy on a Swiss roll manifold.

## Executive Summary
The paper reframes diffusion maps (DMAP) as a spectral representation of a manifold’s intrinsic geometry rather than an inherent dimensionality reduction technique. While DMAP eigenvectors form a basis in which the true low-dimensional coordinates lie, the standard practice of selecting the first few eigenvectors does not, in general, yield the optimal embedding. Using a synthetic Swiss roll manifold with known isometric coordinates, the authors introduce an oracle affine readout that linearly combines all DMAP eigenvectors to reconstruct the ground-truth chart. Under this evaluation, Isomap recovers the chart most efficiently at small latent dimensions, UMAP offers an intermediate trade-off, and DMAP only surpasses both when many eigenvectors are used, implying that the correct embedding exists within the DMAP span but requires a non-trivial combination that standard DMAP does not provide.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a controlled experiment on a Swiss roll manifold, where the 2D intrinsic coordinates are known. They apply three manifold learning methods—diffusion maps (DMAP), Isomap, and UMAP—to obtain low-dimensional embeddings. To assess whether the true chart lies within the embedding’s span, they introduce an oracle affine readout: a linear combination of all computed eigenvectors (for DMAP) or coordinates (for Isomap/UMAP) optimized to minimize the squared Frobenius norm against the ground-truth 2D coordinates. Reconstruction error serves as the metric, allowing a direct comparison of the expressive capacity of each method’s output space.

## Key Results
- The correct 2D chart for the Swiss roll is contained in the span of DMAP eigenvectors but is not identified by the first few eigenvectors alone; combining many eigenvectors via an affine readout yields the optimal reconstruction.
- Isomap achieves the lowest reconstruction error at very low dimensions (e.g., 2–5), demonstrating the most efficient recovery of the ground-truth chart.
- UMAP performs intermediately, while DMAP requires a high-dimensional spectral basis (dozens of eigenvectors) to outperform both, suggesting its strength lies in rich spectral representation rather than direct dimensionality reduction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DMAP constructs a diffusion operator that captures the manifold’s connectivity and geometry; its eigenvectors form a complete basis for functions on the manifold. The ground-truth intrinsic coordinates are such a function, hence they lie in this span. However, the leading eigenvectors correspond to the smoothest, most global modes and may not align with the specific parametrization of the manifold. An affine combination of many eigenvectors—including higher-frequency modes—is necessary to reconstruct the exact chart. Isomap, by directly optimizing for geodesic preservation, aligns more closely with the isometric coordinate structure at low dimensions, while UMAP’s probabilistic approach offers a compromise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Manifold Hypothesis**: Data lies on a low-dimensional manifold embedded in high-dimensional space; why needed: justifies nonlinear dimensionality reduction; quick check: visualize high-D data (e.g., images) for low-D structure.
- **Isometric Manifold**: Intrinsic Riemannian metric equals extrinsic Euclidean metric; why needed: Swiss roll is isometric to a rectangle, providing ground truth; quick check: compute pairwise geodesic vs. Euclidean distances on a rolled plane.
- **Spectral Embedding**: Methods using eigenvectors of a graph Laplacian or diffusion operator; why needed: DMAP, Laplacian Eigenmaps rely on this; quick check: construct a graph and compute its Laplacian eigenvectors on a simple dataset.
- **Geodesic Distance**: Shortest path along the manifold; why needed: Isomap approximates it via graph distances; quick check: compute all-pairs shortest paths on a nearest-neighbor graph.
- **Diffusion Geometry**: Uses random walk semantics to define distances; why needed: DMAP’s diffusion distances capture connectivity; quick check: simulate a random walk on a graph and examine hitting times.
- **Affine Readout**: Linear combination of features; why needed: oracle model to extract optimal embedding from spectral basis; quick check: solve a linear regression from eigenvectors to known coordinates.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Raw Data → k-Nearest Neighbors Graph → Affinity Matrix (ε Gaussian kernel) → Diffusion Operator (normalized) → Eigenvectors (λ₁ ≥ λ₂ ≥ ...) → Standard DMAP Embedding (first *k* eigenvectors)  
Oracle Alternative: All Eigenvectors → Affine Readout (weights *w*) → Optimal Embedding

**Critical Path**: Graph construction (k, ε) and operator normalization are pivotal; errors propagate to eigenvectors. The choice of which eigenvectors to use (first *k* vs. many) determines embedding quality.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- **DMAP**: Highly expressive with many eigenvectors but computationally costly and less interpretable; sensitive to ε and t (diffusion time).  
- **Isomap**: Direct low-dimensional embedding via MDS on geodesics; efficient for isometric manifolds but fails with non-isometric or noisy geometry.  
- **UMAP**: Parametric trade-off between local/global structure; faster but tuning parameters (n_neighbors, min_dist) affects results.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Disconnected graph (ε too small) → isolated components.  
- ε too large → manifold “smearing”, loss of intrinsic structure.  
- Using too few DMAP eigenvectors → high reconstruction error if true chart requires higher-frequency modes.  
- Noise in data → distorted geodesics (Isomap) or affinity (DMAP/UMAP).

**First Experiments**:  
1. **Parameter Sweep on Swiss Roll**: Vary ε and *t* for DMAP, n_components for all methods, and plot reconstruction error vs. embedding dimension to reproduce the key trade-off curve.  
2. **Oracle Ablation**: For DMAP, incrementally include more eigenvectors in the affine readout to show error decay and identify the “elbow” where performance rivals Isomap/UMAP.  
3. **Manifold Variation Test**: Apply the same pipeline to an “S-curve” manifold (non-isometric) to check if the relative ranking (Isomap > UMAP > low-dim DMAP) holds when isometry is violated.

## Limitations
- Findings are restricted to a single synthetic manifold (smooth, isometric, noise-free); generalizability to real or topologically complex data is unproven.
- The oracle affine readout is an idealized, linear readout; performance under nonlinear readout functions (e.g., neural networks) is unknown.
- Comparison excludes other spectral methods (e.g., Laplacian Eigenmaps, PHATE) and modern nonlinear techniques.

## Confidence
- **Primary Claim (DMAP eigenvectors span the true chart but standard selection is suboptimal):** High  
- **Comparative Efficiency (Isomap most efficient at low dimensions; DMAP superior at high dimensions):** Medium  
- **Universal Critique (DMAP is not dimensionality reduction):** Low  

## Next Checks
