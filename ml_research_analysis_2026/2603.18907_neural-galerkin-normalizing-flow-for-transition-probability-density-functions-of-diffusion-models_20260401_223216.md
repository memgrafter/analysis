# Neural Galerkin Normalizing Flow for Transition Probability Density Functions of Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18907
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18907
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** NGNF provides a variational framework for solving high-dimensional Fokker-Planck equations with atomic initial conditions using a conditional normalizing flow, guaranteeing positivity and mass conservation while enabling efficient many-query inference.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of solving high-dimensional Fokker-Planck equations for transition probability densities that originate from Dirac delta initial conditions. Traditional PDE methods struggle with the resulting singularities and non-smoothness. The proposed Neural Galerkin Normalizing Flow (NGNF) addresses this by representing the solution as a time-dependent conditional normalizing flow that maps a reference Gaussian (constructed via an Euler-Maruyama step) to the target density. This ansatz enforces positivity and unit mass by construction. The flow parameters are evolved in time by minimizing the PDE residual under the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle, which yields an ODE system. Residual evaluation uses adaptive sampling from the current flow approximation, focusing computational effort where the density has mass. Numerical experiments on a 2D anisotropic Beneš SDE demonstrate accurate capture of multimodal, non-Gaussian densities and stable relative L² errors. After an offline training phase, online density queries are fast, making NGNF suitable for applications like Bayesian inference requiring many evaluations.

## Method Summary
NGNF parametrizes the transition probability density ρ(x|t,s,x₀) as a deterministic, invertible transformation n_θ(τ,x|x₀) of a reference Gaussian p_Z(z|τ,x₀) derived from an Euler-Maruyama discretization. Here τ = t-s. The flow employs conditional affine coupling layers (Real-NVP) with shift and scale functions implemented by small GRU-based networks conditioned on (x₀ and partial inputs). The initial condition is satisfied exactly by setting θ(0) such that the flow is the identity and p_Z(z|0,x₀)=δ_{x₀}. To evolve θ(τ), the method applies the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle: at each time τ, the residual L★_τ ρ_θ is projected onto the tangent space spanned by ∂ρ_θ/∂θ via Monte Carlo integration. This yields a linear system J_θ η = f_τ, where J_θ contains sensitivities ∂ρ_θ/∂θ and f_τ includes the residual and the time derivative of the reference density. The solution η approximates dθ/dτ, which is then integrated with a standard ODE solver (RK3(2)). Residual evaluation samples x from the current approximate density ν_θ(τ) by first drawing z~p_Z and applying the inverse flow. This adaptive sampling focuses on high-probability regions. After offline integration over τ∈[0,T-s], the stored parameters enable fast online evaluation for any x₀ without retraining.

## Key Results
- NGNF accurately reconstructs the true multimodal, non-Gaussian transition density for the 2D anisotropic Beneš SDE, with bounded relative L² error over the integration interval.
- The framework respects the causal dependence on the initial Dirac location x₀; different x₀ yield distinct, correctly shifted density shapes.
- After offline training, evaluating the density for a new x₀ is orders of magnitude faster than solving the Fokker-Planck PDE from scratch, supporting many-query scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The Normalizing Flow ansatz automatically enforces probabilistic constraints (positivity, unit mass) by construction, avoiding post-processing or penalty terms required by generic neural network surrogates.
- **Mechanism**: The solution is represented as a deterministic, invertible transformation of a known reference density (a scaled Gaussian) via the change-of-variables formula. This guarantees the output is a valid density function for any parameter value.
- **Core assumption**: The chosen flow architecture (Real-NVP coupling layers) provides sufficient expressivity to model the target density's complex, evolving shape from the simple reference.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "parametrizes the solution via a time-dependent conditional Normalizing Flow that transforms a reference Gaussian distribution... automatically ensuring positivity and mass conservation." [section 3.1]: Details the conditional affine coupling layers and their invertibility. [section 3.2]: Explicitly states the change-of-variable formula ensures a valid density.
- **Break condition**: If the flow's architectural inductive bias (e.g., coupling layer partition size `m`) is too restrictive, it may not be able to express the necessary transport map, leading to persistent model error regardless of training.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Adaptive sampling from the *current* Normalizing Flow approximation dramatically reduces the variance of the Monte Carlo estimator for the PDE residual, focusing computations where the solution has mass.
- **Mechanism**: The residual minimization (Dirac-Frenkel principle) is evaluated by sampling state points `x` from the *approximated* density at that time, `ν_θ(τ)(·|x0) ≈ ρ(·|τ+s, s, x0)`. This adaptive measure naturally follows the evolving support, avoiding wasted evaluations in low-density regions.
- **Core assumption**: The current flow approximation is sufficiently accurate that its distribution is a good proxy for the true solution's support, especially in the early stages of training when a poor choice would lead to catastrophic failure.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "...adaptive sampling from the current flow to evaluate the residual efficiently." [section 4]: Derives the Monte Carlo estimator using `ν_θ(τ)` as the sampling measure, arguing uniform sampling is ineffective. [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers on adaptive PINNs/PDE solvers discuss similar concepts but the specific `ν_θ`-sampling is a novel proposal here. Assumption: this heuristic works in practice for the tested anisotropic case.
- **Break condition**: If the initial flow parameters are poorly initialized, the initial `ν_θ(0)` is a poor match for the atomic delta (even with the scaled Gaussian source), potentially missing the nascent solution completely and causing gradient estimates to be null or misleading.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The Neural Galerkin approach (time-evolving parameters via minimizing the space residual) enforces the correct causal dependence on the initial Dirac location `x0` and the PDE dynamics, avoiding the loss-balancing issues of standard space-time collocation PINNs.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of minimizing a static space-time loss, the parameters `θ(τ)` are evolved by solving a small optimization at each time step (or via the derived ODE `dθ/dτ`). This embeds the temporal direction into the training process, naturally propagating information from the initial condition forward.
- **Core assumption**: The parametrization of `θ(τ)` (using GRU-based networks within the flow layers) has the capacity to represent the true solution's trajectory in parameter space.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "...derive a system of ODEs for the time evolution of the Normalizing Flow's parameters." and "enforces the causal relationship between the initial datum and the density function." [section 1]: Critiques PINNs for struggling with atomic ICs and loss balancing; positions Neural Galerkin as the solution. [section 4.1]: Shows the discrete ODE system `J_τ * dθ/dτ ≈ f_τ` derived from the residual minimization.
- **Break condition**: If the time-stepping scheme for integrating `dθ/dτ` is too coarse or the optimization at each step is inaccurate, the accumulated error will break the causal link, making the solution at later times insensitive to `x0`.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Source Distribution `p_Z` -> Time-dependent Conditional Normalizing Flow `n_θ(τ)` (stacked affine coupling layers) -> Parameter ODE System (least-squares estimator) -> Adaptive Sampling Procedure (draw from ν_θ) -> Offline Training (integrate θ(τ)) -> Online Evaluation (instant density for new x₀)

### Critical path
1. **Initialize** all flow parameters to zero: `s_θ^l(0)=0`, `t_θ^l(0)=0` so that `n_θ(0)` is identity; choose `p_Z(z|0,x₀)=δ_{x₀}`.
2. **For** each time step `τ` from `Δτ` to `T-s`:
   a. Sample batch of initial points `{x₀^i}` from the prior distribution.
   b. For each `x₀^i`, draw one sample `x^i ~ ν_θ(τ)(·|x₀^i)` by sampling `z^i ~ p_Z(·|τ,x₀^i)` and applying the inverse flow `n_θ(τ)^{-1}`.
   c. Compute the Jacobian matrix `J_τ` of sensitivities `∂ρ_θ/∂θ` (via autodiff) and the residual vector `f_τ` (which includes the Fokker-Planck operator and `∂_τ p_Z`).
   d. Solve the linear least-squares problem `η = argmin ||J_τ η - f_τ||²` (with optional regularization) to obtain `η ≈ dθ/dτ`.
   e. Integrate `θ` using an ODE solver (e.g., forward Euler or RK3(2)) to obtain `θ(τ+Δτ)`.
3. **Store** the trajectory of parameters `{θ(τ_k)}` for later use.
   - **Online phase**: For a new `x₀_new`, compute the density `ρ(x|θ(τ),τ,x₀_new)` directly using the flow and `p_Z`; no re-training needed.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Flow depth `L`**: More layers increase expressivity but raise inversion/Jacobian cost and risk training instability.
- **Coupling split `m`**: Smaller `m` (e.g., `m=1` in 2D) forces more alternating transformations; may require more layers. Larger `m` reduces depth but may limit each layer's ability to couple variables.
- **GRU hidden size**: Controls capacity of the shift/scale functions. Too small limits representation; too large increases parameters and risk of overfitting.
- **Sampling batch size `N` per `x₀`**: Larger `N` reduces Monte Carlo noise in `J_τ` and `f_τ` but increases per-step cost; adaptive batch sizing could be beneficial.

### Failure signatures
- **Diverging/NaN parameters**: Often from poor `ν_θ(τ)` sampling early on (flow too far from truth) causing `f_τ` huge or `J_τ` ill-conditioned. *Check*: Monitor norm of `η` and residual `||f_τ||` at first few steps.
- **Loss of causal dependence**: Final density at `T` looks similar for different `x₀`. *Check*: Visualize `ρ(x|T,s,x₀)` for distinct `x₀`; they should be clearly separated/shifted.
- **Inability to capture multimodality**: Output density remains unimodal. *Check*: Compare against known solution or SDE samples; look for missing peaks.
- **Mass drift**: Integrated density over domain deviates from 1. *Check*: Periodically compute `∫ ρ(x) dx` via Monte Carlo; should stay near 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does NGNF scale to higher-dimensional PDEs (d > 2), and does the adaptive sampling strategy remain effective in mitigating the curse of dimensionality?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The method is only validated on a 2D Beneš SDE; the authors state that adaptive sampling is "of vital importance to address high-dimensional PDEs" but provide no higher-dimensional experiments.
- **Why unresolved**: The effectiveness of sampling from the current flow (ν_θ) and the stability of the ODE integration for θ(τ) may degrade as dimensionality increases, potentially leading to prohibitive sample complexity or numerical instability.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic numerical experiments on synthetic high-dimensional SDEs (e.g., with known analytical solutions) comparing NGNF's relative L² error and sample efficiency against dimensionality, along with an analysis of the condition number of the Jacobian in the flow.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the optimal architectural choice (e.g., number of coupling layers, hidden size, conditioning strategy) for the Normalizing Flow to accurately capture diverse TPDF features (e.g., multimodality, anisotropy) across different SDEs?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Architectural hyperparameters (10 layers, GRU hidden size=4) are chosen heuristically with limited justification beyond GRU's suitability for "sharp turns"; no ablation study is provided.
- **Why unresolved**: The expressivity of Real-NVP flows for high-dimensional, non-Gaussian densities is not fully understood in this context, and the conditioning on x₀ may interact with architecture in unforeseen ways.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A controlled study varying layer count, hidden dimension, and alternative architectures (e.g., neural ODE-based flows) on a benchmark suite of SDEs with quantified metrics for density approximation (e.g., sliced Wasserstein distance).

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How sensitive is NGNF's long-time accuracy to the choice of the reference source distribution p_Z(·|τ, x₀), and can a theoretically motivated alternative (beyond the Euler-Maruyama approximation) improve performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The source p_Z is selected as the EM discretization density to satisfy the initial condition exactly and for its local convergence property, but no alternatives are considered.
- **Why unresolved**: The EM-based source is only asymptotically accurate as τ → 0; for finite τ, its mismatch with the true TPDF might propagate error through the flow transformation, especially for long-time integration.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comparative experiments using different reference processes (e.g., Ornstein-Uhlenbeck as a linear surrogate) on SDEs with varying drift nonlinearities, assessing error growth over time and robustness to τ.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What is the actual computational trade-off between offline training and online evaluation in NGNF for many-query applications (e.g., Bayesian inference), and how does it depend on the SDE's dimensionality and the number of queries?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The abstract and conclusion claim online evaluation is "significantly more cost-effective," but no quantitative benchmarks (e.g., wall-clock time, memory) are provided against alternative methods (e.g., solving the FP PDE online, MCMC).
- **Why unresolved**: The cost of offline training (solving the ODE system for θ(τ) with adaptive sampling) is not characterized relative to the per-query cost of evaluating the trained flow; the break-even point for query numbers is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A detailed complexity analysis and empirical timing study comparing total time (training + N queries) for NGNF versus direct PDE solvers or sampling-based TPDF estimation, across dimensions d and query counts N.

## Limitations
- Architectural and hyperparameter details (e.g., exact GRU subnetwork design, sampling size, time step, ODE solver settings) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction and sensitivity analysis.
- The adaptive sampling heuristic lacks theoretical guarantees; if the current flow approximation is inaccurate early in training, residual estimates may be biased or degenerate, potentially causing training failure.
- Experimental validation is limited to a single 2D anisotropic Beneš SDE; scalability to higher dimensions and more complex drift/diffusion structures remains unproven.
