# Enhancing Multilingual RAG Systems with Debiased Language Preference-Guided Query Fusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.02956
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02956
- **Reference count**: 24
- **Primary result**: DELTA achieves +4.07% average end-to-end accuracy gain with Qwen3 and low latency (~1.13 sec/query) by fusing global English pivots with localized native anchors using a debiased preference signal.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical evaluation flaw in multilingual retrieval-augmented generation (mRAG): standard benchmarks overestimate English preference due to structural biases like disproportionate English gold passages and cultural priors. The authors introduce DeLP, a ridge-calibration metric that removes these confounds, revealing that retrievers actually prefer monolingual alignment. Leveraging this insight, they propose DELTA, a query fusion framework that combines a global English pivot with localized native-language anchors, weighted by repetition based on debiased preference signals. Experiments on MKQA show DELTA consistently outperforms English-translation and document-level baselines across multiple generators, with significant accuracy gains and minimal latency overhead.

## Method Summary
The paper first quantifies and decomposes three structural priors—gold-availability, exposure, and cultural—that distort observed language preference in mRAG benchmarks. Using these priors as features in a ridge regression, it computes DeLP, a debiased metric that isolates the intrinsic preference score for a query-document language pair. Analysis via DeLP reveals a strong diagonal dominance, indicating retrievers fundamentally prefer monolingual alignment. Motivated by this, DELTA constructs a fused query composed of segments: a global English pivot, the original native query, title bridges, aliases, and a locale hint. A repetition policy upweights local segments for culture-specific queries (per an LLM classifier) and the global pivot otherwise, steering retrieval toward native evidence without retrainng the retriever.

## Key Results
- DeLP calibration reduces correlation of measured preference with structural priors from r > 0.91 to r < 0.15, and debiased matrices show strong monolingual alignment (diagonal dominance).
- DELTA achieves +4.07% average end-to-end character 3-gram recall improvement with Qwen3 over strong baselines (e.g., English translation, DKM-RAG) at ~1.13 seconds per query.
- DELTA recovers gold passages with a mean best rank of 10.39 and 66.2% Top-10 rate, effectively restoring native anchors ignored by English-pivoting baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structural Priors Inflate Observed English Preference
The perceived superiority of English pivoting in multilingual RAG is primarily an artifact of dataset-level structural biases (gold-availability prior, exposure bias, cultural priors), not an inherent model preference for English. Benchmarks like MKQA have disproportionate English gold passage coverage and document density, forcing retrieval into English even for non-English queries. Cultural priors further skew results when queries reference locale-specific entities with native surface forms that appear only in local-language corpora. The core assumption is that model behavior is confounded by the distribution of evidence; if evidence were evenly distributed, the measured preference would change. Evidence: Analysis shows extreme gold-availability imbalance (e.g., 73.29% of query-language instances have English gold) and cultural queries where ~20% have gold only in local Wikipedia.

### Mechanism 2: DeLP Calibration Isolates Intrinsic Monolingual Alignment
After regressing out exposure, gold-availability, and cultural priors, retrievers exhibit a fundamental preference for monolingual query-document alignment rather than English-centric processing. DeLP defines a prior feature vector ϕ(Lq, Ld) containing log-transformed exposure, corpus size, passage length, gold-availability, and cultural priors, plus a same-language indicator. Ridge regression fits coefficients β̂ to predict raw MLRS scores. The debiased DeLP score is the residual (raw score − ϕ⊤β̂) re-centered by the global mean, removing variance explained by structural confounds. The core assumption is that structural priors are measurable and additive components; the residual reflects intrinsic linguistic tendency. Evidence: Pre-calibration MLRS scores correlate highly (r > 0.91) with all priors; post-calibration DeLP correlations drop sharply (r < 0.15). DeLP matrices show strongest scores consistently on the diagonal (Lq = Ld) across three encoders, whereas raw MLRS favored English.

### Mechanism 3: DELTA Query Fusion Exploits Monolingual Alignment via Repetition-Based Cue Weighting
A lightweight query-level fusion strategy that combines a global English pivot with localized native anchors, weighted by repetition according to debiased preference signals, improves retrieval and end-to-end mRAG accuracy. DELTA constructs a fused query Qfused from segments: [GLOB] (English pivot), [LOCAL] (original query), [TITLE_BRIDGE] (paired titles), [ALIASES], and [LOCALE_HINT]. A repetition policy rlocal(rglob) based on culture-specificity (y) and confidence (c) upweights local vs. global segments without modifying the retriever. High-confidence culture-specific queries strongly favor local repeats; non-culture queries mildly favor the global pivot. The core assumption is that injecting native surface-form anchors leverages the retriever’s intrinsic monolingual alignment preference to improve recall of locally relevant evidence, while the English pivot maintains coverage for gold availability. Evidence: DELTA outperforms baselines in end-to-end accuracy (e.g., +4.07 avg with Qwen3) with low latency, recovers gold passages with mean best rank 10.39, and cue ablation shows all segments contribute, with [GLOB] and [TITLE_BRIDGE] providing significant independent gains.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  User Query -> Cultural Classifier -> DELTA Fusion Engine -> Multilingual Retriever -> Generator
  (DeLP Calibrator operates offline to inform design, not in live pipeline)
- **Critical path**: User query → Cultural classification → DELTA fusion (Qfused) → Retrieval → Generation → Answer
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Simplicity vs. Precision: Heuristic repetition is fast but may misweight cues vs. learned fusion.
  - Coverage vs. Discrimination: Global pivot ensures gold availability coverage; local anchors provide discriminative matching—a zero-sum balance.
  - Static vs. Adaptive Thresholds: Fixed τ thresholds balance simplicity and robustness; adaptive thresholds could improve performance but add complexity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Over-localization: Cultural classifier over-triggers local upweighting for globally relevant queries, suppressing English gold.
  - Cue pollution: Poor LLM-generated anchors introduce noise; deduplication mitigates but cannot fix fundamentally wrong anchors.
  - Length explosion: High repetition exceeds retriever’s max length, truncating critical cues; mitigated by final truncation but risks information loss.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **DeLP Validation**: Report Pearson correlation of raw MLRS with priors (high) and DeLP with priors (low); confirm diagonal dominance in DeLP matrices.
  2. **Head-to-head mRAG Evaluation**: Compare DELTA vs. English Translation, MultiRAG, CrossRAG, DKM-RAG, QTT-RAG on MKQA with multiple generators; measure end-to-end character 3-gram recall and latency.
  3. **Ablation under Fixed Evidence**: Keep retrieved passages identical across methods, vary only query formulation (Original, +Global, +Title, +Aliases, +Locale, All cues) to isolate generation-time impact of each cue.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Validated primarily on Wikipedia-based benchmarks (MKQA/KILT); performance on non-Wikipedia, lower-resource, or domain-specific corpora is uncertain.
- **DeLP assumptions**: Ridge regression assumes linear, additive confounds; unmeasured nonlinear interactions or latent biases may persist in residuals.
- **Cultural classifier**: The LLM-based classifier’s heuristic definition of “culture-specific” may not align with human intuition; errors directly impact DELTA’s repetition policy.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical evidence that structural biases inflate English preference in standard benchmarks; DELTA’s end-to-end gains and low latency on MKQA.
- **Medium**: DeLP successfully decouples measured preference from known priors within the tested setup; monolingual alignment as the intrinsic signal.
- **Low**: That debiased monolingual preference universally holds across all multilingual contexts and retrievers beyond those tested.

## Next Checks
1. **Corpus shift test**: Replicate DeLP calibration and DELTA on a non-Wikipedia multilingual benchmark (e.g., mMarco, or a curated news corpus) to assess robustness to different evidence distributions.
2. **Nonlinear confound modeling**: Extend DeLP with polynomial features or a non-linear model (e.g., GAM) to check if residual preference patterns change, testing linearity assumption.
3. **Classifier failure analysis**: Systematically evaluate DELTA’s repetition policy when the cultural classifier is forced to err (e.g., flip labels for known culture-specific queries) to quantify sensitivity and define safe operating thresholds.
