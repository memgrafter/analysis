# SINDy-KANs: Sparse identification of non-linear dynamics through Kolmogorov-Arnold networks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.18548
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18548
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SINDy-KANs unify sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy) with Kolmogorov-Arnold networks (KANs) to provide parsimonious symbolic equations while retaining deep compositional expressivity.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces SINDy-KANs, a method that integrates SINDy's sparse regression framework with the flexible univariate activation functions of KANs. By applying SINDy-like regression to each activation during training, the approach enforces sparsity while leveraging deep function compositions. It achieves high accuracy on symbolic regression and dynamical system identification tasks, recovering ground-truth equations with coefficients matching to four decimal places and maintaining stability under up to 30% noise. Training is efficient, taking minutes on a laptop. The work addresses the lack of guaranteed sparsity in KANs and the limited expressivity of standard SINDy.

## Method Summary
SINDy-KANs extend KANs by incorporating a SINDy-inspired sparse regression component. A KAN backbone consists of layers where each node computes a univariate activation function (typically spline-based). For each activation function φℓ,j,i, a library of candidate functions Θℓ,i is constructed from the activation's input. The method introduces a trainable shadow matrix Λ that approximates sparse coefficients ξℓ,j,i, with a loss combining KAN data fit (L_KAN), sparse KAN fit (L_S), consistency between KAN and sparse KAN (L_Λ), L1 regularization on Λ, and a consistency term between Λ and the least-squares solution Ξₛ. This joint optimization yields a parsimonious symbolic representation while preserving the deep composition capabilities of KANs.

## Key Results
- **Symbolic regression**: On f(x,y)=cos(x²+y), SINDy-KAN recovers coefficients 0.9999 cos(1.0000x²+0.9999y), whereas standard KAN (pykan) misses the quadratic term.
- **ODE identification**: For a 3D linear ODE, all matrix entries are within 0.1% of true values; for the Lorenz system, coefficients are within 0.00001; for Kuramoto oscillators, learned coefficients are ~1.0 with only 3 active nodes out of 6.
- **Efficiency and robustness**: Training takes minutes on a laptop; direct SINDy-KANs (without underlying KAN training) are ~33% faster but slightly less robust; the method remains stable up to ~30% relative noise on ODE data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Sparsity enforcement via SINDy regression on activations**  
During training, for each activation φℓ,j,i, a library Θℓ,i of candidate functions (e.g., polynomials, sines, cosines) is built from its input. A sparse coefficient vector ξℓ,j,i is sought such that φℓ,j,i ≈ Θℓ,i ξℓ,j,i. The trainable shadow matrix Λ mirrors the structure of all ξℓ,j,i and is penalized with L1 regularization (λ₁||Λ||₁) to promote sparsity. A consistency term (λ₂||Λ−Ξₛ||²) aligns Λ with the least-squares solution Ξₛ obtained from the current activations. This setup ensures that only essential functions from the library are used.  
*Core assumption*: The true activation functions can be expressed as sparse linear combinations of the chosen library.  
*Evidence*: Section 3, Eq. (13) and Eq. (16); abstract states "perform SINDy-like sparse regression on each univariate KAN activation function during training".  
*Break condition*: Incomplete library leads to high residual or incorrect terms.

**Mechanism 2: Deep composition beyond standard SINDy**  
Standard SINDy requires predefining all possible terms in the library, which becomes intractable for compositions. KANs naturally compose univariate functions through multiple layers: the output of one activation feeds into the next. By applying SINDy at each activation, the overall network becomes a composition of sparse univariate functions, enabling discovery of equations like cos(x²+y) that standard SINDy cannot represent.  
*Core assumption*: The target system can be decomposed into a finite composition of univariate sparse functions.  
*Evidence*: Abstract and Section 1 contrast with SINDy; example of f(x,y)=cos(x²+y) shows recovery while pykan fails.  
*Break condition*: Excessive depth may yield complex/uninterpretable compositions despite accuracy.

**Mechanism 3: Multi-term loss balancing**  
The loss L combines KAN data fit (L_KAN), sparse KAN fit (L_S), consistency (L_Λ), and regularization: L = λ_KAN L_KAN + λ_S L_S + λ_Λ L_Λ + λ₁||Λ||₁ + λ₂||Λ−Ξₛ||². This ensures (i) the KAN matches data, (ii) the sparse approximation also fits, (iii) Λ stays sparse, and (iv) Λ remains close to the least-squares fit. This joint optimization prevents degenerate solutions and stabilizes training.  
*Core assumption*: Hyperparameters can be tuned to balance all terms.  
*Evidence*: Section 3 Eq. (16); Section 4.1 Figure 3b shows loss plateaus corresponding to sparsity levels.  
*Break condition*: Poor weighting (e.g., λ₁ too small) yields dense Λ; λ_S too large may overfit; λ₂ too low causes divergence.

## Foundational Learning
- **SINDy (Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics)**: A method that builds a library of candidate functions from data and uses sparse regression to discover governing equations. *Why needed*: SINDy provides the per-activation sparse regression scheme. *Quick check*: How does sequentially-thresholded least squares (STLS) work and why might it fail with correlated terms?
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs)**: Neural networks with univariate activation functions (often splines) on edges, composing via sums/products across layers. *Why needed*: KANs define the deep function composition backbone. *Quick check*: How does a KAN represent multiplication of inputs, and what role does the B-spline grid play?
- **Sparse Regression and L1 Regularization**: Technique to induce sparsity in coefficients by adding an L1 penalty. *Why needed*: L1 regularizes the shadow matrix Λ to enforce parsimonious equations. *Quick check*: Why might direct L1 minimization with gradient-based optimizers be problematic, and how does the Λ/Ξₛ split help?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
KAN backbone (stacked layers with univariate activations) -> Per-activation SINDy regression (construct library Θ, solve least-squares Ξₛ) -> Shadow matrix Λ (trainable, L1-regularized) -> Loss aggregator (computes L_KAN, L_S, L_Λ, regularizations) -> Optimizer (ADAM updates KAN parameters and Λ).

**Critical path**  
1. Forward pass: compute KAN output, store activation inputs and outputs.  
2. SINDy step: for each activation, compute least-squares solution Ξₛ.  
3. Loss calculation: evaluate L_KAN, L_S (using Λ), L_Λ, and regularization terms.  
4. Backward pass: compute gradients w.r.t. KAN spline coefficients and Λ; update via ADAM.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Libraries per layer can be tailored (e.g., polynomials in layer 1, trig in layer 2), but overly expressive libraries risk overfitting and increased computation.  
- Multiplication nodes enable quadratic interactions but add computational cost and may reduce sparsity if not heavily regularized.  
- Direct SINDy-KANs (train Ξₛ directly) are faster but less robust; indirect (with KAN training) is more stable.  
- Hyperparameters (λ_S, λ_Λ, λ₁, λ₂) must be balanced; λ₁ controls sparsity, λ_S and λ_Λ balance data fit vs. sparsity, λ₂ couples Λ and Ξₛ.

**Failure signatures**  
- High ||Λ||₁ (dense Lambda) after training: λ₁ too small or library too large.  
- Stagnant L_KAN: KAN fails to fit data (insufficient depth, low learning rate, poor spline grid).  
- Large L_S or L_Λ: Discrepancy between KAN and sparse KAN; λ₂ too low or inconsistent libraries.  
- With noisy ODE data, spurious higher-order terms appear due to amplified derivative noise.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Symbolic regression: f(x,y)=cos(x²+y)** – Verifies composition recovery; SINDy-KAN should yield 0.9999 cos(1.0000x²+0.9999y) while pykan misses x².  
2. **Linear ODE: 3D system** – Tests coefficient accuracy; all entries within 0.1% of ground truth.  
3. **Nonlinear ODE: Damped pendulum** – With one hidden layer, may learn extra linear term; with two layers, should recover exactly −9.7975 sin(x)−0.0499y.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does interpretability scale with input dimensionality?** – Only low-dimensional (≤3) systems tested; high-dimensional scalability unexplored.
2. **What is robustness to noise and how to improve derivative estimation?** – Minimal noise tests; derivative computation (autodiff) may be suboptimal; need evaluation of alternative methods.
3. **Can hybrid B-spline/symbolic activations improve accuracy when library insufficient?** – Current forces all activations into symbolic linear combos; hybrid approach could retain splines for unevaluated functions.
4. **How to automatically determine optimal SINDy-KAN architecture (depth/width)?** – Fixed architectures used; multi-exit KANs could learn structure simultaneously with symbolic representation.

## Limitations
- Implementation details for per-activation regression and derivative computation are underspecified, hindering reproducibility.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity is not systematically studied; defaults may be task-specific.
- Evaluation limited to a few low-dimensional benchmarks; scalability to high-dimensional or stiff systems unclear.

## Confidence
- **Sparsity enforcement**: Medium (empirical sparsity shown but depends on loss weighting).
- **Compositional expressivity**: Medium (success on cos(x²+y) demonstrates concept but deeper limits not explored).
- **ODE identification accuracy**: Medium–Low (promising results on few systems; noise robustness only up to 30%).
- **Training efficiency**: Medium (plausible times but no controlled baseline comparisons).

## Next Checks
1. **Library ablation study**: Vary candidate univariate libraries per layer on a suite of symbolic regression tasks to measure recovery fidelity and sparsity.
2. **Derivative robustness experiment**: Compare autodiff from KAN against smoothed finite differences for noisy ODE data, quantifying how sparsity and coefficient accuracy degrade.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep**: On the damped pendulum, perform grid search over λ₁, λ₂, λ_S, λ_Λ to map Pareto fronts of data fit vs. sparsity and identify robust defaults.
