# VowelPrompt: Hearing Speech Emotions from Text via Vowel-level Prosodic Augmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06270
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06270
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** VowelPrompt achieves state-of-the-art speech emotion recognition accuracy by augmenting transcripts with fine-grained, vowel-level prosodic descriptors, e.g., IEMOCAP WF1 73.46% with LLaMA-3-8B.

## Executive Summary
VowelPrompt addresses the key limitation of LLM-based speech emotion recognition (SER) that relies solely on transcripts: the absence of fine-grained prosodic cues. The method extracts pitch, intensity, and duration features from forced-aligned vowel segments, normalizes them per speaker and vowel type, discretizes into ordinal categories, and converts them into natural language tokens that are appended to the transcript. An LLM is then adapted via a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with reasoning traces from a teacher model, followed by reinforcement learning with verifiable reward (RLVR) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This approach yields substantial improvements over transcript-only baselines and prior work, with LLaMA-3-8B achieving 73.46% WF1 on IEMOCAP. Cross-domain and multilingual results confirm robustness, while human evaluation demonstrates higher reasoning faithfulness.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with forced alignment (e.g., Montreal Forced Aligner) to obtain phoneme timestamps from audio and a transcript. Vowel segments are identified using an IPA-based inventory. For each vowel, six low-level descriptors are computed: mean F0, F0 slope, F0 standard deviation, mean intensity, intensity standard deviation, and duration. These features undergo two-stage z-normalization (speaker-level then vowel-type) to control for individual and phonemic variation. Each normalized value is discretized into five ordinal bins via quantile binning and mapped to a short natural-language phrase (e.g., “high F0, rising”). Concatenating these phrases for an utterance yields a prosodic augmentation block. The final prompt combines the utterance transcript, dialogue context, and the augmentation block, structured to elicit `<think>` reasoning and `<answer>` label from the LLM. Training proceeds in two stages: first, SFT on a small subset using gold labels and reasoning traces from a teacher model (e.g., GPT-4o); second, RLVR with GRPO, where multiple responses per prompt are sampled and ranked by a composite reward (1 for exact label match, 1 for valid formatting). A KL penalty regularizes the policy to stay close to the SFT model, yielding a system that effectively leverages prosodic cues and generalizes across domains and languages.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot with GPT-4o (transcript+context): IEMOCAP UACC 62.26% vs 55.51% (baseline) and 60.07% (SpeechCueLLM); MELD WF1 64.17% vs 63.57%/56.74%.
- Supervised with LLaMA-3-8B (SFT&GRPO): IEMOCAP WF1 73.46%, MELD 69.61%, exceeding SpeechCueLLM by up to 2.17%.
- Cross-domain and multilingual gains: IEMOCAP→MELD 60.28% vs 55.16%; MELD→IEMOCAP 51.75% vs 44.79%; zero-shot CaFE 51.42% WF1, EmoDB 69.85%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Vowel-level prosodic augmentation provides fine-grained, interpretable acoustic cues that improve LLM-based speech emotion recognition compared to coarse utterance-level descriptors.
- **Mechanism:** Forced alignment extracts precise vowel segment boundaries. For each vowel, low-level descriptors (pitch level, pitch slope, pitch variation, intensity level, intensity variation, duration) are computed, normalized per speaker and vowel type, discretized via quantile binning into ordinal categories, and converted into natural language tokens (e.g., "high F0, rising, loud, lengthened"). These tokens are appended to the transcript, allowing the LLM to jointly reason over lexical semantics and localized prosody.
- **Core assumption:** Vowels are primary carriers of affective prosody due to their voiced, sustained nature; thus, vowel-centric features capture emotionally salient variations more precisely than global summaries.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Drawing on phonetic evidence that vowels serve as primary carriers of affective prosody, VowelPrompt extracts pitch-, energy-, and duration-based descriptors from time-aligned vowel segments, and converts these features into natural language descriptions."
  - [Section 3.1]: Detailed description of forced alignment, LLD extraction, two-stage normalization, and discretization.
  - [corpus]: Related work such as "A Discourse-level Multi-scale Prosodic Model for Fine-grained Emotion Analysis" (arxiv:2309.11849) also uses phoneme-level prosody, supporting the trend toward fine-grained cues. However, direct comparative evidence is limited to the paper's own experiments.
- **Break condition:** If forced-alignment quality degrades (e.g., due to noisy audio or mismatched language models), vowel boundaries become inaccurate, propagating noisy descriptors that mislead the LLM.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The two-stage adaptation pipeline (SFT followed by RLVR with GRPO) yields more accurate, robust, and interpretable emotion predictions than SFT alone or transcript-only baselines.
- **Mechanism:** SFT uses gold reasoning traces from a high-capacity LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) to cold-start the model, teaching it to produce both correct labels and coherent reasoning. RLVR then refines the policy using a verifiable composite reward: `R = R_acc + R_format`, where `R_acc` is 1 for exact label match and `R_format` is 1 for valid`<answer>` tags. GRPO optimizes the policy to outperform the group average while maintaining diversity, with a KL penalty to prevent deviation from the SFT reference.
- **Core assumption:** Verifiable, rule-based rewards can steer LLM generation without a learned reward model, and group-relative optimization improves stability and generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We adopt a two-stage adaptation procedure comprising supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR), implemented via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to enhance reasoning capability, enforce structured output adherence, and improve generalization across domains and speaker variations."
  - [Section 3.2]: Formal definition of reward, GRPO algorithm, and training details.
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes "RLAIF-SPA: Optimizing LLM-based Emotional Speech Synthesis via RLAIF" (arxiv:2510.14628), which explores RL for emotional speech tasks, suggesting broader applicability of RLVR. However, direct evidence for GRPO in SER is from the paper's results.
- **Break condition:** If the reward components are poorly balanced (e.g., `R_format` dominates), the model may prioritize formatting over accuracy, or if KL weight is too low, the policy may drift into low-quality regions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An IPA-based, language-agnostic vowel mapping enables VowelPrompt to generalize across multiple languages without language-specific redesign.
- **Mechanism:** Forced alignment produces language-specific phonemes, which are mapped to a shared IPA inventory. Speaker- and language-level z-normalization control for cross-lingual variation in pitch/intensity ranges. Discretized descriptors are converted to English natural language, which multilingual LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Qwen2) can process regardless of the source language.
- **Core assumption:** Vowel-level prosodic cues are broadly comparable across languages after normalization, and English verbalizations suffice for multilingual LLMs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Multilingual extension with IPA-based vowel mapping."
  - [Section 3.3]: "To extend VowelPrompt to multilingual SER, we adopt a language-agnostic framework grounded in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) to unify vowel representations across languages."
  - [Section 4.5]: Zero-shot results on French (CaFE) and German (EmoDB), and supervised results on mixed-lingual ASVP-ESD, demonstrating effectiveness.
  - [corpus]: While not directly about VowelPrompt, the existence of multilingual SER datasets (CaFE, EmoDB) supports the feasibility of cross-lingual evaluation. The paper's own experiments provide the primary evidence.
- **Break condition:** Languages with drastically different prosodic systems (e.g., tonal languages) may require additional features; forced alignment errors in low-resource languages can corrupt vowel extraction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
- Forced Aligner (e.g., MFA): Takes audio + transcript → phoneme-level timestamps.
- Vowel Extractor: Filters phonemes to vowels using IPA inventory.
- Feature Computer: Computes pitch (mean, slope, std), intensity (mean, std), duration for each vowel segment.
- Normalizer: Applies speaker-level and vowel-type z-normalization.
- Discretizer: Quantile binning into K (default 5) ordinal categories.
- NL Converter: Maps discrete values to natural language descriptors (e.g., "high F0, rising").
- Prompt Builder: Combines transcript, context, and vowel descriptors into a structured prompt with `<think>`/`<answer>` tags.
- LLM Backbone: Text-only model (LLaMA, GPT-4o, Qwen2) processes prompt.
- SFT Trainer: Fine-tunes on (prompt, gold reasoning + label) pairs.
- GRPO Trainer: Optimizes policy using composite reward and group-relative comparisons.

**Critical path:**
1. Forced alignment produces vowel boundaries.
2. For each vowel, compute LLDs → normalize → discretize → generate NL descriptors.
3. Construct prompt with transcript, context, and vowel descriptors.
4. LLM generates `<think>` reasoning and `<answer>` label.
5. During training: SFT learns to mimic gold outputs; GRPO refines using reward feedback.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Granularity vs. sparsity: More quantization bins (K) capture finer variation but risk sparse, noisy tokens; K=5 found optimal.
- Interpretability vs. performance: Vowel-level descriptors are human-readable but may omit higher-level patterns that raw audio embeddings capture; still, VowelPrompt outperforms projection-based audio encoders.
- KL weight: Higher KL preserves SFT behavior (better in-domain), lower KL allows more adaptation (better cross-domain); paper finds insensitivity around 0.25–0.5.
- Language coverage: IPA mapping works for languages with supported forced aligners; low-resource languages may suffer.

**Failure signatures:**
- Alignment errors: Inconsistent vowel boundaries lead to mismatched descriptors and transcript; performance drops even with 15% boundary perturbations (Table 21).
- The LLM ignores prosodic descriptors and relies on lexical patterns. **Diagnostic:** Run the “prosody permutation” control: within each batch, shuffle vowel descriptors across utterances while keeping transcripts fixed. Performance should drop significantly if prosody is used (as shown in Appendix A.6). Also, measure the correlation between predicted labels and the pos/neg sentiment of keywords (e.g., “good” vs. “bad”); a high correlation without prosody suggests lexical over-reliance.
- GRPO destabilizes training or causes overfitting to format reward. **Diagnostic:** Monitor the KL divergence between the policy and SFT model during GRPO. If it grows unbounded, reduce the KL coefficient. If format reward (having `<think>/<answer>`) becomes high but accuracy does not improve, increase the batch size or number of GRPO steps to ensure the accuracy reward signal is adequately sampled.
- Discretization into K bins loses too much information. **Diagnostic:** Run the K-ablation (Appendix A.4) in a minimal form: try K=3,5,7 on a dev set. If performance peaks at K=5 (as reported), stick to 5 bins. If your dev set shows a different optimum, adjust accordingly but expect a trade-off between granularity and stability.
- Multilingual failure: If language-level normalization fails (e.g., due to small speaker set), descriptors become incomparable. **Diagnostic:** For non-English datasets (CaFE, EmoDB), verify that forced alignment phonemes correctly map to the shared IPA vowel set. Check the distribution of extracted vowel types; unexpected absence of common vowels (e.g., `/y/` in French) indicates mapping issues. Use MFA’s G2P models specific to the language.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Zero-shot evaluation: Compare VowelPrompt (transcript+context) vs. transcript-only and SpeechCueLLM on IEMOCAP/MELD using GPT-4o and LLaMA-3-8B to establish baseline gains.
2. Ablation on individual features: Remove one descriptor at a time under SFT to quantify each feature’s contribution (Table 8).
3. Cross-domain transfer: Train on IEMOCAP, test on MELD (and vice versa) under zero-shot, SFT, and SFT&GRPO to test generalization (Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does VowelPrompt perform under real-world conditions where transcripts are derived from automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems and may contain errors?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method assumes access to accurate orthographic transcripts for forced alignment and does not evaluate the impact of ASR errors or transcript noise.
- **Why unresolved:** Forced alignment and vowel segmentation rely on precise transcript boundaries; ASR errors could misalign segments, distort prosodic descriptors, and degrade emotion recognition performance. The paper only tests with gold transcripts, leaving robustness to ASR noise unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments using ASR-generated transcripts (with controlled word error rates) on standard benchmarks, measuring the drop in performance for VowelPrompt versus baselines. Additionally, analyze error propagation through the alignment and feature extraction pipeline, and explore mitigation strategies like alignment robust to transcript noise.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the handcrafted vowel-level prosodic descriptors be replaced by differentiable, learned feature extractors that maintain interpretability while adapting to speaker and language variability?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 3.1 describes a fixed pipeline: extract hand-designed low-level descriptors (pitch level, slope, variation; intensity; duration), apply speaker/vowel normalization, discretize via quantile binning, and convert to text. No learning occurs in this stage.
- **Why unresolved:** Handcrafted features may miss subtle or language-specific prosodic cues and lack flexibility to optimize for the downstream LLM. A learned extractor could potentially improve accuracy and transferability, but might sacrifice interpretability unless designed carefully.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose a neural prosodic feature extractor that consumes raw audio (or embeddings) and outputs a sequence of discretized descriptors in a differentiable manner. Train it jointly with the LLM via the same SFT+GRPO pipeline, then compare performance and interpretability (e.g., via human evaluation of reasoning traces) against the handcrafted version.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the vowel-centric approach generalize to tonal languages (e.g., Mandarin, Thai) where fundamental frequency variations encode lexical tone, and to languages with limited forced alignment resources?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The evaluation covers English, French, German, and a mixed‑language corpus, but excludes tonal languages. The method relies on Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA) models that are unavailable for many low‑resource or tonal languages.
- **Why unresolved:** In tonal languages, F0 patterns are used for word distinction, which could confound emotional prosody or require separate modeling. Vowel segmentation might still be feasible, but the discriminative power of the current descriptors may change. Additionally, lack of pretrained alignment tools could hinder deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect or use existing emotional speech corpora in tonal languages (e.g., Mandarin CASIA, Thai), obtain forced alignment (or develop alternative vowel segmentation), and run the full VowelPrompt pipeline. Compare performance to sentence‑level baselines and analyze whether vowel descriptors remain informative. Investigate lightweight alignment methods for low‑resource settings.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the VowelPrompt framework be effectively applied to other paralinguistic tasks beyond emotion recognition, such as sarcasm detection, speaker credibility assessment, or mental health condition identification from speech?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on emotion recognition (IEMOCAP, MELD, CaFE, EmoDB, ASVP‑ESD) and does not explore any other downstream tasks, despite the general nature of the augmentation approach.
- **Why unresolved:** Different paralinguistic tasks may rely on distinct prosodic patterns and reasoning structures; for example, sarcasm often involves prosodic exaggeration and timing, while depression might manifest in reduced vocal variability. It is unclear if the same vowel‑level descriptors are optimal or if the LLM can learn task‑specific mappings without modification.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt VowelPrompt to multiple new tasks by curating appropriate datasets, maintaining the same feature extraction but adjusting prompts and output labels. Compare performance against text‑only and other augmentation baselines; perform feature ablation to see which descriptors are most relevant per task. This would demonstrate the portability and limitations of the approach.

## Limitations
- Forced alignment details (MFA model version, Praat parameters, phoneme-to-IPA mapping) are unreported, risking inconsistent vowel extraction across languages/accents.
- GRPO hyperparameters (group size, learning rate, number of steps, reward weighting) are not fully specified, hindering stable replication.
- SFT teacher data sourcing (size, trace generation method) is ambiguous, affecting generalizability.
- Multilingual evaluation is limited to European languages; performance on tonal or low‑resource languages remains unknown.

## Confidence
- Vowel-level augmentation efficacy: **Medium**
- Two-stage (SFT+GRPO) pipeline: **Medium**
- IPA-based multilingual mapping: **Low**
