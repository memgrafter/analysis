# Less Noise, Same Certificate: Retain Sensitivity for Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03172  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03172  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Retain sensitivity (RS) exactly quantifies the minimal noise needed for certified unlearning, enabling orders-of-magnitude less noise than global sensitivity (GS) when the retain set is fixed.

## Executive Summary
Certified machine unlearning removes the influence of a deletion set from a trained model, ideally matching a retraining baseline. Existing approaches often add noise scaled to global sensitivity—the worst-case change over any neighboring dataset—which can be overly conservative. This paper introduces **retain sensitivity (RS)**, defined as the worst-case output change when deleting from a fixed retain set *R*. While insufficient for differential privacy, RS is shown to be exactly sufficient for unlearning, allowing significantly smaller noise for the same privacy certificate. The authors prove that across canonical problems (median, MST weight, PCA, SVM, ERM), RS can be orders of magnitude smaller than GS due to data-dependent properties like spacing, eigengap, margin, and curvature. For active unlearning algorithms (e.g., Descent-to-Delete, Newton Update), replacing global strong convexity assumptions with retain-set-specific curvature further reduces required noise. Empirical results confirm substantial reductions, particularly when the retain set is well-conditioned. The work positions RS as a fundamental metric for calibrating unlearning noise and suggests that DP-trained models may already inherit unlearning guarantees when analyzed via RS.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is to condition sensitivity analysis on the fixed retain set *R* rather than considering all possible neighboring datasets. For passive unlearning, Gaussian noise scaled to RS ensures the unlearned model is indistinguishable from one retrained on *R*. For active algorithms, deterministic updates exploit the geometry of *R* (via curvature λ<sub>*R*</sub>) to reduce the initial bias before adding noise. Problem-specific RS formulas are derived by analyzing local stability around *R*: for median, it depends on local spacing; for MST, on minimum cut weight; for PCA, on eigengap; for SVM, on margin; for ERM, on empirical curvature λ<sub>*R*</sub>. The side information *T*(*S*) must contain the full retain set *R* to compute RS. This shifts the paradigm from worst-case global guarantees to data-aware certificates.

## Key Results
- Retain sensitivity is exactly sufficient for (*ε*,*δ*)-unlearning and can be far smaller than global sensitivity when the retain set is fixed.
- For median, MST weight, PCA, SVM, and ERM, RS is provably bounded by data-dependent quantities (spacing, eigengap, margin, curvature) that can be orders of magnitude smaller than their global counterparts.
- Active unlearning algorithms that replace global strong convexity λ with retain-set curvature λ<sub>*R*</sub> achieve noise/iteration reductions scaling as (λ/λ<sub>*R*</sub>) or (λ/λ<sub>*R*</sub>)<sup>3</sup>, with empirical reductions up to 10<sup>5</sup>×.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Retain sensitivity (RS) exactly quantifies the minimal noise needed for certified unlearning because the unlearning and retraining distributions share the same baseline retain set *R*. By adding Gaussian noise scaled to RS, the output perturbation mechanism (Theorem 2.11) ensures the two distributions are indistinguishable under the unlearning condition. This contrasts with differential privacy, which must hide an individual’s contribution across all possible neighboring datasets and thus requires global sensitivity (GS). The key requirement is that the unlearning algorithm has access to the full retain set *R* to compute RS and use an identical noise law in both scenarios.

### Mechanism 2
For canonical problems, RS can be orders of magnitude smaller than GS because the analysis conditions on a fixed, typical retain set *R* and exploits local data structure. Closed-form RS bounds are derived that depend on *R*-specific quantities: local spacing (median), minimum cut weight (MST), eigengap (PCA), margin (SVM), and empirical curvature (ERM). These quantities are uniformly bounded by GS but are often much smaller in practice. The magnitude of reduction depends on how well-structured the retain set is—e.g., well-spaced data for median, large eigengap for PCA, large margin for SVM, positive curvature λ<sub>*R*</sub> for ERM.

### Mechanism 3
Active unlearning algorithms (Descent-to-Delete, Newton Update) perform deterministic updates before adding noise. Their analysis traditionally uses global strong convexity λ to bound the distance to the retrained solution. By instead using the retain-set-specific curvature λ<sub>*R*</sub> (which can be much larger when *R* is well-conditioned), the initial bias term shrinks and/or convergence accelerates. This leads to fewer iterations or smaller noise scales, with gains scaling as (λ/λ<sub>*R*</sub>) for gradient-based methods and (λ/λ<sub>*R*</sub>)<sup>3</sup> for Newton’s method.

## Foundational Learning
### Differential Privacy (DP)
DP protects individual privacy by ensuring neighboring datasets produce nearly indistinguishable outputs, using global sensitivity to calibrate noise. Unlearning relaxes this: it only needs to match retraining on a specific retain set *R*, allowing the use of retain sensitivity. This conceptual separation explains why DP-based unlearning can be conservative.

### Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM)
ERM is the primary learning framework studied. The paper derives RS for ERM by leveraging Lipschitzness and strong convexity. Active unlearning algorithms are also formulated within ERM, where curvature (λ or λ<sub>*R*</sub>) controls solution stability.

### Strong Convexity
Strong convexity bounds how much the ERM solution changes when data changes. The key insight is that global strong convexity λ is a worst-case bound, while λ<sub>*R*</sub> measures curvature only on the retain set and can be much larger, leading to tighter (smaller) RS.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Learner (A) -> Unlearning Engine (¯A) -> Output. Side-Information Store (T(S)=R) feeds RS Calculator and Unlearning Engine. Noise Injector sits between RS Calculator and Unlearning Engine.

### Critical path
1. Train A on full S, store T(S)=R (full retain set).
2. On deletion request for U, compute R = S \ U.
3. Compute RS(R) using problem-specific formula.
4. Passive: output A(S) + Gaussian noise (scale RS(R)). Active: apply deterministic update ¯A₀(U, A(S), R) then add noise.
5. Certification follows from Theorem 2.11.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Accuracy vs. guarantee:** Smaller RS yields less noise but must upper-bound true worst-case change on R; under-estimation breaks certificate.
- **Computation vs. noise reduction:** Exact RS may require expensive supremum evaluation; analytical bounds are cheaper but may be loose.
- **Side-information storage:** Full R enables tight RS but increases memory; compressed summaries may limit algorithm class.
- **Algorithm choice:** Passive is simple but may waste noise; active exploits R’s geometry but requires more computation and λ<sub>R</sub>.

### Failure signatures
- **RS over-estimation:** Noise far larger than needed (empirical change << noise scale).
- **RS under-estimation:** Membership inference attacks succeed between ¯A output and A(R).
- **Active update divergence:** For iterative methods, if λ<sub>R</sub> is over-estimated or step size mis-tuned, iterate fails to converge to w<sub>R</sub>.
- **Side-information mismatch:** If T(S) ≠ R (e.g., due to preprocessing), computed RS may not bound true sensitivity.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Passive median sanity check:** Generate synthetic R with fixed median and controlled local spacing. Compute empirical RS by adding single points that maximize median shift. Compare to GS = B/2. Verify ratio ≈ (max gap)/B.
2. **ERM curvature impact:** Use linear regression with controllable condition number. Vary λ and retain set size n. Compute empirical λ<sub>R</sub> and RS bound L/(nλ<sub>R</sub>) vs GS bound L/(nλ). Observe ratio scaling ~ λ/λ<sub>R</sub>.
3. **Active unlearning (Descent-to-Delete) iteration reduction:** Train logistic regression on small dataset. Compute required iterations using global λ vs. λ<sub>R</sub>. Compare counts and test utility of unlearned model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Is full side information T(R)=R necessary?** Whether full retain set storage is essential for RS-based certificates remains open.
- **Minimal side information:** What is the smallest amount of information needed to efficiently compute retain sensitivity?
- **Efficient RS computation:** How can we compute data-dependent statistics (spacing, eigengap, curvature) from R without full retraining or expensive post-processing?
- **Deep networks:** Can retain sensitivity be applied to modern large-scale non-convex models like deep neural networks?

## Limitations
- Requires storing full retain set *R*; compressed summaries may lose tightness.
- RS gains depend on favorable data properties; in worst-case *R*, RS may approach GS.
- Computing tight λ<sub>*R</sub> can be expensive and may offset noise reduction.
- Validation limited to classical ML tasks; deep networks not explored.
- Theory assumes deterministic, Lipschitz algorithms; stochastic pipelines not covered.

## Confidence
- **RS sufficiency for unlearning (Theorem 2.11):** High (rigorous proof).
- **RS << global sensitivity across problems (Section 3):** Medium (analytic bounds exist; empirical support limited but positive).
- **Active unlearning gains via λ<sub>*R</sub> (Section 4):** Medium (sound under strong convexity; practical computation of tight λ<sub>*R</sub> needs more validation).
- **Empirical results (Section 5):** Medium (shows large reductions; small sample size, sparse reproducibility details).

## Next Checks
1. **Distributional robustness of RS/GS ratio:** Test how the ratio varies across diverse data distributions (clustered, heavy-tailed, low-dimensional) to assess how often theoretical gains materialize.
2. **Approximate RS estimation:** Compare exact RS, analytical upper bounds, and finite-sample sampling to identify cheap, safe approximations that avoid under-estimation.
3. **Active unlearning for non-convex models:** Implement Descent-to-Delete for a small neural network and measure whether observed noise/iteration reduction aligns with predicted (λ/λ<sub>*R</sub>) scaling.
