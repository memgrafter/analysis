# WavePhaseNet: A DFT-Based Method for Constructing Semantic Conceptual Hierarchy Structures (SCHS)
arXiv ID: 2602.14419

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
WavePhaseNet addresses the fundamental cause of hallucination in large language models by framing embedding spaces as conditional expectations over σ-algebras. The authors prove that non‑isomorphism between this space and the semantic truth manifold leads to inevitable logical breakdown. To counteract this, they introduce a spectral decomposition method: applying a Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) along token sequences decomposes semantics into frequency bands, with low frequencies capturing global intent and high frequencies encoding local syntax. Under a 1/f power‑law (inspired by Zipf’s law), cumulative energy analysis shows that GPT‑4’s 24,576‑dimensional space can be compressed to approximately 3,000 dimensions with ≥95% energy retention. Complementing this, cohomological regularization over overlapping token windows quantifies inconsistencies as coboundary losses, and harmonic projection (Hodge theory) yields maximally consistent global representations. The paper proposes metrics—Consistency Score, Zipf Deviation—and outlines ablation studies to validate the approach, offering a theoretical and practical framework for hallucination suppression.

## Method Summary
WavePhaseNet operates in three stages. First, a **Spectral Module** computes the DFT of the token embedding matrix; based on a 1/f spectrum, it selects a low‑frequency band (≈3,000 components) and reconstructs a global intent vector via the inverse DFT. Second, the sequence is partitioned into overlapping windows; a graph Laplacian is built from window adjacencies. Local representations (sections) from each window are regularized with a cohomological loss that penalizes their coboundaries (differences across overlaps). A coupling term aligns these local sections with the global intent. Finally, at inference, harmonic projection onto the kernel of the Laplacian (minimizing a quadratic form) produces a globally consistent representation for generation. This combination of spectral decomposition and topological consistency aims to separate global meaning from syntactic noise while ensuring coherence across the sequence.

## Key Results
- Proven that LLM embedding spaces as conditional expectations over σ‑algebras are non‑isomorphic to truth sets, making hallucination inevitable under standard training.
- Derived a theoretical dimensionality bound: assuming a 1/f spectrum, a 24,576‑dimensional representation can be reduced to ~3,000 dimensions while preserving ≥95% of cumulative semantic energy.
- Introduced cohomological regularization and harmonic projection (Hodge theory) to enforce global semantic consistency across overlapping windows; proposed Consistency Score, Zipf Deviation, and ablation studies for empirical validation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hallucination as a measure‑theoretic non‑isomorphism
**Claim:** Hallucination is a structural inevitability in standard LLM embedding spaces because the space of conditional expectations over a σ‑algebra (the model’s internal representations) is not isomorphic to the semantic truth manifold.  
**Mechanism:** The autoregressive framework defines token embeddings as random variables over a σ‑algebra generated by past tokens. The model optimizes an expected loss (L^p norm) over the training distribution’s support, not pointwise truth. If the support S of the training distribution has non‑zero measure outside the truth set T (S⊈T), the model’s representations will converge (in the L^p sense) to points not in T, producing hallucinated outputs. This is framed as a Cauchy sequence in the embedding space whose limit lies outside T.  
**Core assumption:** The training distribution’s support contains non‑truth elements (µ(S\T)>0). The embedding space is a complete L^p space, so limits of sequences within S exist within that space but may fall outside T.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: “embedding space functions as a conditional expectation over a σ‑algebra” and “failure to be isomorphic to the semantic truth set is the fundamental cause.”  
- Section 2.1 formalizes the embedding as a random variable over σ‑algebra F_t.  
- Section 3.1 defines hallucination as S⊈T and Theorem 2 states its inevitability given µ(S\T)>0.  
- Neighbor papers discuss semantic hierarchies but do not provide direct empirical evidence; the claim is primarily theoretical.  
**Break condition:** Hallucination would be avoided if the model’s embedding space were isomorphic to T (S⊆T and the mapping preserved all structure), which the paper argues is impossible given the nature of learned distributions over language.

### Mechanism 2: Spectral decomposition via DFT and dimensionality reduction
**Claim:** Applying a Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) along token sequences decomposes semantics into a hierarchical structure: low‑frequency bands encode global intent/themes, while high‑frequency bands encode local syntax/expression, enabling precise manipulation.  
**Mechanism:** The token embedding matrix V ∈ ℝ^{d×T} is transformed: Ṽ = V F (F = DFT matrix). Under a 1/f spectral decay, energy is concentrated in low‑frequency components. Cumulative energy analysis shows that selecting k ≈ 3,000 low‑frequency components (out of T=24,576) preserves ≥95% of total energy. The inverse DFT on this band (V_reduced) yields a reduced embedding space that suppresses high‑frequency “noise” (local syntactic variation) while preserving global semantic structure.  
**Core assumption:** Natural language semantics exhibit a 1/f spectral property in the token embedding sequence domain, such that global meaning is low‑frequency and syntax is high‑frequency. The specific 3,000‑dimensional bound derives from solving the harmonic series inequality under this 1/f model.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract explicitly states DFT decomposes semantics into bands and derives the ~3,000‑dimensional bound from cumulative energy analysis on a 1/f spectrum.  
- Section 4.2 asserts the power spectral density P(f) ∝ 1/f, β≈1, linking low‑freq to global meaning.  
- Section 4.3 provides the derivation: S(k) = sum_{n=0}^k 1/(n+1) / sum_{n=0}^{T-1} 1/(n+1) ≥ 0.95 → k≈3,000 for T=24,576.  
- Neighbor papers on semantic hierarchies do not use DFT or validate a 1/f spectrum; no direct corpus support.  
**Break condition:** The mechanism fails if the actual spectrum of token embeddings deviates significantly from 1/f decay, making the low‑band selection either insufficient (loss of global intent) or overly reductive (loss of necessary local nuance).

### Mechanism 3: Cohomological regularization and harmonic projection
**Claim:** Cohomological regularization over overlapping token windows, followed by harmonic projection (Hodge theory) onto the kernel of the graph Laplacian, enforces global semantic consistency by treating inconsistencies between local inferences as coboundary losses.  
**Mechanism:** The sequence is covered by overlapping windows {U_i}, each producing a local representation (section) s_i. A graph G is defined with vertices = windows and edges = overlaps. The graph Laplacian L = D – A induces a cochain complex. Inconsistencies between adjacent local sections are measured by the coboundary operator δs_{ij} = s_j – s_i, yielding loss L_coh = λ s^⊤ (L⊗I_r) s. The Hodge decomposition s = s_harmonic + s_gradient + s_curl shows that s_harmonic (in ker(L)) represents the maximally consistent global representation. Projecting onto this subspace (minimizing s^⊤ L s) harmonizes the local sections.  
**Core assumption:** Semantic consistency across overlapping local contexts can be modeled as a cohomology problem on a covering, and the harmonic part of the Hodge decomposition corresponds to the globally consistent “true” representation. The graph constructed from windows adequately captures all relevant semantic dependencies.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract describes “cohomological regularization over overlapping local windows” and “harmonic projection based on Hodge theory” to extract “maximally consistent global representations.”  
- Section 6.3 defines the graph, Laplacian, and coboundary loss (Equation 25).  
- Section 6.4 states harmonic projection as argmin s^⊤ L s and links to “controlling semantic consistency.”  
- Neighbor papers like ConCM and DGNN use consistency‑driven methods but not cohomology or Hodge theory; no direct validation.  
**Break condition:** The approach fails if the graph Laplacian’s harmonic space is trivial (empty) or if the local sections are already perfectly consistent (δs=0), making regularization unnecessary or misleading. Also fails if semantic dependencies are non‑local and not captured by the overlapping window graph.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Spectral Module (DFT, band selection) → Window & Graph Builder (overlap, Laplacian) → Cohomology Regularizer (loss) → Harmonic Projection (inference)

**Critical path:**  
1. During the forward pass of a Transformer layer, obtain token embeddings X ∈ ℝ^{d×T}.  
2. For selected layers/heads, apply Spectral Module: compute X̃ = X F (FFT), select low‑frequency indices Ω (k≈3000), get global intent g via IDFT(X̃[:,Ω]).  
3. Split the layer’s post‑attention/FFN output into overlapping windows {U_i}, extract local sections s_i, and build graph Laplacian L from window overlaps.  
4. Compute cohomology loss L_coh = λ s^⊤ (L⊗I_r) s and coupling loss L_coupling = η Σ ||s_i – P_i(g)||².  
5. Add L_coh and L_coupling to the task loss; backpropagate to update model weights.  
6. *(Optional at inference)* After the final forward pass, collect all s_i, solve min_s s^⊤ L s (or perform a gradient step) to project onto the harmonic subspace, yielding the final harmonized representation for generation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- λ (cohomology weight): High λ forces strong local consistency, potentially over‑smoothing semantics; low λ may leave inconsistencies.  
- µ (spectral KL weight): Balforces the selected band’s energy distribution to match the theoretical 1/f profile; high µ rigidly enforces the spectral shape, which might not hold for all domains.  
- η (coupling weight): Controls adherence of local sections to global intent g; high η may suppress locally valid variations; low η may decouple global/local.  
- Window size & overlap: Larger windows capture more context but increase graph size and compute; overlap must be sufficient to connect all relevant semantics.  
- Band selection threshold: Fixed k=3000 is derived for T=24576; for other sequence lengths T, k must be recalculated via the harmonic series sum.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High Zipf Deviation: Measured spectrum of Ṽ deviates strongly from 1/f; indicates spectral decomposition assumption violated.  
- Coboundary loss L_coh plateau at a high value: Could be due to λ too low, ill‑conditioned L, or inadequate local section capacity.  
- Consistency Score below task‑specific threshold: Overlapping windows produce disagreeing s_i; may stem from insufficient η, poor windowing, or ineffective harmonic projection.  
- Energy preservation S(k) < 0.95 with k=3000: The cumulative energy bound fails for the given data/model; requires recalculating k.  
- No reduction in hallucination metrics after applying WavePhaseNet: Cohomology/harmonic steps may not be properly integrated or hallucination may not stem from the targeted mechanism.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Validate Dimensionality Bound: For a frozen GPT‑4 style model, compute DFT on token sequences from a diverse corpus. Calculate cumulative energy S(k). Confirm k≈3000 for T=24576; if not, analyze spectral fit to 1/f.  
2. Ablation of λ (Cohomology Weight): Train a small model with WavePhaseNet components. Compare λ=0 (no L_coh) vs. λ>0. Measure (a) Consistency Score on overlapping window pairs, (b) performance on a logical reasoning benchmark (e.g., ProofWriter), (c) hallucination rate on a factuality dataset. Expect λ>0 improves (a) and (b), reduces (c).  
3. Harmonic Projection Efficacy: For a trained model with cohomology regularization, at inference time, compare three outputs: (i) no projection, (ii) random projection, (iii) harmonic projection (min s^⊤ L s). Evaluate global consistency (Consistency Score) and downstream task accuracy. Expect (iii) ≻ (i) on consistency with minimal accuracy drop (or gain).

## Limitations
- The 1/f spectral assumption for token embeddings lacks empirical validation across models and domains.  
- The measure‑theoretic hallucination argument relies on an unquantified premise (training support includes non‑truth elements).  
- Windowing parameters (size, stride) and graph construction are underspecified, affecting cohomology regularization.
