# Ref-Adv: Exploring MLLM Visual Reasoning in Referring Expression Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.23898
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23898
- **Reference Count**: 21
- **Primary Result**: A new REC benchmark reveals a 30-40% performance drop for state-of-the-art MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B: 92.7% → 58.3% Acc@0.5), exposing overestimation of visual reasoning abilities.

## Executive Summary
Existing Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) benchmarks fail to evaluate genuine visual reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) because they contain short expressions with redundant descriptors and few distractors, enabling models to rely on superficial shortcuts. This paper introduces Ref-Adv, a challenging benchmark that suppresses shortcuts by requiring linguistically complex, minimally sufficient expressions paired with hard visual distractors. Through an LLM-assisted curation pipeline and human verification, Ref-Adv ensures that models must integrate full textual understanding with fine-grained visual discrimination. Evaluation of 13 contemporary MLLMs shows drastic performance declines compared to standard REC datasets, demonstrating a critical overestimation of MLLMs' reasoning capabilities and motivating the need for more rigorous evaluation.

## Method Summary
Ref-Adv is built from COCO and OpenImages with panoptic annotations. The curation pipeline first filters images to retain only those with at least three instances of the target category. For each target instance, a two-stage LLM prompting process identifies the most visually similar distractor (hard distractor) and elicits discriminative attributes both across the category (group-level) and within the target-distractor pair (instance-level). A second prompt then composes a natural referring expression from a minimal subset of these attributes, ensuring it is just sufficient to disambiguate the target. Generated expressions undergo human verification (3 annotators) for correctness and presence of hard distractors, yielding a final subset (`Ref-Adv-s`) of 1,142 samples. Evaluation uses standard REC metrics (Accuracy at IoU thresholds) with model-specific prompts and, for limited grounding models, Set-of-Marks (SoM) rendering.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art MLLMs experience severe performance drops on Ref-Adv vs. RefCOCO(+/g), e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B (92.7% → 58.3% Acc@0.5) and InternVL-3-14B (92.0% → 52.3%).
- Bag-of-words ablation (shuffling word order) causes a much larger accuracy drop on Ref-Adv than on RefCOCO, indicating Ref-Adv requires faithful syntactic/semantic processing.
- Descriptor deletion tests confirm Ref-Adv expressions contain minimal redundancy; removing even one descriptor significantly harms accuracy, proving each term is necessary.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Suppressing Shortcuts via Minimal Sufficiency
Ref-Adv reduces reliance on superficial descriptor matching by ensuring expressions contain only the information necessary to uniquely identify the target. The LLM-assisted pipeline first identifies discriminative attributes between the target and hard distractors (group-level) and within the target pair (instance-level). It then composes expressions from a minimal subset of these attributes, avoiding redundant descriptors that would allow a model to ground the target without processing the full expression.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [abstract] "redundant descriptors enable shortcut solutions that bypass genuine text understanding and visual reasoning"  
- [section 2.2] "we ensure the presence of 'hard distractors'... To reduce such shortcuts... and composing expressions around them"  
- [section 2.3.1] "To avoid this behavior, we adopt a two stage procedure: we first elicit discriminative attributes... and then compose the final expression from a minimal yet sufficient subset"  

### Mechanism 2: Increasing Visual Reasoning Demand via Hard Distractors
Ref-Adv forces fine-grained visual discrimination by populating images with same-category distractors that partially satisfy the referring expression. The dataset construction filters for images with at least three candidate instances of the target category. The LLM pipeline explicitly identifies the most similar pair (target + hard distractor) and generates expressions using attributes that differentiate this pair from other distractors and between the pair itself. This creates scenarios where a model must verify multiple competing hypotheses.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [abstract] "images often contain few distractors, making the target easy to find"  
- [section 2.2] "Distractor Pressure... we select images that have at least 3 candidate instances..."  
- [section 2.3.1] "Identify the most similar pair... and elicit group-level and instance-level discriminators"  

### Mechanism 3: Elevating Linguistic Complexity
Ref-Adv uses LLMs to generate longer, more diverse referring expressions (average 11.5 words vs. ~3 in RefCOCO), incorporating negation and varied phrasings. The two-stage generation (discriminator elicitation then composition) promotes natural, non-templated language while maintaining precision, increasing the necessity for sequential textual reasoning and compositional understanding.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [abstract] "many expressions are very short, leaving little reasoning demand"  
- [table 1] Avg. length: RefCOCO 3.6 vs. Ref-Adv 11.5  
- [section 2.2] "Language Complexity... we employ LLMs... to generate more natural and diverse referring expressions while maintaining linguistic complexity"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
COCO/OpenImages with panoptic annotations -> Filter images (≥3 same-category instances) -> Tag candidate instances -> LLM Query 1 (similarity judgment & discriminator elicitation) -> LLM Query 2 (expression composition) -> Human verification (accuracy & hard distractor check) -> Evaluation harness (model-specific prompts, SoM for limited grounding models, IoU computation).

**Critical path**:  
1. Filter images for distractor count.  
2. For each target instance, run LLM Query 1 to get group/discriminators.  
3. Run LLM Query 2 to generate candidate expressions.  
4. Human verify (accuracy & hard distractors).  
5. Evaluate models with standardized prompts and IoU computation.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- LLM vs. Human authorship: LLMs scale but introduce hallucination risks; human verification mitigates but reduces yield (keep rate 18.7%).  
- Naturalness vs. Control: Using image context in Query 2 improves naturalness but may introduce model-specific biases.  
- SoM vs. native grounding: SoM removes model-specific grounding limitations but changes the task (proposal-based vs. direct grounding).

**Failure signatures**:  
- Models select the hard distractor instead of the target.  
- Performance on Ref-Adv does not drop significantly from RefCOCO, indicating remaining shortcuts.  
- Bag-of-words ablation shows small degradation, meaning word order matters little (poor textual reasoning).  
- Descriptor deletion test shows high accuracy even when removing one descriptor (redundancy persists).

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Bag-of-words test**: Shuffle word order in expressions; large drop on Ref-Adv vs. small drop on RefCOCO confirms Ref-Adv requires syntactic/semantic fidelity.  
2. **Descriptor deletion sufficiency**: Randomly remove one descriptor; smaller drop on Ref-Adv indicates fewer redundant descriptors (good), but if drop is large, it shows each descriptor is critical (intended).  
3. **Cross-benchmark evaluation**: Run the same model on RefCOCO(+/g) and Ref-Adv; a significant performance gap (e.g., >30%) reveals overfitting to shortcuts in older benchmarks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How do MLLMs perform across different reasoning facets (e.g., negation, spatial relations, attribute combinations) in Ref-Adv, and which facets account for the largest performance gap relative to standard REC benchmarks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that Ref-Adv expressions are annotated with reasoning facets including negation (Abstract, Section 2), but evaluation results report only overall accuracy without any facet-level analysis.
- Why unresolved: Without pinpointing which reasoning types are most challenging, the community cannot target improvements effectively, and it remains unclear whether the observed drop is driven by specific capabilities or general difficulty.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic evaluation of models on Ref-Adv subsets defined by reasoning facets (e.g., expressions with vs. without negation, with vs. without relational descriptors) to measure accuracy differences and correlate with failure modes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific reasoning patterns appear in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) outputs on Ref-Adv, and how do these patterns correlate with correct vs. incorrect grounding?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates models with and without CoT (Section 3.1) and finds CoT generally improves performance on Ref-Adv (Tables 6–7), but provides only a few qualitative examples (Figure 5) and no systematic analysis of CoT content.
- Why unresolved: It is unknown whether CoT helps by guiding visual search, disambiguating descriptors, or performing stepwise attribute verification, limiting insights into how to better elicit reasoning.
- What evidence would resolve it: A large-scale qualitative study coding CoT traces for reasoning steps (e.g., object enumeration, attribute comparison, negation handling) and statistical analysis linking step patterns to success rates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the LLM-assisted data curation pipeline introduce subtle biases (e.g., in descriptor choice, phrasing naturalness) that affect the difficulty or diversity of Ref-Adv, and how do LLM-authored expressions compare to human-authored ones?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The pipeline yields a low keep rate of 18.7% for LLM-generated expressions (Section 2.3.3), yet the rejected expressions are not analyzed, and the paper does not compare model performance between LLM-authored and human-authored subsets.
- Why unresolved: High rejection suggests many LLM outputs were flawed; the accepted expressions might systematically exclude certain challenging patterns or overrepresent others, potentially skewing benchmark difficulty and conclusions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of rejected expressions to identify common failure modes (e.g., overspecification, unnatural phrasing); side-by-side comparison of model accuracy on matched LLM-human subsets; diversity metrics for descriptor types.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Which architectural or training interventions (e.g., CoT fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, model scale) most effectively improve MLLM performance on Ref-Adv, and do these improvements generalize to other vision-language tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Results indicate reasoning-oriented models (e.g., Qwen3 “thinking” variants) outperform their instruct counterparts (Table 6), but the paper does not isolate the contributing factors (e.g., training data, objective, model size).
- Why unresolved: Without identifying key drivers, future model development may rely on trial-and-error rather than principled design; it is also unclear whether gains on Ref-Adv transfer to other benchmarks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled ablation studies comparing model variants (e.g., same architecture trained with/without CoT data, different scales) on Ref-Adv and other REC benchmarks; evaluation of transfer learning from Ref-Adv training to tasks like VQA or NLVR.

## Limitations
- LLM pipeline biases; 18.7% human keep rate risks coverage gaps.
- Undocumented prompts/decoding/SoM settings → replication variance.
- Dataset confined to COCO/OpenImages; generalization unknown.
- SoM changes task to proposal-based, limiting cross-model comparability.

## Confidence
- Ref-Adv suppresses shortcuts & requires full reasoning: **High** (ablations, >30% drops).
- Existing REC benchmarks overestimate MLLMs: **High** (consistent across 13 models).
- Pipeline ensures minimal sufficiency & hard distractors: **Medium** (human verification positive but LLM biases possible).
- Linguistic complexity drives difficulty: **Medium** (correlation only; image complexity uncontrolled).
