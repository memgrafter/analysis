# LLMs Exhibit Significantly Lower Uncertainty in Creative Writing Than Professional Writers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16162  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16162  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Professional human writers produce text with 2–4× higher token surprisal and 2.76–8.82× higher perplexity than LLM continuations; alignment techniques (instruction tuning, RLHF) widen this uncertainty gap, especially in creative writing.

## Executive Summary
This study quantifies a substantial "uncertainty gap" between human-authored creative writing and LLM outputs. Across 28 models, human story continuations exhibit significantly higher token-level surprisal and perplexity, indicating greater unpredictability. The gap widens for aligned models (instruction-tuned, reasoning), and is particularly pronounced in creative versus expository domains. Correlations with an automatic quality metric suggest that elevated uncertainty (within coherent ranges) associates with higher literary quality, forming an inverted-U relationship. The authors conclude that current alignment strategies suppress the constructive ambiguity essential for literary creativity.

## Method Summary
The authors assembled two corpora of professional short stories (*The New Yorker*, *Tell-Me-A-Story*) and segmented each into sentences. For each story, the preceding sentences formed a context T1, and the next sentence was the target continuation T2. They evaluated 28 open-weight LLMs (base, instruction-tuned, reasoning variants) as both generators and evaluators: each model generated a continuation ˆT2 from T1 via sampling (temp=1.0, top_p=1.0) of length matching |T2|. To measure uncertainty, the same model computed token log-probabilities for both the human T2 and the model ˆT2 under the full context T1⊕T2. Metrics included mean token negative log-likelihood (NLL), exponential NLL (perplexity), pointwise mutual information (PMI), and context-adjusted PMI (CPMI). The human-model gap was quantified via ratios for NLL/perplexity (human/model) and differences for PMI/CPMI (human−model). Domain comparisons used a third corpus (Ghostbuster). Quality correlations employed a proprietary reward model on passages extracted from continuations.

## Key Results
- Human-authored continuations show 2–4× higher token surprisal (median NLL ratio 2.03–3.9) and 2.76–8.82× higher perplexity than model-sampled outputs.
- Instruction-tuned and reasoning models exhibit larger uncertainty gaps than base models; the disparity is 25–30% greater in creative writing versus essays/news (ΔPMI).
- Higher token-level uncertainty (NLL/perplexity) correlates weakly positively with automated quality scores (Spearman ρ≈0.07–0.11), while PMI correlates negatively (ρ≈−0.10). Quadratic regression reveals an inverted-U relationship with quality peaking at elevated uncertainty (z*≈1.98), indicating models operate left of this optimum.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Post-training alignment (RLHF, instruction tuning) suppresses output uncertainty by optimizing for factuality and typicality, eliminating the constructive ambiguity needed for literary creativity.
- **Mechanism**: Alignment uses KL-divergence regularization and reward models that favor confident outputs, steering models away from low-probability, high-entropy tokens. This causes mode collapse toward safe, modal predictions, removing the "spots of indeterminacy" prized in human creative writing.
- **Core assumption**: Literary creativity requires preserved distributional uncertainty; current alignment trades this off for reliability in factual tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] Alignment steers away from uncertain outputs; [section 1] Optimization forces mode collapse, penalizing diverse outputs; [corpus] Table 2 shows instruct/reasoning models have larger gaps than base models.
- **Break condition**: If base models also show a comparable gap, alignment may not be the primary driver; the gap could stem from pretraining data or architecture.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Human creative writing inherently occupies a higher-uncertainty regime because literary meaning relies on indeterminacy that invites reader co-creation, which aligned LLMs are trained to eliminate.
- **Mechanism**: Literary theory values semantic openness (multiple interpretations, unresolved tensions), which information-theoretically translates to higher token surprisal and lower contextual predictability (PMI). LLMs trained for instruction-following maximize contextual coherence, flattening this openness.
- **Core assumption**: The used uncertainty metrics (NLL, PMI, CPMI) are valid proxies for literary indeterminacy and ambiguity.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 2] Literary richness arises from simultaneity of conflicting meanings, ambiguity, indeterminacy; [section 2] Aesthetic openness linked to increased information content and entropy; [corpus] Table 3 shows ΔPMI gap amplified in creative writing vs. essays/news.
- **Break condition**: If high-uncertainty human text does not correlate with higher quality scores, the literary-theoretical link may not hold, or metrics may be invalid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Higher uncertainty (within coherent ranges) correlates with better automated writing quality, exhibiting an inverted-U relationship where optimal quality occurs at elevated uncertainty levels; aligned LLMs operate left of this peak.
- **Mechanism**: Increasing uncertainty from very low levels introduces novelty and reduces predictability, improving quality; beyond an optimal point, coherence degrades. Aligned LLMs are constrained to low-entropy regimes and rarely reach the sweet spot.
- **Core assumption**: The WRITINGQUALITYREWARDMODEL accurately reflects human literary judgment; the correlation is causal or at least indicative of a real creative property.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 5.2] Weak positive correlation between NLL/PPL and quality, negative for PMI; [section 5.2] Quadratic regression shows quality peaks at high uncertainty (z*≈1.98); [corpus] Tables 4 and 10 show significant correlations and inverted-U in 31–79% of model–dataset combos.
- **Break condition**: If the reward model is biased toward lower uncertainty (e.g., trained on predictable text), the correlation could be spurious; correlation does not imply causation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Information-theoretic uncertainty metrics (NLL, Perplexity, PMI, CPMI)**  
  - *Why needed here*: These metrics operationalize the core dependent variable—output unpredictability—by quantifying token-level surprise under a model’s probability distribution. Distinctions (token vs. sequence, context-conditioned vs. unconditional) are critical for interpreting the human-model gap.  
  - *Quick check question*: What does a more negative ΔPMI (human – model) indicate about the model’s reliance on narrative context?
- **Concept: Literary theory of indeterminacy/ambiguity/co-creativity**  
  - *Why needed here*: Provides the theoretical foundation that uncertainty is a *positive* feature of creative writing, framing the problem as a misalignment between alignment objectives (which suppress uncertainty) and creative goals.  
  - *Quick check question*: Why might eliminating “gaps” in a story reduce its aesthetic value according to reader-response theory (Iser, Barthes)?
- **Concept: Alignment techniques (RLHF, SFT, KL penalties) and their effect on distribution**  
  - *Why needed here*: Explains *why* LLMs have lower uncertainty: optimization pressures in alignment explicitly discourage uncertainty to improve factuality and coherence, linking system design to the observed gap.  
  - *Quick check question*: How does a KL-divergence penalty in RLHF contribute to mode collapse and reduced output diversity?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Datasets (The New Yorker, Tell-Me-A-Story) -> Models (28 LLMs: OLMo, Llama, Qwen, etc.) -> Evaluation Harness (same-model generation and scoring)  
- **Critical path**:  
  1. For each story, split into sentences; set T1 = cumulative previous sentences, T2 = next sentence.  
  2. Generate model continuation ˆT2 from T1 via sampling (temp=1.0, top_p=1.0) with exactly |T2| tokens.  
  3. Compute token log-probabilities for T2 (human) under T1⊕T2 using the same model.  
  4. Compute same metrics for ˆT2 (model).  
  5. Aggregate per-sequence metrics (mean NLL, exp(NLL), PMI, CPMI).  
  6. Calculate human-model ratios (NLL/PPL) or differences (PMI/CPMI); aggregate across models.  
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Same-model gen/eval avoids distributional mismatch but inherits the model’s biases (e.g., underestimating human uncertainty due to context limits).  
  - Cumulative context prevents fragmentation but limits sequence length.  
  - Imputation for low-prob tokens (-2.0 nats floor) introduces lower-bound bias, though mitigated.  
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Base models show no gap → alignment not primary cause.  
  - Gap reverses on certain datasets → contamination or non-representative human text.  
  - Inconsistent gap direction across PMI vs. NLL → indicates local vs. global uncertainty tradeoff not captured.  
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Replicate core gap on a held-out professional fiction dataset (e.g., *The Best American Short Stories*).  
  2. Isolate alignment effect: Compare base vs. instruct checkpoints of same model family with controlled decoding.  
  3. Test metric sensitivity: Recompute with alternative tokenizers, context window sizes, and full softmax.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can alignment algorithms preserve constructive ambiguity (literary indeterminacy) while suppressing destructive hallucinations?**  
   *Basis*: Explicitly stated in conclusion/abstract. Current alignment optimizes for low perplexity and factuality, which penalizes all uncertainty without distinguishing creative from harmful. Unresolved because no existing method makes this distinction. Resolution would require an alignment objective that rewards entropy on creative tokens while penalizing factual errors.  
2. **Does the uncertainty-quality correlation hold with human judges?**  
   *Basis*: Inferred; paper notes human evaluation would strengthen claims. Unresolved because the automatic reward model may be biased. Resolution via human study rating creativity/literary merit and correlating with uncertainty metrics.  
3. **Can training interventions systematically reduce the uncertainty gap and improve creative quality?**  
   *Basis*: Inferred; gap is correlational, may be inherent to data/architecture. Unresolved because causal manipulation not tested. Resolution: fine-tune or RLHF with modified objectives to encourage higher uncertainty, then measure gap and quality.  
4. **Does the gap generalize to other languages and creative genres (poetry, drama, songwriting)?**  
   *Basis*: Inferred; study only English short stories. Unresolved due to domain/language constraints. Resolution: replicate on professional corpora in other languages/genres; compare gaps.

## Limitations
- **Quality metric validity**: The proprietary WRITINGQUALITYREWARDMODEL may be biased toward predictable, edited text, undermining the uncertainty-quality correlation.
- **Metric implementation ambiguities**: Underspecified imputation (min logprob −2.0 nats), CPMI normalization, and sentence segmentation tools introduce potential systematic bias.
- **Generalizability**: The New Yorker dataset is private; substitutions may not match literary quality. Base models’ uncertainty relative to humans is unknown, leaving the alignment effect uncertain.

2. For a single model family (e.g., Llama 3), compare base, SFT, and RLHF checkpoints on creative prompts with controlled decoding to isolate alignment's effect on the gap.
3. Stress-test metric robustness by recomputing gaps with (a) a different tokenizer (e.g., SentencePiece), (b) varying context window sizes (2048 vs. 4096 tokens), and (c) full softmax instead of top-20 imputation.
