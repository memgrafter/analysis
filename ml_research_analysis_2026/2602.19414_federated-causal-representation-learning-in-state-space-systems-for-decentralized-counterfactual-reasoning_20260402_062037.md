# Federated Causal Representation Learning in State-Space Systems for Decentralized Counterfactual Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19414
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19414
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The proposed federated framework enables accurate cross-client counterfactual inference by learning disentangled latent state representations, converging to a centralized oracle, and providing differential privacy guarantees.

## Executive Summary
Industrial networks often consist of clients with private, high-dimensional data, making centralization infeasible. This paper introduces a federated approach to causal representation learning for counterfactual reasoning across such decentralized clients. Each client learns a low-dimensional latent state that separates its endogenous dynamics from exogenous input effects. A central server then estimates inter-client state-transition and input matrices using only these latent states and inputs, without accessing raw data. The method provably converges to the solution of a centralized oracle and comes with formal differential privacy guarantees. Experiments on synthetic and real industrial control datasets confirm that the augmented client and server models significantly outperform local models in prediction accuracy, while maintaining scalability and strong disentanglement as measured by low constraint violation norms.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in a federated manner between multiple clients and a central server. Each client possesses a proprietary state estimator (e.g., a Kalman filter) that uses locally known diagonal blocks (A_mm, B_mm, C_mm) to produce predicted and refined state estimates. The client augments this estimator with learnable parameters θ_m (state correction) and ϕ_m (input aggregation), forming an augmented state h_t^a and refined estimate ĥ_t^a. These augmented quantities, together with the proprietary predictions and inputs, are communicated to the server. The server, aware of the diagonal blocks, estimates the off-diagonal blocks {A_mn, B_mn} (n≠m) by minimizing a loss that includes a penalty term designed to disentangle the effects of A (endogenous) from B (exogenous). Specifically, the server ensures that the client’s θ-encoded correction aligns with the server’s A-encoded prediction via a large penalty ξ. The client updates its θ and ϕ using a combination of its local reconstruction gradient and the gradient ∇_{h_t^a} L_s received from the server. This iterative exchange yields block-coordinate descent on a global objective that matches the centralized reconstruction error, with theoretical convergence guarantees under standard assumptions (persistent excitation, full column rank, contractive proprietary models). Differential privacy is achieved by clipping and adding Gaussian noise to client messages and server gradients.

## Key Results
- The federated method achieves accurate cross-client counterfactual inference, with augmented client and server models significantly reducing prediction error compared to proprietary local models on both synthetic and real-world industrial control datasets (HAI and SWaT).
- The framework maintains strong disentanglement between endogenous (A) and exogenous (B) effects, as evidenced by low values of the disentanglement penalty and client-side δ_d norms.
- Theoretical analysis proves convergence to the centralized oracle solution and provides (ε, δ)-differential privacy guarantees, while empirical scalability tests show the method scales to moderate numbers of clients and measurement dimensions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method relies on three tightly coupled mechanisms that together enable decentralized counterfactual reasoning without raw data sharing.

**Mechanism 1: Disentangled latent representations at each client**  
Each client learns parameters θ_m and ϕ_m to adjust its proprietary Kalman filter output. The augmented predicted state is h_t^{m,a} = A_mm ĥ_{t-1}^{m,a} + B_mm u_{t-1}^m + ϕ_m, and the refined estimate is ĥ_t^{m,a} = ĥ_t^{m,c} + θ_m y_t^m. The server provides gradients ∇_{h_t^{m,a}} L_s that depend on current estimates of cross-client matrices. Through the chain rule (Eqs. 7–10), the client updates θ_m to capture effects of other clients’ states (endogenous) and ϕ_m to capture effects of other clients’ inputs (exogenous). This disentanglement is formalized by Theorem 6.1, which shows that with a large penalty coefficient ξ, the learned parameters satisfy E[A_mm θ_m y_t] = Σ_{n≠m} E[A_mn ĥ_{t-1}^{n,c}] and ϕ_m = E[Σ_{n≠m} B_mn u_t^n], thus isolating the two sources. Evidence: the abstract states that clients map high-dimensional observations into low-dimensional latent states that disentangle intrinsic dynamics from control-driven influences; Section 5.2 details the augmented model equations; Section 6 provides the theorem and corollary. Potential break condition: if the proprietary model is misspecified or server gradients become uninformative (e.g., due to poor initial off-diagonal estimates), the client may fail to isolate B_mn effects.

**Mechanism 2: Server-side disentanglement through a consensus penalty**  
The server maintains estimates of off-diagonal blocks {Â_mn, B̂_mn}. For each client m, it predicts a server-side state h_t^{m,s} using these matrices and the received proprietary states and inputs from other clients. The server loss L_s (Eq. 13) includes a penalty D = ξ ||A_mm(ĥ_{t-1}^{m,a} - ĥ_{t-1}^{m,c}) - Σ_{n≠m} Â_mn ĥ_{t-1}^{n,c}||². Minimizing L_s forces the client’s θ-encoded correction to match the server’s A-based prediction, thereby disentangling the cross-client effects of A and B. Claim 5.5 states that with large ξ, D achieves this separation. Theorem 6.1 derives the stationarity condition that leads to disentanglement. Evidence: Section 5.3 defines L_s and the penalty; Section 6 presents the theorem. Adjustment: If ξ is too large, training may become unstable; if too small, disentanglement fails. Practical mitigation involves using an Augmented Lagrangian warm-start.

**Mechanism 3: Convergence to centralized oracle**  
The combined client-server updates implement block-coordinate descent on a global loss equivalent to the reconstruction error of a centralized Kalman filter that has direct access to all raw data. Under Assumption 8.1 (inputs are zero-mean, stationary, ergodic, and persistently excited) and the condition that C_mm A_mm has full column rank, the normal equations derived from the stationarity conditions (Lemma 8.2) coincide with those of the centralized model. Consequently, the expected reconstruction matches the oracle up to a bounded bias term J_m (Theorem 8.3). This guarantees that the federated solution converges to the centralized optimum as the number of iterations grows. Evidence: Section 8 contains the theorem and lemma; Section 7 discusses differential privacy, which does not affect convergence under the noise model. The neighbor work on federated Granger causality (arXiv:2501.13890) proves convergence for the special case B=0, while this work extends to general B and A. Break condition: Lack of persistent excitation or rank deficiency in C_mm A_mm can prevent convergence to the oracle.

## Foundational Learning
- **Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) State-Space Models**: Why needed: The entire framework models each client’s dynamics as an LTI system (Eq. 1). The state matrices A, B, C encode the causal structure; counterfactual queries are answered via the transfer function C(zI−A)^{-1}B. Disentanglement of A and B is essential for isolating cross-client influences. Quick check: "What is the average treatment effect (ATE) on output y_t when intervening on input u_{t-1} in a single-client LTI system?" Answer: ATE = C B (u₁ − u₀).  
- **Disentangled Causal Representations**: Why needed: In a multi-client setting, Granger causality and standard state-space models conflate endogenous propagation (A) and exogenous input effects (B). To estimate the effect of one client’s input on another (Eq. 3), we must separate these two sources. The proposed augmented client model and server penalty achieve this. Quick check: "Why can’t we estimate B_mn directly from cross-correlations between y_m and u_n?" Answer: Because y_m depends on both A_mn (through latent states) and B_mn; disentanglement separates these contributions.  
- **Federated Learning with Differential Privacy**: Why needed: Clients cannot share raw high-dimensional data due to privacy and bandwidth constraints. The protocol uses compact latent-state messages and adds calibrated Gaussian noise to meet (ε,δ)-DP guarantees while still allowing convergence. Quick check: "How is differential privacy achieved in this framework?" Answer: By clipping client messages and server gradients to bound ℓ₂-sensitivity and adding Gaussian noise scaled to the sensitivity; composition over rounds yields overall DP.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
The system comprises clients and a central server. Each client runs a proprietary Kalman filter (using known A_mm, B_mm, C_mm) that outputs predicted state h_t^{m,c} and refined estimate ĥ_t^{m,c}. The client augments this with learnable parameters θ_m and ϕ_m to produce h_t^{m,a} = A_mm ĥ_{t-1}^{m,a} + B_mm u_{t-1}^m + ϕ_m and ĥ_t^{m,a} = ĥ_t^{m,c} + θ_m y_t^m. The client sends {ĥ_t^{m,c}, ĥ_t^{m,a}, h_t^{m,a}, u_t^m} to the server and receives ∇_{h_t^{m,a}} L_s. The server knows the diagonal blocks A_mm, B_mm and maintains estimates of off-diagonal blocks {Â_mn, B̂_mn} (n≠m). For each time step, it computes server-predicted states h_t^{m,s} = A_mm ĥ_{t-1}^{m,a} + Σ_{n≠m} Â_mn ĥ_{t-1}^{n,c} + B_mm u_{t-1}^m + Σ_{n≠m} B̂_mn u_{t-1}^n, then forms the server loss L_s containing the disentanglement penalty D. It updates the off-diagonal blocks via gradient descent and sends ∇_{h_t^{m,a}} L_s back to clients.

**Critical path**  
1. Initialize server estimates {Â_mn^0, B̂_mn^0} and client parameters {θ_m^0, ϕ_m^0}.  
2. For each communication round:  
   - Client forward pass: For t=1..T, compute h_t^{m,a}, ĥ_t^{m,a}, and package messages; send to server.  
   - Server aggregate: Using all messages, compute h_t^{m,s}, residual r_t^{m,s}=h_t^{m,a}−h_t^{m,s}, and L_s (including D). Compute gradients ∇_{Â_mn,B̂_mn} L_s and ∇_{h_t^{m,a}} L_s.  
   - Server update: Update {Â_mn, B̂_mn} with learning rates α_A, α_B.  
   - Server broadcast: Send ∇_{h_t^{m,a}} L_s to each client.  
   - Client update: Compute ∇_θ L_s and ∇_ϕ L_s via chain rule (Eqs. 7–8) and combine with local loss gradients (Eqs. 9–10) to update θ_m, ϕ_m.  
3. Repeat until convergence or maximum rounds. After training, counterfactual queries are answered using Eq. 20 (client’s aggregated effect) or Eq. 19 (server’s specific effect).

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Disentanglement penalty ξ**: Large ξ improves disentanglement but may cause optimization instability; too small ξ yields faster convergence but entangled representations. Practical workaround: start with a moderate ξ and increase gradually, or use an Augmented Lagrangian approach.  
- **Privacy budget (ε, δ)**: Tighter privacy (smaller ε) demands more noise, degrading model accuracy. Tradeoff: reduce communication rounds or adjust clipping thresholds; however, the paper does not explore privacy amplification via subsampling.  
- **Message dimensionality**: Messages contain latent states of dimension P_m. With large P_m, communication overhead grows; possible mitigations include quantization or sparsification, not explored.  
- **Proprietary model fidelity**: The framework assumes the given Kalman filter is reasonably accurate; if it is biased, the augmented model must compensate, potentially increasing sample complexity.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Divergence**: Server loss L_s or client loss L_{m,a} increases sharply after some iterations, often due to too large ξ or excessive learning rates.  
- **Poor disentanglement**: The disentanglement penalty D and client-side δ_d^m = ||ϕ_m − avg(Σ_{n≠m} B̂_mn u_t^n)||² plateau at high values (≫0.01), indicating that A and B effects remain entangled.  
- **No improvement over baseline**: The augmented client or server model fails to achieve lower prediction error than the proprietary client model on held‑out (especially shifted) data.  
- **Counterfactual inaccuracy**: Estimated ATE (e.g., C_mm B̂_mn (u₁−u₀) or C_mm ϕ_m) deviates significantly from ground‑truth values on synthetic tests.  
- **Noise‑induced collapse**: Under differential privacy, if added noise variance is too high, reconstruction error skyrockets; monitor ∥y−ŷ∥².

**First experiments**  
1. **Synthetic two‑client LTI system** (Section 9.1): Fixed P_m=2, U_m=2, D_m=2; systems stable with one‑way dependency. Trained for T=200 steps over 200 rounds. Tracked L_s, client losses, disentanglement penalty D, and δ_d. Evaluated on non‑stationary test data; plotted prediction errors (Fig. 4) showing augmented/server models outperforming proprietary model and closely matching centralized oracle. Transfer‑function analysis (Fig. 6) confirmed learned dynamics.  
2. **Real‑world industrial datasets** (HAI and SWaT): Preprocessed each dataset (normalization, subspace identification, block‑diagonal C). Ran federated training; recorded L_s, D, L_{m,a}, and δ_d for each client (Figs. 7, 8). Results showed steady convergence and consistently low disentanglement norms.  
3. **Scalability study**: Varied measurement dimension D_m (16, 32, 64, 128) with P_m=2, M=2; reported final L_s and D (Table 1). Varied number of clients M (2, 4, 8, 16) with P_m=2, D_m=8; reported L_s and D (Table 2). Performance degraded gradually with larger D_m or more clients, but disentanglement remained strong, illustrating scalability limits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Nonlinear extension**: How can the federated framework be extended to nonlinear state‑space systems? The current LTI assumption limits applicability to many real‑world scenarios.  
- **Learning local matrices**: Can clients learn their own A_mm, B_mm, C_mm without prior knowledge? The paper assumes these are known, which may not hold in practice.  
- **Per‑client effect isolation**: Is it possible to estimate individual client‑specific causal effects (B_mn) locally instead of merely aggregated effects? The current protocol only yields aggregated influences at each client.  
- **Bias elimination**: Under what conditions does the bias term J_m in Theorem 8.3 become zero? Resolving this could improve counterfactual accuracy.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (contractive proprietary models, persistently excited inputs, full column rank of C_mm A_mm) may be violated in practice, potentially breaking convergence or disentanglement guarantees.  
- The disentanglement penalty ξ requires careful tuning; large values can cause instability, while small values yield entangled representations.  
- Missing precise hyperparameter values (learning rates, ξ, noise scales, clipping thresholds) hinder exact replication and sensitivity analysis.  
- Real‑world counterfactual evaluation lacks ground‑truth causal effects; privacy guarantees are theoretical and not empirically audited.

## Confidence
