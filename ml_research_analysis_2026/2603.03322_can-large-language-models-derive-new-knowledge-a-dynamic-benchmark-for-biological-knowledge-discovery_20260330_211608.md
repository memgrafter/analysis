# Can Large Language Models Derive New Knowledge? A Dynamic Benchmark for Biological Knowledge Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03322
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03322
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Dynamic benchmark shows current SOTA models struggle with discovering new biological knowledge despite strong retention of existing knowledge

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DBench-Bio, the first dynamic benchmark for evaluating AI's ability to discover new biological knowledge. The benchmark addresses critical limitations of static benchmarks through a monthly updated pipeline that ensures temporal separation between model training and evaluation data. Evaluations of state-of-the-art models reveal significant limitations in knowledge discovery capabilities, with overall performance remaining low across 12 biomedical subdomains. The work establishes a living framework for the AI research community to track progress in biological knowledge discovery as models evolve.

## Method Summary
DBench-Bio employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) Data Acquisition from Q1-tier biology journals published after model release dates to prevent contamination, (2) QA Extraction using LLMs to synthesize scientific hypothesis questions and discovery answers from abstracts, and (3) QA Filter employing LLM-based quality control on relevance, clarity, and centrality. The benchmark is updated monthly and covers 12 biomedical subdomains. Evaluation uses LLM judges scoring answers on a 1-5 scale, with quality thresholds ensuring high benchmark reliability. Agent architectures (ReAct and workflow) are tested to assess whether structured reasoning frameworks can amplify knowledge discovery capabilities.

## Key Results
- SOTA models show low overall performance in discovering new biological knowledge despite strong basic knowledge retention
- Agent architectures improve performance through structured reasoning workflows, while tool use provides minimal benefit
- Mathematical biology proves most challenging, with consistently lower discovery rates across all models
- The dynamic nature prevents data contamination but requires continuous infrastructure for monthly updates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dynamic benchmark construction prevents data contamination by ensuring evaluation knowledge post-dates model training cutoff
- Mechanism: Abstracts from journals published after model release are collected, then LLMs synthesize QA pairs from these abstracts, guaranteeing temporal separation between training and evaluation data
- Core assumption: Journal publication dates accurately reflect knowledge emergence and LLMs cannot access future information during training
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "To guarantee strict temporal separation and prevent data contamination, we crawl abstracts of target journals published only after the model's release date"
  - [section]: "enforcing temporal separation in practice is challenging because modern LLMs are continuously retrained on new web data"
  - [corpus]: Found 25 related papers, average neighbor FMR=0.444, suggesting moderate overlap with existing literature
- Break condition: If models are trained on web data containing future publications or if journal metadata is inaccurate

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-dimensional quality filtering ensures benchmark reliability through relevance, clarity, and centrality assessments
- Mechanism: LLM judges score each QA pair across three dimensions (1-5 scale), filtering out pairs that don't meet strict thresholds (Relevance ≥ 4, Clarity ≥ 5, Centrality ≥ 5)
- Core assumption: LLM judges can reliably distinguish high-quality from low-quality QA pairs without human intervention
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we employ an LLM-based filter to eliminate low-quality pairs based on three quality indicators: relevance, clarity, and centrality"
  - [section]: "Alt-test results show winning rates all surpass 0.5, passing statistical test"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found for LLM judge reliability across domains
- Break condition: If LLM judges exhibit systematic biases or if quality thresholds are too lenient/strict

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Agent architectures amplify base model capabilities for knowledge discovery through structured reasoning workflows
- Mechanism: ReAct and workflow agents integrate reasoning with tool use, creating iterative loops that outperform static base models
- Core assumption: Structured reasoning frameworks can compensate for knowledge gaps through better information synthesis
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Both ReAct and Workflow architectures result in performance boosts"
  - [section]: "Integrating reasoning with external tools, via either iterative planning or pre-set workflows, effectively facilitates the discovery of new knowledge"
  - [corpus]: No corpus evidence found for specific agent architecture effectiveness
- Break condition: If backbone model quality is insufficient or if tool access doesn't provide novel information

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Temporal isolation in benchmark construction
  - Why needed here: Prevents data contamination where models have already seen evaluation knowledge during training
  - Quick check question: Why can't we use papers from before the model release date for evaluation?

- Concept: Quality filtering dimensions (relevance, clarity, centrality)
  - Why needed here: Ensures benchmark questions focus on core scientific discoveries rather than peripheral details
  - Quick check question: What would happen if we only filtered for clarity but not relevance?

- Concept: LLM-as-judge evaluation framework
  - Why needed here: Provides scalable, consistent evaluation of model-generated answers against ground truth
  - Quick check question: How does the scoring scale (1-5) map to answer quality levels?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data Acquisition -> QA Extraction -> QA Filter -> Evaluation
- Critical path: Data Acquisition → QA Extraction → QA Filter → Evaluation
  - Each stage must complete successfully for benchmark construction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Abstract vs full-text: Abstracts provide dense summaries but may lack experimental details
  - Q1 journals only: Ensures quality but limits coverage scope
  - LLM filtering: Scalable but may miss nuanced quality issues
  - Static vs dynamic: Dynamic ensures freshness but requires continuous infrastructure

- Failure signatures:
  - Low QA yield: QA extraction step produces few valid pairs
  - High rejection rate: QA filter removes most generated content
  - Inconsistent scoring: LLM judges show high variance in evaluations
  - Domain bias: Certain sub-domains consistently underperform

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test QA extraction pipeline on a small sample of abstracts to verify prompt effectiveness
  2. Run quality filter on extracted pairs to establish baseline rejection rates
  3. Evaluate a single model on a small benchmark subset to validate overall pipeline functionality

## Limitations
- Abstract-only information may miss crucial experimental details and methodological nuances present in full papers
- LLM judge reliability across diverse biomedical subdomains remains uncertain without human expert validation
- Domain coverage constraints limit benchmark to high-profile research, potentially missing important discoveries in specialized subfields

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Temporal separation mechanism for preventing data contamination. The methodology is clearly specified and the publication date cutoff approach is straightforward to implement and verify.

**Medium confidence**: Multi-dimensional quality filtering effectiveness. While the three-dimensional assessment (relevance, clarity, centrality) is well-defined, the reliability of LLM judges across diverse biomedical subdomains remains uncertain.

**Low confidence**: Agent architecture performance claims. The paper reports performance boosts but provides limited ablation studies or comparative analysis to isolate the specific contributions of reasoning workflows versus base model improvements.

## Next Checks

1. **Human validation study**: Conduct a small-scale expert review comparing LLM judge assessments against human expert evaluations across 100 randomly selected QA pairs to establish reliability baselines and identify systematic biases.

2. **Full-text vs abstract comparison**: Extract and evaluate QA pairs from both abstracts and full-text versions of the same papers to quantify information loss and determine whether the abstract-only approach significantly impacts benchmark quality.

3. **Cross-domain consistency test**: Evaluate the same models across all 12 biomedical subdomains and analyze variance in performance to identify whether certain domains consistently receive lower quality assessments or present unique challenges for the benchmark pipeline.
