# Interpretable-by-Design Transformers via Architectural Stream Independence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07482
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07482
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: Architectural stream independence (LFA) preserves position-dependent signals in deep layers and enables surgical interventions with minimal collateral damage, improving interpretability at moderate performance cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether architectural constraints can enforce interpretability in transformers by design. It introduces the Late Fusion Architecture (LFA), which maintains separate token (symbolic) and contextual (semantic) streams that remain independent until the final layer. Experiments on TinyStories show that LFA preserves distinct position-dependent channels in deep layers (PDS_max=0.276) compared to standard transformers (0.058). Intervention studies reveal that suppressing positional heads in LFA causes little semantic disruption (Cohen's d=-0.158), whereas baselines suffer catastrophic damage (d=-0.672). LFA also achieves higher stability (42%) for position-invariant semantic understanding than Std-T (19%) and CFM (11%). These results demonstrate that architectural separation can create transparent, modular reasoning pathways, though with a performance trade-off.

## Method Summary
The study trains six-layer transformer models on the TinyStories dataset (2M samples) to compare four architectures: a standard transformer (Std-T), a variant with a frozen token stream (D-Cas), the proposed Late Fusion Architecture (LFA) with frozen token stream and independent attention but dense FFN, and a fully constrained version (CFM) with independent FFN. LFA separates the token stream (X_T, frozen) from the embedding stream (X_E, mutable). Attention queries and keys are computed from a combined normalized representation, but values come solely from X_T and outputs update only X_E. The FFN similarly observes combined streams but writes only to X_E, with fusion occurring only at the LM head. Evaluation uses the Token-Position Dependence Score (PDS) to measure position signal retention, coreference resolution metrics (mean attention, Top1 accuracy, stability) on a custom set of 29 prompts, and intervention experiments where top positional heads are suppressed to measure collateral damage via Semantic Preference Score (SPS) and Cohen's d.

## Key Results
- LFA maintains high position-dependent channel separation in deep layers (PDS_max = 0.276) versus Std-T (0.058).
- Suppressing recency heads in LFA leads to minimal semantic degradation (Cohen's d = -0.158), while baselines show catastrophic entanglement (d = -0.672).
- LFA achieves 42% mean stability on position-invariant semantic understanding, substantially higher than Std-T (19%) and CFM (11%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Architectural stream independence preserves functional modularity by preventing premature entanglement of positional and semantic signals.
- **Mechanism:** Maintaining separate X_T (frozen token stream) and X_E (mutable embedding stream) with asymmetric information flow (X_T → X_E only) allows position to remain a clean, independently observable signal throughout all layers, with fusion deferred to the output layer.
- **Core assumption:** Positional information is best processed as a stable, symbolic routing mechanism that should not be corrupted by gradient updates intended for semantic refinement.
- **Evidence anchors:** LFA maintains distinct position-dependent channels in deep layers (PDS_max=0.276) versus standard transformers (PDS_max=0.058); stream separation creates transparent reasoning pathways; corpus evidence weak for architectural separation vs. additive.
- **Break condition:** If frozen stream constraint prevents learning necessary positional patterns (as in CFM, stability 0%), the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Late fusion enables surgical interventions on positional mechanisms without catastrophic semantic damage.
- **Mechanism:** Because X_T remains ungraded and separate, attention heads that track position can be suppressed without disrupting semantic representations evolved in X_E, implying functional independence.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic understanding can develop robustly in X_E using positional cues from X_T without positional signals encoded within semantics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Suppressing LFA's recency heads causes minimal damage (Cohen's d = -0.158) vs baselines (d = -0.672); LFA shows near-linear gate-response curves.
- **Break condition:** If interventions on high-PDS heads still cause significant semantic drop (|d| > 0.3), functional independence is violated.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Channel factorization combined with frozen token stream induces concentrated, interpretable head specialization.
- **Mechanism:** Independent attention heads (via Kronecker-structured mixing) operate on isolated channels of X_T, preventing cross-head interference and encouraging clear, analyzable functions.
- **Core assumption:** Transformers can learn effective models with per-head channel constraints, making head roles more discoverable.
- **Evidence anchors:** LFA shows concentrated coreference specialization (top head L4.H3: 48.3%) while Std-T's best heads are distributed; LFA's top 5 coreference heads all exceed 37% Top1 accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If channel constraint severely degrades validation loss (as in CFM, +10.2% vs LFA), specialization may not emerge.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** The architecture has two streams: X_T (token stream, frozen) and X_E (embedding stream, mutable). For each layer: X_T remains unchanged. Attention: Q and K are computed from CLN(X_T + X_E) from previous layer; V is computed from X_T only; attention output updates X_E. FFN: input is CLN(X_T + X_E); output updates X_E. At final layer, logits from LM Head(LayerNorm(X_T + X_E)).

- **Critical path:** 
  1. Initialize X_T^0 = TokenEmbedding(input), X_E^0 = 0.
  2. For each layer ℓ: Set X_T^ℓ = X_T^{ℓ-1} (frozen). Compute attention update: Q, K = projections from CLN(X_T^{ℓ-1} + X_E^{ℓ-1}); V = projection from X_T^{ℓ-1}; apply attention and add to X_E. Compute FFN update from CLN(X_T^{ℓ} + X_E^{ℓ-1}) and add to X_E.
  3. After final layer L, compute logits = LM Head(LayerNorm(X_T^L + X_E^L)).

- **Design tradeoffs:** Performance cost: D-Cas +1.6% val loss; LFA +5% total; CFM +16%—constraints incur a learnability tax. Interpretability gain: stream separation enables high PDS in deep layers and low-collateral-damage interventions. Sweet spot: LFA's dense FFN (observes both streams but writes only to X_E) balances semantic coordination with stream independence. Independent FFN (CFM) over-constrains.

- **Failure signatures:** 
  - PDS collapse in deep layers (< 0.05 at L4-L5): premature integration.
  - Low stability (< 20%) on competing-noun minimal pairs: model fails to learn position-invariant semantics.
  - High collateral damage (|Cohen's d| > 0.5) upon suppressing top-PDS heads: positional and semantic mechanisms entangled.
  - Validation loss spike >10% relative to Std-T: likely over-constrained (CFM regime).

- **First 3 experiments:** 
  1. **Token-Position Dependence Score (PDS) sweep:** Compute PDS for all heads across layers. Expect LFA to show a bimodal distribution with high-PDS heads concentrated in L4-L5; Std-T unimodal near-zero.
  2. **Coreference head localization:** Identify heads with high Top1 accuracy on coreference dataset. LFA should show 1-2 dominant heads in mid-layers; Std-T's best heads dispersed.
  3. **Intervention sensitivity test:** For top 3 PDS heads, apply soft suppression (gates 1.0→0.0) and measure SPS on competing-noun prompts. LFA should show near-linear SPS decay; Std-T/CFM sharp nonlinear collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the transparency benefit (measured by PDS and intervention metrics) hold for models scaled to billion-parameter sizes on large, diverse datasets?
2. Can architectural stream independence (LFA-style) be combined with additive disentanglement (like DeBERTa) to achieve both high performance and functional transparency?
3. Can the proposed transparency metrics (PDS, stability, intervention Cohen's d) be used as objectives in automated architecture search to design interpretable models?
4. Does architectural stream independence provide benefits for more complex reasoning tasks (e.g., multi-hop inference, abstract planning) beyond coreference resolution and recency bias?

## Limitations
- **Implementation ambiguity:** Critical details (FFN inner dimension, normalization scheme, coreference prompt filtering) are underspecified, hindering exact replication of metrics.
- **Task and scale boundary:** Evidence limited to small models (13M-22M) on TinyStories and a narrow custom coreference suite; generalizability to larger models and diverse tasks is uncertain.
- **Causal attribution:** Not established that architectural constraint alone caused benefits; other training dynamics may contribute, as suggested by CFM variant's failure.

## Next Checks
1. **Gradient Flow Verification:** Implement a debugging hook to confirm zero gradients for the X_T stream parameters across all layers in LFA. Non-zero gradients invalidate the core architectural premise.
2. **FFN Architecture Test:** Train an "LFA-dense-FFN" ablation where the FFN's internal mixing is channel-independent but it still observes CLN(X_T+X_E) and writes only to X_E. If performance and stability drop to CFM levels (~+10% val loss, 0% stability), the dense FFN is critical.
3. **Cross-Dataset PDS Trajectory:** Compute the layer-wise PDS heatmap on a standard language modeling benchmark (e.g., WikiText-103 validation subset). LFA should show high-PDS heads concentrated in layers 4-5; Std-T uniformly low PDS.
