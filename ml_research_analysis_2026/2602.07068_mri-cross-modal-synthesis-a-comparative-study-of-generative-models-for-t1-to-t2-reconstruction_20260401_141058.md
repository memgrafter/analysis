# MRI Cross-Modal Synthesis: A Comparative Study of Generative Models for T1-to-T2 Reconstruction
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07068
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07068
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CycleGAN achieved the highest perceptual quality (PSNR: 32.28 dB, SSIM: 0.9008), while Pix2Pix GAN achieved the lowest pixel-wise error (MSE: 0.005846). VAE showed lower quantitative performance but offered advantages in latent space representation.

## Executive Summary
The paper compares three generative models—CycleGAN, Pix2Pix GAN, and VAE—for T1-to-T2 MRI synthesis using the BraTS 2020 dataset. With 11,439 training and 2,000 test slices, the evaluation uses MSE, PSNR, and SSIM. Results indicate that CycleGAN yields the best structural metrics, Pix2Pix excels in pixel-level accuracy, and VAE provides a probabilistic latent space at the cost of image sharpness. The study discusses trade-offs between quality, data requirements, and stability, and highlights the need for downstream validation and robust evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The study uses the BraTS 2020 dataset, extracting 2D axial slices from paired T1/T2 volumes. After resizing to 256×256 and normalizing to [-1,1], three models are trained: Pix2Pix (paired, U-Net generator, PatchGAN discriminator, L1+adversarial loss), CycleGAN (unpaired, cycle and identity losses, two generators/discriminators), and VAE (encoder-decoder with latent sampling, MSE+KL loss). All models are optimized with Adam (lr=0.0002, β₁=0.5, β₂=0.999) for 30 epochs, batch size 256. Performance is assessed on denormalized test images via MSE, PSNR, SSIM.

## Key Results
- CycleGAN achieved the highest perceptual quality: PSNR = 32.28 dB, SSIM = 0.9008.
- Pix2Pix GAN achieved the lowest pixel-wise error: MSE = 0.005846.
- VAE performance was lower (PSNR = 24.95 dB, SSIM = 0.6573) but offers advantages in latent space representation and sampling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cycle-consistency regularization in unpaired image translation improves anatomical fidelity and structural similarity in T1-to-T2 synthesis.
- Mechanism: By enforcing that a T1→T2→T1 cycle reconstruction matches the original T1, the model is constrained to preserve content-invariant anatomical structures across domain mappings, reducing anatomical distortions.
- Core assumption: The shared anatomical content between modalities is more consistent than the contrast-specific intensity mappings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] Reports CycleGAN achieved highest PSNR (32.28 dB) and SSIM (0.9008), indicating superior structural preservation.
  - [section 3.3.2] Explicitly describes cycle-consistency loss as a key component preventing content loss during translation.
  - [corpus] Neighbors include "Cross-system biological image quality enhancement" and "MRI-to-CT synthesis using drifting models," suggesting cross-modality synthesis benefits from consistency constraints; however, direct evidence for cycle-consistency is absent in provided corpus abstracts.
- Break condition: If the cycle loss weight is too low, anatomical preservation degrades; if too high, the model may fail to learn modality-specific contrast, resulting in T2 outputs that are structurally accurate but diagnostically unrealistic.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adversarial training with a PatchGAN discriminator enhances perceptual quality and texture realism in synthesized T2 images compared to pure pixel-wise losses.
- Mechanism: The discriminator's patch-based classification encourages the generator to produce locally realistic textures and high-frequency details that match the target domain distribution, supplementing L1 loss which tends to blur fine structures.
- Core assumption: Human perception of medical image quality is sensitive to local texture statistics, which are poorly captured by MSE/SSIM.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] Notes Pix2Pix GAN achieved lowest MSE, but CycleGAN (which also uses adversarial loss) achieved higher PSNR/SSIM, suggesting adversarial loss contributes to structural metrics beyond pixel accuracy.
  - [section 3.3.1] Describes PatchGAN discriminator design that focuses on local patches to promote texture realism.
  - [corpus] Multiple neighbors (e.g., "Cross-system biological image quality enhancement," "Lesion-Aware Generative AI") employ GANs for medical image synthesis, indicating community consensus on adversarial training for quality; however, direct causal attribution to texture is not in provided abstracts.
- Break condition: If discriminator capacity is insufficient or training is unstable (mode collapse), the generator may produce repeated artifacts or fail to capture diverse tissue textures, harming clinical utility despite good pixel metrics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The probabilistic latent space in VAEs enables representation learning and uncertainty estimation but at the cost of pixel-level sharpness in T2 reconstruction.
- Mechanism: The KL divergence term regularizes the encoder to map T1 inputs to a structured latent distribution, allowing sampling and interpolation; however, the combined MSE+KL loss tends to produce averaged, blurrier outputs compared to adversarial objectives.
- Core assumption: A smooth latent space enabling meaningful manipulation is valuable for downstream tasks (e.g., anomaly detection) even if reconstruction is less sharp.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] Explicitly states VAE "offers advantages in latent space representation and sampling capabilities" despite lower PSNR/SSIM.
  - [section 3.3.3] Details the VAE's dual loss (reconstruction + KL) and latent dimensionality (128).
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors focus heavily on GANs and transformers; VAE-specific evidence is weak. The closest is "Amyloid-Beta Axial Plane PET Synthesis" using image translation (likely GAN-based), not VAE. Therefore, corpus anchor is limited and does not strongly support the latent space advantage claim.
- Break condition: If the KL divergence weight (β) is too high, latent capacity is wasted on matching prior, leading to blurry reconstructions; if too low, the latent space becomes unstructured, losing sampling benefits.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input T1 (256x256) → Generator (U-Net or encoder-decoder CNN) → Synthetic T2. GANs add Discriminator (PatchGAN). VAE adds latent sampling layer (μ, logσ).

**Critical path**:
1. Preprocess BraTS slices (resize 256x256, normalize [-1,1]).
2. Select model based on data pairing (paired → Pix2Pix; unpaired → CycleGAN; latent needs → VAE).
3. Train with Adam (lr=0.0002, β₁=0.5, β₂=0.999) for 30 epochs, batch size 256.
4. Evaluate on 2000-test slices using MSE, PSNR, SSIM.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Paired data requirement: Pix2Pix needs perfect alignment; CycleGAN works with unpaired but may need more compute.
- Output quality: CycleGAN highest PSNR/SSIM; Pix2Pix lowest MSE; VAE enables sampling.
- Stability: VAE most stable; GANs need careful hyperparameter tuning.

**Failure signatures**:
- VAE: consistently blurry outputs across test set (high MSE, low SSIM).
- GANs: patchy artifacts or mode collapse (some outputs unrealistic despite good average metrics).
- CycleGAN: if cycle loss weight too low, anatomical distortions (e.g., misaligned ventricles).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline Pix2Pix training**: Train with paired data, monitor L1 + adversarial loss convergence. Inspect a validation set for texture preservation vs. blur. Expected: sharp textures, competitive SSIM.
2. **CycleGAN unpaired test**: Train with same paired data but *ignore pairing* to simulate unpaired scenario. Compare PSNR/SSIM to Pix2Pix. Expected: slight PSNR/SSIM gain if cycle consistency regularizes effectively.
3. **VAE latent interpolation**: After VAE training, sample two latent vectors from N(0,1) and decode to T2 images. Assess diversity and anatomical plausibility. Expected: smooth transitions between plausible T2 appearances, but blurrier than GAN outputs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Downstream task impact** – The paper does not evaluate whether synthetic T2 images preserve diagnostically relevant features for tasks like segmentation. To resolve, train segmentation models on synthetic T2 and compare to real T2 using Dice, Hausdorff.
2. **Robustness to diverse pathologies** – Performance across pathologies (beyond gliomas) not assessed; the BraTS dataset is limited. Resolve by testing on external datasets with varied pathologies or analyzing error stratified by tumor characteristics.
3. **Metric validation against radiologist perception** – Reliance on MSE/PSNR/SSIM may not match human perception; no radiologist study. Resolve with a multi-reader study correlating perceptual rankings with metrics like FID, LPIPS.
4. **Data pairing trade-offs** – Unpaired training advantage of CycleGAN not evaluated; all models trained on paired data. Resolve by systematically varying paired data availability and comparing quality metrics.

## Limitations
- Missing reproduction specifics: exact train/test split, loss weighting details, normalization scope, and training hyperparameters (discriminator update frequency, LR schedule).
- No statistical validation of reported metrics (e.g., confidence intervals, significance testing).
- Weak evidence support for the claim that VAE's latent representation provides practical benefits (corpus lacks VAE-focused studies).

## Confidence
