# Dual-Domain Representation Alignment: Bridging 2D and 3D Vision via Geometry-Aware Architecture Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19563
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19563
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** EvoNet-D matches state-of-the-art PSNR (26.41) on novel view synthesis with 88% fewer parameters (44M vs. 354M).

## Executive Summary
EvoNAS tackles two critical challenges in neural architecture search (NAS) for edge vision: prohibitive inference costs and ranking inconsistency in evolutionary weight-sharing NAS. It introduces a hybrid VSS-ViT search space, Cross-Architecture Dual-Domain Knowledge Distillation (CA-DDKD) using DCT for spectral-spatial alignment, and a Distributed Multi-Model Parallel Evaluation (DMMPE) engine. Evaluated across COCO, ADE20K, KITTI, NYU-Depth v2, and RealEstate10K, EvoNAS yields Pareto-optimal accuracy-efficiency trade-offs, with CA-DDKD markedly improving ranking correlation (Kendall’s τ = 0.883) and DMMPE cutting evaluation time by over 70%.

## Method Summary
EvoNAS employs a hybrid VSS-ViT supernet trained via Progressive Supernet Training (PST) to stabilize weight-sharing across heterogeneous operators. To adapt to a target dataset, the supernet is regularized with CA-DDKD: a frozen teacher (Depth Anything) guides student subnetworks by aligning features in both spatial and frequency domains (via DCT), preserving geometric details. Candidate architectures are evaluated using DMMPE, which leverages GPU resource pooling and MPS for hardware-isolated latency measurement. An NSGA-II evolutionary search then optimizes three objectives—task error, latency, and MACs—to identify Pareto-optimal EvoNet architectures, which are subsequently retrained from inherited supernet weights.

## Key Results
- CA-DDKD elevates ranking consistency (Kendall’s τ) from ~0.78 to 0.883.
- DMMPE reduces per-generation evaluation time by >70% (e.g., NYU v2: 705 s → 172 s).
- EvoNet-D on RealEstate10K achieves SOTA PSNR (26.41) with 88% fewer parameters (44M vs. 354M).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: CA-DDKD (Cross-Architecture Dual-Domain Knowledge Distillation)
- **Claim:** CA-DDKD improves ranking consistency among heterogeneous subnetworks in weight-sharing NAS by aligning features in both spatial and frequency domains.
- **Mechanism:** A frozen teacher (Depth Anything) guides the student supernet. For each sampled subnetwork, features from VSS and ViT branches are compared to teacher features via spatial MSE and DCT-based high-frequency MSE. The dual-domain constraint forces shared weights to preserve high-frequency geometric details that are otherwise lost during co-adaptation, reducing representation collapse.
- **Core assumption:** High-frequency components are critical for geometry-sensitive tasks and can be stabilized via explicit DCT regularization.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: “CA‑DDKD improves the representational capacity of the shared supernet and enhances ranking consistency.”  
  - [section IV.B]: “By constraining the high‑frequency spectral components, the supernet maintains boundary precision… ensures that the fitness estimation of a subnetwork reflects its actual geometric modeling capacity.”  
  - [corpus]: The corpus contains related work on knowledge distillation but no direct evidence for this specific combination; the paper’s own experiments serve as primary validation (assumption).
- **Break condition:** If the DCT introduces aliasing or the teacher lacks geometric fidelity, the alignment may misguide the supernet, degrading performance. Excessive weight on frequency loss could override spatial semantics.

### Mechanism 2: DMMPE (Distributed Multi-Model Parallel Evaluation)
- **Claim:** DMMPE provides unbiased, stable latency measurements by eliminating GPU kernel interference, thereby giving a reliable fitness landscape for hardware-aware multi-objective search.
- **Mechanism:** A master‑worker scheduler dispatches candidate architectures to a GPU pool. Within each GPU, multiple worker processes share a CUDA context via MPS, allowing concurrent execution of several subnetworks. Per‑process multi‑model batching amortizes I/O overhead, while strict hardware isolation prevents cross‑kernel interference. Latency is measured after warm‑up and synchronization, yielding deterministic values.
- **Core assumption:** Latency jitter from resource contention is a major source of noise in evolutionary fitness; it can be mitigated by hierarchical isolation and parallel execution.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: “DMMPE improves evaluation efficiency by over 70% through concurrent multi‑GPU, multi‑model execution.”  
  - [section IV.C]: “DMMPE framework based on GPU resource pooling and asynchronous scheduling… ensures unbiased physical latency measurements.”  
  - Table V: Per‑generation time reduced from 705 s (baseline) to 172 s on NYU v2 (>75% improvement).
- **Break condition:** On systems without MPS support or with limited GPU memory, the process model may cause OOM or increased overhead. Over‑subscription (too many models per GPU) can also re‑introduce contention.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid VSS‑ViT Supernet with Progressive Supernet Training (PST)
- **Claim:** A hybrid VSS‑ViT search space trained via PST stabilizes weight‑sharing across heterogeneous operators and preserves geometric priors, enabling accurate fitness estimation for diverse subnetworks.
- **Mechanism:** The supernet starts from the maximal configuration (largest state dimension, MLP ratio, SSD expand) to establish a robust geometric manifold. Smaller variants are activated incrementally; their weights are inherited by slicing corresponding channels/prefix blocks. During each unlocking phase, only new parameters are updated initially, followed by balanced joint optimization across all active configurations. This curriculum minimizes gradient interference and maintains feature alignment.
- **Core assumption:** A strong teacher‑like initialization provides a smooth optimization landscape; careful capacity reduction retains essential geometric features.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section IV.A]: “PST… decouples the optimization complexity of diverse operator dynamics… ensures that the fitness landscape remains smooth for the evolutionary engine.”  
  - Table VII: PST outperforms random sampling and architecture pool on NYU v2 (e.g., Abs Rel 0.085 vs 0.090/0.093).  
  - [section VI.F]: Evolutionary hypervolume converges steadily, indicating a stable fitness landscape.
- **Break condition:** Hasty unlocking (too few adaptation epochs) can cause interference spikes, breaking ranking consistency. Incorrect slicing (e.g., mismatched dimensions) may corrupt inherited weights.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: Knowledge Distillation (KD)
- **Why needed here:** CA‑DDKD builds on the principle of transferring dark knowledge from a large teacher to smaller student subnetworks to regularize training and preserve geometric information.
- **Quick check question:** Besides standard cross‑entropy, what additional loss terms are typically used in KD to match intermediate representations, and why are they helpful for stabilizing a weight‑sharing supernet?

### Concept 2: Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)
- **Why needed here:** DCT converts feature maps to the frequency domain; high‑frequency coefficients correspond to edges and fine textures. CA‑DDKD uses DCT to explicitly supervise these components and combat representation collapse.
- **Quick check question:** In image compression, why are DCT coefficients quantized more aggressively at high frequencies? How does this relate to the importance of preserving high‑frequency details in geometric vision tasks?

### Concept 3: Evolutionary Multi‑Objective Optimization (NSGA‑II)
- **Why needed here:** EvoNAS optimizes three conflicting objectives (accuracy, latency, MACs). NSGA‑II provides a population‑based method to approximate the Pareto front without scalarization.
- **Quick check question:** What is non‑dominated sorting, and how does it differ from selecting based on a weighted sum of objectives in the context of hardware‑aware NAS?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
VSS-ViT Supernet -> CA-DDKD (Teacher: Depth Anything) -> DMMPE Engine -> Evolutionary Search (NSGA-II) -> Pareto Front

Critical path:  
1. **Pre‑train supernet** on ImageNet‑1K using PST (max config → incremental unlocking).  
2. **Adapt supernet** to target dataset (COCO/ADE20K/KITTI/NYU) with CA‑DDKD (teacher guidance + dual‑domain losses).  
3. **Initialize population** of genotypes; run evolutionary loop: offspring generation → **DMMPE** evaluation (supernet accuracy + isolated latency) → NSGA‑II survival selection.  
4. **Extract Pareto front**, retrain standalone architectures from supernet weights (task heads), and evaluate on downstream tasks (including 3DGS transfer).

Design tradeoffs:  
- **Search space expressiveness vs. cost:** More discrete choices enlarge the space and improve Pareto coverage but increase evaluation burden.  
- **Distillation weights (α₁, α₂, θ):** Higher α₂ emphasizes frequency alignment, improving boundary preservation but risking over‑regularization; need empirical tuning per task.  
- **DMMPE concurrency:** More models per process reduces scheduling overhead but increases per‑GPU memory use; more processes improve isolation but add context‑switch cost. Optimal config depends on GPU memory and model size.  
- **PST schedule length:** Longer adaptation epochs improve stability but prolong pre‑training; the trade‑off influences overall search time.

Failure signatures:  
- **Low ranking consistency** (Kendall’s τ < 0.7) after CA‑DDKD: indicates insufficient dual‑domain regularization or inappropriate teacher; check loss weights and teacher quality.  
- **High latency variance** (coefficient of variation > 5%) across repeated DMMPE runs: suggests MPS disabled or inter‑process interference; verify GPU virtualization setup.  
- **Stagnant hypervolume** in evolution: possible premature convergence; consider increasing population size, mutation rate, or extending generations.  
- **Poor 3D transfer** (e.g., RealEstate10K PSNR far below baselines): geometric priors may be weak; increase frequency loss weight or use a more geometry‑focused teacher.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Ablate CA‑DDKD:** Train supernet without dual‑domain losses, sample architectures, retrain independently, compute Kendall’s τ between supernet scores and standalone performance. Expect τ ≈ 0.78 vs. 0.88 with CA‑DDKD.  
2. **Benchmark DMMPE:** Compare per‑generation evaluation time on NYU v2 between baseline data‑parallel (single model per GPU) and DMMPE (multi‑model, multi‑process). Expect > 70% speedup.  
3. **Pareto front validation:** Run EvoNAS on COCO (detection) and ADE20K (segmentation), plot accuracy vs. latency vs. MACs. Verify EvoNet models dominate prior CNN/ViT/Mamba baselines in the low‑latency regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can differential privacy and federated learning be integrated into the EvoNAS framework to enable secure and collaborative architecture search on distributed edge devices?**  
   *Basis:* Future work explicitly mentions exploring differential privacy and federated learning.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current EvoNAS focuses on efficient search but does not address privacy-preserving or distributed training constraints critical for real-world edge deployment.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Development and evaluation of a federated EvoNAS variant with differential privacy, demonstrating maintained Pareto-optimality compared to centralized search.

2. **How does the selection of frequency components in the DCT-based dual-domain knowledge distillation (e.g., which frequency bands are emphasized) affect ranking consistency (Kendall’s τ) and final task accuracy?**  
   *Basis:* CA‑DDKD uses DCT for spectral alignment, but no analysis of sensitivity to DCT parameters or frequency band choices is provided.  
   *Why unresolved:* Without understanding this sensitivity, it is unclear whether the current implementation is optimal or if alternative spectral masks could further improve consistency.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation experiments varying the DCT coefficient mask or weighting scheme, measuring both ranking correlation and downstream performance on geometry-sensitive tasks (e.g., NYU Depth).

3. **To what extent do the benefits of the hybrid VSS-ViT search space stem from this specific pairing versus the general principle of combining local and global operators? Could alternative hybrids (e.g., VSS with convolutions) achieve comparable geometry-aware performance?**  
   *Basis:* The paper argues for VSS-ViT synergy but does not compare against other hybrid search spaces (e.g., VSS+CNN or CNN+ViT).  
   *Why unresolved:* The search space design is not evaluated against alternatives, limiting insight into what makes VSS-ViT particularly effective for preserving geometric priors.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Construct and run evolutionary searches with alternative hybrid search spaces, compare Pareto fronts on geometry-sensitive benchmarks (e.g., KITTI, ADE20K).

4. **Does the geometry-aware encoder discovered by EvoNAS generalize to other 3D vision tasks beyond novel view synthesis (e.g., 3D object detection from point clouds, multi-view stereo)?**  
   *Basis:* Transferability is demonstrated only on RealEstate10K for NVS; other 3D tasks remain unexplored despite claims of universal geometric priors.  
   *Why unresolved:* The claim of universal geometric priors requires validation across diverse 3D scenarios, as different tasks may rely on distinct geometric cues.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Replace the backbone in standard 3D task pipelines (e.g., VoteNet for 3D detection, MVSNet for multi-view stereo) with the EvoNet encoder and evaluate on datasets like ScanNet, KITTI-3D.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters for CA‑DDKD losses (θ, α₁, α₂) are unspecified; DCT loss implementation details (coefficient mask, MSE on DCT vs. spatial after inverse) are ambiguous.
- PST schedule length varies (e.g., 25/120 epochs for ImageNet vs. "5 + 20 epochs per dimension" for monocular depth); optimal adaptation epochs unclear.
- Teacher model (Depth Anything version) not stated; Evo‑Mamba block specification is missing.
- DMMPE configuration (processes per GPU, MPS parameters) omitted, hindering reproducibility of the claimed >70% speedup.
- Evidence comprises internal experiments only; no external replication or comparison to contemporaneous NAS methods.

## Confidence
- **CA‑DDKD ranking enhancement (τ=0.883):** Medium (supported by internal experiments but hyperparameters/config unclear)
- **DMMPE >70% speedup & unbiased latency:** Medium (Table V shows improvement but configuration details missing)
- **PST‑stabilized supernet training:** Medium (Table VII and convergence plots indicate benefit but schedule ambiguity)
- **Overall Pareto‑optimality across tasks:** Low (benchmarks are mostly older CNN/ViT/Mamba baselines; no comparison to recent NAS)
