# SagaScale: A Realistic, Scalable, and High-Quality Long-Context Benchmark Built from Full-Length Novels

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.09723
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09723
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: An automated pipeline using external resources only during QA generation creates complex, answerable questions from full-length novels (250K+ tokens), establishing that direct long-context input significantly outperforms RAG when within the model's window, while Agentic RAG overcomes Naïve RAG's retrieval bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
SagaScale introduces a novel benchmark to resolve the trade-off between realism, scalability, and quality in long-context evaluation. By constructing QA pairs from full-length novels (averaging 250K+ English and 320K+ Chinese tokens) using an asymmetric pipeline—where external knowledge (Wikipedia) is available only during question generation, not evaluation—it creates complex, non-memorized questions that test holistic document comprehension. Comprehensive evaluation across 12 LLMs and three inference methods (Long Context, Naïve RAG, Agentic RAG) demonstrates the superiority of direct context provision when feasible and the efficacy of iterative retrieval for distributed evidence.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is an automated pipeline for generating and filtering high-quality QA pairs from long narrative texts. It begins with a curated collection of full-length novels that have corresponding Wikipedia pages. During generation, an LLM is given both the novel text and the external Wikipedia summary to produce multi-hop questions that are answerable solely from the novel. The generated pairs undergo three sequential filters: (1) correctness verification via web-search-enabled LLM, (2) realism assurance via search-volume thresholds to eliminate unnatural queries, and (3) contamination filtering by testing if state-of-the-art models can answer them correctly in a closed-book setting. The resulting benchmark is then used to evaluate models under three paradigms: providing the entire novel (Long Context), single-round retrieval (Naïve RAG), and iterative retrieval (Agentic RAG).

## Key Results
- Direct Long Context input significantly outperforms both Naïve and Agentic RAG for models with sufficient context windows, by an average of 10.9% and 4.4% respectively on in-window problems.
- Agentic RAG overcomes Naïve RAG's retrieval bottleneck, solving 72.6% of problems completely unsolvable by Naïve RAG and showing less performance polarization.
- Gemini-2.5-Pro uniquely maintains stable performance across dramatically increasing context lengths, while most other state-of-the-art models show significant decay.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Asymmetric Generation for Complex Questions
- **Claim**: Supplying external resources only during QA generation enables creation of complex, answerable questions that test holistic long-context understanding beyond parametric memory.
- **Mechanism**: The pipeline seeds multi-hop question generation with Wikipedia summaries, requiring answers derivable solely from the full novel text. The external knowledge asymmetry ensures questions probe comprehension rather than recall.
- **Core Assumption**: LLMs can generate harder questions when given broader context than they possess at test time; filtering ensures these questions remain solvable from the novel alone.
- **Evidence Anchors**: The paper's abstract and Section 3.1 explicitly describe this asymmetry. The design is a distinctive choice compared to neighbor papers like *Agri-Query* and *WildLong*.
- **Break Condition**: If removing external resources during generation does not reduce question complexity/difficulty, the asymmetry mechanism is invalid.

### Mechanism 2: Agentic RAG Mitigates Single-Shot Retrieval Failure
- **Claim**: Agentic RAG overcomes Naïve RAG’s retrieval bottleneck by iteratively refining search queries, reducing polarization from single-chunk retrieval failures.
- **Mechanism**: Multi-round retrieval allows dynamic query refinement based on intermediate results, retrieving distributed evidence across the novel. This mitigates the "one-shot" failure where a single poor embedding match dooms the answer.
- **Core Assumption**: Complex questions require evidence from multiple non-contiguous chunks; iterative retrieval locates this evidence more reliably than a fixed top-k single-pass.
- **Evidence Anchors**: The abstract and Section 4.2 show that AR solves most problems unsolvable by NR and exhibits no extreme performance clustering.
- **Break Condition**: If Agentic RAG shows no improvement over Naïve RAG on questions where Naïve RAG retrieves irrelevant chunks, the iterative mechanism is not addressing the core limitation.

### Mechanism 3: Holistic Context Superiority
- **Claim**: Direct Long Context processing significantly outperforms RAG methods when the full novel fits within the model’s context window, as it avoids retrieval-induced information loss.
- **Mechanism**: Supplying the entire document eliminates retrieval noise and fragmentation, allowing holistic reasoning over complete narrative structure, character arcs, and dispersed clues.
- **Core Assumption**: State-of-the-art long-context models can effectively attend to and synthesize information across 250K+ tokens without degradation.
- **Evidence Anchors**: The abstract and Section 4.2 show LC consistently surpasses NR and AR for top 1M-context models on in-window problems.
- **Break Condition**: If, for models with sufficient window size, RAG matches or exceeds Long Context accuracy on complex multi-hop questions, the superiority of direct processing is not generalizable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**:  
  Book Collection -> External Resource Collection -> QA Generation -> Correctness Filtering -> Realism Filtering -> Contamination Filtering -> Evaluation Harness (LC/NR/AR)

- **Critical Path**: The pipeline is strictly sequential. Book collection depends on finding novels with Wikipedia entries; QA generation requires both novel chunks and parsed Wikipedia segments; all three filters must pass sequentially for a QA pair to be included. Failure at any stage (e.g., no Wikipedia page, generation failure, filter rejection) halts progress for that book/segment.

- **Design Tradeoffs**:  
  *Automation vs. Quality*: Full automation risks low-quality QAs; mitigated by multi-stage LLM-based filtering.  
  *Realism vs. Contamination*: Using real novels risks training-data contamination; mitigated by aggressive cross-model closed-book filtering, which also reduces dataset size.  
  *Generality vs. Focus*: Novel-only scope ensures narrative coherence but limits task diversity; the pipeline is domain-agnostic but not yet validated beyond fiction.

- **Failure Signatures**:  
  *High discard rate*: >95% discard rate (as in paper) may indicate over-filtering or ineffective generation.  
  *Low closed-book accuracy*: Contamination filter leaving QA pairs with >20% closed-book accuracy suggests potential memorization.  
  *Polarized results*: Extreme 0%/100% accuracy clustering for Naïve RAG indicates a severe retrieval bottleneck and possible "needle-in-haystack" extremes in the dataset.  
  *Formatting errors*: High parsing error rates in Long Context evaluations indicate model inability to follow instructions in long contexts.

- **First 3 Experiments**:  
  1. *Filter efficacy validation*: Randomly sample 100 QA pairs post each filter stage; measure human-verified correctness, answerability, objectivity. Target: >95% pass on final set.  
  2. *Contamination check*: Run 4 contamination-filter models (GPT-4.1, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-Opus-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro) in closed-book on full benchmark; ensure max accuracy <15%.  
  3. *Baseline reproduction*: Evaluate one 128K model (e.g., GPT-4o) on a 10-question subset using all three methods; verify Long Context > Agentic RAG > Naïve RAG for in-window questions, and that Agentic RAG shows lower polarization than Naïve RAG.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can the SagaScale data collection pipeline be adapted to generate large-scale training datasets (e.g., thousands of QA pairs) without compromising its quality controls, particularly the contamination filtering step?
- **Basis in Paper**: The "Limited Dataset Size" limitation states the benchmark size (1,124 QA pairs) is insufficient for training and that the filtering process removes the vast majority of generated pairs.
- **Why Unresolved**: The current pipeline's rigorous filtering is designed for a high-quality benchmark, not scalable dataset creation. Whether a less stringent pipeline yields sufficient, high-quality training data is open.
- **Resolution Evidence**: A systematic study varying filtering stringency (e.g., using one contamination model instead of four) and measuring downstream model performance on a held-out, rigorously filtered test set.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What architectural or training modifications cause models like Gemini-2.5-Pro to maintain stable performance with increasing context length, while most other state-of-the-art models show significant decay?
- **Basis in Paper**: The paper highlights Gemini-2.5-Pro's unique stable performance and notes it requires "further investigation."
- **Why Unresolved**: The evaluation is black-box; no analysis of internal mechanisms (attention patterns, positional encoding, training data) explains this robustness difference.
- **Resolution Evidence**: A detailed mechanistic analysis (e.g., attention rollout visualization, probing for information retrieval fidelity) of Gemini-2.5-Pro vs. a decaying model like GPT-4.1-mini on SagaScale instances. Alternatively, a controlled fine-tuning study where a decaying model is trained on long-context synthetic/novel data.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Is the observed negative correlation between low calibration error in Naïve RAG and its performance (and the positive correlation with Agentic RAG) a causal relationship, and can it be leveraged to design better retrieval-augmented agents?
- **Basis in Paper**: Section 4.2 reports a Spearman correlation: better calibration in NR is negatively correlated with NR performance (ρ = -0.5) but positively correlated with AR performance (ρ = 0.6), described as "seemingly paradoxical."
- **Why Unresolved**: The correlation is derived from a small set of 15 models. It suggests overconfidence is harmful for AR but can help NR, but this is not proven causal, and design implications are untested.
- **Resolution Evidence**: An intervention study where model confidence is deliberately manipulated (via prompt engineering or calibration) and its impact on NR and AR performance is measured. Analyzing agent decision trajectories to confirm overconfident models terminate searches earlier on unsolvable questions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the Agentic RAG paradigm's advantage over Naïve RAG generalize beyond fiction QA to other document types (e.g., legal contracts, technical manuals) and other long-context tasks (e.g., summarization, code reasoning)?
- **Basis in Paper**: The "Limited Task Scope" limitation states SagaScale is confined to QA on novels and suggests adaptation for other domains.
- **Why Unresolved**: The finding that AR overcomes NR's "retrieval bottleneck" is demonstrated on a single task (QA over narrative fiction). It is unclear if this bottleneck is universal or unique to novels' complex, implicit linking.
- **Resolution Evidence**: Replicating the NR vs. AR comparison on existing or new benchmarks for other domains and tasks. Measure how AR's gain correlates with the need for multi-hop, implicit information linking.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: What is the root cause of the widespread formatting instruction failures in the Long Context setting, and are they primarily a symptom of syntactic distraction, positional degradation, or a fundamental limitation in instruction-following robustness for long inputs?
- **Basis in Paper**: Section 4.2 notes some models fail to adhere to formatting instructions in long contexts (Table 6 shows stark differences) and calls this a "critical weakness."
- **Why Unresolved**: The paper identifies the phenomenon but does not diagnose it. Failure causes could be loss of instruction tracking, degraded early-token representation, or general brittleness.
- **Resolution Evidence**: Controlled experiments moving the instruction to different positions within the context or adding varying distractor text. Measure whether answer correctness correlates with formatting adherence.

## Limitations
- The pipeline's high discard rate (~95%) may indicate over-filtering or ineffective generation, limiting dataset scale and diversity.
- The contamination filter’s <15% closed-book accuracy threshold may be insufficient to guarantee non-memorization for future, more capable models.
- The benchmark is restricted to fiction novels; generalizability to other long-context domains (legal, technical, code) remains untested.
- Evaluation reveals significant formatting instruction failures in long contexts for some models, which could confound results if not properly parsed.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Asymmetric Generation)**: *Medium*. The asymmetric design is plausible but lacks independent verification; its necessity for achieving the reported difficulty is assumed.
- **Mechanism 2 (Agentic RAG overcomes bottleneck)**: *High*. Strong empirical support (72.6% of NR-unsolvable problems solved by AR) and alignment with prior work on iterative retrieval.
- **Mechanism 3 (Long Context superiority)**: *High*. Consistent advantage across models when full context is within the window, supported by multiple result tables and figures.
- **Overall (SagaScale resolves conflict)**: *Medium*. The pipeline is innovative but the high discard rate and automation risks may limit its scalability and the benchmark's ultimate quality and size.
