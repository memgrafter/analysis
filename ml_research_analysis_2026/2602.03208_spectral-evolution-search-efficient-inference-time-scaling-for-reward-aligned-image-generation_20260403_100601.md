# Spectral Evolution Search: Efficient Inference-Time Scaling for Reward-Aligned Image Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03208
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03208
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Spectral Evolution Search (SES) consistently outperforms baselines like Best-of-N (BoN) and Zeroth-Order Optimization (ZO-N) under a fixed Number of Reward Evaluations (NRE=200), advancing the Pareto frontier of quality versus computational cost for reward-aligned image generation.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the severe inefficiency of inference-time scaling for aligning text-to-image generative models with black-box reward functions. It reveals a spectral bias: model sensitivity to perturbations in the initial noise decays rapidly with increasing frequency. Guided by a theoretical Spectral Scaling Prediction, the proposed Spectral Evolution Search (SES) constrains gradient-free evolutionary search to the low-frequency subspace via wavelet transforms, dramatically reducing the effective search dimension. Experiments on SDXL and FLUX models with diverse reward metrics confirm that SES consistently improves reward scores under identical evaluation budgets, enabling more efficient alignment.

## Method Summary
SES optimizes the initial noise \(x_0\) of a deterministic generative flow (e.g., probability flow ODE) to maximize a reward \(R\). It first applies a multi-level Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to \(x_0\), extracting low-frequency coefficients \(u\) (the LL band) and freezing all high-frequency bands. The search space is thus reduced from \(D\) to \(D/4^J\) dimensions. A Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) then maintains a diagonal-covariance Gaussian distribution over \(u\). Each iteration samples \(N\) candidates, reconstructs full noise via inverse DWT, runs the full denoising (50 steps), evaluates the reward, selects the top \(K\) elites, and updates the distribution mean and variance with momentum \(\gamma\). After exhausting the NRE budget, a final \(u\) is drawn from the learned distribution to produce the optimized noise and image. The method exploits the empirical spectral bias that low-frequency variations dominate the reward signal.

## Key Results
- SES achieves higher reward scores than BoN and ZO-N with the same NRE=200, scaling more efficiently.
- Gains are consistent across diffusion (SDXL) and flow (FLUX) models and multiple reward models (CLIP, PickScore, HPSv2, Aesthetic).
- Ablations confirm that restricting search to the low-frequency subspace (optimal wavelet level \(J\)=4) is essential; random or high-frequency subspaces underperform significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SES relies on three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Spectral bias in generative models**: Sensitivity of the final image to initial noise perturbations decays as a power law with frequency, \(G(\omega) \propto \|\omega\|^{-\beta/2}\). This stems from the generative flow dynamics: isotropic noise contraction combined with anisotropic signal amplification via the denoiser Jacobian yields a decaying power spectrum. Low-frequency components thus control semantic structure, while high-frequency changes are visually negligible. Evidence: Band-limited perturbation tests show monotonic reward drop as frequency increases.

2. **Low-frequency subspace search**: Wavelet transforms orthogonally decouple the noise space. Freezing high-frequency bands reduces dimensionality (e.g., from 921,600 to ~256 latents for SDXL with \(J=4\)), concentrating optimization on directions that meaningfully affect the reward and avoiding wasted evaluations. Evidence: SES surpasses full-space baselines; ablations show low-frequency search yields peak performance.

3. **Cross-Entropy Method on the manifold**: CEM with a diagonal Gaussian distribution balances exploration and exploitation. Elite-driven updates shift the mean toward high-reward regions while gradually contracting variance, preventing sample collapse. The Gaussian assumption remains valid on the smooth low-frequency manifold. Evidence: SES’s quality-diversity trajectory outperforms resampling-based methods (e.g., Demon, SMC) that suffer from impoverishment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Generative Flow as Deterministic Mapping**: Generation via probability flow ODE is a deterministic function \(\Psi_\theta(z_0, c)\). Thus, aligning outputs reduces to optimizing the initial noise \(z_0\). Quick check: Given fixed prompt \(c\) and model \(\theta\), what is the only variable that determines the final image \(x_1\)?
- **Spectral Decoupling & Wavelet Transforms**: DWT provides a basis for localized frequency analysis (unlike global Fourier), enabling construction of a compact low-frequency manifold. Quick check: What advantage do wavelets offer over Fourier for building the search subspace in image generation?
- **Gradient-Free Evolutionary Search (CEM)**: CEM iteratively samples from a distribution, selects elites, and updates distribution parameters. It is compatible with non-differentiable rewards. Quick check: How does increasing the elite size \(K\) relative to population \(N\) affect the exploration-exploitation trade-off?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Gaussian noise \(x_0\) → DWT (decompose into low-freq \(u\) and fixed high-freq) → IDWT (reconstruct candidate \(x_0\)) → full denoising (50 steps) → reward evaluation → CEM update (mean \(\mu\), diagonal variance \(\sigma^2\)) using top-\(K\) elites → repeat for NRE iterations → final \(u^*\) → IDWT → final image.
- **Critical path**: The DWT is one-time; each CEM iteration requires a full denoise+reward cycle, which dominates runtime.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Wavelet level \(J\)*: Higher \(J\) increases dimensionality reduction but risks over-compression and incoherent structure (inverted-U performance).
  - *CEM parameters*: Population \(N\) vs elite \(K\) controls greediness; diagonal covariance is efficient but ignores correlations.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Over-compression (\(J\) too high): blurry/incoherent images despite high reward.
  - Under-compression (\(J\) too low): scaling gains vanish, performance nears full-space BoN.
  - CEM collapse: variance \(\sigma^2\) becomes near-zero early; monitor \(\text{Tr}(\Sigma)/D'\) per iteration.
  - Proxy inconsistency: if using a fast proxy (e.g., LCM-4step), its Spearman correlation with full-step reward must exceed 0.8.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Spectral bias replication**: Inject equal-energy DWT band-limited perturbations into \(x_0\); measure reward drop vs. frequency; confirm monotonic decay.
  2. **Subspace ablation**: Compare SES with full-space, high-frequency, random subspaces across \(J\)=1–6; identify optimal \(J\) and superiority of low-frequency.
  3. **Cross-reward validation**: Optimize for one reward (e.g., CLIP) and evaluate on others (e.g., PickScore, Aesthetic); ensure no significant degradation on non-target metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Reward quality sensitivity**: How does SES perform with noisy or biased reward models? The paper acknowledges its performance is bound by reward quality.
- **Theoretical assumptions**: To what extent do local linearization and spectral decoupling hold for real generative models, and how do higher-order interactions impact the Spectral Scaling Prediction?
- **NRE vs. wall-clock**: What is the relationship between NRE and actual latency across hardware and models? NRE may not translate linearly to real time.

## Limitations
- The spectral bias assumption may fail on data manifolds without a decaying power spectrum (e.g., synthetic high-frequency data), causing SES to discard important variations.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (\(J\), \(N\), \(K\), \(\gamma\)) requires careful tuning; misconfiguration leads to over/under-compression or CEM collapse.
- Imperfect reward models (biases, noise) directly limit SES’s alignment gains; the method cannot compensate for fundamental reward flaws.

## Confidence
- Spectral bias mechanism: **High** (theory + band-limited perturbation tests)
- Low-frequency subspace benefit: **Medium** (empirical gains but optimal \(J\) and reward dependence unclear)
- CEM effectiveness on low-freq manifold: **Medium** (Gaussian assumption may fail on highly multi-modal landscapes)

## Next Checks
1. **Spectral bias validation**: Inject DWT band-limited perturbations with equal energy; measure reward drop across frequencies; confirm power-law decay.
2. **Subspace ablation sweep**: Run SES with varying wavelet level \(J\) (1–6) and alternative subspaces; identify optimal \(J\) and verify low-frequency superiority.
3. **Proxy-full correlation**: Compute Spearman’s \(\rho\) between proxy (e.g., LCM-4step) and full-step rewards on diverse candidates; require \(\rho > 0.8\) for proxy-guided SES validity.
