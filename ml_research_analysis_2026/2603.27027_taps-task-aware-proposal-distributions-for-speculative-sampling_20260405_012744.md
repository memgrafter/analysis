# TAPS: Task Aware Proposal Distributions for Speculative Sampling
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27027
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27027
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Task-specific draft training yields specialized proposal distributions that improve acceptance length on matched benchmarks; inference-time composition (merged trees, confidence routing) substantially outperforms model weight averaging.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how the training distribution of draft models affects speculative decoding quality. Using HASS and EAGLE-2 drafters trained on MathInstruct, ShareGPT, and mixed datasets, the authors evaluate acceptance length across reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, SVAMP) and a conversational benchmark (MT-Bench). They find that task-specific draft training leads to clear specialization: MathInstruct-trained drafts excel on reasoning tasks, while ShareGPT-trained drafts excel on MT-Bench. Furthermore, inference-time composition techniques—particularly merged-tree verification and confidence-based routing—significantly boost performance compared to naive weight averaging, with merged trees achieving the highest average acceptance length. Confidence proves a more reliable routing signal than entropy for selecting the appropriate specialist.

## Method Summary
The study trains draft models on domain-specific corpora (MathInstruct for reasoning, ShareGPT for dialogue) and mixed datasets (combined or separate 35k/70k subsets). Draft trees are generated from these models and three composition strategies are compared: parameter averaging, confidence-based routing (selecting the best tree per input), and merged-tree verification (parallel verification of multiple trees). A fixed Llama-3-8B-Instruct model serves as the verifier. Acceptance length is measured on four benchmarks at various temperatures, with analyses of depth-dependent acceptance and routing decisions.

## Key Results
- Task-specific draft training yields clear specialization: MathInstruct drafts outperform ShareGPT drafts on GSM8K, MATH-500, and SVAMP; ShareGPT drafts outperform on MT-Bench, for both HASS and EAGLE-2 backbones.
- Inference-time composition, especially merged-tree verification, achieves the highest average acceptance length (e.g., 5.11 for HASS at temperature 0), surpassing both single-specialist and averaged drafts.
- Confidence-based routing reliably selects the domain-matched specialist (>90% for reasoning examples, >80% for MT-Bench), while entropy-based routing yields near-random splits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Task-specific draft training yields domain-specialized proposal distributions that increase acceptance length on matched downstream tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Training the draft on a domain-specific corpus steers its next-token predictions to align more closely with the target model's distribution on that domain, increasing the probability ratios `q(·)/p(·)` that drive acceptance.
- **Core assumption:** The target model's token distribution varies across task domains and can be approximated more accurately by a draft trained on in-domain data.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "task-specific training yields clear specialization"; Section RQ1, Table 1 shows MathInstruct > ShareGPT on reasoning benchmarks, ShareGPT > MathInstruct on MT-Bench.
- **Break condition:** If the target model's distribution is invariant across tasks or if domain data is too scarce for effective specialization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Inference-time composition (confidence routing and merged-tree verification) leverages multiple specialists more effectively than weight-space averaging, yielding higher acceptance length.
- **Mechanism:** Keeping specialists separate and combining outputs at test time preserves distinct proposal distributions; confidence routing selects the best tree per input, while merged-tree verification verifies multiple trees in parallel, increasing proposal diversity without sacrificing losslessness.
- **Core assumption:** Specialized drafts make non-redundant errors, so combining their trees uncovers proposals accepted by the target that any single draft would miss.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "merged-tree verification yields the highest acceptance length overall"; Section RQ3, Table 1: merged trees (5.11 HASS temp0) > best single (5.35 MathInstruct HASS temp0 MATH-500) in average; Figure 6 shows checkpoint averaging performs poorly.
- **Break condition:** If specialist drafts are highly correlated or if merged-tree overhead negates acceptance gains in end-to-end latency.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Draft confidence (average node confidence in a tree) is a more discriminative routing signal than draft entropy for selecting the task-matched specialist.
- **Mechanism:** Confidence, defined as the product of token probabilities along a path, reflects the draft's certainty in its proposed continuation; higher confidence correlates with better alignment to the target on in-domain tasks, while entropy is noisier.
- **Core assumption:** A draft's confidence in its proposed tokens is predictive of acceptance probability by the target, and this predictive power varies with domain match.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "confidence is a more useful routing signal than entropy"; Section RQ4, Table 2: confidence routing selects MathInstruct for >90% of reasoning examples, ShareGPT for >80% of MT-Bench; entropy routing yields ~50/50 splits; Figure 7 shows rejected tokens have higher entropy but this doesn't translate to clear routing.
- **Break condition:** If draft confidence is miscalibrated across depths or if domain differences manifest more in entropy than confidence.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Draft checkpoints (math_inst, sharegpt, mixed variants) -> Composition modules (averaged, confidence_router, merged_tree_verifier) -> Verifier (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) -> Evaluator (benchmarks, acceptance length computation).
- **Critical path:** 1. Train domain-specific draft checkpoints on fixed data splits. 2. Generate draft trees for each benchmark prompt. 3. Apply composition strategy (averaging/confidence/merged). 4. Run lossless verification, measure accepted tokens. 5. Aggregate by benchmark, temperature, depth.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Acceptance vs. latency (merged trees increase verifier input size); specialization vs. robustness (single-domain drafts excel on matched tasks but fail on others); training cost vs. inference flexibility (weight averaging is simple but ineffective; runtime composition requires storing multiple checkpoints).
- **Failure signatures:** Weight averaging produces acceptance lengths ~2.3–2.6, far below specialists (~4–5); entropy routing fails to separate benchmarks (Table 2 near 50/50); Mixed 70k+70k underperforms Mixed 35k+35k at temperature 1.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Run MathInstruct vs. ShareGPT drafts on all benchmarks; expect matched > mismatched. 2. Evaluate Mixed 35k+35k and Mixed 70k+70K; check if larger mixture improves average across temperatures. 3. Compare Averaged, Confidence Routed, Merged Trees; expect merged > confidence > averaged.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to two draft models and one target model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct), limiting generalizability.
- Merged-tree verification increases verifier input size; actual speedups unknown due to unmeasured overhead.
- Mixed-data training effects inconsistent across temperatures; mechanisms not fully explained.

## Confidence
- **Task-specific specialization:** High (clear empirical gaps across benchmarks in Table 1).
- **Composition superiority (merged/confidence over averaging):** Medium (acceptance gains consistent but latency trade-offs unquantified).
- **Confidence vs. entropy routing:** High (decisive benchmark-level selection in Table 2).
