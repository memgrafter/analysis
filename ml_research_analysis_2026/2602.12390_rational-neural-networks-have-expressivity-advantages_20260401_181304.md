# Rational Neural Networks have Expressivity Advantages
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12390
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12390
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Trainable low-degree rational activations yield exponential parameter efficiency over fixed smooth activations, with theoretical separation poly(log log(1/ε)) vs Ω(log(1/ε)) and consistent empirical improvements across vision, RL, and transformers.

## Executive Summary
Rational neural networks, using trainable low-degree rational activations, are shown to be exponentially more parameter-efficient than networks with fixed smooth activations. Theoretically, any smooth-activation network can be uniformly approximated by a rational network with size poly(log log(1/ε)), while some rational networks require Ω(log(1/ε)) parameters to be approximated by bounded-parameter smooth-activation networks, establishing an exponential separation. This gap extends to gated activations and transformers. Empirically, rational activations match or exceed fixed activations on CIFAR-10 classification (VGG8: 84.89% vs 82.19%), offline MuJoCo reinforcement learning (HalfCheetah-medium: 94.7 vs 85.8 normalized score), and Tiny ImageNet transformers (CaiT S24: 56.15% vs 55.30% GELU). Notably, removing normalization layers—which harm rational training—allows rationals to achieve 61.24% on ViT S8 while fixed activations fail to train.

## Method Summary
The authors replace standard fixed activations (ReLU, GELU, etc.) in various architectures (VGG, MLPs for RL, ViT/CaiT/Swin transformers) with trainable rational activations implemented via a "safe" Padé form: numerator ∑ a_i x^i, denominator 1 + |∑ b_j x^j|, ensuring no real poles. Rational coefficients are trained with Adam/AdamW, typically with a separate learning-rate multiplier and zero weight decay. For vision tasks, models are trained on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet using standard augmentations and optimization schedules; for RL, offline datasets (Minari medium) train IQL and TD3+BC agents; for transformers, DeiT-style pipelines with EMA are used. A key finding is that normalization layers (GroupNorm, LayerNorm) interact poorly with adaptive rationals, so a no-normalization variant is evaluated for ViT S8.

## Key Results
- Theoretical separation: Rational networks achieve uniform approximation of smooth-activation networks with size poly(log log(1/ε)), while some rational networks require Ω(log(1/ε)) parameters for smooth-activation approximation.
- CIFAR-10 VGG8 with rational activation reaches 84.89% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best fixed activation (82.19%).
- Offline MuJoCo HalfCheetah-medium: rational policy attains 94.7 normalized score vs 85.8 for fixed activations.
- Tiny ImageNet transformer (CaiT S24): rational activation yields 56.15% vs 55.30% for GELU; no-LayerNorm ViT S8 rational achieves 61.24% while fixed activations fail to train.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Expressivity via rational approximation theory**  
Rational functions can approximate functions with nearby singularities (e.g., piecewise-linear targets) with doubly logarithmic size overhead, while polynomial/smooth networks need logarithmic size. By composing low-degree rationals across layers, the network builds a high-degree rational that places poles adaptively near singularities. This assumes the target function has complex poles at bounded distance from the real interval, typical for sharp transitions. Evidence: Theorem 3.1 gives O(log³log(1/ε)) construction for GELU and Ω(log log(1/ε)) lower bound; AGM-theta and Halley iteration yield doubly exponential convergence.

**Mechanism 2: Compositional depth efficiency**  
Stacking L layers of fixed-degree rationals (e.g., P=5, Q=4) yields a global rational of exponentially high degree while total parameters scale linearly in L·width. This data-sparse representation exploits depth to mimic high-degree rationals cheaply. Core assumption: depth can be increased without severe numerical issues. Evidence: Section 2 notes alignment with Telgarsky (2017) and Boullé et al. (2020); Theorem 3.4 shows O(kM log³log(1/ε)) vs Ω(kM log(1/ε)) separation.

**Mechanism 3: Normalization disruption**  
Normalization layers (affine transforms) introduce redundancy with rational coefficients, creating flat directions and gradient noise amplification. For rational r_θ(γ·+β), reparameterization as r_θ′ causes identifiability issues; data-dependent batch statistics further increase variance via polynomial sensitivities. Core assumption: normalization adds affine degrees of freedom that interact poorly with adaptive rationals. Evidence: Section 3.5 and Appendix D formalize invariances and gradient bounds; experiments show larger accuracy drops for rationals under GroupNorm (Table 2) and ViT no-norm success (Table 5).

## Foundational Learning
- **Rational approximation theory**: Why needed: Central to the paper's expressivity claims; rational functions approximate non-smooth targets more efficiently than polynomials. Quick check: What property gives double-exponential convergence for sqrt(x) or log(x)? (Answer: Proximity of poles to the approximation interval via near-best rational approximants.)
- **Feedforward neural network fundamentals**: Why needed: Translating theoretical size bounds into practical layer/width choices; understanding depth/width trade-offs. Quick check: Given width W and depth L with per-layer degree d, the global rational degree grows roughly exponentially in L? (Answer: Yes, roughly d^L, but constrained by width as per Boullé et al.)
- **Trainable activation parameterization**: Why needed: Implementation uses safe Padé to avoid real poles while maintaining expressivity. Quick check: Why include absolute value in denominator? (Answer: Ensures denominator >0, preventing division-by-zero and stabilizing training; does not reduce representational capacity because sign can be absorbed into coefficients.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Input (normalized to [-1,1]^d) -> Linear layer (Wx+b) -> Rational activation r_θ(y) (safe Padé) -> Repeat (L times) -> Output head (linear/softmax)

Critical path:  
1. Replace all fixed activations in reference architecture with safe rational activation from `rational-activations` package.  
2. Initialize rational coefficients to match a target (e.g., GELU or LeakyReLU) for stable start.  
3. Use AdamW; set rational coefficient LR multiplier (0.5–1× base) and weight decay=0.  
4. Train; monitor for NaNs/gradient spikes; if observed, reduce rational LR or clip gradients (norm ≤1).  
5. If validation lags behind fixed baseline, try removing normalization layers (GroupNorm/LayerNorm) as per paper.  
6. Select best checkpoint by validation accuracy.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Degree: (5,4) balances expressivity and cost; higher degrees may help hard tasks but risk overfitting/instability.  
- Normalization: Including GroupNorm/LayerNorm harms rationals; omitting improves accuracy but may require LR adjustments for other modules.  
- Initialization: GELU-target may converge faster; LeakyReLU-target more robust.  
- Parameter sharing: Tying rationals across layers reduces parameters but may lose layer-specific adaptivity.

Failure signatures:  
- NaNs/loss spikes early: missing safe parameterization; check denominator formulation.  
- Slow convergence or plateau below fixed baseline: normalization present; try no-norm or lower rational LR.  
- Large train/val gap: overfitting; consider lower rational degree or tiny L2 on coefficients.  
- Rational coefficient gradient norms explode: reduce LR multiplier or increase clip norm.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **CIFAR-10 VGG**: Train VGG4/VGG8 with rational (GELU/LeakyReLU init) vs fixed activations under baseline and augmented protocols; with/without GroupNorm; expect rational top-1 ~84.89% (VGG8) vs 82.19% best fixed.  
2. **Offline MuJoCo (Minari medium)**: Train IQL and TD3+BC with 2-layer MLP (width 256) on HalfCheetah/Hopper/Walker2d; evaluate normalized scores; rational should achieve ~94.7 vs 85.8 on HalfCheetah.  
3. **Tiny ImageNet Transformers**: Replace MLP activation in ViT S8, CaiT S24, Swin T; use DeiT augmentations and AdamW; rational matches/exceeds fixed; for ViT S8 no-norm, rational reaches 61.24% while fixed fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do rational activations yield scaling benefits in large language models and massive RL benchmarks? (Future work: not yet evaluated at LLM scale or on larger RL suites.)
2. Can fused kernel implementations mitigate the runtime overhead of rational activations? (Current implementation uses existing package; fused kernels not developed.)
3. Under what conditions does removing normalization improve or harm training of rational networks? (Mixed results; no systematic ablation.)
4. How does the theoretical worst-case expressivity separation translate to typical practical performance gaps? (Lower bounds non-constructive; no link between instance hardness and observed gap.)

## Limitations
- Theoretical advantages assume target functions with nearby singularities; for entire functions (polynomials), the gap shrinks.
- Empirical evaluations are limited to small models and specific benchmarks; domain transfer unknown.
- Key details for reproducing normalization-free ViT and LSUV initialization are unspecified.

## Confidence
- Theoretical exponential separation: Medium (proofs appear sound but rely on specific constructions).
- Empirical performance gains: Low-Medium (strong numbers but few runs; hyperparameter sensitivity).
- Harmful normalization interaction: High (consistent theory and evidence across tasks).
