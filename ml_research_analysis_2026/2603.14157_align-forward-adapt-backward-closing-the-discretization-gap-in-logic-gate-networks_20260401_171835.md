# Align Forward, Adapt Backward: Closing the Discretization Gap in Logic Gate Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14157
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14157
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Hard-ST with CAGE achieves >98% MNIST and >58% CIFAR-10 accuracy with zero selection gap and 30–76× faster convergence than soft methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the training-inference mismatch in networks that use soft mixtures during training but hard selection at inference, which causes a discretization gap. The core insight is forward alignment: using hard selection (argmax) during training exactly matches deployment, eliminating the gap by construction. To maintain stable gradient flow, the authors leverage temperature decoupling—where the backward temperature does not affect the forward output—and propose CAGE (Confidence-Adaptive Gradient Estimation) to dynamically adjust the backward temperature based on selection confidence. This combination yields both zero gap and robust performance across a wide range of temperatures, while converging significantly faster than soft-relaxation methods.

## Method Summary
The method builds on the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) with a key modification: the forward pass uses a hard argmax selection for the discrete gate choice, ensuring identical behavior during training and inference. The backward pass employs a softmax surrogate with an independently tunable temperature (τ_b). This decoupling allows adaptive control of the gradient scale without altering the forward decision. CAGE monitors the average maximum softmax probability (confidence) across all neurons and linearly interpolates τ_b between a high exploration value (τ_max) and a low exploitation value (τ_min) as confidence grows. The approach is evaluated on large logic gate networks (thousands of neurons, 16 Boolean gates each) using binary versions of MNIST and CIFAR-10, with a specialized GroupSum readout for classification.

## Key Results
- Hard-ST with CAGE yields zero selection gap across all temperatures while maintaining >98% MNIST and >58% CIFAR-10 accuracy.
- Gumbel-ST without adaptation suffers a 47-point accuracy collapse at low temperatures (τ ≤ 0.1); CAGE prevents this failure.
- Hard-ST converges 30–76× faster than soft-relaxation baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Hard-ST zero gap:** By using argmax in both training and inference forward passes, the discrete decision is identical, eliminating any mismatch by construction.
- **Temperature decoupling:** Because argmax output depends only on the ordering of logits (scale-invariant), the backward temperature can be tuned without affecting the forward selection, enabling safe adaptive gradient scaling.
- **CAGE stabilization:** Dynamically lowering τ_b as confidence increases accelerates convergence while keeping τ_b high enough early on to avoid gradient collapse, all without compromising the zero-gap guarantee.

## Foundational Learning
- **Straight-Through Estimator (STE)**
  - *Why needed:* Enables training of discrete decisions by using a continuous surrogate (softmax) in the backward pass while keeping argmax in the forward.
  - *Quick check:* "In Hard-ST, what computation is performed in the forward pass, and through what operation is the gradient approximated in the backward pass?"

- **Temperature Scaling in Softmax**
  - *Why needed:* Controls sharpness of the softmax distribution; in hard selection, backward temperature can be tuned independently, enabling adaptive schedules.
  - *Quick check:* "For a hard-selection node, does varying τ change the forward output? Does increasing τ_b increase or decrease gradient magnitude?"

- **Gumbel-Max Trick**
  - *Why needed:* Adding Gumbel noise to logits before argmax yields a categorical sample with the same distribution as softmax, allowing stochastic exploration in Gumbel-ST.
  - *Quick check:* "Why does arg max_i (z_i + G_i) have the same distribution as sampling from softmax(z)?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input -> LogicGateLayer (composed of SelectionNodes) -> GroupSumReadout -> Output  
CAGEScheduler -> SelectionNodes (adjusts τ_b per iteration)

**Critical path:**  
1. Forward: Data -> SelectionNodes (choose gate by argmax or argmax+Gumbel) -> GroupSum (sums class-specific neuron outputs) -> softmax -> loss.  
2. Backward: Loss -> GroupSum -> SelectionNodes (using softmax(z/τ_b) surrogate to compute gradients for logits and parent inputs).  
3. CAGE updates: Compute confidence c(t) = mean(max_i softmax(z)_i), update EMA, set τ_b = τ_max - (τ_max-τ_min)*((c_ema-1/K)/(1-1/K)).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Hard-ST: zero gap, fast convergence, but deterministic → risk of poor early commitment.  
- Gumbel-ST: stochastic exploration, but low τ_b causes gradient concentration and accuracy collapse.  
- CAGE: adds adaptivity to avoid collapse, but introduces hyperparameters (τ_min, τ_max, β) and minor overhead.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Gumbel-ST accuracy collapse (≈ random chance) at low τ_b.  
- Persistent selection gap (>1%) indicates forward pass not aligned (e.g., soft mixture used).  
- τ_b never decreasing → confidence stuck low (check initialization or β).  
- Extreme slowdown in Hard-ST → τ_b too high or learning-rate mis-set.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Selection gap verification** – Train all six methods on MNIST-Binary with τ=1.0; plot gap during training. Expect Hard-ST gap exactly 0%, others non-zero or transient.  
2. **Temperature robustness** – Sweep τ across {0.05,0.1,0.5,1.0,2.0} and record final test accuracy. Hard-ST and CAGE variants should show flat curves; Gumbel-ST should dip sharply at low τ.  
3. **CAGE dynamics** – Run Hard-ST+CAGE on MNIST-Binary; plot confidence c_ema and τ_b over iterations. Verify τ_b starts near τ_max and decreases as confidence rises, remaining above τ_min. Confirm selection gap stays zero.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Forward alignment generality:** Does forward alignment consistently eliminate the training-inference gap in other architectures requiring discrete selection (e.g., NAS, MoE, VQ-VAE)? The paper suggests validation remains future work.  
- **Gumbel-ST collapse universality:** Is the catastrophic accuracy collapse of Gumbel-ST at low temperatures a general failure mode across architectures? Current evidence is limited to logic gate networks.  
- **Alternative adaptive schedules:** Can alternative backward temperature adaptation schemes (beyond CAGE’s linear interpolation) further improve stability or speed while maintaining zero gap?  
- **Effect of candidate set size K:** How does the number of candidate gates K affect the selection gap and convergence of forward-aligned methods? All experiments fixed K=16.

## Limitations
- Connectivity details (parent selection) are unspecified beyond “random distinct,” which may affect results.
- The GroupSum temperature formula is ambiguous; the literal τ = α(C)/√k would cause instability, while τ = α(C)·√k is likely intended.
- Training forward gate computation is assumed soft; any misuse (e.g., hard gates in forward) breaks the zero-gap guarantee.
- CIFAR-10-Binary feature construction (31 thresholds per channel) is an interpretation; alternative binarizations could change outcomes.
- Convergence metric (e.g., 90% training accuracy) is not precisely defined.
- Full-scale networks require >24 GB GPU memory, hindering reproduction on modest hardware.
