# Process-Supervised Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Reliable Clinical Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14160
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14160
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Hybrid reward training yields 0.750 outcome accuracy and 0.520 process fidelity F1 on ClinGen gene-disease validity curation, surpassing outcome-only training (0.732 accuracy, 0.392 F1).

## Executive Summary
We propose a hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for gene-disease validity curation that aligns with clinical evidence assessment workflows. A supervisor agent coordinates six specialized sub-agents, each responsible for a ClinGen evidence category. The supervisor is trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using a hybrid reward that combines outcome accuracy and process fidelity (measured by tool-call F1). On the ClinGen dataset, outcome-only training improves accuracy from 0.195 to 0.732 but degrades process alignment. With hybrid rewards, the model achieves 0.750 accuracy while significantly improving process fidelity to 0.520 F1. The multi-agent design produces structured, evidence-specific outputs, enhancing traceability and interpretability compared to single-agent baselines.

## Method Summary
The task is framed as a hierarchical multi-agent system for gene-disease validity curation. Input instances consist of a gene, disease, and a batch of article abstracts linked to full texts. A supervisor agent (LLM) generates a plan and invokes six sub-agents in parallel, each specialized for one ClinGen experimental evidence category (e.g., Biochemical Function, Model Systems). Sub-agents read the full article and return a structured evidence object containing a subtype label and a rationale. The supervisor synthesizes these observations into a final validity classification. Training uses GRPO with a hybrid reward: \(R_{\text{hybrid}} = \sigma[\beta R_{\text{out}} + (1-\beta) R_{\text{proc}}]\), where \(R_{\text{out}}\) rewards correct classification (ordinal distance) and \(R_{\text{proc}}\) rewards alignment of tool calls with gold evidence pathways (cubic-scaled F1 plus malformed-tool penalty). To reduce training cost and variance, the supervisor is conditioned on ground-truth sub-agent observations (gold labels and clinician summaries) during rollouts, while sub-agents remain frozen. Optionally, sub-agents can be fine-tuned via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on high-quality traces.

## Key Results
- Outcome-only GRPO: accuracy 0.732, process fidelity F1 0.392.
- Hybrid reward (outcome + process): accuracy 0.750, process fidelity F1 0.520.
- Multi-agent system outputs structured evidence, improving traceability and interpretability over single-agent baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
We present three mechanisms explaining the approach's effectiveness.

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Hierarchical delegation to specialized sub-agents produces structured, evidence-specific outputs that mirror clinical curation workflows, improving interpretability and traceability.
**Mechanism:** A supervisor agent, trained with RL, selects which evidence-category sub-agent to invoke for each article abstract. Each sub-agent evaluates the full text and returns a subtype label and a rationale. The supervisor synthesizes these into a final validity classification.
**Core assumption:** Clinical evidence categories are modular and a focused sub-agent can reliably assess its category when given full text.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "the multi-agent system produces structured, evidence-specific outputs, enhancing traceability and interpretability compared to single-agent baselines."
- [section 4.1] "We frame the gene–disease validity curation task as a hierarchical MAS, in which a Supervisor Agent πθ orchestrates the reasoning process by invoking a set of K= 6 specialised sub-agents... Each sub-agent... returns a structured evidence object `(S, M)`."
- [corpus] No direct evidence; related MAS studies report benefits of role specialization but do not evaluate clinical SOP alignment.
**Break condition:** If sub-agents are inaccurate (low evidence accuracy), the supervisor’s synthesis quality deteriorates, particularly if the supervisor was trained assuming perfect observations.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A hybrid reward combining outcome accuracy and process fidelity (via agent call F1) co-optimizes final decision quality and adherence to clinical evidence pathways.
**Mechanism:** GRPO training uses \(R_{\text{hybrid}} = \sigma[\beta R_{\text{out}} + (1-\beta) R_{\text{proc}}]\), where \(R_{\text{out}}\) rewards correct validity classification and \(R_{\text{proc}}\) (cubic-shaped F1 on tool calls) rewards correct routing. The weighted sum balances both goals within a fixed scale.
**Core assumption:** Exact matches of agent calls (correct tool arguments) serve as a reliable proxy for following the clinically defined evidence assessment process; improving this metric leads to better process alignment.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "With process + outcome rewards, MAS ... achieves higher outcome accuracy (0.750) while significantly improving process fidelity to 0.520 F1."
- [section 4.2] Defines process reward as cubic-shaped F1 of tool calls, and sets β=0.5.
- [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., Med-PRM, ProRAG) explore process rewards in other domains, but the specific causal link to clinical reasoning is not validated outside this study.
**Break condition:** If the alignment between tool calls and true reasoning steps is poor (e.g., correct outcome can be reached via different tool sequences), the process reward may not correlate with clinical process quality. Balancing β incorrectly may trade off one objective for the other.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Conditioning the supervisor on ground-truth sub-agent observations during training decouples supervisor learning from sub-agent competence, reducing variance and computational overhead.
**Mechanism:** Instead of calling sub-agent LLMs during supervisor rollout, the training pipeline retrieves gold-standard subtype labels and clinician-written summaries for each requested evidence category. The supervisor thus learns from optimal trajectories without noise from sub-agent errors.
**Core assumption:** The mapping from context to correct evidence categories can be learned from oracle observations alone, even though the actual inference environment will involve imperfect sub-agents.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 4.1] "During supervisor training, rolling out all sub-agents ... To reduce this cost, we adopt a strategy in which the supervisor πθ is conditioned on ground-truth sub-agent observations rather than on sampled LLM sub-agent outputs."
- [section 4.1] "reducing training costs and mitigating the noise introduced by incorrect or incomplete sub-agent predictions during training."
- [corpus] No explicit support; the approach resembles teacher-forcing but its impact on final MAS performance is not studied elsewhere in the corpus.
**Break condition:** The train/inference discrepancy may cause failure if sub-agents are too inaccurate; the supervisor may have overfitted to perfect observations and be unable to recover from sub-agent mistakes at test time.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
  Why needed here: The supervisor is optimized with GRPO, a policy gradient algorithm that uses group-relative advantages and avoids a learned value function, enabling stable multi-turn RL with multiple sampled responses.  
  Quick check question: How does GRPO compute the advantage for a trajectory, and what is the effect of using the group mean as a baseline?

- **Concept: Multi-Agent System (MAS) Coordination and Role Specialization**  
  Why needed here: The core architecture is a hierarchical MAS; understanding how a supervisor delegates to specialized sub-agents is essential to grasp the workflow and the source of interpretable outputs.  
  Quick check question: Compare the information flow in a single-agent pipeline versus a hierarchical MAS with a supervisor; what are the main failure points in delegation?

- **Concept: Clinical Evidence Taxonomies (ClinGen SOP)**  
  Why needed here: The process reward and sub-agent roles are directly derived from the ClinGen standard operating procedure’s evidence categories (e.g., Biochemical Function, Model Systems). Without this domain structure, the reward design and evaluation metrics have no meaning.  
  Quick check question: What are the six experimental evidence categories in ClinGen and how do their subtypes map to the sub-agent outputs?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
The system comprises:
- **Supervisor Agent** (LLM Qwen3): plans tool calls and synthesizes final classification.
- **Sub-Agents** (6 specialized LLM instances): each handles one evidence category, processes full text, and returns evidence subtype set and explanation.
- **Tool Environment**: executes sub-agent calls and formats observations.
- **GRPO Trainer** (via veRL): samples rollouts, computes hybrid reward, performs policy updates.
- **Ground-truth Observation Provider**: during training, replaces sub-agent responses with gold annotations.
- **Reward Calculator**: computes \(R_{\text{out}}\) (ordinal distance) and \(R_{\text{proc}}\) (cubic F1 plus malformed-penalty).

Data flow: Supervisor → tool calls → Tool Environment → parallel Sub-Agents → structured observations → Supervisor → final validity label. Training additionally uses Ground-truth Observation Provider and Reward Calculator within GRPO Trainer.

### Critical Path
**Inference:** Input (gene, disease, abstracts) → Supervisor plans parallel tool calls (PMID/PMCID) → Sub-agents read full text → Observations returned → Supervisor produces final validity label.  
**Training:** Similar flow but supervisor rollouts are conditioned on ground-truth observations (gold subtype labels and clinician summaries) instead of actual sub-agent outputs; rewards computed; GRPO updates supervisor policy. Sub-agents remain frozen (optionally fine-tuned later via SFT).

### Design Tradeoffs
- One-round tool calls limit iterative refinement of evidence assessment.
- Shared LLM for all sub-agents simplifies deployment but may not achieve peak performance on each evidence type.
- Ground-truth conditioning decouples supervisor learning from sub-agent noise but introduces a train/test distribution mismatch.
- Balancing β in the hybrid reward trades off outcome accuracy versus process fidelity.

### Failure Signatures
- Process F1 stagnates or drops while outcome improves → process reward signal being ignored or corrupted.
- High malformed tool call penalty → supervisor generates syntactically invalid tool blocks; needs format reward or prompt adjustment.
- Supervisor reward improves but validation process metrics degrade → likely due to ground-truth observation mismatch; indicates sub-agent weakness.
- Evidence F1 low despite good agent call accuracy → sub-agents fail to predict evidence subtypes correctly even when called.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Reproduce main result:** Compare outcome-only GRPO vs. hybrid GRPO on the test set; confirm outcome and process metric trends.
2. **Ablation on ground-truth injection:** Train supervisor with hybrid reward but condition on sampled sub-agent outputs instead of ground truth; measure impact on stability and final performance.
3. **Sub-agent SFT:** Fine-tune the shared sub-agent LLM on high-quality traces (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.2-generated) and re-evaluate the MAS; check if evidence accuracy/F1 improve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we reduce the train/test discrepancy from ground-truth conditioning without incurring high training costs? The current approach may lead to supervisor overfitting to perfect sub-agent observations.
- Is the cubic-shaped F1 on tool calls a valid proxy for clinical process fidelity? Could alternative process rewards better capture adherence to SOP?
- Would evidence-specific fine-tuning of sub-agents improve overall system performance compared to using a shared LLM?
- Can the hierarchical MAS be extended to iterative refinement (multiple rounds of evidence gathering) to improve outcome accuracy?

## Limitations
- Training-inference mismatch: Supervisor trained on gold sub-agent observations may falter when sub-agents are imperfect.
- Process reward proxy: Exact tool-call F1 may not fully capture clinical process fidelity; alternative reasoning pathways could be valid.
- Sub-agent uniformity: Using a single LLM for all evidence categories may limit per-category performance; evidence-specific fine-tuning is unexplored.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical delegation yields structured, interpretable outputs → **Medium** confidence.
- Hybrid reward co-optimizes outcome and process → **Medium** confidence.
- Ground-truth conditioning decouples supervisor learning → **Low** confidence.
- Reported outcome (0.750 accuracy) and process F1 (0.520) → **High** confidence (empirical), but broader significance → **Medium**.
