# W2T: LoRA Weights Already Know What They Can Do

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15990
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15990
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** W2T resolves LoRA's factorization ambiguity (GL(r) symmetry) via a canonical QR-SVD representation, enabling strong weight-only inference for attribute classification (e.g., +18.34% Macro-F1 on CUB), performance prediction (99.37% Pearson on ARC), and zero-shot adapter retrieval (65.71% NDCG@10).

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether information about a LoRA adapter's task, performance, and similarity to other adapters can be extracted directly from its weight factors (B, A), without model inference or training data. The central challenge is that LoRA's low-rank factorization is not unique: infinitely many (B, A) pairs produce the same weight update ΔW = BA due to GL(r) symmetry, making raw factors an ambiguous representation. W2T addresses this by constructing a provably canonical form for each LoRA update using QR decomposition followed by SVD on a small core matrix. This canonical (U, Σ, V) representation is invariant to the original factorization. These components are tokenized and processed by a hierarchical Transformer (rank-level then position-level) to produce an embedding. Across vision (Stable Diffusion on CelebA, CUB) and language (Llama-3.2 on GoEmotions, ARC) LoRA collections, W2T significantly outperforms baselines that use raw factors or other symmetry-aware methods, demonstrating that LoRA weights reliably indicate behavior when their inherent symmetry is properly resolved.

## Method Summary
W2T processes a LoRA checkpoint containing factor pairs (Bₚ, Aₚ) for each adapted layer p. First, it performs a thin QR decomposition on Bₚ and Aₚᵀ to obtain orthogonal bases Q_B, Q_A and triangular cores R_B, R_A. The core matrix Mₚ = R_B R_Aᵀ is formed, and its SVD (Û, Σ, V̂) is computed. Lifting via Uₚ = Q_BÛ and Vₚ = Q_A V̂ yields the exact, GL(r)-invariant SVD of the true update ΔWₚ = UₚΣVₚᵀ. For each rank component, the left/right singular vectors are separately projected, fused, and modulated by its singular value σ via a FiLM-like MLP. These "rank tokens" for a given layer are processed by a rank-level Transformer. A weighted sum (using normalized σ values) pools them into a single "position token," augmented with layer depth and module type embeddings. A position-level Transformer processes all position tokens, and a final attention pooling produces a global embedding. This embedding is used by a simple task head for classification, regression, or retrieval.

## Key Results
*   **Attribute Classification:** W2T achieves Macro-F1 gains up to 18.34% on CUB and AUROC up to 89.64% on CelebA, dramatically outperforming raw-factor MLP/CNN/ViT baselines and the symmetry-aware GLNet.
*   **Performance Prediction:** On ARC-Easy, it reaches a Pearson correlation of 99.37% and MAE of 0.32%, beating all compared methods.
*   **Adapter Retrieval:** In a zero-shot transfer setting, W2T attains an average NDCG@10 of 65.71% and a top-1 hit rate of 99.14% on ARC-Challenge, surpassing GLNet and raw-cosine similarity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Canonicalization via QR-SVD Resolves GL(r) Symmetry
*   **Claim:** W2T achieves reliable weight-space inference by resolving the GL(r) reparameterization symmetry inherent in LoRA factors (B, A).
*   **Mechanism:** For any LoRA update ΔW = BA, infinitely many factor pairs (BG, G⁻¹A) produce the same update for G ∈ GL(r). W2T applies a thin QR decomposition to B and Aᵀ to obtain orthogonal bases (Q_B, Q_A) and triangular cores (R_B, R_A). It then forms the small r×r core matrix M = R_B R_Aᵀ and computes its SVD, M = ÛΣV̂ᵀ. Lifting via U = Q_BÛ and V = Q_A V̂ yields an exact SVD of ΔW = UΣVᵀ that is invariant to the original factorization.
*   **Core Assumption:** The canonical SVD components (U, Σ, V) uniquely characterize the LoRA update ΔW, independent of how it was factorized.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper's abstract and Section 3 formally prove GL(r)-invariance (Propositions 3.1, 3.2). The largest performance drop in the ablation study (Figure 4) occurs when this canonicalization step is removed.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Transformer on Canonical Tokens
*   **Claim:** The canonical rank-wise components (uₚ,ₖ, vₚ,ₖ, σₚ,ₖ) contain sufficient directional and magnitude information for downstream tasks when processed hierarchically.
*   **Mechanism:** Each rank component is tokenized via separate projectors for left/right singular vectors, concatenated, fused, and modulated by its singular value σ (via FiLM). These rank tokens within a layer (position) are processed by a rank-level Transformer to model intra-position interactions. A σ-guided weighted sum pools them into a position token, which is augmented with layer/module embeddings and processed by a position-level Transformer to model cross-position dependencies, yielding a global embedding.
*   **Core Assumption:** The structured hierarchy (rank → position → checkpoint) effectively captures both local (per-rank) and global (cross-layer) patterns in the canonical weight representation.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The architecture is detailed in Section 4 and Algorithm 1. Ablations in Figure 4 show that removing either the rank-level or position-level Transformer causes consistent, though smaller, performance drops compared to removing canonicalization. W2T outperforms raw-factor baselines across all tasks (Section 5.2).

### Mechanism 3: Generalization via Disentangled Representation
*   **Claim:** The learned canonical embedding space generalizes across base model instances and benefits more from additional supervision than symmetry-aware competitors.
*   **Mechanism:** Because the representation is canonical, it disentangles the update ΔW from the specific base model (e.g., SD1.4 vs. SD1.5) and factorization. This enables zero-shot transfer. Furthermore, the Transformer-based architecture has higher capacity than GLNet's equivariant linear layers, allowing it to better utilize additional training data.
*   **Core Assumption:** The canonical representation captures semantics and performance signals that are stable across minor base model variations and are more expressively modeled by a standard Transformer.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Table 5 shows strong zero-shot transfer from SD1.4 to SD1.5, while raw-factor methods fail. Figure 6 indicates W2T's performance scales more steeply with increased training data than GLNet from a 0.7 data ratio onward.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
    *   **Why needed:** W2T's input is a LoRA checkpoint (B, A). Understanding that ΔW = BA is a low-rank update to a base model weight matrix is essential to grasp the symmetry problem.
    *   **Quick Check:** "If you multiply B by an invertible matrix G and multiply A by G⁻¹, what happens to the product BA and why does this create an identification problem for learning from (B, A) pairs?"
*   **Concept: GL(r) Reparameterization Symmetry**
    *   **Why needed:** This is the core obstacle W2T overcomes. The non-uniqueness of (B, A) for a given ΔW means raw factors are not a valid data representation.
    *   **Quick Check:** "Explain why the set of all factor pairs (BG, G⁻¹A) for G ∈ GL(r) is infinite and how this would confuse a neural network trained to predict attributes from flattened (B, A) vectors."
*   **Concept: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and QR Decomposition**
    *   **Why needed:** W2T's canonicalization relies on QR to get orthogonal bases and SVD on the small core matrix to extract invariant singular values/vectors.
    *   **Quick Check:** "Given B = Q_B R_B and Aᵀ = Q_A R_A, show algebraically how ΔW = Q_B (R_B R_Aᵀ) Q_Aᵀ. Why does this reduction to an r×r core matrix preserve the singular structure of ΔW?"

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Raw LoRA factors (Bₚ, Aₚ) -> Canonicalization Module (QR on B,Aᵀ; M=R_B R_Aᵀ; SVD on M; lift to U,Σ,V) -> Tokenization Module (project uₚ,ₖ, vₚ,ₖ separately; fuse; modulate with σₚ,ₖ) -> Rank-Level Transformer (self-attention over r rank tokens τₚ,₁:ᵣ) -> σ-guided Pooling (weighted sum) -> Position Token Tₚ -> Position Embedding (add layer depth ℓ(p), module m(p)) -> Position-Level Transformer (self-attention over all ˜Tₚ) -> Attention Pooling -> Global embedding h -> Task Head (MLP for classification/regression; cosine for retrieval).
*   **Critical path:** (B,A) -> [QR-SVD canonicalization] -> canonical (U,Σ,V) -> [Tokenize & modulate] -> rank tokens -> [f_rank] -> [σ-pool] -> position tokens -> [f_pos] -> [AttnPool] -> h -> downstream task.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Canonicalization cost vs. invariance:** QR-SVD on r×r matrices is cheap (~93× speedup vs. forming ΔW) and provides invariance.
    *   **Token length:** Total tokens = Σₚ r. For very deep models or high r, sequence length may stress the Transformer.
    *   **Hierarchical vs. flat:** The two-stage hierarchy injects inductive bias about LoRA structure. A flat Transformer over all (position, rank) tokens would be more general but might need more data to learn this bias.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Ablation failure:** Performance drops close to raw-factor baselines when QR-SVD is removed.
    *   **Attribute-specific failure:** Poor separation on fine-grained/local attributes (e.g., "High Cheekbones") where signal may be distributed across many low-σ components.
    *   **Transfer failure:** Significant gap between in-domain and cross-base-model (e.g., SD1.4→SD1.5) results, indicating overfitting to base-model idiosyncrasies.
    *   **Scaling plateau:** If W2T's advantage over GLNet diminishes with very large datasets, it may suggest capacity or data limitations are fundamental.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Ablation Study:** Train W2T with three ablations: (a) no rank-level Transformer, (b) no position-level Transformer, (c) no canonicalization (use raw flattened B||A). Compare Macro-F1 on CelebA-LoRA. Expect: (c) catastrophic drop; (a) and (b) moderate drops.
    2.  **Baseline Comparison:** Train W2T and all baselines (MLP, CNN, ViT, GLNet) on ARC-Easy-LoRA for performance prediction. Report MAE, Pearson. Expect: W2T outperforms raw-factor baselines by large margin and beats GLNet on correlation.
    3.  **Cross-Base-Model Transfer:** Train W2T and baselines on SD1.4 LoRAs (CelebA), evaluate zero-shot on SD1.5 LoRAs. Report Macro-F1. Expect: Symmetry-aware methods (W2T, GLNet) transfer well; raw-factor methods fail. W2T should match or slightly beat GLNet.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Extend to merged, composed, or dynamic multi-adapter LoRA workflows?** The current canonical form is defined per individual checkpoint. Combining adapters (e.g., via merging or sequential fine-tuning) breaks this uniqueness, requiring new compositional canonicalization principles. Evidence would involve developing mathematically sound methods to represent additive or routed combinations of LoRA updates and validating them on tasks involving adapter merging or composition.
*   **Use canonical LoRA representation for generative tasks?** The paper's tokenization is not invertible, and it's unclear how to map a smooth embedding back to weights for generation or how to perform meaningful interpolation in the embedding space. Evidence would require building a decoder from embeddings to (B,A) factors, demonstrating high-fidelity reconstruction, and showing that interpolated embeddings yield coherent generated outputs.
*   **Build a production-grade LoRA retrieval system?** The retrieval study is a proxy using fixed splits and a simple cosine similarity head. A real system needs contrastive training, scalable indexing (e.g., FAISS), and large-scale benchmarks with diverse query patterns and negative mining. Evidence would be a deployed system or a benchmark showing robust, fast retrieval on a massive adapter collection.

## Limitations
*   **Exact hyperparameters missing:** The initial learning rate after the 4-epoch warmup and the exact MLPσ architecture are not specified, hindering precise replication.
*   **Numerical invariance edge cases:** While theoretically GL(r)-invariant, sign/ordering ambiguities in the SVD of near-rank-deficient core matrices M may cause embedding drift under factorization transforms if not carefully post-processed.
*   **Scalability and generalization untested:** The token sequence length grows with the number of adapted layers and rank. Cross-model transfer is only shown between very similar models (SD1.4→SD1.5); performance on architecturally distinct base models (e.g., ResNet → Transformer) is unknown.

## Confidence
*   **Symmetry resolution and canonicalization:** **High** – Formal proofs exist, and the largest ablation drop confirms its primary role.
*   **Hierarchical Transformer over canonical tokens:** **Medium** – Strong empirical results support the design, but the space of alternative architectures (e.g., set-based models) is unexplored.
*   **Cross-model generalization and data scaling:** **Medium** – Zero-shot transfer and improved scaling curves are promising but are restricted to closely related base models; broader applicability needs more evidence.

## Next Checks
1.  **Controlled ablation replication:** Using the official codebase, train W2T with raw (B,A) factors (no QR-SVD) and verify the reported catastrophic performance drop on multiple dataset splits to ensure the effect is not split-dependent.
2.  **Invariance stress test:** For a diverse set of LoRA checkpoints, apply random GL(r) transformations (BG, G⁻¹A), recompute the canonical embedding, and measure the L2 distance to the original embedding. Stress test with ranks having very small singular values to probe numerical stability boundaries.
3.  **Cross-architecture transfer test:** Train W2T on SD1.4 LoRAs (e.g., CelebA) and evaluate zero-shot on SDXL LoRAs (same dataset). Include GLNet as a symmetry-aware baseline. A significant performance degradation compared to the SD1.4→SD1.5 result would indicate overfitting to base-model statistics despite canonicalization.
