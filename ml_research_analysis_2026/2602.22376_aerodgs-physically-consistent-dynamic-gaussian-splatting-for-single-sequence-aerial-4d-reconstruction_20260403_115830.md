# AeroDGS: Physically Consistent Dynamic Gaussian Splatting for Single-Sequence Aerial 4D Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22376
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22376
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Outperforms state-of-the-art dynamic Gaussian splatting methods on aerial 4D reconstruction, achieving up to 4 dB higher Dyn-PSNR on real-world Aero4D (e.g., 17.65 vs. 13.48) and 33.61 PSNR / 15.60 Dyn-PSNR on synthetic UAV3D.

## Executive Summary
AeroDGS tackles monocular aerial 4D reconstruction, where a single moving camera captures scenes with wide depth ranges and small, fast-moving objects. The severe depth and motion ambiguity is addressed through a two-module framework. First, a Monocular Geometry Lifting module uses zero-shot 2D foundation models (depth, segmentation, tracking) to generate a coarse but plausible 3D scaffold of static background and dynamic objects. Second, a Physics-Guided Optimization module embeds differentiable physical priors—ground support, upright stability, and trajectory smoothness—into the Gaussian splatting optimization to resolve ambiguity and enforce physically plausible motion. Evaluated on synthetic (UAV3D) and real-world (Aero4D) datasets, AeroDGS significantly outperforms prior dynamic Gaussian splatting methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of its physics-informed approach for monocular aerial scenarios.

## Method Summary
AeroDGS reconstructs a dynamic 3D scene from a single monocular aerial video using a unified explicit Gaussian representation. It begins with a **Monocular Geometry Lifting** pipeline that leverages off-the-shelf depth, segmentation, and tracking models to produce pseudo-depth maps, temporally consistent object masks, and sparse feature tracks. These are fused via SfM-like triangulation and bundle adjustment to estimate scale-consistent camera poses and a sparse point cloud. Depth scale is corrected, and dynamic objects are identified, segmented into 3D point sets, and fitted with Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBBs) to estimate their canonical 3D geometry and initial world trajectories. The scene is then represented as a union of static Gaussians and per-object canonical Gaussians transformed by SE(3) trajectories. During **Physics-Guided Optimization**, the scene is rendered via 3D Gaussian splatting. The photometric loss is augmented with three differentiable constraints: (1) **Ground Support** penalizes deviation of an object's bottom from the estimated ground plane along the camera ray; (2) **Upright Stability** aligns the object's vertical axis with the ground normal or gravity; (3) **Trajectory Smoothness** enforces temporal continuity in object centers. A dynamic mask weighting scheme focuses optimization on moving regions after the static background converges.

## Key Results
- On the real-world **Aero4D** dataset, AeroDGS achieves up to **4 dB higher Dyn-PSNR** than prior work (e.g., 17.65 vs. 13.48 on the "Intersection-Night" sequence).
- On the synthetic **UAV3D** dataset, it reaches **33.61 PSNR** and **15.60 Dyn-PSNR**, surpassing the previous best by **1.83 PSNR** and **4.97 Dyn-PSNR**.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of each physics constraint (ground support, upright stability, trajectory smoothness) for stabilizing monocular optimization and recovering accurate dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Robust Initialization via Monocular Geometry Lifting
A robust initial scene decomposition and geometry estimate is recovered from monocular footage via a multi-cue lifting process. The Monocular Geometry Lifting module uses zero-shot 2D foundation models (depth estimator, segmentation/tracker) to generate dense pseudo-depth maps and temporally consistent 2D instance masks. Sparse long-term background feature tracks are triangulated and bundle-adjusted to estimate scale-consistent camera poses and a sparse point cloud. A local ratio field corrects monocular depth scale. Object-level 3D point sets are fitted with oriented bounding boxes to estimate centers and footprints, with object height predicted by a pretrained MLP. 3D spatial consistency resolves ID switches/occlusions. Objects below a displacement threshold are static; others are dynamic candidates. This provides a coarse but geometrically plausible scaffold for subsequent optimization.
- **Break condition:** The 2D segmentation/tracking fails to maintain consistent object IDs, or the pseudo-depth is catastrophically wrong and uncorrected, leading to an irreparable initial scaffold where dynamic objects are not properly separated or located.

### Mechanism 2: Resolving Ambiguity with Physics-Guided Optimization
Differentiable physics-based constraints resolve inherent pose and trajectory ambiguity for small, fast-moving monocular objects, guiding them toward physically plausible configurations. The Physics-Guided Optimization module adds three regularization terms to the photometric loss during joint Gaussian optimization. 1) **Ground Support** encourages the object's bottom (center minus half-height) to lie on the local ground plane Γ_t, measured along the camera viewing ray. 2) **Upright Stability** aligns the object's vertical axis with the ground normal (for rigid objects) or gravity (for non-rigid), penalizing unrealistic tilting. 3) **Trajectory Smoothness** applies a second-order smoothness penalty on object centers over time to suppress jitter and enforce continuous acceleration. A dynamic-mask weighting increases focus on dynamic regions after static background convergence. These priors are universally applicable to urban aerial scenes (vehicles on roads) and effectively overcome monocular scale/translation ambiguity.
- **Break condition:** The scene contains dynamic objects that violate the priors (e.g., flying drones, objects on steep slopes), or the ground plane Γ_t is erroneous due to incorrect static/dynamic separation.

### Mechanism 3: Unified Gaussian Representation for Joint Optimization
A unified, explicit Gaussian representation enables efficient, differentiable joint optimization of static backgrounds and multiple dynamic objects with coherent spatio-temporal appearance modeling. Both static and dynamic elements are represented by 3D Gaussian primitives. Static Gaussians are fixed in world coordinates. For each dynamic object, a canonical set of Gaussians G_o is defined in an object-centric axis-aligned box. Object motion is a continuous 6DoF SE(3) trajectory T'_o,t (initialized from lifting, optimized with residual ∆T). Gaussian centers are transformed to world coordinates. Scene composition is a union at each timestep: G(t) = G_static ∪ (∀o, T'_o,t ◦ G_o). Appearance is modeled as a shared field f_ϕ conditioned on spatial position, viewing direction, and time, with an additional object embedding e_o for dynamic instances. This allows modeling of aerial appearance variation (lighting changes) without per-Gaussian SH, improving temporal coherence and memory efficiency.
- **Break condition:** The static/dynamic decomposition fails, an object's motion is highly non-rigid (violating single canonical space), or the scene has many small, densely packed dynamic objects where object-centric canonical spaces cannot be cleanly defined.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structure-from-Motion (SfM) & Bundle Adjustment**
  - **Why needed:** The Monocular Geometry Lifting module relies on SfM principles (feature tracking, triangulation, camera pose estimation) to establish a scale-consistent geometric foundation from a single video. Bundle adjustment jointly refines sparse points and poses.
  - **Quick check:** Why is a standard SfM pipeline insufficient for dynamic aerial scenes, necessitating the lifting module's specific steps (depth correction, 3D object fitting)?

- **Concept: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**
  - **Why needed:** AeroDGS is built upon the 3DGS representation. Understanding anisotropic Gaussians, covariance parameterization, differentiable rasterization (α-compositing), and optimization of position, scale, rotation, opacity is crucial.
  - **Quick check:** How does the 3DGS rendering equation differ from volumetric raymarching in NeRF, and why is it more suitable for real-time optimization of dynamic scenes?

- **Concept: Lie Groups & Trajectories (SE(3))**
  - **Why needed:** Dynamic object motion is modeled as a continuous trajectory in the Lie group SE(3). Understanding the exponential map (exp(ξ)) from twist ξ ∈ se(3) to transformation T ∈ SE(3) and spline interpolation is crucial for temporal modeling.
  - **Quick check:** What is the purpose of representing the object trajectory as T_o,t = exp(ξ_o(t)) and then adding a residual ∆T_o,t? What aspect of the monocular problem does the residual correct?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Pre-processor -> Geometry Lifting Engine -> Scene Composer -> Physics-Guided Optimizer -> Evaluation/Renderer

- **Critical path:**
  1. Run pre-processors (UniDepth, GroundingDINO+SAM2, CoTracker3) to get all 2D cues (dense depth, masks, tracks).
  2. Execute Geometry Lifting Engine to obtain initial 3D seeds (static point cloud, dynamic canonical Gaussians G_o, initial world poses T_o,t, camera poses P_t).
  3. Initialize unified Gaussian scene (static Gaussians + dynamic canonical Gaussians with initial transforms).
  4. Enter Physics-Guided Optimization loop. Renders, computes photometric loss + physics losses (L_support, L_upright, L_traj), applies dynamic mask weighting, updates all Gaussian parameters, appearance field ϕ, and pose residuals ∆T, ∆P.
  5. *Parallel dependency:* The ground plane Γ_t for L_support is inferred from the static Gaussians during optimization, making static geometry stabilization interdependent with dynamic optimization.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Physics Constraint Strength vs. Flexibility:** Tuning λ_sup, λ_upr, λ_traj trades off adherence to priors versus allowing non-standard motions (e.g., jumps). Too high causes rigidity; too low fails to resolve ambiguity.
  - **Dynamic-Static Separation Threshold (3m):** A fixed heuristic; lower risks blurred moving vehicles (misclassified as static), higher risks noisy dynamics from static foliage. Not data-adaptive.
  - **Appearance Field Complexity:** Using a shared hash-grid field f_ϕ (vs. per-Gaussian SH) improves temporal coherence and saves memory but may underfit complex view-dependent specularities.
  - **Monocular Input Scope:** The entire pipeline is designed for single-view. Incorporating sparse multi-view would drastically reduce reliance on physics priors but is out of scope.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Jittering or "Stuck" Objects:** Failure of L_traj or L_upright; objects vibrate or are unnaturally tilted.
  - **Objects Sinking/Floating:** Failure of L_support; often due to erroneous ground plane Γ_t from static Gaussians.
  - **Blurred/Missing Small Dynamics:** Dynamic-mask weighting ineffective, or initial lifting failed to segment/initialize the object (3m threshold misclassification).
  - **Static Geometry Artifacts:** Failure of initial SfM/bundle adjustment or depth correction; background remains sparse/noisy.
  - **Temporal Flickering:** Appearance field f_ϕ insufficient for lighting changes; may need capacity adjustment.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce the Lifting Module in isolation. Visually inspect the output 3D point cloud, camera poses, and object bounding boxes for plausibility.
  2. Ablate each Physics Constraint independently (λ_sup=0, λ_upr=0, λ_traj=0). Compare Dyn-PSNR and inspect trajectories to confirm individual contributions.
  3. Test the Dynamic-Static Separation Threshold (e.g., 1m, 3m, 5m). Evaluate PSNR/Dyn-PSNR and visually check for blurred static cars vs. noisy dynamic trees.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive Dynamic-Static Separation:** The fixed 3m motion threshold may misclassify objects with small localized motion, causing blur. An adaptive mechanism based on object scale or uncertainty is needed.
- **Reconstructing Extremely Small Objects:** Pedestrians are not reconstructed due to limited pixel coverage in high-altitude views. A method to aggregate evidence across frames for tiny objects is required.
- **Generalizing Physics Priors:** The constraints assume rigid, ground-contact vehicles. Applying them to flying drones, animals, or deformable objects could produce unrealistic reconstructions; object-type-aware priors are needed.
- **Robustness to Zero-Shot Model Quality:** The lifting module's reliance on external depth and segmentation models is not analyzed for sensitivity to domain shifts or errors; self-calibration within the loop could improve robustness.

## Limitations
- **Zero-shot model dependency:** Errors in external depth, segmentation, or tracking propagate to the initial scaffold with limited recovery, especially for small, fast objects.
- **Physics prior applicability:** Constraints fail for non-conforming dynamics (flying objects, deformations, steep slopes), where the ground plane or upright assumption is invalid.
- **Heuristic separation:** The fixed 3m motion threshold is ad-hoc and misclassifies objects, leading to blurred static regions or noisy dynamics.
- **Appearance field capacity:** The shared field f_ϕ may underfit complex lighting/specularities, causing temporal flickering.
- **Cascading errors:** Poor initial lifting (e.g., erroneous bounding boxes) propagates into physics optimization with few correction mechanisms.

## Confidence
- **Monocular Geometry Lifting (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Ablation shows importance, but soundness hinges on unvalidated external model consistency and scene assumptions; no independent geometric accuracy metrics.
- **Physics-Guided Optimization (Mechanism 2):** High. Strong ablation evidence and conceptual alignment with aerial urban physics; priors are well-supported for target domains.
- **Unified Gaussian Representation (Mechanism 3):** High. Built on robust 3DGS foundations, with quantitative gains over SOTA; architectural trade-offs (non-rigid objects) are acknowledged but not critical in evaluations.
