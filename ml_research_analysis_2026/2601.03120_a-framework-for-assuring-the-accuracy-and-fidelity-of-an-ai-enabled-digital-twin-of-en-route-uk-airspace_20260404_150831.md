# A framework for assuring the accuracy and fidelity of an AI-enabled Digital Twin of en route UK airspace

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03120
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03120
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The Trustworthy and Ethical Assurance (TEA) framework structures evidence (e.g., Kolmogorov–Smirnov ~0.15 for trajectory prediction, 100% GPT-5 benchmark pass rate) to argue sufficient fidelity of an AI-enabled Digital Twin for UK en route airspace.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an assurance framework to validate the accuracy and fidelity of an AI-enabled Digital Twin of UK en route airspace, intended for training AI air traffic control agents. It adapts the Trustworthy and Ethical Assurance (TEA) approach, structuring arguments across four sequential strategies: data pipeline integrity, virtual environment replay accuracy, trajectory prediction fidelity, and AI agent interoperability. Primary results demonstrate measurable evidence—statistical distances for probabilistic trajectory prediction and hybrid validation for LLM-based scenario generation—supporting the Digital Twin’s suitability within evolving regulatory guidance.

## Method Summary
The method uses TEA to decompose the top-level assurance goal into nested, conditional strategies. Historical operational data (radar, clearances, weather) is curated and fed into an object-oriented virtual environment. A physics-informed probabilistic trajectory predictor is validated against held-out real-world data using distributional similarity metrics (Kolmogorov–Smirnov, Wasserstein). Scenario generation employs GPT-5 with automated benchmarks and human-in-the-loop assessment. The critical path requires upstream validation (e.g., replay accuracy) before downstream evidence (e.g., agent testing) is meaningful, creating a traceable evidence chain.

## Key Results
- TEA framework provides a structured, evidence-based argument for Digital Twin fidelity, linking high-level goals to validated subsystems.
- Probabilistic trajectory predictor for descending Boeing 737-800s achieves Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic ~0.15 and Wasserstein distances 2.73–187 against real-world data.
- GPT-5 scenario generation attains 100% benchmark pass rate after feedback for non-interacting aircraft, with validation via sensitivity analysis and expert assessment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** A structured assurance case (via TEA) can systematically demonstrate sufficient fidelity and accuracy for an AI-enabled Digital Twin in a regulated domain.
- **Mechanism:** The TEA framework decomposes the top-level goal into nested strategies (data pipeline, virtual environment, trajectory prediction, AI agent enablement), each with property claims and evidence requirements, creating a traceable argument from intent to validation.
- **Core assumption:** Trustworthiness for novel AI/ML systems can be built through transparent, evidence-based argumentation rather than solely traditional verification/validation.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section II define the TEA methodology; the corpus shows limited direct overlap with related digital twin/autonomy assurance work.
- **Break condition:** The argument structure becomes unmanageably complex or evidence gaps for key property claims (e.g., LLM controllability) cannot be filled.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Fidelity and accuracy can be assured by isolating and evaluating interconnected subsystems with context-specific ground truths.
- **Mechanism:** Four strategies sequentially shift the ground-truth comparator (raw data → curated data → virtual replay → trajectory predictions → agent outcomes). Each layer’s validity is conditioned on the previous, containing error sources.
- **Core assumption:** System fidelity is composable; validating each transformation step against a defined reference isolates failure modes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III defines the four strategies and their conditioning; Strategy 2 uses replay mode to measure virtual environment accuracy versus curated data.
- **Break condition:** Significant emergent errors arise from interactions between layers (e.g., data pipeline bias amplified by TP and agent decisions) that are not detectable by layer-wise validation.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Novel AI components (probabilistic TP, LLM scenario generation) can be assured through tailored evidence mixes (statistical distances, human-in-the-loop) anchored to operational context.
- **Mechanism:** For probabilistic TP, fidelity is measured via distributional similarity to held-out real-world data for specific maneuvers/aircraft. For LLM scenario generation, a hybrid of automated benchmarks and human expert assessment is used.
- **Core assumption:** Standard software V-cycle validation is insufficient; AI/ML components require evidence aligned with their stochastic nature and generative capabilities, informed by domain operational needs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Deep dive P8.1.1 provides statistical distance evidence for descending B738s; P5.1.1 details GPT-5 benchmark pass rates and human-in-the-loop validation.
- **Break condition:** Statistical distance thresholds prove arbitrary or misaligned with operational impact, or human-in-the-loop assessments reveal consistent, critical failures not captured by automated benchmarks.

  - **Why needed here:** The core innovation is a physics-informed ML model that outputs distributions, not point predictions. Validating this requires understanding statistical distances, calibration, and how epistemic uncertainty is captured.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is Wasserstein distance potentially more informative than KS distance for comparing descent profiles, and what does a calibration curve assess?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Pipeline -> Virtual Environment (replay) -> Trajectory Prediction (validation) -> AI Agent Testing. (Scenario Generator feeds into Agent Testing.)
**Critical path:** Data Pipeline → Virtual Environment (replay) → TP (validation) → Agent Testing. Fidelity evidence flows upstream: replay accuracy must be assured before TP validation against replay is meaningful.
**Design tradeoffs:**
1. Probabilistic TP vs. Deterministic BADA: Tradeoff between capturing real-world uncertainty vs. speed/determinism for agent training. Evidence must show probabilistic model's distributions are realistic *and* its mean-mode is more accurate than BADA.
2. LLM Scenario Generation vs. Curated Data: Tradeoff between scalability/novelty of scenarios vs. guaranteed fidelity/plausibility. Evidence must balance benchmark pass rates with human validation of realism.
3. Live Data Assimilation (future): Tradeoff between relevance and validation latency. Current evidence is for historical data; live mode requires online validation mechanisms, which is a major gap.
**Failure signatures:**
- Replay failure: High initial condition error or action intent error, indicating data pipeline or virtual environment breakdown.
- TP failure: Probabilistic TP distributions diverge significantly from held-out data for key aircraft types/maneuvers; or mean-mode errors exceed BADA with statistical significance.
- Scenario generation failure: LLM fails basic benchmarks even after feedback; or human-in-the-loop scores show implausible traffic patterns.
- Agent failure: Consistent loss of separation in Medway/LMS/LUS scenarios, especially on formative vs. summative data splits, indicating Digital Twin may not support transfer.
**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replay Integrity Test: Run pipeline on held-out historical day; compute max along/cross-track/vertical errors against curated data; threshold: 99th percentile error < 0.5 NM / 500 ft (example, needs definition).
2. Probabilistic TP Distribution Validation: For descending B738s, compute KS and Wasserstein distances for CAS, RoD, time-to-bottom; compare to baseline; thresholds: KS < 0.2, Wasserstein within 10% of model's validation variance (example).
3. Agent Safety Generalisation Test: Train RL agent on formative Medway scenarios; test on unseen summative Medway and held-out LMS sector; measure % scenarios with zero loss-of-separation; threshold: ≥95% of human ATCO pass rate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the Digital Twin and AI agent be assured as an integrated system to account for emergent interactions?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly question whether the Digital Twin can be assured separately from each AI agent, noting that emergent behavior from their interaction may not be anticipated by separate assurance.
- **Why unresolved:** The framework currently treats components in isolation, but operational deployment may require joint assurance due to unanticipated interactions between the twin's fidelity and the agent's decisions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic studies of multi-agent interactions within the Digital Twin, development of integrated assurance arguments, and validation through complex scenario testing where agent actions influence twin-state updates.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can online validation be assured for live data assimilation in a probabilistic Digital Twin?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that live data assimilation requires online validation, but real-time learning is incompatible with current certification processes, and the extrapolation methods used would themselves need assurance.
- **Why unresolved:** Online validation acts as a lagging indicator and current regulatory frameworks are not designed for continuously updating models; the reliability of extrapolation methods is an open challenge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Robust methods for real-time performance monitoring, quantified uncertainty in extrapolation, and demonstrations of stable operation under varying live-data conditions over extended periods.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are appropriate, evidence-based thresholds for distributional similarity metrics (e.g., Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Wasserstein) when evaluating probabilistic trajectory predictors?
- **Basis in paper:** The deep dive on Property Claim 8.1.1 admits no fixed thresholds exist; they rely on visual similarity and incremental model improvements, indicating thresholds are highly context-dependent.
- **Why unresolved:** Without objective thresholds, achieving consistent, comparable, and regulator-acceptable evaluations of probabilistic predictor fidelity across aircraft types and maneuvers remains ad hoc.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale empirical studies correlating metric values with downstream task performance (e.g., AI agent success rates) and industry consensus on acceptable bounds for different operational contexts.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can the assurance case be systematically extended to cover safety and other goals (e.g., security, fairness) for operational deployment of AI-enabled Digital Twins?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note the current case focuses on accuracy and fidelity for training, but operational use would require "more stringent thresholds" and additional evidence for safety, and the case could be extended to security and fairness.
- **Why unresolved:** The pathway from a training-focused assurance case to one suitable for regulatory certification and operational safety assurance is undefined, requiring new property claims, evidence types, and validation methods.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of modular add-ons to the TEA framework covering hazard analysis, failure mode testing, security vulnerability assessments, and fairness audits, validated through regulatory engagement and safety envelope testing.

## Limitations
- Acceptance thresholds for key metrics (e.g., KS, Wasserstein, replay errors) are not defined; results are presented without criteria for "sufficient" fidelity.
- Implementation details for probabilistic trajectory prediction (hyperparameters, training splits) and LLM scenario generation (prompts, feedback loop) are omitted, hindering reproducibility.
- The assurance case does not address potential emergent errors from interactions between the four strategies; validation is layered but not holistic.

- **AI component assurance via tailored evidence:** Low
