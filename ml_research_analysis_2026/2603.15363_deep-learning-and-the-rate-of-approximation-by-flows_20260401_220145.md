# Deep learning and the rate of approximation by flows
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15363
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15363
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The minimal time horizon needed to approximate a diffeomorphism by flows from a control family equals a sub-Finsler geodesic distance; for 1D ReLU networks this is the total variation of the log-derivative.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a geometric framework to quantify the minimal depth (time) required for deep residual networks to approximate arbitrary diffeomorphisms. The core idea is to define a metric \(d_F\) on the space of reachable maps as the infimum over path integrals of a local norm derived from the control family of vector fields (e.g., single-layer architectures). This local norm measures the minimal weighted sum of basis vector fields needed to approximate an infinitesimal perturbation. The framework reveals that approximation complexity is inherently nonlinear and compositional, contrasting with linear norm-based theories. In one dimension with ReLU activations, the complexity reduces to a closed-form expression: the total variation of the log-derivative of the target map.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a Banach sub-Finsler manifold where the base space is a manifold of diffeomorphisms (e.g., \(Diff^{W^{1,\infty}}\)) and the anchored bundle uses the control family \(F\) to define a distribution \(D_\psi = F \circ \psi\). The local norm at \(\psi\) for a perturbation \(u\) is defined as \(\|u\|_\psi = \inf\{ s > 0 : u \in CH_s(F) \circ \psi \}\), where \(CH_s(F)\) is the \(s\)-scaled convex hull of \(F\). A path \(\gamma(t)\) is horizontal if \(\dot{\gamma}(t) \in D_{\gamma(t)}\). The distance \(d_F(\psi_1, \psi_2)\) is the infimum of \(\int_0^1 \|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{\gamma(t)} dt\) over horizontal curves connecting \(\psi_1\) and \(\psi_2\). For a target \(\psi\), the complexity \(C_F(\psi) = d_F(\psi, Id)\). In the 1D ReLU case, the local norm and geodesics can be computed explicitly via the log-derivative, yielding \(C_F(\psi) = \|\ln \psi'\|_{TV}\).

## Key Results
- The distance \(d_F\) coincides with the geodesic distance in the sub-Finsler manifold induced by \(F\).
- For width-2 ReLU networks in 1D, \(C_F(\psi) = \|\ln \psi'\|_{TV([0,1])}\), and the reachable set consists of \(BV^2\) diffeomorphisms with positive derivatives.
- For layer-normalized networks on the circle, the complexity is bounded by the smoothness of the log-derivative ratio between target and identity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works because it directly measures the time required to generate a transformation via the given control system, rather than approximating in a linear norm. The local norm captures the expressive power of a single layer’s convex combination to produce an infinitesimal change. The sub-Finsler geometry then accumulates these local costs along a curve, ensuring the path respects the dynamics (horizontal condition). This reveals that complexity depends on the multiplicative composition of derivatives (log-derivatives) rather than additive errors, explaining why depth can achieve nonlinear transformations efficiently.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sub-Finsler geometry:** A manifold with a distribution (subbundle) and a norm on each fiber; needed to define the horizontal paths and local metric.
- **Reachable diffeomorphisms:** Set of maps generated by flows from \(F\); the space where \(d_F\) lives.
- **Control family \(F\):** The set of vector fields corresponding to a single network layer; defines the atomic moves.
- **Convex hull scaling \(CH_s(F)\):** Allows mixing of basis vector fields; scaling \(s\) bounds the total “weight” used.
- **Horizontal curve:** A curve whose derivative lies in the distribution; ensures the path is realizable by the flow.
- **Total variation (TV):** A measure of oscillation; in 1D it exactly captures the cost of approximating log-derivative changes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Control family \(F\) (e.g., shallow ReLU nets) -> Flows \(\phi_t^v\) -> Reachable set \(T\) -> Metric \(d_F\) via local norm -> Complexity \(C_F(\psi)\).

**Critical path:**  
1. Define \(F\) with uniform boundedness/Lipschitz conditions.  
2. Construct Banach manifold of diffeomorphisms.  
3. Define anchored bundle and distribution \(D_\psi = F \circ \psi\).  
4. Specify local norm via variational principle.  
5. Show geodesic distance equals \(d_F\).  
6. For specific \(F\), solve variational problem for \(\|\cdot\|_\psi\) and geodesics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Infinite-dimensional optimization vs. closed-form: 1D ReLU yields explicit formula; higher dimensions require discretization.
- Generality vs. tractability: The framework applies broadly, but exact results are rare; estimates rely on smoothness.
- Theoretical assumptions (uniform Lipschitz) vs. practical nets: real networks may violate bounds, affecting norm finiteness.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Non-horizontal path: \(\dot{\gamma}(t) \notin D_{\gamma(t)}\) leads to infinite local norm and overestimation.
- Ill-defined local norm: If \(F\) not uniformly bounded, some perturbations may have infinite cost.
- Discretization error: In numerical approximation, coarse sampling may miss singularities in \(BV\) functions.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Implement 1D ReLU case: generate random \(\psi \in T\), compute \(C_F(\psi)\) via TV formula and compare with numerical integration of explicit geodesic.
2. For layer-normalization on \(S^1\), discretize \(F\) (e.g., Fourier modes), solve variational problem for local norm numerically, and approximate \(d_F\) via path optimization; verify against smoothness lower bound.
3. Test robustness: construct \(F\) with large weight magnitudes (violating uniform Lipschitz), compute local norm for a smooth perturbation; expect infinite values if compatibility fails.

## Limitations
- Exact characterization of local norm and geodesics is limited to 1D ReLU; higher-dimensional cases remain open and require numerical approximation.
- Theoretical assumptions (uniform boundedness in \(W^{1,\infty}\), Banach manifold setting) may not hold for all practical deep network architectures.
- Numerical computation of \(d_F\) in higher dimensions involves infinite-dimensional optimization and discretization choices, which are computationally challenging and introduce sampling errors.

## Confidence
- **Core geometric theorem** (\(d_F\) equals sub-Finsler geodesic distance): High
- **1D ReLU closed-form** (\(C_F(\psi) = \|\ln \psi'\|_{TV}\)): High
- **Higher-dimensional estimates** (e.g., layer-norm circle): Medium
- **Nonlinear/compositional complexity claim**: Medium
