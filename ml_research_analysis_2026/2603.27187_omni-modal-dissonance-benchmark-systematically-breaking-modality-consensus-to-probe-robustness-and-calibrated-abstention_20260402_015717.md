# Omni-Modal Dissonance Benchmark: Systematically Breaking Modality Consensus to Probe Robustness and Calibrated Abstention
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27187
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27187
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Omni‑modal models exhibit severe miscalibration in abstention and asymmetric reliance on text, with audio often providing negative contribution; OMD‑Bench isolates these failures via controlled cross‑modal corruption.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OMD‑Bench, a diagnostic benchmark designed to probe modality reliance, robustness to cross‑modal conflict, and calibrated abstention in omni‑modal models. Anchored on 27 objects/events perceivable via video, audio, and text, it generates 4,080 instances by systematically replacing content in subsets of modalities (8 corruption conditions). Evaluating ten state‑of‑the‑art models reveals that text corruption causes the largest accuracy drop, models severely over‑ or under‑abstain relative to human judgments, and predictive confidence remains high even when accuracy collapses, with chain‑of‑thought prompting exacerbating overconfidence.

## Method Summary
The benchmark uses 27 anchors (objects/events) with ~10 knowledge‑grounded questions each, forming 255 seed questions. Each question has four factual distractors and an “I cannot answer” option. For every seed, eight variants are created by independently deciding for each of the three modalities whether to keep the true anchor content or replace it with a different anchor’s content. Data is collected in *real* (Pexels videos, Freesound/Pixabay audio, human text) and *synthetic* (Sora/Grok/Gemini videos, AudioX audio, same text) splits. Human annotators provide ground‑truth answers or abstention labels. Ten models are evaluated under zero‑shot and chain‑of‑thought prompting; GPT‑4o‑mini uses a two‑stage audio transcription. Primary metrics include accuracy, Abstention Calibration Error (ACE), confidence calibration (ECE, RC‑AUC), and Shapley values for modality contribution.

## Key Results
- Asymmetric text reliance: Corrupting text causes the largest mean accuracy drop (~19.5 percentage points); audio often yields negative Shapley values.
- Severe abstention miscalibration: Models over‑abstain when two modalities are corrupted (though one clean channel often suffices) and under‑abstain when all three are corrupted (human abstention ~98%, best model ≤82%).
- Systematic overconfidence: Predictive confidence stays above 60% even as accuracy falls to ~52% under full corruption; chain‑of‑thought amplifies overconfidence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism is controlled induction of cross‑modal conflict. By starting from fully congruent tri‑modal inputs and then replacing content in specific modalities with mismatched anchors, the benchmark isolates the information carried by each channel. This design reveals whether a model truly integrates signals or defaults to a dominant modality (typically text). The inclusion of human‑annotated abstention labels provides a ground truth for when no single modality offers a reliable answer, enabling measurement of calibrated uncertainty. The 2³ corruption space allows computing Shapley values and normalized reliance, quantifying each modality’s marginal contribution to accuracy across all subset conditions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross‑modal conflict**: Deliberate mismatch between modalities to test integration robustness; needed to expose reliance on any single channel.
  - *Quick check*: Does model performance drop more when text is corrupted versus video/audio?
- **Calibrated abstention**: A model should abstain precisely when it cannot answer correctly; measured via ACE against human abstention rates.
  - *Quick check*: Compare model abstention rate to human rate per corruption level; gaps indicate miscalibration.
- **Shapley value attribution**: Cooperative game theory tool to fairly allocate each modality’s contribution to model performance across all corruption subsets.
  - *Quick check*: Compute average marginal contribution of each modality; negative values indicate harm.
- **Confidence calibration**: Alignment between predictive confidence and actual accuracy; assessed via ECE and RC‑AUC.
  - *Quick check*: Bin predictions by confidence and compute accuracy difference; high discrepancy = poor calibration.
- **Chain‑of‑thought prompting**: Elicits intermediate reasoning but can amplify overconfidence by generating plausible yet incorrect rationales.
  - *Quick check*: Compare calibration metrics between zero‑shot and CoT conditions; worsening indicates amplification.

## Architecture Onboarding
The benchmark is not a model but an evaluation suite; its "architecture" is the data generation and assessment pipeline.
- **Component map**: Anchors → Question Bank → Corruption Generator → Dataset (Real/Synthetic) → Model Inference → Metrics Computation.
- **Critical path**: Define anchors → write questions → apply 8 corruption conditions → run models → compute accuracy/Shapley/ACE → interpret results.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Real vs. synthetic data balances ecological validity vs. perfect congruence; 2³ corruption simplifies analysis but assumes independent modality replacement; human abstention labels are costly but necessary for calibration ground truth.
- **Failure signatures**: (1) Large accuracy drop for C001 (text corrupted) indicates text over‑reliance; (2) Abstention rate high at k=2 (two corruptions) but low at k=3 shows inability to recognize when all channels are unreliable; (3) Confidence >60% at k=3 with accuracy ~50% signals severe overconfidence.
- **First experiments**: 1) Run one model (e.g., GPT‑4o‑mini) on the real split under zero‑shot; verify text corruption causes biggest drop. 2) Compute ACE; expect >15% for most models. 3) Check mean confidence vs. corruption level; expect no downward trend as corruption increases.

## Limitations
- Anchor list and 255 seed questions are unreleased, preventing full dataset reconstruction.
- Human annotation protocol (evidence‑based criteria, inter‑annotator agreement) is not detailed.
- Evaluation specifics (prompt templates, generation parameters, frame/audio sampling) are missing.
- Confidence extraction methods (restricted softmax vs. token probability) vary across models but are not standardized, undermining calibration comparison.
- Synthetic data generation parameters for Sora/Grok/Gemini and AudioX are undisclosed, limiting replicability.

## Confidence
- Asymmetric text reliance: **Medium** (depends on unreleased data and Shapley calculations).
- Severe abstention miscalibration: **Medium** (ground truth abstention unverifiable without full annotation details).
- Systematic overconfidence & CoT amplification: **Medium** (confidence extraction not fully described).

## Next Checks
1. Obtain the full anchor list, question bank, and annotation protocol from the authors; if infeasible, create a proxy set of 27 common anchors and validate distributional similarity.
2. Replicate the end‑to‑end evaluation for 2–3 models using recovered prompts; verify the three key patterns: largest accuracy drop on text corruption, over‑abstention at k=2, under‑abstention at k=3, and confidence ≥60% under full corruption.
3. Conduct an independent human annotation on a random sample (~100 instances); compute abstention agreement and compare abstention rates to reported human baselines, assessing ACE robustness.
