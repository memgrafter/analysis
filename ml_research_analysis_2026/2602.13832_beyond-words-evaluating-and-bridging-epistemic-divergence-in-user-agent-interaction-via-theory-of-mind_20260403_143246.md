# Beyond Words: Evaluating and Bridging Epistemic Divergence in User-Agent Interaction via Theory of Mind

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13832
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13832
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** RL training with ToM tokens on Qwen3-8B improves multi-turn collaboration accuracy by ~2% absolute on average across SynchToM domains.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) frequently fail to detect mismatches between user beliefs and reality (epistemic divergence) during multi-turn interactions, leading to ineffective assistance. This work formalizes Theory of Mind (ToM) as a mechanism for detecting and resolving such divergence. It introduces SynchToM, a benchmark of 390 multi-turn scenarios across four domains. Models are trained via reinforcement learning on interaction trajectories using special ToM tokens to emphasize belief alignment. Results show leading models struggle with early belief inference, but injecting ground-truth belief profiles improves outcomes, and RL-trained models achieve consistent downstream gains in solution accuracy.

## Method Summary
The authors first formalize ToM in user-agent interaction as the ability to infer latent user beliefs that may diverge from environmental facts and to adapt responses to resolve this divergence. They construct SynchToM, a diagnostic benchmark containing up to 10-turn dialogues spanning software engineering, preference inference, education, and cultural contexts. Each scenario requires the agent to identify a hidden user belief and act accordingly. For training, they augment reinforcement learning (RL) on trajectory data with special "ToM tokens" that highlight belief-relevant segments, encouraging the model to focus on profile alignment and resolution. Evaluation measures both belief inference accuracy and downstream task success (solution correctness).

## Key Results
- Leading LLMs (11 evaluated) show systematic difficulty in inferring latent user beliefs, particularly within the first few turns of an interaction.
- Artificially injecting the ground-truth belief profile into the model's context significantly improves resolution accuracy, demonstrating a causal link between explicit belief modeling and task success (e.g., +2.4% absolute in solution accuracy).
- RL training with ToM tokens on Qwen3-8B yields consistent downstream gains: 10-turn Preference task solution accuracy rises from 65.6% to 68.0%, and software engineering (SWE) from 48.1% to 50.5%, while maintaining stable belief inference performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The improvement mechanism likely stems from the model's enhanced ability to track and reason about user beliefs. The ToM tokens act as explicit signals that highlight belief-relevant segments of the dialogue, guiding the model's attention to critical information for inferring latent user states. The reinforcement learning objective then reinforces policies that not only infer beliefs accurately but also generate responses that effectively resolve epistemic divergence, as measured by downstream task success. The observation that injecting ground-truth belief profiles boosts performance further supports that explicit belief modeling is a key factor.

## Foundational Learning
The learning foundation is reinforcement learning on interaction trajectories, where the agent's policy is optimized to maximize a reward based on successful resolution of epistemic divergence (e.g., solution correctness). The augmentation with ToM tokens modifies the input representation to emphasize belief-alignment, providing a structured inductive bias. This approach assumes that functional Theory of Mind can emerge from optimizing for collaborative outcomes in multi-turn dialogues, without explicit supervision on belief states.

## Architecture Onboarding
Based on the described method, the architecture likely includes:

**Component Map**  
User Query -> Context Encoder (with ToM tokens) -> Policy Network (RL-trained) -> Action (Response) -> Environment Feedback -> Belief State Update

**Critical Path**  
1. Trajectory data generation (human or synthetic interactions).  
2. Integration of ToM tokens into dialogue history.  
3. Reinforcement learning fine-tuning with reward shaped by resolution success.  
4. Evaluation on held-out SynchToM scenarios (inference + solution).

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Token overhead vs. interpretability:** Adding explicit ToM tokens increases context length but forces model attention on belief-related content.  
- **RL stability vs. sample efficiency:** Trajectory-based RL may be unstable with sparse rewards but leverages historical interaction patterns.  
- **Generalization vs. domain specificity:** Training on SynchToM's four domains may improve in-domain ToM but risks overfitting to scenario structure.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Early-turn belief inference accuracy near random baseline.  
- Degradation when ToM tokens are omitted during inference.  
- Plateauing solution accuracy despite further RL steps, indicating potential reward mis-specification.

**First Three Experiments**  
1. Ablate ToM tokens: Train with/without them to measure their isolated impact on downstream resolution.  
2. Cross-domain transfer: Apply RL-trained model to a held-out domain (e.g., medical advice) to test generalization.  
3. Human belief correlation: Compare model-inferred beliefs against human annotator judgments on SynchToM samples.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark scope:** SynchToM covers only four domains (software engineering, preferences, education, culture); generalizability to other contexts (e.g., healthcare, legal) is unknown.
- **Effect size:** Downstream gains are modest (~2% absolute); real-world impact on user-agent collaboration remains uncertain.
- **Ground-truth injection:** Artificially providing belief profiles may not reflect scenarios where latent beliefs are genuinely ambiguous or contradictory.
