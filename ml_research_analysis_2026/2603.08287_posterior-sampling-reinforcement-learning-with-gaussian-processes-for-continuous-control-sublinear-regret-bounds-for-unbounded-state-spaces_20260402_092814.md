# Posterior Sampling Reinforcement Learning with Gaussian Processes for Continuous Control: Sublinear Regret Bounds for Unbounded State Spaces
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08287
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08287
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Bayesian regret \(\widetilde{O}(H^{3/2} \sqrt{\gamma_T})\) for Gaussian process posterior sampling RL (GP-PSRL) in continuous control with unbounded state spaces, achieving tight dependence on maximum information gain \(\gamma_T\).

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the first theoretical guarantees for posterior sampling reinforcement learning (PSRL) with Gaussian process (GP) priors in continuous state spaces that are unbounded. Previous analyses either assumed compact state spaces or incurred suboptimal dependence on the maximum information gain \(\gamma_T\). The core technical contributions are: (1) a recursive application of the Borell‑Tsirelson‑Ibragimov‑Sudakov (BTIS) inequality to prove that all states visited under bounded actions lie within a ball of near‑constant radius with high probability, and (2) the use of chaining to bound the Gaussian process posterior error tightly in terms of \(\gamma_T\). The resulting Bayesian regret bound is \(\widetilde{O}(H^{3/2} \sqrt{\gamma_T})\), holding for bounded, Hölder continuous kernels. Empirical results on a 2D navigation task confirm the predicted \(\sqrt{\gamma_T}\) scaling across different kernels.

## Method Summary
The algorithm, GP-PSRL, proceeds in episodes: at the start of each episode \(n\), it samples a dynamics function \(f^{(n)}\) from the current GP posterior over the unknown transition dynamics, then computes an optimal policy \(\pi_n\) for the resulting sampled MDP using an optimal control oracle, executes \(\pi_n\) for \(H\) steps, and updates the GP posterior with observed transitions. The theoretical analysis hinges on two main steps: (i) proving that the visited states remain within a bounded region (via recursive BTIS) under assumptions of bounded actions and a bounded Hölder continuous kernel, and (ii) bounding cumulative model estimation errors using chaining, which directly links the expected supremum of the GP posterior error to the maximum information gain \(\gamma_T\). An \(\epsilon\)-cover discretization technique relaxes smoothness requirements to Hölder continuity (no high-order derivatives needed). For reproduction, the environment is a 2D navigation task with known reward and unknown deterministic dynamics plus Gaussian noise; the GP is approximated via random Fourier features; the oracle uses discretized value iteration on a fixed grid.

## Key Results
- Sublinear Bayesian regret \(\widetilde{O}(H^{3/2} \sqrt{\gamma_T})\) for continuous MDPs with unbounded state spaces, under bounded actions and a bounded Hölder continuous kernel.
- Tight dependence on the maximum information gain \(\gamma_T\) via a chaining-based argument, improving over prior work with \(\gamma_T^2\) or \(\gamma_T \log T\) factors.
- Accommodation of general Gaussian process priors: only boundedness and Hölder continuity of the kernel are required; no assumptions about uniformly bounded higher‑order derivatives.
- Empirical validation on a 2D navigation task shows regret scales with \(\sqrt{\gamma_T}\) across kernels (squared exponential vs. Matérn), confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: State Bounding via Recursive BTIS
- **Claim:** With high probability, all states visited by GP-PSRL lie within a ball of radius growing only logarithmically with total steps \(T\), despite the true state space being unbounded.
- **Mechanism:** A recursive application of the Borell‑Tsirelson‑Ibragimov‑Sudakov (BTIS) inequality establishes that the norm of each state \(\|s_{n,h}\|_2\) is sub‑Gaussian conditioned on the previous state being bounded. This creates a chain of tail bounds that prevents explosive growth, yielding a near‑constant radius bound.
- **Core assumptions:** Bounded actions (Assumption 3.1), bounded kernel variance (Assumption 3.3), and kernel Hölder continuity (Assumption 3.4) to control suprema of the Gaussian process dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the BTIS approach; Lemma 4.7 gives an explicit radius \(R\) and proves \(\Pr(\sup_{n,h} \|s_{n,h}\|_2 > R) \leq 2/T\); contrasts with prior work that assumes compact state spaces.
- **Break condition:** If the kernel is not Hölder continuous (e.g., linear kernel with unbounded variance), the BTIS tail bound may fail uniformly, and the radius could grow polynomially with \(H\) or \(T\).

### Mechanism 2: Regret via Chaining Tightly Linked to \(\gamma_T\)
- **Claim:** The Bayesian regret scales as \(\widetilde{O}(H^{3/2} \sqrt{\gamma_T})\), achieving tight dependence on the maximum information gain.
- **Mechanism:** Regret is decomposed into value estimation errors bounded by model estimation errors. Instead of constructing confidence sets (which yields sub‑optimal \(\gamma_T\) dependence), the paper uses chaining to directly bound the expected supremum \(\mathbb{E}[\sup_x \|f^{(n)}(x)-f^\star(x)\|_2]\) under the Gaussian process posterior, tightly linking the bound to \(\gamma_T\) via the entropy integral.
- **Core assumptions:** Bounded, Hölder continuous kernel; visited states lie in a bounded ball (from Mechanism 1); separability of the GP.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions chaining; Lemma 4.10 bounds estimation error by \(O(\sqrt{(d_s+d_a)H \gamma_{N(\sigma^2,\tilde{R})} T \log T})\); contrasts with confidence‑set methods that incur worse \(\gamma_T\) factors.
- **Break condition:** If the kernel does not admit finite \(\gamma_T\) (e.g., linear kernel on unbounded domain), \(\gamma_T\) may grow linearly with \(T\), degrading the bound. Also, if the true dynamics are smoother than Hölder, the bound may be loose.

### Mechanism 3: Weak Smoothness via \(\epsilon\)-Cover Discretization
- **Claim:** The analysis accommodates GP priors with only Hölder continuous kernels, without requiring high‑order derivatives.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of relying on uniform bounds on derivatives of sample paths, the discretization error is controlled via a single‑step discretization using an \(\epsilon\)-cover. The resulting “modified kernel” \(\tilde{c}(x,y)\) is piecewise Hölder, allowing chaining to bound the discretization error tightly. Choosing \(\epsilon \propto T^{-2/\alpha}\) makes this error sub‑linear.
- **Core assumptions:** Kernel bounded and Hölder continuous with exponent \(\alpha \in (0,1]\); covering radius \(\epsilon\) satisfies \(\epsilon \leq (7/(e\sqrt{8}))^{\alpha/2} \tilde{R}\) (Appendix E.2).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states only boundedness and Hölder continuity are required; Lemma 4.10 proof explicitly sets \(\epsilon = 1/(L^{1/\alpha} T^{2/\alpha})\); contrasts with prior work assuming four‑times differentiable kernels.
- **Break condition:** For non‑piecewise Hölder kernels (e.g., non‑stationary) or very small \(\alpha\), the discretization error could dominate, and constants may become large.

## Foundational Learning
- **Gaussian Processes**
  - *Why needed:* The dynamics are modeled as GPs; understanding conditioning, covariance kernels, and posterior variance is essential for modeling uncertainty and updating beliefs.
  - *Quick check:* Given a GP prior \(f \sim \mathcal{GP}(0, c)\) and data \((x_i, y_i)\), how does the posterior variance \(\sigma_n^2(x)\) at a new point \(x\) depend on the kernel and the design points \(x_i\)?

- **Markov Decision Processes and Bellman Operators**
  - *Why needed:* The problem is a finite‑horizon MDP; the regret analysis uses the Bellman equation and decomposes value errors into Bellman errors.
  - *Quick check:* For a fixed policy \(\pi\), how does the value function \(V_\pi^M\) relate to the Bellman operator \(T_\pi^M\)?

- **Information Gain and Chaining**
  - *Why needed:* \(\gamma_T\) (maximum information gain) quantifies the effective dimension of the RKHS and appears in the regret bound; chaining is the probabilistic tool for bounding GP suprema in terms of \(\gamma_T\).
  - *Quick check:* Intuitively, why does collecting data at locations that maximally reduce posterior uncertainty lead to a smaller \(\gamma_T\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  GP prior/posterior -> Posterior sampling -> Optimal control oracle -> Interaction loop (execute policy, observe transitions, update posterior)

- **Critical path:**  
  Initialize GP prior -> For each episode: sample dynamics \(f^{(n)}\) from posterior -> compute optimal policy \(\pi_n\) via oracle -> execute \(\pi_n\) for \(H\) steps -> update GP posterior with new data -> repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Prior choice:* Smooth kernels (e.g., squared exponential) yield smaller \(\gamma_T\) and better sample efficiency but may oversmooth dynamics; Matérn kernels offer a trade‑off between flexibility and information gain.
  - *Policy oracle:* Exact optimal control for continuous MDPs is generally intractable; approximations (e.g., trajectory optimization, random feature sparse GPs) introduce bias that must be controlled.
  - *Discretization:* The analysis uses \(\epsilon\)-covers to avoid derivative assumptions; smaller \(\epsilon\) reduces discretization error but increases covering number and computational cost.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - *State explosion:* If the BTIS bounding fails (e.g., due to unbounded actions or poor prior), visited states may grow exponentially, causing \(\gamma_T\) to scale linearly with \(T\) and regret to become linear.
  - *Prior misspecification:* If the true dynamics lie outside the GP prior’s support (e.g., discontinuities), posterior sampling may not concentrate, leading to persistent high estimation errors.
  - *Planning suboptimality:* The analysis assumes the policy \(\pi_n\) is optimal for \(M^{(n)}\); sub‑optimal oracles increase regret beyond the bound.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *2D navigation with varying kernels:* Implement the reward and dynamics setup from Section 5/Appendix G. Compare cumulative regret for squared exponential vs. Matérn kernels; verify that smoother kernels (lower \(\gamma_T\)) yield lower regret, confirming the \(\sqrt{\gamma_T}\) scaling.
  2. *Horizon scaling:* Run GP‑PSRL across different horizons \(H\) (e.g., 20, 40, 80, 160) and check that regret scales as \(H^{3/2}\); deviations may indicate looseness in the analysis or practical planner limitations.
  3. *State boundary test:* Intentionally loosen the BTIS radius by using a prior with larger kernel variance or adding process noise. Monitor the maximum state norm visited; it should grow beyond the theoretical bound \(R\), confirming the necessity of the boundedness assumptions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can the proof technique of Lemma 4.10 be directly adapted to obtain Bayesian regret bounds for Gaussian process bandits under Hölder continuous kernels, thus removing the need for four‑times differentiable kernels? The adaptation to the bandit setting has not been formally proven.
2. Can the theoretical guarantees be extended to kernels that are only locally bounded (e.g., linear kernel) without incurring exponential dependence on \(H\)? If necessary, does restricting the algorithm to play stabilizing policies yield a comparable bound to Lemma 4.7?
3. The empirical evaluation indicates that Bayesian regret scales approximately linearly with \(H\), whereas the proven bound scales as \(H^{3/2}\). Can the analysis be sharpened to remove the extra \(H^{1/2}\) factor and achieve a tighter dependence?
4. Is the dependence on the maximum information gain \(\gamma\) in the regret bound optimal? Are there problem instances where \(\gamma\) must grow at least as fast as in the bound, and could the \(\sqrt{\gamma T}\) scaling be improved to linear in \(\gamma\)? Lower bounds for GP‑PSRL in continuous, unbounded state spaces are not established.

## Limitations
- **Parameter gaps:** Exact reward function details (goal location, obstacle parameters), action space radius \(R_a\), integration step \(\Delta\), and observation noise \(\sigma\) are unspecified, forcing arbitrary choices for faithful reproduction.
- **Scalability:** Empirical validation is limited to a 2D navigation task; performance on high‑dimensional continuous control benchmarks (e.g., MuJoCo) remains unknown.
- **Assumption sensitivity:** Theoretical guarantees rely on bounded actions, bounded kernel variance, and Hölder continuity. Violations (e.g., unbounded actions, discontinuous dynamics) may break the state‑bounding mechanism and degrade regret to linear.

## Confidence
- **State bounding (Mechanism 1):** High – The BTIS‑based proof is rigorous under clear assumptions; break condition (non‑Hölder kernels) is well‑identified.
- **Regret scaling (Mechanism 2):** Medium – Chaining yields tight dependence on \(\gamma_T\), but constants and potential looseness in the entropy integral warrant cautious interpretation.
- **GP prior accommodation (Mechanism 3):** High – The \(\epsilon\)-cover discretization is explicit and avoids derivative assumptions; only piecewise Hölder continuity is needed.

## Next Checks
1. **State radius verification:** Log maximum \(\|s_{n,h}\|_2\) across seeds for varying kernels and \(T\); confirm logarithmic growth as per Lemma 4.7.
2. **\(\gamma_T\) sensitivity:** Systematically vary kernel smoothness (e.g., Matérn \(\nu\)) and measure empirical regret; check \(\sqrt{\gamma_T}\) scaling and note deviations.
3. **Assumption robustness:** Introduce controlled violations (e.g., unbounded external forces, linear kernel with unbounded variance) to test break conditions and quantify effects on state explosion and regret.
