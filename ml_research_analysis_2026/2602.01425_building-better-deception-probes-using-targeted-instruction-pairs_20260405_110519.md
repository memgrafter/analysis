# Building Better Deception Probes Using Targeted Instruction Pairs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.01425
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01425
- **Reference Count:** 22
- **Primary Result:** Taxonomy-based prompts improve mean AUC by 0.108 (20.5% relative) across 10 deception datasets, with all datasets showing at least one probe beating the baseline.

## Executive Summary
Linear probes for detecting AI deception suffer from spurious correlations, often firing on non-deceptive content like fiction or role-play. This paper identifies that system prompt choice explains 70.6% of performance variance across 2000 probes. To address this, the authors generate targeted instruction pairs based on a human-interpretable deception taxonomy and train linear probes on Gemma-2-9B-IT (layer 20). Using token truncation to isolate deceptive intent, their method achieves a mean AUC improvement of 0.108 across ten diverse deception datasets. Clustering analysis reveals five distinct probe groups, suggesting deceptive mechanisms are heterogeneous. The conclusion is that probes detect intent, not content, necessitating specialized probes for specific threat models rather than a universal detector.

## Method Summary
The core method is contrastive instruction-pair training. For each deception type in a human-defined taxonomy, the authors create a pair of system prompts: one encouraging honest responses and one encouraging deception. Using a fixed set of factual statements (Azaria-Mitchell dataset), they force the model (Gemma-2-9B-IT) to generate responses off-policy according to each prompt. Residual stream activations are extracted at layer 20 for response tokens, excluding the final five tokens to avoid output-style cues. These activation vectors are pooled (mean) and standardized per dataset. A logistic regression probe (L2 regularization λ=1) is then trained to classify responses from the "honest" vs. "dishonest" prompts. For evaluation, the model generates on-policy responses (temperature=1) to prompts from ten deception datasets. Probe scores are computed similarly, and performance is measured by AUC.

## Key Results
- Mean AUC improvement of 0.108 (20.5% relative gain) over the default baseline prompt pair across 10 evaluation datasets.
- Standout improvements: MASK-known-facts (0.374 → 0.697, +86.3%) and sandbagging (+26.8%).
- All 10 datasets have at least one taxonomy-based probe that beats the baseline.
- ANOVA of 2000 probes shows system prompt choice accounts for 70.6% of performance variance; layer choice explains only 2.7%.
- Probe clustering yields five distinct groups, indicating varied deception mechanisms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The improvement stems from targeting *deceptive intent* rather than *deceptive content*. Spurious correlations in baseline probes often capture superficial features of deceptive text (e.g., certain words, styles). By designing instruction pairs that vary only in the *intention* to deceive while holding the factual content constant, the probes learn representations tied to the model's internal decision to lie. The token truncation (removing final 5 tokens) further reduces stylistic artifacts from the output formulation. System prompt dominance (70.6% variance) indicates that how deception is elicited in training fundamentally shapes what the probe detects, more so than architectural details like layer depth.

## Foundational Learning
**Linear Probes**  
*Why needed:* Simple, interpretable classifiers on model activations to test for presence of a concept (here, deception).  
*Quick check:* Train logistic regression on frozen activations; check if weights align with known semantic directions (e.g., truthfulness).

**Contrastive Learning**  
*Why needed:* Creates a discriminative signal by contrasting two subtly different conditions (honest vs. dishonest prompts) with identical content.  
*Quick check:* Verify that only the system prompt differs between positive and negative training examples; all input facts are identical.

**Activation Extraction & Truncation**  
*Why needed:* Activations capture internal model state; truncation removes final-token biases (e.g., conclusion phrasing) to isolate *intent* signals.  
*Quick check:* Compare probe performance with and without last-5-token exclusion; improvement indicates output-style leakage.

**Regularized Logistic Regression**  
*Why needed:* Prevents overfitting to noise in high-dimensional activation spaces; L2 penalty encourages simpler, more generalizable decision boundaries.  
*Quick check:* Sweep λ values; performance should peak at moderate regularization (paper uses λ=1).

**Standardization per Dataset**  
*Why needed:* Activation distributions can shift across different evaluation datasets; standardization ensures probe decisions are based on relative activation patterns, not absolute magnitudes.  
*Quick check:* Apply standardization separately on training vs. evaluation splits; do not mix statistics.

**Variance Analysis (ANOVA)**  
*Why needed:* Quantifies which experimental factors (prompt, layer, etc.) most influence probe performance, guiding efficient design choices.  
*Quick check:* Run multiple probes varying one factor at a time; compute proportion of variance explained (like paper's 70.6% for prompts).

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**  
Model (Gemma-2-9B-IT) -> Activation Extraction (layer 20, truncate last 5 tokens) -> Activation Pooling & Standardization -> Linear Probe (Logistic Regression) -> Score -> Threshold -> Deception Detection

**Critical Path**  
1. Define taxonomy-based instruction pairs (honest/dishonest system prompts).  
2. Generate forced off-policy responses for a fixed fact set using each prompt.  
3. Extract and truncate residual stream activations at layer 20.  
4. Pool (mean) activations over kept tokens, standardize per dataset.  
5. Train logistic regression probe to classify prompt condition.  
6. For new input, generate on-policy response, extract/truncate/pool, apply probe.  
7. Threshold probe score to predict deceptive intent.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Truncation depth*: Fewer tokens reduce output-style leakage but may discard later-intent cues. Paper uses last 5 tokens.  
- *Linearity*: Logistic regression is interpretable and efficient but may miss nonlinear deception patterns.  
- *Single layer*: Focusing on layer 20 simplifies but may ignore earlier/later semantic processing.  
- *Fixed facts*: Controls content variation but limits diversity; may not generalize to novel topics.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Probe fires on control/non-deceptive data (e.g., Alpaca, fiction) → spurious correlation.  
- Performance drops drastically on specific datasets (e.g., MASK-known-facts baseline AUC≈0.37) → prompt mismatch or truncation error.  
- High variance across random seeds or layers → unstable training or weak signal.  
- Clustering shows one probe dominates all datasets → overfitting to superficial cues, not specialized detection.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Activation extraction sanity check**: For one prompt pair, extract activations for a few examples; verify that mean activation vectors differ significantly between conditions (t-test).  
2. **Baseline replication**: Train a probe using the Goldowsky-Dill baseline prompt pair on Azaria-Mitchell facts; evaluate on one dataset (e.g., MASK-known-facts); expect AUC ~0.4.  
3. **Targeted probe test**: Train a probe using one taxonomy-based pair (e.g., `overt_lie`); evaluate on same dataset; expect substantial AUC gain. Confirm token truncation is applied identically in train and eval.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Do taxonomy-based prompts succeed due to deception-type specificity or unrelated prompt features?**  
   *Basis:* Section 8 notes the need to validate that improvements are not due to confounds like prompt length or phrasing.  
   *Why unresolved:* Semantic content was varied but lexical/structural features were not controlled.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Systematically vary prompt phrasing while holding deception type constant; ablate prompt components.

2. **Does system prompt dominance generalize across models, scales, and training procedures?**  
   *Basis:* Limited to Gemma 2 9B IT and primarily layer 20; other architectures/sizes may differ.  
   *Why unresolved:* No cross-model ANOVA replication.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Replicate 2000+ probe ANOVA across multiple model families (Llama, GPT, Claude) and scales (1B–70B).

3. **Are probes equally effective in non-English languages?**  
   *Basis:* All experiments in English; deception pragmatics may vary linguistically/culturally.  
   *Why unresolved:* No multilingual evaluation.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Translate prompts and datasets into diverse languages (e.g., Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic); compare AUC improvements and clustering.

4. **Do probes generalize to multi-step agentic settings?**  
   *Basis:* Evaluation focused on conversational QA; agentic deception may involve temporally extended strategies.  
   *Why unresolved:* Benchmarks are static single-turn.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Build agentic deception benchmarks (tool use, planning); apply probes to intermediate steps; compare to conversational performance.

## Limitations
- Single-model evaluation (Gemma-2-9B-IT) limits generalizability.
- Taxonomy and prompts may omit deception types and introduce lexical biases.
- Excluding final tokens risks losing intent cues present later in generation.
- Linear probes may be too simplistic for nonlinear deception patterns.
- Training (forced off-policy) / evaluation (on-policy) distribution mismatch could inflate results.
- Clustering is descriptive; lacks mechanistic explanation of why groups form.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the ANOVA variance decomposition on at least two other model families (e.g., Llama-3-8B, GPT-2-medium) to test generalizability of system prompt dominance.
2. Systematically vary truncation length (0–20 tokens) and measure performance sensitivity; identify optimal cutoff and assess robustness to this design choice.
3. Conduct a human evaluation: collect model responses rated by annotators for deceptive intent; correlate probe scores with human judgments to validate that probes align with perceived intent.
