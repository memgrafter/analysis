# Latent Algorithmic Structure Precedes Grokking: A Mechanistic Study of ReLU MLPs on Modular Arithmetic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23784  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23784  
- **Reference count:** 2  
- **Primary result:** ReLU MLPs on modular addition develop near-binary square wave input weights during memorization, with output weights obeying a phase-sum relation; this latent structure is sharpened—not discovered—during grokking.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the internal representations of one‑hidden‑layer ReLU MLPs trained on modular addition (mod 97). It finds that during the Memorization phase (high training accuracy, near‑zero validation accuracy), hidden neurons already encode a periodic Fourier‑like structure: input weight vectors are well‑approximated by binary square waves, and output weight phases satisfy ϕ_out ≈ ϕ_a + ϕ_b (mod 2π). This relationship persists even under substantial label noise that prevents generalization. Grokking—the delayed rise in validation accuracy—corresponds not to discovering a new algorithm but to refining this pre‑existing structure: input weights become sharper square waves, and output phases align more precisely. An idealized MLP constructed from the memorized weights (using perfect square waves for inputs and cosines for outputs) achieves >95% validation accuracy, despite the original model being near random at that stage.

## Method Summary
The study trains MLPs (194‑256‑97) on a fully specified modular addition dataset (9409 unique (a,b,c) triples) with a 30/70 train/validation split and optional label noise α ∈ [0, 0.3]. Training uses full‑batch AdamW (lr = 1e‑3, weight_decay = 1.0) with early stopping. After each run, the dominant frequency ν and phases ϕ_a, ϕ_b, ϕ_out of every hidden neuron’s input and output weight vectors are extracted via real‑valued discrete Fourier transform (rfft). Neurons with a periodicity score per(w) = max_k|ẑ_k|/(mean_k|ẑ_k|) > 12 are deemed “structured.” For these, an idealized model is built: W_a and W_b are set to sign(cos(2πν·/97 − ϕ_a)) and sign(cos(2πν·/97 − ϕ_b)), while W_out uses cos(2πν·/97 − ϕ_out); all biases are zero. This model is evaluated on the clean validation set.

## Key Results
- Input weights (W_a, W_b) develop near‑binary square‑wave patterns, not sinusoids; intermediate values concentrate near sign‑change boundaries.
- For structured neurons, the dominant Fourier phases obey ϕ_out ≈ ϕ_a + ϕ_b with near‑perfect circular correlation (r′ ≥ 0.998), even under heavy label noise (α = 0.3).
- An idealized “Sq→Cos” model constructed from weights at the Memorization checkpoint recovers >95% validation accuracy across noise levels, while the actual model at that checkpoint has ≤ 2.3% accuracy; grokking therefore sharpens a latent algorithm.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Square‑Wave Formation in Input Weights
- **Claim:** ReLU MLPs under strong weight decay (1.0) learn input weight vectors well‑approximated by binary square waves.
- **Mechanism:** The piecewise‑linear ReLU activation combined with L2 regularization (via AdamW weight decay) pushes most weights to extreme values (±A), with narrow transition regions at sign changes. This creates a bimodal weight distribution that matches a square wave’s Fourier decomposition.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “input weights are near‑binary square waves… intermediate‑valued weights appear exclusively near sign‑change boundaries.”  
  - [section 3] Visual plots of W_a/W_b show sharp transitions; periodicity scores are bimodal; paired t‑tests confirm square‑wave fits outperform cosine fits.  
  - [corpus] Consistent with Doshi et al. (2024) on Fourier features but differs in activation/noise regime.
- **Break condition:** Reducing weight decay or using a non‑ReLU activation (e.g., quadratic) may yield sinusoidal instead of square‑wave weights.

### Mechanism 2: Phase‑Sum Relation in Output Weights
- **Claim:** For neurons with periodic input weights, the dominant Fourier phases of output weights obey ϕ_out = ϕ_a + ϕ_b (mod 2π), even under label noise that prevents grokking.
- **Mechanism:** The network encodes modular addition as phase addition in Fourier space. Each structured neuron implements a frequency component where the output weight’s phase is the sum of the input weight phases, enabling linear combination of periodic functions to compute (a + b) mod p.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “output weight distributions whose dominant Fourier phases satisfy a phase‑sum relation ϕ_out = ϕ_a + ϕ_b; this relation holds even when the model is trained on noisy data and fails to grok.”  
  - [section 3.1] Figure 5 shows circular correlation r′ ≥ 0.998 across all α.  
  - [corpus] Supported by Gromov (2023) for cosine weights and Nanda et al. (2023) for transformers; this work confirms it with square‑wave inputs and under noise.
- **Break condition:** The relation fails for low‑periodicity neurons (per < 12), which form a separate, negligible subpopulation.

### Mechanism 3: Latent Structure Sharpening During Grokking
- **Claim:** The algorithmic structure (periodic frequencies and phase‑sum relation) is latent during memorization; grokking sharpens it rather than discovering it.
- **Mechanism:** Early in training (saturation checkpoint), the network already encodes a noisy but structurally correct Fourier representation. Grokking gradually refines this: input weights become cleaner square waves (more binary), and output phases align more precisely with ϕ_out = ϕ_a + ϕ_b, leading to validation accuracy rise.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “grokking does not discover the correct algorithm, but rather sharpens an algorithm substantially encoded during memorization.”  
  - [section 4.1] Table 2: idealized Sq→Cos model built from saturation checkpoint weights achieves 64–88% validation accuracy across α, while the real model at that checkpoint has ≤ 2.3%.  
  - [corpus] Aligns with Nanda et al.’s continuous progress measures; contrasts with Doshi et al.’s neuron‑level separation.
- **Break condition:** The latent structure may degrade under extreme noise (α > 0.3) or different architectures, preventing high‑accuracy extraction.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Fourier analysis of weight vectors**  
  *Why needed:* The paper extracts dominant frequencies and phases via DFT to characterize weight patterns and build idealized models.  
  *Quick check:* Given a 97‑element weight vector, how do you compute its dominant Fourier phase, and what does a high periodicity score (per(w)) indicate about its structure?

- **Concept: Modular arithmetic as phase addition**  
  *Why needed:* The core algorithmic insight is that (a + b) mod p equals adding phases of e^{2πi a/p} and e^{2πi b/p}; the network’s ϕ_out = ϕ_a + ϕ_b implements this.  
  *Quick check:* Why does cos(2π(a + b)/p − ϕ_out) equal cos(2πa/p − ϕ_a)·cos(2πb/p − ϕ_b) + sin(…)·sin(…) under certain conditions? How might ReLU’s nonlinearity interact with this identity?

- **Concept: Grokking dynamics**  
  *Why needed:* The paper reframes grokking as sharpening of latent structure. Understanding the typical timeline (train accuracy hits 100% early, validation lags) is essential.  
  *Quick check:* If a model achieves 99% training accuracy but 2% validation accuracy at step T, what does the paper claim about its internal representations at step T versus after grokking?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (194‑dim two‑hot) → Hidden layer (256 ReLU neurons; each has W_a[97], W_b[97], bias) → Output layer (97 linear units; W_out[256×97], no bias).  
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Train model to saturation (99% train accuracy) and final checkpoint.  
  2. For each hidden neuron i: compute rfft of W_a^(i); let ν_i be argmax_{k=1..48} |X_a[ν]|. Extract ϕ_a^(i)=arg(X_a[ν_i]), ϕ_b^(i)=arg(rfft(W_b^(i))[ν_i]), ϕ_out^(i)=arg(rfft(W_out^(i))[ν_i]).  
  3. Compute periodicity score per(w) for each neuron; select structured neurons (per > 12).  
  4. Construct idealized model: set W_a^(i)[j]=sign(cos(2πν_i j/97 − ϕ_a^(i))), W_b^(i)[j]=sign(cos(2πν_i j/97 − ϕ_b^(i))), W_out^(i)[j]=cos(2πν_i j/97 − ϕ_out^(i)); all biases = 0.  
  5. Evaluate idealized model on clean validation set.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Square‑wave inputs (sign(cos)) match real weights better than cosines; cosine outputs align with phase‑sum relation.  
  - Higher noise α reduces periodicity but preserves ϕ_out ≈ ϕ_a + ϕ_b in structured neurons; idealized model still recovers >95% accuracy at α=0.3.  
  - Frequency alignment is crucial: structured neurons share identical ν across W_a, W_b, W_out; mismatched frequencies indicate unstructured neurons.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Periodicity scores uniformly low (< 5) → square‑wave structure absent; likely insufficient weight decay or wrong activation.  
  - ϕ_out deviates significantly from ϕ_a + ϕ_b for structured neurons → phase‑sum relation broken; idealized model accuracy will drop.  
  - Using individual dominant frequencies for each weight vector (instead of tying to W_a’s ν) may misalign phases and degrade performance.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Visualize W_a/W_b for high‑periodicity neurons from a clean model; confirm near‑binary values with sharp transitions.  
  2. Compute periodicity scores for all 256 neurons; verify bimodal distribution and identify structured neurons (e.g., threshold per > 12).  
  3. For a noisy model (α = 0.2) at saturation, extract phases/frequencies from structured neurons, build Sq→Cos idealized model, and show it achieves >90% validation accuracy while the real model is near zero.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do ReLU MLPs with AdamW and weight decay 1.0 learn square wave input weights instead of sinusoids? The paper documents the phenomenon but does not isolate whether it arises from ReLU’s piecewise linearity, the strength of weight decay, or their interaction.
2. Does the latent structure (frequency matching and phase‑sum) generalize to other algorithmic tasks (e.g., multiplication, parity) and architectures (transformers, deeper networks)? The study is limited to modular addition with one‑hidden‑layer ReLU MLPs.
3. Is the phase‑sum relation ϕ_out = ϕ_a + ϕ_b necessary for high accuracy, or merely a sufficient condition that training tends to find? The paper shows structured neurons obey it and that an idealized model using extracted phases works, but does not test whether violating the relation degrades performance.

## Limitations
- The findings are tightly bound to a specific regime: one‑hidden‑layer ReLU MLPs, strong weight decay (1.0), full‑batch training, and modular addition modulo 97. Generalizability to other architectures, activations, or tasks is unknown.
- Key hyperparameters (batch size, weight initialization) are unspecified, introducing reproducibility risk. The DFT extraction assumes dominant frequency alignment across W_a, W_b, W_out; mismatches are ignored but could affect idealized model accuracy.
- The role of “unstructured” low‑periodicity neurons is ambiguous—they may contribute noise or weak signals, and their exclusion from analysis might overstate the clarity of the latent structure.

## Confidence
- **Square‑wave input weights:** Medium. Strong empirical support (bimodal periodicity, visual fits, t‑tests) but likely regime‑specific; breaks with weaker decay or non‑ReLU activations.
- **Phase‑sum relation in structured neurons:** High. Near‑perfect circular correlation (r′ ≥ 0.998) across all noise levels, consistent with prior theoretical work.
- **Latent structure sharpening:** Medium‑High. Idealized model recovers high accuracy from memorization checkpoints, but extraction uses only a subset of neurons; unstructured population contribution is unclear.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Train models with varied weight decay (0.1, 0.5, 1.0) and batch sizes (full vs. mini‑batch). Re‑analyze periodicity and phase‑sum to confirm square‑wave emergence requires high decay/full‑batch.
2. **DFT extraction robustness:** For models where W_a and W_b have different dominant frequencies, compare idealized model accuracy when using ν from W_a only versus individual dominant frequencies per weight vector.
3. **Ablate unstructured neurons:** Build idealized models using only structured neurons (per > 12) versus all neurons. A significant accuracy drop with all neurons would confirm the paper’s focus on the structured subpopulation.
