# EcoFair: Trustworthy and Energy-Aware Routing for Privacy-Preserving Vertically Partitioned Medical Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26483  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26483  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Reduces edge-side inference energy by up to 68% via dynamic selective escalation while maintaining diagnostic performance and often improving worst-group fairness metrics.

## Executive Summary
EcoFair introduces a dynamic routing mechanism for privacy-preserving vertically partitioned medical inference, where image and tabular data remain on separate edge clients. It uses a lightweight image encoder that selectively activates a heavier encoder based on local predictive uncertainty and a neurosymbolic risk score derived from patient metadata (age, lesion site). Evaluated on three dermatology benchmarks, EcoFair achieves substantial edge energy savings (up to 68%) without compromising classification accuracy. Notably, the routing post-hoc enhances subgroup-sensitive fairness—improving malignant-case true positive rates for disadvantaged groups—without modifying training objectives. The approach balances computational efficiency with clinical safety in resource-constrained deployments.

## Method Summary
EcoFair addresses vertically partitioned inference: raw images and tabular metadata (age, lesion site) never leave their respective edge clients; only embeddings are transmitted to a cloud server for multimodal fusion. At inference, the image client first runs a lightweight encoder. Its output distribution yields two routing signals: normalized Shannon entropy (overall uncertainty) and a safe–danger probability gap (clinical ambiguity between benign/malignant classes). Concurrently, the tabular client computes a neurosymbolic risk score \( R_{\text{tab}}(x) = a(x) \cdot \ell(x) \), product of normalized age and site-specific malignancy rate. The image client escalates to the heavier encoder if any signal exceeds a calibrated threshold. The chosen image embedding (lightweight or heavy) and the tabular embedding are sent to the server, concatenated, and classified via a fully connected network. Models are pre-trained and fixed; thresholds are set by sweeping on a validation set to balance energy, accuracy, and fairness.

## Key Results
- **Energy reduction:** Up to 68% lower per-sample edge energy versus always using the heavy encoder, with savings largest when the compute gap between encoders is substantial.
- **Competitive classification:** Macro F1 and Balanced Accuracy match or exceed both lightweight-only and heavy-only baselines across three dermatology datasets.
- **Fairness improvement:** In several model–dataset pairs, worst-group malignant-case true positive rate (WG-TPR) increases and subgroup disparity (GapTPR) decreases, demonstrating post-hoc rescue of vulnerable groups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Selective Escalation for Energy Reduction**  
- **Claim:** EcoFair reduces edge energy by dynamically avoiding unnecessary activation of the heavier image encoder.  
- **Mechanism:** The routing function evaluates the lightweight encoder’s predictive distribution to compute normalized entropy and a safe–danger probability gap. If either exceeds its threshold, the heavier encoder is activated; otherwise, only the lightweight embedding is used.  
- **Core assumption:** The lightweight encoder is sufficiently accurate for a substantial subset of samples, so skipping the heavy encoder does not meaningfully degrade performance.  
- **Evidence anchors:** 68% energy savings [abstract]; routing score definitions in Equations (4)–(10) [section 3.2].  
- **Break condition:** When the computational disparity between encoders is modest or dataset ambiguity is high, triggering frequency rises, diminishing or negating savings.

**Mechanism 2: Metadata-Driven Safety Override**  
- **Claim:** The tabular neurosymbolic risk score improves safety by ensuring heavy-encoder activation when patient metadata indicates elevated malignancy risk, even if the image encoder is confident.  
- **Mechanism:** The tabular client computes \( R_{\text{tab}}(x) = a(x) \cdot \ell(x) \). If \( R_{\text{tab}}(x) \geq \tau_{\text{risk}} \), the routing logic overrides image-based signals and activates the heavy encoder.  
- **Core assumption:** Age and lesion localisation are strongly correlated with malignancy risk and the per-site malignancy rates are stable and generalizable.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Routing combines uncertainty and risk score [abstract]; risk score definition in Equations (12)–(15) [section 3.3].  
- **Break condition:** Missing, noisy, or biased metadata can produce misleading risk scores, causing inappropriate escalation or missed escalations.

**Mechanism 3: Post-hoc Fairness via Targeted Escalation**  
- **Claim:** Selective routing improves worst-group malignant-case TPR and reduces subgroup disparity without modifying the global training objective.  
- **Mechanism:** Samples from underrepresented subgroups often exhibit higher uncertainty or elevated metadata risk due to distribution shift. Routing disproportionately escalates these samples to the heavier encoder, which has greater representational capacity, reducing false negatives on clinically critical malignant cases.  
- **Core assumption:** Subgroup disparities in lightweight model performance correlate with higher uncertainty or metadata-derived risk.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes improvement in subgroup-sensitive behaviour; Table 4 shows positive ∆WG TPR for several pairs/datasets [section 4.5].  
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty/risk signals do not predict subgroup difficulty, or if the heavy encoder shares the same biases, fairness may not improve (e.g., Pair II on PAD-UFES-20 worsens GapTPR).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Image Client (A1) -> Cloud Server (B) <- Tabular Client (A2)  
  Routing logic resides in A1; thresholds determine whether A1 uses lightweight or heavy image encoder.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Image input → lightweight encoder → predictive distribution.  
  2. Compute Shannon entropy \( H(x) \) and safe–danger gap \( \Delta(x) \).  
  3. Tabular client computes risk score \( R_{\text{tab}}(x) \).  
  4. If \( H(x) > \tau_H \) or \( \Delta(x) \leq \tau_\Delta \) or \( R_{\text{tab}}(x) \geq \tau_{\text{risk}} \), activate heavy encoder on A1.  
  5. Transmit image embedding (lightweight or heavy) and tabular embedding to B.  
  6. B concatenates embeddings, applies FC network, outputs final class.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Energy vs performance: Lower thresholds increase heavy-encoder usage, improving accuracy/fairness but raising energy; optimal thresholds are dataset- and model-pair-dependent.  
  - Compute gap: Larger disparity between encoders yields higher potential energy savings but may increase mismatch if lightweight is too weak.  
  - Interpretability vs automation: The neurosymbolic risk score is transparent but may oversimplify complex risk interactions.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Negative energy savings: heavy encoder triggers too often, often due to small compute gap or high dataset ambiguity.  
  - Fairness degradation: worst-group TPR drops relative to lightweight baseline, suggesting routing signals do not correlate with subgroup difficulty or heavy encoder also underperforms.  
  - High routing variance: thresholds not well-calibrated; consider better uncertainty estimation or adaptive thresholds.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Backbone screening: Measure parameter count, latency, energy, and memory for candidate encoders to select lite–heavy pairs with meaningful compute gaps.  
  2. Routing threshold sweep: Vary \( \tau_H \), \( \tau_\Delta \), \( \tau_{\text{risk}} \) to generate energy–performance Pareto frontiers and identify non-dominated operating points.  
  3. Subgroup fairness analysis: For chosen configuration, compute worst-group TPR and TPR gap across demographic splits (e.g., skin tone, age groups) to quantify fairness impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Real hardware deployment:** Does EcoFair maintain energy savings and diagnostic performance on physical edge devices rather than in simulation?  
   *Basis:* Future Work explicitly states evaluation is simulated, not on real distributed hardware.  
   *Why unresolved:* Real-world hardware dynamics (thermal throttling, power fluctuations) can materially impact energy, latency, and threshold effectiveness.  
   *Evidence needed:* Deployment on commodity edge devices (e.g., smartphones, Raspberry Pi) measuring actual per-sample energy, end-to-end latency, and fairness metrics; compare against simulated baselines.
2. **Metadata robustness:** How robust is the neurosymbolic risk score to missing, corrupted, or biased tabular metadata?  
   *Basis:* Future Work notes reliance on metadata availability/quality and calls for examining robustness underMissing/noisy data.  
   *Why unresolved:* Evaluation uses clean, complete metadata from curated datasets; real EHRs often contain missing ages, mislabelled sites, or reporting biases.  
   *Evidence needed:* Ablation studies synthetically introducing missingness, label noise, and demographic skew into benchmark metadata; measure degradation in routing accuracy, energy, fairness; evaluate mitigations (imputation, fallback policies).
3. **Generalization and auto-optimization:** To what extent can EcoFair’s routing generalize across diverse model architectures and be automatically optimized for heterogeneous deployment constraints?  
   *Basis:* Future Work notes pair-dependent effectiveness and calls for extension to broader backbones and adaptive policies.  
   *Why unresolved:* Study evaluates only three CNN pairs; unclear behavior with modern architectures (ViT, Swin) or how to systematically tune thresholds for new models/requirements.  
   *Evidence needed:* Benchmark with wide spectrum of encoders (ViT, ConvNeXt, etc.); develop adaptive threshold tuning (e.g., RL, Bayesian optimization) for deployment-specific utility; validate across datasets/hardware.

## Limitations
- Routing thresholds (\( \tau_H \), \( \tau_\Delta \), \( \tau_{\text{risk}} \)) not explicitly reported in paper; exact operating points unknown, hindering exact reproduction.
- Subgroup definitions for fairness metrics (WG-TPR, GapTPR) are ambiguous (e.g., which skin tone scale or age bins used), making fairness audit difficult.
- Energy measurements conducted on specific NVIDIA GPUs; transferability to other edge hardware (e.g., mobile SoCs) is uncertain.
- Neurosymbolic risk score uses a linear product of age and site malignancy; clinical validity and generalizability are not established.
- Fairness improvements are inconsistent across model–dataset pairs; the rescue effect is not universal (e.g., Pair II on PAD-UFES-20 degrades fairness).

## Confidence
- **Energy reduction (up to 68%):** High – robust per-sample measurements and strong Pareto fronts; depends on sufficient compute gap.
- **Competitive classification (Macro F1/Balanced Acc):** Medium – results on par with baselines but show dataset variability.
- **Fairness gains (WG-TPR ↑, GapTPR ↓):** Medium – positive in several settings, negative in others; mechanism reliability uncertain.
- **Metadata override safety benefit:** Medium – conceptually sound but lacks clinical calibration evidence.
- **Post-hoc fairness rescue via routing:** Medium – mixed empirical support; causal link to subgroup difficulty not proven.
