# Uncertainty-Aware Delivery Delay Duration Prediction via Multi-Task Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20271
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20271
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves MAE of 0.67–0.91 days for delayed shipments, with 41–64% improvement over tree-based regressions and 15–35% over sequential two-step models; conformal calibration improves delayed coverage from 20–48% to 64–70%.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of predicting delivery delay durations in highly imbalanced supply chain data, where delayed shipments are rare but operationally critical. The authors propose an end-to-end multi-task deep learning model that jointly learns to classify delay likelihood and regress delay duration, using dedicated embedding layers for heterogeneous tabular features and conformal prediction to calibrate uncertainty intervals. Evaluated on a real-world dataset of over 10 million shipments, the model significantly improves accuracy for delayed shipments and provides well-calibrated prediction intervals.

## Method Summary
The model transforms categorical and numerical features via learned embeddings and Periodic Linear (PLR) encoding, respectively. A shared MLP backbone feeds into a classification head (SigmoidF1 loss) that predicts delay probability. During inference, samples are routed to one of two quantile regression heads (on-time or delayed) based on the classifier’s output; each head predicts multiple quantiles using pinball loss. Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) is then applied per branch to calibrate intervals. Training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing the combined classification and regression loss, then freezing the backbone and refining the regression heads.

## Key Results
- Delayed shipment MAE reduced by 41–64% compared to single-step tree-based regressions and by 15–35% compared to sequential two-step classify‑then‑regress models.
- Conformal calibration improves coverage for delayed instances from 20–48% to 64–70%, achieving marginal coverage guarantees.
- Dedicated embedding layers and PLR encoding effectively handle high-cardinality categorical and non-linear numerical features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dedicated Embedding Layers
- **Claim:** Dedicated embedding layers transform heterogeneous tabular features into dense, learnable representations that capture complex relationships despite high cardinality and mixed data types.
- **Mechanism:** Categorical features use embedding matrices with dimensions scaled logarithmically with cardinality; numerical features use Periodic Linear (PLR) encoding (sinusoidal transformations) to capture non-linear patterns. Concatenated embeddings feed a shared MLP backbone for joint learning.
- **Evidence:** Equations for categorical (Eq. 2–3) and numerical (Eq. 4) embeddings are provided; PLR is cited from Gorishniy et al. The design targets high-dimensional sparse tabular data common in logistics.
- **Break condition:** Poor representation of rare categorical values (e.g., obscure origin‑destination pairs) due to insufficient training examples degrades performance for those paths.

### Mechanism 2: Unified Classification‑then‑Regression with End‑to‑End Training
- **Claim:** A unified architecture with a shared backbone enables end‑to‑end training, improving detection of delayed shipments and allowing regression heads to specialize, mitigating error propagation from sequential pipelines.
- **Mechanism:** A classification head predicts delay probability using SigmoidF1 loss, optimized for the imbalanced minority class. During training, samples are routed to regression heads based on true labels; at inference, routing uses the classifier’s probability. The shared backbone learns representations useful for both tasks, and regression inputs include classifier confidence.
- **Evidence:** The model achieves 15–35% lower delayed MAE than sequential two‑step baselines (Section 6). The approach follows multi‑task learning principles and includes a two‑stage training regimen.
- **Break condition:** Low classifier recall for delayed shipments causes misrouting to the on‑time head, increasing delayed MAE.

### Mechanism 3: Conformalized Quantile Regression per Branch
- **Claim:** CQR calibrates prediction intervals separately for each routing branch, yielding better coverage and sharper intervals for delayed shipments under dataset shift.
- **Mechanism:** After quantile regression training, calibration uses a hold‑out set partitioned by true delay status. For each branch, conformity scores measure interval violations; the (1−α)‑quantile of these scores becomes a branch‑specific correction added to interval bounds, achieving target marginal coverage.
- **Evidence:** Delayed coverage improves from 20–48% to 64–70% post‑calibration (Tables 6–7). The methodology is explicitly described in Section 4.4.
- **Break condition:** Temporal non‑stationarity causing covariate shift between calibration and test periods violates exchangeability, leading to under‑ or over‑coverage, especially for delayed shipments.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi‑task Learning (Hard Parameter Sharing):** Needed to force the shared backbone to learn representations informative for both delay detection and duration estimation, counteracting severe class imbalance. Quick check: If trained separately, the backbone might ignore patterns specific to the minority delayed class, degrading regression performance for delays.
- **Quantile Regression (Pinball Loss):** Needed to predict interval estimates capturing heteroscedastic uncertainty in delay durations. Quick check: MSE would be dominated by the majority on‑time class and fail to model heavy tails in the delayed subclass.
- **Conformal Prediction (CQR):** Needed to provide finite‑sample coverage guarantees and correct miscalibration of quantile regression, especially for rare delayed instances. Quick check: The conformity score measures absolute deviation from the true value relative to the predicted interval; its (1−α)‑quantile on calibration data sets the correction to ensure that a (1−α) proportion of calibration instances fall within the adjusted interval.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (shipment features) → Embedding Subnetwork (categorical embeddings + PLR for numerical) → Shared Backbone (MLP) → Classification Head (delay probability) → Routing Logic (based on classifier output) → Dual Regression Heads (quantile regression for on‑time and delayed) → Point/Interval Output.
- **Critical path:** Data → Embeddings → Shared MLP → Classifier → (Routing) → Quantile Heads → Output. Key dependency: Classifier quality directly impacts regression specialization.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - End‑to‑end vs. sequential: End‑to‑end improves delayed detection but requires loss balancing; sequential is simpler but propagates errors.
  - Embedding dimensions: Logarithmic scaling prevents overparameterization but may underfit very high‑cardinality sparse features.
  - Calibration split: Must be temporal hold‑out; partitioning by true delay requires sufficient delayed samples for reliable correction terms.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High delayed MAE but good overall MAE: Classifier recall too low, misrouting delayed instances.
  - Low delayed coverage (<60%) pre‑calibration: Quantile heads not capturing tail; may need more capacity or longer training.
  - Post‑calibration coverage >85%: Conformal correction too large, possibly due to distribution shift or insufficient calibration data.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline sanity check:** Train a minimal model per location; confirm delayed MAE >> overall MAE.
  2. **Routing ablation:** Compare end‑to‑end trained model vs. frozen classifier (train classifier first, freeze backbone, then train regression heads) to measure benefit of shared backbone training.
  3. **Calibration branch test:** Apply CQR with a single calibration set vs. dual‑branch calibration; compare delayed coverage and Winkler score.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can transfer learning from high‑volume origin locations improve delay prediction accuracy in data‑sparse locations?
- Does online learning that adapts to temporal drift improve predictive performance under evolving delivery patterns and seasonality?
- Can uncertainty quantification be extended to provide conditional coverage guarantees for operationally relevant subgroups (e.g., specific carriers or regions)?
- Is the two‑stage training regimen (freezing the backbone after classification) suboptimal compared to fully end‑to‑end joint training?
- Could a unified multi‑location model with location embeddings replace separate location‑specific models while maintaining or improving performance, especially for low‑volume locations?

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to a single company’s dataset; generalizability to other logistics networks is untested.
- Model performance relies heavily on classifier recall for rare delayed shipments; poor detection leads to misrouting and increased errors.
- Conformal calibration assumes exchangeability between calibration and test data; temporal shifts may violate this, especially for the dynamic delayed subclass.

## Confidence
- **Embedding effectiveness (Mechanism 1):** Medium (architectural details provided but limited empirical validation on rare categories).
- **End‑to‑end multi‑task architecture (Mechanism 2):** High (strong empirical gains over baselines, well‑aligned with theory).
- **Conformal calibration (Mechanism 3):** High (clear coverage improvement and sound methodology, but bounded by single‑dataset evaluation and shift sensitivity).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑dataset replication:** Evaluate on additional logistics datasets from different companies/regions to confirm generalizability of MAE and coverage improvements.
2. **Embedding ablation:** Replace proposed embeddings with one‑hot and raw numerical inputs to measure isolated impact on delayed MAE.
3. **Temporal robustness test:** Split data chronologically, perform separate conformal calibration per time slice, and track delayed coverage stability to test exchangeability violations.
