# Evaluating FrameNet-Based Semantic Modeling for Gender-Based Violence Detection in Clinical Records

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18124  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18124  
- **Reference count:** 6  
- **Primary result:** FrameNet-based semantic model achieved F1-score 0.772, a 0.311 improvement over the demographic model (F1 0.461), with recall 0.838 vs. 0.701.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the underreporting of gender-based violence (GBV) in Brazilian primary care records by investigating whether FrameNet-based semantic annotation of open-text clinical narratives can improve detection. The authors compared three SVM classifiers: one trained on semantic features derived from frame-annotated text, one combining semantic and structured data, and one using only structured demographic/clinical fields. The semantic model significantly outperformed the demographic model (F1 0.772 vs. 0.461) and achieved higher recall (0.838 vs. 0.701). Adding structured data to the semantic model yielded negligible gains, suggesting that unstructured text contains unique signals. Qualitative analysis revealed that the semantic model captured clinically meaningful patterns (e.g., pregnancy, mental health conditions, referrals) that are often missing from structured fields, while the demographic model relied on potentially biased proxies such as location and race. The work demonstrates the value of domain-specific semantic modeling for identifying sensitive, underreported conditions in clinical practice.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed custom FrameNet domains for Healthcare (35 frames, 2,776 lexical units) and Violence (48 frames, 1,774 lexical units) within FrameNet Brasil (FN-Br). They manually annotated 2,352 sentences to train the LOME parser, which automatically tags frames, frame elements, and lexical units in all open-text narratives. Features included frequencies of frames, frame elements, lexical units, their co-occurrences (within Healthcare/Violence frames), and ternary qualia relations. These were TF-IDF weighted, L1-normalized, filtered for minimum occurrence (frames ≥ 50, LUs ≥ 25), and reduced via PCA to 2,000 components (≈95% variance). Linear SVMs were trained under 5-fold cross-validation. Three models were evaluated: (1) Semantic (PCA-reduced semantic features only), (2) Demographic (one-hot encoded structured fields only), and (3) Mixed (concatenation of semantic PCA and structured one-hot, then PCA to 2,000). The dataset comprised 801 labeled records from e-SUS AB and SINAN (167 GBV cases, 634 non-GBV), with GBV defined by aggression-related ICD codes or temporal linkage to violence notifications.

## Key Results
- Semantic model: F1 = 0.772, Recall = 0.838, Precision = 0.756.  
- Demographic model: F1 = 0.461, Recall = 0.701 (precision extremely low).  
- Mixed model: F1 = 0.771, essentially no improvement over the semantic model.  
- Qualitative analysis: Semantic model’s top features were clinically relevant frames (e.g., `Health_conditions`, `Fear`, `Experience_bodily_harm`) and lexical units (e.g., `gestante.n`, `depressão.n`, `encaminhar.v`), while the demographic model relied on location and race, indicating bias and limited clinical meaning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Semantic Frame Activation for Indirect Indicator Discovery**  
FrameNet-based annotation identifies GBV by evoking frames that capture implicit, context-rich descriptions of health conditions and bodily harm underreported in structured fields. LOME parses narratives, tagging lexical units to frames such as `Health_conditions` and `Experience_bodily_harm`, creating a feature space of frame frequencies, co-occurrences, and lexical choices. The SVM learns that patterns like repeated pregnancy mentions within `Health_conditions`, combined with `Health_service` references (e.g., referrals), correlate with GBV. Evidence: the abstract states semantic annotation outperforms categorical models; Section 4.2 finds `Health_conditions` as the most frequent frame with pregnancy and mental health terms; Section 3.3 describes LOME’s integration of FrameNet parsing.

**Mechanism 2: Structured Data’s Limited Signal and Potential for Harmful Bias**  
Relying solely on structured demographic/clinical fields yields a high-recall but low-precision model that risks reinforcing stereotypes. One-hot encoded sparse features (e.g., neighborhood, race) dominate the decision boundary due to spurious correlations with the few violence cases, reflecting systemic recording biases rather than causal risk. The demographic model showed high recall but extremely low precision, with feature importance highlighting location and race—attributes susceptible to inconsistent field completion and proxy discrimination.

**Mechanism 3: Domain-Specific Semantic Modeling Captures Situational Patterns**  
Manually building custom FrameNet domains for Healthcare and Violence (35/48 frames, thousands of lexical units) enables recognition of configurations of elements that form a narrative signature of GBV. The 12-step modeling process produced a cognitively grounded, domain-tailored lexicon, allowing LOME to tag nuanced expressions (e.g., prenatal care, fear during examinations) as frame-element-LU tuples whose co-occurrence statistics are predictive. Evidence: Section 3.2 reports the domain frame and LU counts; Section 4.2 shows the dominance of healthcare frames in GBV cases.

- **High-Dimensional Feature Sparsity & Dimensionality Reduction (PCA)**  
  *Why needed:* The semantic feature space is extremely sparse (~15k dimensions); PCA creates a dense input for the SVM but risks losing subtle signals.  
  *Quick check:* Why is L1-normalization applied before PCA, and what risk does PCA introduce when reducing from ~15,000 to 2,000 components?

- **Imbalanced Classification & Evaluation Metrics (Precision/Recall/F1)**  
  *Why needed:* The dataset is severely imbalanced (801 total, 167 violence). Underreporting makes recall (catching positives) more critical; F1 balances precision and recall.  
  *Quick check:* Why is an F1-score of 0.772 with recall 0.838 considered successful for this GBV detection task, even though precision is only 0.756?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Sources (e-SUS AB & SINAN records: open-text narratives, structured fields) → Semantic Annotation Pipeline (LOME with XLM-RoBERTa, trained on 2,352 human-annotated FN-Br sentences) → Feature Engineering (compute frame/FE/LU frequencies, co-occurrences, ternary qualia; TF-IDF, L1-normalization, filter low-frequency items; PCA to 2,000) → Classifier (Linear SVM) → Evaluation (5-fold CV, precision/recall/F1).  
*Variants:* (1) Semantic model uses only semantic PCA features; (2) Demographic model uses one-hot structured fields; (3) Mixed model concatenates semantic PCA with one-hot structured fields then PCA again to 2,000.

**Critical path:** Data anonymization → Domain modeling (Healthcare/Violence frames & LUs) → Human annotation of seed corpus → Train LOME → Automatic annotation of full corpus → Feature extraction (TF-IDF, co-occurrences, qualia) → PCA → SVM training/evaluation. A break in domain modeling or LOME training quality corrupts all downstream steps.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Manual vs. Auto Annotation:* Manual annotation (2,352 sentences) provides gold standard but is costly; auto-annotation (LOME F1 = 50.68) scales but introduces noise that propagates to the classifier.  
- *Feature Granularity:* Using lexical unit (LU) features increases granularity and potential signal but also sparsity and overfitting risk; mitigated by minimum frequency thresholds.  
- *PCA Variance Preservation:* 94.8% variance retained (semantic model); trade-off between SVM efficiency and loss of low-variance semantic signals; component count chosen heuristically.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Semantic model recall drops significantly (< 0.7) vs. paper (0.838): indicates degraded LOME quality or incomplete domain frames.  
2. Demographic model feature importance dominated by location/race: model relies on biased proxies; predictions unreliable for new populations.  
3. Mixed model fails to improve over semantic model (or degrades): suggests PCA integration issues or that structured data adds noise.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate Semantic Model Baseline:** Implement exact pipeline on provided anonymized sample; target F1 ≥ 0.75, Recall ≥ 0.82.  
2. **Ablate Feature Components:** Train semantic model with only (a) frames, (b) lexical units, (c) frame-element co-occurrences to identify driving granularity.  
3. **Validate Domain Frame Necessity:** Train a “generic” semantic model using only standard (non-domain-extended) FrameNet-Br frames; compare F1/Recall to domain-specific model; expect a significant drop if custom modeling is essential.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalizability to other violence types or health conditions:** Can the same approach detect self-harm, violence against LGBTQ+ individuals, or early cancer without substantial re-modeling? (Future work mentions these extensions.)  
- **Bias mitigation and clinical validity:** Does the semantic model reduce reliance on race/neighborhood proxies and provide more clinically meaningful features? Demographic model’s top features were flagged as stereotypical; semantic features are interpretable but need professional validation.  
- **Handling implicit references:** How robust is the model when narratives contain highly indirect or ambiguous violence mentions? The dataset includes “likely violence” cases; moderate precision suggests some ambiguity remains unresolved.  
- **Contribution of lexical units vs. frames:** What is the relative impact of specific word choices versus situational frame patterns on performance? The combined feature representation obscures their distinct contributions.

## Limitations
- **Data access:** Original e-SUS AB/SINAN dataset is not publicly available; reproduction requires new acquisition with ethical approval.  
- **Domain frame inventories:** Custom Healthcare/Violence frames and the fine‑tuned LOME weights are not released, hindering exact replication.  
- **Feature engineering & hyperparameter details:** Co‑occurrence counting scope, ternary qualia weighting, PCA leakage prevention, and SVM regularization parameters are unspecified, creating uncertainty in reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Semantic model superiority** (F1 0.772 vs. 0.461): *Medium* (strong metrics but unreleased code/data limit verification).  
- **Structured data’s limited added value** (mixed model gain ~0): *Medium* (consistent with feature analysis but could be artifact of encoding/PCA).  
- **Necessity of custom domain modeling**: *Low to Medium* (qualitative support present but lacking generic FrameNet baseline for firm conclusion).
