# Prior-Guided Symbolic Regression: Towards Scientific Consistency in Equation Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13021
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13021
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PG-SR introduces explicit prior constraints and a PACE annealing mechanism to escape the Pseudo-Equation Trap, achieving state-of-the-art out-of-distribution performance and reducing NMSE by up to two orders of magnitude across scientific benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Symbolic regression often yields *pseudo-equations*—formulas that fit noisy training data but violate fundamental scientific principles, especially under data scarcity or noise. PG-SR addresses this by encoding domain knowledge as *executable prior constraint programs* and progressively enforcing them via a *Prior-Annealed Constrained Evaluation (PACE)* mechanism. The method operates through a three-stage LLM-driven pipeline: **Warm-up** (diverse initialization), **Evolution** (PACE-guided iterative improvement), and **Refinement** (targeted residual analysis). Theoretically, constraining the hypothesis space to valid equations reduces Rademacher complexity, tightening generalization bounds. Empirically, PG-SR sets new out-of-distribution benchmarks in biology, materials, chemistry, and physics, demonstrating robustness to noise, data scarcity, and even some prior inaccuracies.

## Method Summary
PG-SR uses a three-stage pipeline driven by an LLM orchestrator. First, a **Warm-up** phase generates a diverse set of initial equation skeletons from data statistics, with parameters optimized via L-BFGS-B; all candidates are retained in an *Experience Pool* regardless of prior compliance. Second, **Evolution** iteratively selects and modifies candidates using a *PACE* scoring function that gradually penalizes prior violations. This allows early exploration of invalid but promising regions before strictly enforcing constraints. The Experience Pool stores both equations (partitioned into logical islands for diversity) and *Insights* (summaries of successful refinements). Third, **Refinement** periodically analyzes residuals of top candidates to identify defective terms, retrieves similar historical equations/insights, and uses an LLM with reflection to propose targeted structural modifications. Central to the system is a *Prior Constraint Checker*—an executable program (e.g., Python function) that validates candidate equations against domain-specific scientific rules (e.g., monotonicity, boundary conditions).

## Key Results
- PG-SR reduces out-of-distribution NMSE by up to two orders of magnitude compared to state-of-the-art baselines (LLM-SR, Eureqa, etc.) across four scientific domains.
- It maintains robustness under high noise, extreme data scarcity (<20 samples), and even with suboptimal prior constraints, whereas baselines collapse into pseudo-equations.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of all three pipeline stages and the PACE mechanism; removing PACE causes catastrophic performance degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Explicit, executable prior constraints directly enforce scientific consistency, avoiding the unreliability of implicit guidance (e.g., prompt-based or complexity penalties).  
**Mechanism:** A *Prior Constraint Checker* runs as a hard filter during evaluation, rejecting equations that violate codified domain rules (e.g., "growth rate must be zero at zero biomass").  
**Core assumption:** Domain experts can reliably translate key scientific principles into a finite, checkable set of executable constraints that the true equation satisfies.  
**Evidence anchors:** The checker is generated via an LLM-assisted workflow from problem descriptions and data statistics (Section 3.3.1); implicit methods in neighbors (LLM-SR, DrSR) are outperformed.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The Prior-Annealed Constrained Evaluation (PACE) prevents premature convergence by temporarily allowing prior violations early in the search, then progressively enforcing constraints.  
**Mechanism:** PACE modifies the scoring function with time-dependent transformations (`σ(t)`, `δ(t)`). Early iterations (`t≈0`) assign scores close to the raw MSE fit, preserving gradient information. Later (`t→1`), penalties for violations increase sharply, creating a barrier against invalid candidates.  
**Core assumption:** The search must briefly explore regions violating priors to discover high-performing structures that can later be "repaired" into valid ones; abrupt enforcement causes stagnation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Defined in Eq. 8–9; ablation "w/o PACE" shows performance collapse (Section 4.7); analysis notes it eliminates zero-gradient plateaus (Appendix B.1).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The three-stage pipeline with shared *Experience Pool* (`E`) and *Insight Pool* creates cumulative learning where both equation skeletons and diagnostic insights are reused, improving fit and consistency over iterations.  
**Mechanism:**  
1. **Warm-up:** Generates a diverse, prior-agnostic seed set.  
2. **Evolution:** Iteratively evolves candidates using PACE, guided by sampled examples and insights from `E`.  
3. **Refinement:** Periodically analyzes residuals of top candidates to identify defect variables/regions, retrieves similar past candidates/insights, and uses LLM reflection to generate targeted repairs.  
**Core assumption:** Scientific discovery benefits from a memory of past successes and failures (equations + diagnostics), analogous to human heuristic refinement.  
**Evidence anchors:** Detailed in Sections 3.3.2–3.3.4; ablations "w/o Warmup" and "w/o Refine" both degrade OOD performance (Section 4.7).

## Foundational Learning

- **Rademacher Complexity & Generalization Bounds**  
  *Why needed:* Theoretical foundation (Section 3.2, Appendix A) proving that constraining the hypothesis space to `H_C` (equations satisfying priors) reduces complexity, tightening generalization bounds and guaranteeing against pseudo-equations (high training fit, high true risk).  
  *Quick check:* If a prior eliminates the true equation `f_true` from `H_C`, does Proposition 3.5’s guarantee hold? (No—the guarantee assumes `f_true ∈ H_C`.)

- **The Pseudo-Equation Trap**  
  *Why needed:* Core problem formulation. It describes the divergence where empirical risk minimization selects `f_pseudo` with near-zero training MSE but high true risk (violates science), especially under noise/scarcity (Figure 1). All method design aims to escape this.  
  *Quick check:* Can a method using only implicit constraints (e.g., complexity penalty in GP) guarantee escape? (No—the paper argues implicit constraints are dominated by empirical risk.)

- **Prior Constraint as Executable Programs**  
  *Why needed:* Key operational innovation. Constraints are runnable code (e.g., `if b==0 and db > 1e-6: return False`), enabling automated, exact, efficient validation during search, distinguishing PG-SR from prompt-based LLM guidance.  
  *Quick check:* How to encode "the equation must be smooth" as an executable checker for a symbolic tree? (Discretize domain and check finite differences or bound derivative magnitudes via symbolic differentiation.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input Data & Problem Description -> Data Analyzer -> Prior Constraint Checker Generator (LLM-assisted) -> Executable Constraints -> Warm-up (LLM + Optimizer) -> Seed Equations -> Evolution Loop (PACE Controller, Experience Pool, LLM Orchestrator, Optimizer) -> Refinement Engine (Residual Analysis, Retrieval) -> Output Equations.  
The Experience Pool (`E`) contains an Equation Pool (logical islands) and an Insight Pool; the PACE Controller modulates scoring; the LLM Orchestrator manages all prompts; the Optimizer (L-BFGS-B) tunes parameters; the Refinement Engine triggers periodic repairs.

**Critical path:**  
1. Analyze data stats.  
2. Generate executable priors (human-vetted).  
3. Warm-up: LLM generates skeletons → optimize → add all to Equation Pool islands.  
4. Evolution: Select island/cluster → sample skeletons using PACE scores → LLM prompt with examples/insights → generate/optimize/check → score with PACE → update pools.  
5. Refinement: For top candidates, analyze residuals → retrieve similar references → LLM reflection → generate repairs → update Insight Pool.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Explicit vs. implicit priors: explicit ensures precise enforcement but requires human programming; implicit is easier but unreliable.  
- PACE annealing schedule: too slow → pseudo-equations persist; too fast → premature convergence.  
- LLM dependency: performance hinges on LLM’s code-generation capability; hallucinations remain a risk.  
- Computational cost: high due to LLM calls (~17.85M tokens/run); tradeoff is OOD robustness vs. inference cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Prior quality failure: "Wrong Priors" condition (Figure 5) degrades performance; monitor constraint satisfaction rate.  
- Search stagnation: high proportion of candidates failing checks despite PACE → annealing misconfigured or constraints too strict.  
- Refinement inefficacy: "w/o Refine" ablation shows large OOD NMSE increase → residual analysis fails to identify correct defects.  
- Warm-up failure: "w/o Warmup" degradation → seed skeletons are low-quality.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Single-stage ablation:** Run only Warm-up + Evolution (no Refinement) and only Warm-up + Refinement (no Evolution) to isolate iterative PACE-guided evolution vs. one-shot refinement contributions.  
2. **Prior annealing curve visualization:** For a simple dataset, log average `σ(t)`/`δ(t)` and % valid candidates per generation; verify smooth transition from exploration (high invalid %) to exploitation (low invalid %).  
3. **Constraint checker stress test:** Intentionally inject a wrong constraint (e.g., "db/dt must be positive") and run PG-SR; observe if it fails to find any valid equation (if `f_true` violates it) or finds a poor-fitting compromised equation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the generation of executable prior constraint programs be fully automated to eliminate manual intervention in PG-SR?  
  *Basis:* The conclusion states reliance on manual intervention; future work will explore fine-tuning LLMs to synthesize constraints from problem descriptions and data statistics.
- **Open Question 2:** Under what conditions on the prior constraint set C can we guarantee a strict reduction in Rademacher complexity (i.e., $\mathcal{R}_N(\mathcal{H}_C) < \mathcal{R}_N(\mathcal{H})$) rather than the non-strict bound proved?  
  *Basis:* Remark after Proposition 3.5 notes the bound is non-strict; no formal conditions for strict reduction are provided.
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive is PG-SR to the hyperparameters of the Prior Annealing Constrained Evaluation (PACE) mechanism ($\beta$, $\alpha$, $\eta$, $\text{exp\_base}$), and can they be adapted automatically to problem characteristics like noise level or data scarcity?  
  *Basis:* PACE uses fixed defaults; no sensitivity analysis is conducted.
- **Open Question 4:** Which types of incorrect prior constraints degrade PG-SR performance most severely, and what specific mechanisms in PACE enable robustness to misleading priors?  
  *Basis:* Section 4.6 tests only one "wrong prior" setting and does not analyze diverse constraint error types.

## Limitations
- Human-vetted prior constraints limit full automation and scalability to new domains.
- Experience pool management (logical islands, clustering) is underspecified, requiring implementation interpretation.
- Performance degrades with incorrect priors, though PACE provides partial mitigation.
- High computational cost due to frequent LLM calls (~17.85M tokens/run).
- Theoretical guarantee assumes the true equation lies within the constrained hypothesis space; misspecification invalidates the bound.

## Confidence
- **OOD performance & pseudo-equation avoidance:** High (consistent SOTA across domains, strong ablations).
- **Explicit priors superiority:** Medium (empirical support but hinges on prior correctness; wrong prior ablation shows fragility).
- **PACE necessity:** High (ablation without PACE causes severe performance collapse).
- **Cumulative pipeline benefit:** High (warmup/refine ablations both degrade results).
- **Theoretical complexity reduction:** Medium (bound proved but non-strict; practical strict reduction observed but not theoretically guaranteed).

## Next Checks
1. **Full automation of priors:** Implement an end-to-end automatic prior generation (LLM program synthesis + verification loops) and benchmark against the manual version across all datasets.
2. **Robustness to prior errors:** Systematically perturb constraints (add spurious, remove essential, flip inequalities) to quantify performance impact and identify critical priors; dissect PACE’s role in mitigation.
3. **Theory under misspecification:** Extend Rademacher bounds to the case where `f_true ∉ H_C` and validate on synthetic datasets with controlled constraint violations.
