# SIA: A Synthesize-Inject-Align Framework for Knowledge-Grounded and Secure E-commerce Search LLMs with Industrial Deployment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16137  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16137  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SIA-15B improves e-commerce task accuracy (e.g., EKQA from 23.87 to 58.01) and reduces safety ASR (from 16.68 to 13.65); industrial A/B tests on JD.com show +1.339% CTR in search suggestion, +10% F1 in query correction, and +1.93% CVR in title generation.

## Executive Summary
The SIA framework tackles knowledge hallucination and jailbreak vulnerabilities in e-commerce search large language models (LLMs). It consists of three sequential stages: (1) **Synthesize** – transforms structured product knowledge graphs and unstructured behavioral logs into a high-quality natural language corpus enriched with reasoning chains and safety data; (2) **Inject** – performs parameter-efficient pre-training via Depth Up-Scaling, using hierarchical learning rates and data replay to inject domain knowledge while preserving general capabilities; (3) **Align** – applies a dual-path alignment combining multi-task instruction tuning with adversarial safety training, mixed with general instructions. The resulting SIA-15B model demonstrates substantial gains on e-commerce benchmarks and safety, and real-world deployment on JD.com yields measurable business improvements.

## Method Summary
SIA begins by generating a diverse corpus: a powerful LLM (Deepseek-R1) converts multi-source e-commerce data—product KG triples, reviews, user sessions—into fluent descriptions, Q&A pairs, comparisons, and summaries, while also producing chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and a safety-aware dataset (safe responses, jailbreak prompts, boundary cases). Next, the **Inject** stage expands a base model (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) by inserting additional transformer layers (Depth Up-Scaling), initializing them for residual identity, and training with a 6:4 mix of the synthesized e-commerce data and general-domain replay. A tiered learning rate schedule assigns high rates to new layers, medium to top original layers, and very low/frozen rates to lower layers. Finally, **Align** fine-tunes the knowledge-enriched model on a mixed instruction set (100k examples): 60% e-commerce multi‑tier instructions (simple, composite, constrained), 20% general instructions (Alpaca, Dolly, ShareGPT), and 20% safety-aligned instructions, including 10k adversarial examples generated by a red‑team model. The model is deployed with quantization, dynamic batching, and operator fusion on an Ascend 910B cluster.

## Key Results
- **EKQA accuracy** rises dramatically from 23.87 to 58.01.  
- **Safety attack success rate (ASR)** on JDSec drops from 16.68 to 13.65.  
- **Industrial A/B tests** on JD.com: +1.339% CTR for search suggestion, +10% F1 for query correction, +1.93% CVR for title generation, and improved security across five scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Context-Aware Synthesis
- **Claim:** Synthesizing a natural language corpus from structured knowledge graphs and behavioral logs, augmented with reasoning chains and safety data, effectively mitigates knowledge hallucination by providing dense, context‑rich pre‑training material.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM transforms multi‑source heterogeneous e‑commerce data (product KG triples, reviews, user sessions) into coherent narratives (descriptions, Q&A, comparisons, summaries). This “contextual synthesis” encodes fine‑grained SKU relationships, attribute hierarchies, and user decision logic in a format the base model can absorb efficiently, reducing the gap between structured facts and fluent generation.
- **Core assumption:** A semantically unified, narrative‑based corpus is absorbed more effectively by Transformers than raw structured logs or isolated KG triples, leading to deeper internalization of domain knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper details the context‑aware knowledge transformation process in Section 4.1, stating it “translating multi‑source heterogeneous e‑commerce data into information‑dense, fluent, and natural textual descriptions, thereby significantly enhancing the model’s efficiency in absorbing e‑commerce entities and their complex relationships.” The ablation (w/o Context‑Aware Synthesis) shows a major EKQA drop from 58.01 to 41.89.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM synthesizer (Deepseek‑R1) introduces factual inconsistencies during transformation, the injected knowledge becomes corrupted, potentially worsening hallucination or creating new safety blind spots.

### Mechanism 2: Depth Up‑Scaling with Hierarchical Learning Rates
- **Claim:** Depth Up‑Scaling—expanding model depth via parameter‑efficient layer insertion with tiered learning rates—injects massive domain knowledge while preserving general capabilities, avoiding catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** New transformer layers are inserted between most similar original blocks, initialized for residual identity. A hierarchical learning rate schedule assigns high rates to new layers (fast domain absorption), medium rates to top original layers (style adaptation), and very low/frozen rates to lower layers (preserving universal linguistic features). Concurrently, a 6:4 mix of e‑commerce and general‑domain data provides “knowledge replay.”
- **Core assumption:** The internal representation space of a pre‑trained model can be partitioned such that newly added capacity can be specialized for domain knowledge without disrupting the lower‑level, general‑purpose representations frozen via low learning rates.
- **Evidence anchors:** Sections 5.1–5.2 describe the layer insertion, initialization, and the progressive hierarchical learning rate scheduling with a 6:4 data mix. The ablation shows w/o Depth Up‑Scaling causes a 13.28 drop in EKQA and decreases on C‑Eval/CMMLU; w/o Common Replay improves e‑commerce metrics marginally but harms general benchmarks (C‑Eval drops from 83.98 to 81.07).
- **Break condition:** If layer insertion disrupts residual flow or learning rate tiers are misconfigured (e.g., too high a rate for lower layers), catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge occurs, evidenced by sharp drops on MMLU/C‑Eval.

### Mechanism 3: Dual‑Path Alignment with Data Mix
- **Claim:** A dual‑path alignment strategy—multi‑task instruction tuning for performance and systematic adversarial training for safety—with a carefully mixed data recipe (60% e‑commerce, 20% general, 20% safety) produces a model that excels at domain tasks without sacrificing generalization or compliance.
- **Mechanism:** (Task Path) A three‑tiered instruction set (simple → composite → constrained) on Ecom‑MTIT forces structured output and complex reasoning for e‑commerce scenarios. (Safety Path) A two‑stage process creates 20k domain‑specific safety examples: supervised fine‑tuning on safe Q&A, then red‑teaming (using SIA‑Red) to generate deceptive jailbreaks, which are turned into defense examples. These are blended with general instructions (Alpaca, Dolly, ShareGPT) to prevent over‑specialization.
- **Core assumption:** E‑commerce task performance and safety robustness are orthogonal capabilities that require distinct, dedicated data streams and training objectives; a single general alignment dataset is insufficient. The data mixing ratio is critical to balance these.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 6 details the hierarchical instruction design, the red‑teaming pipeline with SIA‑Red generating 10k jailbreaks, and the final 100k mixed dataset with the 60:20:20 ratio. The ablation shows w/o Hierarchical Instructions hurts EKQA (−8.14) and EQEC (−6.44). w/o Common Instructions harms general benchmarks (C‑Eval −1.15) without major e‑commerce gain. w/o Red‑Teaming Alignment increases ASR by 1.65. w/o Safety (all safety data) raises ASR to 16.20.
- **Break condition:** If the safety data does not cover emerging jailbreak methods or the mixing ratio skews too far toward e‑commerce tasks (>70%), the model loses general usability; if too much general data, e‑commerce task performance degrades, or safety erodes if the safety portion is diluted.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept 1: Continued Pre‑training (CPT) & Catastrophic Forgetting**  
- *Why needed:* The paper’s core premise is that base LLMs lack fine‑grained e‑commerce knowledge. CPT on domain corpora is the primary injection mechanism, but the authors identify forgetting as the central trade‑off to manage via Depth Up‑Scaling and data replay.  
- *Quick check:* What are two complementary techniques (one architectural, one data‑centric) used in the Inject stage to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during CPT?  
  **Answer:** Architectural: Depth Up‑Scaling (inserting new layers). Data‑centric: Knowledge replay (mixing 60% e‑commerce + 40% general data).

**Concept 2: Parameter‑Efficient Fine‑Tuning (PEFT) & Depth Up‑Scaling**  
- *Why needed:* Full fine‑tuning on massive e‑commerce corpora is computationally prohibitive and risks forgetting. The paper’s key technical innovation is a specific PEFT method (Depth Up‑Scaling) that adds parameters in a targeted way. Understanding LoRA/Adapter limitations (cited) contextualizes why depth expansion was chosen.  
- *Quick check:* How does Depth Up‑Scaling differ from LoRA in terms of *where* new parameters are introduced and the assumed mechanism for preserving general knowledge?  
  **Answer:** Depth Up‑Scaling inserts new transformer layers (horizontal expansion) whereas LoRA adds low‑rank matrices to existing layers (vertical adaptation). Depth Up‑Scaling preserves general knowledge by freezing lower original layers with very low learning rates, while LoRA updates all existing weights albeit in a low‑rank subspace.

**Concept 3: Adversarial Training & Red Teaming for Safety**  
- *Why needed:* Standard RLHF/DPO uses general safety data. The paper argues e‑commerce has unique, covert risks (e.g., disguised prohibited items). The safety path relies on generating hard, domain‑specific adversarial examples via a dedicated red‑team model to close these blind spots.  
- *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the two‑stage safety data generation (supervised SFT then red‑teaming attacks), and how does it differ from just using a large generic safety dataset?  
  **Answer:** Supervised SFT provides a baseline of safe responses; red‑teaming generates diverse, challenging jailbreak attempts that are converted into defense examples. This creates hard, domain‑specific adversarial examples that generic safety datasets lack, addressing e‑commerce‑specific risks.

**Concept 4: Instruction Tuning Granularity & Data Mixing**  
- *Why needed:* The Align stage’s effectiveness hinges on a precisely engineered instruction hierarchy and a non‑trivial data mixing ratio. Understanding why single‑step instructions fail and why mixing is necessary (to prevent over‑specialization) is key to reproducing the result.  
- *Quick check:* What is the functional role of the “three‑tiered instruction system” (simple, composite, constrained) in the context of e‑commerce search tasks?  
  **Answer:** It progressively trains the model from straightforward fact retrieval to complex reasoning and constraint satisfaction, mirroring the increasing complexity of e‑commerce search intents and ensuring robust performance across varied query types.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Data Synthesis Pipeline → Knowledge Injection Engine → Alignment Orchestrator → Deployment Stack

**Critical path:** The three main stages—Synthesize (data generation), Inject (CPT with Depth Up‑Scaling), Align (mixed instruction tuning)—must be executed sequentially. Skipping Inject leaves the model without deep domain knowledge, making Align inefficient for complex tasks.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Depth vs. Width:** Chose Depth Up‑Scaling (adding layers) over width expansion or LoRA. Tradeoff: More parameters added but potentially cleaner specialization path vs. lower parameter cost of LoRA.
- **Safety Data Volume vs. Diversity:** Synthesized 10k safe QA + 10k jailbreaks. Tradeoff: Scale vs. coverage. Authors used a red‑team model to generate diverse attack types (20+), betting on adversarial synthesis over pure scale.
- **Mixing Ratio:** 60:20:20 (Ecom:Safety:General). Experimentally determined. Tradeoff: Higher e‑commerce % might boost task metrics but risks forgetting or safety erosion. The ablation shows w/o Common Instructions (20% general) hurts general benchmarks.

**Failure signatures:**
- **High ASR on JDSec:** Primary safety failure. If >14%, indicates insufficient adversarial training coverage or red‑teaming synthesis failure.
- **Drop >5 points on C‑Eval/CMMLU:** Indicates catastrophic forgetting from Inject stage (e.g., learning rates too high on lower layers, or insufficient general replay).
- **EKQA/PAU/EQEC degradation >10% vs. ablation‑full:** Suggests Synthesize data quality issue (e.g., LLM synthesizer producing inconsistent product facts) or Inject stage under‑training.
- **Online CTR/CVR lift <0.5%:** Indicates alignment failure to translate internal knowledge gains into actionable business improvements; likely a data mixing or instruction hierarchy problem.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate Synthesis Quality: Manually inspect 100 samples from each of the 4 synthesis types (Description, Q&A, Comparison, Summary) and the CoT examples. Check for factual consistency against source KG/logs. Reject batch if >5% contain contradictions.
2. Test Depth Up‑Scaling Configuration: Run a small‑scale (1B parameter) ablation. Compare full fine‑tuning, LoRA, and Depth Up‑Scaling on a held‑out e‑commerce QA set and MMLU after 1 epoch. Expect Depth Up‑Scaling to show best e‑commerce gain with smallest MMLU drop.
3. Measure Safety ASR Before/After Red‑Teaming: Evaluate the model after Stage 1 safety SFT (w/o red‑teaming data) on the JDSec jailbreak set. Then evaluate after adding the 10k red‑teaming examples. Expect a minimum 1.5–2 point absolute ASR reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the SIA framework be extended to support dynamic, real‑time knowledge updates for rapidly changing e‑commerce data like new products, promotions, and user trends? The current framework uses a static pre‑training corpus; no incremental training mechanism is provided.
- **Open Question 2:** To what extent does Depth Up‑Scaling outperform other PEFT techniques (LoRA, Adapters) in achieving a durable balance between deep domain knowledge acquisition and long‑term preservation of general reasoning? A direct, comprehensive comparison on the same evaluation suite is absent.
- **Open Question 3:** How robust and generalizable is the SIA safety module, trained on a JD‑specific taxonomy and jailbreak techniques, to novel, evolving attack vectors and to different e‑commerce platforms with varying policies? Evaluation is limited to JD’s internal JDSec set.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the causal contribution of each component within the “Synthesize” stage (Context‑Aware KG+Log fusion, CoT synthesis, Safety corpus) to the final model’s performance, and are there diminishing returns or negative interactions when scaling volume or complexity? The ablation removes the entire synthesis step, obscuring individual effects.
- **Open Question 5:** Does the dual‑path alignment strategy’s fixed 60:20:20 mixing ratio represent the global optimum? A fixed global ratio may under‑optimize specific deployed scenarios (e.g., safety‑critical moderation vs. creative title generation); sensitivity analysis is needed.

## Limitations
- **Data provenance & scale:** Exact size, schema, and diversity of the e‑commerce KG and behavioral logs are unspecified; synthesis prompts/templates for Deepseek‑R1 are proprietary.
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Layer insertion count, learning rates per tier, batch sizes, and total training steps for both pre‑training and alignment stages are not fully detailed.
- **Synthesis fidelity:** Dependency on Deepseek‑R1’s accuracy risks propagating its hallucinations into the corpus; no human validation scores are reported.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Synthesis ablation shows dramatic EKQA drop (58.01 → 41.89) when removing context‑aware transformation.
- **Medium confidence:** Red‑teaming reduces ASR by 1.65 in ablation; however, sample size (20k) and attack diversity coverage are unclear.
- **Low confidence:** Industrial A/B results—business lifts are reported without statistical significance tests or longer‑term retention data.

## Next Checks
