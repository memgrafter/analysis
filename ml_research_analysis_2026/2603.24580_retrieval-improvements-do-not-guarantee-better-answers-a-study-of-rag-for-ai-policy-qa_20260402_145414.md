# Retrieval Improvements Do Not Guarantee Better Answers: A Study of RAG for AI Policy QA

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24580
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24580
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Domain-specific retriever fine-tuning improves retrieval metrics (MRR up to 0.748, Recall@5 up to 0.301) but does not consistently enhance end-to-end QA accuracy; stronger retrieval can even increase confident hallucinations when relevant documents are absent.

## Executive Summary
The paper examines the reliability of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems for AI policy question answering—a domain with dense legal language and rapidly evolving regulations. The authors combine a contrastively fine-tuned ColBERT retriever and a Direct Preference Optimized (DPO) Mistral generator, evaluating on the AGORA corpus. Their core finding is that improvements in traditional retrieval metrics do not translate into consistent gains in final answer quality. The best RAG configuration only marginally surpasses a no-RAG baseline in answer accuracy, while a much larger GPT-5.4 model substantially outperforms it. Critically, enhanced retrieval can exacerbate confident hallucinations when the corpus lacks relevant documents, revealing a decoupling between component-level gains and system-level reliability in policy QA.

## Method Summary
The study builds a RAG pipeline for the AGORA corpus (947 policy documents, 7893 semantic chunks). Training data includes synthetic queries generated from document text and metadata, manual labels for 200 queries (8339 query–positive–negative triples) for retriever training, and a DPO preference set of 2000 pairwise comparisons for generator alignment. The retriever (ColBERTv2) is fine-tuned via contrastive learning (InfoNCE loss) with different negative mining strategies. The generator (Mistral-7B-Instruct) is aligned using DPO with LoRA. Evaluation comprises: retrieval metrics (MRR, Recall@k, MAP@k) on 50 manually labeled queries; end-to-end QA on 300 expert-verified questions using answer accuracy and faithfulness (RAGAS, with Mistral-7B as judge); and manual error analysis. Baselines include base retriever/generator combinations and GPT-5.4.

## Key Results
- Domain-specific retriever fine-tuning yields large gains in retrieval metrics (e.g., MRR up to 0.748, Recall@5 up to 0.301) but produces at most marginal improvement in QA accuracy (e.g., Base ColBERT + Base Mistral: 0.605 vs. no-RAG Base Mistral: 0.581).
- Combining the fine-tuned retriever with a DPO-aligned generator does not improve accuracy and may degrade it (CL ColBERT + DPO Mistral: 0.559).
- Stronger retrieval sometimes increases confident hallucinations when the corpus lacks relevant documents; the retriever returns superficially relevant passages that the generator treats as evidence, leading to well-structured but unsupported answers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Domain retriever fine-tuning improves shallow lexical/semantic match but not end-to-end QA.  
Contrastive learning optimizes for query–passage similarity, boosting recall of passage-level matches. However, complex policy questions often require cross-document synthesis or precise legal nuance that retrieved passages may not contain, so the generator still produces incorrect answers even from apparently relevant context.  
*Evidence:* Tables 1–2 show improved retrieval metrics without corresponding QA gains; the CL retriever variant paired with DPO even lowers accuracy (0.559 vs. 0.605).

**Mechanism 2:** Stronger retrieval amplifies confident hallucinations on missing documents.  
A fine-tuned retriever excels at finding passages with high surface relevance, even when no true answer exists. The generator, conditioned on these passages, interprets them as adequate evidence and produces fluent, confident but unsupported answers.  
*Evidence:* Abstract and Section 5.2 error analysis report cases where the retriever returns outdated or cross-jurisdiction documents; the generator then cites them confidently despite the correct answer being absent.

**Mechanism 3:** Component optimizations are not additive and can interfere.  
The retriever (contrastive loss) and generator (DPO) are trained with different objectives. A retriever fine-tuned for policy-domain similarity may retrieve passages optimal for its objective but suboptimal for the generator’s preference for faithful, concise outputs. Conversely, DPO alignment on a limited dataset may not generalize to the diverse contexts returned by the improved retriever, causing misalignment.  
*Evidence:* Table 1 shows that fine-tuning both components degrades accuracy (0.559) compared to using either component alone (0.601–0.605), despite the CL retriever’s superior retrieval metrics (Table 2).

## Foundational Learning
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
  *Why needed:* The entire study revolves around the RAG architecture; understanding its decoupled retriever–generator pipeline explains why component gains may not improve final answers.  
  *Quick check:* What are the two primary failure modes of a RAG system when the retriever returns passages that are high-scoring but factually misaligned with the query’s intent?

- **Contrastive Learning for Dense Retrieval**  
  *Why needed:* It defines the retriever’s fine-tuning objective (optimizing similarity) and its inherent limitation to surface features rather than factual correctness.  
  *Quick check:* How might a contrastive loss, trained on synthetic queries and manually labeled passages, fail to capture the nuanced legal reasoning needed for a policy comparison question?

- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
  *Why needed:* It explains the generator’s alignment target (style, grounding) and the risk of a train–test mismatch when the retriever distribution changes.  
  *Quick check:* If DPO training data is constructed using a base retriever’s context, what risk arises when the retriever is later changed to a fine-tuned version?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Query → ColBERT Retriever (base or contrastive fine-tuned) → Top‑k Chunks → Mistral‑7B Generator (base or DPO‑aligned) → Answer

**Critical path:**  
1. User query is encoded by the retriever.  
2. Retriever searches the indexed AGORA chunks and returns the top‑k.  
3. The generator prompt combines the query and retrieved chunks.  
4. The generator produces the answer.  
5. Metrics are computed against gold answers or via an LLM judge.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Retriever fine‑tuning on synthetic queries + limited manual labels (8339 triples from ~127 queries) may overfit to synthetic query distribution and not generalize to real expert phrasing.  
- DPO alignment uses ~2000 preference pairs; this small dataset may underfit the diversity of policy contexts the retriever can return.  
- A 7B generator introduces a massive capability gap vs. proprietary models like GPT‑5.4, potentially dominating any RAG component effects.  
- Fixed pre‑chunked corpus limits retrieval to segment boundaries; a correct answer may span multiple chunks.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. High retrieval metrics (e.g., MRR >0.7) but low or unchanged QA accuracy versus no‑RAG baseline.  
2. Hallucinations that cite specific retrieved passages confidently when the correct answer is absent from the corpus.  
3. Cross‑jurisdiction confusion: generator attributes content from one authority to a query about another.  
4. DPO‑aligned generator produces overly concise or vague answers missing policy specifics present in the corpus.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Retrieval‑only eval:** On 50 manually labeled queries, compute MRR, Recall@k for Base ColBERT vs. fine‑tuned variants to confirm contrastive gains.  
2. **End‑to‑end QA eval:** Run full RAG pipeline (each retriever × each generator) on 300‑question test set; measure answer accuracy and faithfulness (RAGAS).  
3. **Error case deep dive:** Manually analyze 20–30 failures where (a) relevant document missing, (b) cross‑jurisdiction error, or (c) complex multi‑constraint query to diagnose root causes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why do retrieval improvements fail to boost QA?**  
   Controlled experiments are needed to isolate the generator’s role while holding retriever quality constant, and to categorize errors where better retrieval leads to worse answers.

2. **How can RAG avoid confident hallucinations when the corpus lacks coverage?**  
   Systematic evaluation with artificially removed or outdated documents is required to understand how retrieval confidence misleads the generator and to design uncertainty calibration.

3. **What reduces cross‑jurisdiction retrieval errors?**  
   The retriever underutilizes metadata (e.g., jurisdiction tags); testing jurisdiction‑aware embedding or re‑ranking strategies could mitigate confusion between similarly named authorities.

4. **Does scaling the generator close the performance gap?**  
   Replicating the pipeline with larger open‑source models (e.g., 70B, 140B) would show whether scale changes the retrieval–QA decoupling or matches large proprietary models.

## Limitations
- Prompt templates for query synthesis and DPO are not fully disclosed, hindering exact reproduction.  
- DPO preference annotation guidelines and annotator expertise are underspecified, affecting reproducibility and assessment of preference quality.  
- The static AGORA corpus lacks recent AI policies and may not cover all evaluation queries; synthetic queries may not reflect real expert question distribution.
