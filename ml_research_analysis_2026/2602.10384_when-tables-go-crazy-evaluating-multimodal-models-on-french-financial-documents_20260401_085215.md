# When Tables Go Crazy: Evaluating Multimodal Models on French Financial Documents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10384
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10384
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** VLMs achieve 85-90% on text extraction, 80-86% on tables, but only 34-62% on chart interpretation; multi-turn conversations collapse to ~50% due to error propagation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MULTIMODALFINANCEEVAL, a benchmark for evaluating vision-language models (VLMs) on French financial documents, addressing a critical gap in multimodal assessment for specialized non-English domains where errors can have serious real-world consequences. The benchmark comprises 1,204 expert-validated questions derived from real prospectuses, key information documents (KIDs), and PRIIPs, covering tasks such as text extraction, table comprehension, chart interpretation, and multi-turn conversational reasoning.

Six open-weight VLMs (8B to 124B parameters) were evaluated using an LLM-as-judge protocol. Results indicate strong performance on text extraction (85-90%) and table comprehension (80-86%), but significant weakness on chart interpretation (34-62%), highlighting a modality-specific reasoning gap. Moreover, in multi-turn conversational settings, error propagation across dialogue turns collapses accuracy to around 50% irrespective of model scale, while using gold-standard prior answers yields much higher accuracy (63-86%). This reveals brittleness in interactive, multi-step financial reasoning and points to the need for better error-handling mechanisms.

## Method Summary
The authors created MULTIMODALFINANCEEVAL, a dataset of 1,204 questions from authentic French financial documents. Tasks are categorized into five groups: Text extraction (501), Table comprehension (488), Chart interpretation (128), Multi-turn conversational (65), and Special cases (22). Inputs consist of pure text contexts, images of tables/charts/pages, or multi-turn dialogues with the document image provided only at the first turn.

For each evaluated VLM, the model is prompted with a task-specific template requiring a concise answer prefixed with "Answer:". Greedy decoding (temperature=0, max_new_tokens≈100) is used. For multi-turn tasks, two conditions are simulated: Standard (the model's own previous answers are included in the dialogue history) and Gold (gold reference answers are inserted). Correctness of predictions is judged by majority vote among three LLM judges (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B-Instruct, Gemma-3-27B-it) using an LLM-as-judge protocol with a fixed judging prompt and five in-context examples.

## Key Results
- **Text extraction:** 85-90% accuracy.
- **Table comprehension:** 80-86% accuracy.
- **Chart interpretation:** 34-62% accuracy, indicating a substantial gap compared to tables.
- **Multi-turn conversations:** Standard condition accuracy drops to 46.2-58.5% (approx. 50%) across all models, while Gold condition reaches 63.1-86.2%, demonstrating severe error propagation independent of model scale.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with chart interpretation because they rely on surface-level pattern matching rather than deeper visual abstraction required to infer trends, comparisons, and implicit values from graphical representations.
- **Mechanism:** Charts encode information through spatial relationships, axis scales, and visual encodings (line slopes, bar heights) that require non-textual reasoning. VLMs pretrained on predominantly text-and-table image data may lack sufficient training on abstract graphical reasoning, causing them to default to text-extraction heuristics (e.g., searching for axis labels) that fail when answers require synthesized interpretation.
- **Core assumption:** Performance gaps between table comprehension (70–86%) and chart interpretation (34–62%) indicate a modality-specific deficiency not explained by general vision or language capability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...chart interpretation remains challenging for all tested models, with scores ranging from 34.4% (Pixtral-12B) to 61.7% (Qwen3-VL-32B)...charts require models to infer trends, relative comparisons, and implicit values that are not directly encoded as text."
  - [section 5] "Unlike tables, charts require models to infer trends, relative comparisons, and implicit values that are not directly encoded as text. The persistent difficulty observed here suggests that current VLMs rely heavily on surface-level pattern matching rather than deeper visual abstraction."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; related work (MultiFinBen, FinTMMBench) also notes multimodal financial reasoning as an open challenge, but citation counts are zero in this snapshot.
- **Break condition:** If future VLMs achieve >80% chart accuracy with comparable table performance, the deficiency may be training-data related rather than architectural.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Error propagation in multi-turn conversational tasks collapses accuracy to ~50% regardless of model size because early mistakes contaminate the dialogue context, and current VLMs lack mechanisms for context-sensitive self-correction or uncertainty tracking.
- **Mechanism:** In conversational settings, each turn appends the model's previous response to the prompt. An incorrect answer becomes part of the input for subsequent turns, creating a "context contamination" cascade. The model does not re-verify prior information against the image/document, leading to compounding errors. The gold-context experiment (oracle previous answers) shows higher and size-correlated performance (63–86%), isolating error propagation as the primary bottleneck.
- **Core assumption:** The performance gap between "Conversational Gold" (correct history) and "Conversational Standard" (model's own history) demonstrates that reasoning capacity exists but is undermined by error accumulation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "multi-turn dialogue reveals a sharp failure mode: early mistakes propagate across turns, driving accuracy down to roughly 50% regardless of model size."
  - [section 5] "the comparison with the Conversational Gold setting indicates that this degradation is not primarily due to a lack of reasoning capacity, but rather to error accumulation and context contamination. Scaling the model does not mitigate this effect."
  - [corpus] ConvFinQA (Chen et al., 2022b) and PACIFIC (Deng et al., 2023) also study conversational financial QA, but this paper's specific finding about error-propagation collapse is novel in the French multimodal setting.
- **Break condition:** Introduction of explicit verification steps, retrieval-augmented correction, or uncertainty-aware decoding that maintains >70% accuracy in standard conversational setting with large models.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Semi-automatic question generation using LLMs introduces coverage and lexical diversity biases, overproducing questions about surface-level, easily extractable facts while underrepresenting complex, multi-source reasoning scenarios.
- **Mechanism:** LLM-based generators trained on general text tend to identify "salient" items that are locally prominent and textually explicit (e.g., a single fee percentage in a table header) rather than globally distributed constraints (e.g., fee schedules conditional on investment amount and share class). This creates an evaluation overfit to simple retrieval, masking weaknesses in integrative reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** The 75% manual revision rate (rewriting/removal) indicates severe misalignment between raw LLM outputs and expert-vetted, diverse financial questions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] "generated questions disproportionately focused on short, easily identifiable facts...more complex information—particularly investment rules or constraints distributed across multiple sections...was less frequently captured."
  - [section 6.2] "We observed limited lexical diversity...Similar formulations were often reused...This required manual revision...to ensure adequate coverage of more challenging reasoning scenarios."
  - [corpus] Faysse et al. (2025) and Xue et al. (2025) also use semi-automatic generation but at smaller scale; this paper's revision statistics highlight the problem.
- **Break condition:** If future automatic pipelines achieve <20% revision rate while maintaining coverage of complex reasoning types, generation bias may be mitigated.

## Foundational Learning
### French Financial Document Taxonomy
- **Why needed here:** The benchmark uses prospectuses, KIDs, and PRIIPs—each has specific regulatory structure, terminology, and standard visualizations (e.g., KID performance tables, PRIIP cost breakdowns). Misunderstanding document type leads to misinterpreting context.
- **Quick check question:** Given a table with "Frais ponctuels" and "Frais courants" rows, which document type is most likely, and what does each row represent conceptually?

### Multimodal Reasoning Modes
- **Why needed here:** Tasks split into extraction (text/table value lookup), interpretation (chart trend inference), and integrative reasoning (conversational multi-step). Each requires different model competencies; conflating them obscures failure analysis.
- **Quick check question:** For a chart showing fund vs. index performance over time with no numeric labels, is the primary challenge visual pattern recognition or arithmetic? What model capability is each?

### Error Propagation Dynamics
- **Why needed here:** The core finding is multi-turn collapse. Understanding this requires distinguishing between *reasoning errors* (wrong logic) and *context contamination* (wrong premise). The gold-context ablation isolates the latter.
- **Quick check question:** If a model answers "5%" to a turn-1 fee question but the correct answer is "3.5%", and turn-2 asks "what is the total cost if entry fee is applied on €10,000?", what are the two distinct ways the turn-2 answer can be wrong?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Document ingest & preprocessing → Context builder → VLM inference engine → Conversation manager (for multi-turn) → Evaluation harness

### Critical Path
Raw PDF → image extraction → prompt construction → VLM inference → answer capture → judge evaluation → accuracy aggregation. The conversational branch adds a loop over turns.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Context representation:* Provide full document page images vs. cropped tables/charts? Full pages preserve layout but increase visual noise; cropping improves focus but risks losing cross-reference context.
- *Evaluation granularity:* Use majority-vote judges vs. single expert? Majority vote scales but may miss nuanced legal/financial errors; expert is gold-standard but costly.
- *Conversation design:* Fixed 5–10 turns vs. dynamic? Fixed allows controlled error-propagation study but may not reflect real user behavior.

### Failure Signatures
- **Chart tasks:** Answers that are numeric but unrelated to chart trends (e.g., stating a fee percentage from document text when asked about performance slope).
- **Conversational tasks:** Sharp accuracy drop after first incorrect turn in "Standard" condition; model repeats or elaborates on its own wrong answer in later turns.
- **Table tasks (image-based):** Misreading merged cells or misaligning row/column headers, especially with complex multi-level headers.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablation on context modality:** Run text-only table questions (from converted text) vs. image-based table questions on same items. Quantify the "image gap" to isolate visual table-reading challenges.
2. **Error injection in conversational gold:** In "Gold" conversations, deliberately corrupt turn-3 answer. Measure whether model recovers by turn-5 vs. standard error propagation. Tests if models have any self-correction when premise is known wrong.
3. **Judge consistency analysis:** For a sample of 100 disputed answers (2 judges correct, 1 incorrect), manually verify. Determine if disagreements stem from numerical equivalence (e.g., "10%" vs "0.1") or truly ambiguous cases, informing judge prompt tweaks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does error propagation in multi-turn financial conversations cause performance to collapse to ~50% regardless of model scale?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Results show accuracy in standard conversational settings (46.2–58.5%) is far below the oracle-gold context (63.1–86.2%), with no scaling benefit. The authors attribute this to error accumulation rather than reasoning limits.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the failure mode but does not investigate whether it stems from contextual contamination, lack of error recovery mechanisms, or architectural limitations in state tracking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments isolating error sources (e.g., injecting correct vs. incorrect prior answers), measuring attention to dialogue history, and evaluating architectures with explicit uncertainty estimation or self-correction modules.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific visual reasoning deficits underlie the persistent weakness in financial chart interpretation (34–62% accuracy) compared to table comprehension (80–86%)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Chart scores are substantially lower across all models. The authors suggest charts require inferring trends and implicit values, unlike explicit table data.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze whether deficits arise from insufficient chart-oriented pretraining, inability to map visual elements to numerical relationships, or over-reliance on text-based features.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained error analysis on chart questions (e.g., misreading axes, comparing values), ablation studies with chart-specific pretraining data, and probing visual attention mechanisms to see if models attend to relevant graphical encodings.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the semi-automatic question generation pipeline be improved to reduce biases toward easily extractable facts and increase lexical and structural diversity?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The discussion notes generated questions over-focused on short, salient facts and showed limited lexical diversity, with 75% revision rate. Tables targeted straightforward extraction over complex relationships.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper acknowledges these issues but does not evaluate or propose concrete methods to steer generation toward more challenging, diverse, and document-grounded questions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative studies using different prompting strategies (e.g., retrieval-augmented generation, diversity penalties), human evaluation of question complexity and coverage, and measuring correlation between question diversity and model performance ceilings.

## Limitations
- French-only benchmark; findings may not transfer to other languages or domains.
- LLM-as-judge protocol lacks human validation; judge biases may affect accuracy numbers.
- Semi-automatic question generation required 75% manual revision, indicating poor coverage of complex reasoning, but the effect on benchmark difficulty is not measured.
- Evaluation used greedy decoding; stochastic sampling could alter rankings.
- Proprietary/larger VLMs were not tested, possibly underrepresenting state-of-the-art capabilities.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-lingual replication:** Run the same evaluation on English financial benchmarks (e.g., MultiFinBen) to verify whether chart weaknesses and multi-turn error propagation generalize.
2. **Judge reliability audit:** Have domain experts evaluate a stratified sample of model answers; measure agreement with LLM-as-judge majority and analyze discrepancies.
3. **Generation pipeline improvement:** Design prompts that explicitly target multi-source integrative reasoning, aim for <20% revision, and re-assess model gaps to test if the benchmark was biased toward simple extraction.
