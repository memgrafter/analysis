# Cumulative Utility Parity for Fair Federated Learning under Intermittent Client Participation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13651
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13651
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposes cumulative utility parity to measure equitable long-term benefit per participation opportunity, achieving Utility CV 0.19 vs. 0.64 (q-FFL) and 0.42 (PHP-FL) on CIFAR-10 with real device traces, while maintaining Jain Utility 0.94 and 80.43% accuracy.

## Executive Summary
In federated learning, intermittent client participation can lead to persistent under-representation of certain clients in the global model, a problem standard per-round fairness metrics fail to capture over time. This paper introduces **cumulative utility parity**, a fairness notion that normalizes each client’s accumulated utility gains by its long-term participation rate. To operationalize this, the authors design two key mechanisms: **inverse-availability sampling** to equalize selection frequency across clients with differing availability, and **stale surrogate updates** to credit absent clients’ contributions. Evaluated on CIFAR-10 with real-world availability traces, the method drastically improves long-term utility equality (Utility CV 0.19) and selection parity without sacrificing global accuracy (80.43%), supported by theoretical guarantees under stationary availability models.

## Method Summary
The proposed system operates over T training rounds. It tracks each client’s cumulative utility \( u_k(T) = \sum_{t=1}^T A_k(t) \Delta F_k(t) \), where \( A_k(t) \) indicates participation and \( \Delta F_k(t) \) is the per-round utility increment (e.g., loss reduction). An availability estimator \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \) approximates the client’s long-term participation rate. The core fairness metric is the availability-normalized utility \( \tilde{u}_k = u_k(T) / \hat{\pi}_k \). Client selection uses a score \( q_k(t) = (1/\hat{\pi}_k(t)) \cdot (1 + \lambda \cdot \text{missed}_k(t)) \), promoting under-available and long-absent clients. For missing clients, a weighted surrogate contribution based on their last local model update is added to their cumulative utility (but not to the global model aggregation), mitigating representation loss. Theoretical results show that under stationary, ergodic availability processes, the variance of normalized utilities converges, and inverse-availability sampling equalizes long-run selection frequencies.

## Key Results
- Achieved **Utility CV 0.19** on CIFAR-10 with real device traces, far outperforming q-FFL (0.64) and PHP-FL (0.42).
- **Jain Utility Index 0.94**, indicating near-perfect equality in long-term normalized benefits.
- Maintained high **average accuracy 80.43%** with the lowest selection gap and Gini coefficient, demonstrating no performance trade-off for improved fairness.
- Theoretical analysis confirms variance reduction of normalized utility and asymptotic selection frequency equality under intermittent availability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Cumulative Utility Normalization
- **Claim:** Tracking cumulative utility normalized by long-term availability exposes persistent under‑representation that per‑round fairness metrics miss.
- **Mechanism:** Log per‑round participation \( A_k(t) \) and utility increment \( \Delta F_k(t) \). Accumulate \( u_k(T) = \sum_{t=1}^T A_k(t)\Delta F_k(t) \). Estimate availability \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \approx \frac{1}{t}\sum_{\tau=1}^t A_k(\tau) \). Compute normalized utility \( \tilde{u}_k = u_k(T)/\hat{\pi}_k \). Monitor variance of \( \{\tilde{u}_k\} \) across clients as a fairness signal.
- **Core assumption:** Availability processes \( \{A_k(t)\} \) are stationary and ergodic with positive mean \( \pi_k > 0 \); utility increments are bounded and have finite mean.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "proposes cumulative utility parity… measuring equitable long‑term benefit per participation opportunity via availability‑normalized utility."
  - [Section 2, Lemma 1] Derives convergence of normalized utility variance under stationary availability.
  - [Section 3.1] Describes logging and computation of cumulative utility and normalization.
- **Break condition:** If availability drifts non‑stationarily faster than the estimator \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \) can track (Appendix C), variance may not collapse, masking persistent skew.

### Mechanism 2: Inverse‑Availability Sampling
- **Claim:** Inverse‑availability sampling equalizes long‑run selection frequency across clients, compensating for heterogeneity in \( \pi_k \).
- **Mechanism:** At round \( t \), among currently available clients \( A_t \), compute weight \( q_k(t) = 1/\hat{\pi}_k(t) \). Select \( m \) clients with probability proportional to \( q_k(t) \). Optionally augment with reactive term based on missed rounds (Theorem 2).
- **Core assumption:** Estimators \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \to \pi_k \) and availability processes are stationary/ergodic; selection is conditional on availability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Implements inverse‑availability sampling… for missing clients."
  - [Section 2, Lemma 2] Proves asymptotic selection frequency \( E[S_k(T)]/T \to m/N \) under inverse‑availability weighting.
  - [Section 3.2] Shows fairness variance decreasing with inverse‑availability × missed‑round reweighting vs. random sampling.
- **Break condition:** Highly bursty or adversarial availability violating ergodicity; poor \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \) estimation (e.g., too few observations) leads to mis‑weighting and amplified skew.

### Mechanism 3: Stale Surrogate Updates
- **Claim:** Stale surrogate updates for unavailable clients mitigate representation loss by approximating their contribution, bounded by a decay factor.
- **Mechanism:** When client \( k' \) is missing at round \( t \), retrieve its last model at time \( \tau_{k'} < t \). Compute surrogate loss/gradient on its fixed local data. Weight surrogate contribution by \( \eta_{k'}(t) = \eta_0 e^{-\lambda \delta_{k'}} \) where \( \delta_{k'} = t - \tau_{k'} \). Add to aggregation with decay.
- **Core assumption:** Surrogate error \( \|\tilde{F}_{k'}(w) - F_{k'}(w)\| \) is uniformly bounded by \( \epsilon \); staleness \( \delta_{k'} \) increases error monotonically.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "surrogate updates for missing clients."
  - [Section 2, Lemma 3 & Theorem 3] Bound aggregate bias from surrogate error and its staleness‑weighted impact.
  - [Section 3.3] Table 1 shows surrogate contribution grows then stabilizes; accuracy improves while fairness variance eventually declines.
- **Break condition:** Data distributions shift rapidly (concept drift) so stale surrogates become biased beyond \( \epsilon \); \( \lambda \) not tuned to drift rate causing excessive weight on outdated information.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Server → Client → Logging
- **Critical path:**
  1. Server observes available clients via \( A_k(t) \).
  2. Update \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \) and compute selection scores \( q_k(t) \).
  3. Select subset \( S_t \) (fair branch) or random (vanilla baseline).
  4. For each selected client: local training, send update.
  5. For missing clients: retrieve surrogate, compute weighted contribution.
  6. Aggregate updates (available + surrogate), update global model.
  7. Evaluate per‑client accuracy, compute cumulative utility \( \tilde{u}_k \), record metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Inverse‑availability weight sensitivity: Over‑emphasis on low‑\( \pi_k \) clients can hurt model performance if their data is small/noisy.
  - Surrogate staleness: Larger \( \lambda \) decays faster, reducing stale bias but weakening representation for long‑absent clients.
  - Estimation horizon: Shorter window for \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \) adapts faster to non‑stationarity but increases variance.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Utility CV > 0.5 indicates persistent disparity despite compensation.
  - Selection Gap \( \ell_1 \) > 0.2 shows selection imbalance.
  - Accuracy drop > 5% relative to vanilla suggests over‑correction.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Run 50 rounds on CIFAR‑10 with real traces; compare our method (with/without surrogate) against q‑FFL and PHP‑FL on Utility CV, Jain(Utility), Selection Gap, Avg Acc. Expect Utility CV ≤ 0.28, Jain ≥ 0.88.
  2. **Ablation on surrogates:** Disable surrogates; observe surrogate contribution drops to 0, fairness variance may rise initially, accuracy possibly lower on rare classes.
  3. **Reactive weight sensitivity:** Vary \( \lambda \) in Theorem 2's \( p_k(t) \); plot Selection Gap vs. \( \lambda \). Expect moderate \( \lambda \approx 0.5–0.7 \) minimizes gap without harming accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the proposed framework perform under adversarial or fully bursty client participation patterns, where availability is neither stationary nor ergodic?
- **Basis:** The paper states guarantees are not intended for adversarial or fully bursty participation.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical guarantees rely on stationarity, ergodicity, and i.i.d. Bernoulli processes; adversarial attacks could violate these.
- **Resolution evidence:** Formal analysis extending convergence results to non‑stationary models (e.g., martingale theory); empirical stress tests with coordinated dropout attacks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal design and theoretical impact of surrogate update mechanisms (e.g., weighting, staleness decay) on long‑term cumulative utility parity and convergence?
- **Basis:** Lemma 3 and Theorem 3 bound surrogate error but assume uniform error bounds and exponential decay; interaction with cumulative utility variance is not modeled.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical development separates surrogate error from core availability‑compensation guarantee; interplay between staleness, weighting, and normalization is unanalyzed.
- **Resolution evidence:** Incorporate surrogate error terms into variance decomposition (Theorem 1 proof); experiments varying \( \lambda \) and \( \eta_0 \) to isolate effect on Utility CV and Jain's index.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can cumulative utility parity be defined and enforced without explicit knowledge or estimation of client availability \( \pi_k \), and what are the robustness properties of such an approach?
- **Basis:** Operationalization (Equation 2) requires normalizing by \( \pi_k \); Lemma 2 and Theorem 2 assume \( \hat{\pi}_k(t) \to \pi_k \).
- **Why unresolved:** Guarantees depend critically on accurate \( \pi_k \) estimates; poor estimation (volatility, short traces) could exacerbate disparity.
- **Resolution evidence:** A fairness definition based on observable selection counts alone (e.g., competitive ratios); empirical evaluation under noisy or biased \( \hat{\pi}_k \) estimators.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does cumulative utility parity interact with other canonical fairness notions (e.g., demographic parity, equalized odds) when client attributes (e.g., data distribution groups) correlate with availability?
- **Basis:** Fairness is defined per client; paper uses label‑skew partitions but does not tie availability to protected attributes or evaluate intersectional fairness.
- **Why unresolved:** Intermittent participation may correlate with sensitive characteristics (e.g., device age, region); achieving utility parity may not ensure fairness across groups.
- **Resolution evidence:** Experiments with synthetic partitions where availability correlates with a sensitive attribute; measure both proposed metrics and group‑based fairness (accuracy disparity).

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What is the computational and communication overhead of the proposed inverse‑availability sampling and surrogate mechanisms in large‑scale, heterogeneous FL systems with thousands of clients?
- **Basis:** Evaluation uses 100 clients; per‑client state (availability estimates, cumulative utility, checkpoints) and surrogate transmission add overhead not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** Realistic FL systems involve millions of devices; per‑client state storage and update frequency could be prohibitive; communication from surrogates is unquantified.
- **Resolution evidence:** Complexity analysis (time/space per client vs. total N); large‑scale simulations (e.g., N=10,000) measuring wall‑clock time, memory, and communication volume.

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Availability trace binarization, selection batch size `m`, local training hyperparameters (epochs, LR), aggregation weighting, surrogate credit parameters (`η₀`, `λₛ`), and exact baseline code hinder faithful replication.
- **Theoretical assumptions:** Stationarity/ergodicity of availability and bounded utility increments may not hold in real deployments; break conditions (non‑stationarity, burstiness) could reduce parity gains.

## Confidence
- **Cumulative utility parity:** Medium (Lemma 1 sound under stationarity, but empirical evidence limited to CIFAR‑10 with one trace set).
- **Inverse‑availability sampling:** Medium‑high (asymptotic guarantee strong, but depends on accurate `π̂` estimation; early‑phase bias not fully explored).
- **Stale surrogate updates:** Medium (bounds assume uniform error and slow drift; real concept drift may exceed bounds).
- **Overall superiority claim (Utility CV 0.19, Jain 0.94, no accuracy loss):** Medium (single‑dataset evaluation; potential hyperparameter overfitting).
