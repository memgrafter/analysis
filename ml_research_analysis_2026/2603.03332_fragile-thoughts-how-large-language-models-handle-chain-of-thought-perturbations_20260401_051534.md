# Fragile Thoughts: How Large Language Models Handle Chain-of-Thought Perturbations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03332
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03332
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** Model robustness to chain-of-thought perturbations varies significantly by perturbation type and scales heterogeneously, with MathError showing the steepest improvement with scale and UnitConversion remaining universally challenging.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the robustness of large language models (LLMs) to five distinct types of chain-of-thought (CoT) perturbations—MathError, UnitConversion, Sycophancy, SkippedSteps, and ExtraSteps—by measuring accuracy degradation on GSM8K math problems when errors are injected into partial reasoning traces. Across 13 models ranging from 3B to 1.5T parameters, the findings reveal that perturbation-specific robustness does not universally improve with scale: MathError susceptibility drops dramatically in larger models, UnitConversion errors persist even in the largest models, and ExtraSteps incur minimal cost regardless of size. The work underscores that LLM reasoning reliability is fragile and contingent on the nature of the disturbance, challenging the notion that scale alone yields broadly robust reasoning.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses the GSM8K dataset of grade-school math word problems. For each problem, a correct chain-of-thought trace is first obtained. Five perturbation types are then applied to modify these partial traces: deterministic rule-based injections for MathError (altering an arithmetic result), SkippedSteps (removing a step), and Sycophancy (appending a misleading expert equation); and LLM-assisted generation (using Claude Sonnet 4.5) for UnitConversion (mid-trace unit change) and ExtraSteps (adding irrelevant but plausible context). The perturbed trace is provided as a prefix to the model, which generates the final answer. Accuracy is computed by comparing the extracted final answer to the ground truth. The primary metric is degradation: ΔAcc = Acc_clean − Acc_perturbed. Scaling relationships are analyzed by correlating ΔAcc with model parameter count across 13 models.

## Key Results
- **MathError** causes the largest average accuracy drop (−18%), with small models (e.g., 3B) losing 50–60% and large models (e.g., 235B–1.5T) only 5–10%, indicating strong scaling benefits.
- **UnitConversion** remains highly challenging at all scales, with 20–30% degradation even for the largest models, and shows no consistent scaling trend.
- **ExtraSteps** incur negligible degradation (0–6%) across all models, with a nearly flat scaling slope (−0.001), while **Sycophancy** and **SkippedSteps** yield modest, model-sensitive effects (averaging −3% and −2% respectively).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Robustness to explicit arithmetic errors (MathError) scales with model size.** Larger models appear to develop internal verification mechanisms that detect and correct incorrect intermediate calculations, likely due to richer procedural knowledge and improved error detection during token prediction. This is evidenced by the steep negative slope of ΔAcc vs. log(size) for MathError (−0.170), where small models suffer catastrophic failure while large models are largely resilient.

**Mechanism 2: Unit coherence (UnitConversion) is universally fragile.** Tracking and maintaining unit consistency requires explicit symbolic manipulation and semantic grounding, which is not adequately learned through next-token prediction alone. Even the largest models fail to recognize mid-calculation unit switches, suggesting dimensional reasoning is an out-of-distribution challenge. This is supported by persistent 20–30% degradation across the scale spectrum and examples where models compute in wrong units without noticing.

**Mechanism 3: Models efficiently filter irrelevant context (ExtraSteps).** The minimal impact of extraneous steps suggests that models have learned to down-weight non-essential information, a skill acquired early in training that does not scale with size. The near-zero scaling slope (−0.001) and stable performance across models indicate that robustness to superficial noise is a general language modeling capability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** GSM8K dataset -> clean CoT trace generator -> perturbation generators (rule-based: MathError, SkippedSteps, Sycophancy; LLM-augmented: UnitConversion, ExtraSteps using Claude Sonnet 4.5) -> model inference API (13 LLMs) -> answer extraction -> accuracy comparison -> ΔAcc aggregation.

**Critical path:** 1. Load GSM8K problem and its full solution. 2. Extract a partial correct trace τ. 3. Apply perturbation type p to produce τ′. 4. Prompt model with τ′ and collect completion. 5. Extract final integer answer. 6. Compare to ground truth to compute Acc_perturbed. 7. Repeat with clean τ to get Acc_clean. 8. Aggregate ΔAcc across problems for model-perturbation pair.

**Design tradeoffs:** Rule-based perturbations (deterministic, reproducible, but potentially unnatural) vs. LLM-generated perturbations (more fluent and context-aware, but introduce variability and cost). The study混合 uses both to balance plausibility and control.

**Failure signatures:** (a) **Blind propagation** (model accepts erroneous value and builds on it), (b) **Explicit correction** (model identifies and fixes error), (c) **Reinterpretation** (model changes problem understanding due to perturbation), (d) **Unit cascading** (model computes in wrong units without noticing), (e) **Noise absorption** (model ignores extra context).

**First 3 experiments:** 1. Run MathError on smallest (Ministral 3B) and largest (GPT 5.2 / Qwen 235B) models to confirm ~50% vs ~5% degradation pattern. 2. Apply UnitConversion to all 13 models to verify no model achieves near-zero degradation. 3. Inject ExtraSteps into a mid-scale model (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B) to establish minimal degradation (<2%) as typical.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** To what extent are LLMs genuinely performing step-by-step logical verification versus exploiting surface-level pattern matching when producing chain-of-thought outputs? The study measures accuracy degradation but does not probe internal verification processes.
- **Open Question 2:** Do the reported scaling relationships generalize to longer reasoning chains, harder problems, or perturbations injected at greater depths? All experiments used GSM8K problems with 3–10 steps.
- **Open Question 3:** Can targeted training paradigms (rather than scale alone) mitigate specific perturbation vulnerabilities, particularly UnitConversion? The 13 models differ in both scale and training, confounding effects; UnitConversion remains challenging even in 1.5T models.
- **Open Question 4:** What architectural or training factors cause the heterogeneous scaling slopes across perturbation types (e.g., MathError slope −0.170 vs. ExtraSteps −0.001)? The underlying reasons are unexplored.

## Limitations
- Perturbation generation details for LLM-assisted types (prompts, parameters) are unreported, risking replication inconsistency.
- GSM8K test split size and sampling strategy across perturbations are unspecified, potentially affecting ΔAcc stability.
- Model scale estimates for closed-source models are inferred, not confirmed, weakening regression analysis anchoring.

## Confidence
- **MathError scaling robustness:** High (strong empirical trend across 13 models, consistent with arithmetic scaling).
- **UnitConversion universal fragility:** Medium (clear result, but mechanism speculative; prompt engineering might mitigate).
- **ExtraSteps minimal impact & no scaling:** Low (effect size small, mechanism vague; may not generalize to other noise types/domains).

## Next Checks
1. **Independent scaling replication:** Re-run MathError perturbation on additional open-weight models (e.g., Yi-34B, Phi-3.5) to verify the steep negative slope trend.
2. **Unit prompt sensitivity test:** For UnitConversion, add explicit unit-output specifications (e.g., "answer in meters") to test if fragility persists.
3. **Semantic distraction benchmark:** Create a perturbation variant where "extra steps" introduce competing reasoning paths (e.g., alternative interpretations) to probe the boundary of ExtraSteps robustness.
