# Kernel Dynamics under Path Entropy Maximization

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.27880
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27880
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Develops a Maximum Caliber framework for kernel dynamics, deriving self-consistent fixed points, linking RG flow as a special case, proposing NTK evolution as an empirical testbed, and establishing a thermodynamic work bound for representational change.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework to describe how kernel functions—representing an agent's distinctions—evolve over time. By lifting Maximum Caliber (path entropy maximization) to trajectories in kernel space, kernels become dynamical variables subject to an endogenous optimization landscape. The authors derive fixed-point conditions for self-consistent kernels, show that renormalization group flow is a structured special case, propose neural tangent kernel evolution during deep network training as an empirical instantiation, and prove a thermodynamic work bound δW ≥ k_B T δI_k linking representational change to minimal energy cost. Stable fixed points map to self-reinforcing structures like biological niches or craft mastery.

## Method Summary
The core method extends Maximum Caliber to the space of Mercer kernels 𝕂 equipped with the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. A path measure Π over Bochner‑integrable trajectories γ: [0,T] → 𝕂 is defined relative to a reference measure Q (e.g., a conditioned Wiener process on HS operators). The MaxCal distribution P[γ] maximizes path entropy S[P] subject to constraints on expected thermodynamic work δW, mutual information gain δI_k, and a consistency term D_KL. For a discrete two‑kernel toy model, the approach yields explicit transition probabilities governed by a trade‑off between switching cost and information gain. In continuous settings, the frozen‑kernel entropy S*(k) (the optimal path entropy given a fixed kernel) is used to compute Fréchet derivatives, giving fixed‑point conditions ∇_k S*(k)=0 and a stability criterion via the Hessian D²_k S*|_k*. The thermodynamic bound δW ≥ k_B T δI_k emerges from the variational principle under quasi‑static assumptions.

## Key Results
- Fixed‑point conditions for self‑consistent kernels where the kernel is stationary under its own induced geometry.
- Renormalization group (RG) flow identified as a structured special case of the MaxCal dynamics.
- Neural tangent kernel (NTK) evolution during deep network training proposed as a testable empirical instantiation.
- Thermodynamic work bound δW ≥ k_B T δI_k, linking representational change to minimal energy cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Kernel evolution under MaxCal yields self‑consistent fixed points where the kernel is stationary under its own induced geometry.
- **Mechanism:** The kernel k defines an RKHS and thus an information geometry on probability space. A MaxCal distribution over kernel trajectories γ(t) ∈ 𝕂 maximizes path entropy S[P] subject to constraints. The fixed‑point condition δS/δγ|_(γ≡k*) = 0 means k* is the maximum‑caliber choice given the geometry it itself induces.
- **Core assumption:** (A3) Kernels are Mercer and Hilbert‑Schmidt, ensuring 𝕂 is a separable metric space via the HS norm, well‑defined for Bochner‑integrable trajectories.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement of self‑consistent kernels; Section V Definition 3 and stability criterion (16); lack of direct evidence in neighboring literature.
- **Break condition:** Non‑concavity of S*(k) near k* may cause instability, leading to bifurcations between distinction regimes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Renormalization Group (RG) flow is a structured special case of MaxCal over kernel space.
- **Mechanism:** RG coarse‑graining integrates out short‑scale degrees of freedom, defining a flow dk/dlnΛ = β(k). Under MaxCal, this can be mapped if the constraint enforces scale‑invariance of a partition function and the reference measure Q is uniform over the RG orbit. Fixed points β(k*)=0 then coincide with self‑consistent kernels.
- **Core assumption:** The RG semigroup action on kernels can be embedded into the path measure space Π with a suitable Q. Requires a precise mapping between RG transformations and HS‑norm trajectories.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section VI.A Proposition 1 states the correspondence as a "structural correspondence"; abstract mentions RG as a special case; neighboring work lacks this integration.
- **Break condition:** If the RG transformation cannot be expressed as a Hamiltonian path constraint within the MaxCal variational principle (e.g., involves non‑local information loss not captured by I_k), the mapping fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) evolution during finite‑width network training obeys a MaxCal trajectory with a thermodynamic work bound δW ≥ k_B T δI_Θ.
- **Mechanism:** The NTK Θ_t evolves with training. MaxCal predicts its trajectory maximizes path entropy subject to constraints like loss reduction rate. The work to change the kernel is bounded by the newly unlocked mutual information δI_Θ between network outputs and data under the updated kernel.
- **Core assumption:** (A4–A6) The agent (network) and environment (data) admit a joint distribution; thermodynamic bookkeeping is quasi‑static so Landauer’s bound k_B T per nat is attainable; NTK is a valid element of 𝕂 (Mercer, HS).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section VI.B Conjecture 3 links trained NTK to Hellinger kernel k_H; empirical protocol proposes testing via GPU power draw and representation statistics; Section IV.D derives general bound δW_k ≥ k_B T δI_k.
- **Break condition:** If NTK evolution is dominated by noise or optimization inertia rather than an entropy‑maximizing principle under the given constraints, the MaxCal distribution will not describe the observed trajectory. The bound may be violated if assumptions (A4–A6) fail.

## Foundational Learning

- **Maximum Caliber (MaxCal)**
  - **Why needed:** It is the variational principle lifted from state paths to kernel paths; without it the core optimization over P[γ] is opaque.
  - **Quick check:** How does the MaxCal distribution p[γ] ∝ q[γ] exp(−∑λ_i f_i[γ]) differ from a standard maximum entropy posterior, and what role does the reference measure q[γ] play?

- **Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) Geometry**
  - **Why needed:** The kernel k is the primitive; its induced RKHS defines the representational subspace. The Fisher‑Rao metric and Hellinger kernel are derived given a fixed k. Kernel change means changing this foundational geometry.
  - **Quick check:** If k changes, how does the inner product ⟨ϕ(x), ϕ(x')⟩_H_k and thus the notion of "similarity" between data points x, x' change?

- **Information‑Thermodynamic Link (Sagawa‑Ueda, Landauer)**
  - **Why needed:** Provides the *cost* of kernel change (δW ≥ k_B T δI_k) and anchors the abstract MaxCal dynamics in physical work, making the framework testable via energy consumption.
  - **Quick check:** Why does acquiring *new* mutual information (δI_k > 0) require a minimum work investment, and how does this differ from the cost of *erasing* information?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Kernel Space (𝕂) of Mercer kernels -> Path Measure (Π) over Bochner‑integrable trajectories -> Reference Measure (Q) (e.g., conditioned Wiener process on HS operators) -> Constraints (C: switching cost, G: cumulative I_k, D_KL consistency) -> Optimization (variational inference on path measures).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Define 𝕂 and d_HS for a simple dataset (e.g., Gaussian kernel on ℝ²).
  2. Implement the two‑kernel toy model (Section IV.A): discrete states, Markov Q, constraints C and G; compute P[γ] and transition odds (11).
  3. Scale to continuous 𝕂: parameterize k_t (spectral decomposition), define I_k(t), implement gradient‑based max‑path‑entropy (stochastic control).
  4. Add thermodynamic cost constraint via (13) and compute ∇_k I_k.
  5. Identify fixed points by solving ∇_k S*(k)=0 and check Hessian (16) for stability.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Approximating I_k online is expensive; consider variational bounds or finite‑sample estimators.
  - HS norm integrals require discretization; trade grid resolution for speed.
  - Reference measure Q: a simple Markov chain is easy but may not capture smooth changes; a Wiener process is more physical but requires handling PSD constraints.
  - Thermodynamic cost ∫ ˙W_k dt must be computed via δI_k; a surrogate like kernel alignment may be used initially.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Non‑convergence of k_t: check Q mixing and step size; may indicate too strong constraints or poor Q.
  - k_t leaving 𝕂 (losing PSD): project back (eigen‑value clipping) but this breaks the variational derivation; sign of too large Δt or ˙k.
  - Inflexible trajectories: if λ_G (information gain weight) is too low relative to λ_C (switching cost), kernel never changes; adjust weights.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Two‑kernel toy validation:** Replicate Section IV‑A. Verify transition odds (11) match analytical threshold λ_G ΔI > λ_C via Monte Carlo sampling of γ.
  2. **RG flow mapping:** For a simple 1D φ⁴ theory, coarse‑grain to integrate out high frequencies, obtain k_Λ' from the effective action. Check if discrete steps k_Λ → k_Λ' maximize path entropy under a scale‑invariance constraint.
  3. **NTK thermodynamics:** Train a small finite‑width CNN on MNIST with recorded wall‑power. Estimate Θ_t periodically. Compute a proxy for I_Θ(t) (e.g., mutual information between penultimate layer features and labels via k‑NN estimator). Test if ΔW/Δt ≥ c ΔI_Θ/Δt holds, vary learning rate and width.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** What is the geodesic in (K, d_HS) between two self‑consistent kernels, and does it pass through a saddle point corresponding to a Kuhnian crisis?
  - **Basis:** Question 1 in Section IX.
  - **Why unresolved:** Geodesics in kernel space are uncharacterized; link to paradigm shifts is conjectural.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Compute geodesics in finite‑dimensional kernel families or prove properties in infinite dimensions.

- **Open Question 2:** Can the conjectured bound a(x) ≥ c ||k_x||_H be proved or disproved for specific model systems (e.g., small organic molecules)?
  - **Basis:** Question 3 in Section IX.
  - **Why unresolved:** Assembly index–RKHS complexity link is unproven.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Formal proof for a class of molecules or counterexample via explicit computation.

- **Open Question 3:** Does the NTK of a trained diffusion model converge to the Hellinger kernel k_H of the data distribution in the infinite‑width limit?
  - **Basis:** Question 4 in Section IX.
  - **Why unresolved:** NTK convergence to Hellinger kernel is an untested prediction.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Train wide diffusion models, estimate NTK at convergence, compare with Hellinger kernel from data.

- **Open Question 4:** Can an online kernel‑adaptation policy for dynamic algal blooms improve forecast skill at fixed energy and sample count relative to fixed‑kernel sampling, including for heterogeneous ASV–AUV missions?
  - **Basis:** Question 5 in Section IX.
  - **Why unresolved:** The adaptive planning scheme is described but not empirically evaluated.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Simulations or field trials comparing adaptive vs fixed kernel under identical resource constraints.

- **Open Question 5:** Can the thermodynamic cost bound (13) predict the timescale of scientific paradigm shifts from citation network data?
  - **Basis:** Question 6 in Section IX.
  - **Why unresolved:** Applying the work bound to citation dynamics is speculative; no validation exists.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Construct time‑indexed similarity kernels from paper embeddings, compute kernel trajectory and mutual information change, correlate with historical shift durations.

## Limitations
- Reference measure Q: Construction of a Wiener process on HS operators conditioned to the PSD cone is asserted but not derived, critical for continuous trajectory sampling.
- Computable S*(k): The frozen‑kernel entropy and its Hessian are defined abstractly; no closed‑form for general kernels, blocking numerical stability analysis.
- NTK empirical link: The thermodynamic work bound applied to NTK training relies on assumptions (A4–A6) about physical realizability and quasi‑static processes, which are not validated for finite‑width Gradient Descent.

## Confidence
- Fixed‑point self‑consistency: Medium
- RG as special case: Low
- NTK thermodynamic bound: Low
