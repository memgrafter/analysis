# OdysseyArena: Benchmarking Large Language Models For Long-Horizon, Active and Inductive Interactions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05843
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05843
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs face a fundamental inductive discovery bottleneck; even frontier models achieve ≤44.17% average success without explicit transition rules, far below human performance.

## Executive Summary
OdysseyArena introduces a new inductive evaluation paradigm for LLM agents, requiring them to autonomously discover latent transition laws through long-horizon active interaction. It formalizes four environment primitives—discrete symbolic rules, continuous stochastic dynamics, periodic temporal patterns, and relational graph structures—and provides standardized benchmarks (OdysseyArena-Lite with 120 tasks and OdysseyArena-Challenge with >1000 steps). Evaluating 15+ leading LLMs reveals that even the strongest models perform far below human baselines. Providing agents with explicit transition rules eliminates this gap, identifying inductive discovery as the core limitation. Performance saturates with extended interaction steps, and high action-loop ratios indicate fundamental bottlenecks in long-horizon world-model induction.

## Method Summary
The benchmark generates solvable tasks across four primitives, each encoding hidden transition functions *T*(s<sub>t</sub>, a<sub>t</sub>) → (s<sub>t+1</sub>, r<sub>t</sub>). Tasks use pre-sampled deterministic trajectories to ensure reproducibility. Agents interact via a simple API: `<init>(config)`, `<step>(action) → observation`, `<eval>() → success`. History management retains only actions and feedback (omitting reasoning traces) to fit long horizons. Evaluation uses Avg@4 (average success over 4 runs) and Pass@4 (fraction of tasks succeeded in ≥1 run). For reproduction, one clones the repository, generates fixed tasks from provided seeds, configures model inference (open-source via VLLM, proprietary via API), runs the evaluation script with 4 rollouts per task, and aggregates metrics. Key hyperparameters: temperature 0.6, stop tokens `</action>` and `</finish>`, task-specific step limits (e.g., 200 for Turn On Lights).

## Key Results
- Frontier LLMs perform poorly: Gemini 3 Pro Preview reaches 44.17% average success; GPT-5 28.33%; humans near-perfect on discrete tasks and 92.55% profit in trading.
- Explicit transition rules in prompts close the performance gap completely, isolating inductive discovery as the bottleneck.
- Success rates plateau early with increased interaction steps (up to 200), and high action-loop ratios strongly predict failure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit a fundamental inductive discovery bottleneck—they cannot autonomously synthesize latent transition laws from interaction.
- **Mechanism:** The benchmark shifts evaluation from deductive rule-following to inductive world-model induction. Hidden transition functions *T* can only be solved by agents that actively probe and distill dynamics. This exposes whether agents learn mechanics from scratch.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] Explicit rules eliminate the gap. [section 5.2] Contrast between deductive proficiency and inductive deficiency. [section 6.3] High loop ratio correlates with diminished success.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Performance saturates with more interaction steps, indicating the barrier is not data volume but inductive architecture.
- **Mechanism:** Even with step budgets up to 200, most models reach a success plateau early. More interactions do not yield improvement, suggesting agents fail to accumulate and integrate knowledge about *T* over time.
- **Evidence:** [section 6.2] Negligible marginal gains beyond initial phase. [section B.3] Saturation as step increases shows long-horizon bottleneck.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Action loops are a behavioral signature of inductive stagnation—agents cannot update their world model to avoid ineffective actions.
- **Mechanism:** Failure to infer latent rules prevents causal interpretation of feedback, leading to repetitive execution of unproductive (state, action) pairs.
- **Evidence:** [section 6.3] Prevalent failure mode of persistent action loops. [section B.5] Loop Ratio definition and strong negative correlation with success.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Inductive vs. Deductive Reasoning in Agents
- **Why needed:** The paper’s central thesis is that evaluation overemphasizes deductive reasoning (applying explicit rules) while neglecting inductive reasoning (discovering rules from experience). Grasping this distinction is essential to understand OdysseyArena’s purpose.
- **Quick check:** What would an agent need to do differently to solve a puzzle where the rules are hidden versus provided? Which requires building a model from interaction data?

### Concept: Transition Functions and Latent World Models
- **Why needed:** Environments are defined by *T*(s<sub>t</sub>, a<sub>t</sub>) → (s<sub>t+1</sub>, r<sub>t</sub>), with *T* representing unobservable regularities. Understanding this formalism clarifies what “latent transition laws” means and why inducing *T* is the core challenge.
- **Quick check:** If you only see sequences of (state, action, next state) pairs, how could you infer the deterministic function connecting them? What makes this harder when the function involves logical dependencies or periodic components?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Task Generator → Deterministic Trajectory Store → API-based Environment Interface → LLM Agent Wrapper → Evaluation Harness → Analysis Suite

### Critical path
1. Load a curated task (structural config + deterministic trajectory).
2. For each episode: initialize state, loop until max steps or termination:
   - Agent receives formatted observation (history + current state).
   - Model generates action via inference.
   - Environment applies *T*, updates state, returns feedback.
3. After episode, evaluate final state against success criteria.
4. Repeat across 4 random seeds, aggregate results.

### Design tradeoffs
1. **Context management vs. state richness:** Retains only history of actions and feedback, omitting intermediate reasoning traces. Enables long horizons but discards potentially useful reasoning.
2. **Deterministic trajectories vs. stochastic realism:** All randomness pre-sampled. Guarantees reproducibility and fair comparison but eliminates in-episode stochasticity that could test robustness.
3. **Solvability guarantee vs. maximum difficulty:** Tasks generated via “solution-first” methods ensure at least one solution exists. Prevents impossible tasks but may cap upper difficulty compared to unsolvable distributions.
4. **API lightweight design vs. simulator fidelity:** Scripted logic, not full simulators. Enables rapid evaluation but may miss complexities of real-world systems (e.g., physics, human behavior).

### Failure signatures
1. **Action loops:** Repeated identical (state, action) pairs with no state change.
2. **Performance saturation:** Success rate plateaus early; cumulative success vs. step flattens.
3. **Max-step concentration:** Histogram of steps used spikes at maximum allowed steps.
4. **Rule-provided gap:** Large performance delta between no-rules and explicit-rules conditions.
5. **Environment-specific collapse:** Zero success on specific environments (e.g., Energy Dispatch for open-source models).

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline benchmarking:** Run all 15+ models on OdysseyArena-Lite (120 tasks, step limits per env) with 4 seeds. Record Avg@4 and Pass@4 per environment. Establish current SOTA ceiling and failure distribution.
2. **Rule-provided ablation:** For top models, re-run evaluation with latent transition rules explicitly in the system prompt. Delta isolates pure inductive discovery component.
3. **Step evolution profiling:** For selected models, run single-seed trajectories while computing cumulative success rate at each step. Identify saturation points and compare against random baseline to detect active harm from poor inductive strategies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What specific architectural modifications or training paradigms can enable LLMs to autonomously induce latent transition laws from raw interaction data, overcoming the identified inductive bottleneck?
2. Why does agent performance saturate even with extended interaction budgets (up to 200 steps), and what underlying factors—such as context window limits, credit assignment breakdown, or failure to utilize early observations—cause this plateau?
3. What mechanisms lead to the high prevalence of action loops in LLM agents, and how can they be mitigated to promote effective exploration and rule induction?
4. Are the four proposed environment primitives exhaustive for evaluating inductive world-model learning, or do real-world systems require additional primitives (e.g., hybrid dynamics, non-Markovian dependencies, hierarchical structures)?

## Limitations
- Exact task parameterization (seeds), full environment implementations, and precise prompt templates from code may differ from description, affecting faithful reproduction.
- External validity limited; corpus shows weak related work support, so findings may not generalize beyond tested environments.
- Alternative explanations (e.g., planning deficits, credit assignment issues) remain possible for saturation and action-loop observations.

## Confidence
- **Core inductive bottleneck claim:** High (strong internal ablation evidence, clear rule-provided gap).
- **Saturation indicates architectural limits:** Medium-High (empirical plateau but alternative explanations like planning deficits possible).
- **Action loops as signature of inductive failure:** Medium (correlation shown, causal link to inductive stagnation inferential).
