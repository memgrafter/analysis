# $κ$-Explorer: A Unified Framework for Active Model Estimation in MDPs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20404
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20404
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a parameterized family $U_κ$ of smooth concave objectives that unify average-case ($κ=2$) and worst-case ($κ→∞$) transition estimation, enabling Frank-Wolfe optimization (κ-Explorer) and a lightweight online variant (κ-Explorer-DP); achieves $eO(T^{-1/3})$ regret and empirically reduces worst-case estimation errors and failure rates on discretized benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles efficient active sampling for learning unknown transition probabilities in tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), where different state-action pairs have varying intrinsic complexity (e.g., variance). It proposes a unified objective $U_κ$ that smoothly interpolates between average-case ($κ=2$) and worst-case ($κ→∞$) estimation criteria. Its closed-form gradient $(c_{s,a}/d_{s,a})^κ$ enables Frank-Wolfe optimization over occupancy measures (κ-Explorer) and a fully online variant using per-step dynamic programming (κ-Explorer-DP). Theoretically, κ-Explorer attains $eO(T^{-1/3})$ sublinear regret. Empirically, on discretized Pendulum and Mountain Car environments, large-$κ$ variants achieve near-zero failure rates and drastically lower normalized squared $\ell_2$ errors compared to baselines like MaxEnt, Weighted-MaxEnt, and SMM.

## Method Summary
Two algorithmic variants are presented:
1. **Episodic κ-Explorer (Algorithm 1):** Uses Frank-Wolfe over the occupancy measure polytope $\mathcal{D}^{(p)}_η$. At each episode $m$, it solves a linear subproblem (via an optimistic planning oracle) to find a direction $\psi_{m+1}$ that maximizes $\langle \nabla U_κ(\hat{d}(t_m)), d \rangle$ over a confidence-constrained set. The empirical occupancy $\hat{d}(t_m)$ is updated via $\hat{d}(t_{m+1}) = (1-β_m)\hat{d}(t_m) + β_m \psi_{m+1}$. Episode lengths grow as $\tau_m = \tau_1 m^2$.
2. **Online κ-Explorer-DP (Algorithm 2):** Replaces the episodic oracle with a per-step dynamic programming solve on the current empirical MDP $\hat{P}_t$. At each step $t$, it defines an intrinsic reward $r_t(s,a) = (\hat{c}_{s,a}^+(t) / (T_{s,a}(t))^κ$, where $\hat{c}_{s,a}^+$ is an upper confidence bound on the intrinsic complexity $c_{s,a} = 1 - \sum_{s'} P(s'|s,a)^2$. The policy is then extracted from the resulting value function. This variant is computationally lighter but lacks theoretical guarantees.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantee:** The episodic κ-Explorer achieves high-probability regret $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/3})$ for the $U_κ$ objective.
- **Empirical performance (Pendulum, $n=10^5$):** With $κ=10$, κ-Explorer reduces worst-case normalized error $L_{\text{Worst}}$ to $\sim$600 versus $\sim$4400 for MaxEnt, and achieves a near-zero failure rate.
- **Empirical performance (Mountain Car, $n=10^6$):** κ-Explorer succeeds where baselines like SMM and Weighted-MaxEnt fail completely (high failure rates).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Parameterized objective $U_κ$ enables principled trade-offs.**  
$U_κ(d) = \sum_{s,a} c_{s,a}^κ d_{s,a}^{1-κ}$ (for $κ>1$) interpolates between average-case ($κ=2$) and worst-case ($κ→∞$) estimation. Its gradient $\nabla U_κ(d)_{s,a} = (c_{s,a}/d_{s,a})^κ$ assigns higher marginal gain to state-action pairs with high intrinsic complexity $c_{s,a}$ relative to visitation $d_{s,a}$, naturally prioritizing under-sampled, high-variance transitions. This relies on $c_{s,a}$ accurately reflecting statistical difficulty (e.g., squared $\ell_2$ error). If $c_{s,a}$ is strongly misspecified, the allocation may degrade both average and worst-case performance.

**Mechanism 2: Frank-Wolfe optimization over occupancy measures.**  
The algorithm updates occupancy measures via $\hat{d} \leftarrow (1-β)\hat{d} + β \psi$, where $\psi$ solves a linear subproblem over the polytope $\mathcal{D}^{(η)}_η$ (flow constraints plus uniform lower bound $2η$). This maintains feasibility while leveraging the smoothness of $U_κ$ (Lemma 6) to guarantee $eO(T^{-1/3})$ regret (Theorem 5). The uniform bound $η$ is critical for gradient Lipschitzness; if $η$ is too large, it may exclude the true optimum, and if too small, smoothness fails.

**Mechanism 3: Online variant approximates FW direction via DP.**  
κ-Explorer-DP computes a myopic policy at each step using reward $r_t(s,a) \propto \hat{c}_{s,a}^+(t) / T_{s,a}(t)^κ$ on the empirical MDP $\hat{P}_t$. This approximates the FW linear oracle by directly maximizing the instantaneous gradient. It assumes $\hat{P}_t$ is sufficiently accurate to avoid persistent misallocation; otherwise, early model errors can cascade. The use of planning horizon $H$ balances myopia and lookahead.

## Foundational Learning
- **Occupancy Measures:** A convex reparameterization of policies via state-action visitation frequencies. Why needed: Converts policy optimization into a constrained convex program over $d_{s,a}$, capturing long-term distributional effects and enabling FW updates. Quick check: How do the flow equations $\sum_a d_{s,a} = (1-γ)ρ(s) + γ \sum_{s',a'} d_{s',a'} P(s|s',a')$ and the uniform bound $d_{s,a} \ge 2η$ jointly define the polytope $\mathcal{D}^{(p)}_η$?
- **Frank-Wolfe Algorithm:** An iterative method for constrained convex optimization with a linear minimization oracle. Why needed: $U_κ$ is smooth and concave over a polytope; FW offers dimension-independent convergence and fits the linear subproblem structure (here, planning). Quick check: Why does mixing the current point with a vertex of the polytope $\hat{d} \leftarrow (1-β)\hat{d} + β \psi$ guarantee feasibility?
- **Concentration Inequalities for Empirical Measures:** Bounds relating true transition probabilities to estimates (e.g., $\|\hat{P} - P\|_1 \le \hat{b}$ with high probability). Why needed: Provide probabilistic guarantees for confidence sets $\mathcal{B}_{s,a}(t)$ and the UCB $\hat{c}_{s,a}^+$, enabling optimistic planning and regret analysis. Quick check: How do $\hat{b}_{s,a}(t)$ and the error $\hat{e}_{s,a}(t)$ scale with visitation count $T_{s,a}(t)$, and why is a uniform lower bound $η$ needed for the gradient’s smoothness?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Intrinsic complexity estimator → Upper confidence builder → Confidence set manager → Direction finder (oracle) → Occupancy updater → Policy extractor → Execution & logging. In κ-Explorer-DP, the direction finder is replaced by a DP solver on the empirical MDP.

**Critical path:** Initialize counts $T_{s,a}=0$ and empirical $\hat{P}_0$. Repeat: (1) compute $\hat{c}_{s,a}(t)$ and UCB $\hat{c}_{s,a}^+$; (2) solve for direction (FW oracle or DP); (3) update occupancy $\hat{d}(t)$; (4) extract policy $\pi$; (5) execute $\pi$ for $\tau_m$ (or one step), update $T_{s,a}$ and $\hat{P}_t$.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **$κ$ value:** Larger $κ$ emphasizes worst-case (max-ratio) performance but may hurt average efficiency; smaller $κ$ balances but may not ensure full coverage. Empirically, $κ=10$ works for worst-case on Pendulum.
- **Episode length $\tau_m$:** Growing $\tau_m$ (e.g., $\tau_m \propto m^2$) reduces non-stationarity within an episode, aiding theory, but causes gradient staleness. κ-Explorer-DP uses $\tau_m=1$ for fresher updates.
- **Planning horizon $H$:** $H=∞$ (full DP) gives globally informed exploration but is costly; $H=1$ is myopic and fails to reach low-probability states; $H=2$ offers a practical compromise.
- **Confidence parameters:** The scaling of $\hat{e}_{s,a}(t)$ and $\hat{b}_{s,a}(t)$ controls optimism. Too tight sets risk excluding true transitions; too loose leads to wasteful exploration.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **High failure rate:** Some $(s,a)$ never visited ($T_{s,a}=0$ at end). Common in MaxEnt or $H=1$ variants due to lack of long-horizon planning.
2. **Large $L_{\text{Worst}}$ vs. $L_{\text{Avg}}$ gap:** Indicates insufficient focus on high-$c_{s,a}$ pairs; may require larger $κ$ or more optimistic direction.
3. **Stagnant $U_κ$ gap:** Could signal inaccurate $\hat{c}_{s,a}$ (poor $\hat{P}_t$) or infeasible planning oracle.
4. **Empirical occupancy $\hat{d}(t) < 2η$:** Violates theoretical smoothness assumption, potentially causing unstable gradients.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Pendulum (n=10^5):** Compare full κ-Explorer ($κ=10$, $H=∞$) vs. Random, MaxEnt, Weighted-MaxEnt, SMM, and ablations ($κ=1$, $H=1$, $H=2$). Metrics: failure rate, $L_{\text{Worst}}$, $L_{\text{Avg}}$. Expected: κ-Explorer$(κ=10)$ lowest failure and worst-case error.
2. **Mountain Car (n=10^6):** Stress test with higher difficulty. Expect κ-Explorer to maintain low failure while baselines (SMM, Weighted-MaxEnt) fail.
3. **κ sensitivity:** Run κ-Explorer-DP with $κ∈\{1,2,5,10\}$ on both environments. Expect trade-off: higher $κ$ improves worst-case, lower $κ$ improves average-case.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **κ selection:** How should $κ$ be chosen for a given MDP to balance average-case and worst-case performance? The paper provides empirical variation but no principled method.
2. **Online variant analysis:** Do the convergence guarantees extend to κ-Explorer-DP, which updates the policy at every step ($\tau_m=1$) instead of using increasing episode lengths? The theory assumes growing $\tau_m$ for diminishing step-sizes.
3. **Scalability to large/continuous spaces:** Can κ-Explorer be scaled beyond tabular MDPs? Current occupancy-based optimization scales poorly with state-action size, and no function approximation is proposed.

## Limitations
- Discretization scheme and stochastic perturbation magnitude in the continuous dynamics are unspecified, affecting true transitions and intrinsic complexity $c_{s,a}$.
- Baseline algorithm details (MaxEnt, Weighted-MaxEnt, SMM) are not fully described, hindering exact replication.
- Critical hyperparameters for κ-Explorer-DP (discount $\gamma$, value iteration tolerance, reward floor $\epsilon$) are missing, forcing arbitrary choices.
- Theoretical analysis assumes a uniform occupancy lower bound $\eta$, but no guidance is given for setting $\eta$ in practice or its sensitivity.

## Confidence
- **U_κ as a unified interpolation mechanism:** High (mathematically precise; limits correctly recover average- and worst-case objectives; closed-form gradient is exact).
- **Theoretical $eO(T^{-1/3})$ regret:** Medium (proof appears rigorous but relies on the $\eta$ lower bound and smoothness; edge cases and practical $\eta$ selection are unaddressed).
- **Empirical superiority (low failure rates, reduced errors):** Medium (results are strong but depend on undisclosed environment details and baseline implementations; exact replication currently blocked).
