# Q-Drift: Quantization-Aware Drift Correction for Diffusion Model Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18095
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18095
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Q-Drift consistently improves FID over quantized baselines across six text-to-image models, three samplers, and two PTQ methods, with up to 4.59 FID reduction on PixArt-Sigma (SVDQuant W3A4), while preserving CLIP scores and adding negligible overhead.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion model sampling with post-training quantization (PTQ) suffers from error accumulation due to iterative denoising. Q-Drift addresses this by modeling quantization noise as an implicit stochastic perturbation within the reverse-time SDE framework. It derives a marginal-preserving drift adjustment that rescales the model's noise prediction at each step. The correction factor per timestep is estimated via lightweight calibration (as few as 5 paired full-precision/quantized runs) by measuring the conditional variance of the quantization error. This plug-and-play method is agnostic to model architecture, sampler, and PTQ technique, yielding robust image quality improvements without increasing inference cost.

## Method Summary
Q-Drift operates in two phases. First, an offline calibration runs a short full-precision sampling trajectory on a small prompt set. At each denoising step, it records both the full-precision and quantized model's noise predictions (ϵ and ˆϵ). From these pairs, it estimates a per-timestep, per-channel statistic Vσi representing the conditional variance of the quantization error ∆ϵ = ˆϵ − ϵ given ˆϵ, under simplified Gaussian assumptions. This yields a drift factor ci = |Δσi|/(2σi Vσi). Second, during online sampling, the quantized model's output ˆϵθ is multiplied by (1 + ci) channel-wise before being used in the standard Euler (or equivalent) update xi+1 = xi + Δσi · (1 + ci) ⊙ ˆϵθ. The correction compensates for the effective diffusion introduced by quantization noise, preserving the intended marginal distribution p(x;σ).

## Key Results
- Q-Drift reduces FID compared to the quantized baseline in all 18 evaluated settings (6 models × 3 samplers × 2 PTQ methods).
- Maximum improvement of 4.59 FID on PixArt-Sigma using SVDQuant (W3A4).
- CLIP scores remain stable, indicating preserved semantic alignment.
- Calibration requires as few as 5 prompts; overhead is negligible at inference.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Quantization as Implicit Perturbation
Quantization error at each step acts as an additional stochastic perturbation, causing the sampling trajectory to deviate from the target marginal distribution. Q-Drift re-frames quantized sampling within a generalized SDE family (Eq. 16) where quantization noise ∆ϵ is modeled as an extra diffusion term. By analytically matching the variance of this implicit diffusion to a deterministic drift adjustment, the method preserves the marginal p(x;σ) through a rescaling of the Euler update by (1+ci). The core assumption is that the quantization noise is well-approximated as state-independent Gaussian with variance captured by Vσi. *Break condition:* If the noise is highly non-Gaussian, correlated, or state-dependent, the correction may miscompensate.

### Mechanism 2: Efficient Variance Estimation
The conditional variance statistic Vσi = E[Var(∆ϵ | ˆϵ)] is reliably estimated from very few (≥5) paired calibration runs. The method fits a simplified per-timestep joint Gaussian model to the collected (ϵ, ˆϵ) pairs, assuming diagonal covariance and isotropic structure across elements. This yields a stable scalar (or channel-wise) Vσi even with minimal data. *Break condition:* The estimate fails if the small calibration set is unrepresentative (e.g., narrow prompt diversity) or if the true noise distribution violates Gaussian/diagonal assumptions (e.g., heavy tails, strong correlations).

### Mechanism 3: Sampler-Agnostic Drift Rescaling
The drift-rescaling principle is adapted to common samplers by matching their deterministic update to an SDE integration. For Flow-matching, it rescales the velocity field directly. For DPM-Solver++, the correction factor is computed to match injected variance in the log-SNR SDE, then applied to the solver's deterministic increment. The extension relies on the heuristic that the sampler's update corresponds to a numerical integration of a probability-flow ODE that the generalized SDE family can reproduce. *Break condition:* For solvers whose multi-step nature induces non-local error dependencies, a per-step variance estimate may be insufficient to preserve marginals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reverse-time SDEs & Probability-Flow ODEs**  
  *Why needed:* Q-Drift's derivation starts from the generalized SDE family that preserves a target marginal p(x;σ). Understanding how drift adjustments preserve marginals and the role of the diffusion term is central.  
  *Quick check:* In Eq. (6), modifying only the drift term without adjusting the diffusion term changes the marginal distribution's evolution, generally causing it to diverge from the intended path.

- **Concept: Error Accumulation in Sequential Sampling**  
  *Why needed:* The paper's motivation is that quantization error propagates across steps; a small unbiased error per step can significantly distort the final sample. Recognizing this non-local effect is key to needing a sampler-side correction.  
  *Quick check:* A small unbiased error at each of 50 steps can lead to a large final distortion because errors accumulate in a random walk fashion, with variance growing linearly with steps.

- **Concept: Euler–Maruyama Discretization of SDEs**  
  *Why needed:* The derivation connects continuous SDE theory to the discrete update (Eq. 8). The correction factor ci emerges from matching the variance of the implicit perturbation (from using ˆϵ) to the variance injected by a diffusion term in the Euler step.  
  *Quick check:* In xi+1 = xi + a(xi, ti)∆ti + b(ti)√|∆ti| zi, the term b(ti)√|∆ti| zi is responsible for stochasticity, while a(xi, ti)∆ti is the deterministic drift.

## Architecture Onboarding
Calibration Module -> Sampler Wrapper -> Quantized Model

- **Critical path:** Calibration must complete before sampling; it stores per-timestep correction factors indexed by noise level σi. The Sampler Wrapper applies channel-wise rescaling (1+ci) to the quantized model's output before the standard update.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Channel-wise vs. global correction: Channel-wise is more robust but stores more factors; global is simpler but less effective.  
  - Covariance model: Diagonal + isotropic simplification enables stable estimation with few samples but may miss correlations (some off-diagonal signal exists at high noise).  
  - Calibration set: More samples improve stability but with diminishing returns; prompt diversity is crucial for representative statistics.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Diverging or NaN ci values (e.g., from near-zero Var(ˆϵ) during estimation).  
  2. Worsening FID/CLIP compared to uncorrected quantized baseline, indicating over-correction.  
  3. New structured artifacts (color shifts, patterns) not present in FP or quantized baselines, suggesting model mismatch.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Sanity check:** Apply Q-Drift to a single quantized model (e.g., SDXL-Turbo SVDQuant W3A4). Verify FID improves and CLIP is stable; ensure ci are finite and well-behaved.  
  2. **Calibration efficiency:** Vary calibration prompts K = {5, 10, 50, 500}. Plot FID vs. K; expect significant drop from 0 to 5, then plateau.  
  3. **Ablation of correction component:** Compare (a) quantized baseline, (b) quantized+Q-Drift, (c) quantized+conditional mean subtraction only (D²-DPM style). Expect (b) >> (a) and (c) < (a), confirming rescaling is key.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can the simplified diagonal and isotropic covariance assumptions be relaxed to capture richer noise structures without requiring large calibration sets?
2. Is the heuristic extension of drift-rescaling to samplers like Flow-matching and DPM-Solver(++) theoretically sound under their specific marginal-preserving formulations?
3. How does Q-Drift interact with quantization-aware training (QAT) or fine-tuning, and can it provide gains on top of QAT?
4. What is the optimal granularity for correction factors (per-timestep, per-channel, per-layer, spatial) under strict calibration budget constraints?
5. Does the framework extend to other diffusion tasks (video, 3D, audio) where quantization noise accumulation may differ?

## Limitations
- The assumed Gaussian, diagonal-covariance model may fail under aggressive PTQ (e.g., W2A2) where clipping induces heavy tails and correlations.
- Calibration set must match evaluation distribution; narrow prompt diversity can bias Vσi estimates and harm correction.
- The drift-rescaling for multi-step solvers (e.g., DPM-Solver++) is heuristic; non-local error dependencies may not be fully corrected.

## Confidence
- **FID improvement across models/samplers/PTQ:** High (extensive validation).
- **Gaussian, state-independent noise model:** Medium (supplementary figures support but real PTQ noise may be structured).
- **Calibration efficiency (5 samples):** High (clear plateau in results).
- **Sampler-agnostic principle (especially DPM-Solver++):** Medium (heuristic extension; untested solvers may not map cleanly).
