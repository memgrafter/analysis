# Advancing Multi-Instrument Music Transcription: Results from the 2025 AMT Challenge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27528
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27528
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: The winning MIROS system achieved a Multi-Instrument Note Onset F1 score of 0.5998 (vs. MT3's 0.3932) by integrating a self-supervised MusicFM encoder with a multi-decoder architecture.

## Executive Summary
The 2025 AMT Challenge established a new benchmark for multi-instrument automatic music transcription (AMT) using a cloud-based evaluation system and a novel test set of 76 short pieces across eight instruments. Eight teams submitted valid solutions, with two surpassing the MT3 baseline. The winning MIROS system achieved a Multi-Instrument Note Onset F1 score of 0.5998, more than doubling MT3's 0.3932, by combining a self-supervised MusicFM encoder with a multi-decoder architecture. However, analysis revealed a severe performance drop as polyphony increased: MIROS's F1 fell from 0.7193 on solo pieces to 0.4367 on three-instrument pieces. These results demonstrate significant progress while highlighting persistent challenges in dense polyphony, timbre similarity, and data scarcity for rare instruments.

## Method Summary
MIROS builds upon the MT3 sequence-to-sequence framework. Its core innovations are: (1) a MusicFM encoder, a conformer-based model pretrained via BEST-RQ masked token modeling on vast unlabeled music audio, providing robust general-purpose representations; and (2) a multi-decoder architecture where a recurrent adapter modulates encoder outputs using learned instrument-group embeddings, and separate T5-style decoders generate note tokens for each group. The model was trained on the union of ten public multi-instrument MIDI datasets (including Slakh2100, MusicNet, URMP, and MAESTRO) without cross-stem augmentation. This approach aims to exploit unlabeled data for better feature learning and reduce instrument leakage through specialized decoding pathways.

## Key Results
- MIROS achieved a Multi-Instrument Note Onset F1 of 0.5998, significantly outperforming the MT3 baseline (0.3932).
- Performance degrades sharply with increased polyphony: F1 drops from 0.7193 on solo pieces to 0.4367 on three-instrument pieces.
- The main remaining difficulties are dense polyphony, timbre similarity between instruments, and data scarcity for rare instruments (e.g., viola, bassoon, trombone).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Self-supervised pretraining with MusicFM
**Claim:** Self-supervised pretraining with MusicFM improves transcription accuracy by learning robust, domain-general audio representations from unlabeled data.

**Mechanism:** MusicFM uses BEST-RQ masked token modeling on large corpora of unlabeled music audio. This forces the model to learn high-level acoustic and musical patterns (e.g., timbre, harmony, rhythm) without relying on scarce note-level annotations. The learned encoder weights provide a stronger initialization for the downstream multi-instrument transcription task than supervised pretraining on limited AMT datasets.

**Core assumption:** Unlabeled music audio contains latent structures (e.g., tonal coherence, instrument timbres) that are transferable to the supervised transcription objective.

**Evidence anchors:**
- Abstract: "integrating a self-supervised MusicFM encoder"
- Section 4: "MusicFM is a conformer-based, self-supervised foundation model for music pretrained via BEST-RQ masked token modeling... An advantage of self-supervised foundation models is that they can exploit abundant unlabeled audio"
- Corpus: Neighbors highlight data scarcity as a core problem (e.g., "Quantifying the Corpus Bias Problem in Automatic Music Transcription Systems"), supporting the value of leveraging unlabeled data.

**Break condition:** Performance gains diminish if the unlabeled pretraining corpus lacks stylistic or instrumental overlap with the target transcription domain (e.g., rare instruments like bassoon).

### Mechanism 2: Multi-decoder architecture with instrument-group conditioning
**Claim:** A multi-decoder architecture with instrument-group conditioning mitigates instrument interference ("leakage") in polyphonic transcription.

**Mechanism:** A single shared encoder processes the mixed audio. A recurrent adapter then modulates the temporal features using learned embeddings for predefined instrument groups (e.g., "woodwind," "strings"). Each group has a dedicated T5-style decoder that attends to the modulated features and predicts notes only for instruments in its group. This architectural bias encourages specialization, reducing the tendency for a single decoder to fragment a single instrument's notes across multiple output streams.

**Core assumption:** Instruments can be clustered into groups where intra-group timbral and pitch characteristics are more consistent than inter-group ones, allowing grouped decoding to reduce confusion.

**Evidence anchors:**
- Abstract: "multi-decoder architecture"
- Section 4: "MIROS introduced a recurrent adapter that conditions the temporally downsampled encoder outputs on learned instrument group embeddings. Each instrument group is then decoded in parallel using T5-style decoders"
- Section 4: "models often produced instrument leakage, hallucinating nonexistent instruments, or struggled to disentangle... especially when multiple instruments shared similar pitch ranges or timbres." The architecture is a direct response to this observed failure.

**Break condition:** The grouping strategy fails when instruments from different groups share highly overlapping timbres and pitch ranges (e.g., viola vs. cello in low register), as the decoders still receive strongly correlated signals.

### Mechanism 3: Training on union of public multi-instrument MIDI datasets
**Claim:** Training on the union of all available public multi-instrument MIDI datasets (e.g., Slakh2100, MusicNet, URMP) provides necessary polyphonic and timbral diversity to handle the challenge's instrument set.

**Mechanism:** Each dataset offers different combinations of instruments, playing styles, and recording syntheses. Aggregating them exposes the model to a wider distribution of note co-occurrences (polyphony), onset/offset patterns, and synthetic timbres than any single dataset. This diversity is hypothesized to improve generalization to the new test set's compositions.

**Core assumption:** The combined datasets cover a sufficient subset of the musical feature space (especially for the challenge's eight instruments) to learn a transferable transcription function.

**Evidence anchors:**
- Section 4: "Outside the competition... it attained slightly better accuracy on the competition data... Domain-pretrained encoders like MusicFM thus extends YourMT3+ to longer, richer contexts"
- Section 4: "nearly all top-performing models and the MT3 baseline relied on an almost identical set of ten datasets... This suggests that performance is fundamentally constrained by data scarcity. In particular, currently available public datasets for AMT insufficiently cover less common instruments such as viola, bassoon, and trombone"
- Corpus: "MR-MT3: Memory Retaining Multi-Track Music Transcription to mitigate Instrument Leakage" and "Quantifying the Corpus Bias Problem" both center on dataset limitations as a primary constraint.

**Break condition:** Generalization fails for instruments or ensemble textures poorly represented in the training union (e.g., viola, bassoon, or three-instrument dense polyphony as shown in Table 2).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transformer-based Sequence-to-Sequence Modeling for AMT**
  - Why needed here: The winning MIROS and all top systems extend the MT3 paradigm, which frames transcription as a seq2seq problem: map a spectrogram sequence (input) to a token sequence representing notes (output). Understanding attention, tokenization (e.g., "pitch_onset_velocity"), and parallel decoding is essential to follow the architectural descriptions.
  - Quick check question: How does the model handle variable-length input audio and produce a variable-length MIDI event sequence, and what is the role of the decoder's cross-attention?

- **Concept: Self-Supervised Representation Learning (BEST-RQ)**
  - Why needed here: MusicFM's core innovation is its self-supervised pretraining objective. BEST-RQ (Behavioral Random-Projection Quantization) involves masking audio segments, projecting them to random vectors, and training to predict the quantized projection IDs. This is distinct from supervised note prediction and is cited as the key advantage of MusicFM.
  - Quick check question: What is the training objective of BEST-RQ, and why is it considered more scalable than supervised AMT pretraining?

- **Concept: Polyphonic Note Evaluation Metrics (mir_eval)**
  - Why needed here: The paper's primary result (F1 score) is defined by a specific mir_eval formulation ("Multi-Instrument Note Onset F1"). It requires matching instrument, pitch, and onset within ±50ms. Misunderstanding this metric (e.g., confusing it with frame-level or offset-sensitive metrics) leads to misinterpreting the results, especially the precision/recall trade-offs.
  - Quick check question: What constitutes a "correct" transcription in the challenge's primary metric, and why might precision drop more than recall with increasing polyphony?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Input Preprocessor: Converts raw audio to a time-frequency representation (e.g., log-mel spectrogram).
  2. Encoder Backbone: MusicFM (Conformer) processes the spectrogram into a sequence of contextualized embeddings.
  3. Instrument Adapter: A recurrent network (likely GRU/LSTM) that takes downsampled encoder outputs and injects information via learned instrument group embeddings.
  4. Multi-Decoder Heads: One T5-style decoder per instrument group. Each decoder attends to the adapter's output via cross-attention and generates a token stream (pitch, onset, offset, velocity) for instruments in its group.
  5. Token Decoder/Post-processor: Converts token sequences into standard MIDI events.

- **Critical path:** Spectrogram → MusicFM Encoder → Adapter (with group ID) → Per-group Decoder → Token-to-MIDI.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Single vs. Multi-Decoder:* A single monolithic decoder (MT3) is simpler but prone to instrument leakage. Multi-decoder adds parameters and requires careful grouping but encourages disentanglement.
  - *Encoder Pretraining:* Self-supervised (MusicFM) offers data efficiency but may introduce domain mismatch between pretraining audio and synthesized MIDI audio. Supervised pretraining on existing AMT datasets is more direct but limited by data scarcity.
  - *Inverse Frequency of Rare Instruments:* Training on the aggregated dataset inherently under-represents rare instruments (bassoon, viola). No explicit re-weighting is mentioned, likely a gap.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Sharp Precision Drop with Polyphony:** As seen in Table 2 (MIROS: 0.9067 solo → 0.4643 for 3 instruments). Indicates decoders generating false positives from overlapping content.
  - **Instrument Leakage:** A single note being split across multiple instrument outputs in the MIDI. Addressed by grouping but not eliminated.
  - **Poor Performance on Rare Instruments:** Not directly tabulated but implied by the "data scarcity" discussion; expect low recall for bassoon/trombone/viola.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Solo vs. Polyphony Diagnostic:** Run the trained model on the challenge's solo (1-instrument) pieces and 3-instrument pieces. Quantify the delta in F1, precision, and recall to confirm the polyphony degradation pattern observed in the paper.
  2. **Instrument Group Ablation:** Re-train a variant with a single decoder (reverting to MT3-style) but keeping the MusicFM encoder. Compare its performance on the challenge set to the full multi-decoder model. Is the gain from the encoder or the decoding structure?
  3. **Rare Instrument Hold-out:** Create a mini-evaluation subset containing only pieces with bassoon or trombone. Compare model performance on this subset vs. a subset with only piano/violin/cello. Expect significant Recall deficit for rare instruments, confirming the data scarcity hypothesis.

## Limitations
- Test set unreleased: Exact replication of challenge metrics is impossible without the official 76-piece test set.
- Implementation details missing: Critical hyperparameters, exact dataset list, and audio preprocessing specifications are not provided.
- Generalization limited: Performance degrades sharply with increased polyphony and on rare instruments, indicating the model's operational envelope is constrained to sparse textures and common instruments.

## Confidence
- Self-supervised pretraining (MusicFM) adds value: Medium
- Multi-decoder mitigates instrument leakage: Medium-High
- Performance fundamentally constrained by training data scarcity: High
