# Learned but Not Expressed: Capability-Expression Dissociation in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18013  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18013  
- **Reference count:** 14  
- **Primary result:** LLMs show 0% expression of non-causal solution frames in standard generation (95% CI: [0%, 1.2%]) despite 80–87% capability under conditional extraction.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates a systematic dissociation between learned capability and expressed output in large language models (LLMs). Across 300 prompt-response generations using three models, ten scenarios, and narrative/problem-solving contexts, the models never produced non-causal solution frames (e.g., supernatural intervention). However, conditional extraction trials confirmed the models retained the latent capability to reconstruct such content, with 80–87% success when explicitly prompted. The findings challenge the assumption that training data presence directly predicts generation probability and highlight how alignment-induced generation policies suppress learned content, even in permissive contexts.

## Method Summary
The study conducted 300 standard prompt-response generations across three proprietary LLMs, ten distinct scenarios (e.g., unexpected challenges requiring resourcefulness), and two task contexts (narrative vs. problem-solving), scanning for non-causal solution frames. A separate conditional extraction protocol—using targeted prompts like "List examples of non-causal solutions"—verified whether models retained the latent capability to produce such content. No reproduction notes or code are provided.

## Key Results
- Zero occurrences of non-causal solution frames in standard generation (0%, 95% CI: [0%, 1.2%]).
- High success rates (80–87%) in conditional extraction, confirming latent capability.
- Demonstrates a robust dissociation between memorized/learned content and its expression under normal prompting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Alignment training (RLHF/Constitutional AI) embeds broad suppression policies that filter learned non-causal content during standard generation.
- **Mechanism:** Post-training alignment shapes a generation policy prioritizing causal, implementable solutions, creating a latent "exclusion zone" in the output distribution. This policy activates under standard prompting, overriding retrieval from memorized training data.
- **Core assumption:** Alignment-induced priors generalize beyond narrow safety constraints to shape content-type preferences.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "task-conditioned generation policies can comprehensively suppress learned content"; [section 5.2] "alignment-induced priors create persistent biases... transcending simple instruction-following".
- **Break condition:** Explicit permissibility instructions (e.g., "include supernatural elements") override suppression.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Task framing itself triggers a "problem-solving" schema that excludes non-causal frames, independent of alignment history.
- **Mechanism:** The model's task-conditioned policy interprets scenario structure (e.g., "unexpected challenge requiring resourcefulness") as demanding pragmatic solutions, activating heuristics that filter non-causal options regardless of context label ("narrative").
- **Core assumption:** Schema activation based on scenario semantics overrides contextual labels.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1] "systematic absence... despite the explicit permissibility of fantastical content"; [section 3.3] Scenario definitions were "neutral regarding solution type" yet yielded zero instances.
- **Break condition:** Scenarios with explicit fantasy genre markers (e.g., "fantasy story") elicit non-causal frames.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The dissociation is a selection-effect artifact: standard generation samples from a *constrained* output distribution, while conditional extraction samples from the *full* representational capacity.
- **Mechanism:** During training, models learn a mapping from input conditions to a distribution over next tokens. Standard prompts condition this distribution toward alignment-sanctioned paths. Explicit extraction prompts condition it toward low-probability paths that remain accessible but are rarely visited.
- **Core assumption:** Memorized content resides in parameter space but is gated by context-dependent decoding constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "capability... and selection..."; [section 2.1] Memorization "does not predict deployment"; [section 5.3] Extraction success (80–87%) vs. 0% expression.
- **Break condition:** Suppression diminishes with higher temperature or top-*p* sampling.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Memorization Contiguity
- **Why needed here:** Distinguishes between a model's ability to reconstruct training data (memorization) and its tendency to produce that data naturally. The paper's core finding is a dissociation between the two.
- **Quick check question:** If a model can reproduce a specific training sequence when prompted but never does so naturally, what does that say about the relationship between memorization and generation probability?

### Concept: Alignment-Induced Priors
- **Why needed here:** Explains *why* certain content is suppressed. Alignment reshapes the base model's output distribution, creating persistent biases that control expression even in seemingly permissive contexts.
- **Quick check question:** How might alignment training focusing on "harmlessness" also suppress non-harmful but non-causal narrative solutions?

### Concept: Task-Conditioned Generation
- **Why needed here:** The model's response is conditioned on the *implied task schema* (not just the literal prompt), which is key to interpreting the zero occurrences.
- **Quick check question:** Why might a prompt labeled "Write a short story" still trigger a "practical advice" generation policy if the scenario describes a problem?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pre-training Corpus -> Base Model -> Alignment Stage (SFT/RLHF) -> Generation Policy -> Decoding Sampler

**Critical path:**  
Input Prompt -> Task Schema Recognition -> Alignment-Policy Activation -> Constrained Distribution Sampling -> Output. Suppression occurs at policy activation/sampling.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Safety vs. Expressivity: Alignment reduces harmful outputs but may over-suppress benign creative content.  
- Generality vs. Precision: Broad suppression policies are simpler but may reject valid content; fine-grained policies are harder to train.  
- Capability Retention: Suppressing outputs may not erase underlying capability; hidden knowledge could be extracted via adversarial prompts.

**Failure signatures:**  
- False Negative (this paper's finding): Expected content absent despite capability.  
- False Positive: Unexpected non-causal content appears in a pragmatic context.  
- Context Collapse: Suppression fails when prompts are ambiguous or conflicting.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replication with Explicit Permissivity: Re-run the 300-generation sample adding phrases like "You may include supernatural or metaphysical solutions." Prediction: If Mechanism 2 dominates, suppression may break in narrative context.  
2. Boundary Testing via Temperature Sweep: For a subset of prompts, generate with temperatures {0.2, 0.7, 1.2}. Monitor if non-causal frames emerge at higher temperatures. Prediction: Emergence supports Mechanism 3 (decoding constraint).  
3. Alignment Strength Correlation: Compare models with known light vs. heavy RLHF (e.g., older vs. newer checkpoints). Hypothesis: Heavier alignment correlates with stronger suppression.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the capability-expression dissociation generalize across diverse LLM architectures and training regimes?
- **Basis in paper:** "Future research should extend these observations across broader model families"
- **Why unresolved:** The study evaluated only three proprietary models; it remains unknown whether open-source models, different scales, or varied pre-training corpora exhibit the same pattern.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic replication using a diverse panel of models (e.g., Llama, Mistral, multiple parameter scales) with identical prompts and extraction protocols.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do varying alignment methods (e.g., RLHF, Constitutional AI, or no alignment) modulate the dissociation?
- **Basis in paper:** "alternative alignment protocols"
- **Why unresolved:** The paper compared aligned models but did not manipulate alignment directly; relative contributions of pre-training versus alignment are unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative study of model variants with known alignment differences (e.g., base vs. instructed-tuned vs. RLHF-tuned versions of the same architecture).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the boundary conditions of the suppression? Can prompt engineering (e.g., explicit permission for supernatural content) elicit non-causal solution frames?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred from limitation about "Limited Context Variation" and authors' suggestion to test "explicit permissibility conditions."
- **Why unresolved:** The study used neutral prompts only; it is unknown whether suppression is absolute or context-dependent.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic variation of prompt instructions (e.g., "include magical solutions") across scenarios, measuring if non-causal frames emerge.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the dissociation persist over time as models are updated, or is it specific to the February 2026 model snapshots?
- **Basis in paper:** "temporal snapshots"
- **Why unresolved:** Model versions evolve rapidly; the observed zero-expression pattern may be transient to particular training runs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal testing of the same model families (e.g., successive versions of GPT, Claude, Gemini) at multiple time points.

## Limitations
- Sample limited to 300 generations across 10 scenarios; zero-occurrence confidence interval [0%, 1.2%] leaves small probability of under-detection.
- Scenario definitions ("neutral regarding solution type") may still implicitly favor pragmatic framing; genre markers (e.g., "fantasy") not systematically tested.
- Corpus metrics show low citation impact (avg 0.0) and moderate relatedness (avg FMR 0.512), suggesting limited peer validation to date.

## Confidence
- **Dissociation (Capability vs. Expression):** High (direct extraction vs. generation contrast).
- **Mechanism 1 (Alignment-Induced Suppression):** Medium (theoretically plausible but weak direct evidence in corpus snapshot).
- **Mechanism 2 (Task-Schema Activation):** Medium (supported by neutral scenario results, but genre-marker tests pending).
- **Mechanism 3 (Decoding Constraint):** Medium-High (extraction success supports latent capability, but temperature/consistency tests needed).

## Next Checks
1. **Explicit Permissivity + Genre Cueing:** Re-run 300 generations with prompts augmented by "Include supernatural/metaphysical solutions" and explicit fantasy/mythical genre labels. A significant rise in non-causal frames would break Mechanism 2.
2. **Decoding Parameter Sweep:** For a subset of prompts, generate across temperatures {0.2, 0.7, 1.2} and top-*p* {0.9, 1.0}. Emergence of non-causal frames at higher randomness would support Mechanism 3 and reject hard capability loss.
3. **Alignment Intensity Correlation:** Compare models with documented light vs. heavy RLHF (e.g., older vs. newer checkpoints). Stronger suppression (lower standard-generation occurrence) with heavier alignment would support Mechanism 1; similar extraction success would confirm latent capability retention.
