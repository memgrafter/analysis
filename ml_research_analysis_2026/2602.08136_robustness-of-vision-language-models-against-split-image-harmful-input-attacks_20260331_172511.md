# Robustness of Vision Language Models Against Split-Image Harmful Input Attacks
## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2602.08136
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08136
* **Reference count:** 0
* **Primary result:** Transfer-SIVA with Adv-KD achieves up to 60% higher attack success rate (ASR) in black-box settings versus existing baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a critical safety gap in vision-language models (VLMs): their safety alignment, trained solely on holistic images, fails to detect harmful semantics distributed across multiple split image fragments. The authors introduce Split-Image Visual Attacks (SIVA), progressing from simple splitting to an adaptive white-box attack and finally a black-box transfer attack via Adversarial Knowledge Distillation (Adv-KD). Evaluated on three state-of-the-art VLMs across three jailbreak datasets, the strongest transfer attack substantially outperforms prior work. As a defense, they propose augmented Direct Preference Optimization (aDPO), which efficiently incorporates split-image instances during alignment, reducing attack success by up to 16%.

## Method Summary
The attack methodology evolves in three stages. **Naïve-SIVA** splits a harmful image into fragments (e.g., three vertical parts) and feeds them separately, exploiting the model's inability to aggregate distributed harmful cues. **Adaptive-SIVA** creates a full-size benign image whose embeddings match those of a target harmful split, using PGD optimization; this bypasses a preprocessing visual filtering (VT) defense that merges obvious splits. **Transfer-SIVA** extends this to black-box targets by applying Adv-KD—a combination of reference-free DPO and contrastive learning—to align the attack model's visual feature space (ViT + adapter) with that of the target model. The proposed defense, **aDPO**, augments standard DPO training by generating multiple split variants for each holistic training example and applying the same preference labels, forcing the model to learn consistent refusal across split and whole images without extra human annotation.

## Key Results
* Transfer-SIVA with Adv-KD increases black-box attack success rate (ASR) by up to 60% relative to prior SOTA transfer attacks.
* aDPO reduces ASR for transfer attacks by up to 16%, demonstrating a viable training-time defense.
* Naïve-SIVA achieves high ASR (e.g., ~80% on Pixtral) but is mitigated by a VT merging module; Adaptive-SIVA and Transfer-SIVA bypass this defense effectively.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Safety alignment fails on split images because training data consists only of holistic images. The VLM's refusal mechanism is not tuned to aggregate weak harmful signals from separate fragments. The ViT processes each split in isolation, the adapter passes features unaltered, and the LM decoder—where cross-attention merges all splits—was not optimized during alignment to recognize that the combination constitutes a policy violation. If alignment included diverse split permutations, the model might learn this aggregation.

### Mechanism 2: The attack progression systematically overcomes constraints. Naïve-SIVA exploits the split vulnerability but fails under VT merging. Adaptive-SIVA optimizes a full-size image to embed like a harmful split, satisfying both bypassing VT and triggering the split-based vulnerability. Transfer-SIVA uses Adv-KD to align the attack model's embedding space with a black-box target's, enabling transfer without target gradients. If the target and attack models have incompatible vision architectures or pre-training, latent space alignment may fail.

### Mechanism 3: aDPO mitigates by reusing human preference labels for split variants generated from each holistic training instance. The DPO loss averages advantages across all variants, teaching consistent refusal. This assumes the original preference holds for semantically equivalent splits. If splitting introduces significant semantic drift, the safety signal could be corrupted.

## Foundational Learning
* **VLM Architecture (ViT → Adapter → LLM):** Needed to locate where safety fails. ViT processes patches separately; adapter maps visual tokens to LLM space; LLM decoder merges all tokens via cross-attention but is not safety-tuned for split inputs. *Quick check:* "If an image is split into three parts, at which component does the VLM first have access to features from all three parts simultaneously?"
* **Safety Alignment via Preference Optimization (DPO/RLHF):** Needed to understand the misalignment: instruction tuning generalizes to splits, but safety tuning (on holistics) does not. *Quick check:* "Why might a model trained with DPO on full images still refuse a single harmful split but not a combination of benign-looking splits?"
* **Adversarial Knowledge Distillation (Adv-KD):** Needed for Transfer-SIVA; aligns student attack model's representations to a black-box target using RF-DPO and contrastive losses without target gradients. *Quick check:* "How does Proxy Contrastive Learning in Adv-KD differ from standard CLIP-style contrastive learning when the teacher model is black-box?"

## Architecture Onboarding
* **Component map:** Vision Encoder (ViT) -> Multimodal Adapter -> Language Model Decoder. A preprocessing Visual Filtering (VT) module may sit before the VLM.
* **Critical path:** 1) Harmful image split into k fragments. 2) Each fragment -> ViT -> separate embeddings. 3) Embeddings -> Adapter -> LLM space. 4) Adapter outputs + text query -> LLM decoder. 5) Decoder cross-attention integrates split embeddings -> safety mechanism fails -> harmful generation.
* **Design tradeoffs:** Attacks trade white-box access for effectiveness (Adaptive-SIVA) vs. black-box transferability (Transfer-SIVA with Adv-KD). Defense aDPO is efficient (no new labels) but may not cover all split permutations; VT is lightweight but bypassable by adversarial full-size images.
* **Failure signatures:** High ASR on naïve splits without VT; ASR drop with VT for naïve but not adaptive/transfer attacks; transfer ASR improves 39–60% with Adv-KD; aDPO reduces transfer ASR up to 16% but may hurt utility.
* **First 3 experiments:** 1) Reproduce Naïve-SIVA: split a harmful image (e.g., from FR) vertically into three parts, feed to Qwen-3-VL, compute ASR via LM-Judge. 2) Run Adaptive-SIVA: optimize a full-size benign seed via PGD to match a target split's adapter output, feed with VT enabled, compare ASR to naïve without VT. 3) Test Transfer-SIVA: use Adv-KD on Pixtral (attack) with queries on Llama-3.2-Vision (target) to build `D_KD`, then generate adversarial images, feed to Llama, measure ASR boost over non-KD transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
* **Open Question 1:** How does aDPO scale with preference dataset size, and what is the point of diminishing returns in robustness? The paper evaluated only 1,000 instances, while typical alignment uses 10k–80k.
* **Open Question 2:** Why does aDPO only reduce ASR by up to 16% in black-box transfer attacks? What factors limit complete mitigation? Error analysis of residual attacks and probing internal representations are needed.
* **Open Question 3:** Can inference-time defenses effectively detect and reject split-image attacks without retraining? Current defenses (Llama Guard 3 Vision, ECSO) show only marginal reductions.
* **Open Question 4:** What architectural factors most influence transferability of split-image attacks across VLMs? Transfer ASR varies (e.g., Pixtral as target yields higher ASR than Qwen-3-VL), but specific contributors are not isolated.

## Limitations
* **VT Defense Implementation:** Exact merging thresholds and algorithms are omitted; approximate implementations may not achieve the paper's reported <5% error rate, affecting attack bypass measurements.
* **LM-Judge Evaluation:** Dependence on GPT-5.1 specifics (prompt, temperature) introduces subjectivity; alternative judges may yield different ASR numbers.
* **aDPO Label Validity:** The core assumption that split variants inherit the original holistic preference label is untested; aggressive splitting could cause semantic drift and corrupt the safety signal.

## Confidence
* **Split-Image Vulnerability (High):** Consistently observed across three VLMs and multiple datasets; mechanism is well-anchored in architecture analysis.
* **Attack Transfer via Adv-KD (Medium):** Evidence is strong but relies on unmeasured latent space alignment quality across diverse targets; success may vary with architectural divergence.
* **aDPO Defense Efficacy (Medium-Low):** Results are promising but evaluated on limited split permutations (K≤3); generalization to arbitrary splits and utility impact remain uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Failure Mode Probe:** Systematically generate diverse split permutations (horizontal/vertical, different fractions) and measure the point at which the VLM's refusal activates to test the "aggregation threshold" hypothesis.
2. **Architectural Transfer Stress Test:** Apply Adv-KD between models with significantly different vision encoders (e.g., ConvNeXt vs. ViT) and measure transfer ASR decay to quantify alignment sensitivity.
3. **Human-in-the-Loop aDPO Label Audit:** For a sample of split-image variants used in aDPO, have annotators judge whether the original preference (safe/harmful) holds; calculate label consistency rate to validate the core assumption.
