# MC-Search: Evaluating and Enhancing Multimodal Agentic Search with Structured Long Reasoning Chains

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00873
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00873
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MC-Search benchmark and Search-Align process-supervised fine-tuning improve open-source MLLMs by up to +13.7 F1 and reduce rollout deviation.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents MC-Search, the first benchmark for multimodal agentic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with structured long reasoning chains. The benchmark contains 3,333 examples covering five reasoning topologies and averaging 3.7 hops, filtered by the Hop-wise Attribution and Verification of Evidence (HAVE) pipeline to ensure quality. The authors propose process-level metrics—including LLM-as-a-Judge, Hit per Step, and Rollout Deviation—to assess reasoning fidelity. Using a unified agentic MM-RAG pipeline, six multimodal large language models (MLLMs) were evaluated, revealing systematic issues such as over-/under-retrieval and modality-misaligned planning. To address these, they introduce Search-Align, a process-supervised fine-tuning approach using verified chains, which significantly boosts open-source models: Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains +13.7 F1 and +16.0 HPS while reducing Rollout Deviation by −3.1, and InternVL3.5-8B improves +2.8 F1 and +12.0 HPS with −0.6 RD.

## Method Summary
The authors construct MC-Search by generating candidate reasoning chains and then filtering with HAVE to retain only necessary hops (based on counterfactual utility and navigational entity flow). The resulting dataset provides golden chains with sub-questions, retrieval modality, supporting facts, and intermediate answers. A unified agentic MM-RAG pipeline iteratively prompts an MLLM to generate sub-questions, select retrieval actions (text or image search), retrieve top-1 evidence from a multimodal knowledge base, and produce sub-answers until termination. Evaluation combines final answer metrics (F1, LLM-as-a-Judge) with chain alignment metrics: Hit per Step (exact retrieval of golden evidence) and Rollout Deviation (difference in step count). Search-Align converts golden chains into conversation traces with explicit “thought” steps and fine-tunes open-source MLLMs via supervised fine-tuning to teach procedural competence in planning and retrieval.

## Key Results
- MC-Search benchmark: 3,333 high-quality multimodal reasoning examples across five topologies, avg 3.7 hops.
- Search-Align yields substantial gains: Qwen2.5-VL-7B +13.7 F1, +16.0 HPS, −3.1 RD; InternVL3.5-8B +2.8 F1, +12.0 HPS, −0.6 RD.
- Systematic failures identified: over-/under-retrieval (high Rollout Deviation) and modality-misaligned planning, especially in image-involved queries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: HAVE Filtering
- **Claim**: The Hop-wise Attribution and Verification of Evidence (HAVE) pipeline generates high-quality, non-redundant reasoning chains by verifying the necessity and navigational utility of each evidence hop.
- **Mechanism**: For each hop t, it computes Util(t) (drop in answer F1 if hop removed) and Nav(t) (whether entities in step t’s answer appear in downstream sub-questions). Hops with low Util *and* no Nav are removed as redundant, ensuring every retained step is either directly required or structurally critical.
- **Break condition**: Fails if Nav(t) misses indirect dependencies between non-consecutive hops.

### Mechanism 2: Chain-Level Alignment Metrics
- **Claim**: Hit per Step (HPS) and Rollout Deviation (RD) precisely diagnose retrieval and planning fidelity beyond final answer accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: HPS measures fraction of golden steps whose evidence is exactly retrieved (via exact match or bipartite matching). RD measures absolute difference in step counts between predicted and golden chains. Together they distinguish under-retrieval (RD>0, low HPS), over-retrieval (RD>0, high core HPS), and modality/planning errors (low HPS at specific indices).
- **Break condition**: Brittle if gold chains are non-unique or if exact match is too strict; soft-HPS variant exists.

### Mechanism 3: Search-Align Process Supervision
- **Claim**: Search-Align, a process-supervised fine-tuning framework using HAVE-verified chains, improves multi-step planning and retrieval fidelity of open-source MLLMs.
- **Mechanism**: Converts step-wise annotations into dialogue traces where the assistant thinks aloud, decides actions, and reasons over retrieved results. Supervised fine-tuning on these teaches procedural competence for joint sub-question generation and modality selection.
- **Break condition**: Effectiveness depends on quality and diversity of HAVE-verified chains; may overfit to training patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: MLLM Agent -> Execution Engine -> Multimodal KB & Retriever -> MLLM Agent (loop); Search-Align SFT Trainer (offline) -> MLLM Agent; Evaluator (receives traces).
- **Critical path**: 1. Agent generates <Sub-Question> and <Action> (modality + query). 2. Execution Engine parses <Search> and triggers Retriever. 3. Retriever returns top-1 result. 4. Agent reasons over evidence to produce <Sub-Answer>. 5. Repeat 1-4 until <End>Final Answer. 6. Evaluator computes metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Top-1 vs. Top-k (precision vs. error risk); agentic looping vs. fixed steps (flexibility vs. complexity); exact-match HPS vs. soft-HPS (strict vs. semantic).
- **Failure signatures**: Low HPS + good F1 → under/over-retrieval or misordering; high image-retrieval failure when no input image → modality mismatch; low LLJ despite decent HPS → integration failure.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline replication: Run agentic pipeline on MC-Search for Gemini-2.5-Pro and Qwen2.5-VL-7B with top-1 retrieval; confirm reported F1, HPS, RD patterns.
  2. Search-Align validation: Fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B on Search-Align traces; expect large HPS gain, RD reduction, error type shifts (decreased Modality-Mismatch, Step-Omission).
  3. Retrieval ablation: Manually inspect high-RD failure cases; test with Retrieval@k=3 to see if HPS improves and RD decreases, confirming over-retrieval as mitigation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: How can multimodal LLMs be trained to avoid over-/under-retrieval and modality-misaligned planning during agentic search? Basis: explicit identification of these as systematic weaknesses. Why unresolved: Without root causes, improvements remain ad-hoc. Evidence needed: Ablation studies isolating planning/retrieval modules; error reduction in process metrics; human evaluation.
- **Question 2**: Why does process-supervised fine-tuning (Search-Align) benefit open-source MLLMs, and can reinforcement learning from process rewards achieve further gains? Basis: Search-Align shows large improvements; RL not explored. Why unresolved: SFT may not be optimal; RL could directly optimize long-horizon fidelity. Evidence needed: Train RL/DPO variants on same chains; compare HPS, RD, accuracy; analyze stability.
- **Question 3**: How transferable are MC-Search findings and Search-Align benefits to domains beyond Wikipedia (e.g., science, mathematics) and to other modalities (e.g., audio, video, 3D)? Basis: Authors plan to extend benchmark, implying current results restricted. Why unresolved: Different domains/modalities introduce new challenges; generalizability unknown. Evidence needed: Build comparable datasets in new domains; re-evaluate models and alignment; compare error profiles.
- **Question 4**: What are the root causes of the most frequent error types (retrieval-failure, hallucination, step omission) in multimodal agentic reasoning? Basis: Error taxonomy shows these account for >70% failures. Why unresolved: No causal analysis; mitigation is trial-and-error. Evidence needed: Correlate errors with model components (vision encoder, planner, retriever) and data characteristics; test targeted interventions; measure error changes.

## Limitations
- KB reconstruction fidelity: Exact details of Wikipedia clustering, image-text pairing, and retrieval index are unspecified, making exact replication of top-1 retrieval results uncertain.
- HAVE pipeline parameters: Thresholds for Util(t) and Nav(t) are not fully detailed, affecting dataset version and minimal hop determination.
- Gold chain uniqueness assumption: If multiple valid reasoning graphs exist, HPS and RD metrics become brittle; soft-HPS may be needed.

## Confidence
- **HAVE generates high-quality chains**: Medium (human verification, but counterfactual assumptions lack independent validation).
- **HPS/RD diagnose fidelity effectively**: Medium (metrics correlate with performance but rely on exact-match and unique gold chains).
- **Search-Align improves open-source MLLMs**: High (conditional) (strong empirical gains reported, but effectiveness tied to HAVE-verified chain quality).
