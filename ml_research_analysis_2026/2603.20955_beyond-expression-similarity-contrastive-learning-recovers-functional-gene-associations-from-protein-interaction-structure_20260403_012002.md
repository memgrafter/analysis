# Beyond Expression Similarity: Contrastive Learning Recovers Functional Gene Associations from Protein Interaction Structure

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20955
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20955
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Contrastive Association Learning (CAL) recovers gene functional associations from protein interaction structure, achieving cross-boundary AUC 0.908 on Replogle K562 CRISPRi data versus 0.518 for expression similarity.

## Executive Summary
Gene expression similarity often fails to link genes that participate in the same biological pathway but play different regulatory roles. This work introduces Contrastive Association Learning (CAL), a contrastive MLP trained on protein–protein interaction (STRING) annotations, which transforms gene perturbation embeddings into an association space. Applied directly without modification, CAL substantially outperforms cosine similarity on recovering STRING-paired genes, particularly for pairs with low expression similarity. Cross-dataset validation on DepMap CRISPR-Cas9 data confirms the gains, revealing three key patterns: (1) successful inductive transfer in biology, (2) concentrated improvement on low-degree genes, and (3) superior performance from higher-quality training data over larger noisier sets.

## Method Summary
The method uses a 4-layer MLP with learned residual blending and L2-normalization to map input gene perturbation embeddings (PCA-reduced, L2-normalized) to an association space. It is trained with symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss on STRING protein interaction pairs, using in-batch negatives except when positive pairs cluster excessively (as in DepMap), where uniform random negatives are required. Evaluation uses a half-transformed cosine scoring (f(eᵢ)·eⱼ + f(eⱼ)·eᵢ)/2 to discriminate associated vs. non-associated pairs, with primary metrics being overall AUC and cross-boundary AUC for pairs with |cosine| < 0.2.

## Key Results
- CAL achieves cross-boundary AUC 0.908 on Replogle K562 CRISPRi data and 0.947 on DepMap (after random negatives), far exceeding expression cosine similarity (~0.5–0.52).
- Inductive transfer (node-split) yields +0.127 improvement over cosine, opposite to typical text retrieval failures.
- Gains are largest for low-degree genes (up to 2× improvement), with CAL scores anti-correlating with STRING degree (Spearman r = -0.590).
- Training on tighter-confidence STRING subsets outperforms larger noisier sets, reversing common ML scaling patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The MLP extracts a latent associative signal from gene perturbation embeddings that is orthogonal to cosine similarity.
- **Mechanism:** The 4-layer MLP with residual connection and L2-normalization transforms input embeddings. Trained with symmetric InfoNCE on STRING pairs, it maps genes to an association space where co-occurring genes are close. This transformation captures functional relationships (e.g., kinase-substrate) that manifest as dissimilar expression profiles but similar interaction neighborhoods.
- **Core assumption:** Perturbation profiles encode pathway membership and regulatory relationships that correlate with physical protein interactions, even when direct expression similarity is low.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Replogle cross-boundary AUC 0.908 vs cosine 0.518; DepMap 0.947.
  - [section 3.2] Cross-boundary AUC robust across thresholds; >20% of STRING high-confidence pairs have |cos|<0.2.
- **Break condition:** Embedding lacks predictive signal for the association task (e.g., Morgan fingerprints for co-lethality, Section 5.1).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Negative sampling must align with the distribution of positive pairs; in-batch negatives fail when positives cluster in embedding space.
- **Mechanism:** Contrastive learning uses negatives to define the decision boundary. If positives are densely clustered (e.g., DepMap co-essential genes), in-batch negatives are drawn from the same cluster, causing the model to push apart genes that should be associated. Uniform random negatives from the full entity space resolve this.
- **Core assumption:** In-batch negatives approximate a random sample only when positives are uniformly distributed across the embedding space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] DepMap in-batch negatives yield AUC 0.379 vs cosine 0.896; random negatives rescue to 0.956.
  - [section 4.2] 98% of DepMap top co-essential pairs have cosine>0.5; Replogle only 39.9% at medium confidence.
- **Break condition:** >50% of positive pairs have cosine similarity >0.5 without random negatives.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Improvement concentrates on low-degree genes due to the single-point prediction constraint of contrastive learning.
- **Mechanism:** Each gene's transformed embedding is pulled toward the centroid of its association neighborhood. For low-degree genes (few partners), this centroid is well-defined. For high-degree hub genes (many partners), the centroid is diffuse, weakening the pairwise signal. The model thus yields higher relative gains for low-degree genes.
- **Core assumption:** The MLP's training objective (minimizing distance to positive partners) produces a single embedding per gene, which averages over all its associations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] CAL scores anti-correlate with STRING degree (Spearman r=-0.590); low-degree genes show up to 2× improvement.
  - [section 6.3] Cross-boundary improvement by degree quintile: Q1 +0.221 vs Q5 +0.127.
  - [section 7] PAM-specific clusters are 86% hub-driven—paradox resolved by pairwise vs cluster-level analysis.
- **Break condition:** Average degree per gene exceeds the entity-to-pair ratio threshold (~1:95 from L1000 experiment), leading to degree confounding.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE Loss)
  - *Why needed here:* CAL's core training objective; symmetric cross-entropy on cosine similarity matrix pulls positives together and pushes negatives apart.
  - *Quick check question:* What does temperature τ=0.05 control in the InfoNCE loss?
- **Concept:** Network Degree Centrality
  - *Why needed here:* Explains differential performance; low-degree genes have focused interaction profiles suited to the single-point embedding constraint.
  - *Quick check question:* Why do hub genes form clusters in association space despite lower pairwise scores?
- **Concept:** Negative Sampling Strategy
  - *Why needed here:* Determines whether the model learns genuine associations or dataset artifacts (e.g., degree, clustering).
  - *Quick check question:* What diagnostic indicates that in-batch negatives are inappropriate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (L2-normalized embedding) -> MLP (3 hidden layers, 1024 units, GELU, LayerNorm) -> Learned residual blend (α = σ(learned scalar)) -> L2-normalization -> Output association embedding. Scoring: half-transformed cosine (f(eᵢ)·eⱼ + f(eⱼ)·eᵢ)/2.
- **Critical path:** L2-normalized input -> MLP transformation -> residual blend with input -> L2-normalized output -> half-transformed cosine scoring with target gene.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Batch size (512) vs. negative diversity: larger batches improve in-batch negatives only if positives are not clustered.
  - Residual blend (α learned near 0.5) balances preservation vs transformation.
  - Scoring function: half-transformed avoids degenerate solutions when positives and negatives are imbalanced.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Shuffled ablation (random pairings) ≥ reference model.
  - Training accuracy near chance (1/B).
  - Cosine AUC already >0.85 on association labels.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replogle (success):** STRING≥900, in-batch negatives, verify cross-boundary AUC >0.9, λ-sweep shows monotonic gain with association-only.
  2. **DepMap (negative sampling fix):** Confirm positives cluster (>50% cosine>0.5); switch to random negatives; validate cross-boundary AUC >0.94.
  3. **Drug fingerprints (latent signal absence):** Morgan embeddings + co-lethality; cosine AUC ~0.53; if shuffled > real, abort—embedding lacks predictive signal.

## Limitations
- Exact negative sampling procedure during training (in-batch details, resampling cadence) and precise inductive node-split protocol are unspecified.
- Generalizability beyond CRISPRi (Replogle) and CRISPR-Cas9 (DepMap) perturbation types is unproven.
- Claims rely on predefined association thresholds (STRING confidence) and evaluation negatives; sensitivity to these choices is not fully characterized.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (MLP extracts orthogonal signal):** High (replicated AUC gains + >20% low-cosine STRING pairs).
- **Mechanism 2 (negative sampling alignment):** Medium (DepMap evidence strong but no direct corpus anchor).
- **Mechanism 3 (low-degree focus):** Medium (degree-AUC anti-correlation clear, but break condition theoretical).
- **Inductive transfer success:** Medium (node-split +0.127 shown, but text analogy is circumstantial).

## Next Checks
1. **Positive-pose cosine clustering diagnostic:** On DepMap-equivalent data, compute % of training positives with cosine >0.5. If >50%, enforce random negatives per batch; expect AUC jump toward 0.947.
2. **Scoring function ablation:** Compare half-transformed (f(eᵢ)·eⱼ + f(eⱼ)·eᵢ)/2 vs. full-transform (f(eᵢ)·f(eⱼ)) on Replogle. Full-transform should degrade if positives/negatives imbalanced.
3. **Node-split robustness:** Run 5 random seeds for 70/30 gene holdout. Mean node-split AUC should exceed cosine baseline by >0.1 with SD <0.005, confirming stable inductive gain.
