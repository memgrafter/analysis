# Reliable Thinking with Images

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.12916
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12916
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RTWI attains state‑of‑the‑art accuracy (85.2% on Vstar) while reducing token usage by up to 61.1% compared to self‑consistency.

## Executive Summary
Thinking with Images (TWI) interleaves textual chain‑of‑thought with visual tool calls to enhance multimodal reasoning, but it suffers from Noisy Thinking—errors in visual cue extraction or reasoning that cascade. This paper introduces Reliable Thinking with Images (RTWI), a test‑time scaling method that estimates the reliability of each trace by measuring token entropy in the textual CoT. Low entropy suggests stable, confident reasoning and likely correct visual cues. RTWI generates multiple traces, filters out those with low reliability in either the mining or reasoning stage using adaptive thresholds, and aggregates the remaining answers with a reliability‑weighted vote that considers the reliability leap between stages.

Extensive experiments on seven benchmarks across diverse visual reasoning tasks with Qwen3‑VL show that RTWI consistently outperforms state‑of‑the‑art test‑time scaling methods, achieving higher accuracy and substantial token savings. For instance, on Vstar (Thinking) it reaches 85.2% accuracy (vs 80.0% for self‑consistency) with 61.1% token reduction, and on HR‑Bench 4K FSP it obtains 91.3% accuracy.

## Method Summary
RTWI is a training‑free, test‑time scaling approach for multimodal large language models that refines the Thinking with Images paradigm. Given an image‑question pair, it samples multiple interleaved image‑text CoT traces from a TWI model (e.g., Qwen3‑VL). Each trace is split into a mining stage (turns with tool calls like zoom) and a reasoning stage (final answer). For each stage, reliability is computed as the negative average of the top‑k highest token entropies; lower entropy indicates higher confidence. The value of k is chosen per question from a small set (e.g., {1,5,10,20}) to maximize the average reliability leap (reasoning minus mining reliability). Using a warmup set of traces, adaptive thresholds ηm and ηr are set (e.g., 40‑th percentiles). Traces failing either threshold are discarded. The remaining traces are aggregated via weighted voting; each trace’s weight is Ct = exp(∆t / (|wt|·τ)), where ∆t is the reliability leap (clipped at zero) and wt is the sum of stage reliabilities. For traces without a distinct mining stage, a fallback weight using ηr - ηm is used. An online variant can stop generating a trace early if interim reliability falls below ηm and halt entirely once a candidate answer reaches 90% consensus.

## Key Results
- On Vstar (Thinking), RTWI achieves 85.2% accuracy (vs 80.0% for self‑consistency) and saves 61.1% tokens.
- On HR‑Bench 4K FSP, it reaches 91.3% accuracy.
- Across seven benchmarks, RTWI consistently outperforms SOTA test‑time scaling methods in both accuracy and efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Token entropy in textual CoT serves as a proxy for visual cue reliability and reasoning quality.
- **Mechanism:** For each thinking trace, compute the average entropy of the top‑k highest‑entropy tokens in the textual CoT. Lower average entropy indicates higher confidence in the textual reasoning, which the authors assume correlates with the quality of the preceding visual cue mining (since reliable tool‑invocation instructions produce stable textual predictions) and the subsequent reasoning steps.
- **Core assumption:** The textual CoT’s predictability (low entropy) at decision points implicitly reflects the correctness of the multimodal reasoning process, including the relevance of extracted visual cues.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “…estimates stage reliability from token entropy in the textual CoT, serving as a proxy for visual cue quality.”
  - [section 3.2] “Instead of exhaustively modeling visual uncertainty, we argue that the image‑manipulation textual CoT itself could implicitly serve as a natural proxy for visual cue reliability.”
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on interleaved reasoning architectures but not entropy‑based reliability.
- **Break condition:** When the MLLM exhibits high confidence (low entropy) in a textual CoT step that is factually incorrect due to a subtle visual error, the entropy proxy fails. This is likely when the model is “confidently wrong” on a nuanced spatial or relational detail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dual‑stage filtering with self‑adaptive thresholds removes traces with unreliable cue mining or reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** For a set of sampled traces, compute percentiles of stage‑wise reliability scores (mining and reasoning). Traces falling below a filtering ratio α (e.g., bottom 40%) in *either* stage are discarded. This prevents noisy traces from contaminating the answer aggregation.
- **Core assumption:** A single highly unreliable stage (mining *or* reasoning) is sufficient to doom the entire trace, and this unreliability is detectable via the proposed entropy metric.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “…filters out thoughts with low reliability using adaptive thresholds…”
  - [section 3.3] “To prevent unreliable traces from contaminating the answer, we propose to generate multiple thinking traces and prioritize trustworthy traces among them… Trel = { t | w(tm)≥ηm ∧ w(tr)≥ηr }”
  - [corpus] Not addressed in neighboring papers; this is RTWI’s specific filtering design.
- **Break condition:** If the reliability distributions for correct and incorrect traces overlap significantly at the threshold percentile α, filtering will remove both reliable and unreliable traces, reducing the pool of good candidates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reliability‑weighted voting, favoring traces with high dual‑stage reliability and a significant reliability leap, yields more accurate answers than majority voting.
- **Mechanism:** Among filtered traces, assign a confidence weight Ct = exp(∆t / (|wt| · τ)). ∆t is the “reliability leap” (max(w(tr) - w(tm), 0)), and wt is the sum of stage reliabilities. This boosts weight for traces that become more confident in the reasoning stage after mining, which the paper associates with correct visual cues.
- **Core assumption:** A large positive leap (reasoning entropy drops significantly relative to mining) indicates that the mined visual cues were correct and stabilized the reasoning, a pattern more common in successful traces.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “…aggregates remaining answers via reliability‑weighted voting, favoring traces with high dual‑stage reliability and significant reliability leap.”
  - [section 3.4] “The design of ∆t could mine the potential correct answer by prioritizing traces with substantial reliability leap.”
  - [section 4.3] Analytic study visualizes that correct traces have higher confidence (Ct) distributions.
- **Break condition:** If a trace has a large reliability leap due to a noisy mining stage (high entropy) followed by a deceptively confident but erroneous reasoning step (low entropy), it will be overweighted. This occurs if the model generates a plausible but wrong explanation consistent with a misinterpreted visual cue.

  - **Why needed here:** The entire mechanism operates on textual CoT sequences interleaved with visual tool calls. One must understand standard text‑only CoT to appreciate the added complexity of multimodal “thinking with images.”
  - **Quick check question:** In TWI, what are the two distinct stages (mining vs. reasoning) and how do they differ in terms of tool usage and output?
- **Concept: Entropy as an Uncertainty Proxy**
  - **Why needed here:** The core reliability signal is token entropy. Foundational knowledge of how predictive entropy is computed from model output distributions is necessary.
  - **Quick check question:** Given a model’s next‑token probability distribution p over vocabulary V, how is the entropy H for a token position calculated? What does a high vs. low entropy value intuitively signify?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
1. **Trace Sampler:** Generates `N` interleaved image‑text CoT traces via the TWI model (e.g., Qwen3‑VL). Handles tool calls (e.g., zoom) and final answer extraction.
2. **Reliability Estimator:** Computes stage‑wise reliability `w(tm)` and `w(tr)` for each trace using Eq. 3 (average top‑k token entropy). Also computes trace reliability `wt` and reliability leap `∆t`.
3. **Dual‑Stage Filter:** Computes thresholds `ηm` and `ηr` from a warmup/trace set (percentile α). Filters trace set `T` to `T_rel` (Eq. 4).
4. **Reliable Voter:** Computes confidence weight `Ct` for each trace in `T_rel` (Eq. 6). Aggregates answers via weighted voting to produce final `Â`.

**Critical Path:** Generate multiple traces → Estimate reliability for each stage of each trace → Compute adaptive thresholds from warmup traces (offline) or running percentiles (online) → Filter traces → Compute leap‑based confidence → Weighted vote.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **Text‑centric vs. Visual‑fidelity:** RTWI avoids direct visual uncertainty estimation (computationally hard) at the cost of relying on a proxy (text entropy). If the proxy is misaligned, the system may trust visually erroneous traces with linguistically smooth CoT.
- **Threshold Sensitivity:** Filtering ratio `α` is a hyperparameter. Too low (`α≈0`) filters little; too high (`α≈1`) filters too much, risking loss of correct traces. The paper finds stability in `[0.3, 0.5]`.
- **Early Stopping (Online):** The online version uses a loose threshold `ηm` during generation to prune tokens early, but this risks prematurely stopping a trace that might have recovered. The final dual‑stage filter is more stringent.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **No tool invocation:** If the model never calls tools (e.g., on a task requiring visual search), mining reliability cannot be estimated from tool‑invocation tokens. The system falls back to single‑stage `tr` reliability (Appendix A.1), weakening detection of mining noise.
- **Entropy miscalibration:** If the model’s output distribution is miscalibrated (e.g., low entropy on wrong tokens due to overfitting), reliability scores become meaningless.
- **Single‑stage traces:** Some traces may only consist of reasoning `tr` without a distinct mining `tm` (if no tool use). The leap `∆t` becomes `ηr - ηm` (Appendix A.1), which is a heuristic.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Ablation on Dual‑Stage Filtering & Voting:** Remove DF or RV modules (Table 4) to show both components contribute to accuracy gains and token savings.
2. **Scaling Law Test:** Vary test‑time budget (number of traces) and model size (2B/4B/8B/32B) to demonstrate RTWI’s gains scale with compute and model capacity (Fig. 4, 5).
3. **Visual Cue Consistency Analysis:** On TreeBench (with ground‑truth bounding boxes), show RTWI’s filtered traces have higher mIoU/consistency among visual cues than baselines (Table 6, Appendix C.5), proving it selects more coherent task‑relevant regions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not contain an explicit "Open Questions" section. However, the Limitations section (above) highlights several issues that can be viewed as open research questions: the robustness of the entropy proxy for different visual error modes, the sensitivity of adaptive thresholds across datasets, and the generalizability of RTWI to other MLLM architectures. These represent natural directions for future work.

## Limitations
- Entropy proxy may fail for confident but visually erroneous reasoning.
- Adaptive thresholds require per‑dataset tuning and are sensitive to α.
- Missing reproduction details hinder faithful replication and generalization to other models.
