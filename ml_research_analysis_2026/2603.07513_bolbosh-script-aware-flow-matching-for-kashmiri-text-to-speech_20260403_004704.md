# Bolbosh: Script-Aware Flow Matching for Kashmiri Text-to-Speech

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07513
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07513
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Bolbosh achieves a mean opinion score (MOS) of 3.63 and a mel-cepstral distortion (MCD) of 3.73, far surpassing zero-shot multilingual baselines (MOS 1.86).

## Executive Summary
Kashmiri, a low-resource language written in the Perso-Arabic script, lacks robust text-to-speech systems. Zero-shot multilingual TTS models perform poorly (MOS 1.86) because they fail to model diacritics and phonotactic constraints. The Bolbosh system addresses these challenges through supervised cross-lingual adaptation using Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) within the Matcha-TTS framework, fine-tuned from a pretrained English checkpoint. Innovations include a three-stage acoustic enhancement pipeline to harmonize heterogeneous training data and an expanded 272-grapheme vocabulary that preserves diacritics. Bolbosh attains a MOS of 3.63 and an MCD of 3.73, demonstrating that script-aware modeling and supervised flow-based adaptation are critical for diacritic-sensitive, low-resource TTS.

## Method Summary
Bolbosh builds upon Matcha-TTS, a non-autoregressive TTS system that uses conditional flow matching for mel-spectrogram generation. The authors perform supervised cross-lingual adaptation by fine-tuning a model pretrained on English to Kashmiri. The adaptation employs Optimal Transport CFM (OT-CFM) to improve text-speech alignment. To handle the heterogeneous nature of their combined corpus (RASA and IndicVoices-R), they introduce a three-stage acoustic enhancement pipeline that standardizes acoustic features across sources. Furthermore, they design a grapheme tokenizer with 272 symbols that retain all diacritic marks from the Perso-Arabic script, enabling precise orthographic-phonetic mapping.

## Key Results
- **MOS**: Bolbosh achieves 3.63, compared to 1.86 for zero-shot multilingual baselines.
- **MCD**: Bolbosh attains 3.73, indicating high spectral fidelity (lower is better).
- **Improvement**: A substantial gain of +1.77 in MOS, confirming the effectiveness of the proposed adaptations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Bolbosh succeeds because it directly addresses the key weaknesses of zero-shot models: diacritic insensitivity and poor phonotactic modeling. The script-aware grapheme vocabulary ensures that diacritic information, which dramatically affects pronunciation in Kashmiri, is preserved and learned. Supervised cross-lingual adaptation with OT-CFM leverages the strong priors from the English pretrained model while aligning text and speech in a continuous latent space, requiring relatively little Kashmiri data. The three-stage acoustic enhancement pipeline improves training data quality by normalizing acoustic characteristics from diverse recordings, leading to more stable optimization and better generalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM)**: A flow-based generative model that uses optimal transport to compute transport paths between conditional distributions, enabling efficient and stable training for mel-spectrogram synthesis.
  - *Why needed*: Provides high-quality, non-autoregressive generation with better alignment between text and speech.
  - *Quick check*: Verify that the OT-CFM loss decreases during training and that generated spectrograms show smooth transitions.
- **Script-aware modeling**: The practice of preserving language-specific orthographic details (like diacritics) in the input representation.
  - *Why needed*: Diacritics in Kashmiri alter consonant pronunciation and vowel length; ignoring them leads to semantic errors.
  - *Quick check*: Count the number of diacritic tokens in the vocabulary and confirm they appear in the training text.
- **Supervised cross-lingual adaptation**: Fine-tuning a pretrained model (often on a high-resource language) with limited data from a target language.
  - *Why needed*: Avoids training from scratch for low-resource languages and leverages universal speech representations.
  - *Quick check*: Compare performance of the adapted model against a randomly initialized model on a small validation set.
- **Three-stage acoustic enhancement**: A preprocessing pipeline that sequentially applies operations (e.g., silence removal, loudness normalization, spectral flattening) to harmonize acoustic features.
  - *Why needed*: Mitigates variability in heterogeneous corpora, improving model convergence.
  - *Quick check*: Visualize spectrograms before and after enhancement to confirm reduced variance in energy and spectral tilt.
- **Low-resource TTS**: Techniques for building speech synthesizers with limited labeled data.
  - *Why needed*: Many languages lack large corpora; Bolbosh demonstrates a path using adaptation rather than data collection.
  - *Quick check*: Measure performance as a function of training data size; expect steep improvements with small data.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Raw text -> Text normalization -> Grapheme embedding (272-dim) -> Transformer encoder -> Duration/Pitch/Energy predictors -> OT-CFM decoder -> Mel-spectrogram  

**Critical path**: The sequence of modules that transform input text into an output mel-spectrogram during inference, with the OT-CFM decoder being the core generative component.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Large grapheme vocabulary increases capacity and diacritic fidelity but requires more data and risks overfitting.
- Flow matching yields high-quality outputs but sampling can be slower than autoregressive models.
- Supervised adaptation reduces data needs compared to training from scratch but still requires some labeled speech; gains depend on source-target similarity.
- Three-stage acoustic enhancement improves data consistency but adds preprocessing complexity and may discard useful variability if not tuned.

**Failure signatures**:
- Omission or misuse of diacritics in generated speech, leading to wrong words or mispronunciations.
- Inconsistent or unnatural prosody (pitch/energy contours), especially in long utterances.
- Spectral artifacts such as buzzing or metallic sounds, often due to flow matching sampling errors.
- Poor generalization to out-of-domain text (e.g., foreign names) due to limited Kashmiri training data.

**Three first experiments**:
1. Diacritic ablation test: Modify the tokenizer to remove diacritics and retrain; compare MOS and MCD to isolate their impact.
2. Acoustic pipeline ablation: Train without the three-stage enhancement and evaluate stability and quality.
3. Checkpoint source analysis: Initialize the model with a randomly sampled transformer instead of the English-pretrained weights and measure convergence speed and final performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How can Bolbosh be extended to incorporate multiple Kashmiri dialects, given that the current system does not account for dialectal variation?  
- *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion states that "multi-dialect modeling" will be explored in future work.  
- *Why unresolved*: The training corpus combines RASA and IndicVoices-R without dialect labels, and the model lacks dialect-specific conditioning.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Curate a dialect-annotated Kashmiri speech dataset, add dialect embeddings to the model, and evaluate synthesis quality per dialect using MOS and MCD.

**Open Question 2**: What architectural or training modifications are required to achieve fine-grained prosody control in Bolbosh, beyond the existing pitch and energy predictors?  
- *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion mentions "enhanced prosody control" as a future direction.  
- *Why unresolved*: The current prosody modeling is basic and may not capture the full range of natural prosodic variation, potentially limiting expressiveness.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Integrate latent prosody variables, employ reference audio encoders, or use explicit duration modeling, then measure improvements through listening studies (MOS) and objective prosody similarity metrics.

**Open Question 3**: Can the supervised cross-lingual adaptation strategy using OT-CFM be effectively generalized to other under-resourced languages with diacritic-sensitive orthographies, and what factors (e.g., script similarity, data size) influence its success?  
- *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion states that "cross-lingual transfer to other under-resourced languages" will be investigated.  
- *Why unresolved*: The paper only evaluates on Kashmiri; it is unknown whether the approach generalizes to other languages.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Replicate the Bolbosh pipeline on a diverse set of low-resource languages (e.g., Pashto, Sindhi), compare performance against zero-shot multilingual baselines, and analyze correlations with linguistic distances and data availability.

**Open Question 4**: How can Bolbosh be adapted to support the multiple writing systems used for Kashmiri (Perso-Arabic, Devanagari, Roman) and handle orthographic inconsistencies, given that the current system is designed solely for Perso-Arabic?  
- *Basis in paper*: [inferred] The introduction highlights that Kashmiri is written in multiple scripts, but the text processing and vocabulary are tailored only to Perso-Arabic.  
- *Why unresolved*: Speakers using other scripts cannot directly use the system without transliteration, which may lose diacritic information or introduce errors.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Develop a script-agnostic normalization pipeline that maps all scripts to a common grapheme or phoneme representation, expand the vocabulary accordingly, and evaluate TTS quality on texts in each script.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Evaluation only on Kashmiri; it is unclear if the script-aware OT-CFM pipeline transfers to other diacritic-rich, low-resource scripts (e.g., Urdu, Persian).
- **Isolated contribution**: The three-stage acoustic enhancement pipeline is not evaluated separately; its relative contribution to the performance gain is uncertain.
- **Data heterogeneity**: The heterogeneous corporate corpus sources and quality are not deeply analyzed, potentially confounding results and limiting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Supervised cross-lingual adaptation with OT-CFM** significantly improves zero-shot performance for Kashmiri: **High** confidence.
- **Script-aware modeling with 272-grapheme diacritic-preserving vocabulary** is critical for success: **Medium** confidence.
- **Three-stage acoustic enhancement pipeline** effectiveness: **Low** confidence.
