# Markovian Generation Chains in Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11228
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11228
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Decoding strategy determines whether iterative LLM reprocessing rapidly converges to low-diversity fixed points (greedy) or sustains higher diversity over many steps (sampling), with round-trip translation via LLMs showing more surface variability than deterministic machine translation.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the sentence-level evolution of text when iteratively reprocessed by large language models (LLMs) under a fixed prompt. Framing the process as a time-homogeneous Markov chain—where each state is a sentence string and transitions are governed by the model, prompt, and decoding configuration—the authors analyze behavior over T=50 iterations. Core metrics include the number of distinct sentences produced, the first recurrence time of any sentence, and stepwise similarity scores (METEOR, ROUGE, BLEU). The central finding is that greedy decoding leads to rapid convergence into small recurrent sets (fixed points or short cycles), yielding low diversity. In contrast, sampling-based decoding (temperature=0.7, top-p=0.9) extends the transient phase and increases distinct-sentence counts. The study distinguishes these inference-time dynamics from training-time model collapse, showing that iterative reprocessing can either reduce or increase diversity depending solely on the decoding configuration and initial input.

## Method Summary
The experimental procedure applies a fixed transformation operator iteratively: starting from a seed sentence, a frozen LLM generates a new sentence given a prompt template that instructs a specific task (e.g., rephrasing, translation). The process repeats for T=50 steps, with only the previous sentence carried forward as state. Three datasets (BookSum, ScriptBase-alpha, BBC News2024) provide 150 seed sentences each. Models tested include open-weight LLMs (Mistral-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) via local inference and GPT-4o-mini via API. Two primary decoding regimes are compared: greedy (argmax) and sampling (temperature=0.7, top-p=0.9). For each trajectory, metrics are computed: distinct-sentence count (unique states), first recurrence time (earliest exact repetition), and similarity between consecutive outputs. Round-trip translation experiments chain two translation steps (e.g., English→French→English) and compare LLM output variability against deterministic production MT (Google Translate).

## Key Results
- Greedy decoding quickly converges to small recurrent sets (often single fixed points or short cycles), resulting in very low distinct-sentence counts (often ≤3 over 50 steps).
- Sampling-based decoding (temperature=0.7, top-p=0.9) produces longer transient phases and substantially higher distinct-sentence counts, sometimes exceeding 30.
- Round-trip translation using LLMs exhibits far greater surface-form variability than deterministic machine translation systems, highlighting the role of decoding stochasticity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoding strategy fundamentally alters the sentence-level Markov transition kernel, determining whether trajectories rapidly absorb into recurrent sets (low diversity) or explore a larger transient region (high diversity).
- **Mechanism:** Greedy decoding selects a single highest-probability next sentence, creating a deterministic transition matrix with narrow, high-probability paths that funnel trajectories into small attractors. Sampling flattens the probability distribution, assigning non-negligible mass to a wider set of plausible sentences, which broadens the effective transition matrix and delays absorption.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "greedy decoding leads to rapid convergence to small recurrent sets... Sampling-based decoding... produces longer transient phases and substantially higher distinct-sentence counts."
  - [section 3.1, 3.3] Formal definition of the operator T and discussion of how decoding controls the kernel P, affecting pre-recurrence behavior.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers focus on iterative bootstrapping or diffusion models, not directly on LLM decoding's effect on sentence-level Markov kernels; evidence for this specific mechanism is indirect (empirical recurrence statistics).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The asymptotic structure of the induced Markov chain—recurrent classes and transient states—explains the observed dichotomy between convergence and sustained exploration within a finite horizon.
- **Mechanism:** For a finite state space of sentences, the transition matrix P can be permuted into canonical form with recurrent blocks (C_i) and a transient block (P_tr). The empirical "first recurrence time" τ_T measures entry into a recurrent class. A small τ_T indicates rapid convergence; a large τ_T (or T+1) indicates prolonged exploration in the transient region.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The output either converges to a small recurrent set or continues to produce novel sentences over a finite horizon."
  - [section 3.3] Explicit block form (Eq. 7) and finite-horizon recurrence statistic τ_T linking empirical regimes to Markov class structure.
  - [corpus] Work on Markov vs. non-Markov processes in diffusion models provides analogies but no direct evidence for autoregressive LLMs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Inference-time iterative reprocessing is distinct from training-time model collapse; the former's dynamics are governed by a stationary transformation kernel, while the latter involves parameter updates causing distributional shift.
- **Mechanism:** In this study, model parameters are frozen across iterations, so the kernel P (defined by M, ρ, d) is time-invariant. Diversity outcomes depend on the entropy and contraction properties of P under repeated multiplication. Training collapse involves iterative parameter updates θ_t → θ_{t+1} based on model-generated data, creating a moving target.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We distinguish these inference-time dynamics from training-time model collapse... iterative reprocessing can either reduce or increase sentence-level diversity depending on decoding configuration and input."
  - [section 5.4] Explicit contrast with Shumailov et al. (2024), noting absence of parameter updates.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "How bad is training on synthetic data?" address training-time collapse, not inference-time recursion.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Discrete-Time, Time-Homogeneous Markov Chains**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's core model treats iterative reprocessing as a Markov chain on sentence strings. Understanding states, transition matrices, recurrent classes, and transients is essential to grasp the theoretical framing and metrics like first recurrence time.
  - *Quick check:* If a Markov chain's transition matrix is doubly stochastic (row and column sums equal 1), what happens to the entropy of the state distribution after many steps? (Hint: Such matrices preserve uniform distribution; entropy may increase from non-uniform starts but converge to maximum under irreducibility.)

- **Concept: Decoding Strategies in Autoregressive LLMs (Greedy vs. Sampling)**
  - *Why needed:* Decoding configuration d is the primary knob that reshapes the transition kernel P. Greedy yields deterministic, low-entropy transitions; sampling introduces stochasticity and broadens support.
  - *Quick check:* How does increasing the temperature parameter τ in softmax sampling affect the row sums of the induced sentence-level transition matrix P? (Hint: Temperature scales logits; higher τ flattens the distribution, making row entries more uniform.)

- **Concept: Inference-Time vs. Training-Time Dynamics**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's key contribution is analyzing a fixed inference-time process. Confusing it with training-time model collapse would misinterpret findings and implications.
  - *Quick check:* In the paper's setup, what remains constant across iterations that would *not* remain constant during a typical model collapse training loop? (Hint: Model parameters; training collapse involves updating θ based on model outputs.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Model (M) -> Prompt Template (ρ) -> Decoding Configuration (d) -> State (s^{(t)}) -> Transition Operator (T_{M,ρ,d}) -> Metrics Collector (distinct count U, recurrence τ_T, similarity scores)

- **Critical path:**  
  1.  Initialize seed sentence s^{(0)}.  
  2.  For t = 0 to T-1: format prompt ρ(s^{(t)}), call model with decoding d, parse output to single sentence s^{(t+1)}.  
  3.  Track unique s^{(t)} and detect first exact recurrence.  
  4.  After T steps, compute metrics: U (unique count), τ_T (first recurrence index), and similarity between s^{(t)} and s^{(t-1)} / s^{(0)}.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Greedy decoding is deterministic and fast but risks rapid convergence to low-diversity attractors. Sampling increases exploration and diversity but introduces run-to-run variance and may cause semantic drift.  
  - Prompt design: restrictive prompts (e.g., "rephrase exactly") shrink kernel support; looser prompts expand it. Prompt alternation (time-inhomogeneous chain) can boost diversity but complicates Markov analysis.  
  - Horizon T must balance computational cost against capturing transient vs. recurrent behavior; too short may miss late recurrence, too long may be wasteful if early convergence is certain.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Premature convergence: τ_T very small (<5) and U near 1–2 under greedy decoding for most seeds.  
  - Semantic drift: stepwise similarity (e.g., METEOR) plateaus high (>0.85) but cumulative similarity to s^{(0)} degrades, indicating meaning change within a lexical cycle.  
  - API nondeterminism: Black-box API (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) without fixed seed control may confound decoding effects with backend randomness; requires multiple runs per chain.  
  - Parsing failure: Model outputs multi-sentence text or formatting, violating single-sentence state assumption; requires robust post-processing (stop tokens, length limits).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1.  Reproduce main Figure 2: For BookSum dataset and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, run 150 seed sentences through T=50 iterations under greedy and sampling (τ=0.7, top-p=0.9). Plot average distinct-sentence count U and recurrence time distribution; verify sampling yields significantly higher U and longer τ_T.  
  2.  Temperature sweep: Fix seed and model. Run chains for temperatures ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0}. Plot U and average τ_T vs. temperature; expect monotonic increase in diversity within range.  
  3.  Round-trip translation variability: For 50 English sentences, run iterative EN→French→EN chains using GPT-4o-mini (sampling) and Google Translate v3 (deterministic). Compare distinct-sentence counts after 10 iterations; expect LLM chain to show higher variability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can we measure and mitigate semantic drift in iterative LLM reprocessing?  
  *Basis:* The authors note "sentence diversity does not imply semantic fidelity" and rely on surface similarity metrics only.  
  *Why unresolved:* No direct assessment of meaning preservation; unknown if increased surface diversity correlates with semantic drift.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Human evaluations or embedding-based semantic metrics (e.g., BERTScore) at each iteration; prompts explicitly aimed at preserving semantics.

- **Question:** How do recurrence and diversity dynamics scale to paragraphs or documents, and do sentence-level Markov approximations hold?  
  *Basis:* The paper mentions a limited paragraph ablation finding rare full-paragraph recurrence but persistent sentence-level recurrence.  
  *Why unresolved:* Experiments used only 150 seeds and T=50; impact of text length, coherence, and topic remains open.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Large-scale paragraph/document experiments with varied lengths/genres, measuring distinctness and semantic coherence; comparison with sentence-level chains.

- **Question:** How do iterative reprocessing dynamics vary across different NLP tasks (e.g., summarization, dialogue)?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion states "The behavior of LLMs in a wider range of scenarios also requires further study."  
  *Why unresolved:* Only rephrasing and round-trip translation were studied; other tasks may induce different transformation operators.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Apply the Markovian chain methodology to diverse tasks with appropriate prompts; compare metrics like distinct count, recurrence time, and semantic drift across tasks.

## Limitations
- Markov state defined by exact string equality may be too coarse; semantic recurrence could occur without lexical repetition.
- Finite horizon T=50 may miss late asymptotic dynamics or very long transient phases.
- Prompt templates and generation parameters are underspecified, risking replication fidelity and prompt-drift confounders.
- Results may not generalize across model families, scales, or beyond studied tasks (rephrasing, translation).
- Time-homogeneity assumption may be violated by caching, nondeterminism, or effective prompt changes across iterations.

## Confidence
- *Decoding configuration determines diversity*: **Medium**. Strong empirical patterns but key details omitted; possible confounders (nondeterminism, prompt drift).
- *Sentence-level Markov chain framing*: **Low**. Relies on coarse state (exact string) and time-homogeneity; evidence is indirect (empirical recurrence statistics) rather than rigorous test of Markov property.
- *Inference-time vs. training-time collapse distinction*: **High**. Experimental setup clearly freezes parameters; training collapse requires iterative updates, making separation conceptually and practically sound.
- *LLM round-trip translation variability*: **Medium**. Clear contrast with deterministic MT but limited to few language pairs and models; API nondeterminism may affect reproducibility.

## Next Checks
1.  **Semantic recurrence analysis:** Redefine recurrence using embedding similarity (e.g., cosine >0.9) instead of exact string match. Recompute first recurrence time and distinct-sentence count across decoding regimes; test whether greedy still converges rapidly under semantic equivalence.
2.  **Long-horizon scaling:** Increase iteration count T to 200–500 for a subset of seeds (e.g., 30 per condition). Track whether greedy chains remain in small cycles and whether sampling chains eventually settle or sustain transients.
3.  **Prompt sensitivity test:** For each task (rephrasing, translation), design at least three prompt variants with semantically equivalent instructions. Run the full pipeline and quantify variance in distinct count U and recurrence time τ_T; assess robustness of the decoding effect to prompt wording.
