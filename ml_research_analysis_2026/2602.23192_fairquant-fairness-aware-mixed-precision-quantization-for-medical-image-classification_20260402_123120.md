# FairQuant: Fairness-Aware Mixed-Precision Quantization for Medical Image Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23192
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23192
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** At average 4–6 bits, FairQuant recovers nearly the accuracy of uniform 8‑bit quantization while significantly improving worst‑group accuracy on medical image classification benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
FairQuant addresses the dual challenges of model efficiency and algorithmic fairness in medical image classification via mixed‑precision quantization. It combines group‑aware importance analysis, budgeted mixed‑precision allocation, and a learnable Bit‑Aware Quantization (BAQ) module that jointly optimizes weights and bit‑widths under bitrate and fairness regularization. Evaluations on Fitzpatrick17k and ISIC2019 with ResNet18/50, DeiT‑Tiny, and TinyViT show that at 4–6 average bits, FairQuant attains accuracy similar to uniform 8‑bit while substantially boosting worst‑group accuracy compared to uniform 4‑bit baselines, with stable performance across hyperparameters.

## Method Summary
FairQuant operates in three stages: calibration, static allocation, and bit‑aware fine‑tuning. Calibration uses a few batches to compute group‑restricted loss gradients, approximating each scope’s impact on each sensitive group via a first‑order Taylor expansion; a max‑reduction across groups yields a single importance map. Budgeted mixed‑precision allocation then assigns discrete bit‑widths (e.g., {2,4,8}) to scopes according to quantiles of this map, respecting a global bit budget. Finally, the Bit‑Aware Quantization (BAQ) module introduces a learnable logit per scope to produce a continuous bit proxy; during joint training, the model optimizes task loss plus a fairness penalty (max group loss minus min group loss) and an L2 regularizer on the logits to control average bitrate. Discrete bit‑widths are obtained by rounding the proxies at the end.

## Key Results
- At 4–6 average bits, FairQuant matches the accuracy of uniform 8‑bit quantization while greatly improving worst‑group accuracy over uniform 4‑bit baselines.
- Example on Fitzpatrick17k (ResNet18): uniform 4‑bit (AvgAcc = 23.4%, WorstAcc = 19.0%) vs. FairQuant‑BAQ (4.07 bits, AvgAcc = 45.33%, WorstAcc = 41.53%).
- Fairness metrics (Equalized Odds, Equality of Opportunity) are comparable or improved under shared bit budgets; ablations indicate stable performance across hyperparameter sweeps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Group‑aware Importance Analysis
- **Claim:** Identifies model units most critical for underserved groups, creating a sensitivity map that balances overall performance with subgroup reliability.
- **Mechanism:** During calibration with frozen weights, group‑restricted losses are computed per sensitive group. A first‑order Taylor expansion approximates each scope’s impact on these losses, yielding group‑specific importance tensors. These are aggregated to the scope level and reduced across groups via a max‑normalization (Il = max_g ¯Il,g), producing a single map that highlights units important for any group.
- **Core assumption:** The gradient‑based importance score (Il,g ≈ (∇W_l L_g(W) ⊙ W_l)^2) reliably estimates a unit’s contribution to a group’s loss, and the max‑reducer effectively surfaces high‑importance units for minority groups.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “group‑aware importance analysis”; Section 2 Equation (1) and the reduction step define the max‑normalization.
- **Break condition:** If calibration data lacks representation of a sensitive group, the derived gradients become noisy, causing the max‑reducer to default to majority‑group signals and failing to protect minority‑group performance.

### Mechanism 2: Budgeted Mixed‑Precision Allocation
- **Claim:** Converts the continuous importance map into a discrete, budget‑constrained bit‑width assignment that protects high‑importance units.
- **Mechanism:** Importance values across all scopes are concatenated into a vector v. For a predefined bit palette (e.g., {2,4,8}) and target proportions π, empirical quantiles of v define thresholds t_k. The tiering map ϕ assigns lower bits to low‑importance scopes and higher bits to high‑importance ones, ensuring the total bit budget matches ∑_k π_k · (size of scopes at tier k).
- **Core assumption:** The ranking of importance scores correlates with sensitivity to quantization error, and a deterministic quantile‑based split provides a stable allocation under a fixed budget.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “budgeted mixed‑precision allocation”; Section 2 describes the quantile‑based tiering and budget constraint.
- **Break condition:** If the importance distribution is highly skewed or the budget is too stringent, the quantile thresholds may force high bits on too many scopes or insufficient bits for critical units, degrading worst‑group accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Bit‑Aware Quantization (BAQ)
- **Claim:** Jointly fine‑tunes weights and per‑scope bit‑widths (as continuous proxies) under bitrate and fairness regularization, refining the initial static allocation.
- **Mechanism:** Each scope has a learnable logit b_logit_S. A continuous bit proxy b_cont_S = tanh(|b_logit_S|) · (b_max‑b_min) + b_min is computed and rounded (b_S = round(b_cont_S)) for forward quantization (STE for gradients). Total loss = L_task + λ_fair·L_fair (max_g L_g ‑ min_g L_g) + λ_baq,b·L_baq,b (L2 on logits). Gradients update both weights and logits, dynamically adjusting bits.
- **Core assumption:** Treating discrete bits as differentiable proxies allows gradient‑based optimization to surpass static assignment, and the fairness penalty effectively minimizes group loss disparity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “learnable Bit‑Aware Quantization (BAQ)”; Section 2 Equations (2)‑(5) formalize the joint training.
- **Break condition:** If λ_baq,b is too low, bits may exceed budget; if λ_fair is too high, accuracy may drop without real fairness gains; unstable learning rates for b_logit_S cause oscillation/collapse.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Calibration Module -> Reducer & Allocation Engine -> BAQ Optimizer -> Discretization & Export

### Critical Path
1. Calibration (1‑2 batches) → compute group‑restricted losses and Taylor‑based importance scores.
2. Static allocation (optional) → apply budgeted mix‑precision using quantile thresholds to produce an initial bit assignment.
3. Initialize BAQ logits → from static pattern or default high‑precision.
4. Joint QAT with BAQ loss → train for N epochs with task loss + fairness penalty + bitrate L2 regularizer.
5. Discretize bits → round continuous bit proxies to integers.
6. Evaluate final model.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Budget stringency vs fairness:* Tighter budgets force more aggressive compression on low‑importance units; if too strict, even critical units may be under‑bits, harming worst‑group accuracy.
- *Static allocation vs BAQ:* Static mixed‑precision is simpler and stable but suboptimal; BAQ adapts bits during training but introduces hyperparameter sensitivity (λ_baq,b, λ_fair, learning rate for logits).
- *Granularity:* Per‑channel granularity provides finer control than per‑tensor but increases the number of independent bit logits to optimize.

### Failure Signatures
- **Uniform 4‑bit collapse:** Extreme drop in AvgAcc and WorstAcc (e.g., TinyViT on Fitzpatrick17k to ~3%), indicating insufficient bit budget without group‑guided allocation.
- **BAQ instability:** High variance across seeds in accuracy/fairness metrics during λ_baq,b or learning‑rate sweeps, indicating chaotic bit‑proxy optimization.
- **Fairness‑accuracy degradation:** EOdd increases at high λ_fair despite reduced loss gap, suggesting the fairness penalty harms classifier performance without real equality gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does FairQuant generalize to other medical imaging modalities (e.g., MRI, CT) and sensitive attributes (e.g., age, race)?
**Basis in paper:** The authors state the evaluation is limited to two dermatology datasets and two sensitive attributes (skin type and sex), with potentially noisy group labels.
**Why unresolved:** It is unknown whether the observed improvements hold for modalities or attributes with different distributions and label quality.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically evaluate FairQuant on additional medical imaging datasets covering diverse modalities and sensitive attributes, measuring accuracy and fairness under identical bit budgets.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How does the choice of fairness metric (e.g., demographic parity, equal accuracy) affect FairQuant's bit allocation and accuracy‑fairness trade‑off?
**Basis in paper:** The fairness term focuses on equalized opportunity/odds; the authors note that other definitions can change the preferred operating point.
**Why unresolved:** The method has not been tested with alternative fairness losses; its sensitivity to the fairness metric choice remains unclear.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replace L_fair with other fairness definitions, retrain under the same protocol, and compare the resulting bit distributions and Pareto frontiers across models and datasets.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Would a denser hyperparameter search for the fairness regularization weight λ_fair uncover a stronger accuracy‑fairness trade‑off?
**Basis in paper:** The authors identify as a limitation that they only performed a coarse sweep over λ_fair and suggest a denser sweep may reveal a better operating point.
**Why unresolved:** The coarse sweep likely missed optimal λ_fair values, particularly for certain model‑dataset combinations.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct an exhaustive grid or Bayesian optimization over λ_fair (and possibly λ_baq,b), reconstruct the full Pareto surface, and verify whether any configuration strictly dominates the reported results.

## Limitations
- Precise formulation of group importance normalization (max‑over‑groups) and handling of edge cases (e.g., zero‑importance groups).
- Calibration batch construction: whether batches are group‑balanced or use natural distribution, affecting gradient stability.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity of BAQ bit‑proxy optimization (learning rates, λ_baq,b, λ_fair), risk of bit‑collapse or fairness‑accuracy trade‑off deterioration.
