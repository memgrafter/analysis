# Cost-Sensitive Neighborhood Aggregation for Heterophilous Graphs: When Does Per-Edge Routing Help?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24291
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24291
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** Per-edge routing via CSNA helps node classification on adversarial-heterophily graphs but harms performance on informative-heterophily graphs, with cost-function AUC separating these regimes.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Cost-Sensitive Neighborhood Aggregation (CSNA), a GNN layer that routes messages per edge through separate concordant and discordant channels using a learned cost function. Under a contextual stochastic block model, CSNA preserves class-discriminative signals over mean aggregation when routing weights satisfy w+/w- > q/p. Empirically, CSNA competes with state-of-the-art on adversarial-heterophily datasets (Texas, Wisconsin, Cornell, Actor) but underperforms on informative-heterophily ones (Chameleon, Squirrel). The cost function’s AUC in separating edge types correlates with these outcomes, suggesting per-edge routing is beneficial only when cross-class edges are harmful and exhibit mixed utility.

## Method Summary
CSNA replaces standard mean aggregation in GNNs with a cost-sensitive routing mechanism. Given node features, CSNA first projects them into a latent space and computes pairwise distances. These distances are transformed via a learned cost function into soft routing weights that assign each edge to either a concordant channel (edges likely connecting same-class nodes) or a discordant channel (edges likely connecting different-class nodes). Each channel then applies independent linear transformations before messages are aggregated. This design allows separate processing of edges with presumed different utilities, with routing weights normalized per source node for stability.

## Key Results
- CSNA achieves competitive performance with SOTA on adversarial-heterophily datasets (Texas, Wisconsin, Cornell, Actor) but underperforms on informative-heterophily datasets (Chameleon, Squirrel).
- The learned cost function’s AUC in separating edge types is high on adversarial datasets (0.48–0.83) and low on informative ones (0.55–0.57), indicating that per-edge routing benefits scenarios where cross-class edges are detrimental.
- Theoretical analysis under a contextual SBM shows CSNA preserves discriminative signals over mean aggregation when w+/w- > q/p.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CSNA’s per-edge routing prevents the mixing of class signals that occurs in mean aggregation by separating edges into channels based on their presumed utility. The learned cost function adapts to graph structure, assigning higher weights to likely intra-class edges. The condition w+/w- > q/p ensures the concordant channel’s signal dominates, preserving discriminative information. This mechanism excels when cross-class edges are noisy or harmful (adversarial heterophily) but may discard useful information when cross-class edges carry consistent signals (informative heterophily).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Node Features -> Projection -> Pairwise Distance -> Routing Weights (via cost function) -> Concordant Channel (transformation) and Discordant Channel (transformation) -> Aggregation -> Output

Critical path: Projection and distance computation lead to routing weight assignment, followed by independent channel transformations and final aggregation.

Design tradeoffs:
- Per-edge routing increases computational complexity versus mean aggregation.
- Additional parameters (projection, cost function, dual transformations) increase model capacity and risk of overfitting.
- Channel separation may lose information if edge types are not well distinguished by the cost function.

Failure signatures:
- Cost function fails to separate edge types (weights near uniform/random), leading to performance at or below mean aggregation.
- On informative heterophily, the discordant channel discards useful cross-class signals, causing underperformance.
- High variance across random seeds due to sensitivity of learned cost function.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes a contextual stochastic block model, which may not reflect real heterophilous graphs.
- Evaluation on only six datasets with small-N classification into adversarial/informative regimes limits generalizability.
- No statistical significance testing (multiple runs, p-values) and overlapping AUC ranges weaken claims.

## Confidence
- Theoretical signal preservation condition (w+/w- > q/p): High
- Competitive performance on adversarial-heterophily: Medium
- Underperformance on informative-heterophily: Medium
- Cost-function AUC discriminates regimes: Medium
- Overall conclusion (routing helps only when cross-class edges are harmful): Medium
