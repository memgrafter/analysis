# TiCo: Time-Controllable Training for Spoken Dialogue Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22267
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22267
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TiCo reduces mean absolute error (MAE) to 4.54 seconds and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) to 14.9% on duration control, substantially improving over backbone (13.01s / 42.3%) and cascaded GPT (10.41s / 29.0%), while maintaining response quality.

## Executive Summary
TiCo is a two-stage post-training method for spoken dialogue models (SDMs) to follow explicit duration instructions (e.g., "respond in 15 seconds"). It introduces Spoken Time Markers (STMs) into the model's intermediate text representation to teach time estimation, and then uses reinforcement learning with STM-based rewards to control output duration. On the new TiCo-Bench benchmark, TiCo achieves significant improvements in duration accuracy (MAE 4.54s, MAPE 14.9%) over baselines, with comparable response quality, and generalizes to longer durations and text queries.

## Method Summary
TiCo post-trains the Thinker module of Qwen2.5-Omni-7B in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT): For each speech query, the backbone generates a response; Whisper ASR provides word-level timestamps. The model's intermediate text output is aligned with these timestamps to insert STMs (e.g., `<10.6s>`) after punctuation, creating a supervised dataset of time-annotated plans. The Thinker is fine-tuned with LoRA to predict STMs interleaved with text. Stage 2 (RLVR): Using GRPO, the model generates multiple candidate intermediate sequences (with STMs). The primary reward is a Gaussian centered on the target duration, applied to the final STM. Auxiliary rewards enforce monotonicity, presence, and prevent repetition. A CHORD regularizer mixes off-policy SFT data to maintain general conversational quality. STMs are stripped before speech generation by the fixed Talker.

## Key Results
- Duration accuracy: TiCo achieves MAE of 4.54 seconds and MAPE of 14.9% on TiCo-Bench, far surpassing backbone Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (13.01s / 42.3%) and cascaded GPT (10.41s / 29.0%).
- Quality preservation: Response quality (GPT-score) remains comparable to the backbone, indicating no significant degradation in helpfulness or coherence.
- Generalization: TiCo generalizes to longer durations (up to 60s) and transfers to text-query inputs, matching or exceeding its performance on speech queries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** TiCo enables the model to develop an internal sense of elapsed speaking time during response generation.
- **Mechanism:** During Stage 1 (SFT), the model is trained to predict **Spoken Time Markers (STMs)** (e.g., `<10.6 seconds>`) inserted into its intermediate text representation. These STMs are derived from real speech durations via ASR timestamps aligned to the model's self-generated response. The model learns to associate positions in its token stream with cumulative time estimates.
- **Core assumption:** The intermediate text representation (the "Thinker" output) can be predictively aligned with final acoustic duration. The STM serves as a supervisory signal forcing the model to estimate time at semantic chunk boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "enabling models to estimate elapsed speaking time during generation through Spoken Time Markers (STM)"
  - [section 3.1]: Describes the self-generation and ASR-based alignment process to construct `DSFT` with interleaved STMs.
  - [corpus]: **Evidence absent.** Neighboring papers focus on other SDM benchmarks (turn-taking, emotional intelligence) and do not provide evidence for STM-based time estimation mechanisms.
- **Break condition:** If the correlation between predicted STM values and actual ASR-derived durations degrades significantly, particularly on longer or out-of-distribution responses, the internal clock mechanism fails. This would manifest as non-monotonic or wildly inaccurate STM sequences during inference.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reinforcement Learning with STM-based rewards shapes the generation policy to satisfy explicit duration constraints.
- **Mechanism:** In Stage 2 (RLVR), the model generates multiple candidate intermediate sequences (`˜z`). A primary reward is a Gaussian function centered on the target duration, applied to the **final STM** (`t_last`). This directly incentivizes the model to adjust its content plan (the sequence of STMs and text) so the final estimated time matches the instruction. Auxiliary rewards (monotonicity, presence, penalties) enforce valid STM patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Optimizing the final STM's alignment to the target duration via RL will causally improve the actual speech duration, as the final STM is a reliable predictor of total speech length (as shown in Figure 5).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "reinforcement learning with STM-based rewards to control output duration."
  - [section 3.2]: Details the GRPO objective with `R_main` based on `t_last` and auxiliary rewards.
  - [corpus]: **Evidence absent.** Neighboring papers do not investigate RL with time-marker rewards for SDMs.
- **Break condition:** Reward hacking where the model learns to output the target duration in the final STM without adjusting the actual semantic content or speech rate (e.g., by copying the instruction's duration verbatim in the final marker but generating unrelated or Fixed-content speech). The auxiliary rewards are designed to mitigate this.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Time-controllability learned from speech queries transfers to text queries.
- **Mechanism:** The time-awareness and control are learned at the **intermediate representation (IR)** level (the "Thinker" text plan). Since this IR is modality-agnostic (textual), the capability generalizes to inputs where the user query is text, not speech. The model applies the same STM-based planning process regardless of input modality.
- **Core assumption:** Duration control is a property of the semantic planning process, not tied to the acoustic features of the input speech. The model's internal "clock" operates on its own output plan.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2]: "TiCo is post-trained only with speech queries, yet it transfers well to text-query inputs... TiCo achieves the best overall performance... substantially outperforming its backbone."
  - [Table 2]: Shows TiCo's MAE/MAPE on text queries (LIFEBench, UROBench-text) is comparable to or better than its performance on speech queries in some settings.
  - [corpus]: **Evidence absent.** No neighboring paper demonstrates this specific cross-modality generalization for time control.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades sharply on text queries compared to speech queries, indicating that the learned time-control policy is inadvertently conditioned on speech-specific input features from the backbone model (e.g., prosodic cues from the query) rather than being truly modality-agnostic at the IR level.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Speech Tokenization & Acoustic Duration Variability**
  - **Why needed here:** The fundamental challenge TiCo addresses is that text length (word count) is a poor proxy for speech duration due to varying syllable counts, speaking rates, and phonetic context. The system must reason in a token space that ultimately maps to variable acoustic time. Understanding that speech tokens (phonetic or acoustic) represent variable-duration units is prerequisite.
  - **Quick check question:** Why would constraining the number of generated text words fail to achieve a precise 15-second speech target?

- **Concept: Intermediate Representations (IR) in Spoken Dialogue Models**
  - **Why needed here:** TiCo operates by inserting STMs into the IR (the "Thinker" output) before speech generation. The entire mechanism assumes an architecture where semantic planning (textual) precedes acoustic realization. The engineer must understand that control is exerted at the IR level, not the final waveform.
  - **Quick check question:** In the Qwen2.5-Omni "Thinker-Talker" architecture, what is the role of the Thinker, and where are STMs inserted?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) & KL Regularization**
  - **Why needed here:** Stage 2 uses GRPO, an on-policy RL algorithm, with rewards computed automatically from STMs. The CHORD regularizer mixes in off-policy SFT data to prevent catastrophic forgetting of general conversational quality. An engineer needs to grasp this trade-off between optimizing a narrow objective (duration) and maintaining broad capability.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the CHORD regularizer (`L_SFT` term) in the final loss `L^{(s)}`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Input Encoder:** Processes user query (speech waveform or text) into a contextual representation.
  2.  **Thinker (Intermediate Sequence Generator `p_θ`):** An LLM backbone (e.g., Qwen2.5-Omni's LLM). It generates the IR sequence `z`, which now includes interleaved text and STM tokens. This is the only component trained in TiCo.
  3.  **Speech Generator (`q_ϕ` - "Talker"):** A fixed module (e.g., acoustic token decoder) that consumes the cleaned IR `z` (with STMs regex-removed) and produces the final speech token sequence or waveform.
  4.  **ASR Module (Offline, for data construction):** Used only during Stage 1 training data creation to provide word-level timestamps for STM insertion.
  5.  **Reward Calculator (Stage 2):** Computes `R_main` from the final STM and auxiliary rewards from the entire STM sequence.

- **Critical path:**
  `User Query (speech/text)` → `Input Encoder` → `Thinker (generates IR with STMs)` → `[STM Removal]` → `Talker` → `Speech Output`.
  The **critical training path** for TiCo is: `(Query, Instruction)` → `Self-generated Response` → `ASR Timestamps` → `STM-annotated IR` → **Stage 1 SFT** → `RL Policy (Thinker)` → **Stage 2 GRPO** with `STM-based Reward`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Scope of Training:** Training only the Thinker (as done) vs. fine-tuning the entire SDM. The former is efficient and stable but limits direct control over the acoustic realization; the latter might offer finer-grained control but risks destabilizing speech quality.
  - **STM Granularity:** Inserting STMs after punctuation (as done) vs. every N tokens or every word. Coarser granularity reduces sequence length overhead but provides less frequent temporal feedback during generation.
  - **RL Reward Design:** Relying solely on final STM accuracy (`R_main`) vs. incorporating more granular rewards (e.g., intermediate STM deviations). The former is simple but may lead to last-minute adjustments; the latter could provide denser signals but complicate credit assignment.
  - **Token Budget:** The max generation length (2048 for training, 4096 for eval). A longer budget allows longer responses but increases computational cost and the challenge of accurate long-horizon time prediction.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **STM Pattern Violations:** Non-monotonic STM values, repeated STM values, or absence of STMs entirely (detected via auxiliary reward components).
  - **Duration Error:** High MAE/MAPE on TiCo-Bench, especially in the "short" (10-30s) bins where margin for error is small.
  - **Quality Degradation:** Significant drop in GPT-score (e.g., >0.5 points) compared to backbone, indicating the model is sacrificing coherence/helpfulness to meet duration.
  - **Modality Collapse:** Performance on text queries degrades to backbone levels, indicating the time-control capability is tied to speech-input processing.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline Duration Capability Test:** Run the backbone model (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) on a small sample (e.g., 50) from TiCo-Bench (both speech and text queries) with duration instructions. Confirm high MAE/MAPE (expect ~13s/42%) and note STM generation behavior (likely none or random).
  2.  **Stage 1 Effect Isolation:** Evaluate the model after only Stage 1 SFT. Measure: (a) MAE/MAPE on TiCo-Bench, (b) correlation between final STM value and actual duration, (c) GPT-score. Expect modest improvement in STM prediction but poor duration control (model can estimate time but not regulate it).
  3.  **Ablation of RL Auxiliary Rewards:** Train TiCo without one auxiliary reward (e.g., monotonicity) and compare MAE/MAPE and STM sequence sanity. Expect higher errors and more invalid STM patterns (e.g., decreasing timestamps), demonstrating the auxiliary rewards' stabilizing role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the precision of Spoken Time Marker (STM) prediction be improved to reduce the residual error between the final STM and actual response duration?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "we aim to further improve the precision of time-marker prediction," and Figure 5 shows that the final STM's duration error aligns closely with but does not exactly match the actual duration error, indicating room for improvement.
- **Why unresolved:** Current STM training uses punctuation-based insertion and ASR-based alignment, which may not capture fine-grained temporal dynamics; errors in STM prediction directly limit duration control accuracy.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate alternative STM insertion strategies (e.g., word-level, prosodic boundaries) and training objectives (e.g., direct duration regression) on TiCo-Bench, measuring reduction in MAE between the last STM's predicted time and the actual speech duration.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does TiCo maintain time controllability in multi-turn spoken dialogues with overlapping or interleaved speech, as encountered in full-duplex conversations?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work mentions extending to "longer and more diverse conversational scenarios," implying multi-turn settings are unaddressed; current TiCo-Bench and experiments are single-turn.
- **Why unresolved:** Multi-turn dialogue introduces complexities like turn-taking, cumulative duration tracking across turns, and potential latency constraints, which are not modeled in the current single-turn framework.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt TiCo-Bench or use existing multi-turn benchmarks (e.g., Full-Duplex-Bench) with per-turn duration instructions, measure MAE/MAPE per turn and overall, and analyze degradation or adaptation needs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can TiCo’s time-control capability be effectively combined with other controllable attributes (e.g., speaking style, emotion, or role) without compromising duration accuracy or response quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion explicitly aims to "explore how time control can be integrated with other controllable dialogue behaviors in spoken language systems."
- **Why unresolved:** TiCo focuses solely on duration; simultaneous control of multiple attributes may introduce conflicting optimization signals during RL training or trade-offs in response quality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct multi-attribute control experiments by augmenting instructions with style/emotion tags, evaluate both duration error (MAE/MAPE) and attribute accuracy (via human or automatic style classifiers), and compare performance against single-attribute TiCo.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How robust is TiCo to ASR timestamp errors or natural variations in speaking rate and accent, which could distort Spoken Time Marker supervision during training?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method relies on ASR timestamps for STM generation in Stage 1, but no error analysis or robustness tests are reported; the introduction notes speaking rate variability as a challenge for duration control.
- **Why unresolved:** ASR inaccuracies or speech rate variations might misalign STMs with actual speech, harming time-awareness training; this assumption of perfect alignment is unstated and could limit real-world applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce controlled perturbations in ASR timestamps (e.g., random shifts of ±0.5s) or use datasets with diverse accents/speakers, then measure changes in duration control performance (MAE/MAPE) on TiCo-Bench.

## Limitations
- STM insertion algorithm: Exact method for aligning Whisper word timestamps to the Thinker's token stream and aggregating cumulative times is unspecified.
- CHORD regularization: Precise schedule for mixing coefficient decay and expert trajectory handling are not detailed.
- Duration sampling: Strategy for target durations in RL (e.g., uniform vs. curriculum within 10–60s) is undescribed.
- Evaluation prompt: Template for GPT-5-mini scoring is absent, hindering reproducible quality assessment.

## Confidence
- Internal time estimation via STMs: Medium
- RL reward shaping for duration control: High
- Cross-modality transfer (speech→text): Medium
