# NeuraLSP: An Efficient and Rigorous Neural Left Singular Subspace Preconditioner for Conjugate Gradient Methods

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.20174
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20174
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** NeuraLSP reduces total solve time by up to 53% compared to GNN, SA-AMG, and SVD baselines while retaining significantly more spectral energy under rank truncation.

## Executive Summary
Solving large sparse linear systems from PDEs requires effective preconditioning. Existing neural preconditioners, such as those using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), suffer from rank inflation and suboptimal convergence. NeuraLSP introduces a neural preconditioner that learns the left singular subspace of near-nullspace vectors via a novel NLSS loss. This loss is rank-agnostic and provably recovers the top-k singular vectors. The neural output is orthonormalized (QR) and used as a low-rank prolongation operator in a two-grid Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) solver. Experiments on diffusion, anisotropic, and screened Poisson equations demonstrate that NeuraLSP achieves faster total solve times and greater robustness to rank truncation compared to SVD-based and classical AMG approaches.

## Method Summary
NeuraLSP replaces the heuristic construction of the multigrid prolongation operator with a learned one. For a given system matrix *A*, it first applies a few Jacobi smoothing sweeps to *K* random vectors to generate a smoothed error matrix *S*. A neural network (*f*<sub>θ</sub>) maps *S* to an initial matrix **\hat{P}**. A QR decomposition enforces orthonormal columns, yielding **\tilde{P}**. This **\tilde{P}** serves as the prolongation operator in a two-grid PCG cycle (with restriction *R* = **\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>). The network is trained end-to-end using the NLSS loss, which maximizes the captured Frobenius energy ‖**\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup> across progressive ranks. This objective provably aligns **\tilde{P}**'s column span with the top-k left singular subspace of *S*.

## Key Results
- **Speed:** Reduces median total solve time (inference + PCG) by up to 53% compared to GNN with subspace loss, SA-AMG, and exact SVD baselines on test PDE families.
- **Robustness to Rank Truncation:** Retains significantly more spectral energy (‖**\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>/‖*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>) when the coarse rank *k* is reduced, demonstrating resistance to rank inflation.
- **Generalization:** Shows strong performance across diffusion, anisotropic, and screened Poisson equations with varying heterogeneity, using a fixed *k* during training and truncation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The NLSS loss function provably recovers the top-k left singular vectors of the smoothed near-nullspace matrix *S* up to column-wise signs when singular values are distinct, and recovers the subspace otherwise.
- **Mechanism:** The loss *L*<sup>(k)</sup>(*S*, **\tilde{P}**) = (1/k) Σ<sub>ℓ=1</sub><sup>k</sup> (1 − ‖**\tilde{P}**<sub>ℓ</sub><sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>/‖*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>) is minimized when the orthonormal columns of **\tilde{P**} align with the leading eigenvectors of *SS*<sup>⊤</sup>. The proof rewrites an equivalent maximization problem and applies the von Neumann trace inequality and Ky Fan's principle to show the optimum is achieved when **\tilde{P**}'s columns span the top-*k* eigenspace of *SS*<sup>⊤</sup>.
- **Core assumption:** The smoothed error matrix *S* obtained via a few smoothing sweeps adequately samples the near-nullspace vectors of *A*.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "NLSS loss ... is rank-agnostic and provably recovers the top-k singular vectors."
  - [section 4.1]: Theorem 4.1 and its proof.
- **Break condition:** If the top *k* singular values of *S* are not distinct (σ<sub>*k*</sub> = σ<sub>*k*+1</sub>), the minimizer is only guaranteed to lie in the invariant subspace, not necessarily equal to *U*<sub>*k*</sub> up to signs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Orthonormalization via QR decomposition of the neural network's raw output is necessary to enforce the Stiefel manifold constraint (**\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>**\tilde{P}** = *I*<sub>*k*</sub>) required by the loss's theoretical guarantee and to produce a valid prolongation operator.
- **Mechanism:** The neural model *f*<sub>θ</sub> outputs **\hat{P}** ∈ ℝ<sup>*n*×*r*</sup>. A QR decomposition **\hat{P}** = **\tilde{P}** *R* is performed, and **\tilde{P}** (with orthonormal columns) is used as the final prolongation matrix. This projection ensures the solution lies on the Stiefel manifold, the domain of the NLSS optimization problem.
- **Core assumption:** The neural network's unconstrained output will not naturally satisfy orthonormality; post-processing is required to align with the theoretical setup.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: "To ensure convergence, we perform a QR-decomposition on the learned matrix **\hat{P}** before outputting it from the neural network."
  - [section 4.2]: Algorithm 1 step "Obtain orthonormal **\tilde{U}**<sub>*r*</sub> via a QR-decomposition..."
- **Break condition:** If orthonormalization is omitted, **\hat{P}** may not minimize the NLSS loss as proven, leading to a non-optimal coarse space and degraded convergence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using the learned left singular subspace as the range of the prolongation operator *P* in a two-grid Galerkin scheme guarantees maximum possible reduction (over all rank-*k* projectors) of the Frobenius norm energy of smooth error vectors sampled in *S*.
- **Mechanism:** For error *e* lying in `range(P)`, the coarse-grid correction exactly eliminates that component. The effectiveness depends on how much of the total smooth error energy (captured in *S*) is contained in `range(P)`. Theorem 4.1's guarantee that `span(P*) = span(U_k)` means *P* maximizes ‖*P*<sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup> (captured energy) over all orthonormal *P*, which by Theorem A.1 minimizes the projection residual ‖*S* − *PP*<sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub>. This translates to maximal elimination of smooth error modes in the two-grid cycle.
- **Core assumption:** The smoothing procedure (few Jacobi sweeps) generates *S* that accurately spans the true near-nullspace vectors that impede Krylov convergence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1]: "The result of Theorem 4.1 enables us to construct an effective two-grid preconditioning scheme... by minimizing total energy."
  - [appendix A.5]: Theorem A.1 formally links maximizing `Tr(P^T SS^T P)` to minimizing projection error.
- **Break condition:** If the smoothing fails to generate *S* that spans the actual near-nullspace, even the optimal *P* from NLSS may not target the true slow-converging modes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and left singular subspace.
  - **Why needed here:** The core objective is to learn the matrix *U*<sub>*k*</sub> of the top-*k* left singular vectors of *S* = *UΣV*<sup>⊤</sup>. Understanding SVD is essential to grasp what the NLSS loss targets and why maximizing ‖*P*<sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup> relates to capturing dominant singular vectors.
  - **Quick check:** "In the SVD of the smoothed error matrix *S*, what does the left singular subspace represent, and why is it the correct space for constructing a prolongation operator?"

- **Concept:** The Stiefel manifold and orthonormal constraints.
  - **Why needed here:** The NLSS loss is defined over matrices **\tilde{P}** with orthonormal columns (**\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>**\tilde{P}** = *I*<sub>*k*</sub>), which form the Stiefel manifold `St(n,k)`. The theoretical guarantees (Theorem 4.1) hold on this manifold. Violating this constraint invalidates the proof.
  - **Quick check:** "Why can't we simply use an unconstrained neural network output as the prolongation matrix, and what does the QR step accomplish mathematically?"

- **Concept:** Algebraic multigrid (AMG) and smoothed aggregation (SA-AMG).
  - **Why needed here:** NeuraLSP is a neural alternative to classical AMG's heuristic construction of the prolongation operator *P*. It specifically targets the 'smooth error' issue where standard AMG struggles because smooth errors are near-nullspace vectors. The method uses a two-grid PCG framework, a core component of multigrid.
  - **Quick check:** "What are 'near-nullspace vectors' in the context of AMG, and why are they critical for designing an effective coarse grid?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input matrix *A* → Generate smoothed matrix *S* (Jacobi on random vectors) → Neural network *f*<sub>θ</sub> outputs **\hat{P}** → QR decomposition yields orthonormal **\tilde{P}** → Two-grid PCG cycle uses **\tilde{P}** as prolongation (*P*) and **\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup> as restriction (*R*).
- **Critical path:** `Generate S (smoothing) -> Neural inference (f_θ) -> QR -> Two-grid PCG loop (until convergence)`. Smoothing must precede neural inference; QR must succeed neural inference; the two-grid cycle depends on **\tilde{P}**. Training is separate: backpropagate NLSS loss on (**\tilde{P}**, *S*) pairs from training PDE instances.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **MLP vs. GNN:** Paper uses a global MLP for minimal inference cost and simplicity, but notes GNNs may generalize better to unseen mesh sizes. MLP requires retraining for new problem size *n*.
  - **Rank *r*:** Fixed low-rank *r* (user-chosen) vs. SA-AMG's heuristic coarse space size. Smaller *r* reduces coarse-grid costs but risks missing smooth error components; NLSS's rank-agnostic loss allows training once and truncating.
  - **Smoothing sweeps *s*₁:** More sweeps may better capture the near-nullspace but increase preprocessing cost. Paper uses 1 sweep for efficiency.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Captured energy gap:** For a fixed rank *r* < *K*, NeuraLSP's ‖**\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>/‖*S*‖<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup> is significantly lower than that of SVD truncation. This indicates the learned subspace is suboptimal.
  - **Solve time not improving:** Total solve time (inference + PCG) higher than baselines despite lower-rank *r*. Could indicate poor generalization of *f*<sub>θ</sub> to test *A* or insufficient *S* quality.
  - **Orthonormality violation:** If QR step is skipped or unstable, columns of **\tilde{P}** are not orthonormal, violating Theorem 4.1's conditions; convergence may degrade.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Subspace recovery ablation:** Train NLSS and a GNN baseline with subspace loss to full rank *K*, then truncate to *r* < *K* and compare captured energy against SVD truncation. Expect NLSS to retain much more energy as *r* decreases.
  2. **End-to-end solve time:** For specific PDE families, compare total median solve time of NeuraLSP against: (a) GNN baseline with subspace loss, (b) SA-AMG from PyAMG, (c) SVD-based two-grid (using actual SVD of *S*). Vary coarse-space size *n*<sub>c</sub> (rank *r*) and report improvements.
  3. **Scalability ablation:** For diffusion equation, vary grid size *N* (hence *n* ≈ *N*<sup>2</sup>). For each *N*, set *K*=*N*, *r*=*K*/2. Compare end-to-end time of NLSS (trained for each *N*) vs. PyAMG and exact SVD.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Can the NeuraLSP framework be effectively integrated as a preconditioning module within full multigrid cycles (beyond two-level) to further reduce the number of PCG iterations?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Conclusions state: "In future work, we aim to assess whether this framework can be used as a module within a larger-scale architecture..."
- **Why unresolved:** Current implementation uses only a two-level correction. Unknown if the learned left singular subspace can be recursively applied in a full hierarchy.
- **Evidence needed:** Implement NeuraLSP within a full multigrid V-cycle or W-cycle, comparing iteration counts and total solve time against the two-level version.

- **Question:** Does the NLSS loss guarantee recovery of the true top-k left singular vectors when the singular values of *S* are not distinct (i.e., when there are eigenvalue multiplicities)?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 assume σ₁ > σ₂ > · · · > σ<sub>*k*</sub> > σ<sub>*k*+1</sub>. The paper explicitly states this condition.
- **Why unresolved:** In practice, *S* may have repeated singular values due to symmetries. The recovery guarantee up to signs may break down.
- **Evidence needed:** Theoretical analysis for repeated singular values (e.g., guarantee spanning the eigenspace). Empirically test on PDEs with symmetric meshes and measure subspace alignment vs. SVD.

- **Question:** How well does the trained NeuraLSP model generalize to three-dimensional PDE discretizations and unstructured meshes of different element types (e.g., tetrahedral, hexahedral) without retraining?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Experiments exclusively on 2D triangular meshes. Authors mention potential for 3D and other PDEs in limitations but do not test.
- **Why unresolved:** Smooth error vectors and their subspaces may have different structures in 3D or on other meshes. The global MLP might not capture necessary geometric variations.
- **Evidence needed:** Train on 2D triangular meshes and test zero-shot on 3D tetrahedral meshes for the same PDE family, and vice versa. Also train on mixed mesh types and evaluate transfer.

- **Question:** Can the NeuraLSP approach be extended to non-symmetric or indefinite linear systems, such as those arising from convection-diffusion or incompressible Navier-Stokes equations?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Paper focuses on symmetric positive definite (SPD) systems, as required by PCG and the theoretical analysis. No discussion for non-SPD cases.
- **Why unresolved:** Many important PDEs lead to non-symmetric or indefinite matrices. The theoretical foundation for using left singular subspaces in such contexts is lacking, and PCG is not applicable.
- **Evidence needed:** Adapt NLSS loss and two-level scheme to non-symmetric systems (e.g., using GMRES) and test on convection-diffusion and Navier-Stokes. Theoretical analysis of convergence for indefinite problems.

- **Question:** What is the minimal rank *k* needed for the learned prolongation operator to achieve near-optimal reduction of smooth error for a given PDE family, and how does this relate to the spectral properties of the system matrix?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Paper shows NLSS retains more spectral energy than subspace loss when truncating but does not investigate absolute minimal *k* for a given tolerance. Choice of *k* is tunable but its relation to PDE properties is unstudied.
- **Why unresolved:** To optimize performance, the smallest possible coarse space is desirable. Understanding the trade-off between *k* and convergence rate could enable adaptive rank selection.
- **Evidence needed:** Systematically vary *k* and measure preconditioned system condition number and PCG iterations across PDEs with varying parameters. Correlate optimal *k* with metrics like spectral decay of smoothed vectors or problem heterogeneity.

## Limitations
- **Mesh jitter parameters unspecified:** The exact distribution (e.g., uniform, Gaussian) and maximum displacement for node jitter are not detailed, affecting reproducibility and problem difficulty.
- **GNN baseline details lacking:** Paper notes parameter count matching but does not specify GNN architecture (layer types, aggregation) or training hyperparameters, preventing fair comparison.
- **Smoothing assumption may be brittle:** The method relies on one Jacobi sweep to generate *S* that spans the near-nullspace. This may not hold for all problems; more sweeps or different smoothers could perform better but are unexplored.

## Confidence
- **NLSS recovery theorem under its assumptions (distinct singular values, accurate *S* sampling):** High
- **Necessity of QR orthonormalization for theoretical guarantee:** Medium (proof requires it, but impact of omission is only indirectly evidenced)
- **Robustness to mesh variations and generalization to unseen sizes:** Medium-Low (experiments use a specific jitter regime and fixed training size *N*=64)

## Next Checks
1. **Smoothing depth sensitivity:** For a subset of test matrices, compute *S* using 1, 5, and 10 Jacobi sweeps, then run NeuraLSP and measure subspace captured energy and solve time. A significant change would indicate dependence on smoothing parameter.
2. **Mesh jitter impact:** Re-run end-to-end experiments with controlled jitter variations (e.g., maximum displacement 0.0, 0.01, 0.05 of domain size) and compare solve times and energy capture to quantify robustness.
3. **Orthonormality enforcement:** After QR, compute orthonormality error ‖**\tilde{P}**<sup>⊤</sup>**\tilde{P}** − *I*‖<sub>F</sub> for all test matrices. If errors exceed 1e-6, consider alternative orthonormalization (e.g., SVD-based) or retraining with Stiefel manifold constraints.
