# Dynamic Regret via Discounted-to-Dynamic Reduction with Applications to Curved Losses and Adam Optimizer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08372
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08372
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A modular reduction transforms discounted regret bounds into dynamic regret bounds for FTRL methods, yielding optimal rates for online linear/logistic regression and Adam optimizers without exponential dependence on problem parameters.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a modular reduction that converts any guarantee on discounted regret into a dynamic regret guarantee for Follow-The-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) algorithms. The reduction decouples the choice of discount factor from the underlying online learning method, enabling simpler analyses and stronger results. Applied to online linear regression, it recovers the optimal dynamic regret with a streamlined proof. For online logistic regression—a curved loss—the authors design Discounted AIOLI and a two-layer ensemble over discount factors to eliminate an exponential dependence on the loss bound $B$. Finally, the framework is used to analyze Adam variants (clipped and clip-free) in stochastic non-convex non-smooth optimization, establishing optimal convergence rates to $(c,\varepsilon)$-stationary points under relaxed parameter coupling conditions.

## Method Summary
The core is a reduction that wraps any *discounted* online learner (which observes losses $f_t$ and outputs predictions $x_t$ while maintaining a running discounted sum of gradients) into an algorithm for the *dynamic* regret setting. The wrapper maintains a set of experts, each running the discounted learner with a different fixed discount factor $\beta$, and combines their predictions via a multiplicative-weights-like scheme. The dynamic regret $\sum_t (f_t(x_t)-f_t(u_t))$ is bounded by a weighted sum of the experts’ discounted regrets plus an $O(\log N)$ term, where $N$ is the number of experts. The authors instantiate this with:

1. **Discounted VAW** (linear): A variant of the weighted average forecaster with inverse variance weighting, using a running quadratic form $A_t$.
2. **Discounted AIOLI** (logistic): A FTRL method with a time-varying regularizer built from a discounted sum of outer products of gradients, solved via a quadratic surrogate.
3. **Discounted FTRL for Adam**: The inner algorithm is update of clipped or clip‑free Adam; the outer O2NC (Optimization for Online Non‑Convex) wrapper exponentiates the iterates.

The reduction automatically adapts to the optimal $\beta$ without prior knowledge of the path length $P_\beta^T = \sum_t \|u_t - u_{t-1}\|_\beta$, where $\|v\|_\beta^2 = \sum_{s=1}^t \beta^{t-s}\|v_s\|^2$.

## Key Results
- For online linear regression, Discounted VAW achieves dynamic regret $\tilde{O}\bigl(d \log T + \sqrt{d T P_\beta^{*}}\bigr)$, simplifying prior analyses.
- For online logistic regression, the ensemble of Discounted AIOLI attains $\tilde{O}\bigl(d B \log(BT) + \sqrt{d B T P_\beta^{*}}\bigr)$ with no exponential dependence on $B$.
- For Adam in stochastic non‑convex non‑smooth settings, both clipped Adam (under $\beta_2 \ge \beta_1^4$ or a margin condition) and clip‑free Adam (under $\beta_2 \ge \beta_1^2$) achieve optimal convergence rates to $(c,\varepsilon)$‑stationary points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The reduction separates two challenges: (1) achieving a small *discounted* regret for a fixed $\beta$, and (2) selecting a good $\beta$ without knowing the comparator path. The wrapper treats each $\beta$ as an expert; the dynamic regret decomposes into an *approximation* term (the best expert’s discounted regret, translated via $\beta$‑norms) and an *estimation* term (the cost of learning which $\beta$ works). The discounted algorithms themselves are carefully designed FTRL procedures: the regularizer accumulates past gradients with discounting, which in linear regression yields a closed‑form inverse; in logistic regression a quadratic surrogate ensures a well‑behaved update. For Adam, the discounting interacts with the adaptive moments and the O2NC exponentiation to produce the stationary‑point guarantee. The key insight is that the $\beta$‑norm path length $P_\beta^T$ interpolates between the cumulative variation ($\beta\to0$) and the static regret ($\beta\to1$), so the reduction automatically balances these extremes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dynamic regret**: Sum of instantaneous regrets against a time‑varying comparator sequence; captures non‑stationarity. *Why needed:* The standard metric for online learning with changing environments. *Quick check:* For a fixed comparator $u$, dynamic regret reduces to static regret; for a walking comparator $u_t$, it measures tracking ability.
- **Discounted regret**: Weighted sum of losses with exponential decay; emphasizes recent rounds. *Why needed:* Serves as an intermediate quantity that the reduction can convert to dynamic regret; easier to bound for many algorithms. *Quick check:* With $\beta=1$, discounted regret equals static regret; with $\beta\to0$, it approaches last‑round loss.
- **FTRL (Follow‑The‑Regularized‑Leader)**: Predict by minimizing the sum of losses plus a regularizer. *Why needed:* The underlying template for the discounted algorithms; the regularizer encodes the discounting. *Quick check:* For linear losses and quadratic regularizer, FTRL yields the multi‑layered Gaussian linear model with Gaussian noise; the posterior mean is the FTRL prediction.
- **Curved losses (e.g., logistic)**: Losses that are functions of linear predictions but non‑linear in parameters. *Why needed:* Common in classification; require surrogate quadratic models for tractable updates. *Quick check:* Logistic loss $\log(1+e^{-y x^\top z})$ is 1‑strongly convex in $x$ on a bounded ball.
- **Adam optimizer**: Adaptive method using per‑coordinate momentum and scaling. *Why needed:* State‑of‑the‑art for deep learning; analysis in non‑convex stochastic settings is challenging. *Quick check:* Adam update $x_{t+1}=x_t - \alpha \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}+\nu}$ with bias‑corrected moments $\hat{m}_t,\hat{v}_t$.
- **$(c,\varepsilon)$‑stationary point**: Point where $\|\nabla f(x)\|\le c\varepsilon$ and $f(x)-f^*\le\varepsilon$; a relaxation of true stationarity for non‑convex functions. *Why needed:* Captures convergence when gradient norms become small and function value is near-optimal; standard for stochastic non‑convex analysis. *Quick check:* For $f(x)=x^2$ in 1D, $(c,\varepsilon)$‑stationary with $c=1$ means $|x|\le\varepsilon$ and $x^2\le\varepsilon$ (roughly $|x|\le\sqrt{\varepsilon}$).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Reduction wrapper → (outer) Multi‑exponent selection over $\beta$ grid → (inner) Discounted online learner (VAW/AIOLI/Adam) → predictions $x_t$  
For Adam: outer O2NC wrapper exponentiates inner discounted FTRL iterate.

**Critical path**  
1. Initialize a set of experts, each with a fixed discount factor $\beta_i$.  
2. At each round, receive loss $f_t$, query each expert for its prediction $x_{t,i}$.  
3. Combine predictions via weights $q_{t,i}$ (multiplicative weights over the expert’s cumulative *estimated* dynamic regret).  
4. Update each expert’s internal state (discounted sums $A_t$, $S_t$, etc.) using the observed loss/gradient.  
5. For the ensemble (logistic), periodically reset weights to avoid collapse.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Discounted VAW** trades off matrix inversion cost for optimal static‑dynamic interpolation; Woodbury recursion keeps $O(d^2)$ per round.  
- **Discounted AIOLI** requires solving a quadratic subproblem per round; the surrogate introduces approximation error controlled by curvature.  
- **β‑ensemble** adds a $\log T$ factor in computation and a $d B \log(BT)$ term in regret but removes exponential $e^{O(B)}$ dependence.  
- **Adam variants** clipping (or $\mu>0$ in clip‑free) stabilizes updates at the cost of bias; the parameter coupling ($\beta_2 \ge \beta_1^4$ etc.) arises from relating moment decay to progress.

**Failure signatures**  
- Matrix $A_t$ becomes ill‑conditioned → use Woodbury and jitter $\lambda I$.  
- Logistic AIOLI diverges → monitor $A_t$ PSD; increase $\lambda$ or decrease $\beta$.  
- Adam steps explode → clamp denominator via $D$; monitor $\|\Delta_t\|$.  
- Ensemble weights collapse → track $q_{t,i}$ distribution; enlarge $\beta$ grid around current best.

**First experiments**  
1. **Linear regression**: Implement Discounted VAW with Woodbury; sweep $\lambda\in\{0.1,1,10\}$, $\beta\in\{0.8,0.9,0.95\}$ on synthetic data; verify $\tilde{O}(d\log T + \sqrt{dT P_\beta^*})$ dynamic regret.  
2. **Logistic regression**: Implement β‑ensemble for Discounted AIOLI; test on data with $\|z_t\|\le R$, $|y_t|\le B$; compare single‑β vs. ensemble; confirm $O(d B \log(BT) + \sqrt{d B T P_\beta^*})$.  
3. **Adam convergence**: Wrap clipped/clip‑free Adam in O2NC; run on 1D stochastic $f(x)=|x|$; explore $\beta_1,\beta_2$ grid; validate parameter coupling conditions for $(c,\varepsilon)$‑stationary rate.

## Limitations
- Optimal $\lambda$ and $\beta$ are not specified; they depend on unknown quantities like the path length $P_\beta^T$ or problem constants ($B,R$).  
- The inner solver for Discounted AIOLI (tolerance, Newton iterations) is not detailed; its accuracy may affect the regret bound.  
- Adam analysis requires problem‑specific targets ($\varepsilon$, $c$, $F^*$) to set hyperparameters ($\gamma,D,\mu,\nu$), which are not known a priori.
