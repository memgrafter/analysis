# Are LLM-Enhanced Graph Neural Networks Robust against Poisoning Attacks?

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.26105
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26105
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: LLM-enhanced GNNs consistently achieve higher accuracy and lower Relative Drop in Accuracy (RDA) than shallow embeddings under poisoning attacks, with GraphSAGE as the most robust backbone and TAPE/E5-Large as top enhancers.

## Executive Summary
This paper conducts the first comprehensive robustness evaluation of LLM-enhanced Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) against poisoning attacks that manipulate both graph structure and node text. Evaluating 24 victim models across 4 datasets and 9 attacks, the study finds that LLM/LM-based feature enhancers significantly improve robustness compared to shallow embeddings. The robustness stems from semantically rich embeddings that encode label and structural information more effectively. GraphSAGE emerges as the most robust backbone, and certain enhancers (TAPE, E5-Large) show exceptional resilience. The work also proposes a combined structural-textual attack and an LLM-based graph purification defense.

## Method Summary
The authors establish a robustness assessment framework using 8 LLM/LM feature enhancers (e.g., TAPE, LLaMA, E5-Large) combined with 3 GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE). They evaluate these 24 models on 4 text-attributed graph datasets (Cora, Pubmed, Ogbn-products subset, Tape-arxiv23 subset) under 6 structural attacks (Nettack, SGA, NAG-R, Mettack, DICE, PGA) and 3 textual attacks (DeepWordBug, Bert-Attack, MAY A). Models are trained on perturbed data and tested on a clean test set to compute Accuracy and Relative Drop in Accuracy (RDA). The shallow embedding baseline (BoW/TF-IDF/Word2Vec) is used for comparison. Additional analysis measures embedding separability (DBI, Silhouette), homophily, and mutual information (ELMI, ESMI) to explain robustness differences.

## Key Results
- Under high-perturbation Nettack, the best LLM-enhanced model improved accuracy by up to 13.55% (Cora) and reduced RDA by over 15% (Pubmed) relative to the shallow baseline.
- GraphSAGE was the most robust backbone across attacks, and enhancers like TAPE and E5-Large consistently achieved lower RDA.
- LLM/LM embeddings exhibited higher label and structural mutual information (ELMI/ESMI) and neighbor consistency, correlating with robustness.
- Some textual attacks produced negative RDA (i.e., improved accuracy) for certain LLM-enhanced models, suggesting potential regularization effects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1**: LLM/LM feature enhancers produce semantically rich node embeddings that encode label information more effectively than shallow embeddings, thereby maintaining classification accuracy under poisoning attacks. The deep semantic features provide a stable representation less sensitive to structural perturbations, as the textual semantics remain unchanged. Evidence: The paper shows higher embedding-label mutual information (ELMI) for LLM/LM enhancers, and these metrics remain superior under Mettack attack. Break condition: If textual attributes themselves are adversarially modified, this robustness may degrade.

**Mechanism 2**: LLM/LM embeddings retain more local structural information (e.g., neighbor consistency) than shallow embeddings, helping GNNs resist structural poisoning. The embeddings implicitly capture structural relationships from pretraining, allowing the GNN to leverage both semantic and structural cues even when edges are perturbed. Evidence: The paper reports higher embedding-structural mutual information (ESMI) and neighbor consistency for LLM/LM enhancers on clean and attacked graphs. Break condition: Under extremely high perturbation rates, structural information may be destroyed.

**Mechanism 3**: The GraphSAGE backbone contributes to robustness due to its self-representation preservation and mean aggregation, which reduce the impact of adversarially perturbed neighbors. By concatenating a node's own features with aggregated neighbors and using mean pooling, the final representation becomes less dependent on any single neighbor. Evidence: GraphSAGE consistently achieved lower RDA than GCN and GAT across structural attacks. Break condition: If the attack also targets self-features (textual poisoning) or exploits the aggregation mechanism, GraphSAGE's advantage may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw datasets (graph structure + node text) -> Structural/Textual attacks -> Perturbed graph/text -> Feature enhancer (LLM/LM) -> Node embeddings -> GNN backbone (GCN/GAT/GraphSAGE) -> Trained classifier -> Clean test set -> Accuracy/RDA -> Analysis (t-SNE, DBI, Silhouette, Homophily, ELMI, ESMI, Neighbor Consistency).

Critical path:
1. Load raw dataset.
2. Apply chosen poisoning attack to produce perturbed inputs.
3. For each victim model combination (enhancer + backbone), generate node embeddings using the enhancer and train the GNN on the perturbed graph.
4. Evaluate trained model on clean test set to compute accuracy and RDA.
5. Repeat for all attack settings and model combinations.
6. Perform additional analysis (visualization, separability, information preservation) on selected models.

Design tradeoffs:
- Attack coverage vs. computational cost: Evaluating all 24 models × 9 attacks × 4 datasets is expensive; the paper uses subsets and sampling to manage runtime.
- Model diversity vs. reproducibility: They selected widely-used enhancers and backbones to ensure fair comparison, but newer models (e.g., GPT-4) are omitted due to cost.
- Defense simplicity vs. effectiveness: The proposed graph purification uses a similarity threshold; choosing the threshold requires dataset-specific tuning (higher homophily → lower threshold).

Failure signatures:
- Clean accuracy significantly lower than literature indicates training issues or mismatched hyperparameters.
- RDA values that are negative for many models might indicate that the attack inadvertently improved generalization.
- High variance across random seeds suggests instability in training or attack generation.
- If embedding separability (DBI) is poor for a particular enhancer, expect lower robustness and accuracy.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline establishment: Train and evaluate the shallow embedding baseline on clean datasets for all GNN backbones to obtain reference performance.
2. Structural attack benchmark: Apply Nettack (targeted) and Mettack (non-targeted) with high perturbation rates to the Cora dataset; compare accuracy and RDA across all 24 victim models.
3. Textual attack benchmark: Apply the MAY A sentence-level attack to the Pubmed dataset; measure accuracy and RDA to assess sensitivity to textual perturbations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can adversaries design effective poisoning attacks on LLM-enhanced GNNs with only partial graph structure? The paper suggests using LLMs as link predictors but this remains unexplored.
2. How can structural attacks be made stealthier by preserving key structural properties without compromising effectiveness? Current attacks create detectable anomalies.
3. What architectural designs (e.g., GNN backbones, feature enhancers) inherently confer robustness against poisoning attacks, and how can they be systematically derived? The paper observed GraphSAGE and fine-tuned LMs performed better but the principles are unclear.
4. How can structural and textual perturbations be adaptively coordinated to maximize poisoning attack effectiveness? The paper's combined attack simply adds perturbations without optimization.

## Limitations
- Attack reproducibility: exact library versions, surrogate models, and random seeds are not disclosed, hindering precise replication.
- LLM/LM embedding generation (prompts, max length, batching) lacks detailed documentation, creating hidden variability.
- Datasets were subsampled for large graphs; scalability to full graphs remains unknown.

## Next Checks
1. Exact replication: Use the authors’ code (if released) or match reported library versions and attack settings; precompute and publish embeddings to ensure deterministic results.
2. Broader model and data evaluation: Test on newer LLMs (GPT‑4, Claude) and on full versions of the large datasets to verify scalability claims.
3. Mechanistic ablation: Isolate the contribution of the linear projection layer (from LLM output to GNN input) and systematically compare aggregation schemes (mean, sum, max) under varying perturbation budgets.
