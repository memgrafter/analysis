# TARSE: Test-Time Adaptation via Retrieval of Skills and Experience for Reasoning Agents

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.01241  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01241  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** TARSE improves medical QA accuracy by 10+ percentage points over strong RAG and CoT baselines (e.g., 73.8% on MedQA vs. 63.2% for rStar-14B) while enhancing reasoning trace quality and maintaining a favorable accuracy-latency trade-off.

## Executive Summary
TARSE addresses a key failure mode in clinical reasoning agents: the misapplication of procedural knowledge and prior examples at incorrect reasoning steps. It introduces a dual-resource framework where clinical knowledge is explicitized as *skills* (structured decision rules from guidelines) and *experience* (verified step-indexed reasoning chains). At test time, a step-aware retriever selects relevant skills and experiences for each transition in a provisional reasoning chain, followed by lightweight test-time adaptation to align the model’s intermediate logic with clinically valid patterns. This design prevents heuristic shortcuts and ensures evidence is applied precisely where needed. Evaluations on medical QA benchmarks and MultiHopQA show substantial accuracy gains and improved trace quality with manageable latency overhead, offering a better trade-off than heavy test-time scaling methods.

## Method Summary
TARSE constructs two curated libraries: a **Skills Library** of executable decision rules (e.g., condition→action) parsed from clinical guidelines, and an **Experience Library** of verified step-by-step reasoning chains with entity grounding. At inference, given a query: (1) retrieve a batch of similar solved cases (experience); (2) perform lightweight test-time training (TTT) on the base model to adapt its parameters toward these chains; (3) generate a provisional reasoning chain from the adapted model; (4) for each transition in the chain, retrieve and filter skill items by step-alignment; (5) condition on the provisional chain, retrieved skills, and experience to produce the final answer. The process is fully step-aware, targeting evidence to specific fragile transitions rather than the whole question.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy:** 73.8% on MedQA (vs. 63.2% for rStar-14B) and 48.5% on MultiHopQA (vs. 34.1%).
- **Trace quality:** ROUGE-1 rises to 0.3318; step-level entailment (Atomic Cov.) reaches 0.6112.
- **Efficiency:** Adds only ~32 seconds latency per question vs. CoT baseline, with a better accuracy-latency trade-off than test-time scaling methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** TARSE corrects reasoning errors by retrieving evidence aligned to the specific transition where the model’s logic is fragile, not to the overall question.
- **Mechanism:** For each inference step τₜ in a provisional chain, a query is formed from that transition and used to retrieve skill items (structured rules) that directly support that specific premise.
- **Core assumption:** Many clinical QA failures are due to *misapplication* of rules at the wrong step or omission of a required check (instance–step misalignment), not just missing knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstracts framing medical QA with step-aware retrieval; Section 3’s step-alignment functional showing low alignment for failing steps; ablation in Section 7.1 demonstrating question-level RAG insufficiency.
- **Break condition:** If the provisional chain is qualitatively incorrect early on, later steps become unreachable, and step-aligned retrieval cannot recover the correct path.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Lightweight test-time adaptation (TTT) using retrieved experience traces anchors the model’s provisional chain to high-quality, verified step-by-step reasoning before skills verification.
- **Mechanism:** Retrieve a small batch of similar solved cases; perform a few gradient updates on the base model to align its output distribution to these chains. The adapted model then generates a more faithful, step-indexed provisional chain.
- **Core assumption:** Supervising the model on similar, verified trajectories during inference improves the structure and reliability of intermediate steps, making them suitable as queries for step-aware retrieval.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentioning TTT to align intermediate reasoning; Section 5 (Stage A) describing experience alignment; Section 7.1 showing strongest automatic trace quality for experience-aligned pipeline.
- **Break condition:** If retrieved experience contains subtle errors or is not representative of the query’s reasoning pattern, TTT could reinforce flawed intermediate logic.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Separating knowledge into two retrievable types—skills (reusable procedural rules) and experience (verified case trajectories)—enables precise correction of fragile reasoning links, which generic chunk-based RAG cannot achieve.
- **Mechanism:** Skills act as executable “gates” (e.g., contraindication checks) applied per transition; experience provides concrete examples of rule application. This dual-resource, step-aware design delivers constraint-level precision.
- **Core assumption:** Clinical reasoning failures often hinge on correctly applying a specific check or disambiguation at a precise point; unstructured document retrieval cannot reliably deliver that rule at the right step.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract framing dual resources; Section 2 on skills as stable subroutines; Section 7.1 analysis showing question-level RAG gaps; novelty vs. memory-augmented agents in corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the skills library lacks coverage for a needed decision rule or items are too generic, they may not provide the necessary step-specific constraint.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Step-aligned retrieval**  
  Why needed: Retrieval conditioned on a specific reasoning transition (τₜ) rather than the whole query is core to TARSE’s precision. Without it, evidence is often misplaced.  
  Quick check: Why would retrieving documents that match the entire question fail to provide the correct premise for a particular inference step?

- **Concept: Test-time adaptation (TTT)**  
  Why needed: TARSE uses gradient updates at inference to align the model to retrieved experience traces, differentiating it from standard prompting or zero-shot CoT.  
  Quick check: How does test-time training differ from in-context learning, and why might it produce a more consistent intermediate reasoning chain?

- **Concept: Skills as executable decision rules**  
  Why needed: Skills are structured procedures (conditions→action) that serve as verifiable gates, unlike generic text chunks, enabling precise step verification.  
  Quick check: What property of clinical guidelines makes them suitable to be represented as step-indexed skills rather than as general document chunks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Input query -> Experience Retriever -> TTT Module -> Provisional Chain Generator -> Skills Retriever (per transition) -> Skills Verification & Answer Generator

- **Critical path:**  
  Retrieve M experience traces → Adapt model parameters θ_q via n TTT steps → Generate provisional chain ẑ → For each transition τₜ in ẑ, retrieve K skills → Filter by step-alignment threshold τ → Condition on (experience batch B, ẑ, filtered skills e_S) → Output final answer y′.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Retrieval batch sizes (M for experience, K for skills): larger M improves alignment but increases TTT cost; larger K increases relevant skills chance but adds noise/latency.  
  - TTT steps (n): more steps may improve adaptation but risk overfitting and higher inference time.  
  - Library construction: Skills require high-curation from guidelines; experience traces need verification to avoid error propagation.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low step-alignment scores for many retrieved skills → skills library coverage gaps or poor retriever.  
  - Provisional chain diverges from gold (low trace quality) → TTT ineffective or irrelevant experience.  
  - Final answer correct but chain implausible → over-reliance on skills without coherent integration.  
  - High latency → excessive M/K or TTT steps; need parallelization or reduction.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Evaluate against strong CoT/RAG baselines (e.g., rStar, MedRAG) on MedQA and MultiHopQA.  
  2. **Ablation of components:** Isolate contributions by removing TTT, RAG-Skills, or both to measure synergy.  
  3. **Efficiency analysis:** Measure wall-clock time and token overhead per question vs. CoT baseline and heavy test-time scaling methods.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How does the manual curation effort for the skills and experience libraries scale when extending TARSE to new medical specialties (e.g., pediatrics, psychiatry) or non-medical domains (e.g., legal compliance, software debugging) with different procedural structures and less standardized guidelines?
- **Open Question 2:** Can the step-aware retriever generalize to domains where reasoning does not revolve around clear procedural "gates" (e.g., contraindication checks, mechanism disambiguation) or where step-level transitions are less defined, such as in open-ended creative writing or ambiguous real-world planning tasks?
- **Open Question 3:** What are the practical efficiency limits of TARSE’s test-time adaptation (TTT) when scaling to much larger language models (e.g., 70B+ parameters) or to instances requiring very long reasoning chains (e.g., >50 steps), and can the adaptation be made more computationally efficient (e.g., via parameter-efficient fine-tuning or early stopping) without compromising the accuracy gains from step-aligned retrieval?
- **Open Question 4:** To what extent can TARSE’s Stage B skills verification recover from a flawed provisional chain generated in Stage A, and does reliance on a potentially erroneous initial chain create a bottleneck where early mistakes propagate despite later step-aligned corrections?

## Limitations
- **Major unknowns:** Exact test-time training hyperparameters (n steps, learning rate, M, K, τ); precise normalization schema for skill items; retriever training details (splits, negatives, architectures); implementation of step-alignment scoring; reuse of experience library between TTT stages.
- **Break conditions:** Poor provisional chain quality may invalidate step-aligned retrieval; flawed experience traces could harm TTT; skills library coverage gaps leave errors uncorrected; latency may become prohibitive at scale.

## Confidence
- **High:** Empirical outcome claims (accuracy gains on MedQA/MultiHopQA, trace quality improvements) and novelty of dual-resource step-aware design vs. neighboring memory-augmented agents.
- **Medium:** Mechanistic claims about why it works (causal isolation relies on unreported ablations; break conditions indicate fragility).
- **Low:** Generalization to domains without procedural "gates" or step-indexed reasoning (not empirically validated).

## Next Checks
1. **Step-alignment scoring stress test:** For a sample of failed questions, manually verify if retrieved skills are truly transition-specific vs. question-level matches; low alignment scores should correlate with errors.
2. **TTT stability monitoring:** Track NLL on retrieved experience chains during gradient steps; overfitting or divergence would confirm hyperparameter sensitivity.
3. **Library coverage audit:** Check if gold-standard reasoning steps for error cases exist in the skills/experience libraries; missing coverage would confirm a key failure mode.
