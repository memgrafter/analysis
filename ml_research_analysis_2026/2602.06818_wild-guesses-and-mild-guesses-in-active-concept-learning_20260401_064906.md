# Wild Guesses and Mild Guesses in Active Concept Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06818
- **Reference Count:** 14
- **Primary Result:** In active concept learning with LLM-proposed hypotheses, maximizing expected information gain (EIG) outperforms positive test strategy (PTS) on rules requiring falsification but falls into a "support-mismatch trap" on simple rules, where PTS converges faster by maintaining proposal validity.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies active learning where an LLM proposes executable program hypotheses for a binary classification task (the Number Game). Using a Bayesian particle filter, the authors compare a normatively optimal query policy (maximizing expected information gain, EIG) with a simpler heuristic (positive test strategy, PTS). They find that EIG excels on medium and hard rules that need counterexamples but surprisingly underperforms on easy rules. The failure is traced to a "support-mismatch trap": EIG's preference for highly diagnostic boundary queries yields datasets that push the posterior into regions where the LLM's proposal distribution is sparse, causing invalid or degenerate proposals and particle collapse. PTS, though information-suboptimal, avoids this by selecting safer interior queries, maintaining proposal validity and converging faster on simple concepts. Both methods fail on hard rules, indicating a fundamental proposal-coverage limitation. The work suggests that in LLM-based neuro-symbolic learning, the active learning objective must be aware of the proposal generator's support.

## Method Summary
The system implements a Bayesian particle filter for active concept learning in the Number Game domain (integers 0–100). Hypotheses are Python predicate functions proposed by an LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash) conditioned on the current observed dataset `D_t`. Proposals are filtered for syntactic validity and consistency with `D_t`. The particle set approximates the posterior `p(h|D_t)`, with weights updated via a size-principle likelihood and a simplicity prior (token log-probability). The learner queries according to a policy: the Rational Active Learner (maximizing expected information gain, EIG), the Positive Test Strategy (PTS, sampling positives under the MAP hypothesis), or random passive sampling. After each query, weights are updated. If the effective sample size (ESS) drops below a threshold, rejuvenation occurs: new proposals are solicited from the LLM using the updated `D_{t+1}`, filtered, and incorporated. Convergence is declared when the MAP particle's weight exceeds 95%. The core bottleneck is the LLM's uneven ability to generate valid programs for different dataset constellations.

## Key Results
- EIG converges significantly faster than PTS on Medium-complexity rules (e.g., requiring falsification of combinations), demonstrating the value of information-seeking when proposal validity is not strained.
- On Easy rules (simple extensions like multiples of 4), PTS converges much faster (e.g., 2 vs. 7 queries for "multiples of 4") because EIG's boundary queries induce a support-mismatch trap: the posterior collapses onto a narrow region where subsequent LLM proposals are often invalid, degrading the particle set.
- Both EIG and PTS consistently fail to converge within the query budget on Hard rules (e.g., exceptions or complex patterns), indicating the target concepts lie outside the reliable support of the LLM's proposal distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Support-Mismatch Trap in EIG-Driven Querying
Maximizing expected information gain (EIG) can degrade learning performance on simple concepts by inducing datasets that fall outside the reliable support of the LLM proposal distribution, causing particle collapse. EIG selects queries that maximize predictive entropy under the current particle posterior, typically boundary instances. After observing the label, the posterior updates sharply. During rejuvenation, the LLM is prompted with this new, highly constrained dataset. For simple rules, these boundary-induced datasets can lie in regions where the LLM rarely produces valid or consistent programs, leading to many invalid proposals and a degenerated particle set that recovers slowly. The LLM's proposal distribution `q_LLM(h | D_t)` has uneven support; it produces high-quality, valid programs more reliably for some dataset configurations `D_t` than others. Evidence is drawn from the paper's abstract and sections 4.1 and 5, which describe how EIG's boundary points drive the posterior into regions with few valid hypotheses under the generator.

### Mechanism 2: Stability-Preserving Nature of PTS
The Positive Test Strategy (PTS) accelerates convergence on simple rules not by maximizing information, but by maintaining the learner within regions of high proposal validity, avoiding the support-mismatch trap. PTS queries instances predicted positive under the current MAP hypothesis. These "safe" queries are typically interior points of the current hypothesis's extension, not boundary points. Observing their labels (often positive) refines the hypothesis without imposing sharply new constraints that would push the dataset `D_t` into a low-support region for the LLM. This keeps subsequent rejuvenation within the LLM's reliable generative regime, maintaining a healthy pool of valid particles. The key assumption is that the LLM's proposal quality correlates with how "typical" or "central" the observed dataset `D_t` is relative to its training distribution. Section 6.1 frames PTS as a stability-preserving heuristic, and Table 1 shows faster convergence on Easy rules.

### Mechanism 3: Proposal-Limited Active Learning Objective
The normative value of a query for active learning is not intrinsic (EIG) but *relative to the proposal distribution's capacity to regenerate coherent hypotheses after the update*. The acquisition function should optimize a composite objective: information gain about the *true* concept **and** the expected health of the posterior approximation post-rejuvenation (e.g., expected validity rate of new proposals). A query that is highly informative but leads to a dataset state where `q_LLM` is sparse or produces invalid code has negative expected utility for the overall learning system. The LLM proposal step is a non-negotiable bottleneck; the learner cannot conjure arbitrary hypotheses from the true hypothesis space `H` but is constrained to `support(q_LLM)`. The paper's abstract and section 6.3 argue for generator-aware acquisition functions that penalize queries expected to induce low proposal success.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian Particle Filtering**: The system approximates the posterior `p(h|D_t)` with a weighted set of particles. Understanding resampling, weight updates (via likelihood and prior), and effective sample size (ESS) is crucial to grasp particle degeneracy and rejuvenation. *Quick check:* What happens to particle diversity if a query eliminates most particles and the LLM proposal fails to generate valid replacements?
- **Expected Information Gain (EIG)**: EIG is the normative acquisition function for the Rational Active Learner. It quantifies the expected reduction in posterior entropy from a query. Understanding its computation (based on current particle predictive distribution) and its sensitivity to the current posterior approximation is key to seeing why it favors boundary queries. *Quick check:* Why might a query that maximizes EIG under a particle posterior be a poor choice if the subsequent LLM proposal step is likely to fail?
- **LLM as a Proposal Distribution**: The entire system's capability and failure modes stem from using an LLM `q_LLM(h|D_t)` to generate executable hypotheses. This introduces open-endedness but uneven support. Key facets are prompt design, filtering for syntactic validity/consistency, and the inherent "sparsity" of its coverage in program space. *Quick check:* How does the prompt content (the current dataset `D_t`) steer the LLM toward valid vs. invalid/overly-specific programs, and why might `D_t` from an EIG query be problematic?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  LLM Proposer -> Filter/Validator -> Particle Store (with weights)
  Query Policy (EIG/PTS/rand) -> selects x -> Oracle (true h*) -> label y
  Update & Rejuvenation Module: updates weights, checks ESS, triggers LLM Proposer if ESS low, reweights all particles
  Likelihood/Prior Scorer: computes unnormalized weights for particles
- **Critical path**: Query Policy selects x -> Oracle provides y -> Update weights with (x,y) -> Check ESS -> if low: LLM Proposer (with new D) -> Filter -> new particles -> Reweight all -> Normalize -> repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs**: EIG is theoretically optimal for reducing posterior uncertainty but is computationally fragile due to the proposal bottleneck. PTS is information-suboptimal but computationally robust by avoiding dataset states that break the proposer. Rejuvenation threshold balances LLM call cost vs. diversity maintenance. Filter strictness ensures particle quality but risks starvation if LLM is inconsistent.
- **Failure signatures**: 1) EIG on Easy rules: high query count, long plateaus; indicates particle collapse from invalid proposals after boundary queries. 2) PTS on Medium rules: high query count or DNF; indicates inability to find falsifying queries. 3) All methods on Hard rules: consistent DNF; indicates fundamental proposal coverage failure. 4) Spike in invalid proposal rate after rejuvenation following certain queries.
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Reproduce Table 1: run all rules with EIG, PTS, passive; compare queries to convergence. 2) Probe support-mismatch: for an Easy rule, log valid proposal rate after each rejuvenation; expect lower rate after EIG queries vs. PTS. 3) Forced query-type: on one Easy rule, force only boundary queries vs. only interior positives vs. random interior; compare convergence and particle quality.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited to a single LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash) and the constrained Number Game domain; findings may not hold for other architectures, prompts, or open-ended concept spaces.
- Critical hyperparameters (number of particles, ESS threshold, rejuvenation batch size) and implementation details (exact prompt, prior scoring specifics) are not specified, blocking exact replication.
- The evidence for the proposed mechanisms (e.g., support-mismatch trap) is interpretive and based on observed patterns; the corpus does not provide direct validation for the specific LLM-proposal interaction dynamics.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The empirical pattern (EIG advantage on Medium, PTS on Easy, both fail on Hard) and the explicit description of the support-mismatch trap as EIG's failure mode on Easy rules.
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that PTS's speed on Easy rules is primarily due to maintaining proposal validity (stability-preserving heuristic); supported by query-type contrast but not directly ablated.
- **Low Confidence:** The broader theoretical assertion that active learning objectives must be "proposal-aware"; logically consistent but not formally proven or tested against alternatives. The link between "confirmation bias" and adaptive stability is interpretive.

## Next Checks
1. **Track Invalid Proposal Rate vs. Query Type:** For each Easy rule trial, log the fraction of LLM proposals passing syntactic/consistency filters immediately after rejuvenation. Test if proposals following an EIG-chosen query have a significantly lower valid rate than those following a PTS-chosen query.
2. **Force Query-Type Manipulation:** On a single Easy rule, run three forced-policy conditions: (a) only boundary queries, (b) only interior positive queries (PTS-like), (c) only random interior queries. Compare convergence speed and final particle quality to isolate the effect of query location.
3. **Measure Proposal Coverage Exhaustively:** Before any learning, exhaustively sample the LLM with an empty prompt to estimate the empirical support of its proposal distribution over the full set of valid Number Game Python functions. Compute the overlap of this support with the true Easy, Medium, and Hard target concepts to validate whether Hard rule failure is due to zero coverage.
