# Scaling-Aware Adapter for Structure-Grounded LLM Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.02780
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02780
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Cuttlefish significantly improves all‑atom structure‑grounded reasoning across molecules, proteins, DNA, and RNA, achieving average METEOR 0.428 vs ~0.186 for general LLMs and reducing structural hallucinations (e.g., HR 0.07 vs 0.12).

## Executive Summary
Existing all‑atom LLMs suffer from over‑compression and poor geometric grounding when handling complex structures, leading to structural hallucinations. Cuttlefish introduces two key innovations: a Scaling‑Aware Patching mechanism that dynamically selects anchor atoms and grows variable‑size patches to match the query token budget with structural complexity, and a Geometry Grounding Adapter that refines patch tokens via cross‑attention with molecular embeddings before injecting them into the LLM. Trained in three stages—encoder pretraining, modality alignment, and LLM adaptation—Cuttlefish achieves consistent gains on heterogeneous benchmarks, notably on GEO‑AT (METEOR 0.428, BERTScore 0.864) and substantial hallucination reduction. Ablations confirm both components are essential.

## Method Summary
Cuttlefish processes all‑atom structures using an SE(3)-equivariant EGNN encoder that consumes atom features and coordinates. The encoder outputs node embeddings used by the connector. The connector first applies Scaling‑Aware Patching: an instruction‑conditioned MLP gate scores each atom, selects the top anchors whose cumulative probability exceeds a threshold ρ (0.1), and computes soft assignments based on distance and anchor bias to produce variable‑size patch tokens via weighted pooling. These patch tokens are then refined by the Geometry Grounding Adapter—a stack of fusion blocks where patch queries attend to the full set of node embeddings through cross‑attention, followed by self‑attention and feed‑forward layers—to inject explicit geometric cues. The final patch tokens are projected to the LLM embedding dimension and inserted at a special placeholder token position within the LLM input. The model is trained in three stages: (1) masked reconstruction on large unlabeled all‑atom pools; (2) freezing encoder and LLM while training the connector on the GEO‑AT instruction set using AdamW (lr 1e‑4); (3) unfreezing the LLM and continuing training at a lower learning rate (1e‑5). A masked reasoning augmentation adds teacher‑generated “think” spans that are excluded from the loss.

## Key Results
- Cuttlefish achieves average METEOR 0.428 on GEO‑AT, more than doubling the score of general LLMs (~0.186), and BERTScore 0.864.
- Structural hallucination rates drop markedly; for molecules, HR reduces to 0.07 from 0.12 in baselines.
- Scaling studies show stable performance across entity sizes with sublinear growth in token usage, confirming the effectiveness of the adaptive patching.
- Ablation experiments demonstrate that both the Scaling‑Aware Patching and Geometry Grounding Adapter are critical; removing either degrades performance to baseline levels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core limitation of prior all‑atom LLMs is that they compress entire structures into a fixed number of tokens, which becomes increasingly inadequate as structural complexity grows. Cuttlefish’s Scaling‑Aware Patching directly addresses this by allocating query tokens proportional to the informative parts of the structure, guided by the instruction. The gate selects anchor atoms that are most relevant, and the soft assignment aggregates surrounding atoms, ensuring that the token budget scales with actual complexity. Meanwhile, the Geometry Grounding Adapter enriches these tokens with precise geometric relationships through cross‑attention, providing the LLM with direct, equivariant cues about spatial arrangements. Together, these innovations prevent over‑compression, improve geometric grounding, and thereby reduce hallucinations while maintaining compatibility with existing LLM architectures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Equivariant Graph Neural Networks (EGNNs)**: Why needed: To process 3D molecular structures while preserving rotational and translational symmetry; ensures geometric information is encoded correctly. Quick check: Train a small EGNN to predict atomic positions from noisy coordinates and verify equivariance by rotating inputs.
- **Masked Reconstruction Pretraining**: Why needed: To learn general structural representations without supervision; reconstructs masked atom types, distances, and directions. Quick check: Randomly mask atoms/edges in a batch and check that the model can recover them with high accuracy.
- **Instruction‑Conditioned Gating**: Why needed: To make the selection of anchor atoms dependent on the downstream task, ensuring relevant regions are prioritized. Quick check: Feed different instructions for the same structure and verify that selected anchors differ meaningfully (e.g., via Jaccard distance).
- **Cross‑Attention Fusion**: Why needed: To inject structural details into language tokens without disrupting LLM pretraining; allows the adapter to attend to full node embeddings. Quick check: Visualize attention weights from adapter queries to node embeddings to confirm they focus on geometrically relevant atoms.
- **Three‑Stage Training**: Why needed: Separates representation learning, modality alignment, and language adaptation to avoid catastrophic forgetting. Quick check: Monitor loss curves; stage‑1 encoder loss should decrease, stage‑2 connector loss should drop while LLM loss stable, stage‑3 LLM loss decreases further.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
EGNN Encoder -> Scaling‑Aware Patching -> Geometry Grounding Adapter -> LLM

**Critical path**  
1. Preprocess raw structure (SMILES/PDB/sequence) into atom‑ordered features and coordinates.  
2. Encode structure with EGNN to obtain node embeddings.  
3. Compute instruction vector (LLM last‑token embedding) and feed to gate to select anchor atoms.  
4. Perform soft assignment and pooling to create patch tokens.  
5. Refine patch tokens through fusion blocks (self‑attention, cross‑attention to node embeddings, FFN).  
6. Project refined tokens to LLM dimension and insert at placeholder position in LLM input sequence.  
7. Generate output tokens via LLM autoregressive decoding.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Variable vs fixed token count: adaptive patching increases expressivity but requires careful threshold tuning to avoid context explosion.  
- Adapter depth: deeper fusion improves grounding but adds parameters and risk of overfitting; paper uses 8 blocks with 32 heads.  
- Stage‑wise training: expensive but stabilizes training; possible alternatives: joint end‑to‑end with small lr.

**Failure signatures**  
- No improvement over baseline: likely injection misalignment (check placeholder token handling and attention masks).  
- High hallucination despite training: encoder may not have learned geometry (verify masked reconstruction metrics).  
- Token count exploding: ρ too high or distance scale mis‑set; inspect anchor selection distribution.

**First experiments**  
1. Recreate the data pipeline for a small molecule set (e.g., 100 SMILES) and confirm row‑wise alignment of features and coordinates.  
2. Train EGNN masked reconstruction on 10k samples; verify L_type >0.9 and L_dist/L_dir <0.1.  
3. Implement connector (patching + adapter) and test on dummy data that it produces patch tokens of varying size and that injection into a small LLM (e.g., TinyLlama) yields reasonable token shapes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Segmentation & retrieval for >50K‑atom reasoning?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] extreme‑scale structure understanding via hierarchical segmentation & retrieval (App. F.2).  
*Why unresolved*: LLM context limits prevent processing large atomic graphs.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Coarse‑to‑fine pipeline; evaluate on large entities; demonstrate token reduction without accuracy loss.

### Open Question 2
Handling noisy/erroneous atomic coordinates in all‑atom LLMs?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] robustness to upstream structure noise via uncertainty‑aware tokens (App. F.2).  
*Why unresolved*: Current model assumes perfect geometry; real inputs (e.g., AlphaFold) have variable confidence.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Encode atom‑level confidence; train with multiple conformers; measure robustness on corrupted structures.

### Open Question 3
Dataset design for all‑atom reasoning with geometric attribution?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] domain‑grounded reasoning supervision with evidence‑tethered rationales (App. F.2).  
*Why unresolved*: Existing instructions lack intermediate reasoning citing specific atoms, limiting interpretability.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Build QA pairs with chain‑of‑thought referencing atom IDs and distances; train with masked objective; assess reasoning fidelity.

### Open Question 4
Can post‑hoc verification reduce residual non‑geometric hallucinations?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] post‑hoc verification with structure‑consistency checks (App. F.2).  
*Why unresolved*: LLMs may still produce factual errors not implied by structure, undermining reliability.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Add external validator cross‑checking claims against atomic features; measure hallucination rate reduction.

## Limitations
- Critical implementation details (gate MLP architecture, assignment scale/temperature, fusion block internals) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Missing hyperparameters (λ_d, λ_u) and placeholder token handling (vocabulary extension, initialization) require educated guesses.
- Data alignment between feature matrices and coordinates must be rigorously validated; errors could severely impair performance.
- Multi‑stage training is computationally expensive with no proposed efficiency improvements.
- Benchmarks may not fully represent real‑world distribution; reliance on external 3D predictors (ETKDG, AlphaFold2) introduces additional noise.
