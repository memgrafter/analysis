# Why Instruction-Based Unlearning Fails in Diffusion Models?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01514
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01514
- **Reference count**: 9
- **Primary result**: Instruction-based unlearning prompts (e.g., “Please forget anything about X”) fail to suppress targeted concepts in diffusion models; concept representations remain robust across CLIP text similarity, cross-attention, and generated images.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether instruction-based unlearning—successful in language models—can be applied to diffusion models for concept forgetting. The authors test prompts like “Please forget anything about [target concept]” on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and SD-XL across various concepts (e.g., panda, Vincent van Gogh). Their experiments show that such prompts yield negligible changes in CLIP text similarity, token ablation experiments reveal that concept tokens dominate the representation while instruction tokens are ignored, and generated images retain high CLIP alignment with the targeted concept. Cross-attention analysis further demonstrates that attention mass to concept tokens remains virtually unchanged. The study concludes that effective unlearning in diffusion models requires architectural or parameter modifications beyond inference-time prompting.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates instruction-based unlearning by comparing baseline prompts (“a photo of {concept}”) with unlearning prompts (“Please forget anything about {concept}. Please help me generate a picture of {concept}.”) and a repeat control. Experiments are conducted on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and SD-XL without altering model weights. Four main metrics are used: (1) CLIP text cosine similarity between the prompt and a concept anchor; (2) the effect of token ablation (removing concept tokens or instruction tokens) on that similarity; (3) CLIP image-text similarity between generated images and the concept anchor; and (4) cross-attention mass allocated to concept tokens during denoising, quantified by area under the curve (AUC). For each concept, multiple images are generated (fixed seed, 50 steps, guidance 7.5) and CLIP ViT-B/32 is used for all similarity computations. Cross-attention maps are extracted from all U-Net layers and heads.

## Key Results
- Unlearning prompts produce only a marginal decrease in CLIP text similarity to concept anchors (mean Δ = -0.045), while removing concept tokens from the prompt drastically reduces similarity (Δ = -0.241), indicating that instruction tokens are largely ignored.
- Generated images maintain strong alignment with the targeted concept, with negligible change in CLIP image-text similarity (mean Δ = -0.00061).
- Cross-attention mass to concept tokens remains nearly unchanged (AUC Δ = -0.0041), demonstrating persistent concept conditioning in the denoising process.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: CLIP text encoder bias
**Claim:** Unlearning instructions fail to meaningfully alter CLIP text representations of targeted concepts.  
**Mechanism:** CLIP’s text encoder embeddings are dominated by explicit concept tokens; instruction tokens contribute negligibly to suppressing semantic alignment. CLIP, trained on descriptive captions, weights concrete entity tokens far higher than abstract instructional or negational phrases.  
**Evidence:** Unlearning instructions cause only a small mean decrease in cosine similarity to concept anchors (-0.045). Token ablation: removing concept tokens causes a mean drop of 0.241, while removing instruction tokens yields a mean change of -0.027.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-attention rigidity
**Claim:** Cross-attention mass during diffusion denoising remains allocated to concept tokens despite unlearning instructions.  
**Mechanism:** The U-Net’s cross-attention layers do not reallocate attention from concept tokens to instruction tokens; patterns learned during training are rigid and not easily redirected by prompt-level semantic instructions at inference.  
**Evidence:** AUC for concept-token attention decreases by only a mean of -0.0041 with unlearning prompts. Figure 4(a–c) shows concept-token AUC remains high and decreases only marginally compared to baseline.

### Mechanism 3: Inference-time limitation
**Claim:** Inference-time text instructions lack a mechanism to override deeply learned concept-binding pathways.  
**Mechanism:** Concept knowledge is encoded in the model’s weights and conditioning process; textual prompts only activate pathways, they do not rewrite or suppress them. This leads to persistent concept evidence in generated images.  
**Evidence:** Generated images show high CLIP image-text similarity to concepts (Δ ≈ -0.0006), comparable to repeat-control prompts. Cross-attention analysis confirms no sustained reduction in attention to concept tokens.

## Foundational Learning
- **CLIP text-image alignment bias**: CLIP’s training on image-caption pairs leads to embeddings where concrete nouns dominate, while negations or instructions have minimal impact on similarity scores.  
  *Why needed*: Explains why unlearning instructions are marginalized in CLIP’s representation.  
  *Quick check*: Why would training on image-caption pairs cause CLIP to underweight negation or instruction words?
- **Diffusion cross-attention conditioning**: Cross-attention layers in diffusion U-Nets mediate how text tokens influence denoising; the distribution of attention mass determines which concepts are emphasized.  
  *Why needed*: Directly probes whether unlearning prompts can redirect attention away from targeted concepts.  
  *Quick check*: How does normalized cross-attention mass act as a probe for concept retention during generation?
- **Tokenization and embedding dominance**: Tokenization splits prompts into subwords; the embedding of each token contributes to the final text representation. Ablation experiments show that concept tokens carry the bulk of the semantic signal, while instruction tokens contribute little.  
  *Why needed*: Highlights the representational bottleneck that renders instruction tokens ineffective.  
  *Quick check*: What does a near-zero change in similarity after removing instruction tokens imply about their contribution relative to concept tokens?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  CLIP text encoder (frozen in standard pipelines) → token embeddings → diffusion U-Net with cross-attention layers per resolution → noise scheduler (DDIM/DDPM) → VAE decoder (if used).  
  Data flow: Prompt → CLIP → text embeddings (sequence) → reshape → injected via cross-attention at each U-Net block → iterative denoising → latent → image.
- **Critical path**: Prompt encoding (CLIP) → cross-attention map computation (U-Net) → denoising step update → final latent sampling.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Frozen vs. fine-tuned CLIP: Frozen CLIP is efficient but inflexible to instruction semantics; fine-tuning may help but risks catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge.  
  - Attention re-allocation: Steering attention away from concept tokens could reduce concept evidence but may degrade overall coherence if not carefully regularized.  
  - Inference cost: Dynamic attention manipulation adds overhead; weight-level unlearning (fine-tuning) is costly but one-time.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  1. High CLIP text similarity between unlearning prompt and concept anchor (near baseline).  
  2. Concept-token cross-attention AUC drop < 0.01 relative to baseline.  
  3. Generated images show high CLIP image-text similarity to concept anchor despite unlearning instructions.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Replicate text-only analysis**: For each concept, compute normalized CLIP text cosine similarity between prompt and anchor for baseline, unlearn, repeat prompts. Expect small negative Δ for unlearn vs baseline (≈ -0.045).  
  2. **Token ablation**: Remove all concept-related tokens from unlearn prompt and compute similarity; then remove instruction tokens. Expect large drop from concept removal (≈ -0.24) and minimal change from instruction removal (≈ -0.03).  
  3. **Cross-attention AUC measurement**: Generate images with `output_attentions=True`. For concept and instruction token indices, compute normalized attention mass per timestep and its AUC. Expect concept-token AUC decrease < 0.005; instruction-token AUC may rise but not at concept’s expense.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Hybrid approaches**: What combinations of inference-time instructions with lightweight model adaptations (e.g., adapters, prompt-tuning) can achieve reliable concept unlearning without full retraining? The paper explicitly states it did not explore such hybrids.
- **Non-CLIP text encoders**: Do diffusion models using T5 or BERT-based encoders exhibit different behaviors in response to unlearning instructions? Conclusions may not extend to architectures with fundamentally different conditioning mechanisms.
- **Training objectives for attention regularization**: Can training objectives that penalize attention to concept tokens during unlearning instructions rewire cross-attention dynamics? The paper probes attention but does not modify the training loss.
- **Perceptual dimensions beyond CLIP**: Are there aspects of concept expression in generated images that CLIP-based metrics fail to measure? The analysis relies on CLIP similarity and attention probes, which may not capture all perceptual or semantic aspects of concept expression.

## Limitations
- Model coverage limited to Stable Diffusion v1.5 and SD-XL; findings may not generalize to other diffusion architectures (e.g., Imagen, DALL·E).
- Key hyperparameters (CLIP variant, scheduler, step count) under-specified, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation relies exclusively on automated CLIP metrics; absence of human judgment leaves perceptual unlearning effectiveness uncertain.

## Confidence
- CLIP text encoder bias: Medium confidence.
- Cross-attention rigidity: High confidence.
- Inference-time limitation: Medium-High confidence.

## Next Checks
- **Multi-CLP replication**: Evaluate core metrics across multiple CLIP checkpoints (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, RN50) to verify robustness of instruction insignificance.
- **Prompt diversity test**: Test a variety of forgetting phrasings (e.g., “Exclude {c}”, “Without {c}”) and record changes in attention AUC and image similarity.
