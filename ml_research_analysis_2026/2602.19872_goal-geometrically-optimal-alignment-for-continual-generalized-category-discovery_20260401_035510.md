# GOAL: Geometrically Optimal Alignment for Continual Generalized Category Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19872
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19872
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: GOAL reduces average forgetting by 16.1% and improves novel class discovery by 3.2% compared to Happy on four benchmarks; under a 10-stage protocol, forgetting reduced by up to 23.71% and new class accuracy boosted by up to 10.28%.

## Executive Summary
Continual Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) combines class-incremental learning with the discovery of novel categories, but suffers from catastrophic forgetting and inconsistent feature alignment across sessions. GOAL tackles these issues by introducing a fixed Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) classifier that enforces a stable geometric structure. The framework leverages supervised alignment on labeled data to maintain known class feature consistency and confidence-guided alignment on high-conditional novel samples to gradually incorporate new categories. Experimental results on four GCD benchmarks show substantial reductions in forgetting and improvements in novel class discovery compared to the prior state-of-the-art Happy.

## Method Summary
GOAL employs a backbone feature extractor and a pre-initialized ETF classifier whose weight vectors have unit norm and equal pairwise inner product, creating maximally separated class directions. During each incremental session, the backbone is trained with two alignment objectives: (1) supervised alignment, which minimizes the angular deviation between features of labeled samples and their corresponding ETF vectors; and (2) confidence-guided alignment, which applies the same loss to novel samples whose model confidence exceeds a heuristic threshold, thereby integrating pseudo-labeled novel instances into the ETF geometry. The ETF classifier weights remain fixed throughout, acting as a stable scaffold to prevent forgetting, while the alignment losses steer the feature space to adhere to that scaffold.

## Key Results
- Average forgetting reduced by 16.1% relative to Happy.
- Novel class discovery accuracy improved by 3.2% on four benchmarks.
- Under a 10-stage protocol, forgetting reduced by up to 23.71% and new class accuracy increased by up to 10.28%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The fixed ETF provides a geometrically optimal and unchanging classifier head, ensuring that class weight vectors remain maximally separated and of equal norm. Supervised alignment keeps features of known classes tightly aligned with their ETF vectors, preventing drift that leads to forgetting. Confidence-guided alignment extends this constraint to high-confidence novel samples, pulling them into the structured feature space without disrupting existing classes. This dual alignment preserves the discriminative structure for old categories while gradually accommodating new ones, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting and enhancing discovery performance. The ETF’s equiangular property offers optimal angular separation, which improves class separability and robustness.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF)** – Why needed: Enables maximal angular separation between class weight vectors, providing a stable and discriminative geometric scaffold. Quick check: Verify all ETF vectors have unit norm and constant pairwise inner product across classes.
2. **Catastrophic Forgetting** – Why needed: Central challenge in continual learning; GOAL aims to prevent performance loss on previously learned classes. Quick check: After learning a new session, evaluate accuracy on earlier session classes; a significant drop indicates forgetting.
3. **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD)** – Why needed: Task that merges class-incremental learning with discovery of unseen categories, reflecting real-world open-world scenarios. Quick check: Measure both classification accuracy on known classes and clustering performance on novel classes within sessions.
4. **Confidence-Guided Alignment** – Why needed: Allows incorporation of unlabeled novel samples by leveraging model confidence to generate reliable pseudo-labels. Quick check: Inspect the distribution of confidence scores; only samples above a threshold should contribute to alignment loss.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input → Backbone → Feature Vectors → (ETF Classifier (fixed), Supervised Alignment (with labeled data), Confidence-guided Alignment (with high‑confidence pseudo‑labels)).  
- **Critical path**: Training loop iterates over batches: (a) labeled batch: compute classification loss using fixed ETF, compute supervised alignment loss; (b) novel batch filtered by confidence threshold: compute confidence‑guided alignment loss; total loss = classification + α·supervised alignment + β·confidence‑guided alignment; backpropagate to update backbone only.  
- **Design tradeoffs**: Fixed ETF Grants stability but may limit adaptability if feature distribution shifts significantly. Confidence threshold balances pseudo‑label noise versus novel data usage. Alignment loss weights (α, β) trade off strict geometric adherence vs. flexibility.  
- **Failure signatures**: (1) Increased forgetting (old‑class accuracy drop). (2) Degraded novel clustering (e.g., low clustering purity). (3) High alignment loss indicating features deviate from ETF geometry. (4) Performance collapse if confidence threshold is too low (noisy pseudo‑labels) or too high (insufficient novel data).

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to four standard GCD benchmarks; generalizability to other domains (e.g., video, medical) and extreme class imbalance is unknown.
- Fixed ETF constraint may be brittle under feature distribution shifts; sensitivity to ETF parameters unexplored.
- Confidence‑guided alignment uses a heuristic threshold; robustness and statistical significance of results are not assessed.

## Confidence
- Performance gains over Happy: Medium  
- ETF geometric stability: Medium  
- Efficacy of confidence‑guided alignment: Low
