# Dr. Seg: Revisiting GRPO Training for Visual Large Language Models through Perception-Oriented Design

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.00152  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00152  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Dr. Seg achieves SOTA on 5/6 benchmarks, with gains up to +4.5 on Pixmo counting.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), effective for language reasoning LLMs, underperforms when applied to visual perception tasks in Visual LLMs (VLLMs). The authors identify two key mismatches: perception requires a broader output space to capture diverse visual cues and finer-grained, stable rewards across multiple metrics. They propose Dr. Seg, a plug-and-play solution comprising a Look-to-Confirm mechanism that uses `<look>` tags to encourage exploration of visual evidence, and a Distribution-Ranked Reward that normalizes each metric to its empirical quantile via a FIFO queue, preventing high-variance dominance. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on five of six benchmarks, with significant gains in segmentation, detection, and counting, while maintaining robustness across in-distribution and out-of-distribution scenarios. Ablations confirm the necessity and synergy of both components.

## Method Summary
No reproduction notes were provided. Based on the reported outcome, Dr. Seg introduces two components integrated into the GRPO framework: (1) **Look-to-Confirm**, which inserts `<look>` token prompts during training to encourage the model to generate intermediate visual evidence queries, thereby expanding the output hypothesis space; (2) **Distribution-Ranked Reward**, which normalizes heterogeneous reward signals (e.g., Intersection-over-Union, Average Precision, count accuracy) by mapping each to its empirical quantile within a FIFO buffer, reducing variance and preventing any single metric from dominating the policy update. These modifications are designed to be plug-and-play and require minimal changes to existing VLLM training pipelines.

- Strong in-distribution and out-of-distribution robustness  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GRPO initially succeeded in language tasks where the output space is relatively narrow and rewards are single-scalar. Visual perception, however, demands consideration of many possible visual cues (e.g., multiple objects, spatial relationships) and the combination of multiple evaluation metrics. Dr. Seg addresses the first mismatch via Look-to-Confirm: by explicitly training the model to emit `<look>` tokens that correspond to intermediate visual queries, the policy learns to explore a broader set of candidate answers before finalizing output. The second mismatch—reward instability—is tackled by Distribution-Ranked Reward, which transforms each raw metric score into a quantile based on a maintained empirical distribution (via a FIFO queue). This normalization stabilizes policy gradients, prevents high-variance metrics from dominating, and better balances the multi-objective nature of perception tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Vision Encoder -> LLM Backbone -> Look-to-Confirm Module -> Multi-Metric Evaluator -> Distribution-Ranked Reward -> GRPO Optimizer  

**Critical path:** Training begins with a VLLM that generates sequences possibly containing `<look>` tokens. Each token triggers a visual attention step (e.g., region selection or feature query). After the full sequence is produced, a multi-metric evaluator scores the final answer (and optionally intermediate looks). The Distribution-Ranked Reward normalizes each metric to a quantile using a FIFO buffer of recent values, then aggregates them into a single reward for GRPO. The optimizer updates the LLM policy accordingly, and the buffer is updated with new scores.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Look-to-Confirm increases output length and requires the model to learn the semantics of `<look>` tokens, potentially slowing convergence but yielding broader exploration.  
- Distribution-Ranked Reward adds memory overhead (FIFO buffers per metric) and introduces hyperparameters (buffer size, quantile smoothing) but stabilizes training across heterogeneous metrics.  
- Both components are plug-and-play, balancing implementation simplicity with enhanced performance.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Model generates irrelevant or repetitive `<look>` tokens, indicating poor learning of the exploration mechanism.  
- Reward normalization becomes unstable if FIFO buffers are too small (noise) or too large (slow adaptation).  
- Performance degrades on tasks where metrics are highly correlated, suggesting possible over‑balancing.

**First three experiments to validate deployment:**  
1. Ablate Look-to-Confirm: train with GRPO only (no `<look>` tokens) and compare performance drop.  
2. Replace Distribution-Ranked Reward with raw metric sum and observe training stability and final gains.  
3. Test on a new perception task outside segmentation/detection (e.g., video question answering or 3D part segmentation) to assess generalizability.
