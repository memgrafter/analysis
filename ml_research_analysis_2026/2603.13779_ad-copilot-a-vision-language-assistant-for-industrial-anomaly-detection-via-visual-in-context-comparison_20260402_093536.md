# AD-Copilot: A Vision-Language Assistant for Industrial Anomaly Detection via Visual In-context Comparison

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13779
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13779
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** AD-Copilot achieves 82.3% accuracy on the MMAD benchmark, matching or beating human performance on several tasks, and yields a 3.35× improvement in localization mIoU (25.30) on MMAD-BBox.

## Executive Summary
Industrial anomaly detection (IAD) requires identifying subtle defects by comparing a query image against a normal template. General multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle here because they encode images independently, creating a "semantic comparison bottleneck" that hinders fine-grained visual reasoning. AD-Copilot addresses this by introducing a plug-and-play Comparison Encoder that uses cross-attention to generate explicit difference tokens from paired image features, enabling visual-level in-context learning. Trained on the curated Chat-AD dataset (620k+ contrastive pairs) via a multi-stage curriculum—from general change detection to IAD-specific tasks and reinforcement learning—the system sets a new state-of-the-art on the MMAD and MMAD-BBox benchmarks while generalizing to other IAD and comparison tasks.

## Method Summary
AD-Copilot builds upon a Qwen2.5-VL-7B base MLLM by inserting a novel Comparison Encoder between the vision encoder and the LLM. The encoder processes a query image and its immediately preceding template image (or self) via cross-attention, producing 100 learnable "comparison tokens" that encode pixel-level differences. These tokens are added to the original image token sequence, providing direct visual difference cues to the LLM. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: (1) full fine-tuning of the Comparison Encoder on general change detection datasets (CLEVR-Change, MagicBrush), (2) LoRA fine-tuning on IAD contrastive captions, (3) end-to-end instruction tuning on multi-task IAD dialogues, and (4) reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (multiple-choice accuracy, BBox-Mask IoU) to refine reasoning and localization.

## Key Results
- Achieves 82.3% accuracy on the MMAD benchmark, outperforming all prior models and matching/beating human experts on several tasks.
- Yields a 3.35× improvement in localization performance on MMAD-BBox, reaching a mean IoU of 25.30.
- Demonstrates strong generalization to other IAD benchmarks (UniPCB, OmniDiff-Real) and general comparison datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Comparison Encoder enables fine-grained visual-level comparison between a query image and a normal template, overcoming the "semantic comparison bottleneck" of standard MLLMs.
- **Mechanism:** It uses cross-attention between the paired image features (from the vision encoder) to generate a fixed set of 100 learnable "comparison tokens." These tokens explicitly encode pixel-level differences. They are then merged with the original image tokens and fed to the LLM, providing direct visual difference cues rather than relying solely on the LLM's language-space reasoning over independent image embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** Subtle industrial anomalies are best detected by analyzing fine-grained visual differences at the feature level before final language reasoning, not by comparing high-level semantic labels in the LLM.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "employs cross-attention to generate comparison tokens, enabling visual-level in-context learning."
  - [section IV.B] "uses cross-attention between paired image features to generate... comparison tokens that encode fine-grained visual differences."
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., Cross-Modal Mapping) explore 2D-3D fusion for IAD but do not directly evidence this specific cross-attention token mechanism; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the cross-attention fails to align relevant features (e.g., due to extreme domain shift or poor initialization), the comparison tokens may not carry meaningful difference signals, leading to no performance gain or degradation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Chat-AD dataset provides high-quality, contrastive multimodal training samples that teach the model the *paradigm* of visual in-context comparison for IAD.
- **Mechanism:** The data curation pipeline creates query-template pairs from industrial images. The generated text (captions, VQA) explicitly describes the *differences* between the pair, not just independent descriptions. This instills the core skill: "compare this to that template." It also includes verifiable localization and reasoning tasks.
- **Core assumption:** A large-scale dataset where every sample is framed as a comparison task is necessary to train an MLLM to attend to visual differences, rather than just memorizing defect patterns from single images.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Chat-AD... curated dataset of 620k+ paired query-template samples... with contrastive captions, VQA, and localization."
  - [section III] "each query image is paired with a normal template image... the generated text explicitly describes the visual differences between them—establishing comparison as the foundational data paradigm."
  - [corpus] Corpus signals show high relevance to multimodal IAD benchmarks, but specific evidence for the "paired contrastive" design is not present in neighbor abstracts; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic comparison descriptions are inaccurate or lack diversity, the model learns flawed comparison heuristics or overfits to spurious correlations in the text.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The multi-stage training curriculum progressively builds capability, from learning general visual differences to mastering IAD-specific reasoning, preventing catastrophic forgetting and stabilizing complex skill acquisition.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 0 trains the Comparison Encoder on general change detection (CLEVR-Change, MagicBrush). Stage 1 aligns it to IAD semantics using contrastive captions. Stage 2 performs end-to-end instruction tuning on diverse IAD dialogues. Stage 3 uses RL with verifiable rewards (accuracy for MC, BBox-Mask IoU for localization) to refine reasoning and localization.
- **Core assumption:** Separating the learning of the comparison mechanism (Stage 0) from domain knowledge (Stage 1) and then fine-tuning for instruction following and reasoning (Stages 2-3) yields better performance than joint training from scratch.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section V.A] Details the four sequential stages and their specific data/objectives.
  - [Table VII] Ablation shows consistent improvement as stages are added.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like IAD-R1 and EMIT use RL for IAD but do not describe this exact staged curriculum; evidence is moderate but indirect.
- **Break condition:** If stages are reordered (e.g., RL before sufficient supervised tuning) or if Stage 0 data is insufficient, the Comparison Encoder may not learn general difference representation, limiting downstream performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross-Attention for Multi-Image Feature Interaction**
  - *Why needed:* Core operation in the Comparison Encoder, allowing explicit feature-level interaction between the normal template and query image to extract differences. Standard self-attention within a single image cannot do this.
  - *Quick check:* Why can't self-attention within a single image token sequence achieve the same visual comparison as cross-attention between two image feature maps?

- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**
  - *Why needed:* Used in Stage 3 to optimize for both discrete correctness (multiple-choice anomaly discrimination) and continuous localization quality (BBox-Mask IoU), which cross-entropy loss cannot directly handle. It improves reasoning trace quality and localization fine-tuning.
  - *Quick check:* How does the reward function `R(x, y)` in Equation 1 combine format encouragement with task-specific verifiable signals, and why is the IoU-based reward critical for localization?

- **Industrial Anomaly Detection (IAD) as a Few-Shot, Template-Based Task**
  - *Why needed:* The entire AD-Copilot design is predicated on the IAD paradigm where a normal "template" image is provided per MMAD/1-shot setting. Understanding that defects are subtle deviations from a known good reference explains why independent image encoding fails.
  - *Quick check:* Why do general MLLMs perform poorly on IAD benchmarks even when provided a normal template image, according to the paper's analysis?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Images -> Vision Encoder (Qwen2.5-VL) -> Patch Tokens -> Comparison Encoder (cross-attention on adjacent image tokens) -> 100 Comparison Tokens (appended to current image tokens) -> LLM (Qwen2.5-VL) -> Output.
- **Critical path:** The Comparison Encoder must be inserted between the vision encoder output and the LLM input. It takes the patch tokens of the current image and its immediately preceding image (or self for the first), applies cross-attention with learnable queries, and outputs comparison tokens that are added to the current image's token sequence before LLM processing.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Token budget increases (100 extra tokens per image) leading to higher memory usage; adding tokens preserves base MLLM knowledge versus modifying original tokens; pairing only with immediate predecessor simplifies streaming but may miss long-range multi-shot context.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. No improvement on MMAD vs. base model → Comparison Encoder not trained (Stage 0 failure) or data lacks comparison paradigm.
  2. Poor localization (low mIoU on MMAD-BBox) → Comparison tokens lack spatial precision or RL bbox reward insufficient.
  3. Degradation on single-image tasks → Unlikely due to add-only design, but monitor if comparison tokens interfere.
  4. Training instability in Stage 0 → Full finetuning of random encoder on small change detection sets may diverge; requires careful LR and data.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate the Comparison Encoder: Train base Qwen2.5-VL-7B on Chat-AD with and without the encoder; measure MMAD accuracy and MMAD-BBox mIoU drop without it.
  2. Attention visualization: For query-template pairs, compute LLM layer attention to comparison tokens vs. base image tokens across prompts ("What's the difference?" vs "Describe the second image."); expect high attention to comparison tokens only for difference tasks.
  3. Template ablation: Evaluate on MMAD 1-shot with (a) provided random template, (b) no template (0-shot), (c) retrieved most-similar template; expect significant drop without template and improvement with better template.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can we finetune general MLLMs to serve as reliable assistants for industrial anomaly detection (IAD)?  
  *Basis:* Introduction states this central question. *Why unresolved:* AD-Copilot underperforms human experts in anomaly discrimination (73.95 vs 95.24) and localization (76.40 vs 92.31), and shows domain-specific drops (e.g., lower F1 on UniPCB). *What evidence:* A finetuning recipe consistently matching/exceeding humans across all IAD tasks and diverse domains without data leakage.

- **Open Question 2:** Is cross-attention the optimal mechanism for encoding visual differences between paired images in IAD?  
  *Basis:* Section IV-B introduces cross-attention but does not compare alternatives (e.g., Siamese networks). *Why unresolved:* The design choice lacks justification; performance gain may be architecture-specific. *What evidence:* Systematic ablation comparing different comparison encoder backbones on MMAD and MMAD-BBox.

- **Open Question 3:** How can we significantly improve fine-grained anomaly localization for irregular defects?  
  *Basis:* Tables show low mIoU (25.30) on MMAD-BBox; Section VI highlights irregular defect challenge. *Why unresolved:* Current methods yield poor localization; BBox-Mask IoU improves evaluation but not perception. *What evidence:* A model achieving mIoU >50% on MMAD-BBox with precise localization of tiny, fragmented defects.

- **Open Question 4:** How can we enhance generalization of IAD models to completely unseen industrial domains without category-specific data?  
  *Basis:* Table IV shows performance drop on UniPCB; Section VII-C cites lack of PCB knowledge. *Why unresolved:* Training data bias (327 categories not covering all domains) limits cross-domain applicability. *What evidence:* A training paradigm (e.g., meta-learning) enabling a single model to match domain-specific models on unseen benchmarks with minimal adaptation.

- **Open Question 5:** Does explicit multi-shot training of the Comparison Encoder yield further improvements over 1-shot pretraining?  
  *Basis:* Table IX shows 1-shot model generalizes to multi-shot, but multi-shot training unexplored. *Why unresolved:* Benefits/drawbacks of training with multiple normal templates unknown. *What evidence:* Train comparison encoder with multi-shot data (2,4,8 templates) and compare performance on MMAD/MMAD-BBox against 1-shot model.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of Comparison Encoder hyperparameters (e.g., layer count, attention heads) blocks exact replication.
- Chat-AD curation process is opaque; synthetic captions and bounding boxes may contain noise or bias.
- MMAD-BBox metric (BBox-Mask IoU) is newly introduced and not widely validated externally.
- High computational cost due to 2800x2800 resolution and extended token sequences is not analyzed.
- Performance gaps to human experts persist, especially in discrimination and localization.

## Confidence
- **Comparison Encoder design:** Medium (internal ablations positive, but architecture details missing and no external validation).
- **Chat-AD dataset quality:** Medium (scale claimed but not audited; synthetic data may be noisy).
- **Multi-stage training curriculum:** Medium (ablation shows gains, but stage necessity and ordering not fully tested).
