# Jump Like A Squirrel: Optimized Execution Step Order for Anytime Random Forest Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01588
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01588
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Backward Squirrel Order achieves ~94% of Optimal Order's normalized mean accuracy with polynomial runtime, enabling scalable anytime random forest inference.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses inefficient prediction retention when random forest inference is prematurely stopped on resource-constrained systems. Unlike prior anytime methods that operate on whole trees, the proposed approach executes forests at the granularity of single decision steps and retains class distributions at every inner node. This enables a flexible cross-tree step order. The authors introduce an Optimal Order (found by Dijkstra on a state graph, exponential runtime) and two polynomial-time greedy heuristics: Forward and Backward Squirrel Orders. Evaluations across nine UCI datasets show that the Backward Squirrel Order obtains about 94% of the Optimal Order's normalized mean accuracy and outperforms other orders by roughly 99%, while scaling to much larger forests.

## Method Summary
The method extends standard random forest training by storing, for each node, the empirical class distribution of the training samples that reached it. During inference, a predetermined sequence of single-step advances (one per tree) is followed. After any number of steps, the current prediction is the class with the highest summed probability vector from the currently visited nodes of all trees. The Optimal Order maximizes the mean accuracy under uniformly distributed stopping times by modeling the problem as a shortest‑path search on a state graph where each state is a tuple of steps taken per tree. Edge weights correspond to inaccuracy (1−accuracy) on a held‑out ordering set. Dijkstra’s algorithm yields the path with minimal cumulative inaccuracy. The Squirrel Orders avoid the exponential graph: Forward builds the sequence from the start, greedily picking the next step that gives the highest immediate accuracy; Backward starts from the fully executed state and iteratively selects the previous step (reversing one tree’s last step) that yields the highest accuracy for the preceding state. Backward’s runtime is O(d·t²) for t trees of maximum depth d.

## Key Results
- Backward Squirrel Order attains ~94% of the Optimal Order’s normalized mean accuracy.
- It outperforms alternative step orders (Depth, Breadth, Random, etc.) by about 99% in relative normalized mean accuracy.
- Backward Squirrel scales polynomially, while Optimal Order becomes infeasible beyond a few trees (exponential state space).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Inner‑node prediction
- Claim: Random forest inference can be made truly “anytime” by retaining prediction probability vectors at every inner decision node, not just leaves.
- Mechanism: During CART training, the empirical class distribution of samples reaching each node is stored. At inference, the aggregated prediction from all currently visited nodes across trees yields a valid class probability vector, enabling a meaningful prediction after any single step.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and Section III.C state that inner nodes can return predictions. The corpus lacks direct neighboring support.
- Break condition: Effectiveness may degrade if inner node distributions are highly unstable or uninformative (e.g., early splits on noisy features).

### Mechanism 2: Optimal Order via state graph
- Claim: The optimal step order (maximizing mean accuracy under uniform stopping) is found by modeling inference as a shortest‑path problem on a state graph, solved by Dijkstra’s algorithm.
- Mechanism: A state is a tuple (steps taken in tree 1, steps taken in tree 2, …). A directed edge from state A to B (incrementing one tree) has weight = inaccuracy of state B on an ordering set. The shortest path from (0,…,0) to (d₁,…,dₜ) minimizes total inaccuracy, equivalently maximizing mean accuracy.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and Section IV.B describe the formulation and guarantee optimality on the ordering set.
- Break condition: Optimality holds only for the ordering set; performance may diverge on unseen test data.

### Mechanism 3: Backward Squirrel heuristic
- Claim: The Backward Squirrel Order is a polynomial‑time greedy heuristic that effectively approximates the Optimal Order.
- Mechanism: Starting from the final state, it repeatedly selects which tree to step back (reverse the last move) so that the resulting previous state has the highest accuracy. The selected moves are recorded and later reversed to obtain the forward order. Runtime O(d·t²).
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and Sections IV.C/VI report empirical results (~94% of Optimal).
- Break condition: Greedy choice may be myopic; a locally optimal penultimate state could lead to suboptimal earlier steps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Anytime Algorithms**: Algorithms that produce a valid output after any interruption and improve with more time. Needed to handle unpredictable resource constraints. Quick check: After 3 steps in a 10‑tree, depth‑5 forest, what data is consolidated? → The probability vectors from the current node of each tree are summed and the class with the highest total is returned.
- **Decision Tree Node Statistics**: Storing class distributions at inner nodes during CART training. This information is usually discarded but retained here to enable step‑level predictions. Quick check: How is the probability vector for an inner node computed? → By normalizing the class counts of training samples that reached that node.
- **State Space Representation for Sequencing**: Representing execution as a graph of state tuples (steps per tree) to apply Dijkstra’s algorithm. Quick check: For 2 trees of max depth 3, how many distinct states exist? → (3+1)*(3+1) = 16 states (including start and end).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Ordering Set → Step Order Generator (Optimal/Squirrel) → Step Order Array → Execution Controller (index array) → Tree Representations (with node vectors) → Aggregation & Prediction Module
- **Critical path**: Step Order Generation (offline; potentially exponential for Optimal) → Inference Loop (tight loop: update one tree’s index per step, check abort).
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Optimal vs. Squirrel: Optimal guarantees best mean accuracy on ordering set but scales exponentially; Squirrel (Backward) scales polynomially with near‑optimal empirical results.
  - Step granularity: Single‑step control maximizes flexibility vs. overhead of per‑step loop.
  - Storage: Retaining probability vectors for all nodes increases memory vs. leaf‑only storage.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Step order degenerates to Depth Order if greedy search fails to find cross‑tree benefits.
  - Dijkstra’s graph construction fails for >8 trees due to state explosion.
  - Numerical instability if inner node distributions are uninformative (e.g., very shallow trees).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate anytime property: Abort inference at every possible step; confirm a prediction is always possible and accuracy improves on average.
  2. Backward Squirrel approximation test: Compare normalized mean accuracy (NMA) of Backward Squirrel to Optimal on small forests; expect ratio >93%.
  3. Scalability test: Measure generation time and memory for Optimal vs. Backward Squirrel as forest size increases; verify polynomial vs. exponential behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Implementation for if‑else trees**: How can an efficient anytime random forest be built using the common if‑else tree representation, which requires low‑overhead context switching between trees at the granularity of single decision steps?
2. **Non‑uniform abort distributions**: How do the optimal and Squirrel orders perform when the probability of abort is not uniform over time but follows a realistic distribution (e.g., higher chance of early abort)?
3. **Generalization to test data**: The Optimal Order is optimized on an ordering set, but may not generalize to the final test distribution. How can we generate step orders that better predict accuracy on unseen data?
4. **Computational complexity**: What is the exact complexity of finding the optimal step order? Are there polynomial‑time algorithms with approximation guarantees, or is the problem inherently exponential?

## Limitations
- Generalization gap: Step orders are optimized on an ordering set; real‑world abort distributions may differ, degrading mean accuracy.
- Scalability trade‑offs: Optimal Order is exponential; Squirrel Orders require O(d·t²) accuracy evaluations, which can still be prohibitive for very large forests.
- Uniform stopping assumption: The mean accuracy metric equally weights all steps, but actual resource constraints often cause biased stopping (e.g., earlier aborts).

## Confidence

- Backward Squirrel approximation: Medium
- Overall anytime gain: Medium
