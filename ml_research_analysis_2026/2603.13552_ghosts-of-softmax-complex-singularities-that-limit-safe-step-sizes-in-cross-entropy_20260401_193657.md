# Ghosts of Softmax: Complex Singularities That Limit Safe Step Sizes in Cross-Entropy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13552
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13552
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The safe step size in cross‑entropy training is limited by complex zeros of the softmax partition function ("ghosts"); the normalized step size r = τ/ρa (with ρa = π/Δa) is a universal stability predictor and a ρa‑based controller prevents collapse across architectures and survives extreme learning‑rate spikes.

## Executive Summary
Training neural networks with cross‑entropy loss often encounters sudden loss spikes or accuracy collapse, typically attributed to excessive curvature. This paper reveals an underlying geometric constraint: the Taylor series of the loss along an update direction converges only within a disk whose radius is limited by complex singularities (“ghosts”) of the softmax partition function. These singularities arise even for smooth real‑valued functions due to analytic continuation. Under a logit linearization, the authors derive a computable lower bound on the convergence radius, ρa = π/Δa, where Δa is the spread of directional logit derivatives obtainable with one Jacobian‑vector product. The dimensionless ratio r = τ/ρa separates safe (r<1) from dangerous (r≥1) updates across six architectures, and an adaptive controller that clips steps to τ ≤ ρa survives learning‑rate spikes that break gradient clipping and attains state‑of‑the‑art accuracy on ResNet‑18/CIFAR‑10 without a hand‑tuned schedule.

## Method Summary
The method computes a per‑step upper bound ρa on the Taylor convergence radius from the current update direction v and a mini‑batch. First, v is the unit vector of the raw optimizer update p. Then for each sample, directional derivatives a_k = ∇z_k·v for all logits z_k are obtained via a Jacobian‑vector product (JVP). The per‑sample spread Δa(x) = max_k a_k - min_k a_k is computed, and the batch‑maximum Δa_max = max_x Δa(x) yields ρa = π / Δa_max. The normalized step size r = ‖p‖/ρa indicates whether the step stays within the guaranteed convergence disk (r<1) or not. A controller scales p to ensure r ≤ 1, i.e., τ ≤ ρa. This procedure adds about one forward‑pass of overhead and can be applied after optimizer state computation but before the parameter update. Temperature scaling modifies the bound to ρa(T) = πT/Δa, preserving the r‑threshold.

## Key Results
- No model failed for r < 1 across six architectures (Linear, MLP, CNN, MLP+LN, CNN+BN, TinyTransformer); collapse consistently occurred for r ≥ 1.
- Temperature scaling confirmed the mechanism: normalizing by ρa reduced the spread of failure thresholds by a factor of six.
- A ρa‑controller survived learning‑rate spikes up to 10,000× where gradient clipping failed and achieved 85.3% accuracy on ResNet‑18/CIFAR‑10 without a hand‑tuned schedule.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Complex zeros of the partition function cap the Taylor convergence radius
- **Claim:** The safe step size in cross‑entropy training is limited by complex zeros of the softmax partition function (“ghosts”), not by real‑curvature measures like the Hessian.
- **Mechanism:** Along any update direction `v`, the loss `ℓ(τ)=L(θ+τv)` is analytic in the complex plane. Its Taylor series at `τ=0` converges only within a disk whose radius equals the distance to the nearest singularity. For cross‑entropy, singularity arises from the log‑partition `log F(τ)` where `F(τ)=∑_k exp(z_k(τ))`. Although `F(τ)>0` for real `τ`, it possesses complex zeros (e.g., cancellation yields `e^{iπ} = -1`). Those zeros introduce logarithmic branch points in `ℓ` and cap the radius.
- **Core assumption:** The loss analytic continuation to ℂ exists; the step is taken on ℝ; descent analysis relies on local Taylor approximations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2.2 (binary log‑partition zero at `iπ`), Section 4.3.1 (half‑plane obstruction), Theorem 4.6 (general lower bound `ρ* ≥ π/Δa`), empirical phase transition at `r=1`.
- **Break condition:** When `τ` exceeds the convergence radius, the Taylor series diverges; first‑order decrease guarantees fail, leading to loss spikes or collapse.

### Mechanism 2: Logit linearization yields explicit bound `ρa = π/Δa`
- **Claim:** Under a first‑order (linear) approximation of the logits along `v`, the convergence radius lower bounds to `ρa = π / Δa`, where `Δa` is the spread of directional logit derivatives `a_k = ∇z_k·v`.
- **Mechanism:** Linearizing `z_k(τ) ≈ z_k(0) + a_k τ` gives `F(τ)=∑ w_k e^{a_k τ}`. For complex `τ = u+iy`, the term vectors `w_k e^{a_k τ}` have phases `a_k·y`. If `|Im(τ)| < π/Δa`, all phases fall in an open arc of length `<π`; by the half‑plane argument, no positive combination can cancel to zero. Hence `F(τ)≠0` for `|τ|<π/Δa`, so `ρ* ≥ π/Δa`. The bound is tight for binary classification with zero logit margin.
- **Core assumption:** Logits are approximately linear over the step; higher‑order curvature negligible.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 Theorem 4.3 (exact binary radius `ρ* = sqrt(δ²+π²)/Δa`), Section 4.3.1 Lemma 4.5 (half‑plane obstruction), Section 4.3.2 Theorem 4.6 (general lower bound).
- **Break condition:** Strong logit nonlinearities may make true radius smaller than `π/Δa`; clipping at `ρa` could still permit unsafe steps. Conversely, confident predictions (`δ>0`) yield a larger radius, so the bound is conservative.

### Mechanism 3: Normalized step `r = τ/ρa` predicts stability; adaptive controller prevents collapse
- **Claim:** The dimensionless ratio `r = τ/ρa` separates safe from dangerous updates: `r<1` yields stability across architectures and directions; `r≥1` correlates with loss spikes and accuracy collapse.
- **Mechanism:** Scaling the step distance by the local radius removes architecture‑specific units. When `r<1`, the step stays inside the guaranteed convergence disk of the linearized model; when `r≥1`, the Taylor model may diverge, leading to unpredictable loss changes.
- **Core assumption:** The linearized bound `ρa` is accurate enough to identify dangerous updates; occasional survival for `r>1` does not invalidate the boundary because the bound is intentionally conservative.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 6.2 Figure 7 (test accuracy retained drops sharply near `r=1`), Section 6.4 Figure 9 (temperature scaling collapses thresholds), Section 6.5 Table 1 (controller survives spikes where clipping fails).
- **Break condition:** If logit linearization breaks down (e.g., very deep non‑residual networks), `ρa` might not reflect the true stability limit; clipping at `ρa` could be overly restrictive or, rarely, miss instabilities.

## Foundational Learning
### Taylor series convergence radius (Cauchy‑Hadamard theorem)
- **Why needed:** Determines the interval within which a truncated Taylor series accurately represents the function. Beyond that radius, adding more terms doesn’t improve approximation; divergence can occur.
- **Quick check:** For `f(τ)=1/(τ+2)`, expanded at `τ=0`, what is the convergence radius? If you take a step `τ=1.5`, is a quadratic surrogate trustworthy? Explain.

### Complex analysis of analytic functions and singularities
- **Why needed:** The loss is smooth on ℝ but may have complex singularities (e.g., from `log ∑exp`). The distance to those singularities—not real‑line curvature—bounds the convergence radius.
- **Quick check:** The function `g(x)=log(1+e^x)` is smooth on ℝ. Its Taylor series about 0 converges for `|x|<π`. Why does a singularity at `iπ` restrict convergence on the real line?

### Jacobian‑vector product (JVP)
- **Why needed:** Computing `Δa = max a_k - min a_k` requires directional derivatives `a_k = ∇z_k·v`. A JVP obtains all `a_k` with cost similar to a forward pass, making the bound practical.
- **Quick check:** Given `z(θ)=W h(θ)+b`, how would you compute `a = J_z·v` efficiently with autograd? What geometric meaning does `a_k` have?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - `compute_direction(p)`: convert optimizer update `p` to unit direction `v = p / ‖p‖`.
  - `jvp_batch(batch, v)`: for each sample, compute `a(x;v)=∇_θ z(x)·v` via forward‑mode AD (or a VJP trick).
  - `compute_Delta_a_max(a_batch)`: per‑sample spread `Δa(x)=max a_k - min a_k`; then `Δa_max = max_x Δa(x)`.
  - `compute_rho_a(Δa_max)`: `ρa = π / Δa_max`.
  - `clip_update(p, ρa)`: `s = min(1, ρa / ‖p‖)`; return `s·p`.
  - Integration: apply `clip_update` after the optimizer’s internal state but before parameter update.

- **Critical path (one iteration):**
  1. Forward + backward → optimizer gives raw update `p`.
  2. `v = compute_direction(p)`.
  3. `a_batch = jvp_batch(current_microbatch, v)`.
  4. `Δa_max = compute_Delta_a_max(a_batch)`.
  5. `ρa = π / Δa_max`.
  6. `p_safe = clip_update(p, ρa)`.
  7. `θ ← θ + p_safe`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Linearization vs. safety:** The bound assumes `z_k(τ)` is linear. For deep networks without residuals, curvature may cause `ρa` to underestimate the true safe step, yet clipping remains safe (perhaps conservative). Residual connections keep logits more linear, improving bound accuracy.
  - **Sample set size:** Using the full training set gives the most conservative `ρa` but is costly. A mini‑batch estimate is faster but risks missing a worst‑case sample; a random subset (e.g., 256 samples) balances cost and risk.
  - **Overhead:** One JVP ≈ 1 forward pass; authors report ~66% extra time for finite‑difference JVP on ResNet‑18, ~129% for exact AD. Overhead might be amortized if `v` is reused across several steps (rare).
  - **Adaptivity vs. momentum:** The controller clips the step after momentum computation; this protects against momentum‑amplified steps that could otherwise violate `r<1`.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - `r > 1` sustained over many steps → divergence likely.
  - `ρa` decreasing rapidly while `‖p‖` stays constant → `r` will increase; controller will clip, possibly slowing progress.
  - If instability occurs despite `r<1`, suspect activation singularities (e.g., ReLU pre‑activations near zero) or numerical errors in JVP; consider entire activations (Appendix 2) or analytic normalizations.
  - The controller may clip every step if learning rate is set too high; this signals the need to reduce base `η` or use a smaller target `r` (e.g., `r=0.5`).

- **First 3 experiments (validation):**
  1. **Single‑step r sweep:** After convergence, take one gradient step with scaled learning rates to vary `r`. Confirm that no architecture fails for `r<1` but collapse occurs for `r≥1` (Section 6.2, Figure 7).
  2. **Temperature scaling fingerprint:** Train with different temperatures `T`. Plot loss inflation vs raw `τ` and vs normalized `r_T = τ·Δa/(πT)`. The latter should collapse all curves, verifying `ρa(T)=πT/Δa` (Section 6.4, Figure 9).
  3. **Learning‑rate spike test:** Inject a 1000× spike after some steps. Compare plain Adam, gradient clipping (`‖g‖≤1`), and `ρa`‑controller. Only the controller should survive without collapse (Section 6.1, Figure 6, Table 1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Large-scale validation
- **Question:** Does the ρa convergence‑radius bound hold in large‑scale training (e.g., Pythia, OLMo) as observed in small models?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 8.8 states: “The most immediate next step is large‑scale validation on models such as Pythia and OLMo.”
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments used small architectures (≤128 dimensions); frontier models may exhibit different dynamics, scaling effects, or new failure modes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train large models while tracking r = τ/ρa across checkpoints, compare failure thresholds and stability patterns to small‑model results; test if r<1 remains a reliable safety predictor.

### Open Question 2: Integration with adaptive optimizers
- **Question:** Can the ρa‑controller be effectively combined with adaptive optimizers like Adam without destabilizing training?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 8.8 suggests: “We could also integrate our method with Adam…”
- **Why unresolved:** Controller was demonstrated with SGD and plain Adam; Adam’s momentum and per‑parameter scaling may interact unpredictably with the ρa‑based cap.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Modify Adam to enforce τ ≤ ρa after its update computation, evaluate on standard benchmarks (e.g., ResNet/CIFAR, transformers) comparing convergence, stability, and final accuracy against baseline Adam and the original controller.

### Open Question 3: Activation‑function singularities
- **Question:** Do activation‑function singularities (e.g., ReLU kinks, sigmoid poles) become the dominant constraint on the convergence radius in deep networks, superseding softmax ghosts?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations (Section 8.6) note: “activation singularities… not systematically tested.” Appendix 2 analyzes per‑neuron radii but leaves full treatment to future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments focused on output logit radius; hidden layers with non‑analytic activations may introduce tighter per‑step limits.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Measure layer‑wise ρj (from activation singularities) alongside ρa in deep models; identify which component (output, attention, FFN) binds most often during training with different activations (ReLU, GELU, etc.).

### Open Question 4: Radius‑friendly activations
- **Question:** Do the proposed radius‑friendly activations (RIA, GaussGLU) provide measurable stability gains in practice compared to standard ones?
- **Basis in paper:** Appendix 3 states: “These are suggestions from the theory, not claims of optimality. Whether this theoretical advantage yields measurable stability gains must be tested empirically.”
- **Why unresolved:** Designs are motivated by analyticity but lack empirical validation; trade‑offs with gradient flow, hardware efficiency, or final accuracy are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train networks with RIA/GaussGLU vs. ReLU/SwiGLU under aggressive learning rates, compare training stability (loss spikes, r thresholds), final accuracy, and computational overhead.

### Open Question 5: Multi‑step dynamics
- **Question:** How do multi‑step training dynamics interact with the single‑step convergence radius, especially when r>1 occurs intermittently?
- **Basis in paper:** Paper focuses on single‑step reliability (Section 8.2) and notes “multi‑step dynamics… are natural extensions” (Section 8), but does not analyze accumulation of high‑r steps.
- **Why unresolved:** The bound ensures one‑step Taylor convergence but does not guarantee global convergence; repeated or clustered violations may cause divergence even if individual steps seem recoverable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run long training with controlled r schedules (e.g., fixed r>1 for k steps, then r<1), monitor for delayed collapse or recovery; develop metrics to predict when single‑step hazards compound into training failure.

## Limitations
- The ρa bound assumes logit linearization; strong curvature may make it conservative or, in rare cases, insufficient.
- Handling of BatchNorm/LayerNorm parameters in the direction vector v is unclear; incorrect inclusion may affect Δa computation.
- Empirical validation is limited to six small‑to‑medium architectures; scaling to large models (LLMs) remains untested.

## Confidence
- Complex zeros cap the radius: **High** (rigorous derivation and binary case; plausible extension to networks)
- ρa = π/Δa bound: **Medium** (holds exactly for binary under linearization; empirical r threshold supports it but may be loose)
- Normalized step r predicts stability: **Medium** (clear phase transition in experiments; limited to image/MLP tasks; transformer/LLM validation missing)
