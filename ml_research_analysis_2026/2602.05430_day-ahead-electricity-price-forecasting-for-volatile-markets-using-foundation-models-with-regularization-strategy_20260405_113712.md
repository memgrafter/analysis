# Day-Ahead Electricity Price Forecasting for Volatile Markets Using Foundation Models with Regularization Strategy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05430
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05430
- **Reference Count:** 14
- **Primary Result:** Multivariate fine-tuned Tiny Time Mixers with a novel STL-KF regularization strategy achieve the lowest forecasting errors, improving MAPE by up to 37.4% over conventional methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of day-ahead electricity price forecasting in volatile markets, where extreme price spikes and nonlinear dynamics undermine traditional statistical and deep learning models. The authors evaluate a suite of time series foundation models (TSFMs) enhanced with a new three-stage spike regularization strategy (STL-KF). This strategy combines seasonal decomposition (STL), robust Kalman filtering, and adaptive thresholding to mitigate the distorting effects of outliers. Experiments on half-hourly Singapore market data, incorporating exogenous variables, demonstrate that TSFMs consistently outperform established baselines. The best-performing model is a multivariate fine-tuned Tiny Time Mixer (TTM), and the STL-KF regularization alone yields an average MAPE reduction of 28.69% across all tested models.

## Method Summary
The core method involves applying pretrained time series foundation models (TSFMs) to the forecasting task. These models are either used directly or fine-tuned on the specific electricity price dataset. The key innovation is a preprocessing regularization pipeline (STL-KF) applied to the target price series before model training and inference. First, STL decomposition separates the series into trend, seasonal, and residual components. The residual, which contains spikes and noise, is then processed by a robust Kalman filter to estimate a cleaner state. Finally, an adaptive thresholding mechanism identifies and dampens extreme values in the residual, reconstructing a regularized series that is recombined with the trend and seasonal components for modeling.

## Key Results
- TSFMs, particularly fine-tuned Tiny Time Mixers (TTMs), consistently achieve lower error metrics (e.g., MAE, MAPE, RMSE) compared to traditional statistical models (e.g., ARIMA) and standard deep learning architectures (e.g., LSTM, TCN).
- The proposed STL-KF regularization strategy provides a substantial average MAPE reduction of 28.69% across all evaluated models.
- The best multivariate fine-tuned TTM configuration improves MAPE by up to 37.4% relative to conventional baseline methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The STL-KF regularization addresses a fundamental challenge in volatile electricity markets: the presence of extreme, transient price spikes that are poorly captured by standard models and can dominate loss functions during training. STL decomposition cleanly isolates these irregular components in the residual. The robust Kalman filter then provides a statistically optimal, noise-resistant estimate of the underlying state of this residual series, effectively smoothing transient noise without assuming strict parametric forms. Adaptive thresholding subsequently identifies values that deviate excessively from this filtered state, treating them as spikes and attenuating their magnitude. This multi-stage process produces a target series where the most impactful outliers are suppressed, allowing TSFMs—which excel at learning complex nonlinear relationships from clean sequential data—to focus on the core price dynamics rather than being distorted by anomalies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs):** Large, pretrained architectures (e.g., TTMs, TimesNet) trained on vast, diverse time series corpora. *Why needed:* They capture universal temporal patterns and representations transferable to specific domains like energy prices, overcoming data scarcity and model capability limits of traditional approaches. *Quick check:* Benchmark a pretrained TSFM against a randomly initialized deep network of similar size on a proxy task.
- **STL Decomposition (Seasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess):** A non-parametric method that decomposes a series into seasonal, trend, and remainder components. *Why needed:* To explicitly separate recurring patterns (e.g., daily/weekly cycles) and the underlying trend from irregular spikes, which are then targeted for regularization. *Quick check:* Visually inspect or compute autocorrelation of the remainder component to confirm spike isolation.
- **Kalman Filtering:** A recursive, Bayesian state-space algorithm for estimating the state of a dynamic system from noisy observations. *Why needed:* To provide a robust, optimal (in MSE sense) estimate of the "true" residual series state, filtering high-frequency noise while tracking underlying movements. *Quick check:* Compare forecast accuracy using the filtered residual versus a simple moving average on the residual.
- **Adaptive Thresholding:** Dynamically setting a cut-off (e.g., via median absolute deviation) to identify outliers in the filtered residual. *Why needed:* To avoid static thresholds that are ineffective in non-stationary volatile markets; adaptivity ensures spike detection is context-sensitive. *Quick check:* Vary the threshold multiplier (e.g., 2.0 vs 3.0 MAD) and measure its impact on final forecast error.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Raw Price Series -> STL Decomposition -> (Trend, Seasonal, Residual) -> Robust Kalman Filter on Residual -> Adaptive Thresholding on Filtered Residual -> Reconstruct Regularized Series -> TSFM (Input: Regularized Series + Exogenous Variables) -> Forecast

**Critical Path:** The regularization pipeline (STL -> Kalman -> Thresholding) is a mandatory preprocessing stage before any TSFM ingestion. Model performance is highly sensitive to the quality of this regularized series.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- *Complexity vs. Robustness:* STL-KF adds significant computational and parameter-tuning overhead versus raw input but provides critical robustness to spikes.
- *Generality vs. Specificity:* The adaptive threshold is market-agnostic in design but requires careful calibration; poor calibration risks over-smoothing legitimate extreme events or leaving spikes unaddressed.
- *Model Scope:* Using a TSFM (large pretrained model) versus a custom deep model trades off transfer learning benefits for potential domain mismatch if the foundation corpus is very different from electricity markets.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Forecasts that are overly smooth and fail to capture legitimate high-price events (over-regularization).
- Persistent high errors during known spike periods even after regularization (threshold set too conservatively).
- Model performance degrades significantly when applied to a market with different volatility characteristics or spike patterns (lack of generalization).

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Ablation of STL-KF Components:** Train the best TSFM on series regularized by (a) only STL, (b) STL+Kalman, (c) full STL-KF, and (d) no regularization. Quantify the marginal contribution of each stage.
2. **Cross-Market Generalization Test:** Apply the trained Singapore model (with STL-KF) directly to a held-out volatile market (e.g., PJM or EPEX) without retraining to assess robustness to distributional shift.
3. **Threshold Sensitivity Sweep:** For the STL-KF pipeline, vary the adaptive threshold parameter (e.g., MAD multiplier from 1.5 to 4.0) and plot MAPE to identify optimal stability ranges and failure points.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is confined to a single half-hourly market (Singapore), limiting confidence in generalizability to other volatile electricity markets (e.g., European or North American) with different structural behaviors.
- Baseline model architectures, hyperparameter settings, and exact train-test split procedures are not fully detailed, hindering precise replication and comparison.
- No statistical significance tests (e.g., Diebold–Mariano test) are reported for the observed error differences between models.
- The sensitivity and optimal configuration of STL-KF's hyperparameters (e.g., Loess window, Kalman process/observation noise variances, threshold multiplier) are not thoroughly analyzed.

## Confidence
- **TSFMs outperform traditional baselines:** Medium (strong empirical result on one dataset but lacks multi-market validation and statistical testing).
- **Multivariate fine-tuned TTM is the best model:** Medium (reported metrics support this, but may be dataset-specific and dependent on STL-KF).
- **MAPE improvement "up to 37.4%":** Low (phrasing indicates a best-case scenario; average improvement and variance across test periods are not emphasized, making the typical benefit unclear).
- **STL-KF provides average MAPE reduction of 28.69%:** Medium (consistent improvement reported across models, but without statistical evidence or ablation to isolate its pure effect from model selection effects).
