# Orthogonal Learner for Estimating Heterogeneous Long-Term Treatment Effects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00915
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00915
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LT-O-learners reduce PEHE by up to 39.8% (synthetic) and 30.1% (medical) in low-overlap settings, with lower estimator variance.

## Executive Summary
Heterogeneous long-term treatment effect (HLTE) estimation faces high variance when treatment assignment or long-term outcome observation violates the overlap assumption. This paper introduces LT-O-learners, a family of meta-learners that retarget the learning objective using custom overlap weights to downweight samples with low overlap. The resulting loss is Neyman-orthogonal and equivalent to a weighted oracle loss, yielding theoretical guarantees of quasi-oracle convergence rates. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and semi‑synthetic medical benchmarks show that LT-O-learners—especially the LT-O-DO-learner, which addresses both assignment and observation overlap—significantly improve prediction accuracy, achieving up to 39.8% lower PEHE in synthetic data and 30.1% lower PEHE in medical data, while also reducing estimator variance.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to modify the standard orthogonal meta‑learner objective by weighting each sample according to its overlap propensity. Overlap weights are constructed from estimated propensity scores (for treatment assignment) and, when needed, from probabilities of observing the long‑term outcome. Two variants are proposed: LT‑O‑T‑learner, which handles treatment assignment overlap, and LT‑O‑DO‑learner, which jointly accounts for assignment and outcome observation overlap. Both methods utilize nuisance estimators (propensity and conditional outcome models) to build a Neyman‑orthogonal score. The final heterogeneous treatment effect function is obtained by minimizing the weighted orthogonal loss, which is shown to be equivalent to an oracle loss with known nuisance, enabling robust variance control.

## Key Results
- LT-O-learners achieve up to 39.8% lower PEHE than existing meta‑learners in synthetic low‑overlap settings.
- In semi‑synthetic medical data, the improvement reaches up to 30.1% lower PEHE.
- The estimators exhibit lower variance compared to baselines, particularly in challenging overlap regimes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The custom overlap weights explicitly downweight samples in regions with scarce treatment or outcome observations, which reduces the variance contribution of high‑variance nuisance estimates. The Neyman‑orthogonal construction ensures the loss is insensitive to first‑order perturbations in the nuisance models, providing double robustness. Equivalence to a weighted oracle loss means that, despite using estimated nuisances, the method behaves as if the true nuisances were known, up to higher‑order terms. This combination yields the theoretically guaranteed quasi‑oracle error rates and the observed empirical variance reductions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Propensity Score Estimator → Overlap Weight Calculator → Outcome Model(s) → Orthogonal Loss Constructor → HTE Predictor

**Critical path**: 1. Estimate treatment assignment propensities. 2. Compute overlap weights (and possibly outcome observation weights). 3. Train conditional outcome models for treated and control groups. 4. Construct the Neyman‑orthogonal weighted loss. 5. Solve for the heterogeneous treatment effect function by minimizing this loss.

**Design tradeoffs**: Overlap weighting reduces variance but can introduce bias if weights become extreme; truncation thresholds trade off bias–variance. Flexible machine‑learning models for nuisance estimation lower approximation bias but may increase variance. The DO‑learner adds complexity by modeling both assignment and observation overlap but captures two distinct positivity violations.

**Failure signatures**: (i) Extreme weight values from near‑zero propensities cause numerical instability and inflated variance. (ii) Poor outcome model fits lead to residual confounding despite orthogonalization. (iii) In extremely low overlap regimes, even orthogonal weighting may fail, yielding large bias due to aggressive truncation.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to synthetic and semi‑synthetic medical benchmarks; no real‑world longitudinal data with genuine long‑term outcomes.
- Overlap weight estimation may be unstable under extreme low overlap, potentially introducing bias.
- Theoretical guarantees depend on standard assumptions (nuisance consistency, boundedness); robustness to violations is unknown.

## Confidence
- Variance reduction via custom overlap weighting: Medium
- Neyman‑orthogonal loss equivalence: High
- Quasi‑oracle error rates: Medium
- LT‑O‑DO‑learner superiority: Low
- Cross‑domain applicability: Low
