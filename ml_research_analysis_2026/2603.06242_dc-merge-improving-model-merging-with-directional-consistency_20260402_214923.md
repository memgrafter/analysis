# DC-Merge: Improving Model Merging with Directional Consistency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06242
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06242
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DC-Merge achieves 73.90% normalized accuracy on ViT-B-32 LoRA 8-task benchmark, outperforming Iso-CTS by 1.19%, and 93.55% on full fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
Model merging combines multiple fine-tuned models into a single multi-task model but often suffers performance degradation due to directional inconsistency and imbalanced energy distribution in task vectors. DC-Merge introduces two key interventions: (1) smoothing singular values to balance energy across knowledge components, and (2) projecting task vectors onto a shared orthogonal cover space to align their directional geometries before merging. Evaluations on ViT-B-32 with LoRA and full fine-tuning, as well as LLaVA-v1.5-7B on MM-MergeBench, show consistent improvements over prior state-of-the-art methods like Iso-CTS and RobustMerge, with gains of 1–2% across seen and unseen tasks.

## Method Summary
DC-Merge operates on task vectors—differences between pre-trained and fine-tuned models. First, it smooths the singular values of each task vector’s matrix representation to redistribute energy more evenly, preventing any single component from dominating. Second, it constructs a shared orthogonal cover space from the concatenated task vectors via SVD, then projects each task vector onto this space. This projection aligns directional geometries, mitigating destructive interference from conflicting directions. The projected vectors are then averaged to produce the merged model. The method is agnostic to merging algorithm (e.g., task arithmetic, TIES) and applies to both LoRA and full fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- **ViT-B-32 LoRA:** 73.90% normalized accuracy on 8 tasks (+1.19% over Iso-CTS).
- **ViT-B-32 full fine-tuning:** 93.55% normalized accuracy (+1.77% over Iso-CTS).
- **LLaVA-v1.5-7B on MM-MergeBench:** 59.63% on seen tasks (+2.30% over RobustMerge) and 39.84% on unseen tasks (+1.85%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Model merging fails because task vectors often point in conflicting directions (directional inconsistency) and have imbalanced energy distribution—a few large singular values dominate, obscuring finer-grained knowledge. DC-Merge’s energy smoothing reduces the disparity in singular values, ensuring all knowledge components contribute more uniformly. The orthogonal cover space projection aligns all task vectors onto a common, mutually unbiased basis, minimizing pairwise directional conflicts. Together, these steps reduce destructive interference during averaging, preserving more of each task’s performance in the merged model.

## Foundational Learning
- **Model merging:** Combines fine-tuned models into one multi-task model via task vector arithmetic; needed for efficiency but prone to interference.
  - *Quick check:* What happens when you simply average task vectors from conflicting tasks?
- **Task vectors:** Parameter differences (fine-tuned minus pre-trained); represent learned task knowledge.
  - *Quick check:* How are task vectors extracted from a fine-tuned model?
- **Singular value decomposition (SVD):** Factorizes a matrix into singular vectors/values; reveals energy distribution.
  - *Quick check:* What does a large singular value indicate about a matrix component?
- **Directional inconsistency:** Task vectors pointing in opposing directions cause cancellation when averaged.
  - *Quick check:* Why might two task vectors for similar tasks still conflict directionally?
- **Orthogonal cover space:** A shared subspace where task vectors are projected to align directions.
  - *Quick check:* How does projection onto an orthogonal basis reduce conflict?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Fine-tuned models → Extract task vectors → Smooth singular values → Construct orthogonal cover space (via SVD on concatenated vectors) → Project task vectors → Average → Merged model

**Critical path:** The core sequence is: (1) obtain task vectors, (2) apply energy smoothing per vector, (3) build shared cover space from all smoothed vectors, (4) project each vector, (5) merge by averaging.

**Design tradeoffs:** Energy smoothing risks discarding high-energy but task-specific information; orthogonalization may force compromise directions that lose nuance. The method trades some task-specific fidelity for overall interference reduction.

**Failure signatures:** Performance drop on tasks with highly divergent objectives; sensitivity to smoothing parameter may cause under- or over-smoothing; overhead from SVD on large models (e.g., billion-parameter LLMs) could be prohibitive.

**First three experiments to onboard:**
1. Baseline comparison: naive task arithmetic vs. DC-Merge on a 3-task subset to observe interference patterns.
2. Ablation: run DC-Merge with energy smoothing disabled, then with cover space projection disabled, to isolate each component’s contribution.
3. Sensitivity check: vary the smoothing strength (e.g., singular value compression ratio) and measure impact on merged accuracy and per-task performance.

## Limitations
- Benchmarks limited to ViT-B-32 (LoRA/full) on 8 tasks and LLaVA on MM-MergeBench; generalization to other architectures and larger task sets unknown.
