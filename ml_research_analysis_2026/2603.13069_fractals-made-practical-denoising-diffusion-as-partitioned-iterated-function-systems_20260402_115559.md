# Fractals made Practical: Denoising Diffusion as Partitioned Iterated Function Systems
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.13069
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13069
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** The deterministic DDIM reverse diffusion process is mathematically equivalent to a Partitioned Iterated Function System (PIFS), with three geometric quantities (per-step contraction threshold L*_t, diagonal expansion function f_t(λ), global expansion threshold λ**) fully characterizing denoising behavior and explaining empirical design choices.

## Executive Summary
This paper bridges diffusion models and fractal geometry by proving that the deterministic DDIM reverse chain constitutes a Partitioned Iterated Function System. This equivalence yields three model-agnostic geometric quantities that govern denoising dynamics without requiring model evaluation. The framework explains the observed two-regime behavior: early high-noise steps use diffuse attention for global context, while later low-noise steps release patch-wise suppression to generate fine details. Self-attention emerges as the natural PIFS primitive, training is interpreted as minimizing collage error via an L2‑Wasserstein bridge, and the attractor dimension is computed via a discrete Moran equation. Three derived design criteria recover four common empirical choices (cosine schedule offset, resolution shift, Min‑SNR weighting, Align Your Steps). Comprehensive experiments on CIFAR‑10 and CelebA‑HQ validate all core predictions.

## Method Summary
The empirical validation uses pretrained diffusion models: `google/ddpm-cifar10-32` (32×32, 8×8 patches, M=16, n_k=192) and `google/ddpm-celebahq-256` (64×64 output, 16×16 patches, M=16, n_k=768). Patch covariance matrices Σ_k are estimated from 50,000 CIFAR‑10 training images or 200 CelebA‑HQ images. The deterministic DDIM reverse chain (50‑step subsample for CIFAR‑10, 49‑step for CelebA‑HQ) is used. Jacobians of the update function are measured via power iteration (KPI=20–30, N_seeds=5–8) along random trajectories. Directional suppression S_k,t is computed using directional derivatives along the leading eigenvector v^(1)_k of Σ_k. The global expansion threshold λ** is obtained by solving the Moran equation Q_t f_t(λ**)=1 for the full 1000‑step schedule and the subsampled steps. Additional metrics include attention entropy, off‑diagonal coupling, and scaling of score deviation with √ᾱ_t. No retraining is performed; all analysis relies on existing checkpoints and training‑set statistics.

## Key Results
- **P1–P5 predictions validated:** All patches exceed contraction threshold L*_t and have positive directional suppression (P1); S_k,t improves with λ_k (P2); global spectral norm κ̃_t grows across regimes (P3); per‑patch expansion margin crosses zero with stratified release and negative Spearman correlation with λ_k (P4); score deviation scales linearly with √ᾱ_t at high noise (P5).
- **Two‑regime behavior confirmed:** Diffuse attention dominates early steps (high t), while patch‑wise suppression release governs later steps (low t).
- **Self‑attention as PIFS primitive:** The query‑key mechanism implements soft partitioning, and the value transformation provides the affine map per patch.
- **Design criteria recovery:** The three theoretical criteria recover cosine schedule offset, resolution shift, Min‑SNR weighting, and Align Your Steps.
- **Attractor dimension:** Computed via discrete Moran equation, matches effective dimensionality of generated images.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The deterministic DDIM update x_{t-1} = A_t x_t + b_t yields an affine transformation whose Jacobian J_t acts as a linear contraction/expansion operator. Partitioning arises from self‑attention, which splits the latent space into patches and applies distinct transformations. The three geometric quantities fully describe the dynamics:

- **L*_t** (per‑step contraction threshold) determines if the step is globally contracting (L*_t < 1) or expanding (L*_t > 1).
- **f_t(λ)** (diagonal expansion function) describes eigenvalue‑dependent amplification: for a patch with covariance eigenvalue λ_k, the effective expansion is f_t(λ_k).
- **λ**** (global expansion threshold) separates expanded (λ_k > λ**) from contracted (λ_k < λ**) patches via the Moran equation Q_t log f_t(λ**) = 0, linking to the attractor dimension.

Training minimizes collage error (discrepancy between target and IFS attractor) in an L2‑Wasserstein sense. The two‑regime behavior stems from the interplay: high‑noise steps have small L*_t and nearly flat f_t(λ), so all patches contract uniformly and attention is diffuse to mix global context. Low‑noise steps have L*_t > 1 and λ‑dependent f_t(λ); patches with λ_k > λ** are released from suppression, generating fine details. The crossover aligns with λ**.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partitioned Iterated Function System (PIFS):** Fractal construction where an image is split into regions and each is transformed by a contractive mapping. *Quick check:* After many iterations, the union of transformed patches reconstructs the whole image.
- **Contraction Threshold L*_t:** Scalar derived from the noise schedule; indicates contractivity. *Quick check:* Compute L*_t from ᾱ_t; values <1 imply contraction.
- **Diagonal Expansion Function f_t(λ):** Eigenvalue‑dependent factor describing patch amplification. *Quick check:* For a Gaussian patch with covariance λ, compute f_t(λ) analytically (Eq. 26) and compare to empirical per‑patch expansion.
- **Global Expansion Threshold λ****: Eigenvalue separating expanded vs. contracted patches via Moran equation. *Quick check:* Solve Q_t log f_t(λ)=0; verify many patches have λ_k > λ**.
- **Collage Error:** Discrepancy between target image and union of transformed patches; training implicitly minimizes it in an L2‑Wasserstein sense. *Quick check:* Compute collage error for a trained model; it should decrease with training.
- **Discrete Moran Equation:** Computes attractor dimension of the generated distribution. *Quick check:* Estimate effective dimensionality of generated samples and compare to Moran prediction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Noise schedule (ᾱ_t) -> Denoising network (UNet/Transformer with self‑attention) -> Deterministic DDIM update (x_{t-1}= (x_t - (1-ᾱ_t)ε̂_θ(x_t,t))/√ᾱ_t + σ_t z) -> Jacobian of update -> PIFS quantities (L*_t, f_t(λ), λ**) -> Two‑regime behavior.

**Critical path:**  
1. Load pretrained model and define patch partitions (e.g., 8×8 non‑overlapping).  
2. Estimate patch covariances Σ_k from training data to obtain eigenvalues λ_k and eigenvectors v^(1)_k.  
3. Along DDIM trajectories, compute the update function f_t and its Jacobian via auto‑differentiation.  
4. Use power iteration to estimate global spectral norm κ̃_t and per‑patch block norms κ̃_diag,k,t.  
5. Compute directional suppression S_k,t by projecting the Jacobian onto v^(1)_k.  
6. Derive corrected expansion κ̃_diag,k,t = f_t(λ_k) - S_k,t and margin (1-κ̃_diag,k,t) - C_k,t.  
7. Solve Moran equation for λ**.  
8. Validate predictions (P1–P5) by comparing measured quantities to theoretical thresholds.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Patch size: smaller patches increase M (partitions) and analysis granularity but raise Jacobian estimation cost.  
- Power iteration budget (KPI): higher KPI improves spectral accuracy but is slower, especially at high‑t where κ̃_t≈1 and convergence is poor.  
- Trajectory count (N_traj, N_seeds): more trajectories reduce variance in Λ_eff,k estimates but require more forward passes.  
- Attention resolution: Models may apply attention at multiple resolutions; analysis typically focuses on the finest resolution to isolate patch‑wise effects.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High variance in κ̃_t estimates across seeds for t>950 (near‑contractivity regime).  
- Discrepancy between measured crossover and predicted λ** due to schedule mismatch (e.g., cosine offset s_off).  
- Persistent non‑zero off‑diagonal coupling δ_cross,t in low‑noise steps if encoder skip connections are ignored.  
- Insufficient N_traj leading to ambiguous sign of Λ_eff,k (bootstrap confidence intervals overlapping zero).

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce Table 1:** Compute mean L*_t and coefficient of variation for the DDIM subsampled schedule on CIFAR‑10 to verify schedule correctness.  
2. **Validate P1–P3:** For selected timesteps, measure κ̃_t, κ̃_diag,k,t, and C_k,t; confirm all patches have κ̃_diag,k,t > 1 and small cross‑coupling in Regime I.  
3. **Test P4 (crossover):** Compute per‑patch margin (1-κ̃_diag,k,t) - C_k,t across timesteps; verify it crosses zero near λ** and that Spearman correlation between λ_k and S_k,t is negative.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions beyond its contributions. Potential directions for future work include extending the PIFS framework to stochastic samplers (beyond deterministic DDIM), adapting it to continuous‑time diffusion, and exploring implications for architecture design beyond self‑attention.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes a block‑diagonal Jacobian structure (patches independent), which may be violated by skip connections in UNet architectures.
- Exact mapping between the training schedule and DDIM subsampling is critical; small mismatches in timestep selection or cosine offset can invalidate theoretical thresholds.
- Estimating Λ_eff,k on high‑resolution data (CelebA‑HQ) requires many trajectories; the reported results may be underpowered.
