# Enhancing Foundation VLM Robustness to Missing Modality: Scalable Diffusion for Bi-directional Feature Restoration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03151
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03151
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: State-of-the-art zero-shot robustness on four datasets with up to 7.07% F1-Macro improvement on MM-IMDb and 8.16% Accuracy on MMHS11K.

## Executive Summary
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer severe performance drops when one modality is missing. This work proposes a diffusion-based feature restoration module trained as a mid-stage plug-in within the VLM’s latent space. Two innovations—Dynamic Modality Gating to filter irrelevant noise from conditional features, and Cross-Modal Mutual Learning to enforce bidirectional semantic alignment—enable robust restoration. Evaluated on four datasets with up to 70% missing rate, the method achieves significant gains (e.g., +7.07% F1-Macro on MM-IMDb) over strong baselines, maintains robustness across missing rates from 10% to 90%, and scales with training data and model depth, substantially improving VLM reliability under incomplete inputs without sacrificing generalization.

## Method Summary
The approach introduces a diffusion-based feature restoration module that operates as a mid-stage plug-in between a frozen VLM encoder (CLIP ViT-B/32) and downstream task decoders. Given one modality’s CLIP features, a Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DDIM) implemented via a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) learns to generate the missing modality’s features. Two innovations are key: (1) Dynamic Modality Gating, which adaptively filters irrelevant channels from conditional features within each DiT block, and (2) Cross-Modal Mutual Learning, which enforces bidirectional consistency between image-to-text and text-to-image generation paths during late denoising steps. Training on CC3M (or COCO) proceeds in two phases: first optimizing a noise prediction loss `L_base`, then adding a mutual loss `L_mutual` for timesteps `t < τ` (`τ=50`). After 40 epochs, the diffusion module is frozen. For each downstream task, a lightweight decoder (two self-attention layers and one cross-attention layer) is trained on concatenated available and restored features. At inference, the frozen diffusion model restores the missing feature via 50 DDIM steps, after which the decoder produces the final prediction. This mid-stage design preserves the original VLM’s weights while endowing it with robustness to missing modalities.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art zero-shot performance on four datasets with high missing rates (70%), improving F1-Macro by up to 7.07% on MM-IMDb and Accuracy by up to 8.16% on MMHS11K.
- Robust degradation across missing rates from 10% to 90%, significantly mitigating the sharp drops observed in prior methods.
- Scalability demonstrated: performance improves with larger training data and greater DiT depth (up to 20 layers).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Diffusion-Based Feature Restoration in Latent Space
- **Claim**: A diffusion model trained as a mid-stage plug-in can restore missing VLM features with higher semantic fidelity than deterministic imputation, by modeling the complex manifold of the VLM's latent space.
- **Mechanism**: The model uses a Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model (DDIM) process. Given available modality features `z_m1` as condition, it starts from Gaussian noise `z_T` and iteratively denoises via Equation (1) to produce the missing feature `hat{z}_m2`. This generative prior captures the data manifold, allowing restoration of semantically coherent features rather than simple padding or heuristic guesses.
- **Core assumption**: The latent space of a foundation VLM (e.g., CLIP) possesses a structured, low-dimensional manifold that diffusion models can learn to traverse reversibly.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "the diffusion model exploits its robust generative priors to capture the intricate manifold geometry inherent in the VLM’s semantic space"
  - [section 3.1] "Through step-by-step denoising, the model can completely restore missing VLM features"
  - [section 4.7] T-SNE visualizations (Figure 6) show restored features form tighter, more discriminative clusters than original or imputed features.
  - [corpus] Weak indirect support: The neighbor paper "ADMC: Attention-based Diffusion Model for Missing Modalities Feature Completion" uses a similar diffusion approach for other modalities, suggesting a broader trend, but does not validate this specific VLM application.
- **Break condition**: If the VLM's latent manifold is highly non-Gaussian or discontinuous, the diffusion reverse process may fail to converge to semantically valid points, producing irrelevant features instead of restored ones.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic Modality Gating for Noise Suppression
- **Claim**: A learnable gating mechanism inserted into DiT blocks adaptively filters modality-irrelevant noise from the conditional features, enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio for the diffusion process.
- **Mechanism**: The gating unit (Equations 2-6) takes fused condition `C` (conditional features + timestep embedding), pools it into a global vector `G_pooled`, then produces channel-wise modulation vectors `Z_attn` and `Z_mlp` via MLP and sigmoid. These vectors element-wise scale the outputs of the self-attention and MLP sub-layers, acting as dynamic feature selectors that suppress irrelevant channels and amplify critical semantic pathways.
- **Core assumption**: Irrelevant background information and noise in conditional features can be identified and filtered at the channel level through a learned, timestep-aware gating function.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2] "This learnable gating mechanism acts as a secondary filter, enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio of critical features. By suppressing irrelevant information flow, it effectively preserves the most salient cross-modal representations."
  - [section 4.4] Figure 4 visualizes sparsity in gate values, with most entries near zero and only essential features magnified, confirming the filtering behavior.
  - [section 4.4] Ablation in Table 2 shows gating ("Gating") outperforms "AdaLN" and "Concat" baselines, demonstrating its specific contribution over generic conditioning.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; the concept relates to attention mechanisms but the specific gating design is novel to this work.
- **Break condition**: If the learned gating becomes overly sparse (pruning too many channels) or fails to activate on relevant features, it may remove essential semantic information, degrading restoration quality.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Modal Mutual Learning for Bidirectional Alignment
- **Claim**: A mutual learning mechanism that enforces bidirectional consistency between I2T and T2I diffusion models during late-stage denoising prevents semantic drift and ensures restored features lie within the shared VLM semantic space.
- **Mechanism**: For timesteps `t < τ` (τ=50), the model computes an estimate `hat{x}^0` from the noisy `x_t`. The mutual loss `L_mutual` (Equation 9) then requires that `hat{x}^0` from one modality can serve as effective condition to predict noise in the reverse direction for the other modality (I2T consistency loss + T2I consistency loss). This cyclic consistency forces both diffusion models to produce features that are mutually intelligible.
- **Core assumption**: In the VLM's joint space, the mapping from image to text features and text to image features are approximately invertible; forcing `hat{I} -> T` and `hat{T} -> I` cycles aligns them to the true manifold.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3] "This explicit alignment mechanism effectively solves the problem of cross-modal semantic misalignment in uni-directional diffusion."
  - [section 4.4] Table 2 ablation shows removing mutual learning ("Without Mutual Learning") degrades performance across all missing scenarios compared to the full model ("With Mutual Learning").
  - [section 4.7] Figure 7 shows restored features have clearer diagonal structure (lower inter-class similarity) than original CLIP features, suggesting enhanced discriminativity from alignment.
  - [corpus] The "Retrieval-augmented Methods" (RAGPT, REDEEM) in Related Work also aim for cross-modal consistency but via retrieval, not generative cycles; this work's mechanism is distinct.
- **Break condition**: If `τ` is set incorrectly (too high or too low, per Appendix A.2) or if the two diffusion models' capacities mismatch, the mutual loss may dominate and cause an identity mapping collapse (as noted: "if `L_mutual` is used as the sole loss, the model tends to learn an identity mapping") or fail to correct drift.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Models in Latent Spaces**  
  **Why needed here**: The method relies on diffusion's ability to model complex, high-dimensional manifolds (the VLM's semantic space) rather than simple Euclidean interpolation. Understanding the forward/noising and reverse/denoising process (DDIM) is essential to grasp how missing features are generated from noise conditioned on available features.  
  **Quick check question**: How does the denoising step in Equation (1) use the conditional feature `z_m1` to guide the trajectory from `z_t` to `z_{t-1}`?

- **Concept: Contrastively Aligned Multimodal Embeddings (CLIP-style)**  
  **Why needed here**: The entire restoration operates within the latent space of a frozen pretrained VLM (CLIP). Understanding that this space is jointly aligned (similar concepts map to nearby vectors regardless of modality) is why restoring a missing modality feature to *this* space, rather than a new one, preserves downstream task compatibility.  
  **Quick check question**: Why is it critical that the diffusion model is trained on features from the *same* frozen VLM encoder that will be used at inference?

- **Concept: Mid-Stage Training vs. Fine-Tuning**  
  **Why needed here**: The paper introduces "restorative mid-stage training" as a phase between original VLM pretraining and downstream task training. This concept is key to understanding how the diffusion module gains general restoration capabilities *without* altering the base VLM's weights or hurting its generalization.  
  **Quick check question**: What is the difference between training the diffusion module on a large general dataset (CC3M) versus directly fine-tuning the entire VLM on each downstream missing-modality dataset?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Frozen VLM Encoders -> DiT-Based Diffusion Backbone (with Dynamic Modality Gating Unit and Mutual Learning during training) -> Lightweight Task Decoder. (During training, the Mutual Learning Controller coordinates bidirectional alignment between I2T and T2I diffusion models.)
- **Critical path (inference)**: Input (single modality) -> Frozen Encoder -> z_available -> DDIM Init (z_T ~ N(0,I)) -> For t=T...1: (Condition: z_available + t_emb -> Gating -> DiT Block -> predict ϵ -> DDIM Step -> z_{t-1}) -> hat{z}_missing = z_0 -> Concat(z_available, hat{z}_missing) -> Decoder -> Prediction
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Latency vs. Robustness**: DDIM sampling (50 steps) adds significant inference overhead (~700ms vs. <30ms for baselines, per Table 6) but provides state-of-the-art robustness at high missing rates. Tradeoff is acceptable for offline/robustness-critical applications.
  - **Gating Sparsity vs. Information Loss**: The gating mechanism becomes sparse (mean gate ~0.1), filtering noise but risks dropping subtle features. Initialization bias (`-5.0`) and training stabilize this.
  - Mutual Learning Timestep `τ`: Lower `τ` means fewer steps with alignment loss (efficient but weaker constraint); higher `τ` risks noisy `hat{x}^0` (ineffective constraint). Paper finds `τ=50` optimal (Appendix A.2).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - At high missing rates (>70%): Performance degrades linearly but less than baselines (Figure 5). Failure manifest as restored features that do not cluster well by class in t-SNE (Figure 6).
  - If gating mis-calibrates: Visualizations (Figure 4) would show non-sparse or uniformly active gates, or performance drop in ablation "Gating" vs. "Base".
  - If mutual learning missing: Ablation shows 1-2% absolute drop across metrics (Table 2), especially on "Both" missing.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablation of Core Mechanisms: Run with full model, w/o Gating, w/o Mutual Learning, w/o both (just base diffusion). Verify Table 2 metrics on MMHS11K/N24News. This isolates contributions.
  2. Scalability Test: Train DiT modules with varying depths (16,18,20,22,24 layers) on CC3M, evaluate on downstream tasks (Table 4). Confirms optimal depth ~20.
  3. Robustness Curve: Vary missing rate (10%,30%,50%,70%,90%) for Image/Text/Both missing on MM-IMDb, plot F1-M for our method vs. MAP, MDP, DCP, SyP (Figure 5). Demonstrates degradation slope.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does the diffusion-based restoration method generalize across diverse foundational VLM architectures?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The experimental evaluation is restricted to CLIP ViT-B/32; other VLMs are not investigated.
- **Why unresolved**: Different VLMs have distinct pretraining objectives and latent space structures; the effectiveness of the gating and mutual learning mechanisms may vary.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comprehensive studies using multiple VLMs (e.g., BLIP, SigLIP, Flamingo) on standard missing-modality benchmarks with zero-shot evaluation.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Is a fixed mutual learning threshold τ optimal across varying missing rates and datasets?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors manually set τ=50 after limited sensitivity analysis but do not explore dynamic adaptation.
- **Why unresolved**: The denoising dynamics likely depend on missing modality type and rate; a single τ may not be globally optimal.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Large-scale experiments varying τ as a function of missing rate and dataset, possibly learning τ automatically to maximize performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the proposed framework be extended to scenarios with more than two modalities, where any subset may be missing?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states "Without loss of generality, we study the input case involving M= 2 modalities."
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world multimodal systems often involve three or more modalities; missing patterns become combinatorial and the restoration process more complex.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extending the diffusion restoration to handle N modalities, then evaluating on a dataset with at least three modalities (e.g., audio-visual-text) under various missing combinations.

## Limitations
- Unspecified DiT hyperparameters and decoder architecture hinder full reproducibility.
- High inference latency (~700 ms with 50 DDIM steps) compared to deterministic baselines (<30 ms) limits real-time deployment.
- Gating sparsity designed to filter noise may inadvertently prune subtle but salient semantic features, and mutual learning is sensitive to the threshold τ and loss weighting, with a risk of identity mapping collapse.
