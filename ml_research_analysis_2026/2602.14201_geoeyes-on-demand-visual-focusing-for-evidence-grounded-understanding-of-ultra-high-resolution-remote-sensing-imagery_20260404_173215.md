# GeoEyes: On-Demand Visual Focusing for Evidence-Grounded Understanding of Ultra-High-Resolution Remote Sensing Imagery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14201
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14201
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** GeoEyes (7B) achieves 54.23% accuracy on XLRS-Bench, surpassing larger models like Qwen3-VL-235B (51.1%) via adaptive, evidence-driven zooming.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles "Tool Usage Homogenization" in ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing visual question answering, where existing zoom-enabled multimodal models uniformly overuse zoom tools regardless of task necessity. GeoEyes introduces a two-stage training framework: first, a cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a curated UHR-CoZ dataset that demonstrates when and how to zoom across no-tool, single-call, and multi-round scenarios; second, an AdaZoom-GRPO reinforcement learning stage that optimizes for evidence gain, adaptive efficiency, and process verification to encourage task-adaptive zoom decisions with appropriate stopping. This yields state-of-the-art accuracy on XLRS-Bench with a modest 7B backbone, particularly excelling at fine-grained perception tasks.

## Method Summary
GeoEyes employs a two-phase training regimen. Phase one is cold-start SFT using the proprietary UHR-CoZ dataset, which contains demonstrations of interleaved tool-use strategies—ranging from no zoom to progressive multi-round zooming—paired with questions and answers. This teaches the model the basic mechanics and regimes of zooming. Phase two applies AdaZoom-GRPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm with a tailored reward function. The reward comprises three terms: (1) evidence gain (accuracy improvement from zooming), (2) adaptive efficiency (penalizing unnecessary zoom steps), and (3) process verification (ensuring intermediate zoom actions are justified by current evidence). This RL stage fine-tunes the model to dynamically decide whether to zoom, how many times, and when to stop, based on the evolving evidence needs of the task.

## Key Results
- GeoEyes (7B) achieves 54.23% accuracy on XLRS-Bench, exceeding DeepEyes (50.0%) and Qwen3-VL-235B (51.1%).
- Strongest gains on fine-grained perception: 66.1% on Object Classification and 59.5% on Overall Counting subtasks.
- Demonstrates efficient tool usage, avoiding the homogenized over-zooming behavior seen in baseline zoom-enabled MLLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism is the reward-guided decoupling of zoom initiation, continuation, and stopping decisions. Evidence gain rewards only zoom steps that yield measurable accuracy improvements, preventing gratuitous tool calls. Adaptive efficiency penalizes each additional zoom step, encouraging early stopping once sufficient evidence is gathered. Process verification acts as a per-step rationality check, ensuring the model's internal state justifies the next zoom. Together, these forces shape a policy that matches zoom intensity to task demand—using no tools for global tasks, a single zoom for medium-grained queries, and multi-round zooming only for highly localized, fine-grained analysis—thus solving the homogenization problem.

- **Visual Question Answering (VQA) in Remote Sensing:** Domain-specific VQA requires interpreting complex geospatial contexts and fine details. *Why needed: Highlights the gap between natural and RS-VQA.* *Quick check: Does the model understand remote sensing-specific entities like "airfield" or "tank"?*
- **Tool Usage Homogenization:** The observed bug where zoom-capable models always invoke tools, regardless of query type. *Why needed: Defines the core problem GeoEyes solves.* *Quick check: Do baseline models call zoom on simple "count total buildings" queries?*
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Multimodal Policies:** Demonstrations provide a cold-start distribution over tool-use strategies. *Why needed: Prevents RL from exploring inefficient policies from scratch.* *Quick check: Does SFT alone already reduce over-zooming?*
- **Reinforcement Learning for Decision Stopping:** RL optimizes cumulative reward across a sequence of zoom/stop actions. *Why needed: SFT cannot teach optimal stopping via static examples.* *Quick check: Does the policy learn to stop after the first zoom when evidence is sufficient?*
- **Reward Shaping for Evidence Efficiency:** Decomposing reward into gain, efficiency, and verification terms. *Why needed: A single accuracy reward at the end provides no credit assignment for intermediate zoom steps.* *Quick check: Does removing the efficiency penalty increase average zoom steps?*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
UHR-CoZ Dataset -> Cold-start SFT -> Initial Zoom-Capable Policy -> AdaZoom-GRPO RL (Reward: Evidence Gain + Adaptive Efficiency + Process Verification) -> GeoEyes Model

**Critical Path:**  
1. **Dataset Construction:** Curate UHR-CoZ with interleaved no-tool/single-call/multi-round demonstrations.  
2. **Cold-start SFT:** Train base MLLM (e.g., Qwen-Audio) on UHR-CoZ to acquire basic zoom mechanics.  
3. **RL Fine-tuning:** Use AdaZoom-GRPO to optimize the policy via the multi-component reward, requiring rollouts with simulated or real zoom environments.  
4. **Evaluation:** Test on XLRS-Bench to measure accuracy and zoom behavior (frequency, stopping points).

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Dataset Dependency vs. Generalization:* UHR-CoZ is proprietary and narrow, likely limiting transfer to non-zoom RS tasks.  
- *RL Complexity vs. Stability:* Two-stage training (SFT+RL) increases compute cost and risks instability or reward hacking.  
- *Granular Rewards vs. Overfitting:* Highly specific reward terms may overfit to XLRS-Bench's particular evidence-gain dynamics.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- **Over-zooming:** Policy calls zoom excessively even on global queries (efficiency penalty failed).  
- **Under-zooming:** Fails to zoom on fine-grained tasks, leading to low accuracy (evidence gain signal weak).  
- **Ignored Stopping:** Continuous zoom loops without termination (process verification ineffective).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Ablate cold-start SFT:** Train AdaZoom-GRPO from a randomly initialized or natural-image-pretrained model to quantify SFT's necessity.  
2. **Zero-shot transfer to non-zoom VQA:** Evaluate GeoEyes on standard RS-VQA questions requiring no zoom to test if zoom skills harm general reasoning.  
3. **Reward term sensitivity:** Systematically remove each reward component (evidence gain, efficiency, verification) and measure changes in zoom frequency, stopping accuracy, and final task performance.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Accessibility:** Heavy reliance on the proprietary UHR-CoZ dataset hinders reproducibility and broader application.
- **Task Scope Narrowness:** Evaluation focuses exclusively on zoom-centric VQA, ignoring other critical remote sensing challenges like segmentation or change detection.
- **Benchmark Specificity:** Performance gains are demonstrated solely on XLRS-Bench, which may contain biases favoring the adaptive zoom paradigm.
- **Training Barrier:** The two-stage SFT+RL pipeline raises significant computational costs and may not be feasible for research groups with limited resources.

## Confidence
- Tool usage homogenization problem: **High** (observed baseline behavior, core motivation)
- UHR-CoZ dataset creation & training efficacy: **High** (paper's central contribution, supported by ablations)
- Overall accuracy (54.23% on XLRS-Bench): **Medium** (single benchmark, no external test sets cited)
- Fine-grained perception gains (Object Classification/Counting): **Medium-High** (consistent improvements on specific sub-tasks)
- 7B model efficiency vs. larger models: **Medium** (comparative but limited model zoo)
