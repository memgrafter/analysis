# Uncertainty Quantification for Distribution-to-Distribution Flow Matching in Scientific Imaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21717
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Bayesian Stochastic Flow Matching (BSFM) improves generalization and OOD detection for scientific imaging through stochastic flow matching and Bayesian MC dropout with antithetic sampling.

## Executive Summary
Trustworthy generative modeling for scientific imaging requires both reliability (generalization to unseen distribution shifts) and accountability (detecting out-of-distribution inputs). This paper introduces Bayesian Stochastic Flow Matching (BSFM), which integrates Stochastic Flow Matching (SFM) to capture aleatoric uncertainty and Monte Carlo Dropout with antithetic sampling (MCD‑Antithetic) for scalable epistemic uncertainty estimation. Experiments on cell imaging (BBBC021, JUMP) and fMRI datasets demonstrate that SFM significantly reduces FID/KID under various shifts, while MCD‑Antithetic achieves superior OOD detection performance by disentangling aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty.

## Method Summary
BSFM consists of two main components. First, Stochastic Flow Matching extends deterministic flow matching by augmenting the ODE with a diffusion term and a learned score correction; this ensures that the stochastic process preserves the marginal distributions of the deterministic flow while modeling inherent variability (aleatoric uncertainty). Second, MCD‑Antithetic approximates the Bayesian posterior over the network parameters via MC Dropout. For each of *M* posterior samples, the SFM SDE is solved using *J* antithetic noise pairs, providing per-sample estimates of mean and covariance. Aleatoric uncertainty is the average covariance across posterior samples, while epistemic uncertainty is the covariance of the posterior means. These estimates yield an anomaly score (negative trace) for OOD detection. The method is trained by jointly optimizing a flow matching loss and a score matching loss on interpolated paths.

## Key Results
- SFM reduces FID/KID on unseen scenarios: e.g., BBBC021 unseen perturbations: SFM FID 33.29 vs CellFlux 103.73.
- MCD‑Antithetic outperforms baselines in OOD detection: e.g., BBBC021 unseen perturbations epistemic AUROC 0.807 vs TransDist 0.621; JUMP intensity shift epistemic AUROC 0.906.
- Antithetic sampling improves uncertainty disentanglement and sample efficiency compared to independent MC sampling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Stochastic Flow Matching (SFM) improves generalization to unseen scenarios by capturing aleatoric uncertainty through a diffusion term that preserves the learned marginal distributions.
- **Mechanism:** Extends deterministic flow matching by augmenting the ODE with a diffusion term and a learned score correction. The score term, derived via the Fokker‑Planck equation, compensates for drift introduced by diffusion, ensuring the SDE marginals match those of the deterministic flow at all times.
- **Core assumption:** The conditional distribution \(p(x_1|x_0,c)\) contains inherent variability that can be modeled via a diffusion process; the score network can accurately approximate the true score.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "Stochastic Flow Matching (SFM) augments deterministic flows with a diffusion term to capture aleatoric uncertainty, improving generalization."  
  - [section 4.1]: Derivation of the SDE with score correction and joint training objective.  
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like *Three Forms of Stochastic Injection for Improved Distribution-to-Distribution Generative Modeling* explore stochasticity, but SFM specifically ensures marginal preservation via score correction.
- **Break condition:** If the score network underfits or the diffusion schedule is mis‑specified, the SDE marginals deviate from the intended flow, degrading generation quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** MCD‑Antithetic enables scalable and sample‑efficient epistemic uncertainty estimation by combining Monte Carlo Dropout with antithetic sampling in nested SDE solves.
- **Mechanism:** Approximates the Bayesian posterior over network parameters via MC Dropout. For each of \(M\) posterior samples, solves the SFM SDE \(K=2J\) times using \(J\) antithetic noise pairs. The mean across antithetic pairs reduces Monte Carlo noise in the parameter‑sample mean, yielding a less variance‑corrupted estimate of the epistemic component.
- **Core assumption:** Dropout at inference samples from an approximate posterior; antithetic pairs cancel leading‑order aleatoric fluctuations because the SDE dynamics are symmetric with respect to noise sign reversal.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "MCD‑Antithetic uses Monte Carlo Dropout with sample‑efficient antithetic sampling for scalable Bayesian inference, disentangling aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty."  
  - [section 4.4]: Formal nested decomposition and antithetic variance reduction.  
  - [corpus]: Neighbors on Bayesian deep learning (e.g., *Uncertainty Quantification for Scientific Machine Learning...*) address UQ but not the antithetic extension to flow matching.
- **Break condition:** If dropout does not approximate the true posterior (e.g., due to overfitting or poor regularization), epistemic estimates are biased. If the driving noise distribution is asymmetric, antithetic reduction diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Disentangled aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty scores provide complementary OOD detection signals: epistemic excels under severe distribution shifts, aleatoric under mild shifts.
- **Mechanism:** Computes aleatoric as average conditional variance across posterior samples, epistemic as variance of parameter‑sample means. Uses negative trace as anomaly score and applies a sign flip because both uncertainty measures tend to decrease under large shifts (model collapse) or mild shifts (reduced conditional variability).
- **Core assumption:** Severe OOD inputs trigger model collapse where different posterior samples produce similar outputs, shrinking epistemic uncertainty; mild shifts reduce inherent variability in the conditional distribution, shrinking aleatoric uncertainty. The sign flip restores the expected monotonicity.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "MCD‑Antithetic outperforms baselines in AUROC/AUPR for OOD detection... with antithetic sampling improving uncertainty disentanglement."  
  - [section 5.3]: Empirical pattern across BBBC021, JUMP, fMRI: epistemic AUROC higher on unseen perturbations/intensity shifts (severe), aleatoric higher on unseen cell lines/plates (mild).  
  - [corpus]: Neighbors on OOD detection in diffusion models (e.g., *Diffusionad: Norm‑guided one‑step denoising for anomaly detection*) focus on single‑distribution settings, not distribution‑to‑distribution.
- **Break condition:** If a new dataset exhibits opposite trends (e.g., epistemic uncertainty increases under severe shift), the sign‑flipped score fails and detection degrades.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Velocity network \(v_\theta\) (U‑Net) and score network \(s_\phi\) (U‑Net) with dropout → SDE solver (Euler‑Maruyama, noise schedule \(\sigma_t\)) → nested sampler: \(M\) MC‑Dropout parameter samples, each with \(J\) antithetic SDE trajectories (\(2J\) total) → per‑sample mean \(\hat{\mu}^{(m)}\) and covariance \(\hat{\Sigma}^{(m)}\) → aggregator: aleatoric \(\hat{A} = \frac{1}{M}\sum \hat{\Sigma}^{(m)}\), epistemic \(\hat{E} = \mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\mu}^{(m)})\) → anomaly scorer \(f = -\mathrm{tr}(\hat{A})\) or \(-\mathrm{tr}(\hat{E})\).

**Critical path:**  
1. Joint training: minimize \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_v + \lambda \mathcal{L}_s\) on interpolated paths \((x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1 + \gamma_t z)\) for 100–200 epochs.  
2. Inference: enable dropout, sample \(M\) parameter masks.  
3. For each mask, solve SFM SDE with \(2J\) antithetic noise seeds (Euler‑Maruyama, e.g., 100 steps).  
4. Compute \(\hat{\mu}^{(m)}\), \(\hat{\Sigma}^{(m)}\) from the solution trajectories.  
5. Aggregate to \(\hat{A}\), \(\hat{E}\).  
6. Apply threshold to anomaly score (negative trace) for OOD detection.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MC‑Dropout offers scalability at the cost of posterior fidelity; alternatives like SWAG or Laplace may be more accurate but heavier.  
- Antithetic sampling reduces Monte Carlo noise for a fixed sample budget by exploiting noise symmetry; however it requires that the SDE dynamics are symmetric under noise sign reversal.  
- Using a MAP approximation for aleatoric uncertainty speeds up computation but introduces a bounded error (Proposition 4.1).

**Failure signatures:**  
- High aleatoric uncertainty on in‑distribution inputs suggests score network underfitting or a mismatched diffusion schedule.  
- Epistemic uncertainty that does not separate OOD from ID indicates poor MC‑Dropout approximation (e.g., dropout rate too low).  
- Unexpected drop in epistemic uncertainty on severe OOD inputs, despite high absolute values, may invalidate the sign‑flip assumption, leading to poor detection.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Generalization benchmark:** Compare SFM against deterministic FM (CellFlux), UNSB, and SDEdit on FID/KID across unseen perturbations, intensity shifts, cell lines, plates, and fMRI subjects.  
2. **OOD detection benchmark:** Evaluate MCD‑Antithetic epistemic and aleatoric scores against TransDist, MAP, SWAG, and Laplace on AUROC/AUPR for the same shift scenarios.  
3. **Antithetic sampling ablation:** Compare MCD‑Antithetic with MCD‑Independent (same total sample count) to demonstrate variance reduction and improved uncertainty disentanglement, and measure correlation between aleatoric and epistemic estimates.

## Limitations
- Unspecified architectural details (U‑Net depth, dropout placement, condition encoder) and hyperparameters (λ, σ_t schedule, antithetic pairs J) impede exact replication and may affect performance.
- Core assumptions may not hold universally: score network accuracy, MC Dropout as a posterior approximation, symmetric noise for antithetic reduction, and the sign‑flip heuristic for OOD detection could fail under certain shifts.
- Computational overhead of the nested SDE solves (\(M \times K\)) is not benchmarked against more efficient Bayesian baselines like SWAG.

## Confidence
- **SFM generalization:** High (consistent FID/KID improvements across datasets) but Medium (performance hinges on score network fidelity and correct diffusion schedule).
- **MCD‑Antithetic scalability:** Medium (antithetic variance reduction demonstrated) but uncertainty about the true posterior approximation quality.
- **Disentangled OOD detection:** High for the studied shifts (epistemic for severe, aleatoric for mild) but Medium for generalizability due to the empirical sign‑flip assumption.

## Next Checks
1. **Score network capacity ablation:** Vary the size and depth of the score network \(s_\phi\) on BBBC021; a significant FID increase would confirm sensitivity to score underfitting.
2. **Posterior approximation benchmark:** Compare MCD‑Antithetic epistemic estimates against SWAG/Laplace on JUMP; where feasible, use HMC on a small subset to directly measure posterior approximation error.
3. **Sign‑flip robustness test:** Introduce new OOD types (e.g., Gaussian noise, adversarial patches) in the fMRI dataset; verify whether \(-\mathrm{tr}(\text{epistemic})\) or \(-\mathrm{tr}(\text{aleatoric})\) remains monotonically correlated with anomaly severity.
