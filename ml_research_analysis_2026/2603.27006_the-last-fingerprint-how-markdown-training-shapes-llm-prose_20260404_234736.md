# The Last Fingerprint: How Markdown Training Shapes LLM Prose

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27006  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27006  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** Em dash use persists under markdown suppression instructions, varying by model and fine-tuning approach—from complete suppression (Meta Llama) to high resistance (GPT-4.1)—making it a diagnostic signature of fine-tuning methodology.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates an unexplained artifact in LLM-generated prose: the overuse of em dashes. Through suppression experiments across twelve instruction-tuned LLMs from five providers, it finds that overt markdown formatting (headers, bullets, bold) is eliminated when models are instructed to write in "flowing prose only," but em dash frequency varies dramatically—from zero to nearly 10 per 1,000 words—even under explicit prohibition. This persistence reveals a latent "structural orientation" acquired from markdown-saturated training data, with fine-tuning (RLHF or analogous) determining whether the tendency is amplified or suppressed. Em dash frequency thus functions as a fingerprint of the fine-tuning procedure applied.

## Method Summary
The study conducted two- and three-condition suppression experiments: for each of twelve instruction-tuned LLMs, it generated ten ~1,000-word essays on everyday topics under three prompt conditions: (1) unconstrained, (2) instructed to avoid markdown formatting, and (3) additionally instructed to avoid em dashes. Outputs were analyzed for em dash count (Unicode U+2014) and overt markdown features (headers, bullets, bold, lists), normalized per 1,000 words. A base-vs-instruct comparison using Meta Llama 3.1 8B further isolated the effect of fine-tuning. All generations used provider-default sampling parameters with strict output length controls.

## Key Results
- All models suppressed overt markdown features (headers, bullets, bold) nearly completely under prose-constrained prompts.
- Em dash frequency under suppression formed a wide gradient across models: from 0.0 per 1,000 words (Meta Llama) to 9.1 (GPT-4.1).
- Suppression resistance correlated with provider families: OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepSeek models showed higher persistence; Meta models showed complete suppression. This pattern points to fine-tuning as the determining factor.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Structural Orientation from Markdown-Saturated Training
**Claim:** Markdown-heavy training data installs a latent "structural orientation" where dashes function as architectural boundary markers, generalizing to the em dash in prose.
**Mechanism:** Exposure to high volumes of markdown-formatted text (e.g., GitHub, Stack Overflow) teaches models that dashes (`---`, `-`) signal hierarchical breaks and list separators. This association transfers to the em dash, the only dash-family member valid in natural text.
**Core assumption:** Quality-curated training datasets disproportionately include markdown-native sources, making structural conventions a dominant pattern.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 3.2 link em dashes to markdown leakage; cited audits [5,14] and dataset compositions [6,18,20] imply markdown prevalence.
**Break condition:** Discovery of a major provider using a minimal-markdown corpus that still produces high em dash rates without RLHF amplification would weaken this mechanism.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Register Ambiguity
**Claim:** The em dash persists under prose-constraint instructions because it occupies a "dual-register" status—valid both as punctuation and as a structural marker—so suppression prompts fail to recognize it as formatting.
**Mechanism:** Instructions like "avoid markdown" target explicit formatting syntax (headers, bullets). The em dash, being standard Unicode punctuation, does not match any markdown token and is therefore not inhibited. The underlying structural impulse surfaces through this "legal" channel.
**Core assumption:** Model inhibition mechanisms pattern-match on explicit formatting tokens, not latent functional roles.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 3.3 describe the dual-register; section 5.2 shows universal markdown suppression paired with variable em dash persistence.
**Break condition:** If a model were fine-tuned to associate the em dash *exclusively* with markdown rendering (e.g., via synthetic data mapping `--` → `<em-dash>` only in code blocks) and then suppressed em dashes completely under prose constraints, the dual-register necessity would be challenged.

### Mechanism 3: Fine-Tuning as Amplifier or Suppressor
**Claim:** RLHF (or analogous fine-tuning) determines the final amplitude of em dash production, either amplifying a latent tendency or suppressing it to zero, making em dash frequency a signature of the fine-tuning methodology.
**Mechanism:** Human evaluators in RLHF pipelines may reward outputs perceived as precise, articulate, and structurally clear—correlated with em-dash-heavy prose. Alternatively, fine-tuning data or reward objectives can explicitly downweight the artifact.
**Core assumption:** Cross-provider variation in human rater preferences or fine-tuning data composition drives observed differences after shared pretraining.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 3.4 note the gradient and tunable outcome; section 5.4 shows Llama base→instruct suppression; provider patterns (e.g., GPT-4.1 vs GPT-5.4) imply tunability.
**Break condition:** A controlled study where identical base models are fine-tuned with *identical* RLHF procedures across infrastructures yet produce divergent em dash rates would force reconsideration of non-RLHF factors (e.g., tokenizer differences, system prompts).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Training Data Provenance & Format Bias  
  **Why needed:** The genealogy rests on markdown-dominated high-quality sources (GitHub, Stack Overflow). Understanding *why* such sources are overrepresented in curated corpora is prerequisite to accepting "structural internalization."  
  **Quick check:** If designing a filter for "high-quality, well-structured text" from a web crawl, which formatting layers would be overrepresented and why?
- **Concept:** Dual-Register Artifacts  
  **Why needed:** Mechanism 2 hinges on a symbol belonging simultaneously to a "formatting" register and a "prose" register. Recognizing this ambiguity explains why standard suppression fails.  
  **Quick check:** Give two examples (from programming or typography) of a single character/sequence with valid meanings in both "structural" and "data/content" contexts.
- **Concept:** Fine-Tuning as a Distributional Shaper  
  **Why needed:** The paper argues fine-tuning reshapes low-level token distributions (like punctuation), not just adds high-level skills. This challenges a narrow view of fine-tuning.  
  **Quick check:** What is the difference between a model's *capability* (e.g., "can write an essay") and its *style fingerprint* (e.g., "uses em dashes at X rate"), and how might fine-tuning influence each differently?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Models (12 LLMs) -> Conditions (3 prompt types) -> Metrics (em dash count, markdown features) -> Baseline (human essays) -> Comparison (Llama base vs instruct)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Prepare ten essay topics and generate prompts for all model/condition/topic combinations.
  2. Execute API/interface calls with strict parameter control (provider defaults, max tokens, no penalties).
  3. Post-process outputs: detect Unicode U+2014; parse markdown via regex/parser.
  4. Normalize counts to per 1,000 words; aggregate across samples.
  5. Compare cross-condition and cross-model metrics; perform statistical analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Breadth vs. Depth:** 12 models × 10 topics provides breadth but limited depth per model; supplementary long-form test partially addresses this.
  - **Prompt control:** Single "essay" prompt type tests general prose but may not probe genre-specific em dash use.
  - **Cost/Access:** API reliance limits replicability and sample size; local Llama run is an exception.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **False positive em dashes:** Misidentifying `--` or `–` as U+2014. *Mitigation:* Strict Unicode matching; verify raw outputs.
  - **Incomplete suppression:** Residual markdown in some models (e.g., Claude Haiku 3.5: 0.9/1K) indicates insufficient instruction tuning. *Diagnostic:* Manual inspection of samples.
  - **Provider API drift:** Undocumented default system prompts or tokenization changes. *Mitigation:* Use bare user prompts; log all parameters.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replication of Study 1 (Two-Condition Test):** Run all 12 models with "essay" prompts under Unconstrained and "no markdown" conditions. Expected: markdown features ~0% in Condition B; em dash reduction gradient replicates.
  2. **Base Model Expansion:** Obtain and run base weights for one "amplifying" family (e.g., OpenAI GPT, Anthropic Claude). Expected: base shows nonzero em dash rate; instruct shows higher rate.
  3. **Dual-Register Stress Test:** Prompt requiring em dashes for clause separation while forbidding all markdown (e.g., "Use em dashes to separate clauses—do not use any other formatting"). Test on high-persistence models (GPT-4.1). Expected: overproduction persists, confirming dash-specific structural impulse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does fine-tuning amplify or merely preserve the latent structural tendency in base models from families where RLHF increases em dash frequency?**  
  *Basis:* Explicitly listed in Future Work; base-vs-instruct comparison needed for "amplifying families" but not done.  
  *Why unresolved:* Only Meta's base model (suppressing family) is compared; direct base outputs for OpenAI/Anthropic/DeepSeek absent.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Run identical essay prompts on base and instruct versions of a model like GPT-4 or Claude Sonnet; measure frequency change attributable to post-training.
  
- **What is the actual frequency and contextual usage of dashes at structural boundaries versus inline positions in major markdown-saturated corpora?**  
  *Basis:* Future Work identifies "training corpora analysis" as necessary to verify that dashes signal boundaries in markdown data.  
  *Why unresolved:* Mechanistic claim relies on this statistical premise but lacks quantitative verification in actual pretraining corpora.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Large-scale annotation or parsing of sampled markdown documents from key sources (GitHub, Stack Exchange) to measure dash roles.

- **Do other punctuation marks with dual-register status (e.g., semicolons, colons, parentheses) exhibit similar resistance to formatting suppression?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred gap; paper focuses solely on em dashes and overt markdown.  
  *Why unresolved:* If "last fingerprint" effect generalizes, other structurally used punctuation should persist; this remains untested.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Extend suppression experiments to count semicolons, colons, parentheses across same models and conditions; test if suppression curves mirror em dash gradient.

- **Would a model trained on a corpus deliberately excluding markdown exhibit near-zero baseline em dash frequency regardless of fine-tuning?**  
  *Basis:* Limitations section (Falsifiability) proposes this as a stronger test.  
  *Why unresolved:* Currently only correlative provider comparison and Llama base's small non-zero rate; causal manipulation of training data markdown content is unavailable.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Train comparable base models on corpora with systematically varied markdown proportions (0%, 20%, 50%, 80%) and measure pre-fine-tuning em dash rates.

## Limitations
- Unknown essay topics and provider-default sampling parameters (temperature, top-p) hinder exact replication.
- Lack of direct base model outputs for "amplifying" families (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek) limits causal attribution of fine-tuning effects.
- Mechanism 1's "structural orientation" claim lacks quantitative corpus analysis of dash-boundary contexts in actual training data.
- Testing limited to "everyday essay" genre; em dash behavior in other genres (technical, fictional) remains unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Dual-register persistence (Mechanism 2):** High (asymmetric suppression robustly demonstrated across all 12 models).
- **Fine-tuning amplitude (Mechanism 3):** Medium (Llama base→instruct suppression is strong evidence; amplification case lacks direct base comparison).
- **Training data provenance (Mechanism 1):** Low (link to markdown-saturated data is plausible but speculative without corpus statistics).
