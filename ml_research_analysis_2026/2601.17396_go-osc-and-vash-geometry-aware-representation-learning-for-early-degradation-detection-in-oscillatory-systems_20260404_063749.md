# GO-OSC and VASH: Geometry-Aware Representation Learning for Early Degradation Detection in Oscillatory Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17396  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17396  
- **Reference count:** 36  
- **Primary result:** GO-OSC+VASH detects degradation earlier, requires 16× fewer labeled windows (20 vs. 320), and achieves near-perfect AUROC (~1.0) on phase-only degradations, while RMS only yields ~0.5.

## Executive Summary
Early degradation in oscillatory systems often manifests as geometric phase distortions before any measurable change in energy, rendering traditional energy-based diagnostics ineffective. The GO-OSC framework addresses this by enforcing a canonical real-Schur oscillatory gauge on latent dynamics, ensuring identifiability and stable cross-window comparability. From this representation, VASH defines linear geometric probes that are invariant to gauge transformations and sensitive to phase and frequency perturbations. Theory proves that energy-only statistics have zero first-order detection power under phase-only degradation, whereas geometric probes achieve strictly positive sensitivity. Experiments on synthetic and real vibration data confirm that GO-OSC+VASH detects faults earlier, uses far fewer labeled examples (16× reduction), and remains robust to amplitude nuisances. Canonicalization is essential; without it, probe values become unstable due to gauge drift.

## Method Summary
The approach splits multivariate time series into short, non-overlapping windows. For each window, a linear Gaussian state-space model (SSM) is estimated via maximum likelihood (Kalman smoothing). The transition matrix is then projected onto a canonical real-Schur oscillatory manifold: frequency blocks are ordered, and a sign convention is enforced, removing similarity-transform ambiguities. This canonical representation yields identifiable latent dynamics. From the canonical SSM parameters, six gauge-invariant geometric probes (GSI, PCC, DDI, FWR, MLL, LQF) are computed, capturing phase dispersion and frequency stability. A linear aggregator (e.g., logistic regression) trained on a small labeled subset combines these probes into a per-window degradation score. The entire pipeline is designed for early phase-only faults and tolerates variations in amplitude.

## Key Results
- Theory shows energy-only statistics have zero first-order detection power under infinitesimal phase-only degradation, while geometric probes have strictly positive power.
- Experiments demonstrate GO-OSC+VASH detects degradation earlier than RMS, achieves AUROC ~1.0 vs ~0.5 for RMS, and requires only 20 labeled windows (vs 320) for high performance.
- Canonicalization is critical: without it, geometric probes exhibit high total variation across healthy windows due to gauge drift, making them unreliable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Canonicalization yields identifiable representations
**Claim:** Canonicalization of latent oscillatory dynamics via GO-OSC yields identifiable representations, enabling stable aggregation and comparison of statistics across short, unlabeled windows.  
**Mechanism:** The GO-OSC framework constrains the latent state-space transition matrix to a canonical real-Schur oscillatory gauge. This removes similarity-transform ambiguities (rotations, permutations, scalings) that normally plague learned or estimated latent dynamics. The resulting latent coordinates become unique up to measure-zero degeneracies, meaning the same physical system state maps to deterministically comparable latent vectors in different windows.  
**Core assumption:** The underlying system is well-modeled as a stable, minimal, multi-mode linear Gaussian state-space model with sufficiently separated frequencies (no modal collisions).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract]: "enforces a canonical and identifiable latent parameterization, enabling stable comparison and aggregation across short, unlabeled windows."  
- [section 3.2]: "The real Schur decomposition... yields a unique, interpretable representation."  
**Break condition:** Modal frequency collisions or strong nonlinear coupling violate the separation assumption, breaking identifiability and causing gauge drift.

### Mechanism 2: Geometric probes have first-order sensitivity
**Claim:** Under early phase-only degradation (constant energy), linear geometric probes computed on a canonical representation have non-zero first-order detection power, whereas energy-based statistics (e.g., RMS) have zero first-order power.  
**Mechanism:** Early degradation perturbs phase geometry without changing second-order statistics (energy). In a locally asymptotically normal (LAN) regime, the score (derivative of log-likelihood) in the direction of phase/frequency perturbations is orthogonal to the score for energy-preserving changes. Therefore, any statistic depending only on marginal energy has zero directional derivative along the degradation manifold, making it second-order insensitive. Geometric probes (e.g., PCC, GSI) explicitly measure phase dispersion or frequency stability, aligning their derivatives with the degradation direction.  
**Core assumption:** The degradation is infinitesimal, phase-only, and operates within the LAN regime where the tangent space decomposition separates geometric and energy subspaces.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract]: "theory showing... energy-based statistics have zero first-order detection power, whereas geometric probes achieve strictly positive sensitivity."  
- [section 5]: "Under early phase-only degradation, energy-based statistics... are second-order insensitive... geometric probes... respond linearly."  
**Break condition:** When degradation involves significant amplitude changes or when the system moves out of the weak-perturbation/local regime, energy statistics may become informative, and the dominance gap narrows.

### Mechanism 3: Canonicalization necessary for probe stability
**Claim:** Canonicalization is necessary for the stability and reliability of linear geometric probes; without it, gauge ambiguities cause probe values to drift under stationary conditions, masking degradation signals.  
**Mechanism:** In a non-canonical representation, the same physical state can map to arbitrarily rotated latent coordinates across windows due to unconstrained similarity transforms. Linear geometric probes (e.g., PCC, FWR) depend on phase differences or frequency derivatives, which are not invariant to these rotations. This induces high variance in probe values even when the system is healthy, raising false alarm rates or requiring more data to distinguish signal from noise. Canonicalization fixes a global gauge, making these probes deterministic functionals of the stationary law.  
**Core assumption:** Windows are short relative to system time constants, and the underlying system is stationary; the only source of non-stationarity is degradation.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 6.4 (ablation)]: "without canonicalization, gauge drift causes indicator values to fluctuate even under stationary conditions."  
- [section 6.4 (figure inset)]: Canonicalization dramatically reduces total variation of geometric indicators.  
**Break condition:** If the model class is misspecified (e.g., true dynamics are nonlinear but GO-OSC assumes linearity), canonicalization may stabilize a wrong representation, leading to consistent but biased probes.

## Foundational Learning

- **Identifiability in Latent Variable Models**  
  *Why needed here:* System identification theory states that minimal linear state-space models are only identifiable up to similarity transforms. Without enforcing a canonical form, latent coordinates are not unique, breaking any attempt to compare statistics across time windows or aggregate evidence linearly. GO-OSC's core innovation is imposing a gauge that resolves this ambiguity.  
  *Quick check question:* If two different windows from a healthy machine produce latent states `z1` and `z2` that should represent the same physical condition, what transformation could relate them in a standard SSM? (Answer: a similarity transform `z2 = T z1`; without canonicalization, `T` is arbitrary.)

- **Local Asymptotic Normality (LAN)**  
  *Why needed here:* LAN provides the asymptotic framework to reason about the *first-order* sensitivity of test statistics to infinitesimal parameter changes. The paper uses it to formally separate the tangent space into directions that affect energy (second-order) and those that affect phase geometry (first-order), proving why geometric probes are locally more efficient.  
  *Quick check question:* In a LAN regime, what property of the score function determines whether a statistic has first-order detection power? (Answer: Non-zero inner product between the statistic's efficient influence function and the score in the degradation direction.)

- **Gauge Invariance**  
  *Why needed here:* After canonicalization, residual finite symmetries (e.g., sign flips of individual mode blocks) remain. Probes must be invariant to these to be well-defined. PCC, FWR, etc., are designed to depend only on phase differences, ratios, or norms that are unaffected by such gauge transformations.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is the raw estimated phase `ϕk,t` not a gauge-invariant quantity, while the phase difference `ϕk,t+1 - ϕk,t` is? (Answer: Sign flips invert all phases in a mode, canceling in differences.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input Windower → GO-OSC Encoder (canonical real-Schur projection) → Geometric Probe Calculator (computes six gauge-invariant probes) → Linear Aggregator (fixed linear readout)

### Critical Path
Canonicalization must occur immediately after estimation and before any probe calculation. If probes are computed on non-canonical parameters, their values are gauge-dependent and unstable across windows.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Strict canonical form vs. flexibility:** The real-Schur gauge maximizes identifiability but assumes linearity and modal separation. More flexible models (e.g., nonlinear, strongly coupled) may need different canonicalization strategies.
- **Window length `N`:** Needs to be long enough for reliable SSM parameter estimation (consistency), but short enough to capture early degradation before regime changes. The paper assumes `N → ∞` asymptotically, but in practice, `N` is fixed.
- **Probe selection:** The paper proposes six probes. Using all may be redundant; a subset might suffice. The optimal linear combination (Theorem I.1) requires estimating the covariance of probes under healthy conditions.

### Failure Signatures
- High variance in probe values under simulated healthy data: Indicates failed canonicalization (gauge drift) or insufficient window length.
- No separation between healthy/degraded distributions for any probe: Could indicate wrong degradation model (e.g., fault is amplitude-based), severe model misspecification, or that the degradation is not yet in the "phase-only" regime.
- Energy baseline (RMS) shows perfect separation: Suggests the fault has progressed to amplitude changes, making geometric probes unnecessary; the paper's regime assumption is violated.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Synthetic phase-jitter detection: Generate a constant-amplitude oscillator with injected phase noise at known timesteps. Plot GSI/MSI vs. RMS to show immediate geometric response vs. flat energy.
2. Canonicalization ablation: Train GO-OSC with and without the real-Schur projection. Compute total variation of each geometric probe over many healthy windows. Canonical version should show dramatically lower variation.
3. Data efficiency curve: For increasing numbers of labeled windows (e.g., 5, 10, 20, 50, 100), train a logistic regression on probe vectors. Plot AUROC vs. label count, comparing to a model using RMS and kurtosis features. Should show ~16x label efficiency gain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension beyond vibrations:** Does GO-OSC+VASH extend to non-vibration oscillatory systems (e.g., power grids, EEG) and other sensors? (Basis: Sec. 7; broader validation needed.)
2. **Other degradation modes:** Can theory be extended to other degradation modes (amplitude, damping, transients)? (Basis: Sec. 7; analysis only covers phase-only; no optimality guarantees for other modes.)
3. **Robustness to violations:** How robust is GO-OSC to violations of frequency separation, linearity, stationarity? (Basis: Sec. 7; near modal collisions, strong nonlinearity, extreme noise degrade stability; thresholds unknown.)
4. **Unified representation:** Can a single representation handle both early phase-only and later amplitude-dominant failures? (Basis: Sec. 7; GO-OSC specialized for early geometry; may miss later changes.)

## Limitations
- Missing implementation specifics (e.g., exact real-Schur projection, hyperparameters) hinder exact replication.
- Real datasets used in the paper are not publicly released, limiting external validation on real-world scenarios.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on ideal conditions (linear Gaussian SSM, well-separated frequencies, infinitesimal phase-only degradation), which may be violated in practice.

## Confidence
- GO-OSC identifiability: **High**  
- Geometric probe sensitivity: **Medium**  
- Canonicalization necessity: **Medium**
