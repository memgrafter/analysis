# The effect of whitening on explanation performance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09278
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09278
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Whitening improves explanation correctness for feature attribution methods by reducing feature correlations that cause suppressor misattributions, with effectiveness varying by whitening technique and model architecture.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether data whitening can mitigate errors in feature attribution XAI methods caused by correlated features and suppressor variables. Using the XAI-TRIS benchmark with synthetic images and ground-truth explanations, they evaluate 16 XAI methods combined with 5 whitening transforms across linear and non-linear scenarios. A theoretical analysis on a 2D linear classification problem with a known suppressor provides insights. Results show that whitening generally improves explanation correctness, but the extent depends on the specific whitening method and model architecture. Methods like sphering, symmetric orthogonalization, and optimal signal preservation nearly restore performance to the level of uncorrelated data, while Cholesky and partial regression whitening are less effective. The theoretical analysis confirms that only partial regression completely eliminates the suppressor's influence in the linear case, explaining inconsistent empirical results, especially with non-linear models.

The findings highlight that whitening's benefit is not uniform; it interacts with both the XAI method and the underlying model. The choice of whitening matrix (covariance, overlap, correlation) fundamentally alters the data transformation, affecting how well the signal structure is preserved relative to the background. In non-linear settings or when there are more signals than features, whitening may mix informative and non-discriminative components, potentially harming explanations. The study provides both empirical evidence and theoretical grounding for understanding when and how whitening can enhance XAI reliability.

## Method Summary
The authors employed the XAI-TRIS benchmark, which generates synthetic images containing tetromino shapes (the true signal) on backgrounds with controlled correlation structures. Data is either white (uncorrelated) or correlated via Gaussian smoothing. Five whitening transforms (sphering, symmetric orthogonalization, optimal signal preservation, Cholesky, partial regression) are applied to the correlated data before model training. Three model architectures are trained: linear logistic regression (LLR), multi-layer perceptron (MLP), and convolutional neural network (CNN). Sixteen XAI methods (including gradient-based, perturbation-based, and propagation-based) generate pixel-wise importance maps. These are evaluated against ground-truth masks using precision (top-k hit rate) and earth mover's distance (EMD). A theoretical analysis involves a 2D linear classifier with a suppressor feature, analytically deriving how each whitening affects the classifier's weights on the suppressor.

## Key Results
- Whitening improves explanation correctness across most XAI methods and model architectures, but the magnitude varies substantially.
- Sphering, symmetric orthogonalization, and optimal signal preservation nearly achieve the performance of uncorrelated (WHITE) data.
- Partial regression and Cholesky whitening are less effective, with partial regression only fully removing linear suppressor influence theoretically.
- Theoretical analysis shows that in the linear 2D case, only partial regression sets the suppressor weight to zero; other methods leave residual importance.
- In non-linear scenarios (e.g., MULT, RIGID, XOR), whitening can degrade explanations due to mixing of signal and noise components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper proposes that whitening mitigates suppressor-induced misattributions by reducing linear feature correlations. Its effectiveness hinges on the specific whitening transformation: methods that preserve individual feature variance or minimize distortion (e.g., symmetric orthogonalization, OSP) better maintain the geometric relationship between true signal and background, leading to improved explanations. However, theoretical analysis on a linear 2D problem reveals that only partial regression completely eliminates the suppressor's influence; other methods fail to fully decorrelate in a way that removes spurious importance. This theoretical limitation partly explains inconsistent empirical results, especially in non-linear settings where additional complexities arise. Moreover, when data contains more underlying signals than features, whitening inevitably mixes discriminative and non-discriminative components, which can harm explanations. Thus, whitening's impact is not universal but depends on the interplay between the whitening matrix choice, model architecture, and data nonlinearity.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Generator (XAI-TRIS) -> Whitening Preprocessor -> Model Trainer -> XAI Engine -> Evaluator
- **Critical path:** Raw CORR data → Whitening Transform → Model Training → Explanation Generation → Metric Calculation vs. Ground Truth
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Signal Preservation vs. Decorrelation: Methods like OSP preserve individual feature scales but may leave residual correlations; partial regression aims for unique variance but may not fully decorrelate.
  - Global vs. Sequential: Eigendecomposition methods (Sphering, Symmetric, OSP) are global and permutation-invariant; Cholesky is order-dependent (top-left pixel unchanged), which is arbitrary for images.
  - Linear vs. Non-linear suitability: All whitening transforms are linear; their benefit in non-linear problems is empirical and partial; they don't remove non-linear suppression.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Cholesky underperformance: Due to arbitrary feature ordering causing uneven residual suppression.
  - Partial regression underperformance in non-linear cases: Fails to fully decorrelate globally and doesn't address non-linear dependencies.
  - Architecture interaction: A whitening method effective for MLP may not be for CNN, indicating model-specific interactions with whitened input space.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce XAI-TRIS baseline: Train a CNN on CORR (no whitening) data for LIN scenario. Compute Gradient SHAP and LIME attributions. Verify poor Precision/EMD and diffuse background importance in heatmaps.
  2. Apply best whitening (Symmetric Orthogonalization): Repeat Experiment 1 with whitening. Quantitatively confirm improved Precision/EMD; qualitatively observe more focused tetromino attribution.
  3. Theoretical sanity check: Generate 2D suppressor data (Section 3.3 parameters, c=0.8). Apply each whitening, compute Bayes-optimal weights w. Verify only Partial Regression yields w2=0.

## Limitations
- Results rely on synthetic XAI-TRIS; real-world data may exhibit different correlation structures and model behaviors.
- Non-linear mixing of signals and noise can degrade explanations; the conditions under which whitening helps or harms in such cases are not fully understood.
- Architecture- and method-specific interactions remain unexplained, limiting generalizability.

## Confidence
- Whitening improves XAI correctness: **High** (consistent gains across many methods).
- Improvement varies by whitening method and model architecture: **Medium** (pattern clear but underlying reasons unclear).
- Partial regression uniquely removes linear suppressor influence: **High** (theoretical proof).
- Linear failure of other methods explains non-linear inconsistency: **Medium** (correlation observed but non-linear effects may also contribute).
