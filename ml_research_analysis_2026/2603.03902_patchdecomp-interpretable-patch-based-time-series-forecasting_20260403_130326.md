# PatchDecomp: Interpretable Patch-Based Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03902  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03902  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PatchDecomp achieves predictive accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art models like PatchTST and N-BEATSx while providing interpretable patch-wise contributions, and quantitatively outperforms TFT on the Area over the Perturbation Curve for Regression (AOPCR) metric.

## Executive Summary

PatchDecomp is a neural time series forecasting model that decomposes predictions into additive contributions from fixed-length input patches. It processes all variables (target, historical and future-known exogenous, and static) through a patch-based encoder and a specially designed single‑head attention decoder. The output is a linear combination of patch embeddings, enabling direct attribution of each patch to the final forecast. On seven long‑term forecasting benchmarks, PatchDecomp matches the accuracy of top models. For electricity price forecasting with exogenous variables, it produces clearer qualitative explanations and achieves higher AOPCR than the Temporal Fusion Transformer, demonstrating more faithful identification of influential input segments.

## Method Summary

The method begins by normalizing each variable with reversible instance normalization (RevIN) and splitting all series (including both historical and future‑known exogenous) into non‑overlapping patches of length *P*. Each patch is linearly projected to a *d*-dimensional embedding and augmented with learned positional embeddings and a static variable embedding. An encoder stack of residual MLP blocks processes each patch independently, yielding representations for all input patches (*z_src*) and dummy query representations for future output patches (*z_tgt*). The decoder applies a single‑head attention where *z_tgt* are queries and *z_src* are keys/values. Critically, the attention output is combined with an element‑wise product between *z_src* and a learned bias vector *w_bias* summed over the patch dimension. The resulting *z_pred* is passed through a linear layer to produce the forecast, which is then denormalized. Because the output is a weighted sum of patch contributions, the model provides an inherent decomposition: each patch’s influence can be extracted from the attention weights and the bias‑product term.

## Key Results

- **Accuracy:** MSE/MAE on par with PatchTST and N-BEATSx across seven long‑term forecasting benchmarks (ETTh1/2, ETTm1/2, Weather, ECL, Traffic).  
- **Interpretability (qualitative):** Patch‑wise contribution visualizations for electricity price forecasting (EPF) datasets are clear and sparse, highlighting a few key patches.  
- **Interpretability (quantitative):** PatchDecomp achieves higher AOPCR than both random patch removal and TFT (point‑wise and patch‑aggregated), indicating that its identified patches have a larger impact on the output.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:** Patch‑level additive decomposition.  
Claim: PatchDecomp generates interpretable forecasts by decomposing the final prediction into additive contributions from fixed‑length input patches.  
Mechanism: Inputs are split into patches, encoded to embeddings, and the decoder computes the output as a weighted sum of all input patch embeddings via a single‑head attention plus a bias‑weighted element‑wise product summed across patches. This structure ensures each patch’s influence is explicitly represented and can be summed to reconstruct the forecast.  
Core assumption: Forecast contributions can be expressed as linear combinations of patches.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract states decomposition by aggregating patch contributions; Section 2 (Patch Decoder) describes the element‑wise product and sum operation; literature review shows no prior work combining patch‑level decomposition with exogenous variables in this manner.  
Break condition: If temporal dynamics within a patch are highly non‑additive, the linear assumption may yield inaccurate attributions despite accurate point forecasts.

**Mechanism 2:** Single‑head attention for decomposability.  
Claim: Using a single‑head attention mechanism (with a specific element‑wise product computation) allows direct attribution without sacrificing forecasting ability.  
Mechanism: Standard multi‑head attention concatenates outputs from multiple heads, making per‑patch contributions entangled. PatchDecomp modifies the computation: after attention weighting, it produces a tensor that, combined with the element‑wise product and sum, retains the same expressiveness as multi‑head but maintains an additive structure where each patch’s contribution is isolated.  
Core assumption: One attention head suffices for mapping input to output patches in long‑term forecasting.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract highlights single‑head attention for direct attribution; Section 2 details the product‑sum method; literature indicates no prior explicit advocacy for single‑head for decomposable outputs.  
Break condition: Complex relationships requiring multiple representation subspaces could benefit from multi‑head, potentially harming accuracy if forced into a single head.

**Mechanism 3:** AOPCR as a robustness check for interpretability.  
Claim: PatchDecomp’s attributions identify more influential patches than TFT’s, as shown by higher AOPCR.  
Mechanism: AOPCR quantifies the increase in forecast error when the top‑*k*% important input elements are replaced with dataset means. PatchDecomp uses its inherent patch contributions to rank patches; TFT’s attributions derive from feature importance and attention weights (aggregated to patches). PatchDecomp achieves larger AOPCR, indicating its identified patches more truly affect the output.  
Core assumption: AOPCR is a valid metric for faithfulness of interpretability methods.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract reports AOPCR outperformance; Section 2 defines AOPCR and describes the comparative experiment; literature includes AOPCR usage but no disputes of its validity.  
Break condition: Non‑linear compensation or the choice of replacement value could cause AOPCR to not fully reflect importance.

  Why needed: The core attribution mechanism hinges on a modified single‑head attention that preserves an additive structure, requiring understanding of standard attention.  
  Quick check: In a standard Transformer’s multi‑head attention, why is the output not trivially decomposable by input token, and what structural change does PatchDecomp make to force decomposability?

- **Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN)**  
  Why needed: RevIN standardizes each variable independently before processing and reverses the transformation on outputs, crucial for stable training and for ensuring patch contributions are computed on a normalized scale, aiding interpretability.  
  Quick check: Why is per‑variable instance normalization, with a reversible transformation, preferred over global or batch normalization for time series forecasting models aiming for consistent interpretation across different series distributions?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:  
Input & Preprocessing → Position & Context Embedding → Patch Encoder → Patch Decoder → Output Head

Critical path:  
Data flow: Raw input → RevIN → Patch & Project → Add Pos/Static embeddings → Encoder MLP blocks → *z_src* (input patches) and *z_tgt* (future queries) → Single‑head attention + element‑wise product/sum → *z_pred* → Linear layer → Denormalization → Forecast.  
Attribution flow: Each input patch contributes via two pathways: (1) its attention weight in the attention output and (2) its bias‑product term in the summed representation. These contributions are aggregated and mapped to the final prediction, enabling per‑patch attribution.

Design tradeoffs:  
- **Fixed vs. Variable Patch Length:** Fixed patches improve interpretability but may reduce accuracy if misaligned with temporal patterns.  
- **Single‑Head vs. Multi‑Head Attention:** Single‑head ensures decomposability but might limit representation capacity.  
- **No Patch Mixing in Encoder:** Independent patch processing simplifies attribution but may hinder modeling inter‑patch dependencies.  
- **Exogenous Variable Handling:** Uniform patching for all variables enables attribution but assumes similar temporal dynamics across variable types.

Failure signatures:  
- **Accuracy:** Poor performance on datasets with strong intra‑patch non‑additivity or where cross‑patch dependencies are crucial (e.g., Weather, ETTm).  
- **Interpretability:** Contributions become diffuse across many patches, yielding noisy heatmaps and AOPCR approaching random removal.  
- **Training:** High variance across random seeds suggests optimization fragility.

First 3 experiments:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** How can static exogenous variables be incorporated into the patch‑wise contribution decomposition?  
- Basis: The conclusion explicitly states the current inability to present static variable contributions.  
- Why unresolved: Static variables lack temporal patches, preventing attribution to specific forecast components; they are currently added as embeddings.  
- What evidence would resolve it: An extended architecture that isolates static effects and validation via adapted AOPCR or visual explanations.

**Open Question 2:** Does fixed‑length versus variable‑length patching affect the trade‑off between forecast accuracy and interpretability?  
- Basis: The paper chooses fixed patches for interpretability but acknowledges variable‑length could be more accurate; no comparative experiment is provided.  
- Why unresolved: The assumption that fixed patches align with human intuition is untested; the impact on both accuracy and explanation clarity is unknown.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison across fixed and variable patch lengths, measuring both predictive performance and interpretability metrics (e.g., AOPCR, user studies).

**Open Question 3:** How robust and consistent are the patch‑level attributions across random initializations and diverse datasets?  
- Basis: Appendix shows consistency for EPF markets over five seeds, but generalizability is not discussed.  
- Why unresolved: Interpretability requires stable explanations; high seed sensitivity would undermine trust.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Multi‑seed experiments across all benchmarks, computing stability metrics (e.g., rank correlation of top‑k patches), and correlating with performance variance.

## Limitations

- Architectural details (e.g., dimensions, LayerNorm) of the encoder’s residual blocks are omitted, hindering exact reproduction.  
- Static exogenous variable contributions cannot be decomposed, limiting explanations in settings where static features matter.  
- The reliance on single‑head attention may restrict modeling capacity for complex temporal dynamics, as suggested by weaker results on Weather and ETTm datasets.
