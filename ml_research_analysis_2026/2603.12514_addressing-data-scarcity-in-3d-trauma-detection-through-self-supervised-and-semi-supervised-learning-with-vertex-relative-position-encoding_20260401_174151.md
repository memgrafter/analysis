# Addressing Data Scarcity in 3D Trauma Detection through Self-Supervised and Semi-Supervised Learning with Vertex Relative Position Encoding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12514
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12514
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** With only 144 labeled CT volumes, the proposed semi-supervised method with vertex relative position encoding achieves a 115% improvement in detection mAP@0.50 over a supervised-only baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles severe label scarcity in 3D abdominal trauma detection from CT scans. It combines self-supervised pre-training via masked image modeling (MIM) on 1,206 unlabeled volumes with a semi-supervised detection framework that uses a transformer decoder enhanced by vertex relative position encoding (3D RPE). The approach yields a 115% relative gain in detection mAP@0.50 (45.30% test) using just 144 labeled samples and 2,000 unlabeled volumes. For multi-label injury classification on a larger set (2,244 labeled), a linear probe of the frozen pre-trained encoder achieves 94.07% test accuracy, demonstrating highly transferable features.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in three phases. First, a 3D U-Net encoder is pre-trained in a self-supervised manner using patch-based masked image modeling: it reconstructs randomly masked sub-patches (8×8×8) within 128³ input patches from unlabeled CT volumes. Second, for detection, the pre-trained encoder is frozen initially (Phase I) while a transformer-based VDETR decoder with vertex relative position encoding is trained on 144 labeled volumes. In Phase II, the encoder is fine-tuned and a Mean Teacher consistency regularization is applied to 2,000 unlabeled volumes, with the loss weighting ramped up slowly. For classification, the frozen encoder is combined with a simple global average pooling and a two-layer head, trained with class-weighted binary cross-entropy.

## Key Results
- Detection validation mAP@0.50: 56.57%; test mAP@0.50: 45.30% with semi-supervised learning (115% relative improvement over supervised-only).
- Multi-label classification test accuracy: 94.07% using a frozen encoder linear probe on 2,244 labeled samples.
- Semi-supervised learning unexpectedly degraded classification performance (from 77.7% to 75.4%), attributed to noisy pseudo-labels from distribution mismatch.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Self-Supervised Pre-training via Masked Image Modeling
- **Claim:** Patch-based MIM pre-trains a 3D U-Net encoder to learn robust, transferable anatomical representations from unlabeled CT volumes.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder reconstructs randomly masked 8×8×8 sub-patches within 128³ patches using MSE loss, forcing it to model the statistical structure and spatial relationships of Hounsfield Units (e.g., organ shapes, tissue boundaries).
- **Core assumption:** The unlabeled CT data distribution contains sufficient anatomical structure for a reconstruction task to yield features generalizable to downstream injury tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states MIM pre-training on 1,206 volumes; Section IV.A details the 75% masking strategy and loss; related work in corpus supports MIM in medical imaging but not this specific 3D patch formulation.

### Mechanism 2: Vertex Relative Position Encoding in VDETR
- **Claim:** 3D Vertex RPE provides the geometric inductive bias needed for precise 3D localization of irregular injuries from limited labeled data.
- **Mechanism:** For each object query and voxel token, the model computes offset vectors to all eight vertices of the query's predicted 3D bounding box. These geometric relationships are transformed into attention biases, allowing the cross-attention to weight voxels based on spatial inclusion relative to box corners, not just the center.
- **Core assumption:** Encoding all eight corners provides a rich geometric signal that overcomes locality problems in transformer-based 3D detection under label scarcity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions VDETR with vertex RPE; Section V provides equations and explains focusing on geometric inclusion; citation to V-DETR [7] as origin but no direct comparison in this paper.

### Mechanism 3: Semi-Supervised Consistency Regularization
- **Claim:** Mean Teacher consistency regularization on 2,000 unlabeled volumes stabilizes the detector trained on only 144 labeled samples and prevents catastrophic overfitting.
- **Mechanism:** A teacher model (EMA, no gradient) generates pseudo-labels from weakly-augmented unlabeled volumes. The student model processes a strongly-augmented version. Consistency losses on predicted box centers, sizes, and classification logits (temperature-scaled KL) force augmentation-invariant predictions, providing a dense free supervision signal.
- **Core assumption:** Model predictions on unlabeled data under different augmentations are trustworthy enough for consistent training, and anatomical structures are invariant to the applied intensity/geometric transformations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports the mAP improvement; Section VI details the two-phase training, loss components (L_center, L_size, L_cls), and λ(t) schedule; references foundational Mean Teacher work [10,11].

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for 3D Medical Volumes:** Needed to learn generic anatomical features from 1,206 unlabeled CT scans, addressing >95% label scarcity. The 3D patch-based approach adapts 2D MAE to handle anisotropic spacing and large volumes. Quick check: How does masking 75% of 8³ sub-patches affect reconstruction difficulty and feature quality versus 50% or 90%?
- **Transformer-based 3D Object Detection with Relative Position Encoding:** Needed because standard DETR/3DETR queries fail to learn locality from small medical datasets. RPE must explicitly encode 3D geometry of bounding box corners to guide attention, replacing hand-designed anchors. Quick check: Why is a center-based distance metric insufficient for learning accurate attention in 3D medical detection, and how does referencing all eight box corners address this?
- **Teacher-Student Consistency Regularization for Semi-Supervised Learning:** Needed because with only 144 labeled volumes, the detector overfits catastrophically. Consistency loss on 2,000 unlabeled volumes provides a stabilizing gradient signal that encourages robustness to realistic CT variations. Quick check: Why is the consistency loss weight λ(t) ramped up slowly (epochs 20–60) instead of applied from the start?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input/Preprocessor -> SSL Encoder (3D U-Net) -> Feature Adapter -> VDETR Decoder (with 3D Vertex RPE) -> Detection Heads; also SSL Encoder -> Global Avg Pool -> Classification Head. Semi-Supervised Module wraps VDETR for teacher-student consistency.
- **Critical path:** 1) Pre-train U-Net encoder on all 1,206 volumes via MIM. 2) Detection Phase I (epochs 0–20): freeze encoder, train VDETR decoder+heads on 144 labeled volumes. 3) Detection Phase II (epochs 20–100): unfreeze encoder with LR warmup, activate SSL consistency loss on 2,000 unlabeled volumes (λ ramped 0→0.3 epochs 20–60). 4) Evaluate mAP@0.50.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Patch size (128³) vs. whole volume: smaller patches save memory but lose global context. Frozen vs. fine-tuned encoder: freezing protects pre-trained features early; fine-tuning later risks forgetting but enables task-specific refinement. Augmentation strength: weak (teacher) vs. strong (student) must be domain-appropriate; overly strong deformations create unrealistic anatomies. Token sampling (4,096 from 14,112): reduces compute but may lose small injury voxels if sampling is poor.
- **Failure signatures:** Pre-training: reconstruction PSNR <17 dB on held-out volumes indicates poor anatomical learning. Detection (no SSL): training mAP peaks within 5–10 epochs then collapses to <10%. Detection (with SSL): validation mAP stagnates <30% → possible pseudo-label noise or poor augmentation. Classification: test AUC ~0.5 despite high accuracy → calibration failure; or accuracy <80% with frozen encoder → poor SSL transfer.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Pre-training integrity: after 50 MIM epochs, compute PSNR on 250 held-out unlabeled volumes; target >19 dB. Visualize masked/reconstructed patches for anatomical coherence. 2) SSL activation timing ablation: compare detection performance with (a) encoder frozen throughout, (b) two-phase schedule without SSL, (c) full proposed pipeline. Expected: two-phase + SSL yields highest final mAP. 3) Linear probe classification: freeze pre-trained encoder, train only classification head on full 2,244 labeled samples. Expected test accuracy ≥94%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why does semi-supervised learning with pseudo-labeling degrade classification performance despite improving detection, and how can this distribution mismatch be mitigated? The paper attributes it to "noisy pseudo-labels from distribution mismatch" but does not investigate further.
2. Is the two-phase training (frozen encoder then fine-tuning) necessary for stable semi-supervised detection, or could end-to-end training from random initialization achieve comparable performance? No ablation compares these schedules.
3. How does Vertex Relative Position Encoding (3D RPE) compare to alternative position encoding schemes (e.g., absolute, center-based, or learned embeddings) in 3D medical detection under label scarcity? No experiments compare 3D RPE to other encodings.
4. To what extent do the self-supervised features learned from abdominal CT generalize to other medical imaging tasks and anatomies without fine-tuning? Transfer is only evaluated within the same abdominal trauma domain; broader generalization is unknown.

## Limitations
- Data split criteria for the 1,206 pre-training and 2,000 unlabeled detection volumes are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Critical architectural details are missing: VDETR decoder depth, attention heads, query count, feature adapter sampling method, and elastic deformation parameters.
- No quantitative reconstruction results (e.g., PSNR) are reported for the pre-training phase, leaving feature quality unverified.

## Confidence
- Self-supervised pre-training yields transferable representations: **Medium** (well-motivated but lacking reconstruction metrics).
- Vertex Relative Position Encoding improves 3D localization: **Medium** (mechanism described but no ablation against alternatives).
- Semi-supervised consistency regularization stabilizes detection with 144 labeled samples: **Medium** (standard framework but pseudo-label noise effects unstudied).

## Next Checks
1. **Pre-training integrity check:** Compute PSNR on 250 held-out unlabeled volumes after 50 MIM epochs; target >19 dB. Visualize masked/reconstructed patches to confirm anatomical coherence.
2. **SSL activation timing ablation:** Compare detection performance with (a) encoder frozen throughout, (b) two-phase schedule without SSL, (c) full proposed pipeline. Isolate contributions of fine-tuning and consistency.
3. **Linear probe classification benchmark:** Freeze pre-trained encoder, train only classification head on full 2,244 labeled samples. Expected test accuracy ≥94%. A significant drop indicates poor feature transferability.
