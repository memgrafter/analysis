# Exact Federated Continual Unlearning for Ridge Heads on Frozen Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12977
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12977
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves exact federated continual unlearning for ridge regression heads on frozen foundation models via additive sufficient statistics, matching centralized retraining to within ~1e-9 relative Frobenius error and reducing per-request wall-clock cost by orders of magnitude compared to exact unlearning baselines.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of exact federated continual unlearning—guaranteeing that a model after any sequence of data addition or deletion requests is pointwise identical to one retrained from scratch on the retained data—specifically for ridge regression heads built on top of frozen foundation models. The core insight is that the ridge optimum depends only on two additive sufficient statistics: the feature Gram matrix and the feature-label moment. Clients transmit fixed-size updates of these statistics for each event; the server maintains a global ledger and solves for the head exactly. Two variants are proposed: a robust Cholesky-based solver and a more efficient Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury incremental update. Experiments across vision and text datasets confirm near-perfect equivalence to centralized retraining and substantial efficiency gains over prior exact unlearning methods.

## Method Summary
The protocol centers on server-side maintenance of two global sufficient statistics: the feature Gram matrix \( S_t = \sum_{i \in \text{retained}} f_i f_i^\top \) and the feature-label moment \( G_t = \sum_{i \in \text{retained}} f_i y_i^\top \), where \( f_i = \phi(x_i) \) are features from a deterministic frozen backbone. Upon an add/delete event, affected clients compute their local contributions \( (S^\pm, G^\pm) \) and send them to the server. The server updates the ledger via simple addition/subtraction: \( (S_t, G_t) \leftarrow (S_{t-1} + S^+_t - S^-_t, G_{t-1} + G^+_t - G^-_t) \). The ridge head is then recovered exactly as \( W_t = (S_t + \gamma I)^{-1} G_t \). Variant A recomputes this inverse directly (e.g., via Cholesky). Variant B incrementally updates the inverse \( T_t = (S_t + \gamma I)^{-1} \) using Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formulas, exploiting low-rank structure when clients transmit QR factors of their feature matrices instead of full Gram matrices. Both variants guarantee pointwise equivalence to centralized ridge retraining in exact arithmetic.

## Key Results
- The federated protocol produces a ridge head identical to centralized retraining after every add/delete request, with relative Frobenius error \( \|W_{\text{fed}} - W_{\text{cent}}\|_F / \|W_{\text{cent}}\|_F \) consistently below \( 10^{-9} \) in FP64 arithmetic.
- It handles arbitrary continual streams of sample-level add and delete events with no degradation in exactness over hundreds of simulated rounds.
- Per-request wall-clock time is orders of magnitude lower than FedAvg-based exact unlearning baselines (e.g., Exact-Fun, FATS), which trigger full retraining; the proposed methods involve only smallmatrix factorizations and solves.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Exactness via Additive Sufficient Statistics
Claim: Exact federated unlearning is achieved by maintaining additive sufficient statistics \( (S_t, G_t) \) on the server.
Mechanism: Clients compute and transmit \( (S^\pm, G^\pm) \) for add/delete batches. The server updates a global ledger via \( (S_t, G_t) \leftarrow (S_{t-1} + S^+_t - S^-_t, G_{t-1} + G^+_t - G^-_t) \). The ridge head is recovered exactly as \( W_t = (S_t + \gamma I)^{-1} G_t \). This is pointwise identical to centralized retraining because ridge regression's optimum depends *only* on these statistics.
Core assumption: The frozen backbone \( \phi \) is deterministic at inference time, so feature vectors \( f \) are fixed and reproducible for deletion.
Evidence anchors:
- [abstract]: "the exact optimum depends on the data only through two additive sufficient statistics... The server maintains a head that is, in exact arithmetic, pointwise identical to centralized retraining."
- [section 4]: "the exact optimum is available in closed form and depends on the data only through two second-order sufficient statistics... federated continual unlearning reduces to maintaining these statistics exactly."
- [section 6.1 Theorem 1]: Proof that maintaining \( (S_t, G_t) \) exactly yields \( W_t \) equal to centralized ridge retraining.
Break condition: If a client cannot reconstruct exact \( f \) for a deleted sample (e.g., non-deterministic backbone without caching), the protocol fails exactness.

### Mechanism 2: Efficiency via Incremental Inverse Updates
Claim: Variant B achieves per-round efficiency by incrementally updating the inverse \( T_t = (S_t + \gamma I)^{-1} \) via Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury (SMW) formulas, avoiding full solves.
Mechanism: Clients transmit QR factors \( R_k \) of their feature matrices (\( S_k = R_k^\top R_k \)). The server stacks these into \( U \) and applies SMW add/downdate updates to \( T \) and \( W \) using \( U \) and the label moment \( G \). This exploits the low-rank structure of per-round updates.
Core assumption: The per-round Gram update \( \Delta S = U^\top U \) has a factorable form and the downdate feasibility condition \( I_r - U T U^\top \succ 0 \) holds at each step.
Evidence anchors:
- [section 5 Variant B]: "incremental inverse updates via SMW... Let \( T = H^{-1} \) and write \( \Delta S = U^\top U \)... Delete: \( T_- = T + T U^\top (I_r - U T U^\top)^{-1} U^\top T \)."
- [section 6.3 Theorem 3]: Proves Variant B yields the same \( T_t \) and \( W_t \) as Variant A in exact arithmetic when feasibility holds.
Break condition: Finite-precision drift causes \( I_r - U T U^\top \) to lose positive definiteness, making SMW downdate numerically unstable or invalid.

### Mechanism 3: Bayesian Certificate of Exact Unlearning
Claim: The protocol provides a Bayesian certificate of exact unlearning: it maintains the *entire exact posterior* over ridge weights, implying zero KL divergence to a retrained posterior.
Mechanism: Interpreting ridge regression as Bayesian linear regression with Gaussian prior, the posterior \( W | (F, Y) \sim \mathcal{MN}(M, \Sigma, I_c) \) where \( \Sigma \propto (S+\gamma I)^{-1} \) and \( M = (S+\gamma I)^{-1} G \). Since the protocol maintains \( (S_t, G_t) \) exactly, it maintains the exact posterior parameters after each add/delete.
Core assumption: The Bayesian model specification (Gaussian likelihood \( \mathcal{N}(W^\top f, \sigma^2 I) \) and isotropic Gaussian prior \( \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2 I) \) with \( \gamma = \sigma^2/\tau^2 \)) is correct.
Evidence anchors:
- [section 6.6 Lemma 3]: Derives the matrix-normal posterior form depending exactly on \( (S, G) \).
- [section 6.6 Theorem 4]: "The protocol maintains \( (S_t, G_t) \) exactly and therefore maintains the exact posterior... \( \text{KL}(\Pi_t \| \Pi_t^{\text{retrain}}) = 0 \)."
- [abstract]: Mentions "Bayesian certificate of zero KL divergence."
Break condition: If the true data-generating process violates Gaussian prior/likelihood assumptions, the posterior certificate is mathematically valid but may not correspond to a "retrained" posterior under a different learning objective.

## Foundational Learning
- **Ridge Regression Closed Form**
  - Why needed here: The entire protocol relies on the fact that the ridge optimum \( W^* \) is a deterministic function of sufficient statistics \( (S, G) \). Without this analytic form, exact additive updates are impossible.
  - Quick check: Given \( \min_W \|Y - F W\|_F^2 + \gamma \|W\|_F^2 \), what are the sufficient statistics and the closed-form solution?

- **Frozen Backbone Determinism**
  - Why needed here: Exact unlearning requires that the feature \( f = \phi(x) \) for any \( x \) is reproducible without access to the original \( x \). If \( \phi \) is stochastic, caching \( f \) at add-time is mandatory; otherwise, deletion cannot reconstruct exact statistics.
  - Quick check: Why would a stochastic backbone (e.g., with dropout) break exactness unless features are cached?

- **Sufficient Statistics Additivity**
  - Why needed here: The protocol's ledger update \( S_t = S_{t-1} + S^+_t - S^-_t \) works only because \( S \) and \( G \) are sums over individual sample contributions (\( \sum f_i f_i^\top \), \( \sum f_i y_i^\top \)). This linearity is what enables subtractive unlearning.
  - Quick check: Show that for disjoint datasets A and B, \( S_{A\cup B} = S_A + S_B \) and \( G_{A\cup B} = G_A + G_B \). What would break if we used only \( \sum f_i \) (first moment)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Client (with frozen \( \phi \)) computes local \( (F^\pm, Y^\pm) \) → forms/transmits \( (S^\pm, G^\pm) \) or \( (R^\pm, G^\pm) \) → Server maintains global ledger \( (S_t, G_t) \) and computes \( W_t \) via Variant A or B → broadcasts \( W_t \) to clients.
- Critical path:
  1. Initial add: all clients send initial statistics; server computes first \( W_1 \).
  2. Request round: clients with add/delete events compute and send local statistics.
  3. Server aggregates received \( (S^+, G^+, S^-, G^-) \), updates \( (S_t, G_t) \), computes \( W_t \).
  4. Server broadcasts \( W_t \) to all clients for inference.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Variant A (Cholesky): Robust (FP64 solves), high communication (\( \Theta(d^2 + dc) \) per client), compute \( O(d^3) \). Suitable for moderate \( d \) (e.g., 768) when robustness is critical.
  - Variant B (SMW): Low communication (\( \Theta(dr + dc) \) with \( r \) batch rank), compute \( O(rd^2) \). Requires feasibility checks (\( I_r - U T U^\top \succ 0 \)) and periodic resets to Variant A to control drift. Better for large \( d \) or frequent small updates.
  - Feature caching: Mandatory if \( \phi \) non-deterministic; incurs storage cost on client.
- Failure signatures:
  - Exactness loss: Relative Frobenius error \( \|W_{\text{fed}} - W_{\text{cent}}\|_F / \|W_{\text{cent}}\|_F \) exceeds \( 10^{-5} \) (should be ~\( 10^{-9} \) in FP64). Indicates floating-point drift (Variant B) or incorrect \( f \) caching.
  - SMW failure: Cholesky of \( I_r - U T U^\top \) fails or yields non-positive diagonal. Switch to Variant A reset.
  - Feasibility violation: Downdate requested when \( S_{t-1}+\gamma I + S^+_t - S^-_t \) is not SPD. Must reject request or reset to Variant A recompute.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate retrain equivalence: After initial full add, compare \( W_t \) from Variant A/B to centralized ridge retrain across all retained data via relative Frobenius error. Expect ≤ \( 10^{-9} \).
  2. Stress continual stream: Simulate 200 random single-point delete requests (interleaved with random add-backs). After each, compute \( W_t \) and compare to centralized retrain on current retained set. Track max error; expect ≤ \( 10^{-8} \).
  3. Measure efficiency: For 100 clients, time a single-point delete in Variant B vs. Exact-Fun/FATS baseline. Expect ms vs. seconds, because no full retraining is triggered.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1:** How can the exact unlearning guarantee be extended to scenarios where the foundation model backbone is trainable and was itself trained on revocable user data?  
  Basis: Explicitly stated in Scope: "If the backbone itself was trained on revocable data, removing head influence alone is not sufficient; backbone unlearning is an orthogonal problem and remains challenging."  
  Why unresolved: Requires disentangling influence of deleted data on backbone parameters, unsolved for general deep networks.

- **Q2:** What are the concrete privacy leakage risks of transmitting second-order sufficient statistics (S, G), and how can they be rigorously mitigated without sacrificing exactness?  
  Basis: Explicit in Privacy considerations: "second-order statistics can still leak information... Practical deployments can mitigate this by combining... with differential privacy mechanisms."  
  Why unresolved: No analysis of DP noise vs. exactness trade-off; inherent leakage from (S, G) not quantified.

- **Q3:** For Variant B's SMW updates, what are the precise conditions and a practical schedule for "periodic resets" to Variant A that guarantee bounded error accumulation over very long, arbitrary add/delete streams in finite precision?  
  Basis: Explicit: "periodic resets to Variant A provide a practical safeguard when accumulated updates become ill-conditioned," but no specific schedule.  
  Why unresolved: Feasibility condition \( I_r - U T U^\top \succ 0 \) can drift; optimal reset policy (based on condition number, update count, error monitoring) is not provided.

- **Q4:** Under what conditions can approximate, low-rank/sketched updates to the sufficient statistics be used while still satisfying a useful, verified notion of "approximate exact unlearning"?  
  Basis: Inferred from Appendix A2: perturbation bounds for approximate Gram updates (Theorem 5) and suggestion of periodic exact resets, but no formal criterion for "good enough" approximation in continual setting.  
  Why unresolved: Bounds are one-step static; approximation errors compound. No guidance on how much spectral mass can be discarded per round to ensure aggregate deviation remains within a certified tolerance.

## Limitations
- Missing experimental details (ridge regularization \( \gamma \) values, exact backbone checkpoints and preprocessing, client partitioning hyperparameters, deletion sampling strategy, caching policy) impede precise replication and comparative analysis.
- Variant B (SMW) depends on floating-point stability; even with FP64, drift may accumulate over thousands of updates, potentially breaking exactness without a theoretically guided reset schedule.
- The Bayesian certificate assumes ridge regression's Gaussian posterior aligns with the centralized retraining objective; if retraining uses a different loss (e.g., cross-entropy), the zero-KL claim may not reflect practical model equivalence.
- Experiments are confined to four datasets with feature dimension 768; scalability to higher dimensions or fully-connected heads remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Exactness via additive sufficient statistics** (Mechanism 1): High – rigorous proof (Theorem 1), additive linearity is mathematically airtight.
- **Efficiency via SMW updates** (Mechanism 2): Medium – SMW formulas are correct, but feasibility and finite-precision drift raise practical concerns.
- **Bayesian certificate** (Mechanism 3): Medium – valid under ridge’s Bayesian interpretation; relevance hinges on alignment with centralized retraining objective.
- **Empirical results** (near-zero error, speedups): Medium – plausible given theory, yet missing hyperparameters prevent independent verification.
