# Evidence Slopes and Effective Dimension in Singular Linear Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.01238
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01238
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** In singular linear-Gaussian models, Laplace/BIC suffers a leading error of \((d/2 - \lambda)\log n\) where \(\lambda\) is the real log canonical threshold (RLCT); an RLCT-aware correction eliminates this error. RLCT and exact evidence are invariant to overcomplete reparameterizations preserving the data subspace, while BIC scores differ by \(\approx (d-d')/2\log n\).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates Bayesian model selection in singular models—where the Fisher information matrix is rank-deficient—using linear-Gaussian regression and dictionary models as tractable test cases. The authors derive exact closed-form marginal likelihoods, enabling precise characterization of the asymptotic behavior. They show that the standard Laplace approximation and BIC, which penalize by the ambient parameter count \(d\), are incorrect when the intrinsic rank \(r < d\); the true penalty is governed by the real log canonical threshold (RLCT) \(\lambda = r/2\). Consequently, the error of these approximations grows linearly as \((d/2 - \lambda)\log n = (d-r)/2 \log n\) with sample size \(n\). By replacing \(d/2\) with \(\lambda\), the authors obtain an RLCT-aware correction that recovers the correct evidence slope. Moreover, for dictionary models representing the same signal subspace with different numbers of basis vectors (overcomplete vs. minimal parametrizations), the RLCT and the exact marginal likelihood are invariant, while BIC scores differ by approximately \((d-d')/2\log n\). The paper also proposes a simple empirical method: regressing the log marginal likelihood against \(\log n\) yields an estimator of \(\lambda\), providing a direct finite-sample characterization of the Laplace failure.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze singular linear-Gaussian models (rank-deficient regression and dictionary models) by deriving exact marginal likelihoods in closed form. They identify the real log canonical threshold (RLCT) \(\lambda\) as the effective dimension controlling the asymptotic \(-\lambda \log n\) term. By comparing the exact evidence with the Laplace approximation and BIC (which use a penalty of \(d/2\log n\)), they derive an analytical expression for the leading error: \((d/2 - \lambda)\log n\). They also prove that the RLCT and exact evidence depend only on the intrinsic rank \(r\) of the signal subspace, not on the number of parameters in overcomplete dictionaries, while BIC is not invariant. Empirically, they validate that the slope of \(\log Z_n\) vs. \(\log n\) estimates \(\lambda\) and that the RLCT-corrected approximation error has near-zero slope.

## Key Results
- Laplace/BIC error grows linearly as \((d/2 - \lambda)\log n = (d-r)/2\log n\) for singular models with intrinsic rank \(r\).
- RLCT-aware correction (using \(\lambda\) instead of \(d/2\)) eliminates the leading error, yielding a near-zero slope.
- RLCT and exact evidence are invariant to overcomplete reparameterizations preserving the data subspace; BIC scores differ by \(\approx (d-d')/2\log n\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** In singular linear models (intrinsic rank \(r < d\)), the Laplace/BIC approximation error grows linearly as \((d/2 - \lambda)\log n\). The reason is that Laplace/BIC uses a penalty of \(d/2\log n\) based on the ambient parameter count \(d\), while singular learning theory shows the correct penalty is \(\lambda \log n\), where \(\lambda\) is the RLCT (equal to \(r/2\) for these models). The difference \((d/2 - \lambda)\log n\) reflects the over-penalty due to redundant parameters. This error term dominates unless \(r = d\).

**Mechanism 2:** RLCT and exact marginal likelihood are invariant under overcomplete reparameterizations that preserve the data subspace, while BIC scores differ. This occurs because RLCT depends only on the intrinsic dimension \(r\) of the signal subspace, not on the number of basis vectors \(d\) or \(d'\) used to represent it. BIC, however, penalizes by the ambient dimension, so it artificially favors minimal parametrizations even when they induce identical distributions. The invariance is proven for linear dictionary models; if the subspaces differ, both RLCT and evidence change.

**Mechanism 3:** Regressing the log marginal likelihood against \(\log n\) yields an empirical estimator of \(\lambda\). The asymptotic expansion \(\log Z_n = \log p(D_n|\theta^\star) - \lambda \log n + O(1)\) implies that for large \(n\), the dominant \(n\)-dependence is through the \(-\lambda \log n\) term. Thus the slope of \(\log Z_n\) vs. \(\log n\) converges to \(-\lambda\).

## Foundational Learning
- **Real Log Canonical Threshold (RLCT)**  
  - *Why needed:* Replaces the naive parameter count \(d\) with an effective dimension \(\lambda\) that accounts for model singularities (redundant parameters). It determines the correct \(\log n\) penalty in marginal likelihood asymptotics.  
  - *Quick check:* In a rank‑\(r\) linear regression with \(d\) parameters, what is the RLCT \(\lambda\)? (Answer: \(r/2\))

- **Laplace Approximation & BIC**  
  - *Why needed:* Standard tools for approximating marginal likelihoods, but they assume regularity (full Fisher rank). This paper contrasts their \(d/2\) penalty with the RLCT-based penalty to expose failure modes.  
  - *Quick check:* Why does BIC over‑penalize a singular model with \(d > r\)? (Because it uses \(d/2\) instead of the effective \(\lambda = r/2\).)

- **Linear-Gaussian Models with Rank Deficiency**  
  - *Why needed:* Provide a tractable setting where exact evidence and RLCT are analytically computable, serving as a clean testbed to isolate the effect of singularities.  
  - *Quick check:* How does the Gram matrix \(S_n = A_n^\top A_n\) reflect the intrinsic rank \(r\) as \(n \to \infty\)? (Its rank converges to \(r\), and its eigenvalues related to \(n\) scale linearly, influencing the \(\log\det\) term.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Model definitions -> Exact evidence derivation -> RLCT computation -> Approximation setup -> Error analysis -> Invariance proof -> Experimental validation

**Critical path:**  
Start with linear-Gaussian model → derive exact evidence → extract RLCT from large-\(n\) asymptotics → compute Laplace/BIC → subtract to reveal error slope \((d/2 - \lambda) \log n\) → extend to dictionaries → prove representation invariance → validate with synthetic experiments.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Simplicity vs. generality: Linear-Gaussian assumptions yield closed-form results but may not capture the full complexity of neural network singularities.  
- Analytic vs. empirical RLCT: The paper uses known \(\lambda = r/2\); in practice one would estimate \(\lambda\) from slopes, which requires enough \(n\) values and may be noisy for small samples.  
- Prior sensitivity: The analysis assumes specific Gaussian priors (isotropic); different priors could affect constant terms but (in these linear models) not the leading \(\lambda \log n\) term.

**Failure signatures:**  
- BIC score changes when adding redundant dictionary columns (same subspace).  
- Non-zero slope in \((\log Z_{\text{Lap}} - \log Z_{\text{exact}})\) vs. \(\log n\) when \(r < d\).  
- Estimated evidence slope significantly differs from \(r/2\) (for known \(r\)) in experiments, indicating either model misspecification or insufficient \(n\).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Rank sweep:** For fixed \(d\), vary \(r\) from 1 to \(d\); regress BIC error \(\Delta_{\text{BIC}}(n)\) on \(\log n\). Expect slope \(\approx (d - r)/2\); RLCT-corrected error slope near zero.  
2. **Regular vs singular:** Compare \(r = d\) and \(r < d\) with same \(d\). BIC error slope should be ~0 for regular, negative for singular; RLCT-corrected slope ~0 in both.  
3. **Dictionary invariance:** Construct minimal dictionary (\(d = r\)) and overcomplete dictionary (\(d' > r\)) spanning same subspace. Exact evidences should differ by \(O(1)\); RLCT-corrected approximations identical; BIC differs by \(\approx (d - d')/2 \log n\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can the slope-based empirical estimator of the RLCT, validated for linear-Gaussian models, be extended to accurately estimate effective dimensions in nonlinear or neural network settings?  
2. What are the precise conditions under which Bayesian evidence and its RLCT become invariant to overcomplete reparameterizations in more complex models like deep neural networks?  
3. Does the \((m-1)\log\log n\) term in the singular learning theory expansion have a measurable, systematic impact on evidence slopes in finite samples within the linear-Gaussian models?  
4. How sensitive are the derived error slopes and the representation invariance property to violations of Gaussian noise and prior assumptions, or to random (rather than fixed) design matrices?  
5. Can the insight that BIC's penalty should depend on intrinsic rank \(r\) rather than ambient dimension \(d\) be translated into a practical, automatic model selection criterion for choosing between minimal and overcomplete representations without requiring knowledge of the true RLCT?

## Limitations
- Theoretical results are confined to linear‑Gaussian models; extension to non‑linear singular learning remains open.  
- Experimental hyperparameters (σ², τ², number of replicates, n grid) are not fully reported, impeding exact replication.  
- Finite‑sample bias of the empirical slope estimator is not characterized; O(1) and \(\log\log n\) terms may distort estimates for moderate \(n\).
