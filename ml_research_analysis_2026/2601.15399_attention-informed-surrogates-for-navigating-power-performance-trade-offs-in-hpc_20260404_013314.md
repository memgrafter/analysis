# Attention-Informed Surrogates for Navigating Power-Performance Trade-offs in HPC
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.15399
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15399
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Surrogate-assisted MOBO with attention embeddings achieves orders‑of‑magnitude higher hypervolume and 90–99% lower spread than baselines on two production HPC datasets.

## Executive Summary
HPC schedulers must balance job runtime and power consumption under noisy, irregular telemetry. This paper presents a surrogate‑assisted multi‑objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) framework that uses attention‑based embeddings (TabNet) to extract informative features from job logs, training lightweight regressors (RF, XGBoost) as surrogates. The MOBO loop with qEHVI acquisition efficiently explores the runtime‑power trade‑off space. On two production datasets (PM100: 231k jobs; Adastra: 15k jobs), the approach yields dramatically better Pareto fronts (hypervolume up by orders of magnitude, spread down 90–99%) and reduces training data needs by 25–50% while cutting end‑to‑end runtime by up to 27%, enabling automated optimal node allocation decisions.

## Method Summary
The pipeline starts with preprocessing raw job logs: cleaning, aggregating power, deriving time features, and encoding categoricals into a numeric view. An intelligent sampler selects a difficulty‑proportional subset using lightweight per‑target predictors, balancing sample hardness with randomness. TabNet then generates attention‑based embeddings from this subset, which train fast regressors (Random Forest, XGBoost) to predict runtime and power. These surrogates feed a MOBO loop (BoTorch, qEHVI acquisition) that iteratively proposes node allocations to maximize expected hypervolume improvement. After 300 evaluations per job, the Pareto front is assessed via hypervolume and spread metrics. Baselines include single‑objective BO and random search.

## Key Results
- Orders‑of‑magnitude higher hypervolume and 90–99% lower spread than TabNet regressors and single‑objective baselines.
- Intelligent sampling with 50–75% of data maintains surrogate accuracy (MAPE ≈ 0.99) and improves stability.
- Total runtime reduced by up to 27% (e.g., Adastra: 2699s → 1962s).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Attention‑based embeddings improve surrogate quality
**Claim:** Attention mechanisms filter noise and highlight informative features in irregular HPC telemetry, enabling simpler regressors to generalize better.
**Mechanism:** TabNet learns feature‑level attention weights to assign importance, suppressing irrelevant signals. The resulting low‑dimensional embeddings are robust to missing values and variability.
**Core assumption:** Attention can reliably identify predictive job characteristics in noisy logs.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- [abstract]: hypothesis that embeddings capture performance dynamics more effectively.
- [section 3]: “we use attention‑based embeddings to highlight informative features and suppress irrelevant or redundant inputs.”
**Break condition:** Attention weights become uniform or telemetry too sparse, causing surrogate accuracy to drop below baselines.

### Mechanism 2: MOBO discovers higher‑quality Pareto fronts
**Claim:** Multi‑objective Bayesian optimization with qEHVI finds better runtime‑power trade‑offs than single‑objective BO or random search.
**Mechanism:** Trained surrogates predict both objectives for candidates; qEHVI selects points that maximize expected hypervolume improvement, balancing exploration and exploitation.
**Core assumption:** Surrogates are accurate enough to guide acquisition.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- [abstract]: “MOBO consistently outperformed SOBO and random search.”
- [section 3]: integration of surrogates into MOBO framework.
**Break condition:** Surrogate errors are large, causing misinformative proposals, or acquisition becomes overly greedy.

### Mechanism 3: Intelligent sample acquisition reduces data needs
**Claim:** Loss‑proportional active learning cuts training data by 25–50% with negligible accuracy loss and improves stability, reducing end‑to‑end runtime by up to 27%.
**Mechanism:** Lightweight predictors estimate each sample’s difficulty; sampling probabilities scale with difficulty (clipped), emphasizing hard samples while retaining random floor.
**Core assumption:** Difficulty scores correlate with true information content.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- [abstract]: “intelligent sample acquisition strategy to ensure the approach is data‑efficient” and “drastically reduced training costs while improving the stability.”
- [Supplementary]: algorithm details.
**Break condition:** Initial predictors biased or sampling fraction below ~40%.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Preprocessor → Intelligent sampler → TabNet embedder → Lightweight regressors (RF/XGBoost) → MOBO engine (BoTorch/qEHVI) → Evaluator → Dashboard  
**Critical path:** Preprocess → Sample → Train TabNet → Transform data → Train regressors → Initialize MOBO → Iterate acquisition & surrogate update → Compute Pareto metrics → Output recommendation.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Embedding vs raw: Embeddings add steps but improve robustness; may lose fine details.  
- Sampling fraction: Smaller fractions speed training but risk losing informative points; 50–75% is sweet spot.  
- MOBO vs SOBO: MOBO exposes trade‑offs but costs more per iteration; SOBO cheaper but yields only one objective.  
- Model choice: Tree‑based regressors are fast and interpretable; TabNet regressor heavier.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Surrogate MAPE rises >5% on validation.  
- Hypervolume fails to exceed random search.  
- Spread remains orders of magnitude higher than reported.  
- Attention weights become nearly uniform.  
- Sampling does not reduce training time (overhead cancels gains).  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Embedding impact:** Compare TabNet embedding + RF against TabNet regressor (direct) on full data. Verify higher HV, lower spread.  
2. **MOBO vs baselines:** Run MOBO, SOBO (runtime), SOBO (power), Random using best embedding-informed surrogate. Verify MOBO dominates.  
3. **Sampling sweep:** Vary sampling fraction (0.5, 0.75, 1.0), record MAPE, HV, spread, total runtime. Confirm 50–75% retains accuracy while cutting time.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What are end‑to‑end overheads and latency of the MOBO framework in live HPC schedulers? Can cached embeddings and warm‑started retraining reduce BO cycle time to meet operational budgets?  
2. How does the framework scale when optimizing more than two objectives, and which acquisition functions best balance Pareto quality and computational cost in higher dimensions?  
3. Under varying data regimes (sample size, noise, sparsity), what are the boundaries where lightweight regressors outperform transformer‑based surrogates in accuracy, stability, and compute/latency?  
4. How well do attention‑based embeddings generalize across diverse HPC systems? Can adapter‑based fine‑tuning or re‑embedding with minimal calibration mitigate domain shift?

## Limitations
- Exact TabNet hyperparameters and sampler classification targets are unspecified, hindering exact replication.  
- Evaluation restricted to two production datasets; generalizability to other systems unknown.  
- Reported gains may stem from the combination of embeddings and lightweight regressors rather than attention alone.

## Confidence
- Embedding‑informed surrogates improve surrogate quality: **Medium** (evidence supportive but details missing, risk of overfitting).  
- MOBO consistently outperforms baselines: **High** (consistent orders‑of‑magnitude gains across datasets and seeds).  
- Intelligent sample acquisition reduces data needs: **Medium** (results depend on λ tuning and difficulty metric; performance degrades below ~40% fraction).

## Next Checks
1. **Embedding robustness:** Vary TabNet architecture (n_steps ∈ [1,5], n_d/n_a ∈ [4,16]) and measure impact on surrogate MAPE and hypervolume; confirm gains persist across reasonable hyperparameters.  
2. **Discrete BO validation:** Replace continuous BO with an integer‑valued GP or directly optimize integer node counts; compare Pareto quality to the rounding approach to rule out discretization bias.  
3. **Sampler ablation:** At 50% and 75% fractions, compare difficulty‑proportional sampling against uniform random using identical pipelines; evaluate whether hypervolume and spread improvements hold and quantify stability across seeds.
