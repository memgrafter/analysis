# M$^2$RNN: Non-Linear RNNs with Matrix-Valued States for Scalable Language Modeling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14360  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14360  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** M²RNN achieves perfect state-tracking generalization (S₃) and, in hybrid architectures, reduces perplexity by 0.4–0.5 points over Gated DeltaNet on a 7B MoE model while using 3× smaller recurrent states.

## Executive Summary
M²RNN introduces a novel non-linear recurrent layer that replaces vector-valued hidden states with matrix-valued states and uses an outer-product state expansion to boost effective capacity without proportional parameter growth. This design addresses key limitations of Transformers (TC⁰ complexity) and linear RNNs (poor state tracking and retrieval). A parallelizable forget gate stabilizes training and enables efficient parallelism. In hybrid models interleaved with attention, M²RNN improves language modeling perplexity and long-context performance with minimal throughput impact, demonstrating a promising path for scalable sequence modeling.

## Method Summary
M²RNN is a single sequence-mixing layer that can replace attention or linear RNN layers. It maintains a matrix-valued hidden state $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times V}$ and updates it via $H_t = f_t H_{t-1} + (1 - f_t) \tanh(H_{t-1} W + k_t v_t^\top)$, where $k_t, v_t$ are derived from the input and $f_t$ is an input-dependent forget gate ($f_t = (1+\exp(x_t + \beta_n))^{-\alpha_n}$). The output $y_t = H_t^\top q_t + w_r \odot v_t$ combines a readout from the state and a residual connection. The forget gate depends only on the current input, making the recurrence parallelizable across time via a scan operation while still providing gradient stability. For tensor parallelism, two strategies are proposed: topology-aware (grouped-value) and topology-independent (multi-value with sharded RMSNorm). In practice, M²RNN layers are interleaved with attention (e.g., one attention layer per eight recurrent layers) in hybrid architectures, and trained with standard LM objectives (AdamW, cosine decay, gradient clipping).

## Key Results
- M²RNN achieves **perfect generalization** on the S₃ state-tracking task, while linear RNN baselines fail beyond training lengths.
- In a 7B MoE hybrid model, M²RNN **reduces perplexity by 0.4–0.5 points** compared to Gated DeltaNet while using **3× smaller** recurrent states.
- Adding even a **single M²RNN layer** to a hybrid model improves long-context accuracy by **up to 8 points** on LongBench with **<6% throughput loss**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Outer Product State Expansion
- **Claim:** The outer product $k_t v_t^\top$ in the state update significantly increases effective recurrent state capacity without proportional parameter growth, enhancing language modeling and in-context retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike vector-valued RNNs ($h_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$), M²RNN uses $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times V}$. The rank-1 outer product contributes to the state expansion, allowing storage of many key-value associations while keeping learnable parameters (e.g., $W \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times V}$) modest.
- **Evidence:** Section 5.6 shows state-size ablation: increasing from vector-valued RNN (1,360) to M²RNN (86,016) yields >10× perplexity reduction on WikiText and 280 on LAMBADA. The outer product structure is central to this capacity.
- **Break condition:** If $K$ or $V$ become very large, per-step FLOPs may outweigh benefits; practical models balance $K=64, V=16$.

### Input‑Independent Forget Gate for Stability and Parallelism
- **Claim:** The forget gate $f_t = \psi(x_t)$, computed solely from the current input, stabilizes training by mitigating vanishing gradients and allows the recurrent steps to be parallelized.
- **Mechanism:** The gate controls state retention via $H_t = f_t H_{t-1} + (1 - f_t) Z_t$. Because $f_t$ does not depend on $H_{t-1}$, the recurrence remains amenable to parallel scan while avoiding state‑dependent multiplicative interactions that could break associativity.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.1.1 details the formulation and its role in enabling parallel training; Section 2.5.3 contrasts with inefficient naive non‑linear RNNs.
- **Break condition:** If $f_t$ saturates (near 0 or 1) for most inputs, gradient regulation fails; careful initialization of $\alpha_n, \beta_n$ per head is required.

### Nonlinear State Transition for Expressivity
- **Claim:** The $\tanh$ nonlinearity applied to the combination $H_{t-1}W + k_t v_t^\top$ equips M²RNN with expressivity beyond TC⁰ (e.g., solving S₃, S₅), which linear RNNs cannot achieve.
- **Mechanism:** The recurrence forms a non‑linear dynamical system. Theorem 1 shows M²RNN can simulate any vector‑valued non‑linear RNN, which can recognize all regular languages and solve NC¹‑complete tasks. The matrix state adds degrees of freedom.
- **Evidence:** M²RNN attains **>99.5%** accuracy on S₃ (Figure 3), while Gated DeltaNet and Gated DeltaProduct degrade beyond training length.
- **Break condition:** The non‑linearity forces sequential recurrence, preventing parallel scan training; if length generalization fails on longer tasks, the model may overfit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Recurrent Neural Networks and State Size Bottleneck**  
  *Why needed:* Traditional RNNs (LSTM, GRU) use fixed‑size vector states, limiting capacity linearly with dimension. Attention grows with sequence length, giving Transformers an advantage. M²RNN’s matrix state breaks this bottleneck.  
  *Quick check:* Why do LSTMs lag behind Transformers in language modeling despite gating? (Their hidden state is a fixed‑size vector, whereas attention can attend to all previous tokens.)

- **Hardware‑Efficient Deep Learning: Tensor Cores and GEMM Utilization**  
  *Why needed:* M²RNN’s kernels are designed to avoid padding waste and maximize tensor core occupancy. Understanding GEMM (matrix multiply) and alignment (multiples of 16) explains why FlashRNN’s padding strategy wastes FLOPs for small batches.  
  *Quick check:* Why does padding the batch dimension to multiples of 16 waste computation when the actual batch is small? (Many zero‑valued elements are processed.)

- **Computational Expressivity and Complexity Classes**  
  *Why needed:* The paper positions M²RNN as overcoming the TC⁰ limitation of Transformers and linear RNNs. Familiarity with TC⁰, NC¹, and their circuit‑depth correspondence clarifies why non‑linear recurrence is necessary for tasks like code execution or entity tracking.  
  *Quick check:* Can a Transformer with fixed attention span solve the S₅ permutation task? (No, because S₅ is NC¹‑complete and lies outside TC⁰.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input sequence -> Pre‑recurrence projections (linear + depthwise causal conv + SiLU) -> Split into qt, kt, vt, ft, gt -> Recurrence core (matrix state Ht update via ft, outer product, tanh) -> Readout (Ht^T qt + residual) -> Post‑processing (RMSNorm on yt ⊙ gt) -> Linear projection -> Output.

### Critical Path
For one head on one GPU during training:
1. Compute qt, kt, vt, ft, gt in parallel across batch and sequence.
2. Sequentially update state: load $H_{t-1}$, compute $Z_t = \tanh(H_{t-1}W + k_t v_t^\top)$, combine with forget gate: $H_t = f_t H_{t-1} + (1 - f_t) Z_t$, and produce output $y_t = H_t^\top q_t + w_r \odot v_t$. Cache $H_t$ for backward.
3. Backward: recompute forward recurrence (without readout) while caching $H_t$, then sweep backward.
4. For tensor parallelism (topology‑independent), RMSNorm requires an AllReduce before/after recurrence; activations may be sharded.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **State size (K, V) vs FLOPs:** Larger $K, V$ increase capacity but raise per‑step GEMM cost ($H_{t-1}W$ is $K \times V \times V$). Paper uses $K=64, V=16$ as a sweet spot.
- **Number of M²RNN layers:** Using all recurrent layers yields best accuracy but ~30% slower throughput. One layer (or three at 410M) gives most gain with <6% throughput loss.
- **Forget gate parameterization:** Input‑only gate enables parallelism but may be less flexible than state‑dependent gates; initialization $\alpha_n \sim \text{Uniform}(0.5,1.0), \beta_n \sim \text{LogUniform}(-1.0,1.0)$ is critical.
- **Tensor parallelism:** Topology‑aware (grouped‑value) avoids extra communication but couples parameters to world size; topology‑independent (multi‑value + RMSNormTP) preserves parameter count at cost of AllReduces.

### Failure Signatures
- **Training instability:** Exploding gradients despite forget gate may indicate bad initialization of $W$ or $f_t$ parameters; monitor gradient clipping.
- **Poor retrieval/long‑context performance:** If RULER/LongBench scores are low despite good perplexity, suspect insufficient state size ($K, V$ too small) or too few M²RNN layers.
- **Throughput much lower than expected:** Could be due to excessive M²RNN layers, non‑optimal $K, V$ (not multiples of 16), or inefficient TP (e.g., topology‑independent with many GPUs).
- **State tracking failure on S₃:** Failure to reach >99% accuracy suggests forget gate collapse ($f_t \approx 0$ or $1$) or $W$ initialization too weak; use orthogonal/identity init.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Homogeneous architectures:** Train pure M²RNN, Mamba‑2, Gated DeltaNet, RNN, GRU at 410M and 7B MoE. Evaluate WikiText/LAMBADA perplexity and commonsense benchmarks. Result: M²RNN matches Mamba‑2 and surpasses Gated DeltaNet on LAMBADA.
2. **Hybrid 2‑way models:** Replace all recurrent layers with M²RNN while keeping attention every 8 layers. Compare against Hybrid Mamba‑2 and Hybrid Gated DeltaNet. Result: Hybrid M²RNN reduces perplexity by 0.4–0.5 points and improves downstream accuracy.
3. **In‑context retrieval and long‑context:** Test on RULER (synthetic) and real‑world retrieval tasks plus LongBench. Compare homogeneous/hybrid variants, including with a single M²RNN layer added. Result: Adding M²RNN layers boosts retrieval and long‑context scores (up to +8 on LongBench) with minimal throughput loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can optimized kernels (e.g., CUTLASS) reduce M²RNN's computational overhead while preserving quality?
- **Open Question 2:** How does M²RNN perform at larger scales (70B+ parameters) and longer training contexts (32k+ tokens)?
- **Open Question 3:** Can approximation techniques (e.g., low‑rank, piecewise‑linear) preserve M²RNN's state‑tracking and language modeling performance?
- **Open Question 4:** What is the optimal number and placement of M²RNN layers in hybrid architectures?
- **Open Question 5:** How do the two tensor parallelism strategies (topology‑aware vs. topology‑independent) compare in large‑scale training?

## Limitations
- Exact model dimensions (attention heads, MLP width) and MoE configuration are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Forget gate initialization ranges and precise residual connection design remain unclear.
- Empirical evidence for the input‑independent forget gate’s gradient‑stabilizing effect is limited; saturation risks persist.
- The outer‑product mechanism’s contribution vs. mere parameter increase is not fully isolated in ablations.
