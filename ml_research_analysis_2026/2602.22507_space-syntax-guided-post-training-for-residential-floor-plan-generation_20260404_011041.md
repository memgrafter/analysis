# Space Syntax-guided Post-training for Residential Floor Plan Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22507  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22507  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: SSPT-PPO improves public-space dominance (+0.0424) and living-room advantage (+0.0373), reduces metric variance, and achieves 11.1× higher compute efficiency than iterative retraining, while both methods restore functional hierarchy and align integration profiles with real-world layouts.

## Executive Summary
Pre-trained floor plan generators often overlook architectural priors such as the dominance of public spaces in residential layouts. This paper introduces Space Syntax-guided Post-training (SSPT), a framework that uses a non-differentiable space-syntax oracle to define rewards based on topological integration metrics. Two post-training strategies are proposed: (1) iterative retraining via oracle-based filtering and diffusion fine-tuning (SSPT-Iter), and (2) reinforcement learning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using space-syntax rewards (SSPT-PPO). Evaluated on SSPT-Bench (Eval-8), which tests on 8-room programs after training on ≤7-room data, both methods significantly improve public-space dominance and living-room advantage relative to the baseline. SSPT-PPO additionally shows lower variance in metric dispersion and an 11.1× increase in compute efficiency per iteration compared to SSPT-Iter. The work demonstrates that principled architectural priors can be injected into learned generative models via oracle-guided post-training.

## Method Summary
SSPT post-trains a pre-trained diffusion-based floor plan generator (HouseDiffusion) to embed architectural spatial hierarchy. The backbone is conditioned on program constraints and produces vectorized room polygons. A deterministic space-syntax oracle processes generated layouts: it renders RPLAN-style 4-channel masks, decomposes each room’s walkable core into maximal rectangles, builds a door-mediated adjacency graph, computes HH closeness integration per rectangle node, and aggregates to obtain room-level metrics (e.g., `public_score`, `living_adv`). Two post-training strategies are explored. SSPT-Iter repeatedly samples layouts from the current model, filters the top‑K by a robust living‑room advantage score combined with validity penalties, and fine‑tunes the diffusion model on the filtered set using the standard L2 loss. SSPT-PPO treats the diffusion reverse process as an MDP: full trajectories are rolled out (often with timestep respacing to reduce memory), a terminal reward `R = s(x₀)` (the oracle score) is assigned, and the policy is updated via the clipped PPO surrogate objective with normalized advantages. Evaluation uses the SSPT-Bench’s Eval‑8 split, measuring public‑space dominance, living‑room advantage, and profile alignment distance (`d_profile`) to the ground‑truth RPLAN8 distribution.

## Key Results
- SSPT-PPO yields the largest improvement in `public_score` (+0.0424 over SSPT‑Iter) and `living_adv` (+0.0373), while reducing the IQR of living‑room integration by ~31%.
- SSPT-PPO is 11.1× more compute‑efficient per iteration than SSPT‑Iter, achieving similar metric gains with fewer GPU hours.
- Both SSPT variants restore functional hierarchy (public_score surpasses baseline) and better align the integration profile of generated layouts with real‑world data; PPO attains the smallest `d_profile`.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A deterministic rectangle-space oracle reliably quantifies architectural spatial hierarchy (public-space dominance) from RPLAN‑style masks.
- **Mechanism:** The oracle performs greedy maximal‑rectangle decomposition on room cores, builds a door‑mediated adjacency graph, and computes normalized depth‑based integration (HH/closeness). Room‑instance integration is obtained by averaging node scores, turning pixel masks into a topological graph where integration reflects configurational centrality.
- **Core assumption:** Greedy rectangle decomposition and door‑mediated edges preserve sufficient topological semantics for space‑syntax analysis at scale, and integration dominance in such graphs correlates with architectural “public‑core” logic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “The oracle converts RPLAN‑style layouts into rectangle‑space graphs through greedy maximal‑rectangle decomposition and door‑mediated adjacency construction, and then computes integration‑based measurements.”
  - Sections 3.1.1–3.1.2 detail the mask parsing, decomposition, graph construction, and integration computation pipeline.
  - Corpus: Weak; neighboring papers focus on different paradigms and do not evaluate space‑syntax oracles. No direct validation of the decomposition assumption.
- **Break condition:** If rectangle decomposition fragments functional spaces (e.g., splits a large living room) or misses critical adjacency (e.g., open‑plan connections without doors), the integration profile will misrepresent true spatial hierarchy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Iterative retraining via oracle‑based filtering (SSPT‑Iter) steers the diffusion model toward stronger public‑space dominance by curating a high‑quality fine‑tuning dataset.
- **Mechanism:** The generator produces candidates; the oracle computes a robust living‑room advantage score `z(x₀)` (median‑centered, MAD‑scaled difference between living‑room integration and the median of other rooms) plus hard validity penalties (missing living room, room count, area share). Top‑K samples by this score are selected and the model is fine‑tuned on the filtered set with standard diffusion loss.
- **Core assumption:** A curated dataset of “architecturally superior” plans provides a stronger learning signal than the original distribution, and diffusion fine‑tuning on this subset effectively updates the conditional distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “iterative retraining via space‑syntax filtering and diffusion fine‑tuning”
  - Section 3.4.2 formalizes Top‑K selection, robust score `s(x₀) = z(x₀) + p(x₀)`, and retraining objective.
  - Section 4.2.1 shows SSPT‑Iter improves `public_score` (0.1820 vs baseline 0.1292) and reduces variance.
- **Break condition:** If the filtering score is poorly calibrated (e.g., `z(x₀)` unstable due to MAD=0 fallbacks, or penalties mis‑weighted), curation may select degenerate layouts, causing overfitting to spurious patterns. Repeated fine‑tuning on a shrinking top‑K subset risks mode collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reinforcement learning with PPO (SSPT‑PPO) directly optimizes the diffusion policy for space‑syntax rewards, achieving stronger alignment and efficiency by avoiding full retraining.
- **Mechanism:** The diffusion reverse process is treated as an MDP: each denoising step is an action, states are noisy layouts. Full trajectories are rolled out to `x₀`, the oracle computes a terminal reward `R = r(O(x₀))` (using the same `s(x₀)` as SSPT‑Iter or a hybrid). PPO updates the diffusion policy via the clipped surrogate objective on per‑step log‑probabilities, with optional KL penalty to anchor to the pre‑trained distribution.
- **Core assumption:** The space‑syntax reward is a meaningful, sparse terminal signal that can guide policy optimization across the high‑noise denoising horizon, and the on‑policy PPO gradient step can shift the generator’s distribution without destabilizing geometric fidelity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “reinforcement learning via PPO with space‑syntax rewards”
  - Section 3.4.3 defines the MDP formulation, log‑prob computation, reward shaping, and PPO objective.
  - Section 4.2.1: SSPT‑PPO yields higher `public_score` (+0.0424 over SSPT‑Iter), higher `living_adv`, and ~31% lower `living_room` IQR.
  - Section 4.2.2 reports 11.1× speedup per iteration vs. SSPT‑Iter, confirming higher compute efficiency.
- **Break condition:** If the reward signal is too sparse or high‑variance, PPO may struggle with credit assignment. Poorly tuned KL penalty or clipping could cause catastrophic forgetting of geometric priors or destabilize training. Timestep respacing (optimizing only late denoising steps) is critical; full‑horizon PPO would be prohibitively expensive.

## Foundational Learning
- **Space Syntax Integration Metrics** (Mean Depth, Integration): Provide the theory‑grounded, non‑differentiable objective (public‑space dominance) that pre‑trained models lack. The entire post‑training supervision hinges on these metrics.  
  *Quick check:* For a given room, what does a higher spatial integration value indicate about its topological centrality and accessibility in the floor plan graph?
- **Conditional Diffusion Models** (Reverse Process Parameterization): SSPT treats the diffusion backbone as a stochastic policy. Understanding the `pθ(xₜ₋₁ | xₜ, c)` formulation and exact log‑probability extraction is essential for PPO implementation.  
  *Quick check:* In the diffusion reverse step `pθ(xₜ₋₁ | xₜ, c) = N(μθ, σ²_t)`, what are the roles of `μθ` and `σ²_t` in the PPO log‑prob calculation?
- **Proximal Policy Optimization** (PPO) with Clipped Surrogate: The core optimization algorithm for SSPT‑PPO. Engineers must grasp the probability ratio `ρ_t(θ)`, advantage `A`, and clipping to understand how the reward gradient updates the diffusion policy.  
  *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the `clip(ρ_t(θ), 1-ε, 1+ε)` term in the PPO surrogate objective `L_t(θ)`?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Pre‑trained Diffusion Backbone → (vector polygons) → Space‑Syntax Oracle → (scalar scores)  
SSPT‑Iter: Generator sampler → Oracle scoring → Top‑K selection → Dataset rebuild → Diffusion fine‑tune  
SSPT‑PPO: Rollout collector (full reverse trajectories) → Oracle terminal reward → Advantage normalization → PPO gradient updates on diffusion policy.

**Critical path:**  
- SSPT‑Iter: start from pre‑trained checkpoint → sample layouts → run oracle → filter top‑K → fine‑tune diffusion.  
- SSPT‑PPO: start from pre‑trained checkpoint → rollout full denoising trajectories → compute oracle reward → perform PPO update. Both require the oracle to be wrapped as a deterministic, batched function.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- SSPT‑Iter is simpler and stable (hard filtering) but computationally heavy (full retraining per iteration) and risks curation bias if the top‑K set becomes too narrow.  
- SSPT‑PPO is sample‑efficient (11.1× faster per iteration), directly optimizes the reward, and converges smoothly; however it demands careful reward shaping, PPO hyperparameter tuning (ε, KL penalty), and timestep respacing to limit memory.  
- PPO wins on efficiency and final metric dispersion; Iter may be more robust to reward misspecification because it reshapes the data distribution.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Oracle failure: rectangle decomposition returns zero rectangles → integration NaN → entire batch skipped. Inspect `build_house` errors from RPLAN preprocessing.  
- PPO collapse: `living_room` median plummets or `public_score` becomes negative → generator over‑optimizes (e.g., giant living room). Monitor `d_profile` and per‑category integration.  
- Iterative overfitting: after 2–3 iterations, the top‑K set becomes nearly identical → no further improvement. Detect by measuring diversity (e.g., unique layouts) in `D_top`.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Oracle Sanity Check:** Run the oracle on 100 real RPLAN8 plans and 100 hand‑crafted pathological plans (e.g., living room isolated at maximum depth). Verify `public_score` > 0.4 for reals and < 0 for pathological ones.  
2. **Efficiency Baseline:** On a fixed compute budget (e.g., 8 GPU‑hours), run SSPT‑Iter for 1 full iteration and SSPT‑PPO for the equivalent number of PPO updates. Compare `Δpublic_score` per hour; expect ~10× gain for PPO.  
3. **Profile Alignment Tracking:** After each post‑training step/iteration, sample 500 plans, compute per‑category relative integration, and calculate `d_profile` to RPLAN8. Plot evolution; PPO should show a steady decrease, while Iter may plateau after 1–2 iterations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Applicability to non‑diffusion backbones:** Can SSPT be applied to transformers or graph‑based models? The authors claim the framework is architecture‑agnostic, but only diffusion is evaluated. Resolving this requires implementing SSPT on at least two alternative generators (e.g., transformer, GNN) and measuring injection of architectural priors via public_score and living_adv.
2. **Oracle efficiency:** How can the oracle’s computational cost be reduced for larger‑scale or real‑time use? Suggested solution: learn a neural surrogate that predicts oracle metrics directly from layout representations with high correlation, then substitute it in SSPT‑Iter or PPO and verify comparable improvements at lower cost.
3. **Beyond integration:** What additional space‑syntax metrics (choice, control, circulation efficiency) could be incorporated? Proposed evidence: design a multi‑component reward combining integration with other metrics, then run ablations measuring effects on `d_profile` and human preference.
4. **Generalization to larger programs:** Does SSPT work for layouts with >10 rooms or atypical compositions? The current Eval‑8 trains on ≤7 rooms and tests on 8 rooms. To resolve, extend evaluation to 9–15 rooms (using expanded RPLAN patterns or synthetic programs) and compare SSPT‑PPO and Iter in terms of maintained public‑space dominance.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Exact values for SSPT‑Iter (top‑K ratio, fine‑tuning epochs, validity thresholds) and SSPT‑PPO (learning rate, PPO epochs, KL coefficient) are undisclosed, hindering precise replication.
- **Oracle robustness:** The greedy rectangle decomposition assumption may fail on diverse or open‑plan layouts, misrepresenting topological semantics.
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluation is restricted to 8‑room programs; performance on other room counts or non‑rectilinear geometries remains unknown.
