# I Can't Believe It's Not Robust: Catastrophic Collapse of Safety Classifiers under Embedding Drift

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01297
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01297
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Safety classifiers with frozen embeddings fail catastrophically under minimal embedding drift (σ=0.02), with ROC-AUC collapsing from ~85% to ~50% and 72% of errors being high-confidence.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that safety classifiers, when deployed with frozen embeddings from a base model, experience a near-total collapse in performance under extremely small embedding drifts—as little as a 1° angular shift (σ=0.02). The failure is silent: classifier confidence decreases only marginally (~14% on average) while accuracy plunges, leading to high-confidence incorrect predictions. Instruction-tuned models exacerbate the problem, showing 20% worse class separability. These findings challenge the common production assumption that embeddings remain stable across model updates and imply that safety classifiers must be retrained after every model change.

## Method Summary
No reproduction details or experimental methodology are provided in the supplied notes. ## Key Results
- Classifier ROC-AUC collapses from ~85% to ~50% under minimal embedding drift (σ=0.02).
- Mean confidence drops only ~14%, resulting in 72% of errors being high-confidence (silent failures).
- Instruction-tuned models exhibit 20% worse class separability than base models, increasing vulnerability to drift.
- Failure is described as sharp, mechanism-invariant, and irreversible under tested conditions.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Pretrained Encoder (Base/Instruct) -> Frozen Embeddings -> Safety Classifier (Linear/MLP) -> Decision (Safe/Harmful)

Critical path: Embedding extraction → Classifier inference. The entire failure mode resides in the mismatch between the classifier's learned decision surface and the drifted embedding geometry.

Design tradeoffs:  
- *Frozen embeddings* offer computational efficiency and stability for downstream tasks but assume embedding invariance across model updates, which this paper disputes.  
- *Joint retraining* of classifier and encoder preserves separability but is computationally expensive and requires original training data or updates.

Failure signatures:  
1. Sharp drop in ROC-AUC/AUC-PR with minimal measured embedding drift (σ≈0.02).  
2. Confidence distribution remains peaked, indicating miscalibrated uncertainty.  
3. Performance does not recover with post-hoc scaling or simple fine-tuning.

First three experiments:  
1. **Drift-Accuracy Curve:** Systematically vary Gaussian noise σ (e.g., 0.001 to 0.05) and plot classifier metrics to locate collapse threshold.  
2. **Architecture Sweep:** Test drift robustness across different classifier architectures (logistic regression, SVM, shallow MLP) using identical drifted embeddings.  
3. **Confidence-Accuracy Dissociation:** On drifted data, bin predictions by classifier confidence and compute accuracy per bin to quantify silent failure rate.
