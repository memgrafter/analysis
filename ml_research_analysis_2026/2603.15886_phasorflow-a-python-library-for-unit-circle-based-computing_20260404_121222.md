# PhasorFlow: A Python Library for Unit Circle Based Computing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15886
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15886
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Achieves 100% validation accuracy on a high-dimensional non-linear synthetic classification task with only 16 trainable parameters, demonstrating extreme parameter efficiency.

## Executive Summary
PhasorFlow introduces a deterministic, lightweight paradigm for continuous phase-based data using unit circle computing. By encoding inputs as phasors on the N-Torus and processing them through a circuit of unitary gates, it achieves significant parameter efficiency—e.g., a single-layer model reaches perfect accuracy on complex synthetic tasks with just 16 parameters. For sequence modeling, the Phasor Transformer replaces quadratic self-attention with a parameter-free DFT token mixer, reducing complexity to O(T log T) while maintaining competitive performance. The library supports diverse applications, from time-series forecasting to associative memory, all executing on classical hardware via analytic complex tensor operations.

## Method Summary
The core methodology maps input data to phase angles, representing them as complex phasors \( z_k = e^{i\phi_k} \). These are processed through a variational phasor circuit comprising unitary gates: Shift (diagonal phase rotations), Mix (pairwise interference via SU(2) beam-splitters), and DFT (global frequency mixing). The circuit's state evolves through interference while conserving the global \( \ell_2 \)-norm, with intermediate magnitudes allowed to drift for expressivity. For sequences, the Phasor Transformer uses blocks \( B(\theta) = S(\theta^\text{post}) F^T S(\theta^\text{pre}) \), where the DFT matrix \( F^T \) mixes tokens in the frequency domain without learned parameters. Outputs are read deterministically from phase angles, enabling end-to-end differentiable training.

## Key Results
- Single-layer Variational Phasor Circuit attained 100% validation accuracy on a high-dimensional non-linear binary classification task using only 16 trainable parameters, contrasting with >1,000 for an equivalent dense MLP.
- Phasor Transformer reduced token mixing complexity from O(T²) to O(T log T) while achieving competitive sequence prediction MSE ~0.075.
- Validated applications include time-series forecasting, unsupervised financial volatility detection (F1=0.386), associative memory recovery (phase error <0.05 rad), and neural binding via Kuramoto synchronization (order parameter →1.0).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unit Circle Encoding for Geometric Stability
- **Claim:** Encoding real-valued data as phase angles on the \( S^1 \) unit circle (\( \mathbb{T}^N \)) provides a geometrically stable and continuous representation for oscillatory dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** Inputs are mapped via `EncodePhase` to \( z_k = e^{i\phi_k} \), constraining initial state magnitudes to unity. This embeds data on a compact manifold where operations are inherently periodic and interference-based.
- **Core assumption:** Problem structures involving cyclical relationships or phase-dependent patterns are more naturally captured by angular relationships than Euclidean distances.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "Inputs are encoded as complex phasors \( z = e^{i\theta} \) on the \( N \)-Torus (\( \mathbb{T}^N \))."
  - [section 2.1] "A phasor circuit state is a vector \( z \in \mathbb{C}^N \), \( z_k = e^{i\phi_k} \), ... with admissible encoded states constrained to \( \mathbb{T}^N = (S^1)^N \) by \( |z_k| = 1 \) for all \( k \)."
  - Corpus signals show strong thematic alignment with "phasor," "unitary," and "quantum/classical" computing.

### Mechanism 2: Unitary Gates and Wave Interference for Expressivity
- **Claim:** Sequential application of unitary gates (Shift, Mix, DFT) enables wave interference, allowing the state to dynamically explore \( \mathbb{C}^N \) expressivity while conserving global \( \ell_2 \)-norm.
- **Mechanism:** Diagonal `Shift` gates add trainable phase offsets. `Mix` gates (SU(2) beam-splitters) create pairwise interference. Global `DFT` mixes all threads via the Fourier basis. Although the initial state lies on \( \mathbb{T}^N \), non-diagonal unitaries cause individual \( |z_k| \) to drift from 1, expanding the effective representation space without norm explosion.
- **Core assumption:** Intermediate state magnitudes need not be bounded to 1; leveraging transient amplitude variations increases model capacity for complex boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 2.2] "Since unitary matrices preserve the \( \ell^2 \)-norm... the total energy of the system is conserved. ... individual thread magnitudes \( |z'_k| \) generally drift away from 1 due to interference."
  - [appendix A] Provides mathematical proof that a `Mix` gate can produce \( |z'|^2 = 1 \pm 1 \), showing explicit departure from \( \mathbb{T}^N \).
  - [section 3.1.10] "This drift is intentionally allowed during linear propagation for representational expressivity."

### Mechanism 3: Parameter-Free DFT Token Mixing for Efficiency
- **Claim:** Replacing learned dot-product attention with the parameter-free DFT gate achieves global token mixing in \( O(T \log T) \) complexity while retaining competitive performance.
- **Mechanism:** In the Phasor Transformer block \( B(\theta) = S(\theta^\text{post}) F^T S(\theta^\text{pre}) \), the DFT \( F^T \) acts as a fixed, orthogonal token mixer. It transforms the sequence into the frequency domain, enabling global context aggregation without \( QK^T \) projections.
- **Core assumption:** Global spectral interactions (frequency-domain coupling) are sufficient for many sequence tasks, reducing the need for data-dependent attention weights.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "The Phasor Transformer replaced quadratic self-attention with a parameter-free DFT-based token mixer... reducing mixing complexity from \( O(T^2) \) to \( O(T \log T) \) while maintaining competitive sequence prediction performance (MSE ~0.075)."
  - [section 3.3.1] "Token Mixing (DFT Attention): A global DFT gate mixes all \( T \) sequence tokens in the frequency domain."
  - [section 3.3.1, Theorem 3.2] "Replacing dot-product attention with DFT mixing yields a token-mixing cost of \( O(T \log T) \) per block and linear parameter growth in \( T \)."

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Complex Phasor Arithmetic**
  - **Why needed here:** All operations are defined on \( z = e^{i\phi} \). Understanding Euler's formula, complex multiplication (phase addition), and magnitude is essential to trace state evolution and design gates.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the result of \( e^{i\alpha} \cdot e^{i\beta} \) and \( e^{i\alpha} + e^{i\beta} \) in terms of phase and magnitude?

- **Concept: Unitary Operators & Group Structure (U(1), U(2), U(N))**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire circuit is a product of unitary matrices. Knowing that `Shift` is \( U(1) \) (phase rotation), `Mix` is \( SU(2) \) (beam-splitter), and `DFT` is \( U(N) \) (global orthogonal transform) explains parameter scaling, norm preservation, and gate composability.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does a product of unitary matrices remain unitary? How does this property relate to the "wave interference" metaphor?

- **Concept: Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) as a Linear Transform**
  - **Why needed here:** The Phasor Transformer's core mixing operation is the DFT matrix \( F_N \). One must understand it as a fixed, orthogonal basis change from the "spatial" (token) domain to the "frequency" domain.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the computational complexity of multiplying a vector by the \( N \times N \) DFT matrix directly? How does FFT reduce this?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** The PhasorFlow library consists of five interconnected modules: `circuit` (builder API for assembling phasor circuits), `gates` (22 primitive operations including Standard, Non-Linear, Neuromorphic, and Encoding gates), `models` (pre-defined architectures like VPC and PhasorTransformer that utilize `gates`), `visualization` (tools for diagramming circuits), and `engine` (a PyTorch-based simulator for analytic complex tensor computations). The typical flow is: `circuit` constructs a model using `gates` and `engine`; `models` provide high-level interfaces; `visualization` aids debugging.

- **Critical path:** For a basic Variational Phasor Circuit (VPC): Input data → `EncodePhase` (maps to phasors on \( \mathbb{T}^N \)) → Variational layer (application of trainable `Shift` gates and fixed `Mix`/`DFT` gates) → `measure` (extract phase angles) → Deterministic readout (e.g., `sin(arg(z0))` for binary classification). For the Phasor Transformer: Token embeddings → Pre-FFN `Shift` → Token mixing via `DFT` → Post-FFN `Shift` → readout.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Parameter Efficiency vs. Expressivity:* Diagonal `Shift`-only feed-forward layers are parameter-lean (\( N \) per layer) but cannot learn arbitrary linear maps. Expressivity is regained through structured off-diagonal `Mix`/`DFT` gates, which are parameter-free.
  - *Deterministic Execution vs. Probabilistic Sampling:* No shot noise simplifies debugging and gradient flow but forfeits quantum-inspired probabilistic inference capabilities.
  - *Strict Manifold Adherence vs. Internal Drift:* Enforcing \( |z_k|=1 \) at every layer (`Normalize`) maintains geometric purity but can cause gradient singularities; allowing drift into \( \mathbb{C}^N \) improves optimization but complicates physical interpretation.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Phase unwrapping errors:** `arg(z)` is discontinuous at \( -\pi/\pi \). If downstream logic is sensitive to \( 2\pi \) wraps, predictions may jump. Mitigate by using modulo-consistent operations.
  - **Vanishing coherence:** Excessive `Mix`/`DFT` without compensating `Shift` parameters can cause destructive interference (\( | \sum z_k | \approx 0 \)), leading to near-zero signal for readout.
  - **Gradient explosion from `Normalize`:** The Jacobian of \( z/|z| \) becomes singular as \( |z| \to 0 \). Use only if strictly required by the application (e.g., neuromorphic memory).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Basic gate composition:** Build a 4-thread circuit: `encode_phase([0, π/2, π, 3π/2]) → mix(0,1) → mix(2,3) → dft()`. Manually compute expected phases and verify against engine output. Validates unitary algebra and DFT mixing.
  2. **VPC binary classifier:** Replicate Section 6.1. Use the synthetic sum-of-cosines dataset (\( N=16 \)). Train a single-layer VPC (16 params). Expect 100% validation accuracy within ~40 epochs. Tests end-to-end differentiable training and readout mapping.
  3. **Phasor Transformer sequence prediction:** Replicate Section 6.2. Use the 3-sinusoid+noise dataset with context \( T=32 \). Build a 2-block transformer (\( 2 \times 2T = 64 \) params). Compare test MSE (~0.07) against a tiny self-attention baseline. Validates parameter-free mixing efficacy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** Can PhasorFlow be extended to higher-dimensional manifolds like \( S^3 \) (quaternions) while preserving deterministic execution and parameter efficiency? Basis: Future Directions proposes quaternion extension; unresolved due to non-commutative algebra requiring new operations.

- **Open Question 2:** Can photonic or neuromorphic hardware provide practical speedup and energy efficiency for PhasorFlow simulations compared to classical GPUs? Basis: Section 7.5 notes natural mapping to photonic hardware; unresolved due to analog noise, precision limits, and lack of prototype.

- **Open Question 3:** Does PhasorFlow retain its parameter efficiency advantage on large-scale real-world datasets, or is the improvement restricted to synthetic tasks? Basis: Limitations section notes validation on synthetic datasets only; unresolved as real-world scaling issues are untested.

- **Open Question 4:** For which sequential modeling tasks does parameter-free DFT token mixing fail compared to learned attention, and can we characterize this gap theoretically? Basis: Inferred from Phasor Transformer's fixed DFT vs. adaptive attention; unresolved due to lack of theoretical comparison and empirical evaluation on attention-suited tasks.

## Limitations

- Implementation details such as Mix connectivity pattern for N=16, Shift parameter initialization, and readout scaling are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Break conditions exist: unit circle encoding fails for magnitude-dependent tasks, and DFT mixing may not capture dynamic token interactions suitable for learned attention.
- Optimization challenges include phase unwrapping discontinuities and gradient singularities from manifold constraints, potentially destabilizing training.

## Confidence

- **Mechanism 1 (Unit Circle Encoding):** High – mathematically rigorous with clear evidence.
- **Mechanism 2 (Unitary Gates & Interference):** Medium – theory solid but dependent on unspecified implementation details like connectivity and normalization.
- **Mechanism 3 (DFT Token Mixer):** High for complexity reduction (theorem proven), Medium for broad performance due to limited benchmarks on complex tasks.
