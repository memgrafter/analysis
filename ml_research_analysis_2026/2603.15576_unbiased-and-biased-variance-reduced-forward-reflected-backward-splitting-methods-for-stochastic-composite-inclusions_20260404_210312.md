# Unbiased and Biased Variance-Reduced Forward-Reflected-Backward Splitting Methods for Stochastic Composite Inclusions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15576
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15576
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Provides variance-reduced forward-reflected-backward splitting (VrFRBS) for stochastic composite inclusions with O(1/k) best-iterate rate and oracle complexities O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2}) (unbiased) and O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2}) (biased) in the finite-sum setting, and O(ε^{-10/3}) and O(ε^{-5}) in the expectation setting. Numerical tests show unbiased variants typically outperform biased ones in minimax tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops VrFRBS, a unified variance-reduced forward-reflected-backward splitting framework for solving stochastic composite inclusions, covering both monotone and nonmonotone operators. The method extends deterministic FRBS by incorporating stochastic variance-reduced estimators for the forward-reflected direction, handling both unbiased (e.g., SVRG, SAGA) and, for the first time, biased (e.g., SARAH, Hybrid SGD) families. Theoretically, VrFRBS achieves an O(1/k) best-iterate convergence rate for the expected squared residual norm and almost-sure iterate convergence. In the finite-sum setting, unbiased estimators achieve oracle complexity O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2}) while biased ones achieve O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2}); under the expectation setting, the complexities are O(ε^{-10/3}) and O(ε^{-5}), respectively. Numerical experiments on AUC optimization and reinforcement learning policy evaluation demonstrate that unbiased variants typically converge faster than biased ones in these minimax tasks.

## Method Summary
VrFRBS solves stochastic composite inclusions \(0 \in G(x) + T(x)\) where \(G\) is Lipschitz and possibly nonmonotone, and \(T\) is a set‑valued operator (e.g., normal cone of a convex set). The algorithm produces a sequence \(\{x_k\}\) by updating
\[
x_{k+1} = J_{\eta T}(x_k - \eta \tilde{S}_k),
\]
where \(\tilde{S}_k\) is a variance-reduced estimator of the forward‑reflected direction \(S_k = 2G(x_k)-G(x_{k-1})\). Two families of estimators are supported:
- **Unbiased**: e.g., L‑SVRG, SAGA, increasing mini‑batch SGD, satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[e_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k]=0\) and a recursive error bound.
- **Biased**: e.g., SARAH, Hybrid SGD, Hybrid SVRG, with a persistence factor \(\tau<1\) such that \(\mathbb{E}[e_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k] = (1-\tau)e_{k-1}\).
The convergence analysis constructs a Lyapunov function that combines squared distances \(\|y_k-x^\star\|^2\) and past variance terms. Under a weak Minty condition and stepsize constraints (\(\eta\) bounded above by \(1/(3\sqrt{2}L)\) for unbiased and \(L/\sqrt{134}\) for biased), this Lyapunov yields a one‑step descent, leading to an O(1/k) best‑iterate rate for the expected squared residual norm. The method is loopless (no inner loops) and adapts to both finite‑sum and expectation oracles.

## Key Results
- Convergence: O(1/k) best‑iterate rate for the expected squared residual norm; almost‑sure iterate convergence.
- Finite‑sum setting: unbiased estimators achieve O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2}) oracle complexity; biased estimators achieve O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2}).
- Expectation setting: unbiased O(ε^{-10/3}); biased O(ε^{-5}).
- Numerical experiments on AUC optimization and reinforcement learning policy evaluation show unbiased variants typically outperform biased ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: VrFRBS iteration and Lyapunov structure
The algorithm generates iterates converging to a solution of the stochastic composite inclusion under Assumption 1.1, with a best‑iterate rate O(1/k) for the expected squared residual norm. At iteration k, compute a stochastic variance‑reduced estimator \(\tilde{S}_k\) of the forward‑reflected direction \(S_k = 2G(x_k)-G(x_{k-1})\), then update \(x_{k+1} = J_{\eta T}(x_k - \eta \tilde{S}_k)\). The analysis introduces an auxiliary variable \(y_k = x_k + \eta \hat{w}_k\) (where \(\hat{w}_k\) approximates \(G(x_{k-1})+\xi_k\)) to obtain a one‑step inequality (Lemma 2.1) that bounds \(\|y_{k+1}-x^\star\|^2\) in terms of \(\|y_k-x^\star\|^2\), the error \(e_k = \tilde{S}_k - S_k\), and Lipschitz terms. Descent requires either \(\kappa L^2 \ge \Theta+\hat{\Theta}\) (unbiased) or \(4L^2\tau^2\kappa \ge 22(\Theta+\hat{\Theta})\) (biased).

### Mechanism 2: Unbiased variance‑reduced estimators
For unbiased estimators satisfying Definition 3.1 (zero conditional mean and error recursion (13)), VrFRBS attains O(1/k) best‑iterate rate and the averaged iterate \(\bar{x}_K\) satisfies \(\mathbb{E}\|G\bar{x}_K + \bar{\xi}_K\|^2 \le O(1/K)\) with oracle complexities O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2}) (finite‑sum) and O(ε^{-10/3}) (expectation). The Lyapunov candidate \(P_k\) (Definition 3.4) combines \(\|y_k-x^\star\|^2\), a weighted \(\|y_k-x_{k-1}\|^2\), and the past variance \(\Delta_{k-1}\). Lemma 3.4 shows that under \(\kappa L^2 \ge \Theta+\hat{\Theta}\) and an appropriate stepsize, \(\mathbb{E}[P_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k] \le P_k - \text{positive definite terms} + \text{weighted }\delta_k\), yielding the rate (Theorem 3.1). Concrete estimators (L‑SVRG, SAGA, increasing mini‑batch SGD) satisfy these conditions (Corollaries 3.1–3.3).

### Mechanism 3: Biased variance‑reduced estimators
For biased estimators where \(\mathbb{E}[e_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k] = (1-\tau)e_{k-1}\) (Definition 4.1), the algorithm still enjoys O(1/k) best‑iterate rate but with worse oracle complexities O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2}) and O(ε^{-5}) due to bias accumulation. The cross‑term \(\langle e_{k-1}, y_{k-1}-x^\star\rangle\) is controlled by augmenting the Lyapunov with extra terms (see \(L_k\) in Lemma 4.5). Descent now requires \(4L^2\tau^2\kappa \ge 22(\Theta+\hat{\Theta})\) and a smaller stepsize range (\(\eta \in [32\rho, L/\sqrt{134})\)). Biased estimators (SARAH, Hybrid SGD, Hybrid SVRG) fit this framework (Corollaries 4.1–4.3).

## Foundational Learning

### Stochastic Composite Inclusions and Weak Minty Condition
**Why needed**: The paper solves \(0 \in G(x) + T(x)\) in stochastic settings. The weak Minty assumption (Assumption 1.1(d)) replaces strong monotonicity and is crucial for proving convergence of FRBS when cocoercivity is absent.
**Quick check**: Does the weak Minty condition imply monotonicity? Under what circumstances does it hold, and can you give an example of a nonmonotone operator that satisfies it?

### Forward‑Reflected‑Backward Splitting (FRBS)
**Why needed**: FRBS is the underlying deterministic method that VrFRBS extends. It uses the reflected direction \(S_k = 2G(x_k)-G(x_{k-1})\) to avoid the need for cocoercivity, making it suitable for nonmonotone inclusions.
**Quick check**: How does the forward‑reflected direction differ from the standard gradient \(G(x_k)\)? What is its role in the inequality (12) and why does it enable a single‑call scheme?

### Variance Reduction in Stochastic Approximation
**Why needed**: The stochastic estimators reduce variance to allow constant stepsizes and achieve faster convergence rates. The paper unifies both unbiased (SVRG, SAGA) and biased (SARAH, Hybrid) families under the VrFRBS framework.
**Quick check**: What distinguishes unbiased from biased variance‑reduced estimators in terms of their update rules and error recursions? Give one example of each (e.g., L‑SVRG for unbiased, SARAH for biased) and explain how they satisfy Definitions 3.1 and 4.1, respectively.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Data source (finite‑sum \(\{G_i\}_{i=1}^n\) or expectation oracle \(G(\cdot,\xi)\)) → Stochastic oracle (samples minibatch/single \(\xi\)) → Variance‑reduced estimator (maintains state to produce \(\tilde{S}_k\)) → FRBS core (computes \(x_{k+1} = J_{\eta T}(x_k - \eta \tilde{S}_k\)) → (optional) Convergence monitor → Hyperparameter controller (sets \(\eta\), minibatch sizes, probabilities \(p\), mixing weights \(\omega\)).

### Critical Path
1. **Initialization**: Set \(x_0\) (and \(x_{-1}=x_0\)), initialize estimator (e.g., compute full gradients for SAGA, set \(w_0=x_0\) for SARAH).
2. **Iteration loop** (for \(k=0,1,\dots,K\)):
   a. Sample required data (minibatch \(B_k\)).
   b. Compute \(\tilde{S}_k\) using estimator’s recipe; update estimator’s internal state.
   c. Perform FRBS update: \(x_{k+1} = J_{\eta T}(x_k - \eta \tilde{S}_k)\).
   d. Optionally record metrics (e.g., residual).
3. **Output**: Average iterate \(\bar{x}_K = \frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{j=0}^K x_j\) (or last iterate) and report residual.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Unbiased vs. biased**: Unbiased (SVRG, SAGA) give better theoretical complexities (e.g., O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2})) but often require larger minibatches or occasional full gradient passes. Biased (SARAH, Hybrid) can use smaller batches and fewer \(G\) evaluations per iteration, at the cost of an extra factor in complexity (e.g., O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2})).
- **Memory usage**: SAGA stores \(O(n)\) past gradients; L‑SVRG uses only snapshots; biased methods need only the previous estimator \(e_{k-1}\).
- **Stepsize range**: Unbiased requires \(\eta \ge 8\rho\) and \(\eta < 1/(3\sqrt{2}L)\); biased requires \(\eta < L/\sqrt{134}\) and \(\eta \ge 32\rho\). The parameter \(\rho\) (weak Minty constant) is often unknown and may need tuning.
- **Minibatch size**: Theory suggests \(b = \Theta(n^{2/3})\) for SVRG/SAGA, \(b = \Theta(n^{3/4})\) for SARAH, and an increasing schedule \(b_k = \min\{n, \lfloor 0.01 n (k+1)^{3/4}\rfloor\}\) for SGD‑type biased estimator. Practical tuning remains important.

### Failure Signatures
- **Divergence/explosion**: Stepsize too large or inaccurate resolvent computation. Check that \(\eta\) respects theoretical bounds and that \(J_{\eta T}\) is correctly implemented.
- **Stagnation at nonzero residual**: Violation of weak Minty condition or insufficient decay of \(\delta_k\). Increase minibatch size or adjust probabilities \(p\) (for SVRG) or \(\omega\) (for Hybrid).
- **High‑variance oscillations**: Biased estimator with too small persistence \(\tau\) (or \(\omega\)). Increase mixing weight or probability \(p\) (for SARAH).
- **Memory overflow**: SAGA infeasible for very large \(n\); switch to L‑SVRG or a biased method.
- **Poor reproducibility**: Random reshuffling and stochasticity affect bias/variance; ensure consistent random seeds when comparing variants.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Synthetic monotone VI**: Construct a linear monotone inclusion \(G(x)=Ax\) with \(A\succeq0\) and \(T=N_C\) for a simple convex set \(C\) (e.g., box). Compare VrFRBS‑SVRG against deterministic FRBS; verify residual decay as O(1/√k) and that stepsize respects bounds.
2. **Finite‑sum logistic regression**: Minimize average logistic loss with L2 regularization. Use SAGA estimator; benchmark against plain SGD and standard SVRG. Track gradient norm vs. epochs; test robustness to minibatch size and distributed setting.
3. **Nonmonotone min‑max (AUC optimization)**: Implement the AUC maximization model. Run VrFRBS with unbiased (SVRG) and biased (SARAH) side‑by‑side. Confirm the empirical gap predicted by theory; sweep minibatch sizes and \(p\)/\(\omega\) to illustrate the trade‑off between per‑iteration cost and convergence speed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can biased variance-reduced estimators achieve better oracle complexity than unbiased ones in VrFRBS?** The current theory shows biased methods have worse complexities (e.g., O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2}) vs O(n^{2/3}ε^{-2})), but the authors suggest this may be due to analysis looseness.
2. **Can the biased estimator analysis be improved to close the oracle complexity gap in the expectation setting?** The O(ε^{-5}) complexity for biased estimators is worse than the O(ε^{-10/3}) for unbiased; the gap might be an artifact of the condition \(4\kappa\tau^2L^2 \ge 22(\Theta+\hat{\Theta})\) being too strict.
3. **Why do biased variance-reduced estimators underperform unbiased ones in minimax tasks like AUC optimization and policy evaluation?** It is unclear whether this is solely due to theoretical complexity differences or also due to bias accumulation in nonmonotone settings.

## Limitations
- **Weak Minty assumption** (Assumption 1.1(d)) is theoretically necessary but may be hard to verify in practice; concrete examples where it holds while strong monotonicity fails are scarce.
- **Experimental details** are not fully specified (e.g., exact bound \(R\) and noise \(\sigma\) for AUC; regularization \(\tau\) for policy evaluation; precise residual computation), hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Theoretical complexities for biased estimators** (O(n^{3/4}ε^{-2}) and O(ε^{-5})) may be loose, and their practical advantage at large scale remains unproven.
