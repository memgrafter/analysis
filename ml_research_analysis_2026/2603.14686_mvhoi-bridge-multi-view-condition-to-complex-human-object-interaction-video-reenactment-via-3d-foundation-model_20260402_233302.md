# MVHOI: Bridge Multi-view Condition to Complex Human-Object Interaction Video Reenactment via 3D Foundation Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14686
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14686
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MVHOI significantly outperforms SOTA on HOI video reenactment (FID 17.90 ↓29.53 vs. VACE, FVD 86.6, O-CLIP 0.848) and enables stable long-duration generation via cross-iterative inference.

## Executive Summary
Human-Object Interaction (HOI) video reenactment aims to transfer object manipulation from a source video to a target object. Existing 2D-based methods struggle with complex 3D motions (e.g., out-of-plane rotations) due to representation gaps and view-inconsistent texture synthesis. MVHOI introduces a two-stage framework that leverages a 3D foundation model (Depth Anything 3) as a Unified Object Anchor (UOA) to produce geometrically consistent coarse guidance without explicit pose estimation. Stage II then refines the output using a multi-reference DiT video generator, with attention bias derived from UOA’s internal maps to retrieve viewpoint-appropriate textures. The cross-iterative inference loop stabilizes long videos by alternating between coarse generation and high-fidelity refinement. Extensive experiments show MVHOI substantially surpasses prior methods (e.g., VACE, HunyuanCustom) on both self- and cross-reenactment benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art FID, FVD, and O-CLIP scores.

## Method Summary
MVHOI processes a source video `V_src` (frames `X_t`) and a set of multi-view reference images `I_ref` of the target object. Stage I first uses a motion extractor (based on DisMo) to encode frame transitions into latent motion embeddings `m_t`. These embeddings modulate a 3D foundation model’s feature aggregator (the Unified Object Anchor, built on DepthAnything3-Large). The UOA ingests the current object crop `O_t`, the reference views, and `m_t` to predict the next object frame in the target view. This coarse prediction is autoregressively rolled out to generate a short, geometrically consistent sequence. Stage II employs a DiT-based video generator (initialized from Wan2.1-T2V-14B) equipped with a multi-reference adapter (cloned from VACE). The adapter ingests the coarse guidance along with the reference views, and the DiT backbone synthesizes high-fidelity HOI video, guided by an attention bias derived from UOA’s internal attention maps. For long videos, a cross-iterative loop periodically reinitializes the UOA with the final refined frame of the last clip, preventing drift.

## Key Results
- **FID 17.90** (↓29.53 vs. VACE), **FVD 86.6** (↓118.0), **O-CLIP 0.848** – state-of-the-art on self- and cross-reenactment benchmarks.
- Substantial improvements in handling complex 3D manipulations (out-of-plane rotations) where prior 2D methods fail.
- Cross-iterative inference enables stable long-duration HOI video generation (evaluated up to 81 frames).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Implicit 3D Motion Anchor
- **Claim:** Achieves robust motion transfer for complex 3D manipulations without explicit 6D pose estimation.
- **Mechanism:** Motion latents from a motion extractor modulate the UOA (a 3D foundation model). The UOA jointly processes the current object frame, multi-view references, and motion embeddings to predict the next frame in the target view, yielding coarse but geometrically consistent results.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained 3D model’s latent space serves as a stable, view-invariant anchor unifying appearance and structure.
- **Evidence:** Paper abstracts and Section 3.2 describe querying the Unified Object Anchor implicitly to bypass pose estimation.
- **Break condition:** If the UOA’s latent space is insufficiently expressive or generalizable, coarse outputs become distorted (shape/orientation errors), breaking the pipeline.

#### Geometry-Aware Texture Retrieval
- **Claim:** Ensures viewpoint-appropriate texture synthesis and prevents appearance drift during object rotation.
- **Mechanism:** Stage-II’s DiT generator receives a bias computed from UOA’s attention maps (capturing spatial correspondence) and injects it into the self-attention layers of a multi-reference adapter. This steers the generator to fetch textures from the correct reference view.
- **Core assumption:** UOA’s attention patterns encode a reliable proxy for geometric correspondence between synthesized and reference views.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.3 report that the bias eliminates semantic drifting and viewpoint confusion.
- **Break condition:** If UOA attention maps misrepresent correspondence (e.g., due to symmetric objects), the injected bias may misguide attention, causing texture mixing.

#### Cross-Iterative Long-Video Stabilization
- **Claim:** Maintains long-term object identity and temporal stability in generated videos.
- **Mechanism:** For long sequences, the pipeline alternates between UOA coarse generation and Stage II refinement. After refining a clip, the final frame’s segmented object is fed back to reinitialize the UOA for the next segment. This periodically re-anchors the generation on a high-quality state, breaking error accumulation cycles.
- **Core assumption:** High-fidelity Stage II outputs provide a more reliable prior than coarse UOA outputs for subsequent motion queries.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.5 describe the cross-iterative inference loop suppressing cumulative drift.
- **Break condition:** If Stage II introduces errors in the final frame (e.g., color shift), these errors propagate to the next segment and may amplify drift.

## Foundational Learning
- **3D Foundation Models (e.g., Depth Anything 3)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a pre-trained, geometry-aware latent space that unifies appearance and structure across views, serving as the "Unified Object Anchor".  
  *Quick check:* How does a feed-forward 3D foundation model differ from traditional reconstruction (e.g., NeRF) in terms of suitability as a latent space anchor for video generation?

- **Latent Motion Representations (DisMo-style)**  
  *Why needed:* Encodes complex object dynamics, including out-of-plane rotations, as compact temporally coherent embeddings, avoiding the discontinuity of explicit 6D pose tracking.  
  *Quick check:* What are the failure modes of using explicit keypoints or 6D poses for HOI with rapid rotations compared to implicit motion latents?

- **Attention Mechanism as a Spatial Correspondence Proxy**  
  *Why needed:* Cross-attention maps from the UOA during motion transfer implicitly learn view-matching relationships, which can bias the video generator’s attention for correct multi-view texture retrieval.  
  *Quick check:* Why might standard appearance similarity in a video generator’s attention fail for view-consistent texture synthesis during object rotation, and how does a geometry-aware bias address this?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Source video `V_src` (frames `X_t`) and multi-view target references `I_ref` flow through:
- Stage I: Motion Extractor `M_θ` → motion embeddings `m_t`. Unified Object Anchor (UOA, DepthAnything3 backbone) ingests `O_t`, `I_ref`, `m_t` → coarse object frame `O_{t+Δt}`. Autoregressive rollout yields coarse sequence.
- Stage II: DiT-based video generator (Wan2.1-T2V-14B) with Multi-Reference Adapter (cloned blocks) ingests coarse guidance, `I_ref`, and other conditions (pose, text) → high-fidelity HOI video.
- Inference loop (long videos): final refined frame segmented → re-initializes UOA for next segment.

### Critical Path
The pipeline’s core data flow: Motion extraction → UOA coarse generation (per frame/segment) → extract UOA attention maps → convert to bias → inject into DiT’s adapter → video synthesis. For long videos, Stage II output feeds back into UOA input.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Two-stage separation vs. end-to-end: Allows leveraging specialized pre-trained models, but risks sub-optimal interface between stages; mitigated by training Stage II with proxy guidance (augmented original crops).
- Multi-view references vs. monocular: MVHOI requires ≥1 reference (ideally multiple) for anchoring and retrieval; monocular approaches fail on out-of-plane motion but are simpler.
- Attention enhancement strength (α): Needs tuning; too weak fails to prevent view confusion, too strong over-constrains generation and loses fine details.

### Failure Signatures
- **UOA failure:** Coarse sequence shows distorted shapes, orientation jumps, or hand-object desynchronization.
- **Retrieval failure:** Textures flicker between reference views or blur/mix on rotating surfaces.
- **Drift accumulation (long video):** Gradual color shift, texture degradation, or object identity drift in later clips.
- **Generalization failure:** Poor performance on novel object categories not well represented in Stage I’s synthetic Objaverse data.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Isolate UOA:** Train/evaluate Stage I alone against a pose-based baseline (using estimated 6D poses) on a synthetic dataset with known ground-truth poses and multi-view renders. Metric: pose error (ADD-S) and view-consistent FID.
2. **Validate attention bias mechanism:** Ablate attention enhancement (α=0) in Stage II while keeping UOA and multi-view references. Compare against full model on cross-reenactment with >90° object rotations. Metric: O-CLIP, view-confusion artifacts.
3. **Test cross-iterative loop:** Generate a 10s video with and without the loop (pure autoregressive). Quantify long-term consistency via LPIPS between object in frame 1 and frame N or human evaluation of identity stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Single-view operation?** The framework explicitly requires multiple reference views for the UOA and multi-reference adapter. Can MVHOI work with only a single reference view, perhaps by hallucinating missing views? This remains unexplored.
- **Robustness to challenging attributes?** How does MVHOI perform on textureless, symmetric, or reflective objects? The paper lacks per-attribute analysis.
- **Very long-duration stability?** Does cross-iterative inference eliminate drift for videos longer than 10 seconds? Evaluation stops at 81 frames.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete architectural details:** The exact tokenization, motion modulation, and transformer specifications for the UOA and multi-reference adapter are not provided, hindering faithful replication.
- **Undisclosed training data:** The proprietary ~100‑hour multi‑view HOI dataset is not described in sufficient detail (camera setup, object categories, interaction types) to reconstruct.
- **Missing hyperparameter specifics:** Attention enhancement parameters (α, injection layers) and training schedules (e.g., Stage II initial learning rate, batch size) are not fully disclosed.

## Confidence
- Implicit 3D motion anchor (UOA): **Medium** – strong metrics but core assumption not directly tested.
- Geometry-aware texture retrieval (attention bias): **Medium** – no ablation of the bias mechanism; correlation assumed.
- Cross-iterative stabilization: **Medium** – no direct baseline for drift; risk of error propagation remains.

## Next Checks
1. **UOA geometry test:** Evaluate coarse outputs from Stage I on a synthetic HOI dataset with known 6D poses to measure pose error and view-consistent FID.
2. **Attention bias ablation:** Disable attention enhancement (α=0) in Stage II and measure O-CLIP and visual artifacts on >90° rotations.
3. **Cross-iteration vs autoregressive:** Compare identity drift (e.g., LPIPS over time) between the proposed loop and a purely autoregressive rollout on long videos.
