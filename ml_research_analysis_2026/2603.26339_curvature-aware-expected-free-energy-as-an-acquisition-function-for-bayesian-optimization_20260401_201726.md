# Curvature-aware Expected Free Energy as an Acquisition Function for Bayesian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.26339  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26339  
- **Reference count**: 15  
- **Primary result**: Introduces a curvature-aware Expected Free Energy acquisition function that outperforms six state-of-the-art Bayesian optimization methods on 1D benchmarks and improves learning in high-curvature regions.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel acquisition function for Bayesian optimization based on the Expected Free Energy (EFE) from active inference. EFE naturally balances exploitation (pragmatic value) and exploration (epistemic value). The authors introduce an adaptive update rule for the temperature parameter τ² that responds to the local curvature and uncertainty of the Gaussian process posterior, making the method curvature-aware. Theoretically, they show that EFE generalizes standard acquisition functions (UCB, LCB, EIG) and, for strictly concave functions, unbiased convergence to the optimum is guaranteed when τ² is set appropriately. Empirically, on 50 randomly generated one-dimensional functions, the adaptive EFE achieves the lowest final simple regret and near-minimal GP mean squared error compared to six benchmarks. A case study on system identification for a Van der Pol oscillator confirms that the adaptive method learns high-curvature regions more accurately than non‑adaptive variants.

## Method Summary
The method uses a Gaussian process (GP) surrogate to model the unknown function. At each iteration, it computes the Expected Free Energy acquisition function  
G(x) = (μ(x) – y*)²/(2τ²) + (σ²(x)+σₙ²)/(2τ²) – ½·ln(1+σ²(x)/σₙ²),  
where μ(x) and σ²(x) are the GP posterior mean and latent variance, σₙ² is noise variance, and y* is a reference target (typically the current best observed value). The key innovation is an adaptive temperature τ² that varies across the input space: τ⁻²(x) = |μ''(x)| + 1/σ²(x), which is then normalized to a fixed interval [τ_min², τ_max²] (e.g., [1,30]). This rule increases τ⁻² (decreases τ²) in regions of high curvature and low uncertainty, shifting the balance toward exploitation, while in flat or uncertain regions τ² increases to favor exploration. After updating τ², the acquisition is evaluated at all candidate points, the point minimizing G(x) is queried, and the GP is updated with the new observation. The process repeats for a fixed budget.

## Key Results
- EFE generalizes UCB, LCB, and EIG under specific linearization assumptions (Gaussian preference, marginal information gain).
- For locally strictly concave functions, EFE with τ² = σ²(x⋆) + σₙ² yields unbiased convergence to the global optimum.
- Empirically, on 50 random 1D functions, adaptive EFE achieves final simple regret 0.0125 ± 0.0156 and GP MSE 0.0286 ± 0.0075, outperforming six baseline methods (UCB, EI, PI, TS, VAR, KG). The Van der Pol case study shows adaptive EFE better captures high‑curvature dynamics than non‑adaptive EFE.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Expected Free Energy Generalizes Standard Acquisition Functions
- **Claim**: Under specific assumptions (Gaussian preference distribution and marginal information gain approximation), the EFE functional reduces to forms that specialize to Upper Confidence Bound (UCB), Lower Confidence Bound (LCB), and Expected Information Gain (EIG).
- **Mechanism**: By setting a Gaussian prior over desired outcomes p(y) = N(y*, τ²) and expanding the EFE using a linear Taylor approximation around a reference point, the pragmatic and epistemic terms combine to yield linear expressions in μ(x). In the limit where y* is much larger than μ₀, the pragmatic term dominates and the acquisition becomes LCB; when τ² is large relative to predictive variance, it becomes UCB; dropping the pragmatic term recovers EIG.
- **Core assumptions**: Gaussian preference prior; Assumption 1 that the information gain is approximated using only the marginal posterior at the query location (ignoring spatial correlations).
- **Evidence**: Theorems III.1, III.2, III.3 provide derivations; abstract states the generalization.
- **Break condition**: If the preference distribution is non‑Gaussian or full information gain (non‑marginal) is necessary, the linear relationships fail.

### Mechanism 2: Unbiased Local Convergence for Concave Functions
- **Claim**: For a locally strictly concave true function f, EFE with τ² = σ²(x⋆) + σₙ² converges unbiasedly to the global optimum x⋆.
- **Mechanism**: A second‑order Taylor expansion of the GP posterior mean μ(x) and variance σ²(x) around x⋆ yields a quadratic surrogate for the (maximization‑form) EFE. The specific choice of τ² cancels the linear term in h = x – x⋆, forcing the stationary point to be at h=0. Under the condition that the posterior variance gradient g ≠ 0, this stationary point is a local maximum, ensuring convergence to x⋆ as the GP posterior tightens.
- **Core assumptions**: f is locally strictly concave; GP posterior is accurately approximated quadratically near x⋆.
- **Evidence**: Theorem IV.1 gives the full proof.
- **Break condition**: If f is not concave or the GP posterior curvature estimates are poor (due to noise or insufficient data), the guarantee breaks.

### Mechanism 3: Curvature‑Aware Temperature Adaptation
- **Claim**: The adaptive update rule τ⁻² = |μ''(x)| + 1/σ²(x) makes EFE aware of local curvature and uncertainty, biasing queries toward high‑curvature, low‑uncertainty regions to improve joint optimization and learning.
- **Mechanism**: The rule increases the inverse temperature (decreases τ²) where the GP posterior mean is highly curved (large |μ''(x)|) and predictive variance is low. This amplifies the pragmatic term in EFE, pulling queries toward local peaks in confident regions. Conversely, large τ² in flat/uncertain regions amplifies the epistemic term, promoting exploration. Normalization and clipping to [τ_min², τ_max²] bound the strategy between pure exploration and exploitation.
- **Core assumption**: High posterior curvature correlates with proximity to a local optimum; learning is best served by denser sampling in such regions.
- **Evidence**: Section IV‑B defines the update; Section V‑A (Van der Pol case study) shows adaptive EFE explores high‑curvature regions better than non‑adaptive EFE.
- **Break condition**: If GP mean curvature is poorly estimated (e.g., over‑smoothing, noisy derivatives), the heuristic may misdirect queries; the rule lacks theoretical guarantees.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
GP Model (maintains posterior μ(x), σ²(x), μ''(x)) → EFE Computation (evaluates G(x) with current τ²) → Adaptive τ² Update (computes τ_i⁻² = |μ''(x)| + 1/σ²(x), normalizes to [τ_min², τ_max²]) → Acquisition Maximization (selects x_next = argmin_x G(x)) → Data Update (adds (x_next, y_next) to dataset, updates GP posterior) → back to GP Model.

**Critical path** (per iteration):
1. Update GP posterior with all available data.
2. For all candidate x, compute μ(x), σ²(x), and μ''(x).
3. Compute adaptive τ² using the update rule and normalize.
4. Evaluate EFE G(x) for all x.
5. Choose next query point x_next = argmin G(x).
6. Observe noisy y_next = f(x_next) + ε.
7. Append (x_next, y_next) to dataset and repeat.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **τ² bounds**: The interval [τ_min², τ_max²] controls the strength of adaptivity; too narrow limits the dynamic range, too wide may cause instability (e.g., τ² → 0 leads to over‑exploitation, τ² → ∞ to pure exploration).
- **Curvature estimation cost**: Computing μ''(x) adds overhead; can be done via analytical derivatives (possible for RBF kernels) or finite differences, trading fidelity for speed.
- **Myopia**: The one‑step EFE is myopic; non‑myopic planning could improve efficiency but requires multi‑step GP belief propagation, increasing complexity.

**Failure signatures**:
- **τ² collapse**: If max(τ_i²) is very small, most τ² values stick near τ_min², causing premature exploitation and insufficient exploration.
- **Noisy curvature**: Erratic μ''(x) estimates (from poor GP hyperparameters or insufficient data) lead to oscillatory τ² updates and unstable query patterns.
- **GP mismatch**: If the true function is non‑stationary or kernel length‑scale is poorly set, μ''(x) may not correlate with true optima curvature, misguiding adaptation.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Reproduce the Van der Pol system identification case study: compare adaptive vs. non‑adaptive EFE and verify that adaptive EFE samples more densely in high‑curvature regions (as shown in Fig. 1 of the paper).
2. Sweep τ_min² and τ_max² on the 1D random functions benchmark: vary the bounds and measure performance; expect degradation if bounds are too restrictive.
3. Ablation on curvature term: compare adaptive EFE against a variant where τ_i⁻² = 1/σ²(x) only (no |μ''| term). The full adaptive rule should outperform on functions with distinct high‑curvature optima.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalable non‑myopic multi‑step EFE**: How to extend EFE to a multi‑step planning framework that propagates GP beliefs over action sequences? The paper identifies this as a future direction but lacks a derivation and empirical validation.
2. **Impact of Assumption 1 (marginal information gain)**: To what extent does approximating full information gain with marginal gain degrade performance in problems with strong spatial correlations or higher dimensions? The paper's analysis is limited to 1D.
3. **Curvature‑aware τ² in higher dimensions**: Does the update rule τ⁻² = |μ''(x)| + 1/σ²(x) remain effective in multi‑dimensional input spaces? The rule uses a 1D curvature measure; generalization to higher dimensions is unclear.
4. **Convergence on non‑concave functions**: Under what conditions does EFE with adaptive τ² converge to a global optimum for multi‑modal functions? The theoretical guarantee only covers strictly concave functions; empirical results on random 1D functions are promising but not understood theoretically.
5. **Sensitivity to τ_min² and τ_max²**: How sensitive is performance to the choice of these hyperparameters, and can they be set automatically? The paper uses fixed values [1,30] without analysis.

## Limitations
- **Preference target y***: The paper does not specify how y* is set during BO when the true optimum is unknown, which is critical for practical implementation.
- **Non‑adaptive EFE τ² not reported**: The value used for comparison in the Van der Pol case study is missing, hindering reproduction.
- **Adaptive τ² heuristic lacks theory**: The curvature‑based update rule is not supported by theoretical guarantees and may be sensitive to curvature estimation errors.

## Confidence
- Generalization to UCB/LCB/EIG: **High** (rigorous proofs given assumptions).
- Unbiased convergence for concave functions: **Medium** (proof solid but relies on strong local approximations and GP accuracy).
- Adaptive τ² performance: **Medium** (strong empirical evidence on 1D, but no theory and possible overfitting).
