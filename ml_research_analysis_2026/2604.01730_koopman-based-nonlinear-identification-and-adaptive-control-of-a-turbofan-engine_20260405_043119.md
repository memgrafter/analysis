# Koopman-Based Nonlinear Identification and Adaptive Control of a Turbofan Engine

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01730  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01730  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A single Koopman model identified via meta‑heuristic EDDM accurately predicts spool speeds and EPR, enabling two controllers (AKMPC with disturbance observer, K‑FBLC) that outperform PI in IAE under sea‑level and varying flight conditions.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses multivariable turbofan engine control by constructing a data‑driven Koopman model that linearizes nonlinear dynamics in a lifted space. The model is identified using a meta‑heuristic extended dynamic mode decomposition (MH‑EDMD) with a cost function jointly minimizing prediction errors for spool speeds (N1, N2) and engine pressure ratio (EPR). This single model is reused for two control strategies: an adaptive model predictive controller (AKMPC) with a disturbance observer for robustness, and a Koopman‑based feedback linearization controller (K‑FBLC) as a benchmark. Results show accurate multi‑output predictions (MAE: N1 ~20 RPM, N2 ~15 RPM, EPR ~0.011) and superior tracking performance, especially under varying flight conditions, where AKMPC reduces IAE compared to PI and K‑FBLC.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a component‑level model (CLM) of a turbofan engine, validated against GasTurb, to generate training data under closed‑loop excitation. They apply MH‑EDMD to identify a Koopman operator: lifting functions (e.g.,IQ RBFs) are parameter‑optimized via PSO and Nelder‑Mead to minimize multi‑output MAE while imposing stability, controllability, and observability constraints. The resulting discrete‑time Koopman system is transformed to eigenfunction form and augmented with actuator dynamics. For control, (i) AKMPC uses an incremental state formulation and a disturbance observer (EKF) to compensate model mismatch, solving a QP at each step; (ii) K‑FBLC derives a control‑affine representation from the Koopman model and applies feedback linearization with PD virtual inputs. Both are simulated on the CLM under sea‑level and time‑varying flight profiles, with performance measured by IAE.

## Key Results
- **Prediction accuracy:** MAE on validation set: N1 20.3 RPM, N2 15.17 RPM, EPR 0.0114.
- **Tracking performance (N1‑N2 control):** AKMPC IAE: sea‑level 14869 vs. PI 17381 and K‑FBLC 16763; varying flight 23835 vs. PI 24632 and K‑FBLC 27971.
- **EPR‑based control:** AKMPC improves thrust response with reduced overshoot; disturbance observer mitigates oscillations under flight condition variations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
A single Koopman model accurately predicts multiple engine outputs by optimizing a cost function that jointly minimizes MAE for all targets, while adding penalties for unstable eigenvalues and promoting observable diversity. This yields a linear lifted‑state model (A, B, C, D) that can be reused across different control schemes. The MH‑EDMD algorithm (PSO + Nelder‑Mead) tunes parameters of IQ RBF lifting functions, ensuring the eigenfunctions capture both spool‑speed and EPR dynamics. The C matrix maps specific lifted states to each output. If the training envelope is exceeded or cost weights are unbalanced, accuracy may degrade for some outputs.

### Mechanism 2
The disturbance observer in AKMPC compensates for model mismatch and unmodeled disturbances (e.g., flight condition changes) by augmenting the Koopman system with an integrator state and using an extended Kalman filter. The innovation (measurement minus prediction) updates the disturbance estimate, which is added to the MPC’s output prediction. This adaptive offset correction assumes slowly varying additive disturbances and sufficient observability of the Koopman model. If disturbances change too rapidly or are state‑dependent, the observer may lag or diverge.

### Mechanism 3
The Koopman model is transformed into a control‑affine form in the original state space (spool speeds are lifted observables). Differentiating the lifting functions yields expressions for ẋ and ẍ in terms of Φ and its Jacobians, leading to equations of the form ẋ = f(x) + g(x)u, ẍ = f₂(x) + g₂(x)u, and ˙EPR = f₃(x) + g₃(x)u. Stacking derivatives gives a square decoupling matrix G; if invertible, virtual inputs v impose linear error dynamics via u = G⁻¹(v – f). This requires accurate relative degrees (2 for spool speeds, 1 for EPR) and a nonsingular G within the operating region. Model inaccuracies or singularities break the cancellation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Koopman Operator Theory** – Linearizes nonlinear dynamics by lifting states into a space where evolution is linear. *Why needed:* Enables use of linear control techniques (MPC, LQR) with nonlinear models. *Quick check:* "How does lifting via eigenfunctions render nonlinear dynamics linear?"
- **Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD)** – Data‑driven approximation of the Koopman operator from trajectories. *Why needed:* Identifies A, B matrices from measured data. *Quick check:* "What role do observables play in EDMD accuracy, and how is the regression solved?"
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** – Optimizes control moves over a horizon subject to constraints. *Why needed:* Handles multivariable tracking and input limits. *Quick check:* "Why is the Koopman model evaluated at the current time and held constant over the prediction horizon in AKMPC?"
- **Disturbance Observer (DO)** – Estimates and cancels unmodeled disturbances. *Why needed:* Improves robustness to flight condition changes and model mismatch. *Quick check:* "How does the DO in AKMPC integrate with the Koopman model and Kalman filter?"
- **Feedback Linearization** – Transforms a nonlinear system into a linear one via state feedback. *Why needed:* Provides a benchmark controller (K‑FBLC) directly from the Koopman model. *Quick check:* "What assumptions (relative degree, decoupling matrix invertibility) are required for K‑FBLC to work?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** CLM (MATLAB) → MH‑EDMD module → Koopman matrices (A, B, C, D) → [AKMPC (with DO & Kalman filter) / K‑FBLC (Jacobians & gains)] → Closed‑loop simulation with CLM as plant.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Validate CLM against GasTurb.
  2. Generate training data via closed‑loop excitation (steps, sinusoids) with added noise; normalize using corrected parameters.
  3. Select observable family (e.g., IQ RBF) and run MH‑EDMD (PSO → Nelder‑Mead) with cost function (MAE + constraints) to obtain Koopman matrices.
  4. Eigen‑decompose A; form eigen‑system (Λ, G_φ, C_φ). Augment with actuator dynamics.
  5. Design Kalman filter on eigen‑system (Q_φ, Q_d, R_o).
  6. For AKMPC: build incremental state, set horizons (np=30, nc=5), tune weights (Q_y, R, Q_T), implement DO.
  7. For K‑FBLC: compute Jacobian of lifting functions, derive F(x), G(x), choose PD gains.
  8. Simulate both controllers under sea‑level and varying flight; compute IAE.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Higher model order improves accuracy but increases MPC computation; cost function weights balance accuracy vs. stability; time‑varying B(x) captures nonlinearity but needs online evaluation; DO adds tuning complexity vs. model fidelity trade‑off.
- **Failure signatures:** High prediction MAE (poor identification); QP infeasibility (MPC); large/disturbance estimates chasing noise; oscillations due to inaccurate model or singular G; degraded tracking under flight variation (DO/model insufficiency).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. CLM validation against GasTurb.
  2. Koopman model selection: compare MAE for different basis functions (LF, GAU, IQ) on validation set.
  3. Controller benchmarking under sea‑level: PI vs. K‑FBLC vs. AKMPC IAE for N1‑N2 step tracking.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

2. How does the approach perform on physical engine hardware?
3. Can Koopman models enable effective direct thrust control with reliable real‑time thrust estimation?

## Limitations
- Exact GasTurb component maps and scaling procedure undisclosed; CLM replication requires approximations.
- Training reference trajectory and noise seed not specified; flight profiles only qualitatively shown.
- MH‑EDMD hyperparameters (RBF parameters, eigenvalue bounds, tolerances) omitted.
- Absolute input saturation limits not given; only Δu increment constraint provided.
- Sampling time Δt not explicitly stated.
- Disturbance observer assumes slowly varying additive errors; may fail for fast, state‑dependent mismatches.
- Feedback linearization assumes exact relative degrees and globally invertible decoupling matrix.

## Confidence
- **A. Single Koopman model for multi‑output prediction:** Medium – Supported by MAE metrics but relies on unspecified hyperparameters and a specific training set.
- **B. AKMPC with DO improves robustness:** High – Direct before/after DO comparisons with quantified IAE gains under varying flight.
- **C. Koopman‑based feedback linearization works:** Medium – Comparable to AKMPC in steady‑state but sensitivity to model errors and singularities not fully explored.
