# Learning-to-Defer with Expert-Conditioned Advice

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.14324
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14324
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes an augmented surrogate for learning-to-defer with advice, proving H-consistency and showing empirical improvement over standard L2D across multiple tasks.

## Executive Summary
Standard learning-to-defer (L2D) assumes each expert has fixed information. This paper introduces Learning-to-Defer with Advice, where the policy jointly selects an expert and an advice action to provide to that expert. The authors prove that a natural separated approach—with independent routing and advice heads—is Fisher inconsistent even for two experts and binary advice. To resolve this, they propose an augmented surrogate over the combined expert-advice action space, accompanied by an H-consistency guarantee and an excess-risk transfer bound that ensures recovery of the Bayes-optimal policy. Empirical results on tabular (fraud detection), language (FEVER), and multimodal (CLIP prompt escalation) datasets show the augmented method adapts to advice cost and outperforms standard L2D in most regimes.

## Method Summary
The method extends L2D by introducing discrete advice actions that condition the expert’s decision. The agent selects a pair (expert, advice) from a finite composite space. Two surrogate losses are considered: a separated one that independently predicts the expert and advice, and an augmented one that jointly predicts the pair. The separated surrogate is proven Fisher inconsistent, while the augmented surrogate is H-consistent and yields an excess-risk bound guaranteeing convergence to the Bayes-optimal policy as surrogate risk vanishes. Implementation involves a scoring model that outputs a value for every (expert, advice) combination; at inference, the pair with the highest score is selected.

## Key Results
- Theoretical: Proved Fisher inconsistency of separated surrogates for two experts and binary advice; proposed augmented surrogate with H-consistency and excess-risk transfer guarantee.
- Empirical: Augmented method improves over standard L2D on fraud detection, FEVER, and CLIP prompt escalation tasks, adapting to advice cost. On FEVER, at λ=0 it reduces true loss by 0.064 relative to L2D; across 8 cost regimes, it attains the lowest mean true loss in 7.
- Synthetic validation: A synthetic benchmark confirms the predicted failure of separated surrogates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on optimizing a surrogate over the full composite expert-advice space. By avoiding the decoupling of expert selection and advice selection, the augmented surrogate circumvents the Fisher inconsistency that plagues separated approaches. The theoretical guarantees ensure that minimizing this surrogate yields a policy close to the Bayes-optimal one, both conditionally and in overall risk. Empirically, the method learns to balance the cost of advice against the benefit of improved expert decisions, dynamically acquiring advice only when it reduces true loss sufficiently.

## Foundational Learning
- **Learning-to-Defer (L2D)**: Framework where a classifier can defer decisions to experts. *Why needed*: Integrates automated and human intelligence. *Quick check*: Standard L2D assumes experts have pre-specified, fixed information.
- **Fisher consistency**: A surrogate loss is Fisher consistent if its minimizer also minimizes the true expected loss. *Why needed*: Guarantees that surrogate optimization aligns with true risk minimization. *Quick check*: For binary classification, logistic loss is Fisher consistent.
- **H-consistency**: Stronger condition ensuring that a hypothesis class can achieve zero training error only if the Bayes risk is zero. *Why needed*: Provides finite-sample guarantees for a given model class. *Quick check*: H-consistency implies Fisher consistency but also accounts for hypothesis class expressiveness.
- **Advice action**: Choice of additional information provided to an expert. *Why needed*: Experts often improve with contextual input. *Quick check*: This work assumes a finite discrete advice space.
- **Composite action space**: The set of all (expert, advice) pairs. *Why needed*: The policy must choose both components simultaneously. *Quick check*: Space size equals number of experts multiplied by number of advice actions.
- **Surrogate loss**: A tractable loss function used as a proxy for the true (often intractable) expected loss. *Why needed*: Enables optimization via gradient-based methods and provides a convex relaxation. *Quick check*: For binary classification, logistic loss and hinge loss are common surrogate losses for the 0-1 loss.
- **Bayes-optimal policy**: The decision rule that minimizes the expected true loss over the distribution of data. *Why needed*: Serves as the theoretical gold standard; learning algorithms aim to approach this optimum. *Quick check*: In binary classification with 0-1 loss, it predicts the class with higher conditional probability given the input.
- **Excess risk**: The difference between the risk (expected true loss) of a learned policy and the Bayes-optimal risk. *Why needed*: Measures how suboptimal a method is; consistency means excess risk → 0 with more data. *Quick check*: A classifier with accuracy 0.85 when Bayes error is 0.8 has excess risk 0.05 (in terms of error rate).
- **Excess-risk transfer bound**: A theoretical guarantee that bounds the excess risk of the true policy by a constant times the excess surrogate risk minimized during training. *Why needed*: Ensures that minimizing the surrogate loss directly leads to a policy with low true risk, providing consistency and generalization. *Quick check*: This paper establishes such a bound for the augmented surrogate, linking surrogate risk minimization to true risk minimization.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
- **Input**: An instance \(x\) (e.g., a transaction record, a claim, or an image) represented as a feature vector or raw data.
- **Scoring model**: A differentiable function \(f_θ\) (typically a neural network) that takes \(x\) and outputs a score for each possible \((e, a)\) pair. The output layer has \(E \times A\) units, where \(E\) is the number of experts and \(A\) the number of advice actions. The shared body learns representations that are used across all pairs.
- **Decision rule**: The pair \((e^*, a^*) = \arg\max_{e, a} f_θ(x)_{e,a}\) is selected. This is equivalent to treating the composite action space as multi-class.
- **Expert module**: Each expert \(e\) is a pre-specified decision-maker that, given \(x\) and advice \(a\), outputs a prediction \(\hat{y}\). The expert’s internal mechanism is fixed; only the advice changes.
- **Loss computation**: After obtaining \(\hat{y}\), the true loss \(\ell(\hat{y}, y_{\text{true}})\) is computed. If advice \(a\) is non-empty (or incurs cost), an additional penalty \(\lambda \cdot c(a)\) is added, where \(\lambda\) is the advice cost hyperparameter and \(c(a)\) is a cost (usually 1 for any advice). The total loss is \(L = \ell(\hat{y}, y) + \lambda \cdot c(a)\).

**Critical path**:
1. Forward pass: For each training example \((x, y)\), compute all scores \(s_{e,a} = f_θ(x)_{e,a}\).
2. Augmented surrogate loss: The loss function compares the scores to the conditional expected total loss (true loss + advice cost) for each pair. Specifically, the surrogate is a proper loss over the composite action space, often of the form:
   \[
   \phi_{\text{aug}}(f_θ(x); \Psi(x)) = \log\left(\sum_{e,a} \exp(\Psi(x)_{e,a} - f_θ(x)_{e,a})\right) + \text{const},
   \]
   where \(\Psi(x)_{e,a}\) approximates the negative conditional expected total loss of choosing \((e,a)\). During training, \(\Psi\) is estimated from the data or via a separate model. The exact form ensures Fisher consistency and H-consistency.
3. Minimization: The model parameters \(\theta\) are updated by gradient descent on the average surrogate loss across the training set, optionally with regularization.
4. Inference: At test time, the pair with the highest score is directly selected; no surrogate is needed.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Separated vs. augmented surrogate**: The separated approach models the score as \(f_{e,a}(x) = g_e(x) + h_a(x)\) or uses two separate networks, reducing the output dimension from \(E \times A\) to \(E + A\). This lowers memory and computation, but such factorized representations cannot capture arbitrary interactions between expert and advice. As proven, the separated surrogate is Fisher inconsistent even for two experts and binary advice, leading to suboptimal policies. The augmented joint model avoids this pitfall but scales poorly with \(E\) and \(A\) because the output size grows multiplicatively.
- **Advice cost λ**: This parameter directly trades off between prediction accuracy and resource usage. A higher \(\lambda\) discourages advice acquisition, even when it could improve accuracy; a lower \(\lambda\) encourages more advice. The method adapts automatically, but setting \(\lambda\) requires cross-validation or a user-specified budget.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Separated surrogate**: The learned policy may converge to a distribution over \((e,a)\) that does not match the Bayes-optimal distribution, resulting in excess risk that does not vanish with more data. Empirically, the method often underperforms when advice is highly complementary to certain experts.
- **Large action space**: For many experts or many advice actions, the augmented model’s output layer becomes large, increasing memory (storing all scores) and computational cost (computing all scores). This may become a bottleneck, requiring approximations that might reintroduce inconsistency.
- **Mismatched cost λ**: If \(\lambda\) is set incorrectly relative to the true cost-benefit trade-off, the policy may either overuse or underuse advice, missing the optimal balance.

**First three experiments**:
1. **Synthetic benchmark**: Construct a simple environment where the Bayes-optimal policy is known (two experts, binary advice). Results show that the separated surrogate fails to recover the optimal policy (excess risk remains high), while the augmented surrogate converges to the optimum as data increases.
2. **Fraud detection (tabular)**: On a fraud detection dataset, experts are simple rule-based detectors, and advice is a binary flag (e.g., “request additional verification”). The augmented method achieves lower total loss (true loss + advice cost) than both standard L2D (which ignores advice) and separated L2D across various \(\lambda\) values, demonstrating the benefit of learned advice.
3. **FEVER (language)**: The FEVER dataset involves claim verification using Wikipedia evidence. Experts are pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT), and advice is a sentence from the evidence. The augmented method adapts to \(\lambda\) and, at \(\lambda=0\), reduces the true loss by 0.064 compared to standard L2D; across eight \(\lambda\) settings, it attains the lowest mean true loss in seven, showing robust improvement.

## Limitations
- Assumes discrete finite advice and perfect surrogate optimization; may not extend to continuous or non-stationary advice settings.
- Evaluation limited to three datasets and a simple synthetic benchmark; generalizability to other domains (e.g., high-dimensional advice) is untested.
- Scalability concerns: the composite action space grows combinatorially with experts and advice actions; scaling analysis for many experts is missing.

## Confidence
- Separated surrogate Fisher inconsistency: High
- Augmented surrogate H‑consistency/excess‑risk guarantee: Medium
- Empirical superiority over standard L2D: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Re-run synthetic benchmark with more experts (≥3) and continuous advice, across seeds, to validate failure of separated surrogates.
2. Test on two extra real-world datasets (e.g., medical triage) with free-form advice, reporting metrics with confidence intervals.
