# A Case Study of Selected PTQ Baselines for Reasoning LLMs on Ascend NPU

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17693
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17693
- Reference count: 11
- This paper presents an empirical study of post-training quantization (PTQ) baselines on Huawei Ascend NPUs, focusing on reasoning-oriented models such as DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen and QwQ-32B.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an empirical study of post-training quantization (PTQ) baselines on Huawei Ascend NPUs, focusing on reasoning-oriented models such as DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen and QwQ-32B. It evaluates four PTQ methods—AWQ, GPTQ, SmoothQuant, and FlatQuant—across 3-bit to 8-bit precision settings. Results show that 4-bit weight-only quantization is viable for larger models, while 8-bit weight-activation quantization remains numerically stable. However, 4-bit weight-activation schemes suffer from layer-wise calibration instability on Ascend NPUs, leading to logic collapse in long-context reasoning tasks. A real-world INT8 deployment demonstrates that optimized kernels can reduce latency, but dynamic quantization overheads limit end-to-end acceleration. Overall, the study highlights platform-specific sensitivity in low-bit quantization and provides practical guidance for deploying quantized reasoning models on Ascend NPUs.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates post-training quantization methods on Huawei Ascend NPUs for reasoning-oriented large language models. Four PTQ algorithms (AWQ, GPTQ, SmoothQuant, FlatQuant) are applied across 3-bit to 8-bit configurations to models including DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen and QwQ-32B. Experiments use fake quantization for most evaluations and implement real INT8 quantization for validation. Performance is measured across reasoning and QA benchmarks (AIME-120, MATH-500, GSM8K, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench, ARC-Easy/ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, PIQA, WinoGrande), with latency comparisons between pseudo-quantization and real quantization. The methodology includes layer-wise MSE analysis to identify sensitivity patterns and assess calibration stability.

## Key Results
- 4-bit weight-only quantization is viable for larger reasoning models on Ascend NPUs
- 8-bit weight-activation quantization maintains numerical stability across all tested models
- 4-bit weight-activation quantization suffers from severe layer-wise calibration instability, causing logic collapse in long-context reasoning tasks on Ascend NPUs

## Foundational Learning
- Post-training quantization (PTQ): Converts pretrained models to lower precision without retraining, reducing memory and compute requirements. Why needed: Enables deployment of large models on resource-constrained hardware. Quick check: Verify PTQ reduces model size by expected factor (e.g., 4x for 4-bit vs 16-bit).
- Layer-wise calibration: Independent quantization of each layer using statistics from representative data. Why needed: Prevents global information loss but can cause isolated layer failures. Quick check: Monitor per-layer MSE during calibration to identify outlier layers.
- Weight-activation quantization: Quantizes both model weights and activation tensors to the same bit-width. Why needed: Maximizes compute efficiency but increases sensitivity to numerical instability. Quick check: Compare W8A8 performance against W4A4 to assess stability vs efficiency tradeoff.
- Kernel optimization for INT8: Custom implementations of low-bit operations to minimize overhead. Why needed: Native operators may not be optimized for specific hardware or bit-widths. Quick check: Profile latency of quantized ops versus FP16 baseline.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Model -> PTQ Method (AWQ/GPTQ/SmoothQuant/FlatQuant) -> Bit-width (3-8) -> Calibration -> Inference Backend (vLLM-Ascend)
- Critical path: Calibration data preparation -> Layer-wise statistics computation -> Quantization parameter generation -> Kernel execution -> Accuracy/latency measurement
- Design tradeoffs: 4-bit W-A-KV offers maximum efficiency but suffers from instability; 8-bit W-A-KV is stable but less efficient; dynamic quantization enables flexible deployment but adds overhead
- Failure signatures: Logic collapse in reasoning tasks, MSE oscillations during calibration, PPL spikes in specific layers, increased latency in INT8 deployment
- First experiments: 1) Establish baseline W8A8KV8 stability with AWQ/GPTQ/SmoothQuant on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, 2) Implement and test FlatQuant-W4A4KV4 with original hyperparameters, 3) Compare platform sensitivity by running same quantization on X2000 GPU

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the fundamental cause of 4-bit weight-activation quantization instability on Ascend NPUs for long-context reasoning tasks, and how can it be systematically mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that FlatQuant-W4A4KV4 exhibits severe instability on Ascend NPUs, leading to logic collapse in long-context reasoning tasks, while the same algorithm works stably on X2000 GPUs.
- Why unresolved: The authors note platform-specific numerical sensitivity but do not identify the precise hardware or software factors causing the instability, nor do they provide a systematic solution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative profiling of operator implementations, analysis of numerical precision differences between NPU and GPU backends, and development of NPU-specific calibration strategies that address the observed layer-wise MSE oscillations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the overhead from dynamic quantization operators in real-quantized INT8 inference on Ascend NPUs be eliminated to achieve end-to-end acceleration?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors report that while optimized INT8×INT8 kernels reduce latency, dynamic quantization overheads currently prevent full end-to-end speedup compared to full-precision inference.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the bottleneck but does not propose or evaluate solutions for eliminating these overheads.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of fused quantization-aware operators, operator fusion strategies, or hardware-level support for eliminating separate quantization steps in the inference pipeline.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can layer-wise calibration objectives be modified to prevent catastrophic failure from outlier layers in low-bit quantization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that performance degradation is dominated by one or few layers with abnormally large quantization error, and suggest this relates to independent layer-wise calibration.
- Why unresolved: While the problem is identified, the paper does not propose specific alternative calibration objectives or methods that expose cross-layer effects.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of joint calibration strategies, global reconstruction error objectives, or multi-layer optimization approaches that prevent isolated layer failures from dominating overall model quality.

## Limitations
- Platform-specific sensitivity of low-bit quantization on Ascend NPUs is not fully characterized across different NPU architectures
- Layer-wise calibration instability in 4-bit W-A-KV quantization lacks systematic mitigation strategies
- Study is limited to a specific set of reasoning models and benchmarks, which may not represent all deployment scenarios

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Claims about 4-bit weight-only quantization being viable for larger models and 8-bit weight-activation quantization remaining numerically stable
- Medium Confidence: Assertions about platform-specific sensitivity and calibration instability based on study observations but lacking comparative analysis
- Low Confidence: Practical guidance for deploying quantized reasoning models on Ascend NPUs is tentative due to single-platform basis
