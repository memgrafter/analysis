# HiFloat4 Format for Language Model Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11287  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11287  
- **Reference count:** 39  
- **Primary result:** HiF4 reduces quantization error vs. NVFP4 by 24% on Gaussian data and achieves lower mean accuracy degradation (0.5-1.7%) across 7 LLMs (7B–671B) on 8–10 benchmarks, with 66% less hardware area and 10% less power for 64-length dot products.

## Executive Summary
HiFloat4 (HiF4) is a 4-bit block floating-point format designed for efficient LLM inference. It uses 64-element groups with a three-level hierarchical scaling metadata (32 bits/group, ~0.5 bits/value) and E1M2 (3-bit significand) in-group elements. A wide E6M2 global scale maximizes dynamic range utilization, while two 1-bit micro-exponents (E1_8, E1_16) handle intra-group outliers. Compared to NVIDIA’s NVFP4, HiF4 reduces quantization error on synthetic data, shows markedly better accuracy on real LLMs (even rescuing models like Mistral-7B where NVFP4 fails), and requires significantly less hardware area and power for dot products. An enhanced PTQ method, HiGPTQ, further narrows the accuracy gap with BF16.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates HiF4 for post-training quantization (PTQ) of LLM weights and activations. It introduces a direct-cast quantization algorithm (Algorithm 1) that computes a three-level scale hierarchy per 64-element block: an E6M2 global scale normalizes the block peak, then 1-bit micro-exponents for 8- and 16-element subgroups adjust for local outliers before rounding to E1M2 (S1P2) 4-bit elements. For hardware, it designs a 64-lane dot-product unit that absorbs micro-exponents as pre-shifts, enabling mostly integer accumulation with a single final floating-point scale multiplication. Performance is assessed via: (1) normalized MSE on Gaussian tensors, (2) accuracy on 8–10 NLP benchmarks across 7 LLMs (7B–671B), comparing HiF4 direct-cast, NVFP4 direct-cast, NVFP4 with per-tensor scaling (PTS), and HiF4 with a tailored GPTQ variant (HiGPTQ), and (3) area/power estimates for a 64-element dot-product implementation.

## Key Results
- **Gaussian data:** HiF4 reduces quantization error by 24% vs. NVFP4 (direct-cast) and by 53% vs. MXFP4.
- **LLM accuracy (mean degradation over benchmarks):** HiF4 achieves 0.5–1.7%, NVFP4+PTS 1.0–9.9%, NVFP4 direct-cast 1.0–40.9%. HiF4 prevents NVFP4’s catastrophic failure on Mistral-7B (NVFP4: ~26% on ARC-E; HiF4: ~58%).
- **Hardware:** For 64-length dot products, HiF4 requires 66% less area and 10% less power than NVFP4 by using only one small FP multiplier vs. four and eliminating complex partial-sum merging.
- **HiGPTQ:** Matching or exceeding BF16 accuracy on some models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) and further reducing degradation to ~0.2–0.9% on others.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Three-Level Hierarchical Scaling
- **Claim:** A three-level scaling hierarchy (E6M2 global + E1_8 + E1_16 micro-exponents) better captures inter- and intra-group dynamic range than two-level or flat designs.
- **Mechanism:** The E6M2 global scale normalizes each 64-element block’s peak to utilize the full intra-group representational space. The 1-bit micro-exponents for 8- and 16-element subgroups provide binary power-of-two adjustments to accommodate outliers within subgroups, reducing clipping from extreme values.
- **Core assumption:** Outlier-driven dynamic range variation within a 64-element block can be effectively modeled by a coarse global scale and two levels of binary exponents.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "...three-level scaling hierarchy, capturing inter- and intra-group dynamic range..."  
  - [section II.A] Equation (2) defines the value representation: `value = (significand) × 2^(E6M2 + E1_8 + E1_16)`.  
  - [corpus] Neighbor work (e.g., RaZeR) identifies NVFP4’s lack of hierarchical micro-exponents as a limitation, indirectly supporting HiF4’s novelty.

### Mechanism 2: Fixed-Point 64-Lane Dot Product
- **Claim:** A large 64-element group size enables near fixed-point dot product computation with minimal floating-point operations, drastically reducing hardware area and power.
- **Mechanism:** The 64-element block exactly matches a 64-lane dot-product unit. Micro-exponents are absorbed as pre-shifts into the 4-bit elements (forming 5-bit integers), allowing 64 multiply-accumulates using pure integer arithmetic except for one final `E6M2_A × E6M2_B` floating-point multiply.
- **Core assumption:** Modern matrix-compute units (e.g., Tensor Cores) have a fixed 64-lane dot-product pipeline; aligning the block format eliminates partial-result merging overhead.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section III.B] Figure 4 shows HiF4 requires "only one small floating-point multiplier" vs. NVFP4’s "four small floating-point multipliers" and more complex accumulation.  
  - [abstract] "...enables matrix multiplications to be executed in a highly fixed-point manner, significantly reducing hardware area and power consumption."

### Mechanism 3: 3-Bit Significand (E1M2/S1P2)
- **Claim:** Using a 3-bit significand (E1M2) instead of a 2-bit significand (E2M1) provides a higher precision ceiling more robust across diverse LLM distributions after hierarchical scaling.
- **Mechanism:** E1M2 offers 4 representable magnitudes per sign (±0.25, ±0.5, ±1.0, ±1.75) vs. E2M1’s 3 (±0.5, ±1.0, ±1.5). This reduces rounding error on values within the post-scaling representable range, especially for fine magnitude distinctions.
- **Core assumption:** LLM tensors contain many values whose optimal quantization requires more than 3 distinct magnitudes per sign, even after hierarchical scaling.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section V] "we first argue that E1M2 (S1P2) with 3-bit significand offers higher precision ceiling than E2M1..."  
  - [Table II] Contrasts HiF4’s S1P2 (3-bit) with NVFP4’s E2M1 (2-bit).  
  - [corpus] The active exploration of alternative 4-bit integer formats (e.g., BCQ) implies the significand precision trade-off is not settled.

## Foundational Learning
- **Block Floating-Point (BFP):** A format where a block of elements shares a single exponent (scale); elements store only significand/sign. *Why needed:* HiF4 is a BFP variant; understanding per-block vs. per-element storage is foundational to the metadata-element trade-off. *Quick check:* In standard BFP, what information is stored per block versus per element?
- **Dynamic Range & Binades:** The ratio between max/min representable positive values; measured in binades (log₂ of the range). *Why needed:* HiF4’s innovation is managing dynamic range (inter-group via E6M2, intra-group via micro-exponents) within tight bit budgets. *Quick check:* If a format’s local dynamic range is 4.81 binades, what is the max/min value ratio?
- **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):** Quantizing a pre-trained model without retraining. *Why needed:* The paper evaluates HiF4 via direct-cast and a GPTQ-based method (HiGPTQ); PTQ’s challenges (outliers, calibration) explain why format design matters. *Quick check:* What is the key difference between "direct-cast" quantization and a PTQ method like GPTQ?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  HiF4 Block Structure → Conversion Engine → Dot-Product Unit → Metadata Caches  
  (Offline Calibration)      (Runtime)      (On-chip)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Offline/Calibration: For a tensor, run conversion (Alg 1) to produce HiF4 blocks and store them.  
  2. Runtime Load: Fetch a block pair (A,B) into dot-product unit registers.  
  3. Pre-processing: Absorb E1_16 and E1_8 into S1P2 as left-shifts, yielding 5-bit integers.  
  4. Compute: Execute 64 integer MACs, then multiply-accumulate final sum by product of two E6M2 scales.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Group Size (64):** Larger groups improve hardware efficiency (fixed 64-lane match) and reduce metadata/value but increase sensitivity to intra-group outliers and latency for small tensors.  
  - **Significand Bits (3-bit E1M2):** Increases precision for non-outliers but reduces raw intra-group dynamic range vs. 2-bit E2M1; compensated by hierarchical micro-exponents.  
  - **Global Scale Width (E6M2):** Wide 69-binade range avoids per-tensor scaling overhead but uses 8 bits of metadata; narrower scale would reduce metadata but require software scaling, hurting performance.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Dynamic Range Exhaustion:** Model collapse (e.g., Mistral-7B with NVFP4 direct-cast) when values exceed combined global+local range. HiF4’s wider E6M2 mitigates.  
  - **Hardware Mismatch:** If accelerator’s native dot-product lane count ≠ 64, area/power benefit diminishes due to block splitting/merging logic.  
  - **Ineffective Micro-exponents:** For very uniform distributions, the two-level 1-bit exponents provide no benefit but still incur 0.5 bits/value overhead.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Gaussian Error Validation:** Implement HiF4 and NVFP4 direct-cast quantizers. Generate Gaussian matrices across scales (σ ∈ [1e-3, 1e3]). Compute normalized MSE; verify HiF4:NVFP4 ratio ≈ 1:1.32 (24% improvement).  
  2. **Dot-product Hardware Model:** Build a cycle-accurate/RTL model of a 64-lane dot-product unit. Implement HiF4 flow (micro-exponent pre-shift, single final scale multiply) vs. NVFP4. Measure area (gate count) and power; confirm ~66% area / ~10% power reduction.  
  3. **Small Model Sanity Check:** Quantize LLaMA2-7B weights/activations via direct-cast HiF4 (Alg 1). Run inference on ARC-E and MMLU. Replicate mean accuracy drop ~1% vs. BF16 (Table III) to establish baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Training Potential:** Can HiF4 maintain advantages during training (backward passes, gradient quantization)? The paper only evaluates PTQ for inference; training stability/convergence with HiF4 is unexplored.
- **Per-Component Benefit:** For which specific model architectures, layer types, or tensor distributions does the three-level scaling hierarchy provide the most significant accuracy benefit over simpler schemes? The paper aggregates results; it does not dissect the contribution of each scaling level (E6M2 vs. E1_8 vs. E1_16) per layer type.
- **Optimal Group Size:** What is the optimal group size for HiF4 across different hardware targets and model scales? The paper fixes group size at 64; trade-offs between hardware efficiency (larger groups) and dynamic range adaptation/latency (smaller groups) are not explored.

## Limitations
- **HiGPTQ underspecified:** The exact adaptation of GPTQ to HiF4’s three-level scaling (optimization objective, hyperparameters) is not described, hindering faithful reproduction of the accuracy claims.
- **Evaluation setup ambiguity:** Precise model checkpoints (revisions, tokenizers), benchmark prompt templates, and evaluation code (e.g., `lm-eval-harness` versions) are undocumented, risking metric mismatch.
- **NVFP4+PTS pipeline unclear:** The implementation of per-tensor scaling (peak to 2688) for NVFP4 is underspecified (per-tensor vs. per-group, clipping rules), critical for fair comparison and understanding the Mistral-7B failure case.

## Confidence
- **Hardware efficiency claims (area/power 66%/10%):** High. Based on clear architectural argument (64-lane fixed-point flow) and explicit comparison in Section III.B, assuming the dot-product microarchitecture matches the paper’s assumed design.
- **Gaussian data error reduction (24%):** Medium. Supported by a defined synthetic experiment, but statistical significance and distribution parameters (variance, scale) are not fully specified.
- **LLM accuracy degradation numbers (0.5-1.7% vs. NVFP4):** Low-Medium. Dependent on many unreproducible factors (exact quantization implementation, model versions, benchmark suites). Claims are compelling but lack methodological granularity for independent verification.
