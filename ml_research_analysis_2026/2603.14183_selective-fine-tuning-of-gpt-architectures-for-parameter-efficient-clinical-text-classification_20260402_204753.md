# Selective Fine-Tuning of GPT Architectures for Parameter-Efficient Clinical Text Classification
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14183
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14183
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** Achieved 91.0% test accuracy and AUROC 0.96 while training only <6% (7.08M) of GPT-2’s 124M parameters; full fine-tuning attained 96.23% accuracy but required updating all parameters and took >2× longer per epoch.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a selective fine-tuning framework to efficiently adapt large pretrained language models for clinical text classification with limited labeled data. The approach freezes all but the final Transformer block, final layer normalization, and a lightweight linear classification head of GPT‑2 small, resulting in less than 6% of parameters being trainable (7.08 M out of 124 M). Evaluated on 50 000 MIMIC‑IV radiology reports using a binary label derived from a CheXpert‑style rule‑based extractor, the method reaches 91.0% test accuracy and an AUROC of 0.96. Compared to full fine‑tuning (96.23% accuracy), selective fine‑tuning cuts training time per epoch by more than half while drastically reducing memory for gradients and optimizer states. Head‑only training performed poorly (67.53% accuracy), demonstrating the necessity of adapting higher layers for this domain. The results establish a practical performance‑efficiency trade‑off for resource‑constrained clinical NLP.

## Method Summary
The study uses 50 000 radiology reports from MIMIC‑IV‑Note v2.2. Labels are generated via a CheXpert‑style rule‑based extraction that handles negation and uncertainty, yielding a binary target (“positive/uncertain” vs. “negative” for any disease). The model starts from the pretrained GPT‑2 small (124 M parameters). Only the final Transformer block (block 11), the final LayerNorm (`ln_f`), and a newly added linear classification head (mapping the final token’s 768‑dimensional hidden state to 2 classes) are trained; all earlier layers and embeddings remain frozen. Training uses AdamW (learning rate 2e‑5, batch size 16) for 10 epochs with cross‑entropy loss. The dataset is split randomly into 70% train, 15% validation, and 15% test. This configuration updates ≈7.08 M parameters (5.7% of the model). Training time per epoch is measured on an A100 GPU.

## Key Results
- Selective fine‑tuning: 91.0% test accuracy, AUROC 0.96, training only <6% of parameters (7.08 M), and ≈15 min per epoch.
- Full fine‑tuning: 96.23% accuracy but required updating 100% of parameters and ≈35 min per epoch (>2× longer).
- Head‑only training: 67.53% accuracy, confirming that simply training a linear head on frozen features is insufficient for this clinical task.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Freezing lower layers prevents catastrophic forgetting:** Lower Transformer blocks capture general linguistic knowledge, while higher layers encode task‑specific features. By freezing the first 11 blocks and embeddings, the model retains broad language understanding, and only the final block is fine‑tuned to adapt to the clinical domain. Evidence: [section III.D] supports hierarchical representations; neighboring work on selective PEFT provides indirect support. *Break condition:* If the clinical domain diverges drastically from pretraining, frozen layers may become maladapted, causing underfitting.
- **Training the final LayerNorm stabilizes adaptation:** The final LayerNorm adjusts the scale and shift of representations from the frozen backbone, re‑normalizing them for the clinical task. This lightweight adaptation corrects suboptimal pretrained normalization statistics. Evidence: The method explicitly lists `ln_f` as trainable and includes it in the small parameter count. *Break condition:* If pretrained statistics are already well‑aligned, updating LayerNorm could introduce noise or instability.
- **Parameter efficiency yields speedups:** Only the trainable subset (final block, LayerNorm, head) requires gradient computation and optimizer states, drastically reducing memory and compute. This leads to per‑epoch times only slightly higher than head‑only training and far lower than full fine‑tuning. Evidence: [Table II] reports 12.79 min (head‑only), 14.96 min (selective), 35.21 min (full).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Input Tokens -> Frozen Embeddings & Blocks 1‑11 -> Trainable Block 12 -> Trainable Final LayerNorm -> Final Token Hidden State -> Trainable Linear Classification Head -> Output Probabilities

Critical path: Gradients flow only through Trainable Block 12, Final LayerNorm, and the Classification Head; all other parameters remain frozen during backpropagation.

Design tradeoffs:
- Performance vs. Efficiency: Adding more trainable layers (e.g., last 2 blocks) could improve accuracy but increases compute, reducing the efficiency benefit. The final‑block‑only choice is a tuned compromise.
- Layer Selection Depth: Freezing too many layers may cause underfitting; tuning too many risks catastrophic forgetting and higher compute. The final block is hypothesized to contain sufficient adaptable capacity.
- Classification Head Complexity: A single linear head minimizes parameters; adding a small MLP might increase capacity but also risks overfitting on limited data.

Failure signatures:
- Severe underfitting: Both training and validation accuracy are low and close to the majority baseline (~67%), indicating the frozen backbone features are incompatible or the trainable subset is too small.
- Overfitting: Training accuracy approaches 100% while validation/test accuracy is significantly lower, suggesting the small trainable set is memorizing or the frozen features lack discriminative power.
- No learning: Both head‑only and selective models fail to beat the baseline, implying pretrained features are not transferable; domain‑adaptive pretraining may be needed.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline replication:** Train head‑only, selective (final block + LN + head), and full models on the same MIMIC‑IV split. Confirm metrics: selective ~91% accuracy, AUROC 0.96, ~6% parameters, ~15 min/epoch; full ~96% accuracy, 100% parameters, ~35 min/epoch.
2. **Ablation – Layer depth:** Systematically vary the number of top layers made trainable (1, 2, 4, 8, all). Plot accuracy vs. % trainable parameters and time/epoch to validate the final‑block‑only design.
3. **Data scaling:** Train selective and full models on increasing fractions of the training set (5k, 10k, 25k, 50k). Compare performance curves; expect full’s advantage to diminish with less data, and selective to be more data‑efficient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other clinical domains:** The method has only been evaluated on MIMIC‑IV radiology reports; its performance on discharge summaries, progress notes, or datasets with different labeling protocols remains unknown.
2. **Comparison to other PEFT methods:** The paper does not experimentally compare against adapters or LoRA; relative efficiency and effectiveness are still open questions.
3. **Optimal layer selection strategy:** The choice to unfreeze only the final block is not justified by an ablation; alternative configurations (e.g., last two blocks, selective within‑block) may yield better performance‑efficiency trade‑offs.
4. **Robustness to label noise/complexity:** The binary CheXpert‑style task may not reflect behavior on multi‑class tasks or labels with higher clinical uncertainty.
5. **Scaling to larger models:** For larger GPT‑2 variants (Medium, Large, XL), the optimal fraction of trainable parameters may differ; the ~6% figure from the small model may not hold.

## Limitations
- Incomplete documentation of data preprocessing (labeling rules, random seed) and model details (head initialization) hampers exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to a single dataset (MIMIC‑IV radiology reports) and a binary classification task, raising concerns about generalizability.
- Lack of ablation studies isolating the contributions of the final LayerNorm and the depth of tuned layers; the hierarchical representation rationale remains partly conjectural.

## Confidence
- Performance‑efficiency trade‑off: **High**
- Hierarchical representation justification: **Medium**
- Final LayerNorm tuning benefit: **Low**
