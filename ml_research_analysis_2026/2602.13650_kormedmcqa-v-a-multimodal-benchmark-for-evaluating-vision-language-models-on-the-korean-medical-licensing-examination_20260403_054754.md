# KorMedMCQA-V: A Multimodal Benchmark for Evaluating Vision-Language Models on the Korean Medical Licensing Examination

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.13650
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13650
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces a 1,534-question Korean medical VQA benchmark; top proprietary (Gemini-3.0-Pro: 96.9%) and open-source (Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking: 83.7%) models achieve high accuracy, but Korean-specialized models lag significantly (~43%), with all models degrading on multi-image questions.

## Executive Summary
This study presents KorMedMCQA-V, the first multimodal benchmark derived from the Korean Medical Licensing Examination (KMLE). It comprises 1,534 multiple-choice questions with 2,043 medical images spanning 12 years (2012–2023). The authors conduct a comprehensive zero-shot evaluation of over 50 vision-language models. Key findings reveal that the largest proprietary and reasoning-oriented models approach near-perfect performance, while models specialized for the Korean language or medical domains perform substantially worse. Performance consistently drops on questions requiring multi-image reasoning and varies by imaging modality. The work highlights that, for this complex task, model scale and explicit reasoning capabilities are more critical than domain or language specialization.

## Method Summary
The evaluation task is zero-shot, closed-book multimodal multiple-choice question answering. Models receive a question stem, five answer choices (A–E), and one or more associated medical images (e.g., X-ray, CT, endoscopy) in their original exam order. The primary metric is exact match accuracy on the predicted option label. The dataset is split into single-image and multi-image subsets (~30% multi-image), and secondary analyses break down performance by imaging modality and on combined text-vision "Mixed" benchmarks. Official Korean medical licensing pass thresholds (≥40% per session, ≥60% overall) are also applied. All models are evaluated using a single, consistent prompt template and their default generating parameters without any fine-tuning on the benchmark.

## Key Results
*   **Scale and reasoning are paramount:** The best proprietary model (Gemini-3.0-Pro) achieves 96.9% accuracy, and the top open-source reasoning-oriented model (Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking) reaches 83.7%. Reasoning-focused variants outperform their instruction-tuned counterparts by up to +20 percentage points.
*   **Multi-image reasoning is a major challenge:** All evaluated models show significant performance degradation on questions with multiple images. For example, Gemini-3.0-Pro drops from 97.7% on single-image questions to 87.2% on questions with three or more images.
*   **Specialization does not transfer:** Models specifically developed for Korean language (e.g., VARCO-VISION-2.0-14B: 43.2%) or medical domains underperform general-purpose models of comparable scale, suggesting a lack of effective medical visual reasoning capabilities in these specialized variants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not experimentally dissect the *internal mechanisms* behind the observed performance differences. It reports empirical correlations: larger model scale and training objectives that encourage explicit reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought) are strongly associated with higher accuracy. The improvement from reasoning-oriented variants suggests that generating intermediate reasoning steps, even if not medically accurate, may help the model structure its response. Conversely, the poor performance of Korean-specialized models indicates that their training data and objectives did not equips them with the necessary medical visual reasoning skills, regardless of linguistic alignment with the test language. The consistent multi-image degradation points to a systemic limitation in current vision-language architectures for fusing and reasoning over multiple medical evidence images.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Zero-Shot Multimodal Evaluation:** Assessing a model on a new task without any task-specific examples. **Why needed:** Provides a clean measure of a model's innate reasoning and generalization capabilities from its pre-training. **Quick check:** Does a model's performance collapse when the prompt is minimally perturbed (e.g., "Answer with A, B, C, D, or E." vs. "Output JSON: {"answer": "A"}")?
*   **Medical Visual Question Answering (VQA):** A task requiring integration of visual information from medical images with textual clinical context to answer a question. **Why needed:** Tests a model's ability to perform domain-specific perception and reasoning. **Quick check:** Can the model correctly identify a pathology in a chest X-ray and relate it to the clinical finding in the question stem?
*   **Multimodal Fusion:** The architectural process of combining information from multiple input modalities (text and one or more images). **Why needed:** Critical for answering questions that depend on relations between multiple images (e.g., comparing a pre-op and post-op scan). **Quick check:** How does model accuracy change when the number of input images increases from one to three?
*   **Benchmark Construction from High-Stakes Exams:** Using standardized, expert-created exam questions as a test set. **Why needed:** Questions are vetted for difficulty, clinical relevance, and solvability by human experts, providing a gold-standard evaluation. **Quick check:** Do human medical students/licensees achieve a high, consistent pass rate on the benchmark's text-only subset?
*   **Reasoning-Oriented Model Training:** Fine-tuning models to generate step-by-step reasoning traces (chain-of-thought) before a final answer. **Why needed:** May improve performance on complex, multi-faceted problems by forcing intermediate computation. **Quick check:** Does the model's final accuracy improve when forced to output a reasoning paragraph before its answer, compared to a direct answer?
*   **Language- vs. Domain-Specialization in VLMs:** The distinction between training a model for a specific language (Korean) and for a specific knowledge domain (medicine). **Why needed:** To understand what capabilities transfer (or fail to transfer) across domains. **Quick check:** Does a Korean-specialized VLM perform better on *non-medical* Korean VQA tasks compared to a general-purpose model?

## Architecture Onboarding
The evaluation architecture is a pipeline, not a novel model. Components: **Dataset Loader** (reads questions, images, metadata) → **Prompt Formatter** (injects text and images into model's template) → **Model API/Inference Engine** (processes multimodal input and generates text) → **Output Parser** (extracts the chosen option letter A–E) → **Accuracy Scorer** (compares prediction to ground truth). The critical path is maintaining fidelity of image input and prompt format through to the parser. A key design tradeoff is between strict adherence to the paper's unreported prompt/format (fidelity) and using a model's native optimal chat template (performance). Failure signatures include: non-letter outputs (explanations, JSON errors), image loading failures, and crashes on multi-image inputs. First experiments should: 1) Run the pipeline on 10 samples to validate end-to-end function; 2) Test the impact of a minor prompt variation on a single model's accuracy; 3) Benchmark a single model's performance on just the single-image vs. multi-image subsets to confirm the degradation pattern.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Multi-image degradation:** What architectural or training modifications can effectively mitigate the consistent performance degradation observed on multi-image Korean medical VQA questions?
*   **Modality-specific gaps:** Why do certain imaging modalities (e.g., NST, endoscopy) consistently yield lower accuracy across all model categories, and can this modality gap be closed?
*   **Korean-specialized underperformance:** Why do Korean-specialized VLMs (e.g., VARCO-VISION-2.0) significantly underperform general-purpose models on Korean medical VQA despite Korean-language training?
*   **Reasoning variant gains:** What specific mechanisms in reasoning-oriented VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-Thinking) drive the large performance gains (+20 percentage points) over instruction-tuned counterparts?

## Limitations
*   The exact zero-shot QA prompt template and precise multi-image formatting (ordering, interleaving) are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
*   Evaluation is zero-shot only; results may differ under fine-tuning or few-shot conditions.
*   The benchmark is specific to Korean medical licensing exams, limiting generalizability to other languages, domains, or exam formats.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Dataset construction, curation, and public availability.
*   **Medium:** Reported absolute accuracy numbers for top models; relative ranking of model families; observed multi-image degradation trend; modality-specific variation patterns; conclusions about passing official thresholds.
*   **Low:** The strong claim that "scale outweighs medical/Korean specialization" (correlational without controlled ablations); precise magnitude of the reasoning-oriented model improvement (+20 pp).

## Next Checks
1.  **Prompt/Format Fidelity Audit:** Obtain the exact QA prompt and multi-image input specification from the official repository or authors. Run a pilot evaluation on a 100-question subset to verify the baseline accuracy for a standard model matches the paper's reported value.
2.  **Parameter Sensitivity Sweep:** For the top-performing proprietary and open-source models, systematically vary key generation parameters (temperature, top_p, max_new_tokens) and measure the resulting change in accuracy to bound the impact of the paper's unreported "default" settings.
3.  **Model Version Pinning:** Record the precise model identifiers (e.g., exact Hugging Face revision hashes for open-source models, specific API model strings and timestamps for proprietary models) used in the original evaluation. Re-run a small, shared subset to confirm outputs are consistent with those versions and guard against API/model drift.
