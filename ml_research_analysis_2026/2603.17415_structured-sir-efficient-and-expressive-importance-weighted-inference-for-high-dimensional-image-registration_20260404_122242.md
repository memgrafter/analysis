# Structured SIR: Efficient and Expressive Importance-Weighted Inference for High-Dimensional Image Registration

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.17415
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17415
*   **Reference Count:** 27
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces an amortized importance-weighted inference framework with a memory-efficient structured covariance that significantly improves uncertainty calibration for high-dimensional (1.6M-D) 3D medical image registration while maintaining accuracy and sub-5-second runtimes.

## Executive Summary
Standard variational inference for image registration yields poorly calibrated, unimodal uncertainty estimates. This paper introduces Structured SIR, an amortized inference framework that uses Sampled Importance Resampling (SIR) to approximate flexible, multi-modal posterior distributions over dense deformation fields. A key innovation is a memory-efficient covariance parameterization combining a low-rank matrix with a sparse, spatially structured Cholesky precision factor. On 3D brain MRI registration, the full model (S+LC) reduces calibration error (ECE to 0.1064, AUSE to 0.0659) and shows a strong negative correlation between uncertainty and error, while matching the mean accuracy of variational baselines. The method processes a pair in under 5 seconds with 1,200 samples.

## Method Summary
Structured SIR formulates posterior inference over a displacement field **Z**. A U-Net predicts parameters for a proposal distribution *q*(**Z**): a mean **μ**, a sparse lower-triangular Cholesky factor *L* of the precision (modeling local, spatially structured correlations), and a low-rank matrix *R* (capturing global modes). The full covariance is Σ = *RR*ᵀ + (*LL*ᵀ)⁻¹. During training, many samples are drawn from *q*, their unnormalized importance weights **α** (based on an image likelihood and smoothness prior) are computed and normalized with a learned temperature. A multinomial resample selects a small subset (*N*ₖ=80), whose samples are used to compute a SIR loss that approximates the importance-weighted ELBO. This amortized approach avoids per-pair optimization, enabling fast test-time inference.

## Key Results
*   Significantly improves uncertainty calibration: Expected Calibration Error (ECE) reduced to 0.1064 and Label-Entropy AUSE to 0.0659 for the S+LC model.
*   Demonstrates effective multi-modal posterior capture via a large performance gap between the mean and oracle (best sample) deformation.
*   Maintains equivalent mean segmentation Dice score (0.801) to variational baselines while running in under 5 seconds per pair using 1,200 samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Importance-Weighted Resampling for Multi-Modal Posteriors
*   **Claim:** Sampled Importance Resampling (SIR) enables expressive, multi-modal posterior approximations by focusing learning and inference on high-probability samples, unlike variational inference's unimodal restriction.
*   **Mechanism:** A large set of samples is drawn from a proposal *q*(**Z**); their unnormalized importance weights αᵢ = *p*(I<sub>f</sub>|I<sub>m</sub> ∘ **Z**ᵢ)*p*(**Z**ᵢ)/*q*(**Z**ᵢ) are computed. Resampling (multinomial) selects *N*ₖ high-weight samples, and gradients are computed only through these, forming a loss that approximates the importance-weighted objective. This allows the proposal to be exploratory (covering modes) without penalizing low-weight regions during training.
*   **Core Assumption:** The proposal distribution can generate samples with non-negligible weight in all major posterior modes; importance weighting correctly re-weights to approximate the true posterior.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Introducing an amortised Importance Sampling procedure, Sampled Importance Resampling (SIR), to produce a proposal distribution from which samples can be efficiently drawn and weighted to establish a more flexible posterior than variational inference."
    *   [section 3.1] Details the resampling pass and loss computation.
*   **Break Condition:** If the proposal distribution completely misses a mode (zero weight samples), the posterior will be misrepresented regardless of resampling.

### Mechanism 2: Memory-Efficient Structured Covariance
*   **Claim:** The low-rank plus sparse Cholesky precision parameterization Σ = *RR*ᵀ + (*LL*ᵀ)⁻¹ captures complex spatial correlations in 1.6M-D displacement fields while keeping memory and compute tractable.
*   **Mechanism:** *L* is a sparse lower-triangular matrix with a spatially local 3×3×3 kernel and cross-channel connections; *R* is a low-rank matrix. Sampling uses Eq. (4) with independent draws from each component. Log-probability evaluation leverages the Woodbury identity to avoid full matrix inverses/determinants, reducing cost to *O*(N<sub>v</sub>*r*² + N<sub>v</sub>·sparsity).
*   **Core Assumption:** The spatially structured sparsity in *L* approximates local dependencies well; low-rank *R* captures global modes; the combination suffices for the posterior's correlation structure.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "novel memory-efficient high-dimensional covariance parameterisation as the sum of a low-rank covariance and a sparse, spatially structured Cholesky precision factor."
    *   [section 3.2] Derivation of efficient log-prob computation using Woodbury identity.
*   **Break Condition:** If true posterior has strong long-range correlations beyond the sparse kernel, or if low-rank *r* is insufficient, the covariance will be misspecified.

### Mechanism 3: Amortized Inference with Single-Pass Proposal
*   **Claim:** Amortizing proposal parameter prediction via a convolutional network yields fast test-time inference (under 5 seconds) while maintaining accuracy.
*   **Mechanism:** A U-Net style backbone predicts mean **μ**, sparse Cholesky factor *L*, and low-rank *R* in one forward pass. These parameters define *q*(**Z**). Training optimizes the SIR loss over many pairs, enabling generalization to new test pairs without per-pair optimization.
*   **Core Assumption:** The network architecture and training data allow learning a mapping from image pairs to adequate proposal parameters; the loss landscape is amenable to gradient-based optimization.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "amortised inference framework... runs in under 5 seconds per pair with 1,200 samples."
    *   [section 3.3] Architecture details.
    *   [section 4.1] Training details confirm practical scalability.
*   **Break Condition:** If the network fails to produce positive-definite covariances (e.g., *L* not full rank after soft-plus), sampling or log-prob fails.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    U-Net backbone (4-level encoder-decoder) → Three prediction heads (mean **μ** at half-res, sparse Cholesky *L* at half-res, low-rank *R* at quarter-res) → Upsampling (**μ** and *L* via cubic spline, *R* bilinear) → SIR engine (draw samples, compute weights, resample, compute loss)
*   **Critical Path:**
    *   *Training:* Forward pass (predict **μ**, *L*, *R*) → Draw *N*<sub>L</sub>=1200 samples (no grad) → Compute weights **α** (likelihood × prior / *q*, normalized with temperature) → Multinomial resample *N*<sub>k</sub>=80 indices → Redraw selected samples with gradients → Compute SIR loss over selected samples → Backprop.
    *   *Inference:* Forward pass → Draw many samples → Compute weights → Optionally resample → Aggregate (mean, oracle best DSC, entropy maps).
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   *Sample budget:* Larger *N*<sub>L</sub> improves coverage but increases memory; *N*<sub>k</sub> balances gradient noise vs cost.
    *   *Covariance complexity:* Rank *r* and sparsity pattern affect memory/accuracy; too sparse may miss correlations, too dense blows up.
    *   *Head resolution:* Lower resolution for **μ** and *L* reduces memory but may lose fine details; upsampling must preserve structure.
    *   *Temperature scaling:* Prevents weight collapse but too high flattens distribution.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   *Numerical:* Non-positive definite covariance (e.g., *L* diagonal not positive after soft-plus) → sampling/log-det NaNs.
    *   *Geometric:* High folding percentage (Jacobian ≤0) indicates unrealistic deformations; often worsens with more exploratory SIR.
    *   *Calibration:* High ECE/AUSE or positive correlation between label entropy and Dice means uncertainty is not meaningful.
    *   *Mode collapse:* Small selection gap (Oracle - mean DSC) suggests unimodal posterior despite SIR; might indicate proposal too restrictive or resampling ineffective.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  *Sanity check:* Train on 2–3 image pairs with small *N*<sub>L</sub> (e.g., 100). Monitor loss decrease, check folding rate (<1%), verify resampled samples have higher average weights.
    2.  *Baseline comparison:* Reimplement a diagonal Gaussian variational baseline (V+D). Compare segmentation DSC (mean and oracle) and calibration (ECE) on a held-out pair. Expect S+LC to have larger selection gap and lower ECE.
    3.  *Multi-modality visualization:* For a test pair, compute PCA on SIR samples, cluster (e.g., k=2), and visualize deformation differences and propagated segmentation masks. Confirm cluster means produce distinct, anatomically plausible alignments with similar DSC.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** How does parameterizing the deformation using stationary velocity fields instead of direct displacements affect the rate of topological folding and uncertainty calibration metrics compared to the current formulation?
*   **Question 2:** Can post-training optimization of the temperature scaling parameter *T* reduce ECE and improve the correlation between label entropy and Dice?
*   **Question 3:** What impact does incorporating segmentation-based losses (e.g., Dice loss) into the training objective have on the quality and calibration of the posterior, despite these losses being unavailable at test time?
*   **Question 4:** How does replacing the U-Net backbone with a vision transformer architecture affect computational efficiency and registration performance?

## Limitations
*   The exact sparsity pattern for the Cholesky factor *L* (3×3×3 spatial kernel with cross-channel connections) is not fully specified, which affects memory efficiency and correlation modeling.
*   Key implementation details are missing: NCC likelihood (global vs local), data augmentation definitions (coherent/independent shifts), and temperature scaling procedure (per-batch vs global EMA). Variations could change results.
*   Calibration metrics (ECE, AUSE) rely on specific binning and confidence mapping from label entropy; without explicit definitions, reproducibility of reported numbers is uncertain.
