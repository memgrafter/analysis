# Revisiting Robustness for LLM Safety Alignment via Selective Geometry Control
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07340
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07340
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ShaPO reduces attack success rates by selectively controlling optimization geometry on a small, probe-identified safety-critical parameter subspace, outperforming robust baselines under domain shift and noisy preferences.

## Executive Summary
Safety-aligned LLMs remain vulnerable under distribution shifts and noisy supervision because existing robust alignment methods overlook fragility induced by optimization geometry. ShaPO addresses this by identifying a small set of alignment-critical parameters via a linear probe and enforcing worst-case perturbations only on that subspace. This selective geometry control avoids the over-regularization that harms out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness when using uniform constraints. Two instantiations—token-level (stabilizing likelihood-based objectives like DPO) and reward-level (using a pretrained reward model)—allow robustness to be applied where safety signals concentrate. Across five safety benchmarks and multiple models, ShaPO significantly lowers attack success rates (ASR) and maintains higher win rates under noisy preferences, demonstrating that targeting optimization geometry is a complementary and effective axis for robust safety alignment.

## Method Summary
ShaPO introduces selective geometry control for robust safety alignment. First, a linear probe is trained on a frozen model’s final-layer representations to capture variations predictive of safety labels. The resulting probe direction is compared against value vectors across all transformer blocks; neurons with the highest absolute cosine similarity define a safety-critical parameter subspace *S* (typically the top 1%). During alignment training (e.g., with DPO or a reward-based loss), ShaPO applies a Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)-style perturbation but restricts it to *S*. Every *τ* steps, a two-step update is performed: compute the gradient *g\_S* on *S*, construct a perturbation *ϵ\_S = ρ · g\_S / (∥g\_S∥ + ε)*, then evaluate the alignment loss at the perturbed parameters *θ + ϵ\_S* and update all parameters using that gradient. This selectively flattens the loss landscape only along safety-critical directions. Token-level ShaPO (ShaPO-T) applies this to a DPO-like loss, while Reward-level ShaPO (ShaPO-R) uses a pretrained safety reward model to compute a cross-entropy loss, providing a more stable signal under noisy preferences.

## Key Results
- Under domain shift (trained on PKU-SafeRLHF-30K, tested on other benchmarks), ShaPO-T on LLaMA-3-8B achieves average ASR 1.37% vs. Dr.DPO's 2.09%; ShaPO-R reaches 0.70%.
- Under noisy preference supervision (up to 40% label flips), ShaPO variants maintain higher win rates compared to standard DPO and Dr.DPO.
- ShaPO composes with data-robust objectives (e.g., Dr.DPO), yielding additive robustness gains, confirming optimization geometry as an independent axis for improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** ShaPO identifies a small, alignment-critical parameter subspace whose perturbation accounts for most worst-case alignment loss.
- **Mechanism:** A linear probe trained on frozen model representations yields a direction *p* predictive of safety. By computing |⟨*v\_j*, *p*⟩| for each value vector *v\_j*, the top-K neurons form the subspace *S*. The core assumption is that safety-critical behavior is localized.
- **Evidence:** Figure 1 shows that perturbing <0.5% of probe-ranked neurons accounts for >80% of cumulative worst-case loss, while random neurons contribute far less.
- **Break condition:** Probe failure to capture true safety directions (due to dataset bias or underfitting) leads to a mis-specified *S*, harming robustness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Enforcing worst-case perturbations only on *S* avoids over-regularization of non-safety parameters, preserving OOD generalization.
- **Mechanism:** ShaPO applies SAM-style perturbations exclusively to *S*, selectively flattening the loss landscape along safety-critical directions while leaving other degrees of freedom free to adapt.
- **Evidence:** Table 3 shows uniform geometry control yields higher average ASR than selective control under OOD evaluation; selective control performs best.
- **Break condition:** If *S* is too small or mis-specified, robustness may be insufficient; if too large, over-constraint returns.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ShaPO instantiations at token-level and reward-level target robustness where safety concentration differs; reward-level generally outperforms token-level.
- **Mechanism:** Token-level ShaPO stabilizes likelihood-based DPO loss; reward-level ShaPO uses a pretrained reward model's cross-entropy loss, anchoring optimization to a semantic signal decoupled from corrupted preference labels.
- **Evidence:** Section 4.3 and Table 2 consistently show ShaPO-R lower ASR than ShaPO-T (e.g., 0.70% vs 1.37% on LLaMA-3-8B).
- **Break condition:** Reward-level ShaPO depends on a high-quality, safety-aligned reward model; token-level ShaPO remains vulnerable if token likelihoods poorly reflect safety.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
  *Why needed:* ShaPO builds upon DPO-like objectives; understanding its likelihood-based formulation shows how ShaPO modifies optimization geometry.  
  *Quick check:* Why does DPO convert preference learning into a supervised objective, and what implicit reward does it define?

- **Concept: Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)**  
  *Why needed:* ShaPO’s two-step update is inspired by SAM; grasping worst-case perturbation within a radius is central to selective geometry control.  
  *Quick check:* How does SAM’s “sharpness” term relate to model robustness, and why might uniform SAM be problematic for alignment?

- **Concept: Linear Probing for Mechanistic Analysis**  
  *Why needed:* Subspace identification relies on training a linear probe to find safety-correlated directions; this connects model internals to alignment behavior.  
  *Quick check:* What does a linear probe measure in a frozen LLM, and why is cosine similarity to value vectors used to select parameters?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Probe trainer -> Trains linear classifier on frozen representations -> Neuron ranker -> Computes cosine similarity between probe direction and value vectors -> Selects top-K indices to form *S* -> ShaPO optimizer -> Wraps base alignment optimizer (DPO or reward-based) with SAM-style perturbation restricted to *S* -> Base alignment loss module (token-level or reward-level).
- **Critical path:**  
  Probe training → Subspace selection → (Every *τ* steps: SAM perturbation on *S* → Loss evaluation at *θ+ϵ\_S* → Update). SAM frequency *τ* balances overhead and robustness.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Subspace size (*K*): Larger *K* covers more worst-case directions but risks over-regularization; paper uses Top-1%.  
  - Perturbation radius (*ρ*): Larger *ρ* enforces stronger flatness but may distort alignment; requires tuning.  
  - SAM frequency (*τ*): More frequent improves robustness but increases compute (Table 4: ShaPO-T ~+30% time vs DPO; ShaPO-R ~+50%).  
  - Reward model dependency (for reward-level): Adds external component; reward quality directly impacts robustness.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High OOD ASR despite low IID ASR → subspace misidentified or too small.  
  - Degraded win rate or increased refusals → over-regularization (large *K* or *ρ*) or poor probe.  
  - Probe accuracy <70% → probe underfitting; revisit data/representation layer.  
  - No improvement over Dr.DPO under noisy labels → reward model misaligned.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Probe validation:** Train probe on held-out safety set; ensure >80% accuracy. Decode top-ranked neurons for safety-related tokens (Table 5).  
  2. **IID sanity check:** Train ShaPO on PKU-SafeRLHF-30K and evaluate on its test split (Table 1). Verify WR comparable to DPO and ASR lower.  
  3. **OOD robustness probe:** Test on one OOD benchmark (e.g., HarmBench) with small *ρ*. Confirm ASR reduction vs DPO; if not, increase *K* or *ρ* incrementally.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can computational overhead be reduced for very large-scale LLMs (e.g., >70B parameters)? Basis: Conclusion notes efficiency and scalability as future work; Table 4 shows ~2x longer training for ShaPO-R. Unresolved due to lack of memory-efficient approximations for multi-hundred-billion parameter models.
- **Open Question 2:** To what extent is probe-based subspace identification architecture- and task-agnostic? Inference: Probe is re-trained per backbone; unclear if directions generalize across architectures (e.g., MoE) or safety domains (privacy, cybersecurity). Evidence needed: transfer of probe from source to target model/domain without retraining.
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive are reward-level ShaPO gains to the choice of external reward model? Inference: Uses a single Beaver-7b cost model; assumes it is a stable, noise-resilient proxy. Unresolved: reward model's own biases/robustness under selective perturbations not analyzed. Evidence needed: systematic evaluation with multiple diverse reward models, including those with known biases.
- **Open Question 4:** Does the assumption of structurally localized safety behavior hold universally? Inference: Based on Fig 1 showing <0.5% neurons account for >80% worst-case loss for one model. Unresolved: Distributed failure modes (e.g., jailbreaks via chain-of-thought) might require global adjustments. Evidence needed: failure analysis on cases ShaPO misses, causal tracing/ablation, and extending neuron selection beyond value vectors.
- **Open Question 5:** Can the selective geometry principle be theoretically formalized to guarantee worst-case safety? Inference: PAC-Bayes and bypass boundary arguments connect sharpness to generalization but do not provide deterministic safety guarantees against black-box judges. Unresolved: no certified robustness framework linking alignment loss landscape to judge decision boundaries. Evidence needed: sound over-approximation of safe parameter sets or adversarial parameter perturbation tests.

## Limitations
- **Missing specs:** Value vector extraction details (layers, token positions), probe data split, SAM radius ρ, and exact reward negation form are unspecified, hindering replication.
- **Probe reliability:** Subspace depends on probe accuracy (>80% needed); probe lacks independent validation and localization assumption may not generalize.
- **Reward bias:** Reward-level ShaPO vulnerable to misaligned pretrained reward models.

## Confidence
- **Localization via probe-ranked neurons:** Medium. Figure 1 supports concentration, but probe effectiveness is unverified.
- **Selective geometry improves OOD robustness:** Medium. Table 3 shows selective beats uniform/no control, but confounding possible.
- **Reward-level > token-level under noise:** High. Consistent ASR reduction across benchmarks.
- **Probe direction captures safety:** Low-Medium. Probe training accuracy not reported; assumption requires validation.
- **Generalization beyond tested models/benchmarks:** Low. Evidence limited to specific model families and safety benchmarks.

## Next Checks
1. **Probe validation:** Train probe on a held-out safety probe set; require >80% accuracy. Verify top-ranked neurons decode to safety-related tokens. Ablate by randomizing rankings—worst-case loss should increase.
2. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Vary subspace fraction *K* ∈ {0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%} and radius *ρ* ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2} on a validation OOD benchmark. Defaults should yield stable ASR (Δ<0.2%).
3. **Reward model alignment:** Compute correlation between negated Beaver-7b scores and safety labels; target >0.7. If low, test alternative reward models or score standardization.
