# Multimodal Language Models Cannot Spot Spatial Inconsistencies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00799  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00799  
- Reference count: 20  
- Primary result: State-of-the-art multimodal LLMs achieve only 29.4–34.2% accuracy on detecting spatial inconsistencies, far below human performance (84.8%).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a counterfactual benchmark for spatial inconsistency detection in multi-view images. By pasting an object from one view into another with a different camera pose, they create photorealistic yet geometrically impossible scenes. Evaluation of leading multimodal language models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) shows a large performance gap compared to humans. Ablations confirm that models are not leveraging inpainting artifacts; instead, they fail to reason about 3D geometry. The work reveals a fundamental limitation: current MLLMs lack robust 3D spatial understanding, and even test-time scaling degrades performance. Fine-tuning on a small set of generated pairs yields modest improvements on related 3D tasks, suggesting potential for self-supervised pretraining.

## Method Summary
The authors generate 615 image pairs from the Hypersim and DL3DV multi-view datasets. For each triplet of frames (V1, V2, V3) with a common target object O, they remove O from V2 using LaMa inpainting (with slight expansion) and paste O from V3 into V2, scaled to match the original size and composited with correct occlusion ordering. V1 is annotated with letters A–Z labeling all large objects, including O. The task is a forced-choice: “Which labeled object is inconsistent?” The pipeline ensures that the only geometric violation is the pasted object’s 3D pose mismatch with the rest of the scene. Human participants achieve 84.8% accuracy, while top MLLMs range between 29.4% and 34.2%, with performance varying dramatically across depth, lighting, and object categories. Ablations (self-paste and no-change baselines) confirm that models do not rely on inpainting artifacts.

## Key Results
- MLLMs (GPT-5: 34.2%, Gemini 2.5 Pro: 29.4%) significantly underperform humans (84.8%) on spatial inconsistency detection.
- Model accuracy is highly unstable across object depth, lighting, and category, contrasting with stable human performance.
- Ablation studies show that models are not succeeding by detecting inpainting artifacts; performance remains at chance for self-paste and no-change baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The cut-and-paste synthesis introduces a photorealistic yet geometrically impossible object placement that forces the model to detect a violation of multiview projective geometry.
- **Mechanism:** Starting from three real views of a static scene, an object instance O from view V3 is pasted into view V2 after removing the original O and inpainting the background. Because V2 and V3 have different camera poses, the pasted O appears with a 3D pose inconsistent with the geometry implied by the reference view V1. All other scene elements remain spatially consistent, isolating the inconsistency to a single object.
- **Core assumption:** MLLMs primarily process 2D image patterns and semantic priors rather than constructing an explicit 3D scene representation capable of checking cross-view consistency.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "...an object from one view is inserted into another view from a different camera pose, creating a controlled 3D geometric violation."
  - [section 3.2] "We select three frames (V1, V2, V3) and select an object O visible in all views... paste the object from V3 back into V2."
  - [corpus] The neighboring paper "Generation Models Know Space: Unleashing Implicit 3D Priors for Scene Understanding" similarly reports that MLLMs suffer from "spatial blindness" and struggle with fine-grained geometric reasoning.
- **Break condition:** If MLLMs suddenly achieve near-human accuracy on this task without any architectural changes, the assumed lack of 3D representation would be falsified, suggesting a simpler pattern-matching explanation might suffice.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** MLLMs’ performance on spatial inconsistency detection is highly unstable across object depth, lighting, and category, indicating brittle internal representations that fail to capture universal 3D principles.
- **Mechanism:** Models trained on large-scale image-text corpora may develop shortcuts that correlate with specific visual statistics (e.g., typical object sizes, illumination conditions). When tested on combinations that deviate from these training distributions (e.g., far-depth objects, unusual lighting), accuracy fluctuates dramatically unlike human observers who maintain stable performance.
- **Core assumption:** Robust spatial understanding requires generalizing across diverse viewing conditions; high variance in accuracy signals that models lack such an invariant 3D understanding.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Model accuracy is highly unstable across object depth, lighting, and category, unlike robust human performance."
  - [section 4.2] Figure 3 and Table 5 show accuracy differences exceeding 15 percentage points between close and far objects for GPT-5, while humans stay within a few points.
  - [corpus] "SpatialBench: Benchmarking Multimodal Large Language Models for Spatial Cognition" also finds that MLLMs’ spatial skills are often narrow and brittle across different spatial tasks.
- **Break condition:** Should future models demonstrate consistent high accuracy (>80%) across all stratified groups (depth, lighting, plausibility, categories), it would indicate that brittle reliance on dataset biases has been overcome.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Models are not succeeding by detecting inpainting artifacts; their failures are due to a genuine inability to recognize the geometric inconsistency.
- **Mechanism:** The generation pipeline leaves minor inpainting seams (5% bounding box expansion). Control experiments—self-pasting (artifact present, no inconsistency) and no-change (no artifact, no inconsistency)—both yield similarly low scores. Additionally, adding many self-pasted objects (creating many artifacts) does not reduce accuracy. Thus, models neither rely on nor are distracted by artifacts.
- **Core assumption:** If models were using artifact cues, their accuracy would improve when artifacts are more salient (e.g., larger inpainting boxes or many self-pastes). The stability of performance against these perturbations suggests they are attempting (and failing) to judge 3D consistency directly.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Ablation studies confirm models are not succeeding by detecting inpainting artifacts."
  - [section 4.3] Tables 2–4 and accompanying text show that self-pasting and no-change baselines perform similarly, and accuracy only rises when inpainting regions become unrealistically large (~50% expansion).
  - [corpus] "Multimodal Inconsistency Reasoning (MMIR)" also uses artifact-controlled counterfactuals to isolate reasoning, though it targets layout inconsistencies rather than 3D geometry.
- **Break condition:** If models start performing significantly above chance on the self-pasting baseline (especially when many artifacts are introduced), it would suggest they can indeed detect artifacts and might be using them as a proxy for spotting the inconsistency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multiview Projective Geometry**
  - **Why needed here:** The core task hinges on understanding how static scene elements transform across different camera poses (e.g., epipolar constraints, scaling with depth, occlusion changes). Without this foundation, one cannot grasp why pasting an object from one view into another creates an impossible configuration.
  - **Quick check question:** When a static object appears in two images taken from different viewpoints, how must its apparent size and position change relative to other scene elements?

- **Concept: Counterfactual Benchmark Design**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper constructs a diagnostic that isolates spatial reasoning from high-level semantics. This counterfactual approach—creating a minimal pair that differs only in a geometric violation—enables pinpointing a specific failure mode rather than general task difficulty.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is it more informative to test “which object is inconsistent?” rather than “describe the scene” when probing 3D understanding?

- **Concept: Artifact-Controlled Evaluation**
  - **Why needed here:** Any pixel-level manipulation (e.g., inpainting) could introduce unintended cues. Properly designed ablations (self-paste, no-change) ensure that measured performance reflects the intended construct (spatial inconsistency detection) rather than peripheral artifacts.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the self-pasting baseline (copying the object from the same view) help disentangle artifact detection from geometric inconsistency detection?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The evaluation pipeline consists of (1) Multi-view dataset loader (Hypersim/DL3DV) providing RGB frames, instance masks, camera poses; (2) Triplet sampler that selects (V1, V2, V3) with a common object O meeting overlap constraints; (3) Editor that removes O from V2 via LaMa inpainting and pastes O from V3; (4) Label generator that tags all sufficiently large objects in V1 with letters A–Z; (5) Prompt composer that presents labeled V1 and edited V2 with the question “which letter marks the inconsistent object?”; (6) Model inference wrapper that queries the target MLLM and extracts the chosen label; (7) Scoring module that compares the prediction to the ground-truth O label.

- **Critical path:** To reproduce the main experiment: (a) Download Hypersim and extract a subset of scenes; (b) Implement the triplet selection criteria (object visible in all three views, ≤75% overlap between V1–V2 and V2–V3, object size 5–10% of image); (c) Run LaMa inpainting with a 5% bounding box expansion; (d) Generate the 615 manually verified pairs (or use the provided list); (e) For each MLLM, craft the exact prompt (see appendix) and parse the output letter; (f) Compute accuracy across all pairs.

- **Design tradeoffs:** Cut-and-paste vs. full generative view synthesis: Cut-and-paste is extremely fast (~5 pairs/sec on a single GPU), uses real textures, and guarantees photorealism except at inpainting seams. However, it limits the magnitude of the geometric violation to what naturally occurs between the real views. Generative methods (e.g., Zero123) could produce larger violations but are slower, may hallucinate content, and introduce their own biases; cut-and-paste was chosen for scalability and controlled counterfactuals.

- **Failure signatures:** (1) Human validation accuracy on a small held-out set is below ~80% → the pair may be ambiguous or labeling is incorrect; re-inspect the inconsistency. (2) Inpainting produces visible seams or distortions → adjust LaMa or mask expansion; consider synthetic rendering fallback. (3) All models answer the same letter for most queries → likely a parsing bug or prompt mismatch; verify prompt wording and response parser. (4) Model accuracy increases when inpainting expansion is much larger → indicates artifacts are dominating; revert to 5% expansion.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Manual sanity check: Visualize 50 random pairs with labels; confirm that the inconsistent object is indeed geometrically impossible and that other objects remain consistent.
  2. Human pilot: Recruit 5 participants to label 30 pairs; ensure agreement (>75% accuracy) and that the task is well-defined.
  3. Baseline sanity: Run a random-choice selector and a trivial vision-backend (e.g., CLIP-based similarity between object crops and query) to verify that the evaluation script correctly computes chance-level (~7.9%) and that no bugs inflate scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does increasing reasoning effort (test-time compute) often degrade MLLM performance on spatial inconsistency detection, contrary to typical scaling trends?
- **Basis:** The authors explicitly state that “increasing test-time compute does not lead to improved performance. In fact, we consistently find that increasing reasoning effort often degrades model performance… This finding is counterintuitive to the promise of visual reasoning and merits further investigation, which is out of the scope for this paper.”
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding this counterintuitive effect is crucial for designing effective inference strategies and reveals potential flaws in how models utilize additional computation for spatial tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments varying reasoning steps (e.g., chain-of-thought length) on the spatial inconsistency task; analyze model attention, hidden states, or generated intermediate rationales to determine whether overthinking introduces noise, hallucination, or distraction from geometric cues.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What architectural or training deficiencies cause current MLLMs to exhibit fragile 3D spatial understanding that varies sharply with depth, lighting, and object category, unlike robust human perception?
- **Basis:** The paper shows models’ accuracy fluctuates significantly across depth, lighting, plausibility, and object/scene categories (Figures 3–4, Tables 5–7), while humans remain stable. They also note that even spatially fine‑tuned models (e.g., SpaceQwen) fail, indicating fundamental gaps beyond simple finetuning.
- **Why unresolved:** Identifying the specific missing components or biases would guide the development of more robust multimodal models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies modifying model architectures (e.g., incorporating explicit depth estimation, cross‑view attention modules) or training objectives (e.g., multi‑view consistency losses, synthetic data augmentation) and evaluating on the benchmark; correlation analyses linking performance to training data distributions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can learning to detect spatial inconsistencies serve as a general pretraining objective to improve diverse 3D perception tasks, and what mechanisms drive such transfer?
- **Basis:** The authors fine‑tuned Qwen3‑4B on 10K auto‑generated pairs and observed improvements of ~2.3 pp on BLINK’s “Multiview Reasoning” and “Relative Depth” and ~1.3 pp on “Visual Correspondence” (Table 10). They hypothesize the task implicitly teaches depth, correspondence, and camera motion, but this transfer remains under‑explored.
- **Why unresolved:** If true, inconsistency detection could become a self‑supervised pretraining signal, yet the limited experiments do not confirm scalability or generalizability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large‑scale pretraining using inconsistency detection as a proxy task on massive multi‑view datasets (e.g., YouTube‑360, Hypersim) followed by zero‑shot evaluation on a broad suite of 3D benchmarks; probing internal representations for geometry‑aligned features; diagnosing which aspects of 3D understanding improve.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Are the dramatic variations in MLLM accuracy across object categories and scene types (e.g., chairs vs. lamps, lounges vs. bedrooms) primarily caused by dataset biases during training or by representational limitations in handling certain geometries and appearances?
- **Basis:** Figure 4 and Tables 6–7 reveal stark performance differences: for instance, Qwen3‑VL 8B Instruct ranges from 0 % to 67 % across object classes and shows large swings across scene categories, while humans are consistent. The authors do not analyze the source of this brittleness.
- **Why unresolved:** Pinpointing the cause (e.g., under‑representation of certain objects, poor modeling of material/lighting interactions) is essential for building balanced and robust systems.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Examine frequency and viewpoint distributions of object categories in pretraining corpora; test models on curated balanced subsets; generate controlled synthetic variations that isolate factors like shape complexity, texture, or lighting; measure correlation between category accuracy and data statistics.

## Limitations
- The exact prompt wording and parsing rules are unspecified (appendix missing), risking prompt sensitivity.
- Dataset generation details are ambiguous: precise definitions of instance overlap (pixel vs. mask count) and projection-area measurement (depth map source, occlusion handling) could affect pair difficulty distribution.
- Human baseline is from a separate study; no direct human performance on the exact 615 pairs, so comparative claims rely on matched conditions.

## Confidence
- MLLMs fail at this controlled spatial inconsistency detection task: **High**
- Failure is not due to inpainting artifacts: **High**
- Generalizes broadly across MLLMs/spatial reasoning scenarios: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Exact human replication:** Recruit participants to label the identical 615 pairs with the same interface; compute accuracy and inter-rater agreement to directly benchmark MLLMs.
2. **Cross-dataset robustness:** Apply the pipeline to at least two additional multi-view datasets (e.g., RealEstate10K, KITTI-360) covering indoor/outdoor scenes and varying baseline angles; re-evaluate MLLMs.
3. **Failure mode probing:** Run activation/attention analysis on MLLMs for inconsistent vs. consistent pairs; also test with synthetic data that systematically varies the magnitude of geometric violation (e.g., paste with scale/shift offsets) to identify sensitivity thresholds.
