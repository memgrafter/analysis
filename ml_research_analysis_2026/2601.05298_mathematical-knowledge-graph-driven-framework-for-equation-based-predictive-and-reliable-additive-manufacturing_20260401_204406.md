# Mathematical Knowledge Graph-Driven Framework for Equation-Based Predictive and Reliable Additive Manufacturing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05298
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05298
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Ontology-guided knowledge extraction improves structural coherence and quantitative reliability; subgraph-conditioned LLM generation yields stable, physically plausible extrapolations; a multi-factor confidence score effectively highlights unreliable predictions with extrapolation distance as the dominant signal.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles unreliable knowledge extraction and extrapolation in additive manufacturing (AM) caused by fragmented literature and LLM hallucinations. It introduces a framework that integrates an AM mathematical knowledge graph (AM-MKG) with large language models (LLMs). A minimal, competency-question-driven ontology structures equations, variables, and assumptions during extraction. LLM-based equation generation is conditioned on MKG-derived subgraphs to enforce physical consistency. A confidence score combines normalized extrapolation distance, statistical stability, and physics-based checks to assess reliability. Evaluated on vat photopolymerization literature and an experimental dataset, the approach demonstrates improved extraction quality, stable extrapolation trends, and a discriminative confidence metric, though generalizability beyond the tested domain remains open.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with PDF-to-markdown conversion and equation-centered chunking. An LLM extracts entities and relations guided by a minimal ontology, followed by corpus-wide normalization and validation. Entities are embedded via a hybrid scheme (70% natural language, 30% formula descriptions) and organized into a hierarchical knowledge graph using relation-weighted and similarity edges with Louvain clustering. For prediction, a RAG module retrieves a physics-rich subgraph via hybrid search and lowest common ancestor expansion. An LLM generates candidate equations constrained to subgraph symbols, which are fitted to data via nonlinear least squares. Finally, a confidence score is computed from extrapolation distance (exponential decay), statistical metrics (R², bootstrap uncertainty), and physical consistency checks (assumption count, sign alignment with `influences` relations).

## Key Results
- Ontology-guided extraction significantly improves structural coherence and quantitative reliability, evidenced by higher Partial F1 scores against a manually curated reference.
- Subgraph-conditioned equation generation produces stable, saturating extrapolations (e.g., for cure depth), contrasting with diverging trends from unguided LLM outputs.
- The confidence score successfully integrates multiple factors; extrapolation distance (`S_dist`) emerges as the primary differentiator, while physics-based components (`S_phys`) showed no variation under the current evaluation due to consistent subgraph guidance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Ontology-guided extraction yields coherent, reliable machine-interpretable representations.**  
A minimal ontology (entities: `Equation`, `Variable`, `ProcessParameter`, etc.; relations: `has_input`, `has_output`, `influences`) imposes a strict schema during LLM extraction. The model maps text chunks to predefined semantic roles rather than generating free-text summaries, suppressing hallucinations. Ontology-aligned hints further reduce ambiguity.  
*Core assumption:* Representational discipline via a task-specific ontology mitigates extraction errors.  
*Evidence anchors:* Abstract and Section 2.2 describe the competency-question-driven design and its constraint to match equation-based extrapolation needs; extraction quality improves Partial F1.

**Mechanism 2: Subgraph conditioning enforces physically meaningful functional forms and mitigates unstable extrapolation.**  
The MKG-RAG retrieves a task-relevant, physics-rich subgraph containing equations, variables, assumptions, and validity regimes. The LLM composer is prompted to generate equations only using symbols present in this subgraph, grounding generation in validated relationships (e.g., Jacobs working curve) rather than statistical correlations alone.  
*Core assumption:* A physically coherent subgraph provides sufficient contextual constraint for stable, plausible extrapolation.  
*Evidence anchors:* Abstract and Section 2.6.2 detail prompt constraints; Section 3.2 and Figure 4 show stable vs. unguided divergent trends.

**Mechanism 3: Confidence score integrates distance, stability, and physical consistency into a reliability indicator.**  
The score is a weighted linear combination: `S_dist` (exponential decay with normalized Euclidean distance), `S_stat` (fit quality and bootstrap uncertainty), and `S_phys` (assumption count, sign consistency with `influences`). Weighting (α=0.4, β=0.3, γ=0.2) prioritizes distance as the most universal risk proxy.  
*Core assumption:* Normalized distance is the most parsimonious unreliability proxy; other signals complement it.  
*Evidence anchors:* Abstract and Section 2.7 define components; Section 3.4 and Figure 7 show `S_dist` dominating variation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graph Ontology Design (Minimal, Task-Driven):**  
  *Why needed:* The extraction pipeline relies on a clean schema separating entities and relations. Understanding the minimal set chosen via competency questions is key to extension.  
  *Quick check:* What two competency questions directly necessitate the `influences` relation with a directional sign annotation?

- **Hybrid Embedding for Mathematical Entities:**  
  *Why needed:* Retrieval and clustering depend on embeddings that fuse LaTeX structure and natural language. Misunderstanding the 70%/30% weighting risks poor subgraph selection.  
  *Quick check:* Why are equation entities (~4.8% of nodes) given a lower weight in the hybrid embedding compared to textual entities?

- **Extrapolation Distance Metric:**  
  *Why needed:* The cornerstone of confidence scoring is normalized Euclidean distance in input space, mapped via exponential decay.  
  *Quick check:* For a single variable with training range [10, 50], what is the normalized distance `d_norm` for an input value of 60? *Answer: (60–50)/(50–10) = 0.25.*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Preprocessing & Chunking → MKG Construction → Hierarchical Structuring → (Query → MKG-RAG Retrieval → Subgraph-conditioned Generation → Fitting → Confidence Assessment)

**Critical path:** Document preprocessing → Ontology-guided extraction → Hierarchical MKG construction → Query processing (RAG retrieval → Subgraph-conditioned generation → Fitting → Confidence scoring).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Ontology minimalism vs. coverage: Smaller schema reduces extraction errors but may omit nuances for complex multi-physics models.  
- Embedding weighting (0.7 text / 0.3 formula): Prioritizes semantic context but may under-represent pure symbolic similarity.  
- Confidence weighting (α=0.4, β=0.3, γ=0.2): Emphasizes distance; if statistical stability is high and distance moderate, physics violations could be masked.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Extraction: Low Partial F1, many isolated `Equation` nodes, entity duplication after normalization.  
- Clustering: Non-converging clusters, meaningless higher-layer summaries.  
- RAG: Retrieved subgraph lacks target equation or key variables; excessive/insufficient expansion.  
- Generation: Non-LaTeX syntax, symbols not in subgraph, failure to satisfy `y=f(X)` structure.  
- Confidence: `S_phys` always 1.0 (no KG checks applied), `S_dist` not decaying monotonically.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ontology sanity check:** Run extraction on 2–3 known paper excerpts with a hand-curated reference KG. Measure Partial F1 and verify every `Equation` has ≥1 `has_input` and `has_output`.  
2. **RAG retrieval validation:** For query “cure depth as function of exposure,” inspect top-10 retrieved entities and final subgraph. Confirm it contains the Jacobs equation and key variables (`E`, `Cd`, `D_p`, `E_c`).  
3. **Confidence calibration test:** Using Dataset_22, fit one equation within training range (≤250 mJ/cm²) and predict at 300 mJ/cm². Systematically vary α (distance weight) and observe `C_total` changes for models with good R² but unstable extrapolation vs. moderate R² but stable form.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability to other AM processes:** The framework is evaluated only on vat photopolymerization; transferability to powder bed fusion or multi-physics models is unknown.  
2. **Robustness across extrapolation distances:** Confidence scoring was tested at a single 20% out-of-range point; behavior over a continuous range remains uncharacterized.  
3. **Discriminative power of `S_phys`:** Under subgraph-guided generation, all equations satisfied physical constraints, so `S_phys` never varied; its sensitivity to violations is untested.  
4. **Extension to multi-equation systems:** The current framework handles single-equation univariate relationships; coupled multi-physics models are unexplored.  
5. **Sensitivity to confidence weight parameters:** Weights (α, β, γ, δ) were set heuristically without validation; their impact on score robustness is unclear.

## Limitations
- Missing exact LLM prompts, symbolic parser details, and optimizer settings impedes faithful reproduction.
- Hyperparameter values (e.g., confidence weights, similarity threshold) lack sensitivity analysis; may not generalize.
- Manually curated reference knowledge graph for extraction evaluation is unavailable, preventing exact metric replication.
- The `S_regime` component of confidence scoring was not implemented, weakening `S_phys`.
- Evaluation restricted to a focused corpus (20 papers) and a single experimental dataset in vat photopolymerization, limiting domain generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Ontology-guided extraction improves coherence/reliability:** Medium (validated via Partial F1, but break conditions untested).
- **Subgraph conditioning enforces physical extrapolation:** Medium (evidence from stable trends, but dependent on subgraph completeness).
- **Confidence score reliability:** Low (distance dominates; unimplemented `S_regime` and uncalibrated weights limit robustness).
