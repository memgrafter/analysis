# Stateful Token Reduction for Long-Video Hybrid VLMs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00198
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00198
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Training-free token reduction in Nemotron-Nano-V2 VL 12B hybrid VLM achieves 3.8–4.2× prefilling speedup at 25% token budget with ~0.5 point average accuracy loss across VideoMME, LongVideoBench, and LVBench; light finetuning exceeds non-reduction baseline by +1.37 points.

## Executive Summary
Token reduction for long-video vision-language models (VLMs) is challenging in hybrid architectures that interleave attention layers with state-space (Mamba) blocks. Standard pruning methods fail because token importance rankings are unstable across layers: while each layer relies on a sparse set of tokens, the specific important tokens change unpredictably with depth. This work identifies two key properties of hybrids: (1) Mamba layers maintain a recurrent latent state that persists information even after tokens are pruned, and (2) early-layer importance scores are noisy, making aggressive initial pruning unreliable. The proposed solution combines a progressive low-to-high reduction schedule (preserving more tokens early, pruning aggressively later) with a unified query-conditioned scoring mechanism that assigns importance to both attention (via text-to-vision attention) and Mamba layers (via an implicit-attention proxy derived from the selective scan update, omitting decay terms). On the 12B Nemotron-Nano-V2 VL hybrid model, this approach yields substantial prefilling speedups with minimal accuracy loss under aggressive reduction, while similar reduction on a pure Transformer (Qwen3-VL 8B) causes severe degradation, confirming hybrid-specific design.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a training-free test-time token reduction schedule tailored to hybrid Mamba-Transformer VLMs. A progressive reduction curve (sigmoid or step-decay) starts with high retention (near 100%) in early layers and gradually increases pruning intensity, avoiding early discarding of tokens whose importance may only emerge later. For each reduction layer, a unified scoring mechanism computes token importance conditioned on the text query: in attention layers, it sums text-to-vision attention weights; in Mamba layers, it uses the content-alignment component |¯b_i^T c_m| from the selective scan, which acts as a query-relevance proxy without the temporal decay bias present in the full implicit-attention weight. The top-K tokens by score are retained in order, and the Mamba state is recomputed over only these tokens before continuing through the remaining layers. This design leverages the Mamba state’s capacity to compress and carry forward information from pruned tokens, enabling aggressive late-stage reduction without catastrophic information loss.

## Key Results
- Under a 25% token budget, the method achieves 3.8–4.2× prefilling speedup on the Nemotron-Nano-V2 VL 12B hybrid model.
- Accuracy loss is minimal: average score within ~0.5 points of the non-reduction baseline on VideoMME, LongVideoBench, and LVBench.
- Light finetuning under the reduction improves performance, exceeding the non-reduction baseline by an average of +1.37 points while maintaining the same speedup.
- In contrast, applying similar aggressive reduction to a Transformer model (Qwen3-VL 8B) causes significant accuracy degradation, highlighting the hybrid-specific nature of the approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Stateful Compensation
- **Claim:** Hybrid models tolerate aggressive token reduction because Mamba's recurrent state acts as a compressed memory that persists information after token pruning.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike Transformers where pruning permanently discards a token's data, Mamba layers maintain a latent state S_t that aggregates contributions from all preceding tokens. Even if a token is pruned early, a summary of its information remains encoded in the state. This statefulness allows recovery from early pruning, with the compensatory effect strengthening as depth increases and the state accumulates more content.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states hybrid models maintain a recurrent latent state that aggregates information over time, allowing persistence after token removal; [section 2.2] describes this as compression rather than dropping; neighboring work TimeViper also explores hybrid efficiency but does not analyze state persistence.
- **Break condition:** Fails if aggressive reduction in late Mamba layers causes severe degradation or if resetting the state leads to disproportionate accuracy drops compared to Transformers.

### Mechanism 2: Progressive Schedule Necessity
- **Claim:** A progressive low-to-high reduction schedule is necessary because token-importance rankings are unstable in early layers but become more reliable after state accumulation.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-layer correlations of token importance (Kendall's τ) are low, meaning early-layer scores poorly predict later relevance. Aggressive pruning in the first layer discards tokens that might be needed later. The progressive schedule keeps tokens early (when scores are noisy and state is empty) then increases reduction rates as depth grows, allowing the state to capture important information before pruning intensifies.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes token importance sets change across layers, making early pruning unreliable; [section 2.2] highlights early-layer instability; [section 3.5] Table 7 shows first-layer reduction on Qwen3-VL causes ~11-point drop vs. progressive's ~3–4 point drop.
- **Break condition:** Benefit diminishes if cross-layer importance correlation is high or if the state accumulates poorly in early Mamba layers.

### Mechanism 3: Unified Query-Conditioned Scoring
- **Claim:** A unified language-aware scoring mechanism for both attention and Mamba layers enables effective reduction across hybrid architectures.
- **Mechanism:** For attention layers, importance is derived from summed text-to-vision attention weights (Equation 1). For Mamba layers, the standard implicit-attention weight includes a cumulative decay term that biases importance toward recent tokens. The proposed method uses only the content-alignment component |¯b_i^T c_m| (Equation 3), which acts as a query-key similarity proxy without decay distortion. This treats both layer types as performing content-based relevance scoring.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions unified scoring; [section 2.1] Figure 6 shows decay concentrates on recent tokens; [section 3.4] Table 4 shows query-based selection outperforms average pooling by >3 points on VideoMME.
- **Break condition:** Fails if |¯b_i^T c_m| has weak correlation with task performance or if the decay term is essential (contradicted by ablation).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Architecture**  
  *Why needed:* Mechanisms depend on interleaved structure (stateful Mamba vs. stateless attention). Understanding layer types and state propagation explains why reduction behaves differently than in pure Transformers.  
  *Quick check:* Why does pruning a token in an attention layer permanently remove its information, while pruning in a Mamba layer may not?

- **Concept: Token Importance Metrics**  
  *Why needed:* Reduction relies on query-conditioned scores. Distinguishing attention-based scores from the Mamba implicit-attention proxy (with/without decay) is central to design choices.  
  *Quick check:* What two components make up the full Mamba implicit-attention weight (w_t,j), and which one does the proposed scoring use?

- **Concept: Progressive vs. Early Reduction Schedules**  
  *Why needed:* The core innovation is the progressive low-to-high pattern, contrasting with prior work that prunes early and fixes the token set.  
  *Quick check:* What property of token-importance rankings across layers motivated moving from early to progressive reduction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Vision encoder (SigLIP2) -> patch aggregation -> visual token sequence -> interleaved LLM backbone layers (Attention <-> Mamba-2 <-> MLP) -> output. Attention layers at positions 7,16,25,34,43,52 (0-indexed); Mamba layers maintain per-token state S_t. Reduction module injected into selected layers: attention layers use text-to-vision attention sum; Mamba layers use |¯b_i^T c_m| from selective scan. Scheduler controls retention ratio r(ℓ) per layer (step-decay or sigmoid-decay from start ratio 1.0 to end ratio e.g., 0.125).

- **Critical path:**
  1. Score computation: For reduction layer, compute token importance s_i (attention: attention weights; Mamba: extract ¯b and c projections, compute |¯b_i^T c_m|).
  2. Selection: Rank tokens by s_i, select top-K while preserving temporal order (K = r(ℓ) * current token count).
  3. State management: For Mamba layers, state S_t computed only over retained tokens (pruning changes recurrence inputs).
  4. Schedule application: r(ℓ) follows curve as depth increases.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Reduction granularity: All-layer reduction maximizes speedup but adds Mamba score overhead; attention-only simpler but may not prune enough early.
  - Schedule shape: Sigmoid provides smooth transitions; step-decay simpler but may cause sharp drops at cut points; both must avoid reducing too early.
  - Score fidelity: Using full w_t,j (with decay) biases toward recent tokens; dropping decay risks including irrelevant early tokens; requires empirical validation.
  - Minimum token threshold: Must preserve enough tokens for single-image inputs (paper uses 144 minimum).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Early-drop failure: First-layer reduction causing >5-point drop on LVBench indicates schedule too aggressive initially.
  - Mamba-specific failure: Adding Mamba reduction between attention layers hurts accuracy (Table 5: All A+2M (1–3) –0.96), suggesting chosen Mamba layer is too early or score miscomputed.
  - Pooling substitution failure: Replacing query-based scoring with average pooling in Mamba layers degrades by ~3 points (Table 4), indicating proxy necessity.
  - Scaling failure: Latency vs. frames curve should widen with longer videos (Figure 4 left); if gap closes, reduction overhead dominates or state handling inefficient.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate Mamba scoring proxy: On a single sample, visualize token scores (|¯b_i^T c_m|) at different Mamba depths. Expect distributed pattern across temporal positions; if scores near-zero except last tokens, decay still dominating (check ¯A values).
  2. Test progressive vs. early schedule: Train two models with identical hyperparams: (a) early reduction (first attention layer, fixed 25% thereafter), (b) progressive (sigmoid x0=0.11). Compare accuracy on LVBench; progressive should match or exceed early.
  3. Compare attention-only vs. all-layer reduction: Fix budget ~25%. Measure TTFT and average score. All-layer should achieve similar speedup with better or equal accuracy (Table 1: All vs. All attn). If all-layer degrades, overhead too high or Mamba reduction disrupts state.

## Limitations
- Generalization is demonstrated on a single hybrid architecture (Nemotron-Nano-V2 VL 12B); mechanisms may not hold for other hybrid designs (e.g., different Mamba/Attention ratios, alternative SSMs).
- Direct evidence for the state-compensation hypothesis is weak; no probing of Mamba state content before/after pruning.
- Cross-layer importance instability measured on only 20 samples, possibly not capturing variability across diverse videos/tasks.
- No comparison to concurrent hybrid VLM TimeViper, missing validation of mechanism universality.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (State compensation):** Medium – Plausible given Mamba design and observed tolerance, but direct evidence weak.
- **Mechanism 2 (Progressive schedule necessity):** High – Supported by quantitative correlation analysis (low Kendall's τ) and strong ablation results.
- **Mechanism 3 (Unified query-conditioned scoring):** High – Ablations show proxy with decay removed outperforms alternatives by >3 points.

## Next Checks
1. **State ablation test:** Reset Mamba state (S_t) to zero after each reduction step. If accuracy drops dramatically compared to standard method (especially on long videos), confirms statefulness is critical; minor degradation would suggest smaller state role.
2. **Importance stability scaling:** Measure cross-layer Kendall's τ for token importance across 100+ video samples from all three benchmarks, and repeat on a distinct hybrid architecture (e.g., TimeViper). Consistently low τ strengthens progressive schedule justification; high τ on any model invalidates the core premise.
3. **Scoring proxy correlation validation:** For a set of Mamba layers, compute proposed |¯b_i^T c_m| scores and full w_t,j (with decay). Train a lightweight importance predictor using ground-truth answer correctness as reward to derive "oracle" rankings. Compare correlation of both scores with oracle across layers and datasets. Low correlation for full w_t,j but high for |¯b_i^T c_m| validates the proxy design.
