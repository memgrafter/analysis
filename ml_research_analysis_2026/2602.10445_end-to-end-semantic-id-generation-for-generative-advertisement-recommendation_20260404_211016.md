# End-to-End Semantic ID Generation for Generative Advertisement Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10445
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10445
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: UniSID improves next-ad prediction Hit Rate@5 by up to 4.62% and ad retrieval Recall@1 by 45.46% over the strongest RQ-Kmeans baseline, while also enhancing SID quality (V-measure) by 2.14%.

## Executive Summary
Existing generative recommendation methods generate Semantic IDs (SIDs) via a two-stage residual quantization pipeline, leading to objective misalignment, semantic degradation, and error accumulation. UniSID proposes a unified end-to-end framework that jointly optimizes item embeddings and SIDs directly from raw multimodal ad data. Key innovations include an ad-enhanced input schema, multi-granularity contrastive learning that leverages hierarchical category labels, and a summary-based reconstruction mechanism that uses a frozen LLM to capture high-level semantics. On industrial ad datasets with hundreds of thousands of items, UniSID yields up to 4.62% relative improvement in Hit Rate@5 for next-ad prediction, a 45.46% boost in Recall@1 for ad retrieval, and a 2.14% gain in SID V-measure. The approach also attains state-of-the-art performance on a public recommendation benchmark, demonstrating both effectiveness and potential for large-scale deployment.

## Method Summary
UniSID reformulates Semantic ID generation as a single, differentiable process. Given an ad's multimodal data (text, image, structured attributes), the model first applies an ad-enhanced input schema to fuse heterogeneous signals. A backbone Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) produces contextual representations. These are fed into two parallel heads: one generates the discrete SID via residual quantization layers, while the other produces a continuous embedding token. The training objective combines three losses: (1) SID distortion loss ensuring the quantized codes reconstruct the representation; (2) embedding loss aligning the embedding with the representation; (3) summary-based reconstruction loss where a frozen LLM-generated textual summary of the ad is reconstructed from the SID/embedding, forcing capture of latent semantics. Multi-granularity contrastive learning is applied across the hierarchical category structure, pulling together representations of ads sharing the same category at any level. All components are tuned jointly, enabling gradient flow between the discrete SID selection and the dense embedding.

## Key Results
- **Next-ad prediction**: Hit Rate@5 improved by up to 4.62% relative to the best RQ-Kmeans baseline.
- **Ad retrieval**: Recall@1 increased by 45.46%.
- **SID quality**: V-measure rose by 2.14%.
- **Generalization**: Achieved state-of-the-art on a public recommendation benchmark (specific metric not reported).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
UniSID's gains stem from three intertwined mechanisms. First, end-to-end joint optimization eliminates the two-stage pipeline's objective misalignment, allowing SID codes and embeddings to co-adapt. Second, multi-granularity contrastive learning leverages the provided category taxonomy to align representations at multiple semantic resolutions, mitigating semantic degradation. Third, summary-based reconstruction injects high-level, implicit knowledge via an LLM-generated textual summary, which acts as a rich auxiliary target that cannot be trivially memorized from raw attributes. Together, these ensure that the discrete SIDs retain both fine-grained and abstract semantics, while the continuous embedding benefits from the same signals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Residual Quantization (RQ)**: Encodes a vector into a sequence of discrete codes from multiple codebooks, each refining the residual. *Why needed*: Produces compact Semantic IDs while preserving reconstruction fidelity. *Quick check*: Does increasing RQ layers improve V-measure but increase codebook collisions?
- **Semantic ID (SID)**: A discrete, index-based representation of an item intended for generative recommendation. *Why needed*: Enables efficient retrieval and generation over large catalogs. *Quick check*: Are SIDs stable across similar items and do they correspond to meaningful clusters?
- **Multi-granularity Contrastive Learning**: Contrastive loss applied at different levels of a semantic hierarchy (e.g., industry, L1, L2, L3). *Why needed*: Encourages representations to respect both coarse and fine-grained category boundaries, improving semantic richness. *Quick check*: Does removing any granularity level degrade V-measure or HR@K?
- **Summary-based Reconstruction**: Using an LLM to generate a textual summary of ad attributes, then reconstructing that summary from the model's internal representation. *Why needed*: Forces the model to capture high-level, implicit semantics beyond surface attributes. *Quick check*: Does replacing the LLM summary with raw attributes reduce performance?
- **End-to-End Joint Optimization**: Simultaneous training of SID generator and embedding predictor with shared backbone. *Why needed*: Aligns objectives and avoids error propagation from separate stages. *Quick check*: Does joint training outperform alternately training the two heads?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**:
  Raw Ad Data -> Ad-Enhanced Input Schema -> MLLM Backbone -> {RQ SID Head, Embedding Head} -> SID Sequence, Embedding Token
  Additional path: Frozen LLM -> Summary Generation -> Decoder (consumes SID+Embedding) -> Reconstruction Target
- **Critical Path** (per training example):
  1. Preprocess raw ad (text, image, attributes) into unified input schema.
  2. Encode with MLLM to get item representation.
  3. Generate SID: sequentially apply RQ layers, each selecting nearest codebook entry and computing residual.
  4. Produce embedding token: project representation or pooled features.
  5. Generate or retrieve cached LLM summary for the ad; feed concatenated SID+embedding into a decoder to predict the summary tokens.
  6. Compute total loss: L_sid (distortion) + L_emb (embedding alignment) + λ * L_rec (summary reconstruction), plus multi-granularity contrastive losses across batch.
  7. Backpropagate and update parameters (except frozen LLM).
- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - Fixed codebook size (2,048 per layer) vs catalog growth: small codebooks may cause collisions as item count scales; increasing layers adds length without enlarging vocabulary.
  - Frozen LLM for summaries: reduces training cost and ensures consistency but limits adaptability to domain-specific terminology; introduces inference dependency on external LLM.
  - End-to-end differentiability with discrete SIDs: relies on straight-through estimator; may introduce gradient noise compared to continuous relaxation.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Low codebook utilization (many codes rarely used) indicates wasted capacity or poor initialization.
  - Reconstruction loss stagnates while L_sid decreases: suggests the model ignores the summary signal, possibly due to weighting imbalance.
  - Discrepancy between SID V-measure and downstream HR@K: SIDs may cluster semantically but fail to preserve information needed for next-ad prediction.
- **Three First Experiments**:
  1. Ablate joint optimization: train SID head and embedding head separately (task-specific) vs jointly; measure impact on V-measure and HR@5.
  2. Replace LLM summary with raw attribute reconstruction: compare L_rec using raw attributes vs LLM summary; evaluate SID quality and retrieval metrics.
  3. Vary number of RQ layers (1-4) and codebook size (512-4096) to study scaling effects on V-measure and codebook utilization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does UniSID's performance and SID generation quality scale when applied to recommendation scenarios with vastly larger item catalogs (e.g., billions of items) compared to the industrial ad datasets (60W-100W) used in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments are conducted on industrial ad datasets with item counts in the hundred-thousands. The paper's efficiency discussion (Appendix B.1) notes computational parity with RQ-based methods but does not address the fundamental scalability challenge of discrete codebook learning (size 2,048 per layer) in a truly massive item space, nor does it explore potential vocabulary size growth or codebook utilization degradation.
- Why unresolved: The core promise of SIDs is to enable efficient generative recommendation for large-scale catalogs. While UniSID improves SID quality on moderate-scale datasets, its end-to-end joint optimization with a fixed-size, per-layer codebook may face different scalability bottlenecks (e.g., codebook collisions, wasted capacity) than the two-stage RQ-VAE approach when the item cardinality increases by orders of magnitude. This is not tested.
- What evidence would resolve it: A large-scale experiment where the item catalog size is systematically varied (e.g., from 100K to 10M+ items), measuring (a) the stability of V-measure and HR metrics as catalog size grows, (b) codebook utilization statistics (e.g., average tokens per code), and (c) training/inference time/memory overhead compared to a strong two-stage RQ baseline scaled to the same catalog.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent is the performance gain of UniSID's multi-granularity contrastive learning dependent on the quality and hierarchical structure of the provided ad category labels (e.g., Industry -> L1 -> L2 -> L3), and could alternative, learned granularity definitions (e.g., from multimodal clustering) outperform or be more robust?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The multi-granularity contrastive learning objective (Section 3.3) explicitly constructs positive sets P𝑙 at each level based on items sharing the same *semantic category* at that hierarchy level. The case studies and dataset description confirm reliance on a provided, structured multi-level category taxonomy.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes the pre-defined, human-annotated category hierarchy is the optimal structure for defining semantic granularity. This is a strong assumption. Categories may be noisy, incomplete, or misaligned with the actual multimodal feature space. The method does not explore whether the granularity structure could be *learned* from the data (e.g., via clustering on the MLLM's representations) to better match the true semantic hierarchy of the item space.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study replacing the category-hierarchy-based positive sampling with (a) a single flat contrastive loss, (b) a loss using clusters derived from an unsupervised method (e.g., HDBSCAN on multimodal embeddings), and (c) a learnable hierarchical clustering module within the framework. Compare V-measure and downstream HR@K to isolate the contribution of the *provided hierarchy* versus the *multi-granularity mechanism itself*.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Is the summary-based reconstruction mechanism's success primarily due to the intrinsic quality of the LLM-summarized "high-level semantics" or simply an additional form of semantic regularization, and can its effect be achieved with a simpler, non-LLM-based auxiliary task?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The summary-based reconstruction (Section 3.4) uses a frozen LLM to generate a semantic summary from ad attributes, which is then reconstructed from the SID/embedding. The ablation study (Table 5, lower section) shows that "LLM summary (Ours)" outperforms "Attributions" (reconstructing raw attributes), and the case study highlights recovered implicit semantics.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not disentangle the contribution of (1) using an LLM to *create* a new, high-level target (the summary) versus (2) the *act of reconstruction* itself as a regularization force. Since the reconstruction loss (L<sub>rec</sub>) is an auxiliary objective, it's unclear if a simpler target (e.g., a bag-of-words from the summary, or even the concatenated raw attributes) would provide a similar benefit, or if the specific, LLM-generated textual summary is crucial. This has practical implications for deployment cost and complexity.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation where the reconstruction target is systematically varied: (a) raw attributes (done), (b) LLM-generated summary (done), (c) a distilled, fixed-length vector representation of the summary (bypassing text generation), and (d) a contrastive loss against the summary embedding instead of generation. Measuring the change in SID V-measure and recommendation metrics would clarify the necessity of the LLM-based generative reconstruction.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Does the end-to-end joint optimization of SIDs and the embedding token (Section 3.2) truly lead to *mutually reinforcing* representations, or does one (typically the embedding, which is denser) dominate the learning signal, potentially leaving the discrete SIDs under-optimized?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The joint training objective L<sub>total</sub> = L<sub>sid</sub> + L<sub>emb</sub> + λL<sub>rec</sub> (Eq. 11) and the ablation in Figure 3 ("Joint Train" vs. "Task-Specific Train") show that joint training improves both SID quality and embedding quality. The architecture (Figure 2) shows the embedding token is conditioned on the SID token sequence.
- Why unresolved: The ablation demonstrates the *benefit* of joint training but does not diagnose the *dynamics*. The SID tokens are discrete (via argmax) and non-differentiable, while the embedding token is a dense vector. The gradient flow from L<sub>emb</sub> and L<sub>rec</sub> may predominantly influence the MLLM and projection heads in a way that primarily shapes the embedding token's context, with the SID selection (argmax) being a weaker, noisier signal. This could mean the SIDs are not as co-adapted as presumed.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of (a) gradient norms flowing back to the SID head vs. the embedding head during training, (b) the correlation between SID stability (token consistency across epochs) and embedding quality, (c) an experiment where the embedding token is frozen after initial warm-up to see if SID quality (V-measure) degrades, indicating dependence. Alternatively, treating SID selection as a straight-through estimator and analyzing its gradient magnitude could provide insight.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gap**: No code, data splits, or hyperparameters provided; industrial dataset details are proprietary, preventing independent verification.
- **Evaluation scope**: Results demonstrated on one public benchmark only; cross-domain generalization remains unproven.
- **Scalability assumptions**: The fixed codebook design (2,048 per layer) may not scale to billion-item catalogs without degradation, yet this is not empirically tested.

## Confidence

- **Industrial dataset performance (Hit Rate@5, Recall@1)**: High confidence (results are internally consistent and on large-scale proprietary data).
- **Public benchmark state-of-the-art claim**: Medium confidence (single benchmark insufficient for broad validation).
