# Multi-agent imitation learning with function approximation: Linear Markov games and beyond

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.22810  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22810  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Introduces a feature-level concentrability coefficient \(C_{\phi,\max}\) and an interactive algorithm (LSVI-UCB-ZERO-BC) that eliminates dependence on concentrability, yielding sample complexity scaling only with feature dimension \(d\); empirical results on Gridworld, Tic-Tac-Toe, and Connect4 show clear superiority over behavior cloning.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the sample complexity bottleneck in multi-agent imitation learning (MAIL) caused by the all-policy deviation concentrability coefficient (\(C_{\max}\)). By focusing on linear Markov games—where transition dynamics and rewards are linear in a known feature map—the authors introduce a feature-level coefficient (\(C_{\phi,\max}\)) that can be dramatically smaller when features generalize across states. For non-interactive settings, MAIL sample complexity scales with \(C_{\phi,\max}\) rather than \(C_{\max}\). To remove this dependence entirely, they propose LSVI-UCB-ZERO-BC, an interactive algorithm that couples reward‑free exploration (using LSVI‑UCB with synthetic bonuses) with behavior cloning. Theoretically, its sample complexity depends only on the feature dimension \(d\). A deep variant, DQN‑Explore‑BC, replaces fixed features with DQN‑learned representations and outperforms deep behavior cloning on Tic‑Tac‑Toe and Connect4. The analysis and experiments together demonstrate that interactive exploration is key to overcoming concentrability limitations in MAIL.

## Method Summary
The core of the approach is the LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC algorithm. For each player, it constructs a Markov decision process where the opponent is fixed to the expert’s policy. It then runs LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO—a linear‑MDP solver equipped with a synthetic exploration bonus \(r_h(x,a) = \|\phi(x,a)\| (\Lambda_h)^{-1}\)—to collect a dataset that uniformly covers the feature space. Finally, behavioral cloning on this dataset yields the player’s policy. This two‑phase design ensures that the imitation learner observes states arising from a diverse set of opponent responses, not just the expert’s trajectories, thus eliminating any need for a concentrability coefficient. For settings without a known feature map, DQN‑Explore‑BC adapts the same principle: the penultimate layer of a DQN provides features, and a covariance‑driven bonus guides exploration.

## Key Results
- Introduced the feature‑level concentrability coefficient \(C_{\phi,\max}\), which upper‑bounds \(C_{\max}\) and can be much smaller for expressive feature maps.
- Designed LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC, the first computationally efficient interactive MAIL algorithm for linear Markov games with sample complexity scaling only with the feature dimension \(d\) (Theorem 4.1).
- Empirically, LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC achieved zero exploitability in a Gridworld environment for both tabular and relational features, while behavior cloning failed for the latter. The deep variant DQN‑Explore‑BC outperformed deep BC on Tic‑Tac‑Toe (Nash gap ~0 vs. >4) and Connect4 (97% vs. 54% win rate against a noisy solver).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper’s success rests on three interconnected insights.

**1. Feature‑level concentrability.** In linear Markov games, the state occupancy measure is an affine function of feature expectations. By performing the change of measure on feature vectors rather than state‑action distributions, the sample complexity of non‑interactive behavior cloning depends on \(C_{\phi,\max} = \max_h \max_{\pi_{-n}} \max_{\pi_n^* \in \mathrm{br}(\pi_{-n})} \|\phi_{\pi_n^*,\pi_E}\|_{\Lambda_{E,h}^{-1}}\). This coefficient can be orders of magnitude smaller than \(C_{\max}\) when features smooth over state space, e.g., relational features in Gridworld (Theorem 3.3). The key assumption is linearity of dynamics and rewards plus expert realisability in the softmax linear policy class.

**2. Interactive reward‑free exploration.** LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC eliminates any dependence on concentrability by constructing an exploration dataset that uniformly covers the feature space. For each player \(n\), it runs LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO on the MDP where the opponent plays the expert policy. The synthetic reward \(r_h^{n,k}(x,a) = \|\phi(x,a)\| (\Lambda_h^{n,k})^{-1}\) incentivizes visiting state‑action pairs whose features lie in directions with small empirical covariance. After \(K\) episodes, the dataset \(D_n\) satisfies \(\max_{\pi_{-n}} \|\phi_{\pi_n^E,\pi_{-n}}\|_{\Lambda_{-n,K}^{-1}} \le \widetilde{O}(\sqrt{d^3 H^4 B^2 / K})\), removing \(C_{\phi,\max}\) from the bound (Lemma 4.4). The resulting sample complexity scales only with \(d\) (Theorem 4.1).

**3. Deep extension with DQN‑Explore‑BC.** To apply the principle beyond linear features, the last layer of a DQN is used as a learned feature map. The same covariance‑driven bonus is applied to these features, and the DQN is trained to predict cumulative exploratory rewards. After a warm‑up phase, the collected data is used for behavioral cloning. Although no formal guarantees exist for this deep variant, it consistently outperforms pure behavior cloning on Tic‑Tac‑Toe and Connect4, confirming the practical value of interactive exploration even with approximate features.

## Foundational Learning
**Linear Markov Games**  
*Why needed:* Linear dynamics and rewards (Assumption 2.1) make value functions linear in a known feature map, enabling efficient RL subroutines and concentration arguments.  
*Quick check:* What are the two key linearity conditions? (Answer: For each player \(n\), \(\sum_{a_{-n}} \pi_{-n}(a_{-n}|x) P(x'|x,a_n,a_{-n}) = \phi(x,a_n)^\top M_{\pi_{-n}}(x')\) and \(\sum_{a_{-n}} \pi_{-n}(a_{-n}|x) r_n(x,a_n,a_{-n}) = \phi(x,a_n)^\top w_{\pi_{-n}}\), with \(\|\phi\|\le 1\).)

**Concentrability Coefficient**  
*Why needed:* Quantifies the mismatch between expert and learner state‑action distributions; \(C_{\max}\) appears in lower and upper bounds for non‑interactive MAIL and becomes prohibitively large when expert coverage is sparse.  
*Quick check:* How does \(C_{\phi,\max}\) differ from \(C_{\max}\) and why might it be smaller? (Answer: \(C_{\phi,\max} = \max_h \max_{\pi_{-n}} \max_{\pi_n^* \in \mathrm{br}(\pi_{-n})} \|\phi_{\pi_n^*,\pi_E}\|_{\Lambda_{E,h}^{-1}}\) uses the expert feature covariance to normalize; if features generalize, a small expert dataset can bound feature expectations for many states, whereas \(C_{\max}\) measures raw state‑distribution ratios that can be infinite.)

**Reward‑free Exploration**  
*Why needed:* To gather data that covers all states potentially visited under a best response to the expert, without knowing the true reward. The synthetic bonus derived from feature uncertainty drives uniform coverage of the feature space, enabling subsequent behavior cloning without concentrability assumptions.  
*Quick check:* In LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO, what is the synthetic reward and how does it encourage exploration? (Answer: \(r_h^{n,k}(x,a) = \|\phi(x,a)\| (\Lambda_h^{n,k})^{-1}\). The bonus is large for actions whose features point in directions with small empirical covariance, thus directing the policy to explore those under‑sampled directions.)

## Architecture Onboarding
The interactive linear MAIL algorithm (LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC, Algorithm 1) proceeds as follows.

**Component Map**  
Inputs: Known feature map \(\phi\), horizon \(H\), warm‑up episodes \(K\), expert policy oracle.  
Process: For each player \(n\):  
 • Build MDP \(M_n\) with opponent fixed to expert \(\pi_{-n}^E\).  
 • Run LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO on \(M_n\) to collect dataset \(D_n\).  
 • Perform behavioral cloning on \(D_n\) to obtain \(\pi_n^{\text{out}}\).  
Output: Policy pair \((\pi_1^{\text{out}}, \pi_2^{\text{out}})\).

**Critical Path**  
1. Initialize feature map and expert oracle.  
2. For each player, run \(K\) episodes of LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO: the opponent follows the expert; the active player follows the current exploration policy derived from optimistic Q‑values. Each episode updates covariance matrices \(\Lambda_h\) and collects (state, expert action) pairs.  
3. Train a softmax linear policy via BC on the collected dataset.  
4. Repeat for the other player.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Number of episodes \(K\): Larger \(K\) improves coverage and reduces Nash gap but increases expert queries.  
- Feature dimension \(d\): Affects computational cost (matrix inversion \(O(d^3)\)) and sample complexity; low‑dimensional expressive features are ideal.  
- Exploration bonus scaling: Uses \((\beta+1)\) to ensure optimism; \(\beta\) must be set according to confidence level.  
- BC temperature \(\eta\): Higher \(\eta\) densifies the policy class but may need more data.  
- Deep variant: Introduces numerous hyperparameters (network size, learning rate, replay buffer size) and lacks theoretical guarantees; careful tuning required.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Ill‑conditioned covariance matrices \(\Lambda_h\) (small eigenvalues) causing unstable bonuses and inefficient exploration.  
- Violation of linear realizability (Assumption 2.1) or expert policy not in \(\Pi_{\text{softlin}}\), leading to large Nash gaps even with interactive method.  
- Deep DQN training instability (diverging Q‑values, policy collapse).  
- Overfitting in BC: training loss goes to zero while held‑out validation loss rises.  
- High computational cost from repeated matrix inverses in large‑\(d\) settings.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Linear Gridworld:** Tested BC and LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC on a 3×3 two‑agent grid with three feature maps (tabular \(d=288\), relational \(d=80\), deep linear \(d=262\)). BC failed (Nash gap ~10–15) for tabular and relational features but succeeded for deep linear; LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC converged to zero exploitability for both tabular and relational features.  
2. **Tic‑Tac‑Toe:** Compared DQN‑Explore‑BC and deep BC against a minimax solver. DQN‑Explore‑BC achieved Nash gap ~0, whereas deep BC had gap >4.  
3. **Connect4:** Evaluated win rates against opponents with varying solver noise (Boltzmann noise on action values). DQN‑Explore‑BC steadily reached 92%–97% win rates (noise 4–5), while deep BC peaked at 54%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can the expert policy realizability assumption be removed?** The current theory requires the expert equilibrium to lie in the closure of the softmax linear policy class. Removing this would broaden applicability.  
2. **How can \(C_{\phi,\max}\) be estimated from data?** Knowing this coefficient would tell whether non‑interactive MAIL is feasible with given features, but no practical estimation procedure exists.  
3. **Can interaction be made adaptive?** Present interactive algorithms require a fixed \(K\) upfront. An adaptive method that leverages any pre‑collected dataset to determine the minimal number of additional queries would be more efficient.  
4. **Can theoretical guarantees be extended to deep MAIL?** The deep heuristic lacks finite‑sample analysis; establishing such guarantees under realistic assumptions (e.g., neural tangent kernel) is an important open direction.

## Limitations
- Strong linear assumptions and expert realizability are required for theoretical guarantees; these may not hold in complex real‑world domains.  
- Interactive expert queries can be expensive or impossible in many applications.  
- Theoretical sample complexity bounds are high‑degree polynomials; the actual sample needs in practice might be lower but are not well understood.  
- The deep extension (DQN‑Explore‑BC) lacks formal analysis and has only been tested on small discrete games.  
- No direct empirical measurement of \(C_{\phi,\max}\) versus \(C_{\max}\) is provided; the claim of their gap relies on a single feature example.

## Confidence
- **Non‑interactive MAIL with \(C_{\phi,\max}\):** Medium. The derivation is rigorous under assumptions, but empirical validation is limited to one gridworld setting.  
- **Interactive LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC:** Medium‑high. The algorithm is well‑motivated and achieves zero exploitability in Gridworld experiments; however, it requires linear features and does not address simultaneous learning of both agents.  
- **Deep DQN‑Explore‑BC:** High for small discrete games (consistent empirical superiority), but low for generalization to larger or continuous domains due to lack of theory and limited testing.

## Next Checks
1. **Validate \(C_{\phi,\max}\) vs. \(C_{\max}\):** Compute both coefficients for multiple feature maps (tabular, random, autoencoded) in Gridworld and correlate their ratios with the success/failure of behavior cloning.  
2. **Scale interactive linear MAIL:** Test LSVI‑UCB‑ZERO‑BC on a larger linear Markov game (e.g., 5×5 grid) and measure sample complexity as a function of feature dimension \(d\) and horizon \(H\), confirming the poly\((d)\) scaling and independence from concentrability.  
3. **Ablate DQN‑Explore‑BC on Connect4:** Remove the covariance‑driven exploration bonus, vary the covariance update frequency, and change network architecture to isolate the contribution of each component and assess robustness.
