# MedObvious: Exposing the Medical Moravec's Paradox in VLMs via Clinical Triage

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23501
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23501
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art VLMs achieve only 63.2% accuracy on a pre-diagnostic sanity-check benchmark, far below human performance (88.4%), with critical failures in false alarms, scaling, and format robustness.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) can generate fluent medical narratives but may fail fundamental pre-diagnostic sanity checks (e.g., modality, anatomy, orientation, integrity), especially with multiple images. This poses a safety risk. The paper introduces MedObvious, a benchmark of 1,880 tasks across five progressive tiers, testing whether models can identify an outlier or confirm consistency in small image grids (2x2, 3x3). Evaluating 17 VLMs zero-shot reveals best accuracy of 63.2%, with high false-alarm rates on normal inputs, performance drops on larger grids, and strong format dependence, exposing a "Medical Moravec's Paradox" where narrative fluency masks basic visual verification failures.

## Method Summary
MedObvious constructs 1,880 tasks from ROCO (radiology) and Kvasir (endoscopy) datasets. Each task presents an n×n grid (n=4 or 9) where one panel may be an outlier (different clinical attribute) or all are consistent (negative control). Tasks are organized into five tiers increasing in clinical specificity, from basic modality/orientation (T1) to subtle anatomical landmarks (T5). Evaluation wraps each grid in five formats: Detection MCQ/Open, Referring MCQ/Open, and Visual Referring (Yes/No). Seventeen VLMs are evaluated zero-shot; outputs are parsed to a closed label space (position, option, Yes/No) and accuracy is computed, split by positive (outlier) and negative (no outlier) cases.

## Key Results
- Best overall accuracy is 63.2% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), well below human 88.4%.
- Negative-control accuracy ranges widely (5.3%–60.9%), indicating high false-alarm bias.
- Performance degrades significantly on larger grids (T3 scaling tier).
- Large format dependence: e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B referring task accuracy is 75.3% (MCQ) vs. 29.7% (open).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Set-level comparison failure
Models degrade on 3×3 grids because they cannot maintain exhaustive cross-image verification, relying instead on a few salient cues. This exposes an inability to perform deliberate, sequential consistency checks across multiple panels.

### Mechanism 2: Over-commitment bias
High false-alarm rates on normal inputs (negative controls) show models prefer detecting an anomaly over abstaining. This stems from training objectives that prioritize fluent generation over input validation, leading to uncalibrated uncertainty and unsafe gatekeeping.

### Mechanism 3: Format-dependent reasoning
Large gaps between multiple-choice and open-ended variants indicate models exploit format-specific scaffolding rather than mastering the underlying task. MCQ constraints artificially boost performance by limiting output space, while open-ended prompts expose grounding failures.

### Critical path
1. Run all models on T1 Detection MCQ; filter those with negative accuracy >50%.
2. For top models, evaluate full T1–T5 in Detection MCQ to quantify scaling degradation.
3. Compare Detection MCQ vs. Detection Open on T2; if open-ended accuracy drops >25%, format sensitivity is severe.

### Design tradeoffs
Grid size (2×2 vs. 3×3) trades cognitive load vs. ecological validity; synthetic inconsistencies trade realism for isolating visual reasoning; format multiplicity trades comprehensiveness against compute cost. Prioritize negative-control consistency over raw accuracy.

### Failure signatures
1. High positive accuracy but negative accuracy <30% → dangerous over-commitment.
2. >20% accuracy drop from T2 to T3 → inability to scale set-level comparison.
3. >40% variance between MCQ and open formats → interface-dependent reasoning.

### First 3 experiments
1. T1 Detection MCQ screen for safe gatekeeping candidates (neg acc >50%).
2. Full T1–T5 in Detection MCQ to measure T2→T3 scaling degradation.
3. Detection MCQ vs. Open on T2 to quantify format sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do medical VLMs exhibit high false-alarm rates on normal inputs, indicating a "find-something" bias that compromises safe gatekeeping?
- **Basis:** Paper identifies this as a core failure; negative-control accuracy ranges 5.3%–60.9%. Models may lack calibrated abstention or overfit to anomaly-detection heuristics.
- **Why unresolved:** Contradicts clinical need to proceed on normal studies; requires analysis of calibration or uncertainty-aware decoding.
- **Resolution evidence:** Analyze confidence calibration on negatives; test explicit "no anomaly" training; compare human abstention.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does performance degrade scaling from 2×2 to 3×3 grids, and what does this reveal about limits of set-level consistency reasoning?
- **Basis:** RQ2 targets set-level consistency; Table 3 shows cross-model T3 drops. Models likely rely on salient cues, not exhaustive comparison.
- **Why unresolved:** Scaling exposes pairwise vs. holistic reasoning; failure limits use in multi-slice studies.
- **Resolution evidence:** Vary distractor count; probe attention patterns; test trained set-level comparison modules.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why is pre-diagnostic sanity checking highly format-dependent, and can models achieve format-robust decisions?
- **Basis:** RQ5 investigates interface robustness; large MCQ/open gaps (e.g., 75.3% vs 29.7%) suggest format-specific shortcuts.
- **Why unresolved:** Clinical workflows require binary, localization, or free-text justification; inconsistency implies lack of robust competence.
- **Resolution evidence:** Evaluate on format-agnostic prompts; train with multi-format consistency losses; measure decision stability under prompt variation.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What architectural/training modifications address the Medical Moravec's Paradox—fluent narratives yet basic visual sanity check failures?
- **Basis:** Paper centers on this paradox; pre-diagnostic verification remains unsolved. Models may prioritize language generation over robust visual validation.
- **Why unresolved:** Lacks mechanisms to verify modality, anatomy, orientation, integrity before reasoning.
- **Resolution evidence:** Incorporate pre-diagnostic sanity checks as a front-end module; test whether specialization improves downstream safety; explore architectures for set-level coherence.

## Limitations
- Benchmark not publicly released; reconstruction from ROCO/Kvasir requires unreleased filtering/assembly code.
- Exact prompt templates and output normalization rules are unspecified, blocking reliable format comparisons.
- Claims about over-commitment bias and format dependence are benchmark-specific; real-world triage includes additional context (history, urgency).

## Confidence
- **High:** Negative-control variability and T2→T3 scaling degradation are directly reported with per-model numbers.
- **Medium:** Format dependence is documented but may be influenced by unreleased prompt design.
- **Low:** Mechanistic explanations (e.g., "reliance on salient cues") are post-hoc interpretations, not proven by ablation.
