# Missing-Aware Multimodal Fusion for Unified Microservice Incident Management

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25538
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25538
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ARMOR achieves state-of-the-art unified incident management (AD, FT, RCL) under missing modalities, with >60% RCL improvement over ART and lower latency.

## Executive Summary
ARMOR tackles the critical challenge of missing telemetry (metrics, logs, traces) in microservice incident management, which can cripple existing unified frameworks reliant on static imputation. It proposes a modality-specific asymmetric encoder to handle distribution disparities across heterogeneous streams and a missing-aware gated fusion mechanism that uses learnable placeholders and dynamic negative bias compensation to suppress imputation noise and prevent cross-modal interference. The framework is trained via a self-supervised auto-regression objective, jointly optimizing anomaly detection (AD), failure triage (FT), and root cause localization (RCL) without requiring fault labels for AD/RCL and only failure-type annotations for FT. On complete data, ARMOR attains state-of-the-art performance (e.g., AD F1-score up to 0.997). Under severe modality loss, it maintains robust diagnostic accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline by over 60% in RCL accuracy while keeping end-to-end latency low.

## Method Summary
ARMOR processes aligned minute-level windows of metrics, logs, and traces. Each modality passes through a dedicated asymmetric encoder (ModernTCN-based) tailored to its statistical properties: a high-capacity encoder for continuous metrics and lightweight encoders for sparse logs and traces. A missing-aware gated fusion module then combines the modality embeddings: missing tokens are replaced by a learnable placeholder and receive a dynamic negative bias during attention gating, actively suppressing their influence. The fused representation is propagated over the microservice invocation topology using a graph attention network (GAT). During offline training, the model predicts the next-step observation via an auto-regressive head; the loss is masked and applied to both observed and synthetically missing channels, with stochastic modality dropout augmentation. At inference, reconstruction errors and latent embeddings are concatenated into a unified failure signature. Anomaly detection uses a dynamic threshold on deviation scores; failure triage employs a frozen encoder with an XGBoost classifier on pooled errors; root cause localization ranks instances by cosine similarity to a global failure vector.

## Key Results
- **Complete-data benchmark:** ARMOR achieves AD F1-score 0.961–0.997, FT F1-score 0.869–0.938, and RCL Avg@5 0.893–0.910, setting new state-of-the-art.
- **Missing-modality robustness:** Under simulated full-channel blackouts (e.g., missing metrics), ARMOR outperforms the strongest baseline (ART) by >60% in RCL accuracy and exhibits lower end-to-end latency.
- **Ablation confirmation:** Systematic removal of core components (asymmetric encoder, gated fusion, graph network, reconstruction features) leads to consistent performance drops, validating each design choice.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Modality-specific asymmetric encoder isolates distribution disparities among metrics, logs, and traces, preventing the absence of one modality from corrupting representations of others.
- **Mechanism:** Continuous metrics and sparse discrete events (logs, traces) are processed by separate encoder blocks with different capacities (high-capacity for metrics, lightweight for logs/traces). Each encoder hierarchically disentangles temporal, channel, and spatial dependencies, yielding embeddings with distinct hidden dimensions.
- **Core assumption:** Heterogeneous telemetry streams have incompatible statistical properties; forcing a shared encoder causes negative transfer, especially under missing data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "modality-specific asymmetric encoder that isolates distribution disparities among metrics, logs, and traces"
  - [Section 4.2] "To address this, we partition the sequence into modality-specific sub-matrices… employing a domain-guided asymmetric encoder based on ModernTCN… applying symmetric extractors to such heterogeneous inputs risks overfitting on sparse events or underfitting on volatile resources."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; neighboring papers address unified frameworks or missing modalities but do not describe this specific encoder design.
- **Break condition:** If the modality dropout during training is not aligned with the actual missing patterns in deployment, the encoder may still overfit to the surviving modalities and fail to generalize when a different modality is missing.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Missing-aware gated fusion with learnable placeholders and dynamic negative bias compensation suppresses imputation noise and prevents cross-modal interference from incomplete inputs.
- **Mechanism:** For each modality, if it is missing (mask 0), its token is replaced by a learnable placeholder embedding *E*<sub>miss</sub> plus a modality-specific embedding. After cross-modal attention, a gating logit is computed as *s*<sub>*k*</sub> = *W*<sub>*s*</sub>*U*<sub>*k*</sub> + (1−*o*<sub>*k*</sub>) * *b*<sub>miss,*k</sub>, where *b*<sub>miss,*k</sub> is a learnable negative bias. Softmax produces fusion weights, and the final representation is a weighted sum. The negative bias actively penalizes absent modalities.
- **Core assumption:** Static zero-padding misleads the network into interpreting missing data as a healthy idle state; a dynamic, learned suppression of placeholder contributions mitigates this.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "missing-aware gated fusion mechanism utilizing learnable placeholders and dynamic bias compensation to prevent cross-modal interference"
  - [Section 4.3] "Instead of employing static imputation, this module explicitly recognizes absent inputs and adaptively compensates for their routing contributions… We further add a modality-specific embedding… The mask-adjusted token *v*'<sub>*k*</sub> is formulated as… We then dynamically calculate a gating weight… adding a learnable missing bias *b*<sub>miss,*k</sub> ∈ ℝ<0 exclusively when *o*<sub>*k*</sub>=0."
  - [corpus] Related works on missing modality (e.g., [46] Wu et al. 2026 survey) discuss generic strategies but not this specific gated bias mechanism; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the missing bias *b*<sub>miss,*k</sub> is not properly constrained to negative values or if the placeholder *E*<sub>miss</sub> fails to learn a distinct representation from normal data, the gating may not sufficiently suppress noisy contributions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Self-supervised auto-regression objective jointly optimizes anomaly detection (AD), failure triage (FT), and root cause localization (RCL) without fault labels for AD/RCL, requiring only failure-type annotations for FT.
- **Mechanism:** During offline training, the model predicts the next-step multimodal observation (all channels) from a window of past data. The loss masked by the simulated missingness is applied to both observed and (synthetically) missing channels. Unified failure representations are built by concatenating reconstruction errors and latent embeddings; these feed into AD (threshold on deviation score), FT (XGBoost classifier on pooled errors), and RCL (cosine similarity to global failure vector).
- **Core assumption:** Predicting future sensor readings forces the network to learn normal system dynamics; deviations from predictions signal anomalies, and the learned representations are transferable to downstream tasks without full supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "By employing self-supervised auto-regression with mask-guided reconstruction, ARMOR jointly optimizes anomaly detection (AD), failure triage (FT), and root cause localization (RCL). AD and RCL require no fault labels, while FT relies solely on failure-type annotations for the downstream classifier."
  - [Section 4.4] "During this stage, we optimize the entire framework via a self-supervised auto-regression task… The training objective comprises two masked mean squared error terms… For downstream diagnostic tasks, we construct a unified failure signature by concatenating reconstruction errors and latent embeddings."
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., ART [41], TrioXpert [39]) also use self-supervised reconstruction but lack missing-aware fusion; evidence for the specific joint optimization exists in the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the normal training data does not adequately cover the system’s operational state space, the model’s predictions may be systematically biased, leading to high false positive rates in AD or mislocalization in RCL.

## Foundational Learning
#### Concept: Self-Supervised Auto-Regression for Time Series
- **Why needed here:** The core training signal in ARMOR comes from predicting the next time step of multimodal data. Understanding how reconstruction errors relate to anomalies is essential to grasp why labels are unnecessary for AD and RCL.
- **Quick check question:** What is the typical loss used in auto-regressive self-supervised learning for multivariate time series, and how are missing modalities handled during training?

#### Concept: Heterogeneous Modality Representations
- **Why needed here:** Metrics, logs, and traces differ in density and semantics. ARMOR’s asymmetric encoder explicitly acknowledges this; knowing why a single encoder would fail helps understand the design rationale.
- **Quick check question:** How would you design separate processing paths for continuous metric streams versus sparse discrete event logs? What architectural choices might you make for each?

#### Concept: Gated Fusion with Missing-Awareness
- **Why needed here:** The fusion mechanism adaptively reweights modalities based on availability. Understanding learnable placeholders and dynamic bias is key to appreciating how ARMOR avoids static imputation pitfalls.
- **Quick check question:** In a multimodal fusion network, how can you signal to the model that a particular modality is missing without resorting to fixed zero values? What might be the effect of a negative bias term in the gating logit?

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component map
Raw telemetry -> Preprocessing -> Modality-specific encoders -> Missing-aware gated fusion -> Graph attention network -> (Offline) Auto-regressive head; (Online) Unified representation -> AD/FT/RCL heads

#### Critical path
1. During inference, a sliding window of multimodal data enters the system.
2. Each modality passes through its dedicated encoder, producing H^M, H^L, H^T.
3. Tokens are projected to unified dimension, masked tokens replaced by E_miss, then fused via attention and gating to get E.
4. E flows through the GAT layers to produce topology-aware node embeddings Z.
5. MLP predicts next-step data; the difference (reconstruction error) is computed.
6. For each time step t, R(t) = [error, Z(t)] is fed to the three downstream task heads in sequence (AD → FT → RCL).

#### Design tradeoffs
- **Asymmetric encoder vs. symmetric:** Asymmetric design matches capacity to data sparsity, avoiding overfitting on sparse logs and underfitting on volatile metrics. Tradeoff: increased complexity (multiple encoder tracks) vs. better performance.
- **Missing-aware gating vs. static imputation:** Learnable placeholder and negative bias allow the model to treat missing data as a distinct case rather than noisy zeros. Tradeoff: added parameters and training instability (biases must be constrained negative) vs. robustness under modality dropout.
- **Joint self-supervised objective vs. per-task losses:** Single reconstruction loss naturally unifies tasks without manual weighting. Tradeoff: reconstruction may not perfectly align with diagnostic error surfaces; however, the paper shows it works well.

#### Failure signatures
- **High false positive rate in AD:** Could indicate reconstruction model is unable to predict normal dynamics (e.g., training data non-representative) or that the dynamic threshold is misconfigured.
- **Rapid performance drop when metrics are missing (while logs/traces present):** Would suggest the negative bias for metrics is not sufficiently penalizing placeholder contributions, or the encoder for metrics is over-reliant on complete data.
- **Ablation impact of gating:** Removing gating (A4 in Table 3) significantly degrades Avg@5 under missing modalities, confirming gating’s role.
- **Stagnant bias values:** If learned b_miss_k does not become negative during training, the mechanism fails to suppress missing modality contributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Partial modality missingness:** How does ARMOR perform under sub-channel outages (e.g., individual metric failures) rather than complete modality blackouts? The current evaluation masks entire modalities; finer-grained assessment is future work.
- **Contrastive learning replacement:** Can cross-instance contrastive learning replace the reconstruction objective when historical archives lack certain modalities entirely? The authors propose aligning incomplete instances against fully observed peers as a viable path.
- **Dynamic topology adaptation:** How can ARMOR adapt to evolving microservice topologies (e.g., scaling, churn) without periodic full retraining? The static graph built from historical records may become outdated; incremental graph learning is suggested.
- **Scalability and latency:** Does ARMOR scale efficiently to larger systems (hundreds of instances) and maintain low latency in real-world deployments? Evaluations used smaller testbeds; industrial-scale scalability remains unverified.

## Limitations
- **Critical unknowns:** Exact dataset sources and preprocessing pipelines for 𝒟1/𝒟2 are unspecified; essential hyperparameters (λ loss weight, GAT layers, POT tail index) are not reported, requiring extensive tuning.
- **Assumption risks:** Model robustness hinges on synthetic modality dropout accurately reflecting real infrastructure failure patterns; a mismatch could lead to overfitting to surviving modalities. The self-supervised objective may also fail if normal training data lacks adequate state-space coverage.
- **Evidence strength:** Core mechanism claims (e.g., dynamic negative bias suppressing noise) rely mainly on internal ablations; neighboring literature provides weak/absent direct validation for this specific design.

## Confidence
- **High:** Reported SOTA performance on complete-data benchmarks (AD F1 0.961–0.997) and robustness gains under simulated missing modalities (>60% RCL improvement over ART). These are direct empirical results.
- **Medium:** Effectiveness of key architectural components (asymmetric encoder, gated fusion, graph network). Confidence derives from in-paper ablations showing performance drops upon removal, but external verification is lacking.
- **Low:** Generalizability of the specific gated fusion mechanism (learnable placeholder + dynamic negative bias) to other contexts. The mechanism is novel and untested independently of ARMOR’s full pipeline.

## Next Checks
1. **Parameter inspection:** After training, verify all *b*<sub>miss,*k</sub> parameters are strictly negative and that placeholder *E*<sub>miss</sub> embeddings are distinct from normal token embeddings. Stagnant or positive biases indicate mechanism failure.
2. **Missing pattern alignment test:** Evaluate ARMOR under realistic missing patterns extracted from historical infrastructure outage logs (not just random dropout). A significant performance gap versus synthetic settings would invalidate the training augmentation assumption.
3. **Supervised fine-tuning comparison:** Fine-tune the frozen ARMOR encoder with a simple classifier head on a small, labeled failure subset for FT/RCL. Substantial improvement would question whether reconstruction loss alone yields optimal diagnostic representations.
