# SpeakerSleuth: Evaluating Large Audio-Language Models as Judges for Multi-turn Speaker Consistency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04029
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04029
- **Reference count:** 39
- **Primary result:** LALMs show severe limitations in multi-turn speaker consistency evaluation, with best detection accuracy at 60.1% and localization F1 below 25%, revealing a critical bias toward textual coherence over acoustic cues.

## Executive Summary
SpeakerSleuth is a benchmark designed to evaluate Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) on multi-turn speaker consistency through three tasks: Detection (binary judgment), Localization (identifying inconsistent turns), and Discrimination (selecting the best-matching audio). Across 1,818 human-verified instances from four datasets and nine LALMs, the study finds that LALMs fundamentally struggle with acoustic analysis. Their performance collapses when textual context is introduced, demonstrating a strong bias toward linguistic coherence. While they show some ability in relative acoustic discrimination (GPT-4o at 82.6% accuracy), they fail at absolute judgment and precise localization. Speaker embedding methods achieve near-perfect discrimination but exhibit their own biases, highlighting a modality imbalance in current LALMs that undermines reliable speaker consistency assessment.

## Method Summary
The study constructs a benchmark using four dialogue datasets (Bazinga, AMI, Behavior-SD, DailyTalk). For each target speaker segment with exactly five audio turns, it generates two inconsistent scenarios: one with a gender-switched turn (S2) and one with a similar-speaker swap (S3), using voice conversion (FreeVC). All scenarios undergo human verification for audio quality and naturalness. Nine LALMs are evaluated via zero-shot prompting with a reference audio sample and, in some conditions, text transcripts for other speakers. Tasks include detecting overall inconsistency, localizing the problematic turn(s), and discriminating which audio variant is most consistent with the reference. Metrics are accuracy for Detection/Discrimination and F1-score for Localization. Speaker embedding baselines (ECAPA-TDNN, WavLM) use fixed thresholds on pairwise/centroid/reference cosine similarities.

## Key Results
- **Detection:** Best LALM accuracy is 60.1% (audio-only), but accuracy for inconsistent scenarios (S2/S3) drops below 10% when textual context is added, indicating a severe textual bias.
- **Localization:** LALMs achieve F1 scores below 25% on inconsistent turns, failing to pinpoint problematic utterances even when they detect inconsistency, suggesting holistic rather than turn-level analysis.
- **Discrimination:** LALMs improve substantially (GPT-4o: 82.6%), showing inherent relative acoustic discrimination, but still lag behind speaker embedding methods (>93% accuracy), which suffer from high false positive rates on consistent speech.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LALMs prioritize textual coherence over acoustic cues when textual context is available, causing a dramatic performance drop in acoustic inconsistency detection.
- **Mechanism:** The multi-modal fusion architecture allocates more attention weight to text tokens than audio tokens during inference. When non-target speaker turns are provided as text, linguistic coherence overrides acoustic evidence that would signal speaker change.
- **Core assumption:** LALMs are trained on predominantly text-heavy data, leading to an inherent attention imbalance favoring the textual modality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...adding textual context causes a dramatic performance drop on acoustic inconsistency detection (S2/S3 accuracy <10% for most models), revealing a strong bias toward textual coherence over acoustic cues."
  - [section 7.1] "When other interlocutors’ turns are provided together, models overwhelmingly tend to prioritize textual coherence over acoustic features."
- **Break condition:** If textual context is removed or attention mechanisms are retrained to balance modalities, the bias should diminish, improving acoustic detection in context-rich settings.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LALMs lack stable internal thresholds for absolute speaker consistency judgment, leading to inconsistent detection (some models are overly strict, others overly lenient).
- **Mechanism:** LALMs are not explicitly trained to calibrate decision boundaries for acoustic similarity; their probabilistic outputs vary unpredictably across speakers and scenarios. Without a fixed threshold, binary judgments become unstable.
- **Core assumption:** LALMs are optimized for generative tasks, not calibrated classification, so their internal representations do not map cleanly onto binary consistency decisions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Given audio samples of the same speaker's turns, some models overpredict inconsistency, whereas others are overly lenient."
  - [section 6.1] "LALMs exhibit two critical limitations. First, they lack balanced internal thresholds... MiniCPM-o impartially accept consistent speakers... while Gemini-2.5-Flash overly reject consistent speakers."
- **Break condition:** If models are fine-tuned with consistency-specific supervision and explicit threshold learning, detection accuracy should stabilize and approach embedding-method performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LALMs rely on holistic dialogue-level impressions rather than fine-grained turn-by-turn acoustic analysis, causing poor localization even when detection succeeds.
- **Mechanism:** The models aggregate audio representations over the entire sequence without mechanisms to isolate and compare individual turns acoustically. This prevents identifying the exact turn with speaker drift.
- **Core assumption:** LALMs process inputs as a continuous stream, lacking explicit turn-level acoustic feature extraction or attention heads dedicated to per-turn consistency.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Models further struggle to identify the exact turns that are problematic."
  - [section 6.1] "Localization results reveal that models cannot identify which specific turn contains speaker inconsistency, even when they detect that inconsistency exists..."
- **Break condition:** If models are prompted to isolate each turn or if architecture includes explicit turn-level acoustic feature pooling, localization F1 should rise significantly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Speaker consistency in speech synthesis
  - *Why needed here:* The paper evaluates whether LALMs can judge if multiple utterances from the same speaker maintain acoustic identity (timbre, pitch, voice quality) across a dialogue.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between speaker consistency and traditional speaker identification/diarization?
- **Concept:** Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs)
  - *Why needed here:* LALMs are the judges being evaluated; understanding their multi-modal architecture and training is key to interpreting results.
  - *Quick check question:* How do LALMs typically handle audio versus text inputs, and why might this cause modality imbalance?
- **Concept:** Speaker embedding methods (e.g., ECAPA-TDNN, WavLM)
  - *Why needed here:* These are baseline methods that compute acoustic similarity; comparing LALMs to them highlights LALMs' limitations.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between pairwise similarity, centroid distance, and reference comparison in speaker verification?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Datasets (Bazinga, AMI, Behavior-SD, DailyTalk) -> Dialogue Segment Extraction (5 target speaker turns) -> Scenario Generation (FreeVC for S2/S3) -> Human Verification -> LALM/Embedding Evaluation (Detection, Localization, Discrimination) -> Metric Computation (Accuracy, F1, Exact Match)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Data collection from four sources.
  2. Dialogue segment extraction (5 target speaker turns, max 20 total turns).
  3. Scenario generation via voice conversion (FreeVC) for S2/S3.
  4. Human verification (audio quality, naturalness, scenario validity).
  5. Model inference for each task under different conditions (audio-only, with text context).
  6. Metric computation and analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Voice conversion vs. natural speaker variations:** Using FreeVC creates controlled acoustic changes but may not fully capture real-world speaker drift; human verification mitigates artifacts.
  - **Dataset diversity vs. controllability:** Including real (AMI, Bazinga) and synthetic (Behavior-SD) speech tests robustness but introduces acoustic variability that could confound results.
  - **Audio-only vs. with textual context:** Audio-only isolates acoustic ability; adding text reveals modality bias but complicates interpretation.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - In Detection: Large accuracy gaps between S1 (consistent) and S2/S3 (inconsistent), especially when context is added.
  - In Localization: F1 scores near random (≤25%) despite decent detection, indicating no turn-level precision.
  - In Discrimination: High accuracy for LALMs (>60%) but much lower than embeddings (>93%), showing relative judgment works but absolute does not.
  - Modality bias: S2/S3 accuracy collapses (<10%) when text context is present, while S1 improves.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline detection evaluation:** Run all LALMs on audio-only input with reference audio for all three scenarios. Record accuracy per scenario; expect low S2/S3 accuracy and model-specific threshold biases.
  2. **Textual context impact:** Repeat detection with full dialogue (other speakers as text). Compare to audio-only; expect S2/S3 to drop dramatically while S1 rises, confirming textual prioritization.
  3. **Embedding method comparison:** Implement ECAPA-TDNN Pairwise with threshold sweep. Benchmark against LALMs; expect high detection but possible false positives in S1, and perfect discrimination, highlighting LALMs’ relative judgment capability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do LALMs generalize to multilingual dialogues for speaker consistency evaluation?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "First, our study focuses on English dialogues where target speakers appear a fixed number of times per conversation. Consequently, it may not capture the complexities of multilingual settings." (Limitations section)
  - *Why unresolved:* SpeakerSleuth contains only English dialogues, so models' performance on other languages is unknown, which is critical for global deployment of speech systems.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Extend SpeakerSleuth to include dialogues in multiple languages with parallel acoustic scenarios, and evaluate state-of-the-art multilingual LALMs to measure cross-lingual transfer.
- **Open Question 2:** How does the number of turns per speaker impact detection and localization accuracy in multi-turn conversations?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "our synthetic data generation methodology can be readily extended to varying turn counts and longer dialogues." (Limitations section)
  - *Why unresolved:* All instances have exactly 5 target speaker turns; real conversations vary widely in turn count, and scalability is unexplored.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Generate additional benchmark instances with 3, 5, 10, and 15 target speaker turns using the same pipeline, then analyze performance trends across turn counts.
- **Open Question 3:** Which specific acoustic factors (e.g., background noise, reverberation) most degrade LALMs' speaker consistency judgment?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "we do not separately analyze performance by specific acoustic factors such as background noise, reverberation, or recording quality." (Limitations section)
  - *Why unresolved:* Performance across datasets with naturally varying acoustics is reported, but isolating individual factors would reveal model robustness to real-world conditions.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply controlled acoustic perturbations (e.g., noise at varied SNR, room impulse responses) to a held-out set of SpeakerSleuth instances and measure performance degradation per factor.
- **Open Question 4:** Can architectural modifications to LALMs reduce their bias toward textual context and improve acoustic sensitivity?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "Developing methods to better leverage dialogue context while maintaining acoustic sensitivity would be a promising future direction." (Section 7.1)
  - *Why unresolved:* Models collapse on acoustic detection when textual context is present, indicating an imbalance in multimodal fusion that undermines reliability.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement and evaluate altered attention mechanisms (e.g., audio-token upweighting) or training objectives that enforce acoustic grounding, then test on SpeakerSleuth with and without text context.
- **Open Question 5:** Do LALMs exhibit demographic biases (e.g., based on speaker age, accent, gender) in speaker consistency evaluation?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] "we did not explicitly analyze model biases regarding speaker demographics... which warrants further investigation to ensure equitable performance across diverse populations." (Limitations section)
  - *Why unresolved:* The benchmark includes 152 speakers but lacks demographic annotations; biased performance could systematically disadvantage certain groups.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Annotate speaker demographics in SpeakerSleuth, then stratify detection/localization metrics by demographic subgroups to identify and quantify disparities.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark design constraints:** Voice conversion (FreeVC) may introduce artifacts not present in natural speaker drift; human verification mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **Evaluation scope:** Only 9 models tested; newer LALMs (e.g., post-2024) might show different modality biases.
- **Embedding baseline gaps:** Fixed thresholds (τ=0.4/0.3) may not generalize; optimal thresholds per dataset/model are unexplored.
- **Parsing fragility:** Localization relies on exact turn-number extraction; minor output format deviations could skew F1 scores.

## Confidence
- **Textual bias (when context added):** High – Supported by consistent >50% accuracy drops in S2/S3 across models.
- **Threshold instability (absolute judgment):** High – Clear model-specific bias patterns (e.g., Gemini over-rejecting) are reproducibly shown.
- **Localization failure (turn-level analysis):** High – F1 scores ≤25% despite detectable inconsistencies confirm holistic processing.
- **Relativistic vs. absolute judgment:** Medium – Discrimination accuracy (82.6% max) lags behind embeddings (>93%), but evidence is indirect—no explicit test of absolute vs. relative calibration.
- **Generalization to real-world acoustics:** Low – The controlled voice-conversion setup may not reflect natural speaker drift or acoustic noise.

## Next Checks
1. **Attention probing:** Run attention-head analysis on LALMs during audio+text detection to quantify audio-token vs. text-token attention weights; test if high text attention correlates with S2/S3 failures.
2. **Threshold learning:** Fine-tune LALMs on a speaker-consistency dataset with explicit positive/negative pairs and a calibration loss; evaluate if detection F1 stabilizes near embedding baselines.
3. **Turn-isolation ablation:** Modify prompts to force per-turn analysis (e.g., “For each turn individually, is it consistent with the reference?”) and measure localization F1 improvement versus holistic prompting.
