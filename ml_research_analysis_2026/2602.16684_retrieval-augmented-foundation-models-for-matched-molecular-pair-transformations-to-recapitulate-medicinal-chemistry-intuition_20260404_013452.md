# Retrieval-Augmented Foundation Models for Matched Molecular Pair Transformations to Recapitulate Medicinal Chemistry Intuition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16684
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16684
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Retrieval augmentation improves recall of ground-truth transformations (82.1% vs 67.6%), maintains high validity (~99%), and boosts novelty (30.1% novel/valid).

## Executive Summary
Existing machine learning methods for analog generation lack precise, localized edit control and struggle to learn medicinal chemistry transformations at scale. This work introduces a variable-to-variable framework where a foundation model (MMPT-FM) is trained on millions of matched molecular pair transformations to generate plausible variable substitutions. User-specifiable structural prompts and a retrieval-augmented component (MMPT-RAG) further steer generation using project-specific reference analogs. Across three tasks—general MMPT recovery, within-patent expansion, and cross-patent prediction—MMPT-RAG consistently improves recall, maintains high validity, and significantly increases novelty without retraining.

## Method Summary
The method treats analog generation as a variable-to-variable transformation problem, where molecules are decomposed into constant scaffolds and variable fragments based on matched molecular pair transformations (MMPTs). A foundation model (MMPT-FM), fine-tuned from ChemT5 on millions of MMPTs, learns the conditional distribution p(v_B | v_A) to generate candidate variable substitutions. Users can impose structural preferences by providing SMARTS-encoded masked templates, which the model fills via constrained infilling. For project-specific adaptation, the retrieval-augmented MMPT-RAG system retrieves similar v_A examples from a reference set using Morgan fingerprints and HNSW indexing, expands to their paired v_B, clusters these v_B based on similarity, and extracts maximum common substructures (MCS) from each cluster to form templates. The foundation model then generates under these templates, with cluster weights implementing a Bayesian interpolation between the model's prior and the reference distribution. This design allows steering without retraining, combining prior knowledge with project-specific patterns.

## Key Results
- Recall of ground-truth transformations substantially improved (e.g., 82.1% vs. 67.6% for MMPT-FM on ChEMBL).
- Chemical validity remained high (~99% valid structures).
- Novelty increased notably (e.g., 30.1% of outputs were novel and valid).
- Gains consistent across general, within-patent, and cross-patent prediction tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Variable-to-variable formulation enables localized edit control.**  
The model learns *p*(*v*<sub>B</sub> | *v*<sub>A</sub>), where *v*<sub>A</sub> and *v*<sub>B</sub> are variable fragments from MMPTs. This isolates the edit from a constant scaffold, allowing users to specify which part to modify. The core assumption is that medicinal chemistry edits can be decomposed into context-independent substitutions learnable at scale. Evidence includes the definition of MMPTs and the success of variable-centric models in the literature. This fails if scaffolds cannot be reliably identified or variables are not chemically meaningful.

**Mechanism 2: Masked template prompting injects structural preferences without retraining.**  
A user-specified constraint *S* is converted to a masked SMARTS template *T*. The model performs constrained infilling over *T* conditioned on *v*<sub>A</sub>, ranking candidates by likelihood. This assumes chemists can express intents as partial SMARTS templates and the model respects them while exploring valid completions. Evidence includes abstract statements, formal task formulation, and results showing high validity/recall under strict masking. Overly rigid or chemically conflicting constraints reduce diversity or validity.

**Mechanism 3: Retrieval augmentation steers generation toward project-specific patterns.**  
For a query *v*<sub>A</sub>, similar *v*<sub>A</sub><sup>'</sup> are retrieved; their paired *v*<sub>B</sub><sup>'</sup> are clustered, and MCS per cluster forms templates *T*<sub>k</sub>. Generation under each *T*<sub>k</sub> is budgeted by cluster weight, implementing a convex interpolation *p*<sub>RAG</sub> = (1−*ᾱ*)*p*<sub>θ</sub> + *ᾱp*<sub>ref</sub><sup>*</sup> (Theorem 4.1). This assumes retrieved patterns are relevant and MCS clusters represent reusable edits. Evidence includes UMAP visualizations showing RAG outputs filling structural voids and consistent recall gains. Irrelevant/noisy reference data or failed clustering yields trivial templates and poor generations.

## Foundational Learning

- **Matched Molecular Pair Transformations (MMPTs):** Atomic operational units in analog design where molecules share a constant scaffold and differ by one variable fragment. *Why needed:* The framework treats analog generation as variable-to-variable edits, requiring decomposition of molecules into invariant context and variable parts. *Quick check:* Given an MMP pair, can you identify the shared scaffold and the two variable fragments? Do the variables represent standalone, chemically meaningful edits?

- **SMARTS as molecular substructure language:** A symbolic notation for representing molecular patterns with explicit attachment points (e.g., `[*:1]C`). *Why needed:* Variables and templates are encoded as SMARTS to enable precise, unambiguous chemical edits and prompting. *Quick check:* How would you represent a methyl group attached via a single bond versus a double bond? Why are atom mappings (`:1`) critical?

- **Distribution steering via Bayesian interpolation:** Theoretically, RAG blends the foundation model’s prior *p*<sub>θ</sub> with a reference distribution *p*<sub>ref</sub><sup>*</sup> derived from retrieved clusters, controlled by *ᾱ*. *Why needed:* This explains how RAG shifts generation toward project-specific patterns without retraining. *Quick check:* If *ᾱ* → 0, what distribution governs outputs? If retrieved cluster templates perfectly match the query, how do *α*<sub>k</sub> values behave?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (*v*<sub>A</sub>) → MMPT-FM (generates candidates) ↔ Retriever (HNSW index on Morgan fingerprints) → Label Expander (fetches paired *v*<sub>B</sub><sup>'</sup>) → Clusterer (agglomerative clustering on Morgan embeddings) → Template Extractor (MCS → SMARTS, masking) → Prompted Generator (tree-search infilling) → Output Aggregator (union of valid, unique candidates).

**Critical path:** Retrieval → Label Expansion → Clustering → MCS → Prompted Generation → Output. The most fragile link is Clustering → MCS: if retrieved *v*<sub>B</sub><sup>'</sup> are too diverse, MCS may be trivial (e.g., single atom), yielding uninformative prompts.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Beam size in MMPT-FM*: Larger beams (e.g., 1200) increase candidate pool but yield diminishing validity returns; 400–800 balances validity >99.9%.
- *Number of clusters expanded*: More clusters improve coverage but risk low-quality clusters; default 10 balances gains; >20 yields minimal improvement.
- *Mask length range in templates*: Wider ranges (±7 tokens) increase novelty under prompt but may raise invalid rates if model struggles with long spans.
- *Retrieval K*: Larger K (e.g., 500) increases relevant pattern chance but adds noise, requiring aggressive clustering/filtering.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Low validity (<90%) in RAG outputs: overly broad masking (small MCS) or infilling parameters too permissive.
2. RAG outputs identical to MMPT-FM baseline: irrelevant retrieval or failed clustering (templates not distinct).
3. High duplicate rate across clusters: overlapping clusters or similar MCS templates; reduce clustering threshold.
4. No recall improvement on out-of-training data: reference set lacks novel patterns or *ᾱ* too low; increase budget per cluster or loosen retrieval similarity.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Reproduce MMPT-FM baseline on Task 1 (ChEMBL): expect ~67.6% recall, ~99% validity.
2. Apply RAG with defaults on Task 2 (PMV17): expect recall lift from ~41% to ~49%. If lift is small, diagnose retrieval similarity.
3. Hyperparameter sensitivity on number of clusters (vary {3,5,10,20}): plot recall vs. cluster count to find saturation; if performance drops at high clusters, investigate cluster quality (size, MCS meaningfulness).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Performance in data-scarce domains:** How does the framework behave when historical transformation data is sparse, and can RAG generalize without relevant reference examples? All evaluations used well-covered datasets; no systematic data-scarcity experiments.
2. **Impact of clustering and template extraction:** To what extent do choices of clustering algorithm (HDBSCAN) and template method (MCS) affect diversity, relevance, and validity? No comparisons to alternatives (e.g., k-means, frequent subgraph mining).
3. **Disparity in cross-patent vs. within-patent gains:** Why is RAG’s relative improvement smaller in Task 3 (cross-patent) than Task 2 (within-patent)? No analysis of temporal distribution shift effects on retrieval or gating (*α*<sub>k</sub>).
4. **Extension to multi-site or property constraints:** Can prompting handle multiple simultaneous edits or non-structural constraints (e.g., "add polar group while maintaining logP")? Current prompting is limited to one masked template; trade-offs in quality and complexity unexplored.

## Limitations
- Variable decomposition may fail for symmetric molecules or non-separable fragments.
- SMARTS templating requires expert knowledge, limiting accessibility.
- RAG heavily depends on reference data quality; poor clustering yields trivial templates.
- No comparisons to competing analog generation methods (e.g., SynTwins) weaken superiority claims.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity not fully explored; optimal values may be task-specific.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Variable-to-variable framework):** Medium. Baseline works but no head-to-head with alternatives.
- **Mechanism 2 (Masked template prompting):** High. Table 3 validates constrained generation.
- **Mechanism 3 (Retrieval steering):** High. Theorem 4.1 and UMAP show steering; gains consistent across tasks.
- **Overall outcome (recall boost, high validity, novelty):** High. Multi-task improvements robust.

## Next Checks
