# Context-specific Credibility-aware Multimodal Fusion with Conditional Probabilistic Circuits
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26629
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26629
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: C²MF improves accuracy by up to 29% over static PC baselines under high conflict and achieves higher RMIS, confirming better reliable modality identification.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal fusion systems typically assume static reliability across modalities, which fails when contextual factors render a modality unreliable in specific instances. The proposed Context-specific Credibility-aware Multimodal Fusion (C²MF) framework addresses this by dynamically estimating per‑instance modality credibility. C²MF employs unimodal encoders, a context aggregator, and a Conditional Probabilistic Circuit (CPC) whose sum‑node weights are learned via a hypernetwork conditioned on the joint context. The resulting context‑dependent fusion enables accurate adaptation to situational corruptions. Additionally, C²MF defines Context‑Specific Information Credibility (CSIC) as an exact KL‑divergence between full and marginal posteriors conditioned on context, providing an interpretable measure of each modality’s influence. Evaluated on a new Conflict benchmark (with class‑specific corruptions) and using the Reliable Modality Identification Score (RMIS), C²MF achieves up to 29% higher accuracy than static PC baselines under high conflict and superior RMIS, while retaining exact inference for transparency.

## Method Summary
C²MF comprises unimodal encoders \(E_m\) mapping raw inputs \(x_m\) to embeddings \(h_m\), and unimodal classifiers \(f_m\) producing predictive distributions \(p_m\). A context aggregator \(M\) combines all embeddings into a joint context \(z\). A hypernetwork \(g_\phi\) then maps \(z\) to the sum‑node weights \(\Theta\) of a Conditional Probabilistic Circuit (CPC). The CPC takes the unimodal distributions \(p_m\) and parameters \(\Theta\) to produce the fused posterior \(P(Y|p_1,\dots,p_M; \Theta)\). Training uses a total loss \(L_{\text{total}} = L_f + \mu L_u\), where \(L_f\) is the multimodal cross‑entropy and \(L_u\) sums unimodal cross‑entropies. During inference, the CPC’s bottom‑up pass yields the fused prediction, while CSIC is computed by exact KL‑divergence between the full posterior and marginal posteriors obtained by internal marginalization of one modality’s distribution. This design allows context‑aware weighting without sacrificing tractability.

## Key Results
- On AV‑MNIST and NYUD, C²MF improves classification accuracy by up to 29% over static probabilistic circuit baselines under high conflict (λ_test=0.75).
- C²MF attains higher Reliable Modality Identification Score (RMIS), demonstrating its ability to correctly down‑weight corrupted modalities.
- The framework provides exact probabilistic inference, enabling the computation of CSIC without approximation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** C²MF achieves dynamic per‑instance credibility by conditioning the weights of a Conditional Probabilistic Circuit (CPC) on a learned joint context, enabling the fusion function to adapt to situational corruptions.
- **Mechanism:** Unimodal encoders produce embeddings \(h_m\). A context aggregator \(M\) combines these into a joint embedding \(z\). A hypernetwork \(g_\phi\) maps \(z\) to the CPC’s sum‑node weights \(\Theta\). During inference, unimodal predictive distributions \(p_m\) are propagated through the CPC with weights \(\Theta\) to compute the fused posterior \(P(Y|p_1,\dots,p_M; \Theta)\). If \(z\) indicates corruption in a modality, \(g_\phi\) generates weights that down‑weight its corresponding branch.
- **Core assumption:** The joint embedding \(z\) contains sufficient information about which modalities are unreliable in the current instance, and the hypernetwork can reliably map \(z\) to valid CPC parameters that preserve tractability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "CPC weights are conditioned on the context via a hypernetwork, enabling dynamic per-instance credibility estimates."
  - [section III.C] "We map this context to the internal parameters of a Conditional Probabilistic Circuit (CPC) through a hyper‑network... allowing the fusion logic to adapt dynamically."
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence limited; this is a novel extension of CPCs to context‑specific fusion. Prior work [7] uses static PCs.
- **Break condition:** If the context aggregator fails to extract discriminative cues about modality reliability (e.g., if unimodal embeddings lack corruption signals), conditioning becomes ineffective and performance degrades toward static baselines. Additionally, if the hypernetwork produces weights that violate CPC structural constraints (e.g., negative sum weights), tractability may be lost.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Context‑Specific Information Credibility (CSIC) provides an exact, instance‑level, interpretable measure of each modality’s contribution to the fused prediction under the current context.
- **Mechanism:** For a given instance with context \(z\), CSIC for modality \(i\) is defined as \(D_{KL}(P(Y|X,z) \| P(Y|X\backslash x_i, z))\). Both posteriors are computed by exact inference on the CPC: the first using all unimodal inputs, the second with modality \(i\) marginalized out. The relative CSIC normalizes these values across modalities to sum to 1.
- **Core assumption:** The KL divergence meaningfully quantifies a modality’s influence; that marginalizing out \(p_i\) approximates removing its information; and that the CPC’s joint distribution accurately captures true dependencies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Context‑Specific Information Credibility (CSIC), computed exactly as KL‑divergence between full and marginal posteriors conditioned on context, quantifies each modality's influence."
  - [section III.D] Formal definition and explanation that it generalizes the static credibility measure of [7].
  - [corpus] The static credibility measure from [7] is a special case; CSIC extends it to conditional settings. Corpus does not contain this exact definition.
- **Break condition:** If the CPC’s learned distribution is mis‑specified (due to limited capacity or training issues), the computed posteriors may be biased, leading to misleading CSIC values. Moreover, when modalities are highly correlated, marginalizing one may not change the posterior much even if it is reliable, resulting in low CSIC despite high actual utility.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Conflict benchmark and Reliable Modality Identification Score (RMIS) enable systematic evaluation of a fusion model’s ability to resolve context‑specific conflicts and identify the reliable modality.
- **Mechanism:** Conflict benchmark: For each modality, a subset of classes is designated for corruption. During training/testing, a fraction \(\lambda\) of samples from those classes has the modality replaced with data from other classes, inducing controlled cross‑odal disagreement. RMIS: For a test instance where modality \(m_{\text{corrupt}}\) is corrupted and \(m_{\text{clean}}\) is intact, RMIS=1 if the model assigns lower credibility to \(m_{\text{corrupt}}\) than to \(m_{\text{clean}}\) (using CSIC or static \(C_i\)); else 0. The mean RMIS over all corrupted instances measures reliable modality identification.
- **Core assumption:** Class‑specific corruptions create a context where the corrupted modality is systematically less reliable, and that the model can learn to associate the context (e.g., image class 5) with audio corruption. Also, that lower credibility correctly indicates unreliability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "To evaluate, we propose the Conflict benchmark with class‑specific corruptions and the Reliable Modality Identification Score (RMIS)."
  - [section IV.A] Detailed protocol for constructing Conflict‑AV‑MNIST and Conflict‑NYUD.
  - [section IV.C] Formal RMIS definition.
  - [corpus] This is a novel benchmark; corpus contains no direct prior work with the same evaluation protocol.
- **Break condition:** If the corruption strategy does not reflect real‑world context‑dependent failures (e.g., real corruptions are not class‑aligned), high RMIS may not transfer to practical scenarios. Additionally, if the model learns superficial shortcuts (e.g., relying on the context aggregator alone without assessing modality reliability), it might achieve high RMIS on the benchmark but fail on new corruption patterns.

## Foundational Learning
### Probabilistic Circuits (PCs)
**Why needed:** C²MF uses a CPC, a conditional variant of PCs, as the fusion function. PCs provide tractable exact inference for complex probabilistic queries, enabling both fused predictions and credibility measures without approximation.  
**Quick check:** What structural constraints (e.g., smoothness, decomposability) on sum‑product networks ensure that marginal inference can be computed in linear time relative to the circuit size?

### Conditional Probability and Context Conditioning
**Why needed:** The core innovation is modeling reliability as conditional on context. The CPC represents \(P(Y|p_1,\dots,p_M, z)\), where \(z\) is the context. This requires understanding how conditioning changes dependence structures and how to learn conditional distributions.  
**Quick check:** How does \(P(Y|X, z)\) differ from \(P(Y|X)\) in terms of the roles of \(X\) and \(z\)? When can conditioning on \(z\) make otherwise independent variables dependent?

### Kullback‑Leibler (KL) Divergence
**Why needed:** CSIC is defined as a KL divergence between two posteriors. Interpreting CSIC scores—whether a modality is highly influential—requires understanding KL as an asymmetric measure of distributional difference and its information‑theoretic interpretation.  
**Quick check:** What does a KL divergence of zero between two distributions indicate about their relationship? Why is KL divergence not symmetric?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
\(E_m: x_m \to h_m\); \(f_m: h_m \to p_m\); \(M: \{h_m\} \to z\); \(g_\phi: z \to \Theta\); CPC: \((p_1,\dots,p_M,\Theta) \to P(Y|\cdot)\). Training loss: \(L_{\text{total}} = L_f + \mu L_u\).

### Critical Path (Inference)
1. Input modalities \(x_m\) → encoders \(E_m\) → embeddings \(h_m\).
2. Embeddings → context aggregator \(M\) → context \(z\).
3. Context \(z\) → hypernetwork \(g_\phi\) → CPC weights \(\Theta\).
4. Unimodal predictions \(p_m\) from \(f_m\) fed into CPC with weights \(\Theta\).
5. Bottom‑up pass through CPC yields fused posterior \(P(Y|\cdot)\); prediction is \(\arg\max_Y\).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Static vs. dynamic fusion:** Static PC (DPC/CWM) is simpler and more robust to missing modalities but cannot adapt to context. C²MF adds the context pathway, increasing model capacity and risk of overfitting, especially with limited conflict data.
- **Decoupled vs. joint training:** Decoupled training (freeze unimodals after pre‑training) is stable but may limit context learning. Joint training (end‑to‑end) allows gradients to flow into encoders, potentially improving context representation but requiring careful hyperparameter tuning (e.g., \(\mu\)).
- **Context representation:** \(M\) is a simple MLP; a more powerful aggregator (e.g., attention) might better capture corruption cues but could overfit. Simpler \(M\) may fail if corruption signals are subtle.
- **Hypernetwork design:** Must output valid CPC weights (e.g., non‑negative for sum nodes, normalized). A small hypernetwork may limit expressivity; a large one may cause training instability.

### Failure Signatures
- **RMIS near 0.5** under high conflict: model not learning to distinguish reliable from corrupted modalities.
- **CSIC scores uniform** across modalities: context \(z\) is not influencing CPC weights; conditioning ineffective.
- **Training instability:** Gradients vanishing/exploding through CPC and hypernetwork; check weight initialization and learning rates.
- **Loss of tractability:** If hypernetwork outputs invalid weights (e.g., negative), CPC may no longer represent a valid distribution; monitor sum weights and normalization.
- **Performance drop when \(\lambda_{\text{test}} > \lambda_{\text{train}}\):** poor generalization to unseen corruption levels; indicates overfitting to training corruption pattern.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline replication:** Train static DPC and CWM on clean Conflict‑AV‑MNIST (\(\lambda=0\)) and verify they match published accuracies (~99%). This validates unimodal encoders, predictors, and static PC implementation.
2. **Ablation of context:** Replace context aggregator \(M\) with a constant zero vector (or random) so that \(z\) carries no information. Then C²MF should statistically reduce to static PC. Compare performance on Conflict‑AV‑MNIST with \(\lambda_{\text{test}}=0.75\); if C²MF does not outperform static baselines, the context pathway is not functioning.
3. **CSIC sanity check:** Create a tiny synthetic dataset (e.g., two modalities, deterministic corruption: modality 1 always random noise for class 0, clean otherwise). Train C²MF and compute CSIC on test instances. Expect CSIC for corrupted modality near 0 and for clean modality >0. Also verify that \(\sum_m \text{CSIC}_m \approx 1\). This confirms the credibility measure behaves as intended.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to real‑world sensor failures:** How does C²MF generalize to large‑scale real‑world multimodal datasets with naturally occurring sensor failures, as opposed to synthetic class‑aligned corruptions?  
   *Basis:* The paper states that evaluating on large‑scale real‑world datasets is an important next step.  
   *Why unresolved:* Only evaluated on synthetic Conflict benchmark; real‑world corruptions may follow different patterns.

2. **Handling missing modalities:** Can C²MF be extended to handle missing modalities during inference?  
   *Basis:* Future work suggests replacing the neural context aggregator with a PC.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current context aggregator requires all modalities; sensor failures may cause missing inputs.

3. **Combining pretrained foundation models:** Does the CSIC‑based fusion approach effectively combine outputs from multiple pretrained foundation models?  
   *Basis:* The paper notes C²MF can combine pretrained models.  
   *Why unresolved:* Unimodal encoders are task‑specific; performance with diverse foundation models unknown.

4. **Integrating domain knowledge:** How can domain knowledge about the relationship between context and modality reliability be formally integrated into the C²MF framework?  
   *Basis:* Integrating domain knowledge is identified as important.  
   *Why unresolved:* Hypernetwork learns mapping entirely from data; prior knowledge not incorporated.

## Limitations
- **Architectural details missing:** Exact CPC structure (nodes, decomposability), hypernetwork architecture, and training hyperparameters (learning rates, \(\mu\)) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Hypernetwork validity risk:** The hypernetwork must output CPC sum‑weights that preserve tractability (non‑negative, normalized); invalid weights could break the probabilistic model.
- **CSIC dependence on CPC correctness:** CSIC relies on exact inference on the learned CPC; mis‑specification of dependencies could lead to misleading credibility scores.
