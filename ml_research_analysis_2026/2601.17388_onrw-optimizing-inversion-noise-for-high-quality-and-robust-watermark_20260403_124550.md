# ONRW: Optimizing inversion noise for high-quality and robust watermark

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.17388
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17388
*   **Reference count:** 24
*   **Primary result:** ONRW achieves state-of-the-art image quality (PSNR 27.11, SSIM 0.88) and robustness, outperforming the Stable Signature baseline by an average of 10% in bit accuracy across 12 image transformations, with >0.95 average bit accuracy against geometric and photometric attacks.

## Executive Summary
ONRW is a framework for embedding robust, imperceptible watermarks into images using pre-trained diffusion models, without additional training. It addresses the trade-off between robustness and image quality by first inverting a clean image into a controllable latent noise representation via null-text optimization. Watermark information is encoded by optimizing this noise. A key innovation is using the model's iterative denoising process as a "purification" mechanism that filters out optimization artifacts while preserving the coherent watermark signal. To further protect semantic quality, watermark embedding is constrained to foreground objects using a pseudo-mask derived from the model's cross-attention maps. Evaluations on COCO and ImageNet show superior performance in both image fidelity and resilience against a wide range of attacks compared to prior diffusion-based watermarking methods.

## Method Summary
Given a clean image `x₀` and a watermark bitstring `k`, ONRW operates in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2 model. First, it employs **null-text inversion** to map `x₀` to a deterministic latent noise `x′ₜ` at a chosen timestep `t`. This inversion noise is then optimized using AdamW for `T` steps to minimize a composite loss: `L = αL_decode + βL_self-attention + γL_mse`. `L_decode` uses a pre-trained HiDDeN decoder on a version of the reconstructed image that has been subjected to a random **simulated attack layer** (e.g., crop, JPEG). `L_self-attention` penalizes deviations in the U-Net's self-attention maps between the optimized and a fixed reference inversion, preserving structure. `L_mse` enforces fidelity to the original image `x₀`. A **pseudo-mask `M`**, generated by aggregating and upsampling the text-image cross-attention maps from the inversion/denoising process, is used to blend the optimized reconstruction `x′₀` back toward `x₀`, restricting modifications primarily to the foreground. Finally, the optimized noise `x′ₜ` is denoised to produce the watermarked image `x′₀`. No model weights are updated during this process.

## Key Results
*   On COCO and ImageNet, ONRW achieves PSNR 27.11 and SSIM 0.88, significantly improving image quality over the prior diffusion-based watermarking baseline (Stable Signature).
*   It demonstrates superior robustness, outperforming the baseline by an average of **10% in bit accuracy** across 12 common image transformations (geometric and photometric).
*   The method shows high resilience, maintaining an **average bit accuracy >0.95** against these attacks and improved robustness under neural autoencoder compression and diffusion-based reconstruction attacks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Null-Text Inversion as a Stable Latent Substrate
The framework converts a clean image into inversion noise via null-text optimization. This process inverts the forward diffusion trajectory by refining the unconditional text embedding to minimize reconstruction error, yielding a deterministic and differentiable latent noise `x′ₜ`. This optimized noise serves as a stable substrate for subsequent watermark embedding while still being able to reconstruct the original image through the denoising process. The core assumption is that the refined embedding accurately reverses the diffusion process, placing `x′ₜ` near the data manifold. If `x′ₜ` deviates too far, semantic distortions appear, failing the imperceptibility goal.

### Mechanism 2: Diffusion Denoising as Purification
The iterative denoising process of the diffusion model acts as a powerful purification mechanism. After embedding the watermark by perturbing `x′ₜ`, the standard reverse diffusion steps map the modified latent back to the image space. The model's learned generative prior inherently filters out high-frequency, inconsistent perturbations introduced during optimization (which may look like noise) while preserving the coherent watermark signal that is aligned with the natural data distribution. This non-linear filtering enhances robustness against common corruptions without additional training.

### Mechanism 3: Foreground-Constrained Embedding via Pseudo-Masks
Watermark embedding is restricted to foreground objects using a soft pseudo-mask `M` derived from aggregated cross-attention maps between image latents and a text caption `C`. The mask is created by normalizing and upsampling these maps. The final watermarked latent `x′₀(masked)` is a blend of the optimized and original latents using `M`. This strategy protects background semantics (improving imperceptibility) and places the watermark in semantically salient regions that are more likely to be preserved under transformations, enhancing robustness. The assumption is that cross-attention reliably indicates the primary subject(s) described by the caption.

## Architecture Onboarding
Pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2 (Latent Diffusion) -> Null-Text Inversion Module (gets x′ₜ) -> Optimization Loop (AdamW updates x′ₜ using L = αL_decode + βL_self-attention + γL_mse) -> Diffusion Denoising (produces x′₀) -> Pseudo-Mask Generator (from cross-attention) -> Apply Mask (x′₀(masked)) -> Simulated Attack Layer (W) -> Watermark Decoder (HiDDeN D) -> Bit Accuracy.

*   **Critical path:** Image (`x₀`) -> Null-Text Inversion -> Optimize `x′ₜ` (with `L_self-attention`, mask, and simulated attacks) -> Diffusion Denoising -> `x′₀(masked)` -> Simulated Attack (`W`) -> Decoder (`D`) -> Bit Accuracy.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Imperceptibility vs. Robustness:** Controlled by α (robustness) vs. γ (MSE quality) weights. Higher α boosts accuracy under attack but may increase visible artifacts.
    *   **Semantic Fidelity vs. Robustness:** β (self-attention loss) preserves structure but may constrain embedding strength.
    *   **Quality vs. Time:** More denoising/inversion iterations (e.g., 120) improve results but increase inference time (~1 min/image).
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Low Bit Accuracy (<0.8) under mild attacks:** Likely insufficient α, poor pseudo-mask, or unstable inversion.
    *   **Low PSNR/SSIM (<25/0.8):** γ too low, β too high, or mask too aggressive.
    *   **High LPIPS:** Indicates perceptual distortion in key regions; check self-attention loss and mask validity.

## Limitations
*   Caption source and cross-attention mask generation specifics are unspecified; mask quality may degrade for complex scenes or vague captions.
*   Simulated attack parameters during optimization (e.g., crop ratio, JPEG quality range) are missing, hindering exact replication and sensitivity analysis.
*   Stability of null-text inversion under watermark optimization and the precise purification effect of denoising are inferred but not directly measured; the watermark might be partially removed if embedding is not manifold-aligned.

## Confidence
*   **Null-text inversion yields a stable latent for perturbation:** Medium (relies on prior work; lacks direct validation under watermark optimization).
*   **Iterative denoising acts as a purification mechanism:** High (strong theoretical support from diffusion purification literature and consistent empirical results).
*   **Foreground masking via cross-attention improves robustness and quality:** Medium (plausible but untested; depends on cross-attention accuracy which varies).
