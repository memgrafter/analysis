# A Geometric Analysis of Small-sized Language Model Hallucinations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14778
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14778
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Genuine responses cluster more tightly than hallucinations in embedding space, enabling high-accuracy detection (F1 >90%) with only 30–50 labeled examples via geometric analysis.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles hallucination detection in small language models by analyzing the geometric structure of response embeddings. The core insight is that when a model generates multiple responses to the same factual prompt, correct answers converge around a stable semantic core, while hallucinations diverge due to retrieval instability. This manifests as tighter clustering of genuine responses compared to hallucinated ones in a sentence-embedding space. The authors apply regularized Fisher Discriminant Analysis to project embeddings into a one-dimensional space that maximizes class separability, improving the inter-/intra-class distance ratio from ~1.13 to ~7.26. A label propagation method then exploits this geometric signature: for an unlabeled response, the distribution of its distances to labeled examples is compared to reference intra-class distributions via the 1-Wasserstein metric. This approach achieves F1 scores above 90% consistently across ten small models (7B–32B) using only 30–50 manually labeled examples per prompt.

## Method Summary
The workflow starts by sampling many responses (e.g., 150) per prompt from a small base LLM. Each response is embedded using SBERT’s all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384D). A powerful LLM judge (Claude Sonnet) provides binary labels (Genuine/Hallucinated), discarding ambiguous cases. Statistical tests confirm that intra-genuine pairwise distances are significantly smaller than intra-hallucinated distances. A regularized Fisher Discriminant Analysis (λ≈1.2) is then fitted on a small labeled subset to find the optimal linear projection that maximizes between-class variance relative to within-class variance. For each unlabeled response, this projection is applied; the resulting scalar is used to compute its point-to-set distances to the labeled genuine and hallucinated points. The distributions of these distances are compared to the reference intra-class distance distributions (from the labeled set) via the 1-Wasserstein metric, and the closer match determines the label. This propagation requires no model retraining and scales with minimal supervision.

## Key Results
- FDA projection amplifies separability: inter-/intra-class distance ratio improves from ~1.13 to ~7.26 across all tested models.
- Label propagation with only 30–50 labeled examples achieves an average F1-score of 90.7% over ten small models.
- The geometric signature (tighter genuine clustering) holds robustly across models of varying sizes and prompts, enabling few-shot hallucination detection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method’s efficacy rests on three linked geometric mechanisms. First, genuine responses exhibit tighter semantic clustering than hallucinations because correct answers reflect retrieval of a stable factual representation, while hallucinations arise from inconsistent retrieval failures, leading to divergent content. This is evidenced by significantly smaller intra-genuine pairwise distances across models. Second, Fisher Discriminant Analysis concentrates this discriminative signal into a single dimension by maximizing the ratio of between-class to within-class scatter; the consistent improvement in separability ratio (~7.26) validates its effectiveness. Third, the distribution of point-to-set distances in this Fisher space is characteristic for each class: genuine points tend to be closer to other genuine points, while hallucinations are more isolated. Comparing an unlabeled response’s distance distribution to reference intra-class distributions via Wasserstein distance thus provides a robust classification heuristic, exploiting the stable geometric signature with minimal labeled data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sentence Embeddings (SBERT)**: Converts text to vectors where semantic similarity corresponds to geometric proximity. *Needed to map responses into a common space for distance-based analysis.* Quick check: How might using a factuality-tuned encoder change intra-class distance distributions?  
  Assumption: A factuality-tuned encoder could further tighten genuine clusters and widen hallucination spread, improving separability. However, this may come at the cost of generalizability to unseen domains. Unknown without empirical validation.
- **Wasserstein Distance**: Measures the minimal "cost" to transform one distribution into another, sensitive to both central tendency and spread. *Needed to robustly compare distance distributions without parametric assumptions.* Quick check: Why prefer Wasserstein over KL divergence for binned distance histograms?  
  Answer: Wasserstein accounts for the underlying geometry between bins and avoids infinite values when supports differ, making it more suitable for comparing binned histograms.
- **Fisher Discriminant Analysis (FDA)**: Finds a linear projection maximizing class separation relative to within-class spread. *Needed to concentrate discriminative information into one dimension for effective propagation.* Quick check: What happens if the within-class scatter matrix is ill-conditioned, and how does regularization λ address it?  
  Answer: Ill-conditioning causes numerical instability; regularization (λ) adds a diagonal term to ensure invertibility and control overfitting.

## Architecture Onboarding
Response Generator -> Embedder -> Labeler -> Structural Analyzer -> Label Propagator

**Critical path:** Data generation (multiple responses per prompt) → Embedding (SBERT) → Labeling (LLM judge) → FDA fitting (on small labeled set) → Label propagation (for remaining responses). The FDA step is pivotal: it transforms raw embeddings into a space where the Wasserstein-based heuristic becomes effective.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Embedding choice:** SBERT is lightweight and deterministic but may not capture fine-grained factual nuances; task-specific embeddings could improve separability but reduce generality.  
- **Projection method:** FDA is simple, low-dimensional, and robust (single optimal λ≈1.2 across models); nonlinear methods (UMAP) harm calibration.  
- **Reference distribution:** Using the full labeled set as reference assumes stationarity; performance may drop if prompt topics shift drastically.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Inter/intra-class ratio in Fisher space <1.2 indicates weak geometric signature.  
- High F1 variance across prompts (e.g., phi4-14B) signals model-specific instability.  
- Signed margin near zero suggests overlapping clusters.  
- Performance degrades with extreme class imbalance (<5 labels per class) or topical shift from labeled set.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Clustering validation:** For a fixed model-prompt, compute intra-genuine vs. intra-hallucinated distance distributions from 30+ labeled responses; expect Wilcoxon p<0.05 and Wasserstein > null baseline.  
2. **FDA separability test:** Fit FDA, project all responses; compute inter/intra-class distance ratio in 1D; expect >3 (paper avg 7.26); tune λ, expect optimal ~1.2.  
3. **Propagation evaluation:** With 30–50 labeled responses, propagate to rest; compute F1 against held-out test; expect >90%; vary training size to reproduce saturation curve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross-prompt coherence of Fisher directions:** The study focuses on prompt-local analysis and does not explore whether discriminative directions are consistent across prompts or tasks, limiting scalability to a general detector.  
2. **Generality of geometric signature:** The observed separability depends on the specific SBERT embedding and time-sensitive factual queries; it is unclear if the pattern holds for other embedding spaces, response types, or hallucination categories.  
3. **Geometric signature under knowledge deficit:** Prompts where the model entirely lacks knowledge were excluded; hallucinations stemming from complete knowledge absence may exhibit a different embedding-space structure.  
4. **Performance at extreme low annotation budgets:** The method is evaluated down to 5 examples, but the critical region below 30 annotations is not detailed; comparison to unsupervised detectors in this regime is missing.  
5. **Universality of λ≈1.2:** A single regularization value optimized all tested models; the reason (e.g., embedding-induced scale) and its validity beyond 7B–32B base models remain speculative.

## Limitations
- **Labeling dependency:** Ground truth relies on an external LLM judge (Claude Sonnet) with unspecified prompts and settings, risking reproducibility and consistent annotation.
- **Prompt topicality assumption:** Propagation assumes geometric stability across prompts, but only 200 time-sensitive factual prompts were tested; performance on new domains is unknown.
- **Embedding space lock-in:** Results are tied to all-MiniLM-L6-v2; using a different encoder may weaken the separability ratio.

## Confidence
- **Genuine responses cluster more tightly than hallucinations:** High (consistent statistical significance across ten models).  
- **FDA improves inter/intra-class ratio dramatically:** High (reproducible improvement from ~1.13 to ~7.26 on all models).  
- **Label propagation achieves >90% F1 with 30–50 examples:** Medium (some models like phi4-14B exhibit higher variance, indicating sensitivity to architecture or prompt).

## Next Checks
1. **Judge agreement audit:** Sample 200 responses, have 3 human annotators label factuality; target human–Claude agreement >85%.  
2. **Cross-domain geometry test:** Evaluate propagation on 50 non-factual prompts (e.g., opinion, creative) with 2 models; check if inter/intra ratio remains >4.  
3. **Embedding sensitivity scan:** Replace SBERT with two alternative encoders; re-compute separability ratios on 5 prompts × 2 models; if ratio changes >20%, embedding choice is critical.
