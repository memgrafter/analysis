# OpenResearcher: A Fully Open Pipeline for Long-Horizon Deep Research Trajectory Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20278
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20278
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A 30B student model fine-tuned on offline-generated trajectories achieves 54.8% on BrowseComp-Plus, surpassing GPT-4.1, while remaining fully open and reproducible.

## Executive Summary
OpenResearcher introduces a fully open pipeline for synthesizing long-horizon research trajectories without relying on live web APIs. It decouples one-time corpus bootstrapping from trajectory generation, constructing a 15M-document searchable environment that merges broad web data (FineWeb) with gold documents. A teacher model (GPT-OSS-120B) interacts via three explicit browser primitives—search, open, and find—to generate over 97K trajectories, many exceeding 100 tool calls. Supervised fine-tuning a 30B student on these trajectories yields state-of-the-art accuracy on deep research benchmarks while eliminating API costs and ensuring perfect reproducibility.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of four stages: (1) build an offline corpus by merging the FineWeb dataset with gold documents retrieved from MiroVerse questions; (2) use a teacher model to generate browsing trajectories within this environment via search, open, and find tools; (3) filter trajectories for structural validity and optionally correctness; (4) fine-tune a student model on the filtered trajectories with long-context packing. This decoupling removes per-turn API costs, enables deterministic parallel generation, and supports reproducible long-horizon research agent training.

## Key Results
- Generated 97K+ trajectories, with many containing 100+ tool calls.
- Student model achieves 54.8% accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus (+34.0 over base), surpassing GPT-4.1 and other proprietary systems.
- Remains competitive on open-web benchmarks: BrowseComp 26.3%, GAIA 64.1%, xbench-DeepSearch 65.0%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Offline corpus decoupling enables scalable, reproducible trajectory synthesis.
By performing one-time online gold document retrieval and merging with a large static corpus (FineWeb), the search-and-browse loop runs entirely offline. This removes per-turn API costs, eliminates web volatility, and allows deterministic parallel generation of long trajectories. Evidence: Abstract and Section 3.2 describe the decoupling; Section 4.4 shows offline synthesis costs $0 versus $5,760+ for API-based synthesis. Break condition: If the offline corpus lacks critical evidence for target questions, trajectories become ambiguous failures rather than meaningful search demonstrations.

### Mechanism 2: Explicit browser primitives teach hierarchical information-seeking.
The three tools (search, open, find) model progressive focus: search identifies candidate sources, open inspects full documents, and find localizes evidence. This forces the teacher to interleave query formulation, document navigation, and evidence grounding, producing realistic multi-scale browsing traces. Evidence: Section 3.3 defines the primitives; RQ4 ablation shows the full toolset (62.17% accuracy) outperforms search-only (43.86%) and search+open (56.39%), with higher gold-hit rates and earlier first hits. Break condition: Tasks requiring interactions beyond these primitives (e.g., form filling) may not transfer.

### Mechanism 3: Correctness-agnostic filtering preserves diverse search strategies.
Rejection sampling based solely on final answer correctness discards only malformed or incomplete trajectories. This retains both efficient and inefficient search traces, exposing the student to a spectrum of query reformulation patterns, document inspection sequences, and stopping behaviors—including how failures unfold. Evidence: RQ1 shows training on correct-only (54.81%), incorrect-only (55.06%), and all trajectories (54.46%) yields nearly identical BrowseComp-Plus accuracy. Failed trajectories have ~2× more tool calls, primarily from excessive searches, indicating rich data on search drift. Break condition: If incorrect trajectories systematically contain invalid tool syntax or hallucinated observations, they may corrupt the student’s policy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Long-horizon credit assignment:** The student must learn which early search decisions contributed to eventual success over 100+ turns, not just immediate next-step correctness. Quick check: In a 50-turn trajectory, how does the model assign credit for opening a gold document on turn 20 when the final answer appears on turn 48?
- **Tool grounding in high-noise corpus:** With 15M distractors, the model must distinguish signal from noise across search snippets, open full pages, and verify via find—avoiding shallow pattern matching. Quick check: When search returns 10 snippets, what determines whether the model opens link #3 versus #7, and how does it decide to keep searching after opening?
- **Multi-scale evidence synthesis:** Answers require stitching facts from multiple documents, possibly found at different stages. The model must maintain working memory of gathered evidence across turns. Quick check: After opening three documents containing partial facts, how does the model decide it has enough to answer, and how does it cite them without over-quoting?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Offline Corpus Builder → Teacher Synthesis Engine → Trajectory Filter → Student Trainer → Evaluation Harness

**Critical path:** Corpus construction → teacher trajectory generation → filtering → student SFT → evaluation. Any break in corpus coverage (missing gold docs) or tool alignment (teacher vs. student specs) causes downstream failure.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Breadth vs. depth in corpus:* More FineWeb adds noise but improves generalization to live web; too few gold docs causes low synthesis success.
- *Tool simplicity vs. expressivity:* Three primitives are clean but may under-specify actions like scroll-to-anchor; richer tools increase complexity but may harm reproducibility.
- *Teacher scale vs. cost:* GPT-OSS-120B is expensive but generates high-quality traces; cheaper teachers may produce shorter, less diverse trajectories.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low gold-document hit rate in synthesized trajectories (<5%) indicates corpus coverage failure.
- Trajectories with >100 turns but zero `find` calls suggest the model isn’t localizing evidence.
- Student performance on closed-web (BrowseComp-Plus) >> open-web (BrowseComp) indicates overfitting to corpus quirks; << indicates poor generalization.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Corpus coverage ablation:** Re-run trajectory synthesis without bootstrapped gold docs; measure gold-hit rate and downstream accuracy (RQ2 shows collapse from 54.81% → 6.35%).
2. **Tool ablation on teacher:** Compare trajectories generated with search-only vs. full tools; evaluate student performance (RQ4 shows search-only teacher yields 43.86% vs. 62.17%).
3. **Filtering strategy test:** Train three students on correct-only, incorrect-only, and all trajectories; measure variance (RQ1 shows <0.6% difference, validating correctness-agnostic filtering).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does the size and diversity of the offline corpus affect student model performance on deep research tasks, especially generalization to live web?** The paper constructs a fixed 15M-document corpus and shows that removing gold documents collapses performance, but does not vary corpus size or composition. Systematic experiments varying corpus scale, source, and recency while keeping gold documents would resolve this.
2. **Does the teacher model's capability materially impact trajectory quality and student performance? Could cheaper teachers suffice?** The authors use GPT-OSS-120B as teacher but never compare with other teachers. Generating trajectories using teachers of varying sizes/costs, training students on each, and comparing benchmark performance would clarify teacher requirements.
3. **Would expanding the browser primitive set beyond search, open, find improve agent effectiveness?** The paper shows the necessity of the three primitives but does not test additional ones like scroll or click. Augmenting the environment with extra tools, synthesizing new trajectories, and training students with expanded toolset compared to baseline would test this.
4. **Can learned trajectory quality metrics replace final-answer correctness as filters for SFT, improving data efficiency?** RQ1 found correctness not required for good performance, yet they still filter by correctness for their 55K set. Designing alternative filters (e.g., gold-hit rate, tool efficiency) and evaluating students trained on filtered subsets versus correctness-filtered baseline would test better filtering.

## Limitations
- Offline corpus may lack niche or up-to-date information; gold document bootstrapping parameters are underspecified.
- Teacher GPT-OSS-120B is likely proprietary, hindering exact replication; impact of teacher scale unablated.
- Open-web evaluations rely on the Serper API, introducing non-determinism and cost.

## Confidence
- Decoupled offline pipeline scalability: **High**
- Explicit browser primitives yield hierarchical search: **Medium**
- Correctness-agnostic filtering preserves utility: **Medium**
- Student beats GPT-4.1 on BrowseComp-Plus: **Medium-High**

## Next Checks
1. Replace GPT-OSS-120B with a fully open teacher (e.g., Llama-3.1-405B), re-run trajectory synthesis, and compare student accuracy to assess teacher dependence.
2. Quantify corpus coverage: For a sample of MiroVerse questions, measure the recall of gold document facts in the 15M index; report the rate.
3. Evaluate the student model across multiple live-web runs with varied Serper configurations; compute accuracy variance to estimate API instability effects.
