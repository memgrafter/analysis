# CageDroneRF: A Large-Scale RF Benchmark and Toolkit for Drone Perception

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03302  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03302  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** 39 classes across 23 drone models; raw I/Q augmentation with label-consistent transforms; YOLOv11n baseline mAP@[.5:.95] = 0.948 (clean) / 0.838 (augmented); combined indoor-outdoor training raises hierarchical classification model accuracy from 42.07% to 69.63%.

## Executive Summary
CageDroneRF (CDRF) addresses the scarcity and limited diversity of existing RF drone datasets, which hinder robust real-world model generalization. It introduces a large-scale benchmark comprising 39 classes across 23 drone models, collected in both a controlled Faraday cage (high‑SNR, low‑interference) and a complex outdoor campus environment. The core contribution is a raw‑signal augmentation pipeline operating directly on complex I/Q data, enabling precise control over SNR, interference injection, and frequency shifts while automatically recomputing YOLO‑format bounding‑box annotations to maintain label consistency. This end‑to‑end traceability allows researchers to generate unlimited, reproducible dataset variants. Baseline results demonstrate high detection performance (mAP@[.5:.95] = 0.948 on clean data, 0.838 on augmented data) and show that training on the union of indoor and outdoor data drastically reduces the indoor‑outdoor domain gap for hierarchical classification (model‑level accuracy improves from 42% to 70%). The open‑source toolkit ensures reproducible benchmarking and cross‑dataset compatibility.

## Method Summary
The CDRF toolkit processes raw I/Q samples via a configurable pipeline: raw `.dat` files are memory‑mapped and sliced into time‑aligned windows, optionally normalized, then subjected to raw‑signal augmentations (AWGN for SNR control, interferer mixing, and complex exponential frequency shifts). For detection tasks, any frequency shift is accompanied by an analytical recomputation of YOLO bounding boxes to preserve alignment, including wrap‑around handling on the frequency axis. The (optionally augmented) I/Q stream is converted into a spectrogram using STFT, after which the annotation engine produces label files in YOLO format. The dataset provides two distinct collection environments: an RF‑shielded Faraday cage for clean reference signals and an open outdoor campus for realistic interference. Models are trained on spectrogram images: YOLOv11n for object detection and a multi‑head ResNet‑18 for hierarchical classification. By storing raw data and parameterized processing scripts, the toolkit eliminates “annotation lock‑in,” enabling researchers to systematically explore the effects of STFT parameters and augmentations without manual re‑labeling.

## Key Results
- **Detection performance:** YOLOv11n trained on clean cage spectrograms achieves mAP@[.5:.95] = 0.948; training on augmented data (SNR conditioning, interferer mix, frequency shifts) yields mAP@[.5:.95] = 0.838 with precision 0.940 and recall 0.855.
- **Domain generalization:** Combining indoor and outdoor data for training drastically reduces the indoor‑outdoor gap for hierarchical classification: model‑level accuracy increases from 42.07% (indoor‑only) to 69.63% when evaluated on outdoor‑only test data.
- **Open‑source release:** The full toolkit, raw I/Q data, and label‑consistent augmentation pipeline are publicly available, facilitating reproducible benchmarking and cross‑dataset comparisons.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Raw‑Signal Augmentation with Label‑Consistent Transforms
**Claim:** A raw‑signal augmentation pipeline operating on complex I/Q data enables precise, label‑consistent control over SNR, interference, and frequency shifts.  
**Mechanism:** Augmentations (AWGN addition, interferer mixing, complex exponential frequency shifts) are applied directly to the baseband I/Q stream *before* STFT conversion to a spectrogram. Any frequency shift is paired with an exact, analytical recomputation of the YOLO‑format bounding‑box coordinates, including correct wrap‑around handling on the frequency axis. This maintains a deterministic link between the transformed signal and its detection labels.  
**Core assumption:** Training object detection models on spectrograms derived from augmented raw signals, with perfectly aligned labels, will yield models that generalize better to real‑world variations in SNR, interference, and carrier offset than models trained only on clean, fixed spectrograms.  
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section IV.D.1], [section IV.E.1]  
**Break condition:** If the analytical annotation recomputation fails for certain spectrogram parameter settings (e.g., extreme frequency shifts or non‑standard windowing) or if the augmented signal distributions become too divergent from the underlying physics captured in the real recordings, the purported generalization benefit may not materialize, and models could learn spurious correlations from augmentation artifacts.

### Mechanism 2: Indoor‑Outdoor Data Combination Mitigates Domain Shift
**Claim:** Combining controlled, clean recordings from a Faraday cage with diverse, complex outdoor captures mitigates domain shift and improves model generalization across environmental conditions.  
**Mechanism:** The dataset construction deliberately uses two collection environments: (1) an RF‑shielded Faraday cage to obtain high‑SNR, low‑interference “clean” reference captures of many drone models, and (2) an open‑campus outdoor setting to capture signals under natural interference and channel effects. Training on the union of these sources exposes models to both ideal signal characteristics and realistic distortions.  
**Core assumption:** A model trained on this mixed indoor‑outdoor data will learn features that are invariant to the background noise and interference profile, focusing instead on the discriminative “RF fingerprint” of the drone signal itself.  
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section III.C], [section V.C.3]  
**Break condition:** This mechanism fails if the domain gap between the indoor cage and outdoor environments is too large and irreducible by simple data mixing (e.g., if outdoor signals are corrupted by unique multipath or Doppler effects not present indoors). The improvement hinges on the indoor captures providing a sufficiently clean latent representation of drone signals that can be “aligned” with the noisier outdoor variants.

### Mechanism 3: End‑to‑End Traceability Enables Reproducible Benchmarking
**Claim:** Maintaining a complete, parameterized processing chain from raw I/Q through spectrogram generation to detection annotation enables reproducible benchmarking and unlimited, label‑consistent dataset variants.  
**Mechanism:** The toolkit does not distribute pre‑rendered, fixed spectrogram images as the primary data asset. Instead, it stores raw `.dat` files and a scriptable pipeline. Any change to STFT parameters (FFT size, window, hop) or signal‑level augmentation (SNR, shift, interferer) triggers an automated regeneration of the spectrogram *and* a recomputation of all bounding boxes according to the new parameters. This end‑to‑end traceability is implemented via analytical transformations of box coordinates.  
**Core assumption:** Eliminating the “annotation lock‑in” problem (where changing signal processing parameters invalidates existing labels) will allow the research community to systematically stress‑test models across a vast range of realistic operating points without manual re‑labeling, leading to more robust and comparable evaluations.  
**Evidence:** [section II.E], [section IV.F]  
**Break condition:** The mechanism breaks if the analytical annotation models (e.g., for frequency shift wrap‑around) are inaccurate for certain signal morphologies or if the parameter space for STFT becomes too large, leading to an unmanageable combinatorial explosion of possible dataset variants that cannot be effectively evaluated or compared.

## Foundational Learning

### Short‑Time Fourier Transform (STFT)
- **Why needed:** It is the mandatory first step to convert the raw complex I/Q time series into a 2D time‑frequency spectrogram image, the standard input format for CNN‑based detectors (YOLO) and classifiers (ResNet) used in the paper.
- **Quick check:** If you increase the FFT size (`N`) in the STFT while keeping the sampling rate constant, what happens to the time and frequency resolution of the resulting spectrogram, and how might this affect a YOLO bounding box’s height (frequency extent)?

### YOLO Bounding Box Format & Wrap‑Around
- **Why needed:** The entire label‑consistent augmentation promise depends on correctly transforming normalized YOLO box coordinates (center_x, center_y, width, height) after a frequency shift, including the edge case where a signal’s frequency span crosses the 0/f_max boundary of the normalized spectrogram.
- **Quick check:** A drone signal spans frequencies near the top of a normalized [0,1] spectrogram. After a positive frequency shift, parts of the signal appear at the bottom. How should the single original bounding box be represented in the new image’s label file?

### Hierarchical Classification Loss
- **Why needed:** The paper’s hierarchical classification task uses a multi‑head ResNet‑18 with a combined loss. The learner must understand how to weight and sum per‑head cross‑entropy losses to train a single backbone to predict labels at multiple semantic levels (Modulation, Protocol, Model) simultaneously.
- **Quick check:** In a 3‑level hierarchy, if the loss weights are [0.2, 0.3, 0.5] for [Modulation, Protocol, Model], what is the total loss if the individual head losses are L1=0.5, L2=0.4, L3=0.6?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Raw Data Store (`.dat` files) → Loader & Slicer → Preprocessor/Normalizer → (optional: Augmentor) → Spectrogram Engine (STFT) → Annotation Engine → Dataset Interface → Model Heads (YOLOv11n / ResNet‑18)

### Critical Path
`Raw .dat file` → `Loader (slice)` → `[Augment (optional)]` → `STFT` → `Annotation Recomputation` → `Image/Label Pair`. The critical dependency is that **augmentation must precede STFT and must trigger annotation recomputation**.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **YOLOv11n vs. larger detectors:** Chose smallest YOLO variant for edge‑deployable realism, accepting lower absolute mAP for relevance to constrained systems.
- **Whole‑signal annotation:** Annotates entire burst sequence as one box to capture temporal occupancy. Tradeoff: loses fine‑grained per‑hop timing information which might be useful for some classifiers.
- **20 MHz sampling rate:** Balances spectral coverage with edge hardware feasibility (vs. RFUAV’s 100 MHz), but may miss some narrowband signals outside the 20 MHz window.

### Failure Signatures
- **Detection:** Large recall drop under augmentation (clean: 98.2% → augmented: 85.5%) indicates model struggles with low‑SNR/interference despite augmentation training. Check if augmentation magnitude is too extreme or if model capacity is insufficient.
- **Classification:** Significant indoor→outdoor performance gap (Model‑level: 42%→70% with combined training) indicates residual domain shift. Inspect feature distributions (e.g., via t‑SNE) of indoor vs. outdoor spectrograms.
- **Open‑Set:** Unknown detection rate ~74% suggests the model’s confidence/novelty scoring (MetaMax) is not perfectly calibrated; unknown classes may still receive high predicted confidence for known classes.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replicate Clean YOLO Baseline:** Train YOLOv11n on *unaugmented* cage spectrograms. Expect mAP@[.5:.95] ~0.948 (Table II). Validates data pipeline and baseline code.
2. **Replicate Augmented YOLO Baseline:** Train YOLOv11n on augmented data (frequency shift + interferer mix + SNR conditioning). Expect mAP@[.5:.95] ~0.838, precision ~0.940, recall ~0.855. Validates the augmentation‑annotation linkage.
3. **Hierarchical Indoor/Outdoor Gap:** Train ResNet‑18 hierarchical head on *indoor‑only* data, evaluate on *outdoor‑only* data. Expect large model‑level accuracy drop (to ~42%). Then train on *combined* indoor+outdoor, expect significant recovery (~70%). Validates the dual‑environment diversity claim.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** How does expanding CDRF with additional drone models and diverse environments influence the robustness and generalizability of RF‑based detection models?  
*Basis:* Future Work: “Dataset expansion: Regular updates with new and emerging drone models, more diverse background and interference signals from a wider range of environments (e.g., urban, industrial, and rural), and integration with other public datasets to create a more comprehensive benchmark.”  
*Why unresolved:* The current dataset is fixed; it lacks many modern drone models and varied real‑world conditions. Gathering, labeling, and integrating new data requires significant effort.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A subsequent release of CDRF with expanded class coverage and multi‑environment recordings, plus empirical evaluation showing improved accuracy and resilience on out‑of‑distribution test sets.

**Open Question 2:** Can advanced neural architectures (e.g., Transformers, graph neural networks) yield significant improvements over current baselines for RF drone perception tasks?  
*Basis:* Future Work: “Advanced model architectures: Exploration of more sophisticated models, such as Transformers and graph neural networks, which may better capture the complex temporal and spectral dependencies in RF signals.”  
*Why unresolved:* Benchmark experiments only used YOLOv11n and ResNet‑18; newer architectures remain unevaluated on the CDRF suite.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic training and testing of candidate architectures on CDRF’s detection, classification, and open‑set tasks, demonstrating statistically significant gains in mAP, accuracy, or robustness metrics.

**Open Question 3:** How can augmentation techniques be extended to simulate realistic channel effects (e.g., multipath fading, Doppler shifts) to further enhance model robustness?  
*Basis:* Future Work: “Enhanced augmentation techniques: Development of more advanced augmentation techniques that more realistically simulate a wider range of real‑world channel effects, such as multipath fading and Doppler shifts.”  
*Why unresolved:* The current augmentation pipeline focuses on SNR control, interferer mixing, and frequency shifts; it does not model spatial or mobility‑induced channel dynamics.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Integration of fading/Doppler modules into the raw‑I/Q augmentation stack, followed by validation showing improved detection recall under corresponding channel conditions.

**Open Question 4:** What methods are most effective for multi‑label and open‑set classification in RF drone detection when multiple drones and unknown threats are present?  
*Basis:* Future Work: “Advanced tasks: Further investigation of multi‑label and open‑set classification, which are critical for real‑world applications where multiple drones may be present and novel threats are a constant concern.”  
*Why unresolved:* CDRF’s baseline experiments address single‑label classification and basic open‑set recognition; multi‑drone scenarios and scalable OSR remain under‑explored.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* New benchmarks on CDRF that define multi‑label and multi‑class OSR protocols, along with algorithms that achieve high detection rates for known classes while reliably rejecting unknowns, supported by detailed error analysis.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified protocol details:** Exact train/validation/test splits, full YOLO/ResNet hyperparameters (e.g., input size, NMS thresholds, optimizer settings), and background sample integration are not provided in the paper, requiring assumptions for reproduction.
- **Validation of augmentation realism:** The physical realism of generated interferers for replicating outdoor conditions is not validated; the augmented data might not fully capture complex outdoor RF phenomena.
- **Wrap‑around and parameter sensitivity:** The analytical annotation recomputation may fail for extreme frequency shifts or non‑standard STFT parameters, potentially breaking the label consistency guarantee.

## Confidence
- **Augmentation‑annotation linkage:** High. Detailed algorithms and evidence anchors provided; break conditions clearly stated.
- **Indoor‑outdoor domain mixing:** Medium. Strong empirical result shown, but relies on assumption that indoor captures provide a clean latent representation that aligns with outdoor distortions.
- **Open‑set recognition efficacy:** Low. Preliminary result presented; MetaMax calibration and feature separation not deeply analyzed.
