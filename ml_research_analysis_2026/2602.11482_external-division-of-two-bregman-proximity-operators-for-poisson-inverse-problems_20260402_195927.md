# External Division of Two Bregman Proximity Operators for Poisson Inverse Problems

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.11482
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11482
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: A novel external-division Bregman operator integrated into NoLips significantly reduces bias in Poisson inverse problems, achieving lower NMSE on synthetic data and a PSNR of 20.46 dB on image restoration, far surpassing forward and reverse KL baselines.

## Executive Summary
Poisson inverse problems arise in various imaging and scientific applications where observations follow a Poisson noise model. Standard ℓ₁-regularization promotes sparsity but introduces shrinkage bias, especially for large-magnitude components. This paper introduces an external-division operator constructed from two Bregman proximity operators. The operator is designed to behave like the identity for large inputs, thereby mitigating bias, while still providing sparsity for small coefficients. Embedded within the NoLips algorithm via a plug-and-play strategy, the method only requires proximity operator computations. Numerical experiments on synthetic data demonstrate a substantial reduction in normalized mean squared error (NMSE) compared to forward and reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence baselines, with more stable convergence. In an image restoration task, the proposed approach attains a peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) of 20.46 dB, dramatically outperforming the baselines (13.75 dB and 8.57 dB). These results confirm the effectiveness of the external-division operator in reducing estimation bias while maintaining sparsity.

## Method Summary
The method addresses the composite minimization problem $\min_x D_\phi(Ax, b) + g(x)$, where $D_\phi$ is the Bregman divergence corresponding to the Poisson likelihood (Boltzmann-Shannon entropy), $A$ is a linear operator, $b$ is the Poisson-distributed measurement, and $g$ is the regularizer. Instead of the conventional $\ell_1$ norm, the authors define $g$ via an external-division construction that utilizes two Bregman proximity operators. This construction yields an operator that acts approximately as the identity on large-magnitude inputs, thus avoiding shrinkage, while retaining a sparsity-inducing effect for small inputs. The optimization is performed using the NoLips algorithm, a splitting method that iteratively applies the proximity operators of $f$ and $g$. The external-division operator is incorporated in a plug-and-play fashion, meaning it can be swapped into the algorithm without altering the underlying framework, provided its proximity operator can be computed (or approximated).

## Key Results
- Synthetic data experiments show a significant reduction in normalized mean squared error (NMSE) compared to both forward-KL and reverse-KL baselines, with more stable convergence behavior.
- On an image restoration task (deblurring with Poisson noise), the method achieves a PSNR of 20.46 dB, while the forward-KL and reverse-KL baselines yield only 13.75 dB and 8.57 dB, respectively.
- The external-division operator effectively acts as the identity for large-magnitude coefficients, thereby mitigating the bias inherent in $\ell_1$-regularization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The external-division operator's core mechanism is its dual behavior: for large-magnitude inputs it approximates the identity function, thus avoiding the shrinkage that biases $\ell_1$-regularization; for small inputs it enforces sparsity through a non-convex Bregman-based penalty. This is achieved by combining two Bregman proximity operators in an external division formulation, which yields a proximal map that interpolates between shrinkage and identity depending on the magnitude relative to a threshold. By plugging this operator into the NoLips algorithm, the overall optimization benefits from the stability of first-order splitting methods while requiring only proximal computations. The result is a method that reduces bias without sacrificing sparsity, leading to superior reconstruction accuracy as evidenced by the NMSE and PSNR improvements.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (data/control flow in plain text):  
(b, A) -> f(x)=D_ϕ(Ax,b) and g(x)=external-division -> NoLips algorithm (alternating prox_f and prox_g) -> x̂

**Critical path**:  
- Correct implementation of the external-division operator's proximity operator (which involves nested calls to two Bregman proximity subroutines).  
- Satisfaction of the NoLips convergence condition (a Lipschitz-like condition on the operators).  
- Proper tuning of hyperparameters (ω, η1, η2, a) to balance bias reduction and sparsity.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- The parameter *a* governs the identity region; larger *a* reduces bias but may weaken sparsity for medium coefficients.  
- Weights η1, η2 balance the two Bregman divergences inside the external-division operator; improper values can degrade performance.  
- Computational cost per iteration is higher than for ℓ₁ regularization due to the nested prox evaluations, trading off speed for accuracy.  
- Using an inexact proximity operator for *f* (as permitted by NoLips) offers a tradeoff between iteration count and per-iteration work.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Non-convergence or oscillation of NoLips (monitored via objective value or residual norms) suggests violation of condition (5) or errors in prox implementations.  
- Excessively shrunk nonzero coefficients (high bias) indicate *a* is too small.  
- Lack of sparsity (many small nonzeros) suggests η parameters are not properly tuned, making the regularizer too weak.  
- Large runtime per iteration compared to baseline methods reveals the overhead of the external-division prox.

**3 first experiments**:

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to synthetic data and a single image restoration task; broader validation across diverse datasets is needed to establish generalizability.
- Baselines are limited to forward and reverse KL divergences, omitting modern plug-and-play, deep learning, and other Bregman-based methods, which may overstate the reported improvement.
- No statistical testing or confidence intervals are provided for the NMSE/PSNR improvements, leaving their significance uncertain.
