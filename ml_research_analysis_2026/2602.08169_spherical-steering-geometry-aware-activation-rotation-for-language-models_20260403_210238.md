# Spherical Steering: Geometry-Aware Activation Rotation for Language Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.08169
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08169
*   **Reference count:** 36
*   **Primary result:** Introduces a norm-preserving rotation method that achieves state-of-the-art truthfulness accuracy on multiple-choice benchmarks while simultaneously improving generative quality, establishing a superior Pareto frontier over additive steering techniques.

## Executive Summary
Spherical Steering tackles the core limitation of existing activation steering methods—unnatural magnitude distortion—by replacing additive offsets with norm-preserving geodesic rotation on the hypersphere. This approach steers model activations toward a learned "truthful" direction while retaining their original magnitude, thus preserving representational integrity for open-ended generation. A von Mises–Fisher (vMF) confidence gate dynamically modulates the rotation strength based on input alignment, providing adaptive control. Evaluated on LLaMA-3.1 and Qwen-2.5, the method delivers significant absolute gains (e.g., +10% on TruthfulQA) across multiple-choice and open-ended benchmarks and demonstrates superior collapse-efficiency, yielding larger performance improvements per unit of representational degradation compared to additive methods like ITI, CAA, and SADI.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two phases. First, an offline **prototype module** constructs a truthful direction vector **μₜ** from a contrastive dataset (e.g., TruthfulQA). For a selected layer *l*, it extracts last-token activations for correct (**m⁺**) and incorrect (**m⁻**) answers, computes their difference **Δ = m⁺ – m⁻**, and normalizes it to **μₜ** (with **μₕ = –μₜ**). Second, during inference, for each token generation and target layer *l*, the **steering module** extracts activation **h**, normalizes it to **ĥ**, and uses the **vMF confidence gate** to compute a steering strength *t* based on cosine similarities to **μₜ** and **μₕ**. If *t* > 0, it applies a spherical linear interpolation (Slerp) to rotate **ĥ** toward **μₜ** by angle *t*θ, then re-scales by the original norm ‖**h**‖. The gate parameters (α, β, κ) are tuned to balance accuracy and generative fidelity.

## Key Results
*   Achieves state-of-the-art multiple-choice accuracy on TruthfulQA (MC1/MC2), COPA, and StoryCloze, with up to +10% absolute gain over prior methods.
*   Simultaneously improves open-ended generation quality (TRUE×INFO), overcoming the accuracy-generation trade-off that plagues additive steering.
*   Establishes a Pareto frontier: for a given level of representational collapse (measured by effective rank drop), rotation yields significantly larger performance gains than activation addition.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Claim:** Norm-preserving rotation maintains representation integrity while steering behavior, avoiding the magnitude distortion that degrades open-ended generation in additive methods.  
**Mechanism:** The method treats normalized activations as points on a unit hypersphere. It computes a geodesic (great-circle) path from the current activation direction toward the contrastive truth prototype using spherical linear interpolation (Slerp). The original activation magnitude is reapplied after rotation, ensuring ‖h'‖ = ‖h‖. The core assumption is that semantic/behavioral signals are encoded primarily in direction, not magnitude, supported by the architectural prior of normalization layers like RMSNorm that stabilize magnitudes. Evidence includes empirical analysis showing overlapping ℓ₂ norms for truthful vs. hallucinated activations across all layers.  
**Break condition:** If magnitude is discovered to be a critical discriminative feature for certain tasks or layers, norm-preserving rotation would discard useful signal.

**Claim:** The von Mises–Fisher (vMF) confidence gate enables selective, input-adaptive steering strength, preventing over-correction on already-aligned inputs and preserving generation diversity.  
**Mechanism:** For each activation direction, cosine similarities to the truthful (μₜ) and hallucinated (μₕ) prototypes are transformed via a vMF-inspired exponential to yield probabilities pₜ, pₕ. The difference δ = pₕ − pₜ is thresholded (β) and scaled (α) to produce steering strength *t* ∈ [0,1]. Steering is suppressed when δ ≤ β. The mapping assumes δ provides a calibrated measure of an input's "need for steering."  
**Break condition:** If the mapping from cosine similarity to required steering strength is highly non-monotonic or task-dependent, the linear scaling (α·(δ−β)/(1−β)) would be insufficient, requiring a learned gating function.

**Claim:** Spherical steering's superior collapse-efficiency (performance gain per unit of representation degradation) stems from manipulating direction on the hypersphere, which aligns with the intrinsic geometry of LLM representations and avoids the "shattering" effect of additive magnitude changes.  
**Mechanism:** By contrast with activation addition (h' = h + λμ), which changes norm unpredictably, spherical rotation changes only direction. Effective-rank analysis shows that for a given drop in effective rank (a proxy for representational collapse), rotation yields significantly larger gains in both MC accuracy and TRUE×INFO than addition. The assumption is that the representational manifold's utility is more sensitive to directional distortion than to controlled angular adjustments, consistent with normalization layers making direction the primary computational degree of freedom.  
**Break condition:** If the effective rank metric is unreliable or rotation's gains vanish under more stringent analyses (e.g., causal mediation), the collapse-efficiency claim would be weakened.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept:** Unit Hypersphere & Geodesics (S^{d-1})  
**Why needed:** The core operation treats normalized activations as points on a hypersphere. Understanding that the shortest path between two points on a sphere is an arc (geodesic), not a straight line, explains why Slerp is used.  
**Quick check:** Why does moving along a geodesic (Slerp) preserve vector norm, while linear interpolation (Lerp) does not?

**Concept:** Effective Rank & Representation Collapse  
**Why needed:** The paper's efficiency argument uses effective rank drop to quantify "representational degradation." Understanding that effective rank measures the entropy of the singular value spectrum is necessary to interpret the collapse-efficiency trade-off.  
**Quick check:** If an intervention makes the activation matrix's singular values more equal, does its effective rank increase or decrease? What does a large drop imply?

**Concept:** von Mises–Fisher Distribution  
**Why needed:** The confidence gate maps cosine similarities to δ using an exponential family form f(u; m, κ) ∝ exp(κ m⊤u). Knowing this is the spherical analogue of a Gaussian explains why the exponential of dot product is a natural alignment measure.  
**Quick check:** On the unit circle (S¹), what does the vMF distribution reduce to when κ → 0? What does it represent when κ → ∞?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Offline Prototype Module -> Inference-Time Steering Module (with embedded vMF Confidence Gate)

**Critical path:** `[Offline: Contrastive Data → Activations → μ_T, μ_H] → [Online: h_{t+1} = Decode(Steer(Decode(h_t)))]`. The critical dependency is that the prototype μ must be well-calibrated from high-quality contrastive data and the chosen layer *l* must encode the target concept directionally. Gate parameters (α, β) must be tuned per model/layer.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
*   **Layer Selection (K):** Single-layer (K=1) maximizes MC gains with minimal disruption. Multi-layer (K=2-3) drastically improves INFO/TRUE×INFO but risks collapse if K≥4.  
*   **Gate Selectivity (β) vs. Strength (α):** High β (e.g., 0.3) makes steering sparse and safer for generation. Low β (e.g., -0.05) applies steering more often but risks degrading INFO. α directly scales rotation magnitude. Optimal pairs are layer-specific.  
*   **Gated vs. Ungated:** Ungated rotation is simpler and effective but shows sharp INFO degradation at high α. Gating stabilizes the accuracy-generation frontier.

**Failure signatures:**  
1.  **Rank collapse without accuracy gain:** MC accuracy plateaus/drops while effective rank plummets during α sweep. Indicates intervention distorts manifold without aligning to useful μ.  
2.  **INFO collapse while TRUE rises:** TRUE increases but INFO drops sharply (e.g., α≥0.7). Means steering forces safe/"I don't know" responses. Sign β is too low or α too high.  
3.  **No improvement over baseline:** Both MC and TRUE×INFO remain near baseline. Likely causes: poor μ (bad contrastive data/wrong layer) or concept not linearly steerable in that layer.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1.  **Rotation vs. Addition Sanity Check:** On a single layer (e.g., layer 24 for LLaMA), sweep intervention strength for Spherical Steering (ungated) and CAA. Plot MC accuracy and TRUE×INFO vs. strength. Expect rotation to show superior Pareto frontier.  
2.  **vMF Gate Ablation:** Run gated vs. ungated spherical steering (same layer, sweep α). Measure maximum TRUE×INFO and α at peak. Expect gating to shift peak to higher α and raise maximum.  
3.  **Multi-Layer Sensitivity:** For fixed α, β, intervene on 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 layers (e.g., layers 18, 20, 22, 24, 26). Record MC1 and INFO. Expect MC1 plateaus after K=2, while INFO peaks at K=2-3 then drops.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Question 1:** How can we automatically select the optimal layers and per-layer rotation strengths for Spherical Steering without extensive manual tuning?  
**Basis:** The conclusion states "Future work includes principled multi-layer layer selection and layer-adaptive calibration."  
**Why unresolved:** Current approach requires choosing intervention layers ℒ and fixed hyperparameters; ablations show asymmetric effects but no systematic selection method.

**Question 2:** How sensitive is Spherical Steering to errors or biases in the contrastive dataset used to construct the truthfulness prototype?  
**Basis:** Inferred; method relies on contrastive pairs to define μ_T, but real-world data may contain label noise or biases that could misalign the prototype.  
**Why unresolved:** No experiments on robustness to synthetic noise or alternative data sources; appendix shows only standard splits.

**Question 3:** Does Spherical Steering maintain its benefits on model architectures that do not use RMSNorm or that have different activation distributions?  
**Basis:** Inferred; paper evaluates only on LLaMA and Qwen (both use RMSNorm); method assumes normalized activations approximate directions due to normalization layers.  
**Why unresolved:** No evaluation on diverse architectures; geometric justification based on these models' properties only.

**Question 4:** What theoretical properties of norm-preserving rotation explain its superior collapse-efficiency compared to additive shifts?  
**Basis:** Inferred; paper provides empirical rank analysis but lacks a formal framework linking geometry to representation collapse and downstream performance.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper suggests rotation changes direction while keeping norm, but no formal analysis is provided.

## Limitations
*   **Unclear layer selection for Qwen and other benchmarks:** Only LLaMA-3.1 layer 24 specified for TruthfulQA; optimal layers for other models/tasks are unknown.
*   **Magnitude non-discriminativity assumption weakly validated:** Overlapping norm distributions (Fig. 3) may not hold universally; if magnitude encodes useful signal, norm-preserving rotation discards it.
*   **vMF gate's linear scaling is ad-hoc:** The mapping from cosine similarity to steering strength is hand-crafted and may fail for non-monotonic relationships; no comparison to learned alternatives.
*   **Effective rank as collapse proxy is not universally established:** The collapse-efficiency argument hinges on this metric, which may not fully capture representational health.

## Confidence
*   **Rotation preserves magnitude (Mech. 1):** Medium. Strong empirical (norm overlap) and architectural (RMSNorm) support, but universality unproven.
*   **vMF confidence gate (Mech. 2):** Low-Medium. Innovative but hand-crafted; no ablations vs. learned alternatives.
*   **Pareto collapse-efficiency (Mech. 3):** Medium. Backed by Fig. 4, but hinges on validity of effective rank metric.
*   **Overall SOTA gains:** Medium-High. Multi-benchmark results, but reproducibility hindered by some undisclosed hyperparameters and layer selections.
