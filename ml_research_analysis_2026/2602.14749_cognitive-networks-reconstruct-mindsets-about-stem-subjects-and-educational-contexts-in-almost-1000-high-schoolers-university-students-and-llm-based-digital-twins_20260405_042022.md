# Cognitive networks reconstruct mindsets about STEM subjects and educational contexts in almost 1000 high-schoolers, University students and LLM-based digital twins

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14749  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14749  
- **Reference count:** 30  
- **Primary result:** A robust STEM–science dissonance exists: science is framed positively and concretely, while mathematics/statistics are framed negatively and abstractly, especially in high-math-anxiety individuals; LLMs replicate population-level valence but fail to capture the tight coupling between mathematics and anxiety observed in humans.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how attitudes toward STEM subjects develop from conceptual knowledge, educational experiences, and affect, with a focus on cognitive dissonance in STEM perceptions and the ability of large language models (LLMs) to replicate human educational mindsets. Using behavioral forma mentis networks (BFMNs) built from free associations and valence ratings of nearly 1000 participants—including high schoolers, university students, and STEM experts—alongside GPT-simulated counterparts, the analysis quantified semantic frames around key concepts (e.g., mathematics, science, professor) via valence auras, emotional profiles, network overlap, and concreteness. Primary findings reveal a consistent dissonance: science and research are positively framed, while core quantitative subjects (mathematics, statistics) carry negative, anxiety-related auras, amplified in high-anxiety subgroups and coupled with significantly less concrete representations. Human networks show substantially higher overlap between mathematics and anxiety frames compared to GPT simulations, which broadly replicate valence patterns but produce more abstract representations and miss the experiential coupling. This indicates that current LLMs lack the lived, affectively grounded knowledge that shapes human educational mindsets.

## Method Summary
The study constructed Behavioral Forma Mentis Networks (BFMNs) for multiple human subgroups (e.g., by education level, math anxiety) and GPT-simulated “digital twins” prompted with matching personas. For each subgroup, participants’ free associations for cue words were aggregated into unweighted, undirected graphs; nodes were assigned valence labels (positive/neutral/negative) via Kruskal-Wallis tests on individual valence ratings. Semantic frames—the immediate neighbor sets of target concepts (e.g., “mathematics”)—were extracted. Frame characteristics were quantified: valence aura (modal neighbor valence), mean concreteness (compared to a random baseline using Italian-adapted Brysbaert norms, yielding z-scores), and Jaccard similarity between frames (e.g., mathematics vs. anxiety). Non-parametric statistics (Kruskal-Wallis, z-tests) assessed differences across groups, with effect sizes where applicable.

## Key Results
- **STEM–science dissonance:** Science/research are consistently framed positively and concretely, while mathematics/statistics are framed negatively and with significantly lower-than-chance concreteness in high-anxiety subgroups (e.g., psychology undergraduates: mathematics frame z = -2.26, statistics z = -2.13).
- **Math-anxiety coupling:** Human networks show substantial Jaccard overlap between mathematics and anxiety frames (up to 0.13 in high-anxiety psychology students), whereas GPT simulations show near-zero overlap (<0.02).
- **LLM limitations:** GPT models replicate population-level valence patterns (e.g., positive science framing) but systematically produce more abstract representations for science and statistics and fail to capture the tight mathematics-anxiety coupling seen in humans, indicating gaps in modeling experience-based educational affect.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: BFMNs reconstruct group-level mindsets from associative knowledge and affect.
*Claim:* Behavioral forma mentis networks (BFMNs) reconstruct group-level mindsets by modeling associative knowledge (free associations) and affective valence together, revealing semantic frames around target concepts.  
*Mechanism:* Participants generate free associations for cue words and rate each word’s valence. These associations form unweighted, undirected graphs where nodes are words (with group-aggregated valence labels). A semantic frame is the immediate neighbor set of a target node. The valence aura (modal neighbor valence) and frame concreteness characterize the affective-cognitive neighborhood.  
*Core assumption:* Spontaneous free associations reflect latent cognitive organization, and aggregated group patterns reveal shared mindset structures; valence ratings annotate this structure with affect.  
*Evidence anchors:* The abstract describes BFMNs combining free associations and valence to analyze frames. Section 2.5.1 details network construction. Prior work (e.g., Abramski et al. 2023) validates BFMNs for capturing cognitive-affective biases like math anxiety.  
*Break condition:* If free associations are not spontaneous (e.g., influenced by task instructions or social desirability), the network may not reflect natural cognitive structure; group aggregation could also mask individual variation.

### Mechanism 2: STEM–science dissonance manifests as negative, abstract frames for anxiety-inducing quantitative subjects.
*Claim:* A robust STEM–science dissonance exists: “science” and “research” are framed positively and concretely, while core quantitative subjects (“mathematics,” “statistics”) are framed negatively and abstractly, especially in high-math-anxiety subgroups.  
*Mechanism:* Compare valence auras and concreteness z-scores of target frames across subgroups. Negative auras for math/statistics coupled with significantly lower-than-chance concreteness (z < -1.6449) indicate abstract, decontextualized representations. Positive, concrete frames for science indicate an idealized, outcome-oriented perception.  
*Core assumption:* Negative affect toward threatening domains (math/statistics) manifests as abstract, symbolic representations divorced from concrete experience, per cognitive anxiety models (Beck & Clark, 1997).  
*Evidence anchors:* The abstract reports science consistently positive; math/statistics negative/anxiety-related; high-anxiety frames less concrete than chance. Section 3.1 shows mathematics/statistics frames with negative auras and significant negative z-scores for concreteness in high-anxiety groups. Corroborated by Ciringione et al. (2025) on math anxiety and associative knowledge.  
*Break condition:* If concreteness norms are culturally mismatched or if anxiety measures do not capture trait/state distinctions reliably, the abstractness claim may not generalize.

### Mechanism 3: GPT digital twins replicate valence trends but miss experience-based affective coupling.
*Claim:* GPT-based “digital twins” replicate population-level valence patterns (e.g., positive science framing) but fail to capture experience-based, context-sensitive affect—specifically, they show weaker math-anxiety frame overlap and more abstract representations for key concepts.  
*Mechanism:* Construct GPT-simulated BFMNs using persona prompts matching human subgroups. Compare (a) Jaccard similarity between mathematics and anxiety frames; (b) frame concreteness z-scores. Humans show higher overlap (up to 0.13) and concrete frames for institutional concepts; GPT shows lower overlap (<0.02) and abstract science/statistics frames.  
*Core assumption:* LLMs, lacking lived experience, produce discursive, decontextualized associations that approximate cultural stereotypes but not the affective-cognitive integration formed through personal educational experiences.  
*Evidence anchors:* The abstract states GPT captures valence trends but is more abstract for science/statistics and fails to replicate math-anxiety coupling. Section 3.1.1 (Fig 2, Table 5) shows human math-anxiety Jaccard up to 0.13 vs. GPT <0.02 and GPT science/statistics frames more abstract. Prior work (Abramski et al. 2023) found GPT biases mirror human math anxiety only at aggregate level.  
*Break condition:* If LLM training incorporates more experiential data or fine-tuning on educational dialogues, the gap might close; current architecture may inherently lack embodiment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Behavioral Forma Mentis Networks (BFMNs)**  
  *Why needed here:* BFMNs are the core representational model linking associative knowledge and affect to reconstruct mindsets; understanding their construction (free associations + valence) is essential to interpret frame analyses.  
  *Quick check:* How does a valence aura differ from a node’s own valence rating, and why is this important for studying anxiety?

- **Concept: Semantic Frame and Concreteness Analysis**  
  *Why needed here:* Frames localize the cognitive-affective neighborhood of a target concept; concreteness quantifies experiential grounding versus abstraction, central to the anxiety-abstraction hypothesis.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a concept like “mathematics” be rated neutrally on its own but still have a negative valence aura?

- **Concept: LLM Simulation as Digital Twins**  
  *Why needed here:* The study compares human BFMNs to GPT-simulated ones; understanding the prompting strategy and limitations of LLMs is key to interpreting divergence in concreteness and overlap.  
  *Quick check:* What does it mean for an LLM to “mirror” human valence patterns but not their math-anxiety coupling?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Data ingestion -> BFMN construction (per-group aggregation, valence assignment) -> Frame extraction (1-hop neighbors) -> Feature computation (valence aura, concreteness z-score, Jaccard overlap) -> Statistical testing (non-parametric, effect sizes)

- **Critical path:**  
  Data cleaning → group-level BFMN → target frame extraction → concreteness matching (lemmatization, Brysbaert norms) → z-test against null → Jaccard overlap for anxiety coupling → interpret with cognitive anxiety theories.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Unweighted vs. weighted networks:** Simplicity (unweighted) vs. losing frequency information; chosen to mirror prior BFMN studies.  
  - **Group aggregation:** Captures shared mindset but obscures individual variation; anxiety split via median MAS-IT within sample.  
  - **α=0.1:** Tolerant threshold due to small/suboptimal samples; increases false positive risk but balances power.  
  - **Concreteness baseline:** Random sampling from Italian-converted Brysbaert norms; assumes word list representativeness for null.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low Jaccard between human math and anxiety frames in high-anxiety groups (<0.13) indicates weak coupling; GPT showing near-zero overlap signals missed experiential integration.  
  - GPT frames for science/statistics with significantly negative z-concreteness (e.g., z ≈ -2.4) reveal over-abstraction.  
  - Discrepancy between node’s own valence and its aura (e.g., neutral node in negative aura) may be missed if only direct ratings are used.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Mathematics frame analysis:** Compare valence auras and concreteness z-scores across human subgroups (high/low anxiety) and GPT; test Hypothesis 1 (negative, abstract in high anxiety).  
  2. **Science vs. Mathematics dissonance:** Directly compare science frame (expected positive/concrete) with mathematics frame (expected negative/abstract) within the same groups; quantify divergence.  
  3. **Math-anxiety overlap:** Compute Jaccard(Frame(mathematics), Frame(anxiety)) for all human and GPT groups; test Hypothesis 3 (GPT shows significantly lower overlap than humans, especially high-anxiety).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Causality of dissonance:** Does the observed STEM–science cognitive dissonance and abstract framing of quantitative subjects in high-anxiety students reflect a cause of math anxiety or a consequence of repeated negative educational experiences? The cross-sectional design forbids causal inference.
2. **Pedagogical mechanisms:** What specific experiential or pedagogical mechanisms cause core quantitative subjects to be perceived as more abstract and anxiety-inducing compared to science, and can targeted interventions that concretize these subjects reduce this dissonance?
3. **LLM discrepancy:** Why do current LLM-based digital twins systematically produce more abstract representations for science and statistics and fail to replicate the tight coupling between mathematics and anxiety observed in humans, despite capturing population-level valence patterns? Is it due to training data, architectural limitations, or prompting strategy?
4. **Cultural specificity:** To what extent are the observed cognitive-affective signatures culturally specific to the Italian educational context and samples, versus universal across different educational systems and cultures?
5. **Predictive validity:** Do the network-level signatures (e.g., high Jaccard overlap between Mathematics and Anxiety frames) predict behavioral outcomes such as course selection, dropout rates, or academic performance in STEM fields beyond self-reported anxiety scales?

## Limitations
- **Concreteness baseline:** Sampling method for null distributions (with/without replacement, frequency constraints) is unspecified, risking inflated z-scores.  
- **GPT simulation parameters:** API details (temperature, seed, prompt engineering) and how “creative/original” responses were enforced are unreported, limiting reproducibility of digital twin results.  
- **Aggregation and cultural bias:** Group-level BFMNs may obscure individual variation; Italian concreteness norms and language-specific associations restrict generalizability to other linguistic contexts.

## Confidence
- **High:** BFMN methodology—robust construction from prior work, consistent network logic, and validated for cognitive-affective biases.  
- **Medium-High:** STEM–science dissonance—replicated across multiple subgroups with convergent valence and concreteness metrics.  
- **Medium-Low:** GPT digital twins—failure to capture math-anxiety coupling likely reflects inherent LLM limitations (lack of lived experience), but depends on unreported prompt strategies.
