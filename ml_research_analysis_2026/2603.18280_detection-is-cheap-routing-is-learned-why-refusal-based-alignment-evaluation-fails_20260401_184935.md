# Detection Is Cheap, Routing Is Learned: Why Refusal-Based Alignment Evaluation Fails

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18280  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18280  
- **Reference count:** 6  
- **Primary result:** Linear probing train-set accuracy is non-diagnostic for alignment encoding, and refusal-based evaluation misses narrative steering; censorship is controlled by a learned, lab-specific routing mechanism that can be surgically edited.

## Executive Summary
This paper exposes a fundamental flaw in how we evaluate AI alignment: focusing on concept detection (via probing) and refusal rates overlooks the critical "routing" mechanism that maps detected sensitive concepts to behavioral policies like censorship. Using political censorship in Chinese-origin models as a case study, the authors show that perfect probe accuracy on training data is trivial even for meaningless topic pairs, while held-out tests confirm genuine concept encoding. More importantly, they identify a directional routing flag in middle layers; surgically removing it eliminates censorship in most models but causes factual corruption in one due to knowledge entanglement. Crucially, newer Qwen models shift from hard refusal to invisible narrative steering, rendering refusal-only benchmarks blind to ongoing censorship. The work demonstrates that alignment mechanisms are model-specific and lie in the routing logic, not just concept detection.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes nine open-weight models from five labs (Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, MiniCPM, Phi) using two Chinese political prompt corpora, each containing approximately 48 prompts per category. Linear probes (ridge classifiers) are trained on hidden states to test separability of sensitive vs. control topics, including null controls (e.g., food vs. technology). Probe performance is evaluated on held-out prompts to distinguish genuine political-sensitivity encoding from trivial separability due to high dimensionality. Political-safety directions are extracted via mean activation differences. Surgical ablation applies rank-one projection (`h' = h - α*(h·v_hat)*v_hat`) at various middle layers to disrupt the routing flag. Behavioral effects are measured via refusal rates, content taxonomy (accurate, evasive, wrong event), and a Gemini judge’s narrative steering score (1–5). Layer-wise cosine similarity between political and safety directions assesses routing geometry. Cross-model direction transfer tests lab-specificity.

## Key Results
- Probe train-set accuracy reaches ~100% even for null topic pairs, while held-out cross-validation distinguishes genuine political-sensitivity encoding (91.7–100% vs. ~50% chance).
- Ablating the political direction eliminates censorship in three of four tested models but causes 72% confabulation in Qwen3-8B due to knowledge entanglement.
- Within the Qwen family, hard refusal drops from 25% to 0% across generations while narrative steering rises to the maximum score (5.0), making censorship invisible to refusal-only metrics.
- Political-safety directions are orthogonal in four models but coupled in one (GLM), and cross-model direction transfer fails (cosine ~0.004), confirming lab-specific routing geometries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Probe train-set accuracy is non-diagnostic for alignment-specific encoding.
- **Mechanism:** In high-dimensional spaces with small sample sizes, linear classifiers achieve perfect separation on any two distinct groups due to the curse of dimensionality. Null controls (e.g., food vs. technology) yield 100% accuracy, proving that train-set performance alone does not indicate a meaningful alignment-related concept.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 100% train accuracy on null topics; Section 3.1 shows null probes hit 100% at all layers; permutation baselines also reach 100%. Corpora like *PsychEthicsBench* rely on refusal signals, mirroring this pitfall.
- **Break condition:** Sample size becomes comparable to hidden dimension or groups are not linearly separable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Routing from detected concepts to behavioral policies is a learned, lab-specific mechanism that can be surgically edited.
- **Mechanism:** Post-training installs a direction in middle layers acting as a routing flag; ablating this flag removes censorship in most models. Confabulation in Qwen3-8B reveals architecture-specific entanglement of factual knowledge with the flag. Geometry (orthogonal vs. coupled) and failed cross-model transfer confirm lab-specificity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: ablation eliminates censorship in 3/4 models but causes 72% confabulation; geometry varies; cross-transfer fails. Section 3.3: ablation yields 0% wrong-event confabulation except Qwen3-8B (39%). Section 3.4: cosine analysis shows orthogonality in four models, coupling in GLM, cross-model cosine ~0.004. *HiddenGuard* uses a "specialized representation router", aligning with this mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Knowledge entangled with policy flag (ablation causes confabulation); routing distributed beyond a single direction (ablation ineffective).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Refusal-based evaluation systematically misses steering-based content control, which can replace hard refusal while maintaining censorship.
- **Mechanism:** Models can shift from refusing sensitive queries to answering with maximally steered narratives (high CCP framing scores). This evolution is invisible to benchmarks that only count refusals.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: Qwen refusal drops 25%→0%, steering rises to 5.0 across generations. Section 3.6 (Table 7b): Qwen2.5 refusal 6.2%, steering 4.33; Qwen3.5 refusal 0%, steering 5.00. Section 3.7: AI judges overdetect framing but trend holds. *Can LLMs Refuse Questions They Do Not Know?* highlights refusal metric limitations for knowledge-aware behavior.
- **Break condition:** Evaluation includes both refusal counts and steering quality metrics; models do not adopt steering as dominant mode; steering cannot be distinguished from neutral answers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Linear probing generalization vs. train-set performance.  
  **Why needed:** Distinguish trivial separability from meaningful alignment encoding.  
  **Quick check:** With 48 samples in a 4096-dimensional space, 100% train accuracy is expected for any two distinct categories; held-out cross-validation reveals genuine generalization.

- **Concept:** Contrastive activation analysis and surgical ablation.  
  **Why needed:** Identify a causally influential direction and test its role; without it, only correlations are visible.  
  **Quick check:** Extract political direction as mean activation difference between sensitive/control prompts; ablating it should change outputs on political queries but not unrelated ones.

- **Concept:** Output policy spectrum (refusal, steering, factual answering).  
  **Why needed:** Alignment manifests at various points on this spectrum; measuring only refusal ignores other censorship modes.  
  **Quick check:** Two models detect a sensitive concept: one refuses, the other gives a steered answer. Measure difference via narrative framing scores or content taxonomy.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input → Embedding → Early layers (concept encoding) → Middle layers (routing flag application) → Late layers (policy-committed generation)

**Critical path:**  
1. Token embedding.  
2. Early layers build representations; political vs. control become linearly separable by layer 3.  
3. Middle layers (~40–65% depth) apply routing: activation projected onto routing direction; if magnitude exceeds threshold, censorship policy triggers.  
4. Late layers generate text conditioned on selected policy (refusal, steered narrative, or factual answer).  
5. Surgical ablation removes routing component at chosen layer, forcing alternative path (often factual).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Knowledge vs. policy flag separation: Orthogonal (e.g., GLM, DeepSeek) allows clean ablation; entangled (Qwen3-8B) risks confabulation when edited. Tradeoff: entanglement may make censorship robust but harms factual integrity on intervention.  
- Refusal vs. steering metrics: Refusal is easy to measure but can be replaced by stealthier steering. Systems optimized for refusal-only benchmarks may appear safe while still censoring via narrative control.  
- Direction specificity: Extracted political direction depends on prompt corpus; narrow corpus may couple political with safety directions, affecting cross-concept interventions.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High confabulation after political-direction ablation → knowledge entanglement.  
- Cross-model direction transfer fails (cosine ≈ 0) → routing geometry is model-specific; interventions must be discovered per model.  
- Zero refusal but maximum steering → refusal-only evaluation blinded; need steering metrics.  
- No behavioral change after ablation despite high probe accuracy → no routing installed (e.g., Yi model).  
- Small behavioral screens (n=8) yield unstable estimates → require larger prompt sets for reliable effects.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Null control probing:** Train ridge probes on unrelated topic pairs (food/technology, sports/arts, geography/music). Expect near-perfect train accuracy while held-out political probe performance varies significantly.
2. **Held-out category generalization:** Perform leave-one-category-out cross-validation on political prompts. If probe generalizes to unseen topics, it indicates genuine political-sensitivity encoding.
3. **Surgical ablation sweep:** Extract political direction from v1 corpus at layers 6,9,12,15,18,24. For each layer, sweep α∈{2,5,8,12,20} on a held-out Tiananmen subset. Pick smallest α that reduces refusal to near zero on a separate held-out set. Apply to full adversarial corpus and measure refusal/content distribution changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions, but its findings and limitations point to several important research directions:

- How do routing mechanisms generalize to other alignment targets (e.g., toxicity, bias) and other model families beyond the nine Chinese-origin models studied?
- Can we develop robust, judge-free metrics for narrative steering that correlate with human judgment?
- What is the effect of model scale and architecture on the entanglement between factual knowledge and routing flags?
- Under what conditions does ablation cause confabulation, and can we design disentanglement techniques to enable safe editing?
- How distributed is the routing mechanism across layers, and does a single-direction ablation capture the full policy signal?

## Limitations
- **Data availability:** The prompt corpora (v1, v3, Chinese set) used in the study are not publicly available. Reconstructing them from descriptions risks experimental misalignment and hinders reproducibility.
- **Steering metric dependency:** Narrative steering scores rely on the proprietary Gemini 2.5 Flash model. Its inherent biases, scale interpretation, and lack of cross-judge consistency verification limit the robustness and generalizability of steering quantification.
- **Generalizability scope:** The study focuses exclusively on Chinese-origin models and political censorship. It is unknown whether similar routing mechanisms exist for other alignment domains (e.g., toxicity, bias) or in models from different labs (e.g., Llama, Claude) trained with alternative alignment techniques.
- **Methodological assumptions:** Direction extraction is based on a small number of prompts (~48), which may yield unstable direction estimates. Moreover, the rank-one ablation approach assumes that routing is captured by a single vector; if the routing signal is distributed across multiple dimensions or layers, ablation may be incomplete or miss critical components.

## Confidence
- **Train-set probe accuracy is non-diagnostic:** High. Null controls consistently reach 100%; held-out CV shows meaningful spread.
- **Routing is learned, lab-specific, and surgically editable:** Medium. Ablation alters behavior in 3/4 models and geometry varies, but confabulation in Qwen3-8B and limited coverage reduce certainty.
- **Refusal-only evaluation misses steering:** Medium. Qwen family shows clear shift, but steering metric is judge-dependent and only one family examined.

## Next Checks
1. **Null control replication** – For all nine models, train probes on three unrelated topic pairs (food/technology, sports/arts, geography/music). Confirm near-perfect train accuracy while held-out political probe performance varies.
2. **Blind human steering validation** – Recruit expert human raters to score the same 1–5 CCP framing scale on outputs from Qwen3.5 and DeepSeek (baseline/ablated). Compute inter-rater reliability and compare with Gemini scores.
3. **Cross-family direction transfer** – Extract political directions from each lab’s flagship model and apply as ablations to all other models. Measure refusal changes and (human-validated) steering scores to test lab-specificity.
