# The Phenomenology of Hallucinations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13911
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Models detect uncertain inputs in a high-dimensional geometric subspace but fail to integrate this signal with output generation, causing hallucinations; direct intervention restores refusal with near-perfect reliability.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often produce confident yet incorrect statements—hallucinations—despite internally recognizing uncertainty. This paper investigates the geometric and topological properties of internal representations that underlie this phenomenon. Across multiple architectures, uncertain inputs are found to occupy distinct representation spaces with up to 2–3 times higher intrinsic dimensionality than factual inputs, indicating successful geometric detection. However, this uncertainty signal becomes functionally silent: it migrates into low-sensitivity subspaces of the unembedding matrix, Fisher sensitivity collapses along the boundary direction, and gradients from uncertainty-related tokens are blocked. Topologically, the uncertainty manifold fragments into over a hundred disconnected components, preventing a coherent abstention state. Crucially, causal interventions that directly connect internal uncertainty signals to output logits restore appropriate refusal behavior with near-perfect reliability, confirming that detection succeeds but integration fails.

Standard training objectives reward confident prediction uniformly, creating a loss landscape without minima for expressing uncertainty. As a result, the internal uncertainty signal is amplified in low-sensitivity subspaces and functionally disconnected from the output layer. Improving model honesty may require architectural changes or training losses that preserve readout connectivity for uncertainty signals.

## Method Summary
The study employs a suite of representational geometry and topology tools to diagnose hallucination mechanisms. Representations from various layers are analyzed to compute Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID), boundary vectors separating factual and uncertain inputs, Fisher information sensitivity, and persistent homology (β0) to quantify manifold fragmentation. Additionally, linear probes are trained to detect uncertainty, and a readout bypass intervention directly injects uncertainty signals into the output logits. The experiments primarily focus on LLaMA 3.2 3B with some additional models, using datasets of factual and unanswerable queries. The authors provide no detailed reproduction notes, but the methodology is described with mathematical rigor in the paper.

## Key Results
- Uncertain inputs occupy representation spaces with 2–3× higher intrinsic dimensionality than factual inputs.
- Topological analysis reveals fragmented, disconnected uncertainty manifolds (β0 > 100).
- Causal interventions that directly connect internal uncertainty signals to output logits restore appropriate refusal behavior with near-perfect reliability (e.g., 100% on Llama 3.2 3B).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Models reliably detect uncertain inputs but encode this signal in a high-dimensional, geometrically distinct subspace ("reservoir") separate from output-sensitive directions.
- **Mechanism:** During forward processing, representations for unanswerable queries diverge from factual ones along a stable "boundary vector" `b_l`. These uncertain representations exhibit 2–3× higher Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID), greater spectral entropy, and occupy a "low-sensitivity reservoir" where their magnitude grows but their projection onto vocabulary-aligned directions (`W_U`) remains weak. Detection succeeds geometrically but remains functionally silent.
- **Core assumption:** Representational geometry can be decomposed into an "answerability" axis (`b_l`) and orthogonal subspaces with differential coupling to output logits.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "uncertain inputs are reliably identified, occupying representation spaces with 2–3× higher intrinsic dimensionality than factual inputs."
  - [section 5] "Early in training, models partition their representation space into... a higher-dimensional, weakly vocabulary-coupled reservoir used for contextual storage... and uncertainty encoding."
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; neighbors discuss detection but not the geometric partitioning mechanism. Assumption: the "reservoir" is a learned structural feature.
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty representations lacked consistent directionality (low boundary stability `cos(b_l, b_{l-1})`) or did not show elevated LID relative to factual inputs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The manifold of uncertain representations fragments topologically into disconnected components (`β0 > 100`) rather than converging to a unified "abstention" state.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of forming a single cluster for "I don't know," the model's internal processing splits uncertain inputs into many disconnected regions. These fragments drift toward different output vocabulary basins. This fragmentation prevents a coherent abstention signal from emerging; instead, partial, conflicting activations are amplified separately.
- **Core assumption:** A unified uncertainty state would manifest as a single connected component (`β0 ≈ 1`) in the representation space near the decision boundary.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] "Persistent homology shows progressive fragmentation... β0 increases from 1 at layer 0 to 93 at layer 15 and 119 at layer 27."
  - [section 5] "If uncertain inputs converged toward a unified refusal state, β0 would remain near one; instead, the model partitions uncertainty into multiple clusters."
  - [corpus] No direct anchor; neighbors do not apply persistent homology to hallucination. Assumption: topological fragmentation is a cause, not just a correlate, of failed integration.
- **Break condition:** If `β0` remained low (≤2) across layers for uncertain inputs, indicating a unified manifold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Gradient-based training pressures (cross-entropy loss) actively suppress the functional coupling between the detected uncertainty signal and the output layer, causing "Fisher sensitivity collapse" and "gradient blockage."
- **Mechanism:** The next-token cross-entropy loss provides no attractor for abstention; it uniformly rewards confident prediction. Optimization therefore amplifies activity magnitude along any direction that reduces loss, even if that direction is the uncertainty boundary. This leads to: (a) low Fisher sensitivity (`F(b_l) ≈ 0`)—perturbing along `b_l` does not change output probabilities; (b) gradient blockage—gradients from uncertainty-related tokens have near-zero alignment with `b_l`. The detected signal exists but is readout-invisible.
- **Core assumption:** The loss landscape under standard LM training contains no basin corresponding to "express uncertainty"; vertices of the probability simplex are the only stable minima.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "uncertainty migrates into low-sensitivity subspaces, becoming geometrically amplified yet functionally silent... Fisher sensitivity collapses, and gradient flow from uncertainty-related tokens is blocked."
  - [section 6] "The loss landscape contains no local minima corresponding to high-entropy (uncertain) states... Training effectively partitions the representation space into Voronoi cells centered on vocabulary embeddings."
  - [section 4, Table 4] "Readout bypass intervention... restores appropriate refusal behavior with near-perfect reliability... confirming that detection succeeds but integration fails."
- **Break condition:** If Fisher sensitivity along `b_l` remained high in late layers, or if gradients from uncertainty tokens showed positive alignment with `b_l`.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID)**
  - *Why needed here:* LID is the primary metric demonstrating that uncertain inputs occupy fundamentally different (higher-dimensional) geometric regimes than factual inputs. This is the first piece of evidence for "detection."
  - *Quick check question:* If LID for uncertain inputs is *lower* than for factual inputs across late layers, what would that imply about the paper's core claim?
- **Concept: Boundary Vector (`b_l`)**
  - *Why needed here:* This operationalizes the abstract notion of "uncertainty" as a concrete, trackable geometric direction. All subsequent analysis (probe sensitivity, gradient alignment, low-sensitivity ratio) is referenced to this vector.
  - *Quick check question:* The boundary vector is computed as the difference between class centroids. What would it mean if `cos(b_l, b_{l-1})` was consistently near -1?
- **Concept: Unembedding Matrix (`W_U`) Low-Sensitivity Subspace**
  - *Why needed here:* This explains the *mechanism* of decoupling. Even large activations along `b_l` have little effect on logits if `b_l` lies primarily in the null space of `W_U`. This bridges geometry (hidden states) and function (output).
  - *Quick check question:* The paper computes `lowSens_m(b_l) = ||P_{V^⊥_m} b_l|| / ||b_l||`. If this value were near 0 for a moderate `m`, what would that say about the coupling?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Residual Stream -> Attention Layers -> MLP Layers -> Unembedding (`W_U`)
- **Critical path:** Input → Early Layer detection (formation of `b_l`, LID rise) → Mid Layer fracture (β0 increase, MLP amplification) → Late Layer breach (`F(b_l)` collapse, gradient blockage) → Output (logits from weak coupling + amplified magnitude).
- **Design tradeoffs:** The "reservoir" (low-sensitivity subspace) is adaptive: it allows complex internal computation without premature output commitment. The cost is that uncertainty signals stored there are vulnerable to MLP amplification and weak readout coupling.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **High LID gap:** `LID_Hallucination / LID_Factual > 2x` in mid-late layers.
  2. **Fisher sensitivity collapse:** `F(b_l)` drops to near-zero while `||b_l||` remains large.
  3. **Gradient blockage:** `cos(∇_h Σ z_k, b_l) ≈ 0` for uncertainty tokens `k∈U`.
  4. **Topological fracture:** `β0` increases dramatically (>50) for uncertain inputs.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate LID gap:** Compute per-layer LID for factual vs. impossible/hallucination inputs on a target model (e.g., Llama 3.2 3B). Confirm the 2–3× ratio appears and deepens with depth.
  2. **Probe boundary stability and visibility:** For each layer, compute `b_l`, `cos(b_l, b_{l-1})`, and `lowSens_m(b_l)` for `m=100`. Expect high stability (>0.9) and low visibility (<0.5) in late layers for uncertain inputs.
  3. **Test readout bypass intervention:** Train a linear logistic regression probe on layer-16 hidden states (factual vs. uncertain). On inference, add `γ * P(uncertain|h_16) * e_unsure` to final logits. Expect refusal rate to approach 100% if integration is the sole failure.

## Limitations
- Causal nature of geometric partitioning and fragmentation remains inferential; break conditions untested.
- Study restricted primarily to LLaMA 3.2 3B; broader model coverage needed.
- Gradient-blockage analysis relies on linear alignment metrics; non-linear effects unexplored.

## Confidence
- Overall geometric disconnect: High
- Mechanism 1 (high-dimensional reservoir): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (topological fragmentation): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (gradient-driven decoupling): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Generalization sweep: Repeat core measurements (LID, β0, Fisher sensitivity) across ≥5 model families and scales up to 70B.
2. Fragmentation-reduction intervention: Add contrastive loss to collapse uncertain representations into a single cluster; assess if refusal emerges spontaneously.
3. Break-condition falsification: Force uncertainty representations into low-LID, high-coupling subspaces via targeted fine-tuning; verify if hallucination rate drops.
