# Towards a Mechanistic Understanding of Large Reasoning Models: A Survey of Training, Inference, and Failures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.19928
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19928
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) compresses model output entropy and improves benchmark pass@1 without expanding underlying reasoning capability beyond the base model.

## Executive Summary
This survey synthesizes recent research to address the mechanistic opacity of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). It finds that reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) leads to more deterministic, higher-performing outputs on benchmarks but does not enhance the base model's intrinsic reasoning skills, whereas supervised fine-tuning (SFT) promotes exploration of diverse solution paths. RL training dynamics follow a two-stage process featuring an "aha moment" and internal changes such as activation distribution shifts and layer-consistent parameter rotations. LRMs develop distinct reasoning topologies and behavioral primitives (e.g., verification, backtracking) encoded as steerable latent directions, while base models only possess latent skills that RL strategically activates. Unintended behaviors include overthinking (inverted U-shaped accuracy), RL-induced hallucinations from reduced abstention, and unfaithful chain-of-thoughts where hidden states better predict decisions than verbalized reasoning.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes findings from empirical studies on LRMs, primarily using Llama-based models and benchmarks like GSM8K. It analyzes training dynamics (RLVR, SFT), internal representations (activation patching, parameter similarity), and behavioral outcomes (reasoning topologies, failure modes). No original experiments are presented; the conclusions are drawn from a literature review of 38 references.

## Key Results
- RLVR reduces output entropy and boosts pass@1 without expanding true reasoning capability; SFT increases exploration of solution paths.
- RL training exhibits a two-stage dynamic with an "aha moment" and internal effects including activation changes and layer-consistent parameter rotations.
- LRMs encode reasoning strategies as steerable latent directions; base models have latent skills, and RL teaches strategic activation, leading to failures like overthinking, hallucinations, and unfaithful CoTs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RLVR compresses output entropy by optimizing for verifiable rewards, which favors deterministic, high-reward answers and reduces exploratory or uncertain responses. SFT increases exploration by imitating diverse chain-of-thought examples, encouraging the model to sample multiple reasoning paths. The two-stage RL dynamics may emerge from an initial phase of rapid reward learning followed by a refinement phase where internal representations reorganize. Steerable latent directions arise because reasoning primitives (e.g., verification) are encoded linearly in activation space, allowing targeted interventions. Unfaithful CoTs occur when the model's internal decision-making diverges from its verbalized explanation, often due to post-hoc rationalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**
  - *Why needed:* Enables training on tasks with automatic correctness checks (e.g., math) without human labels, driving performance on benchmarks.
  - *Quick check:* Train with RLVR on GSM8K; compare pass@1 and output entropy (token-level variance) to a supervised baseline.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
  - *Why needed:* Provides explicit reasoning trajectories to bootstrap exploration and diversity in solution generation.
  - *Quick check:* Measure uniqueness of generated chain-of-thought paths (e.g., distinct token sequences) for SFT vs. RLVR models.
- **Activation Patching**
  - *Why needed:* Identifies which internal states causally influence specific behaviors by swapping activations between inputs.
  - *Quick check:* Patch activations from a "correct" input into an "incorrect" one during forward pass; assess accuracy transfer.
- **Steerable Latent Directions**
  - *Why needed:* Linear probes reveal directions in activation space corresponding to reasoning strategies (e.g., verification), enabling controlled generation.
  - *Quick check:* Extract a "verification" direction via logistic regression on activations labeled by verification occurrence; steer along it and count verification phrases.
- **Layer-Consistent Parameter Rotations**
  - *Why needed:* Suggests coordinated, global changes in model parameters during RL training, possibly representing strategy consolidation.
  - *Quick check:* Compute cosine similarity of parameter updates (before vs. after RL) across layers; look for consistent rotation patterns (e.g., orthogonal shifts).
- **Unfaithful Chain-of-Thought**
  - *Why needed:* Exposes misalignment between a model's stated reasoning and its actual decision process, impacting interpretability.
  - *Quick check:* Train a probe on hidden states to predict final answer; compare accuracy to using the verbalized CoT as input. Higher probe accuracy indicates unfaithfulness.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Base Language Model → Training (SFT/RLVR) → Internal Representations (activation distribution shifts, layer-consistent rotations) → Behavioral Primitives (steerable latent directions for verification/backtracking) → Inference (CoT generation with potential failures).
- **Critical path:** Training data (verifiable rewards or SFT examples) induces representation changes (activation, parameters), which enable emergent reasoning strategies (steerable topologies), ultimately shaping inference behavior and failure modes.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Exploration (SFT) vs. exploitation (RLVR); base capability preservation vs. efficiency gains (entropy compression); interpretability via linear steerability vs. full non-linear distributed representations.
- **Failure signatures:** Overthinking (excessive tokens before answer, U-shaped accuracy curve); RL-induced hallucinations (increased false positives due to reduced abstention); unfaithful CoT (hidden states better predict answer than verbalized reasoning).
- **First experiments:**
  1. Compare layer-wise activation statistics (mean, variance) for RLVR vs. SFT models on identical inputs to quantify compression and distribution shifts.
  2. Extract steerable vectors for verification/backtracking via linear probes, then apply steering and measure changes in primitive frequency and task accuracy.
  3. Ablate activation components identified in layer-consistent rotations (e.g., zero out top-k PCA directions) and evaluate impact on pass@1, solution diversity, and overthinking rates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly enumerated; the survey implies future work through its limitations and suggested validation checks.

## Limitations
- Over-reliance on specific model families (Llama-based) and synthetic benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K) limits generalizability across architectures and real-world tasks.
- Short-horizon evaluations dominate; long-term stability and deployment impacts of mechanisms like activation steering are unknown.
- Causal mechanisms for key phenomena (e.g., "aha moment," layer-consistent rotations) remain correlational; the *why* behind observed changes is not fully established.

## Confidence
- RLVR compresses output entropy & boosts pass@1 without expanding base capability: Medium
- SFT increases exploration: Medium
- Two-stage RL dynamics & internal effects (activation changes, rotations): Low-Medium
- Reasoning topologies as steerable latent directions; RL teaches strategic activation: Low
- Failure modes (overthinking U-shape, RL hallucinations, unfaithful CoT): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Cross-architecture replication: Re-run core mechanistic analyses (activation distribution, steering vector extraction) on non-Llama models (e.g., Mistral, GPT-style) using identical training recipes and benchmarks.
2. Longitudinal & transfer study: After RL/RLVR training, evaluate (a) immediate in-distribution performance, (b) held-out similar tasks, and (c) after 30 days for stability and drift in "aha moment" dynamics and activation patterns.
3. Ablation of internal signals: Intervene on specific activation clusters or steerable directions (e.g., zeroing, negating) and measure differential impacts on pass@1, solution path diversity, and failure rates to establish necessity.
