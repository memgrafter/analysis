# On the Ability of Transformers to Verify Plans

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19954
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19954
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Transformers exhibit inconsistent performance on planning tasks; well‑formed/delete‑free domains generalize to longer plans, while STRIPS and conditional effects fail.

## Executive Summary
Transformer models are evaluated for their ability to verify plans in AI planning domains, focusing on length generalization when both sequence length and vocabulary (object count) increase. The paper introduces C*-RASP, an extension of the C-RASP formal language that supports simultaneous growth in sequence length and alphabet size, and defines a learning framework based on Symbolic Limit Transformers that guarantees length generalization when a task is expressible in C*-RASP. Theoretically, the authors show that plan verification lies in C*-RASP for delete‑free and well‑formed STRIPS domains, but not for full STRIPS or domains with conditional effects. Empirical experiments across three domains (Heavy Grippers, Colors, Lights Out) confirm these predictions: well‑formed and delete‑free variants maintain near‑perfect accuracy on plans up to 200 steps even when trained on ≤100 steps and variable objects, while STRIPS and conditional‑effects variants show degraded or chance performance on longer or more complex plans. This work provides a formal explanation for previously inconsistent transformer performance on planning tasks and demonstrates that domain reformulation (e.g., to well‑formed) yields reliable length generalization.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a theoretical framework based on C*-RASP, an extension of the C‑RASP language that accounts for simultaneously increasing sequence length and vocabulary size. Within this framework, a Symbolic Limit Transformer—an idealized model with translation‑invariant, local product functions—can express any C*-RASP program, and its finite‑width approximations are guaranteed to generalize to longer sequences when the target function lies in the class. The authors theoretically classify plan verification tasks (a core planning problem) into those that are in C*-RASP (delete‑free and well‑formed STRIPS) and those that are not (full STRIPS, conditional effects). Empirically, they train standard decoder‑only transformers (GPT‑2 style) on synthetic planning datasets derived from three domains, varying plan length and number of objects. The models use absolute positional encodings or no positional encodings, a locality‑encouraging regularizer, and are trained to output a binary verdict token. Performance is measured in‑distribution (≤100 steps) and out‑of‑distribution (>100 steps) to assess length generalization.

## Key Results
- Plan verification is expressible in C*-RASP for delete‑free and well‑formed STRIPS domains, guaranteeing length generalization; it is not expressible for full STRIPS or domains with conditional effects.
- Empirically, models trained on well‑formed/delete‑free variants maintain >95% accuracy on plans up to 200 steps, even with variable object counts and OOD lengths.
- In contrast, STRIPS variants degrade with length (e.g., Colors STRIPS: 100% → 80%) and conditional‑effects variants stay at ~50% accuracy, matching theoretical predictions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Planning domains that are *delete-free* or *well-formed* allow transformers to learn a length-generalizable verification algorithm.

**Mechanism:** In these domains, the truth value of every ground proposition can be tracked by a simple counter (delete‑free) or a deterministic toggle (well‑formed). The C*-RASP program constructs a sequence of local operations that, for each action, match its arguments to the relevant initial/goal propositions via the match predicate. Counting additions and deletions yields a boolean “valid plan” output independent of number of objects or plan length.

**Core assumption:** Transformers with absolute positional encodings can simulate any C*-RASP program when the program is expressed using only local operations and translation‑invariant interactions.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “Plan verification is in C*-RASP for delete‑free and well‑formed STRIPS domains (guaranteeing generalization).”  
- [section 3.1 & 3.2] The constructive proofs give explicit C*-RASP programs that verify plans by counting or toggling, using the match predicate to link action arguments to proposition arguments.  
- [corpus] The neighboring papers (e.g., *Quantitative Bounds for Length Generalization in Transformers*) reinforce that tasks expressible in C‑RASP‑like languages exhibit length generalization; our extension to variable alphabets preserves this property for the planning subclasses.

**Break condition:** If the domain’s actions do not satisfy well‑formedness or delete‑free constraints (e.g., full STRIPS with conditional effects), the verification task may fall outside C*-RASP, breaking the guarantee.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The *match predicate* χ(i,j) in C*-RASP enables transformers to reason about object identity without memorizing absolute IDs, thus supporting generalization to new objects.

**Mechanism:** χ(i,j) checks that a set of tokens at positions near i and j are equal after fixed offsets. This implements a form of pattern matching that is invariant under renaming of the extended alphabet. In the C*-RASP program, it ties an action’s object arguments to the corresponding proposition arguments regardless of the specific symbol names used, allowing the same logic to apply whenever novel object names appear.

**Core assumption:** The transformer’s product functions can realize the match predicate with translation‑invariant and local pairwise interactions between token embeddings. The model learns to compare embeddings rather than rely on fixed embeddings for specific object IDs.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “C*-RASP … an extension of C‑RASP designed to … simultaneously growth in sequence length and vocabulary size.”  
- [section 3.2] Definition 3.3 introduces the match predicate; later, the C*-RASP constructions for delete‑free and well‑formed domains crucially rely on it to align actions and propositions.  
- [corpus] The length‑generalization framework of Huang et al. (2025b) shows that transformers with product functions can implement such local equality checks; our results extend that to variable alphabets.

**Break condition:** If the plan representation requires comparing non‑local or non‑linear mappings between object references (e.g., arbitrary renamings not captured by fixed offsets), the match predicate as defined would be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** For STRIPS with arbitrary delete effects or with conditional effects, plan verification is not in C*-RASP, implying that transformers cannot learn to generalize on those domains.

**Mechanism:** Those verification tasks can encode the FlipFlop or PARITY regular languages. For STRIPS, determining whether a proposition holds at the end requires identifying the *last* action that affected it—a FlipFlop pattern. For conditional effects, the reduction yields PARITY. Both languages are provably outside C‑RASP[Pos] (and thus C*-RASP) because they require non‑local, non‑star‑free computations that exceed the expressive power of local, translation‑invariant product functions.

**Core assumption:** The class of functions computable by a symbolic limit transformer (and thus by any transformer that converges under the inference procedure) equals C*-RASP. If a task is not in this class, no finite‑width APE/NoPE transformer can length‑generalize on it.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “STRIPS variant accuracy drops significantly… Conditional effects variant remains at chance level (~50%).”  
- [section 3.1] Theorem 3.1 explicitly states that STRIPS and conditional effects are not in C‑RASP[Pos]; Theorem 3.6 lifts this to C*-RASP.  
- [corpus] Huang et al. (2025b) proved that PARITY and FlipFlop are not in C‑RASP[Pos]; our Theorem 3.5 shows they remain outside C*-RASP.

**Break condition:** When the planning domain admits a reduction to FlipFlop or PARITY (i.e., contains actions with delete effects or conditional branches), the verification language is outside C*-RASP, so no length generalization is expected regardless of training data size.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
- **Token embeddings:** Finite‑alphabet matrix `E` for predicates/action names; extended embeddings `c_k` (learnable) for object IDs.  
- **Positional encodings:** `p_t` for absolute positions (APE) or none (NoPE).  
- **Attention blocks:** Scaled dot‑product with causal mask; computed via product functions `α_attn`.  
- **MLP blocks:** Applied after attention; implemented with `A_l`, `B_l`, activation ψ; contributes `β_mlp` product functions.  
- **Unembedding:** Linear map `U` over the full vocabulary Ψ; final logits before the verdict token.  
- **Regularizer:** Penalizes distant attention and large norms, encouraging locality.

**Critical path:**  
1. Input sequence `<init>…<plan>…<goal>…` is tokenized.  
2. Each token is embedded as `E[w]` if w∈Σ, else `c_k + p_i`.  
3. The token embeddings pass through `L` transformer layers (attention+MLP).  
4. The final hidden state at the `<verdict>` position is unembedded to produce logits for “correct” vs “incorrect”.  
5. Training uses causal language modeling on the verdict token; at inference we compare the two verdict logits.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **APE vs NoPE:** APE is required for tasks that need absolute positional relations (e.g., counting up to a position); NoPE suffices for tasks without.  
- **Finite precision:** Rounding of attention logits and exp() emulates hardware constraints and prevents infinitesimal changes from snowballing.  
- **Regularizer ζ(T):** Simpler than Huang et al.’s rank penalty, but still penalizes distant token‑token interactions to promote length generalization.  
- **Model width `d`:** Unbounded in theory; practical implementations must balance width/depth for expressivity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High in‑distribution (ID) accuracy but a significant drop on out‑of‑distribution (OOD) longer plans → violation of length generalization.  
- Accuracy near chance on a domain (e.g., Lights Out conditional effects) → task outside C*-RASP.  
- Inconsistent results across random seeds on otherwise similar domains (e.g., STRIPS Colors) → optimization instability due to lack of inductive bias.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Heavy Grippers** – compare well‑formed vs delete‑free variants. Both domains show >95% accuracy up to 200 steps despite training only ≤100 steps, confirming positive theoretical results.  
2. **Colors** – well‑formed variant maintains near‑perfect accuracy, while the STRIPS variant’s performance declines from ~100% on ID lengths to ~80% on OOD lengths, illustrating the cost of removing well‑formedness.  
3. **Lights Out** – the well‑formed variant generalizes perfectly; the conditional‑effects variant stays at ~50% even with fixed objects, matching the negative prediction for domains not in C*-RASP.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the C*-RASP framework be extended to Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) to provide theoretical guarantees on length generalization under growing alphabets?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Much like C-RASP, our theory also only applies to APE and NoPE encodings, extending our theory to cover Rotary Positional embeddings is a natural next step." (Limitations & Future Work)
- **Why unresolved:** The current analysis relies on translation invariance and locality constraints tailored to APE/NoPE. RoPE introduces rotational interactions that may violate these constraints, so the existing formalism does not apply.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal extension of C*-RASP that captures RoPE's algebraic structure, together with proofs showing that key positive classes (delete‑free, well‑formed) remain expressible and length‑generalizable under the new constraints. Empirical validation on planning tasks with RoPE would further support the extension.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do the theoretical results distinguishing well‑formed/delete‑free from full STRIPS and conditional effects also apply to the task of plan generation, and what new factors determine length generalization in generation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Finally, our focus was on verification and not plan generation. As Stechly et al. (2025) mentions, their respective computational complexity (P vs PSPACE‑complete for STRIPS) should not be expected to translate to relative transformer performance. Thus generalizing our findings to plan generation is non‑trivial." (Limitations & Future Work)
- **Why unresolved:** Plan generation is a harder, constructive task that may require maintaining search‑like state exploration, not just evaluating a given plan. The C*-RASP conditions derived for verification might not be sufficient or necessary for generation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a formalism (e.g., C*-RASP‑based) for generation tasks, prove classification results for planning subclasses, and conduct systematic experiments comparing generation performance on well‑formed vs. STRIPS domains with varying lengths and object counts.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do chain‑of‑thought or scratchpad mechanisms influence the ability of transformers to length‑generalize on planning problems that are not expressible in C*-RASP (e.g., full STRIPS or conditional effects)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Our theoretical results on length generalization apply to standard transformers without scratchpads (no chain of thought). However, our results are still relevant, as chain of thought is expensive and knowing what is possible without chain of thought is important." (Limitations & Future Work)
- **Why unresolved:** Adding scratchpads changes the computational dynamics by allowing intermediate reasoning steps, potentially enabling algorithms that go beyond the local and translation‑invariant operations of Symbolic Limit Transformers. It is unknown whether scratchpads can bridge the gap to tasks outside C*-RASP.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train GPT‑2‑style models with and without explicit scratchpad objectives on STRIPS and conditional‑effect planning datasets, measuring accuracy as plan length and object count increase. Complement with a theoretical analysis that extends Symbolic Limit Transformers to models with internal scratchpad representations and determines whether this strictly increases expressive power.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis assumes ideal Symbolic Limit Transformers with infinite width and perfect translation invariance, which real finite‑precision transformers may not satisfy.
- Experimental evaluation is limited to three synthetic domains and a single model configuration, limiting external validity.
- Full STRIPS accuracy decays but remains above chance, a phenomenon not fully explained by the current C*-RASP framework.
