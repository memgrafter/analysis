# Prism-$Δ$: Differential Subspace Steering for Prompt Highlighting in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10705  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10705  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Matches or outperforms SEKA on 19 of 20 benchmark-model configurations, with relative gains up to +10.6%; halves fluency cost; improves long-context retrieval by up to +4.8%; negligible memory overhead and FlashAttention-compatible.

## Executive Summary
Prism-$Δ$ introduces a method for prompt highlighting in LLMs that steers both the Key (routing) and Value (content) channels of attention. It learns discriminative subspaces via differential cross-covariance decomposition, which removes shared structural patterns between relevant and irrelevant contexts. Each attention head receives a continuous softplus weight based on discriminability, allowing weak-but-useful heads to contribute. Evaluated across four benchmarks and five models, Prism-$Δ$ achieves state-of-the-art performance with minimal overhead and reduced fluency degradation compared to prior approaches.

## Method Summary
Given synthetic contrastive data (neutral context, relevant question, irrelevant question), Prism-$Δ$ extracts Key/Value representations of the answer token under three conditions. It computes the differential cross-covariance matrix between positive and negative representations, then applies SVD to obtain projection directions that maximize discriminative signal while canceling shared variance. Discriminability scores per head are transformed via softplus with offset $\delta_{\min}$ to produce continuous importance weights. At inference, both Key and Value tensors of user-highlighted tokens are edited using these projections and weights, amplifying attention and enriching content from highlighted spans. The method integrates with FlashAttention and incurs negligible memory cost.

## Key Results
- Outperforms SEKA on 19 of 20 benchmark-model configurations, with relative gains up to +10.6%.
- Halves the fluency cost compared to Key-only steering methods.
- Improves long-context retrieval by up to +4.8%.
- Negligible memory overhead and compatible with FlashAttention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Differential Subspace Extraction
**Claim:** Prism-$Δ$ learns steering directions that capture only discriminative signal between relevant and irrelevant contexts by eliminating shared structural patterns.  
**Mechanism:** Computes $\Omega_{\Delta} = \mathbf{H}^{\top}(\mathbf{H}^{+} - \mathbf{H}^{-})/N$ from contrastive representations. SVD on $\Omega_{\Delta}$ yields top singular vectors that maximize the difference in cross-covariance; shared directions cancel out because $\Omega^{+}\mathbf{u} = \Omega^{-}\mathbf{u} \Rightarrow \Omega_{\Delta}\mathbf{u} = 0$.  
**Core assumption:** Shared structural variance is equally present in positive and negative conditions.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.2 (Definition of $\Omega_{\Delta}$ and Proposition 1b); comparison with SEKA’s independent SVD (Figure 3).  
**Break condition:** Contrastive data construction is biased (e.g., positive/negative share little meaningful variance), leading to removal of useful signal or noise amplification.

### Mechanism 2: Per-Head Softplus Weighting
**Claim:** Continuous softplus importance weights allow weak-but-useful heads to contribute at reduced strength, avoiding harsh cutoff and improving robustness.  
**Mechanism:** Discriminability $D_{\ell,h} = \frac{1}{N}\sum \| \mathbf{r}_i^{+} - \mathbf{r}_i^{-} \|^2$ per head is passed through $w = \log(1 + \exp(D - \delta_{\min}))$. Strong heads ($D \gg \delta_{\min}$) get near-linear weights; weak heads ($D \ll \delta_{\min}$) are suppressed smoothly.  
**Core assumption:** Head usefulness varies continuously with discriminability; binary thresholds discard marginally helpful heads.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.2 (Equation 6 and discussion); Figure 6a (robustness across $\delta_{\min}$ vs. SEKA’s drop).  
**Break condition:** Discriminability scores poorly correlate with actual head utility (e.g., due to noisy synthetic data), causing misallocation.

### Mechanism 3: Dual-Channel Steering
**Claim:** Steering both Key and Value channels captures complementary discriminative signals, improving accuracy while reducing fluency degradation.  
**Mechanism:** Same differential projection and softplus weighting are applied separately to Key and Value spaces. For highlighted token $j$, edits: $\mathbf{k}' = \mathbf{k} + g_K \cdot w \cdot \mathbf{P}_K \mathbf{k}$ and $\mathbf{v}' = \mathbf{v} + g_V \cdot w \cdot \mathbf{P}_V \mathbf{v}$. Key amplifies attention; Value enriches transmitted content.  
**Core assumption:** Value representations carry independent discriminative signal not reflected in Key-only methods.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.3 (Equations 9–10); Figure 2 (low correlation $r=0.342$ between Key/Value shifts, complementary depth profiles).  
**Break condition:** Tasks with signal concentrated in routing (e.g., CounterFact) may be harmed by aggressive Value steering; noisy Value signal in some architectures could introduce artifacts.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Synthetic Data Generator → Representation Extractor → Projection Learner (with Hyperparameter Tuner) → Steering Applier

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate synthetic QA triplets (neutral, relevant, irrelevant).  
2. Extract Key/Value tensors for answer token under three conditions (offline).  
3. Compute differential cross-covariance, perform SVD, select rank via $\gamma$, compute softplus weights.  
4. Load projections and weights.  
5. During inference, apply per-head edits to highlighted tokens’ Key/Value; integrate with FlashAttention.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Differential vs. independent SVD: Differential removes shared directions but may lose useful signal if contrastive pairs are mismatched.  
- Softplus vs. hard threshold: Softplus provides graceful degradation; hard threshold (SEKA) is simpler but brittle.  
- Key-only vs. dual-channel: Dual-channel improves fluency/accuracy but adds ~0.02s latency; Key-only is faster and often nearly as effective.  
- Projection rank ($\gamma$): Higher $\gamma$ retains more subspace (conservative) but risks noise; lower $\gamma$ is aggressive but may discard useful dimensions.  
- Gain parameters ($g_K$, $g_V$): Task-specific; too high degrades fluency, too low ineffective. Value gain typically smaller than Key gain.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Accuracy drop: Likely due to poor contrastive data, mis-set $\delta_{\min}$ (too low includes noisy heads), or incorrect $g_K$ sign.  
- Fluency collapse: Indicates $g_K$ or $g_V$ too large; reduce gains or increase $\gamma$.  
- No improvement on a benchmark: Model’s default behavior may already align with highlighting; steering could be counterproductive.  
- Memory/latency spikes: Caused by applying edits to all tokens instead of highlighted ones, or non-FlashAttention kernels.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Contrastive data sanity check*: Extract representations for held-out synthetic triplets; verify separability of $\mathbf{H}^{+}$ and $\mathbf{H}^{-}$ in projected subspace (e.g., linear classification accuracy).  
2. *Projection stability test*: Construct projections from different random subsets of synthetic data; measure variance in downstream accuracy (should be low).  
3. *Channel isolation ablation*: Run with $g_V = 0$ (Key-only) and $g_K = 0$ (Value-only) on validation set; quantify each channel’s contribution to accuracy and fluency vs. full dual-channel.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What determines the optimal value and sign of the steering gain $g_K$ across different models and tasks? No predictive framework exists; requires expensive validation sweeps.
- **Open Question 2:** Why do Key and Value discriminative signals peak at different network depths, and is this depth specialization consistent across Transformer architectures? Causal factors behind this division are unknown.
- **Open Question 3:** For which tasks and models does Value channel steering yield accuracy gains, and what characterizes such scenarios? Conditions for successful dual-channel steering are unclear.

## Limitations
- Relies on synthetic contrastive data; misrepresentation harms generalization to real tasks.
- Differential cancellation may discard useful signal if contrastive pairs are biased.
- Dual-channel gains require careful tuning ($g_K/g_V$) to avoid fluency degradation.
- Evaluation on five models leaves scalability to larger or novel architectures (e.g., MoE) untested.

## Confidence
- **Performance vs. baselines:** High (4 benchmarks, 5 models).
- **Mechanisms (differential subspace, softplus, dual-channel):** Medium (theory and ablations support, but assumptions may not always hold).
- **Efficiency (negligible overhead, FlashAttention):** High (directly measured).
- **Generalization to unseen models/tasks:** Medium-Low (limited model diversity tested).
