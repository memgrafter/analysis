# A Polynomial-Time Axiomatic Alternative to SHAP for Feature Attribution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00496
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00496
- **Reference Count:** 23
- **Primary Result:** ESENSC_rev2 achieves SHAP-like attribution accuracy with nearly linear computational scaling, enabling practical use in high-dimensional settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the exponential computational cost of exact SHAP by introducing ESENSC_rev2, a polynomial-time feature attribution rule grounded in cooperative game theory. By modeling explanations as transferable utility (XAI-TU) games, the authors derive a rule that combines Equal Surplus and Egalitarian Nonseparable Contribution principles, modified to satisfy the null-player axiom. Experiments on tabular regression data demonstrate that ESENSC_rev2 matches Permutation SHAP in accuracy while scaling almost linearly with feature count. The method is uniquely characterized by a set of axioms, including a novel reduction-in-computational-complexity axiom, offering a theoretically justified and scalable alternative to SHAP for modern machine learning explainability.

## Method Summary
The authors reframe feature attribution as a cooperative game where features are players and the model's output serves as the coalition value. They propose ESENSC_rev2, an allocation rule that mixes concepts from Equal Surplus (ensuring fair surplus distribution) and Egalitarian Nonseparable Contribution (promoting equity), while enforcing the null-player property. The rule is defined through restricted differential marginality and an intermediate inessential-game property, which together enable polynomial-time computation without evaluating all permutations. This contrasts with Shapley values, which require exponential time for exact calculation.

## Key Results
- ESENSC_rev2 yields attribution values with small deviations from exact SHAP, comparable to Permutation SHAP approximations.
- Computational complexity scales nearly linearly with the number of features, in stark contrast to SHAP's exponential behavior.
- The rule is axiomatically unique, determined by efficiency, null-player property, restricted differential marginality, intermediate inessential-game property, and a reduction-in-computational-complexity axiom.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ESENSC_rev2 reduces computational burden by avoiding exhaustive permutation evaluations. It leverages the cooperative game framework to compute contributions based on marginal changes in coalition values, but with constraints (restricted differential marginality) that limit the number of required model evaluations. The rule ensures efficiency (attributions sum to model output) and null-player satisfaction (irrelevant features get zero contribution) while distributing surplus equitably. This design allows for polynomial-time algorithms, making it feasible for high-dimensional data where traditional SHAP fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations):** Baseline method based on cooperative game theory's Shapley values; defines unique, locally accurate attributions. *Why needed:* Establishes the standard for feature attribution and its axioms. *Quick check:* List Shapley's four axioms (efficiency, symmetry, dummy, additivity) and their role in XAI.
- **Cooperative Game Theory:** Studies coalition formation and payoff allocation in multi-agent settings. *Why needed:* Provides mathematical tools for fair division; underpins SHAP and this work's XAI-TU games. *Quick check:* Define a characteristic function game and explain the Shapley value formula.
- **Transferable Utility (TU) Games:** Cooperative games where utility is freely transferable among players. *Why needed:* Simplifies allocation by focusing on total value distribution; used to model feature contributions as divisible payoffs. *Quick check:* Contrast TU with non-transferable utility games in terms of allocation complexity.
- **Equal Surplus Principle:** Allocates surplus (above equal shares) equally among contributors. *Why needed:* Promotes fairness when outcomes exceed baseline expectations. *Quick check:* Describe how equal surplus relates to the "surplus" in model explanations.
- **Egalitarian Nonseparable Contribution:** Rewards players based on their non-separable impact on group outcomes. *Why needed:* Captures synergistic effects where features jointly influence predictions. *Quick check:* Explain why nonseparable contributions matter in nonlinear models.
- **Computational Complexity:** Study of algorithm resource requirements. *Why needed:* SHAP's exponential cost limits scalability; this work targets polynomial-time alternatives. *Quick check:* What is the time complexity of exact SHAP for *n* features, and why is it prohibitive for large *n*?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Model & Data → XAI-TU Game Formulation (define coalition values) → ESENSC_rev2 Rule Computation (apply allocation formula) → Feature Attribution Output
- **Critical Path:** For a given prediction, compute model outputs for relevant coalitions as per ESENSC_rev2's restricted marginal contributions, then aggregate into attributions. Avoids full permutation enumeration.
- **Design Tradeoffs:** Trades exact adherence to all Shapley axioms (e.g., possible deviation from additivity) for polynomial-time feasibility; relies on game-theoretic assumptions that may not capture all model behaviors.
- **Failure Signatures:** High attribution error on models with complex, higher-order feature interactions beyond pairwise effects; assumptions of transferable utility or linear surplus distribution may not hold for certain neural architectures.
- **First Experiments:** 1. Validate accuracy against exact SHAP on small subsets of California Housing data. 2. Measure runtime scaling with increasing feature count (up to 512). 3. Compare with Permutation SHAP in both deviation metrics and computation time on the same tabular regression tasks.

## Limitations
- Experimental evaluation is confined to tabular regression (California Housing) with up to 512 features; lacks validation on high-dimensional domains like text, images, or classification tasks.
- Novelty and positioning relative to existing SHAP approximations (e.g., KernelSHAP) are unclear, with potential overlap given active research in this area.
- The practical impact of small deviations from exact SHAP on downstream trust or decision-making is unaddressed, and new axioms require broader theoretical scrutiny.

## Confidence
- **Computational Efficiency:** High (empirical results show clear linear vs. exponential scaling).
- **Theoretical Axiomatic Foundation:** Medium (proofs appear sound, but community validation of new axioms is pending).
- **General Applicability as SHAP Replacement:** Low (limited to specific data types and tasks; no tests on diverse models or human-centric XAI scenarios).
