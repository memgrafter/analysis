# Extending Precipitation Nowcasting Horizons via Spectral Fusion of Radar Observations and Foundation Model Priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21768
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21768
- **Reference count**: 24
- **Primary result**: PW-FouCast extends the reliable precipitation nowcasting horizon by fusing Pangu-Weather spectral priors, achieving state-of-the-art performance with up to 7.28% HSS improvement and reduced MSE/MAE on SEVIR and MeteoNet.

## Executive Summary
Precipitation nowcasting using radar observations degrades at longer lead times due to missing large-scale atmospheric context. This paper introduces PW-FouCast, a frequency-domain fusion framework that incorporates forecast outputs from the Pangu-Weather foundation model as spectral priors. The system employs Pangu-guided Frequency Modulation for spectral alignment, Frequency Memory for phase correction and temporal evolution, and Inverted Frequency Attention to preserve high-frequency details. Evaluations on SEVIR and MeteoNet benchmarks show consistent gains over both unimodal and multimodal baselines, reducing error metrics and improving event detection scores, thereby extending the useful forecast horizon.

## Method Summary
PW-FouCast processes radar sequences and aligned Pangu-Weather fields through an encoder-decoder backbone based on Adaptive Fourier Neural Operators (AFNO). Inputs are embedded to a common latent resolution, transformed via FFT to the frequency domain, and then fused using three core modules: PFM reweights hidden magnitudes and interpolates phases using meteorological priors; FM stores historical spectral phase patterns during training and retrieves them at inference to correct phase drift; IFA recovers high-frequency details lost by standard spectral attention by adding back a gated residual. The output is inverse-transformed and decoded to predict future radar frames. Losses combine spatial reconstruction (MSE) and spectral regularization (L1). The method shows strong performance without increasing computational complexity.

## Key Results
- Reduces MSE by 2.28% and MAE by 2.15% compared to the best unimodal and multimodal baselines.
- Improves average Critical Success Index (CSI) by 6.84% and Heidke Skill Score (HSS) by 7.28% across multiple precipitation thresholds.
- Demonstrably extends the reliable nowcasting horizon, maintaining structural fidelity up to 200 minutes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Pangu-guided Frequency Modulation (PFM)
PFM aligns the hidden spectral representations with Pangu-Weather forecasts by matching magnitudes and phases. It computes channel-wise phase similarity scores between hidden features and embedded meteorological fields, using these scores as attention to reweight amplitudes. Phases are fused via a learned interpolation between hidden and meteorological phasors, producing a unit-magnitude complex coefficient. *Core assumption*: Pangu-Weather and radar reflectivity share meaningful phase alignment in the frequency domain. *Evidence*: [abstract] and [section IV.B] define the alignment; support is limited to the paper’s experiments. *Break condition*: If phase similarity fails, PFM may misalign representations and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2: Frequency Memory (FM)
FM corrects phase discrepancies over long horizons by storing normalized spectral phase patterns from ground-truth sequences in a learned memory bank. During training, memory slots are populated. At inference, the input feature’s Fourier representation is matched to stored patterns via normalized complex inner product; the retrieved pattern rotates the hidden phase toward the retrieved phase. *Core assumption*: A finite set of canonical phase patterns generalizes to unseen precipitation evolutions. *Evidence*: [section IV.C] details storing, matching, and phase alignment operations; no prior spectral memory precedents. *Break condition*: Rare storm morphologies absent from memory may lead to incorrect corrections.

### Mechanism 3: Inverted Frequency Attention (IFA)
IFA restores high-frequency details that standard spectral attention tends to suppress. Standard frequency attention output \(F_{out}\) (low-pass) is subtracted from the input \(F_{in}\) to obtain a high-frequency residual \(F_{high}\). A learned gating vector controls the reinjection of \(F_{high}\) into the output. *Core assumption*: High-frequency attenuation by spectral attention is systematic and reversable. *Evidence*: [abstract] and [section IV.D] define the residual and gating; reference [20] notes attention suppresses high frequencies but the paper does not measure it directly. *Break condition*: If the residual contains noise or artifacts, gated reinjection might amplify errors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) & Adaptive FNO (AFNO)**  
  *Why needed*: The backbone operates entirely in the frequency domain; AFNO blocks mix channels via MLPs on spectral coefficients, enabling efficient global relationships and phase manipulation.  
  *Quick check*: Why does Fourier-space processing capture global spatial relationships more efficiently than convolutional layers?

- **Phase as Structural Prior in Signal Processing**  
  *Why needed*: PFM relies on the assumption that meteorological forecasts provide a structural prior encoded in phase; phase encodes object location, shape, and motion.  
  *Quick check*: In weather radar imagery, what does a consistent phase difference between two frames imply about storm motion, and why would a foundation model’s phase be informative?

- **External Memory for Long-Term Context**  
  *Why needed*: FM stores spectral patterns to correct phase drift over 200-minute horizons, analogous to memory-augmented networks but in frequency space.  
  *Quick check*: How does storing normalized *phase patterns* (rather than full complex coefficients) help generalize across different amplitude scales?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Radar sequence + Pangu-Weather fields -> Spatial Encoder -> FFT -> PFM (align with Pangu phase) -> FM (correct with memory) -> IFA (recover high-freq) -> IFFT -> Decoder -> Output

**Critical path**: The PFM and FM modules form the core spectral alignment loop; IFA is applied within temporal mixing blocks to restore details.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Frequency vs. Spatial domain: Frequency processing enables phase manipulation and global context but loses explicit spatial locality; IFA partially restores high-frequency details.
- Memory size \(S\): Larger \(S\) stores more patterns (better coverage) but increases matching cost and risk of overfitting; optimal \(S\) is dataset-specific (e.g., 240 for SEVIR, 160 for MeteoNet).
- Loss weight \(\lambda\): Balances spatial fidelity (MSE) and spectral fidelity (L1); \(\lambda\) around 0.55–0.57 is optimal.

**Failure signatures**:
1. Blurred forecasts at long lead times (>120 min) may indicate insufficient memory coverage or PFM misalignment.
2. Echo structure fragmentation suggests IFA gating is over-injecting high-frequency noise.
3. Poor generalization to new regions may arise from inadequate preprocessing of Pangu fields or mismatched spectral patterns in memory.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. *Ablation study*: Remove PFM, FM, IFA individually to quantify contributions (expect PFM → event detection, FM → error reduction, IFA → perceptual quality).
2. *Hyperparameter sweep*: Vary memory slots \(S\) and spectral loss weight \(\lambda\); performance should peak and then degrade, confirming non-linear impact.
3. *Qualitative long-sequence visualization*: Compare predictions at +120 min, +180 min against baselines; success = sharp, physically consistent echoes; failure = blurring or spatial displacement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Satellite integration** – How can satellite imagery be incorporated? Current spectral fusion is tailored for Pangu-Weather and radar; adapting to satellite bands would require modifications to PFM/FM/IFA.
2. **Robustness to foundation model errors** – The framework assumes accurate Pangu-Weather priors; sensitivity to biases in those forecasts is unknown.
3. **Automatic memory size** – Memory size \(S\) is manually tuned per dataset; an adaptive allocation method would improve efficiency and generalizability.
4. **Frequency-dependent spectral loss** – The paper uses uniform \(\lambda\); weighting different frequency bands could further improve high-frequency preservation and metrics like SSIM.

## Limitations
- Phase alignment assumption in PFM lacks direct validation; no ablation with randomized Pangu phases.
- Frequency Memory may not generalize to rare storm morphologies; the memory bank is built only from training data and could produce incorrect corrections.
- Inverted Frequency Attention could amplify noise if the high-frequency residual is not properly regularized.
- Reproduction is hindered by missing implementation details (exact AFNO block configuration, FM initialization, preprocessing specifics).
