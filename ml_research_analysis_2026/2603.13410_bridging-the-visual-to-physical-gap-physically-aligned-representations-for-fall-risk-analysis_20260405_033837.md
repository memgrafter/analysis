# Bridging the Visual-to-Physical Gap: Physically Aligned Representations for Fall Risk Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13410
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13410
- **Reference count**: 2
- **Primary result**: PHARL learns physically aligned fall representations without injury labels, achieving superior risk-aligned metrics (Spearman ρ 0.4800, POA 0.7983, Binary Contact AUC 0.8223) and emergent zero-shot ordinality.

## Executive Summary
PHARL is a self-supervised framework for learning RGB-based fall risk representations by aligning motion embeddings with physics-simulation outcomes. It uses trajectory-level temporal consistency to stabilize learning and multi-class physics alignment (via contact outcomes: Head, Trunk, Supported) to impose outcome-aware geometry without requiring clinical injury labels. The method yields efficient feed-forward inference and demonstrates strong cross-dataset performance, with zero-shot ordinality reflecting expected severity hierarchy.

## Method Summary
PHARL processes sliding RGB windows through a ResNet-18 encoder. Weak supervision is derived from an offline physics module: 3D pose estimation is used to simulate a short-horizon continuation, yielding per-window contact outcomes and impulses. The loss combines three terms: (1) a trajectory contrastive loss with denominator masking for binary contact windows, (2) a physics alignment loss that attracts cross-trajectory windows with identical contact classes, and (3) a variance regularization. Only the encoder is updated at training time; the physics module is frozen. At inference, the encoder alone produces embeddings whose geometry correlates with fall severity.

## Key Results
- PHARL achieves Spearman ρ=0.4800, POA=0.7983, Binary Contact AUC=0.8223, and Fall Detection AUC=0.8996, substantially outperforming motion-only baselines.
- Emergent zero-shot ordinality: Head > Trunk > Supported, matching clinical expectations.
- Ablations confirm that temporal denoising and exact class-matching are critical for ordinal separation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: Trajectory-level temporal consistency stabilizes representation learning by anchoring visually and kinematically coherent windows from the same fall trajectory.  
**Mechanism**: A contrastive loss treats other windows from the same video trajectory as positives, enforcing that their embeddings remain close in latent space. This creates a smooth temporal manifold where adjacent motion phases do not drift apart arbitrarily.  
**Core assumption**: Temporal proximity within a single fall event correlates with underlying kinematic continuity, even if visual appearance changes due to viewpoint or occlusions.  
**Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "trajectory-level temporal consistency for stable representation learning"; [section 3.3] Defines $L_{\text{motion}}$ with $P_i^{\text{traj}}$ as positives from same trajectory. Corpus provides weak support.  
**Break condition**: If a trajectory contains abrupt, non-physical transitions (e.g., cut edits, sensor dropouts), temporal positives may incorrectly force similarity between physically distinct phases (e.g., pre-impact standing and post-impact floor contact), blurring outcome-relevant distinctions.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Denominator masking (false-negative removal) prevents harmful repulsion between contact windows from different trajectories, preserving physics-consistent geometry.  
**Mechanism**: During contrastive optimization, cross-trajectory windows that share a contact outcome label ($\text{Head}$ or $\text{Trunk}$) are masked from the negative denominator (set $M_i$ in Eq. 5). This stops the model from treating physically analogous impact events as hard negatives simply because they occur in different videos.  
**Core assumption**: Contact outcome categories ($\text{Head}, \text{Trunk}, \text{Supported}$) reflect underlying physical equivalences that should transcend video-specific appearance; repelling same-outcome contacts distorts the latent space.  
**Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "denominator masking (false-negative removal)"; [section 3.4.3] Defines $M_i$ and the masked loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}'$. Corpus provides limited support.  
**Break condition**: If simulation-derived contact labels are noisy (e.g., missed impacts, false positives), masking may incorrectly preserve harmful negatives or remove valid ones, weakening separation between truly distinct physical outcomes.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Exact class-matching in multi-class physics alignment induces zero-shot ordinality ($\text{Head} \succ \text{Trunk} \succ \text{Supported}$) by pulling cross-trajectory windows with identical contact outcomes together.  
**Mechanism**: An auxiliary contrastive term $\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$ (Eq. 7) attracts embeddings of windows sharing the exact same coarse contact label ($y^{\text{phys}}$) across different trajectories, while contrasting them against all other cross-trajectory samples. This creates class-specific clusters whose relative positions reflect severity ordering without explicit ranking supervision.  
**Core assumption**: Distinct contact outcomes ($\text{Head}$, $\text{Trunk}$, $\text{Supported}$) correspond to separable kinematic signatures; aligning only exact matches forces the embedding to preserve the natural severity hierarchy as an emergent geometric property.  
**Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "multi-class physics alignment" and "zero-shot ordinality emerges"; [section 3.4.3] Defines $P_i^{\text{phys}}$ (same-class cross-trajectory positives) and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$; [section 4.6] Ablation shows dropping exact class-matching reduces Spearman $\rho$ from 0.4800 to 0.4282. Corpus provides weak support.  
**Break condition**: Severe class imbalance (e.g., rare $\text{Head}$ impacts) yields few same-class positives for minority outcomes, leading to under-represented clusters and compressed ordinal margins. The model may then merge $\text{Head}$ and $\text{Trunk}$ distinctions.

## Foundational Learning
### Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE with Temporal Positives)
**Why needed here**: Forms the base optimization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}$) that ensures embeddings retain motion coherence across windows; without it, representations lose temporal structure and fallback to static appearance cues.  
**Quick check question**: If you define positives only by data augmentation (e.g., cropping, color jitter) instead of trajectory identity, what risk does this pose for learning fall outcomes?

### Weak Supervision via Simulation-Derived Equivalence
**Why needed here**: Provides outcome-relevant structure without requiring costly, noisy injury labels. Simulation contacts act as equivalence relations (same outcome $\Rightarrow$ similar embedding), not as classification targets.  
**Quick check question**: How does using physics outcomes as *relation constraints* differ from training a separate head to predict $y^{\text{phys}}$?

### Embedding Geometry Control via Constrained Contrastives
**Why needed here**: Standard contrastive learning optimizes instance discrimination, which may not align with physical outcomes (e.g., two visually different falls with same head impact should be close). Denominator masking and exact class-matching explicitly reshape the contrastive landscape to reflect outcome groupings.  
**Quick check question**: Why might a vanilla contrastive model (e.g., SimCLR) still show poor ordinality ($\text{Head} \succ \text{Trunk} \succ \text{Supported}$) even after training on fall videos?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- **RGB Encoder $f$**: ResNet-18 + projection head, maps input window $x_i$ to $\ell_2$-normalized embedding $z_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Shared across experiments.
- **Physics Module** (training-only): Offline pose estimation → physics simulation → contact descriptor extraction. Outputs per-window $y^{\text{phys}}$ and impulse data after denoising (temporal alignment + continuation + reliability filtering).
- **Relation Builder**: Constructs (a) trajectory-positive sets $P_i^{\text{traj}}$, (b) denominator masks $M_i$, (c) same-class cross-trajectory positives $P_i^{\text{phys}}$ from physics metadata.
- **Loss Composer**: Combines $\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}$ (with masking), $\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}$ (Eq. 3).

Flow: Physics Module (offline) -> Relation Builder -> Loss Composer -> Optimize RGB Encoder.

### Critical path
1. **Input segmentation**: Sliding windows over RGB trajectory.
2. **Offline physics labeling**: For each window, compute $y^{\text{phys}}$ using temporal overlap + short-horizon continuation + max-impulse aggregation.
3. **Contrastive graph construction**: Build $P_i^{\text{traj}}$, $M_i$, $P_i^{\text{phys}}$ for all windows in batch.
4. **Forward + loss**: Compute embeddings $z_i$, then $\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}'$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}$.
5. **Backward**: Update only encoder $f$; physics module is frozen.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Simulation only in training**: Preserves feed-forward inference speed but ties representation quality to simulation fidelity and label denoising heuristics.
- **Exact class-matching vs. binary grouping**: Exact matching preserves ordinality but risks sparse gradients for rare $\text{Head}$ samples; binary grouping ($\{\text{Head, Trunk}\}$ vs. $\text{Supported}$) improves signal but loses severity ordering.
- **Denominator masking aggressiveness**: Over-masking (e.g., masking all contact windows) may under-discriminate; under-masking leaves false negatives. Empirically set via binary contact indicator $C_i$.

### Failure signatures
- **Low Spearman $\rho$ / POA (< 0.3)**: Ordinal structure not emerging. Check: (1) Is $\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$ active? (2) Are $y^{\text{phys}}$ labels temporally denoised? (3) Is exact class-matching implemented?
- **High PCR (>1.05) or low inter-class variance**: Embedding collapse. Verify $\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}$ weight $\lambda_{\text{var}}$ and that masking isn't over-aggressive.
- **Binary Contact AUC near 0.5**: Contact separation failed. Inspect denoising: Are window-level labels aligned with actual impacts? Check impulse thresholds and continuation horizon.
- **Fall Detection AUC drops**: General motion representation degraded. Ensure $\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}$ is contributing (trajectory positives exist) and batch sampling includes diverse trajectories.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablate denoising**: Train with raw trajectory-level $y^{\text{phys}}$ (no temporal alignment/continuation). Expect drop in ordinal metrics (Table 3 row 1) due to pre-impact frames inheriting severe labels.
2. **Replace exact class-matching with binary**: Change $P_i^{\text{phys}}$ to include all cross-trajectory contact windows ($\text{Head}$ or $\text{Trunk}$) as positives. Expect Spearman $\rho$ decrease (Table 3 row 2) as ordinal signal weakens.
3. **Window-only vs. continuation-only supervision**: Train two variants: (a) $y^{\text{phys}}$ from in-window contacts only; (b) from short-horizon continuation only. Compare Binary Contact AUC (window-only should win) vs. Spearman $\rho$ (continuation-only should win) per Table 3 rows 3–4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question**: Can incorporating an explicit ordinal ranking loss (e.g., margin-based constraints) further strengthen the zero-shot ordinal structure in PHARL's representations without converting the framework into a direct outcome predictor?  
**Basis**: [explicit] The authors state in Section 5.2 that "PHARL does not explicitly optimize an ordinal ranking loss" and suggest future work may introduce such constraints to test if monotonic structure can be further strengthened without reframing as an outcome or risk predictor.  
**Why unresolved**: The current physics-term uses contact equivalence and binary grouping, so ordering emerges post-hoc. An explicit loss might improve alignment but risks shifting the objective toward supervised prediction, which the framework intentionally avoids.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation experiments adding ordinal losses (e.g., triplet loss with ordered margins) to PHARL's objective, measuring changes in ordinal metrics (Spearman ρ, POA) while evaluating whether the representation remains suitable for general analysis tasks beyond ordinal prediction, such as retrieval or low-shot transfer.

### Open Question 2
**Question**: How sensitive are PHARL's learned representations to variations in physics simulation hyperparameters (e.g., friction, restitution, contact thresholds), and can environment-aware simulation improve real-world transfer?  
**Basis**: [explicit] Section 5.2 notes that "simulation hyperparameters... can influence specific yphys assignments" and calls for systematic sensitivity analysis and environment-aware modeling for better real-world transfer.  
**Why unresolved**: The framework relies on a simulator that abstracts real dynamics; hyperparameter choices may affect label consistency and embedding geometry, but their impact is not quantified or controlled for.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct sensitivity analyses by varying key simulator parameters across plausible ranges and measuring changes in ordinal metrics (Spearman ρ, POA) and cross-dataset performance. Integrate environment-specific factors (e.g., surface compliance, furniture contact) into simulation and evaluate on datasets with diverse environmental conditions to assess improvement in representation robustness.

### Open Question 3
**Question**: To what extent do dataset-specific biases (e.g., viewpoint, subject demographics) affect PHARL's ability to generalize across different fall datasets, and how can bias-aware evaluation protocols be designed?  
**Basis**: [explicit] Section 5.2 explicitly states that "the current framework does not explicitly model dataset-specific biases" and that "cross-dataset generalization remains an open question."  
**Why unresolved**: Evaluation aggregated multiple public datasets without explicit domain-shift analysis; bias may cause the learned physics-equivalence to overfit dataset-specific visual or demographic artifacts rather than true physical outcomes.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Perform cross-dataset training and testing (e.g., train on Le2i/URFD, test on CAUCAFall/GMDCSA-24) with bias-aware metrics that stratify performance by viewpoint, subject age/gender, and scene context. Design evaluation protocols that isolate physics consistency from dataset-specific shortcuts, such as using held-out datasets with different capture setups.

### Open Question 4
**Question**: Does the use of zero-velocity initialization for motion reconstruction in PHARL attenuate dynamic effects relevant to fall outcomes, and can improved initialization or better decomposition of voluntary protective responses enhance representation fidelity?  
**Basis**: [explicit] Section 5.2 mentions that "zero-velocity initialization improves numerical stability but may attenuate certain dynamic effects and reduce physical realism" and suggests exploring more physically grounded initialization strategies and improved motion decomposition.  
**Why unresolved**: Initialization strategy may affect contact outcome labels and thus the quality of weak supervision, but its impact on the learned representation's ordinal structure and contact localization is not measured.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Experiment with alternative initialization strategies (e.g., physics-guided priors incorporating momentum) and assess changes in ordinal metrics (Spearman ρ, POA) and Binary Contact AUC. Introduce explicit decomposition of voluntary protective responses (e.g., arm bracing) versus involuntary motion components and evaluate whether such decomposition yields representations with clearer ordinal separation and better cross-video neighborhood consistency.

## Limitations
- Underspecified physics simulation details (humanoid model, contact thresholds, impulse integration) block exact replication.
- 3D pose estimation errors likely contaminate contact labels; no robustness analysis provided.
- Hyperparameter choices (λ_phys, λ_var, τ, window size) may be dataset-sensitive; sensitivity not reported.
