# Guess & Guide: Gradient-Free Zero-Shot Diffusion Guidance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07860
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07860
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Gradient-free zero-shot diffusion guidance achieving 2–50× speedup with state-of-the-art perceptual quality (LPIPS) and established Pareto optimality in speed–accuracy trade-offs.

## Executive Summary
Zero-shot diffusion guidance for Bayesian inverse problems requires expensive vector-Jacobian products (VJPs) at every denoising step, creating a computational bottleneck. Guess & Guide addresses this by introducing a gradient-free framework with two phases: a warm-start phase that obtains a high-quality initial latent estimate via pixel-space optimization at an intermediate timestep, and a guided denoising phase that enforces data consistency through lightweight optimizations requiring gradients only through the forward operator. Evaluated on FFHQ and ImageNet across linear and nonlinear tasks (deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting, JPEG, phase retrieval, HDR), the method achieves significant speedups (at least 2×, up to 50× for latent diffusion comparisons) and lower memory usage than gradient-based baselines, while attaining best or second-best perceptual quality in most settings and positioning on the Pareto frontier of speed versus accuracy.

## Method Summary
Guess & Guide comprises two sequential phases. First, the warm-start phase performs pixel-space optimization directly on an intermediate noisy image (at timestep \( t_{\text{warm}} \)) to find a latent that, after deterministic diffusion sampling, yields a high-quality initial estimate at that timestep. This avoids backpropagation through the diffusion model. Second, the guided denoising phase iteratively denoises from \( t_{\text{warm}} \) to \( t=0 \), but at each step enforces data consistency via a lightweight optimization (e.g., a few gradient steps) on the denoised image. Crucially, gradients are required only through the forward measurement operator, not through the diffusion model itself, eliminating the need for costly VJPs. The warm-start latent thus provides a strong initialization that reduces the number of required guided denoising steps.

## Key Results
- **Computational efficiency:** At least 2× speedup over gradient-based zero-shot diffusion guidance (e.g., DDS, DPS), up to 50× for latent diffusion baselines, with lower memory footprint.
- **Perceptual quality:** Achieves best or second-best LPIPS scores across FFHQ and ImageNet for deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting, JPEG correction, phase retrieval, and HDR reconstruction.
- **Pareto optimality:** Establishes a favorable speed–accuracy trade-off, lying on the Pareto frontier compared to evaluated gradient-based and diffusion-based inverse problem solvers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method circumvents the primary computational bottleneck—repeated VJPs through the diffusion U-Net—by decoupling the optimization into two stages. The warm-start obtains a high-quality latent early via pixel-space optimization, which requires only forward passes through the diffusion decoder (deterministic sampling) and avoids any gradient through the denoising network. This initial estimate dramatically reduces the number of subsequent guided denoising steps needed. In the guided denoising phase, data consistency is enforced via a lightweight optimization on the spatial domain image at each step; gradients flow solely through the forward measurement operator (often a simple, cheaper model like a blur kernel or downsampling matrix), not through the diffusion model. Thus, the expensive per-step cost of VJPs is replaced by cheaper operator gradients, yielding overall speed gains while maintaining data fidelity.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Pixel-Space Optimizer (warm-start) → Initial Latent → Guided Denoising Loop (Data Consistency Module + Diffusion Step) → Final Image

Critical path: Warm-start optimization produces latent at \( t_{\text{warm}} \), which is decoded to an initial image estimate. Then, for each denoising timestep from \( t_{\text{warm}} \) down to 0: (1) Data consistency module performs a few gradient steps on the current image to reduce measurement error, (2) diffusion model denoises the latent corresponding to the updated image.

Design tradeoffs: Warm-start resolution is limited by pixel-space optimization memory/compute; guided denoising requires a differentiable forward operator; quality depends on warm-start fidelity and number of guided steps.

Failure signatures: Warm-start fails to find a good latent → poor final quality; non-differentiable or complex forward operator breaks guided denoising; too few guided steps after warm-start leads to measurement inconsistency.

First three experiments:  
1. **Warm-start quality ablation:** Compare LPIPS/PSNR after warm-start versus random initialization to quantify initialization benefit.  
2. **Resolution scaling test:** Measure runtime and memory for warm-start at 256×256, 512×512, 1024×1024 to assess scalability.  
3. **Operator robustness check:** Evaluate on a linear (e.g., Gaussian blur) and a nonlinear (e.g., phase retrieval) task to confirm guided denoising works with both.

## Limitations
- Warm-start pixel-space optimization may become computationally prohibitive at very high resolutions, potentially offsetting overall speed gains.
- Experimental validation is restricted to two datasets (FFHQ, ImageNet); generalization to other domains (e.g., medical, satellite) remains uncertain.
- Perceptual quality relies solely on LPIPS; lack of human evaluation or multiple perceptual metrics (e.g., FID, NIQE) limits assessment of real-world视觉质量.
