# CASHG: Context-Aware Stylized Online Handwriting Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.02103
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02103
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CASHG significantly improves connectivity and spacing metrics (e.g., F1Cursive from 0.07 to 0.45) while maintaining or improving DTWnorm, validated across multiple protocols and human evaluation.

## Executive Summary
CASHG addresses the challenge of generating natural, sentence-level online handwriting that remains faithful to a writer’s style. Prior methods implicitly model inter-character connectivity, leading to failures at longer scales, especially under limited data. CASHG introduces explicit mechanisms: a Character Context Encoder that disentangles character identity from sentence context, a bigram-aware sliding-window Transformer decoder focusing on predecessor-current transitions, gated context fusion for sentence-level modulation, and connectivity-aware supervision with a three-stage curriculum. Evaluations using a new Connectivity and Spacing Metrics (CSM) suite show consistent improvements across multiple benchmarks and in human studies.

## Method Summary
The method separates character identity (cached per Unicode codepoint) from sentence-context modulation via a Character Context Encoder. The decoder processes a sliding window comprising the predecessor’s full trajectory and the current character’s partial output, forcing explicit learning of boundary dynamics (kerning, cursive joins). Gated context fusion injects position-dependent context memory into the style-conditioned decoder states. Training follows a curriculum: first isolated glyphs, then bigrams with vertical drift loss, and finally full sentences. Pen-state prediction (including a dedicated `CursiveEOC` label) and vertical drift regularize inter-character alignment.

## Key Results
- On DSD, F1Cursive rose from 0.07 to 0.45, CRE from 0.84 to 0.90, KGS from 0.39 to 0.44, SSS from 0.49 to 0.63, while DTWnorm dropped from 1.25 to 0.99.
- Similar gains were observed under DeepWriting and OLHWG protocols.
- Human evaluation favored CASHG for connectivity and spacing quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Separating character identity from sentence-context modulation preserves legibility while enabling adaptive connectivity.
- **Mechanism:** The Character Context Encoder produces a deterministic identity embedding (cached per Unicode codepoint) and a position-dependent context memory from the full sentence. Gated context fusion blends the context memory into the style-conditioned decoder states, allowing the same character to vary its shape and boundary behavior according to local neighbors.
- **Core assumption:** Inter-character connectivity is primarily driven by local predecessor character and sentence position, not by the intrinsic character identity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] Character Context Encoder to obtain character identity and sentence-dependent context memory; [section 3.1] equations for identity (A24) and context (A26); Fig. A6 shows identity embeddings collapse by character while context memory disperses.
- **Break condition:** If identity embeddings are not sufficiently discriminative or context memory does not vary meaningfully with position, the model fails either legibility or adaptation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Bigram-aware sliding-window decoding directly supervises predecessor–current transitions, improving robustness to sparse transition coverage.
- **Mechanism:** The decoder attends only to a window comprising the predecessor character’s full trajectory and the current character’s trajectory so far. This forces explicit learning of boundary dynamics for each bigram, rather than implicitly inferring them from long sequences where such transitions may be rare.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant cues for inter-character connectivity are local to the immediate predecessor; larger n-gram windows increase compositional sparsity under limited sentence data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] bigram-aware sliding-window Transformer decoder; [section 3.2] rationale for predecessor–current windows; [section 4.5] ablation (Table 4) shows removing Bi-SWT degrades F1Cursive and KGS.
- **Break condition:** Under scripts where connectivity depends on characters beyond the immediate predecessor (e.g., some Indic scripts), a bigram window may be insufficient. At sentence start, the model relies solely on the current character, possibly leading to inconsistent initial spacing.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Connectivity-aware supervision (pen states + vertical drift loss) explicitly regularizes boundary alignment and cursive continuation.
- **Mechanism:** The model predicts four pen states, including a dedicated `CursiveEOC` label for pen-down character continuation. Additionally, a Vertical Drift Loss penalizes mismatches in vertical offsets (centroid, top, bottom) between predecessor and current characters at their boundaries.
- **Core assumption:** Cursive joins can be captured by a specific pen state, and vertical misalignment is a primary indicator of spacing/kerning errors.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] connectivity-aware supervision with three-stage curriculum; [section 3.4] pen state classification and VDL (Eq. A9); [section 4.5] ablation confirms contributions of Bi-SWT and VDL.
- **Break condition:** If pen-state labeling is noisy or VDL weight is too high, the model may overfit to boundary geometry at the expense of glyph shape. If VDL is too low, boundary alignment may drift.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Inputs: Unicode text sequence, writer-style reference images → Character Context Encoder (CANINE) produces identity embeddings (cached) and context memory (position-dependent); Style Encoder (CNN+Transformer) yields writer-style and glyph-style memories; Bi-SWT Decoder fuses identity, style, and gated context to predict trajectory tokens and pen states.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocess text: compute identity embeddings for all unique characters in the batch (cached).  
  2. Encode full text → context memory (varies by position).  
  3. Encode reference images → style memories.  
  4. For each character position s in autoregressive loop: construct bigram window [s‑1, s]; fetch identity embeddings for s‑1 and s; fetch style memories; fetch context memory for position s; gate fusion; decode trajectory tokens autoregressively.  
  5. Compute losses on character, bigram, and sentence streams (cumulative curriculum).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Bigram window vs. longer context: Bigram reduces sparsity and stabilizes boundary learning but cannot capture non‑local influences; longer context is handled separately by context memory.  
  - Gated fusion vs. naive addition: Gate prevents context from overwhelming style/glyph signals but introduces extra parameters and may require careful tuning.  
  - Curriculum staging: Starting with glyphs ensures legibility before introducing connectivity; but staging may slow convergence and requires careful coefficient scheduling.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor legibility: Identity embeddings not properly separated → check identity embedding similarity matrix.  
  - Broken cursive joins: Low F1Cursive despite correct pen‑state predictions → VDL may be underweighted or bigram window insufficient.  
  - Inconsistent spacing: Low KGS/SSS → gate may be stuck near 0 (no context injection) or context memory not informative.  
  - Style drift: Writer‑style memory not properly utilized → style contrastive loss weight too low or style encoder not trained.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Identity vs. context probe: Feed a sentence with repeated characters (e.g., “abaca cababa”) and visualize identity embedding similarity (should be 1.0 for same char) vs. context memory similarity (should vary).  
  2. Gate activation visualization: Render a generated trajectory colored by gate values; expect higher responses at character boundaries and cursive connections; low values within strokes.  
  3. Ablation checklist: Train three ablations: (a) no Bi‑SWT (character‑isolated decoder), (b) no gated fusion (add context directly), (c) no curriculum (train sentences from start). Evaluate CSM and DTW to confirm contributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can CASHG be adapted to generate handwriting for diverse scripts (e.g., Arabic, Devanagari) beyond Latin and Chinese?
- **Basis in paper:** The Conclusion states: “As future work, we will extend CASHG to broader scripts.”
- **Why unresolved:** Only English and Chinese have been evaluated; other scripts may have different connectivity rules requiring changes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply CASHG to multiple new scripts, measure CSM/DTW, and analyze necessary modifications.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does CASHG perform when style references are scarce (few samples) or mismatched (different content/instrument), and how can robustness be improved?
- **Basis in paper:** The Conclusion lists “improve robustness under scarce or mismatched style references” as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses a fixed number of reference samples; it is unclear how few samples are tolerated or how sensitive the model is to domain shifts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying reference sample count and diversity, evaluating CSM/human judgment, and developing adaptation techniques.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What standardized evaluation protocols and metrics should be adopted for sentence-level online handwriting generation to enable fair cross-method comparison?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors write: “This work highlights the need for standardized sentence-level protocols and metrics…” and “encourage more standardized benchmarks and evaluation practices.”
- **Why unresolved:** Existing comparisons are pairwise and benchmark‑specific, with differing inputs and normalization, preventing unified assessment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Community benchmark with fixed splits, reference metric implementations (including CSM), and a public leaderboard; CASHG’s code could serve as a starting point.

## Limitations
- Exact benchmark protocols (DSD, DeepWriting, OLHWG) are not fully documented, impeding replication.
- Key hyperparameters (layers, dimensions) and pen-state labeling algorithm (CursiveEOC) are missing.
- Generalization to non-Latin scripts is unexplored; the bigram window may be insufficient for complex connectivity.
