# Phase space integrity in neural network models of Hamiltonian dynamics: A Lagrangian descriptor approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00473
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00473
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A Lagrangian Descriptor (LD)-based framework using KL divergence on LD-weighted PDFs reveals that Reservoir Computing faithfully reproduces global phase-space geometry (e.g., homoclinic orbits) in complex Hamiltonian systems, outperforming symplectic neural networks despite lacking explicit Hamiltonian constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Lagrangian Descriptor (LD)-based diagnostic to evaluate neural network models of Hamiltonian dynamics beyond standard pointwise trajectory errors. LDs transform finite-time trajectory geometry into scalar fields that explicitly reveal invariant manifolds and homoclinic structures. By converting LDs into weighted probability density functions and measuring their Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence from a reference, the method quantifies geometric fidelity. Benchmarking on the Duffing oscillator and a three-mode nonlinear Schrödinger equation, the study finds that while symplectic architectures (SympNet, HénonNet, GHNN) preserve energy, they often distort phase-space topology in non-separable systems. In contrast, Reservoir Computing (RC) accurately reconstructs global structures, achieving very low KL divergence. The results highlight the critical importance of global dynamical integrity metrics and surprisingly demonstrate that unconstrained recurrent models can surpass explicitly symplectic ones in capturing complex Hamiltonian geometry.

## Method Summary
The proposed evaluation pipeline begins by training models (SympNet, HénonNet, GHNN, RC) on trajectory data generated from reference Hamiltonian systems. For assessment, a phase-space grid is defined. From each grid point, forward and backward trajectories are integrated using the trained model over a symmetric finite interval $[-\tau, \tau]$. Along these trajectories, a positive-definite functional $M = |\dot{q}|^c + |\dot{p}|^c$ (with $c \in (0,1)$) is summed to compute the Lagrangian Descriptor (LD). This LD field, which exhibits minima on hyperbolic structures like stable/unstable manifolds, is then transformed into a probability density function (PDF) via a monotonic weighting function $g$ (e.g., $g(x)=1/x$) and normalization. Finally, the Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(\rho_{\text{ref}} \| \rho_{\text{model}})$ between the reference and model PDFs is computed, providing a single scalar measure of geometric fidelity that emphasizes discrepancies in dynamically significant regions.

## Key Results
- LDs successfully reveal global structures (homoclinic orbits, separatrices) for all models, exposing geometric distortions not captured by pointwise prediction errors.
- For the Duffing oscillator (separable Hamiltonian), all models recover the homoclinic geometry, but Reservoir Computing achieves the lowest KL divergence ($6.20 \times 10^{-5}$ for 200 trajectories).
- For the three-mode NLS (non-separable Hamiltonian), symplectic NNs preserve energy but exhibit contracted/misplaced homoclinic orbits, while RC faithfully reproduces the reference structure with KL as low as $1.04 \times 10^{-5}$.
- Sensitivity analysis confirms that RC consistently outperforms symplectic NNs across variations in integration time $\tau$, exponent $c$, and weighting function $g$, demonstrating the robustness of the comparative ranking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Lagrangian Descriptors (LDs) capture global phase-space structures like homoclinic orbits and separatrices, providing a "climate" view of the dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** LDs integrate a positive-definite functional (e.g., arc length raised to exponent $c \in (0,1)$) along forward and backward trajectories over a finite symmetric interval $[-\tau, \tau]$. This transforms trajectory geometry into a scalar field where hyperbolic structures (e.g., stable/unstable manifolds) appear as singular minima or maxima due to slow dynamics near these invariant sets, while regular regions yield smoother, higher values.
- **Core assumption:** Finite-time integration of a trajectory-dependent functional can reveal invariant manifolds and homoclinic tangles without explicit knowledge of the governing equations, as these structures inherently influence trajectory speed over finite windows.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "LDs capture global phase-space structures like homoclinic orbits and separatrices." Section 2.1 notes "LDs reveal invariant manifolds, homoclinic tangles, and other global geometric structures." Corpus includes applications to reaction dynamics [24] and molecular systems [25], but the diagnostic use for NN model evaluation is novel.
- **Break condition:** If $\tau$ is too short, singular features may not fully develop (e.g., $\tau=2$ in Duffing fails to capture homoclinic minima). In purely elliptic regions, LDs converge to constants and lack distinguishing features.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Constructing LD-weighted probability density functions (PDFs) and comparing them via Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence quantifies geometric fidelity by emphasizing dynamically significant regions.
- **Mechanism:** The LD field is mapped to a PDF via a monotonic weighting function $g$ (e.g., $g(x)=1/x$) and normalization. This highlights regions of slow dynamics (e.g., homoclinic orbits) as high-probability zones. KL divergence between reference and model PDFs measures the discrepancy in the distribution of these critical structures, prioritizing errors where the reference assigns high probability.
- **Core assumption:** Dynamically important phase-space regions correspond to characteristic LD values, and a reference-weighted divergence metric like KL will sensitively detect geometric distortions in these regions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "By constructing LD-weighted probability density functions... the method quantifies geometric fidelity." Section 2.2: "By interpreting the state space U as a probability space, we construct a weighted PDF... $\rho_U(u) = g(LD_\Phi(u))/Z$." Corpus includes PDF comparisons for attractors [20], but LD-weighted PDFs for Hamiltonian phase-space integrity is novel.
- **Break condition:** The choice of $g$ drastically alters the PDF (e.g., $g(x)=x$ emphasizes high-LD fast dynamics). The paper shows model rankings remain stable across $g$, but absolute KL values vary. KL is asymmetric and may under-penalize missing structures if they have low reference probability.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reservoir Computing (RC) can reproduce global phase-space geometry in complex Hamiltonian systems more accurately than explicit symplectic neural networks, despite lacking Hamiltonian constraints.
- **Mechanism:** RC's high-dimensional recurrent reservoir, with fixed random weights and trained linear readout, approximates the Hamiltonian flow map implicitly. The reservoir's state transformation captures global geometry without being constrained by symplectic priors, which may be too restrictive for non-separable Hamiltonians (like the three-mode NLS).
- **Core assumption:** RC's universal approximation property allows it to learn any dynamical system from data, and for the three-mode NLS, the implicit representation in the reservoir state better captures the coupled nonlinear geometry than the explicit, potentially limited parameterization of symplectic NNs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "for the three-mode nonlinear Schrödinger equation, symplectic architectures preserve energy but distort phase-space topology, while Reservoir Computing ... reproduces the homoclinic structure." Section 5.4: "RC closely matches the reference structure... SympNet, GHNN, and HénonNet exhibit distorted density distributions." Corpus includes RC for Hamiltonians [11] and symplectic NNs [9,10], but the finding that RC outperforms symplectic NNs in geometric fidelity for non-separable Hamiltonians is novel.
- **Break condition:** RC does not conserve energy exactly and may suffer long-term drift. Performance degrades with insufficient training data or in higher-dimensional systems. For separable Hamiltonians (Duffing), symplectic NNs and RC perform similarly well.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Training data generation -> Model training (Adam for NNs, ridge regression for RC) -> Trained model -> Evaluation grid -> Forward/backward trajectory integration -> LD computation -> LD-weighted PDF construction -> KL divergence calculation.

**Critical path:**
1. Generate training trajectories from reference Hamiltonian (numerical integration with specified Δt).
2. Train each architecture: SympNet, HénonNet, GHNN (MSE loss, Adam); RC (ridge regression on warm-up states; backward model via time-reversed sequences).
3. For evaluation, define a phase-space grid. For each grid point, use the trained model to generate forward/backward trajectories, compute LDs (τ=4, c=0.7), then PDFs (g(x)=1/x).
4. Compute KL divergence between reference and model PDFs.
5. Visualize LD fields, PDFs, and homoclinic orbits.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Symplectic NNs:* Guarantee energy conservation and symplecticity, enabling cheap backward integration (exact inverse). May struggle with non-separable Hamiltonians due to limited expressivity of parameterization.
- *RC:* Flexible, captures complex geometries without physical constraints, trains quickly via linear regression. Lacks built-in invariants, requires separate backward model, and performance is hyperparameter-sensitive.
- *LD parameters:* τ must be long enough to resolve singularities; c controls singularity sharpness; g shapes emphasized regions. Rankings robust but absolute KL sensitive.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Symplectic NNs:* Distorted homoclinic orbits (contracted, misplaced fixed points) in LD plots; high KL in complex systems; energy drift if integration imperfect.
- *RC:* Energy drift over long rollouts; failure if training data biased (e.g., only inside homoclinic); high KL if reservoir too small or hyperparameters suboptimal.
- *General:* τ too small → homoclinic minima not captured; coarse grids miss fine structures; improper g emphasizes wrong regions.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Duffing oscillator benchmark:** Train all models on 200 trajectories. Compute short-term errors and LDs. Show all recover homoclinic orbit, but RC achieves lowest KL (6.20e-5).
2. **Three-mode NLS benchmark:** Train on 500 trajectories. Show symplectic NNs preserve energy but distort homoclinic geometry, while RC accurately reconstructs it (KL ~1.04e-5).
3. **Sensitivity analysis:** Vary τ, c, g for Duffing. Show absolute KL changes but model rankings (RC best) stable. Also demonstrate insufficient τ fails to resolve homoclinic features.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can LD-based diagnostics be incorporated into the training loss?** Basis: Authors state this is an important future direction. Why unresolved: LD is post-hoc; integration raises computational and architectural questions. Evidence: Demonstrate an LD-informed loss (e.g., KL on LD-weighted PDFs) yields lower KL and better homoclinic reconstruction while remaining trainable.
- **How sensitive is the framework to LD parameters and metrics?** Basis: Authors note sensitivity to c, M, g, and distance metrics needs investigation. Why unresolved: Only τ, c, g varied on one system; effect of M and metrics unknown in higher dimensions. Evidence: Parameter sweeps across multiple Hamiltonians to derive selection guidelines.
- **Can deeper symplectic architectures match RC on non-separable systems?** Basis: Authors speculate GHNN's shallow architecture may be insufficient for NLS. Why unresolved: No experiments with deeper GHNN reported. Evidence: Train GHNN with increasing depth/width on three-mode NLS; compare KL and errors to RC.
- **Why does RC outperform symplectic NNs on non-separable Hamiltonians?** Basis: RC's superior performance is observed but not explained; described as "surprising." Why unresolved: Underlying reasons (e.g., implicit representation, time-reversal training) not analyzed. Evidence: Comparative analysis of learned dynamics (energy conservation, reservoir states, testing on other non-separable systems).

## Limitations
- Exact implementations for SympNet/HénonNet and full RC reservoir configuration are not fully specified; random seeds are unreported, hindering reproducibility.
- LD metric’s sensitivity to parameters τ and g was explored only on Duffing; behavior in other systems and higher dimensions is unknown.
- Generalizability to higher-dimensional or strongly chaotic systems remains untested; long-term physical consistency (energy/volume preservation) not assessed.

## Confidence
- *LD framework evaluates global geometry:* **Medium** – convincing on two systems but needs broader validation.
- *RC outperforms sympleptic NNs in geometric fidelity for non-separable Hamiltonians:* **Medium** – based on a single non-separable case; could be system-specific.
- *KL-based metric robust to τ and g:* **Low-Medium** – robustness shown only for Duffing; other systems may differ.

## Next Checks
1. Extend experiments to additional Hamiltonian systems (e.g., 4-mode NLS, coupled rotors) to confirm whether RC’s geometric advantage generalizes.
2. Conduct multi-seed training for all models and report variance in KL divergence to quantify result stability.
3. Evaluate long-term energy conservation and phase-space volume preservation over many Lyapunov times to assess physical consistency beyond short-term geometry.
