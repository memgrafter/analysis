# STAMP: Selective Task-Aware Mechanism for Text Privacy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12237
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12237
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** STAMP improves privacy-utility trade-offs for local differentially private text by selectively allocating noise based on token sensitivity and task importance, combined with a polar mechanism that perturbs only embedding direction, outperforming isotropic baselines on SQuAD, Yelp, and AG News.

## Executive Summary
STAMP introduces a novel framework for task-aware text privatization under local differential privacy. It addresses the limitation of uniform noise allocation by selectively assigning privacy budgets to tokens based on two factors: their privacy sensitivity (via NER/PII detection) and their importance to the downstream task (via cosine similarity to a task representation). Crucially, it employs a polar mechanism that perturbs only the angular component of token embeddings using von Mises-Fisher noise, aligned with cosine-based decoding. Experiments on question answering, sentiment classification, and topic classification show significant utility gains over isotropic Laplace noise at matched per-token privacy budgets, with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
STAMP operates in three stages. First, a public grouping function partitions tokens into four groups by intersecting privacy sensitivity (high/low) and task importance (high/low, measured by cosine similarity to a task embedding, with threshold τ). Second, group-specific privacy budgets ε are allocated according to a fixed ratio (e.g., 2:1:4:3), giving larger ε to high-importance groups and to sensitive tokens. Third, each token's embedding is normalized to a unit vector, perturbed via von Mises-Fisher noise with concentration κ ∝ ε, and decoded to a token by a cosine nearest-neighbor search over the vocabulary embeddings. This geometry-preserving approach ensures the perturbation matches the decoding metric, maintaining semantic neighborhoods.

## Key Results
- On SQuAD, STAMP achieves up to 0.654 cosine similarity vs. 0.341 for Laplace at matched ε.
- On Yelp sentiment classification, STAMP reaches 0.560 accuracy vs. 0.140 for Laplace.
- On AG News topic classification, STAMP reaches 0.760 accuracy vs. 0.520 for Laplace.
- STAMP's computational overhead is minimal (≈0.6% slower than Laplace).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Directional Perturbation Preserves Utility
- **Claim:** Perturbing only the *direction* of token embeddings while preserving magnitude improves utility vs. isotropic noise.
- **Mechanism:** Normalize embeddings to unit vectors on the sphere. Sample angular perturbations from the von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution, which is concentrated around the original direction. Decode via cosine nearest-neighbor, which depends solely on direction.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic relationships in embedding space are primarily angular; magnitude carries less task-relevant signal (e.g., frequency or stylistic cues).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states the polar mechanism maintains semantic neighborhoods. [section 4] defines the normalized polar mechanism and proves decoding equivalence (Proposition 1). [section 5.2] shows consistent outperformance of Laplace across three datasets.
- **Break condition:** Fails if the downstream task relies on embedding norm (e.g., frequency-sensitive tasks) or uses Euclidean-distance decoding.

### Mechanism 2: Selective Task-Aware Budget Allocation
- **Claim:** Allocating privacy budgets non-uniformly across token groups (by privacy sensitivity and task importance) improves the privacy-utility trade-off vs. uniform allocation.
- **Mechanism:** A public grouping map g_T assigns each token to one of four buckets: (high/low privacy) × (high/low task importance). Budgets are assigned inversely to task importance and directly to privacy sensitivity (e.g., ratio 2:1:4:3). Only tokens in selected groups are perturbed according to their group’s ε.
- **Core assumption:** Token-level contributions to privacy risk and task utility are approximately separable and can be estimated by simple heuristics (NER for privacy, cosine similarity to task embedding for importance).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] emphasizes selective allocation across tokens. [section 3.1] formalizes the grouping and budget allocation; Figure 1(b) illustrates the 2×2 grid. [section 5.3] shows STAMP variants dominating uniform allocation.
- **Break condition:** Fails if task importance is mismeasured (e.g., τ threshold too low/high) or privacy detector misses sensitive entities. Also, assumes group boundaries are meaningful; ambiguous tokens may be misassigned.

### Mechanism 3: Alignment of Perturbation and Decoding Geometry
- **Claim:** Aligning the perturbation distribution (vMF on the sphere) with the decoding metric (cosine similarity) minimizes distortion of semantic structure.
- **Mechanism:** Because decoding depends only on direction, discarding radial perturbation (via normalization) prevents wasted noise budget. The vMF’s angular noise matches the cosine metric, preserving local neighborhoods on the hypersphere.
- **Core assumption:** Cosine similarity correctly captures semantic proximity for the downstream task, and token embeddings are approximately normalized and semantically organized by direction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] proves that cosine decoding is equivalent to nearest-neighbor search on the unit sphere (Proposition 1). [section 5.2] shows polar’s smooth utility improvement vs. Laplace’s plateau. [corpus] notes alignment pursuit in related work but STAMP’s specific combination is novel.
- **Break condition:** Fails if decoding uses a different metric (e.g., Euclidean) or if embedding norms carry crucial semantic signal.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Local Differential Privacy (LDP) & Metric LDP**
- *Why needed here:* STAMP’s guarantees are built on (ε,δ)-LDP per token. Metric LDP scales indistinguishability by semantic distance, allowing stronger protection for similar tokens (e.g., synonyms). STAMP instantiates metric LDP with angular distance for polar noise.
- *Quick check question:* Why is metric LDP better suited than pure LDP for embedding-based text privatization?

**Concept: Embedding Space Geometry**
- *Why needed here:* The polar mechanism decomposes embeddings into radial and angular components. The assumption is that magnitude often correlates with token frequency/style while direction encodes semantics, so perturbing only direction preserves task-relevant signal.
- *Quick check question:* If a token’s embedding norm is large, does that necessarily mean it is more important for a classification task? Explain.

**Concept: Task-Aware Budget Allocation**
- *Why needed here:* STAMP’s core innovation is selective budget distribution. Privacy budgets are a limited resource; allocating more noise to less important tokens saves budget for critical ones.
- *Quick check question:* In a question-answering task, why might the token “Einstein” have a high task importance score for one query and a low one for another?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Tokenizer → Embedder → Grouping Module → Budget Allocator → Polar Perturber → Decoder → (Optional) Coherence Repair

**Critical path:** Tokenization → Grouping (via NER & task embedding) → Polar perturbation (vMF sampling) → Cosine decoding. Errors in grouping propagate to noise application.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Grouping granularity:* Coarser than 4 groups simplifies analysis; finer (e.g., continuous ε weighting) may improve trade-off but complicates composition.
- *τ threshold:* Lower τ increases high-importance group size → higher utility but less privacy on those tokens; requires tuning (Figure 5).
- *Normalization:* Using normalized polar (fixed norm=1) guarantees 0-metric LDP for magnitude but discards norm signal. If norm is useful, use full polar (radial + angular) with two budgets.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Collapse to uniform:** Grouping fails (e.g., NER misses all PII); STAMP reduces to uniform. Check group counts (#G1..#G4) are non-zero.
2. **Utility cliff:** ε for high-importance group (G3) too low → task accuracy drops sharply. Monitor per-group ε values.
3. **Semantic drift:** vMF concentration κ too low (high privacy) → cosine decoding retrieves unrelated tokens; ensure privatized context retains key query terms.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce Polar vs. Laplace (Table 1):** At fixed per-token ε (e.g., 450), compare cosine similarity (SQuAD) and accuracy (Yelp/AG News) using identical embedding model and decoding.
2. **STAMP vs. Uniform (Figure 6):** Sweep base angular budget ε₀; for STAMP, allocate per group ratios. Expect STAMP to dominate in low–mid ε range.
3. **Group contribution ablation:** Fix total ε, vary allocation ratios (e.g., reverse: G1 high, G4 low). Utility should peak when high-importance groups get larger ε.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed embedding model (only d=768) and NER/PII pipeline hinder exact replication.
- Grouping relies on heuristic thresholds (τ=0.5) and NER accuracy; mismeasurement may cause collapse to uniform allocation.
- Assumes cosine decoding alignment; fails for Euclidean-distance models or norm-sensitive tasks.
- Group budget ratio 2:1:4:3 presented without ablation; may be suboptimal.
- Coherence repair evaluated with GPT-4; rule-based fallback utility not reported.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (directional perturbation preserves utility): High – consistent cross-dataset gains and theoretical support (Proposition 1).
- **Mechanism 2** (task-aware budget allocation): Medium – benefits observed but vulnerable to grouping errors; threshold sensitivity not fully characterized.
- **Mechanism 3** (geometry alignment): Medium – strong theory for cosine-decoding tasks; unknown for other metrics.

## Next Checks
1. **Embedding sensitivity:** Replicate Table 1 using RoBERTa (d=1024) and a non-normalized space; verify STAMP polar still outperforms Laplace.
2. **Grouping robustness:** Sweep τ from 0.1 to 0.9 on SQuAD and Yelp; record group proportions and utility; identify stable operating region.
3. **Norm-dependence test:** Construct synthetic classification where embedding norm encodes label (direction random); compare STAMP vs. Laplace; expect STAMP utility drop if norm carries signal.
