# A Cognitive Distribution and Behavior-Consistent Framework for Black-Box Attacks on Recommender Systems
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10633
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10633
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The proposed method improves surrogate model extraction (Agr@1 up to 0.601 vs. DFME's 0.571) and achieves high attack success (N@10 up to 0.8996) while generating behaviorally plausible, stealthy pollution sequences.

## Executive Summary
Existing black-box attacks on sequential recommender systems face two key limitations: extraction methods overlook ranking position importance, yielding incomplete surrogate models, and adversarial sequences often lack behavioral plausibility, making them detectable. This paper introduces a dual-enhanced framework to address both. First, a cognitive distribution-driven extraction maps discrete top‑k rankings into continuous attention‑decay distributions, aligning surrogate and target via a combination of KL divergence and pairwise ranking loss. Second, a behavior‑consistent pollution generator fuses gradient guidance with collaborative item relations to produce semantically coherent injection sequences. Comprehensive experiments on three datasets (ML‑1M, Steam, Beauty) demonstrate that the method significantly improves surrogate extraction fidelity and attack success rates, with both components contributing—the cognitive distribution mechanism providing the largest gain. Generated sequences show higher stealth, aligning with real user behavior.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of two coupled components. (1) Cognitive extraction: Given black‑box rankings, the system reconstructs a soft probability distribution using an exponential decay function *v*(*j*) = *α*<sup>*j*−1</sup> to model position‑sensitive attention. The surrogate model is trained to match this distribution via KL divergence while preserving local order with a pairwise margin loss. (2) Pollution generation: For a target item, candidate items are scored by a weighted sum of gradient‑based effectiveness and collaborative‑filtering‑based stealth. The top items are greedily selected to form an injection sequence that promotes the target. A validator queries the black‑box with the perturbed sequence to confirm promotion. This dual design ensures both a faithful surrogate and plausible attacks.

## Key Results
- Surrogate extraction agreement (Agr@1) reaches 0.601 for NARM, outperforming DFME (0.571).
- Attack success (N@10) peaks at 0.8996 for NARM on ML‑1M; success rates exceed 0.93 on at least two datasets.
- Ablation studies confirm the cognitive distribution alignment contributes the most, and the generated sequences exhibit higher behavioral plausibility (e.g., genre coherence) compared to baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Mapping discrete top‑k rankings into continuous attention‑decay distributions improves surrogate model fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** The system transforms a black-box ranking list into a soft probability distribution using an exponential decay function *v*(*j*) = *α*<sup>*j*−1</sup>, where *α* ∈ (0,1). This distribution is optimized via KL divergence against the surrogate's output distribution, aligning the surrogate's internal attention pattern with the target's implied cognitive weighting of positions.
- **Core assumption:** The target model's outputs implicitly encode a position‑sensitive attention pattern consistent with cognitive primacy/position bias, and this pattern can be approximated by a continuous decay function.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "maps discrete top‑k rankings into continuous attention‑decay distributions, aligning surrogate and target via KL divergence"; [section 3.2] "Assuming user attention intensity decays exponentially with position... converts to a soft black-box distribution" and "our attention-based cognitive distribution smoothly approximates the NDCG discounting mechanism"; neighboring papers discuss extraction but not cognitive distribution alignment.
- **Break condition:** If the target model's ranking behavior is not position‑sensitive (e.g., random or uniformly weighted), the decay assumption fails and KL loss may misalign the surrogate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Injecting items with high collaborative similarity to the target—combined with gradient guidance—produces behaviorally plausible yet effective pollution sequences.
- **Mechanism:** Candidate pollution items are scored by a weighted sum: *S*(*j*) = *w*<sub>*g*</sub>·sim<sub>*g*</sub>(*j*) + *w*<sub>*s*</sub>·˜*s*(*j*|*i*<sup>*</sup>), where sim<sub>*g*</sub> is cosine similarity to the gradient direction (effectiveness) and ˜*s* is a collaborative relation score (e.g., co‑occurrence) from historical logs (stealth). Top items are greedily sequenced.
- **Core assumption:** Historical co‑occurrence patterns reliably indicate semantic/behavioral coherence for a target item, and this coherence is orthogonal to gradient‑based effectiveness signals.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "fuses gradient guidance with collaborative item relations to generate semantically coherent injection sequences"; [section 3.3] "candidate pool via collaborative prior... gradient-guided scoring... dual‑signal fusion"; [section 4.6, Fig. 6] Case study showing baseline (DFME) produces genre‑incongruent items (westerns) vs. proposed method maintaining crime/drama coherence.
- **Break condition:** If collaborative data is sparse or non‑stationary (e.g., new item cold‑start), the ˜*s* signal becomes unreliable, forcing over‑reliance on gradients and reducing stealth.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Fusing global distribution alignment (KL) with local pairwise ranking preservation stabilizes surrogate training and prevents overfitting to order alone.
- **Mechanism:** The distillation loss *L*<sub>distill</sub> = *λL*<sub>pair</sub> + (1−*λ*)*L*<sub>KL</sub> combines a pairwise margin loss (ensuring local order consistency) and a KL loss (ensuring global attention distribution match). *λ* trades structural precision for cognitive imitation.
- **Core assumption:** A surrogate trained only on pairwise order (as in DFME/ME‑MIA) loses continuous value information critical for downstream gradient‑based attacks, while KL‑only alignment risks violating local ranking constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] Equation (6) and preceding discussion: "distribution alignment alone can compromise local ranking order... thus incorporate pairwise... The final objective combines both"; [section 4.4, Fig. 4] Ablation: "cognitive distribution alignment mechanism is the core factor... using the fusion loss alone significantly improved... far exceeds... behavioral consistency alone".
- **Break condition:** If *λ* is mis‑set (e.g., too high), the surrogate overfits to pairwise noise; if too low, the surrogate mimics coarse distribution but loses precise ranking needed for effective gradient direction in pollution.

## Foundational Learning
- **KL Divergence for Distribution Alignment**  
  *Why needed:* Measures discrepancy between continuous attention distributions; core to cognitive alignment beyond hard rankings.  
  *Quick check:* If *p* = [0.6, 0.3, 0.1] and *q* = [0.5, 0.3, 0.2], compute KL(*p*∥*q*) and interpret the value.

- **Attention Decay / Position Bias**  
  *Why needed:* Provides the cognitive prior *v*(*j*) = *α*<sup>*j*−1</sup> to convert discrete rankings into continuous distributions.  
  *Quick check:* How does *α* = 0.97 vs. *α* = 0.7 change the weight of the top‑1 ranked item relative to top‑5?

- **Collaborative Filtering Signal (Co‑occurrence)**  
  *Why needed:* Supplies the ˜*s*(*j*|*i*<sup>*</sup>) score to ensure injected items are semantically/behaviorally related to the target.  
  *Quick check:* Given a co‑view matrix, how would you compute ˜*s*(*j*|*i*<sup>*</sup>) for candidate *j*? What if counts are zero?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Query Data Generator → Cognitive Extractor → Collaborative Builder → Pollution Generator → Validator

**Critical Path:**  
Data Generation → Cognitive Extraction (train f_w) → Pollution Generation (using f_w gradients + S) → Validation. The surrogate must be adequately extracted (Agr@1 > ~0.6) before pollution gradients are meaningful.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- α (decay rate): Higher α increases cognitive fidelity but may overemphasize top‑1; tuned via Agr metrics (optimal ~0.95–0.97 per Fig. 5).  
- w_g vs w_s: More w_g ↑ attack success but ↓ stealth; more w_s ↑ plausibility but may ↓ ranking impact. Default 0.5/0.5 or tune on validation set.  
- Candidate pool size K: Larger K improves search but ↑ queries; balance with budget.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Extraction: Low Agr@1 (< 0.5) indicates surrogate does not mimic target's position sensitivity; revisit α or λ.  
- Pollution: High target promotion but high detection (e.g., sequence entropy outlier, genre mismatch) indicates w_s too low; increase collaborative weight.  
- General: If black‑box API rate‑limits, autoregressive generation may timeout; reduce sequence length or query budget.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to Non‑Sequential Recommenders**  
   *Question:* How can the cognitive distribution alignment mechanism be generalized to non‑sequential recommender systems, such as matrix factorization or graph‑based models, which lack inherent sequence modeling?  
   *Basis:* The framework is tailored for sequential recommenders; experiments exclusively use NARM, SASRec, and BERT4Rec without discussing applicability to other paradigms.  
   *Why unresolved:* The attention decay assumption depends on item order in sequences, which may not translate to systems where rankings arise from static user‑item interactions, limiting the attack's universality.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Extending the distribution reconstruction to aggregate or implicit feedback contexts in non‑sequential models, then measuring extraction agreement (e.g., Agr@K) across diverse architectures.

2. **Theoretical Bounds on Surrogate Fidelity**  
   *Question:* What theoretical bounds exist for the fidelity of surrogate models extracted via cognitive distribution alignment, given a finite query budget and varying model complexities?  
   *Basis:* The paper demonstrates empirical gains using KL divergence and pairwise loss but provides no analytical framework relating query count, surrogate accuracy, or black‑box model properties.  
   *Why unresolved:* Without sample complexity analysis, attackers cannot predict extraction efficiency or defenders cannot formulate query‑rate limits to mitigate theft.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Deriving probabilistic or information‑theoretic bounds on distillation error as a function of API calls, model dimensionality, and data sparsity, validated through controlled experiments.

3. **Robustness Against Adaptive Defenses**  
   *Question:* How effective are behavior‑consistent pollution attacks against adaptive defenses that explicitly detect cognitive distribution anomalies or semantic incoherence in user sequences?  
   *Basis:* The conclusion calls for defenses that audit "semantic plausibility and cognitive consistency," implying current stealth evaluations (e.g., genre coherence) may not withstand tailored detectors.  
   *Why unresolved:* The attack stealth is assessed via statistical metrics and case studies, but not against adversarial detectors designed to identify the very cognitive or behavioral inconsistencies the attack aims to hide.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Developing detectors that monitor for deviations from learned attention decay patterns or collaborative coherence, then quantifying attack success rates under such active defenses.

## Limitations
- The cognitive distribution assumes exponential attention decay, which may not match all target models' position bias; the decay factor α is tuned on validation and its transferability is uncertain.
- Behavioral consistency relies on item co‑occurrence statistics, which are sparse for new/niche items, making the collaborative signal unreliable and potentially forcing over‑reliance on gradients.
- Hyperparameters (w_g, w_s, K) likely require dataset‑specific tuning; no automatic selection method is provided.

## Confidence

- Behavior‑consistent pollution: Medium
- Fusion loss (KL + pairwise): High

## Next Checks
1. **α transfer test**: Fix α to the value tuned on the best‑performing dataset and apply it unchanged to a new dataset (e.g., Yelp). Compare Agr@1 to locally tuned α.
2. **Collaborative sparsity robustness**: Systematically mask increasing percentages of item‑item co‑occurrences (e.g., 0%, 30%, 60%) and measure attack success (N@10) and stealth (genre mismatch rate).
3. **Query budget sensitivity**: Vary the number of queries used for surrogate training (e.g., 1k, 5k, 10k) and record resulting N@10 to identify minimal viable budget and scaling behavior.
