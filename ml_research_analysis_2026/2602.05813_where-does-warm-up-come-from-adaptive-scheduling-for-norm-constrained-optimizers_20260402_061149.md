# Where Does Warm-Up Come From? Adaptive Scheduling for Norm-Constrained Optimizers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05813
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05813
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Adaptive scheduler automatically determines warm-up duration and matches or outperforms manually tuned schedules for norm-constrained optimizers on LLaMA pretraining.

## Executive Summary
The paper provides a theoretical justification for learning rate warm-up in norm-constrained optimizers (Muon, Lion, normSGD) under a generalized smoothness assumption, where local curvature decreases with the suboptimality gap. This analysis naturally yields a warm-up-then-decay schedule. The authors then present a practical adaptive scheduler that automatically selects the warm-up duration using only a target loss estimate and standard hyperparameters (peak learning rate, divisor). Evaluations on LLaMA (124M, 210M) pretraining with various optimizers and batch sizes show the adaptive scheduler consistently matches or surpasses the best manually tuned warm-up schedules, without extra hyperparameter search.

## Method Summary
The method combines theoretical analysis with an algorithmic scheduler. Theoretically, under generalized smoothness—where the gradient norm bound is a decreasing function of the loss suboptimality gap—the optimal learning rate is derived as a rational function η(Δ)=Δ/(K0+K1Δ+K2Δ²) that transitions from warm-up to decay. Practically, the scheduler (Algorithm 1) estimates the initial loss Δ₀ and a candidate transition threshold Δ′ (related to target loss f*). It solves for coefficients K0, K1, K₂ by enforcing: η(Δ′)=peak lr, η(Δ₀)=peak lr/divisor, and matching a desired warm-up+decay shape in a neighborhood of Δ′ via Gaussian-weighted MSE (closed-form). The Δ′ that minimizes this MSE is selected. During training, the scheduler uses η(Δ) while Δ≥Δ′, then switches to cosine decay. The approach requires only peak lr, divisor, total steps, and a reasonable f* estimate.

## Key Results
- Adaptive scheduler matches or outperforms best manually tuned warm-up schedules across Muon, Lion, and normSGD on LLaMA 124M/210M with batch sizes 32/256.
- On LLaMA 124M, batch size 32, validation loss was lower than or equal to all manual warm-up sweeps.
- Ablation shows robustness to target loss choice within a reasonable range.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Warm-up emerges naturally from the convergence analysis: as the optimizer approaches the minimum (small Δ), the generalized smoothness constant decreases, necessitating smaller steps to maintain stability. The derived rational learning rate function inherently includes a warm-up phase when initial Δ₀ is large. The adaptive scheduler automates the critical choice of the transition point Δ′ by fitting the function to a generic warm-up+decay profile, using only a rough estimate of f*. The closed-form solution ensures the schedule respects the given peak lr and initial divisor, while the Gaussian weighting yields a smooth, robust match. The scheduler's robustness to f* stems from its reliance on Δ′ and Δ₀; moderate errors in f* shift Δ′ but preserve a functional warm-up length.

## Foundational Learning
- **Generalized Smoothness**: Gradient norm bound L(Δ) decreases as suboptimality Δ→0. *Why needed*: Relaxes standard smoothness for non-convex deep learning and directly yields adaptive lr schedule. *Quick check*: Compare to constant L; here L(Δ) ~ 1/Δ or similar.
- **Norm-Constrained Optimizers**: Muon (matrix normalizer), Lion (sign+momentum), normSGD (gradient norm scaling). *Why needed*: They normalize updates, making them sensitive to lr scaling; warm-up prevents early explosion. *Quick check*: Their updates are of the form x ← x - η * normalized_gradient.
- **Suboptimality Gap (Δ)**: f(x) - f*. *Why needed*: lr is expressed as function of Δ. *Quick check*: Δ₀ = initial loss minus f*.
- **Closed-Form Coefficient Matching**: The scheduler solves for K0, K1, K2 by meeting two point constraints (η(Δ′)=peak lr, η(Δ₀)=peak lr/divisor) and minimizing a Gaussian-weighted MSE between η(Δ) and a desired warm-up+decay profile near Δ′, yielding a closed-form solution. *Why needed*: Ensures fast, deterministic schedule computation without iterative methods. *Quick check*: Confirm that the derived coefficients satisfy the constraints and that η(Δ) is positive and unimodal across the warm-up and decay phases.
- **Target Loss Estimation**: Estimate f* to compute Δ. *Why needed*: Δ depends on f*. *Quick check*: Can use a short run or set f* slightly below initial loss.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Initial loss estimator → Δ′ selector → Coefficient solver (K0,K1,K₂) → Scheduler (η(Δ) while Δ≥Δ′ then cosine decay) → Optimizer step → Loss evaluation → Update Δ.

Critical path: Start: compute Δ₀ from initial forward pass. Then for candidate Δ′ values, solve for coefficients satisfying η(Δ′)=lr and η(Δ₀)=lr/div, plus Gaussian-weighted shape matching; pick Δ′ with minimal MSE. During training: at each step compute current Δ; if Δ≥Δ′ use η(Δ), else use cosine decay.

Design tradeoffs: Scheduler is simple and automatic but introduces hyperparameters κ and σ_F² derived from model shapes. It replaces manual warm-up tuning with a robust closed-form solution, at the cost of theoretical optimality. The reliance on f* estimate is a practical requirement.

Failure signatures:
1. Warm-up too short/long → divergence or slow convergence.
2. Incorrect κ calculation → mis-scaled steps, especially for sign-based optimizers.
3. Δ′ too close to Δ₀ → numerical instability (division near zero in K₂ formula).
4. For normSGD, missing weight decay/gradient clipping → theory violation, distance unbounded.

First three experiments:
1. Replicate LLaMA 124M (bs=32): compare adaptive scheduler vs best manual sweep (e.g., warm-up steps 100,500,1000) on final validation loss and stability.
2. Ablate target loss estimate: vary f* by ±20% from paper value; observe impact on Δ′ selection and final loss.
3. Compute κ per LLaMA layer; verify initial lr = lr/div and that warm-up length scales with batch size as predicted.

## Limitations
- Requires reasonable estimate of target loss f*; poor guesses may degrade.
- κ calculation depends on per-layer shapes; implementation complexity.
- Generalized smoothness assumption not verified beyond LLaMA transformers.
- Empirical validation only on two small LLaMA models (124M/210M) on FineWeb.
- For normSGD, theory assumes weight decay/gradient clipping to bound distance; may not hold generally.

## Confidence
- Theory (warm-up under generalized smoothness): Medium
- Scheduler design (closed-form): Medium
- Empirical results: Medium
