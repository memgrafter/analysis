# Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning for Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21175
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21175
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning (RSA-FT) consistently boosts multiple reward metrics (HPSv2.1, PickScore, ImageReward) across four RDRL baselines and multiple diffusion models by mitigating reward hacking through dual perturbations.

## Executive Summary
Reward hacking in diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL)—where models exploit non-robust reward signals without improving perceptual quality—is addressed by Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning (RSA-FT). RSA-FT flattens the reward landscape using two complementary perturbations: an adversarial image-space perturbation and a parameter-space perturbation inspired by Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM). Applied as a plug-in to existing RDRL methods (DRaFT-K, AlignProp, ReFL, DRTune), RSA-FT yields consistent, significant improvements across three key automated metrics (HPSv2.1, PickScore, ImageReward) on Stable Diffusion 1.5, SDXL, and SD3, with human evaluations also favoring its outputs. Ablations confirm the synergy of both perturbations.

## Method Summary
RSA-FT modifies the standard RDRL gradient update by evaluating the reward at a worst-case perturbation in both image and parameter space. Given a diffusion model with parameters θ, a text prompt c, and a frozen reward model r, the method: (1) generates an initial image x₀ = Diffuse(x_T, c; θ); (2) computes an image-space adversarial perturbation δx₀ = –ρ ∇ₓ₀ r / ||∇ₓ₀ r||; (3) computes a parameter-space perturbation εθ = –ρ_w ∇_θ r / ||∇_θ r|| (detached); (4) regenerates an image using perturbed parameters x₀′ = Diffuse(x_T, c; θ + εθ); (5) forms the perturbed image x_pert = x₀′ + δx₀; and (6) updates θ via gradient ascent on r(x_pert, c). This single-step min-max approximation encourages convergence to flat minima in the reward landscape, improving robustness without retraining the reward model. RSA-FT is compatible with gradient-based RDRL frameworks that maintain a computational graph through sampling (e.g., DRaFT-K) and is typically combined with LoRA fine-tuning for efficiency.

## Key Results
- On SD1.5 with AlignProp, HPSv2.1 improved from 24.93 to 32.02 and ImageReward from 0.032 to 0.528.
- Gains were consistent across SDXL, SD3, and multi-reward optimization setups.
- Human preference studies favored RSA-FT outputs over baselines.
- Ablations showed both image-space and parameter-space perturbations contribute, with their combination yielding the largest improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Input-space adversarial flattening
**Claim:** Suppresses non-robust reward directions by evaluating the reward at a worst-case image perturbation within an ρ-ball.
**Mechanism:** Computes δx₀ = –ρ ∇ₓ₀ r(x₀, c) / ||∇ₓ₀ r|| and uses r(x₀ + δx₀, c) to approximate the minimum reward locally, avoiding sharp, hackable regions.
**Core assumption:** Reward function is locally smooth; a single-step perturbation accurately captures the minimum in a small neighborhood.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "perturbations of its generated samples"; Section 4 formalizes in Equations (15,16).
**Break condition:** ρ too large → image leaves plausible manifold; ∇ₓ₀ r near-zero/noisy → unreliable direction.

### Mechanism 2: Parameter-space flattening via SAM
**Claim:** Encourages convergence to flat minima in the reward landscape w.r.t. model parameters.
**Mechanism:** Computes εθ = –ρ_w ∇_θ r(x₀, c) / ||∇_θ r|| (detached), regenerates x₀(θ+εθ), and updates using ∇_θ r(x₀(θ+εθ), c), approximating worst-case reward in a parameter neighborhood.
**Core assumption:** Reward function w.r.t. θ is smooth enough for single perturbation to capture local sharpness.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract references "parameter perturbations" and "SAM"; Section 4, Equations (17,18).
**Break condition:** Excessive ρ_w or inaccurate ∇_θ (e.g., from truncated diffusion) destabilizes training; doubles compute cost.

### Mechanism 3: Dual-space synergy
**Claim:** Joint image and parameter flattening yields doubly robust optimization, most effectively mitigating reward hacking.
**Mechanism:** Objective evaluates r(x₀(θ + εθ) + δx₀, c), addressing both input-level and parameter-level non-robustness simultaneously.
**Core assumption:** Worst-case directions in image and parameter spaces can be approximated independently and combined without destructive interference.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states joint use amplifies benefits; Section 5.2 ablation (Table 6) shows largest gains with combined perturbations.
**Break condition:** Mis-scaled ρ/ρ_w over-flattens; combined overhead (two diffusion forwards) may bottleneck.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Diffusion Model (θ) -> Sampler -> x₀ -> Reward Model (r) -> Score; Input Perturbation (δx₀) adds to x₀; Parameter Perturbation (εθ) perturbs θ; Optimizer (AdamW) updates θ using final reward gradient.

**Critical path (one training step):**
1. Sample x_T ∼ N(0,I) and text condition c.
2. Generate x₀ = Diffuse(x_T, c; θ). (first diffusion forward)
3. Compute ∇ₓ₀ r(x₀, c) → δx₀ = –ρ ∇ₓ₀ r / ||∇ₓ₀ r||.
4. Compute ∇_θ r(x₀, c) → εθ = –ρ_w ∇_θ r / ||∇_θ r|| (detach).
5. Generate x₀′ = Diffuse(x_T, c; θ + εθ). (second diffusion forward)
6. Form perturbed image: x_pert = x₀′ + δx₀.
7. Compute reward R = r(x_pert, c).
8. Backward: ∇_θ R (εθ constant) → AdamW step on θ (often LoRA parameters).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Perturbation scales (ρ, ρ_w)*: Control flattening strength; paper found 0.01 optimal. Too small → insufficient flattening; too large → distorted images, reward degradation.
- *One-step vs multi-step*: One-step used for efficiency and better correlation with generalization; multi-step could improve robustness at higher cost.
- *Detaching εθ*: Stabilizes training (SAM) but ignores higher-order effects.
- *Computational overhead*: Two diffusion forward passes and extra reward gradients roughly double sampling cost; may require fewer diffusion steps or gradient checkpointing.
- *Compatibility*: Requires RDRL frameworks that keep computation graph for last few diffusion steps (e.g., DRaFT-K, ReFL, DRTune); not for inference-only alignment.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Metric stagnation*: No improvement or decline in multiple reward metrics → likely hyper-parameter issue.
- *Visual quality loss*: Blurry or artifact-ridden images → over-flattening.
- *Training instability*: NaNs, spikes, divergence → excessive ρ/ρ_w or noisy gradients (e.g., too few diffusion steps).
- *No synergy*: Combined perturbations not better than single → misconfiguration or already robust reward model.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Implement DRaFT-K on SD1.5 with HPSv2; train 2500 iterations. Document reward hacking (e.g., HPS up but PickScore down).
2. **Full RSA-FT:** Add both perturbations (ρ=ρ_w=0.01) to identical baseline. Compare metrics and images; expect balanced gains and better visual quality.
3. **Ablation study:** Train input-only (ρ=0.01, ρ_w=0), parameter-only (ρ=0, ρ_w=0.01), and full RSA-FT. Quantify contributions; also sweep ρ, ρ_w around 0.01 to confirm insensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can RSA-FT be effectively integrated with PPO and DPO-based diffusion RL algorithms?** (Explicitly stated as future work; untested with policy gradient or direct preference optimization approaches.)
- **Would selective sharpness-aware weighting further improve robustness and interpretability compared to uniform flattening?** (Suggested in Discussion; current method applies uniform flattening, which may over-flatten smooth regions.)
- **Does replacing the one-step adversarial approximation with Gaussian averaging (as in randomized smoothing) provide certified robustness or better performance?** (Mentioned as an alternative for enhanced robustness but not adopted due to efficiency.)
- **Are the improvements from RSA-FT sustained in large-scale human evaluations?** (Inferred; paper acknowledges small human study (17 annotators) is not statistically powered.)

## Limitations
- Exact training prompt dataset is unspecified, creating a gap for faithful replication.
- Precise implementation details of the four RDRL baselines (schedulers, guidance, LoRA targets) must be inferred from original sources, risking deviation.
- Theoretical mechanism relies on smoothness assumptions not formally validated; evidence for "flattening" is correlative (improved metrics) without direct measurement of landscape sharpness.

## Confidence
- **High:** Claim that RSA-FT improves all three evaluated reward metrics (HPSv2.1, PickScore, ImageReward) across multiple models and baselines. Supported by extensive quantitative tables and human studies.
- **Medium:** Claim of dual-space synergy (input + parameter perturbations). Strongly supported by ablation results (Table 6), but independent contribution of each space's flattening is less quantified.
- **Low:** General mechanistic claim that "flattening" inherently leads to robust, non-hacked solutions. Evidence is correlative; lacks direct measurement of reward landscape sharpness before/after.
