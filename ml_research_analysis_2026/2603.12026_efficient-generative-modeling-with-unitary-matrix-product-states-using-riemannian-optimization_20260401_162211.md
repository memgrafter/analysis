# Efficient Generative Modeling with Unitary Matrix Product States Using Riemannian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12026
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12026
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Unitary MPS with Riemannian optimization yields up to 27× faster convergence and higher-quality samples compared to standard MPS.

## Executive Summary
Matrix Product States (MPS) are a powerful tensor network formalism for generative modeling, but their training suffers from slow convergence and instability due to a scaling ambiguity. This paper introduces Unitary MPS (UMPS), which fixes the partition function to 1 via a unit-norm constraint, eliminating the ambiguity. To efficiently optimize under this constraint, the authors develop a Riemannian optimization approach with a space-decoupling algorithm that handles the intersection of low-rank and unit-sphere manifolds. On Bars-and-Stripes and EMNIST datasets, UMPS achieves significantly faster convergence (up to 27× in negative log-likelihood reduction) and produces cleaner samples with better reconstruction fidelity than standard MPS, while remaining computationally efficient.

## Method Summary
The proposed UMPS-SD algorithm trains a generative MPS under a unitary constraint (Z=1) using Riemannian optimization. The MPS is maintained in a mixed-canonical form where the norm (partition function) is concentrated on a two-site merged core. For each pair of neighboring cores during sweeps, the core matrices are collapsed into a single matrix X. The space-decoupling parameterization expresses X as X = HV^T with H normalized (unit norm) and G = I - VV^T, mapping the original nonsmooth feasible set onto a smooth auxiliary manifold. The Riemannian gradient is computed from the Euclidean gradient of the negative log-likelihood and projected onto the tangent space of this manifold. A retraction operation updates (H, V) while respecting the constraints, after which X_new is factored via SVD and the cores are redistributed to preserve the mixed-canonical structure. This process avoids the loss of progress from projection steps and directly handles the scaling ambiguity, leading to stable and fast convergence.

## Key Results
- Up to 27× faster reduction in negative log-likelihood on EMNIST compared to standard MPS.
- Generated samples exhibit less noise and higher visual fidelity (e.g., clearer Bars-and-Stripes patterns, more coherent EMNIST digits).
- Improved reconstruction quality when completing half-images.
- Model efficiency is maintained; the effective bond dimension remains well below the prescribed maximum, demonstrating that the constraints do not waste capacity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Enforcing a unitary (unit-norm) constraint on the MPS wavefunction eliminates a global scaling degree of freedom, which otherwise causes slow convergence and oscillatory updates in standard gradient-based MPS training.
- **Mechanism:** The standard MPS generative model defines probabilities as \( P(v) = |\Psi(v)|^2 / Z \). Scaling all \(\Psi(v)\) by a constant \(c\) leaves \(P(v)\) unchanged but changes \(Z\) and the loss landscape, creating flat directions. By constraining \(\|\Psi\|_F^2 = Z = 1\) (unitary MPS), the optimization is restricted to a manifold where the normalization is fixed. This forces gradient updates to directly modify relative probabilities rather than global scale, providing a unique optimal representative and stabilizing descent.
- **Core assumption:** The scaling ambiguity is the primary cause of inefficient updates in unconstrained MPS optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "unitary MPS [...] offers clear benefits for unsupervised learning by reducing ambiguity in parameter updates and improving efficiency."
  - [section III.A & B]: Derives the constrained problem (P1) with \(Z=1\) and discusses how projection onto \(Z=1\) still loses progress, motivating unitary MPS.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring papers focus on MPS applications but do not analyze scaling ambiguity as a core bottleneck.
- **Break condition:** If the data distribution fundamentally requires a non-unitary representation for expressive efficiency (e.g., certain continuous or non-normalized models), the unitary constraint could limit model capacity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Formulating optimization directly on the Riemannian manifold (e.g., unit sphere) via retraction, instead of Euclidean gradient descent followed by projection, avoids losing update progress and yields more stable, efficient convergence.
- **Mechanism:** Standard projected gradient descent computes a Euclidean step and then projects back onto the constraint set, which can shrink the effective step size. Riemannian optimization computes a gradient in the tangent space and applies a retraction (e.g., Eq. 27) that respects the manifold's geometry. This preserves the intended search direction and avoids the "boundary oscillations" caused by projection, leading to a more direct descent trajectory.
- **Core assumption:** The loss of progress from projection is a significant factor in slow MPS training, and retraction provides a sufficiently accurate and stable approximation to exponential map.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Riemannian optimization approach [...] yields fast adaptation [...] stable updates."
  - [section II.B & III.C]: Defines Riemannian gradient, retraction, and presents Eq. 26 (Riemannian gradient representation) and Eq. 27 (retraction).
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like [24] establish the general utility of Riemannian optimization on manifolds; this paper adapts that framework to MPS-specific manifolds.
- **Break condition:** If the chosen retraction is a poor approximation to the exponential map or if the manifold has high curvature, convergence guarantees may weaken or require more careful step-size tuning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The space-decoupling parameterization transforms the original nonsmooth optimization problem (intersection of low-rank set \(\mathcal{M}_{\leq r}\) and unit sphere \(\mathcal{S}^{m\times n}\)) into a smooth Riemannian optimization problem on an auxiliary manifold \(\mathcal{M}^h\), enabling efficient application of Riemannian algorithms.
- **Mechanism:** The feasible set \(\mathcal{M}_{\leq r} \cap \mathcal{S}^{m\times n}\) is nonsmooth because \(\mathcal{M}_{\leq r}\) is not a manifold at points with rank < \(r\). Space-decoupling introduces auxiliary variables \((X, G)\) with \(XG = 0\), \(\|X\|_F=1\), and \(G = I - VV^\top\) (where \(V\) has orthonormal columns). This parameterization maps the smooth manifold \(\mathcal{M}^h\) onto the original nonsmooth set (Eq. 23). Optimization on \(\mathcal{M}^h\) uses a weighted Riemannian metric (Eq. 25) and a simple retraction (Eq. 27), avoiding explicit rank constraints during updates while implicitly maintaining low rank.
- **Core assumption:** The transverse intersection property (Appendix) ensures that once the optimal rank is found, projection onto the unit sphere is valid, and the parameterization correctly captures all feasible points without loss.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "space-decoupled strategy [...] enables efficient updates at the intersections of tensor manifolds."
  - [section III.C]: Defines \(\mathcal{M}^h\), the tangent space (Eq. 24), Riemannian gradient (Eq. 26), and retraction (Eq. 27). Explicitly states that the method "circumvents the nonsmooth constraints."
  - [corpus]: The method builds directly on Yang et al. [23], cited as the source of the space-decoupling framework.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal rank is unknown and the maximum allowed rank \(r_{\max}\) is much larger than needed, the auxiliary space dimension grows, potentially increasing computational cost. The transverse intersection proof assumes full-rank factorization; if the true optimal rank is strictly less than \(r_{\max}\), gradient-based search on \(\mathcal{M}^h\) may still converge but with possible inefficiency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Tensor Train (MPS) Decomposition**  
  *Why needed:* The entire model is built upon MPS. Understanding core tensors \(A^{(k)}\), bond dimensions \(\mathbf{r}\), and the wavefunction contraction \(\Psi(v) = \text{Tr}[A^{(1)}(v_1)\cdots A^{(d)}(v_d)]\) is essential.  
  *Quick check:* Given an MPS with bond dimension \(r\), what is the maximum number of independent parameters per core for binary data?  
  **Answer:** \(r \times 2 \times r = 2r^2\), since each core is \(r_{k-1} \times 2 \times r_k\).

- **Mixed-Canonical Form & Normalization**  
  *Why needed:* The algorithm maintains a mixed-canonical form where \(\|A^{(k,k+1)}_{\langle 2\rangle}\|_F^2 = Z\). This simplifies gradient computation (Eq. 22) and ensures the unit-norm constraint is equivalent to \(\|A^{(k,k+1)}_{\langle 2\rangle}\|_F = 1\) after each update.  
  *Quick check:* If cores \(A^{(1)},...,A^{(k-1)}\) are left-canonical and \(A^{(k+2)},...,A^{(d)}\) are right-canonical, where is the norm of the full MPS concentrated?  
  **Answer:** On the merged core \(A^{(k,k+1)}\), per Proposition 2.

- **Riemannian Optimization on Matrix Manifolds**  
  *Why needed:* The optimization is performed on the unit sphere manifold \(\mathcal{S}^{m\times n}\) (and its intersection with a low-rank set). Knowing how to compute tangent spaces (Eq. 15), Riemannian gradients via projection (Eq. 11), and retractions (Eq. 13) is crucial.  
  *Quick check:* For \(X \in \mathcal{S}^{m\times n}\), what is the tangent space \(T_X\mathcal{S}^{m\times n}\)?  
  **Answer:** \(\{ \dot{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n} \mid \langle X, \dot{X} \rangle = 0 \}\).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
MPS Core Store -> Two-Site Updater -> Space-Decoupling Engine -> Riemannian Optimizer -> SVD & Redistribution -> Sampling Module

**Critical path:**  
Initialize MPS cores in right-canonical form (with \(\|A^{(1)}\|_F=1\)). Loop over sweeps: for each neighboring pair \(k\), contract cores to matrix \(X\), apply space-decoupling parameterization (extract \(H, V\)), compute Riemannian gradient \(\nabla f(X)\) and transform to \((K, V_p)\), perform retraction to obtain new \(H_{new}, V_{new}\), reconstruct \(X_{new}\), SVD to get \(U_k, \Sigma_k, V_k\), reshape into updated cores while preserving mixed-canonical form. Repeat for \(l_{\max}\) loops.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Rank (`r_max`) vs. Capacity:* Larger `r_max` increases expressive power but raises computational cost \(O(n^2 r_{\max}^3)\) for SVDs and gradient. Appendix shows transverse intersection holds for any fixed rank; unknown optimal rank requires trial-and-error.  
- *Learning rate \(\theta\):* Too large causes divergence on manifold; too small slows convergence. Paper uses fixed \(\theta\); adaptive schemes (Adam-like on manifold) are future work.  
- *Space-decoupling weight \(\omega\):* Paper sets \(\omega=1\); different weights change the Riemannian metric (Eq. 25) and could affect convergence.

**Failure signatures:**  
- NLL does not decrease or increases erratically across loops → likely learning rate too high, or implementation error in retraction/gradient.  
- Bond dimension `r_max` is reached too early (Table II shows `r_mean` stabilizing far below `r_max` is healthy; if `r_mean` hits `r_max` quickly, model capacity is insufficient for data).  
- Generated samples remain noisy or invalid (e.g., non-stripes in BAS) after many loops → either model capacity (`r_max`) too low, or algorithm not converging due to incorrect manifold handling.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **BAS dataset convergence scan:** Train with `rmax=500`, `|T|=400`, `lmax=5`. Observe NLL drop (Fig 4a) and sample quality improvement (Fig 4b). Verify `r_mean` stabilizes below `rmax` (Table II).  
2. **EMNIST convergence speed comparison:** Against baseline MPS [13] with same `rmax` (200 or 400) and `|T|=100`. Record NLL vs. loops (Fig 6) and time to target NLL (Table III). Expect UMPS-SD to converge ~27x faster in effective updates.  
3. **EMNIST generative quality & reconstruction:** Train both models (`rmax=400`, `|T|=300`, `lmax=25` for MPS vs. `lmax=4` for UMPS). Generate samples (Fig 7) and perform half-image reconstruction (Fig 8). Expect UMPS to produce less noise and more coherent reconstructions with fewer updates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
- **Question:** How can the UMPS framework be extended to handle RGB images and more expressive two-dimensional tensor networks like PEPS?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "At present, the UMPS model is applicable only to binarized images... Future work may explore more expressive two-dimensional tensor networks, such as projected entangled pair states (PEPS)."  
- **Why unresolved:** 1D MPS lacks sufficient entanglement capacity for complex 2D image data with multiple channels; PEPS introduces high computational complexity and requires new optimization strategies.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A working PEPS-based generative model trained on RGB datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10) using Riemannian optimization, with demonstrated sample quality and scalability.

**Open Question 2**  
- **Question:** Can adaptive learning rate methods (e.g., Adam, Adagrad) be effectively formulated for Riemannian optimization on UMPS manifolds?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "one may design adaptive learning-rate schemes that maintain first- and second-order moment estimates of gradients in the tangent space, assigning individualized learning rates."  
- **Why unresolved:** Existing adaptive methods rely on Euclidean geometry; manifolds have curvature and varying tangent spaces, making direct translation nontrivial, especially for intersection manifolds in UMPS.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A Riemannian Adam algorithm tailored to UMPS constraints, with convergence guarantees and empirical superiority over fixed-step methods on BAS/EMNIST.

**Open Question 3**  
- **Question:** How can variance reduction techniques be integrated with stochastic Riemannian optimization to improve convergence for large datasets?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "when stochastic mini-batches are used... the variance of stochastic gradients on manifolds can severely affect convergence. Variance-reduction techniques provide a promising remedy."  
- **Why unresolved:** Variance reduction (e.g., SVRG) requires careful handling of retractions and tangent space projections; the non-convex manifold intersections in UMPS pose additional challenges.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A stochastic variance-reduced Riemannian gradient algorithm for low-rank tensor optimization, proving linear convergence and demonstrating faster training on full EMNIST.

**Open Question 4**  
- **Question:** What is the impact of gauge freedom on variational optimization of UMPS, and can standardized gauge-fixing strategies improve training stability?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "analyzing the impact of gauge freedom on variational optimization and adopting standardized gauge-fixing strategies can effectively suppress artificially low variational energies."  
- **Why unresolved:** Gauge degrees of freedom can cause ill-conditioning or spurious minima; systematic gauge fixing compatible with Riemannian constraints is not established for generative MPS.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis of gauge effects on the UMPS loss landscape; a gauge-fixing procedure that improves stability and sample quality in experiments.

**Open Question 5**  
- **Question:** How can efficient approximate contraction methods be combined with Riemannian optimization for higher-order tensor networks (e.g., PEPS) to maintain scalability?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Developing efficient approximate-contraction schemes and Riemannian-optimization strategies tailored to higher-order tensor networks represents a valuable... research direction."  
- **Why unresolved:** Exact contraction of 2D networks is intractable; existing approximations (e.g., boundary MPS) lack integration with manifold-constrained optimization and may break constraints.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An approximate contraction algorithm that computes gradients and partition functions within Riemannian updates, enabling training of larger PEPS models with competitive performance.

## Limitations
- **Reporting gaps:** Missing data splits, seeds, and hyperparameter details (learning rates, batch sizes) impede replication.
- **Ablation confound:** UMPS vs. standard MPS differs in both constraints and optimizer; cannot isolate which factor yields speedup.
- **Theoretical assumptions:** Space‑decoupling assumes transverse intersection; performance when true rank < r_max is not studied.
