# HASS: Hierarchical Simulation of Logopenic Aphasic Speech for Scalable PPA Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26795
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26795
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: HASS synthetic speech enables superior cross-site lvPPA detection (AUC 0.892 vs 0.850, macro F1 0.800 vs 0.778, recall 0.899 vs 0.659) compared to models trained on limited real data.

## Executive Summary
The HASS framework tackles the critical data scarcity challenge in automated diagnosis of logopenic variant primary progressive aphasia (lvPPA). By hierarchically simulating both lexical retrieval and phonological encoding deficits, and conditioning these impairments on clinical severity, HASS generates a large, diverse corpus of synthetic speech that mirrors the multi-level deficits observed in real patients. The generated data is used to train a Wav2Vec 2.0 model with LoRA adapters. In rigorous cross-site evaluations, the HASS-trained model significantly outperforms baseline models trained on small sets of real clinical recordings, demonstrating both higher diagnostic accuracy and better generalization to unseen clinical sites. This work establishes a scalable path for data-limited neurological speech disorders.

## Method Summary
HASS synthesizes speech in two stages. First, a clinician-guided prompt to a large language model (Gemini) produces text with embedded dysfluency markers representing lexical substitutions (due to impaired retrieval) and phoneme-level errors, pauses, repetitions, etc. (due to phonological encoding impairment), with marker density scaled by severity level (mild, moderate, severe). Second, the marked text is synthesized into audio using VITS neural TTS, preserving the inline markers as acoustic cues. The final corpus contains 12.81 hours of audio from 95 speakers, covering 40 prompts and three severity levels plus healthy controls. For detection, a Wav2Vec 2.0 base model is fine-tuned with LoRA on the concatenated HASS data, employing on-the-fly augmentations (speed, noise, volume, reverberation). Evaluation uses a strict cross-site paradigm: train on one institutional domain (Baycrest+Delaware) and test on another (Hopkins+Capilouto), and vice versa, with balanced test sets. Performance is compared to a baseline trained on limited real clinical data via 5-fold speaker-grouped cross-validation.

## Key Results
- HASS-trained model achieved AUC 0.892, macro F1 0.800, and recall 0.899 for the lvPPA class in cross-site evaluation.
- Baseline models trained on real clinical data achieved lower metrics: AUC 0.850, macro F1 0.778, recall 0.659.
- The improvement demonstrates that synthetic speech from HASS can effectively overcome data scarcity and generalize across clinical sites.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical simulation captures the cascaded nature of lvPPA deficits: impaired lexical access leads to word substitutions, while subsequent phonological encoding errors produce sound distortions and dysfluencies. Clinician-guided prompts ensure that the simulated patterns align with clinical descriptions. Severity conditioning modulates marker frequencies, approximating disease progression. This results in a synthetic dataset that, despite potential acoustic limitations, presents dysfluency patterns that Wav2Vec 2.0 can learn for robust detection. The model's LoRA-based fine-tuning efficiently adapts the pre-trained speech representation to these atypical patterns without overfitting to synthetic artifacts, especially when benefitting from large-scale data diversity.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Clinician -> Hierarchical Text Generator (LLM) -> Marked Text -> VITS TTS (with marker synthesis) -> HASS Audio Corpus -> Speech Augmentations -> Wav2Vec 2.0 + LoRA -> lvPPA Classifier -> Cross-site Evaluation.

Critical path: (1) Define impairment parameters (lexical retrieval deficit, phonological encoding deficit) and severity levels; (2) Generate disordered text with inline markers using LLM; (3) Convert text to audio with VITS, preserving markers; (4) Aggregate and augment audio; (5) Fine-tune Wav2Vec 2.0 with LoRA; (6) Evaluate on held-out real clinical data from different sites; (7) Compare against baseline trained on real data.

Design tradeoffs: Using an LLM (Gemini) for text generation offers flexibility but may produce out-of-distribution errors; rule-based alternatives would be more controllable but less expressive. VITS provides high-quality synthesis but may struggle with severe dysfluencies; a diffusion-based TTS could model artifacts better but is computationally heavier. LoRA reduces trainable parameters but may limit adaptation capacity if rank is too low. Cross-site evaluation ensures robustness but reduces available training data per site.

Failure signatures: Model performance drops dramatically on real test sets despite high validation on HASS data indicates overfitting to synthetic artifacts. If synthetic speech lacks perceptual plausibility (e.g., unrealistic pauses), expert review would reveal mismatch. Unexpectedly low cross-site improvement could indicate insufficient domain adaptation in HASS.

First experiments: 1) Generate a small batch of HASS samples for each severity and manually verify the presence and types of dysfluency markers. 2) Train a minimal Wav2Vec 2.0 model on a subset of HASS data (e.g., 10% clips) to confirm the training pipeline runs and yields reasonable loss curves. 3) Perform an ablation by removing augmentation or LoRA to measure their individual impact on validation performance on a held-out HASS set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the HASS framework's hierarchical simulation approach be extended to accurately model the distinct multi-level deficits of non-logopenic PPA variants (e.g., semantic or agrammatic)?
- To what extent do limitations of the VITS neural TTS architecture constrain the acoustic and perceptual fidelity of HASS-generated speech at severe impairment levels?
- How clinically valid and interpretable is HASS's severity conditioning, and does its multi-level marker distribution correspond to established quantitative measures of PPA severity?

## Limitations
- Exact LoRA hyperparameters and training schedule are unreported, risking suboptimal replication.
- Speaker-level train/test splits for baseline CV and cross-site domain partitions are not provided, raising potential data leakage concerns.
- No perceptual or fine-grained acoustic validation confirms that HASS outputs faithfully represent real lvPPA speech patterns, leaving open the possibility of artifact-driven performance gains.

## Confidence
- HASS synthetic data improves lvPPA detection over real clinical data alone: Medium.
- Hierarchical simulation is clinically grounded (high for design, medium for ecological validity): High for the hierarchical architecture; Medium for clinical realism.
- Cross-site generalization claim: Medium.

## Next Checks
1. Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep: Vary LoRA rank (4–16), dropout (0.0–0.3), and augmentation probabilities on a held-out HASS validation set; confirm reported AUC/F1 are attainable under multiple configurations.
2. Speaker leakage audit: Reconstruct speaker IDs from metadata or use embedding clustering to verify no speaker appears in both train and test for baseline CV and cross-site folds.
3. Speech realism assessment: Conduct a blinded perceptual evaluation with clinicians rating dysfluency type/severity, or compare key acoustic features (articulation rate, pause frequency) between HASS and real lvPPA samples.
