# Cross-Domain Uncertainty Quantification for Selective Prediction: A Comprehensive Bound Ablation with Transfer-Informed Betting

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.08907
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08907
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** TIB yields up to 5.4× coverage gain on NyayaBench v2 and LTT+EB gives 27% relative gain on MASSIVE at α=0.10.

## Executive Summary
Selective prediction in agentic caching systems requires finite‑sample risk guarantees to control the unsafe cache hit rate with limited calibration data. This paper systematically evaluates nine statistical bound families and introduces Transfer‑Informed Betting (TIB), which warm‑starts the Waudby‑Shah‑Ramdas (WSR) wealth process using a source domain’s risk profile. TIB remains valid under source‑target divergence and formally dominates standard WSR when domains match. Empirical results on four benchmarks show that combining LTT fixed‑sequence testing with Empirical Bernstein concentration yields dramatically tighter bounds: on MASSIVE (n=549 calibration) LTT+EB achieves 94.0% guaranteed coverage at α=0.10 versus 73.8% for Hoeffding+union (27% relative improvement), and on NyayaBench v2 (n=134) TIB achieves 18.5% coverage versus 3.4% for LTT+Hoeffding (5.4× improvement).

## Method Summary
The work evaluates a comprehensive suite of concentration bounds—Hoeffding, Empirical Bernstein, Clopper‑Pearson, WSR betting, Wasserstein DRO, CVaR, PAC‑Bayes—combined with multiple‑testing corrections (union bound and LTT fixed‑sequence). The proposed Transfer‑Informed Betting modifies the WSR wealth initialization by blending source‑domain empirical risk and variance via an effective sample size (neff=50) and a GROW strategy. Experiments use four datasets: MASSIVE, NyayaBench v2, CLINC‑150, Banking77. A SetFit classifier (all‑MiniLM‑L6‑v2 backbone, 8 examples per class) produces confidence scores (max softmax). Calibration/test splits are 50%/50% with seed 42. Thresholds are gridded at K=100 points in [0,0.99]. For each (α,δ) pair, the minimal threshold τ with upper confidence bound ≤ α is selected, and test coverage (fraction of test samples with confidence ≥ τ) is reported while enforcing ≤δ type‑I error and zero guarantee violations.

## Key Results
- On MASSIVE (n=549), LTT with Empirical Bernstein achieves 94.0% guaranteed coverage at α=0.10 versus 73.8% for Hoeffding+union (27% relative gain).
- On NyayaBench v2 (n=134), TIB achieves 18.5% coverage at α=0.10, a 5.4× improvement over LTT+Hoeffding.
- Across all four benchmarks, WSR betting combined with LTT fixed‑sequence testing yields the tightest non‑transfer bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TIB transfers source‑domain risk information to initialize the WSR wealth process, effectively shrinking the initial confidence width and dominating the standard WSR bound when source and target match. Under distribution shift, the GROW blending strategy conservatively mixes source estimates with a prior to retain validity. LTT fixed‑sequence testing allocates the total error probability δ sequentially to thresholds in descending order, spending the full δ at each test and avoiding the multiplicative K penalty of the union bound. This yields substantially tighter upper confidence bounds, especially for early (high‑confidence) thresholds. Additionally, Empirical Bernstein leverages empirical variance to obtain concentration tighter than Hoeffding when confidence scores exhibit low variability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Calibration data → Empirical risk estimation at each threshold → Bound computation (choose family + multiple‑testing correction) → Upper confidence bound (UCB) calculation → Threshold selection (min τ with UCB ≤ α) → Test set evaluation → Coverage & violation report.

**Critical path:**  
1. Train SetFit and extract confidence scores for calibration and test sets.  
2. For each threshold in the grid, compute empirical risk on the calibration set.  
3. For a given bound family and (α,δ), compute UCB(τ) for every τ.  
4. Choose τ* = min{τ : UCB(τ) ≤ α}.  
5. Apply τ* to test set: compute coverage = fraction of test samples with conf ≥ τ* and verify test risk ≤ α.  
6. Repeat across α,δ combinations.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Bound family*: Hoeffding is simple but distribution‑agnostic; Empirical Bernstein tighter when variance is low; WSR betting adapts to the stochastic process.  
- *Multiple‑testing*: Union bound incurs a factor K penalty; LTT fixed‑sequence spends δ sequentially, gaining power but requiring thresholds sorted and full δ per test.  
- *Transfer vs non‑transfer*: TIB leverages source data to tighten bounds but risks negative transfer if domains diverge; non‑transfer bounds are always valid but looser.  
- *Calibration size*: All bounds improve with n; TIB’s advantage grows with source informativeness (neff).

**Failure signatures:**  
- Unexpected guarantee violations (test risk > α) indicate bound implementation errors or incorrect multiple‑testing.  
- LTT performing worse than union bound suggests flawed threshold ordering or δ spending (e.g., not using full δ per test).  
- TIB not dominating WSR on identical source‑target distributions points to mis‑initialization of wealth or incorrect blending.  
- Unstable Empirical Bernstein (negative variance estimates) requires variance clipping; wealth process for WSR/TIB must remain a supermartingale (check via simulation).

**First experiments:**  
1. **Baseline reproducibility:** Implement Hoeffding + union bound on MASSIVE (seed 42) and verify coverage at α=0.10 is near 73.8% (±1%).  
2. **LTT vs union bound:** Using Empirical Bernstein, implement LTT fixed‑sequence and confirm coverage on MASSIVE reaches ~94% at α=0.10, significantly outperforming the union‑bound version.  
3. **TIB warm‑start sanity check:** Generate synthetic source and target data with identical Bernoulli distributions, apply TIB (neff=50) and standard WSR, and verify TIB’s UCB(τ) ≤ WSR’s UCB(τ) for all thresholds.

## Limitations
- **SetFit training details:** The exact hyperparameters beyond “8 examples per class” (e.g., epochs, learning rate, data augmentation) are not specified, which may affect confidence score distributions and replicability.
- **Synthetic score generation for CLINC‑150/Banking77:** The method for transforming published accuracy distributions into per‑sample confidence scores is unclear, hindering exact replication on large datasets.
- **Advanced bound implementation nuances:** Critical details for WSR/TIB (initial wealth, λ clipping, zero‑variance handling) and PAC‑Bayes transfer (discretization of source risk, λ optimization) are omitted, potentially causing deviations in achieved tightness.

## Confidence
- **TIB dominance over WSR when source‑target match:** Medium (theoretical guarantee holds, but empirical validation depends on unknown implementation details).
- **Coverage improvements (e.g., 27% relative gain on MASSIVE):** Medium (results hinge on correct bound implementations and classifier calibration).
- **Non‑transfer bound ranking (LTT+EB tightest):** High (direct empirical ablation; less sensitive to unknowns).
