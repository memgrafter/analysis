# Gated Removal of Normalization in Transformers Enables Stable Training and Efficient Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10408
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10408
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** TaperNorm matches normalized baselines' training loss (~2% gap) and yields up to 1.22× inference speedup on H100 by eliminating per-token statistics via gated tapering and weight folding.

## Executive Summary
Pre-norm Transformers rely on per-token normalization (RMSNorm/LayerNorm) for training stability, but this adds computational overhead and reduces interpretability. This paper introduces TaperNorm, a gated normalization layer that initially behaves exactly like standard normalization during a warmup period, then smoothly transitions to a fixed, sample-independent affine map. A global gate decays to zero, after which per-token statistics vanish and the static scalings can be folded into adjacent linear projections, reducing inference cost. For cases where the final normalization is also removed, a fixed-target auxiliary loss on the pre-logit residual stream scale acts as an explicit scale anchor, preventing unbounded logit growth ("logit chasing"). Empirical results on TinyStories (1M–30M parameters) and GPT-2 fine-tuning show that TaperNorm closely matches baseline training loss and achieves up to 1.22× throughput speedup on an NVIDIA H100 in a last-token logits microbenchmark.

## Method Summary
TaperNorm replaces standard normalization layers in pre-norm Transformers. During a warmup phase, a global gate `g` is held at 1, making TaperNorm behave identically to RMSNorm/LayerNorm while collecting exponential moving averages (EMAs) of activation statistics. At warmup end, a per-layer scalar `c` is calibrated from these EMAs to align the scaling branch with the normalization branch. Then `g` is cosine-decayed to 0 while `γ` and the calibrated `c` remain trainable. When `g=0`, TaperNorm outputs a fixed affine transformation (for LayerNorm) or scaled tensor (for RMSNorm), eliminating per-token computation. The static parameters can be folded into the subsequent linear layer's weight (and bias). If the final normalization is also removed, an auxiliary loss `L_aux = λ (s(h) - s_tgt)^2` is added, where `s(h)` is a token-scale statistic (e.g., RMS) and `s_tgt` is a fixed target estimated during warmup, providing an explicit scale anchor to prevent logit chasing.

## Key Results
- Training loss matches normalized baselines within 0.7–1.8% relative gap on TinyStories across 1M–30M parameter scales.
- Competitive validation perplexity when fine-tuning GPT-2 models.
- Inference throughput speedup up to **1.22×** on NVIDIA H100 (bf16) in a last-token logits microbenchmark after folding static scalings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Gated Tapering and Calibration
TaperNorm stabilizes training by exactly matching standard normalization during warmup, then transitioning to a fixed affine map. A global gate `g` is kept at 1 while EMAs of activation statistics are collected. Upon warmup completion, a per-layer scalar `c` is calibrated from these EMAs to ensure the scaled branch matches the normalized branch at the transition point. As `g` cosine-decays to 0, the layer’s output becomes `c·h·D·γ̃` (RMSNorm) or an affine transformation (LayerNorm), eliminating per-token statistics. The core assumption is that EMAs accurately capture the necessary distribution for calibration; otherwise, a mismatch during decay could disrupt training.

### Mechanism 2: Final Normalization as Scale Anchor
The final normalization in pre-norm Transformers acts as a (near) 0-homogeneous map, which removes radial gradients at the output. For a 0-homogeneous function `f`, the Jacobian satisfies `h·J_f(h) = 0`. Consequently, for logits `z = Norm_final(h)·W_out`, the radial component `⟨∇_h ℓ, h⟩` is zero, preventing the model from reducing loss by simply scaling `h` radially. Without such an anchor, cross-entropy loss has a radial component that pushes `||h||` upward whenever the correct class has a positive margin, leading to "logit chasing" and unstable training.

### Mechanism 3: Auxiliary Loss as Explicit Anchor
When the final normalization is removed, a fixed-target auxiliary loss on the pre-logit residual stream scale provides an explicit alternative anchor. The loss `L_aux = λ E[(s(h) - s_tgt)^2]` uses a token-scale statistic `s(h)` (e.g., RMS) and a fixed target `s_tgt` estimated during warmup. Its gradient injects a radial restoring force that counteracts the radial push from cross-entropy, stabilizing training. The assumption is that `s_tgt` remains appropriate throughout tapering; if not, the loss may over- or under-constrain the scale.

- **Pre-norm Transformer architecture**: Normalization is applied before each sublayer (MHSA/MLP), differing from post-norm where it follows. - **RMSNorm/LayerNorm operations**: RMSNorm normalizes by root mean square without mean subtraction; LayerNorm subtracts mean and normalizes by standard deviation. Both are (near) 0-homogeneous. *Why needed*: TaperNorm directly replaces these layers; their formulas define the two branches and the homogeneity property central to scale anchoring. *Quick check*: What does 0-homogeneous mean? Are RMSNorm and LayerNorm exactly 0-homogeneous?
- **Radial gradients and logit chasing**: Cross-entropy with softmax encourages scaling up logits if the correct class margin is positive, leading to unbounded growth. A 0-homogeneous normalization removes this radial gradient component. *Why needed*: The paper’s theory hinges on this dynamic to explain the role of final normalization. *Quick check*: Show that for cross-entropy, if the correct class margin is positive, scaling all logits up reduces loss. Why does this imply a radial gradient?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Token embedding → [ (Input Norm → MHSA → Residual add) → (MLP Norm → MLP → Residual add) ] × L → Output Norm (optional) → Output projection (tied/untied). Global gate controller `g(k)` shared across all TaperNorm layers.
- **Critical path**:
  - *Training*: Warmup (`g=1`) with standard pre-norm; collect EMAs. At warmup end, calibrate `c` from EMAs, freeze `c`, copy `γ→γ̃`. Cosine-decay `g` to 0; `L_aux` may be active if final norm removed. At convergence, `g≈0` and TaperNorm behaves as fixed affine.
  - *Inference*: Set `g=0`. For each internal TaperNorm, fold `c·D·γ̃` (or affine parameters) into the next linear layer's weight (and bias). Remove TaperNorm from graph. Fold output norm similarly if tapered.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Internal vs. final tapering: Internal tapering yields most speedup; final tapering aids interpretability but requires `L_aux` for stability.
  - Auxiliary loss: Without it, All-Taper may diverge; λ requires tuning.
  - Warmup length and decay: Warmup must allow EMA convergence; decay rate affects transition smoothness.
  - Calibration precision: EMA decay rate and bias correction impact `c` quality; poor calibration causes transition mismatch.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Loss divergence after taper start, especially when final norm removed without `L_aux`.
  - Exploding pre-logit token scale or logit norm in All-Taper runs.
  - Gradient magnitudes clustering by presence/absence of final normalization.
  - Folding artifacts if `c` or `γ̃` are extreme; verify folded outputs match within tolerance.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Warmup match**: Plot training loss of TaperNorm (`g=1`) vs. baseline RMSNorm; curves should nearly overlap during warmup.
  2. **Scale anchoring**: Remove final norm and plot pre-logit token scale (e.g., RMS) and logit norm vs. steps; with `L_aux` these should stabilize, without they should grow unbounded.
  3. **Folding correctness**: After training, set `g=0` and fold internal scalings. Compare logits of folded model vs. unfused TaperNorm (`g=0`) on a batch; max absolute difference should be <1e-5 (bf16 tolerance).

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to models ≤30M parameters; scalability to LLM sizes unproven.
- Reported speedups from last-token logits microbenchmark may not generalize to full autoregressive generation with KV caching.
- EMA calibration assumes stationary activation statistics; non-stationary distributions or insufficient warmup could cause transition instability.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (gated tapering/folding): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (final norm as scale anchor): High
- Mechanism 3 (auxiliary loss anchor): Medium
- Overall empirical claims: Medium (due to small-scale experiments)
