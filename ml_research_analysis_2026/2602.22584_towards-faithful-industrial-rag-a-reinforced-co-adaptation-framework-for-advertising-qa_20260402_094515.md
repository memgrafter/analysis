# Towards Faithful Industrial RAG: A Reinforced Co-adaptation Framework for Advertising QA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22584
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22584
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 92.7% reduction in URL hallucination, 28.6% increase in like rate, 46.2% decrease in dislike rate in a two-week online A/B test.

## Executive Summary
Hallucination—particularly the generation of fabricated URLs—poses severe financial and compliance risks in industrial advertising question answering. This paper introduces a reinforced co-adaptation framework that jointly optimizes retrieval and generation to combat this problem. The system combines Graph-aware Retrieval (GraphRAG) over a curated high-citation knowledge subgraph for multi-hop evidence selection with evidence-constrained reinforcement learning (GRPO) that uses a multi-dimensional reward signal (faithfulness, style compliance, safety, URL validity). Offline experiments show a 72% reduction in hallucination rate. A two-week online A/B test with millions of interactions delivered a 28.6% increase in like rate, a 46.2% decrease in dislike rate, and a 92.7% reduction in URL hallucination. The framework has now been in production for over six months with acceptable latency.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework tightly couples two components: GraphRAG and GRPO. GraphRAG operates on a high-citation knowledge subgraph to retrieve multi-hop evidence chains for a query, ensuring that the most relevant and trustworthy information is surfaced. The generator (a large language model) is then fine-tuned via GRPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm that directly optimizes a composite reward. The reward penalizes deviations from the retrieved evidence (faithfulness), enforces domain-specific style and safety rules, and explicitly checks URL validity. Crucially, retrieval and generation are co-adapted: the retriever learns to supply evidence that helps the generator achieve high rewards, while the generator learns to ground its outputs in the provided evidence. This alignment creates a feedback loop that systematically suppresses hallucination at both stages.

## Key Results
- Offline hallucination rate reduced by 72%.
- Online A/B test (2 weeks, millions of interactions): like rate +28.6%, dislike rate -46.2%, URL hallucination -92.7%.
- System has been in production for over six months with latency within acceptable bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The co-adaptation design aligns the objectives of retrieval and generation through a shared reward signal. GraphRAG leverages structural relationships in a high-quality knowledge subgraph to retrieve multi-hop evidence, closing information gaps that often lead to fabrication. GRPO then optimizes the generator to remain faithful to that evidence while also satisfying style, safety, and URL constraints. Because the reward explicitly penalizes unsupported content, the generator learns to rely on retrieved evidence rather than "making up" answers. Simultaneously, the retriever is implicitly encouraged to provide evidence that covers the query's intent, creating a virtuous cycle: better retrieval yields better generation, and better generation reinforces retrieval quality. This joint optimization directly targets the root causes of hallucination in industrial QA.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Graph-aware Retrieval (GraphRAG)**
   - *Why needed*: To retrieve multi-hop evidence from a structured knowledge base, improving relevance and reducing information gaps.
   - *Quick check*: Does the retriever produce evidence chains that cover entities and relations relevant to the query?

2. **High-citation knowledge subgraph**
   - *Why needed*: Acts as a trusted, high-quality subset of the full knowledge graph, reducing noise and focusing on reliable sources.
   - *Quick check*: Is the subgraph coverage sufficient for the target domain? Are sources well-cited?

3. **Evidence-constrained Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)**
   - *Why needed*: To optimize the generator beyond token-level likelihood, explicitly penalizing deviations from retrieved evidence and other constraints.
   - *Quick check*: Does the reward model accurately capture faithfulness, style, safety, and URL validity?

4. **Multi-dimensional reward design**
   - *Why needed*: Hallucination is multi-faceted; a composite reward addresses different failure modes simultaneously.
   - *Quick check*: Are reward components properly balanced to avoid trade-offs that degrade other metrics?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Raw Text Corpora -> High-citation Knowledge Subgraph Builder -> Knowledge Subgraph -> GraphRAG Retriever -> GRPO-Optimized LLM Generator -> Response

Critical path:  
User query → Query encoding → Multi-hop retrieval on knowledge subgraph (GraphRAG) → Evidence selection → Conditional generation (LLM) → Post-processing checks (URL validity, style, safety) → Response

Design tradeoffs:
- GraphRAG vs. vector retrieval: GraphRAG provides interpretable evidence chains and multi-hop reasoning but may increase latency; the trade-off is acceptable given production latency targets.
- Multi-dimensional reward: Balancing competing objectives (faithfulness vs. style vs. safety) requires careful weight tuning; misconfiguration can harm relevance or conciseness.
- Joint co-adaptation: Tight coupling increases system complexity but yields greater hallucination reduction compared to independent pipelines.

Failure signatures:
- URLs in the response that do not appear in any retrieved evidence.
- Statements that contradict the retrieved evidence.
- Violations of prescribed style guidelines or safety rules.
- Excessive retrieval latency leading to timeouts.

First experiments:
1. Evaluate retrieval hit rate: measure the percentage of queries where GraphRAG retrieves at least one gold evidence passage from a labeled test set.
2. Ablate GRPO reward terms: train models with subsets of the multi-dimensional reward and compare hallucination rates and user engagement metrics.
3. Load testing: Measure end-to-end latency under increasing query rates to ensure scalability and acceptable performance under production traffic.

## Limitations
- Methodology transparency: Exact construction of the high-citation knowledge subgraph and implementation details of GraphRAG are not described, hindering reproducibility.
- Domain specificity: Evaluation is limited to advertising QA; generalizability to other domains (e.g., medical, legal) with different hallucination patterns is unclear.
- Unmeasured reward trade-offs: The multi-dimensional reward may introduce hidden trade-offs (e.g., reduced relevance or conciseness) that are not evaluated.

## Next Checks
1. Independent Knowledge Graph Audit: Reconstruct or obtain access to the proprietary high-citation knowledge subgraph to audit source quality, coverage of advertising-specific entities/URLs, and determine whether GraphRAG's gains stem from superior graph construction versus the base retrieval method.
2. Ablation Study of Framework Components: Isolate the effects of (a) GraphRAG versus standard vector retrieval, and (b) GRPO with multi-dimensional rewards versus supervised fine-tuning or single-reward RL, on hallucination rates and other metrics.
3. Cross-Domain Portability Test: Apply the same co-adaptation framework (with domain-appropriate reward modeling) to a different high-stakes QA domain with known hallucination issues (e.g., biomedical literature QA) to measure transferability of performance gains and identify domain-specific reward tuning requirements.
