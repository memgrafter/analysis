# A Loss Landscape Visualization Framework for Interpreting Reinforcement Learning: An ADHDP Case Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14600
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14600
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: The visualization framework reveals that standard stabilizers smooth TD errors and critic landscapes but fail to correct a strongly anisotropic wedge-shaped actor loss geometry, which biases policy updates toward saturation and causes divergence.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning (RL) training dynamics, particularly in actor‑critic algorithms such as ADHDP for spacecraft control, are difficult to interpret. This paper introduces a visualization framework that projects parameter trajectories into 2‑D using PCA and generates four complementary plots: critic match loss surfaces, actor loss landscapes under a frozen critic, actor weight‑TD‑time trajectories, and state‑TD maps. Applied to ADHDP variants, the framework shows that while common stabilizers (target networks, Huber loss, cost scaling) smooth the critic landscape, they do not resolve a fundamental wedge‑shaped anisotropy in the actor loss. This geometric bias pushes policy updates toward a single dominant direction, eventually saturating control actions and causing closed‑loop divergence even when TD errors appear stable. The framework thus highlights that policy failure can stem from unfavorable actor optimization geometry rather than solely from critic instability.

## Method Summary
The framework records critic and actor parameters, training batches, and TD errors during ADHDP training. Principal component analysis (PCA) reduces the high‑dimensional weight and state trajectories to their dominant 2‑D subspaces. Four visualizations are then produced:
1. Critic match loss surface: a 3‑D plot of the critic’s fitting objective over the PCA plane.
2. Actor loss landscape: evaluation of the actor’s loss with the final frozen critic over the actor PCA plane.
3. Actor weight‑TD‑time trajectory: the first principal component of actor weights plotted against TD error and time.
4. State‑TD map: the first two principal components of state variables colored by TD error magnitude.
These plots expose optimization geometry, anisotropy, and the relationship between parameter drift and TD fluctuations.

## Key Results
- The critic match loss surface is smoothed by target networks, Huber loss, and cost scaling, but retains a characteristic ridge structure.
- The actor loss landscape exhibits a strongly anisotropic wedge shape: a narrow valley along one direction and steep walls along another, which biases policy updates toward a single dominant direction.
- This wedge geometry persists despite stabilizers and leads to control saturation and closed‑loop divergence, even when TD error metrics remain stable.
- The actor weight trajectory shows monotonic drift along the first principal component while TD decreases, and the state‑TD map identifies extreme state regions that trigger large TD errors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The critic match loss landscape makes the optimization geometry of the critic explicit, showing how TD targets shape the loss surface and indicating critic stability.
- **Mechanism:** PCA projects recorded critic parameters onto a 2‑D plane. The critic match loss (Eq. 3) is evaluated over a grid in that plane, creating a 3‑D surface. Its shape (e.g., smooth basin vs. sharp ridge) reveals how the TD targets condition the fitting objective.
- **Core assumption:** The principal components capture the dominant directions of variation in the critic trajectory, so the projection is representative.
- **Break condition:** Low variance explained by the first two PCs (< 80 %) or minimal critic variation can misrepresent the geometry.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The actor loss landscape under a frozen critic reveals the policy optimization geometry, exposing anisotropy (e.g., wedge shape) that biases updates toward saturation.
- **Mechanism:** After training, the final critic is frozen. Actor parameters recorded during training are PCA‑projected, and the actor loss (Eq. 4) is evaluated over the PCA plane. The resulting surface shows steep and flat directions.
- **Core assumption:** The frozen critic accurately represents the value function at training’s end and the actor’s loss landscape is representative.
- **Break condition:** Unstable or poorly trained critic; PCA misalignment with true dominant actor directions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The time‑TD‑actor weight and state‑TD maps link parameter evolution and state distribution to TD error, identifying instability drivers and policy response.
- **Mechanism:** Actor weights (or states) and TD errors are recorded. PCA reduces dimensionality; the first principal component(s) are plotted against TD and time (or state variables). This shows how policy parameters move relative to TD fluctuations and which state regions cause large TD.
- **Core assumption:** Principal components of weight/state trajectories capture the main modes of variation relevant to instability.
- **Break condition:** Low PCA variance; drastic state distribution shifts.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Recorded weights/batches/probe states → PCA projection → Grid construction → Loss evaluation (Eqs. 3,4) → Plotting (4 visualizations)
- **Critical path:**
  1. During training, record critic/actor parameters at episode ends or fixed intervals.
  2. Collect a representative batch of (state, action, TD target) tuples for critic loss.
  3. For critic match loss: use final critic as reference, run PCA on recorded critic trajectory, define grid in PCA plane, evaluate Eq. (3) at grid points.
  4. For actor loss: freeze final critic, collect a fixed probe state set D, run PCA on actor trajectory, evaluate Eq. (4) over PCA grid.
  5. For time‑TD‑actor: record actor weights and TD errors per step, apply PCA to actor weights, plot first PC vs. TD vs. time.
  6. For state‑TD: record states and TD during simulation, apply PCA to states, plot first two PCs against TD.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - PCA vs. nonlinear methods: PCA is linear and interpretable but may miss nonlinear structures; t‑SNE/UMAP capture nonlinearity but are harder to map back to parameters.
  - Reference point choice: Using final parameters is common, but intermediate references can show local geometry changes.
  - Grid resolution: Finer grids yield smoother surfaces but increase computational cost (typical 25–50 points per axis).
  - Probe state set for actor loss: Should cover typical state distribution; too narrow a set may not reflect full policy behavior.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low explained variance by PCA (first two PCs < 70%): projection likely omits important directions.
  - Critic loss landscape with extreme spikes or checkerboard artifacts: indicates numerical instability or insufficient data.
  - Actor loss landscape completely flat: may mean critic is nearly constant or actor already at bound.
  - State‑TD map showing all low TD errors: could indicate over‑fitting or lack of challenging states.
  - Discrepancy between low TD metrics and poor control performance: suggests geometric bias not captured by scalar TD.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce basic ADHDP: Run the basic variant, generate all four visualizations, confirm wedge‑shaped actor loss and saturation.
  2. Compare target network effect: Add a target critic network, visualize; observe smoother critic landscape but persistent actor wedge, linking target networks to reduced TD jitter but not geometry fix.
  3. Test action scaling: Reduce torque limits or adjust actor output scaling; check if the actor loss wedge narrows and whether control avoids saturation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the anisotropic wedge-shaped actor loss landscape identified in ADHDP generalize to other actor‑critic algorithms and control tasks?
- Basis in paper: The study only applied the visualization framework to ADHDP variants for spacecraft attitude control; no other algorithms or domains were tested.
- Why unresolved: It is unclear whether the geometry is specific to ADHDP's structure (e.g., heuristic DP, quadratic cost) or a common issue in deep actor‑critic methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply the same four‑component visualization framework (with PCA) to standard benchmarks (e.g., MuJoCo, PyBullet) using diverse algorithms (DDPG, SAC, TD3) and compare the actor loss landscape shapes (e.g., eigenvalue spectra from PCA) to see if similar anisotropy occurs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What algorithmic interventions can reshape the actor loss landscape to be more isotropic and prevent policy saturation under action constraints?
- Basis in paper: The authors noted that standard stabilizers (target network, Huber loss, cost scaling) smooth the critic landscape but fail to correct the actor geometry; they suggested possible directions (e.g., regularization on control effort, output scaling, saturation penalties) but did not implement or test them.
- Why unresolved: The root cause of the wedge shape is not understood; thus, effective modifications remain unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement candidate interventions (e.g., add a penalty term for actions near saturation, rescale actor outputs relative to torque limits, use alternative policy parameterizations) in ADHDP and use the visualization framework to quantify changes in the actor loss landscape (e.g., condition number of the PCA covariance, distribution of gradients) and monitor closed‑loop stability and torque saturation levels.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Is the unfavorable actor loss geometry in ADHDP inherent to the combination of quadratic cost functions and neural network approximators, or can it be altered by changing the critic's loss function or network architecture?
- Basis in paper: The paper used fixed MLP architectures and standard TD losses; the relationship between critic design and actor geometry was not investigated theoretically or experimentally beyond adding Huber and cost scaling.
- Why unresolved: The critic's match loss landscape influences the actor's loss surface; thus, the critic's formulation may determine the actor geometry, but this coupling is not understood.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments varying critic architecture (depth, width, activation functions) and loss functions (e.g., quantile Huber, adaptive weighting) while keeping the actor fixed; use the visualization framework to correlate changes in critic loss landscape (e.g., curvature, anisotropy) with changes in actor loss landscape geometry; also attempt theoretical analysis of the loss Hessian under overparameterization.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter gaps: The exact Huber loss threshold κ_huber and the precise actor update schedule for variant 2 are unspecified, potentially influencing critic smoothness and update stability.
- Probe state ambiguity: The composition and sampling method of the fixed probe state set D for the actor loss landscape are not defined; its distribution could bias the observed wedge geometry.
- PCA projection risk: The framework assumes the first two principal components capture the dominant variance; if explained variance is low (<80%), the 2‑D landscapes may misrepresent the true high‑dimensional geometry.
- Limited scope: Results are confined to one task (spacecraft attitude control) and ADHDP variants; generalizability to other algorithms or domains remains unverified.

## Confidence
- Critic match loss visualization: High – The method directly evaluates the critic’s fitting objective over the PCA plane and consistently surfaces TD‑target‑induced surface shapes.
- Anisotropic actor loss wedge: Medium – The paper clearly shows a wedge‑shaped valley and its link to saturation, but sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., cost scaling, probe states) and PCA assumptions tempers certainty.
- Stabilizers smooth critic but not actor geometry: Medium – While trends are demonstrated across variants, missing hyperparameter details (Huber κ, TPS σ) and single‑task evaluation limit the robustness of this conclusion.
- Generalizability to other algorithms: Low – No cross‑algorithm experiments were performed; the paper only covers ADHDP variants.

## Next Checks
1. **Huber threshold sweep:** Train ADHDP with multiple κ_huber values (e.g., 0.5, 1.0, 2.0) and quantify changes in critic landscape curvature and actor wedge persistence.
2. **Probe state distribution test:** Evaluate the actor loss landscape using probe states drawn from early, mid, and late training phases; verify whether the wedge shape is consistent across distributions.
3. **Cross‑algorithm replication:** Apply the identical visualization pipeline to SAC and PPO on a standard continuous control task (e.g., MuJoCo HalfCheetah) to test if anisotropic actor geometry is a broader phenomenon in actor‑critic RL.
