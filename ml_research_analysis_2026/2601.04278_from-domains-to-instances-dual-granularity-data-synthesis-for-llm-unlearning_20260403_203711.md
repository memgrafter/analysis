# From Domains to Instances: Dual-Granularity Data Synthesis for LLM Unlearning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04278
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04278
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BiForget synthesizes high-quality forget data using the target model itself, improving relevance by ~20% and diversity by ~0.05 while halving data size on Harry Potter, and enabling robust forgetting and utility preservation across multiple benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical limitation in machine unlearning for large language models: current benchmarks often produce forget sets that are misaligned with the true forgetting scope and depend on external data generators, resulting in inefficient or incorrect unlearning. To address this, the authors introduce BiForget, a fully automated framework that generates high-quality forget data by leveraging the target model itself, using seed-guided and adversarial prompting strategies tailored for both domain-level (broad concepts) and instance-level (specific facts) unlearning. BiForget achieves superior relevance and diversity while halving data volume on the Harry Potter benchmark, and across multiple datasets (Harry Potter, WMDP, TOFU) it enables more robust forgetting and better preservation of model utility. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of adversarial probing for robustness against membership inference attacks.

## Method Summary
BiForget synthesizes forget sets at two granularities without external data. For domain-level, it first generates a diverse seed set of domain concepts from the target model, then creates samples by instantiating handcrafted prompt templates with these seeds. A domain indicator ϕ filters out-of-domain outputs. Semantic diversity (SimCSE) is monitored, and synthesis stops when changes fall below a threshold. In a second phase, adversarial probing using jailbreak and membership inference attack templates elicits deeply memorized samples, which are filtered by a Min‑k% probability threshold. For instance-level, the target instance is rephrased via a dedicated template until semantic saturation. The resulting synthetic forget set is evaluated on relevance, diversity, efficiency, and downstream unlearning performance across standard benchmarks.

## Key Results
- On Harry Potter, BiForget improves relevance (centroid distance) by ~20% and diversity (remote-clique) by ~0.05 while reducing data size by half compared to SOTA.
- Across Harry Potter, WMDP, and TOFU benchmarks, BiForget achieves more robust forgetting (lower memorization, better privacy) and better utility preservation.
- Ablation studies confirm that removing adversarial probing (jailbreaking and MIA) significantly degrades privacy robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Target‑Model‑Guided Data Generation
Claim: Synthetic forget data generated by the target model itself better aligns with the model's internal knowledge distribution than data from external generators, leading to more effective unlearning.  
Mechanism: The target model is prompted to generate samples via seed‑guided and adversarial prompts. Because the model was trained on the source corpus, its generations statistically reflect patterns and knowledge encoded in the forget set, creating a synthetic proxy that approximates the ideal forget set with lower Wasserstein distance to the true forget distribution.  
Core assumption: The target model's output distribution over relevant prompts is a closer proxy to the pre‑training data distribution for the forget domain than a stronger, external model's distribution.  
Evidence: Abstract states BiForget exploits the target model; Section 3.1 describes the target‑model‑guided paradigm; related work (DUET, GUARD) also use model‑guided synthesis.  
Break condition: If the target model has severely deficient knowledge in the forget domain (e.g., due to safety fine‑tuning), self‑generated data may have low coverage, making an external generator preferable.

### Mechanism 2: Dual‑Granularity Synthesis for Scope Control
Claim: Explicitly distinguishing between domain‑level (broad concept) and instance‑level (specific statement) forgetting and synthesizing data accordingly yields forget sets with appropriate semantic scope, preventing incomplete forgetting from template overfitting or insufficient coverage.  
Mechanism: Domain‑level: generate a seed pool of domain‑related "points" (concepts, facts) from the target model, then instantiate diverse prompt templates to create broad coverage; monitor embedding diversity (SimCSE) and stop when change < ϵ. Instance‑level: rephrase a single target statement into numerous surface variants using a rephrasing prompt; convergence based on semantic similarity.  
Core assumption: The ideal forgetting scope is the set of all semantically equivalent expressions of the targeted knowledge, and this scope can be approximated by maximizing embedding diversity until saturation.  
Evidence: Section 2.2 defines ideal forget sets; Sections 3.2 & 3.3 describe distinct pipelines; Figure 3 shows convergence of semantic similarity.  
Break condition: If the domain indicator ϕ is inaccurate or rephrasing prompts fail to generate meaning‑preserving variants, the synthetic set misrepresents the intended scope.

### Mechanism 3: Adversarial Probing for Robustness
Claim: Adversarial probing (jailbreaking & membership‑inference‑style prompts) during synthesis exposes deeply memorized or implicit knowledge that standard prompting misses, making the forget set more robust against recovery attacks.  
Mechanism: After seed‑guided synthesis, the model is prompted with jailbreak templates to elicit suppressed content and with MIA‑style prompts that generate QA pairs. Samples with high Min‑k% token probability (indicating memorization) are retained, surfacing high‑risk data points that are more likely to be recoverable post‑unlearning.  
Core assumption: Some forgettable content is only accessible through adversarial prompts; including such samples in D_f ensures unlearning targets the model's most resilient memories.  
Evidence: Section 3.2 details the two probing stages; Table 5 shows ablation removing probes increases privacy leakage; Figure 6 shows BiForget lower adversarial accuracy under Enhanced GCG.  
Break condition: If adversarial prompts are ineffective (e.g., model is strongly aligned and refuses jailbreaks), this stage may add no new samples, reducing its contribution.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (domain‑level): Target Model -> Seed Generation -> Domain Synthesis (P_dom) -> Domain Indicator (ϕ) -> Convergence Monitor -> (loop needed) -> Adversarial Probing (J, A) -> MIA Threshold (τ) -> Ω_dom_f
Component map (instance‑level): Target Model -> Instance Queries -> Instance Synthesis (P_inst) -> Convergence Monitor -> Ω_inst_f
Evaluation: Ω_f -> Evaluation Suite (relevance, diversity, efficiency, unlearning metrics)

Critical path:
- Domain‑level: Seed Generation → Seed‑guided Synthesis (iterative with Convergence Monitor) → Adversarial Probing (Jailbreak + MIA) → Final Ω_dom_f.
- Instance‑level: Instance Generation → Rephrasing (iterative with Convergence Monitor) → Final Ω_inst_f.

Design tradeoffs:
- Relevance vs. Diversity: Aggressive adversarial probing may add low‑relevance but high‑diversity samples; remote‑clique attempts to balance.
- Efficiency vs. Coverage: Convergence threshold ϵ and batch size d control trade‑off between synthesis volume and semantic saturation.
- Target Model Quality: If the target model lacks domain knowledge, synthetic data quality degrades; external generator may be needed.

Failure signatures:
- High centroid distance & low remote‑clique: Synthesis is misaligned and homogeneous.
- Utility drop after unlearning: Forget set too large or contains non‑forgettable content.
- No convergence: Diversity metric never stabilizes, indicating ineffective prompt/seed design.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Synthesis Quality Profiling**: Run BiForget on Harry Potter with high max_rounds; plot Figure 3 to verify convergence; inspect samples for coherence and ϕ compliance.
2. **Downstream Unlearning Efficacy**: Use Ω_f as forget set with GA_KL; compare memorization (C1‑C4), utility, and privacy against using original book text or textbook‑style data (Table 2).
3. **Ablation of Adversarial Probes**: Disable Stage II (jailbreaking/MIA); run unlearning and measure PrivLeak (Table 5); expect significantly worse robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can BiForget be effectively extended to large-scale and continual unlearning scenarios with multiple, sequential forgetting requests?
Basis: Conclusion states future work.
Why unresolved: Current framework evaluates single‑request static forget sets; continual unlearning introduces challenges like catastrophic forgetting and interference not addressed.
What evidence would resolve: Experiments simulating sequential unlearning across domains/instances, measuring retention degradation and synthesis efficiency over time.

### Open Question 2
How can BiForget generate high‑quality forget sets for target domains where the model has limited inherent knowledge or strong safety alignments (e.g., cybersecurity)?
Basis: Limitations note constraints from safety alignment or limited knowledge; results show underperformance on cybersecurity.
Why unresolved: Target‑model‑guided synthesis relies on model's internal knowledge; if model is weakly calibrated or over‑aligned, it may refuse or produce low‑quality off‑domain content.
What evidence would resolve: Analysis of refusal rates and semantic alignment across models with varying cybersecurity knowledge; techniques like controlled decoding or boundary‑aware prompting.

### Open Question 3
Do the proposed relevance and diversity metrics (centroid distance, remote‑clique) reliably correlate with actual unlearning efficacy and robustness in the absence of an ideal forget set?
Basis: Acknowledged in Section 3.4; no ideal forget set exists.
Why unresolved: Metrics measure dataset properties, not unlearning outcomes directly; unclear if improved metrics guarantee better forgetting/utility across methods.
What evidence would resolve: Large‑scale correlation studies across synthetic datasets (varying metrics) and multiple unlearning methods, with statistical validation against adversarial probing success as ground truth.

### Open Question 4
Can the seed‑guided and adversarial prompting stages be made more adaptive to minimize semantic drift and ensure comprehensive coverage of the ideal forgetting scope?
Basis: Limitations note reliance on prompt quality and sampling randomness causing minor drift or uneven coverage; fixed prompts and static threshold ϵ.
Why unresolved: Prompts are heuristic; stopping criterion may saturate before full coverage, especially for complex domains.
What evidence would resolve: Ablation with dynamic prompt generation (model‑generated refinements) and alternative convergence metrics; higher remote‑clique without proportional data increase.

## Limitations
- Exact implementation of domain indicator ϕ, convergence thresholds, batch sizes, and chunking method are missing, hindering faithful reproduction.
- The full set of prompt templates for seed‑guided, adversarial, and rephrasing stages is undisclosed; only examples are provided.
- Critical evaluation metric definitions (remote‑clique formula, t‑SNE parameters) are not fully specified, risking reproducibility of reported results.
