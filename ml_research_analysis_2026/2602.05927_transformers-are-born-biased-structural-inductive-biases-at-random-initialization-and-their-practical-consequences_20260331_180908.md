# Transformers Are Born Biased: Structural Inductive Biases at Random Initialization and Their Practical Consequences

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.05927
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05927
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Randomly initialized transformers exhibit extreme, statistically significant token preferences (up to 10.2%) due to representation contraction from asymmetric activations and self-attention.

## Executive Summary
Randomly initialized transformer language models are not neutral blank slates; they already harbor severe, systematic biases in token preferences. This work mechanistically reveals that asymmetric nonlinear activations (e.g., GELU) in MLP sublayers induce inter-sequence representation contraction, aligning token outputs across sequences toward a seed-specific direction. Self-attention then amplifies this via intra-sequence aggregation. Empirically, top-1 token frequencies reach 0.06%–10.2% with statistical significance. The findings enable SeedPrint, a fingerprinting method distinguishing same-seed models post-training, and explain the attention-sink phenomenon via inherent positional variance decay, mitigable by variance scaling without performance loss.

## Method Summary
Reproduction involves constructing a decoder-only transformer (e.g., nano GPT-2: 12 layers, 768d, 32k vocab) with standard initialization. Generate random token sequences (e.g., length 1024), perform a forward pass, and record argmax token IDs per sequence to compute top-1 frequency and Bonferroni-corrected p-values. For representation contraction, extract last-token hidden states after each layer and compute average pairwise cosine similarity across sequences, expecting monotonic increase with depth. For attention-sink mitigation, pretrain models with variance scaling interventions (e.g., multiply attention output by √i or √(i/T)) on OpenWebText for 200k steps, then evaluate sink rate and perplexity on WikiText-2.

## Key Results
- Top-1 token frequencies reach 0.06%–10.2% across architectures (nano GPT-2, Nano LLaMA-2, 1.2B GPT-2), all statistically significant (Bonferroni-corrected p<0.01).
- SeedPrint reliably fingerprints same-seed models after pre-training and fine-tuning, even at 7B scale and across domain shifts, with p<0.01; competing baselines fail.
- Variance equalization reduces attention-sink rates to near zero while maintaining competitive perplexity (e.g., 21.02 vs. baseline 21.33 on WikiText-2).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: MLP sublayers with asymmetric nonlinear activations induce inter-sequence representation contraction, aligning token outputs from different sequences toward a common, seed-dependent direction.
- **Mechanism**: The asymmetric activation function (e.g., GELU) applied after LayerNorm causes the representations of random inputs to collapse along a dominant direction. LayerNorm increases input variance, pushing values into GELU’s asymmetric regime. Repeated MLP blocks amplify this effect, making outputs from different sequences increasingly cosine-similar.
- **Core assumption**: LayerNorm is present and uses learnable scale parameters; initialization follows standard GPT-2/LLaMA schemes.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “asymmetric nonlinear activations in MLP sublayers induce global (inter-sequence) representation concentration”; [section 4.2] Empirical comparison of ReLU vs. tanh; Figure 5 shows ReLU causes rapid increase in pairwise cosine similarity while tanh does not; [section 4.2] Theoretical Proposition 2 proves ReLU leads to monotonic increase and eventual collapse to similarity 1.
- **Break condition**: Removal of LayerNorm (inputs stay near zero, GELU becomes effectively symmetric); use of a symmetric activation function (e.g., tanh); residual connections dampen but do not eliminate the effect.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Self-attention amplifies the inter-sequence contraction seeded by MLP layers by inducing intra-sequence aggregation, pushing token representations within a single sequence toward a shared direction.
- **Mechanism**: At initialization, query-key dot products have small variance, making attention weights approximately uniform. The resulting value aggregation acts as a running average: each token’s output becomes an average of prior tokens. This averaging operation reinforces any pre-existing directional bias from MLPs and further contracts representations across sequences.
- **Core assumption**: Attention weights are near-uniform at initialization due to small weight-scaled Gaussian initialization; simplified model (Attn0) where attention is uniform average is valid.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “self-attention further amplifies this effect through local (intra-sequence) aggregation.”; [section 4.3] Figure 7 shows self-attention-only model does not contract, but full model (MLP + attention) contracts faster than MLP-only; [section 4.4] Proposition 5 analytically derives that intra-sequence cosine similarity increases with attention layers and converges to 1.
- **Break condition**: Highly non-uniform attention weights (e.g., from specialized initialization or architectural constraints); skipping attention layers; using attention variants that do not aggregate values (e.g., linear attention with orthogonal projections).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The intra-sequence aggregation in self-attention creates a positional variance decay (variance ~ 1/i for position i), making the first token an outlier. This positional discrepancy is causally linked to the attention-sink phenomenon.
- **Mechanism**: Because each token’s output is an average of all previous tokens, the variance of later tokens is reduced by a factor of 1/i relative to the first token. The first token retains full initial variance, becoming a stable numerical reference. Optimization then latches onto this high-variance token as an “attention sink” to absorb probability mass.
- **Core assumption**: Value vectors are i.i.d. zero-mean with equal variance; attention weights uniform; no residual or normalization interventions at this stage.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “fundamental positional discrepancy inherent to the attention mechanism’s intra-sequence contraction that is causally linked to the attention-sink phenomenon.”; [section 6.2] Figure 12 shows variance scaling interventions (positional attenuation/amplification) nearly eliminate attention sinks while preserving perplexity; [section 4.4] Figure 9 empirically validates the theoretical 1/√i variance decay curve.
- **Break condition**: Variance equalization (e.g., scaling outputs by √i); architectural changes that break the uniform averaging property (e.g., learned positional biases, ALiBi).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Representation contraction in nonlinear networks**
  - *Why needed*: Understanding how initial random weights already distort the geometry of token representations is key to diagnosing the source of extreme token preferences.
  - *Quick check*: Why does a symmetric activation like tanh not cause inter-sequence contraction while ReLU does?
- **Concept: Self-attention as a weighted averaging operator at initialization**
  - *Why needed*: The amplification mechanism relies on attention behaving like a uniform average due to small random weights. This assumption explains why intra-sequence contraction occurs.
  - *Quick check*: How does the assumption of uniform attention weights simplify the analysis of intra-sequence contraction?
- **Concept: Positional variance propagation in recurrent averaging**
  - *Why needed*: The attention-sink mechanism is mathematically rooted in the 1/i variance decay derived from the averaging process. Recognizing this statistical pattern is essential for designing interventions.
  - *Quick check*: What is the expected variance of the output at position i in a sequence of length T when each position averages all previous values?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input → Embedding → [Pre-norm → MHA → Residual Add] × L → [Pre-norm → MLP → Residual Add] × L → Final Norm → LM Head (with tied embeddings)
- **Critical path**: MLP blocks seed inter-sequence alignment; alternating MHA blocks amplify through intra-sequence averaging; final LM head translates aligned representations to extreme logits.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Depth intensifies contraction (more blocks = more alignment). Choice of activation (GELU vs. ReLU vs. SwiGLU) affects contraction rate via asymmetry. Normalization choice (LayerNorm vs. RMSNorm) alters activation distribution variance. Attention head count and sequence length moderate amplification (longer sequences slightly reduce intra-sequence contraction).
- **Failure signatures**:
  1. Extreme top-1 token frequency (>0.1% on random sequences) well above uniform baseline.
  2. High average pairwise cosine similarity (>0.3) of last-token representations across random sequences.
  3. Attention sink rate >0.2 on random or real text (first token’s average attention weight >0.25).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Token preference profiling**: Generate N random sequences, run forward pass, record argmax token IDs per sequence. Compute empirical frequency distribution and Bonferroni-corrected p-value against uniform. Expect significant deviation.
  2. **Architectural ablation**: Compare full model, MLP-only, and attention-only variants. Measure (a) top-1 token frequency and (b) average pairwise cosine similarity of last-token reps. Expect MLP-only and full to show bias; attention-only not.
  3. **Variance scaling intervention**: Modify attention output by multiplying by √i (positional amplification) or √(i/T) (attenuation). Retrain small model and measure (a) attention sink rate on WikiText-2 and (b) validation perplexity. Expect sink rate near zero and perplexity comparable to baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: How do initialization-induced biases interact with training dynamics, particularly gradient-based optimization and semantic knowledge acquisition?
  - *Basis*: [explicit] "While we have shown that initial biases remain detectable after training, a more rigorous theoretical treatment is needed to understand how these structural tendencies interact with gradient-based optimization and the acquisition of semantic knowledge."
  - *Why unresolved*: The current work provides empirical evidence of persistence but lacks a formal model linking the seed-dependent contraction geometry to optimization trajectories and the emergence of semantic structure.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Develop a mathematical theory describing the evolution of the contracted direction under common optimizers (SGD, Adam) and validate it by tracking bias correlations across checkpoints on controlled semantic learning tasks.
- **Question 2**: Do the observed seed-specific biases and their downstream effects persist or transform in ultra-large language models (e.g., >100B parameters)?
  - *Basis*: [explicit] "while we evaluated models up to the 7B parameter scale, the behavior of these biases in ultra-large-scale regimes remains an area for further empirical verification."
  - *Why unresolved*: Scaling may profoundly change optimization, data diversity, and architectural stability, potentially amplifying, diminishing, or qualitatively altering representation contraction and its consequences.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Train multiple models at selected large scales (13B, 70B, 100B+) with different seeds; measure token preference intensity, representation cosine similarity, fingerprint stability, and attention-sink rates across layers and sequence lengths.
- **Question 3**: Can we intentionally manipulate the initial representational geometry to yield beneficial properties such as improved model alignment, faster convergence, or increased stability?
  - *Basis*: [explicit] "Future research should explore how to intentionally manipulate the initial representational geometry to improve model alignment or enable more efficient pre-training, ultimately demystifying the transition from a structured initialization to a fully capable language model."
  - *Why unresolved*: The paper demonstrates that initialization is not neutral but does not provide design principles for beneficial initialization schemes; it remains unknown whether controlled deviations from standard Gaussian initialization could steer training.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Propose and test alternative initialization distributions that directly address identified pathologies (e.g., variance-preserving across positions, isotropic hidden states); compare training speed, final perplexity, downstream task performance, and robustness against baselines.
- **Question 4**: How do architectural choices—particularly activation functions beyond GELU and normalization types beyond LayerNorm—affect the magnitude and nature of the inter- and intra-sequence contraction mechanisms?
  - *Basis*: [inferred] The mechanistic analysis centers on GELU and LayerNorm (or RMSNorm in some models), but the paper suggests the mechanism may be general; systematic comparisons across popular variants (SwiGLU, SiLU, RMSNorm vs. LayerNorm) are not provided.
  - *Why unresolved*: Understanding architectural sensitivity is crucial for generalizing the findings to the diverse landscape of modern LLMs, where different components may modulate the asymmetry-driven contraction.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Perform controlled ablations on identical transformer architectures, swapping only the activation and normalization layers while keeping other variables fixed; measure contraction metrics (pairwise cosine similarities), token preference distributions, and attention-sink prevalence after random initialization and throughout training.

## Limitations
- **Generalization scope**: Evidence primarily covers decoder-only GPT/LLaMA-style models with pre-norm and LayerNorm; unknown if findings hold for encoder-only (BERT), post-norm, or alternative normalizations (RMSNorm without learnable scale).
- **Causal strength for attention-sink**: The link between positional variance decay and attention-sink is correlational; other factors (e.g., initialization geometry, training dynamics) may contribute.
- **Fingerprinting practicalities**: SeedPrint’s robustness to different pretraining corpora, longer training, or architectural mutations (e.g., added layer, changed head count) is not fully stress-tested.

## Confidence
- **MLP-induced inter-sequence contraction**: High – Theoretical proof (Prop. 2) and consistent empirical validation across multiple activation functions.
- **Self-attention amplification**: Medium – Relies on uniform-attention approximation (Attn0); empirical support but assumes small initial weight variance, which may not hold with scaled initialization or attention biases.
- **Attention-sink as variance-decay artifact**: Medium-Low – Variance scaling mitigates sinks, but mechanism inferred from initialization-state analysis; post-training dynamics could override it.
