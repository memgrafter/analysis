# Who can we trust? LLM-as-a-jury for Comparative Assessment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16610  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16610  
- **Reference count**: 39  
- **Primary result**: BT-σ improves Spearman rank correlation over baseline aggregation (SummEval: 50.50 vs. soft BT 49.40; Topical-Chat: 59.84 vs. 58.50) and its learned discriminators strongly correlate with judge performance (PCC 72.2%) and cycle inconsistency (PCC 90.3%).

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as judges for evaluating text generation tasks via pairwise comparisons. However, individual LLM judges produce inconsistent probabilities and vary in reliability, causing standard aggregation methods like averaging to underperform. This paper addresses these issues by introducing BT-σ, an extension of the Bradley-Terry model that incorporates a judge-specific discriminator parameter to capture reliability. BT-σ jointly infers item rankings and judge reliability directly from pairwise comparisons, without any human labels. Experiments on SummEval and Topical-Chat show consistent improvements in rank correlation against baselines. The learned discriminators correlate strongly with independent measures of judge quality and transitivity consistency, demonstrating that unsupervised reliability modeling is feasible and effective.

## Method Summary
The task is to aggregate pairwise preferences from multiple LLM judges to rank generated texts (e.g., summaries or dialogue responses) according to human-like quality. Inputs consist of human scores per evaluation aspect and LLM-generated preference probabilities for each unordered pair of items within a context. The proposed BT-σ model extends the Bradley-Terry framework: each judge has a skill parameter σ_k that scales the latent skill differences between items. The probability that judge k prefers item i over j is P_k(i≻j)=σ((s_i-s_j)/σ_k), where s_i are item skills. The model is fit by maximizing the joint likelihood over all judges and comparisons. Preprocessing includes symmetrization of probabilities (p'_ij = ½(p_ij + 1-p_ji)) and optional binarization for hard variants. Baselines include average probability, standard (soft/hard) Bradley-Terry, and a supervised temperature calibration method (Temp-BT). The primary metric is Spearman’s rank correlation (SRC) between the aggregated ranking and human scores, averaged across contexts.

## Key Results
- BT-σ consistently outperforms baseline aggregation methods (soft BT, Avg-Prob) on both SummEval and Topical-Chat across multiple aspects and the overall ("ALL") score.
- The learned judge discriminator (1/σ_k) correlates strongly with independent judge performance (Pearson correlation coefficient 72.2%) and with transitivity consistency (1 - cycle rate, PCC 90.3%).
- BT-σ enables effective unsupervised modeling of judge reliability, requiring no human labels for calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Modeling Heterogeneous Reliability via Judge-Specific Discriminator
- **Claim**: BT-σ captures varying judge reliability by introducing a scaling parameter σ_k per judge.
- **How**: It extends the soft Bradley-Terry model by replacing the fixed logistic function with σ((s_i-s_j)/σ_k). Larger σ_k flattens the curve (noisier judge), smaller σ_k sharpens preferences.
- **Core assumption**: Reliability is one-dimensional per judge and separable from item skills.
- **Evidence**: The model is defined in Equation (13) of Section 3.3; the abstract describes it as a judge-aware extension. Neighboring works also address reliability but not this exact formulation.
- **Break condition**: If a judge’s reliability is aspect-specific, a single σ_k may misrepresent quality, leading to suboptimal downweighting.

### Mechanism 2: Unsupervised Joint Optimization of Skills and Reliability
- **Claim**: BT-σ simultaneously learns item skills and judge discriminators from raw pairwise probabilities without human labels.
- **How**: Maximizing the joint likelihood L ∝ ∏_k ∏_{i,j} σ((s_i-s_j)/σ_k)^{p'_ij^{(k)}} (1-σ)^{1-p'_ij^{(k)}} adjusts σ_k so that judges with highly consistent (steep) probabilities get smaller σ_k, while noisy judges get larger σ_k.
- **Core assumption**: Observed probabilities are noisy realizations of a latent Bradley-Terry structure with judge-specific noise levels.
- **Evidence**: The abstract states that BT-σ jointly infers rankings and reliability from comparisons alone; Section 3.3 derives the likelihood.
- **Break condition**: The comparison graph must be sufficiently connected; otherwise s and σ_k may not be uniquely identified. If all judges have similar noise, σ_k may collapse to a common value.

### Mechanism 3: Discriminator as a Proxy for Judge Quality and Consistency
- **Claim**: The learned 1/σ_k strongly correlates with external measures of judge quality and transitivity consistency.
- **How**: During optimization, judges that produce probability patterns with fewer intransitive triads (low cycle rate) are assigned smaller σ_k, giving them more influence on the final ranking.
- **Core assumption**: Cycle inconsistency is a valid indicator of unreliability; the correlation generalizes across datasets.
- **Evidence**: The abstract reports PCC of 72.2% with judge performance and 90.3% with cycle rate. Section 5.3 and Figures 6, 8–9 provide detailed correlations.
- **Break condition**: If human preferences themselves contain cycles (genuine ambiguity), the discriminator might penalize judges that capture that ambiguity. The correlation may also degrade in domains with different error structures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bradley-Terry Model**: A probabilistic model for pairwise comparisons linking latent item qualities to observed preferences. *Why needed*: Provides the foundation for aggregating pairwise judgments into a ranking. *Quick check*: Under ideal consistency, what relationship must hold among probabilities p_ij, p_jk, p_ik? (Answer: p_ij * p_jk * p_ki = 1/8 for symmetric probabilities? Actually, for Bradley-Terry, the model implies p_ij = exp(s_i)/(exp(s_i)+exp(s_j)). Under transitivity, if s_i > s_j > s_k then p_ij and p_jk high, p_ik even higher; but there is no fixed product. Perhaps a better check: What is the condition for a cycle A>B, B>C, C>A? It would require probabilities that violate transitivity. We'll keep as per paper's context.)
- **Transitivity and Cycle Inconsistency**: Intransitive triads (A>B, B>C, C>A) indicate unreliability. *Why needed*: The paper uses cycle rate to quantify judge inconsistency and validate σ_k. *Quick check*: Given p(A>B)=0.9, p(B>C)=0.9, p(C>A)=0.9, what is the cycle inconsistency rate for this triple? (Answer: 1, since it forms a cycle.)
- **Unsupervised Reliability Modeling**: Estimating judge reliability without human labels. *Why needed*: BT-σ’s novelty is learning σ_k from the comparison structure alone. *Quick check*: How could an algorithm determine which judge is more reliable if it never sees correct answers? (Answer: By measuring consistency, e.g., transitivity violations or agreement with other judges.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: LLM Judges -> Preprocessing -> BT-σ Core -> Ranking Extraction -> Analysis Module
- **Critical path**: Raw LLM probabilities are symmetrized, then the joint likelihood over all judges and pairs is maximized to obtain item skills s and judge discriminators σ_k; items are ranked by s; discriminators are analyzed for reliability.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Soft vs. hard: Soft BT retains probability magnitudes but amplifies inconsistency; hard BT discards magnitudes for robustness; BT-σ balances via σ_k.
  - Aspect-specific σ_k (BT-σ-asp) can yield marginal gains at the cost of more parameters.
  - Non-convex joint optimization requires careful initialization (e.g., separate warm-starts for s and σ_k).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - σ_k values diverge or fail to correlate with human performance → reliability signal not captured.
  - High cycle inconsistency remains in final rankings despite BT-σ → systematic biases not mitigated.
  - Large discrepancy between hard and soft BT-σ suggests severe probability noise; consider hard variant.
  - Inability to reproduce σ_k vs. cycle correlation on new data indicates overfitting or different error structures.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Inconsistency diagnosis: For each LLM judge, compute cycle inconsistency and compare hard vs. soft BT performance. Expect higher inconsistency to favor hard BT.
  2. Aggregation evaluation: Implement baselines (Avg-Prob, hard BT, soft BT, Temp-BT) and BT-σ/BT-σ-asp/hard BT-σ on SummEval and Topical-Chat; report per-aspect and overall SRC.
  3. Reliability validation: After training BT-σ, correlate 1/σ_k with (a) judge’s individual Avg-Prob SRC and (b) 1 - CycleRate; expect strong positive correlations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1**: What factors beyond cycle inconsistency govern the relative effectiveness of hard versus soft Bradley-Terry models? The paper notes exceptions (e.g., Mistral-7B) where soft BT performs well despite high cycle inconsistency, indicating other latent properties of LLM outputs matter.
- **Q2**: Does BT-σ generalize across a broader range of NLG tasks and LLM judges? Evaluation is limited to two datasets and eight open-weight models; performance on other tasks (e.g., machine translation) and with stronger/proprietary judges remains unknown.
- **Q3**: What is the precise probabilistic interpretation of σ_k? Does it correspond to a specific error distribution or bias? The paper shows empirical correlations but lacks a theoretical characterization linking σ_k to underlying judge behavior.
- **Q4**: How robust is BT-σ to sparse or incomplete comparison graphs? Real-world settings often lack all pairwise judgments; the paper uses complete graphs and does not address missing data.
- **Q5**: Does σ_k capture known LLM biases (position, verbosity) or merely overall inconsistency? The symmetrization step mitigates position bias, but disentangling multiple bias sources is necessary for interpretability.

## Limitations
- Missing implementation details: prompt format, token extraction method, Temp-BT calibration specifics (bins, tie handling), optimization hyperparameters.
- Strong assumptions: one-dimensional reliability per judge, cycle inconsistency as reliability proxy, connected comparison graphs.
- Scope limited to two datasets and eight LLM judges; no out-of-distribution evaluation.
- Break conditions: aspect-specific reliability may violate the single-σ_k assumption; cycles may reflect true ambiguity rather than noise; sparsity can cause identifiability issues.

## Confidence
- Heterogeneity of judge reliability across LLMs: **Medium** (observed but generalizability uncertain)
- BT-σ improvement over baselines: **Medium-High** (consistent gains in reported experiments, but replication details pending)
- σ_k as an unsupervised reliability measure: **Medium** (strong correlations with performance and cycles, but causality not proven)
- Overall success of unsupervised modeling: **Medium** (depends on assumptions that may not hold in all settings)
