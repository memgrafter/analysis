# Listen to the Layers: Mitigating Hallucinations with Inter-Layer Disagreement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09486  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09486  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: CoCoA-SIG reduces hallucinations across multiple tasks, e.g., improving TruthfulQA T×I by 12.39 percentage points over greedy decoding.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often produce plausible but factually incorrect content, known as hallucinations. This paper hypothesizes that factual outputs correlate with representational stability in the model's middle layers. To leverage this, the authors propose CoCoA, a training-free decoder that computes two inter-layer disagreement metrics (ConMLDS and fMLDS) from middle-layer hidden states and uses them to penalize unstable candidate tokens during generation. An extension, CoCoA-SIG, incorporates self‑information gating to dynamically increase penalties for high‑surprise tokens. Experiments across Llama‑3, Mistral, and Qwen models on truthfulness‑focused benchmarks show consistent improvements: e.g., +12.39 pp on TruthfulQA (vs. greedy), +1.57 pp over the previous best baseline, with gains also observed in open‑domain QA, summarization, code generation, and mathematical reasoning. The approach incurs a modest latency overhead (~1.3×) and achieves statistical significance (p ≪ 0.05).

## Method Summary
CoCoA operates during decoding: for each generation step, the model retrieves hidden states from a predefined set of middle layers. For each candidate token (typically the top‑k from the initial distribution), it computes two disagreement metrics—ConMLDS and fMLDS—that capture the spread of representations across those layers. These metrics are combined (optionally multiplied by a self‑information gate in the SIG variant) to produce a penalty score. The penalty is subtracted from the candidate’s logit, thereby reducing its sampling probability. This process does not require any model modification or extra training; it simply alters the decoding distribution based on internal consistency signals.

## Key Results
- **TruthfulQA (Llama‑3‑8b)**: CoCoA‑SIG improves T×I by 12.39 pp vs. greedy decoding and 1.57 pp over DeCoRe.  
- **Natural Questions**: Exact match increases to 51.20% from a 48.30% baseline.  
- **SAMSum summarization**: Truthfulness rises to 74.30% vs. 72.97%.  
- **Additional gains**: MBPP +6.73%, GSM8K +1.21%; all with statistical significance (p ≪ 0.05) and ~1.3× latency overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The central idea is that factual generations are accompanied by stable hidden representations across the model's processing stages. If the middle layers disagree significantly on what token to produce, the model is in a state of uncertainty that often leads to hallucinations. CoCoA quantifies this instability via ConMLDS and fMLDS, which reflect the divergence of layer‑wise features. By penalizing candidates that exhibit high disagreement, the decoder nudges the output toward tokens that maintain internal consistency, thereby reducing the likelihood of hallucinated content. The self‑information gating in CoCoA‑SIG further refines this by increasing the penalty for tokens that are both surprising and unstable, leveraging the intuition that high‑surprise tokens are more prone to being erroneous when the model is also internally conflicted.

## Foundational Learning
- **Representational Stability**: Consistency of hidden states across different layers for a given token; hypothesized to correlate with factual accuracy.  
  *Why needed*: Provides an internal signal for detecting potential hallucinations without external knowledge.  
  *Quick check*: Compute variance or similarity of representations across middle layers; lower variance should correspond to more reliable generations.

- **Inter‑Layer Disagreement Metrics (ConMLDS, fMLDS)**: Quantitative measures of divergence among middle‑layer representations.  
  *Why needed*: Convert the abstract notion of stability into a usable penalty.  
  *Quick check*: Compare metric values on tokens known to cause hallucinations vs. truthful ones; high disagreement should be associated with hallucinations.

- **Decoding Penalty**: Adjustment applied to logits based on disagreement scores.  
  *Why needed*: Directly influences token selection to favor stable options.  
  *Quick check*: Without penalty, high‑disagreement tokens are sampled; with penalty, their probability drops.

- **Self‑Information Gating (SIG)**: Modulates the penalty using the negative log‑likelihood of the token.  
  *Why needed*: Prevents over‑penalizing low‑probability tokens that may be acceptable, and amplifies penalties for high‑surprise tokens.  
  *Quick check*: Examine correlation between self‑information and applied penalty; high‑surprise tokens should face stronger penalties.

- **Training‑Free Decoding**: The method modifies only the decoding step, without fine‑tuning.  
  *Why needed*: Enables plug‑and‑play application to existing LLMs with minimal overhead.  
  *Quick check*: Apply CoCoA to a pretrained model and verify immediate improvement in truthfulness.

- **Middle Layers Selection**: Choosing which transformer layers to use for computing disagreement (typically mid‑range layers).  
  *Why needed*: Different layers capture different abstraction levels; middle layers are hypothesized to be most informative for factual consistency.  
  *Quick check*: Ablate layer choices; optimal range should yield the largest truthfulness gain.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Token -> Embedding -> Transformer Layers -> Middle Layer Extractor -> Disagreement Computation (ConMLDS, fMLDS) -> Penalty Combiner (with SIG) -> Adjusted Logits -> Sampling -> Output Token

**Critical Path**:  
During generation, the model performs a forward pass. Hidden states from selected middle layers are collected for each candidate token (e.g., top‑k). For each candidate, ConMLDS and fMLDS are computed across these layers. The scores are combined (optionally gated by self‑information) to produce a penalty that modifies the candidate’s logit. The final token is sampled from the modified distribution. Overhead arises from extra metric computations and accessing multiple layer states.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- **Overhead vs. Quality**: Evaluating more candidates (k) improves quality control but increases latency.  
- **Penalty Strength**: Aggressive penalties reduce hallucinations but may harm fluency or diversity.  
- **Layer Selection**: More middle layers give a richer signal but add computation; too few layers may lose essential information.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- **Scalability Issues**: High memory usage when storing middle‑layer states for long contexts or large models.  
- **Fluency Degradation**: If penalty overwhelms the language model prior, outputs become conservative, repetitive, or incoherent.  
- **Incompatible Architectures**: Models where middle layers do not correlate with factual consistency will see little benefit.

**First Experiments**:  
1. **Ablation Study**: Remove ConMLDS and fMLDS separately to quantify each metric’s contribution to performance.  
2. **Layer Sensitivity**: Test different middle‑layer ranges (e.g., early vs. late) to identify the optimal configuration.  
3. **Transferability**: Apply CoCoA‑SIG to a different architecture (e.g., GPT‑2) and a novel domain (e.g., biomedical QA) to probe generalizability.

## Limitations
- The theoretical link between representational stability and factual output is not deeply elaborated, leaving the core hypothesis tentative.  
- Evaluation is restricted to three model families (Llama‑3, Mistral, Qwen) and four tasks, limiting claims about generalizability to other architectures or domains.  
- No reproducibility details (code, data, hyperparameters) are provided, hindering independent verification.

## Confidence
- Core effectiveness (CoCoA/CoCoA‑SIG reduces hallucinations): **High**  
- Theoretical justification (inter‑layer disagreement as stability proxy): **Medium**  
- Generalizability beyond tested settings: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. Broad architecture evaluation: Test on at least two additional model families (e.g., GPT‑4, Claude) and three new domains (e.g., biomedical QA, legal summarization).  
2. Ablation on penalty design: Isolate the impact of ConMLDS vs. fMLDS and the SIG gating by removing each component, then measure truthfulness/fluency trade‑offs on a held‑out TruthfulQA split.  
3. Efficiency profiling: Measure per‑token latency and memory overhead for context lengths ≥4K tokens, comparing against baseline decoders to quantify scalability for production deployment.
