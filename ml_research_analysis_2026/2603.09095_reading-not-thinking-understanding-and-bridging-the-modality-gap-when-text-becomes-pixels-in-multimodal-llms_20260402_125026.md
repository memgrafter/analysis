# Reading, Not Thinking: Understanding and Bridging the Modality Gap When Text Becomes Pixels in Multimodal LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09095  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09095  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Multimodal LLMs show a large performance gap when processing text as images versus text tokens; this gap can be nearly closed via self-distillation on text-mode chain-of-thought traces, raising image-mode accuracy on GSM8K from 30.71% to 92.72%.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle to solve textual tasks when the input is rendered as an image rather than provided as text tokens. Through systematic evaluation across seven models and benchmarks, the authors find the gap is highly dependent on the task and data distribution: synthetic renderings of math problems cause severe accuracy drops (>60 points), while natural document images often match or exceed text performance. A detailed error analysis of over 4,000 failures reveals that image input selectively amplifies perceptual errors in calculation and formatting, while knowledge and reasoning errors remain stable; additionally, some models exhibit a chain-of-thought reasoning collapse under visual input. To bridge the gap, the authors propose a self-distillation method where the model is trained on its own high-quality text-mode reasoning traces paired with image inputs. This approach nearly eliminates the modality gap on GSM8K, transfers to unseen benchmarks without catastrophic forgetting, and primarily improves the language model component rather than the vision encoder.

## Method Summary
The study combines three methodological components. First, a large-scale systematic evaluation measures zero-shot accuracy of seven MLLMs across seven benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, GPQA, GSM8K, HumanEval, QASPER, SQuAD v2) under five input modalities: pure text, pure image (synthetic/natural), instruction+image, OCR-1P (text extracted from image), and OCR-2P (text extracted then solved). Synthetic images are rendered with controlled parameters (font, resolution). Second, a grounded-theory error analysis involving human-in-the-loop coding of 4,000+ failure cases classifies errors into categories (e.g., calculation, formatting, knowledge, reasoning) to isolate the impact of visual input. Third, a self-distillation fine-tuning procedure collects chain-of-thought (CoT) traces from the model’s own text-mode inference on the GSM8K training set. These traces are paired with the corresponding synthetic image inputs and used to train the model via LoRA, with variants filtering for correct traces and different module adaptations (ViT-only, LM-only, both).

## Key Results
- The modality gap is task- and data-dependent: synthetic math images cause >60 point accuracy drops, while natural document images often match or exceed text performance.
- Image input selectively amplifies perceptual errors (calculation and formatting) but leaves knowledge and reasoning errors largely unchanged; a chain-of-thought reasoning collapse is observed in some models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B output shrinks 19× on GSM8K).
- Self-distillation on text-mode CoT traces raises image-mode accuracy on GSM8K from 30.71% to 92.72%, nearly closing the gap; the approach transfers to unseen benchmarks (ARC, MMLU) without catastrophic forgetting.
- Ablations show language model adaptation is the primary driver of gains, with LM-only fine-tuning reducing the gap from 62.85 to 1.37 points; vision encoder adaptation offers marginal additional benefit.
- The method fails on tasks where visual structural cues are essential (e.g., code indentation on HumanEval with OCR-2P yields near-zero scores for several models).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The primary cause of the modality gap is the model’s reduced ability to accurately read and extract text from pixels, particularly when visual rendering diverges from its pretraining distribution. When text is rendered as an image, the vision encoder must convert pixels into visual tokens representing characters. Unfamiliar fonts, low resolution, or atypical styles cause misrecognition, corrupting the input fed to the LLM. This leads to calculation and formatting errors, while knowledge retrieval and logical inference remain intact. The core assumption is that MLLMs’ vision encoders are optimized for natural images and lack robust character-level invariance; they struggle on out-of-distribution text renderings. Evidence includes the abstract’s finding that image mode selectively amplifies reading errors, Section 5.2/Figure 5a showing increased calculation and format errors under image input, and related work (e.g., VisTIRA) documenting drops in visual math reasoning. The gap shrinks when image distributions match pretraining data (e.g., natural PDFs, screenshots), as shown in Section 4.1; handwriting (out-of-distribution) causes the steepest declines.

### Mechanism 2
Under image input, some MLLMs experience a chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning collapse, drastically shortening or omitting intermediate steps crucial for complex tasks. The visual modality may alter generation dynamics, reducing the likelihood of producing step-by-step reasoning. Shorter outputs bypass intermediate calculations, causing arithmetic and logical errors to propagate unchecked. The core assumption is models are capable of CoT when prompted in text mode, but the image representation interferes with triggering that behavior. Evidence includes the abstract’s identification of a CoT collapse, Section 5.2 showing Qwen3-VL-8B’s output shrinks from 618 to 32 characters (19× reduction) on GSM8K image mode and Pixtral reduces 5×. Direct corpus evidence is sparse; the “Thinking with Comics” work studies CoT in multimodal settings but does not report collapse, confirming this is a novel observation. The collapse is not universal; InternVL3.5-8B retains CoT length and high accuracy, suggesting architecture choices (e.g., Visual Resolution Router) or training that encourages CoT under visual input may mitigate it.

### Mechanism 3
Self-distillation—training the model on its own text-mode CoT traces paired with image inputs—realigns the vision-LM representations and restores CoT generation, effectively bridging the gap. The model first solves the problem in pure text mode, generating a high-accuracy CoT trace. During fine-tuning, the same model receives the image and is trained to reproduce that exact CoT. This teaches the vision encoder to produce features that the LLM can reason over with multi-step logic, and simultaneously trains the LLM to produce CoT even when the input is visual. The core assumption is text-mode CoT traces are sufficiently correct and representative of desired reasoning; the model can learn from its own outputs without external teacher supervision. Ablation results (filtered vs. unfiltered) support this. Evidence includes the abstract’s reported GSM8K gain, Section 6.2 showing LM-only fine-training reduces the gap from 62.85 to 1.37 points, ViT-only helping less, and unfiltered traces performing nearly as well as filtered. Prior cross-modal distillation (e.g., Cross modal distillation) shows benefits of transferring representations across modalities; this work extends to self-distillation within a single model. The approach may fail on tasks where visual structural cues (e.g., code indentation, table alignment) are essential and destroyed by tokenization, as indicated by near-zero scores on OCR-2P HumanEval.

## Foundational Learning
**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**  
- *Why needed:* The solution depends on extracting high-quality CoT traces and training the model to reproduce them; understanding CoT explains why multi-step reasoning improves accuracy and how its collapse harms performance.  
- *Quick check:* “What is chain-of-thought reasoning, and why does it improve accuracy on tasks like GSM8K? Provide an example.”

**Self-Distillation / Knowledge Distillation**  
- *Why needed:* The core intervention is a form of self-distillation where the model teaches itself across modalities; this concept explains how improvement occurs without external teachers.  
- *Quick check:* “Explain how self-distillation differs from standard knowledge distillation. What are the risks of using a model’s own outputs as training targets?”

**Vision-Language Alignment in MLLMs**  
- *Why needed:* The gap originates from misalignment between the vision encoder and LLM; understanding the flow from image pixels to LLM tokens clarifies why certain images cause failures.  
- *Quick check:* “In a typical MLLM, describe the flow of information from an input image to the final text output. What role does the vision encoder play, and how might it fail on rendered text?”

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Vision Encoder -> Projection Layer -> LLM

**Critical path:** For pure image input: image -> vision encoder -> visual tokens -> projection -> LLM -> generation. For mixed (instruction+image), text tokens are inserted before visual tokens.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Resolution:** Higher resolution captures finer characters but increases visual token count and compute (see Table S3). Choose the lowest resolution that maintains accuracy; smart compression (e.g., 10pt font) can help.  
- **LoRA rank & frozen modules:** LM-only adaptation yields the biggest gains; ViT-only helps less but adds little when LM is tuned. Use LoRA rank 64 as a starting point; freeze vision encoder if compute limited.  
- **Dataset:** Use text-mode CoT traces; filtered or unfiltered traces both work, so full unfiltered data is acceptable.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Large accuracy drop on synthetic benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K image vs. text > 60 pp).  
- Shrinking output length and absence of CoT steps (measure token count).  
- High OCR Character Error Rate (CER) in OCR-2P settings, though correlation with accuracy is weak.  
- Sensitivity to rendering parameters (font, resolution) indicated by > 10 pp swings.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline gap measurement:** Run zero‑shot evaluation on a small synthetic benchmark (e.g., 100 GSM8K samples) in Pure Text and Pure Image modes to quantify the gap.  
2. **Diagnose reading vs. reasoning:** Execute OCR-2P (extract text then solve) and compare accuracy to Pure Image. A large gain suggests reading errors dominate; use CER/WER to confirm.  
3. **Self-distillation rollout:** Collect text-mode CoT traces on the GSM8K training set. Fine-tune the target MLLM with LoRA on image-input paired with these traces (LM-only first). Evaluate image-mode test accuracy; expect gap reduction near 90 pp if reading was the main issue.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How do specific rendering parameters (e.g., font, resolution) internally alter vision-language model representations to cause large accuracy variations?** Rendering choices swing accuracy by up to 47pp, and resolution sensitivity varies by architecture (Section 4.3). Unresolved because the paper documents these effects empirically but does not analyze how they propagate through the vision encoder or affect token alignment. Probing studies of vision encoder activations under controlled renderings, or feature attribution methods isolating font/resolution impacts, would resolve it.

2. **What underlying mechanisms cause the chain-of-thought reasoning collapse in some models when processing visual text, and why is it model-specific?** Error analysis reveals Qwen3-VL-8B and Pixtral produce drastically shorter outputs in image mode, bypassing step-by-step reasoning (Section 5.2). Unresolved because the collapse is identified but not causally explained—whether due to training data biases, vision encoder limitations, or decoding behavior remains unknown. Comparative architecture analyses (e.g., attention patterns), controlled training experiments varying visual-text alignment, or studies on decoding strategies would resolve it.

3. **Why does self-distillation primarily improve the language model component, and what are the boundaries of this approach for tasks requiring perceptual-spatial reasoning?** Ablations show LM-only training bridges the gap as effectively as full fine-tuning, with vision encoder adaptation offering marginal gains (Section 6.2). Unresolved because the paper demonstrates success on math/QA tasks but does not test on layout-heavy tasks (e.g., tables, diagrams) where visual features may be more critical, nor explain why LM adaptation dominates. Evaluation on benchmarks with rich spatial layouts (e.g., chart QA), analysis of LoRA weight distributions across components, or scaling vision encoder capacity during distillation would resolve it.

## Limitations
- Exact synthetic rendering pipeline specs (text layout engine, anti-aliasing, margins) are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- Self-distillation implementation details omitted: CoT prompt wording, final-answer extraction method, data mixture ratios, and whether training prompts match evaluation format.
- CoT collapse evidence relies on output length changes; the mechanistic cause (e.g., representation bias vs. decoding heuristics) remains unclear.
- Approach appears ineffective for tasks where visual structure is essential (e.g., indentation in code), as indicated by near-zero scores on OCR-2P HumanEval.

## Confidence
- **Modality gap exists and is data-dependent:** High (systematic eval across 7 models, 7 benchmarks).
- **Reading errors are primary cause:** Medium (error taxonomy shows selective amplification, but causality not proven via intervention).
- **CoT collapse in some models:** Medium (observed length reduction in 2 models; sample size small; not all models exhibit).
- **Self-distillation bridges gap:** High (massive GSM8K gain, transfer to new benchmarks, ablations confirm LM adaptation as driver).

## Next Checks
1. Re-implement the exact synthetic image generator (specify font, resolution, margin, anti-aliasing) and evaluate baseline modality gap on a fixed GSM8K subset; correlate OCR character error rate with accuracy drop.
2. Ablate the self-distillation pipeline: vary CoT prompt style, filtering (correct vs all traces), and proportion of original text data mixed; measure image-mode gains and text-mode forgetting.
3. Swap vision encoder features between text-mode and image-mode inputs (or freeze components) to isolate whether LM adaptation alone suffices; compare LM-only, ViT-only, and full fine-tuning.
