# Parallel Swin Transformer-Enhanced 3D MRI-to-CT Synthesis for MRI-Only Radiotherapy Planning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05387
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05387
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Achieved mean absolute error (MAE) of 72.0 HU for brain and 72.1 HU for pelvis, mean Dice scores of 0.90 for brain and 0.82 for pelvis, and a mean target dose error of 1.69%, outperforming baselines like MTT-Net, nnU-Net, and Med-DDPM.

## Executive Summary
MRI-only radiotherapy planning requires synthetic CT (sCT) images to provide electron density information absent in MRI. This paper introduces a 3D Parallel Swin Transformer-Enhanced Med2Transformer that fuses convolutional encoding with dual Swin Transformer branches to capture both fine-grained local anatomy and long-range contextual dependencies. The model is trained with an adversarial Wave3D Discriminator and a composite loss (L1, perceptual, GAN) to ensure realistic intensity and texture. Evaluated on public SynthRAD2023 and clinical NTUH datasets, the method achieves state-of-the-art performance: MAE of 72.0 HU (brain) and 72.1 HU (pelvis), Dice scores of 0.90 and 0.82, and clinically acceptable dose error of 1.69%, outperforming recent baselines like MTT-Net, nnU-Net, and Med-DDPM.

## Method Summary
The Med2Transformer processes 3D MRI patches (128×128×48) through an encoder that contains parallel branches: a convolutional stream (3×3×3 kernels, dilation rates {1,2}) and a Swin Transformer stream with multi-scale shifted window attention. Features from both streams are concatenated and projected via a 1×1×1 convolution, enabling multi-scale fusion. The bottleneck consists of two Swin Transformer blocks with different dilation settings to deepen context modeling. The decoder upsamples features trilinearly and applies 3×3×3 convolutions, with skip connections from the encoder to preserve detail. The final 1×1×1 convolution with tanh activation outputs HU-normalized sCT. Training uses an adversarial setup: a Wave3D Discriminator classifies real vs. synthetic CT patches, while the generator minimizes L_total = 1·L_GAN + 20·L_L1 + 1·L_perc. Optimization employs Adam (β₁=0.5, β₂=0.999) with a maximum learning rate of 2e-4 over 100 epochs. Full-volume inference uses a sliding window with overlap-aware averaging. Experiments on SynthRAD2023 (139 brain, 5 pelvis train; 36 test) and NTUH head-and-neck (1,439 cases) demonstrate the method’s efficacy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The parallel integration of convolutional and Swin Transformer branches in the encoder and bottleneck enables joint modeling of fine-grained local anatomy and long-range contextual dependencies, improving geometric and intensity fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** A convolutional stream (3×3×3 kernels) extracts high-resolution local features, while a parallel Swin Transformer branch applies shifted window self-attention to capture global relationships across the volume. Features from both streams are concatenated and residual-projected (`Conv1×1×1([fc, ft]) + fc`), fusing multi-scale information before progressing to deeper stages.
- **Core assumption:** Local texture (e.g., bone细节) and global anatomical coherence (e.g., organ symmetry) are complementary and must be modeled simultaneously for accurate HU mapping and structure preservation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "integrates convolutional encoding with dual Swin Transformer branches to model both local anatomical detail and long-range contextual dependencies."
  - [section 2.2] Describes the parallel fusion equation and dilation rates (`d ∈ {1,2}`) to diversify receptive fields within the transformer branch.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., MTT-Net [5]) also explore transformer-based MRI-to-CT synthesis, but this work’s specific parallel design with dilated convolutions is not directly cited—evidence for its novelty relative to corpus is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the parallel branches fail to fuse effectively (e.g., one stream dominates), the model may lose either local precision (blurred bone boundaries) or global consistency (anatomical misalignments), degrading Dice scores and MAE.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The adversarial framework with a Wave3D Discriminator and composite loss (L1 + perceptual + GAN) enforces realistic CT texture and structural fidelity beyond simple voxel matching.
- **Mechanism:** The generator minimizes a weighted sum: L1 loss for intensity accuracy, VGG-based perceptual loss for high-level structural similarity (comparing feature maps), and a GAN loss via Wave3D Discriminator that uses wavelet-based frequency decomposition to penalize unrealistic textures. This combination addresses the ill-posed nature of intensity mapping.
- **Core assumption:** Pixel-wise losses alone are insufficient for capturing complex tissue textures and stochastic variations in CT; adversarial and perceptual supervision is needed to match the "clinical plausibility" of real CT.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.4-2.5] Explicitly defines `Ltotal = λGAN LGAN + λL1 LL1 + λperc Lperc` with weights (λGAN=1, λL1=20, λperc=1) and describes Wave3D Discriminator’s role in emphasizing fine details.
  - [abstract] Mentions "spatial and perceptual losses preserve voxel-level and anatomical accuracy."
  - [corpus] Med-DDPM [7] (a baseline) uses diffusion, not adversarial training, indicating this paper’s reliance on GANs for efficiency; corpus does not provide direct evidence for Wave3D’s specific advantages.
- **Break condition:** If adversarial training collapses (e.g., discriminator saturates), generated sCT may exhibit mode collapse (repetitive patterns) or intensity drift, increasing MAE and failing dosimetric tests.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multi-scale shifted window attention within the transformer branches, combined with hierarchical feature aggregation, improves anatomical fidelity across varying tissue scales (e.g., small bone structures vs. large soft-tissue regions).
- **Mechanism:** Swin Transformer’s windowed attention reduces computation while shifted windows enable cross-window connections. Applied in parallel across encoder stages with different dilation rates, this creates a hierarchy of receptive fields—shallow stages capture fine details, deep stages integrate broader context. Feature aggregation via skip connections preserves multi-scale information in the decoder.
- **Core assumption:** Fixed receptive fields (standard CNNs) or non-hierarchical transformers fail to adapt to the multi-scale nature of human anatomy (e.g., cortical bone vs. brain ventricles); a hierarchical, shifted-window approach is necessary.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Multi-scale shifted window attention with hierarchical feature aggregation improves anatomical fidelity."
  - [section 2.2] Mentions "multi-scale shifted-window self-attention" and dilation rate variation (`d ∈ {1,2}`) within transformer blocks.
  - [section 3] Qualitative results (Fig. 2) show sharper osseous boundaries, indirectly supporting multi-scale modeling.
  - [corpus] MTT-Net [5] (a baseline) also uses a "tokens-aware transformer," but the paper’s parallel/dilated design is distinct; corpus lacks direct comparison evidence.
- **Break condition:** Inadequate window shifting or poor scale alignment could cause "gridding artifacts" or loss of fine structures, reducing Dice scores for small objects (e.g., bone spicules in sinus).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Input MRI patch -> Parallel Encoder (CNN + Swin Transformer branches) -> Bottleneck (Swin Transformer blocks) -> Decoder (with skip connections) -> Output sCT patch

Critical path: MRI patch → encoder multi-branch feature extraction → bottleneck deep context → decoder multi-scale upsampling with skip connections → sCT patch. Adversarial loop: generator output → discriminator → GAN loss backprop.

Design tradeoffs:
- Parameter efficiency vs. computation: Parallel branches increase parameters modestly but improve accuracy; however, 3D Swin attention is memory-intensive.
- Global vs. local balance: Heavy reliance on transformer may lose fine details; CNN-only may miss large anatomical context. This design attempts to balance both.
- Stability: Composite loss weights (λL1=20, λGAN=1, λperc=1) require tuning; too high adversarial weight can cause training divergence.

Failure signatures:
- Training instability: Discriminator loss oscillates or collapses → generator fails to learn realistic textures.
- Anatomical inconsistencies: Low Dice scores in small structures → transformer hierarchy insufficient or skip connections weak.
- Intensity drift: MAE > 100 HU → L1 weight too low or normalization mismatch.
- Memory overflow during 3D training → reduce patch size or batch size; enable mixed precision.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline replication: Train MTT-Net and nnU-Net on SynthRAD2023 with identical preprocessing to verify reported performance gaps.
2. Ablation study: Remove parallel branches (CNN-only or Transformer-only) to quantify contribution of each stream to MAE/Dice.
3. Loss weight sweep: Vary λ_perc and λ_GAN to assess impact on perceptual quality (SSIM) and training stability; identify optimal ratio for clinical dose compatibility.
