# From Prompting to Preference Optimization: A Comparative Study of LLM-based Automated Essay Scoring

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.06424
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06424
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: k-instruction tuning with RAG achieved an F1-score of 93% on IELTS Writing Task 2.

## Executive Summary
This paper compares four LLM-based automated essay scoring (AES) paradigms on a unified IELTS Writing Task 2 benchmark. The evaluated approaches include discriminative fine-tuning, zero/few-shot prompting, instruction tuning with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with direct preference optimization (DPO) and RAG. The best system combines criterion-specific parameter-efficient fine-tuning (four LoRA adapters) with RAG and attains an F1-score of 93%. Trade-offs between accuracy, computational cost, and robustness are identified: k-SFT+RAG offers the most favorable efficiency, while SFT+DPO+RAG yields higher-quality feedback at greater expense.

## Method Summary
The experiments use a dataset of 10,328 IELTS essays (9,833 train, 495 test) with analytic scores originally generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro. Four AES paradigms are evaluated: (i) discriminative fine-tuning of encoders (e.g., RoBERTa) as classifiers; (ii) zero/few-shot prompting of large LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Llama-3-70B); (iii) k-instruction tuning, where four separate LoRA adapters are trained on criterion-specific tasks and predictions are aggregated; (iv) SFT followed by DPO for feedback quality, both combined with RAG. The RAG component retrieves two exemplar essays at inference time. Performance is measured using multi-class F1-score across 19 band levels (0–9 in 0.5 increments).

## Key Results
- The best configuration: k-instruction tuning (four criterion-specific LoRA adapters) with RAG, achieving an F1-score of 93%.
- RAG provides a large accuracy boost: adding RAG to k-instruction tuning increased F1 from ~0.80 to ~0.935.
- SFT+DPO+RAG improves feedback quality but at higher computational cost; k-SFT+RAG offers the best accuracy-cost trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Mechanism 1: Criterion-specific Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (k-SFT)
k-SFT decomposes holistic scoring into four independent criterion-specific tasks (Task Response, Coherence/Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range/Accuracy) by training separate LoRA adapters. This modular supervision forces the model to internalize rubric nuances for each dimension, and the final band score is the arithmetic mean of the four predicted scores. The approach assumes the rubric dimensions are relatively independent and that mean aggregation approximates holistic grading. Evidence shows k-SFT with RAG achieves 93% F1, significantly outperforming a single-criterion SFT model (~80% F1). A break condition: if the criteria are highly interdependent in the data, independent scoring could yield incoherent aggregates (e.g., a high score in one criterion paired with an unrealistically low score in another), harming overall accuracy.

#### Mechanism 2: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
RAG retrieves two exemplar essays from the training set at inference and appends them to the prompt, grounding the model's judgment in concrete, rubric-aligned examples. This mitigates hallucination, improves rubric adherence, and stabilizes score calibration, particularly for borderline proficiency levels. The core assumption is that in-context examples of essays at various band levels with examiner comments enhance discriminative ability more than instructions alone. Empirical evidence: adding RAG to k-SFT raised F1 from 0.800 to 0.935, a dramatic gain. Break condition: if the retrieval corpus is small, unrepresentative, or misaligned with the target distribution, it may provide misleading exemplars and degrade performance.

#### Mechanism 3: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for Feedback Quality
DPO is applied after SFT to improve the quality and naturalness of feedback text. Human-ranked preference pairs over feedback outputs are used to fine-tune the model via the DPO loss, steering outputs toward more pedagogical, coherent, and examiner-like phrasing without necessarily changing the predicted band score. The underlying assumption is that human preferences for feedback style can be captured through pairwise comparisons and optimize the output distribution. Evidence is primarily qualitative: case studies indicate SFT+DPO+RAG produces more globally coherent and examiner-like feedback compared to SFT+RAG, albeit with occasional slight score mismatch. Break condition: if the preference data is noisy, culturally biased, or misaligned with rubric priorities, DPO may optimize stylistic flair over scoring accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
**Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**  
*Why needed*: The study trains multiple LoRA adapters (four criteria and DPO) on a single GPU; LoRA’s low-rank updates make multi-stage training feasible without full model fine-tuning.  
*Quick check*: For a 7B parameter model using LoRA with rank `r=16` targeting Q/K/V/O and FFN layers, approximately what percentage of parameters are trainable? How does this affect memory usage versus full fine-tuning?

**Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
*Why needed*: RAG is the critical component that lifts performance from ~0.80 to ~0.93 F1 in the best model. Understanding retrieval corpus composition, shot count, and context injection is key to replication.  
*Quick check*: In this paper’s RAG setup, what is retrieved (essays, rubrics?), how many shots are used (`k=?`), and where is retrieved text placed in the final prompt? What is a risk of poor retrieval quality?

**Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
*Why needed*: DPO is the mechanism for enhancing feedback quality. It avoids training a separate reward model and requires constructing preference pairs (`f+`, `f-`) plus a β hyperparameter.  
*Quick check*: How does the DPO loss differ from standard SFT (sequence likelihood) loss? What does the β parameter control, and what might happen if it is set too high?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (data flow)**:
Input Processor → Retriever → Prompt Formatter → LLM Backbone (with active LoRA adapter) → Output Parser → (Aggregator if k-SFT)

**Critical path**:  
Essay + original prompt → [if RAG: retrieve top-2 exemplars] → construct instruction prompt → LLM + active LoRA generates JSON → parser extracts scores/feedback → [if k-SFT: average four criterion scores] → final band & feedback.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Accuracy vs. cost: Discriminative fine-tuning and zero-shot are cheap but inaccurate; k-SFT+RAG is expensive but accurate (F1 0.935); SFT+DPO+RAG is most expensive with marginal accuracy gain but better feedback.  
- Granularity vs. coherence: k-SFT provides precise, criterion-level feedback but can be fragmented; SFT+DPO produces holistic, examiner-style narratives with slightly less score precision.  
- RAG dependency: Performance collapses without retrieval; system critically depends on quality and representativeness of retrieval corpus.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Output parsing failures (JSON non-compliance).  
- Score inconsistency in k-SFT (four criterion scores diverge wildly for a single essay).  
- Feedback degradation without DPO (repetitive, generic comments).  
- Calibration drift (predicted scores cluster around training distribution mean and fail to distinguish extremes).

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Baseline replication**: Train a single-criterion SFT model (no RAG, no DPO) on Llama-3.1-8B; expect F1 ~0.75–0.80.  
2. **RAG impact test**: Enable 2-shot RAG at inference for the baseline model; expect F1 increase of ~0.10–0.15.  
3. **k-SFT verification**: Implement 4-LoRA (k-instruction tuning) with RAG; train each adapter on its specific criterion; ensure aggregated score meets or exceeds single-criterion performance (target F1 >0.90).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization beyond IELTS**: The study evaluates only on a single IELTS Writing Task 2 benchmark; it is unclear if the observed accuracy-cost-robustness trade-offs hold for other L2 writing assessments (e.g., TOEFL) or languages.
- **Feedback quality dimensions enhanced by DPO**: The paper claims DPO improves feedback quality but does not decompose which specific aspects (e.g., coherence, specificity, alignment) are improved or how they map to human examiner preferences.
- **Impact of retrieval corpus composition**: While RAG boosts performance, the paper does not investigate whether gains stem from high-scoring samples, error-corrected examples, or rubric definitions; the optimal corpus content remains unknown.
- **Bias from LLM-generated analytic labels**: Analytic scores used for training/evaluation are produced by Gemini-2.5-Pro rather than humans; this weak supervision may introduce systematic noise that differentially affects the compared paradigms.

## Limitations
- Unspecified RAG implementation details (retrieval corpus, embedding model, shot selection) hinder faithful replication of the 93% F1 result.
- The SFT prompt used for the DPO pathway (Approach 4) is not provided, blocking exact reproduction of that configuration.
- The claim that DPO enhances feedback quality relies on qualitative case studies; no controlled ablation quantitatively isolates DPO’s marginal contribution.
- Use of LLM-generated analytic scores may bias comparative results, especially for criterion-specific methods like k-instruction tuning.

## Confidence
- **High**: k-instruction tuning + RAG yields top accuracy (F1 93%). Supported by direct experimental comparison (Table 6) and large RAG effect.
- **Medium**: Decomposing scoring into criterion-specific adapters reduces task interference and improves accuracy. Inferred from performance gains vs. single-criterion SFT, but interdependence not explicitly tested.
- **Low**: DPO primarily enhances feedback quality rather than raw score accuracy. Evidence is qualitative (Section 4.5 case study); no quantitative ablation separating DPO effects from SFT+RAG.
