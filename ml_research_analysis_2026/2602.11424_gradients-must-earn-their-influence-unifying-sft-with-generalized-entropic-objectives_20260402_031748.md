# Gradients Must Earn Their Influence: Unifying SFT with Generalized Entropic Objectives

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11424
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11424
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DEFT, a parameter-free SFT objective using Rényi-2 entropy to modulate token-level gradients, consistently outperforms NLL and other baselines across model capability regimes (e.g., +1.46% accuracy on math for LLaMA-3.1-8B).

## Executive Summary
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with negative log-likelihood (NLL) treats all token errors uniformly, leading to a plasticity-stability dilemma: over-emphasis on low-probability targets amplifies noise and disrupts learned priors, while the signal for confident predictions is weak. This paper unifies token-level SFT objectives within a deformed-log family, exposing a universal gradient structure of the form gate × error, where the gate depends on the model’s confidence and controls learning signal allocation. By deriving a state-dependent focus trajectory via the Cayley transform, the authors obtain a smooth interpolation from coverage-oriented (NLL-like) behavior at low confidence to sharpening (probability-loss-like) behavior at high confidence. This trajectory is approximated by Dynamic Entropy Fine-Tuning (DEFT), a practical parameter-free objective that uses the Rényi-2 entropy (distribution concentration) as a proxy for uncertainty.

Across extensive experiments covering Model-Strong (math), Model-Intermediate, and Model-Weak regimes with seven model backbones, DEFT consistently achieves higher accuracy than NLL and strong baselines, demonstrating improved knowledge acquisition and better cross-domain generalization. The analysis confirms DEFT’s dynamic gate adapts to the model’s predictive state, balancing exploration and exploitation without additional hyperparameters.

## Method Summary
DEFT modifies standard SFT by replacing the uniform token weighting of NLL with an uncertainty-aware gate. For each training token, given the model’s predictive distribution P(·|c) over the vocabulary, DEFT computes α_DEFT(c) = Σ_v P(v|c)² (Rényi-2 concentration). This scalar, treated as a stop-gradient, determines the gate p^{α_DEFT(c)} where p = P(target|c). The per-token loss is L(p,α) = (1 - p^α)/α, which converges to −log p as α→0 and to 1−p as α→1. Thus, when α is small (high uncertainty, diffuse distribution), the loss resembles NLL, encouraging broad coverage; when α is large (low uncertainty, concentrated distribution), the loss becomes sharpening, reinforcing confident correct predictions. The training loop is otherwise identical to standard SFT: forward passes compute logits and softmax, α_DEFT is computed per context, the DEFT loss is aggregated over tokens, and backpropagation updates the model. No additional hyperparameters are introduced beyond the choice of optimizer (AdamW) and learning rate, making DEFT a drop-in replacement for NLL in SFT pipelines.

## Key Results
- DEFT yields consistent improvements over NLL and the probability loss (−p) across all model capability regimes: in the Model-Strong setting (NuminaMath), DEFT improves average accuracy by ~1.46% for LLaMA-3.1-8B; in the Model-Weak regime (FigFont), DEFT significantly exceeds both −p and NLL, demonstrating superior knowledge acquisition.
- In mixed-dataset fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-1.5B, DEFT achieves the highest overall average (24.04% vs. 22.26% for NLL and 23.23% for −p).
- Analyses show DEFT’s dynamic gate adapts to the model’s state: in strong-prior regimes α starts high (~0.65) and shifts toward sharpening; in weak-prior regimes α starts low (~0.04) favoring coverage. DEFT also preserves pre-trained competencies better, with superior cross-domain generalization (e.g., retaining medical knowledge after math fine-tuning).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: Token-level SFT objectives can be unified under a deformed-log family, revealing a universal gradient structure of the form gate × error, where the gate depends on model confidence and controls learning signal allocation.  
**Mechanism**: For any differentiable, nonincreasing loss function f(p) with p the target probability, the gradient on the target logit is ∂L/∂z_ŷ = −s_f(p)(1−p), where s_f(p) = −f'(p)·p ≥ 0 serves as the confidence-dependent gate. The error term is (1−p). This factorization makes explicit how different f weight gradients across confidence levels.  
**Core assumption**: The loss decomposes token-wise and depends only on the scalar p = P_θ(ŷ|c).  
**Evidence anchors**: The paper’s Lemma 2 derives the factorization; the abstract mentions the gate × error structure. Neighboring work (EAFT) incorporates entropy but does not expose this factorization, highlighting the theoretical novelty.  
**Break condition**: The factorization fails if the loss couples tokens (e.g., sequence-level rewards) or if f is not nonincreasing (s_f may become negative).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: A parameter-free focus trajectory α*(p) derived via the Cayley transform smoothly transitions between coverage (NLL-like) at low confidence and sharpening (linear-probability-like) at high confidence, addressing the exploration–exploitation tension.  
**Mechanism**: Using the Fisher-Rao radius z = √(1−p) as an intrinsic uncertainty coordinate, boundary conditions α(0)=0 (coverage) and α(1)=1 (sharpening) are imposed. The Möbius map with surprisal linearization uniquely selects the Cayley transform α*(p) = (1−√(1−p))/(1+√(1−p)). The gate then becomes p^{α*(p)}.  
**Core assumption**: The Fisher-Rao radius accurately reflects the model’s distance to certainty; the endpoints are the only necessary constraints.  
**Evidence anchors**: Section 5.1 provides the full geometric derivation and Theorem 3; the abstract states the smooth transition property. The Cayley transform is not used in neighboring papers, suggesting novelty.  
**Break condition**: If the relationship between p and true uncertainty is non-monotonic (e.g., overconfidence in low-probability tokens), the trajectory may misallocate gradients. Also, pointwise p dependency may not capture distributional ambiguity.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Dynamic Entropy Fine-Tuning (DEFT) approximates the ideal state-dependent trajectory by using the Rényi-2 entropy as a distribution-level proxy for uncertainty, modulating the gate without hyperparameters.  
**Mechanism**: For each context c, compute α_DEFT(c) = Σ_v P_θ(v|c)². This quantity is low when the distribution is diffuse (high uncertainty) and high when concentrated (low uncertainty). The gate becomes G(p,c) = p^{α_DEFT(c)}. α_DEFT(c) is treated as a stop-gradient to avoid curvature issues.  
**Core assumption**: The concentration of the full predictive distribution better distinguishes between learnable gaps and confident conflicts than the target token’s probability alone.  
**Evidence anchors**: The abstract introduces DEFT; section 5.2 defines α_DEFT and the stop-gradient; section 6.3 empirically shows α_DEFT correlates with expected regime (high initial α in Model-Strong, low in Model-Weak, and increases with training). DEFT outperforms EAFT in experiments (Tables 2–3), demonstrating the advantage of distribution-level gating.  
**Break condition**: If the model becomes confidently misaligned (peak probability on a wrong token), α_DEFT(c) becomes high, which may still allow harmful updates on target tokens if p is not too low; extreme miscalibration could break conflict suppression.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with NLL**: Standard post-training method minimizing token-level negative log-likelihood on demonstrations. *Why needed*: The paper’s critique starts from NLL’s uniform weighting; understanding its gradient (∂L/∂z_ŷ = −(1−p)) is essential. *Quick check*: For p=0.9, NLL gradient magnitude = 0.1.
- **Information-Theoretic Entropies**: Shannon entropy H = −∑ p log p, Rényi entropy H_α = (1/(1−α)) log ∑ p^α, Tsallis entropy S_q = (1−∑ p^q)/(q−1). *Why needed*: DEFT uses Rényi-2 (α=2) as a concentration measure; the loss index α relates to Tsallis entropy order (1+α) via optimization–entropy duality. *Quick check*: For uniform distribution over |V| tokens, ∑ p² = 1/|V|, so H_2 = log|V|.
- **Fisher-Rao Metric**: The natural Riemannian metric on probability distributions; for Bernoulli, ds² = dp²/(p(1−p)). *Why needed*: The radius z = √(1−p) equals sin(½ d_FR(p,1)), providing an intrinsic uncertainty coordinate for deriving the Cayley trajectory. *Quick check*: Fisher-Rao distance from Bern(p) to Bern(1) is 2 arccos(√p).
- **Convexity and Gradient Allocation**: For a loss f(p), the gradient magnitude W_f(p) = −f'(p)·p·(1−p). If f is concave (NLL’s f(p)=−log p is convex? Actually −log p has f''>0 so convex), then any maximizer of W_f lies in [0,1/2] (Proposition 11). *Why needed*: Explains why NLL overemphasizes low-probability tokens and −p emphasizes high-probability ones. *Quick check*: For f(p)=−log p (convex), any maximizer of W_f lies in [0,1/2].

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: input context c -> model logits -> softmax -> α_DEFT (stop-gradient) -> compute p (target probability) and gate = p^{α_DEFT} -> DEFT loss ( (1 - p^α)/α ) -> backpropagation.

**Critical path**: Input context c is passed through the model to obtain logits; a softmax yields the full predictive distribution P(·|c). From this distribution, α_DEFT(c) = Σ_v P(v|c)² is computed and detached from the graph. For each token in the sequence, the target token’s probability p is extracted, the gate p^{α_DEFT(c)} is applied, and the loss term (1 - p^α)/α is accumulated. The total loss is then backpropagated to update model parameters. α_DEFT must be recomputed each forward pass from the current distribution.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Rényi-2*: Computationally cheap (L2 norm) and bounded, but may be less sensitive to tail mass than higher-order Rényi entropies.
- *Stop-gradient*: Avoids second-order effects on α, but means the gate does not directly push α toward values that would reduce loss; a moving average could refine this.
- *Distribution-level vs token-level*: Using the full distribution captures ambiguity better than just p, with negligible extra compute beyond softmax.
- *Parameter-free*: No α to tune, but may lack explicit control over the coverage–sharpening balance; could be combined with a temperature if needed.

**Failure signatures**:
1. *α_DEFT saturation*: If α_DEFT(c) is constantly near 1 (or the minimum 1/|V|), the gate degenerates to −p (or NLL). Monitor batch statistics of α_DEFT.
2. *Gradient histogram shifts*: Compare per-token gradient magnitudes to expected profiles (Figure 2). If low-p, low-entropy tokens still dominate, the gating is ineffective.
3. *Probability collapse*: After training, inspect distribution of p values; excessive mass at p≈1 without intermediate values indicates over-sharpening.
4. *Cross-domain performance drop*: Evaluate on a held-out domain (e.g., medical after math fine-tuning). Significant forgetting suggests the gate is not preserving prior knowledge.
5. *Training instability*: Sudden loss spikes may indicate α changing too rapidly; consider gradient clipping or learning rate warm-up.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. *Gradient landscape validation*: For a fixed validation batch, compute and plot token-level gradient magnitudes (normalized) against p and entropy (as in Figure 2). Compare DEFT against NLL and −p; expect DEFT to suppress gradients for low-p, low-entropy tokens while maintaining signal for high-p tokens.
2. *α_DEFT trajectory tracking*: Run a short training (e.g., 10 steps) on a Model-Strong (NuminaMath) and Model-Weak (FigFont) dataset. Log α_DEFT(c) per batch and the mean target p. Verify α_DEFT starts high in Model-Strong (~0.6) and low in Model-Weak (~0.04), and increases as training progresses.
3. *Cross-domain generalization test*: Fine-tune LLaMA-3.1-8B on NuminaMath (Model-Strong) and evaluate on medical benchmarks (MedMc, MedQA, etc.) as in Table 5. Confirm DEFT outperforms NLL and −p in preserving pre-trained medical knowledge.

## Limitations
- Missing dataset preprocessing and training hyperparameter details impede exact replication.
- Lack of statistical evaluation (multiple seeds) limits robustness of reported gains.
- The Rényi-2 entropy proxy may be insufficient under confident miscalibration.

## Confidence

- **Cayley focus trajectory**: Medium
