# PurSAMERE: Reliable Adversarial Purification via Sharpness-Aware Minimization of Expected Reconstruction Error

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06269
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06269
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves 69.08% adversarial accuracy on CIFAR-10 under BPDA20-det (ℓ∞ 8/255) with clean accuracy up to 91.18%.

## Executive Summary
PurSAMERE introduces a deterministic adversarial purification framework that leverages score-based generative models and sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) to defend neural networks against strong white-box attacks. The method purifies adversarial inputs by minimizing the expected reconstruction error of noise-corrupted data, using SAM to guide purified samples toward flat regions of the error landscape, which correlates with local maxima of the data density where classifiers are more reliable. On CIFAR-10, PurSAMERE dramatically outperforms prior art, achieving 69.08% adversarial accuracy under BPDA20-det (ℓ∞=8/255) versus the previous best of 39.16%, and an average of 66.23% across multiple norms under the more demanding BPDA200+EoT20 attack—all while preserving high clean accuracy (85.68%–91.18%). Its deterministic design avoids gradient obfuscation and eliminates the need for expectation over transformations during evaluation.

## Method Summary
The approach comprises three stages: (1) a score-based generative model (U-Net) is pre-trained on CIFAR-10 using denoising score matching across 232 geometrically spaced noise levels (σ from 50 to 0.01); (2) for a given adversarial input, purification iteratively minimizes the expected reconstruction error R(x;σ) over decreasing σ. At each noise level, a Monte Carlo estimate of ∇R is computed using fixed seeds, SAM is applied to take a worst-case step within a small ℓ₂ ball (ϱ_sam), an Adam update follows, and the result is projected onto the ℓ₂ ball around the original adversarial example (radius ϱ_pur) and the pixel range [0,1]^d. The Adam state and learning rate are annealed across all levels; and (3) a WideResNet-28-10 classifier is trained on a mixture of original CIFAR-10 training images and purified versions of those images (generated with SAM disabled). The entire purification pipeline is deterministic and does not rely on expectation over transformations at test time.

## Key Results
- Under BPDA20-det (ℓ∞=8/255), PurSAMERE achieves 69.08% adversarial accuracy, a 30% absolute improvement over the prior best (39.16%).
- Under BPDA200+EoT20 across ℓ∞, ℓ₁, ℓ₂ norms, average adversarial accuracy is 66.23%.
- Clean accuracy remains high, ranging from 85.68% to 91.18% depending on the purification radius ϱ_pur.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Purifying by minimizing the expected reconstruction error **R(x;σ)** drives samples toward local maxima of the smoothed data density **p<sub>Yσ</sub>**, where classifiers are more reliable.
- **Mechanism:** **R(x;σ)** measures the expected squared distance between a candidate **x** and its denoised reconstruction **E[X|Y<sub>σ</sub>=x+σΞ]**. Minimizing this objective biases **x** toward regions where the score **s(x;0)=∇log p<sub>X</sub>(x)** vanishes (stationary points) and the Hessian trace **tr(∇²log p<sub>X</sub>(x))** is negative (local concavity), as shown by the Taylor expansion in Proposition 1.
- **Core assumption:** The learned score function **s<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** accurately approximates the true score of the data distribution within the purification search neighborhood.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...minimizing the expected reconstruction error biases the purified sample toward local maximizers of the Gaussian-smoothed density"
  - [section 3.3] Proposition 1 formally derives that **R(x;σ)** expansion includes terms penalizing large score magnitude and Hessian trace, promoting density peaks.
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus evidence; neighbors discuss SAM for robustness but not this specific reconstruction error formulation.
- **Break condition:** If the score model **s<sub>θ</sub>** is highly inaccurate in the local neighborhood (e.g., due to distribution shift or poor training), minimizing **R<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** may converge to spurious minima unrelated to true density maxima.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Applying Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) **during purification** on the **R<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** landscape enhances robustness by steering purified samples toward flat regions, mitigating the impact of score approximation errors.
- **Mechanism:** SAM solves a min-max problem: find **x** that minimizes the *worst-case* **R<sub>θ</sub>** within a small ℓ<sub>2</sub>-ball around **x**. This discourages selection of sharp, narrow minima of **R<sub>θ</sub>** that could be artifacts of the learned score model and are sensitive to tiny perturbations.
- **Core assumption:** Flat minima of the expected reconstruction error correlate with purified samples that are more robust to subsequent adversarial perturbations on the classifier.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...sharpness-aware minimization is used to guide the purified samples toward flat regions of the expected reconstruction error landscape, thereby enhancing robustness."
  - [section 3.2] Equation (12) defines the purification objective with SAM as `max_{∥ε∥≤ϱ_sam} R(x+ε;σ)`.
  - [corpus] Neighbors show SAM improves generalization/robustness in *training* (e.g., "Sharpness-Aware Minimization Alone can Improve Adversarial Robustness"), but PurSAMERE's *purification-time* SAM is a novel application; corpus provides contextual support but not direct evidence for this specific use.
- **Break condition:** If the SAM radius **ϱ<sub>sam</sub>** is poorly chosen (too large, causing optimization instability; too small, ineffective), or if the landscape **R<sub>θ</sub>** is inherently flat everywhere, the SAM step provides no benefit or degrades performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Under local structural assumptions (piecewise affine score model, strong concavity of log-density), the minimizer **x′<sub>σ</sub>** of **R<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** converges exponentially to the local density maximizer **x⋆<sub>σ</sub>** as noise level **σ → 0**.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 2 formalizes that when **f<sub>θ</sub>** (with **∇f<sub>θ</sub>=s<sub>θ</sub>**) is µ-strongly concave in a region **D**, **R<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** approximates a quadratic with minimum near **x⋆<sub>σ</sub>**. The proof bounds the error term from the score model's domain boundaries and approximation, showing convergence.
- **Core assumption:** The score model's induced potential **f<sub>θ</sub>(·,σ)** is locally strongly concave, and the maximizer lies strictly inside the concave region, away from the partition boundaries (Assumption 3).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Theorem 1 (informal) and Theorem 2 (formal) state the exponential convergence result.
  - [appendix A.3] Provides full proof relying on Lipschitz continuity, piecewise linearity, and strong concavity.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; this is a novel theoretical contribution of the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the true data density has no local maximum in the purification neighborhood, or if **f<sub>θ</sub>** is not strongly concave (e.g., flat or convex regions), the theorem's conditions fail, and no convergence guarantee exists.

## Foundational Learning

**Score-Based Generative Models & Denoising Score Matching**  
*Why needed:* The entire purification method relies on a pre-trained score model **s<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** that approximates ∇log p<sub>Yσ</sub>(x). This model is trained via denoising score matching (Equation 6), which connects conditional expectation (reconstruction) to the score function.  
*Quick check:* Given the denoising objective `||Ξ + σ sθ(X+σΞ;σ)||²`, why is minimizing its expectation equivalent to minimizing the L2 distance between **s<sub>θ</sub>** and the true score (see Lemma 1)?

**Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM)**  
*Why needed:* SAM is repurposed from model-weight regularization to an *input-space* worst-case optimization during purification (Equation 12). This is crucial to avoid sharp minima of **R<sub>θ</sub>** caused by score-model approximation errors.  
*Quick check:* How does the SAM perturbation `ε = ϱ_sam * ∇R/∥∇R∥` in the purification step (Equation 15, lines 6-7) change the effective objective compared to standard gradient descent on **R<sub>θ</sub>**?

**Expected Reconstruction Error Formulation**  
*Why needed:* **R(x;σ)** (Equation 8) is the core purification objective. Its normalization by σ² and connection to MMSE (Equation 9) make it stable across multiple noise scales and theoretically link minimization to density maximization.  
*Quick check:* Why does the term `2σ² tr(∇xs(x,0))` in the expansion (Equation 14) encourage movement toward regions where **log p<sub>X</sub>** is locally concave?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Input: Adversarial image **x<sub>adv</sub>** (ℓ<sub>p</sub>-bounded perturbation) -> Score Model (pre-trained **s<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ)** for multiple noise levels) -> Purification Engine (sequential minimizer of R(x;σ) with SAM) -> Output: Purified image **x<sub>L</sub>** -> Classifier (WideResNet trained on original + purified samples)

### Critical path
1. **Initialization:** `x⁰ = x_adv`.
2. **Sequential refinement over L noise levels (σ decreasing):**
   - For **k=1 to L**:
     - Generate fixed MC noise **{ξ<sub>ki</sub>}**.
     - Compute `∇bR<sub>θ</sub>(x<sub>k-1</sub>; σ<sub>k</sub>)`.
     - SAM perturbation: `x⁺ = x<sub>k-1</sub> + ϱ_sam * (∇R/∥∇R∥)` → project to `[0,1]ᵈ` → obtain `x<sub>max</sub><sup>k</sup>`.
     - Compute `∇bR<sub>θ</sub>(x<sub>max</sub><sup>k</sup>; σ<sub>k</sub>)`.
     - Adam update → `x′<sub>k</sub>`.
     - Project onto `[0,1]ᵈ ∩ B₂(x_adv, ϱ_pur)` → `x<sub>k</sub>`.
     - Anneal Adam learning rate linearly from η<sub>max</sub> to η<sub>min</sub>.
3. Return **x<sub>L</sub>**.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Determinism vs. Approximation:** Fixed MC seeds ensure deterministic purification but may introduce bias with small *m* (default m=4). More samples reduce variance at higher computational cost.
- **Purification radius ϱ<sub>pur</sub>:** Larger radius allows correction of larger adversarial perturbations but risks moving out-of-distribution; smaller radius may be insufficient. Tuned to balance clean vs. robust accuracy.
- **SAM radius ϱ<sub>sam</sub>:** Should be smaller than ϱ<sub>pur</sub> but of same order. Too large causes instability; too small nullifies SAM effect. Set to 1.5 in experiments.
- **Noise level schedule:** Geometric decay from σ₁=50 to σ<sub>L</sub>=0.01 (L=232) balances coarse structure and fine detail. Adjusting L impacts runtime and convergence.

### Failure signatures
1. **Poor score model:** `s<sub>θ</sub>` inaccuracies cause spurious minima in R<sub>θ</sub>. Symptom: purified outputs unrealistic or classifier accuracy on purified clean images drops.
2. **SAM misconfiguration:** ϱ<sub>sam</sub> too large → purification diverges; too small → purified samples in sharp minima, robustness degrades.
3. **Projection violations:** If `B₂(x_adv, ϱ_pur)` is too restrictive, minimization may fail to correct large perturbations; final x often on ball boundary.
4. **Classifier mismatch:** If classifier is not trained on purified data (or purified with SAM), purified inputs yield low accuracy, negating defense.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Score model validation:** On clean validation set, compute `||Ξ + σ sθ(x+σΞ;σ)||²` across σ; should be low. Visualize reconstructions `x̂ = x + σΞ + σ² sθ(x+σΞ;σ)` for small σ; must match clean x.
2. **Single-noise-level purification:** Fix σ<sub>k</sub>, run Algorithm 1 without SAM (ϱ<sub>sam</sub>=0). Check: R<sub>θ</sub>(x;σ) decreases; purified x becomes more plausible; classifier accuracy on purified clean images remains high (>90% on CIFAR-10).
3. **Full pipeline with SAM toggle:** Compare adversarial accuracy under BPDA20-det with SAM on vs. off. Expect significant drop without SAM (from 69.08% lower). If no drop, SAM radius may be ineffective or score model already flat.

## Limitations
- Exact score model architecture and classifier training hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, augmentations) are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strong concavity and local density maxima assumptions that may not hold universally across real datasets.
- Determinism depends on fixed Monte Carlo seeds with only *m*=4 samples, introducing potential small-sample bias during purification.

## Confidence
- Reconstruction error minimization drives purified samples toward density maxima: **Medium**
- SAM during purification steers toward flat regions, enhancing robustness: **Medium**
- Deterministic purification achieves high robust accuracy without EoT: **High**
- Clean accuracy remains high: **High**
