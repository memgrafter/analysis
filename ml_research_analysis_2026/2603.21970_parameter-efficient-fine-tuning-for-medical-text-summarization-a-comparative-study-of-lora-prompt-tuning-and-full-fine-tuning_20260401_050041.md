# Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Medical Text Summarization: A Comparative Study of Lora, Prompt Tuning, and Full Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21970
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21970
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** LoRA achieves higher ROUGE-1 (43.52±0.18) than full fine-tuning (40.67±0.21) on Flan-T5-Large while training only 0.6% of parameters, reducing training time by 64% and peak GPU memory by 75%.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods—LoRA, Prompt Tuning, and Full Fine-Tuning—for medical text summarization using the Flan-T5 model family on the PubMed dataset. The core finding is that LoRA, which updates a small set of low-rank adaptation matrices, consistently outperforms full fine-tuning in both accuracy (ROUGE and BERTScore) and computational efficiency. On Flan-T5-Large, LoRA achieves a ROUGE-1 of 43.52 with only 0.6% trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning's 40.67. Sensitivity analysis identifies an optimal LoRA rank of 16 and prompt token count of 20. The authors posit that LoRA's low-rank constraint acts as implicit regularization, its preservation of pre-trained weights avoids catastrophic forgetting, and its smaller parameter space yields a smoother optimization landscape, collectively explaining its superior performance.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes Flan-T5 Small, Base, and Large models on the PubMed summarization dataset (119,924 train, 6,633 validation, 6,658 test examples). Each medical article (≤512 tokens) is summarized into an abstract (≤150 tokens) using a fixed instruction prefix. Three adaptation methods are compared:
- **LoRA:** Injects low-rank matrices (BA) into the query and value projection layers of all transformer blocks. Rank r=16, scaling α=32, dropout 0.1. Only these matrices are trained (~0.6% of parameters for Large).
- **Prompt Tuning:** Prepends 20 soft prompt tokens to the input; only their embeddings are trained.
- **Full Fine-Tuning:** Updates all model parameters.
Common training settings across methods include AdamW optimizer, batch size 4 per device with gradient accumulation 4 (effective batch 16), bfloat16 precision, 10 max epochs with early stopping (patience 3), and 3 random seeds (42, 123, 456). Learning rates differ: LoRA (3e-4), Prompt Tuning (3e-2), Full FT (5e-5). Performance is evaluated using ROUGE-1/2/L (F1) and BERTScore.

## Key Results
- LoRA significantly outperforms full fine-tuning across all Flan-T5 sizes, with Flan-T5-Large achieving ROUGE-1 of 43.52±0.18 vs. 40.67±0.21.
- LoRA reduces training time by 64% and peak GPU memory by 75% compared to full fine-tuning.
- Optimal LoRA rank is 16; performance plateaus beyond this. Optimal prompt token count is 20 for Prompt Tuning.
- All methods benefit from larger model scale, but the performance gap between LoRA and full fine-tuning narrows with increasing model size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The low-rank constraint in LoRA acts as implicit regularization, preventing overfitting on the medical summarization dataset.
- **Mechanism:** By restricting weight updates to a low-dimensional subspace (ΔW = BA with rank r ≪ min(d,k)), LoRA limits the model's capacity to memorize noise or dataset-specific quirks, encouraging learning of more generalizable patterns relevant to summarizing medical articles.
- **Core assumption:** The intrinsic dimensionality of the task-specific adaptation manifold is low; i.e., effective updates to pre-trained weights for this task lie within a subspace of much lower rank than the full parameter matrix.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our findings suggest the low-rank constraint provides beneficial regularization, challenging assumptions about the necessity of full parameter updates."
  - [section 5] "First, the low-rank constraint functions as implicit regularization... preventing the model from memorizing idiosyncratic patterns in the training data that would not generalize to the test set."
  - [corpus] Related work (e.g., "RST-LoRA", "Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning with Layer Pruning") explores structured constraints for summarization, providing contextual support but not direct evidence for this paper's regularization claim. **Corpus evidence is indirect; the claim is primarily anchored in the paper's own analysis and discussion.**
- **Break condition:** If sensitivity analysis showed performance degradation with *lower* ranks (which it does not—performance increases up to r=16), this regularization mechanism would be undermined. The observed diminishing returns beyond r=16 support an optimal capacity hypothesis rather than a simple "more rank is better" one.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LoRA preserves pre-trained representations by freezing original weights, avoiding catastrophic forgetting of general linguistic and domain knowledge acquired during pre-training.
- **Mechanism:** Only the low-rank adaptation matrices (A and B) are trained; the base model weights (W) remain unchanged. This prevents destructive overwriting of weights that encode broad language understanding, syntax, and even latent medical knowledge from the Flan-T5 pre-training.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained weights contain essential, reusable knowledge for the summarization task, and full fine-tuning risks altering these weights in ways that harm general capabilities or robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "We apply low-rank adaptation matrices... while keeping the majority of pre-trained weights frozen."
  - [section 4.1] LoRA's superior performance over full fine-tuning suggests the frozen weights' knowledge is beneficial and not detrimental by being "outdated."
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper "LoFT: Low-Rank Adaptation That Behaves Like Full Fine-Tuning" explicitly addresses the gap between LoRA and full FT, implying the preservation mechanism is a known differentiator. **Corpus evidence confirms the mechanism is a known PEFT advantage.**
- **Break condition:** If full fine-tuning consistently matched or exceeded LoRA on tasks requiring significant deviation from pre-trained knowledge, this mechanism's dominance would be questionable. The consistent LoRA advantage here suggests preservation is net beneficial.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The smaller, optimized parameter space of LoRA (vs. full FT) yields a smoother loss landscape and more stable gradient updates, leading to better final solutions.
- **Mechanism:** Training a tiny fraction of parameters reduces optimization complexity and potential for sharp minima. The low-rank structure may impose a smoother optimization path, making convergence to a robust solution more likely given the same computational budget.
- **Core assumption:** The high-dimensional, non-convex loss landscape of full fine-tuning contains many poor local optima or unstable gradients; constraining updates to a low-rank subspace smooths this landscape.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5] "The smaller parameter space may yield smoother loss landscapes and more stable gradient updates, leading to better final solutions." This is presented as one of three proposed explanations.
  - [section 4.2] LoRA shows lower variance across random seeds (e.g., R-1: 43.52±0.18 vs Full FT 40.67±0.21 on Large), consistent with more stable optimization.
  - [corpus] **No direct anchor.** This is a speculative mechanism from the paper's discussion; related corpus papers do not specifically test loss landscape smoothness.
- **Break condition:** If LoRA's performance was highly sensitive to learning rate or seed, showing instability, this mechanism would be weakened. The reported low standard deviations actually support it.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
  - Why needed here: It is the core PEFT method evaluated. Understanding that it factors weight updates (ΔW = BA) into two small matrices is essential to grasp its efficiency (0.6% parameters) and the hypothesized mechanisms (regularization, preservation).
  - Quick check question: "If LoRA applies matrices A (r×k) and B (d×r) to a frozen weight matrix W (d×k), what is the rank of the effective update ΔW, and why does this drastically reduce trainable parameters?"
- **Concept: Full Fine-Tuning as Baseline**
  - Why needed here: It represents the "upper bound" adaptation capacity. Comparing LoRA and Prompt Tuning to it establishes the surprising result that *fewer* trainable parameters can yield *better* performance, challenging default assumptions.
  - Quick check question: "Why is full fine-tuning computationally expensive (memory/time) for a 783M parameter model like Flan-T5-Large, and what does it implicitly assume about parameter updates?"
- **Concept: Model Scaling Effects**
  - Why needed here: The study tests Small, Base, and Large Flan-T5. All methods improve with scale, but LoRA's *relative* advantage pattern (largest on Small, smallest on Large) informs whether mechanisms are scale-dependent.
  - Quick check question: "How does increasing model size (from Small to Large) affect the performance gap between LoRA and full fine-tuning, and what might this imply about the 'size' of the adaptation manifold?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Flan-T5 Encoder-Decoder (frozen backbone) -> LoRA Modules (inject BA into 'q' and 'v' projections of every transformer block) -> Input/Output Processors (Tokenizer with 512/150 limits, instruction prefix) -> Evaluation Pipeline (ROUGE/BERTScore calculation).
- **Critical path:** Tokenization -> Model Forward Pass (LoRA matrices merged with frozen weights on-the-fly) -> Loss Calculation -> Backward Pass (gradients flow only to LoRA params) -> Parameter Update (A and B only).
- **Design tradeoffs:** LoRA rank (r=16 optimal; lower saves memory but hurts performance, higher yields diminishing returns), alpha scaling (α=32 gives scale α/r=2), prompt token count (20 optimal; more increases capacity but risks interference), precision (bfloat16 saves memory vs. float32).
- **Failure signatures:** 1) Vanishing gradients on LoRA matrices if learning rate too low (they start near zero). 2) Catastrophic forgetting in Full FT: potential drop in general capabilities or high variance. 3) Prompt Tuning collapse: model ignores prompt, outputs pre-training behavior. 4) Out-of-memory: batch size/gradient accumulation exceeds 48GB A6000.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate LoRA baseline on Flan-T5-Base/PubMed with r=16, α=32, target q/v. Verify ~41.9 ROUGE-1 and ~0.7% trainable params.
  2. Perform rank sensitivity sweep on Base: test r ∈ {4,8,16,32,64} with 3 seeds each. Plot ROUGE-1 vs. trainable params to confirm optimal rank.
  3. Compare LoRA to full fine-tuning with identical data/hyperparams (except learning rate). Run Full FT with LR=5e-5 for 10 epochs to directly measure LoRA > Full FT claim.

## Limitations
- **Dataset fidelity uncertainty:** The exact PubMed train/val/test split files are not specified; alternative dataset versions may lead to different evaluation results.
- **Prompt Tuning initialization ambiguity:** The description "initialized from the embedding of the task description text" lacks procedural clarity (e.g., mean of instruction tokens vs. special token), impacting reproducibility.
- **Mechanism evidence is indirect:** Claims about regularization, preservation, and optimization smoothness are inferred from performance and variance, not directly measured (e.g., no loss landscape analysis or gradient norm tracking).
- **Scope constraints:** Experiments limited to Flan-T5 on PubMed; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., decoder-only LLMs) or medical datasets is untested.
- **Ablation gaps:** No comparison of LoRA target modules beyond q/v; adapter-style or IA³ variants not evaluated.

## Confidence
- **LoRA superiority in performance & efficiency:** High – Consistent across model sizes with statistical tests, memory/time logs.
- **Optimal hyperparameters (rank=16, prompt tokens=20):** Medium – Sensitivity analysis done but may not capture all interactions (e.g., learning rate × rank).
- **Mechanisms (regularization, preservation, smooth optimization):** Low – Largely speculative; indirect evidence (variance reduction) but no causal validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Exact split replication:** Obtain and train on the precise dataset split files cited (Cohan et al., 2018) to confirm ROUGE gains hold without distribution shift.
2. **Prompt initialization ablation:** Systematically vary prompt token initialization (random, instruction-embedding mean, special token) to isolate its impact on Prompt Tuning convergence and final performance.
3. **Direct mechanism test:** For LoRA vs. Full FT, compute gradient norms per layer and loss landscape curvature (e.g., Hessian spectrum) on a validation subset to empirically evaluate smoothness and memorization claims.
