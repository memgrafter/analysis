# Relational graph-driven differential denoising and diffusion attention fusion for multimodal conversation emotion recognition

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25752  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25752  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ReDiFu achieves 75.17% weighted accuracy and 74.87% weighted F1 on IEMOCAP, and 66.52% w-Acc / 66.62% w-F1 on MELD, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary

Multimodal Conversation Emotion Recognition (MCER) must handle noisy audio/visual signals and modality imbalance, while existing methods often neglect noise and treat modalities implicitly, underutilizing text’s dominant role. The proposed ReDiFu framework addresses these issues with three innovations: (1) a differential Transformer for audio and visual streams that subtracts adjacent attention maps to suppress time‑irrelevant noise while preserving consistent dynamics; (2) relation‑aware subgraphs (InterGAT and IntraGAT) that model speaker‑dependent emotional dependencies within and across modalities; and (3) a text‑guided cross‑modal diffusion attention fusion that lets text absorb audio/visual information via normalized cross‑attention and a learnable gating mechanism. On IEMOCAP and MELD benchmarks, ReDiFu sets new state‑of‑the‑art results, and ablations confirm the complementary contribution of each module.

## Method Summary

Inputs are first preprocessed: text is tokenized with RoBERTa (using [CLS] embeddings), audio features are extracted with openSMILE, and visual features come from a pre‑trained DenseNet. All modalities are projected to a common dimensionality (512 for IEMOCAP, 256 for MELD) and combined with learnable speaker embeddings and sinusoidal positional encodings.

The audio and visual encoders use a differential Transformer. For each token, two attention maps are computed: one for the current token and a one‑step shifted reference. The differential output \((\alpha - \lambda \alpha_{\text{ref}})V\) (with learnable \(\lambda\)) undergoes GroupNorm, a linear projection, and a residual connection. A gated filter \(F = \sigma(Z_{\text{res}}W_g + b_g)\) then produces the denoised features \(Z'_m\).

The text encoder constructs two relation graphs over a sliding window of length \(L\) (20 for IEMOCAP, 25 for MELD). Edges are defined by temporal proximity (\(|i-j| \le L-1\)) and speaker identity (intra‑ vs. inter‑speaker). Three relation types (self‑loop, forward, backward) are embedded. Both graphs (InterGAT and IntraGAT) employ a two‑head Graph Attention Network (output dimension \(d/2\) per head, concatenated back to \(d\)) and are applied sequentially to yield \(Z^{\text{out}}_t\).

Fusion is mediated by text. For each modality \(m \in \{t,a,v\}\), self‑attention \(S^m = \text{softmax}(Q^m(K^m)^\top/\sqrt{d})\) is computed and optionally normalized to \(\hat{S}^m\) by symmetric normalization. Cross‑modal diffusion uses \(\gamma = 1\): \(S^{t\to m} = \gamma (\hat{S}_t \hat{S}_m^\top) + (1-\gamma)(S_t+S_m)\). The resulting attention is applied to \(V^m\) to obtain \(U^{t\to m}\). A gating scalar \(T = \sigma(W_g[U^{t\to a} \| U^{t\to v}])\) controls the blend: \(Z'_t = Z^{\text{out}}_t + T \odot U^{t\to a} + (1-T) \odot U^{t\to v}\).

Separate linear heads produce logits \(L_a, L_t, L_v\). They are fused with dataset‑specific fixed weights (\(\alpha_t=1.0,\alpha_a=1.0,\alpha_v=0.4\) for IEMOCAP; \(\alpha_t=3.0,\alpha_a=1.0,\alpha_v=0.3\) for MELD) and trained with a combined loss \(L = L_f + 0.1(L_a+L_t+L_v)\) using Adam and cosine annealing.

## Key Results

- IEMOCAP: 75.17% weighted accuracy, 74.87% weighted F1.  
- MELD: 66.52% weighted accuracy, 66.62% weighted F1.  
- Ablations show that removing any of the three main components (denoising, graph, diffusion) degrades performance, confirming their necessity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

The differential Transformer directly removes time‑invariant noise from audio/visual streams by canceling adjacent attention maps, preserving only dynamics relevant to emotion. The relation graphs explicitly capture how emotional states propagate between speakers and across turns, addressing the lack of speaker‑aware context modeling. By making text the central fusion hub, the diffusion attention prevents noisy audio/visual signals from contaminating the final representation; instead, text selectively absorbs information from the other modalities through a normalized, gated mechanism, thereby mitigating modality imbalance. Together these design choices yield robust, noise‑tolerant multimodal emotion recognition.

## Foundational Learning

- **Differential Attention**: Subtracts a reference attention map to isolate time‑varying components. *Why needed*: Removes stationary noise (e.g., background sound, still posture) without extra supervision. *Quick check*: Add constant noise to a synthetic audio stream and verify that the differential layer’s output has reduced noise amplitude.  
- **Graph Attention Networks (GAT)**: Applies attention over graph‑structured edges to weight neighbor contributions. *Why needed*: Speaker dependencies are not uniformly temporal; they depend on conversation dynamics. *Quick check*: Print adjacency matrices for a batch; IntraGAT should connect only same‑speaker nodes within the window, InterGAT only cross‑speaker nodes, and self‑loops should be present.  
- **Cross‑modal Diffusion Fusion**: Diffuses information from auxiliary modalities into text via normalized cross‑attention. *Why needed*: Avoids naive concatenation that can amplify noise; uses text as a stable anchor. *Quick check*: Ablate the normalization step and observe a significant drop in validation performance.  
- **Gated Filtering**: Applies a sigmoid gate to features after the differential layer. *Why needed*: Further suppresses residual irrelevant dimensions. *Quick check*: Monitor average gate activation; it should be sparse (e.g., mean < 0.5), indicating selective modulation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Text → (RoBERTa + speaker + position) → InterGAT → IntraGAT → Z_t_out  
Audio/Visual → (Projection) → Differential Transformer → Gated filter → Z_a_out / Z_v_out  
Fusion: (Z_t_out, Z_a_out, Z_v_out) → Diffusion attention (γ=1) → U^{t→a}, U^{t→v} → Gate T → Z'_t = Z_t_out + T⊙U^{t→a} + (1−T)⊙U^{t→v}  
Classification: Linear heads on Z'_t, Z_a_out, Z_v_out → L_t, L_a, L_v → weighted sum (α) → prediction.

**Critical path**  
1. Text enrichment via two relation graphs.  
2. Audio/visual denoising via differential Transformer.  
3. Text‑guided diffusion fusion to incorporate complementary signals.  
4. Modality‑specific classifiers and weighted logit fusion.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Text‑centric fusion* simplifies training but may limit novel cross‑modal interactions not mediated by text.  
- *Differential Transformer* adds parameters and compute but is effective for denoising; benefit is highest on raw audio/visual streams.  
- *Sliding window* for graphs balances context capture and memory; larger windows give longer range but increase cost.  
- *Fixed fusion weights* avoid overfitting on training data but may not generalize to new datasets without retuning.  
- *Normalization in diffusion* stabilizes training but can be redundant after softmax; removing it may speed inference with minor performance loss.

**Failure signatures**  
- Differential attention mis‑implementation: λ not learnable or incorrectly initialized; check that differential attention maps are sparse.  
- Graph construction error: wrong speaker ID handling or edge condition; verify adjacency matrices (InterGAT only cross‑speaker, IntraGAT only same‑speaker).  
- Fusion mis‑configuration: wrong γ or α causing performance drop; sweep these on validation.  
- Overfitting on IEMOCAP: small dataset; monitor validation loss gap and increase regularization.  
- Numerical instability: row‑stochasticity of S^m ensures D positive; if NaNs appear, add epsilon to diagonal.

**First experiments** (for a new implementation)  
1. **Unimodal baseline**: Train text‑only, audio‑only, and visual‑only models; expect text to be strongest and audio/visual to be weaker but not random.  
2. **Differential attention sanity**: Add constant synthetic noise to audio/visual features; after the differential layer, the noise component should be attenuated.  
3. **Graph adjacency validation**: For a few dialogue windows, output binary adjacency matrices; confirm that edges obey speaker identity and window constraints and that self‑loops are included.

## Next Checks

1. **Differential attention stress test** – Inject synthetic time‑irrelevant noise into audio/visual streams; compare signal‑to‑noise ratio after ReDiFu versus a standard Transformer encoder.  
2. **Graph structure verification** – Extract attention weights from InterGAT and IntraGAT on a held‑out dialogue; confirm that edges respect speaker identity and temporal window, and that self‑loops are utilized.  
3. **Fusion normalization ablation** – Remove the symmetric normalization step and systematically vary γ (0.0, 0.5, 1.0) on the validation set; measure impact on weighted F1. Also test static versus dynamic unimodal loss weighting.
