# Sample-efficient evidence estimation of score based priors for model selection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20549
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20549
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DiME achieves 0.3-1.5% relative error on 1000-D Gaussian mixtures and correctly selects MNIST digit models, using only ~20 posterior paths without prior score evaluation.

## Executive Summary
DiME addresses the intractable model evidence computation problem for diffusion-based priors in Bayesian inverse problems. Traditional estimators require many samples or accurate prior scores, which are problematic for high-dimensional, out-of-distribution cases. DiME integrates KL divergence between posterior and prior along diffusion time-marginals using intermediate samples naturally produced during diffusion posterior sampling (DAPS). It requires only a handful of posterior paths (e.g., 20) and avoids explicit prior score evaluation. On synthetic 1000-D Gaussian mixture tasks, DiME achieves relative errors 0.3-1.5%, matching or beating thermodynamic integration, annealed importance sampling, and sequential Monte Carlo despite not using the true score. On MNIST phase-retrieval tasks, DiME consistently selects the correct digit-specific diffusion prior from ten candidates, while SMC fails. On real M87* black hole data, DiME identifies GRMHD simulations as the most likely prior and confirms in-distribution validity (z-score -0.81). A Gaussian-approx DAPS variant provides 7× speedup with comparable accuracy.

## Method Summary
DiME estimates Bayesian model evidence by integrating the KL divergence between posterior and prior along diffusion time-marginals. The method leverages intermediate samples naturally obtained during diffusion posterior sampling (DAPS or PnP-DM), avoiding explicit prior score evaluation. It computes the score of the likelihood at noised states using two complementary estimators (Θ_high and Θ_low) with complementary variance properties across the diffusion timeline, selecting the lower-variance option per timestep. The method uses an improved Gaussian covariance approximation for p(x0|x_t) and requires only a handful of posterior paths (e.g., 20) to achieve accurate evidence estimates. The approach works for both normalized (DAPS) and unnormalized (PnP-DM) marginal distributions, with DAPS providing better out-of-distribution performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: KL Integration Along Diffusion Path
Model evidence log p(y) can be expressed as an integral over diffusion time-marginals of the KL divergence between posterior and prior. The identity log p(y) = E_{x0~p(x0|y)}[log p(y|x0)] - D_KL(p(x0|y)||p(x0)) holds. The KL term transforms via the probability flow ODE into a time integral using only posterior marginals and likelihood scores at noised states. This works because diffusion forward/backward processes define smooth marginal distributions satisfying PF-ODE continuity equations. Break condition: if posterior marginals deviate significantly from assumed form (e.g., posterior sampling method does not anneal correctly), bias emerges.

### Mechanism 2: Intermediate Sample Reuse from Posterior Sampling
Diffusion posterior samplers naturally generate intermediate states x_t and denoised samples x0 that provide cheap, direct access to required expectations, bypassing explicit prior score computation. Algorithms like DAPS produce samples following (approximately) the path x_t ~ p(x_t|y), and at each step generate x0 ~ p(x0|x_t,y) via Langevin or Gaussian methods. These samples allow estimation of the score term ||∇_{x_t} log p(y|x_t)||^2 using unbiased estimators Θ_high or Θ_low, avoiding any need for ∇_{x_t} log p(x_t). Core assumption: posterior sampling procedure (DAPS/PnP-DM) accurately targets intended marginal distributions throughout annealing. Break condition: posterior sampler fails to maintain correct marginal path (e.g., too few Langevin steps, aggressive approximations), causing biased estimates.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive High/Low-Noise Scoring with Variance Control
Two complementary score estimators (Θ_high, Θ_low) have complementary variance properties across the diffusion timeline; selecting lower-variance option per timestep yields robust overall evidence estimates. Under high noise (σ_t≈1), Θ_high = (a_t/σ_t^2)(x0 - E[x0|x_t]) has low variance because prior is nearly isotropic. Under low noise (σ_t≈0), Θ_low = (a_t/σ_t^2)(Σ_{x0|x_t} ∇_{x0} log p(y|x0)) has low variance because likelihood dominates. An unbiased estimate of squared norm requires two independent x0 samples per x_t. Core assumption: Gaussian posterior approximation p(x0|x_t) ≈ N(E[x0|x_t], Σ_{x0|x_t}) is sufficiently accurate; linearization of likelihood yields valid score approximations. Break condition: extreme outliers in x0 or likelihood non-linearity breaks linear approximation, inflating variance beyond correction by path length.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Diffusion model (score network) -> Likelihood function -> Annealing schedule -> Posterior sampler (DAPS/PnP-DM) -> Estimator module -> Covariance approximation -> Evidence output

### Critical path
1. Choose diffusion schedule and posterior sampler
2. For each sample path: run DAPS/PnP-DM, storing all intermediate x_t
3. Post-process: for each x_t, compute E[x0|x_t] (Tweedie), draw two x0 via Langevin with guidance
4. Compute Θ_high and Θ_low; pick lower variance using sample-path statistics
5. Accumulate integrals Σ c_{t_i} Δt_i ||Θ||^2
6. Combine with E_{x0~p(x0|y)}[log p(y|x0)] to obtain final evidence

### Design tradeoffs
- Standard marginals (DAPS) vs unnormalized marginals (PnP-DM): DAPS yields normalized evidence and better out-of-distribution performance; PnP-DM is simpler but requires constant factor for comparison
- Covariance approximation: Diagonal vs full vs approximation with Σ_0. Full Σ_0 improves high-noise accuracy but costs O(d^3) memory; diagonal basis (e.g., PCA) is practical
- Number of paths: More paths reduce variance but linearly increase compute; 20-50 paths typically sufficient for medium-dimensional problems
- Annealing steps: More steps improve integral accuracy but increase memory for storing samples; uniform t_i or geometrically spaced schedules are common

### Failure signatures
- Evidence estimates vary wildly across sample paths (high variance) → likely too few paths or poor Σ_{x0|x_t}
- Model selection picks wrong prior when ground truth is out-of-distribution → PnP-DM path bias or DAPS with original covariance heuristic
- All evidence estimates cluster near same value for different priors → insufficient annealing steps or sampling error dominates

### First 3 experiments
1. Setup Mixture of Gaussians with known analytic evidence; test in-distribution, out-of-distribution, saddle point; verify DiME's bias remains <~1% across conditions
2. Run MNIST phase retrieval with ten digit-specific diffusion priors; confirm DiME selects correct model while SMC fails
3. Apply to M87* data with five candidate priors; verify GRMHD is selected and compute z-score for prior validation using in-distribution simulations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
*Can DiME be theoretically guaranteed for arbitrary annealing paths, and do different paths yield significantly different evidence estimates?* The paper provides a generalized estimator in Appendix B but only applies it to DAPS/PnP-DM without analyzing path choice. The bias from path-specific approximations is unknown; path selection may affect accuracy. Evidence needed: theoretical error bounds as function of path smoothness; empirical comparison across diverse paths on tasks with known evidence.

### Open Question 2
*How robust is DiME's Gaussian prior approximation for non-Gaussian priors, and can more flexible approximations reduce bias?* Lemma 1 uses Gaussian prior; paper notes this may be inaccurate for complex priors but does not test alternatives. Gaussian covariance may misrepresent multi-modality, leading to KL estimate errors; non-Gaussian approximations are unexplored. Evidence needed: evaluate DiME with non-Gaussian approximations (e.g., mixture models, normalizing flows) on synthetic non-Gaussian priors; measure evidence estimation error.

### Open Question 3
*Can we reduce the number of required posterior sample paths in DiME without increasing variance, e.g., by improved score estimation?* The method uses 20 paths and two samples per timestep; variance of Θ_low is noted to be higher; no variance reduction discussed. More sample paths increase computational cost; reducing paths would improve efficiency. Evidence needed: develop and test low-variance estimators (e.g., control variates, coupled samples) showing fewer paths achieve same accuracy.

### Open Question 4
*How does DiME perform on large-scale, high-dimensional inverse problems (e.g., full-resolution EHT imaging) and what are the main scaling bottlenecks?* Experiments are on low-dimensional or small 2D data; conclusion suggests extension to scientific imaging but not demonstrated. Covariance computation and Langevin dynamics may not scale; memory and time could become prohibitive. Evidence needed: application to high-resolution real-world dataset with profiling; identification of computational bottlenecks and mitigation strategies (e.g., diagonal/low-rank approximations).

## Limitations

- DiME's accuracy depends critically on posterior sampling method (DAPS/PnP-DM) accurately targeting correct marginal distributions; convergence issues cause bias
- The Gaussian prior approximation and linear likelihood assumption may break down for strongly non-Gaussian priors or highly non-linear likelihood functions
- The method requires careful tuning of multiple hyperparameters (annealing steps, Langevin steps, learning rates) which are not fully specified and affect performance
- Computational cost scales poorly with dimension due to cubic cost in covariance computations and memory requirements for storing intermediate samples

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Mathematical derivation of KL integration identity and variance analysis of Θ_high/Θ_low estimators are sound and well-supported by theory
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical performance on synthetic and real data demonstrates viability, but absence of public data limits independent verification of most compelling real-world results
- **Low confidence**: Method's behavior in pathological cases (multimodal posteriors, strong prior-data conflicts, or when prior is far from Gaussian) is not fully characterized; potential failure modes not extensively explored

## Next Checks

1. **Benchmark scaling**: Test DiME on Gaussian mixture problems with increasing dimension (500D, 2000D, 5000D) to quantify how relative error and computational cost scale, identifying at what dimension cubic covariance cost becomes prohibitive
2. **Out-of-distribution robustness**: Systematically vary ground truth parameters relative to assumed prior to assess DiME's bias and variance in model selection when true data-generating process is misspecified
3. **Posterior sampling quality dependency**: Run DiME with degraded posterior sampling (fewer Langevin steps, larger step sizes, or early stopping) and quantify resulting bias in evidence estimates to characterize method's sensitivity to sampling errors
