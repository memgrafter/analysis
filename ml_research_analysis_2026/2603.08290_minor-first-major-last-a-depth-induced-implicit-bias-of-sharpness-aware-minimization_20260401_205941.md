# Minor First, Major Last: A Depth-Induced Implicit Bias of Sharpness-Aware Minimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08290
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08290
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: For depth-2 linear diagonal networks, ℓ2-SAM exhibits a finite-time "sequential feature amplification" where minor data coordinates dominate early training before major ones take over; ℓ∞-SAM with depth ≥2 shows extreme sensitivity to initialization and can collapse or align with arbitrary features, unlike gradient descent.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the implicit bias of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) on linear diagonal networks for linearly separable classification. Using continuous-time SAM flows, the authors reveal that depth introduces qualitatively new behaviors beyond gradient descent. For single-layer networks, both ℓ2- and ℓ∞-SAM recover gradient descent’s ℓ2 max-margin solution. In contrast, for depth L≥2, ℓ∞-SAM’s limiting direction becomes highly sensitive to initialization and perturbation radius, potentially collapsing to zero or aligning with any feature. For depth-2, ℓ2-SAM asymptotically converges to an ℓ1 max-margin solution, but its finite-time dynamics exhibit a "sequential feature amplification": training initially amplifies minor data coordinates before shifting to major ones. This behavior is governed by three regimes determined by the initialization scale α: collapse (too small), sequential amplification (intermediate), and direct major-feature dominance (large). Theoretical analysis is corroborated by synthetic and real-data experiments, emphasizing the importance of finite-time analysis for understanding SAM.

## Method Summary
The study employs a theoretical approach based on continuous-time dynamics to analyze SAM’s implicit bias. The authors consider a linear diagonal network with L layers, where the predictor is the element-wise product of layer weights. Training uses logistic loss on a single-example dataset with a feature vector μ (strictly positive, ordered coordinates). Two SAM variants are studied: ℓ2-SAM (perturbation along normalized gradient) and ℓ∞-SAM (perturbation along sign of gradient). The discrete SAM updates are approximated by rescaled ordinary differential equations (ODEs) that reparameterize time to eliminate the loss derivative, yielding coordinate-wise dynamics. For ℓ2-SAM, the rescaled flow is dw/dt = μ ⊙ (w - ρ (μ⊙w)/nθ(t)), where nθ(t) = √{2∥μ⊙w∥₂^2}. For ℓ∞-SAM, the flow decouples across coordinates: ˙w_j = μ_j (w_j - ρ sign(w_j^{(L-1)})). The analysis tracks key quantities such as mc(t) = nθ/(2ρ) and the weighted moments M_r = Σ p_j μ_j^r (with p_j proportional to μ_j² β_j). The depth-2 ℓ2-SAM dynamics reduce to a two-dimensional system revealing three regimes based on initial mc(0), which depends on initialization scale α. The theoretical findings are validated via numerical integration of the ODEs and discrete SAM training on synthetic and real datasets.

## Key Results
- Depth 1: Both ℓ∞- and ℓ2-SAM recover gradient descent’s ℓ2 max-margin direction.
- Depth L≥2: ℓ∞-SAM’s limit direction depends critically on initialization and radius; it can collapse to zero or align with any feature, unlike GD.
- Depth 2 ℓ2-SAM: Asymptotically matches GD’s ℓ1 max-margin, but finite-time dynamics exhibit sequential feature amplification: training starts with minor features and shifts to major ones as training proceeds or initialization increases; three distinct regimes (collapse, sequential amplification, direct major dominance) are controlled by initialization scale α.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper uncovers three core mechanisms:

1. **ℓ2-SAM’s gradient normalization** introduces a coordinate-dependent discount factor (1 - ρμ_j/nθ) in the rescaled flow. This suppresses coordinates with large μ_j when nθ is small (early training or small initialization α), allowing smaller μ_j coordinates to grow faster initially. As nθ increases, minor coordinates become amplified relative to major ones during the pre-asymptotic phase, leading to sequential feature amplification.

2. **Depth-2 ℓ2-SAM exhibits three regimes** based on initialization scale α. The dynamics of mc(t) = nθ/(2ρ) determine the behavior: if mc(t) decreases indefinitely (α below α₁) the system collapses; if mc(t) eventually increases while crossing the threshold mH = M₂/(2M₁) (α between α₁ and α₂) sequential amplification occurs; if mc(t) is always large and above mH (α above α₂) the predictor converges directly to the major feature. The thresholds α₀, α₁, α₂ arise from the interplay of feature ordering and the time-varying moments.

3. **ℓ∞-SAM with depth L≥2** decouples coordinates: ˙w_j = μ_j (w_j - ρ sign(w_j^{(L-1)})). Each coordinate evolves independently with a threshold at ρ. For even L, coordinates with initial w_j(0) < ρ decay to 0; for odd L, they converge to ρ^L. The dominant direction emerges from the set S = {j: w_j(0) > ρ} via argmax μ_j (w_j(0) - ρ)^{L-2}, making initialization a key determinant of which feature survives. This sensitivity allows convergence to minor features or collapse, in stark contrast to GD.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Linear diagonal network (L layers, weights w^(ℓ) ∈ ℝ^d, predictor β = ⊙_{ℓ=1}^L w^(ℓ)) → SAM variants (ℓ2-SAM: ε₂ = ρ ∇L/‖∇L‖₂; ℓ∞-SAM: ε∞ = ρ sign(∇L)) → Rescaled continuous-time ODE (coordinate-wise dynamics) → Key quantities (mc(t)=nθ/(2ρ), mH(t)=M₂/(2M₁)) → Regime determination (based on initial mc(0)= (2ρ)^{-1}‖μ‖₂√{2L} α) → Behaviors: collapse, sequential amplification, direct major dominance.

### Critical path
1. Input: μ, ρ, α (initialization scale).
2. Compute initial mc(0) = (2ρ)^{-1} ‖μ‖₂ √{2L} α.
3. For ℓ2-SAM: mc'(t) = λM₁(mc - mH); compare mc(0) to thresholds α₀, α₁, α₂ (Theorem 4.4) to determine regime.
4. Sequential amplification arises when mc evolves from below mH toward major features; instantaneous growth rate r_j(t) ∝ μ_j(1 - ρμ_j/nθ) causes minor features to grow fastest when nθ is small.
5. For ℓ∞-SAM: coordinate-wise independent dynamics with threshold ρ; dominant direction emerges from set S={j: α_j > ρ} via argmax μ_j(α_j - ρ)^{L-2}, making initialization critical.

### Design tradeoffs
- ℓ2-SAM vs ℓ∞-SAM: ℓ2 preserves ℓ1 max-margin limit but introduces sequential amplification in finite-time; ℓ∞ loses max-margin alignment and can collapse to arbitrary features depending on initialization.
- Depth: L=1 → SAM ≈ GD; L=2 → new SAM-specific behaviors; L>2 → ℓ∞-SAM sensitivity increases.
- ρ (perturbation radius): Larger ρ enhances normalization effect in ℓ2-SAM, potentially strengthening early minor-feature amplification; for ℓ∞-SAM, ρ sets threshold separating decay vs. convergence.
- Initialization scale α: Must be in (α₁, α₂) for ℓ2-SAM to exhibit sequential amplification; too small → collapse, too large → GD-like major-feature focus.

### Failure signatures
- ℓ2-SAM: α ≤ α₀ → β(t) → 0 (loss never vanishes).
- ℓ2-SAM: Unintended minor-feature dominance when α is in lower part of Regime 2 if goal is to recover major features early.
- ℓ∞-SAM with L≥2: Extremely sensitive to α; minor variations can switch limit direction from major to minor or cause collapse.
- Diagnostic: Monitor mc(t) trajectory. If mc(t) declines below μ_L/2 and stays → collapse. If mc(t) increases while tracking near values between μ_k and μ_{k+1} for significant time → sequential amplification; implicated k indicates which intermediate feature gets overemphasized.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. For ℓ2-SAM on depth-2 linear diagonal networks, does sequential feature amplification persist for deeper networks (L>2), and what are the analogous thresholds (α₁, α₂) that determine the regimes?
2. How does breaking the balanced initialization across layers (e.g., setting w^(1)(0) and w^(2)(0) with different scales) affect the sequential feature amplification of ℓ2-SAM?
3. Does sequential feature amplification under SAM occur only for logistic loss, or does it also arise with other commonly used losses such as exponential loss (boosting) or hinge loss (SVMs)?
4. Does sequential feature amplification persist in nonlinear deep neural networks beyond linear diagonal models?

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes an idealized setting (linear diagonal networks, logistic loss, balanced initialization), limiting direct generalization to practical deep learning scenarios.
- Characterization of thresholds α₁, α₂ is implicit and data-dependent; predicting them precisely for arbitrary data remains challenging.
- Extensions to nonlinear architectures are speculative; the paper’s CNN experiments are preliminary and do not systematically verify the regimes.

## Confidence
- ℓ2-SAM sequential amplification: High confidence (under stated assumptions).
- Three α-regimes for depth-2 ℓ2-SAM: High confidence in regime structure; Medium confidence in exact threshold prediction.
- ℓ∞-SAM initialization sensitivity and collapse: High confidence for the analyzed model; Medium confidence for broader generalization.
- Transfer to deep nonlinear networks: Low confidence.
