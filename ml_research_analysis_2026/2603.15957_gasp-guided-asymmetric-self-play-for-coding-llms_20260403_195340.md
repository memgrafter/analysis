# GASP: Guided Asymmetric Self-Play For Coding LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15957
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15957
- **Reference Count**: 32
- **Primary Result**: GASP improves pass@20 on LiveCodeBench by 2.5% over unguided self-play (AZR), solves 11 unique goalposts that baselines cannot, and reaches 34.46% pass@20 when combined with real-data RL.

## Executive Summary
GASP introduces a guided asymmetric self-play framework for post-training coding LLMs. It anchors the teacher’s question generation to a curated set of hard, real-data “goalpost” problems. For each goalpost, the teacher generates an easier “lemma” variant and then a harder “lift” variant, forming a curriculum that incrementally builds the student’s abilities toward the target. Learnability-based filtering (pass‑rate bands) ensures generated problems lie within the student’s zone of proximal development, while diversity enforcement prevents mode collapse. Experiments on LiveCodeBench show GASP significantly outperforms unguided self-play, solves previously unsolved challenging problems, and demonstrates complementarity with real‑data reinforcement learning.

## Method Summary
GASP post-trains a code model (e.g., Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) using a teacher-student loop. The teacher receives a hard goalpost \(h\) from a curated set \(H\) of LiveCodeBench problems that the base model cannot solve. It first generates an easier lemma \(\ell_0\) preserving the goalpost’s high‑level motif; then it generates a harder lift \(\ell_1\) from \(\ell_0\). Both candidates are validated via estimated student pass rate (over \(N\) trials): lemmas must have \(p \in [0.3,0.7]\), lifts \(p \in [0.1,0.5]\). The teacher receives a normalized learnability reward that peaks at the target difficulty (0.5 for lemmas, 0.1 for lifts). Rejection sampling based on cosine similarity (>0.95) to previously accepted problems enforces diversity. Accepted problems update the teacher via REINFORCE++. The student is updated with PPO on the combined batch, receiving binary reward for solving. Optionally, real-data RL on original training problems can be mixed.

## Key Results
- GASP achieves a **2.5% improvement** in pass@20 on LiveCodeBench v5 compared to the AZR baseline.
- GASP solves **11 unique goalpost questions** that remain unsolved by all baselines.
- The hybrid **GASP + Real-data RL** variant reaches **34.46% pass@20**, demonstrating complementarity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Goalpost‑guided curriculum generation
- **Claim:** Generating lemma and lift variants for each hard goalpost produces a curriculum that incrementally builds the skills required to solve the target, improving generalization to the benchmark.
- **Mechanism:** The teacher generates an easier lemma \(\ell_0\) from the goalpost \(h\) while preserving its high‑level motif, then a harder lift \(\ell_1\) from \(\ell_0\). Each step stays within the student’s learnable range, progressively raising the frontier toward \(h\).
- **Core assumption:** The goalpost set \(H\) is representative of the skills needed for LiveCodeBench; closing the gap to these problems transfers to benchmark gains.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes anchoring with real‑data goalposts and two‑stage generation; [section 3.2] details filtering to keep only pass@100=0 questions; [section 3.3] explains generation process; corpus context notes novelty over prior self‑play methods.
- **Break condition:** If \(H\) is unrepresentative or the goalposts remain unsolvable even after generation, transfer may fail.

### Mechanism 2: Learnability‑based filtering and reward shaping
- **Claim:** Enforcing pass‑rate bands (lemma: [0.3,0.7]; lift: [0.1,0.5]) and using a reward \(R(p)=p(1-p)^\alpha\) that peaks at the target difficulty shapes teacher output to be optimally challenging, i.e., within the student’s zone of proximal development.
- **Mechanism:** Candidate problems are accepted only if the estimated student pass rate falls within the specified interval. The teacher’s reward is normalized to incentivize problems whose difficulty yields maximal learning signal (peak at \(p=0.5\) for lemmas, \(p=0.1\) for lifts).
- **Core assumption:** Pass‑rate estimates (over \(N\) trials) accurately reflect student capability and the bands correspond to optimal learning difficulty.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3.1] defines bands and reward formulas; Figure 3 shows reward curves; corpus reference to Sundaram et al. (2026) on edge‑of‑learnability.
- **Break condition:** Too few trials (\(!N\)) cause noisy estimates; overly narrow bands reduce acceptance; too wide bands admit non‑optimal difficulties.

### Mechanism 3: Diversity enforcement via rejection sampling
- **Claim:** Rejecting proposals with high cosine similarity (>0.95) to already accepted problems prevents mode collapse and ensures broad coverage of problem variations necessary for diverse goalposts.
- **Mechanism:** For each generated \(\ell_0\) or \(\ell_1\), embeddings of problem text/code are computed. If similarity exceeds 0.95 with any entry in the global buffer, the proposal is rejected.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity in the embedding space captures redundancy; maintaining diversity improves generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3.1] describes the novelty check; [section C.1] ablation shows diversity collapse without rejection (decreasing buffer dissimilarity); limited direct corpus comparisons.
- **Break condition:** Threshold too low discards valid novel proposals; too high fails to prevent collapse. Embedding quality must be adequate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Asymmetric Self‑Play**  
  *Why needed:* The framework uses a single model in two roles: teacher (generator) and student (solver). Understanding this duality is key to following the training loop.  
  *Quick check:* "What are the two roles and their objectives?" (Teacher: generate problems at the student’s frontier; Student: solve problems to maximize verifiable reward.)

- **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)**  
  *Why needed:* The learnability bands embody the educational principle that tasks slightly beyond current ability yield optimal learning, justifying the reward shaping.  
  *Quick check:* "Why does the lemma reward peak at \(p=0.5\) while lift peaks at \(p=0.1\)?" (Lemma should be moderately challenging; lift harder; both lie within ZPD.)

- **Curriculum Learning**  
  *Why needed:* The lemma‑lift sequence forms an automatic curriculum, ordering problems from easier to harder within the same motif, which is central to closing the gap to goalposts.  
  *Quick check:* "How does GASP’s two‑stage generation differ from sampling problems at a fixed intermediate difficulty?" (It provides stepping stones that build on each other, enabling incremental skill acquisition.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Goalpost set \(H\) → Sample \(h\) → Teacher \(\pi_T^\theta\) → Proposition Lemma \(\ell_0\) → Rejection sampler (format, safety, determinism, novelty) → Pass‑rate estimator → Accept if \(p_0 \in [0.3,0.7]\) → Lemma buffer \(B_0\) and Global \(G_{\ell_0}\). Repeat until \(|B_0|=M\).  
Sample \(\ell_0 \in B_0\) → Generate lift \(\ell_1\) → Rejection sampler → Pass‑rate estimator → Accept if \(p_1 \in [0.1,0.5]\) → Lift buffer \(B_1\) and Global \(G_{\ell_1}\). Repeat until \(|B_1|=M\).  
\(B_0 \cup B_1\) → Student \(\pi_S^\theta\) (with format conversion) → Solve → Binary reward → PPO update for student; REINFORCE++ update for teacher → Evaluation on LCB v5 → Checkpoint manager.

### Critical path
1. Sample goalpost \(h \sim H\).
2. Generate lemma \(\ell_0\): loop until valid and pass rate \(p_0 \in [0.3,0.7]\); add to lemma buffer \(B_0\) and global \(G_{\ell_0}\).
3. Repeat until \(|B_0| = M\); update teacher with REINFORCE++ using lemma reward.
4. Generate lift \(\ell_1\): sample \(\ell_0 \in B_0\), loop until valid and \(p_1 \in [0.1,0.5]\); add to lift buffer \(B_1\) and \(G_{\ell_1}\).
5. Repeat until \(|B_1| = M\); update teacher with lift reward.
6. Construct solver batch \(D_S = B_0 \cup B_1\); randomly assign induction/deduction/abduction format; compute binary reward (pass if any of \(N\) attempts succeeds).
7. Update student with PPO on \(D_S\).
8. Log, evaluate, and repeat.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Batch size \(M\)**: larger stabilizes teacher but increases compute; smaller yields faster iteration but higher variance.
- **Pass‑rate bands**: narrow ensures tight difficulty control but reduces acceptance; wide risks non‑optimal difficulties.
- **Diversity threshold (0.95)**: higher enforces stricter novelty but may reject valid proposals; lower risks mode collapse.
- **Rejection sampling overhead**: multiple retries increase teacher compute; tradeoff between diversity and efficiency.
- **Two‑stage vs one‑stage generation**: two‑stage adds extra update but provides finer curriculum; one‑stage simplifies but may miss intermediates.
- **Global buffer size**: unlimited growth costs memory; could use age‑based eviction.

### Failure signatures
- Lemma/lift acceptance rate < 20%: teacher cannot meet criteria (check bands/capacity).
- Buffer dissimilarity (Eq.7) decreasing: diversity collapse.
- Student pass rate on goalposts stays zero: curriculum ineffective or goalposts too hard.
- High variance in final pass@20 across seeds (>1% std): unstable training.
- Many format errors/non‑deterministic generations: heavy rejection.
- Lift pass rates not lower than lemmas: reward mis‑shaping.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline replication**: Train AZR for 350 steps from Qwen2.5‑Coder‑7B; evaluate LCBv5 pass@20 every 50 steps. GASP (50 steps) should outperform despite fewer steps.
2. **Diversity ablation**: Run GASP with/without rejection sampling; track buffer dissimilarity, mean/std pass@20 across three seeds, and unique goalposts solved. Expect higher dissimilarity, lower variance, and more goalpost progress with rejection.
3. **Difficulty axis ablation**: Train GASP using only functional axis or only I/O axis; compare to full GASP. Full GASP should be more stable and solve more goalposts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we reliably validate that the teacher’s generated lemma and lift questions provide correct, incremental guidance toward the intended goalpost, rather than diverging superficially?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Lightweight checks that measure alignment to the goalpost, or introducing a judge/reward model, could address both issues." (Page 9, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** The approach relies on pass‑rate bands and novelty filters but does not measure semantic or conceptual alignment. Curricula may be challenging yet irrelevant.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An automated alignment metric (e.g., embedding similarity to goalpost’s core logic, or a validator LLM judging incremental relevance) correlating with higher goalpost solve rates.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can we improve the teacher’s ability to generate lemma/lift questions that capture the underlying algorithmic concept of the goalpost, rather than borrowing surface‑level features or adding trivial constraints?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "current LLMs are not always good at abstracting the underlying concept of a task. They often borrow surface‑level metaphors... Relatedly, when asked to increase difficulty, they frequently do so by adding extra constraints, which is not always the most informative form of complexity." (Page 9, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** Teacher’s failure mode limits curriculum quality; stepping stones may not build the right conceptual bridges.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Human or automated evaluation (e.g., clustering by algorithmic motif) showing that guided curricula produce more conceptually consistent triples, leading to better performance on novel problems sharing the same motif.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How should the set of goalpost questions be dynamically updated over time to maintain meaningful guidance as the student model improves?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "An open question is what should happen once a goalpost is reached: ideally, the goalpost set should be updated over time so that guidance remains meaningful as the model improves. We leave dynamic goalpost updating to future work." (Page 10, Discussion)
- **Why unresolved:** The current GASP uses a fixed hard set identified from initial model failures. As the student learns, some goalposts become solved and no longer push the frontier, possibly causing the teacher to generate less relevant curricula.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A curriculum that periodically refreshes goalposts (e.g., sampling new unsolved hard problems from the training split) and demonstrates sustained or increased benchmark gains compared to a static set.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does scaling GASP to larger and more diverse real‑code corpora (beyond LiveCodeBench) yield consistent improvements, or does performance plateau due to diminishing returns in goalpost relevance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We use LCB pre‑2024.08 as our sole real‑data source. Scaling GASP with larger code training corpora is a natural next step." (Page 9, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments are confined to a single benchmark source. Generalizability to other coding domains is unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running GASP on multiple large‑scale code datasets (e.g., CodeContests, SWE‑bench) with analogous goalpost filtering, and reporting whether improvements are consistent, improved, or diminished.

## Limitations
- Teacher prompt template and few‑shot examples are undisclosed.
- Embedding model for novelty checks and hyperparameter details are unspecified.
- Goalpost set selection may suffer from motif bias, affecting representativeness.
