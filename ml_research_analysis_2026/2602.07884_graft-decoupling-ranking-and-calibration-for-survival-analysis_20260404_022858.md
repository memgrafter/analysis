# GRAFT: Decoupling Ranking and Calibration for Survival Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07884
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07884
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** GRAFT achieves state-of-the-art discrimination (C-index) and calibration (IBS) on six benchmarks while demonstrating superior robustness to high-dimensional noise, with minimal performance degradation even when noise features outnumber true features 10:1.

## Executive Summary
GRAFT introduces a hybrid Accelerated Failure Time (AFT) model that explicitly separates the objectives of ranking (discrimination) and calibration. It combines an interpretable linear component with a non-linear residual MLP, augmented by stochastic gates for automatic feature selection. The model is trained using a differentiable ranking loss aligned with the C-index, with censoring handled via stochastic imputation from local Kaplan-Meier estimators. After training, prognostic scores are calibrated with a post-hoc Cox Proportional Hazards model. This decoupled approach yields superior predictive performance and robustness, particularly in high-dimensional, noisy settings, though performance degrades under extreme censoring (≥92%).

## Method Summary
GRAFT operates in two phases. First, it learns a prognostic score \( s_i = \beta^T \phi_i + \mu \), where \( \phi_i = \tilde{x}_i + f_\theta(\tilde{x}_i) \). Features \( x_i \) are gated via stochastic binary masks \( \tilde{x}_i = g \odot x_i \), with \( g \) sampled from a relaxed Bernoulli distribution parameterized by \( \eta \). The linear term \( \beta^T \tilde{x}_i \) captures main effects, while the MLP \( f_\theta \) learns non-linear residuals. Training minimizes a soft-Spearman ranking loss between \( s_i \) and imputed log-event times \( Y^*_i \). For censored cases, \( Y^*_i \) is stochastically sampled from a conditional Kaplan-Meier distribution estimated from the k=10 nearest uncensored neighbors in the standardized feature space. An L0-approximation penalty on the gates encourages sparsity. After convergence, a 1-dimensional CoxPH model fits the training scores \( s_i \) to estimate a baseline hazard \( \Lambda_0(t) \). Final survival predictions are \( \hat{S}(t|x) = \exp(-\Lambda_0(t))^{\beta_{\text{cox}} s} \).

## Key Results
- GRAFT significantly outperforms classical (CoxPH, Weibull) and deep learning (DeepHit, DeepSurv) baselines in both C-index and Integrated Brier Score (IBS) across six public datasets.
- It exhibits exceptional robustness to noise: with 10× noisy features, GRAFT's C-index drops by <0.5%, while competitors drop by 2–10%.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of stochastic gates; removing them (No STG variant) leads to severe performance degradation under noise.
- Performance advantage diminishes under extreme censoring (≥92%), where simpler parametric models like CoxPH remain competitive.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Linear/Non-linear AFT Architecture
GRAFT models log-survival time as \( \log T = \beta^T \phi + \epsilon \), with \( \phi = \tilde{x} + f_\theta(\tilde{x}) \). The additive decomposition separates interpretable linear main effects (\( \beta^T \tilde{x} \)) from non-linear residuals (\( f_\theta(\tilde{x}) \)). This allows the model to capture both simple and complex relationships within a unified AFT framework, improving performance over purely linear or purely non-linear alternatives. The core assumption is that the true data-generating process contains a substantial linear component plus an additive non-linear residual. Evidence is drawn from the architecture description in Section 3.2 and the abstract. The approach may fail if the true relationship is purely linear (unnecessary complexity) or requires multiplicative interactions (not captured by additive residuals).

### Mechanism 2: Integrated Stochastic Feature Selection via Gates
Stochastic gates apply a sample-wise binary mask \( g \) (relaxed Bernoulli, parameterized by \( \eta \)) to input features. A differentiable L0-approximation penalty \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} = \lambda \sum \Phi(\eta_j/\sigma) \) encourages \( \eta_j \to 0 \) for irrelevant features. Gradients flow through the reparameterization trick, enabling end-to-end sparse training. This mechanism suppresses noise features, driving robustness in high-dimensional settings. The core assumption is that a sparse subset of features is truly predictive. Evidence is in Section 3.4 and the abstract; the STG technique is adapted from Yamada et al. (2020) but novel in survival analysis. Risks include discarding weak true signals if all features are relevant, or selection instability under high feature correlation.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupled Ranking-Centric Training with Post-hoc Calibration
GRAFT optimizes a soft-ranking loss directly aligned with C-index, using imputed log-times \( Y^* \) from local KM estimators. This focuses training on discrimination without imposing distributional assumptions on survival times. After convergence, a separate CoxPH model fits the prognostic scores \( s_i \) to estimate the baseline hazard \( \Lambda_0(t) \), converting scores into well-calibrated survival probabilities. The decoupling assumes that optimal ranking scores can be calibrated post-hoc without harming discrimination. Evidence is in Sections 3.5 and 3.6. The approach breaks down under extreme censoring where local KM imputation becomes unreliable, and the CoxPH calibration assumes covariate-independence of the baseline hazard.

## Foundational Learning
- **Accelerated Failure Time (AFT) Model:** Models log-survival time as a linear function of covariates plus error: \( \log T = \beta^T x + \epsilon \). Unlike CoxPH, which models the hazard, AFT directly models survival time and yields interpretable time ratios. *Quick check:* How does an AFT model differ from CoxPH in interpreting covariate effects?
- **Concordance Index (C-index):** A pairwise ranking metric measuring the fraction of comparable subject pairs where the predicted risk order matches the actual survival order. It is threshold-independent and robust to censoring. *Quick check:* What does a C-index of 0.7 imply about pairwise ranking performance, and why might a ranking loss be preferred over a likelihood loss under censoring?
- **Stochastic Gates (STG):** A differentiable feature selection method where binary masks are sampled from a continuous relaxation (e.g., hard-sigmoid concrete distribution). An L0-approximation penalty on mask parameters encourages sparsity during end-to-end training. *Quick check:* How does the expected L0 penalty \( \Phi(\eta_j/\sigma) \) encourage feature sparsity during optimization?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input features \( x \) pass through a stochastic gate layer to produce \( \tilde{x} = g \odot x \). \( \tilde{x} \) is fed into a residual pathway: an MLP computes \( f_\theta(\tilde{x}) \), and the final feature representation is \( \phi = \tilde{x} + f_\theta(\tilde{x}) \). The prognostic score is \( s = \beta^T \phi + \mu \). Training uses imputed targets from local KM, and post-hoc calibration fits CoxPH to the scores.

**Critical path:** Precompute local KM distributions for censored training subjects. During training: sample gates \( g \), compute \( s \), sample imputed \( Y^* \) for censored cases, compute soft-ranking loss \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{rank}} \) between \( s \) and \( Y^* \), add L0 and L2 penalties, backpropagate. After training: fit CoxPH on training scores \( s \) to get \( \beta_{\text{cox}} \) and \( \Lambda_0(t) \); predict \( \hat{S}(t|x) = \exp(-\Lambda_0(t))^{\beta_{\text{cox}} s} \).

**Design tradeoffs:** Hybrid architecture balances interpretability (linear) and capacity (non-linear residual) vs. pure linear or pure MLP. STG provides smooth gradients for sparse selection vs. discrete gates (REINFORCE). Ranking loss avoids distributional assumptions vs. likelihood-based losses but introduces smoothing bias via \( \tau \). Post-hoc CoxPH calibration is efficient vs. isotonic regression (more flexible but higher variance).

**Failure signatures:** Collapse under extreme censoring (≥92%) due to unreliable local KM imputation. Ablation without STG causes severe overfitting to noise. Gate degeneracy (all zeros or diffuse) from mis-tuned \( \lambda_{\text{L0}} \) or \( \sigma \). Calibration failure if CoxPH's PH assumption is violated for the scores.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline comparison (Sec 4.1, Table 1): Train GRAFT and all baselines on six datasets with 3-fold CV; report mean C-index and IBS.
2. Ablation study (Sec 4.2, Fig 1): Create variants (Full, No STG, Linear Only); augment with increasing Gaussian noise (3×,5×,7×,10×); plot C-index vs. noise multiplier.
3. Noise robustness (Sec 4.3, Fig 2): Apply heavy-tailed Student’s t noise to all models; plot C-index degradation to demonstrate GRAFT's superiority.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptation to extreme censoring:** How can GRAFT maintain robustness when censoring exceeds 92%, where local KM imputation fails due to insufficient neighbor events?
- **Competing risks extension:** Can GRAFT be effectively extended to model multiple distinct event types simultaneously?
- **Sensitivity to informative censoring:** How robust is the stochastic imputation to violations of conditional independent censoring (Y ⊥⊥ C | X)?
- **Neighborhood parameter sensitivity:** How do choices of k (neighbors) and distance metric affect accuracy in high-dimensional/noisy spaces?
- **Suboptimality of decoupled training:** Does post-hoc CoxPH calibration introduce a gap vs. joint optimization of ranking and calibration?

## Limitations
- Under extreme censoring (≥92%), GRAFT's local KM imputation becomes unreliable, and simpler models like CoxPH outperform it.
- The stochastic gate mechanism may discard weak true signals if all features are relevant, and performance depends on careful tuning of the sparsity strength \( \lambda_{\text{L0}} \).
- The post-hoc CoxPH calibration assumes the baseline hazard is independent of covariates; violations can harm calibration even with good discrimination.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid architecture:** Medium. Evidence from experiments and ablations is strong, but the approach may not help if the true relationship is purely linear or requires multiplicative interactions.
- **Stochastic gates:** Medium. STG is established in other domains, but its survival application is novel; risk of discarding weak signals exists.
- **Decoupled training:** Medium. Novel and empirically effective, but acknowledged to fail under extreme censoring.
- **Robustness claims:** Higher. Supported by comprehensive noise experiments, though limited external validation (low citation/FMR) reduces generalizability confidence.
