# EvidenceRL: Reinforcing Evidence Consistency for Trustworthy Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19532
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19532
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: EvidenceRL improves evidence grounding (e.g., Gmax@3 from 47.6 to 78.2 on cardiac diagnosis) and reduces hallucinations nearly 5×, while maintaining or improving task accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often produce plausible but unsupported outputs in high-stakes domains like medicine and law. EvidenceRL introduces a reinforcement learning framework that directly optimizes for evidence grounding during training. It uses a Focus-Then-Verify NLI reward to assess sentence-level entailment against retrieved documents and a correctness reward, combined via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). On cardiac diagnosis, the method raises F1@3 from 37.0 to 54.5 and grounding (Gmax@3) from 47.6 to 78.2; on legal reasoning, Faithfulness jumps from 32.8% to 67.6%. The approach consistently increases evidence-supported predictions without sacrificing accuracy.

## Method Summary
EvidenceRL trains an LLM policy with LoRA adapters using reinforcement learning. For each input, it samples multiple completions and computes three rewards: format (JSON validity), correctness (domain-specific accuracy proxy), and grounding (sentence-level entailment against retrieved evidence). The grounding reward employs a Focus-Then-Verify mechanism that splits reasoning into sentences, pairs each with focused evidence chunks, scores entailment with a frozen NLI cross-encoder, and aggregates the maximum signed score. These rewards are weighted (format:1, correctness:1, grounding:2) and normalized within the completion group to compute advantages. The policy is updated with a clipped PPO-style objective and a KL penalty to a frozen reference model, using GRPO. This directly steers the model to produce outputs that are both accurate and evidence-grounded.

## Key Results
- **Cardiac diagnosis (Llama-3.2-3B)**: F1@3 improves from 37.0 to 54.5; grounding (Gmax@3) from 47.6 to 78.2; hallucinations drop nearly 5×.
- **Legal reasoning (Llama-3.1-8B)**: Faithfulness increases from 32.8% to 67.6%.
- Gains hold across model families, with consistent improvements in evidence-supported predictions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Focus-Then-Verify NLI Reward
- **Claim**: Sentence-level entailment scoring against focused evidence premises reduces signal dilution and provides a more reliable grounding reward than document-level NLI.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of scoring entire reasoning against a concatenated evidence set, the method constructs focused premise–hypothesis pairs by combining an anchor context (e.g., chief complaint) with individual evidence sections. Each pair is scored by a frozen NLI cross-encoder. The grounding reward aggregates the maximum absolute score (with sign) across all premises, capturing the strongest supporting or contradicting evidence.
- **Core assumption**: Individual reasoning sentences are best verified against specific, focused evidence chunks rather than a long, noisy document concatenation.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“Focus-Then–Verify reward architecture”); Section 3.2 (formalizes premise construction, NLI scoring, aggregation); Corpus comparison (distinctive fine-grained approach vs. neighbors like “When Evidence Contradicts”).
- **Break condition**: If the NLI model is not domain-adapted (e.g., using a general NLI model for medical text), entailment scores become noisy, degrading the reward signal.

### Mechanism 2: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
- **Claim**: GRPO stabilizes on-policy learning by normalizing rewards within a group of completions, enabling the model to improve outputs relative to its current behavior without relying on offline preference data.
- **Mechanism**: For each input, sample G completions from the current policy. Compute rewards (format, correctness, grounding). Normalize each reward by the group mean and standard deviation to obtain advantages. Update the policy with a clipped PPO-style objective and a KL penalty to a frozen reference policy. This comparative approach ensures that improvements are meaningful relative to the model’s own baseline.
- **Core assumption**: Within-group ranking is a reliable and stable signal for policy gradient estimation, even if absolute reward scales are unknown.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“optimizes the generator using Group Relative Policy Optimization”); Section 3.3 (defines group sampling, advantage normalization, loss); Corpus comparison (distinct from “TruthRL” and other RL methods).
- **Break condition**: If group size G is too small (e.g., 2), normalization becomes high-variance; if too large, memory and compute costs rise excessively.

### Mechanism 3: Reward Decomposition and Weighting
- **Claim**: Decomposing the reward into format, correctness, and grounding—with a higher weight on grounding (w_g=2)—steers the model to prioritize evidence adherence without sacrificing task accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: The total reward is a linear combination: r = w_f·r_f + w_c·r_c + w_g·r_g. r_f enforces valid JSON output. r_c uses a domain-specific correctness proxy: for medical, BioLORD embedding similarity (τ=0.80); for legal, exact match. r_g is the normalized Focus-Then-Verify grounding score. The weighting emphasizes grounding as the primary objective.
- **Core assumption**: These components are sufficiently orthogonal and their weighted sum can balance the competing demands of structure, accuracy, and factuality.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“scores candidate responses for grounding and correctness”); Section 3.2 (equations define each reward and fusion); Section 5.5 (ablation shows adding r_g to r_c+r_f improves grounding with minor accuracy trade-off).
- **Break condition**: If the correctness proxy is miscalibrated (e.g., embedding similarity fails for certain diagnoses), the model may overfit to the proxy. The authors validated high precision (98%) but recall was limited (58%), potentially under-rewarding near-correct answers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**
  - **Why needed here**: EvidenceRL extends RAG by making evidence use a training objective. Understanding RAG’s core limitation—models ignore retrieved context (context-memory conflict)—is essential.
  - **Quick check**: What is “context-memory conflict” in RAG? (Answer: The model’s parametric knowledge competes with retrieved evidence, often leading to outputs that disregard the provided context.)

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its misalignment with factuality**
  - **Why needed**: EvidenceRL replaces human feedback with automated rewards. Standard RLHF rewards plausibility and fluency, which can incentivize ungrounded answers; EvidenceRL’s rewards directly target evidence adherence.
  - **Quick check**: Why might human preferences not align with evidence grounding? (Answer: Human labelers often prefer confident, coherent answers without verifying whether they are supported by the given evidence.)

- **Concept: Natural Language Inference (NLI) for factual consistency**
  - **Why needed**: The grounding reward uses NLI to check if reasoning sentences are entailed by evidence. Familiarity with NLI (entailment, contradiction, neutral) is required to understand how entailment scores quantify grounding.
  - **Quick check**: How does sentence-level NLI differ from document-level NLI in this paper? (Answer: Sentence-level NLI avoids signal dilution by checking each reasoning sentence against focused evidence chunks, rather than a noisy concatenation of all documents.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input processor -> Policy network (LLM with LoRA adapters) -> Reward modules (format validator, correctness scorer, grounding scorer) -> GRPO trainer -> Reference model (frozen)

### Critical path
1. For each batch, generate G completions from the current policy (temperature 0.7–1.0).
2. Parse completions; invalid JSON gets format reward 0 (all completions still scored for other rewards).
3. For each valid completion, extract reasoning and predictions.
4. Compute correctness reward r_c: medical compares predicted diagnoses to ground truth via BioLORD similarity (τ=0.80); legal uses exact match.
5. Compute grounding reward r_g: for each reasoning sentence, create premises with each evidence section, run domain NLI, take max signed score across premises, then average across top-k predictions.
6. Combine: r = 1·r_f + 1·r_c + 2·r_g.
7. Normalize each r within the group: advantage = (r − μ_G) / σ_G.
8. Compute GRPO loss: L = −mean(min(ratio·advantage, clip(ratio,1−ε,1+ε)·advantage)) + β·KL(π_θ || π_ref).
9. Backpropagate and update LoRA parameters.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Reward weights**: w_g=2 emphasizes grounding; may need tuning for other domains.
- **NLI model**: Domain-specific (medical) vs. general (legal). Using a general model may limit performance; consider fine-tuning if domain data exists.
- **Group size G**: Larger G stabilizes advantages but increases memory/time; G=8 is a good default.
- **Temperature**: Higher temperature (e.g., 1.0 for Gemma) boosts exploration but may increase invalid outputs; tune per model family.
- **Sequence length**: Prompt length (6,144) must fit query and evidence; completion length (4,096) must accommodate reasoning and predictions. Adjust for longer contexts.

### Failure signatures
- **Grounding collapse**: Gmax@3 near zero despite moderate accuracy (as in SFT). Likely cause: grounding reward weight too low or NLI model not domain-adapted.
- **Accuracy degradation**: If correctness proxy is misaligned (e.g., τ too strict), model ignores correctness. Monitor correctness reward trends.
- **Format failures**: High rate of invalid JSON. Ensure format reward is active and prompt instructions are clear (e.g., “Begin your response IMMEDIATELY with {”).
- **Reward hacking**: Model generates text that manipulates NLI scores (e.g., copying phrases from evidence). Detect by evaluating with a different NLI model or human annotation.
- **KL divergence issues**: β too low → policy drifts; too high → learning stalls. β=0.04 worked; adjust based on KL monitoring.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Reward sanity check**: On a small held-out set (20 examples), manually inspect outputs and compute each reward. Confirm format reward distinguishes valid/invalid JSON; correctness reward correlates with true correctness (via LLM judge or ground truth); grounding reward distinguishes evidence-based vs. unsupported reasoning (via human or strong NLI).
2. **GRPO stability pilot**: Train for 5 steps on 10 examples with G=2,4,8. Plot group reward distributions and policy loss. Expect advantages to center near zero and loss to decrease steadily. If loss spikes, reduce learning rate or increase β.
3. **End-to-end mini-reproduction**: Train EvidenceRL on 100 medical cases (1 epoch, Llama-3.2-3B). Evaluate on 50 held-out cases. Compare zero-shot, SFT, and EvidenceRL on F1@3 and Gmax@3. Expect improvements in both metrics. If not, debug reward computation pipeline and LoRA setup.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other domains**: How does EvidenceRL generalize to other high-stakes domains and evidence sources beyond cardiac diagnosis and legal reasoning? The authors explicitly state that evaluating across additional domains remains an important future direction.
2. **Within-model preference optimization**: Can within-model grounding-only preference optimization achieve similar improvements as EvidenceRL without the confounding effect of cross-model distillation? The authors note that fDPO uses preference pairs from different models, and a within-model variant would be a cleaner ablation.
3. **Robustness to noisy evidence**: How robust is EvidenceRL to noisy, irrelevant, or contradictory retrieved evidence? The paper assumes retrieved evidence is reliable, but real-world retrieval often yields imperfect passages; this scenario is not tested.
4. **Model family variation**: Why do different model families (e.g., Llama vs. Gemma) exhibit varying degrees of improvement under EvidenceRL? The paper observes larger gains for Llama models but does not analyze the underlying architectural or pretraining differences.

## Limitations
- **Retrieval corpus accessibility**: The cardiology chunk corpus and BarExam legal passage corpus may not be publicly available, blocking full reproduction.
- **Token‑aware truncation underspecification**: The legal NLI implementation’s premise truncation logic (to preserve the hypothesis) is not detailed, risking inconsistent grounding computation.
- **Correctness proxy calibration**: BioLORD embedding similarity threshold τ=0.80 yields high precision (98%) but low recall (58%), possibly under-rewarding near‑correct diagnoses.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Focus-Then-Verify)**: Medium – Strong theoretical and experimental support, but hinges on using a domain‑adapted NLI model; break condition not fully excluded.
- **Mechanism 2 (GRPO)**: High – Advantage normalization and within‑group ranking are well‑defined and consistently yield stable learning across model families.
- **Mechanism 3 (Reward decomposition)**: Medium – Ablations confirm benefit, yet reliance on a fixed τ and susceptibility to reward hacking leave residual doubt.

## Next Checks
