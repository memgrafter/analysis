# NoLan: Mitigating Object Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models via Dynamic Suppression of Language Priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22144
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22144
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** NoLan, a training-free decoding framework, reduces object hallucinations in LVLMs by dynamically suppressing language decoder priors, achieving up to 6.45 point improvement for LLaVA-1.5 7B and 7.21 points for Qwen-VL 7B on POPE without additional training.

## Executive Summary
Object hallucinations—where large vision-language models (LVLMs) generate non-existent objects—are a critical flaw. This paper identifies the language decoder’s strong priors, not vision encoder failures, as the primary cause. To mitigate this, the authors propose NoLan, a training-free decoding method that contrasts output distributions from multimodal (image+text) and text-only inputs. By computing modulation logits based on the difference between these distributions and scaling them with a constant or dynamically derived factor (using symmetric KL divergence), NoLan suppresses tokens favored by language priors but unsupported by the image. Evaluations on POPE, MME, and other benchmarks show consistent accuracy gains across multiple LVLM architectures, outperforming prior contrastive methods like VCD without any model retraining or external tools.

## Method Summary
NoLan operates at inference time by altering the decoding process of an existing LVLM. For each token generation step, it performs two forward passes through the shared language decoder: one with the multimodal input (image and text) yielding logits \(l_m\), and one with text-only input (same text, no image) yielding logits \(l_u\). The modulation logits are computed as \(l_\Delta = \alpha \times (l_m - l_u)\). The final next-token distribution is derived from \(\text{softmax}(l_m + l_\Delta)\). The scaling factor \(\alpha\) can be fixed (NoLan-Base, \(\alpha=1\)) or dynamic (NoLan-Plus). In NoLan-Plus, \(\alpha\) is adjusted per token based on the symmetric KL divergence \(\gamma\) between the output probability distributions \(p_m\) and \(p_u\): \(\alpha = \beta \times (\tanh(1/\gamma) + 1)\). A smaller \(\gamma\) indicates higher reliance on language priors, triggering stronger suppression. This contrastive approach requires no model modifications or training and can be plugged into any LVLM with a separate vision encoder and autoregressive decoder.

## Key Results
- **Improved accuracy on POPE:** Up to +6.45 points for LLaVA-1.5 7B and +7.21 points for Qwen-VL 7B, surpassing VCD.
- **Consistent gains across models and benchmarks:** Benefits observed for InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL on MME (attribute/object hallucination reduction) and MM-Vet/LLaVA-Bench (open-ended tasks).
- **Training-free and efficient:** NoLan matches or exceeds VCD’s performance with lower computational overhead, as it avoids image distortion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Language decoder priors are the primary cause of hallucinations.** Vision encoders (e.g., CLIP-ViT) robustly detect object presence; hallucinations arise when the autoregressive language decoder’s strong priors override visual signals during generation. This is evidenced by significantly lower KL divergence between multimodal (\(p_m\)) and text-only (\(p_u\)) output distributions for hallucinated responses (0.46) versus non-hallucinated (1.20). The core assumption is that the vision encoder provides accurate grounding, and failures occur when the decoder’s bias dominates, especially for ambiguous or low-salience objects.

**Mechanism 2: Contrasting multimodal and text-only distributions suppresses priors.** NoLan computes modulation logits \(l_\Delta = \alpha(l_m - l_u)\), which are added to the multimodal logits before sampling. This reduces confidence in tokens that are favored by the language prior (\(p_u\)) but lack visual support (\(p_m\)). The text-only forward pass isolates the decoder’s inherent next-token bias, approximating the hallucinatory prior. The effectiveness comes from directly counteracting the prior’s influence at the logit level.

**Mechanism 3: Token-specific dynamic modulation (NoLan-Plus) adapts suppression to local risk.** The symmetric KL divergence \(\gamma = \frac{1}{2}(D_{KL}(p_m||p_u) + D_{KL}(p_u||p_m))\) measures per-token distributional overlap. A smaller \(\gamma\) indicates higher reliance on language priors. NoLan-Plus sets \(\alpha = \beta \times (\tanh(1/\gamma) + 1)\), increasing suppression for tokens with high prior dominance. This adaptivity improves over the fixed α in NoLan-Base by avoiding both under- and over-suppression across different generation steps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional mutual information \(I(y; v|x)\):** Formalizes the dependency of the response \(y\) on the visual input \(v\) given text \(x\). Lower \(I\) indicates stronger hallucination (output independent of vision). *Why needed:* Provides a probabilistic lens to quantify hallucination severity; Appendix links \(I\) to expected KL divergence. *Quick check:* How is \(I(y;v|x)\) related to KL divergence between \(p_m\) and \(p_u\)? Answer: \(I(y;v|x) = \mathbb{E}_{v|x}[D_{KL}(p_m||p_u)]\).
- **KL divergence as a measure of distributional shift:** Quantifies how much the multimodal output distribution \(p_m\) departs from the text-only prior \(p_u\). Smaller values indicate greater influence of language priors. *Why needed:* Core metric for identifying hallucination-prone tokens and driving dynamic modulation. *Quick check:* Why use symmetric KL (\(\gamma\)) instead of standard KL in NoLan-Plus? Answer: To capture similarity regardless of direction, improving generalization across diverse tokens and models.
- **Contrastive decoding principle:** Isolates visual contributions by comparing model outputs conditioned vs. not conditioned on vision. *Why needed:* Foundation of NoLan’s modulation; by contrasting \(p_m\) and \(p_u\), it suppresses tokens driven by non-visual priors. *Quick check:* How does NoLan differ from VCD? Answer: VCD distorts the image input; NoLan uses a clean image and text-only branch, avoiding visual noise and providing a cleaner contrastive signal.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Vision Encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT) -> LLM Decoder (shared) -> Contrastive Module (computes \(l_m\), \(l_u\), then \(l_\Delta\)) -> Sampling Head (applies \(\text{softmax}(l_m + l_\Delta)\)).

**Critical path:**  
1. **Analysis:** Validate language prior hypothesis via KL divergence on hallucination vs. non-hallucination subsets (Section 3.1, Table 2).  
2. **Design:** Formulate modulation with constant (\(\alpha\)) or dynamic (\(\alpha\) from \(\gamma\)) rates (Section 3.2, Eqs. 3–8).  
3. **Integration:** Implement as a plug-in module during decoding, requiring no training (Algorithm 1).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **NoLan-Base** (fixed \(\alpha=1\)): Simpler, faster, less adaptive; suitable when overhead must be minimized.  
- **NoLan-Plus** (dynamic \(\alpha\)): Better F1 on POPE (Table 4) but adds per-token KL computation overhead (Table 22).  
- **Efficiency:** Requires one extra text-only forward pass per token; memory similar to VCD but faster because it avoids image perturbation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Over-suppression:** Excessive \(\alpha\) attenuates valid tokens, reducing recall. Diagnose by monitoring precision-recall trade-off; high precision but low recall indicates over-suppression.  
- **Under-suppression:** Hallucinations persist if \(\gamma\) fails to spike for risky tokens; inspect token-wise KL values.  
- **Distribution mismatch:** If instruction tuning alters the text-only prior \(p_u\) significantly, the contrastive signal misaligns. Verify by comparing top-k tokens from \(l_m\) and \(l_u\); high similarity suggests contamination.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate Table 2:** On a held-out POPE split, compute KL(\(p_m||p_u\)) for correct vs. hallucinated responses; confirm lower KL for hallucinations (~0.46 vs. 1.20).  
2. **Ablate \(\alpha\) (Table 8):** Sweep \(\alpha \in \{0,1,2,3\}\) on POPE random; identify best constant for your LVLM and observe accuracy/F1 trends.  
3. **Compare Base vs. Plus (Table 3):** Run both variants on POPE (MSCOCO) with identical settings; verify dynamic modulation yields higher F1 without harming precision.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Which component primarily causes hallucinations?** The paper attributes hallucinations to language decoder priors based on experiments with LLaVA, InstructBLIP, and Qwen-VL. However, this may not hold for LVLMs with different fusion strategies (e.g., encoder-decoder) or for other hallucination types (attributes, relations). Resolving requires testing diverse architectures and probing internal representations across hallucination categories.
- **Is symmetric KL the best metric for dynamic modulation?** The choice of symmetric KL divergence \(\gamma\) is heuristic; alternatives (e.g., Jensen-Shannon, Wasserstein) or learned measures might better distinguish hallucination risk. The transformation \(\tanh(1/\gamma)\) and scaling factor \(\beta\) are tuned empirically without theoretical justification. Resolving requires extensive ablation of divergence measures and theoretical analysis linking divergence to hallucination probability.
- **Does suppression degrade reasoning performance?** NoLan improves object hallucination metrics but may over-suppress useful linguistic priors, harming reasoning-heavy tasks. MM-Vet results show mixed subcategory performance (e.g., spatial reasoning sometimes decreases). Resolving requires evaluation on broad reasoning benchmarks (science QA, math) and constructing a Pareto frontier of hallucination reduction vs. reasoning accuracy.
- **How does NoLan perform in multi-turn conversations?** All evaluations are single-turn; multi-turn contexts with history could alter the KL divergence signal and suppression dynamics. The text-only prior might incorporate previous outputs, amplifying errors. Resolving requires multi-turn hallucination benchmarks and analysis of KL evolution across dialogue rounds.
- **Can computational overhead be reduced?** NoLan’s two forward passes per token may be prohibitive for real-time use. The paper does not explore optimizations like caching, selective application, or distillation of the prior. Resolving requires developing lightweight variants and measuring speed-accuracy trade-offs across hardware.

## Limitations
- The text-only prior \(p_u\) may be contaminated by instruction tuning, weakening the contrastive signal for some models.
- Per-token symmetric KL \(\gamma\) is noisy, especially early in generation, potentially causing unstable \(\alpha\) and over-suppression.
- Evaluations focus on POPE’s binary object presence; not tested on attribute/relationship hallucinations or diverse open-ended generation scenarios.
- Decoding overhead (extra forward pass) could be problematic for high-resolution images or long contexts, though faster than VCD.
- No exploration of combining NoLan with training-time mitigation (e.g., fine-tuning), which might yield synergistic gains.

## Confidence
- **Language priors cause hallucinations:** Medium. Strong correlation via KL divergence (Table 2), but no causal ablation of the decoder; may not generalize to all architectures.
- **Contrastive decoding (NoLan-Base) helps:** High. Reproducible improvements across models (LLaVA, Qwen-VL) and benchmarks (POPE, MME) inTables 3–5.
- **Dynamic modulation (NoLan-Plus) further improves:** Medium. Gains depend on stable \(\gamma\); sensitive to \(\beta\) (Table 9) and yields mixed results on reasoning tasks (MM-Vet).
