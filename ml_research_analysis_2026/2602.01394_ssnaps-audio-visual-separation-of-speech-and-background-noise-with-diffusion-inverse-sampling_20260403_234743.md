# SSNAPS: Audio-Visual Separation of Speech and Background Noise with Diffusion Inverse Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.01394
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01394
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Unsupervised audio‑visual speech separation that achieves lower word error rate than supervised baselines for 1–3 speakers plus noise, extends to off‑screen speakers via a crosstalk‑minimization loss, and yields high‑fidelity noise estimates that improve acoustic scene detection.

## Executive Summary
SSNAPS tackles single‑microphone speech separation and enhancement in noisy environments using synchronized lip videos. It is fully unsupervised: diffusion priors for clean speech and ambient noise are trained on clean data only, with no exposure to mixtures. The method factorizes the posterior distribution over sources under a statistical independence assumption and employs a reformulated Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (DAPS) algorithm to sample from it. Visual guidance for each speaker is injected via classifier‑free guidance in the speech diffusion model, and an additional crosstalk loss handles off‑screen speakers. Evaluations show that SSNAPS outperforms top supervised baselines in word error rate across mixtures containing 1, 2, or 3 speakers plus noise. Moreover, the separated noise component consistently improves acoustic scene classification accuracy compared to the raw mixture. These results demonstrate the power of diffusion‑based generative modeling for flexible, high‑quality audio‑visual source separation.

## Method Summary
SSNAPS formulates the task as Bayesian inference: given a single‑channel mixture \(y\) and lip videos \(\{V_i\}\) for visible speakers, recover clean speech signals \(\{x_i\}\) and ambient noise \(n\). The posterior is factorized as \(p(\{x_i\}, n \mid y) \propto p(y \mid \sum_i x_i + n)\, p(x_i)\, p(n)\) assuming independence. The priors \(p(x_i)\) and \(p(n)\) are provided by two pre‑trained diffusion models: a speech denoiser \(F_\theta\) conditioned on visual features (from a BRAVEn encoder) via classifier‑free guidance, and an unconditional noise denoiser \(G_\phi\). Both operate in the time domain and use an STFT front‑end for likelihood calculations.

Inference uses a reformulated Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (DAPS) loop. Starting from a Gaussian sample at the highest diffusion scale \(\tau = T_{\text{max}}\), for each decreasing \(\tau\): (a) obtain unconditional estimates \(\hat{x}_i^0\) and \(\hat{n}^0\) by running a few Euler steps backward through the respective diffusion ODEs; (b) perform coupled Langevin updates where each component is drawn toward its prior (gradient from \((\text{component} - \hat{\text{component}})/\tau^2\)) while also minimizing the STFT magnitude reconstruction error \(\|S(y) - S(\sum_i x_i + n)\|^2\) scaled by \(\alpha^{-2}\), with added Gaussian noise; (c) re‑anneal by sampling \(h_{\tau-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(h_0^{(N_{\text{MC}})}, \sigma_{\tau-1}^2 I)\). For an off‑screen speaker, a crosstalk loss (spectral cosine similarity) is incorporated into the reconstruction term for low \(\tau\). The final outputs are the separated speech waveforms \(x_i^0\) and noise \(n^0\).

## Key Results
- SSNAPS achieves lower word error rate (WER) than top supervised baselines for mixtures of 1, 2, and 3 speakers plus noise, despite never having seen noisy mixtures during training.
- The method naturally extends to off‑screen speakers by adding a crosstalk‑minimization loss, significantly improving WER for the unguided speaker (e.g., from 55.7 to 38.4 in one condition).
- The separated noise component yields higher acoustic scene classification accuracy than the raw mixture, demonstrating high‑fidelity noise estimation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Independent Priors and Factorized Posterior
- **Claim:** SSNAPS separates speech and noise by modeling them with independent diffusion priors and jointly sampling from their posterior given the mixture.
- **Mechanism:** The joint posterior \(p(c, n \mid y)\) is factorized as \(p(y \mid c, n)\, p(c)\, p(n)\) under an independence assumption. Two diffusion models provide prior score functions. Langevin dynamics samples \(h_0\) given \(h_\tau\) and \(y\) using gradients from the prior scores and a reconstruction likelihood \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}} = \| S(y) - S(\sum_i x_i^{(j)}_0 + n^{(j)}_0) \|^2\) in the STFT magnitude domain. After \(N_{\text{MC}}\) updates, noise perturbation yields \(h_{\tau-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(h^{(N_{\text{MC}})}_0, \sigma^2_{\tau-1} I)\).
- **Core assumption:** Speech sources and ambient noise are statistically independent, and their distributions are accurately captured by separate diffusion models trained on clean data only.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “We model clean speech and ambient noise with dedicated diffusion priors…”; Section 4 gives equations (11)–(17); prior work by the same authors (arxiv:2509.14379) also uses dual priors.
- **Break condition:** If independence is violated or either prior is mis‑specified (e.g., noise test distribution mismatched), the factorization fails and the reconstruction gradient may not correctly disentangle sources, leading to performance drops (as observed in Table 2 unmatched noise experiments).

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling for Multiple Signals
- **Claim:** The DAPS sampler avoids the “trapping” problem of standard diffusion inverse samplers and enables global adjustments during sampling.
- **Mechanism:** DAPS decouples two successive diffusion steps: given \(h_\tau \sim p(h_\tau \mid y)\), first draw \(h_0 \sim p(h_0 \mid h_\tau, y)\) via Langevin, then set \(h_{\tau-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(h_0, \sigma^2_{\tau-1} I)\). SSNAPS adapts this to a multi‑signal setting by running separate ODE solvers for each speech/noise component (computing \(\hat{x}_i^0(\hat{x}_i^\tau)\) and \(\hat{n}^0(n_\tau)\)), while the Langevin updates are coupled only through the shared \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}\).
- **Core assumption:** The conditional independence property \(p(h_{\tau_2} \mid h_{\tau_1}, y) \approx p(h_{\tau_2} \mid h_0, y)\) holds for staged sampling, and the noise schedule \(\sigma_\tau\) ensures \(p(h_{T_{\text{max}}} \mid y) \approx \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{T_{\text{max}}} I)\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “We reformulate DAPS to address our scenario”; Section 2.2 provides theoretical justification; Section 4 shows parallel ODE solvers (14a,b).
- **Break condition:** Too aggressive annealing (large \(\Delta\sigma\)) or too few steps (\(T_{\text{max}}\)) reduce flexibility; 3‑speaker mixtures required more annealing steps (\(N_A=400\)) than fewer speakers.

### Mechanism 3: Audio‑Visual Guidance via Classifier‑Free Guidance
- **Claim:** Audio‑visual guidance helps disambiguate overlapping speech and suppress noise, particularly when speakers have similar acoustics.
- **Mechanism:** The speech diffusion denoiser \(F_\theta\) is trained with classifier‑free guidance: at training, visual features \(V_i\) are sometimes replaced by a null token \(\emptyset\). At inference, the conditional score is \(s_\theta(x^i_\tau, \tau, V_i) = (1+\omega)s_{\theta,\text{cond}} - \omega s_{\theta,\text{uncond}}\), where \(\omega\) is the guidance scale.
- **Core assumption:** Lip movements provide a strong, temporally‑aligned cue orthogonal to the acoustic mixture; the BRAVEn encoder produces features correlated with speech content.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract assumes lip‑region videos; Section 4 implements the audio‑visual score; Section 6.3 shows the off‑screen ablation: removing visual cues (or not using the crosstalk loss) dramatically increases WER for the off‑screen speaker (e.g., 27.3 → 55.7).
- **Break condition:** Poor video quality (low resolution, occlusion) or an off‑screen speaker without the crosstalk loss lead to severe degradation; the crosstalk loss mitigates this but requires careful tuning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion models (score‑based)**
  - *Why needed:* SSNAPS uses pre‑trained diffusion models as priors for clean speech and ambient noise. Understanding the forward/backward diffusion process, the denoising objective, and Tweedie’s formula is essential to grasp how prior scores are derived.
  - *Quick check:* Given a diffusion denoiser \(D_\theta(x_\tau, \tau)\), how is the score function \(\nabla_{x_\tau} \log p(x_\tau)\) approximated, and what assumptions does this rely on?

- **Inverse problem sampling with diffusion (DPS/DAPS)**
  - *Why needed:* The core algorithm reformulates DAPS to perform posterior sampling. One must understand the decoupled two‑step process (sample \(h_0 \mid h_\tau, y\) then \(h_{\tau-1} \mid h_0\)) and why it improves over directly modifying the reverse SDE (as in DPS).
  - *Quick check:* What is the key difference between DPS (Chung et al., 2023) and DAPS (Zhang et al., 2025) in terms of how the diffusion trajectory is updated at each step?

- **Classifier‑free guidance for conditional generation**
  - *Why needed:* The speech prior is conditioned on visual features via classifier‑free guidance, allowing conditioning at inference without retraining.
  - *Quick check:* If a diffusion model is trained with classifier‑free guidance, how do you combine the conditional and unconditional denoisers at inference to control the guidance strength \(\omega\)?

- **Audio‑visual speech separation**
  - *Why needed:* The problem domain involves using lip video to separate speech from noise and other speakers. Knowledge of why visual cues help (e.g., lip movements are correlated with speech content and unaffected by acoustic interference) is necessary to appreciate the setup.
  - *Quick check:* In a multi‑speaker mixture, how does visual information for each speaker help the separation algorithm avoid crosstalk between sources?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input: single‑channel audio mixture \(y\), lip videos \(\{V_i\}\) for each visible speaker.
- BRAVEn encoder converts each video to a feature vector \(V_i\).
- Two pre‑trained diffusion priors: speech denoiser \(F_\theta\) (time‑domain NCSN++M U‑Net with FiLM conditioning on \(V_i\)) and noise denoiser \(G_\phi\) (unconditional). Both use an STFT front‑end.
- DAPS sampler:
  * Initialize \(h_{T_{\text{max}}}\) randomly.
  * For \(\tau\) from \(T_{\text{max}}\) down to \(T_{\text{min}}\):
    a. Run ODE solvers (Euler steps) on each source: for speech \(i\), use \(F_\theta\) to estimate \(\hat{x}_i^0\); for noise, use \(G_\phi\) to estimate \(\hat{n}^0\).
    b. Set \(h_0 = [\hat{x}_1^0, ..., \hat{x}_K^0, \hat{n}^0]\). Perform \(N_{\text{MC}}\) Langevin iterations: each component is updated using gradient \((\text{component} - \hat{\text{component}})/\tau^2\) plus \(\alpha^{-2} \nabla_{\text{component}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}\) where \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}} = \|S(y) - S(\sum_i x_i + n)\|^2\); add stochastic noise.
    c. Sample next state \(h_{\tau-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(h_0^{(N_{\text{MC}})}, \sigma_{\tau-1}^2 I)\).
  * If an off‑screen speaker exists, augment \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}\) with a crosstalk loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{ct-ss}}\) (spectral cosine similarity) for \(\tau < \sigma_{\text{os}}\).
- Output: separated speech waveforms \(\{x_i^0\}\) and noise \(n^0\).

**Critical path:** The main loop iterates over annealing steps. For each \(\tau\): (1) ODE solvers provide unconditional source estimates; (2) coupled Langevin dynamics refine these estimates using reconstruction loss; (3) noise injection produces the next diffusion state. The decoupling allows large effective steps and avoids trapping.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Sampling budget vs. quality:** More annealing steps \(N_A\) and Langevin iterations \(N_{\text{MC}}\) improve WER/SI‑SDR but increase runtime (e.g., 3‑speaker case uses \(N_A=400\), \(N_{\text{MC}}=100\)).
- **Guidance scale \(\omega\):** Higher \(\omega\) enforces visual consistency but risks overfitting; typically 0.5–0.8.
- **Likelihood scaling \(\alpha\):** Balances mixture fidelity vs. prior; too high causes crosstalk, too low underutilizes mixture; tuned per condition (e.g., 0.0005 or 0.001 for DNS).
- **Off‑screen crosstalk loss:** Requires tuning weight \(g_{\text{ct-ss}}\) and threshold \(\sigma_{\text{os}}\); without it, off‑screen WER degrades markedly.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Visual degradation:** Missing/poor lip videos or off‑screen speakers without crosstalk loss cause high WER (off‑screen WER ~55.7 without loss vs. 38.4 with).
- **Noise prior mismatch:** Testing on noise from a different distribution (e.g., DCASE) reduces SI‑SDR and WER compared to matched DNS noise.
- **Insufficient sampling:** Reducing \(N_A\) or \(N_{\text{MC}}\) harms performance, especially for 3‑speaker mixtures.
- **Residual crosstalk:** Even with visual guidance, acoustically similar speakers (same gender) may exhibit leakage; off‑screen cases worsen this.

**First 3 experiments (reproduction priority):**
1. **Single‑speaker + noise (DNS):** Validate basic enhancement. Expect WER ~11.3 (vs. mixture 13.3) and SI‑SDR ~11.8.
2. **Two‑speaker + noise (DNS):** Test two on‑screen speakers. Expect WER ~19.2, beating supervised baselines (RAVSS 24.1, FlowAVSE 30.2).
3. **Off‑screen two‑speaker + noise:** Introduce one speaker without video. Compare with/without crosstalk loss; expect off‑screen WER to drop from ~55.7 (no loss) to ~38.4 (with loss) while on‑screen remains stable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can SSNAPS be extended to handle scenarios with two or more speakers lacking visual guidance, where ambiguity in source attribution becomes significant?
2. Beyond speech separation and enhancement, what other speech processing tasks (e.g., speech recognition, speaker diarization, voice conversion) can benefit from SSNAPS's unsupervised generative framework?
3. How does SSNAPS's performance scale when separating mixtures with more than three speakers, given the claimed ability to handle arbitrary numbers of speakers?
4. Can the sampling efficiency of SSNAPS be improved by orders of magnitude while maintaining its performance advantage over supervised methods?
5. How robust is SSNAPS to violations of the statistical independence assumption between speech sources, noise, and auxiliary white noise, particularly in real‑world environments with correlated or non‑Gaussian background sounds?

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Key hyperparameters (diffusion training settings, BRAVEn specifics, exact STFT configuration) are not fully specified, hampering exact replication.
- **Dependence on high‑quality lip videos:** Poor video quality or missing visual data (without crosstalk loss) leads to significant performance degradation.
- **High inference cost:** The algorithm requires up to 400 annealing steps and 100 Langevin iterations, making real‑time deployment challenging.
