# A Blueprint for Self-Evolving Coding Agents in Vehicle Aerodynamic Drag Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21698
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21698
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Best system achieves Combined Score 0.9335 (sign-accuracy 0.9180) with adaptive sampling and island migration driving convergence.

## Executive Summary
Industrial vehicle aerodynamic drag prediction is bottlenecked by workflow friction such as CAD cleanup and meshing retries. This paper proposes a contract-centric framework of self-evolving coding agents that automatically discover and optimize executable surrogate pipelines. The system employs an island evolution model with structured mutations and multi-objective selection under strict reproducibility and leakage contracts. The best surrogate reaches a Combined Score of 0.9335 with sign accuracy 0.9180, and ablation studies confirm that adaptive sampling and island migration are critical for convergence. Deployment uses a "screen-and-escalate" strategy: surrogates rank designs, and uncertain or out-of-distribution cases are escalated to CFD, enabling high-throughput ranking while preserving decision-grade reliability and auditability.

## Method Summary
The task is to discover executable surrogate pipelines for predicting vehicle drag coefficient (Cd) that satisfy industrial constraints on reliability, safety, and reproducibility. Inputs consist of geometric descriptors (3D meshes/point clouds) paired with high-fidelity CFD labels, governed by a data contract that mandates versioning, leakage-free features, and hard splits by vehicle family/topology to prevent temporal/spatial leakage. The primary objective metric is the Combined Score, a weighted sum of sign accuracy (direction of Cd change), inverse RMSE, and inverse MAE, supplemented by secondary fitness criteria: reliability (low seed variance), model complexity, and strict adherence to contract (no leakage, resource limits, determinism). The core method is an evolutionary loop over executable programs (genome = configuration + code). It uses an island model with migration, structured mutations (altering data preprocessing, model architecture, loss, or split policies), and multi-objective selection akin to NSGA-II. Each candidate is evaluated by a harness that enforces multi-seed and multi-fold validation, reproducibility checks, and resource budgets. Adaptive sampling and LLM-driven error reflection guide the search. The final deployment employs a "screen-and-escalate" workflow: surrogates rank new designs, and high-uncertainty or out-of-distribution cases are escalated to CFD, balancing throughput with reliability.

## Key Results
- Best Combined Score reaches 0.9335 with sign accuracy 0.9180.
- Ablation shows adaptive sampling and island migration significantly improve convergence.
- Screen-and-escalate deployment achieves measurable throughput gain over CFD-only baseline while maintaining decision-grade reliability via CFD fallback.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach succeeds due to a synergy of rigorous contract enforcement, evolutionary search, and adaptive resource allocation. The contract-as-code harness guarantees that every surrogate candidate meets industrial safety and reproducibility standards before being considered, eliminating leakage and non-determinism. The island-based evolutionary algorithm maintains genetic diversity across parallel subpopulations, reducing the risk of premature convergence and allowing beneficial mutations to spread through controlled migration. Structured mutations systematically explore the space of data, model, loss, and split configurations, while multi-objective selection (NSGA-II) balances predictive performance, complexity, and reliability, yielding a Pareto front of trustworthy surrogates. Adaptive sampling directs evaluation effort (e.g., CFD label queries) toward uncertain or impactful candidates, maximizing information gain per expensive simulation. Together, these mechanisms enable the automated discovery of high-performing, robust surrogate pipelines that bypass traditional workflow friction and can be safely deployed in an industrial setting.

## Foundational Learning
**Evolutionary Algorithms**
- *Why needed*: Provide the overarching search mechanism to iteratively improve executable pipelines through selection and variation, without requiring gradient-based optimization.
- *Quick check*: Implement a simple evolutionary loop that evolves binary strings to maximize the number of 1s; verify that average fitness increases over generations.

**Island Model (Parallel Populations)**
- *Why needed*: Preserves diversity by isolating subpopulations, reducing the chance of genetic drift and allowing parallel exploration of different niches.
- *Quick check*: Run two islands with occasional migration; measure genotype diversity (e.g., Hamming distance) and compare to a single population of equivalent size.

**Multi-Objective Optimization (NSGA-II)**
- *Why needed*: Simultaneously optimizes competing objectives (accuracy, complexity, reliability), producing a set of trade-off solutions for downstream selection.
- *Quick check*: Apply NSGA-II to minimize error and model size on a synthetic dataset; inspect the Pareto front for diversity and convergence.

**Contract-as-Code Governance**
- *Why needed*: Encodes domain constraints (e.g., no leakage, reproducibility) as automated, binary checks that must be satisfied for a candidate to survive, ensuring industrial acceptability.
- *Quick check*: Create a contract validator that rejects any pipeline that reads a forbidden column; test with a pipeline that accesses that column and confirm it is blocked.

**Adaptive Sampling**
- *Why needed*: Efficiently allocates limited high-fidelity simulation budget by querying labels for data points where the current surrogate is most uncertain, accelerating learning.
- *Quick check*: Implement an active learning loop with a Gaussian process; compare the number of queries needed to reach a target error versus random sampling.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: 
Contract Definition -> Evaluation Harness -> Island Populations (multiple) -> Fitness Computation -> NSGA-II Selection -> Mutation Operators -> Migration Module -> Next Generation. Global Archive (Pareto front) -> Deployment Engine (Screen-and-Escalate).

**Critical path**: 
1. Initial population of candidate pipelines (different architectures, losses, splits) is seeded.
2. Each candidate is executed within the evaluation harness, which runs multi-seed, multi-fold validation under the contract constraints.
3. Fitness scores (Combined Score components, reliability, complexity) are computed.
4. Non-dominated sorting selects elite individuals.
5. Structured mutations are applied to elites to create offspring; migration periodically exchanges individuals between islands.
6. The cycle repeats for many generations, gradually improving the population.
7. The best surrogates (from the global archive) are deployed: they rank new designs; cases with high uncertainty or out-of-distribution flags are escalated to CFD for labeling and possible retraining.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Exploration vs exploitation*: Higher mutation rates and lower selection pressure increase diversity but may slow convergence.
- *Evaluation cost vs reliability*: Multi-seed validation reduces false positives but increases computational budget.
- *Contract strictness vs flexibility*: Tight gates ensure safety but may reject innovative pipelines that technically violate a constraint but could be repaired.
- *Island count vs migration frequency*: More islands boost diversity; too frequent migration can cause homogenization and reduce the benefit.

**Failure signatures**:
- *Non-reproducible pipelines*: High variance in fitness across fixed seeds (>0.02 std in sign accuracy).
- *Contract violations*: Any access to forbidden features or resource overuse during evaluation.
- *Stagnation*: No improvement in Combined Score over >50 generations.
- *Deployment degradation*: Escalation rate exceeds acceptable threshold, indicating surrogate overconfidence or distribution shift.

**First experiments**:
1. **Baseline pipeline generation**: Hand-craft 2–3 simple surrogates (e.g., MLP on handcrafted geometric features) to establish a baseline fitness and validate the evaluation harness.
2. **Island vs single-population comparison**: Run evolution with 3 islands versus one large panmictic population, keeping total population size constant, and compare convergence speed and diversity of solutions.
3. **Mutation category ablation**: Disable one class of mutations (e.g., model architecture changes) and measure impact on final Combined Score and time-to-convergence to understand contributions of different mutation types.

## Limitations
- Mutation operator specifications are anonymized in the paper; exact code transformations are undisclosed, making precise replication challenging.
- Dataset composition, mesh density standards, and CFD solver settings are not detailed, risking potential data leakage or inconsistency in independent validation.
- The generalizability of findings to domains beyond automotive aerodynamics or to different simulation bottlenecks is uncertain.
