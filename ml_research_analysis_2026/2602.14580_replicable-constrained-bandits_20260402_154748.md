# Replicable Constrained Bandits
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14580
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14580
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The DEBORA family of algorithms achieves optimal regret and violation rates (Õ(√(KT))) for constrained bandits while ensuring algorithmic replicability, with DEBORA-H providing per‑round safety guarantees.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies algorithmic replicability in stochastic constrained multi‑armed bandits, where an agent must maximize cumulative reward while respecting multiple linear constraints. It introduces a family of replicable algorithms—DEBORA for unconstrained bandits, DEBORA‑S for soft constraints (where violations are allowed but must be sublinear), and DEBORA‑H for hard constraints (which must be satisfied every round). All algorithms rely on a replicable mean estimator (RepMean) and a dynamic epoch‑based UCB approach that avoids arm elimination. The results show that for both soft and hard constraints, the cumulative regret grows as Õ(√(KT)), matching the best non‑replicable rates, while DEBORA‑S enjoys sublinear violation and DEBORA‑H guarantees high‑probability safety in every round.

## Method Summary
The core building block is RepMean, a discretization‑based estimator that uses shared internal randomness ξ to achieve ρ‑replicability. DEBORA operates in dynamic epochs: at the start of an epoch, UCB indices for all arms are computed using fresh mean estimates from RepMean; the arm with highest index is played repeatedly until its pull count doubles relative to the epoch start, then a new epoch begins. This epoch‑doubling scheme avoids permanent arm elimination and keeps the number of epochs logarithmic. For constrained settings, DEBORA‑S constructs an optimistic safe set using cost lower bounds derived from RepMean and selects the arm maximizing the reward upper confidence within that set. DEBORA‑H mixes the optimistic arm with a known strictly feasible arm to ensure safety: a mixing coefficient σ_h is computed to satisfy constraints worst‑case. Confidence widths incorporate the replicability parameters ρ and δ; all randomness comes from the single source ξ to maintain ρ‑replicability.

## Key Results
- For both soft and hard constraint settings, cumulative regret RT = Õ(√(KT)), matching non‑replicable optimal rates.
- DEBORA‑S achieves sublinear cumulative violation VT = o(T).
- DEBORA‑H satisfies every constraint with high probability in all rounds while maintaining sublinear regret.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Dynamic Epoch‑Based UCB Without Arm Elimination.**  
DEBORA selects an arm via UCB at epoch start and continues playing it until its count doubles from the epoch start. This dynamic epoch structure avoids the need to eliminate arms permanently, which is often required for replicability. The confidence width ζ_h (from RepMean) shrinks as more samples are gathered, ensuring UCB indices become accurate. Evidence: Section 3.2 formalizes the termination condition and selective updating. A break condition occurs if an arm never doubles its count, potentially stalling progress.

**Replicable Mean Estimation with RepMean.**  
RepMean discretizes [0,1] into bins of size α, adds a random offset α_off drawn from U(0,α) using a fixed internal randomness ξ, and outputs the midpoint of the bin containing the sample mean. The same ξ across runs yields identical offsets, giving ρ‑replicability. Required sample complexity is O(1/(ε²(ρ−2δ)²)). Evidence: Section 2.2.1 and Lemma 2.5. Break condition: fails if ρ ≤ 2δ.

**Constraint Handling via Optimistic Safe Sets and Mixing.**  
For soft constraints, an optimistic safe set X^h = { x | (ĝ_i − ζ_h)ᵀx ≤ α_i ∀ i } is constructed to contain the true safe set with high probability. The chosen strategy maximizes the reward upper confidence inside X^h. For hard constraints, a known strictly safe strategy x^⋄ (with margin λ_min) is mixed with the optimistic strategy: a mixing coefficient σ_h ensures worst‑case costs stay below thresholds. This preserves replicability while guaranteeing per‑round safety. Evidence: Section 4.1 (soft) and 5.1 (hard). Break conditions: DEBORA‑S may exclude the optimal arm if estimates are poor; DEBORA‑H requires a reasonably large λ_min, otherwise regret inflates or safety fails.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
RepMean → Epoch Manager → Strategy Selector → Action Sampler  
(Safety Enforcer → Strategy Selector for DEBORA‑H)

**Critical Path**  
1. Initialize epoch h=0, counts eNh(a)=0, reward estimates br(a)=1/2, cost estimates as needed.  
2. For each round t:  
   - If current chosen arm a^∘_h (or strategy x^∘_h) satisfies N_t(a^∘_h) ≥ max{2·eNh(a^∘_h), 1}, start a new epoch: increment h, set eNh(a)=N_t(a) for all arms, compute fresh replicable estimators for rewards and costs from all past samples, compute confidence widths ζ_h(a).  
   - For constrained variants: construct optimistic safe set X^h (DEBORA‑S) or compute mixing coefficient σ_h (DEBORA‑H) using cost estimates.  
   - Select new arm/strategy via UCB maximization (constrained if applicable).  
   - Play the selected arm, observe reward and costs, update counts.  
3. Continue until T rounds.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Dynamic epoch lengths avoid arm elimination but may cause long epochs if one arm dominates; careful count bookkeeping is needed.  
- Replicability parameters (ρ, δ) enlarge confidence widths, increasing sample complexity.  
- DEBORA‑S tolerates early constraint violations (soft); DEBORA‑H enforces safety via mixing but performance is sensitive to the safety margin λ_min (Slater’s condition).  
- Absence of arm elimination simplifies analysis but relies on epoch doubling to achieve logarithmic pulls.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Linear regret despite small gaps → ζ_h too large due to tiny (ρ−2δ) or denominator misuse.  
- Constraint violations in DEBORA‑H → check x^⋄ is strictly feasible, verify σ_h calculation, and ensure correct sign of ζ in safe‑set checks.  
- Non‑replicable runs → any randomness besides ξ (e.g., tie‑breaking, LP solver nondeterminism) breaks replicability; enforce deterministic decisions.  
- Epoch count H ≫ K log T → likely eNh(a) not updated properly at epoch transitions.  
- Stalled epochs → termination condition N_t(a)≥max{2eNh(a),1} never met; confirm integer count updates.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Replicability test:** Run DEBORA on a 2‑arm Gaussian bandit (Δ=0.1) with fixed ξ, 100 repetitions. All action sequences should be identical; empirical failure ≤ ρ. Compare regret to theoretical bound.  
2. **Soft‑constraint validation:** Use DEBORA‑S on a 3‑arm CMAB (m=1, α=0.5). Ensure cumulative violation VT = o(T) and regret matches unconstrained scaling; vary ρ to observe overhead.  
3. **Hard‑constraint safety:** Use DEBORA‑H with a known strictly safe arm (x^⋄ = uniform over safe arms). Check per‑round violations are zero with high probability. Stress with λ_min = 0.01; observe regret blowup ~1/λ_min.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the DEBORA UCB‑based approach be extended to constrained MDPs (CMDPs) while preserving replicability and achieving sublinear regret and violation?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper focuses on constrained bandits but mentions constrained MDPs as a related direction and suggests the UCB‑like method may apply beyond bandits.
- **Why unresolved:** CMDPs involve state transitions and partial observability, requiring estimation of value functions rather than simple means; current replicable mean estimators do not directly scale.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A ρ‑replicable algorithm for CMDPs with O(√T) regret and constraint violation (matching non‑replicable counterparts) would demonstrate feasibility.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Are there replicable algorithms for constrained bandits when constraint thresholds αᵢ are unknown?
- **Basis in paper:** The presented algorithms assume αᵢ are known to define the optimistic safe set X^h; the case of unknown thresholds is not discussed.
- **Why unresolved:** Unknown thresholds add extra estimation complexity; the safe set construction may become non‑optimistic and degrade guarantees unless robust joint estimation is developed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An algorithm that learns αᵢ online while maintaining ρ‑replicability and comparable regret/violation bounds would show it is possible.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the information‑theoretic lower bounds for replicable constrained bandits, and are the polynomial dependencies on K and m in current upper bounds inherent?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper provides upper bounds with factors like mK²√(KT) but does not prove any lower bounds; the necessity of ρ‑dependence is cited from prior work, but K and m dependencies remain untested.
- **Why unresolved:** Without lower bounds, it is unknown whether the current rates are optimal or can be improved; fundamental limitations of replicability in this setting are unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Minimax lower bounds that match the upper bounds up to logarithmic factors, or algorithms with significantly smaller polynomial factors, would clarify the truth.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can hard constraints be satisfied in a replicable manner without assuming Slater's condition or the availability of a strictly safe strategy x^⋄?
- **Basis in paper:** DEBORA‑H explicitly requires a known strictly safe strategy x^⋄ and Slater's condition (Assumptions 2.1 and 2.2) to guarantee per‑round safety (Section 5).
- **Why unresolved:** The mixing technique relies on x^⋄ to enforce safety; without it, achieving zero violation each round under replicability is non‑trivial and may require fundamentally different exploration strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A ρ‑replicable algorithm that provides high‑probability per‑round safety with sublinear regret, without Slater's assumption or a provided safe strategy, would answer this positively.

## Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical; no empirical validation of constants, regret/violation magnitudes, or parameter effects exists.
- Performance hinges on a positive gap ρ−2δ and a reasonably large safety margin λ_min; small values may degrade rates or break guarantees.
- Implementation subtleties (e.g., deterministic tie‑breaking, proper consumption of internal randomness across RepMean calls) are not addressed but are essential for achieving replicability in practice.
