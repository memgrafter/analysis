# Fedcompass: Federated Clustered and Periodic Aggregation Framework for Hybrid Classical-Quantum Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.03052
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03052
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** Achieves up to 10.22% higher test accuracy on CIFAR-10 under severe non-IID (α=0.3) compared to FedAvg.

## Executive Summary
FEDCOMPASS is a federated learning framework designed for hybrid classical-quantum models operating under non-IID data distributions. It addresses two core challenges: the drift of classical feature extractors due to heterogeneous client data and the misalignment of periodic quantum rotation angles during aggregation. The framework introduces a layered server-side aggregation process. First, it employs spectral clustering to group clients with similar class distributions and performs cluster-wise weighted averaging for classical parameters. Second, it uses a circular mean operation combined with an adaptive Adam-style optimizer to aggregate and update the periodic quantum parameters. Evaluated on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 under non-IID settings, FEDCOMPASS demonstrates superior accuracy and convergence stability against six baseline methods, with ablation studies confirming the necessity of both its clustering and circular mean components.

## Method Summary
FEDCOMPASS proposes a two-stage server aggregation strategy for hybrid models. Clients train a local model with a classical feature extractor (e.g., CNN) and a parameterized quantum circuit classifier. During each communication round, clients send their updated classical parameters, quantum rotation angles, and a summary class distribution vector to the server. The server first computes a similarity matrix from the class distributions and applies spectral clustering to partition clients. Classical parameters are then aggregated *within each cluster* using a weighted average based on local sample sizes. For quantum parameters, the server computes a weighted circular mean across *all clients* for each angle to handle periodicity, computes a gradient from this mean, and applies an adaptive moment estimation (Adam) update to produce the new global quantum state. Cluster-specific classical models and a single global quantum model are then distributed back to clients.

## Key Results
*   FEDCOMPASS achieves up to **10.22% higher test accuracy** on CIFAR-10 (α=0.3) and **5.22% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.7)** compared to FedAvg.
*   It outperforms six baseline methods (including FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, and clustered FL variants) in accuracy and convergence stability across three datasets.
*   Ablation studies confirm that removing either the **spectral clustering** (for classical params) or the **circular mean** (for quantum params) leads to significant performance degradation, validating both design choices.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spectral Clustering for Classical Feature Aggregation
*   **Claim:** Mitigates performance degradation from non-IID data by grouping clients with similar class distributions and aggregating classical feature extractors within clusters, reducing feature extractor drift.
*   **Mechanism:** Clients upload class distribution vectors; server computes a similarity matrix using Jensen-Shannon divergence and sample size difference; applies spectral clustering (Normalized Cut) to partition clients; then performs weighted average aggregation of classical parameters within each cluster based on client sample sizes.
*   **Core assumption:** Clients sharing similar class distributions learn analogous feature representations, so cluster-wise aggregation preserves local patterns better than global averaging, aligning classical layers across similar data distributions.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "employs spectral clustering to group clients by class distribution similarity and performs cluster-wise aggregation for classical feature extractors."
    *   [section]: Section 2.2 details the similarity matrix construction and spectral clustering implementation.
    *   [corpus]: Corpus includes clustered FL papers (e.g., CADIS, Stochastic Clustered FL), but this paper does not cite them directly; evidence from corpus is weak for this specific mechanism.
*   **Break condition:** If similarity vectors are noisy or sparse (e.g., few classes per client), clustering may group dissimilar clients, leading to within-cluster variance that harms convergence; or if clusters become too small (e.g., 1-2 clients), aggregation loses statistical benefits and overfits local data.

### Mechanism 2: Circular Mean for Quantum Parameter Aggregation
*   **Claim:** Prevents periodicity-induced misalignment in quantum rotation angles by using circular statistics for aggregation, avoiding phase wrapping errors that destabilize training.
*   **Mechanism:** For each quantum parameter angle, compute weighted circular mean via `atan2(∑ω_i sin(ϕ_i), ∑ω_i cos(ϕ_i))`, mapping angles to the unit circle for averaging before wrapping back to [-π, π].
*   **Core assumption:** Quantum parameters are angular and periodic; arithmetic averaging can place the mean in a different period (e.g., averaging π and -π yields 0 instead of π), causing inconsistent global updates.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "uses circular mean aggregation"
    *   [section]: Equation (3) in Section 2.3 explicitly defines the circular mean formula for quantum parameters.
    *   [corpus]: No direct citation; corpus covers quantum FL but not circular mean specifically; evidence is weak.
*   **Break condition:** If quantum parameters include non-angular components mixed with angles, or if client updates have extreme weight imbalances, circular mean may produce biased estimates; also, if angles are not uniformly distributed around the circle, circular mean can be sensitive to outliers.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Optimization with Circular Mean
*   **Claim:** Enhances convergence stability for the global quantum classifier by adapting updates based on historical gradient information, compensating for variance from non-IID aggregation.
*   **Mechanism:** After circular mean, compute gradient `g_t = ϕ_t - ¯ϕ`; update first (`m_t`) and second (`v_t`) moment estimates with momentum (β1) and squared gradients (β2); apply bias correction and Adam-style parameter update: `ϕ_{t+1} = ϕ_t - η·m̂_t / (√v̂_t + ϵ)`.
*   **Core assumption:** Adaptive learning rates can mitigate oscillations from heterogeneous quantum updates, as momentum smooths noise and variance scaling controls step sizes per parameter.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "combined with adaptive optimization to ensure stable global updates."
    *   [section]: Algorithm 1 and equations (4)-(6) in Section 2.3 describe the full adaptive update process.
    *   [corpus]: Not directly cited; Adam is common in FL, but its integration with circular mean is novel per paper; corpus evidence is absent.
*   **Break condition:** If hyperparameters (β1, β2, η, ϵ) are poorly tuned (e.g., η too high), the adaptive optimizer may diverge; or if circular mean gradients are inherently noisy (e.g., from small client counts), moment estimates may lag, causing delayed corrections and instability.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Client (local dataset, hybrid model, local training) -> Server (global model, similarity matrix, spectral clustering, cluster-wise classical aggregation, circular mean + Adam for quantum, distribution) -> Clients (update cluster-specific classical params, global quantum params)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Server initializes hybrid global model; distributes to all clients.
    2.  Clients perform local training (5 epochs); compute updates for classical (θ) and quantum (ϕ) parameters; send updates with class distribution vector (p) to server.
    3.  Server computes similarity matrix S from all p vectors; runs spectral clustering to assign clients to M clusters.
    4.  For each cluster, aggregate classical parameters: `θ^{(m)}_c = (∑_{i∈C_m} n_i θ^{(i)}_c) / (∑_{i∈C_m} n_i)`.
    5.  For each quantum parameter dimension j, compute circular mean ¯ϕ_j; form aggregated ¯ϕ; compute gradient `g_t = ϕ_t - ¯ϕ`; update ϕ via Adam.
    6.  Server sends updated `θ^{(m)}_c` for each cluster and `ϕ_{t+1}` to respective clients.
    7.  Repeat steps 2-6 for T communication rounds (e.g., 5 rounds).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Clustering overhead: Spectral clustering adds O(N²) similarity computation and O(N³) eigenvalue decomposition on server; acceptable for small N (e.g., 10 clients) but may not scale to thousands without approximations.
    *   Circular mean cost: Trigonometric functions (sin, cos, atan2) per parameter dimension; negligible for small quantum circuits but could add latency with large parameter counts.
    *   Adaptive optimizer sensitivity: Requires careful tuning of β1, β2, η, ϵ; default Adam values may not transfer across datasets, risking instability if circular mean gradients are sparse.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Accuracy drop: Test accuracy lower than FedAvg on non-IID data, especially with α=0.3, indicating clustering or circular mean failure.
    *   Convergence instability: Accuracy or loss curves oscillate or diverge after 2-3 rounds; check if circular mean is used correctly vs. arithmetic mean.
    *   Clustering anomalies: Cluster sizes highly imbalanced (e.g., one cluster has >80% clients) or many singleton clusters; suggests similarity matrix or spectral clustering issues.
    *   Quantum parameter wrap-around: Histograms of ϕ parameters show multiple modes post-aggregation, indicating period mismatch from incorrect circular mean implementation.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline replication:** Run FEDCOMPASS on CIFAR-10 with α=0.3 and α=0.7, compare test accuracy after 5 rounds to Table 1 values; confirm ≥77% accuracy for α=0.3.
    2.  **Clustering ablation:** Disable spectral clustering (use FedAvg for classical aggregation globally) while keeping circular mean; expect significant drop, especially on CIFAR-10 α=0.3, per Table 2.
    3.  **Circular mean ablation:** Disable circular mean (use arithmetic mean for quantum parameters) while keeping clustering; expect unstable convergence and lower final accuracy, as in Table 2 "No Circular Mean" column.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question:** Does FEDCOMPASS maintain its performance advantages when scaled to hundreds of clients and real quantum hardware with noise?
    *   Basis: Experiments used 10 clients and noiseless simulators only (inferred from Section 3.1).
    *   Why unresolved: Spectral clustering cost grows superlinearly; noise may disrupt circular mean aggregation.
    *   What evidence: Large-scale simulations with noise models; runtime complexity analysis; hardware experiments.
*   **Question:** Do class distribution vectors uploaded for clustering leak private client information?
    *   Basis: Privacy claim based on not uploading raw data, but no analysis of summary leakage (inferred from Section 2.2).
    *   Why unresolved: Distribution vectors could enable membership inference in skewed settings.
    *   What evidence: Apply differential privacy to vectors; measure attack success rates.
*   **Question:** How robust is FEDCOMPASS to variations in λ1, λ2 and Adam hyperparameters?
    *   Basis: Fixed hyperparameters throughout (inferred from Eq.1 and Algorithm 1).
    *   Why unresolved: Hyperparameters may critically affect clustering quality and convergence yet no sensitivity study.
    *   What evidence: Grid search over plausible ranges; report accuracy variance; identify robust settings.
*   **Question:** Could joint aggregation of classical and quantum layers improve over the layered approach?
    *   Basis: The design enforces separate aggregation, assuming independence (inferred from Section 2).
    *   Why unresolved: Interlayer parameter interactions might be misaligned by independent updates.
    *   What evidence: Compare with schemes that aggregate all parameters uniformly or with learned weighting; analysis of gradient alignment.

## Limitations
*   **Mechanism novelty & justification:** Core innovations (spectral clustering for non-IID hybrid FL, circular mean for quantum parameters) have weak direct corpus support; key clustered FL works are not cited, making true novelty versus adaptation uncertain.
*   **Scalability unproven:** Spectral clustering's O(N³) eigendecomposition is a bottleneck; evaluation uses ≤100 clients, so performance with thousands (realistic cross-silo) is an open question.
*   **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** The adaptive optimizer (Adam with circular mean gradients) is sensitive to β1, β2, η, ϵ. The paper uses defaults; stability under non-IID variance, especially with small cluster sizes or extreme weight imbalance, is not rigorously stress-tested.

## Confidence
*   **Performance Claims (10.22% gain vs. FedAvg):** High. These are empirical results from controlled experiments on three datasets.
*   **Clustering Necessity (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Ablation shows benefit, but the claim that spectral grouping is *optimal* for hybrid models is less certain without comparisons to other client grouping strategies at scale.
*   **Circular Mean & Adaptive Opt. (Mechanisms 2 & 3):** Medium-Low. Ablation indicates contribution, but the specific synergy and robustness of the circular mean + Adam pipeline lack deep analysis (e.g., gradient distribution analysis, failure cases).
