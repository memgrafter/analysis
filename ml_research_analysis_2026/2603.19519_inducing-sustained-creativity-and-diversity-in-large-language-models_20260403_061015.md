# Inducing Sustained Creativity and Diversity in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19519
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19519
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Recoding-Decoding (RD) increases sustained output diversity and creativity by 60+ percentage points while maintaining comparable relevance, generating over 68x more unique solutions than ordinary decoding in a long-horizon exploration task.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) often fail at open-ended, long-horizon "search quest" tasks where users need to explore a vast, unknown solution space. Standard decoding methods produce homogeneous, repetitive outputs by favoring high-probability tokens, which systematically excludes heterodox knowledge. This paper introduces Recoding-Decoding (RD), a model-agnostic decoding scheme that injects random "priming phrases" and "diverting tokens" at sentence boundaries to steer generation toward under-explored, low-probability regions of the model's knowledge without retraining or weight changes. RD operates via standard APIs and demonstrates dramatic, sustained improvements in diversity and creativity across multiple benchmarks with minimal relevance loss.

## Method Summary
RD modifies the input context at each sentence boundary to disrupt the model's default high-probability decoding trajectory. For each new sentence, it samples a random "priming phrase" (e.g., "**Related to NOUN**") to activate a broad conceptual subspace and appends a random "diverting token" (a three-letter word stem) to force lexical redirection into low-probability but semantically coherent continuations. The generation proceeds iteratively: after producing a sentence, the process repeats with a new random diverting token. A post-processing grammatical correction step is optionally applied to improve fluency. The method requires only access to a completion API (or a chat-based simulation) and predefined vocabularies of common nouns and word stems.

## Key Results
- In a battlefield location generation task (1,000 runs), RD produced 1,307 unique global locations versus 19 for ordinary decoding, with comparable relevance scores (~0.98–1.00).
- Across 50 open-ended brainstorming topics, RD's cumulative cluster growth remained nearly linear over hundreds of outputs, while ordinary decoding plateaued quickly (~8 outputs).
- RD increased LLM-rated diversity scores by over 60 percentage points (e.g., 0.27 to 0.70 on GPT-5.1) with less than 2% absolute relevance loss.
- Relative creativity metrics show RD covers ~80–90% of ordinary decoding's clusters, while ordinary decoding covers only ~30–40% of RD's clusters. RD's absolute creativity (distance to nearest prior cluster) remained consistently higher over time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: RD increases diversity by steering generation into under-explored, low-probability regions of the token distribution.
- **Mechanism**: It injects priming phrases at the prompt start and diverting tokens at sentence boundaries, exploiting the transformer's positional bias (greater attention to sequence boundaries). Priming phrases (e.g., "**Related to FOOD**") activate broad conceptual subspaces, shifting focus away from modal outputs. Diverting tokens (e.g., "Pas" → "Pasta") force lexical redirection into semantically appropriate but low-probability continuations, accessing knowledge stored in the tail of the distribution that standard decoding (top-k, nucleus) discards.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] steers toward under-explored, low-probability regions; [section 4.1] OD produced 19 European/American battlefields vs. RD's 1,307 globally distributed locations; [section 4.3.1] cluster growth plateaus for OD but remains linear for RD.
- **Break condition**: Heavily RLHF-tuned models may have overly peaked distributions, suppressing low-probability heterodox knowledge and limiting RD's ceiling.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: RD sustains diversity across long horizons by repeatedly resetting the generation context, preventing convergence to local clusters.
- **Mechanism**: At each sentence boundary, a new random diverting token is injected, creating a moving "contextual anchor." This alters the hidden state trajectory, preventing the model from settling into repetitive high-probability loops or attractor basins. The per-sentence priming phrase also ensures each segment starts from a slightly shifted conceptual basis.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.3.1] OD cluster growth plateaus after ~8 runs; RD remains nearly linear at 1,000 runs; [section 4.3.2] RD's absolute creativity (distance to nearest prior cluster) stays ~0.8 vs. OD's drop to ~0.5.
- **Break condition**: For topics with a finite answer space (e.g., "fashion accessories"), novelty eventually saturates and growth slows.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: RD maintains relevance despite diversifying because its interventions are semantically constrained.
- **Mechanism**: Diverting tokens are drawn from the first three letters of top 5,000 common English words, ensuring they are syntactically valid prefixes (e.g., "Tib" → "Tibetan"). Priming phrases use common nouns. This keeps continuations within the prompt's semantic field (e.g., "world history" → "Tibetan sky burials") while exploring unconventional corners.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.3.1] RD relevance 0.94–0.99 vs. OD 0.98–1.00; [appendix E] RDp3 (priming only) and RDd3 (diverting only) both maintain >0.99 relevance.
- **Break condition**: If diverting tokens are too obscure, fluency drops; the optional grammar corrector mitigates but may over-edit and remove meaningful unconventionality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Decoding strategies (top-k, nucleus, temperature)**  
  *Why needed*: RD directly contrasts with these standard methods, which optimize for high-probability, homogeneous outputs and exhibit mode collapse.  
  *Quick check*: Why does increasing temperature sometimes reduce both diversity and coherence? (Hint: Undirected randomness vs. RD's directed, semantically constrained randomness.)
- **Concept: Semantic embeddings and clustering**  
  *Why needed*: RD's diversity evaluation relies on embedding-based cosine similarity to define conceptual clusters; lexical metrics (n-grams) would miss conceptual novelty.  
  *Quick check*: How does the cluster similarity threshold (τ=0.73) affect perceived diversity? What are the effects of setting τ too high or too low?
- **Concept: Positional bias in transformers**  
  *Why needed*: RD exploits that tokens at sequence boundaries (start/end) receive disproportionate attention, making interventions there effective at shifting generation paths.  
  *Quick check*: How might RD differ when using a model with rotary position embeddings (RoPE) versus absolute positional encodings?
- **Concept: Long-horizon exploration vs. single-batch diversity**  
  *Why needed*: RD addresses sustained novelty over many outputs, unlike standard benchmarks measuring diversity within a single batch.  
  *Quick check*: Why does single-batch diversity (e.g., high temperature) not guarantee sustained exploration over hundreds of outputs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  User Prompt -> Priming Phrase Generator -> Combined Input -> Diverting Token Selector -> LLM Completion API -> (Optional) Grammar Corrector -> Output Sentence -> Concatenate & Repeat Loop
- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize empty output string `Y`.
  2. For each sentence to generate:
     a. Sample a random diverting token `d`.
     b. Construct input: `priming_phrase + user_prompt + Y + d`.
     c. Call LLM completion, prepend `d` to the generated sentence `s`.
     d. Append `s` to `Y`.
  3. Repeat until token or sentence limit is reached.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Priming vs. diverting*: Priming shifts broad topic space; diverting tweaks lexical path. Using both maximizes diversity but increases cost and may slightly increase irrelevance.
  - *API compatibility*: True completion endpoints work best; simulating via chat (with a system prompt) can introduce format noise and reduce gains for some models (e.g., GPT-5.1, Gemini-3).
  - *Relevance vs. novelty*: Higher intervention frequency (e.g., diverting token every sentence) boosts diversity but risks off-topic outputs; the grammar corrector helps but may over-correct and sanitize truly novel ideas.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Repetition persists**: Cluster growth plateaus early. Diagnose: verify diverting tokens are injected at *sentence boundaries*, check randomness in sampling, ensure API is in continuation mode (not chat role-playing).
  - **Low relevance**: Many outputs are off-topic. Diagnose: diverting tokens may be too obscure; reduce frequency or refine stem vocabulary; audit grammar corrector for over-editing.
  - **API errors/misformatting**: Chat-based simulation may misinterpret injected tokens. Adjust system prompt to "Simulate completion API" and test with simple prompts first.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Ablation study**: Test RDp (priming only), RDd (diverting only), and full RD on a single prompt (e.g., "Brainstorm 5 book topics on 18th century world history"). Compare cumulative clusters and relevance.
  2. **Token responsiveness**: For 50 random diverting tokens, generate 10 outputs each. Cluster embeddings; each token should activate ≥6 distinct clusters (indicating it steers to different subspaces).
  3. **Temperature vs. RD comparison**: On GPT-3.5, run ordinary decoding with temperature T=1.0–1.8 (post-processed for quality) vs. RD. Plot diversity (clusters) vs. relevance; RD should dominate the upper-right frontier.

## Limitations
- **API simulation fidelity gap**: The use of a chat-based workaround for models without native completion APIs (GPT-5.1, Gemini-3) may overstate RD's gains; the performance difference vs. a true completion endpoint is unquantified.
- **Correction-induced novelty loss**: The optional grammatical corrector (likely GPT-4o) may "sanitize" unconventional but valid outputs, artificially inflating relevance metrics while suppressing true diversity.
- **Tail knowledge accessibility assumption**: RD assumes heterodox knowledge resides in low-probability tokens. In heavily RLHF-tuned models, these tails may be suppressed, creating a ceiling effect where RD's diversity gains diminish.

## Confidence
- **RD dramatically increases sustained diversity/creativity (empirical)**: High. Supported by large-scale experiments (1,000 runs) and multiple datasets with clear metrics (cluster growth, coverage).
- **Mechanism: steering via boundary injection exploiting positional bias**: Medium. Plausible and aligned with known transformer attention patterns, but lacks direct ablations isolating positional effects (e.g., injecting mid-sequence).
- **Relevance is preserved via plausible token constraints**: Medium-Low. Minimal loss in automated evaluations, but LLM judges may conflate fluency with topical relevance; human validation is limited.

## Next Checks
1. **API Fidelity Test**: Run identical RD prompts on GPT-5.1 using both the simulated chat interface and any available native completion endpoint. Compare diversity metrics (cluster count, linear growth slope) to quantify simulation bias.
2. **Human Audit of Correction Artifacts**: Sample 100 RD outputs before/after grammatical correction. Have human evaluators judge (a) topical relevance, (b) creativity/novelty, and (c) whether correction removed meaningful unconventional ideas. Calculate correlation with automated scores.
3. **Tail Probability Analysis**: For a fixed prompt, generate 500 outputs via ordinary decoding and RD. Compute average log-probability of generated tokens. Verify RD consistently samples from lower-probability regions (e.g., below top-100) without catastrophic fluency drop, and test if this effect is diminished in newer RLHF models (compare GPT-3.5 vs. GPT-5.1 tail distributions).
