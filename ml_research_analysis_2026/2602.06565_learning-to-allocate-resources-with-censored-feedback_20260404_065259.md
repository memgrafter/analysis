# Learning to Allocate Resources with Censored Feedback
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06565
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06565
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Establishes an information-theoretic regret lower bound Ω(T^{1/3}) for online resource allocation with censored feedback and proposes algorithms (RA-UCB, MG-UCB) achieving eO(√T) regret (or poly-log under strong assumptions), validated on real datasets.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies an online resource allocation problem where each round a fixed budget B is allocated across K arms. Each arm i has an unknown activation probability p_i and a random threshold X_i ~ F_i(·|λ_i). A reward is obtained if the arm is activated and the allocated budget exceeds the threshold. Feedback is censored: the threshold is only observed upon success. The authors prove a problem-dependent lower bound of Ω(T^{1/3}) regret, highlighting the fundamental difficulty introduced by censoring. For known per-round budgets, they propose RA-UCB, an optimistic algorithm that decouples reward collection from parameter estimation via a "boosting" technique: one arm is cyclically boosted with a large allocation to obtain low-censoring samples, while others are pessimistically estimated. RA-UCB achieves eO(√T) regret, and under strong distributional assumptions (e.g., exponential family with strong concavity) even poly-logarithmic regret. For unknown but bounded per-round budgets, they introduce MG-UCB, which uses continuous water-filling with boosted UCB indices and inherits the same guarantees. Empirical evaluations on EdNet (education) and Criteo (advertising) datasets confirm the theoretical scaling.

## Method Summary
The problem involves K arms; each arm i is characterized by (p_i, λ_i) where p_i is the activation probability and F_i is a threshold distribution with CDF G(x, λ_i). The learner allocates a budget vector x_t across arms, subject to Σ x_{t,i} ≤ B (known or unknown but bounded). The reward is Σ Y_{t,i} where Y_{t,i} = 1 if activated and x_{t,i} ≥ X_{t,i}, else 0. Feedback includes (Y_{t,i}, X_{t,i} if Y_{t,i}=1). Estimation of λ_i relies on truncated observations: given success, E[X|X≤B] is a known function μ(λ_i); thus λ_i can be estimated via inverse of empirical truncated mean. However, censoring induces high variance, especially when allocations are small. To address this, RA-UCB cycles a "estimation arm" every K rounds, allocating a large budget (often full B) to that arm to gather many successes and obtain accurate λ estimates. In those rounds, other arms receive minimal allocations (pessimistic estimates). The algorithm constructs surrogate confidence bounds for p_i and λ_i (pessimistic or optimistic depending on whether the arm is boosted). Using these, it solves a surrogate resource allocation problem eP: maximize Σ p_i eG(x_i, λ_i, λ_i') s.t. Σ x_i ≤ B, where eG is a smoothed, optimistic version of G satisfying boosting monotonicity. For unknown budgets, MG-UCB replaces the batch solution with an online water-filling procedure: within a round, it greedily allocates infinitesimal amounts to the arm with highest current marginal gain based on boosted parameters, thus matching the solution of eP for any realized budget. Both algorithms rely on the property that the boosted allocation for the estimation arm is at least as large as the true optimal allocation (Oracle Monotonicity), ensuring that sufficient successes are collected for accurate learning.

## Key Results
- Information-theoretic lower bound: Ω(T^{1/3}) regret for any uniformly good algorithm.
- RA-UCB (known budget): eO(√T) regret; O(poly-log T) under strong concavity of eG (e.g., exponential/Weibull with shape ≤1).
- MG-UCB (unknown budget): same regret guarantees as RA-UCB via continuous water-filling.
- Empirical validation on EdNet and Criteo datasets shows regret scaling consistent with theory.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
We present three key mechanisms that enable the algorithms to overcome censored feedback.

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Decoupling reward collection from parameter estimation via batched boosting prevents misestimation from censored feedback.
- **Mechanism**: The algorithm cycles a “boosted” arm every K rounds, allocating a large budget to that arm to obtain informative (low-censoring) observations for parameter updates. All other arms are pessimistically estimated in that round. This structured exploration generates high-quality samples for the boosted arm while exploiting current estimates for others.
- **Core assumption**: Oracle Monotonicity—when arm i is boosted (pessimistic others), the surrogate optimal allocation assigns weakly more budget to i than under true parameters. This ensures the boosted allocation collects at least as many successes as the optimal policy would on that arm, stabilizing estimation.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: “RA-UCB achieves eO(√T) regret… proposes RA-UCB, an optimistic algorithm that leverages non-trivial parameter estimation and confidence bounds… decouples reward collection from parameter estimation using boosted UCB indices.”
  - Section 4.1: “We now describe how the parameter λi is estimated from the censored feedback… The conditional distribution… depends only on the unknown parameter λi.”
  - Corpus: Weak/no direct evidence; no paper in corpus explicitly studies censored threshold feedback with batched boosting like this work.
- **Break condition**: If the boosting does not guarantee x_t,i ≥ x*_i for the boosted arm (violating Oracle Monotonicity), then the lower bound on successes for that arm fails, leading to poor parameter estimates and linear regret.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Constructing a surrogate objective eG with boosting monotonicity converts the censored learning problem into a UCB-style optimistic optimization with tractable confidence propagation.
- **Mechanism**: Define eG(x, λ, λ') such that (i) eG(x, λ, λ)=G(x, λ) (consistency), (ii) eG is concave and non-decreasing in x, (iii) its derivative is nondecreasing in λ' (optimism) and nonincreasing in λ (pessimism). This allows using standard UCB indices (combined confidence bounds for λ and p) while maintaining concave optimization structure.
- **Core assumption**: The surrogate eG exists satisfying Properties 4.4(i)-(v). In particular, boosting monotonicity (iii) and uniform bias control (iv) are necessary for the regret analysis.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Section 4.3: “The surrogate function eG(x, λ, λ’)… has to satisfy Assumption 4.4… (i) Consistency… (iii) Boosting Monotonicity…"
  - Section D.2: Provides explicit construction for exponential-family distributions, showing how scaling by g(λ')/g(λ) achieves boosting.
  - Corpus: Weak evidence; corpus contains works on surrogate objectives in bandits but not this exact construction for censored thresholds.
- **Break condition**: If the surrogate eG is not strongly concave or its derivative is not monotone in λ, λ', the KKT-based stability argument in Lemma 4.6 fails, and the UCB indices may not track the optimum.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: For unknown per-round budgets, water-filling with boosted UCB indices inherits RA-UCB’s guarantees by pointwise matching its allocation for any realized budget.
- **Mechanism**: Within a round, instead of pre-solving an optimization problem, the algorithm greedily allocates infinitesimal ds to the arm with highest current marginal gain (per boosted parameters). Under concavity, this continuous-time water-filling is equivalent to solving eP with the exact realized budget B_t.
- **Core assumption**: eG(·, λ, λ') is differentiable and concave in x over [0, B_max], and the marginal gains are strictly decreasing in x. This ensures the water-filling procedure converges to the unique optimizer of the surrogate problem.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: “For unknown, round dependent budget, we introduce MG-UCB, which allows within-round switching and infinitesimal allocations, and inherits the same regret guarantees.”
  - Section 5: “We implement, without prior knowledge of the budget, the same allocation principle as RA-UCB, and inherits its regret bounds… Lemma 5.2… the allocation produced by MG-UCB coincides with RA-UCB when the latter solves eP with budget B.”
  - Corpus: Weak; some corpus papers address budget uncertainty but not this water-filling equivalence under censored feedback.
- **Break condition**: If eG is not concave or the marginal gains are not monotone, the water-filling may not equal the oracle solution, breaking the regret equivalence. Also, non-infinitesimal steps (discretization) add O(Δ T) error if not corrected.

## Foundational Learning
We outline three foundational concepts necessary to understand the paper.

### Concept: Multi-armed bandit with semi-bandit feedback
- **Why needed here**: The problem reduces to standard bandits if thresholds were known (no censoring). Understanding UCB, optimism, and exploration-exploitation trade-offs is essential to appreciate why censoring fundamentally changes regret scaling (T^{1/3} vs √T).
- **Quick check**: In a standard K-armed bandit, why does regret scale as Ω(√T)? How would censored feedback (observing threshold only with success) alter the information-theoretic lower bound argument?

### Concept: Censored data estimation
- **Why needed here**: Feedback is censored: threshold observed only on success. Estimating λ requires conditioning on successes and using truncated distributions. The estimator ˆλ_t,i = μ^{-1}( empirical mean of Ft,i | Ft,i ≤ B ) relies on properties of truncated exponential/Weibull moments.
- **Quick check**: Given X ∼ Exp(λ) and you observe X only when X ≤ B, derive E[X | X ≤ B] and explain why its inverse gives an estimator for λ.

### Concept: Lagrange multipliers / KKT for concave resource allocation
- **Why needed here**: The benchmark optimal allocation solves a concave maximization under a linear budget constraint. RA-UCB and MG-UCB solve surrogate variants. Understanding KKT conditions is used to prove stability: Δx ≤ (1/μ)||Δθ||, leading to quadratic regret under smoothness.
- **Quick check**: Write the KKT conditions for maximizing ∑_i p_i G(x_i, λ_i) s.t. ∑_i x_i ≤ B. What do they imply about the relationship between marginal gains across arms at optimum?

## Architecture Onboarding
We break down the algorithmic system into components and describe its operation.

**Component map**:
Initialization -> Parameter estimators -> Confidence bounds -> Boosting module -> { Surrogate optimizer (RA-UCB) -> Allocation } OR { Water-filling allocator (MG-UCB) -> Allocation } -> Feedback -> (Parameter estimators update). Boosting module also provides boosted parameters to optimizer/allocator.

**Critical path**:
1. Initialization: Allocate full budget for ⌊log T⌋ rounds to each arm to collect initial samples.
2. Main loop (repeated every K rounds):
   a. Select next estimation arm i (cyclic).
   b. Update confidence bounds for all arms using data since last estimation round.
   c. Form boosted parameter tuple: for estimation arm i use optimistic (λ^-, λ^+, p^+), for others use pessimistic (λ^+, λ^-, p^-).
   d. For known budget: solve surrogate problem eP with boosted parameters to obtain allocation x_t (using convex solver or water-filling for separable concave eG).
      For unknown budget: run MG-UCB water-filling: sequentially allocate infinitesimal ds to arm with highest current marginal gain until budget exhausted.
   e. Play allocation x_t, observe censored feedback (success flags and thresholds for successes).
   f. Update only the estimation arm’s parameters (n, ˆμ, ˆλ, ˆp) from the new data.
3. Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Surrogate construction: Strong concavity of eG yields O(poly-log) regret but requires restrictive distribution families (e.g., exponential, Weibull with shape ≤ 1). Weaker concavity gives only eO(√T).
- Boosting intensity: Confidence radii use α = (3 log t)/(2 n_t,i). Overly aggressive boosting may starve other arms; too mild risks insufficient exploration.
- Discretization (MG-UCB-Δ): Finite Δ adds O(Δ T) bias. Choose Δ small enough that bias dominated by estimation error (Δ = o(1/√T)).
- Computational vs. statistical: Exact eP solver may be heavy; water-filling (for concave eG) is O(K log K) per iteration.

**Failure signatures**:
- High variance in λ estimates: If n_t,i grows too slowly (e.g., if boosting fails, Oracle Monotonicity broken), confidence intervals widen, leading to overly pessimistic allocations and linear regret.
- Surrogate bias mismatch: If eG(x, λ, λ') deviates from G(x, λ) by more than L_Δ |λ-λ'| uniformly (violating Assumption 4.iv), the regret decomposition fails to bound the true gap.
- Non-monotone water-filling: If eG is not concave in x, marginal gains may cross, breaking greedy water-filling optimality. Check second derivative sign.
- Budget spikes (unknown budget setting): If B_t varies widely, replacing B with B_max in confidence radii might be loose, increasing constant factors; but regret order unchanged under B_max bound.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **EdNet calibration**: Use EdNet-KT3 logs to fit truncated Weibull thresholds for 20 questions. Ground-truth parameters estimated by MLE on response times. Run RA-UCB with B = 700s per student, compare cumulative regret vs. oracle over T=1000 students. Verify scaling ~√T.
2. **Criteo advertising simulation**: Build discrete Weibull model for impressions-before-click from Criteo logs. User budget B_u = session impressions. Run MG-UCB with unknown per-round budget. Check that discretized variant (Δ small) matches continuous water-filling and same regret curve.
3. **Synthetic exponential case**: Validate information-theoretic lower bound Ω(T^{1/3}) by testing near-optimal arm identification difficulty. Compare RA-UCB to RA-ETC (Explore-Then-Commit) and NO UCB (no confidence bounds), confirming √T vs T^{2/3} vs linear regret respectively.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Unknown and stochastic budgets without within-round switching**: The current MG-UCB allows switching within a round. Removing this assumption changes the problem fundamentally; characterizing the optimal policy and regret bounds remains open.
2. **Cascading reward model**: Extending to a setting where the round reward is binary (1 if at least one arm succeeds) is nontrivial due to interaction among arms and censored feedback.
3. **Tightness of lower bound**: The lower bound is Ω(T^{1/3}) while the best upper bound is Õ(√T). Is the true minimax rate T^{1/3} or √T? Resolving the gap requires either an improved lower bound or a matching upper bound algorithm.
4. **Budget-dependent activation probability**: The model assumes constant p_i. If p_i depends on allocated budget x_i, the factorization and estimation become more complex; new algorithms and analysis are needed.
5. **Multiple resource types**: Extending to vector-valued allocations (e.g., time and money) under censored feedback is open. The curse of dimensionality and lack of separable structure pose challenges.

## Limitations
- Surrogate generalizability: The construction of eG for Weibull distributions with shape k>1 is not fully specified; strong concavity (needed for poly-log regret) may fail, relying on unverified Oracle Monotonicity.
- Discretization error: For MG-UCB-Δ, the analysis of step-size Δ introduces an additive O(ΔT) term that is not explicitly bounded; the condition Δ = o(1/√T) is suggested but not proven sufficient for the claimed regret order.
- Implementation gaps: Tie-breaking rules for equal marginal gains are not detailed; confidence radius constant (3log t/(2n)) is taken from proofs but its empirical sensitivity is unknown.

## Confidence
- Lower bound Ω(T^{1/3}): High (information-theoretic proof, consistent with censored bandit hardness).
- RA-UCB eO(√T) regret: Medium (analysis hinges on Oracle Monotonicity and surrogate properties; strong concavity gives poly-log but is distribution-restricted).
- MG-UCB equivalence: Medium-Low (continuous water-filling equivalence assumes idealized infinitesimal steps; discretization may degrade order).
- Empirical validation on real data: Medium (datasets used but scaling matched; however, real-world complexity may hide model mismatches).

## Next Checks
1. **Numerical concavity test**: For Weibull(k>1) with fitted λ ranges, compute ∂_{xx}eG(x,λ,λ') across x∈[0,B_max] to verify non-positivity; identify parameter regimes where concavity fails.
2. **Δ-sensitivity experiment**: Run MG-UCB-Δ with Δ ∈ {0.1B, 0.01B, 0.001B} on synthetic data; fit regret curve to isolate O(ΔT) term and confirm Δ ≤ o(1/√T) suffices.
3. **Tie-breaking stress test**: Compare regret under deterministic (arm-index) vs. random tie-breaking when marginal gains are equal; quantify variance increase and potential order deviation.
