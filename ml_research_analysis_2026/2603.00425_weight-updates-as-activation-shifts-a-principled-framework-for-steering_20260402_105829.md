# Weight Updates as Activation Shifts: A Principled Framework for Steering
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00425
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00425
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Post-block activation steering achieves near full fine-tuning performance (within 0.2%–0.9%) with only 0.04% trainable parameters, and joint adaptation with LoRA via an orthogonality constraint can exceed full fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a theoretical equivalence between small weight updates and activation-space steering interventions for transformer MLP blocks. It identifies the post-block residual stream (after the final skip connection) as the theoretically optimal locus for steering, as it captures the full layer update. Building on this, the authors propose a simple post-block steering method using global linear bottleneck adapters, which matches full fine-tuning efficiency across tasks. Furthermore, they demonstrate that weight-based (LoRA) and activation steering updates are complementary; when trained jointly with an orthogonality constraint to prevent subspace collapse, the combined method surpasses both individual approaches and can even exceed full fine-tuning, offering a new paradigm for parameter-efficient adaptation.

## Method Summary
The core method involves inserting a low-rank linear adapter (`h → h + W₂ϕ(W₁h)`) at the **post-block output** of every transformer layer. For joint adaptation, a standard LoRA is applied to the MLP's weight matrices (`W_g`, `W_u`, `W_d`) alongside the post-block adapter. To prevent the two updates from learning redundant subspaces, an orthogonality constraint is enforced: after each backward step, the steering adapter's output projector `W₂` is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the LoRA update's output space (spanned by the right singular vectors of the LoRA `B` matrix). This ensures each method contributes distinct directions to the residual update.

## Key Results
- Post-block steering (0.04% params) achieves average accuracy within 0.2%–0.9% of full fine-tuning across Llama and Pythia models on GLUE and reasoning tasks, significantly outperforming ReFT and LoRA at comparable parameter budgets.
- Joint training with orthogonality constraint surpasses the best individual method (LoRA or steering) by up to 3.8% and exceeds full fine-tuning on several tasks.
- The linear bottleneck adapter (`ϕ=id`) is sufficient; adding a non-linearity like SiLU yields marginal gains at the cost of potential instability on small models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Activation steering can replicate full weight fine-tuning under a first-order approximation, differing only by a term arising from intervention location relative to the MLP's skip connection.
- **Mechanism:** A first-order Taylor expansion shows a post-MLP steering update (`GLU(h) → GLU(h) + δh`) accounts for the `(ΔW_d)m` term missing from a pre-MLP update (`GLU(h+δh)`). Equivalence holds for small perturbations where `O(||Δh||²)` terms are negligible.
- **Core assumption:** Perturbations (`δh`, `ΔW`) are small; the MLP's feature map is smooth (differentiable non-linearity).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The post-block output (after the skip connection) is theoretically optimal because it captures the complete residual stream update, including attention and skip contributions.
- **Mechanism:** An oracle (exact difference between fine-tuned and base hidden states) shows MLP outputs constitute only 40–70% of a block's total output norm. Post-block intervention thus has access to the full layer update, making it strictly more expressive than steering confined to the MLP pathway.
- **Core assumption:** Per-layer hidden state shift can be approximated by a linear adapter applied post-block; residual stream is the primary functional update locus.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Jointly training weight (LoRA) and activation (post-block) updates surpasses either alone because they learn complementary, orthogonal subspaces of the residual update.
- **Mechanism:** Without constraint, weight and activation updates converge to the same dominant subspace early in training (gradient alignment), causing redundancy. Enforcing orthogonality on output projectors (`W₂` for steering, `B` for LoRA) forces distinct directions, effectively increasing total update rank.
- **Core assumption:** The residual error has directions partitionable between weight and activation updates; top singular vectors of `RY⊤` and `RF⊤` are initially aligned on real data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: First-order Taylor expansions in deep learning**
  - *Why needed:* The entire equivalence analysis approximates the effect of small weight/activation changes on MLP output, neglecting `O(||δ||²)` terms to simplify comparison to a linear mapping.
  - *Quick check:* Under what conditions does the first-order approximation break down, and how might that affect the paper's conclusions about equivalence?
- **Concept: Subspace alignment and singular value decomposition (SVD)**
  - *Why needed:* The expressivity analysis (Theorem 3.1) and joint training redundancy (Proposition 4.2) rely on understanding alignment of dominant subspaces of data matrices (`X+Y`, `RY⊤`, `RF⊤`).
  - *Quick check:* In Proposition 4.2, why do the left singular vectors of `RY⊤` and `RF⊤` tend to be aligned on real datasets like Winogrande?
- **Concept: Orthogonal projections and constrained optimization**
  - *Why needed:* The orthogonality constraint in joint adaptation is implemented via projection `(I - VV⊤)W₂`, where `V` spans the LoRA update's output space, ensuring the steering adapter operates in the orthogonal complement.
  - *Quick check:* How does the orthogonality constraint on output projectors relate to the functional goal of preventing "subspace collapse" in the residual update?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Transformer block → Attention → Residual Add → LayerNorm → MLP (GLU) → Residual Add → [Post-block adapter inserted here]
- **Critical path:**
  1. Identify intervention site as post-block output (`h_block = h_attn + h_mlp`).
  2. Apply a global bottleneck adapter at every layer (not sparse selection).
  3. For joint training, add LoRA to MLP weights (`W_g`, `W_u`, `W_d`). After each backward step, compute LoRA's output space via SVD of `B`; project steering adapter's `W₂` onto orthogonal complement.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Expressivity vs. parameters:* Post-block is most expressive but requires global application; sparse intervention reduces params but may lose benefit.
  - *Linearity vs. non-linearity:* Linear adapters (`ϕ=id`) suffice; adding `SiLU` gives marginal gains at cost of slight instability on small models.
  - *Global vs. local:* Applying adapters at all layers consistently outperforms layer-selection heuristics (cf. ReFT's sparse variant).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Pre-MLP or post-MLP steering leads to larger gaps to SFT (Table 1: ReFT gaps up to 6.5% avg), especially on tasks requiring attention updates (ListOps).
  - Naive joint training without orthogonality yields no gain over strongest individual method (Table 5: "Joint" often < "LoRA (r=16)").
  - High cosine similarity (>0.5) between steering shift and LoRA shift across layers indicates subspace collapse (Figure 5, "Vanilla").
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate post-block locus: Train linear adapters at pre-MLP, post-MLP, and post-block on BoolQ. Post-block should match SFT within ~1% while others lag by >3%.
  2. Test orthogonality constraint: Run joint training (LoRA + post-block adapter) with/without projection. Without, measure cosine similarity of per-layer output shifts (should be diagonal); with, ensure improvement on GSM8K exceeds LoRA alone.
  3. Parameter efficiency: Compare rank-1/vector steerers vs. rank-8 at same param budget. Higher rank should not significantly improve linear variants (Fig 4), confirming linear adapter sufficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under what conditions does the first-order equivalence between weight updates and activation steering break down when scaling to larger perturbation magnitudes?
2. Is global application of post-block steering across all layers necessary, or can a selective subset of layers achieve comparable performance?
3. Is the orthogonality constraint between weight and activation update subspaces optimal for joint adaptation, or could a learned complementarity metric (e.g., via contrastive loss) yield better results?
4. Does the post-block steering paradigm and joint adaptation framework extend to non-NLP modalities (e.g., vision transformers) or to decoder-only architectures with markedly different activation distributions (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts)?

## Limitations
- Theoretical scope limited to single GLU-based MLP blocks; generalization to attention layers, non-GLU MLPs, or multi-block interactions is not formally established.
- Framework assumes linear bottleneck adapters; non-linearities are marginally beneficial but not theoretically justified.
- Global post-block application may incur non-trivial inference overhead not measured.
- Empirical evaluation on limited models (Llama, Pythia) and tasks; performance on very large models, multimodal architectures, or non-English tasks is unknown.

## Confidence
- **First-order equivalence (Mechanism 1):** High. Derivation is explicit and standard for small perturbations in smooth systems.
- **Post-block optimality (Mechanism 2):** Medium. Strong empirical evidence and geometric reasoning support it, but "theoretically optimal" holds within the class of linear, per-layer interventions.
- **Joint training with orthogonality (Mechanism 3):** Medium-Low. Compelling empirical gains (up to 3.8%, exceeding full FT) but shown on limited dataset mix; consistency across diverse tasks and scales needs further confirmation.
