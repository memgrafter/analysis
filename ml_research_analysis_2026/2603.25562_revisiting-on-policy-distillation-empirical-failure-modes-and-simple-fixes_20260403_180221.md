# Revisiting On-Policy Distillation: Empirical Failure Modes and Simple Fixes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.25562
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25562
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposed teacher top‑K local support matching method improves single‑task math average to 41.5 and multi‑task math to 41.7 while preserving strong agentic performance (97.7% ALFWorld success) versus sampled‑token OPD baselines.

## Executive Summary
Sampled-token on‑policy distillation (OPD) for long‑horizon LLM post‑training is fragile due to three failure modes: an imbalanced one‑token learning signal where most sampled tokens yield negative rewards, unreliable teacher guidance on student‑generated prefixes as rollouts drift, and distortions from tokenizer or special‑token mismatches. The paper introduces **teacher top‑K local support matching**, which replaces single‑token comparison with a distribution‑level comparison over the teacher’s top‑K token support. Implemented as truncated reverse‑KL with top‑p rollout sampling and special‑token masking, this approach yields more stable optimization (smaller gradient norms, lower clipping ratios) and better downstream performance in both single‑task and multi‑task settings.

## Method Summary
The core method, teacher top‑K local support matching, operates as follows: for each prefix during student rollout, the teacher model provides its top‑K highest‑probability tokens, forming a local support set. Both teacher and student distributions are renormalized over this set, and the reverse‑KL divergence is computed between them. This spreads the learning signal across multiple plausible tokens rather than concentrating on a single sampled token. To keep rollouts in regions where the teacher signal is reliable, student generations use top‑p (nucleus) sampling (p=0.9). Special tokens (e.g., `<think>`, `<EOS>`) are masked to avoid penalties from tokenization mismatches. The loss is summed over all token positions in the rollout and used to update the student via gradient descent.

## Key Results
- Single‑task math reasoning (Qwen2.5‑7B‑Instruct student, OpenThinker3‑7B teacher): average score 41.5 vs 40.7 for sampled‑token OPD with masking.
- Multi‑task training (alternating math and ALFWorld): math average improves from 36.6 to 41.7; ALFWorld success rate reaches 97.7% with masking.
- Ablations confirm that top‑p rollouts and support renormalization are necessary for stability and performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Balanced Local Supervision via Top‑K Support Matching
Replaces the imbalanced one‑token signal with a distribution‑level comparison over the teacher’s locally plausible token support. The teacher’s top‑K tokens define a support set; both distributions are renormalized over it before computing reverse‑KL. This spreads updates across multiple tokens, reducing variance. **Break condition**: K too small (e.g., 1) degenerates to sampled‑token OPD; K too large increases compute and may introduce noise from low‑probability tokens.

### Rollout Stabilization via Top‑p Sampling
Top‑p (nucleus) sampling during rollout generation trims the tail of the student distribution, keeping trajectories in regions where the teacher’s distribution is well‑calibrated and the local signal is reliable. **Break condition**: p too low limits exploration and risks collapse; p too high reduces stabilization effect.

### Tokenization Artifact Mitigation via Special‑Token Masking
Masks problematic special tokens (e.g., `<think>`, `<EOS>`) to avoid spurious penalties when teacher and student tokenizers produce different IDs for semantically identical content. This is especially important for token‑level losses, though the local support method is less sensitive. **Break condition**: Over‑masking may discard genuinely useful tokens; mismatches within regular vocabulary remain unaddressed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reverse KL divergence**: Mode‑seeking divergence used in the truncated loss; understanding its contrast with forward KL is key to why it avoids averaging over low‑probability modes.
- **On‑policy sampling and exposure bias**: Student‑generated rollouts can drift from the data distribution; OPD attempts to mitigate this by using the current policy, but drift still causes teacher unreliability.
- **Subword tokenization**: Different tokenizers split text differently, leading to token‑ID mismatches that produce false negatives in token‑level distillation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input prompt → Student rollout generator (top‑p sampling) → Prefix computation → Teacher top‑K query → Distribution renormalization → Loss calculator (truncated reverse‑KL with masking) → Optimizer → Student model update.

**Critical path**: For each generated token position, compute the prefix, query the teacher for top‑K tokens, renormalize teacher and student distributions over that support, compute the KL loss, accumulate over the rollout, and backpropagate.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Support size K: larger K reduces bias but increases compute and may include irrelevant tokens; smaller K increases bias and risk of degeneracy.
- Top‑p value: balances stability (lower p) vs. exploration (higher p); requires task‑specific tuning.
- Masking: prevents tokenization noise but may remove useful signal if over‑applied.
- Renormalization: essential for numerical stability; omission causes training collapse.
- Cost: full reverse‑KL is intractable; truncation makes it feasible but introduces approximation error.

**Failure signatures**:
- Gradient norm spikes or high clipping ratios indicate unstable updates.
- Growing teacher‑student log‑prob gap on sampled tokens signals misalignment.
- Repetition loops, over‑long chain‑of‑thought, or gibberish outputs in rollouts.
- Sudden drop in downstream evaluation metrics (e.g., AIME24 score).
- Degradation on special‑token heavy tasks if masking is omitted.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Ablate top‑p and renormalization on single‑task math: train with/without each, track gradient norms and AIME24 avg@32 to confirm necessity.
2. Token‑mismatch analysis: compare masked vs. unmasked runs on a held‑out set, visualizing token‑level teacher‑student probability alignment for special tokens.
3. Support size sweep: vary K (e.g., 16, 32, 48, 64), plot training reward and final benchmark averages to identify stability regions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How should the sampled token be incorporated into the teacher top‑K local support? Hybrid support definitions (e.g., teacher top‑K + sampled token) show mixed results across benchmarks.
2. Are importance‑weighting corrections necessary when the rollout policy (e.g., top‑p) differs from the training policy to reduce off‑policy bias?
3. How does OPD compare to reinforcement learning (RL) for multi‑task transfer, and when does teacher‑guided transfer align with environment reward?
4. Does local support matching consistently mitigate forgetting in continual learning scenarios under distribution shift?
5. What is the causal mechanism behind teacher signal unreliability on student prefixes—sharp teacher distributions, distributional shift, or both? How can it be formally quantified?
6. Can teacher uncertainty be better exploited (e.g., entropy‑weighted KL or Bayesian variants) to improve robustness?
7. How do local support methods combine with other stabilization techniques (EMA anchors, perturbation corrections, logit fusion) and are interactions synergistic?

## Limitations
- Rollout details (temperature, number of rollouts) and multi‑task scheduling (alternating frequency, batch mixing) are ambiguously specified.
- Exact special‑token mask list and tokenizer compatibility details are unclear; masking is a heuristic.
- Scalability beyond 7B models and to a broader set of tasks remains unevaluated.
- Required teacher‑student performance gap for effective transfer is not quantified.
- The method’s sensitivity to support size K and top‑p threshold is only partially mapped.

## Confidence
- **Top‑K support matching**: High (theoretically sound, ablations confirm renormalization necessity).
- **Top‑p rollouts**: Medium (benefit shown, but sensitivity to p and interaction with K not fully mapped).
- **Special‑token masking**: Low (heuristic, lacking systematic validation across tokenizers).
- **Overall downstream improvement**: Medium (modest gains on two benchmarks; broader generalization untested).

## Next Checks
1. Sweep K ∈ {16,32,64,128} and top‑p ∈ {0.8,0.9,0.95,1.0} to map stability/performance trade‑offs.
2. Test cross‑tokenizer (e.g., Llama‑3‑8B) without masking to isolate masking effect and assess generalizability.
3. Scale multi‑task training to 5+ diverse domains; track per‑task metrics to detect interference and verify top‑p robustness.
