# FRIEND: Federated Learning for Joint Optimization of multi-RIS Configuration and Eavesdropper Intelligent Detection in B5G Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10977
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10977
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FL + RIS integration improves average secrecy rate by ~30% vs. non-RIS baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes FRIEND, a federated learning (FL) framework for secure Beyond 5G networks that integrate cell-free mmWave architecture with multiple reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS). The method trains a deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) on locally observed channel state information (CSI) at access points without sharing raw data, while jointly optimizing multi-RIS phase shifts to enhance eavesdropper detection. Experiments show a ~30% improvement in average secrecy rate over non-RIS baselines, robust detection accuracy (0.71–0.93), and an early-exit mechanism that cuts inference time by up to 45% while maintaining accuracy above 0.83.

## Method Summary
FRIEND employs a standard FL client-server architecture. Each access point (AP) acts as an FL client, training a local DCNN on its partitioned CSI dataset (generated by a MATLAB simulator) and sending weight updates to a central server. The server aggregates updates via Federated Averaging (FedAvg) to update a global DCNN model, which is redistributed to all APs. The DCNN processes CSI represented as 2D "images" alongside metadata (UE/AP/RIS coordinates, power). An early-exit branch after the second convolutional block allows samples with high confidence to exit inference prematurely, reducing computational load. The evaluation simulates joint optimization with a RIS controller that adjusts phase shifts to maximize secrecy rate, using a cell-free topology with 18 APs, 3 RIS, and 500 UEs at 28 GHz.

## Key Results
- FL + RIS integration improves average secrecy rate by ~30% compared to non-RIS baseline methods.
- DCNN achieves robust detection metrics: accuracy 0.71–0.93, high F1-score.
- Early-exit mechanism reduces inference time by up to 45% while maintaining accuracy >0.83.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Federated Learning for Privacy-Preserving Detection
**Claim:** FL enables distributed eavesdropper detection without sharing raw CSI.  
**Mechanism:** APs act as FL clients, training local DCNNs on their CSI partitions and sending weight updates to a central aggregator, which uses FedAvg to synthesize a global model. This avoids raw data transmission while aggregating knowledge from diverse channel conditions.  
**Core assumption:** Local data distributions across APs are not highly non-IID, allowing FedAvg to converge to a useful global model that generalizes.  
**Evidence:** The abstract and Section IV.B explicitly state that raw data is not exchanged and FedAvg is used. However, the paper does not quantify non-IIDness or test convergence under extreme heterogeneity.  
**Break condition:** Extreme non-IID data across APs may cause FL divergence or a globally degraded model.

### Mechanism 2: Joint Optimization with Multi-RIS
**Claim:** Jointly optimizing multi-RIS configuration with the FL detector improves average secrecy rate (ASR).  
**Mechanism:** RIS elements adjust phase shifts to constructively boost signals for legitimate UEs and cause destructive interference for eavesdroppers. The FL-trained DCNN leverages this RIS-enhanced CSI for more accurate detection, and RIS configuration is jointly optimized in simulation to maximize ASR.  
**Core assumption:** RIS reconfiguration timescale matches channel dynamics and channel estimation is sufficiently accurate.  
**Evidence:** The abstract reports ~30% ASR gain; Section III.B includes RIS in the channel model; Section V.B shows ASR variation across RIS phases.  
**Break condition:** Noisy/outdated channel estimates or slow RIS reconfiguration relative to UE mobility would degrade the benefit.

### Mechanism 3: Early-Exit for Computational Efficiency
**Claim:** An early-exit mechanism reduces inference time at resource-constrained APs while maintaining acceptable accuracy.  
**Mechanism:** An auxiliary classifier attached after the second convolutional block enables early termination if a sample’s confidence exceeds a predefined threshold (55% or 70%), skipping remaining layers.  
**Core assumption:** A significant portion of CSI samples can be confidently classified from intermediate features.  
**Evidence:** Section IV.A describes the design; Section V.A reports up to 45% time reduction with accuracy >0.83.  
**Break condition:** Poorly tuned thresholds increase error rates or yield negligible latency savings.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for structured data.**  
*Why needed:* CSI is transformed into 2D images where spatial correlations (e.g., multipath) are critical; CNNs effectively extract local patterns from grid-like data.  
*Quick check:* Why might a fully connected network be less suitable than a CNN for processing a channel matrix input?

**Concept: Federated Averaging (FedAvg).**  
*Why needed:* It balances communication efficiency with model convergence in distributed FL, allowing infrequent global aggregation with local multi-epoch training.  
*Quick check:* What is the key parameter in FedAvg that controls how often global aggregation occurs relative to local training epochs?

**Concept: Physical Layer Security (PLS) metrics – Secrecy Rate & SINR.**  
*Why needed:* The ultimate goal is secure communication, quantified by Secrecy Rate (difference between legitimate and eavesdropper channel capacities).  
*Quick check:* If the SINR of the eavesdropper channel exceeds that of the legitimate channel, what is the resulting Secrecy Rate and what does it imply?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
MATLAB Simulator (generates CSI datasets + metadata) -> FL Clients (at APs, train local DCNNs) -> Central FL Server (aggregates via FedAvg, broadcasts global model) -> FL Clients; RIS Controller (integrated in simulator, adjusts phase shifts for joint optimization).

**Critical path:**  
1. Define network topology (APs, RIS, UEs, eavesdroppers) in MATLAB.  
2. Run channel simulation to generate CSI dataset.  
3. Split dataset per AP (non-IID by location/RIS association).  
4. Initialize global DCNN model.  
5. For each FL round: each AP trains locally for E epochs, sends weights; server averages and updates global model.  
6. Evaluate global model on test set (accuracy, F1) and compute ASR for selected RIS configurations.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *FL vs. Centralized:* Privacy/scalability (FL) vs. faster convergence/heterogeneity handling (centralized).  
- *RIS Complexity vs. Gain:* More RIS elements increase beamforming flexibility but raise channel estimation overhead and optimization search space.  
- *Early-Exit Threshold:* Lower threshold (e.g., 55%) saves more compute but risks accuracy drop; higher threshold (e.g., 70%) is safer but offers less latency reduction.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *FL divergence:* Global model accuracy stagnates or deteriorates across rounds, often due to high non-IID data.  
- *Poor detection:* Low recall for eavesdropper class (<0.8) indicates model cannot distinguish malicious CSI patterns.  
- *ASR mismatch:* A RIS phase yields high DCNN accuracy but low ASR (or vice versa), suggesting detector decisions are misaligned with physical-layer secrecy.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Baseline FL Convergence Test:* Run FL with a simple network on a small dataset (e.g., 2 APs, fixed RIS) to verify pipeline convergence.  
2. *Single RIS Impact Isolation:* Fix FL model, sweep one RIS’s phase shifts, record DCNN accuracy and oracle ASR; correlate accuracy with ASR.  
3. *Early-Exit Threshold Sweep:* With best RIS phase, run inference at varying confidence thresholds (50%, 60%, 70%, 80%); plot inference time vs. accuracy to find the Pareto frontier.

## Limitations
- The robustness of FL convergence under high non-IID data across APs is not validated; the core assumption of sufficient similarity may not hold in realistic cell-free deployments.  
- The claimed synergy between FL detector and multi-RIS is demonstrated only in simulation, with unclear real-time integration and a potential reality gap.  
- The early-exit mechanism’s effectiveness under varying channel conditions, noise levels, or hardware constraints is not explored; fixed thresholds may not generalize.

## Confidence
- **FL for privacy-preserving detection:** **High** – Uses standard FL and FedAvg, a well-understood paradigm.  
- **RIS joint optimization for ASR gain:** **Medium** – Positive simulation results but lacking real-world validation or details on optimization loop.  
- **Early-exit for computational savings:** **Medium-Low** – Results from a single controlled simulation; sensitivity and hardware impact unexamined.

## Next Checks
1. Stress-test FL convergence by deliberately maximizing data non-IIDness across AP clients (e.g., each AP sees CSI from a unique RIS subset) and measure global model decay.  
2. Correlate detection accuracy with instantaneous secrecy rate on a held-out test set via statistical analysis (e.g., Pearson correlation) to validate alignment beyond averaged simulation.  
3. Benchmark early-exit against an adaptive threshold that adjusts based on input SNR or channel randomness to assess robustness across varying channel qualities.
