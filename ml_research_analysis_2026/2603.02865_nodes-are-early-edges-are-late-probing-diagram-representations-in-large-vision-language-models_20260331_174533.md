# Nodes Are Early, Edges Are Late: Probing Diagram Representations in Large Vision-Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02865
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02865
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Edge information (e.g., direction, style) is not linearly separable in the vision encoder and only becomes linearly decodable in the language model's text tokens, causing a "delayed emergence."

## Executive Summary
This study investigates why large vision-language models (LVLMs) struggle with relational understanding in diagrams, particularly edges like arrows. Using a synthetic directed-graph dataset and linear probing, the authors analyze where node, edge, and global structural information becomes linearly decodable within the model. Results show node attributes and global counts are linearly separable in individual image patches within the vision encoder, while edge attributes are not linearly separable there and only become so in the text tokens of the language model. Causal intervention confirms that corrupting these linearly encoded vision features damages performance on node-related and global questions, but not on edge-related ones. This delayed emergence of edge representations explains LVLMs' persistent difficulty with relational diagram tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors generate synthetic directed-graph diagrams with controlled attributes (colors, shapes, edge styles/directions, counts) to isolate visual elements. For each concept (e.g., "color of node A"), they train linear probes on hidden states extracted from every layer of the vision encoder (per patch) and the language model (per token). Probe accuracy indicates whether the concept is explicitly encoded in a linearly separable way at that position. They also perform causal interventions: for vision patches with high probing accuracy on a given concept, they replace those patch embeddings with a mean vector and measure the drop in VQA accuracy, testing whether the linearly decodable information is causally necessary. Evaluation uses fixed-layout test diagrams to avoid data leakage from probe training.

## Key Results
- Node attributes (color, shape) and global counts (total nodes, degrees) become linearly decodable from individual vision patches, especially in deeper encoder layers, with accuracy localized to patches containing the relevant node.
- Edge attributes (color, style, direction, existence, multi-hop path) remain near chance-level when probed on any single vision patch across all encoder layers but become linearly separable only in text tokens of the language model that reference the edge.
- Causal intervention (corrupting high-accuracy vision patches) significantly reduces VQA performance on node/global tasks but has minimal effect on edge-related tasks (except Edge Direction, which is at chance overall), indicating a causal reliance on vision-encoded linear codes for nodes/globals but not for edges.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Edge information (e.g., direction, style) is not linearly separable within individual image patches of the vision encoder and only becomes linearly decodable in the text-conditioned tokens of the language model, causing a "delayed emergence."
- **Mechanism:** The vision encoder processes local, object-like features (nodes) effectively, but relational information (edges) requires compositional integration across spatially distinct patches. This integration is deferred to the language model layers where text tokens act as query points that dynamically aggregate and bind information from multiple visual patches.
- **Core assumption:** Linear probing accuracy (MaxAcc) is a valid indicator of explicit, readily accessible representation. If a linear classifier can decode information from a single patch/token, that information is explicitly represented there.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "edge information is not linearly separable in the vision encoder and becomes linearly encoded only in the text tokens in the language model."
  - [section 5.3]: "For aspects in the Single and Global categories, accuracy increases gradually with depth... accuracies for aspects in the Multiple category remain relatively low... edge information becomes linearly encoded in a single text token."
- **Break condition:** If edge attributes become linearly decodable from single vision patches in other architectures/datasets, the "delayed emergence" mechanism would not hold universally.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Node attributes and global counts are linearly decodable from individual vision patches because they are spatially local or can be aggregated via simple pooling, whereas edge attributes inherently require relational binding across patches.
- **Mechanism:** The vision encoder's patch-based processing naturally encodes properties present within a single patch (e.g., a node's color/shape). Global counts may be distributed across background patches as a form of distributed storage. Edges, defined between two distinct nodes, cannot be captured in a single patch's representation without cross-patch integration, which the vision encoder's feed-forward/strictly local attention may not perform sufficiently for linear separability.
- **Core assumption:** A single patch's representation can only contain information about visual elements that are spatially contained within or immediately adjacent to that patch. Relationships spanning patches require explicit cross-attention mechanisms beyond the local receptive field.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.3]: "For aspects in the Single category, high-accuracy patches are localized around the bottom-right area where the target node A is located... For aspects in the Global category, accuracy rises above the threshold across almost all positions... Global information is distributed over a wide range of hidden states, including background regions."
  - [section 5.3]: "For aspects in the Multiple category... accuracies... remain relatively low... suggesting these aspects are more difficult to decode linearly from any single hidden state."
- **Break condition:** If edge direction/style become linearly decodable from patches containing one endpoint (e.g., the arrowhead), the mechanism that edges require cross-patch integration would be challenged.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The model causally relies on linearly decodable representations from the vision encoder for tasks involving node attributes and global counts, but for edge-related tasks, it may rely on non-linear representations or alternative pathways.
- **Mechanism:** Causal intervention (corrupting patches with high probing accuracy) significantly damages performance on node/global tasks, confirming those linear codes are used in the forward pass. For edge tasks, intervention effect is minimal, implying the answer is derived from information that was not linearly separable in the vision encoder—likely the late, text-token representations identified by probing, or other non-linear interactions.
- **Core assumption:** Replacing a patch's activation with the mean of other patches selectively destroys linearly decodable information while preserving (or even creating) other signals. A large drop in VQA accuracy indicates the corrupted information was causally necessary.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 6.2]: "For Node Color, Node Shape, Edge Color, Edge Style, Node Count, In-degree Count, and Out-degree Count, AccVQA decreased after the intervention... For Edge Existence and Multi-hop Path, we did not observe a decrease... For Edge Direction, the model’s accuracy is already near chance level."
  - [section 6.2]: Controlled random patch replacement caused much smaller drops, supporting the specificity of the intervention.
- **Break condition:** If intervention on edge-related patches (even those below threshold) also severely damages edge task performance, it would suggest reliance on non-linear codes spread more broadly, not just late text-token codes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Synthetic Controlled Dataset Design**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper isolates visual elements (nodes, edges, counts) from natural image confounds (textures, backgrounds, domain knowledge). Using a synthetic directed-graph dataset allows precise manipulation of one variable (e.g., edge color) while holding others constant, enabling clear attribution of probing results.
  - **Quick check question:** Why would using natural diagram images (e.g., from textbooks) be problematic for probing internal representations of basic visual elements?
- **Concept: Linear Probing as a Diagnostic Tool**
  - **Why needed here:** Probing tests whether a specific piece of information (e.g., "color of node A") is encoded in a format that is *linearly separable* from a single layer's hidden state. This is a standard, interpretable metric for "explicit" representation, contrasting with "implicit" information that might only be accessible via more complex readouts.
  - **Quick check question:** What does it mean if a linear probe achieves chance-level accuracy on a target concept from all vision encoder patches? What might that imply about the model's internal representation?
- **Concept: LVLM Architecture Pipeline (Vision Encoder → Projector/Adapter → LLM)**
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding *where* (which component) and *when* (which layer) information becomes linearly decodable requires knowing the model's data flow. The paper probes the vision encoder's image patches and the LLM's image-conditioned and text-conditioned tokens separately.
  - **Quick check question:** In a typical LVLM, what is the purpose of the "projector" or "adapter" layer between the vision encoder and the language model? How might it transform visual representations?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Diagram → Vision Encoder (ViT-style) → Projector/Adapter → LLM (Decoder-only)
- **Critical path:**
  The diagram is split into patches processed by the vision encoder. Patch embeddings are projected and fed as continuous tokens into the LLM, interleaved with text question tokens. The LLM generates answers autoregressively. Probing extracts activations at any (layer, position) to train linear classifiers. Causal intervention replaces selected vision patch embeddings (before projection) with a mean vector and measures VQA accuracy drop.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Early vs. Late Fusion:** Fusing visual tokens early as input allows the LLM to perform relational binding conditioned on text, which is beneficial for edge reasoning but may be inefficient if the vision encoder could have extracted strong relational features (which it apparently does not for edges).
  - **Synthetic vs. Real Data:** Synthetic data offers control and interpretability but may not capture the complexity and noise of real diagrams. Findings may not generalize to diagrams with overlapping elements, varied scales, or embedded natural language.
  - **Linear Probe vs. Nonlinear Readout:** Linear probes test for *explicit* codes. A concept might be present but only decodable with a complex probe, meaning the model can use it without it being "linearly separable." The paper's causal intervention partly addresses this by showing that the *linearly decodable* info is *causally used*.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Edge-Direction Blindness:** VQA accuracy near chance for edge direction questions.
  2. **Patch Localization Mismatch:** Probing heatmaps show no patch with above-threshold accuracy for edge attributes in the vision encoder.
  3. **Intervention Insensitivity:** Corrupting vision patches with high edge-probing accuracy does *not* reduce edge-direction VQA performance.
  4. **Text-Token Aggregation:** Probing accuracy for edge attributes spikes only at text tokens referencing the specific edge (e.g., "between node A and node B").
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate VQA Baseline:** Run the synthetic diagram VQA (questions from Table 4) on the target LVLM (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B) to confirm baseline performance patterns (good on nodes/global, poor on edges).
  2. **Vision Encoder Probing:** For each aspect (Node Color, Edge Direction, etc.), train linear probes on each layer's patch embeddings. Plot MaxAcc vs. layer and heatmaps of per-patch accuracy (as in Figures 3-4). Verify: node/global info rises in deeper layers and localizes; edge info stays near chance.
  3. **Causal Intervention:** For a node-related aspect (e.g., Node Color) and an edge-related aspect (e.g., Edge Style), identify patches in the final vision encoder layer where probing accuracy > threshold. Replace those patches' vectors with the mean vector of other patches. Measure ΔAccVQA. Expect large drop for node aspect, minimal drop for edge aspect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do LVLMs combine basic node and edge attributes to represent higher-order diagram structures (e.g., graph motifs, subgraph isomorphisms)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] In the Limitations section: "Although complex information may be represented by combining these cues, how the model represents composite visual patterns and higher-order structures remains unexamined."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only investigates basic visual aspects (color, shape, degree counts, etc.) and does not test whether these basic features can be integrated by the model to understand more abstract structural patterns. Without understanding how these features compose, we cannot fully explain LVLM capabilities on real-world diagram tasks that often require recognizing complex relational patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up study with synthetic datasets that vary higher-order graph properties (e.g., presence of specific subgraphs, graph isomorphism classes, cyclic vs acyclic patterns) and use probing or causal methods to test if and where these higher-level structures become linearly decodable within the model, perhaps also correlating with performance on downstream VQA tasks requiring such reasoning.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the nonlinear mechanisms that LVLMs employ for edge-related attributes (e.g., Edge Existence, Multi-hop Path) that appear resistant to linear probing and causal intervention in the vision encoder?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] From Causal Intervention: "For Edge Existence and Multi-hop Path, we did not observe a decrease in AccVQA due to the intervention. This suggests that the model may rely on information that is encoded nonlinearly for inference."
- **Why unresolved:** The intervention analysis showed that corrupting linearly decodable vision features does not impact these aspects, indicating they are not primarily supported by linearly separable representations in the vision encoder. Yet the model achieves above-chance accuracy on these tasks (except Edge Direction), so there must be alternative forms of encoding or processing that are not captured by linear probes. Understanding these mechanisms could explain why edges are generally challenging and how to improve them.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Applying more expressive probing (e.g., using deeper networks or polynomial probes) to the vision encoder activations for these aspects; analyzing neuron-level activations or attention patterns that correlate with edge existence or path queries; performing targeted ablations on specific layers or heads to identify which parts of the network are critical for these tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent do the discovered representational patterns (early linear encoding of node attributes and global counts; late linear encoding of edge attributes in the language model) generalize to real-world diagram understanding benchmarks, which often contain richer visual complexity and natural language annotations?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] In the Limitations: "We use synthetic diagrams to minimize the influence of dataset biases... our setup does not necessarily capture all complex situations found in real-world diagrams."
- **Why unresolved:** The controlled synthetic setup isolates variables, but real-world diagrams (e.g., flowcharts, circuit diagrams, scientific figures) include varied rendering styles, overlapping elements, and domain-specific symbols that could alter how information is encoded. The findings might not directly transfer, and there may be additional representational phenomena in more natural settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running the same probing and causal intervention experiments on a diverse set of real-world diagram VQA datasets (e.g., FlowchartQA, ChartQA, SciCAP, Diagram VQA) and comparing the layer-wise and position-wise accessibility of node, edge, and global features. Cross-dataset consistency would support generality; deviations would reveal new factors.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Within the language model component, what architectural mechanisms (e.g., specific layers, attention heads, or feed-forward networks) are responsible for constructing linearly separable representations of edge information from the vision encoder outputs?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The probing results show that edge attributes only become linearly decodable in the text tokens of the language model, whereas node and global features are decodable already in the vision encoder. However, the paper does not drill down into which internal components of the language model enable this transformation.
- **Why unresolved:** Identifying the precise computational steps that convert vision features into edge representations would provide insight into why edge information is delayed and how the model reasons about relationships. This could inform architecture modifications to better handle relational data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Performing targeted layer ablation or head pruning (e.g., zeroing out specific attention heads or MLPs) in the language model and measuring the impact on edge-related VQA performance; using activation patching to trace the flow of edge-relevant information from vision encoder to text tokens; training probes on intermediate representations within the language model to map the progression of edge encoding across layers.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic dataset generalizability:** Findings may not transfer to real-world diagrams with noise, overlapping elements, or natural language, limiting ecological validity.
- **Linear probe assumption:** MaxAcc as a proxy for "explicit" representation is indirect; edge information could be present but non-linearly encoded in vision patches.
- **Causal intervention specificity:** The patch selection threshold (τ) and mean-replacement method may inadvertently destroy non-linear signals or preserve some linear ones, complicating interpretation.

## Confidence
- **Node/Global early vision encoding:** High – Strong, localized probing accuracy increases in deeper vision layers, validated by targeted intervention drops.
- **Edge delayed emergence in LM tokens:** Medium – Consistent probing pattern across edge attributes, but relies on synthetic data and linear separability criteria; alternative architectures may differ.
- **Causal reliance on linearly decodable codes:** Medium – Intervention effects are selective and align with probing, but assumes linear codes are necessary and that replacement only affects those codes.

## Next Checks
1. **Probe patches containing arrowheads/edges directly:** Retrain linear probes *only* on patches visually containing arrowheads or edge segments. If edge direction/style become linearly separable here, it challenges the cross-patch integration requirement.
2. **Test a different LVLM architecture (e.g., LLaVA, InternVL):** Replicate probing and intervention on an LVLM with a different vision-to-LM fusion strategy (e.g., deeper cross-attention vs. simple token concatenation) to assess architectural dependency.
3. **Measure probing on perturbed natural diagrams:** Collect a small set of real textbook diagrams, synthetically edit edge attributes (color, direction) while keeping nodes identical, and run probing. This tests whether the "delayed emergence" pattern holds under natural visual clutter.
