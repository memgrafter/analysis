# Unifying approach to uniform expressivity of graph neural networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18409
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18409
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** For any finite set of templates T, the uniform expressivity of bounded counting T-GNNs is exactly captured by the graded template modal logic GML(T), unifying AC-GNNs, AC⁺-GNNs, and k‑hop subgraph GNNs.

## Executive Summary
Graph neural networks (GNNs) based on neighborhood aggregation are known to be limited by the 1‑Weisfeiler‑Leman (1‑WL) test. This paper overcomes that limitation by introducing Template GNNs (T‑GNNs), a framework that updates node features by aggregating embeddings of specified graph templates (small subgraph patterns). Alongside T‑GNNs, the authors define template‑based WL algorithms, graded bisimulation, and graded template modal logic (GML(T)). Their central contribution is a meta‑theorem proving that for any finite template set T, the uniform expressivity of bounded counting T‑GNNs coincides precisely with the logic GML(T). This result unifies several known GNN families—AC‑GNNs, AC⁺‑GNNs, and k‑hop subgraph GNNs—as special cases, showing that their expressive power corresponds to specific fragments of GML(T).

## Method Summary
The method revolves around Template GNNs (T‑GNNs). A T‑GNN processes a graph by first extracting all occurrences (injective homomorphisms) of a predefined finite collection of template graphs T. For each node, it aggregates the embeddings of the template occurrences that contain that node, using a permutation‑invariant operation (e.g., sum). This aggregation is followed by a neural update of the node feature. Over multiple layers, the model builds rich node representations that capture structural patterns beyond immediate neighbors. To analyze expressivity, the paper defines a template‑based Weisfeiler‑Leman procedure that iteratively colors nodes based on multisets of template‑local neighborhood colors. It also introduces graded bisimulation, a notion of equivalence between node‑labeled graphs that refines standard bisimulation by accounting for template counts. Finally, graded template modal logic (GML(T)) extends modal logic with counting operators over templates. The meta‑theorem shows that two graphs are indistinguishable by any bounded counting T‑GNN if and only if they are graded bisimilar, which in turn is equivalent to satisfying the same GML(T) sentences.

## Key Results
- Introduced Template GNNs (T‑GNNs), a unified framework for graph neural networks based on template‑wise aggregation.
- Defined corresponding template‑WL algorithms, graded bisimulation, and graded template modal logic (GML(T)).
- Proved a meta‑theorem: for any finite set of templates T, bounded counting T‑GNNs have uniform expressivity exactly characterized by GML(T). This unifies AC‑GNNs, AC⁺‑GNNs, and k‑hop subgraph GNNs as template instantiations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The meta‑theorem holds because T‑GNNs, the template‑WL process, and GML(T) all operate on the same principle: they consider the multiset of template occurrences around each node. Graded bisimulation formalizes when two nodes have identical template‑wise neighborhood statistics. A T‑GNN, with bounded counting and sufficient depth, can compute the graded bisimulation equivalence classes exactly; conversely, any property definable in GML(T) can be decided by checking bisimulation. The proof uses a back‑and‑forth construction between the T‑GNN update rule and the GML(T) semantics, showing a tight correspondence: the inductive invariant maintained by the T‑GNN matches the bisimulation condition, and the logic can express precisely those invariants. This mechanism ensures that no more and no less can be distinguished by either side.

## Foundational Learning
- **Weisfeiler‑Leman (WL) test:** The classical method for graph isomorphism testing; forms the baseline for GNN expressivity. *Why needed:* Standard GNNs are at most as powerful as 1‑WL. *Quick check:* Can 1‑WL distinguish a cycle of length 6 from two triangles connected by an edge?
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):** Neural models that update node representations by aggregating neighbor information. *Why needed:* Understanding the limitations of neighborhood aggregation motivates the template extension. *Quick check:* What aggregation operators (sum, mean, max) preserve permutation invariance?
- **Graph templates:** Small graph patterns (e.g., edges, 2‑hops) used to define the scope of aggregation in T‑GNNs. *Why needed:* They allow capturing higher‑order structure beyond immediate neighbors. *Quick check:* Give an example of a template that could detect triangles in a graph.
- **Graded bisimulation:** An equivalence relation that compares the multiset of template occurrences around nodes, with graded counting. *Why needed:* It provides the exact indistinguishability notion for T‑GNNs. *Quick check:* Are two nodes that have the same number of adjacent triangles necessarily graded bisimilar with the triangle template?
- **Modal logic (and GML(T)):** A logical formalism for describing graph properties; GML(T) adds template‑modalities and counting. *Why needed:* It offers a rigorous way to characterize what T‑GNNs can express. *Quick check:* Write a GML(T) formula that says “a node has at least two distinct triangles attached”.
- **Uniform expressivity:** The ability of a model family to decide graph properties independently of parameter choices. *Why needed:* The meta‑theorem concerns uniform expressivity, i.e., the supremum over all possible instantiations of T‑GNNs. *Quick check:* Does adding a non‑linear MLP after aggregation change uniform expressivity? Why or why not?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map (plain text):**  
Input Graph → Template Extraction (match all T‑templates) → Template Embedding Computation → Aggregation over Templates → Node Feature Update → (Repeat across layers) → Graph‑level Readout → Prediction
- **Critical path:** To determine if two graphs are T‑GNN‑distinguishable, one initializes node features, then iteratively: (1) for each node, collect all template occurrences containing it, (2) aggregate the embeddings of those occurrences, (3) update the node feature via a neural network. After a sufficient number of layers (or using a readout), the final representations are compared; equivalence under graded bisimulation implies identical outputs for any bounded counting T‑GNN.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Template set size:* Larger T yields higher expressivity but increases computational cost of subgraph matching.  
  - *Bounded vs. unbounded counting:* Bounded counting ensures uniform expressivity matches GML(T); unbounded counting may go beyond but breaks the neat equivalence.  
  - *Aggregation operator:* Sum and mean are common; max may lose count information needed for graded bisimulation.  
  - *Depth vs. over‑squashing:* Deeper T‑GNNs can propagate information farther but risk over‑squashing; residual connections may help.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - T‑GNN fails to distinguish two graphs that are not graded bisimilar (i.e., there exists a GML(T) sentence separating them).  
  - If the template set is not rich enough (e.g., missing k‑hop patterns), the model may be blind to certain structural differences.  
  - When counting is effectively bounded by the implementation but the underlying property requires unbounded counts, the T‑GNN may misclassify.
- **First three experiments (concrete):**  
  1. **AC‑GNN instantiation:** Implement a T‑GNN with single‑edge templates (AC) and test on pairs of graphs that 1‑WL cannot distinguish but AC‑GNN can (e.g., certain regular graphs).  
  2. **AC⁺‑GNN instantiation:** Add node degree information via a node‑label template; verify that the model distinguishes graphs that AC‑GNN cannot.  
  3. **k‑hop GNN instantiation:** Use templates consisting of all paths of length up to k; compare expressivity against k‑WL by testing on known benchmarks like the EXP‑3WL dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions; its discussion focuses on presenting the unified theory and its instantiations.

## Limitations
- The meta‑theorem is restricted to finite template families and bounded counting; infinite or continuous extensions remain open.  
- Uniform expressivity abstracts away parameterization and optimization, which may limit real‑world applicability of the theoretical guarantees.  
- The relationship between graded bisimulation and other equivalence notions (e.g., k‑WL) for arbitrary templates is not fully explored.

## Confidence
- **T‑GNNs as a unified framework:** High – the paper demonstrates explicit instantiations matching prior definitions.  
- **Equivalence between counting T‑GNNs and GML(T):** Medium – the proof is technical and not independently verified.  
- **Generality for any finite template set:** Medium – the constructive argument seems sound but may rely on subtle combinatorial properties.

## Next Checks
- **Formal verification:** Re‑formalize the meta‑theorem and its lemmas in a proof assistant (e.g., Lean) to uncover hidden assumptions.  
- **Empirical instantiation test:** Implement T‑GNNs for AC⁺ and k‑hop templates; evaluate on standard expressivity benchmarks (e.g., EXP‑3WL) to confirm predicted distinguishing power.  
- **Cross‑check with logical characterizations:** Derive GML(T) fragments for known GNN families (e.g., GIN, GatedGCN) within the T‑GNN framework and compare against published logical characterizations; discrepancies would challenge the unification claim.
