# Function-Space Empirical Bayes Regularisation with Student's t Priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22015
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22015
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ST-FS-EB attains state-of-the-art in-distribution accuracy (e.g., 99.33% on MNIST) and OOD detection AUROC (e.g., 99.86% on MNIST vs FMNIST), with improved robustness to rotational shifts.

## Executive Summary
Uncertainty estimation in Bayesian deep learning is challenged by the inadequacy of Gaussian priors in function-space variational inference (FSVI), which cannot capture heavy-tailed behaviour in neural network outputs. This paper introduces ST-FS-EB, a novel framework that employs heavy-tailed Student’s t priors in both parameter and function spaces within an empirical Bayes regularisation scheme. By deriving an evidence lower bound (ELBO) that leverages Monte Carlo dropout for scalable posterior approximation, ST-FS-EB avoids intractable KL divergences while maintaining expressive inductive biases. Evaluations on multiple image classification benchmarks demonstrate superior in-distribution performance, OOD detection, and robustness to distribution shifts compared to existing VI‑based baselines.

## Method Summary
ST-FS-EB combines a Student’s t prior over network weights with a multivariate Student’s t functional likelihood derived from a stochastic linear model on features extracted from a randomly initialised subnet. This forms an empirical prior \(p_{\text{st}}(\theta|y_c,x_c) \propto p_{\text{st}}(\theta) \, p_{\text{st}}(y_c|x_c,\theta)\). Variational inference is performed via Monte Carlo dropout, where the implicit posterior \(q(\theta)\) is defined by dropout masks. The resulting ELBO (Eq. 14) aggregates, over \(S\) MC samples, the data log‑likelihood on a training minibatch, a functional regulariser involving the kernel matrix \(K(x_c,x_c)\), and a weight‑space penalty. The optimisation is minibatch‑based with Adam, early stopping, and hyperparameter selection (degrees of freedom \(\nu_\theta\), scales \(\sigma_\theta,\tau_1,\tau_2\), dropout rate \(\rho\)). At test time, predictions are averaged over \(\Xi=10\) stochastic forward passes.

## Key Results
- **In-distribution accuracy**: 99.33% (MNIST), 92.38% (FMNIST), 86.52% (CIFAR‑10).
- **OOD detection (AUROC)**: 99.86% (MNIST vs FMNIST), 99.96% (MNIST vs NotMNIST).
- **Robustness**: Under rotational shifts (±30°), ST-FS-EB maintains higher accuracy and lower negative log‑likelihood than all VI baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 – Heavy‑tailed priors capture outlier behaviour.** Student’s t distributions have polynomially decaying tails, giving higher probability to extreme function values than Gaussian priors. This matches the heavy‑tailed statistical characteristics often observed in neural network outputs, leading to improved robustness under outliers and distribution shifts. *Break condition*: If the true predictive distribution is truly light‑tailed (Gaussian), the Student’s t prior may inject unnecessary variance and degrade accuracy.

**Mechanism 2 – Joint parameter/function‑space empirical Bayes regularisation.** ST‑FS‑EB constructs an empirical prior that simultaneously regularises weights (via an i.i.d. Student’s t prior) and outputs (via a multivariate Student’s t likelihood on context points using a stochastic linear model on extracted features). This provides a more expressive inductive bias than function‑space‑only or weight‑space‑only approaches without requiring linearisation of the full network. *Break condition*: The linearization in the functional likelihood may become inaccurate for highly nonlinear networks, weakening the weight‑function link.

**Mechanism 3 – Monte Carlo dropout for scalable inference.** Because the Student’s t prior is non‑conjugate, the KL divergence in the ELBO is intractable. ST‑FS‑EB sidesteps this by using MC dropout: the variational posterior is implicit in dropout masks, and the KL term reduces to a constant entropy that depends on the dropout rate \(\rho\). This yields a simple, scalable optimisation that avoids Jacobian evaluations of linearised FSVI methods. *Break condition*: The mean‑field independent dropout assumption can produce underdispersed posteriors; computational cost is higher than pure weight‑space VI due to multiple forward passes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Base Neural Network f(x;θ) → Feature Extractor h(x;ϕ₀) → Empirical Prior p_st(θ|y_c,x_c) → Variational Posterior q(θ) via MC dropout → ELBO Objective → Predictive Distribution (average over MC forward passes)

**Critical path**:
1. Sample a context minibatch \((x_c, y_c)\) from an auxiliary dataset.
2. For each training minibatch \((x_B, y_B)\), extract features \(h(x_c)\) and compute kernel matrix \(K = \tau_1 h_c h_c^\top + \tau_2 I\).
3. Perform \(S\) MC dropout forward passes: for each sample \(s\), obtain effective parameters \(\theta^{(s)}\), compute logits on \(x_B\) and \(x_c\), then evaluate the ELBO terms.
4. Average the ELBO across samples and back‑propagate.
5. Repeat until early stopping; at test time, average predictions over \(\Xi\) forward passes.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Heavy‑tailed vs. Gaussian: Student’s t offers robustness but introduces hyperparameter \(\nu_\theta\); poor choices can hurt performance.
- Context point source: Using auxiliary (shifted) data improves OOD detection but may slightly reduce in‑distribution accuracy.
- Computational cost: Higher than weight‑space VI due to MC sampling and per‑sample Cholesky on \(K\) (size \(N_c^2\)).
- Feature extractor: Randomly initialised vs. pretrained affects inductive bias; paper uses random but allows pretrained.

**Failure signatures**:
- Poor OOD detection (low AUROC): \(\nu_\theta\) too large or context distribution insufficiently shifted.
- High NLL / low ACC on in‑distribution: Excessive regularisation (high \(\rho\), small \(\sigma_\theta\)) or inappropriate \(\nu_\theta\).
- Training instability or NaN: \(\nu_\theta \leq 2\) or ill‑conditioned \(K\) (e.g., \(\tau_2\) too small, high feature collinearity).
- No improvement over baselines: Dataset predictive distribution may be light‑tailed, or hyperparameters not properly tuned.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **In‑distribution prediction & OOD detection**: Train on MNIST/FMNIST/CIFAR‑10/PathMNIST/OrganAMNIST, evaluate accuracy, ECE, NLL, and AUROC against corresponding OOD datasets.
2. **Robustness to rotational distribution shift**: Apply rotations (±30°) to test images; plot accuracy and NLL vs. rotation angle.
3. **Ablation on degrees‑of‑freedom \(\nu_\theta\)**: Train with \(\nu_\theta \in \{2.1, 3.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0, \text{Gaussian}\}\); compare performance to identify optimal tail heaviness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the degrees of freedom parameter \(\nu_\theta\) in Student's t priors be automatically tuned for different datasets without manual hyperparameter search?
2. What is the optimal strategy for selecting context points in ST-FS-EB to maximize robustness while maintaining in-distribution accuracy?
3. Can the computational efficiency of ST-FS-EB be improved to rival weight-space Bayesian methods without compromising its robustness benefits?
4. What theoretical guarantees (e.g., posterior consistency, uncertainty calibration) does ST-FS-EB provide under Student's t priors in function space?
5. How does ST-FS-EB generalize to non-classification tasks like regression or generative modeling, and how does it compare to Gaussian-based function-space methods in these settings?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Performance heavily depends on choices of \(\nu_\theta\), \(\sigma_\theta\), \(\tau_1\), \(\tau_2\), and \(\rho\); extensive random search is required.
- Computational overhead: MC dropout and per‑sample Cholesky factorisation increase time and memory compared to pure weight‑space VI.
- Ambiguities in implementation: Clarity issues around feature extractor training, context likelihood residual definition, and ELBO scaling could affect reproducibility and results.

## Confidence
- Heavy‑tailed priors improve robustness: **Medium**
- Joint parameter/function‑space empirical Bayes regularisation: **Medium**
- MC dropout scalability: **High**
- State‑of‑the‑art performance: **Medium**
