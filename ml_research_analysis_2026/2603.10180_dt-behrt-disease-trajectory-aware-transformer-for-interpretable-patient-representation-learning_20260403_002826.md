# DT-BEHRT: Disease Trajectory-aware Transformer for Interpretable Patient Representation Learning

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10180  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10180  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art on MIMIC-III/IV and eICU; readmission AUROC 80.30 vs 77.68 (MIMIC-III) and phenotyping macro‑AUPRC 48.15 vs 43.61 for patients with ≥3 visits (MIMIC‑III).

## Executive Summary

Electronic Health Record (EHR) models typically treat all medical codes uniformly, overlooking that diagnoses uniquely drive disease progression. DT‑BEHRT addresses this by disentangling diagnosis‑centric interactions within organ systems using Disease Aggregation (DA) tokens and capturing asynchronous longitudinal patterns via a Disease Progression (DP) graph. A tailored pre‑training regimen—Global Code Masking Prediction combined with ontology‑informed Ancestor Code Prediction—aligns representations across modules. On multiple benchmarks (MIMIC‑III/IV, eICU), DT‑BEHRT outperforms prior work, especially on readmission and phenotyping tasks, while offering clinically interpretable attention patterns.

## Method Summary

DT‑BEHRT processes a patient’s flattened sequence of medical codes (diagnoses, medications, labs, procedures) through a four‑module architecture per Transformer layer. The **Sequence Representation (SR) module** embeds codes with type and visit position and applies standard self‑attention. The **Disease Aggregation (DA) module** adds 19 fixed tokens—one per top‑level ICD‑9 chapter—appearing only if a patient has ≥*k* diagnoses in that chapter; DA tokens attend exclusively to codes from their own chapter and the `[SEQ]` token, compressing organ‑system‑level comorbidity information. The **Disease Progression (DP) module** builds a heterogeneous graph where each hospital visit becomes a node (initialized with age) connected to its diagnosis nodes and to temporally adjacent visit nodes; Graph Attention Networks propagate historical disease signals forward. The **Patient Representation (PR) module** concatenates the final `[SEQ]` token, all DA token vectors, and all DP visit vectors, then uses `[SEQ]` as a query in an attention pooling layer to produce the final `[CLS]` embedding. Pre‑training jointly optimizes Global Code Masking Prediction (mask all occurrences of selected unique codes and predict them from `[CLS]`) and Ancestor Code Prediction (predict ICD‑9 chapter of masked diagnoses using both SR and DP perspectives), plus a covariance regularizer to keep DA tokens diverse. Fine‑tuning adds a single linear head for the downstream task.

## Key Results

- Readmission prediction (MIMIC‑III): AUROC 80.30 vs 77.68 (best baseline).  
- Phenotyping macro‑AUPRC (patients with ≥3 visits, MIMIC‑III): 48.15 vs 43.61.  
- Consistent improvements on mortality and prolonged LOS across MIMIC‑III/IV and eICU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Disease Aggregation (DA) tokens**  
- **Claim:** DA tokens disentangle diagnosis‑centric interactions by summarizing organ‑system‑level disease patterns, improving representation of comorbid conditions within the same physiological system.  
- **Mechanism:** For each of 19 top‑level ICD‑9 chapters, a dedicated DA token is appended to the patient sequence if the patient has ≥*k* distinct diagnoses from that chapter. During self‑attention, each DA token is restricted via a custom mask to attend only to diagnosis codes from its own chapter and to the `[SEQ]` token. This forces the model to compress information about all diagnoses within an organ system into a single vector, capturing intra‑system comorbidity semantics.  
- **Core assumption:** Diagnoses belonging to the same ICD‑9 chapter exhibit meaningful, learnable co‑occurrence patterns that are clinically distinct from cross‑chapter interactions.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.3 define DA tokens and the chapter‑restricted attention mask (Eq. 4).  
- **Break condition:** If diagnosis codes do not cluster by ICD‑9 chapter in latent space, or if the ablation study (Table 3, “DA” row) shows no gain.

**Mechanism 2: Disease Progression (DP) graph**  
- **Claim:** The DP module captures asynchronous disease development trends over time by treating each hospital visit as a node that aggregates diagnoses from that visit and connects sequentially to other visits.  
- **Mechanism:** A heterogeneous graph is constructed with a DP node for each visit (initialized with age embedding), connected to all diagnosis nodes from that visit and to the previous/next DP node (with self‑loops). Graph Attention Networks propagate information from past diagnoses (via DP nodes) to future DP nodes, so a future visit’s representation encodes the patient’s longitudinal disease history.  
- **Core assumption:** A patient’s disease state at a visit can be summarized by a single vector (the DP node) and temporal progression is best modeled by message passing between these summaries.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.4 describe the DP graph structure and GAT‑based message passing (Eqs. 6‑9).  
- **Break condition:** DP contribution negligible in ablation (Table 3, “DP” row), or model fails on single‑visit patients (Section 4).

**Mechanism 3: Tailored pre‑training (GCMP + ACP)**  
- **Claim:** The pre‑training framework aligns representations across SR, DA, and DP modules by forcing them to jointly learn ontology‑aware semantics, improving robustness.  
- **Mechanism:** GCMP masks all occurrences of uniquely sampled codes at the trajectory level and predicts them from `[CLS]`. ACP is an auxiliary task: for each masked diagnosis, predict its ICD‑9 chapter ancestor using both SR’s `[SEQ]` token and DP’s final visit token. This teaches non‑graph (SR) and graph (DP) modules to encode the same hierarchical knowledge.  
- **Core assumption:** Misalignment exists between SR (sequence), DA (ontology‑structured), and DP (temporal graph) spaces; a shared prediction task on a common ontological structure reduces this misalignment.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.6 describe GCMP and ACP; ablation (Section 3.3.3) shows ACP gives pronounced gains on mortality.  
- **Break condition:** Removing ACP does not degrade performance relative to GCMP‑only (Table 3, “ACP” row), or ontology‑aware signals are already captured by GCMP.

## Foundational Learning

- **Medical Code Heterogeneity** – Diagnoses, medications, procedures, and labs play distinct clinical roles (e.g., diagnoses drive trajectories, treatments are temporal). Treating them uniformly discards crucial signal.  
  *Quick check:* Why is it problematic to feed all code types into a single embedding lookup table without architectural differentiation?

- **Graph Attention Networks (GATs)** – GATs compute attention over neighbors to propagate information. In DT‑BEHRT, GAT layers in the DP module aggregate messages from diagnosis nodes (capturing current disease burden) and previous DP nodes (capturing history) to update a visit representation.  
  *Quick check:* In the DP graph, what are the two sources of messages incoming to a DP node `ṽt`, and how do they capture different aspects of disease progression?

- **Masked Language Modeling (MLM) Adaptation** – GCMP adapts BERT’s MLM to EHR by masking at the unique‑code level across the whole trajectory, not at each token occurrence. This prevents trivial predictions from repeated codes and respects visit‑order ambiguity.  
  *Quick check:* How does trajectory‑level, unique‑code masking differ from standard BERT MLM, and what EHR‑specific problem does it aim to solve?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (patient trajectory `V`) -> SR module (with embedded codes and type/visit signals, outputs `[SEQ]` and diagnosis embeddings) -> DA tokens (fixed, 19 chapter‑restricted vectors).  
Separately: DP graph construction (visit nodes + diagnosis nodes) -> DP module (GAT layers, outputs visit vectors).  
PR module: Concatenate `[SEQ]`, DA tokens, DP tokens -> attention pooling (query=`[SEQ]`, keys/values=DA+DP) -> `[CLS]` -> downstream task head.

**Critical path:** The SR and DP branches process the input in parallel and only merge in the PR module. Pre‑training tasks supervise both branches.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- DA tokens give organ‑system interpretability but add 19 fixed tokens and a complex chapter‑restricted attention mask.  
- DP graph captures temporal trends but increases computational overhead and fails for single‑visit patients.  
- ACP improves alignment but adds auxiliary heads and a more complex loss, increasing pre‑training time and hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., `λ_anc`).  
- The model specializes in diagnoses; other code types receive no dedicated modules, possibly underutilizing their information.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Single‑visit patients: DP graph has no temporal edges → degenerate structure → DP module provides no signal.  
2. Sparse diagnosis chapters: If a patient has diagnoses from fewer than *k* chapters, no DA tokens are activated, losing system‑level abstraction for that patient.  
3. Chapter‑restricted mask misimplementation: DA token attends across chapters or to other DA tokens, breaking disentanglement.  
4. Hyperparameter sensitivity: Poor choices for `k`, masking rate `α`, or `λ_cov` degrade performance.

**First experiments:**  
1. Ablation Study (Table 3): Systematically remove DA, DP, GCMP, and ACP to quantify each mechanism’s contribution.  
2. Subgroup Analysis (Figure 2): Evaluate performance across comorbidity groups to test diagnosis‑centric design for multi‑system disease.  
3. Case Studies (Figures 4‑5): Visualize attention weights in DA and DP modules, confirming alignment with clinical reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Scalability:** How can the computational overhead of multi‑head self‑attention and graph attention be reduced to enable deployment on large‑scale EHR datasets without sacrificing performance?  
2. **Single‑visit adaptation:** For patients with only one hospital visit, how can the DP module be adapted to generate robust representations when the temporal graph lacks edges?  
3. **Non‑diagnosis code modeling:** How can medications, procedures, and laboratory tests be incorporated with dedicated structures that capture their distinct clinical roles (e.g., temporal sequencing of medications, sparse interactions of procedures)?

## Limitations

- The preprocessing pipeline (Appendix E) defers to HEART [12] without providing scripts, leaving ambiguity in NDC→ATC mapping, lab quantization, and cohort filters.  
- The DA token threshold `k` is not reported for the main results; only the search space `{3,4}` is given.  
- The attention pooling implementation in the PR module (Eq. 10) is underspecified (e.g., single vs. multi‑head, exact query/key formulation).  
- The scope of the pre‑training data (“subset”) is undefined, obscuring the true benefit and complicating baseline comparisons.

## Confidence

- **Mechanism 1 (DA tokens):** Medium  
  Ablation shows gains, but reliance on ICD‑9 chapter coherence is not clinically validated.  
- **Mechanism 2 (DP graph):** Medium‑Low  
  Novel design and ablation contribution to readmission are positive, yet explicit failure for single‑visit patients suggests brittleness.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Pre‑training alignment):** Medium‑High  
  Removing ACP notably hurts mortality, supporting its alignment role, though gains appear task‑specific.
