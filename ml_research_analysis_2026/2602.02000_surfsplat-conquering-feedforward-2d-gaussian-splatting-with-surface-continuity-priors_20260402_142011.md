# SurfSplat: Conquering Feedforward 2D Gaussian Splatting with Surface Continuity Priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02000  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02000  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** SurfSplat, using 2D Gaussian Splatting with surface continuity priors and forced alpha blending, achieves state-of-the-art feedforward 3D reconstruction, significantly outperforming prior methods in geometric accuracy and rendering consistency across multiple datasets and resolutions.

## Executive Summary
SurfSplat tackles the challenge of feedforward 3D reconstruction from sparse images by replacing the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) and introducing two key components: a surface continuity prior that enforces coherent surface geometry by binding Gaussian rotation and scale to spatial positions, and a forced alpha blending strategy that prevents opacity collapse to ensure all Gaussians contribute during rendering. The method also proposes the High-Resolution Rendering Consistency (HRRC) metric to evaluate geometric fidelity at high resolutions. Comprehensive experiments on RealEstate10K, DL3DV, and ScanNet demonstrate SurfSplat's superiority in both standard metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and HRRC, with ablations confirming the necessity of both proposed components.

## Method Summary
SurfSplat uses a dual-path encoder: a single-view branch (Depth Anything V2) extracts monocular cues, and a multi-view branch (ResNet + Swin Transformer + cost volumes) aggregates cross-view geometry. Features are fused in a U-Net with DPT heads to predict depth, scale multipliers, spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients, and opacity. The core innovation lies in the Gaussian processor: the surface continuity prior computes target normals from image-space neighbors (via Sobel filters) and sets Gaussian rotation accordingly, while scale is derived from image-space distances with learned multipliers. Forced alpha blending clips opacity to a maximum (τₒₚₐ=0.6) and normalizes rendered color when accumulated alpha exceeds a threshold (τα). The scene is rendered via differentiable 2DGS splatting and trained with a photometric loss (MSE + LPIPS, λ=0.05).

## Key Results
- SurfSplat significantly outperforms prior feedforward methods on RealEstate10K, DL3DV, and ScanNet in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS at both 512×512 and 1024×1024 resolutions.
- The proposed HRRC metric reveals SurfSplat’s superior geometric fidelity, with large margins over baselines at high resolutions.
- Ablations show that removing either the surface continuity prior or forced alpha blending leads to drastic drops in HRRC scores, confirming their critical roles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Anisotropic 2D Gaussian Splatting
SurfSplat employs 2DGS surfels—thin, disk-like Gaussians oriented along surfaces—instead of 3D ellipsoids. This anisotropy better captures planar structures and avoids the spherical degeneracy of 3DGS that yields point-cloud-like outputs. The surface-aligned orientation is enforced by the surface continuity prior.

### Mechanism 2: Surface Continuity Prior
For each pixel, a surface normal is estimated from 3D position neighbors using Sobel filters; the Gaussian rotation aligns with this normal via Rodrigues’ formula. Scale is derived from image-space neighbor distances with learned multipliers. This binds geometry to spatial positions, enforcing smooth, continuous surfaces by leveraging the local smoothness prior.

### Mechanism 3: Forced Alpha Blending
Opacity is clipped to τₒₚₐ (<1) so even occluded Gaussians retain non-zero influence. Rendered color is normalized by total alpha when it exceeds τα, preventing opacity collapse where front splats dominate and background Gaussians are ignored. This ensures 3D alignment and stable SH color estimation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input images → Dual-path encoder (monocular branch + multi-view branch with Swin Transformer and cost volumes) → U-Net fusion → DPT heads (depth, scale multipliers, SH, opacity) → Gaussian processor (surface continuity prior + forced alpha blending) → Differentiable 2DGS renderer → Output image.

**Critical path:** The surface continuity prior operates immediately after depth prediction, before rasterization, to set rotation and scale; forced alpha blending is applied during rendering.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- 2DGS provides better anisotropy but introduces instability; handled by continuity prior.  
- Fixed opacity thresholds (τₒₚₐ, τα) balance preventing collapse vs. color washout; requires tuning.  
- Single Gaussian per pixel simplifies representation but may undersample; compensated by high-quality attributes.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Without surface continuity prior: holes and voids in high-res renders; unnatural depth transitions.  
- Without forced alpha blending: spatial misalignment across views; background occlusion.  
- Extreme viewpoint extrapolation: sharp HRRC drops.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Visualize Gaussian orientations (render normals/meshes) to confirm smooth, consistent normals with the prior vs. noisy ablations.  
2. Plot opacity distributions before/after clipping to verify forced alpha blending shifts values away from 1.0.  
3. Cross-dataset generalizability: train on RE10K, test on DL3DV/ScanNet; SurfSplat’s priors should generalize better.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Joint pose elimination:** How can feedforward 2DGS frameworks be enhanced to jointly estimate camera poses and 3D geometry from sparse images, removing the dependency on known poses?
- **Compact adaptive representations:** How can we develop compact and adaptive Gaussian representations (e.g., variable number, merging, pruning) that reduce redundancy while maintaining high-fidelity reconstruction, especially under HRRC?
- **Edge-aware surface prior:** The surface continuity prior assumes smoothly varying surfaces; how can it be made robust to sharp edges and geometric discontinuities without over-smoothing?
- **Adaptive opacity thresholds:** Can forced alpha blending’s opacity thresholds (τₒₚₐ, τα) be made adaptive or learned, rather than fixed, to handle diverse scene transparencies and improve generalization?

## Limitations
- The exact differentiable 2DGS rasterization implementation and full training schedule (e.g., LR warmup, data augmentations) are unspecified, blocking direct replication.
- Generalization to highly non-planar surfaces (e.g., foliage, hair) and sharp edges is theoretical; empirical validation on such datasets is absent.
- Fixed opacity thresholds may not generalize to all materials and lighting conditions; sensitivity analysis is limited.

## Confidence
- **Geometric accuracy gains (HRRC, PSNR improvements):** High. Validated on three datasets with ablations.
- **Necessity of proposed components (SCP, FAB):** High. Ablations show drastic drops when removed.
- **Surface continuity prior mechanism:** Medium. Formulation is clear, but edge-handling lacks validation.
- **Generalization to arbitrary scenes:** Low. Tests are on structured indoor/outdoor datasets; complex topologies untested.
