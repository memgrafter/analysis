# From Weak Cues to Real Identities: Evaluating Inference-Driven De-Anonymization in LLM Agents
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18382
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18382
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** LLM agents reconstruct real identities from anonymized data using weak cues and public information, achieving up to 79.2% success in the Netflix setting and outperforming a classical 56.0% baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates a critical privacy threat: LLM agents can stitch together scattered, non-identifying cues from anonymized artifacts with publicly available information to re-identify individuals—a process termed inference-driven linkage. The authors evaluate this threat across three distinct settings: classical linkage attacks (Netflix Prize and AOL logs), a controlled synthetic benchmark (InferLink), and modern text-rich artifacts (Anthropic Interviewer and ChatGPT logs). Results demonstrate that agents can achieve high linkage success rates, even under benign implicit prompts, and that simple mitigation strategies (e.g., privacy-aware prompts) introduce a measurable privacy–utility trade-off. The work highlights the need for more nuanced privacy safeguards in LLM agent deployments.

## Method Summary
The study employs a multi-setting evaluation framework. Classical settings reuse established datasets: Netflix Prize ratings (anonymized pool plus auxiliary noisy fragments) and AOL search logs (single-user histories with live web retrieval). The core contribution is the InferLink benchmark—a controlled generator producing paired synthetic tables with exactly one overlapping individual, varying fingerprint type (intrinsic, coordinate, hybrid), intent (implicit vs. explicit re-identification request), and attacker knowledge (zero-knowledge vs. membership-knowledge). Modern trace studies apply the agent to redacted research interviews and chat logs, requiring public evidence corroboration. Primary metrics are Linkage Success Rate (LSR) for controlled settings and Confirmed Linkage Count (CLC) for open-ended cases, with Utility (U) measuring benign task completion.

## Key Results
- Agents achieve 79.2% identity reconstruction on Netflix data (with only two overlapping movies), significantly surpassing the classical heuristic baseline of 56.0%.
- In InferLink, linkage occurs even under benign implicit prompts (e.g., Claude 4.5 reaches up to 80% success) and increases sharply under explicit re-identification requests, with near-perfect success under explicit membership-knowledge.
- In modern text-rich traces, agents successfully corroborate multiple linkages by intersecting contextual cues (e.g., research niche, affiliations) with publicly retrieved evidence.
- Privacy-aware mitigation prompts reduce linkage but incur a clear utility loss, quantifying a privacy–utility trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Aggregation of Sparse Quasi-Identifiers
Agents combine multiple weak, non-identifying signals from an anonymized artifact into a distinctive quasi-identifier that narrows the candidate set. Evidence: With only two overlapping movies (m=2) and injected noise, GPT-5 achieves 79.2% linkage in the Netflix setting—well above the classical baseline. The mechanism relies on the artifact containing enough overlapping behavioral patterns (ratings, timestamps) that the agent can extract without domain-specific heuristics. Linkage fails if shared cues are extremely sparse or too common.

### Mechanism 2: Retrieval and Integration of Public Evidence
In open-ended settings (AOL, modern traces), agents form a coarse profile from the anonymized data, then generate targeted search queries to retrieve public records (business registries, academic databases). By intersecting multiple retrieved signals, they progressively narrow candidates to a single individual. Evidence: The agent cross-references AOL search queries with incorporation/medical records and matches Anthropic Interviewer snippets to scholarly publications. Failure occurs if no relevant public information exists or if retrieved evidence is contradictory.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Representation Reasoning
Agents successfully link identities across heterogeneous data representations (e.g., structured tables vs. unstructured text) without bespoke engineering. InferLink results show high success across intrinsic, coordinate, and hybrid fingerprint types, demonstrating flexibility in aligning semantically equivalent features that may be expressed differently (e.g., "department" vs. "research group"). The mechanism assumes shared features are meaningfully alignable. Linkage fails if representations are too disparate or require specialized domain knowledge.

## Foundational Learning
- **Inference-driven linkage:** The core threat—reconstructing identity from scattered cues rather than direct disclosure. Quick check: Can an agent identify a specific person from two sources sharing only indirect attributes like product categories and vague timestamps?
- **Quasi-identifiers and k-anonymity:** Provides theoretical lens; attacks exploit combined quasi-identifiers to reduce anonymity set below k. Quick check: If a dataset satisfies k=10 for known quasi-identifiers, does that guarantee protection against LLM-based linkage?
- **LLM agent capabilities (tool use, retrieval, reasoning):** Enables the attack via web search, multi-step reasoning, and unstructured data parsing. Quick check: What specific capabilities enable AOL linkage: parsing queries, generating search terms, or both?
- **Privacy-utility trade-off:** Mitigations reduce linkage but harm legitimate task performance. Quick check: In Table 1, how much utility does Claude 4.5 lose in the EXPLICIT-MK setting after mitigation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Generator → Agent (LLM with tools) → Evaluator; optional Privacy Mitigation Module; Experiment Harness orchestrates runs.
- **Critical path:** (1) Select seed (fingerprint, intent, knowledge). (2) Generate scenario/dataset (10×10 paired tables, one true overlap). (3) Run multi-turn agent interaction, output (ˆı, E). (4) Score LSR and U. (5) Repeat across seeds/models.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Controlled vs. realistic (InferLink isolates variables but lacks complexity); synthetic vs. real data (synthetic ensures ground truth but may not reflect distributions); utility metric (explicit task success is narrow); mitigation strength (stronger guardrails reduce linkage but risk over-refusal).
- **Failure signatures:** Incorrect identity returned; true identity missed; evidence insufficient; mitigation causes over-refusal on legitimate tasks; schema violation (using non-shared features as linkage keys).
- **First 3 experiments:** (1) Netflix replication: run agent on fragments with m∈{2,4,6,8}, T∈{14,21}; compare LSR to classical heuristic baseline. (2) InferLink baseline: evaluate across all fingerprint types and (ι,κ) conditions; map conditional risk surface. (3) Mitigation assessment: apply privacy-aware prompt; compute LSR and U gaps to quantify trade-off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can InferLink be extended to capture realistic linkage ambiguity (multiple matches, larger candidate pools, probabilistic ground truths)?
- What is the real-world prevalence of inference-driven linkage in everyday LLM agent usage, and which contextual factors (domain, data density, public info availability) most influence it?
- What architectural or training modifications could suppress inference-driven linkage without causing over-refusal that degrades legitimate cross-source utility?
- Why do models exhibit different susceptibilities across fingerprint types and task framings, and which capabilities (sparse reasoning, temporal correlation) predict linkage success?
- How does semantic richness of modern text-rich artifacts (interviews, chat logs) qualitatively change linkable cues, and does this create new classes of "idiosyncratic identifiers"?

## Limitations
- **Artifact gaps:** Exact system prompts, LLM judge details, and the ChatGPT logs dataset are not public, hindering precise replication.
- **Model ambiguity:** Results depend on GPT-5/Claude 4.5, but API endpoints, versions, and parameters are unspecified; performance may drift.
- **Baseline opacity:** The classical Netflix baseline implementation (Narayanan & Shmatikov 2008) is unavailable, complicating fair comparison.

## Confidence
- Inference-driven linkage (Netflix, InferLink): **Medium** (synthetic control strong but prompt/model access uncertain)
- Public evidence corroboration (AOL, modern traces): **Low–Medium** (CLC measurement lacks transparency)
- Cross-representation reasoning: **Medium** (supported by InferLink, limited to curated data)
- Privacy-utility trade-off: **High** (direct measurements across mitigated/unmitigated conditions are robust)
