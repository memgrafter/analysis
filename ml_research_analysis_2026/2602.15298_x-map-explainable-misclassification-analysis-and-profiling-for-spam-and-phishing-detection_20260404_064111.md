# X-MAP: eXplainable Misclassification Analysis and Profiling for Spam and Phishing Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15298
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15298
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** X-MAP detects misclassifications with up to 0.98 AUROC and recovers up to 97% of falsely rejected legitimate messages while limiting error leakage to ~15%

## Executive Summary
X-MAP introduces an explainable framework for identifying misclassifications in spam and phishing detection systems. It leverages SHAP feature attributions and non-negative matrix factorization to build interpretable topic-level profiles for reliably classified messages. By measuring Jensen-Shannon divergence between individual messages and these reliable profiles, X-MAP flags likely errors with high accuracy while providing human-understandable explanations. Evaluations on SMS and phishing datasets demonstrate that misclassified messages exhibit at least twice the divergence of correctly classified ones. As a standalone detector, X-MAP achieves strong performance (up to 0.98 AUROC) and, when used as a repair layer on top of uncertainty detectors, recovers most falsely rejected legitimate messages with controlled error leakage.

## Method Summary
X-MAP operates in three stages: First, it computes SHAP values for a base classifier and splits them into positive (spam/phishing) and negative (legitimate) supports per message. Features are ranked by a composite score combining conditional mean contribution and presence frequency, with a fixed quota allocated across feature types (TF-IDF n-grams, structural features). The top-ranked features form two non-negative matrices (one per class). Second, non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) decomposes each matrix into additive topics; features are assigned to topics by maximum weight, and group-level topic distributions are constructed from true positive and true negative messages. Third, for any new message, its topic distribution (aggregated from SHAP supports) is compared via Jensen-Shannon divergence to the appropriate group profile (TP if predicted positive, TN if predicted negative). Higher divergence indicates greater misclassification likelihood. The scores can be used directly or as a repair layer to re-accept messages rejected by uncertainty detectors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: SHAP-based Feature Support Extraction
- **Claim:** SHAP feature attributions identify the most discriminative features for correct classification patterns when split into class-specific supports.
- **Mechanism:** For each message, SHAP values are partitioned into non-negative supports for positive (spam/phishing) and negative (legitimate) classes. Global importance scores combine feature presence frequency and average conditional contribution to rank features. Top-ranked features per class are selected via a quota allocation across feature types (TF-IDF n-grams, structural features).
- **Core assumption:** SHAP values provide stable and meaningful feature importance rankings that reflect patterns in correctly classified messages.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "combines SHAP-based feature attributions with non-negative matrix factorization to build interpretable topic profiles"
  - [section 3.2] Equations for splitting SHAP values (Eq. 1), computing presence and conditional mean (Eq. 2), and ranking score with quota (Eq. 3)
- **Break condition:** SHAP explanations become unstable or inconsistent across similar messages, leading to noisy feature rankings that do not reflect coherent semantic patterns.

### Mechanism 2: Topic Profile Construction via NMF
- **Claim:** Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) clusters top-ranked SHAP features into semantically coherent topic-level profiles for true positives (TP) and true negatives (TN).
- **Mechanism:** Separate NMF decompositions are applied to the non-negative SHAP matrices X⁺ (positive support) and X⁻ (negative support), yielding message-topic (W) and topic-feature (H) matrices. Each feature is assigned to the topic with maximum weight in H. The average SHAP contribution of all features in a topic, summed over correctly classified messages in TP or TN, forms a group-level topic distribution (Eq. 8–9).
- **Core assumption:** NMF applied to SHAP support matrices produces topics that represent interpretable, high-level semantic patterns correlated with correct classification decisions.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "build interpretable topic profiles for reliably classified spam/phishing and legitimate messages"
  - [section 3.3] "apply Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) to each SHAP matrix... to derive interpretable topics"
- **Break condition:** NMF topics become indistinct or fail to capture meaningful semantics (e.g., topics mix unrelated features), or group-level profiles do not differentiate TP from TN patterns.

### Mechanism 3: Divergence-based Misclassification Scoring
- **Claim:** Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence between an individual message's topic distribution and the reliable group profile effectively signals misclassification likelihood.
- **Mechanism:** For a message d predicted as class ±, its SHAP-derived topic contributions are aggregated into a distribution p(±)ₔ. This is compared via JS divergence to the group profile p(±)ᵍ (g = TP if ŷ=1, TN if ŷ=0). Higher divergence indicates greater deviation from reliable patterns and thus higher misclassification probability (Eq. 10). This score can be used directly or as a repair layer on top of uncertainty detectors.
- **Core assumption:** Correctly classified messages cluster tightly around their group's topic distribution, while misclassified messages exhibit statistically significant divergence.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "measures each message’s Jensen–Shannon divergence from the appropriate reliable profile, where larger divergence signals higher misclassification likelihood"
  - [section 5.1] Table 1 shows mean JS divergence for TP→TP vs FP→TP and TN→TN vs FN→TN, confirming the separation
- **Break condition:** The divergence distributions for correctly and incorrectly classified messages overlap substantially (e.g., due to topic modeling failure or dataset shift), making thresholding unreliable.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
- **Why needed here:** To quantify each feature's contribution to a model's prediction for individual messages, enabling the extraction of class-specific feature supports.
- **Quick check question:** How does splitting SHAP values into positive and negative supports help separate spam-indicative from legitimate-indicative features?

### Concept: Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF)
- **Why needed here:** To reduce the high-dimensional, sparse SHAP feature matrices into a small set of additive, non-negative topics that are interpretable as semantic patterns.
- **Quick check question:** Why is non-negativity important for interpretability in this context, and how does it differ from PCA or SVD?

### Concept: Jensen-Shannon Divergence
- **Why needed here:** To provide a symmetric, bounded measure of similarity between two probability distributions (individual topic distribution vs. group profile), suitable for scoring deviation.
- **Quick check question:** Why might JS divergence be preferred over KL divergence for comparing topic distributions in this application?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Classifier training → SHAP computation → feature selection → NMF topic modeling → profile construction → divergence scoring
- **Critical path:** The profile construction stage (NMF topic modeling and group profile aggregation) is pivotal; errors here cascade to scoring. All prior steps feed into it.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Number of topics (M)*: Smaller M yields coarser, more robust profiles but loses granularity; larger M risks overfitting to noise. Paper uses m=10.
  - *Top-K features*: Larger K captures more patterns but introduces noise; smaller K may omit key features. Paper uses K=200 with presence floor τₚ=0.05.
  - *Topic representation*: Original distribution vs. uncertainty transformations (vacuity, dissonance). Original often performs best for negative predictions; aleatory uncertainty excels for positive predictions (Table 2).
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - *Profile collapse*: TP and TN profiles are nearly identical (check p(±)ᵍ distributions), indicating feature/topic selection failure.
  - *High divergence on known correct messages*: Many TPs have divergence similar to FPs (violates Table 1 pattern).
  - *Repair layer backfires*: When re-accepting UQ-rejected messages, leakage rate (LeakR) approaches recovery rate (RecovR), netting few correct fixes (Table 4).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Divergence sanity check:** Replicate Table 1. Compute mean JS divergence for TP, FP, TN, FN groups. Confirm misclassified groups show >2× divergence of correct groups.
  2. **Standalone detector evaluation:** Train base classifier, build profiles on train set, score test set. Compute AUROC and FRR@95%TRR for X-MAP vs. UQ baselines (Table 2). Expect strong performance on positive predictions.
  3. **Repair layer integration:** For a fixed UQ detector (e.g., REL-U) at 95% TRR, apply X-MAP to rejected set. Report RecovR, LeakR, and Correct Fixes (Table 4). Expect high recovery (>90% for LogReg) with moderate leakage (~15%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How effectively does X-MAP scale to transformer-based models (e.g., BERT) and multimodal inputs (e.g., email with attachments)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will extend X-MAP to more complex or multimodal models" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation uses only linear/Naïve Bayes models on TF-IDF features; transformers and multimodality introduce different attribution and topic modeling challenges.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing X-MAP's performance (AUROC, FRR) on transformer-based classifiers and multimodal datasets, with analysis of SHAP computation cost and topic coherence.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can integrating subjective logic or belief-based uncertainty measures at the topic level improve X-MAP's detection accuracy beyond the current Jensen-Shannon divergence?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Incorporate various forms of uncertainty and belief modeling at topic level to enhance both interpretability and detection power" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper tests several topic-level uncertainty variants (vacuity, dissonance) but only uses JS divergence on topic distributions for scoring; belief functions are not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement belief-based topic uncertainty metrics, evaluate them as misclassification scores, and compare performance against the current JS-based approach on multiple datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do topic-level explanations from X-MAP improve the efficiency and accuracy of security analysts in triaging spam/phishing alerts compared to traditional uncertainty scores or raw features?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Integrate X-MAP into interactive interfaces and conducting user studies with security analysts would assess the practical value of topic-level explanations" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates X-MAP's quantitative performance but does not evaluate human interpretability or utility in operational settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled user studies where analysts use X-MAP's topic profiles versus baseline tools to identify misclassifications, measuring task completion time, accuracy, and perceived usefulness.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the X-MAP framework generalize to other high-stakes domains like network intrusion detection or medical diagnosis, where misclassifications have severe consequences?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Apply X-MAP to other high-stakes domains such as intrusion detection, fraud detection, and medical decision support would help demonstrate its generality" (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation is limited to text-based spam/phishing; other domains may have different feature structures, model behaviors, and error patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the X-MAP pipeline on benchmark datasets from intrusion detection (e.g., KDD Cup) or medical diagnosis (e.g., MIMIC), examining whether misclassifications also show larger JS divergence from reliable group profiles.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Why does X-MAP's performance degrade on negative predictions (false negatives) relative to positive predictions, and can the framework be adjusted to improve detection of false negatives?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] From Table 2, X-MAP's FRR@95%TRR is higher for negative predictions across classifiers, and the paper notes "for negative predictions, UQ detectors tend to perform better" (Section 5.2). Also, Table 1 shows weaker separation for REL-U on negatives, suggesting topic patterns may be less discriminative.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze why topic-level patterns are less effective for false negative detection; possible reasons include class imbalance, feature sparsity, or inherent difficulty of legitimate messages exhibiting spam-like features.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Qualitative analysis of SHAP features and topic assignments for false negatives versus true negatives; experiments with different feature selection quotas or NMF parameter tuning for negative class; investigating whether false negatives have more diverse or ambiguous topic distributions.

## Limitations
- SHAP stability for sparse text models: KernelExplainer sampling may produce noisy attributions, especially with many features; LinearExplainer assumptions might not hold for SVM/NB.
- Topic profile quality: NMF on SHAP support matrices may yield non-semantic topics due to sparsity; no validation of topic coherence or class-discriminative power.
- Generalizability: Results depend on a specific feature set (7000 word/3000 phrase n‑grams + 17 handcrafted) and LLM‑augmented data; not tested on other datasets or classifiers.

## Confidence
- SHAP feature ranking yields class‑discriminative supports: **Medium**
- NMF produces interpretable, separable topics: **Low**
- JS divergence effectively separates misclassifications: **Medium‑High**
- Standalone/repair‑layer performance claims (e.g., up to 0.98 AUROC, 97% recovery): **Medium**
