# MentalBlackboard: Evaluating Spatial Visualization via Mathematical Transformations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19357
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19357
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art VLMs achieve at most 25% exact match on text-based prediction tasks, revealing severe deficits in multi-stage spatial visualization.

## Executive Summary
MentalBlackboard is a benchmark for assessing spatial visualization in vision-language models using paper folding and hole punching tasks. It generates over 12,000 unique configurations via VPython simulation, covering prediction (unfolding) and planning (reverse) tasks across text, 2D image, and video modalities. Evaluation shows that even top models like o3 and Claude Opus perform poorly, with exact match scores peaking at 25% on text-based prediction and 10% on planning. Models struggle with symmetric transformations, rotations, and multi-step working memory, indicating a lack of robust sequential visuospatial reasoning. A simpler spatial transfer task yields much higher accuracy (∼71%), highlighting a gap between data mapping and true mental manipulation.

## Method Summary
The dataset is procedurally generated using a VPython 3D simulation. A paper mesh (4×4 grid of 32 triangles) undergoes folding actions (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) and rotations (90°, 180°, 270°), followed by hole punches with varied attributes (shape, size, location, direction). Configurations are rendered into videos, 2D abstract images, and textual grids. Two core tasks are evaluated: *prediction* (unfold to original state given final folded/hole state) and *planning* (infer folds from unfolded holes). A *spatial transfer* task isolates relational reasoning without mental transformation. Models are assessed zero-shot using exact match and partial accuracy metrics, with responses parsed as JSON.

## Key Results
- Best model (o3) achieves only 25% exact match on text-based prediction, 10.5% on 2D images, and 8% on video; planning accuracy tops at 10% (Claude Opus 4.1).
- Spatial transfer task yields ∼71–63% accuracy, demonstrating a large gap between data mapping and mental transformation.
- Failures concentrate on applying symmetry, handling rotations, and tracking layer depth across multiple folds; performance declines monotonically with fold count.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Symmetry Transformation Deficit
- Claim: VLMs fail to apply symmetrical transformations correctly, even when they predict the unfolding step sequence exactly.
- Mechanism: Mental simulation requires reflecting hole positions across crease lines while tracking orientation and layer stacking; models compute reflection locations but mishandle direction or create holes on nonexistent layers.
- Core assumption: Models lack a robust spatial transformation module that composes reflections and maintains a consistent layer model.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and Section 5.1 note low exact match despite correct sequences; corpus shows neighboring benchmarks do not isolate symmetry failures.

### Mechanism 2: Limited Visuospatial Working Memory
- Claim: Working memory overflow causes degradation as fold count increases, leading to incoherent multi-step reasoning.
- Mechanism: Each fold alters paper state (visible regions, layer order); models must maintain and reverse intermediate states; overflow yields missing/extra holes or incorrect sequences.
- Core assumption: Current VLM architectures do not maintain explicit, mutable state for spatial transformations and rely on surface-level pattern completion.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and Section 5.1 describe lack of sequential reasoning; Figure 9 shows performance drops with more folds; ORIGAMISPACE does not isolate working memory.

### Mechanism 3: Rotation-Induced Physical Awareness Challenge
- Claim: Rotations disrupt models' physical situational awareness; they cannot update coordinate frames or integrate rotation into unfolding.
- Mechanism: Rotation changes paper orientation and crease directions; unfolding must adjust fold types accordingly; models often ignore rotation or misapply mapping rules.
- Core assumption: Models lack an internal physical model of transformation composition, especially how rotations affect subsequent symmetry operations.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract notes rotation challenge; Section 5.1 shows accuracy drops with rotations; transformation rules in Section 3.1 are not learned; DynaSolidGeo does not cover folding rotations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spatial Visualization**: Mental manipulation of objects; core cognitive ability benchmarked. Quick check: After folding a paper vertically and punching a hole, sketch the hole pattern when unfolded.
- **Symmetry and Reflection**: Folding creates mirror symmetry; unfolding reverses reflection precisely, including orientation. Quick check: Fold along a diagonal, punch a triangular hole—what are the shape, size, location, orientation after unfolding?
- **Visuospatial Working Memory**: Holding intermediate states (layer order, occlusion) across multi-stage folds; losing track causes errors. Quick check: After a horizontal then vertical fold, how many layers exist at each region and their top-to-bottom order?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Encoders -> Spatial Reasoning Module -> Output Generator (plus optional Dataset Generation Pipeline)

Critical path:
1. Parse input to identify action sequence and initial holes.
2. Reverse action sequence; adjust unfolding steps using transformation rules if rotations present.
3. Starting from final folded state, apply each unfolding: reflect holes across crease, considering layer connectivity.
4. Accumulate all hole manifestations on original unfolded plane.
5. Format results into required JSON.

Design tradeoffs:
- Modality: Text is symbolic but omits direction; 2D adds visual complexity; video provides 3D context but is heavy.
- Metrics: Exact match is strict; partial accuracy gives fine-grained feedback but requires careful penalty handling.
- Open-ended vs multiple-choice: Open-ended exposes reasoning chains but risks format errors; multiple-choice may hide failure modes.

Failure signatures:
- Extra holes: Over-generation from incorrect symmetry, ignoring layer depth, or extra unfolding steps.
- Missing holes: Under-generation from forgetting reflection, incorrect stacking, or impossible fold ordering.
- Incorrect unfolding sequence: Steps not properly reversed or transformation rules misapplied, especially after rotations.
- Attribute errors: Shape/size often correct but location and direction frequently wrong; direction errors spike with rotations.
- Group-wise: Performance drops sharply with more folds and combined rotations (Groups 5–9).

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline benchmarking: Evaluate top VLMs (e.g., o3, Claude Opus) across text, 2D, and video prediction; quantify exact match, partial scores, and error types.
2. Backward folding ablation: Generate tasks with all folds performed away from camera; compare to forward folding to test view-dependent representations.
3. Generalization task: Present analogical reasoning where one hole attribute changes between two folding sequences; measure transfer accuracy vs. full mental transformation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do state-of-the-art VLMs fail to correctly apply symmetrical transformations even when they accurately predict the unfolding sequence?
- Basis: Abstract states models struggle with applying symmetrical transformations despite correct sequence prediction.
- Why unresolved: Dissociation between sequential reasoning and spatial manipulation; unclear if due to geometric understanding, working memory, or symmetry representation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments decoupling sequence correctness from symmetry application; introspective analyses of model internal states during symmetry reasoning.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do rotations specifically disrupt VLMs' physical situational awareness in multi-stage spatial visualization tasks?
- Basis: Abstract notes rotations introduce a significant challenge to physical situational awareness.
- Why unresolved: Performance drops with rotations but not dissected; may stem from inability to update coordinate frames, misinterpret rotational effects, or fail to integrate rotation into planning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Isolate rotation in minimal tasks; measure accuracy with/without rotation; analyze error patterns for orientation tracking vs. fold-type transformation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What cognitive or architectural limitations cause the dramatic performance gap between spatial transfer tasks (no mental transformation) and true spatial visualization tasks?
- Basis: Abstract highlights o3 attains 71.6% on generalization (transfer) but only 25% on text-based prediction.
- Why unresolved: VLMs can reason relationally over spatial data but cannot execute mental manipulations; may lack iterative transformation mechanisms, sufficient dynamic sequence training, or rely on pattern matching.
- What evidence would resolve it: Compare architectures (transformer vs. recursive) on both tasks; see if simpler multi-step prediction predicts full visualization success; fine-tune on synthetic transformation sequences.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Why do textual representations yield higher accuracy for VLMs compared to 2D image or video inputs?
- Basis: Inferred from results: o3 achieves 25% exact match on text vs. 10.5% on 2D and 8% on video; paper suggests text mitigates mental transformation symbolically.
- Why unresolved: Unclear if advantage comes from LLMs' symbolic strength, removal of direction attributes, or absence of perceptual noise; text may discretize spatial relations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Test vision-only models on textual descriptions; re-introduce direction into text; compare attention patterns across modalities to see if visual inputs overload spatial processing.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Do failures in backward folding tasks indicate that some VLMs rely on view-dependent spatial representations rather than canonical, orientation-invariant ones?
- Basis: Inferred from backward prediction: Claude Opus drops to 0.56% exact match while o3 remains at 11.11%; paper infers some models rely on view-dependent representations.
- Why unresolved: Backward folding requires unfolding from an unseen side; divergence suggests encoding from fixed perspective, but direct evidence of representation bias is missing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Test on variants with varying camera angles or fold directions; probe internal representations for viewpoint consistency; train on mixed forward/backward data to see if view-invariance emerges.

## Limitations
- The exact VPython implementation (mesh indexing, layer stacking, validation rules) is unreviewed; subtle geometric differences could alter task difficulty.
- Model inference details (API parameters, video frame sampling, prompt templating) are underspecified, risking performance discrepancies.
- Dataset splits and random seeds are unavailable, preventing exact replication of reported scores.

## Confidence
- Symmetry/reflection failures: High (supported by controlled experiments and isolated transformation analysis).
- Working memory limits: High (clear monotonic drop with fold count and gap vs. spatial transfer task).
- Rotation challenges: Medium (correlated with complexity; evidence relies on group-wise comparisons not isolated ablation).
