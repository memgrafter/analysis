# Computation and Communication Efficient Federated Unlearning via On-server Gradient Conflict Mitigation and Expression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13795  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13795  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** FOUL reduces time-to-forget by over 50% compared to retraining (0.32 vs. 0.13 per round on PACS), achieves similar or better retain and test accuracy, and lowers communication (16.02 MB vs. 42.73 MB for retraining) and computation costs (2.35e16 FLOPs vs. 5.81e16) across multiple datasets.

## Executive Summary
Federated Unlearning (FUL) aims to efficiently remove specific clients' data from a trained federated learning model while preserving performance for remaining clients, avoiding the high computational and communication costs of full retraining. The proposed Federated On-server UnLearning (FOUL) framework introduces two stages: (1) Learning to Unlearn (L2U), which disentangles the model into domain-invariant causal and domain-specific non-causal subnetworks during training, and (2) on-server gradient matching that performs unlearning by manipulating only non-causal gradients on the server without accessing client data. Experiments on domain generalization datasets demonstrate that FOUL achieves faster forgetting, lower resource consumption, and comparable or better accuracy than retraining and state-of-the-art baselines.

## Method Summary
FOUL comprises two stages. First, during the Learning-to-Unlearn (L2U) phase, the model is trained with a disentangled architecture: a shared encoder feeds into two branches—a causal featurizer that captures domain-invariant features (encouraged via a prototypical invariance loss) and a non-causal featurizer that captures domain-specific details (encouraged via a variance-maximizing hinge loss). A reconstruction loss ensures representation completeness, and classification losses are applied. Second, for unlearning, the server freezes the causal branch and updates only the non-causal encoder. Clients compute local gradients on their data for the non-causal encoder and transmit them to the server. The server solves a closed-form optimization (Theorem 1) to find an aggregated gradient that aligns positively with retain clients' gradients and negatively with forget clients' gradients, then updates the global non-causal encoder. This process requires no client data on the server and only transmits a subset of parameters.

## Key Results
- Time-to-forget reduced by >50% (0.32 vs. 0.13 per round on PACS).
- Communication cost lowered to 16.02 MB vs. 42.73 MB for retraining.
- Computation cost reduced to 2.35e16 FLOPs vs. 5.81e16 for retraining.
- Achieves comparable or better retain/test accuracy across PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, and TerraIncognita.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Disentangling Causal and Non-Causal Representations
**Claim:** Disentangling the model into domain-invariant (causal) and domain-specific (non-causal) subnetworks enables unlearning to target only the non-causal part, preserving general knowledge.  
**Mechanism:** In L2U, two feature spaces are learned: causal features (Z_K) are forced invariant across domains via a prototypical loss (Eq. 4), while non-causal features (Z_V) capture domain-specific variance via a hinge loss on intra-class variance (Eq. 5). A reconstruction loss (Eq. 8) ensures both parts sufficiently represent the data.  
**Core assumption:** The label is determined solely by causal factors; non-causal factors are irrelevant for classification on retain domains but encode client-specific information to forget.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sec 3.1.1 describe the disentanglement; neighbor set provides limited direct evidence for representation separation.  
**Break condition:** If disentanglement fails (e.g., causal representations still contain domain-specific cues or non-causal representations capture label info), unlearning may degrade retain performance. Indicators: high invariant loss L_K or low variant loss L_V during L2U training.

### Mechanism 2: On‑Server Gradient Matching for Conflict Mitigation
**Claim:** Optimizing aggregation coefficients to align the aggregated gradient with retain clients and oppose forget clients achieves effective unlearning without client data.  
**Mechanism:** After L2U, only the non-causal encoder is updated. Clients compute local gradients on this branch; the server solves for per-client coefficients Γ that maximize cosine similarity with a weighted sum of retain gradients (Γ_R) and minimize similarity with a weighted sum of forget gradients (Γ_F). Theorem 1 provides a closed-form solution.  
**Core assumption:** Retain and forget gradients on the non-causal encoder are sufficiently distinguishable; optimizing a small set of coefficients suffices.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Sec 3.2.1, and Theorem 1; neighbor works on gradient conflict support the principle.  
**Break condition:** If retain and forget gradients are nearly aligned, the optimization cannot produce a discriminative gradient. Also, for very large client counts coefficient optimization may become unstable or slow.

### Mechanism 3: Parameter-Efficient Unlearning
**Claim:** Restricting unlearning to the non-causal subnetwork and performing gradient matching on-server drastically reduces communication and computation.  
**Mechanism:** Only the non-causal encoder parameters are updated; the causal featurizer and classifier remain frozen. Clients transmit only the non-causal gradient (or update), not the full model. The server aggregates via the closed-form solution, avoiding full retraining.  
**Core assumption:** The non-causal subnetwork contains all forget-relevant information; the causal subnetwork is truly invariant; gradient matching converges quickly.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Table 2, Fig 7; comparative results against 9 baselines confirm efficiency advantages.  
**Break condition:** Imperfect disentanglement leaves forget information in the causal branch, requiring more rounds and eroding efficiency gains. If the non-causal subnetwork is not substantially smaller than the full model, communication savings diminish.

## Foundational Learning
### Federated Averaging (FedAvg)
**Why needed:** FOUL modifies the standard FL aggregation process; understanding FedAvg (local SGD + server weighted average) is essential to see how unlearning deviates from normal operation.  
**Quick check:** In FedAvg, what determines the weight of each client’s update in the global model aggregation? (Answer: Typically the number of local data samples.)

### Causal Representation Learning
**Why needed:** The L2U stage relies on separating invariant (causal) and variant (non-causal) features; knowing the causal invariance principle explains why unlearning only the non-causal part preserves retain knowledge.  
**Quick check:** According to the causal structural model (Definition 5), what property must causal factors Z_K satisfy with respect to the label Y? (Answer: Y is a function of Z_K and noise, i.e., Z_K causally influences Y.)

### Gradient Conflict in Multi-Task/Domain Learning
**Why needed:** The on-server gradient matching uses cosine similarity to enforce alignment with retain gradients and misalignment with forget gradients, building on the idea that gradients from different domains can conflict.  
**Quick check:** What does a negative cosine similarity between two gradients imply about their update directions? (Answer: They point in roughly opposite directions, indicating conflicting information.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Training (L2U): shared encoder θ_E splits into causal encoder θ_K and non-causal encoder θ_V; both feed into decoder θ_D and classifier ξ. Losses combine reconstruction, variance maximization, invariance, and classification.  
Unlearning: Server holds global non-causal encoder θ_V,g and per-client coefficients Γ. Clients receive θ_V,g, compute gradient ∇θ_V on local data, send to server. Server receives gradients, computes aggregated gradient g_FOUL via Theorem 1 (using Γ to align with retain and oppose forget), updates θ_V,g. Clients pull updated θ_V,g and repeat.

**Critical path:**  
1. Training: Run FedAvg with L2U losses until convergence (full participation).  
2. Unlearning rounds: clients compute ∇θ_V on frozen non-causal branch; upload to server; server solves for Γ, computes g_FOUL, updates θ_V,g; clients download new θ_V,g; repeat for R rounds.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Disentanglement strength (loss coefficients) vs. overall accuracy: aggressive separation may hurt reconstruction or classification.  
- Dimensionality of causal/non-causal channels: higher causal capacity preserves more invariant knowledge but leaves less for unlearning, potentially reducing efficiency.  
- Coefficient optimization space: using only |U| coefficients is computationally cheap but may not fully capture gradient diversity; using full gradient vectors would be more expressive but infeasible for high-dimensional models.  
- Communication: sending only non-causal updates reduces bandwidth, but if the non-causal subnetwork is large, savings are limited.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Unlearning ineffective: Forget accuracy (FA) does not drop significantly after many rounds.  
- Over-unlearning: Retain/test accuracy (RA/TA) declines sharply after unlearning starts.  
- Poor disentanglement: During L2U, invariant loss L_K remains high or variant loss L_V remains low.  
- Gradient matching fails: Average cosine similarity between aggregated gradient and forget gradients stays positive.  
- Unexpected communication cost: Per-round communication similar to full-model transmission, suggesting non-causal subnetwork is not smaller or implementation error.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Main evaluation:** Compare FOUL against 9 baselines (Retraining, FATS, NoT, FedCDP, FedRecovery, FedOSD, FFMU, FUSED, MoDE) on PACS, TerraIncognita, VLCS, OfficeHome using FA, RA, TA, MIA, communication, and computation metrics. FOUL achieves competitive or better accuracy with lower cost.  
2. **Time-to-forget analysis:** Plot FA and RA over unlearning rounds for FOUL vs. retraining (with/without reset). Compute T2F (average FA reduction per round). FOUL reaches optimal forgetting in <50 rounds vs. ~75 for retraining, with T2F >0.32/round vs. 0.13/round.  
3. **Ablation on L2U:** Vary loss coefficients (α_rec, α_v, α_iv, α_gtc) and invariant channel dimensionality; monitor convergence, losses, and final unlearning performance. Identifies stable settings (e.g., α_rec=1, others ~1) and shows optimal invariant channels ≥16 on VLCS.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can the gradient-alignment optimization in FOUL be scaled to a large number of clients (e.g., >100) without degrading unlearning performance?  
*Basis:* The paper states that the gradient-alignment optimization performs best when the number of clients is moderate (e.g., fewer than 100). When the client population becomes extremely large, the optimization may converge to suboptimal solutions. No scaling solution is proposed.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent does FOUL’s reliance on separable domain-invariant (causal) and domain-specific (non-causal) features limit its applicability to FL scenarios where such disentanglement is not naturally present?  
*Basis:* The core L2U stage is built on the causal structure model, which assumes data can be decomposed into invariant causal and variant non-causal factors. Experiments are restricted to domain generalization datasets; no evaluation for heterogeneities that do not match this assumption (e.g., label skew, feature entanglement).

**Open Question 3:** Can the weighting coefficients of the L2U loss (αV, αK, αgtc, αrec) be set automatically to reduce manual tuning and improve robustness across FL tasks?  
*Basis:* The combined L2U loss involves manually chosen coefficients. The paper conducts sensitivity analysis by sweeping one coefficient at a time but offers no principled method for automatic selection, leaving hyperparameters fixed for all experiments.

**Open Question 4:** Is the time‑to‑forget (T2F) metric alone adequate to evaluate the efficiency‑quality trade‑off in federated unlearning, or does it need augmentation to capture retain accuracy stability?  
*Basis:* T2F is defined as the average reduction in forget accuracy per communication round and focuses exclusively on forgetting speed. It does not incorporate the behavior of retain accuracy during the process, which may temporarily decline in aggressive unlearning strategies.

## Limitations
- **Disentanglement validation:** The paper lacks direct evidence that the causal subnetwork ($Z_K$) is truly domain-invariant and that the non-causal subnetwork ($Z_V$) captures all client‑specific information.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Optimal values for loss coefficients ($\alpha_{\text{rec}}, \alpha_v, \alpha_{iv}, \alpha_{\text{gtc}}$) and the gradient‑matching parameter $\kappa$ are dataset‑specific and not thoroughly explored.
- **Gradient distinguishability assumption:** The method relies on retain and forget gradients pointing in sufficiently different directions; this may fail under high heterogeneity.
- **Evaluation setup:** Experiments use simulated IID clients per domain; real‑world non‑IID distributions could violate assumptions.
- **Implementation gaps:** Details on prototype maintenance, exact dataset splits, and the Time‑to‑Forget formula are missing, hindering exact replication.
