# Towards Distillation-Resistant Large Language Models: An Information-Theoretic Perspective

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03396
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03396
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A defense using conditional mutual information minimization and gradient angle maximization significantly degrades student accuracy (e.g., -11.98% on GSM8K for Qwen2.5-1.5B with AlphaNet) while preserving teacher performance.

## Executive Summary
Logit-based knowledge distillation (KD) poses a serious threat to proprietary large language models (LLMs) by extracting richer information from output logits than text-only distillation. Existing defenses only address text-based attacks, leaving a critical vulnerability. This paper introduces an information-theoretic defense that quantifies the distillation-relevant context in teacher logits using conditional mutual information (CMI). A learnable transformation matrix is applied to the teacher’s logits to minimize CMI while preserving label accuracy, guided by the Information Bottleneck principle. The method employs two losses: cross-entropy to retain task-relevant information and a gradient angle maximization term with a surrogate student to disrupt the correlation between inputs and logits. Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5 and Llama 3 models across GSM8K, MMLU, and MATH datasets demonstrate substantial drops in student accuracy (up to 11.98% on GSM8K) under various KD algorithms (KD, AlphaNet, MiniLLM, ABKD), with negligible impact on teacher utility, thereby effectively protecting intellectual property.

## Method Summary
The defense introduces a low-rank matrix \(M = E + AB\) (with rank \(r=32\)) that transforms the teacher’s logits \(Z\) into \(Z' = M \cdot Z\) during inference. During an offline training phase, \(M\) is optimized using two objectives: (1) cross-entropy loss \(L_{CE}\) ensures \(Z'\) still predicts the correct labels, preserving utility; (2) gradient angle loss \(L_{grad} = -\lambda \cdot \cos(\theta)\) maximizes the cosine distance between gradients of a surrogate student model when trained on \(Z\) versus \(Z'\), thereby corrupting the knowledge transfer signal. This formalism approximates CMI minimization via the Information Bottleneck trade-off. After training, \(M\) is fixed and integrated into the teacher’s pipeline, so all subsequent outputs are transformed before being used for distillation. The approach is validated across multiple teacher-student pairs and datasets, showing strong robustness against several advanced KD methods.

## Key Results
- Significant student accuracy degradation: e.g., 11.98% drop on GSM8K for Qwen2.5-1.5B attacked by AlphaNet.
- Defense effective across multiple KD algorithms (KD, AlphaNet, MiniLLM, ABKD) and datasets (GSM8K, MMLU, MATH-500).
- Teacher performance remains largely unaffected, preserving utility while protecting intellectual property.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Defense effectiveness is achieved by characterizing and minimizing the distillation-relevant information in teacher logits using Conditional Mutual Information (CMI).
- **Mechanism:** CMI, \(I(X;Z|Y)\), quantifies the contextual information in teacher logits \(Z\) about the input query \(X\) beyond the ground-truth label \(Y\). This contextual information is what logit-based knowledge distillation (KD) algorithms exploit. By minimizing CMI, the defense removes this exploitable signal while preserving label accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** The contextual information in logits that aids KD is operationally captured by the CMI between inputs and logits conditioned on labels. This links an information-theoretic quantity to the practical success of KD.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We characterize distillation-relevant information in teacher outputs via the conditional mutual information (CMI) between teacher logits and input queries, conditioned on ground-truth labels."
  - [section 4.1]: Theorem 2 formally proves that a learned logit transformation \(M\) reduces \(I(X;Z|Y)\) by forming a Markov chain \(Y→X→Z→Z'\).
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors include papers applying CMI to KD (e.g., "Bayes Conditional Distribution Estimation for Knowledge Distillation Based on Conditional Mutual Information"), providing contextual validation for using CMI as a measure of distillation-relevant information, though not for defense specifically.
- **Break condition:** If the transformation matrix cannot effectively reduce \(I(X;Z|Y)\) while preserving \(I(Z;Y)\) (e.g., due to poor optimization or model capacity limits), CMI minimization fails, and the defense provides little protection.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A learnable transformation matrix \(M\) is applied to teacher logits to achieve CMI reduction while preserving output utility, guided by the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle.
- **Mechanism:** The matrix \(M\) (initialized as identity) post-processes logits \(Z\) to produce \(Z' = M·Z\). Optimizing \(M\) decomposes the CMI minimization goal (\(I(X;Z|Y) = I(X;Z) - I(Z;Y)\)) into two sub-tasks: (1) minimizing \(I(X;Z)\) to compress input contextual information, and (2) maximizing \(I(Z;Y)\) to preserve task-relevant information for accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** The IB principle's trade-off between input compression (\(I(X;Z)\)) and label preservation (\(I(Z;Y)\)) is a valid and effective framework for designing a defense that removes contextual information without harming performance. The matrix transformation is sufficient to alter this information flow.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: Theorem 3 shows \(I(X;Z|Y)\) is an IB objective with \(\beta=1\). Lemma 4 provides a tractable upper bound for \(H(Y|Z)\), justifying cross-entropy loss (\(L_{CE}\)) as a surrogate for maximizing \(I(Z;Y)\).
  - [section 5.2]: Figure 3(a) shows teacher accuracy is preserved after transformation, confirming the \(L_{CE}\) component successfully maintains \(I(Z;Y)\).
  - [corpus]: Corpus contains work on "Fairness Overfitting in Machine Learning: An Information-Theoretic Perspective" and IB applications, but direct evidence for using a *transformation matrix* for *distillation defense* via IB is novel and not present in neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If the matrix's capacity (low-rank decomposition rank \(r\)) is too low, it cannot capture the needed transformations to reduce \(I(X;Z)\) effectively. If \(L_{CE}\) is weighted too low, teacher utility (\(I(Z;Y)\)) may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The contextual information in \(Z\) about \(X\) that aids KD is operationally carried by the gradients it induces in a surrogate student model. Maximizing the angular deviation between gradients from original (\(Z\)) and transformed (\(Z'\)) logits disrupts this information transfer.
- **Mechanism:** Since directly estimating \(I(X;Z)\) is intractable, the defense uses a proxy student. The gradients \(g\) of the total KD loss (\(L_{total}\)) with respect to student parameters \(\theta\) represent the learning signal derived from teacher logits. The loss term \(L_{grad} = -\langle g, g' \rangle / (||g||_F ||g'||_F)\) maximizes the cosine distance between \(g\) and \(g'\), thereby distorting the guidance signal transmitted from \(Z'\) compared to \(Z\).
- **Core assumption:** The *direction* of the student's gradient update during KD is the practical carrier of the distillation-relevant information from \(Z\) about \(X\). Altering this direction effectively attacks the distillation process, even if \(Z'\) still contains some statistical traces of \(X\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: Proposition 6 defines \(g\) as the gradient of the combined KD+SFT loss. \(L_{grad}\) is explicitly designed to maximize angular deviation between \(g\) and \(g'\).
  - [section 5.3]: Figure 5 shows \(L_{grad}\) drops to negative values (~-0.2, ~101.5° angle), confirming substantial gradient misalignment is achieved.
  - [section 5.2]: Significant student accuracy drops across all KD algorithms (e.g., -11.98% for AlphaNet on Qwen2.5-1.5B/GSM8K) demonstrate that distorted gradients successfully mislead distillation.
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors discuss gradient-based methods in KD (e.g., "In Good GRACEs" uses a score for teacher selection), but the specific use of *gradient angle maximization as a defense* is a novel contribution not directly mirrored.
- **Break condition:** If the surrogate student model is not a good analog for a real attacker's student (e.g., different architecture), the gradient distortion may not transfer. If \(\lambda\) is too low, \(L_{grad}\) has insufficient influence, and contextual information leakage persists.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Conditional Mutual Information (CMI)**
  - **Why needed here:** CMI (\(I(X;Z|Y)\)) is the core metric defining what "distillation-relevant information" is. Understanding CMI as "how much \(Z\) tells us about \(X\) after already knowing \(Y\)" is essential to grasp why minimizing it defends against KD.
  - **Quick check question:** If a teacher's logits always exactly match the one-hot label (\(argmax(Z)=Y\)), what is \(I(X;Z|Y)\) and why is this the ideal defended state?
- **Concept: Information Bottleneck (IB) Principle**
  - **Why needed here:** IB provides the theoretical framework that decomposes the CMI minimization goal into two complementary, tractable sub-problems: compressing input (\(I(X;Z)\)) and preserving label information (\(I(Z;Y)\)). This decomposition directly informs the two-loss design (\(L_{grad}\), \(L_{CE}\)).
  - **Quick check question:** In the IB objective \(I(X;Z) - \beta·I(Z;Y)\), what does minimizing \(I(X;Z)\) versus maximizing \(I(Z;Y)\) intuitively compel the model to do to its outputs?
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (Logit-based)**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire defense is predicated on understanding *how* logit-based KD works—using soft distributions (KL divergence) to transfer nuanced knowledge beyond hard labels. The defense targets the specific signal (logit distributions) that makes logit-based KD more powerful than text-only distillation.
  - **Quick check question:** Why are output logits (\(Z\)) considered a richer source of knowledge for KD than sampled text tokens? What specific information in \(Z\) does KD exploit that SFT does not?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Teacher Model (f_T) → Transformation Matrix (M) → Surrogate Student (f_S)
### Critical path
1. Offline Training Phase: With teacher frozen, iterate over dataset D. For each batch (q, o): compute Z = f_T(q); Z' = M·Z; forward student f_S(q) to get z_S; compute total KD loss L_total for student using Z and Z' separately to get gradients g and g'; compute L_CE and L_grad; backpropagate L_M = L_CE + λ·L_grad to update A, B.
2. Online Deployment Phase: Integrate the trained matrix M into the teacher's inference pipeline; for any query, compute logits Z and transform to Z' before decoding or API response.
### Design tradeoffs
- Defense Strength (λ) vs. Teacher Utility: Higher λ strengthens defense but risks lowering teacher accuracy if L_CE is overwhelmed.
- Matrix Capacity (r) vs. Efficiency: Higher rank r allows more expressive M for better CMI reduction but increases parameter count (~|V|×r). Default r=32 balances.
- Surrogate Student Fidelity: A better proxy student (same family, similar size) yields a more realistic gradient signal, making L_grad more effective, but increases training cost.
### Failure signatures
- Teacher Collapse: Defended teacher accuracy drops significantly (>2-3%) on key tasks, indicating L_CE failed to preserve I(Z;Y).
- Ineffective Defense: Student accuracy under distillation remains near vanilla levels, suggesting L_grad did not create sufficient gradient misalignment.
- Training Instability: L_grad or L_M fails to converge, or L_grad stays near 0 (cosine similarity ~1), invalidating the gradient proxy assumption.
### First 3 experiments
1. Baseline Replication: Without defense (M=E), run standard KD (e.g., AlphaNet) on a teacher-student pair (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B → 1.5B) on GSM8K. Verify student outperforms SFT-only baseline, confirming the attack is viable.
2. Defense Application: Integrate trained M (with λ=1, r=32) into the teacher. Rerun the same KD. Measure student accuracy drop. Expect significant degradation (e.g., >5% on GSM8K) with minimal teacher accuracy loss, validating the core mechanism.
3. Ablation - L_CE Component: Train M with λ=1 but set L_CE weight to 0. Evaluate teacher accuracy. Expect severe drop, proving L_CE is necessary for utility preservation and validating the IB decomposition's "max I(Z;Y)" sub-task.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Is optimizing the gradient angle loss (L_grad) theoretically equivalent to maximizing the mutual information I(X;Z) for LLMs, and under what conditions can this equivalence be formally proven?
**Basis:** The authors state in Appendix E: "due to the high-dimensional and intractability of text distributions, we do not provide a rigorous theoretical proof that optimizing L_grad is equivalent to maximizing the mutual information I(X;Z)."
**Why unresolved:** The paper relies on an operational interpretation connecting gradient distortion to information suppression but lacks a formal information-theoretic derivation for autoregressive language models. This gap limits understanding of the method's foundational guarantees.
**What evidence would resolve it:** A proof or disproof establishing the equivalence (or quantifying the gap) between gradient angular deviation and conditional mutual information reduction in the context of multi-token generation and high-dimensional text distributions.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How robust is the proposed defense against adaptive attacks where the attacker uses a student model architecture or distillation algorithm that is different from the proxy student employed during defense training?
**Basis:** The defense optimizes the transformation matrix against a specific surrogate student (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct for the Qwen teacher). The experiments evaluate on black-box students of varying sizes but from the same family, assuming similar inductive biases. The paper does not consider cross-architecture or cross-family attacks (e.g., a Llama teacher defended with a Qwen proxy, or vice versa).
**Why unresolved:** The surrogate student serves as a stand-in for the attacker's model. An adaptive attacker might choose a model with different capacity, attention patterns, or training data, potentially exploiting distributional shifts to recover contextual information from the transformed logits. The defense's transferability across diverse student architectures is untested.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments where the defended teacher is attacked by students with significantly different architectures (e.g., transformer vs. Mamba), training objectives (e.g., sequence-level KL vs. reverse KL), or even from different model families, measuring degradation in defense efficacy.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** What is the theoretically optimal or data-dependent way to set the loss weighting hyperparameter λ that balances task accuracy (L_CE) and defense strength (L_grad), beyond empirical tuning?
**Basis:** The paper ablates λ and observes a trade‑off: larger λ increases defense but risks harming teacher accuracy. The choice λ=1 is made empirically to balance these. No theory is provided to guide λ selection based on dataset properties, teacher/student model capacity, or distillation algorithm.
**Why unresolved:** λ controls the trade‑off between preserving label information (utility) and corrupting contextual gradients (defense). The optimal point likely depends on the difficulty of the task, the inherent mutual information in the teacher's outputs, and the attacker's strategy. A heuristic for setting λ would make the method more practical.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Analytical derivation linking λ to a desired budget on I(X;Z'|Y) or a validation‑based protocol that predicts optimal λ from a small calibration set of teacher‑student interactions.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Does the defense remain effective when the teacher model is queried with sampling‑based decoding (e.g., nucleus sampling, temperature > 0) instead of greedy decoding, and how does the stochasticity of the generation process interact with the learned transformation?
**Basis:** All experiments use greedy decoding for reproducibility. The transformation matrix is learned on logits from greedy sampling. In practice, API services often use stochastic decoding. The interaction between stochastic outputs and the fixed matrix transformation is unexplored.
**Why unresolved:** Sampling introduces randomness into the logit distribution seen by the student during distillation. The transformation matrix, optimized for deterministic greedy logits, might not effectively suppress contextual information under stochastic conditions, or could even amplify unintended biases. The defense's robustness to decoding strategy is an open practical concern.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate defense performance under various decoding parameters (temperature, top‑p) during the distillation process, comparing student accuracy to the greedy‑decode baseline.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** Can the proposed transformation matrix be detected or inverted by an attacker who has white‑box access to the teacher's API output logits, and if so, does this compromise the defense?
**Basis:** The paper assumes a black‑box attack scenario where the attacker only sees the output logits. The transformation is a learned linear operation M. While M is not directly published, an attacker collecting many (X, Z') pairs could potentially estimate M via system identification if the original distribution of Z is known or can be approximated.
**Why unresolved:** The security of the defense relies on the secrecy of M. However, if the attacker can access the teacher model directly (even if only to query inputs and receive transformed logits), and has prior knowledge about the teacher's typical output distribution (e.g., from a similar public model), they might attempt to recover or compensate for M. The paper does not analyze such inversion attacks.
**What evidence would resolve it:** An analysis of the identifiability of M from the mapping (X → Z') and empirical tests where an attacker attempts to approximate the original logits (e.g., via matrix inversion using collected data) before performing distillation, measuring any recovery in student performance.

## Limitations
- Implementation details unclear: exact application of M (replacement vs. additive), which student layer's gradients are used, inclusion of SFT in L_total, and surrogate student fidelity.
- Low-rank decomposition may restrict achievable CMI reduction and thus defense strength.
- Training instability and potential lack of generalization to unseen model families or adaptive attack strategies.
