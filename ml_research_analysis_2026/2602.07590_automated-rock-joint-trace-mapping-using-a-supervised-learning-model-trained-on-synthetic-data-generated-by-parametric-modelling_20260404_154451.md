# Automated rock joint trace mapping using a supervised learning model trained on synthetic data generated by parametric modelling

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.07590
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07590
*   **Reference Count:** 20
*   **Primary Result:** Synthetic data from discrete fracture network (DFN) models enables supervised learning for rock joint trace mapping; training strategy (mixed vs. fine-tuning) depends on real label quality, and qualitative geological assessment is essential beyond pixel metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scarcity of labeled images for automated rock joint trace mapping by generating synthetic data with perfect labels using parametric discrete fracture network (DFN) models. These models produce geologically realistic joint networks, capturing persistence and connectivity. Segmentation models (U-Net, DeepLabv3+) are trained via either mixed training (synthetic and real data from the start) or fine-tuning (synthetic pre-training then real data). The study finds that synthetic data effectively provides a structural prior for transfer learning. Mixed training excels when real labels are clean and consistent, while fine-tuning is more robust to noisy, imperfect real labels. Although pixel-based Dice scores are moderate, qualitative evaluation often shows geologically superior traces, highlighting that expert visual assessment is necessary when ground truth is imperfect.

## Method Summary
The method generates a large synthetic dataset (25,584 images) by parameterizing joint sets, block shapes, and chronology in DFN software (FracMan), then rendering 2D slope images with perfect binary masks via Rhino/Grasshopper. Two real-world datasets are used: a "box" domain with nearly perfect manual labels (968 images) and a "slope" domain with noisy, subjective semi-automatic labels (3,000 images). For training, standard segmentation architectures (U-Net, DeepLabv3+) are trained using two strategies: 1) **SimpleMixed**: simultaneous training on synthetic and real data, and 2) **Finetune**: pre-train on synthetic, then fine-tune on real. Experiments vary the proportion of real data (0–100%) and test generalization between different real sites. Evaluation combines standard metrics (Dice, IoU) with a custom qualitative "mean quality score" based on geological recognisability, persistence, boundary localisation, and noise.

## Key Results
*   Synthetic DFN data provides a viable structural prior; zero-shot performance on real slopes is poor, but fine-tuning with moderate real data yields useful generalization despite moderate Dice scores.
*   Training strategy is determined by label quality: SimpleMixed performs best on the clean "box" domain, while Finetune is superior for the noisy "slope" domain.
*   Pixel-level Dice scores frequently misalign with geological usability; models selected for highest Dice can be qualitatively worse, necessitating expert-driven model selection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Synthetic data from DFN models encodes a geologically meaningful structural prior (joint persistence, connectivity, node distributions) that is difficult to obtain from imperfect manual annotations. The model first learns this prior, which is then adapted to real-world visual variations, enabling effective supervised learning despite label scarcity. Break condition: if synthetic patterns fail to capture site-specific joint genesis or mechanical realism, the prior may mislead transfer.

### Mechanism 2
The optimal training strategy depends on real label noise. Clean labels (box-domain) allow immediate alignment via mixed training. Noisy, inconsistent labels (slope-domain) destabilize early training; fine-tuning first builds a stable synthetic prior, then adapts gradually, preventing corruption by label bias. Break condition: extreme label sparsity or oversimplified synthetic data can cause both strategies to fail.

### Mechanism 3
Standard pixel metrics (Dice) are insufficient for model selection because real labels are imperfect and subjective; a prediction can be geologically superior (e.g., more persistent, cleaner boundaries) yet score lower due to label noise. Hence, qualitative, geology-driven evaluation is mandatory to identify usable models for engineering.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Synthetic DFN Generator (FracMan + Rhino/Grasshopper) -> Rendered Images & Masks -> ML Training Module (PyTorch + U-Net/DeepLabv3+) -> Evaluation Module (Dice + Qualitative Scoring) -> Experiment Controller.

**Critical path:** Define DFN parameters -> generate synthetic dataset -> configure experiment (domain/strategy/real proportion) -> train model with real-data validation -> compute test Dice -> select epochs for qualitative review -> compute mean quality score -> compare strategies.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Synthetic/real ratio:** More synthetic strengthens prior but widens appearance gap; more real adapts but requires scarce labels.
*   **Strategy:** SimpleMixed (one-stage) is sensitive to label noise; Finetune (two-stage) is robust but needs careful LR scheduling to avoid overwriting prior.
*   **Architecture:** U-Net vs. DeepLabv3+ showed minimal difference; simpler U-Net is preferable unless context is proven critical.
*   **Metrics:** Optimising for Dice may overfit noisy labels; qualitative inspection is cheap but requires expert time.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Quantitative:** Validation Dice (joints) remains <0.2 after fine-tuning with 50% real slope data or in domain generalization.
*   **Qualitative:** Predictions show fragmented traces, excessive thickness, or spurious networks; mean quality score <2.
*   **Training:** In Finetune Stage 2, Dice increases but visual quality degrades (thickening, noise) → overfitting to noisy labels.
*   **Zero-shot:** Synthetic-only model produces no coherent traces on real slopes, only on simple box textures.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Synthetic-only baseline:** Train U-Net on 100% Synthetic DFN, test on real box/slope. Establishes zero-shot ceiling; expects slope failure, possible box success.
2.  **SimpleMixed low real data:** Train with 10% real + 90% synthetic (e.g., Larvik) under SimpleMixed, test on held-out Larvik. Tests mixed training with minimal data in consistent-label domain; expects good results only for box.
3.  **Finetune moderate real data:** Pretrain on 100% Synthetic DFN, then finetune on 30% real slope (Larvik), test on held-out Larvik. Tests fine-tuning robustness under noisy labels; expects improved qualitative scores despite moderate Dice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   How do structural characteristics of synthetic DFN data (e.g., joint set configurations, connectivity, chronology) influence the transferability of trained models to new geological settings?
*   How can qualitative assessments of predicted joint traces be standardized into reproducible quantitative metrics that align with expert evaluation of geological usability?
*   How can the synthetic data generation pipeline be extended to incorporate more complex geological structures (e.g., bedding, foliation, faults) while maintaining parametric control and producing visually realistic images?
*   What post-processing steps are needed to convert raw segmentation masks into reliable discontinuity orientation and spacing measurements for engineering analysis, and how can these be integrated into the ML pipeline?

## Limitations
*   Synthetic DFN realism may not capture site-specific joint genesis (e.g., mechanical layering), risking misleading priors; validation beyond Larvik/Rv4 is lacking.
*   Qualitative evaluation rubric is textually described but not formally quantified or blind-tested; inter-rater reliability is unknown.
*   Exact training commands and epoch selection logic for qualitative ranking are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
*   Generalization failure between Larvik and Rv4 is shown, but disentangling geological vs. imaging shift contributions remains open.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (DFN synthetic prior):** Medium – Supported by internal experiments and DFN theory, but lacks external geological validation of pattern representativeness.
*   **Mechanism 2 (strategy vs. label quality):** High – Strong empirical evidence from controlled box/slope comparisons; consistent with noise-robust learning literature.
*   **Mechanism 3 (metrics insufficiency):** Medium – Paper’s qualitative examples are convincing, but scoring rubric opacity prevents full confidence in generalizability.
