# FD$^2$: A Dedicated Framework for Fine-Grained Dataset Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25144
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25144
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Improves SRe²L++ by up to 12.1% and FADRM+ by up to 13.8% on fine-grained datasets with IPC=1.

## Executive Summary
Dataset distillation compresses training data into synthetic sets, but existing decoupled methods fail on fine-grained datasets due to retained intra-class variation and oversimilar samples. FD² introduces a plug-in framework using Counterfactual Attention Learning (CAL) to localize discriminative regions and construct class prototypes. During distillation, it applies a fine-grained characteristic constraint that aligns each sample with its class prototype while repelling others, and a similarity constraint that diversifies attention across same-class samples. Evaluated on CUB-200-2011, FGVC-Aircraft, and Stanford Cars, FD² significantly boosts state-of-the-art baselines with minimal overhead, yielding clearer local structures and enhanced inter-class separability.

## Method Summary
FD² augments decoupled dataset distillation by modifying the sample distillation stage. In pretraining, a teacher model with a CAL head generates attention maps to localize discriminative regions and updates online class prototypes via momentum-based aggregation. For distillation, samples are generated per class in groups of size \(N_S\). For each sample, features and attention are extracted; the fine-grained characteristic constraint (\(L_F\)) aligns the sample with its class prototype and repels it from others using a normalized Euclidean distance. The similarity constraint (\(L_S\)) maximizes dispersion between the current sample’s attention and those of previous same-class samples. The total loss combines baseline losses (\(L_{\text{other}}\), \(L_{\text{cls}}\)) with weighted \(L_F\) and \(L_S\) (\(\lambda\) controls balance). After distillation, soft labels from the teacher’s backbone train student models.

## Key Results
- On CUB-200-2011, FGVC-Aircraft, and Stanford Cars, FD² improves SRe²L++ by up to 12.1% and FADRM+ by up to 13.8% at IPC=1 with ResNet18.
- Demonstrates strong cross-architecture generalization to DenseNet121 and ConvNeXt.
- Adds minimal time/memory overhead while producing distilled samples with clearer local structures and reduced intra-class similarity.
- Theoretical analysis confirms constraints enhance intra-class compactness and inter-class separability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### 1. Counterfactual Attention Learning (CAL) localizes discriminative regions and constructs class prototypes
- **Mechanism:** CAL applies counterfactual intervention by replacing attention maps to compute factual and counterfactual representations, enhancing reliability. Class prototypes are updated online via momentum and regularized to aggregate discriminative features.
- **Core assumption:** Attention maps consistently identify key local regions, and prototypes summarize these for class-level supervision.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.3 details CAL equations; Fig. 6 shows FD² samples have clearer local structures than baselines.

### 2. Fine-grained characteristic constraint improves intra-class compactness and inter-class separability
- **Mechanism:** Uses symmetrically normalized Euclidean distance to pull samples toward same-class prototypes and push away from others, weighted by \(\beta\).
- **Core assumption:** Distilled samples should exhibit reduced intra-class variation and increased inter-class separation in prototype space.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.4 defines \(L_F\); Fig. 3a t-SNE shows higher intra-class compactness; Fig. 3b larger nearest-neighbor center distances; ablation (Tab. 5) shows \(\beta=0.5\) optimal.

### 3. Similarity constraint diversifies attention across same-class samples
- **Mechanism:** For each sample in a class group, maximizes distance between its attention map and those of previous samples via Eq. (10).
- **Core assumption:** Diverse attention maps lead to varied local cues, providing richer information.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.4 defines \(L_S\); Fig. 1b shows FD²’s diverse vs. baseline’s homogeneous attention; ablation on \(N_S\) (Tab. 6) shows \(N_S=4\) optimal; \(N_S=5\) saturates.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Teacher model (Backbone + CAL head) -> Sample distillation (with \(L_F\) and \(L_S\) constraints) -> Loss integration (\(L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{other}} + L_{\text{cls}} + \lambda L_F + (1-\lambda)L_S\)) -> Soft label generation (backbone branch) -> Student training.
- **Critical path:** 1) Pretrain Backbone+CAL on full dataset, maintaining online prototypes. 2) For each class, distill IPC images in groups of size \(N_S\): for \(i\)-th sample, extract features \(z\) and attention \(A\); compute \(L_F\) (prototype alignment) and \(L_S\) (if \(i>1\), attention diversity with previous samples); compute \(L_{\text{cls}}\) from both classifiers; optimize \(L_{\text{total}}\). 3) Generate soft labels using backbone branch for student training.
- **Design tradeoffs:** CAL ratio \(\alpha\) (higher \(\alpha\) increases supervision but may destabilize; \(\alpha=0.5\) optimal). Hyperparameters \(\beta\) (alignment vs. repulsion; \(\beta=0.5\)) and \(\lambda\) (weights \(L_F\) vs. \(L_S\); \(\lambda=0.8\)). Group size \(N_S\) (diversity vs. cost; \(N_S=4\) recommended). Minimal overhead vs. baselines.
- **Failure signatures:** No accuracy gain on fine-grained datasets → constraints inactive or attention poor. Poor cross-architecture performance → samples lack generalizable features. High intra-class similarity → \(L_S\) under-weighted or \(N_S\) small. Training instability → \(\alpha\) or \(\lambda\) mis-set.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Integrate FD² into SRe²L++ on CUB-200-2011 IPC=1 ResNet18; expect ≥3% top-1 improvement (53.4% → 56.4%).
  2. Constraint ablation: \(L_F\) only, \(L_S\) only, both; expect both together highest (64.9% vs 63.4% baseline).
  3. Attention visualization for a class (e.g., Black-footed Albatross); expect more diverse and focused heatmaps with FD².

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to non-decoupled paradigms:** How to integrate FD² into matching-based or generative distillation? *Basis:* Conclusion states future work on broader paradigms. *Why unresolved:* Constraints rely on CAL teacher pretraining, which may not fit paradigms without explicit pretraining.
2. **Inconsistent gains on general datasets:** Why smaller gains on simpler datasets like ImageNette? *Basis:* Paper notes reduced gains for small-capacity students on simpler datasets. *Why unresolved:* Interaction between student capacity, dataset complexity, and constraints not systematically tested.
3. **Fixed group size \(N_S\) optimality:** Is \(N_S=4\) optimal across all settings? *Basis:* \(N_S=4\) from ablations on CUB IPC=5; saturation at \(N_S=5\). *Why unresolved:* Not generalized across datasets/IPC; no adaptive strategies explored.
4. **Robustness to noisy annotations:** How does FD² handle label noise? *Basis:* Not addressed; all experiments on clean splits. *Why unresolved:* Noisy labels could corrupt prototypes and attention; sensitivity unexamined.
5. **Prototype sophistication beyond averages:** Can part-aware or hierarchical prototypes improve alignment? *Basis:* Single prototype per class may oversimplify fine-grained structures. *Why unresolved:* No comparison to more complex prototypes; single vector may not capture multimodal distributions.

## Limitations
- Localization dependency: CAL attention may fail on complex scenes.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Optimal \(\beta\), \(\lambda\), \(N_S\), \(\alpha\) require tuning; robustness across datasets unproven.
- Limited evaluation: Only three fine-grained datasets and ResNet18 teachers; broader generalizability unknown.
- Theoretical link not directly verified: Claims of compactness/separability not correlated with accuracy.

## Confidence
- Performance gains on fine-grained datasets: High
- CAL localization quality: Medium
- Constraint impact on feature space: High
- Cross-architecture generalization: Medium
