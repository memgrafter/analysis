# Realistic Face Reconstruction from Facial Embeddings via Diffusion Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13168  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13168  
- **Reference count:** 19  
- **Primary result:** FEM-KAN achieves 83.7% ASR on standard FR (IRSE50) and 84.4% on PPFR (DCTDP), with 17× less training time than FaceTI.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces the Face Embedding Mapping (FEM) framework to evaluate privacy risks in face recognition (FR) and privacy-preserving FR (PPFR) systems by reconstructing realistic high-resolution face images from leaked embeddings. FEM uses a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) to map target embeddings into the latent space of a pre-trained identity-preserving diffusion model (IPA-FaceID), which then generates the reconstructed images. The approach significantly outperforms prior attacks (MAP2V, FaceTI) in success rate and efficiency, while remaining effective against partial and protected embeddings, establishing FEM as a robust tool for assessing PPFR leakage.

## Method Summary
FEM consists of a lightweight mapping network (KAN or MLP) trained to translate embeddings from a target FR/PPFR model into the embedding space expected by a frozen, pre-trained diffusion model (IPA-FaceID). Training uses public face images (FFHQ) to extract paired embeddings: one from the target model and one from IPA-FaceID’s built-in FR. The mapping network is trained with mean squared error (MSE) to align the mapped target embedding with the IPA embedding. At inference, a leaked target embedding is passed through the trained mapper, and the output conditions IPA-FaceID (via cross-attention) to generate a face image with a fixed text prompt. Success is measured by verifying the generated image against separate evaluation FR models at a low false accept rate (FAR=0.01).

## Key Results
- FEM-KAN achieves average attack success rates (ASR) of 83.7% (IRSE50) and 84.4% (DCTDP), surpassing MAP2V by ~6% and FaceTI by ~11%.
- Maintains robustness with partial embeddings (32.5% ASR at 50% leakage on ArcFace) and protected embeddings (71.6% ASR against MLP-Hash).
- Reduces training time by 17× compared to FaceTI and inference time by 42× compared to MAP2V.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: KAN for Non-Linear Embedding Mapping
FEM-KAN uses Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks, which replace fixed linear weights with learnable univariate functions (splines) on edges. The Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem guarantees that any continuous multivariate function can be represented through compositions of sums and univariate functions, enabling accurate modeling of complex, non-linear relationships between disparate FR/PPFR embedding spaces and IPA-FaceID’s space. Evidence: The paper states KAN is used for the embedding-to-face attack and cites the theorem’s suggestion that high-dimensional functions can be reconstructed via lower-dimensional operations. Break condition: If target embedding spaces are non-composable or lie outside IPA-FaceID’s generative manifold, KAN fails to converge.

### Mechanism 2: Pre-trained Diffusion as a Strong Decoder
Leveraging IPA-FaceID as a frozen generator avoids costly end-to-end training. Trained to generate faces conditioned on identity embeddings, its strong prior on natural face manifolds produces high-fidelity images from any embedding within its latent space once properly aligned. Evidence: The "None" baseline (direct IPA-FaceID use) yields ~7.5% ASR, confirming raw target embeddings are out-of-distribution; mapping is essential. Break condition: If target embeddings encode identity in a way fundamentally incompatible with IPA-FaceID’s conditioning (e.g., orthogonal latent factors), no shallow mapping preserves identity.

### Mechanism 3: Lightweight Mapping for Efficiency
FEM freezes the massive IPA-FaceID parameters (~1B+) and trains only a small mapping network (3 layers) with MSE loss. This bypasses the instability and resource demands of adversarial training (FaceTI) or latent search (MAP2V). Evidence: FEM trains in 3 hours vs FaceTI’s 51 hours; inference is 42× faster than MAP2V. Break condition: If the required mapping is highly non-linear or involves extreme distribution shifts (e.g., due to strong privacy transformations), a shallow network underfits, degrading reconstruction quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem**  
  *Why needed:* Justifies KAN’s architectural choice; guarantees any continuous multivariate function (like an embedding space mapping) can be represented as sums of compositions of univariate functions.  
  *Quick check:* What two types of functions (univariate and bivariate) are sufficient to compose any continuous multivariate function per the theorem?

- **Diffusion Models for Conditional Generation**  
  *Why needed:* IPA-FaceID is a diffusion model; understanding its denoising process and conditioning on embeddings explains why it serves as a powerful, frozen decoder.  
  *Quick check:* In a latent diffusion model like IPA-FaceID, what role does cross-attention play when conditioning on a face embedding?

- **Face Embedding Spaces and Metric Learning**  
  *Why needed:* The attack operates on embeddings from FR/PPFR systems; knowing embeddings are learned via metric losses (e.g., ArcFace) explains identity clustering and mapping feasibility.  
  *Quick check:* Why do embeddings from the same identity from a single FR model tend to be close in the embedding space, while different identities are far apart?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Target FR/PPFR -> FEM (KAN/MLP) -> IPA-FaceID -> Eval FRs

- **Critical path:**  
  *Training:* Public face images → extract target_embedding (from Target FR/PPFR) and ipa_embedding (from IPA-FR) → train FEM to minimize MSE(FEM(target_embedding), ipa_embedding).  
  *Inference:* Leaked target_embedding → FEM → mapped_embedding → IPA-FaceID (fixed prompt) → reconstructed image → eval FRs for ASR.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *KAN vs MLP:* KAN offers better non-linearity; paper shows FEM-KAN consistently outperforms FEM-MLP.  
  - *Diffusion vs GAN:* Diffusion gives higher realism than StyleGAN-based baselines but requires a mapping network; MAP2V is training-free but slow.  
  - *Mapping network size:* Paper uses 3-layer networks; deeper networks risk overfitting without gain; shallow designs rely on strong decoder prior.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Low ASR (<50%) on clean FR targets:** FEM failed to map into IPA-FaceID’s latent region; check training convergence, embedding normalization, or target model distance.  
  - **High artifact rate / low visual quality:** Mapped embeddings fall into boundary region; may need more training data, perceptual loss, or larger FEM.  
  - **No degradation with partial embeddings:** If ASR drops <10% from 100% to 50% leakage, FEM is robust; collapse indicates memorization; try dropout.  
  - **Training instability:** Rare for MSE; check learning rate, batch norm in MLP, or KAN grid settings.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Reproduce Table 1:** Train FEM-KAN on FFHQ (90%) with IRSE50/DCTDP; generate 5 images per test embedding from CelebA-HQ; compute ASR against MobileFace, ElasticFace, GhostFaceNet, ArcFace at FAR=0.01; expect ~83-84% average ASR.  
  2. **Partial embedding robustness (Fig. 6b):** Zero-out last 50% of embedding dimensions; reconstruct and compute ASR on ArcFace; expect ~32-35% ASR with FEM-KAN.  
  3. **Protected embedding attack (Table 3):** Train FEM-KAN on MLP-Hash protected embeddings from HFCF; evaluate ASR on 4 eval FRs; expect high ASR on MobileFace/ElasticFace (~80%+) but lower on ArcFace/GhostFace (~55-71%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What design choices in PPFR systems make them more/less vulnerable to diffusion-based reconstruction?**  
  *Basis:* Paper tests several PPFRs (DCTDP, HFCF, PartialFace, MinusFace) with varying ASRs but no algorithmic analysis.  
  *Why unresolved:* Understanding correlations could guide development of robust privacy-preserving techniques.

- **How does mapping architecture (KAN vs MLP) affect fidelity and efficiency?**  
  *Basis:* FEM-KAN outperforms FEM-MLP in ASR but the reason for KAN’s advantage in embedding space deformation is not analyzed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Could inform future attack designs or defenses by highlighting critical aspects of embedding deformation.

- **To what extent does domain shift (e.g., FFHQ vs private identities) degrade attack performance?**  
  *Basis:* ASR drops on LFW (lower resolution), implying sensitivity to data distribution; demographic/pose variation impact unquantified.  
  *Why unresolved:* Real-world attacks involve out-of-distribution identities; assessing this gap is crucial for realistic risk evaluation.

- **Are there embedding protection methods beyond those tested that can thwart diffusion attacks while maintaining accuracy?**  
  *Basis:* Current protections (PolyProtect, MLP-Hash, SlerpFace) still leak significant identity; newer or adversarial obfuscation techniques unexplored.  
  *Why unresolved:* Robust PPFR requires schemes specifically countering diffusion-based attacks without sacrificing utility.

- **How do real-world factors (image quality, occlusions, poses) affect bypass of commercial verification systems?**  
  *Basis:* Paper uses fixed front-facing prompts and controlled datasets; only brief FAS test on LFW; no evaluation of commercial APIs under varied conditions.  
  *Why unresolved:* Practical attacks involve diverse conditions; without this, true threat level in deployed systems remains uncertain.

## Limitations
- KAN’s theoretical guarantee (Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem) may not fully align with discrete, high-dimensional face embeddings; external validation is lacking.
- Assumption that IPA-FaceID’s conditioning accepts any mapped embedding is unverified; the boundary of acceptable mappings is undefined.
- Results rely on specific datasets and models; generalizability to unseen FR/PPFR systems or real-world demographics is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Main ASR results (83-84%):** High (reproducible tables, multiple targets)
- **KAN mechanism (superior non-linear mapping):** Medium (ablation shows benefit vs MLP, but theoretical link is indirect)
- **Efficiency gains (17× training reduction):** High (explicit timing comparison)
- **Robustness to partial/protected embeddings:** Medium (results shown, but protection generation assumptions)

## Next Checks
1. **IPA-FaceID Sensitivity Test:** Train FEM-KAN on a different identity-preserving diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion with IP-Adapter FaceID). If ASR drops below 70%, it confirms dependence on the specific IPA-FaceID prior.
2. **KAN Capacity Ablation:** Systematically vary KAN grid size and spline order while matching MLP parameter count. If performance gains vanish with minimal capacity, KAN’s advantage may be architectural rather than theorem-driven.
3. **Partial Embedding Protocol Audit:** Replicate the paper’s partial embedding experiment exactly (random 50% zeroing with padding) versus using native partial features from PPFR models like PartialFace. Discrepancy in ASR would indicate robustness claims are methodology-specific.
