# SCE-LITE-HQ: Smooth visual counterfactual explanations with generative foundation models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.17048
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17048
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves high-resolution (up to 1024×1024) counterfactual explanations without dataset-specific training, with strong performance (e.g., 86.7% Non‑Adversarial rate on CelebA‑Blond) and low computational cost (0.66 seconds per sample).

## Executive Summary
SCE‑LITE‑HQ presents a scalable method for generating visual counterfactual explanations (CFEs) that eliminates the need for training dataset‑specific generative models. It leverages pretrained foundation models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3) as a latent‑space prior, combined with a knowledge‑distilled, smoothed surrogate classifier for stable gradient guidance, and a dual‑phase masking strategy for sparse, diverse outputs. Evaluated on benchmarks including CelebA‑Blond, a poisoned variant, and Camelyon17, SCE‑LITE‑HQ delivers state‑of‑the‑art validity, sparsity, and diversity while reducing computational cost by an order of magnitude versus prior art. Ablation studies confirm that the surrogate classifier is critical for producing non‑adversarial, semantically meaningful explanations.

## Method Summary
The pipeline comprises three stages. **(1) Surrogate training:** A classifier mimicking the target model is trained with regularizations (MixUp, label smoothing, adversarial training) to yield a smooth loss landscape. **(2) Latent optimization:** The input image is encoded into a latent code via the foundation model’s encoder. This latent is iteratively refined over fixed timesteps: at each step, a denoised estimate is computed using the model’s deterministic (Rectified Flow) dynamics; gradients from the surrogate guide the latent toward the target class; a spatially adaptive mask restricts changes to salient regions; for multiple CFEs, a cumulative exclusion mask prevents overlap with prior modifications. **(3) Decoding:** The optimized latent is decoded to pixel space. The process requires no task‑specific generator training and scales to 1024×1024 resolution.

## Key Results
- **CelebA‑Blond:** 86.7% Non‑Adversarial rate, 72.1% sparsity, 62.4% diversity, 72.0% NAFR.
- **Poisoned CelebA‑Blond Male:** 100% NA, 71.6% sparsity, 82.8% NAFR, 35.8% diversity, 21.6% debiasing gain.
- **Camelyon17:** 75.8% NA, 53.9% sparsity, 47.5% NAFR, 38.9% diversity, 47.9% gain.
- **Ablation:** Surrogate guidance increases NA from 44.4% to 86.7%.
- **Efficiency:** 0.66 seconds per sample, 11.5 GB peak GPU memory—far below baselines like SCE and ACE.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent Space Constraint via Foundation Models
Operating within the latent space of a pretrained generative foundation model (GFM) constrains counterfactual search to the natural data manifold. The input image is encoded into a compressed code; optimization perturbs this code; the GFM’s decoder maps it back to high‑dimensional pixel space, ensuring outputs remain realistic. **Core assumption:** The GFM’s vast pretraining yields a semantically structured latent space that generalizes across domains and resolutions. **Evidence:** The method achieves high‑resolution results without dataset‑specific training. **Break condition:** If the GFM’s latent space does not align with the target domain (e.g., medical images), outputs may become unrealistic.

### Mechanism 2: Smoothed Surrogate Gradient Guidance
Instead of noisy gradients from the original black‑box classifier, a surrogate classifier is trained via knowledge distillation with MixUp, label smoothing, and adversarial training. This creates a smoother loss landscape with Lipschitz‑continuous gradients, preventing optimization from finding adversarial, non‑robust perturbations. **Core assumption:** A classifier with locally linear gradients yields human‑interpretable feature changes. **Evidence:** Ablation on CelebA‑Blond shows replacing vanilla gradients with surrogate gradients raises NA from 44.4% to 86.7%. **Break condition:** Excessive regularization may misalign surrogate with original classifier, reducing flip rate.

### Mechanism 3: Dual‑Phase Masking for Sparsity and Diversity
A residual‑based masking strategy enforces spatial sparsity (minimal edits) and diversifies multiple CFEs. *Phase I:* per‑step adaptive mask highlights regions with significant pixel residuals, allowing changes only there. *Phase II:* for subsequent CFEs, a cumulative exclusion mask fixes all regions modified by previous CFEs, forcing alternative edits. **Core assumption:** Smoothed pixel residuals provide an accurate map of causal features. **Evidence:** High sparsity scores (e.g., 72.1% on CelebA‑Blond) and visualizations show fewer attribute changes than baselines. **Break condition:** Inappropriate mask thresholds either prevent flips or yield excessive edits; cumulative mask may block critical regions if earlier CFEs are suboptimal.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input image `x₀` and black‑box classifier `f` → Surrogate module `f̂` (trained separately) and foundation model `GFM` (encoder `E`, decoder `D`, velocity network `v_θ`) → Optimization loop (iterative latent updates) → counterfactual `x_cf = D(z_0)`.

**Critical path:**  
`z₀ = E(x₀)`. For `t = T` down to `1`: compute denoised estimate `ẑ_t` using `v_θ(z_t,t)`; compute gradient `g_t = β∇_z ℓ_c(f̂(D(ẑ_t)), y') + λ∇_z ℓ_d(‖z_t–z₀‖)`; apply adaptive mask `m_t` (and for `k>1` cumulative exclusion mask); update `z_t ← z_t – η g_t`. After loop, decode `x_cf = D(z_0)`.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Foundation model choice:* Rectified Flow (SD3) offers deterministic stability but may have less expressive latents than advanced diffusion.  
- *Surrogate strength vs. fidelity:* Over‑regularization yields smooth gradients but risks decision boundary misalignment.  
- *Mask thresholds (`τ_inpaint`):* balance sparsity and flip success; requires tuning per dataset.  
- *Speed vs. quality:* Single‑trajectory masked approach prioritizes speed (0.66 s) over some diversity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low NA: surrogate misalignment, mask too restrictive, or GFM cannot generate valid CF.  
- Implausible/blurry outputs: latent update diverged from GFM manifold (excessive guidance or missing mask).  
- Identical CFEs: ineffective cumulative mask or optimization stuck in local mode.  
- High memory/latency: using very high‑resolution GFM without optimizations.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Single sample debug:** Run pipeline on a clear CelebA‑Blond image; verify each step (latent inversion, mask highlights hair region, final flip and realism).  
2. **Surrogate ablation:** Replace surrogate gradients with original classifier gradients; expect more artifacts and lower NA (as per Table 2).  
3. **Mask sensitivity sweep:** Vary `τ_inpaint` (0.1, 0.3, 0.5) over a batch; plot NA vs. sparsity to find Pareto front and calibrate threshold.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (β, λ, τ_inpaint, T) and full surrogate training details (epochs, optimizer, regularization strengths) are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- The approach relies on latent‑space alignment of a pretrained foundation model; domain mismatch (e.g., medical images) may lead to implausible counterfactuals, as suggested by the break condition for Mechanism 1.
- Mask threshold τ_inpaint requires per‑dataset tuning; its sensitivity is not thoroughly analyzed, which could affect usability across tasks.

## Confidence
- **Latent Space Prior (Mechanism 1):** Medium confidence. High‑resolution results support it, but potential failure on out‑of‑distribution domains is a concern.
- **Smoothed Surrogate Guidance (Mechanism 2):** High confidence. Ablation (NA 44.4% → 86.7%) provides strong causal evidence.
- **Dual‑Phase Masking (Mechanism 3):** Medium confidence. Sparsity metrics and visuals are promising, but threshold robustness is unquantified.
- **Efficiency claim:** High confidence. Reported 0.66 s per sample vs. ~25 s baselines is dramatic, though it depends on precise SD3 implementation and GPU.

## Next Checks
1. **Surrogate gradient ablation:** On a held‑out set, replace the surrogate’s gradients with the original classifier’s gradients; expect a significant drop in Non‑Adversarial rate and more adversarial artifacts.
2. **Mask threshold sweep:** Vary τ_inpaint (e.g., 0.1, 0.3, 0.5) and plot NA against sparsity to assess whether the chosen threshold lies near the Pareto front or on a steep performance cliff.
3. **Cross‑domain latent inversion test:** Encode and reconstruct images from a novel domain (e.g., satellite or sketches) using the same foundation model; compute LPIPS/PSNR to detect latent misalignment that would compromise counterfactual plausibility.
