# Agentic Critical Training
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08706
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08706
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ACT improves agent task performance by ~5 points over IL/RL and transfers gains to general reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond) without task-specific data.

## Executive Summary
Agentic Critical Training (ACT) addresses a key limitation of imitation learning (IL) for LLM agents: IL teaches what action to take but not why, leading to poor critical reasoning and failure recovery. ACT instead uses reinforcement learning to train agents to judge which of two candidate actions (expert vs. model-generated) is better in a given state. The model receives a reward only for correct selections, forcing it to autonomously develop chain-of-thought reasoning about action quality rather than imitating pre-specified reflection text. Across ALFWorld, WebShop, and ScienceWorld, ACT consistently boosts performance when combined with IL or RL, and it generalizes to out-of-distribution agent tasks and standard reasoning benchmarks.

## Method Summary
ACT employs a two-stage Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) pipeline. First, it constructs a critique dataset `D_critic` by pairing each expert state-action `(s, a⁺)` with an alternative action `a⁻` sampled from the initial policy `πθ₀`. The model is trained on `D_critic` to select the better action using a composite reward: `R_acc` for correct selection, `R_adm` for admissible but wrong actions, and `R_fmt` for format compliance. No reasoning text is supervised; the model must generate its own CoT to maximize reward. Second, the ACT-enhanced model undergoes standard RL action training on expert trajectories, further aligning it to generate expert-matching actions. This injects critical reasoning without relying on external reflection corpora.

## Key Results
- Average gains of **+5.07 points over IL** and **+4.62 points over RL** on ALFWorld, WebShop, and ScienceWorld.
- Outperforms the Early Experience method by **+2.42 points on average**.
- Demonstrates **strong out-of-distribution generalization** on agent tasks and improves on **MATH-500 and GPQA-Diamond** without any reasoning-specific training data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards forces the model to autonomously develop actionable reasoning about action quality rather than imitating pre-specified reflection text.
- **Mechanism:** ACT presents two candidate actions (expert vs. model-generated) and uses a verifiable reward (correct selection) as the sole training signal. The model must generate chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning that causally leads to the correct choice to maximize reward. No target reasoning text is provided; the model must discover the discriminative features of action quality internally.
- **Core assumption:** Expert actions are generally superior to model-generated alternatives from the initial policy, making the binary comparison a valid training objective.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "By rewarding whether the model's judgment is correct, ACT drives the model to autonomously develop reasoning about action quality, producing genuine self-reflection rather than imitating it."; [section 2.3] "Unlike Early Experience... that generates reflection text and then trains on it via IL, ACT uses verifiable rewards to drive the emergence of critical thinking."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Constructing contrastive state-action pairs (expert vs. alternative) creates a curriculum that teaches the model to evaluate actions relative to suboptimal options, building critical awareness absent in standard IL.
- **Mechanism:** For each expert state-action pair, an alternative action is sampled from the current policy. The model is trained to prefer the expert action. This forces the model to contrast outcomes and infer why the expert action succeeds where the alternative fails, internalizing a model of action quality.
- **Core assumption:** Model-generated alternatives are, on average, inferior to expert actions, providing meaningful negative examples.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.2] "For each expert state-action pair... we construct an ACT example... Pair the expert action a⁺ with each alternative a⁻... These preference pairs are formed based on the hypothesis that expert actions are generally superior to model-generated ones."; [section 4.2] "IL models, having never observed failure states during training, repeat the same failed action indefinitely... The ACT model diagnoses the root cause through its internal reasoning."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The critical reasoning learned via ACT generalizes beyond the specific agent tasks to broader reasoning capabilities because it instills a meta-cognitive pattern of evaluating and comparing candidate solutions.
- **Mechanism:** ACT training optimizes for correct selection among alternatives, which requires checking consistency, verifying constraints, and backtracking from failures. These patterns transfer to general reasoning problems (e.g., MATH, GPQA) where multiple solution paths or answer choices exist.
- **Core assumption:** The cognitive process of discriminating between action quality in an agentic context shares computational structure with evaluating reasoning steps or answer options in abstract problems.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "ACT improves performance on general reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond) without any reasoning-specific training data."; [section 4.4, case study] "ACT exhibits self-verification behavior: after performing an initial derivation, the model checks its answer by substituting back into the original equations."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**
  - **Why needed here:** ACT uses RL where the reward is a verifiable outcome (correct action selection) rather than a learned reward model or token-level supervision. This is essential for training the model to align its internal reasoning with ground-truth action quality without mimicking text.
  - **Quick check:** Why does RL with a binary correctness reward incentivize genuine reasoning more effectively than supervised fine-tuning on reflection text?
- **Concept: Imitation Learning (IL) and its Pitfalls**
  - **Why needed here:** IL is the standard baseline for agent training but suffers from "what to do, not why" and catastrophic forgetting of reasoning skills. Understanding IL's limitations motivates the shift to a discriminative RL objective.
  - **Quick check:** How does fine-tuning on short, action-centric trajectories cause reasoning collapse on general benchmarks?
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning at the Action Level**
  - **Why needed here:** ACT's core operation is constructing preference pairs (expert vs. alternative) to create a discriminative task. This requires understanding that the learning signal comes from relative judgment, not absolute action prediction.
  - **Quick check:** Why is sampling alternatives from the current policy important for the contrastive signal, and what would happen if alternatives were random or from a fixed distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Expert Trajectory Database -> Alternative Action Sampler (from πθ₀) -> Filter & Pair Constructor (D_critic) -> ACT Trainer (GRPO) -> RL Action Trainer (GRPO) -> Evaluation Harness
- **Critical path:** Data construction (sampling alternatives from πθ₀) → ACT training (binary judgment) → RL action training → Evaluation. The ACT stage must precede RL action training to inject critical reasoning.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Cost of alternative sampling: collecting alternatives from πθ₀ is expensive but reusable across model sizes.
  - K=1 vs. larger K: paper uses K=1; larger K could provide more negatives but increases noise if πθ₀ is poor.
  - GRPO group size G: Larger G improves reward estimation but increases memory; paper varies G by model size.
  - KL penalty (β): set to 0.0 in paper; adding KL may stabilize but could slow learning.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Model ignores candidate actions and outputs a fixed response regardless of input.
  - Model alternates selection between Action 1 and Action 2 without reasoning (random or position bias).
  - Reasoning traces appear but selection accuracy remains at chance (50%), indicating decoupled "thinking."
  - OOD performance degrades more than IL, suggesting overfit to contrastive patterns.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train IL and RL on ALFWorld using provided expert trajectories. Verify RL > IL. This establishes the starting point.
  2. **ACT integration:** Train ACT-only, then IL w/ ACT and RL w/ ACT. Verify ACT w/ RL achieves highest score. Check failure recovery traces.
  3. **Generalization test:** Take ALFWorld-trained models and evaluate on MATH-500/GPQA-Diamond without fine-tuning. Verify ACT improves over CoT baseline and IL collapses. Inspect self-verification traces.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the mechanism by which ACT transfers critical reasoning skills to general reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond) despite domain mismatch?
- **Basis in paper:** ACT improves on these benchmarks without any reasoning-specific data, and case studies show self-verification behavior, but the causal link is unexplained.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper hypothesizes a general mechanism but lacks ablations to isolate whether the gain comes from RL, contrastive learning, or the agentic domain itself.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablations comparing ACT to supervised contrastive training, and probing models for reasoning strategy changes on general tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is reinforcement learning necessary for ACT's success, or could a supervised contrastive objective on the same (state, expert, alternative) pairs yield comparable critical reasoning?
- **Basis in paper:** ACT uses RL (GRPO) to reward correct action selection, arguing that RL forces autonomous development of reasoning. No supervised ACT baseline is included.
- **Why unresolved:** The advantage over Early Experience (supervised) may be due to data differences, not RL; a direct comparison is missing.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train a supervised ACT model with cross-entropy loss on D_critic and compare to RL-ACT in both agentic and general reasoning performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the number of alternative actions (K) and their source affect ACT's data efficiency and performance? The paper uses K=1 with alternatives from the initial policy.
- **Basis in paper:** Section 2.2 describes sampling K alternatives; K=1 is used. The design space is unexplored.
- **Why unresolved:** K=1 may limit learning signal richness, while larger K increases cost and potential noise. Optimal values are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run experiments varying K and sampling strategies (e.g., from multiple policies) and measure performance gains and sample complexity.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why does ACT exhibit stronger out-of-distribution generalization than IL or RL? The paper notes larger OOD gains but does not explain whether this stems from better reasoning generalization or broader state coverage in D_critic.
- **Basis in paper:** ACT's improvement over RL is larger on OOD ALFWorld (3.73pp) than ID (2.15pp), but no controlled analysis is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear if ACT's training data naturally covers more diverse states or if the learned evaluation criterion generalizes better; these factors are confounded.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze state distribution overlap between D_critic and test sets; train ACT on subsets with constrained diversity to isolate algorithmic effect.

## Limitations
- **Data construction ambiguity:** Exact procedure for collecting expert trajectories (`D_expert`) for ALFWorld relies on external work (Feng et al.) without implementation details.
- **Initial policy (`πθ₀`) unclear:** Unclear whether it is the raw base model or a lightly fine-tuned variant; this affects alternative quality and contrastive signal strength.
- **Insufficient ablations:** The role of `K` (number of alternatives) and reward components (`R_adm`, `R_fmt`) is not systematically evaluated.

## Confidence
- **ACT > IL/RL on agent tasks:** High – Consistent gains across three benchmarks with standard deviations.
- **ACT improves general reasoning (MATH, GPQA):** Medium – Positive out-of-distribution results but no mechanistic explanation or testing on additional suites.
- **RL-driven autonomous reasoning development:** Medium – Evidence from case studies and comparison to Early Experience, yet direct proof of "genuine" reasoning vs. shortcut learning is lacking.
- **Contrastive curriculum effect:** Low – The necessity of pairing expert with model-generated alternatives is not isolated; simpler RL formulations might suffice.
- **Cross-domain transfer via meta-cognition:** Low – The hypothesized shared computational structure is speculative; no in-depth analysis of reasoning patterns is presented.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate K (number of alternatives):** Train ACT with K=1,2,4 on ALFWorld; compare final success rates and training stability to confirm whether more negatives strengthen contrastive learning.
2. **Measure alternative action quality:** Execute all sampled `a⁻` in the environment; compute their success rates. If the success gap between expert and `a⁻` is small, alternative sampling must be improved.
3. **Cross-domain generalization test:** Take an ACT-trained model from ALFWorld and evaluate without further training on a held-out embodied task (e.g., MetaWorld or a new WebShop split). This probes the claimed meta-cognitive transfer and defines boundary conditions.
