# SMAC: Score-Matched Actor-Critics for Robust Offline-to-Online Transfer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17632
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17632
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SMAC enables stable offline-to-online transfer with no initial performance drop and reduces online regret by 34–58% relative to the best baselines across six D4RL tasks.

## Executive Summary
Offline reinforcement learning often produces policies that fail catastrophically when fine‑tuned online, immediately losing performance. This failure is attributed to low‑reward valleys separating offline and online optima in the loss landscape. SMAC (Score‑Matched Actor‑Critic) addresses this by (1) regularizing the Q‑function to match the action‑gradient of the dataset’s score function—estimated via a return‑conditioned diffusion model—and (2) using the Muon optimizer to encourage flat minima. This combination aligns the offline and online solutions, ensuring they are linearly connected. As a result, standard online algorithms like SAC and TD3 can fine‑tune SMAC’s pretrained actor‑critic without an initial drop, achieving lower online regret than strong baselines such as CalQL, IQL, and TD3+BC.

## Method Summary
SMAC first trains a return‑conditioned diffusion model via Reinforcement via Supervision (RvS) on the offline dataset to approximate the score ∇ₐ log π_D(a|s). During offline SAC pretraining, it adds a score‑matching loss L_SM = E[‖∇ₐ Q_θ(s,a) − α_ψ(s) ϵ_ω(s,a,w,1)‖²] to the critic loss, aligning the Q‑function’s gradients with the estimated score. All networks (critic, actor, α_ψ) are optimized with the Muon optimizer, which biases updates toward flat minima. The pretrained actor‑critic is then fine‑tuned online using any standard algorithm (e.g., SAC, TD3) with a mixed replay buffer containing both offline data and new interactions. This process yields a smooth transfer path without performance degradation.

## Key Results
- SMAC transfers stably to SAC and TD3 with **no initial reward drop** on all six D4RL tasks, whereas CalQL, IQL, and TD3+BC suffer significant drops.
- In four tasks, SMAC reduces online regret by **34–58%** relative to the best baseline.
- SMAC attains the **lowest average normalized regret** across all tested online algorithms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SMAC prevents the typical fine‑tuning performance drop by regularizing the Q‑function to match the action‑gradient of the dataset’s score, aligning offline and online optima.
- **Mechanism:** SMAC adds a loss L_SM(θ,ψ) = E[‖∇ₐ Q_θ(s,a) − α_ψ(s) ϵ_ω(s,a,w,1)‖²] to the SAC critic loss, where ϵ_ω is a diffusion model trained with return‑conditioned RvS to approximate ∇ₐ log π_D(a|s). This enforces a first‑order condition akin to the exact max‑entropy identity ∇ₐ log π* ∝ ∇ₐ Q*, but applied to the data distribution. By making Q’s gradients consistent with the dataset’s score, the critic disincentivizes out‑of‑distribution actions proportionally to their deviation, smoothing the Q‑landscape.
- **Core assumption:** The dataset’s score provides a reliable direction for shaping Q; the dataset should contain sufficiently expert behavior or cover relevant state‑action regions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “regularizes the Q‑function to match the action‑gradient of the dataset’s score function.” Section 6 derives L_SM and connects it to the Max‑Entropy identity. Corpus: Neighbors (ENOTO, CalQL) focus on Q‑pessimism or explicit policy constraints, not gradient alignment via a learned dataset score.
- **Break condition:** A poor diffusion score estimate (due to limited data or inadequate RvS conditioning) may push Q into a misaligned basin. Datasets with only suboptimal trajectories could also break the assumption.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using the Muon optimizer encourages flat minima, which are more linearly connected to future online optima, thereby avoiding low‑reward valleys during finite‑step fine‑tuning.
- **Mechanism:** Muon updates parameters under the spectral norm (largest singular value) rather than Adam’s max‑of‑max norm. This bias toward flatter solutions reduces the Hessian’s condition number, a property linked to better generalization and linear mode connectivity in supervised learning. SMAC applies Muon to the critic, actor, and α‑network updates during offline training, steering parameters into a wide basin likely containing both offline and online maxima.
- **Core assumption:** Flatness in the loss landscape correlates with linear connectivity in the reward landscape; two optima in the same flat basin have a high‑reward straight line between them.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “using the Muon optimizer to encourage flat minima.” Section 6.3 describes Muon’s spectral‑norm updates and cites links between flatness and transfer. Appendix I shows that replacing Muon with Adam causes performance drops in 3/6 environments. Corpus: Work on flat minima (SAM, SWA) exists, but none apply Muon specifically for offline‑to‑online RL.
- **Break condition:** If the task requires sharp minima (e.g., precise contact dynamics), Muon’s bias may prevent reaching the best offline performance. Muon’s effectiveness depends on large batch sizes; insufficient batch size could impair its ability to find flat minima.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The combination of score‑matching and Muon yields offline maxima that are linearly connected to online maxima, allowing any standard online RL algorithm to fine‑tune without an initial performance drop.
- **Mechanism:** Score‑matching ensures the Q‑function obeys a structure consistent with the dataset’s policy gradients, reducing mis‑specification between offline and online objectives. Muon further places the parameters in a flat basin that likely spans both the offline solution and the online optimum. As a result, the straight‑line interpolation between offline and online checkpoints is monotonic in reward, as verified experimentally. Gradient‑based fine‑tuning therefore starts on a “ridge” and ascends without crossing low‑reward valleys.
- **Core assumption:** Linear interpolation is a valid proxy for the path taken by typical gradient descent; if the line is monotonic, the optimizer will not need to exit the flat basin to improve. Also assumes the online algorithm does not drastically change the objective.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “This alignment ensures offline and online maxima are linearly connected, enabling smooth transfer.” Section 5 presents reward landscapes: SMAC shows no valley during interpolation, while baselines show clear dips. Section 7 empirical results: SMAC transfers smoothly to SAC and TD3 in all 6 tasks, with 34–58% regret reduction in 4 tasks. Corpus: Recent work identifies connectivity as a factor but does not implement SMAC’s combined mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Severe mismatch between offline and online tasks (e.g., domain shift) could break linear connectivity even with SMAC. Fine‑tuning with algorithms that heavily regularize the policy (TD3+BC) may also push updates out of the flat basin, as seen in two environments.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Mode Connectivity
- **Why needed:** SMAC’s hypothesis hinges on the idea that two performant policies (offline and online) lie in the same connected basin, so fine‑tuning follows a high‑reward path. This concept originates from supervised learning and is operationalized via linear interpolation.
- **Quick check:** How would you experimentally test whether two neural network checkpoints are linearly connected in the RL reward landscape?

### Concept: Offline Reinforcement Learning Challenges
- **Why needed:** Offline RL must overcome distributional shift and Q‑value overestimation. Understanding why standard methods fail (e.g., CQL’s pessimism, IQL’s implicit Q) clarifies SMAC’s departure toward gradient alignment.
- **Quick check:** What is the core issue when applying TD‑learning to a fixed dataset, and how do offline RL algorithms typically address it?

### Concept: Diffusion Models for Score Estimation (RvS)
- **Why needed:** SMAC’s regularizer needs an estimate of ∇ₐ log π_D(a|s). It obtains this via a diffusion model trained with reinforcement via supervision (RvS), which conditions on trajectory returns to capture multi‑modal behavior.
- **Quick check:** In RvS, what does the conditioning variable (e.g., normalized return) represent, and why is it set to a high value during inference?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Diffusion model (ϵ_ω) -> Critic ensemble (Q_θ) -> Actor (π_ϕ) -> Alpha network (α_ψ) -> Muon optimizer -> Offline replay buffer (D) -> Online replay buffer -> Online RL algorithm (SAC/TD3)
- **Critical path (offline training):**  
  1. Pre‑train diffusion model ϵ_ω on D with RvS: minimize denoising score matching, conditioning on min‑max normalized trajectory return. Freeze ϵ_ω.  
  2. Initialize critic Q_θ (with target networks), actor π_ϕ, α_ψ.  
  3. For N offline gradient steps:  
     - Sample batch (s,a,r,s′) from D.  
     - Compute L_SM: sample actions from 50/50 mixture of π_ϕ and uniform; compute score estimate ϵ_ω(s,a,w,1); L_SM = ‖∇ₐ Q_θ(s,a) − α_ψ(s) ϵ_ω‖².  
     - Critic loss: L_AC + κ·L_SM. Update θ and ψ with Muon.  
     - Actor loss: L_π = E[−Q_θ(s,a) + log π_ϕ(a|s)]; update ϕ with Muon.  
     - Polyak‑average target network: Q_¯θ ← λ Q_θ + (1−λ) Q_¯θ.  
  4. Save final (Q_θ, π_ϕ) for online fine‑tuning.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Diffusion pre‑training cost: Significant compute; may use large pre‑trained VLAs with fine‑tuning.  
  - κ balancing: Too small fails to align; too large may over‑fit the score and degrade offline performance.  
  - Muon batch size: Muon benefits from large batches; offline batch size must be scaled accordingly.  
  - RvS requirement: Needs trajectory‑level returns; not available for infinite‑horizon datasets (e.g., hopper‑medium‑replay).  
  - Gradient computation: Computing ∇ₐ Q adds autograd overhead but is feasible.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High L_SM loss throughout offline training indicates poor alignment.  
  - Immediate reward drop after the first online step signals a breakdown of linear connectivity.  
  - Offline reward plateau far below optimal suggests either κ mis‑set or diffusion model inaccurate.  
  - Ablations: Replacing Muon with Adam introduces a valley in the interpolation plot, confirming reliance on flatness.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Linear interpolation test:** Pre‑train offline, fine‑tune with SAC, evaluate reward along the line between offline and final online parameters. SMAC should show monotonic improvement; baselines should show a dip.  
  2. **Component ablation:** Run SMAC without L_SM (κ=0), without Muon (Adam), and without RvS conditioning. Compare offline performance and transfer smoothness.  
  3. **Cross‑algorithm transfer:** Pre‑train SMAC on all D4RL tasks, fine‑tune with SAC, TD3, and TD3+BC. Compute normalized regret. Expect SMAC to achieve lowest regret and smooth transfer regardless of the online algorithm.

## Limitations
- Dataset score reliability depends on data quality; random or highly suboptimal data may mislead the regularizer.
- Muon’s bias toward flat minima may prevent reaching the best offline performance in tasks requiring sharp solutions.
- Smooth transfer is not guaranteed with all online algorithms (e.g., TD3+BC fails in some tasks), indicating sensitivity to the fine‑tuning objective.

## Confidence
- Alignment via score‑matching: Medium
- Flat minima via Muon: Medium
- Linear connectivity and smooth transfer: Medium
- Empirical superiority: High

## Next Checks
1. **Expand environment suite:** Evaluate SMAC on additional D4RL tasks (e.g., ant‑medium‑replay) and on datasets without episode returns to test the RvS conditioning requirement.
2. **Dataset quality ablation:** Train diffusion models on datasets of varying expertise (random to expert) and measure SMAC’s offline performance and transfer smoothness. Expect degradation on random data.
3. **Optimizer comparison:** Replace Muon with SAM/SWA and vary batch sizes. Measure flatness (e.g., Hessian spectrum) and linear connectivity to isolate Muon’s specific contribution.
