# Position: LLMs Must Use Functor-Based and RAG-Driven Bias Mitigation for Fairness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07368  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07368  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Proposes a dual strategy of functor-based transformations and RAG to mitigate LLM biases, with functor methods claimed to reduce gender stereotyping by up to 72% compared to adversarial training.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) exhibit harmful biases, such as stereotyping developing countries with manual labor and developed countries with high‑skill jobs. This position paper argues for a two‑pronged mitigation strategy combining category‑theoretic functor transformations with retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG). The functor component mathematically remaps the model’s internal biased semantic structures to unbiased canonical forms, while RAG dynamically injects diverse, factual external knowledge during inference to counteract parametric biases. The integrated framework targets both ingrained representational bias and contextual blind spots, aiming for robust fairness beyond incremental fixes. Preliminary evidence suggests functor methods can reduce gender stereotyping by up to 72% compared to adversarial training, and RAG improves evidence grounding.

The paper outlines a concrete architectural plan, including a functor transformation module, a fairness‑aware RAG engine, and an integration layer. It discusses design tradeoffs, failure modes, and foundational concepts from category theory, attention mechanisms, and RAG. While the proposal is theoretically grounded, uncertainties remain regarding implementation details, corpus curation, and synergy validation, prompting calls for further empirical research.

## Method Summary
The proposed system integrates two bias mitigation techniques. The functor‑based debiasing models the LLM’s semantic space as a category where concepts are objects and associations are morphisms; a linear projection \(P^*\) derived from scatter matrices of demographic (\(S_D\)) and occupational (\(S_O\)) concepts is applied to embeddings to orthogonalize demographic directions while preserving occupational relationships. The RAG component retrieves documents from a curated external corpus, filters them for fairness, and fuses them via cross‑attention to ground generation in evidence. The combined pipeline processes input through tokenization, functor projection, LLM backbone, concurrent retrieval, and context injection before generation. Key design choices include the trade‑off parameter \(\lambda\), target dimension \(d_u\), and the fairness‑aware reranker. Evaluation metrics include Demographic Parity Deviation (DPD), Occupational Preservation Score (OPS), Stereotype Alignment Rate (SAR), and utility indices like ROUGE and MMLU.

## Key Results
- Functor‑based debiasing reduces gender stereotyping in occupation predictions by up to 72% versus adversarial training.  
- RAG dynamically injects diverse, factual external knowledge during inference, improving fairness through evidence‑grounded outputs.  
- Integrated functor + RAG framework targets both ingrained structural bias and contextual blind spots for robust, equitable model behavior.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Functor‑Based Structural Debiasing
The functor‑based approach models the LLM’s conceptual space as a category \(C\) with objects as tokens/concepts and morphisms encoding learned associations (e.g., attention scores). A functor \(F: C \rightarrow U\) is implemented as a linear projection \(P\) optimized to minimize intra‑group distances for demographic concepts (\(S_D\)) while preserving inter‑group distances for occupational concepts (\(S_O\)), via eigendecomposition of \(C = S_D + \lambda S_O\). This yields a projection matrix \(P^*\) that approximately nullifies demographic directions in the embedding space while preserving task‑relevant semantics. The core assumption is that biases are systematic distortions in the categorical structure that can be removed by a structure‑preserving map. Evidence includes a 72% reduction in gender stereotyping versus adversarial training; however, poor estimation of \(S_D\) or \(S_O\) or a mis‑specified projection dimension can cause over‑debiasing or loss of semantic information.

### RAG‑Driven Contextual Grounding
RAG combats parametric biases by dynamically injecting diverse, factual external knowledge during inference. For a query, relevant documents are retrieved from a curated corpus (e.g., balanced labor statistics, counter‑stereotypical biographies), reranked for fairness, and fused into the context via cross‑attention. This allows the model to condition generation on external evidence that overrides biased internal associations. The approach assumes the retrieval corpus is sufficiently diverse and unbiased, and that the retriever accurately matches queries to relevant documents. Evidence suggests RAG improves fairness through evidence‑grounded outputs; risks include retrieving biased documents or failing to override parametric biases.

### Integrated Synergy for Robust Fairness
The synergy aims to address both ingrained structural bias and contextual blind spots. The functor module preprocesses representations to remove demographic leakage, while RAG supplies external evidence to correct residual bias and ensure factual grounding. They operate at different stages (representation vs. generation) and are designed to be compatible. The assumption is that LLM biases are multi‑faceted and require multiple intervention points. However, the synergy is largely conceptual; potential issues include functor distorting semantics needed for retrieval, or RAG context overwhelming debiased representations.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Functor Transformation Module (linear projection \(P^*\)) -> LLM Backbone  
RAG Engine (retriever + reranker + context fusion) -> Context Injection -> LLM Backbone  
Integration Layer -> coordinates timing and compatibility between functor and RAG

### Critical path
1. Input text → tokenization → initial embeddings.  
2. Functor projection: embeddings transformed via \(P^*\) to debiased space.  
3. Debiased embeddings processed by LLM backbone.  
4. Concurrent retrieval query formulated from last token representation sent to external index.  
5. Top‑k documents reranked for fairness and injected into context window.  
6. LLM attends jointly to internal states and retrieved tokens (cross‑attention) to generate final output.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Fairness vs. Utility**: Strong debiasing may erase subtle distinctions; aggressive RAG filtering may omit relevant context. Tuning \(\lambda\) and retrieval fairness thresholds is crucial.  
- **Latency vs. Granularity**: RAG adds retrieval overhead; per‑layer functor adapters increase parameters and memory but may improve fine‑grained bias control.  
- **Static vs. Dynamic**: Functor is fixed after training (or slowly updated), while RAG adapts per query. Balancing influences (e.g., always apply functor, enable RAG only for high‑risk queries) affects performance and cost.

### Failure signatures
- **Demographic parity violation**: Post‑functor embeddings still cluster by demographic attribute.  
- **Retrieval bias**: Retrieved documents show skewed sentiment or association toward protected groups.  
- **Context override failure**: LLM disregards retrieved evidence and reverts to parametric biases.  
- **Performance drop**: Significant degradation on standard NLU/generation benchmarks.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Isolated functor evaluation**: Load a pretrained LLM (e.g., LLaMA‑7B), inject linear projection \(P^*\) between embedding layer and first transformer block. Compute bias metrics (WEAT, SEAT) on occupation‑gender analogies and compare to baseline and adversarial debiasing baselines.  
2. **RAG fairness audit**: Use a biased prompt (e.g., “Recommend jobs for people from [developing country]”) with base LLM + RAG. Vary retrieval corpus (balanced vs. Wikipedia) and measure output stereotype strength (e.g., via StereoSet). Log retrieval diversity (proportion of documents mentioning counter‑stereotypical examples).  
3. **Integrated system benchmark**: Deploy full system on occupational recommendation scenario. Evaluate using DPD, OPS, utility (ROUGE‑L on non‑bias tasks). Perform ablation study (remove functor, remove RAG) to quantify each component’s contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can LLM outputs be restructured to mitigate demographic association biases while preserving contextual relevance and accuracy?  
- **Basis in paper:** Explicitly posed in Problem 1 regarding job recommendation stereotyping.  
- **Why unresolved:** Existing methods fail to address deep structural biases; the proposed functor + RAG framework is theoretical and unvalidated for this scenario.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical evaluations on controlled occupational recommendation tasks (e.g., MUSE dataset) showing reduced country‑based disparities without drop in recommendation quality.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How can RAG retrieval be designed to avoid introducing new biases from external knowledge sources while still promoting fairness?  
- **Basis in paper:** Acknowledged in Section 5.2 as a critical risk: retrieval from historically marginalizing sources may perpetuate biases.  
- **Why unresolved:** The proposed “bias‑aware retrieval pipeline” is conceptual; no concrete algorithms or audits guarantee neutrality at scale.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarking RAG with curated, bias‑audited corpora, demonstrating via metrics like Stereotype Alignment Rate that retrieval consistently reduces versus amplifies demographic biases.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How can functor‑based transformations and RAG be compositionally integrated—beyond parallel application—to create synergistic bias mitigation where retrieval dynamically influences functor mappings?  
- **Basis in paper:** Listed as a future direction in Section F.2: compositional integration where retrieval candidates are filtered, re‑ranked, or transformed according to categorical consistency constraints.  
- **Why unresolved:** The current proposal applies functor debiasing and RAG independently; their interaction (e.g., retrieval adjusting functor parameters in real‑time) is unexplored theoretically or empirically.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Architectures with tight coupling (e.g., retrieval‑guided functor modulation) evaluated on combined fairness‑utility trade‑offs, showing improvement over isolated components.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Does the proposed framework effectively mitigate intersectional biases (e.g., race‑gender‑occupation) that compound across multiple demographic dimensions?  
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: The paper mentions intersectionality in Related Work and claims category theory can handle multiple biases, but provides no specific formulation or evaluation for intersectional cases.  
- **Why unresolved:** Functor mappings are defined for single protected attributes; scaling to intersections requires complex colimit constructions not detailed. RAG’s coverage of intersectional contexts is also unclear.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on intersectional bias benchmarks (e.g., BiasBios with race‑gender subgroups) showing reduced bias amplification across all subgroups compared to single‑axis methods.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps**: Exact token lists for demographic/occupational concepts and full multi‑layer functor blueprint are unspecified.  
- **Corpus dependency**: RAG fairness relies on external knowledge store and fairness‑aware reranker details; retrieved content may reintroduce biases.  
- **Synergy evidence**: No empirical validation that combining functor and RAG yields better fairness; the cited “CatRAG” paper remains uncited and lacks evidence.

## Confidence
- Functor‑based structural debiasing (72% reduction claim): Medium  
- RAG‑driven contextual grounding: Medium  
- Integrated synergy for robust fairness: Low

3. **Ablation study on integrated system performance:** Compare base LLM, base + functor, base + RAG, and full system on an occupational recommendation task, measuring fairness metrics (DPD, SAR), utility (MMLU, ROUGE), and computational overhead.
