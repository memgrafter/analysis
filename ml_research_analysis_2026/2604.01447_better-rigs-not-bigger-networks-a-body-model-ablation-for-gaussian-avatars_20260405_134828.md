# Better Rigs, Not Bigger Networks: A Body Model Ablation for Gaussian Avatars

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01447
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01447
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: Replacing SMPL with the Momentum Human Rig (MHR) estimated via SAM-3D-Body in a minimal Gaussian splatting pipeline achieves state-of-the-art PSNR (e.g., 36.43 average on PeopleSnapshot, 32.54 on ZJU-MoCap) and competitive SSIM/LPIPS without any learned deformations or pose-dependent corrections.

## Executive Summary
3D Gaussian splatting avatars have advanced human reconstruction but often rely on the SMPL body model, whose limited expressiveness necessitates complex learned deformation modules. This paper demonstrates that simply upgrading the upstream body model to the higher-capacity Momentum Human Rig (MHR), obtained from SAM-3D-Body, eliminates the need for such downstream complexity while improving fidelity. Experiments on PeopleSnapshot and ZJU-MoCap show that MHR, with its denser mesh and richer articulation, yields state-of-the-art geometric accuracy (PSNR) and competitive perceptual quality. Controlled ablations confirm that both the improved pose estimation from SAM-3D-Body and MHR’s representational capacity independently contribute to the gains, suggesting that previous architectural complexity was largely compensating for SMPL’s limitations.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a minimal 3D Gaussian splatting pipeline that replaces SMPL with the Momentum Human Rig (MHR). Given monocular or multiview video, SAM-3D-Body estimates MHR pose and shape parameters. The posed MHR mesh is generated via linear blend skinning with built-in non-linear pose correctives. Gaussians are embedded uniformly on each mesh triangle (30,000 initial, up to 100,000 after densification) with per-triangle transforms derived from the triangle vertices. Training minimizes L1 image loss, SSIM, LPIPS, and a mask regularization, using Adam with exponential learning rate decay. No learned deformation networks, pose-dependent color modules, or spherical harmonics are used. The pipeline is evaluated on PeopleSnapshot and ZJU-MoCap for novel pose and view synthesis.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art PSNR (36.43 on PeopleSnapshot, 32.54 on ZJU-MoCap) and competitive SSIM/LPIPS versus prior arts.
- Ablation reveals that both SAM-3D-Body’s superior pose estimation and MHR’s denser mesh independently boost reconstruction; MHR improves both aspects simultaneously.
- The minimal architecture, lacking any learned deformations, matches or exceeds more complex Gaussian avatar methods, indicating that much prior complexity compensated for SMPL’s limited expressiveness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: Replacing SMPL with MHR eliminates the need for learned deformation modules and achieves state-of-the-art geometric accuracy (PSNR).
**Mechanism**: MHR’s higher vertex count (18,439 vs. SMPL’s 6,890), more twist joints (127 vs. 24), and non-linear pose correctives provide finer Gaussian attachment and analytically handle limb deformations. Triangle-embedded Gaussians use per-triangle transforms from posed vertices; a denser mesh yields more accurate transforms.
**Core assumption**: The primary bottleneck is the upstream body model’s representational capacity, not downstream architecture.
**Evidence**: Abstract states the minimal pipeline achieves highest PSNR; Section 4.4 translates poses and shows MHR expressiveness alone boosts performance; neighbor literature highlights SMPL-family expressiveness gaps.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: The pose estimation quality from SAM-3D-Body independently contributes to improved reconstruction.
**Mechanism**: More accurate joint rotations, head orientation, eye gaze, and foot placement yield correct linear blend skinning transformations, leading to better Gaussian placement and rendering.
**Core assumption**: Downstream quality is sensitive to pose parameters; systematic errors propagate.
**Evidence**: Abstract notes SAM-3D-Body meshes translated to SMPL-X outperform prior fits; Section 4.4 and Figure 2 show superior head and foot alignment; SAM-3D-Body’s robustness demonstrated in related motion retargeting work.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Upgrading the body model simplifies the overall architecture; learned components in prior works primarily compensate for SMPL’s deficiencies.
**Mechanism**: MHR intrinsically captures non-linear deformations and higher articulation, removing the need for separate MLPs or feature triplanes. A triangle-embedded Gaussian approach with per-triangle transforms suffices.
**Core assumption**: Learned modules approximate expressiveness that MHR provides analytically.
**Evidence**: Abstract and Section 1 explicitly state that complexity is unnecessary with MHR; neighbor GST method exemplifies added complexity to address SMPL limits.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
Video/masks input → SAM-3D-Body → MHR mesh generation → Gaussian assignment (30k Gaussians per mesh triangle) → Per-triangle transforms → Gsplat rasterizer → Loss (L1+SSIM+LPIPS + mask regularization) → Adam optimizer (exponential decay)

**Critical path**
1. SAM-3D-Body must produce plausible MHR parameters.
2. MHR must generate a well-formed mesh (no self-intersections).
3. Triangle transforms must correctly map Gaussians; errors here corrupt rendering regardless of other components.

**Design tradeoffs**
MHR’s high vertex count improves geometric fidelity and Gaussian distribution flexibility but increases memory and computational cost; a fixed Gaussian count balances coverage and speed. Eliminating learned modules reduces model size, training time, and overfitting risk but may underfit fine texture details, as seen by a perceptual gap (SSIM/LPIPS). The densification schedule (prune/split until 100k Gaussians) is standard.

**Failure signatures**
- Implausible mesh (excessive twists, collapsed limbs) indicates SAM-3D-Body failure; check pose visualizations.
- Holes or noise in renderings suggest Gaussian count too low or densification misconfiguration; monitor Gaussian density during training.
- Training instability or divergence often stems from incorrect transform implementation or too high learning rate.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **SMPL-X baseline**: Convert SAM-3D-Body’s MHR fits to SMPL-X using provided fitting tools; train identical minimal pipeline to isolate mesh capacity effects.
2. **Pose quality ablation**: Keep SAM-3D-Body poses but convert mesh to SMPL-X; compare to using original dataset SMPL poses with MHR.
3. **Full MHR pipeline**: Reproduce authors’ Table 1 & 2 results using the published codebase to validate implementation and establish performance ceiling.

## Limitations
- Exact training hyperparameters (initial learning rate, mask regularization weight) are not specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Implementation details of per-triangle transforms and Gaussian assignment logic are omitted, which are critical for correct canonical‑to‑posed mapping.
- Robustness to SAM-3D-Body failures under extreme poses or heavy occlusions is unevaluated; reported gains may diminish under such conditions.

## Confidence
- Body model expressiveness as primary bottleneck: **High** (controlled ablations fixing pose show MHR’s denser mesh significantly outperforms SMPL‑X)
- Pose estimation quality independently boosts reconstruction: **High** (isolated pose experiments confirm superior joint accuracy)
- Upgrading body model eliminates need for learned deformations: **Medium** (SOTA achieved without deformations, but direct minimal-pipeline comparison of SMPL vs. MHR is absent)
