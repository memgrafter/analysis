# Disentangling Ambiguity from Instability in Large Language Models: A Clinical Text-to-SQL Case Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12015
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12015
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** CLUES disentangles input ambiguity from model instability, improving failure prediction (AUROC +0.16) and enabling targeted triage via uncertainty regimes.

## Executive Summary
Clinical Text-to-SQL systems must handle uncertainty arising from ambiguous user queries and model instability. Traditional uncertainty metrics conflate these factors, preventing appropriate interventions. CLUES addresses this by explicitly disentangling ambiguity and instability through a two-stage generation process: first generating multiple interpretations of the query, then producing answers for each interpretation. A bipartite semantic graph captures relationships among interpretations and answers, and a Schur complement isolates answer variation unexplained by interpretation similarity, yielding an instability score (H_{R|I}) alongside an ambiguity score (H_I). Evaluated on open-domain QA and a clinical benchmark, CLUES improves failure prediction over Kernel Language Entropy by up to +0.16 AUROC. Regime analysis reveals that the high-ambiguity/high-instability quadrant contains 51% of errors while covering only 25% of queries, enabling efficient triage through targeted clarification or model review. The decomposition provides diagnostic resolution unattainable with a single uncertainty measure.

## Method Summary
CLUES processes a clinical query through five core components: an Interpretation Generator creates N candidate reformulations; an Answer Generator samples M SQL queries and executes them per interpretation; a Similarity Encoder (LLM judge) produces semantic similarity scores for all pairs of interpretations and answers; a Graph Builder assembles these into the bipartite matrix W = [[W_II, W_IR], [W_RI, W_RR]]; a Heat Kernel & Entropy Calculator smooths W_II and the Schur complement S = W_RR - W_RI (W_II + εI)^{-1} W_IR with a heat kernel (τ=10), normalizes to probability matrices, and computes von Neumann entropies H_I and H_{R|I}; finally, a Regime Classifier thresholds these scores to assign one of four uncertainty regimes. The system uses LLM-based judges for equivalence and correctness. The instability metric captures answer variation that cannot be explained by interpretation variation. Evaluation uses AUROC for predicting answer failure and measures error concentration across regimes.

## Key Results
- CLUES's conditional instability score H_{R|I} outperforms Kernel Language Entropy (H_R) for failure prediction, achieving up to +0.16 AUROC improvement.
- Error analysis shows that 51% of all incorrect answers fall into the high-ambiguity/high-instability regime, which covers only 25% of queries, allowing a triage system to focus review resources efficiently.
- The decomposition into H_I and H_{R|I} yields a diagnostic granularity that a single uncertainty score cannot provide, enabling targeted interventions (query clarification vs. model review).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Disentangling Ambiguity and Instability via Schur Complement
CLUES computes instability by isolating the portion of answer similarity that cannot be explained by interpretation similarity. A bipartite semantic graph is constructed with interpretations I and answers R as node sets, and similarity matrices W_II, W_RR, and W_IR. The Schur complement S = W_RR - W_RI (W_II + εI)^{-1} W_IR mathematically corresponds to the conditional covariance in a Gaussian model and captures residual similarity among answers after removing the influence of interpretations. The entropy of S, denoted H_{R|I}, serves as the instability score. This mechanism assumes that a well-constructed W_II accurately reflects true input ambiguity; if interpretations are redundant or miss key ambiguities, the subtraction may misattribute systematic error to instability or fail to isolate genuine instability.

### Mechanism 2: Two-Stage Generation for Uncertainty Regimes
By explicitly generating multiple interpretations before answering, CLUES separates ambiguity (diversity among interpretations, quantified by H_I) from instability (variability of answers given an interpretation, quantified by H_{R|I}). Thresholding these scores yields four regimes: high H_I with low H_{R|I} indicates the model understands the query but interpretations differ—needing input clarification; low H_I with high H_{R|I} indicates consensus on interpretation but inconsistent answers—needing model review. This mapping rests on the orthogonality of across-interpretation diversity versus within-interpretation stability. If interpretation generation itself is unstable (producing different I sets across runs), H_I becomes an unreliable indicator of query ambiguity.

### Mechanism 3: Heat Kernel Regularization for Stable Entropy
Raw semantic similarity scores from LLM judges are noisy and scale-dependent. CLUES applies a heat kernel K_τ = e^{-τL} to the graph Laplacian L = D - W, diffusing similarity across the graph and producing a smoothed matrix. Normalizing K yields a density-like matrix ρ from which von Neumann entropy is computed. The hyperparameter τ (set to 10) controls the diffusion scale; too small retains noise, too large merges distinct clusters. This regularization, borrowed from Kernel Language Entropy, makes entropy estimates robust to minor variations in similarity judgments and ensures that H_I and H_{R|I} are stable and comparable across queries.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Interpretation Generator -> Similarity Encoder -> Graph Builder -> Heat Kernel & Entropy Calculator -> Regime Classifier

**Critical path**: Input clinical question → (1) Generate interpretations → (2) For each interpretation, generate M answers → (3) Compute all pairwise similarities → (4) Build W → (5) Compute H_I from W_II → (6) Compute S (Schur complement of W) → (7) Compute H_{R|I} from S → (8) Compare to thresholds → Output regime.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Cost vs. granularity**: More interpretations (N) and samples (M) improve robustness but increase LLM calls linearly.
- **Interpretation quality**: Relies on LLM capability; weaker models may produce trivial or redundant interpretations, invalidating H_I.
- **Threshold calibration**: Paper uses pooled medians; deployment may need per-model or per-query calibration to balance false positives/negatives.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Negative H_{R|I}**: Indicates broken mathematical construction (e.g., not using Schur complement properly).
- **Uniformly low H_I**: All interpretations identical; suggests generator failed to surface ambiguity.
- **Extreme H_{R|I} values** (near 0 or max): May indicate perfect stability or chaos; requires manual inspection.
- **Regime IV dominance**: >25% of queries in Compound regime may point to systemic issues (e.g., poor schema docs).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Validation on Gold Interpretations**: Use AmbigQA/SituatedQA with gold interpretations to isolate mathematical correctness; compare H_{R|I} vs H_R for failure prediction.
2. **Clinical Benchmark with Silver Interpretations**: Apply full pipeline on EpiAskKB (iterative disambiguation generator) to test domain-specific performance; measure AUROC improvement and regime accuracy gaps.
3. **Production-like Deployment**: Test with on-the-fly interpretation generation and RAG; evaluate whether H_{R|I} predicts final answer correctness when gold interpretations are unavailable and accuracy is high (sparse errors); assess error concentration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Computational Overhead**: How can CLUES reduce its computational overhead from multiple LLM calls per query while maintaining diagnostic accuracy? The paper identifies computational cost as a limitation but does not explore efficiency-accuracy trade-offs. Resolution would require benchmarking approximate inference strategies (adaptive sampling, caching, distillation) on clinical benchmarks.
2. **Interpretation Coverage**: What ensures automatically generated interpretations meaningfully cover the true ambiguity space of epidemiological queries? Interpretation quality may degrade with weaker models or produce trivial/redundant reformulations, undermining the ambiguity score H_I. Evidence needed: human evaluation of interpretation diversity on held-out queries.
3. **Routing in Sparse Error Regimes**: Can uncertainty-based routing be effective in high-accuracy deployment settings with extremely sparse errors? With ~95% base accuracy (4% errors), fine-grained uncertainty signals have low discriminative power. Resolution: incorporate auxiliary features (query complexity, domain specificity) and conduct cost-benefit simulations.
4. **Schur Complement Validity for Non-Linear Semantics**: Does the Schur complement construction validly compute conditional entropy H_{R|I} for non-linear semantic relationships? The method assumes a linear projection of answer similarity given interpretations; semantic equivalence may be non-linear, potentially biasing instability estimates after PSD projection. Evidence: controlled experiments with synthetic graphs where true conditional entropy is known.

## Limitations
- **Prompt Sensitivity**: Exact prompts for semantic similarity and answer correctness are undisclosed, making exact replication difficult and results potentially prompt-dependent.
- **Interpretation Generator Reliability**: Framework assumes generated interpretations faithfully surface true input ambiguity; poor diversity or accuracy invalidates H_I and regime mapping.
- **Domain Transfer of τ**: Heat kernel smoothing parameter τ=10 is calibrated on open-domain QA; its robustness in other clinical subdomains or with different embedding models is unvalidated.

## Confidence
- Decomposition Validity (Schur Complement): Medium
- Intervention Mapping (Regimes): High
- Heat Kernel Regularization Robustness: Medium
