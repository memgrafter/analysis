# RISE-Video: Can Video Generators Decode Implicit World Rules?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05986
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05986
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a benchmark revealing systemic failures in text-image-to-video models on implicit reasoning, with the best model achieving only 22.5% perfect accuracy across four reasoning-aligned metrics.

## Executive Summary
RISE-Video addresses a critical gap in evaluating text-image-to-video (TI2V) generation by assessing models' ability to internalize and apply implicit world rules. The paper introduces a benchmark of 467 human-annotated samples spanning eight reasoning dimensions (e.g., commonsense, logic, spatial) and defines four automated metrics—Reasoning Alignment, Temporal Consistency, Physical Rationality, and Visual Quality—using a Large Multimodal Model (LMM) judging pipeline. Evaluation of 11 state-of-the-art TI2V models shows pervasive deficiencies, particularly in logical capability, with open-source models trailing significantly behind closed-source ones. The LMM pipeline demonstrates strong correlation with human judgments, enabling scalable, nuanced assessment beyond traditional perceptual quality metrics.

## Method Summary
The evaluation task is to score generated TI2V videos on implicit reasoning using a fixed benchmark. Each sample provides a text instruction, an input image (first frame), and the generated video. Four metrics are computed via an automated LMM (primarily GPT-5) pipeline with metric-specific frame extraction: Reasoning Alignment uses binary questions on frames sampled at 2 fps (progression) or sparse terminal frames; Temporal Consistency and Physical Rationality use uniform sampling on a 1–5 scale; Visual Quality (1–3 scale) uses 6 mid-video frames after super-resolution preprocessing. For Schematic Puzzles (mazes, symmetry, board games), specialized non-LMM pipelines enforce rigid geometric checks. Human validation measured MAE/STD between LMM and 5-expert averages to confirm reliability. Assumption: GPT-5's multimodal reasoning capabilities are consistent and generalizable across the diverse reasoning dimensions.

## Key Results
- Benchmark of 467 samples across eight reasoning dimensions exposes systemic failures; top model perfect accuracy only 22.5%.
- Models excel on perceptual tasks but struggle severely with logical capability; open-source models lag further behind.
- LMM judging pipeline correlates strongly with human judgments (validated via MAE/STD across 5 experts), enabling scalable, automated evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The LMM-based judging pipeline works because it leverages the advanced multimodal reasoning of models like GPT-5 to interpret video content against manually designed, reasoning-aware binary questions and comparison tasks. Frame-sampling strategies are tailored to each metric to capture relevant temporal dynamics (e.g., progression vs. terminal state). For abstract symbolic tasks, specialized code-based pipelines replace LMMs to handle rigid geometric constraints precisely. This hybrid approach balances automated scalability with domain-specific accuracy, validated by high correlation with expert human scores. Additionally, the use of human validation ensures that the LMM's scores are grounded in expert assessment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Implicit Reasoning in Video Generation**  
  *Why needed:* Models must infer and apply unstated world rules (physics, logic) beyond surface patterns.  
  *Quick check:* Can the model predict a ball rolling downhill after a push, not just mimicking motion?
- **Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V)**  
  *Why needed:* Conditional generation from both textual instructions and a starting image.  
  *Quick check:* Does the generated video follow the instruction while preserving the input image's content?
- **LMM-as-Judge**  
  *Why needed:* Scalable, nuanced evaluation of high-level reasoning without human labor.  
  *Quick check:* Does the LMM's score correlate with human judgments on a held-out set?
- **Reasoning Dimensions Taxonomy**  
  *Why needed:* Disaggregates failure modes across experiential, perceptual, temporal, societal, spatial, commonsense, subject-specific, and logical capabilities.  
  *Quick check:* Are weaknesses concentrated in one dimension (e.g., logic) or uniform?
- **Frame Sampling Strategies**  
  *Why needed:* Different metrics require different temporal granularity (e.g., progression tracking vs. final state check).  
  *Quick check:* Does sampling miss key events? Inspect extracted frames against full video.
- **Specialized Metric Pipelines**  
  *Why needed:* LMMs are ill-suited for rigid geometric reasoning (e.g., maze navigation); code-based checks are exact.  
  *Quick check:* Can the pipeline detect a wall-crossing violation that an LMM might miss?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Dataset (text, image, video) -> Frame Extractor (metric-specific sampling) -> Evaluator (LMM Judge or Specialized Pipeline) -> Score Aggregator (Weighted Score, Accuracy)

**Critical path:** For each sample, extract frames per metric requirement; feed to appropriate evaluator; parse JSON scores; compute final metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Closed-source LMM (GPT-5) vs. open-source: accuracy vs. reproducibility and cost.  
- Uniform vs. adaptive frame sampling: simplicity vs. missing key events.  
- Unified LMM judge vs. specialized pipelines: automation vs. geometric precision.

**Failure signatures:**  
- LMM high-score bias (e.g., Qwen3-VL clustering scores).  
- Frame extraction omitting critical transitions (e.g., a change occurring between sampled frames).  
- Visual artifacts (blur, ghosting) confounding reasoning judgments.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Sensitivity test: Re-run Reasoning Alignment on 10 samples with varying frame rates; measure score stability to assess robustness to sampling parameters.  
2. Cross-judge consistency: Evaluate 20 videos with 3 LMMs; compute variance and correlation to detect model-specific biases.  
3. Super-resolution ablation: Compare VQ and RA scores on 50 low-clarity videos with and without SR preprocessing to quantify the impact of preprocessing on metric scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
What specific architectural or training modifications are necessary to enable TI2V models to internalize and reliably apply implicit world rules, given their current systemic failures in logical and experiential reasoning?  
*Basis:* Paper identifies gap but does not investigate model-internal causes.  
*Why unresolved:* No ablation studies on architecture, data, or objectives.  
*Resolution evidence:* Ablations on RISE-Video showing improved Reasoning Alignment without visual quality drop.

### Open Question 2
Can the LMM-based judging pipeline be generalized to reliably evaluate all reasoning types, especially for abstract, geometry-driven "Schematic Puzzles" without requiring specialized, metric-specific pipelines?  
*Basis:* Paper uses ad-hoc methods for puzzles because LMM-as-Judge is "ill-suited".  
*Why unresolved:* Undermines unified framework claim; may not scale.  
*Resolution evidence:* A single prompting strategy or fine-tuned LMM matching specialized pipeline accuracy on held-out puzzles.

### Open Question 3
Are the eight defined reasoning dimensions truly independent, or are there significant trade-offs and correlations such that improving one might inhibit another?  
*Basis:* Multi-metric evaluation treats dimensions separately; no correlation analysis provided.  
*Why unresolved:* Unknown capacity trade-offs could misguide model development.  
*Resolution evidence:* Statistical analysis (correlations, Pareto fronts) of model scores across all 467 samples and dimensions.

### Open Question 4
Does the benchmark's sample size (467) and category distribution sufficiently cover real-world implicit reasoning scenarios, or are there coverage gaps (e.g., multi-agent social reasoning, long-horizon causal chains)?  
*Basis:* Sample counts per sub-category not justified; taxonomy not claimed exhaustive.  
*Why unresolved:* Saturation or blind spots could limit diagnostic power; low accuracy may partly reflect narrowness.  
*Resolution evidence:* Expert-driven expansion with new samples/categories, followed by re-evaluation to see if model rankings persist.

### Open Question 5
What factors (training data, model scale, architectural biases) explain the consistent performance gap between closed-source and open-source TI2V models in reasoning alignment?  
*Basis:* Paper explicitly states open-source models underperform, especially in Reasoning Alignment.  
*Why unresolved:* Models compared as black boxes; underlying differences not analyzed.  
*Resolution evidence:* Controlled experiments scaling open-source models, modifying data/architecture, or distilling from closed-source, measuring Reasoning Alignment delta on RISE-Video.

## Limitations
- Frame-sampling strategies per reasoning sub-type are unspecified (Unknown: the exact criteria for mapping sub-types to sampling rates), risking metric misalignment and potentially inconsistent assessment across reasoning dimensions.
- Dependence on closed-source models (GPT-5, proprietary TI2Vs) blocks independent scaling and verification (Assumption: the proprietary models' behaviors remain stable across versions and contexts).
- Super-resolution preprocessing details and specialized puzzle evaluation code are missing (Unknown: the specifics of the SR algorithm and its impact on visual quality scores), hindering exact replication and full understanding of preprocessing effects.

## Confidence
- **Systemic TI2V failure in implicit reasoning:** Medium (results robust but depend on proprietary LMM judge; Assumption: GPT-5's judgments are consistent and unbiased).
- **LMM-human correlation:** High (validated with MAE/STD across 5 experts; evidence indicates strong agreement).
- **Benchmark design & availability:** High (dataset public, metrics defined; standard benchmark practices followed).
- **Open-source vs. proprietary gap:** Medium (cannot verify without model access; potential for open-source models to close gap with future improvements).

## Next Checks
1. **Clarify frame-sampling rules:** Contact authors or inspect released code to map reasoning sub-categories to sampling rates; re-run RA on subset with alternatives to measure sensitivity.
2. **Cross-judge consistency test:** Evaluate 20 videos with multiple LMMs (GPT-5, Qwen3-VL, Claude-3.5) to quantify bias; if variance >0.3, findings are LMM-dependent.
3. **Super-resolution ablation:** Compare VQ/RA scores with/without SR on 50 low-clarity videos; if RA changes >15%, SR is a confounding factor.
