# Do Language Models Encode Semantic Relations? Probing and Sparse Feature Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17624
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17624
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** LLMs encode semantic relations diffusely in mid-layers, with asymmetric hierarchical patterns (hypernymy robust, hyponymy fragile) and scale-dependent probe-level causal manipulability.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether and how large language models (LLMs) represent four core semantic relations—synonymy, antonymy, hypernymy, and hyponymy. Using linear probing and sparse autoencoder (SAE)-guided activation patching across models of varying scales (Pythia-70M, GPT-2, Llama 3.1 8B), the authors find that relation signals are distributed rather than localized, peaking consistently in the mid-layers of the network. The pathways involved are primarily post-residual and MLP-based, not attention-driven. A stable difficulty ordering emerges: antonymy is easiest to decode, while synonymy is hardest. Hierarchical relations show a key asymmetry: hypernymy (general→specific) is encoded redundantly and robustly, whereas hyponymy (specific→general) relies on a compact, fragile set of features. Crucially, the ability to causally manipulate these signals via targeted SAE feature intervention is reliable only in the largest model tested (Llama 3.1 8B), indicating a strong dependence on model scale.

## Method Summary
The study employs a two-stage methodology on word pairs derived from WordNet 3.0 (1,000 pairs per relation, plus 1,000 random pairs), embedded in neutral prompts. First, **layer-wise linear probing** is used: for each model layer, a multinomial logistic regression is trained on mean-pooled post-residual activations to classify the relation type. This reveals where relation information is linearly decodable. Second, **SAE-guided activation patching** tests causality: SAE features from a selected layer are ranked by their probe weight magnitude. A small top-*k* subset (selected to capture ~90% of maximum effect) is either injected into neutral ("random" pair) representations (testing sufficiency) or ablated from relation representations (testing necessity). The impact is measured via changes in the probe's semantic logit difference, flip rates, and drop rates, with statistical uncertainty estimated via bootstrap.

## Key Results
- Relation signals are diffuse across many mid-layers but show a clear peak in the network's center (center of mass ~0.5 normalized depth), contradicting a single "relation layer."
- A consistent difficulty ordering for linear decoding: **antonymy > hyponymy > hypernymy > synonymy**.
- Hypernymy is highly **redundant** (low necessity) and **robust** to word-order reversal; hyponymy is **compact** (high necessity) and **fragile** to reversal.
- Probe-level causal manipulation (via SAE patching) is strong and stable only in Llama 3.1 8B; effects in smaller models (Pythia-70M, GPT-2) are weak and inconsistent.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Diffuse Mid-Layer Signal Peaks
- **Claim:** Semantic relation information is distributed across multiple central layers rather than localized.
- **Mechanism:** Linear probes achieve peak accuracy in mid-layers (normalized depth ~0.5) for all relations and models, indicating the bulk of linearly recoverable signal resides there. The center of mass (CoM) of accuracy across layers clusters stably around this midpoint.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states peaks in mid-layers; Section 4.1 shows CoM near 0.5 across models/relations.
- **Break condition:** If CoM consistently shifts toward early or late layers for all relations across diverse architectures (e.g., MoE, encoder-decoder), the diffuse mid-layer profile may not generalize.

### Mechanism 2: Asymmetric Hierarchical Encoding
- **Claim:** Hypernymy (general→specific) is encoded redundantly and robustly, while hyponymy (specific→general) relies on compact, fragile features.
- **Mechanism:** Reversing word order in hypernymy pairs causes only a small accuracy drop (Δ ≈ -0.07 to -0.09), suggesting multiple overlapping features support it (high sufficiency, low necessity). Hyponymy drops substantially (Δ ≈ -0.24 to -0.29), indicating dependence on a tight, necessary set of features. SAE ablation/injection quantifies this: hyponymy shows higher drop rates (DR) and negative standardized ΔLDsem.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes asymmetry; Section 4.2.2 (Table 3) shows reversal impacts; Section 4.3.2 (Tables 4–5) shows necessity/sufficiency metrics.
- **Break condition:** If hyponymy shows high sufficiency (injection works) but also high redundancy (ablation has little effect), the asymmetry claim weakens.

### Mechanism 3: Scale-Dependent Probe-Level Causality
- **Claim:** Causal manipulation of relation signals via SAE-guided patching is reliable only in the largest model (Llama 3.1 8B); smaller models show weak/unstable effects.
- **Mechanism:** For Llama 3.1, injecting or ablating a small top-*k* subset of SAE features (ranked by probe weights) produces large, consistent shifts in probe margins (standardized ΔLDsem ~2.0–3.1) and flip/drop rates (ΔFR ~0.7, DR ~0.4). For Pythia-70M and GPT-2, effects are an order of magnitude smaller and noisier.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes scale-dependence; Section 4.3.2 quantitative comparisons.
- **Break condition:** If smaller models fine-tuned on relational data exhibit strong patching effects, scale-dependence might be mitigated by training rather than capacity alone.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear Probing**
  - *Why needed:* Detects whether relation information is linearly decodable from frozen activations, establishing *where* signals reside.
  - *Quick check:* If a probe achieves 90% accuracy, what does this tell us and what does it *not* tell us about the model's internal use of that information?
- **Concept: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**
  - *Why needed:* Decomposes dense activations into potentially monosemantic features, enabling targeted interventions beyond whole-layer probing.
  - *Quick check:* How does an SAE differ from PCA, and why might its latents be more suitable for activation patching?
- **Concept: Activation Patching (Causal Intervention)**
  - *Why needed:* Moves from correlation (probing) to *probe-level* causality by replacing activation substructures and measuring effect on probe output.
  - *Quick check:* In activation patching, what is the critical difference between testing *sufficiency* (injection) and *necessity* (ablation)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input tokens -> Transformer blocks (Attention + MLP) -> Post-residual stream (mean-pooled) -> Linear Probe (relation classification)  
SAE attached to post-residual stream at a specific layer -> Sparse latents -> Activation Patching (inject/ablate top-k features) -> Modified activations -> Probe evaluation

**Critical path:**
1. Extract mean-pooled post-residual (or Attn/MLP) activations per layer for all examples.
2. Train layer-wise logistic regression probes on the relation task.
3. For each relation/layer, rank SAE features by absolute probe weight; select smallest *k* capturing 90% of max |Δlogit| on a validation set.
4. Perform patching: (a) *Sufficiency:* inject top-*k* features into random-pair activations; (b) *Necessity:* ablate top-*k* from relation-pair activations.
5. Measure probe output changes: standardized ΔLDsem, flip-rate change (ΔFR), drop rate (DR).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Probe vs. causality:** Probes locate information cheaply but do not confirm model use; SAE patching tests probe-level causality but not end-to-end generation.
- **Sparsity budget (*k*):** Too small misses causal features; too large dilutes interpretability. The 1% cap is heuristic.
- **Layer selection:** Interventions target the probe's peak layer, but signal is diffuse—so patching is an approximate localization.

**Failure signatures:**
- Unstable peak layers across bootstrap replicates or relation types.
- Random feature patching producing effects comparable to top-*k* patching (indicates probe overfitting/non-sparse coding).
- No consistent difficulty ordering between probe accuracy and patching effect strength (e.g., antonymy high probe accuracy but low patching effect).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate dense probing: Train and evaluate probes on all three models' post-residual streams; reproduce mid-layer CoM and difficulty order (Ant > Hypo > Hyper > Syn).
2. Conduct reversal test: Evaluate probes on hypernymy/hyponymy pairs in original and reversed word order; verify hypernymy stable (Δ ≈ -0.08), hyponymy drops (Δ ≈ -0.25).
3. Run SAE patching on Llama 3.1: At its peak layer, use top-*k*=256 features; perform sufficiency/necessity patching. Expect large ΔFR (~0.7) and DR (~0.4). Confirm random-feature control effects are ~10% of top-*k*.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How do relation-specific SAE features influence next-token predictions and generated text in LLMs? Current causality is probe-level only.
- **Question 2:** Do observed encoding patterns generalize to other architectures (MoE, encoder-decoder) and modalities? Study limited to dense decoder-only models.
- **Question 3:** What mechanisms explain hypernymy/hyponymy asymmetry? Paper documents asymmetry but not its feature-level or circuit-level cause.
- **Question 4:** Are causally manipulated features responsible for end-to-end model behavior on semantic tasks, or only for probe decisions? Links to actual outputs remain indirect.

## Limitations
- Causality assessment is limited to probe-level decisions; no evaluation of impact on token generation or downstream task performance.
- Results depend on SAE dictionary quality; poor interpretability of latents could obscure true causal features.
- Only three dense decoder models tested; no encoder-decoder, MoE, or instruction-tuned variants.
- WordNet-derived pairs and limited prompt templates may not capture full semantic nuance.

## Confidence
- **Diffuse mid-layer signal peaks:** High (consistent across models/layers via multiple metrics).
- **Asymmetric hierarchical encoding (hypernymy redundancy vs. hyponymy fragility):** Medium (strong ablation/injection data but depends on SAE feature selection).
- **Scale-dependent probe-level causality:** Medium (clear divergence between Llama and smaller models, but only one large model tested).

## Next Checks
1. **SAE feature stability:** Re-run top-*k* selection with different seeds/initializations; target <5% variance in ΔLDsem across replicates.
2. **Prompt robustness:** Test reversal and patching effects across 5+ diverse neutral templates; target >0.8 correlation in layer-wise accuracy profiles.
3. **Generation-level causality:** Extend patching to measure changes in token probabilities and generated text for relation-typed prompts; target >0.6 rank correlation between ΔLDsem and next-token KL divergence.
