# R2RAG-Flood: A reasoning-reinforced training-free retrieval augmentation generation framework for flood damage nowcasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10312
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10312
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** R2RAG-Flood achieves competitive damage classification accuracy (0.613–0.668 overall) and high reasoning quality without any task-specific training, while offering superior severity-per-cost efficiency.

## Executive Summary
R2RAG-Flood is a training-free retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework for post-storm property damage nowcasting. It transforms tabular geospatial predictors into text summaries and LLM-generated reasoning trajectories, builds a knowledge base with free-shot prototypes and boundary cases selected via divergence-informed feature profiling, and during inference retrieves nearby properties’ reasoning to condition a large language model. Predictions follow a two-stage ordinal classification with a conditional downgrade step that corrects over-predicted severity. Evaluated on Hurricane Harvey data in Harris County, TX, R2RAG-Flood approaches a supervised tabular baseline’s accuracy while providing interpretable rationales. Lightweight LLM variants achieve substantially higher cost-efficiency, demonstrating a practical path for adaptable disaster assessment.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in three stages. First, divergence metrics (Jensen‑Shannon and Kolmogorov‑Smirnov) rank predictors by their ability to separate damage classes; this ordering guides the creation of concise text‑mode summaries for each record. Second, for all training data, an LLM generates reasoning trajectories (think/answer blocks) aligned with the true label, and divergence‑weighted distances select per‑watershed free‑shot libraries containing class prototypes and hard‑boundary examples. Third, at inference, the system retrieves up to three nearest neighbors within 1 km, supplements with free‑shots if the neighborhood is sparse, and prompts the LLM with the target’s text summary and retrieved reasoning. The LLM returns a damage label and its own reasoning, which may be downgraded by a rule‑based post‑check if uncertainty cues are present. The entire pipeline requires no task‑specific training and works with any LLM backbone.

## Key Results
- Against a supervised baseline (overall accuracy 0.714, damage‑class accuracy 0.859), R2RAG‑Flood across seven LLMs achieved 0.613–0.668 overall accuracy and 0.757–0.896 damage‑class accuracy, while producing structured rationales.
- Lightweight variants (e.g., gpt‑4o‑mini) attained severity‑per‑cost efficiency scores of 35.9–81.9, far exceeding the supervised baseline (28.6) and larger LLMs (4.0–6.2).
- Ablations confirmed the individual contributions of neighbor retrieval, free‑shot injection, and the conditional downgrade; reasoning quality metrics showed high label‑reasoning agreement and saliency of divergence‑informed features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Retrieval of nearby geospatial neighbors' reasoning trajectories improves prediction accuracy and interpretability by providing analogous evidence.
- **Mechanism:** For a target property, the system retrieves labeled records within 1 km (HUC12 watershed) and includes their text summaries and LLM‑generated reasoning in the prompt. The LLM backbone then conditions on these neighbor rationales to infer the target's damage extent, effectively transferring knowledge from similar contexts without training.
- **Core assumption:** Geographically proximate properties share similar flood exposure and built environment characteristics, so their labeled reasoning is relevant.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "retrieve and condition on relevant reasoning trajectories from nearby geospatial neighbors"  
  [section 2.6.1] "neighbors are labeled records whose centroids fall within 1 km of the target"  
  Baseline FloodDamageCast lacks interpretable reasoning; R2RAG‑Flood adds retrieval for transferability.
- **Break condition:** Sparse neighborhoods (fewer than 3 neighbors) trigger free‑shot injection; if both neighbors and free‑shots are unrepresentative, accuracy may degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The divergence‑informed feature profile ensures that text‑mode summaries and free‑shot selection focus on the most discriminative predictors for occurrence and severity boundaries, enhancing efficiency and robustness.
- **Mechanism:** Using Jensen‑Shannon and Kolmogorov‑Smirnov statistics, the system ranks features by distributional separation between damage classes (Low vs. damage, Medium vs. High). This ordering guides text‑mode generation (most salient features first, within length limit) and selects prototypes (closest to class centroids) and hard‑boundary cases (closest to decision margins) from the reasoning pool.
- **Core assumption:** Features with higher distributional divergence across PDE classes are more predictive and should be prioritized in human‑readable summaries and example selection.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "divergence‑informed feature profile"  
  [section 2.2] computes divergence scores and creates ordered feature lists  
  [section 2.5] uses divergence‑weighted z‑distance to select prototypes and boundary cases  
  Tabular baseline uses all predictors equally; R2RAG‑Flood explicitly weights features.
- **Break condition:** Covariate shift between training and target regions may invalidate divergence rankings, leading to suboptimal ordering and selection.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The conditional downgrade mechanism reduces over‑prediction of severe damage by detecting contradictions between the predicted label and the supporting rationale, acting as a lightweight calibration step.
- **Mechanism:** After the LLM outputs a label and reasoning, a rule‑based post‑check scans the reasoning for uncertainty or light cues and compares against severity cues. If the narrative lacks strong severity support but the label is high (2) or medium (1), the label is downgraded by one level. The rule also considers neighbor evidence as secondary when target text is weak.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs tend to overestimate severity when evidence is ambiguous; a simple rule based on cue sets can correct a subset of these errors without retraining.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "conditional downgrade step to correct over‑predicted severity"  
  [section 2.6.2] defines DOWNGRADE_RULE with SEVERITY_CUES, LIGHT_CUES, UNCERTAIN_CUES  
  [section 5.4] ablation shows downgrade produces neutral or slightly positive macro‑F1 changes  
  Novel contribution not addressed in prior corpus.
- **Break condition:** Fixed lexical cues may miss many over‑prediction patterns; incomplete or domain‑specific cue sets could incorrectly downgrade valid high‑severity predictions.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
- **Why needed here:** R2RAG‑Flood is fundamentally a RAG system that retrieves neighbor reasoning and free‑shots to condition the LLM, improving factuality and interpretability.  
- **Quick check:** How does retrieval of external context differ from fine‑tuning an LLM on task data?

**Concept: Ordinal Classification**  
- **Why needed here:** The PDE labels are ordered (Low < Medium < High). The framework uses a two‑stage ordinal approach (occurrence then severity) and a downgrade step that respects the ordering.  
- **Quick check:** Why is ordinal classification more appropriate than multi‑class classification for damage severity?

- **Why needed here:** Divergence metrics (KS, JS) quantify how well features separate damage classes, driving text‑mode ordering and free‑shot selection.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
1. Preprocessing & Divergence Analysis → 2. Knowledge Base Construction → 3. Retrieval & Prediction

**Critical path:**  
Input tabular predictors → text‑mode generation → retrieve neighbors and free‑shots → construct prompt with target text, neighbor reasoning, free‑shots → LLM generates reasoning and label → apply downgrade rule if needed → output PDE label and rationale.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Training‑free vs. accuracy: Avoids regional retraining but yields lower accuracy than supervised baseline (0.668 vs 0.714); provides interpretable rationales.  
- Cost vs. performance: Lightweight backbones (gpt‑4o‑mini, gpt‑5‑mini) achieve higher severity‑per‑cost efficiency than larger models and the supervised baseline because they avoid training costs and have lower inference costs.  
- Local vs. global free‑shots: Using HUC12‑level libraries improves relevance, but global fallback may introduce less relevant examples when local data is sparse.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sparse neighborhoods (fewer than 3 neighbors) force free‑shot injection; if free‑shots are also uninformative, predictions become unreliable.  
- Over‑reliance on neighbor reasoning: erroneous neighbor labels or reasoning can bias the target prediction.  
- Downgrade over‑correction: The fixed rule may downgrade valid high‑severity cases if the reasoning inadvertently contains light or uncertain cues.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Compare R2RAG‑Flood across seven LLM backbones against the supervised FloodDamageCast baseline on overall accuracy, damage‑class accuracy, severity score, and high‑damage recall.  
2. Conduct ablation study: remove neighbor reasoning, remove free‑shots, disable downgrade to isolate contributions.  
3. Evaluate reasoning quality using five instance‑level metrics (LRA, SFC, FDC, PAS, BTS) to assess rationale structure and faithfulness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does R2RAG‑Flood generalize to other geographical regions, storm types, and claim‑reporting regimes beyond the single Harris County Hurricane Harvey case?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We evaluate a single Harris County Hurricane Harvey use case, so the reported trends may not generalize to other regions, storm types, or claim‑reporting regimes."
- **Why unresolved:** The framework’s performance and robustness outside this specific context remain unknown; factors like different flood mechanisms, building code practices, or claim behaviors could affect retrieval and reasoning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the evaluation on multiple regions exposed to diverse storms (e.g., coastal vs. inland, flash vs. riverine) and with varying claim datasets; compare accuracy, reasoning quality, and efficiency scores.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the impact of decoding parameters (e.g., temperature) on the predictive accuracy, reasoning quality, and cost efficiency of R2RAG‑Flood across different LLM backbones?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We will also test multiple decoding settings, including several temperature levels per backbone, to characterize robustness and cost–performance tradeoffs."
- **Why unresolved:** The current study uses fixed decoding settings, so the sensitivity and optimal operating points for each backbone are unclear; this could affect deployment decisions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary temperature (and possibly top‑p) for each backbone on the same test set, measuring classification metrics, reasoning scores, and cost; identify trade‑off curves and robust configurations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does incorporating complementary modalities such as remote sensing imagery and textual damage reports improve the accuracy and interpretability of R2RAG‑Flood predictions?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will expand evaluation across additional events and regions and incorporate complementary evidence sources such as remote sensing imagery and text damage reports."
- **Why unresolved:** The current text‑only approach may miss valuable spatial or unstructured information; multi‑modal integration could enhance predictive power but also complicate retrieval and context window management.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the knowledge base to include image embeddings and/or text reports, design retrieval strategies for these modalities, and run controlled experiments comparing performance and reasoning metrics with and without the additional data.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do domain‑expert curated text‑mode summaries and reasoning trajectories compare to the current LLM‑generated ones in terms of prediction accuracy and reasoning quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "A practical next step is to introduce domain experts through structured surveys that elicit how they would summarize tabular inputs into text‑mode descriptions and how they would articulate reasoning trajectories, enabling expert‑derived guidance to replace or augment prompt‑written templates."
- **Why unresolved:** Expert mental models might produce more consistent, accurate, and domain‑true rationales; LLM‑generated rationales could contain subtle errors or omissions that hurt reliability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect expert annotations for a representative sample (text summaries and reasoning chains), use them in place of or alongside LLM‑generated ones, and evaluate on the full test set comparing key metrics and possibly human evaluation of rationale quality.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Only evaluated in Harris County, TX; assumptions about spatial proximity and feature divergence may not hold in other flood regimes or geographies.
- **Reproducibility:** Critical parameters (divergence weights, salient feature count, LRA extraction) are not fully specified; LLM randomness adds variability.
- **Downgrade rule:** Fixed lexical cues likely miss many over‑prediction patterns; no analysis of false corrections.

## Confidence
