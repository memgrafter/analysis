# Tiled Prompts: Overcoming Prompt Underspecification in Image and Video Super-Resolution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03342  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03342  
- **Reference count:** 31  
- **Primary result:** Tiled Prompts mitigate prompt underspecification in tiled diffusion super-resolution, yielding measurable improvements in perceptual quality and text alignment for both images and videos with negligible additional inference cost.

## Executive Summary
Text-conditioned diffusion models for super-resolution often rely on tiling to handle high-resolution inputs, but a single global prompt leads to sparse and misleading guidance for individual tiles. This paper introduces Tiled Prompts, a framework that employs a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate a dedicated text prompt for each latent tile. By providing localized conditioning, the approach enhances semantics at the tile level, reduces artifacts, and improves alignment with the text description. Evaluations on image and video benchmarks show consistent gains in no-reference quality metrics (e.g., NIQE) and text-visual alignment (e.g., CLIP Score, LanguageBind), all while adding minimal overhead.

## Method Summary
The method adapts the inference pipeline of a pretrained diffusion-based super-resolution model (DiT4SR for images, STAR for videos) without altering its weights. First, the low-resolution input is divided into overlapping spatial (images) or spatio‑temporal (videos) tiles. For each tile, a VLM extracts a concise, keyword‑rich description focused solely on the tile’s content; for videos, the VLM also receives the full low‑resolution sequence to capture motion context. During diffusion, each tile is denoised conditioned on its local prompt, and the predictions are aggregated across the latent space using Gaussian blending. The final high‑resolution output is obtained by decoding the merged latent.

## Key Results
- **Image super‑resolution:** On LSDIR1K, NIQE decreases from 2.94 to 2.90 and CLIP Score rises from 25.57 to 27.58. Similar gains appear on Urban100 and OST300.
- **Video super‑resolution:** On VideoLQ, NIQE improves from 4.70 to 4.52, and LanguageBind (video‑text alignment) increases from 0.43 to 0.60.
- **Efficiency:** Additional VLM calls add negligible latency (≈0.2 s per 2048×2048 image) and reduce hallucinations and tile‑level seams compared to a global prompt baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Resolving Prompt Sparsity
A global prompt often lacks specific details for individual tiles, leading to low mutual information between the high‑resolution tile content and the condition. By querying a VLM on each tile independently, Tiled Prompts generate a local prompt `c_local` that captures fine‑grained semantics (e.g., “red brick texture”, “SIXTH AVE sign”) crucial for that region. This raises the conditional mutual information `I(x_H; c_local | x_L)` relative to a global prompt, providing denser guidance. Evidence: The paper formalizes prompt sparsity and reports improved CLIP Scores; qualitative examples (Appendix B, Fig 6) show richer details in tiled‑prompt outputs.

### Mechanism 2: Reducing Prompt Misguidance in CFG
Classifier‑Free Guidance (CFG) amplifies the difference between conditional and unconditional predictions. When a global prompt contains irrelevant tokens for a specific tile, the guidance direction `Δe` includes an error term `δ_i` that pushes generation toward incorrect modes. Tile‑specific prompts are designed to be semantically consistent with the tile, minimizing `∥δ_i(c_local)∥` and thus the erroneous component scaled by the CFG scale `s`. Evidence: The authors decompose `Δe` theoretically and show reduced artifacts in examples where global prompts introduced nuisance descriptions; quantitative gains (e.g., CLIP Score) further support cleaner guidance.

### Mechanism 3: Context‑A aware Tiling for Video
Videos suffer from *dual sparsity*: both spatial and temporal underspecification. Tiled Prompts for video let the VLM ingest the full low‑resolution sequence alongside the local spatio‑temporal tile, enabling descriptions that capture dynamics (e.g., “spinning wheel”, “person walking”) that would be ambiguous from the tile alone. Evidence: The paper reports larger improvements on video‑text alignment (LanguageBind) than on static images, suggesting that motion context is effectively exploited. An ablation (Section 5.3) implies context assistance is beneficial.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Model Tiling & Latent Space Processing:** Latent diffusion models operate on small latent tiles to handle high resolutions. Understanding tile boundaries, overlap, and aggregation is essential to see why a global prompt is underspecified for individual tile denoising.  
  *Quick check:* If you split a 2048×2048 image into 16×16 latent tiles with overlap, how many tiles are processed, and what seams might appear from naive aggregation?
- **Classifier‑Free Guidance (CFG):** CFG steers generation by linearly combining unconditional and conditional model outputs: `e_uncond + s*(e_cond - e_uncond)`. Irrelevant prompt tokens distort `e_cond`, and increasing `s` amplifies that distortion.  
  *Quick check:* What happens to the influence of irrelevant words in the prompt as you increase the CFG scale `s`?
- **Vision‑Language Model as a Zero‑Shot Prompt Extractor:** A pre‑trained VLM (Qwen‑VL) is used to produce descriptive text from a degraded tile. Success requires the VLM to ignore artifacts and focus on intended content, guided by a strict user prompt.  
  *Quick check:* What are two specific instructions in the user prompt (Appendix A.2) that force the VLM to avoid mentioning degradation?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input → Tiling Module → VLM Prompt Extractor → Tiled SR Engine → Decoder → Output

The Tiling Module splits the low‑resolution input into overlapping tiles. The VLM Prompt Extractor queries a pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B‑Instruct for images, Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Instruct for videos) with each tile (and full video for context) to produce `c_local`. The Tiled SR Engine (DiT4SR/STAR) runs diffusion: at each timestep it crops the current latent to each tile region, denoises with the local prompt, aggregates predictions via weighted sum, and updates the latent. The Decoder produces the final high‑resolution output.

**Critical path:** The core innovation is inserting the VLM Prompt Extractor between tiling and SR. The pipeline first runs all VLM calls (offline) to generate prompts, then iterates over diffusion timesteps, processing each tile in parallel across the grid, aggregating predictions, and stepping the scheduler.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **VLM cost vs. quality:** More tiles increase specificity but linearly increase VLM calls. The paper uses 25 tiles for 2048×2048 images and 12 for videos, finding minimal overhead.  
- **Video context:** Providing the full low‑resolution video to the VLM for each tile adds compute but may be necessary for temporal dynamics.  
- **Prompt specificity:** The user prompt enforces keyword‑only output (“Output ONLY the inferred high‑quality keywords…”), trading fluency for dense conditioning better suited to diffusion.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. **VLM hallucinations:** The local prompt describes objects not present in the tile, leading the SR model to generate false content.  
2. **Boundary artifacts:** Despite Gaussian blending, seams appear if adjacent tile prompts cause sharp semantic transitions.  
3. **No quantitative gain:** If metrics (NIQE, CLIP Score) show no improvement over the global baseline, possible causes include ineffective prompts, model insensitivity to prompt details, or inappropriate tile size.

**First 3 experiments to reproduce:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Implement the global‑prompt baseline (one prompt from the full LR input) and verify metric differences against Table 1.  
2. **Ablation – Global+Local:** Test the “Global + Local” condition (concatenating global and local prompts) to assess whether global context helps.  
3. **Runtime & tile‑count scaling:** Measure total inference time (VLM + SR) for grids of varying size (e.g., 4×4, 8×8, 16×16) to confirm the reported overhead.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **VLM robustness:** How can prompt extraction be made robust to hallucinations and misinterpretations of local tile content?  
2. **Multi‑object tiles:** How does the method handle tiles containing multiple distinct objects or ambiguous content where a single prompt may remain underspecified?  
3. **Optimal tiling configuration:** What is the best tile size, overlap, and spatial/temporal granularity for different content types and resolutions?  
4. **VLM choice impact:** How does the selection of the Vision‑Language Model affect the effectiveness of Tiled Prompts, and are there architectural features that particularly benefit tile‑specific prompting?

## Limitations
- **Unreported VLM decoding parameters** (temperature, top_p, max tokens) may affect prompt quality and reproducibility.  
- **Gaussian blending details** (variance, edge handling) are not fully specified, potentially influencing artifact reduction.  
- **Causal attribution ambiguity:** Gains could partly stem from longer prompt token counts rather than semantic locality; the baseline does not match global prompt length.

## Confidence
- **Overall metric improvements:** High (consistent quantitative gains across datasets)  
- **Mechanism 1 (prompt sparsity resolved):** Medium (theoretical mutual‑information argument, lacking direct validation)  
- **Mechanism 2 (CFG misguidance reduced):** Medium (qualitative support, no probing of guidance vectors)  
- **Mechanism 3 (video context necessity):** Low (no ablation removing context; video gains may also arise from temporal tiling)

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt quality audit:** Sample 100 tile prompts; annotate hallucination rate and keyword relevance against the tile content. Expect ≤5% hallucinations and high relevance.  
2. **Video context ablation:** Run video SR with tile‑only prompts (no full‑video context). If LanguageBind drops >0.1, the context mechanism is critical.  
3. **Decoding robustness:** Sweep VLM temperature (0.1, 0.5, 1.0) and top_p (0.9, 1.0). Stable CLIP Score gains would alleviate concerns about cherry‑picked parameters.
