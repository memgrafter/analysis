# SciMDR: Benchmarking and Advancing Scientific Multimodal Document Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12249
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12249
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A 7B model fine-tuned on SciMDR achieves 49.1 on SciMDR-Eval, matching GPT-5.2 (49.9) and significantly outperforming the base model (19.8).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the faithfulness-realism trade-off in synthetic dataset generation for scientific multimodal document reasoning. Existing methods either use simplified contexts (ensuring faithfulness but lacking realism) or full documents (realistic but prone to hallucination). The proposed solution is a synthesize-and-reground framework: first, atomic QA pairs with explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) are generated on isolated, claim-focused contexts to ensure high faithfulness; second, these verified pairs are programmatically re-embedded into full-document contexts with injected information localization steps, creating realistic training instances. The resulting SciMDR dataset (300K QA pairs from 20K STEM papers) and benchmark SciMDR-Eval (907 expert-annotated pairs) enable a Qwen2.5-VL-7B model to achieve dramatic improvements on scientific QA tasks, demonstrating that careful synthetic data design can close the gap with much larger proprietary models.

## Method Summary
The pipeline comprises two stages. **Stage 1 (Claim-Centric QA Synthesis):** Scientific papers are parsed (using MinerU2.5 OCR) into multimodal context units (a text segment, its referencing visual, and caption). A claim extractor (GPT-5.1) distills declarative claims from the text alone, then a visual grounder matches claims to visuals. Using these verified claims as answers, three QA synthesizers generate questions and backward reasoning chains (CoT) for text-only (TQA), visual-only (VQA), and multimodal (MQA) queries. **Stage 2 (Document-Scale Regrounding):** Each QA-CoT pair is bound to its original evidence locations (e.g., "Section 3, Figure 2"). The full paper text is included as context, and a deterministic template injects an "information localization" step (e.g., "First, consult Section X and Table Y") before the original CoT. This forces the model to practice evidence filtering within noisy, realistic contexts. The final dataset is filtered for quality and diversity.

## Key Results
- On SciMDR-Eval, fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on SciMDR yields a +29.3 point gain (from 19.8 to 49.1) over the base model.
- On ChartQA and CharXiv-Detailed, SciMDR training provides gains of +1.7 and +10.6 points respectively, outperforming the SPIQA synthetic dataset.
- The 7B model achieves 49.1 on SciMDR-Eval, nearly matching GPT-5.2 (49.9) in this domain-specific benchmark.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Faithfulness via Atomic, Claim-Centric Synthesis
Operating on small, claim-focused contexts minimizes attention dilution and simplifies verification for the generator model. The pipeline first decomposes documents into context units, extracts declarative claims from the text alone (without visuals), and then cross-checks claims against visuals to determine grounding. This backward construction—providing the claim as the known answer—transforms open-ended inference into a constrained justification task, making outputs more traceable and less hallucinatory. The core assumption is that a model's ability to generate verifiable reasoning chains is much higher when the input is short, focused, and the final answer is given.

### Mechanism 2: Realism via Programmatic Regrounding with Localization
Verified QA-CoT pairs are re-embedded into full-document contexts with injected information localization steps. Because each pair is bound to specific evidence locations (e.g., "Section 3, Figure 2"), a deterministic template generates a localization instruction (e.g., "To answer, consult Section X and Table Y") and prepends it to the CoT. This teaches the model to first filter noise and locate evidence within a realistic, complex context before reasoning. The core assumption is that learning to solve full-document QA requires explicit supervision on *where* to look, not just *what* to conclude.

### Mechanism 3: Critical Role of Explicit CoT and Localization
The training format `(Full Document, Question) → (Localization + CoT + Answer)` jointly trains two synergistic skills: meta-reasoning about *how to search* the document, and domain reasoning to *synthesize* the answer. Ablation studies show catastrophic drops (~26-32 points) when either the CoT or the localization step is removed, confirming they are not redundant. The localization step addresses the "needle-in-a-haystack" problem of long contexts, while the detailed CoT scaffolds complex multimodal logical integration. The core assumption is that performance gains on complex, long-context tasks are bottlenecked by the availability of training data that demonstrates the *process* of problem-solving (the path to the answer), not just the final answer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Backward Reasoning Construction**  
  *Why needed here:* The innovation for faithfulness is flipping the standard paradigm: instead of asking a model to *find* an answer from context (prone to hallucination), provide the ground-truth claim first and ask for justification. This reduces cognitive load from open-ended inference to constrained explanation.

- **Concept: Information Localization as a Learnable Skill**  
  *Why needed here:* Real-world scientific QA is a "needle-in-a-haystack" problem. Models need explicit training on *where* to look, not just *what* to conclude. Injecting deterministic localization steps turns this implicit skill into an explicit part of the reasoning trace.  
  *Quick check:* Why would a model fail on full-paper QA even if it knows the answer, and how does explicitly stating "First look at Section 4.2" in training CoT address this?

- **Concept: Faithfulness-Realism Trade-off in Synthetic Data**  
  *Why needed here:* This is the central dilemma. Faithfulness (data accuracy) is achieved with simplified contexts; realism (task complexity) requires noisy, full documents. The framework's novelty is decoupling these goals into separate stages.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Paper Processor -> Context Unit Builder -> Claim Extractor -> Visual Grounder -> QA Synthesizer -> Regrounding Engine -> Dataset Validator

**Critical path:** The pipeline is linear but has quality-critical checkpoints after claim extraction, visual grounding, and QA synthesis. Failure at any checkpoint propagates low-quality data.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Using a proprietary model (GPT-5.1) for synthesis maximizes claim/CoT quality but creates a dependency; open-source alternatives may lower faithfulness.
- Atomic context size: too narrow (<1 paragraph) may miss necessary context; too broad (>1 section) reintroduces noise, hurting faithfulness.
- Localization template specificity: highly specific ("See Table 2 in Section 3.1") is more instructive but requires perfect metadata; generic ("Look at the relevant figure") is robust but less helpful.

**Failure signatures:**
- Claim extraction: non-declarative claims (questions, hedging) or claims with no visual counterpart mis-routed to MQA.
- Visual grounding: false negatives (claim is visually supported but marked `exists_in_visual: false`) or false positives (tangential visuals labeled "Supports").
- Regrounding: localization step points to wrong section/figure, or source metadata corrupted during OCR/parsing.
- Final data: model overfits to localization template phrasing without learning actual evidence-filtering behavior.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Claim Extraction Unit Test:** Annotate 50 paragraphs manually. Run Claim Extractor prompt. Measure: % of claims that are (a) declarative, (b) text-supported, (c) have plausible visual correlate. Target >90% for (b).
2. **Localization Token Ablation:** Train two models on identical SciMDR: one with injected localization step, one without. Evaluate on a noise-controlled subset of SciMDR-Eval (e.g., papers with exactly 5 figures). Expect localization model to have higher accuracy on questions requiring figure/table identification.
3. **Noise Robustness Curve:** Evaluate a trained SciMDR model on SciMDR-Eval under three contexts: (a) Oracle (only true evidence, no distractors), (b) Standard (limited noise), (c) Full-paper (max noise). Plot accuracy drop. A shallow drop from (a)→(c) indicates effective localization learning.

## Limitations
- Dataset provenance filtering: exact criteria/prompt for selecting "original experiment-driven studies" via GPT-5.1 is unreported, potentially biasing corpus composition.
- Claim extraction reproducibility: details for "context-aware pre-processing" and multi-claim handling are vague; using open-source LLMs instead of GPT-5.1 may degrade claim quality.
- Generalization: performance matching GPT-5.2 is domain-specific (SciMDR-Eval) and may not transfer to other scientific QA or multimodal tasks.

## Confidence
- **Faithfulness-Realism Trade-off Solution (Two-stage pipeline)**: Medium
- **Explicit CoT and Localization Criticality (Ablation results)**: High
- **7B Matching GPT-5.2 as General Capability**: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce claim extraction fidelity:** Implement the Claim Extractor prompt with a local LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B). Sample 100 paragraphs, have annotators verify: (a) claims are declarative and text-supported, (b) visual grounding labels are correct. Target >85% accuracy for viability.
2. **Audit localization step correctness:** For 200 regrounded QA instances, programmatically verify that every referenced section/figure/table exists in the source paper and that the claim's evidence is actually located there. Quantify mismatches.
3. **Controlled noise robustness test:** Evaluate the trained SciMDR model on a matched-noise subset of SciMDR-Eval where distractors are systematically varied (e.g., 0, 1, 5, 20 irrelevant sections). Plot accuracy drop curve. A shallow slope indicates effective localization learning; a steep drop suggests overfitting to template cues.
