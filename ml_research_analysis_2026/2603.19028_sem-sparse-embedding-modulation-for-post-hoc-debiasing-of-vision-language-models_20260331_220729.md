# SEM: Sparse Embedding Modulation for Post-Hoc Debiasing of Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19028
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19028
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Post-hoc debiasing of CLIP via sparse autoencoder modulation yields up to 28.2‑point worst‑group accuracy gain on Waterbirds and consistent fairness improvements across retrieval and classification benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
SEM introduces a post‑hoc method to mitigate social biases in vision‑language models like CLIP by operating directly on text embeddings. It trains a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) to map embeddings into a disentangled latent space, scores neurons for content relevance and bias sensitivity, and modulates activations to suppress bias while preserving semantics. Evaluated on four fairness benchmarks, SEM substantially improves worst‑group accuracy and retrieval fairness, and can be combined with other debiasing techniques for additive gains.

## Method Summary
SEM first trains a Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoder (MSAE) on a large corpus of CLIP text embeddings (e.g., CC12M‑cleaned). Given a query, its embedding is encoded into a sparse latent vector. Two scoring functions quantify each neuron’s relevance to the query’s core content (based on activation patterns across diverse neutral prompts) and its sensitivity to biased attributes (by comparing bias‑class signatures against neutral and other biases). A modulation coefficient combines these scores, attenuating bias‑related neurons while preserving content‑bearing ones. The modified latent is decoded back to the embedding space, yielding a debiased representation used for zero‑shot classification or retrieval. The approach is model‑agnostic and requires no retraining of the original CLIP.

## Key Results
- Zero‑shot classification worst‑group accuracy on Waterbirds improves by up to 28.2 points.
- Retrieval fairness metrics show consistent reductions; e.g., FairFace Race KL divergence drops from 0.237 to 0.170.
- SEM complements BendVLM, providing additional fairness improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SEM exploits the disentangled representations learned by sparse autoencoders. Neurons in the SAE latent space tend to activate for specific concepts, enabling separate identification of content‑bearing and bias‑bearing dimensions. By scoring neurons based on their consistency across neutral prompts (content) and correlation with bias classes (bias), SEM selectively suppresses bias signals while largely retaining semantic content. The modulation formula ensures that neurons highly relevant to content are preserved even if they have some bias sensitivity, whereas purely bias‑driven neurons are attenuated. Operating in the SAE latent space avoids the entanglement present in the original CLIP embeddings, allowing precise intervention.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAE)**: Train to reconstruct inputs with a sparsity constraint, yielding disentangled features. *Why needed*: Provides interpretable, separable neurons for manipulation. *Quick check*: Inspect top‑k activated neurons for a set of prompts; they should correspond to distinct concepts.
- **CLIP Text Embeddings**: Pre‑trained vision‑language model. *Why needed*: Base representations to debias. *Quick check*: Verify zero‑shot accuracy on a standard dataset (e.g., ImageNet) matches reported CLIP performance.
- **Disentanglement**: Separation of factors of variation. *Why needed*: Enables targeting bias without affecting semantics. *Quick check*: Compute a disentanglement score (e.g., from a labeled set of prompts); should be >0.5.
- **Bias Metrics (KL Divergence, MaxSkew)**: Measure distributional fairness in retrieval. *Why needed*: Quantify bias reduction. *Quick check*: Compute metrics on a biased retrieval benchmark before and after intervention.
- **Neuron Scoring**: Assigns content/bias importance. *Why needed*: Determines modulation coefficients. *Quick check*: Correlate S_concept with human‑annotated concept relevance; S_bias with known biased attributes.
- **Matryoshka SAE**: Multi‑granularity representation. *Why needed*: Captures features at different abstraction levels. *Quick check*: Compare reconstruction quality across granularities g.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Prompt set (P_div, P_bias, P_q) → CLIP text encoder → embeddings → SAE encoder → latent h → neuron scoring (S_concept, S_bias) → modulation coefficient M → masked latent h_debias → SAE decoder → debiased embedding z → downstream zero‑shot task.

**Critical path**:
1. SAE training on large text corpus.
2. Pre‑computation of neutral median m_div and bias signatures.
3. For each query: encode, compute scores, modulate, decode.
4. Use z_debias for classification/retrieval.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Sparsity level*: Higher sparsity improves disentanglement but risks information loss; tuned via MSAE granularities.
- *Prompt diversity*: More diverse P_div yields better content relevance; biased P_bias must be carefully constructed.
- *Modulation aggressiveness*: Balancing bias removal vs. content preservation via exponential scaling of scores.
- *Granularity selection*: Using multiple g provides robustness but increases compute.

**Failure signatures**:
- Reconstruction MSE > threshold (e.g., 1e‑3) indicates SAE not capturing embeddings.
- Disentanglement score <0.3 suggests neurons are not separable.
- Content preservation (cosine similarity) drops below 0.9, indicating semantic distortion.
- Worst‑group accuracy decreases, signalling over‑attenuation.

**First experiments**:
1. Train MSAE on CC12M text embeddings; validate reconstruction quality and sparsity.
2. Compute S_concept and S_bias distributions on a held‑out prompt set; verify that content neurons have high S_concept and bias neurons have high S_bias.
3. Run SEM on one benchmark (e.g., FairFace retrieval) with both variants; measure KL divergence and worst‑group accuracy against baseline CLIP.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly discuss open questions or future work. However, based on the limitations, the following questions arise:
- How do the exact bias prompt templates and paraphrasing prompts affect the debiasing performance?
- What are the precise sparse autoencoder hyperparameters (e.g., TopK, L1 coefficient) and how do they trade off disentanglement and reconstruction?
- Under what conditions does the assumption of prompt neutrality break down, and how can the scoring functions be made more robust?

## Limitations
- Exact bias prompt templates and paraphrasing prompts are not provided, requiring reconstruction from limited descriptions.
- Sparse Autoencoder sparsity specifics (TopK, L1 coefficient) are omitted; implementation may affect neuron disentanglement and modulation efficacy.
- Scoring functions assume ideal prompt neutrality, which may not hold in practice, potentially leading to incomplete bias mitigation.
