# How Vision Becomes Language: A Layer-wise Information-Theoretic Analysis of Multimodal Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.15580
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15580
- **Reference count**: 28
- **Primary result**: Multimodal Transformers translate visual evidence into language space early (layers 0–10) and perform late reasoning primarily within language representations, with vision-unique information decaying monotonically and language-unique information surging to account for ~82% of final predictive information.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a layer-wise analysis framework, PID Flow, to investigate how multimodal large language models (MLLMs) integrate visual and linguistic information during reasoning. Applying Partial Information Decomposition (PID) to LLaVA models across six GQA tasks reveals a consistent pattern of **modal transduction**: visual information is converted into language-space representations in early layers, after which reasoning proceeds largely independently within the language modality. The Image→Question attention pathway is identified as a causal bottleneck for this transduction via attention knockout experiments. Task semantics modulate the absolute information magnitudes but not the underlying transduction pattern, with redundancy providing robustness to pathway disruption.

## Method Summary
The core method, PID Flow, estimates layer-wise information decomposition in high-dimensional Transformer activations. For each layer, image and text token representations are mean-pooled and concatenated. Dimensionality is reduced via PCA (retaining ~95% variance, ~32 dimensions). A normalizing flow (RealNVP) is trained to Gaussianize these reduced vectors. Closed-form mutual information and PID components (redundant, vision-unique, language-unique, synergistic) are then computed under a latent Gaussian assumption using the transformed variables and the scalar model output logit. This pipeline is repeated across all 32 layers. Causal validation uses an attention knockout that sets all Image→Question attention logits to −∞, severing the hypothesized transduction route, and the PID analysis is repeated to measure shifts in information trajectories.

## Key Results
- Layer-wise PID shows a **modal transduction pattern**: vision-unique information peaks in layers 0–1 and decays monotonically; language-unique information surges in late layers (20–32), accounting for ~82% of final predictive bits; cross-modal synergy remains below 2% throughout.
- Trajectory correlations between LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-1.6 exceed 0.96 for all PID components, indicating robustness across model variants.
- Disrupting the Image→Question attention pathway increases trapped vision-unique information and compensatory synergy, raising total information cost—effects are strongest in vision-dependent tasks, confirming its causal role in transduction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Multimodal Transformers perform **modal transduction**—they translate visual evidence into language-space representations early (layers 0–10), then conduct the bulk of reasoning within language-only computations.
- **Mechanism**: Layer-wise PID trajectories show vision-unique information (Uᵥ) peaks at layers 0–1 and decays monotonically, while language-unique information (Uᴸ) surges dramatically in late layers (20–32), ultimately accounting for ~82% of predictive bits. Cross-modal synergy (S) remains persistently below 2%.
- **Core assumption**: Mean-pooled token summaries preserve modality-specific predictive information structure across Transformer depth, and Gaussianized PID approximations maintain relative ordering and trends.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "vision-unique information peaks in early layers and decays monotonically, while language-unique information surges in late layers, accounting for approximately 82% of final predictive information"
  - Section 5.1: Figure 2 shows consistent U-shaped Uᴸ trajectories and decaying Uᵥ across six GQA tasks; Table 3 quantifies final-layer dominance (UL share 48–97%)
  - Corpus: Includes frameworks establishing PID for multimodal interaction analysis.
- **Break condition**: If synergy exceeds 15% of total predictive information at final layer, the system operates under a "persistent synergy" regime instead of transduction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The **Image→Question attention pathway** is a causal bottleneck for visual transduction; disrupting it traps visual evidence in the image stream, forcing compensatory synergy and inflating total information cost.
- **Mechanism**: Blocking Image→Question attention increases Uᵥ (visual information stays trapped), increases S (model resorts to inefficient joint processing), and raises total predictive information budget—effects strongest in vision-dependent tasks.
- **Core assumption**: Attention logits directly control information routing between modalities; setting them to −∞ cleanly severs the dominant transduction route without affecting other pathways.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Section 5.3: Table 8 shows ΔUᵥ>0 and ΔS>0 for CHOOSEREL and LOGICALOBJ; Figure 9 visualizes full trajectory shift with elevated Uᵥ.
  - Abstract: "disrupting that pathway increases trapped vision-unique information, compensatory synergy, and total information cost, with strongest effects in vision-dependent tasks"
  - Corpus: Related work supports attention knockout as a causal intervention.
- **Break condition**: If knockout produces ΔUᵥ<0 or decreases total information, the identified pathway is not the primary transduction route.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: **Task semantics modulate the information fingerprint** (magnitudes of PID components) but not the underlying modal transduction pattern; redundancy levels explain robustness to pathway disruption.
- **Mechanism**: High-redundancy tasks (e.g., category selection) show large R and lower UL share (~48%), providing alternative informational routes that buffer against Image→Question knockout. Low-redundancy, vision-dependent tasks show near-zero R and UL>93%, making them sensitive to pathway disruption.
- **Core assumption**: Semantic overlap between visual and linguistic representations creates redundancy that can compensate for blocked transduction.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Section 5.1: CHOOSECAT has R=7.74 bits and lowest UL share (48.1%), while QUERYATTR has R≈0 and UL=97.3%.
  - Section 5.3: CHOOSECAT and CHOOSEATTR show minimal ΔTotal (<0) despite ΔUᵥ>0; task-dependence score ranks vision-dependent tasks highest.
  - Corpus: Discusses redundancy as a measure of alignment strength.
- **Break condition**: If all tasks showed identical ΔTotal under knockout regardless of baseline R, redundancy would not govern robustness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Partial Information Decomposition (PID)
  - **Why needed here**: PID decomposes joint predictive information into redundant, unique, and synergistic components, enabling identification of modal transduction.
  - **Quick check question**: If Uᴸ ≈ 0 but Uᵥ > 0 and S > 0, what does that say about the model's reliance on each modality?

- **Concept**: Normalizing flows for Gaussianization
  - **Why needed here**: Transform raw high-dimensional activations into approximately Gaussian variables so closed-form mutual information and PID can be computed; without this, estimation is intractable.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does a bijective transformation preserve mutual information, and why is that property essential for PID Flow?

- **Concept**: Attention as information routing
  - **Why needed here**: The knockout intervention targets attention pathways hypothesised to carry visual evidence into language tokens; understanding attention's role is key to causal validation.
  - **Quick check question**: Does blocking Image→Question attention affect early, middle, or late layers most according to the transduction hypothesis?

## Architecture Onboarding
Vision encoder (CLIP ViT-L) -> linear projection -> LLM backbone (32-layer Vicuna Transformer). Modality regions: Image tokens (576 fixed in 1.5, variable up to 2304 in 1.6) vs. question tokens. Cross-attention flows between image and question tokens at every layer.

**Critical path**:
1. Representation extraction: For each layer ℓ, pool image tokens → xᵥ(ℓ), pool question tokens → xᴸ(ℓ)
2. PID Flow: PCA → normalizing flow (RealNVP) → Gaussian PID (Imin redundancy rule)
3. Trajectory assembly: Collect (R, Uᵥ, Uᴸ, S) across all 32 layers
4. Mechanism classification: Check for modal transduction signatures (early Uᵥ peak, late Uᴸ surge, S<2%)
5. Intervention: Set Image→Question attention logits to −∞, recompute trajectories, compare shifts

**Design tradeoffs**:
- PCA dimensionality (d′): smaller → more stable but risk losing signal; they use d′≈32 (95% variance)
- Flow capacity: more coupling layers → better Gaussianization but risk overfitting; they use 8 blocks
- Mean pooling vs. attention-weighted: mean pooling is simpler and more stable; attention-weighting may capture token importance but introduces noise

**Failure signatures**:
- High synergy (>15%) at final layer → indicates persistent fusion rather than transduction
- Non-monotonic Uᵥ trajectory (rising after layer 10) → suggests visual evidence re-injected late
- Cross-model correlation <0.9 → patterns are not architecture-robust
- Knockout produces ΔUᵥ<0 → blocked pathway not used for visual evidence extraction

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline traversal: Run full PID Flow on LLaVA-1.5-7B across all 6 GQA tasks, verify modal transduction pattern (early Uᵥ peak, late Uᴸ surge, S bounded)
2. Cross-model replication: Repeat on LLaVA-1.6-7B, compute layer-wise Pearson r between models; expect >0.96 correlation on all components
3. Causal knockout: Apply Image→Question attention block to LLaVA-1.6-7B, measure ΔUᵥ, ΔS, ΔTotal at final layer; expect strongest positive Δ in vision-dependent tasks (CHOOSEREL, LOGICALOBJ)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question**: Do multimodal architectures with fundamentally different fusion mechanisms (e.g., Flamingo's cross-attention) exhibit the same modal transduction pattern observed in LLaVA?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state that their experiments are restricted to the LLaVA family and that testing other architectures like Flamingo is an important next step.
  - **Why unresolved**: The modal transduction mechanism might be specific to the encode-project-decode pipeline of LLaVA; if other architectures show different PID trajectories, the generality of the findings would be limited.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Apply the same layer-wise PID analysis to models like Flamingo, BLIP-2, or other architectures with cross-attention fusion, and compare trajectory signatures and final-layer composition.

- **Question**: What are the causal effects of disrupting the Image→Question attention pathway at specific layers rather than uniformly across all layers?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors note that their attention knockout is applied uniformly across all 32 layers and that layer-specific interventions would provide finer-grained causal evidence.
  - **Why unresolved**: Uniform knockout cannot pinpoint which layers are most critical for the transduction process or how early vs. late disruptions differentially affect the information trajectory.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct layer-wise knockout experiments (e.g., blocking only layers 0–10, 10–20, 20–32) and analyze the resulting PID shifts, especially focusing on the three-stage structure.

- **Question**: How do PID trajectories change on complex multi-step visual reasoning tasks that potentially require stronger synergistic computation?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors mention that GQA tasks are relatively simple and that more demanding benchmarks may reveal stronger synergistic contributions.
  - **Why unresolved**: The near-absence of synergy (<2%) in their results might be an artifact of task simplicity; complex tasks could exhibit different information structures, challenging the modal transduction conclusion.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Extend the analysis to datasets like ScienceQA, VQAv2 with compositional questions, or synthetic reasoning tasks, and compare PID components, especially synergy, at the final layer.

- **Question**: Can architectural or objective-level interventions (e.g., auxiliary losses, separate visual streams) preserve vision-unique information and mitigate premature transduction?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors suggest that preserving modality-unique information requires such interventions and that PID trajectories can guide targeted modifications.
  - **Why unresolved**: Without such interventions, the observed transduction might be a fixed property of current MLLMs; it is unknown whether we can shift the information trajectory toward greater vision-unique retention.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Design models with added regularization (e.g., penalizing early loss of UV) or alternative fusion mechanisms (e.g., maintaining separate visual pathways) and evaluate whether PID trajectories show higher UV in later layers and reduced UL dominance.

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to decoder-only Vicuna backbones (LLaVA family); generalizability to other architectures (e.g., Flamingo, BLIP) is unknown.
- GQA tasks are synthetic and relatively simple; the near-absence of synergy may not reflect more complex, real-world multimodal reasoning.
- PID Flow relies on Gaussian approximations and mean pooling, which may miss non-linear interactions or subtle token-level effects.

## Confidence
- **Modal transduction pattern** (early vision-unique decay, late language-unique surge, low synergy): High confidence (consistent across tasks and models, cross-model correlation >0.96).
- **Causal bottleneck** (Image→Question attention as necessary for transduction): Medium confidence (knockout results align, but intervention is blunt and not yet replicated on LLaVA-1.5).
- **Task modulation** (redundancy buffers disruption): Low confidence (limited task diversity, correlational evidence).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate PID Flow on Flamingo and BLIP-2 to test architectural generality.
2. Perform layer-specific and head-specific Image→Question knockouts to pinpoint critical routing stages.
3. Evaluate on natural VQA datasets (e.g., VQA v2) to assess ecological validity of the transduction pattern.
