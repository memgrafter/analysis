# PerSoMed: A Large-Scale Balanced Dataset for Persian Social Media Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19333
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19333
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: TookaBERT-Large achieved state-of-the-art performance with an F1-score of 0.9621 on the balanced PerSoMed dataset.

## Executive Summary
PerSoMed addresses the scarcity of large-scale, balanced datasets for Persian social media text classification. The authors collected 60,000 raw posts from Persian social media platforms, which they preprocessed and labeled using a hybrid approach: ChatGPT few-shot prompting followed by human verification. To create a balanced dataset across nine categories (4,000 posts per class), they applied undersampling with semantic redundancy removal and hybrid data augmentation (lexical replacement and generative prompting). Transformer-based models, particularly the Persian-specific TookaBERT-Large, achieved an F1-score of 0.9621, outperforming multilingual and generic baselines. An ablation study demonstrated that balancing the dataset was crucial, improving TookaBERT-Large's F1-score by 0.1 points. The Social and Political categories were more challenging due to inherent ambiguity.

## Method Summary
The dataset construction pipeline began with the collection of 60,000 raw Persian social media posts. After preprocessing to clean the text, the authors employed a hybrid annotation strategy: initial labels were generated via ChatGPT few-shot prompting, and then verified and corrected by human annotators. To address severe class imbalance in the raw data (e.g., Political: 16,637 posts vs Historical: 497), the team balanced the dataset using two complementary techniques. For majority classes, they undersampled by computing ParsBERT embeddings for all posts, calculating pairwise cosine similarities, and removing highly similar samples to eliminate redundancy. For minority classes, they oversampled through a hybrid augmentation combining lexical replacement (synonym substitution) for approximately 10% of samples and ChatGPT-based generative prompting for the remaining 90%, thereby producing diverse and contextually appropriate synthetic posts. The final PerSoMed dataset contains 36,000 posts evenly distributed across nine categories. The authors evaluated a suite of models, including BiLSTM, XLM‑RoBERTa (with LoRA/AdaLoRA), FaBERT, SBERT+Attention, and TookaBERT‑Base/Large. Training and testing were conducted on the balanced set, and an ablation compared performance with the original unbalanced data.

## Key Results
- TookaBERT‑Large achieved an F1‑score of 0.9621, precision 0.9622, and recall 0.9621 on the balanced dataset.
- Ablation: Balancing improved TookaBERT‑Large's F1‑score from 0.8621 on the unbalanced data to 0.9621 on the balanced version.
- Social and Political categories showed slightly lower performance, likely due to inherent label ambiguity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The hybrid annotation and balancing pipeline produces a high‑quality, balanced dataset that enables superior classification performance.
- **Mechanism**: Combining ChatGPT few‑shot prompting with human verification ensures accurate labeling while scaling annotation; undersampling with semantic redundancy removal (using ParsBERT embeddings and cosine similarity) eliminates redundant samples from overrepresented classes; hybrid data augmentation (lexical replacement + generative prompting) synthetically boosts underrepresented classes. This yields a balanced dataset with 4,000 samples per class.
- **Core assumption**: ChatGPT few‑shot prompting can generate sufficiently accurate initial labels that humans can efficiently correct, and that semantic similarity‑based undersampling effectively reduces redundancy without losing informative diversity.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] describes "hybrid annotation combining ChatGPT‑based few‑shot prompting with human verification" and "undersampling with semantic redundancy removal and advanced data augmentation strategies integrating lexical replacement and generative prompting."
  - [section 3.1] details the process: collection of 60,000 raw posts, preprocessing, ChatGPT labeling, human review, then balancing via undersampling using ParsBERT embeddings and cosine similarity, and augmentation for minority classes.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers like *Constructing Colloquial Dataset for Persian Sentiment Analysis* and *A comprehensive study on Frequent Pattern Mining…* show ongoing efforts in Persian NLP data construction, but do not directly anchor this specific hybrid method; thus corpus support is weak.
- **Break condition**: If ChatGPT few‑shot prompting yields low agreement with human annotators (below acceptable threshold), or if semantic redundancy removal inadvertently removes too many distinct samples (e.g., similarity threshold too high), the dataset quality degrades, leading to poor model performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Persian‑specific pretraining (TookaBERT) outperforms multilingual models (XLM‑RoBERTa) and generic architectures (BiLSTM) on Persian social media text classification.
- **Mechanism**: TookaBERT is pre‑trained on extensive Persian corpora, capturing language‑specific morphology, syntax, and colloquialisms. Its architecture (Byte‑Pair Encoding, whole‑word masking) is optimized for Persian. When fine‑tuned on the balanced dataset, it leverages this specialized knowledge to achieve higher accuracy, especially on ambiguous categories.
- **Core assumption**: Language‑specific pretraining provides representations that are more discriminative for Persian text than multilingual or general‑purpose models.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] states "transformer‑based models, especially the Persian‑specific TookaBERT‑Large, achieved state‑of‑the‑art performance" with F1 0.9621.
  - [section 4.1] Table 3 shows TookaBERT‑Large outperforms all others, and the text notes "FaBERT demonstrated solid results, emphasizing the value of Persian‑focused language representations."
  - [section 3.2] details TookaBERT's architecture: pre‑trained on Persian corpora, uses BPE, whole‑word masking, Flash Attention, ZeRO Stage 2.
  - [corpus] Neighbors include *FaBERT: Pre‑training BERT on Persian blogs* and *ParsBERT: Transformer‑based model for Persian language understanding*, which support the trend toward Persian‑specific pretraining. Direct comparison to TookaBERT is not in neighbors.
- **Break condition**: If the pretraining corpus of TookaBERT does not cover the linguistic diversity of the target social media data (e.g., slang, dialects), its advantage may diminish. Also, if the task is extremely low‑resource, the large model might overfit.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Mitigating class imbalance via undersampling and hybrid augmentation is crucial for achieving uniformly high performance across all categories, especially for minority classes.
- **Mechanism**: The original raw data had severe imbalance (e.g., Political: 16,637 vs Historical: 497). Undersampling of majority classes with semantic redundancy removal ensures that selected samples are diverse and not merely duplicates. Hybrid augmentation (10% lexical replacement, 90% generative prompting) expands minority classes with varied, contextually appropriate synthetic data. This balanced dataset prevents models from being biased toward majority classes.
- **Core assumption**: Balanced class distribution is necessary for the model to learn discriminative features for all categories, and the augmentation methods produce data that preserves semantic integrity.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] explicitly says "ablation study confirmed that addressing class imbalance was critical, as TookaBERT‑Large's F1‑score improved from 0.8621 on the unbalanced data to 0.9621 on the balanced dataset."
  - [section 4.2] Table 5 shows performance drops on unbalanced data across all models, and the text states "Applying undersampling and oversampling significantly improves results."
  - [section 3.1] describes the balancing techniques in detail.
  - [corpus] Neighbors include *CrisisSense‑LLM: Instruction Fine‑Tuned Large Language Model for Multi‑label Social Media Text Classification in Disaster Informatics* which deals with imbalanced multi‑label classification, providing indirect support but not a direct anchor.
- **Break condition**: If augmentation introduces noise (e.g., generative prompts produce incoherent or off‑topic posts), the model may learn spurious patterns. Similarly, if undersampling removes too many samples, majority classes may become unrepresentative.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. Data collection: Scrape Persian social media platforms (APIs or public datasets).
  2. Preprocessing: Clean text (remove punctuation, normalize, remove emojis/mentions, non‑Persian chars, extra whitespaces).
  3. Annotation: Hybrid labeling with ChatGPT few‑shot prompting → human verification.
  4. Balancing:
     - Undersampling: Compute ParsBERT embeddings for all posts in majority classes, calculate pairwise cosine similarity, remove samples with high similarity (redundancy), then select 4,000 most diverse samples.
     - Augmentation: For minority classes, apply lexical replacement (synonym substitution, ~10%) and ChatGPT generative prompting (~90%) to reach 4,000 samples.
  5. Model zoo: Train/evaluate BiLSTM, XLM‑RoBERTa (with LoRA/AdaLoRA), FaBERT, SBERT+Attention, TookaBERT‑Base/Large.
  6. Evaluation: Compute precision, recall, F1 per class and overall; perform ablation comparing balanced vs unbalanced.

- **Critical path**: The most resource‑intensive and crucial steps are: (a) human verification of ChatGPT labels (to ensure quality), (b) semantic redundancy removal using ParsBERT embeddings (computationally heavy), (c) generative augmentation (time‑consuming due to API calls). Any bottleneck here delays dataset release.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - ChatGPT vs human‑only annotation: ChatGPT scales cheaply but requires human verification to fix errors; tradeoff between cost and label noise.
  - Undersampling vs oversampling: They used undersampling for majority classes to reduce redundancy and oversampling via augmentation for minorities; alternative could have been pure oversampling but that might cause overfitting.
  - Lexical vs generative augmentation: Lexical is fast but limited diversity; generative is slower but more varied. Hybrid balances both.
  - Model choice: TookaBERT‑Large gives best performance but is computationally expensive; LoRA/AdaLoRA offer efficiency at slight cost.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High label disagreement between ChatGPT initial labels and final human‑verified labels (low inter‑annotator agreement) indicates poor prompt design or ambiguous categories.
  - After redundancy removal, if the number of retained samples in majority classes drops below 4,000, aggressive threshold may have removed too much diversity.
  - Augmented samples scoring low on fluency or relevance (checked by human evaluation) would degrade dataset quality.
  - Model performance on minority classes remains low even after balancing, suggesting augmentation didn't capture true distribution.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline: Train BiLSTM on the raw unbalanced dataset to establish a lower bound.
  2. Balanced training: Train TookaBERT‑Large on the balanced dataset to reproduce the claimed F1‑score.
  3. Ablation: Train TookaBERT‑Large on unbalanced data and compare metrics to isolate effect of balancing.

- Augmented data quality not evaluated; generative prompting may introduce noise or semantic drift.
