# Towards Autonomous Mathematics Research

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10177
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10177
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Aletheia, an autonomous math research agent using iterative generation/verification/revision with tool integration, produced publishable papers, solved 6/10 FirstProof research problems, and found 4 novel Erdős problem solutions (though only 6.5% of 200 candidate Erdős solutions were meaningfully correct).

## Executive Summary
Aletheia is a mathematical research agent designed to operate autonomously on problems at the research level, extending beyond Olympiad-style competition math. It employs a generate-verify-revise loop with a powerful base model (Gemini Deep Think), inference-time scaling, and integrated tools (search, Python). The system demonstrated end-to-end capability by producing a publishable paper without human intervention, achieving strong results on a new research-problem benchmark (FirstProof), and contributing novel verified solutions to longstanding Erdős problems. However, overall success rates remain modest, and hallucination and reliability issues persist, highlighting both progress and fundamental challenges in automating mathematical discovery.

## Method Summary
Aletheia orchestrates three subagents—Generator, Verifier, and Reviser—operating in an iterative loop. The Generator produces an initial natural-language solution trace. The Verifier evaluates this trace for correctness and flags flaws. The Reviser then refines the solution based on feedback. This cycle repeats until the solution passes verification or a maximum iteration limit is reached. The base model is an advanced version of Gemini Deep Think. Inference-time scaling (increasing computational budget during reasoning, such as via parallel sampling or tree search) is applied to improve exploration of reasoning paths. Tool integration is critical: web search is used to retrieve current literature and verify citations, while a Python sandbox executes computations. The model is specifically fine-tuned for reliable tool use.

## Key Results
- Fully autonomous generation of a publishable paper (Feng26) on eigenweights without human intervention.
- Human-AI collaboration led to a publishable paper (LeeSeo26) on independent set bounds.
- Evaluation of 700 Erdős problems yielded 4 novel solutions verified by experts (Erdős-652, 654, 1040, 1051), but only 6.5% of 200 candidate solutions were meaningfully correct.
- Solved 6 of 10 FirstProof research-level problems (P2, P5, P7, P8, P9, P10), with P7 judged publication-grade; achieved 82% conditional accuracy on FutureMathBasic (PhD-level exercises).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Generation and Verification
- **Claim:** Separating generation and verification allows the model to identify flaws overlooked during initial generation.
- **Mechanism:** The loop uses distinct Generator and Verifier subagents (same base model, different prompts). The Verifier critiques the Generator's output, and the Reviser acts on this feedback. This explicit scaffolding counteracts the model's tendency to "bluff" or produce shallow reasoning in a single pass.
- **Core assumption:** A model's training may prioritize plausible-sounding outputs over correctness; a verification step can surface and correct these issues.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] describes end-to-end iterative generation/verification/revise; [section 2.2] discusses decoupling final output from intermediate thinking tokens to recognize overlooked flaws.
- **Break condition:** If the Verifier itself is unreliable (consistent blind spots), the loop may reinforce rather than correct errors.

### Mechanism 2: Inference-Time Scaling
- **Claim:** Increasing compute during reasoning improves performance on research-level problems, with different scaling dynamics than for Olympiad problems.
- **Mechanism:** More inference-time compute (e.g., multiple parallel reasoning paths, tree search) explores a larger space of potential solutions. Research problems, being less constrained, require more exploration.
- **Core assumption:** Solution quality for hard mathematical problems correlates with the breadth of reasoning paths explored at inference.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] mentions a scaling law extending from Olympiad to PhD-level; [section 2.1] Figures 2a/b show accuracy improves with compute scale but plateaus, with research problems needing larger scales for marginal gains.
- **Break condition:** Scaling becomes computationally prohibitive; accuracy plateaus early for certain problem types.

### Mechanism 3: Tool Integration with Specialized Training
- **Claim:** Tools (search, Python) are essential to reduce hallucinations and access current literature, but mere access is insufficient; the model requires training to use them correctly.
- **Mechanism:** The agent calls external tools during any subagent step. A fine-tuning regimen teaches the model to issue appropriate queries, parse results, and incorporate findings accurately.
- **Core assumption:** Hallucinations stem from knowledge cutoffs and lack of grounding; tools provide factual anchors, but misuse can introduce new errors.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] cites "intensive tool use"; [section 2.3] states that granting internet access alone is insufficient, but extensive tool-use training led to a substantial reduction in obvious citation hallucinations; Python provided only marginal improvements.
- **Break condition:** Tool outputs are noisy or the model misquotes legitimate references, shifting errors from fabrication to subtle misinformation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Base Model (Gemini Deep Think) -> Generator -> Verifier -> Reviser -> (loop back to Generator)
    Tool Interface (Search, Python) callable from any subagent.
    Orchestrator manages loop limits, tool routing, and final output.
- **Critical path:**
  Start -> Generator produces solution -> Verifier evaluates -> if approved, End; if not, Reviser revises -> back to Generator. Tools invoked as needed along this path.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Compute vs. accuracy:* Higher inference scales improve success but increase cost/time (Fig 2).
  - *Tool overhead:* Search reduces citation hallucinations but adds latency; Python gave marginal gains, suggesting prioritization of search over symbolic computation for this problem class.
  - *Loop depth:* More iterations may correct more errors but risk infinite loops or degradation; preset limits are necessary.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Hallucinated citations (even with search, model misrepresents valid references).
  - Interpretation gaming (solving a trivial variant, not the intended problem).
  - Verifier failure (misses flaws in Generator output).
  - Tool misuse (incorrect parsing of search/Python results).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run Aletheia on a known IMO-ProofBench problem with tools disabled. Verify it matches reported accuracy (e.g., 93% on Advanced subset).
  2. **Ablation of verification:** Replace the Verifier with a single-pass Generator at equal total compute. Expect lower accuracy (Table 4 shows Deep Think alone solved 8/13 Erdős problems that Aletheia solved, using ~2x compute).
  3. **Tool impact test:** Enable/disable search on a subset of Erdős problems. Measure change in citation hallucination rate and overall correctness. Predict: search reduces obvious hallucinations but may not significantly improve core reasoning accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can AI hallucination in mathematical research be systematically eliminated?**
  - *Basis:* Hallucinations include superficial understanding, spurious citations, and fabricated results. The Verifier helps but doesn't solve the core issue.
  - *Why unresolved:* Creative exploration conflicts with factual rigor; tool integration (e.g., Python) showed only marginal improvements.
  - *Resolving evidence:* A benchmark demonstrating <1% factual error rate across diverse research-level problems with expert verification.
- **What objective taxonomy can the mathematical community adopt to consistently classify AI contribution levels and mathematical significance?**
  - *Basis:* Section 6 proposes a two-axis taxonomy but defers specifics to the community, citing an "evaluation gap" enabling hyperbolic claims.
  - *Why unresolved:* Current categories are too coarse; significance assessment remains subjective, leading to inconsistent evaluations and overclaiming.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Adoption by major mathematics journals as a required reporting standard, validated through retrospective classification of known AI-assisted results.
- **Which specific cognitive or methodological capabilities are missing from current agents that prevent consistent PhD-level performance, despite inference-time scaling?**
  - *Basis:* Inference-time scaling alone is insufficient; Aletheia's success rates are modest (6.5% correct on Erdős, 6/10 on FirstProof). Solutions often rely on "clever technical manipulations" rather than "genuine creativity."
  - *Why unresolved:* Missing capabilities are described qualitatively but not precisely diagnosed; ablation studies haven't isolated critical bottlenecks.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Comprehensive error analysis on unsolved problems isolating specific deficient subskills (e.g., long-horizon planning, conceptual abstraction, literature synthesis) across domains.

## Limitations
- Proprietary base model (Gemini Deep Think) details undisclosed, blocking direct replication and precise scaling comparison.
- Operational definition of "inference-time scaling" (e.g., scale=27/28) not publicly specified.
- Full system prompts and fine-tuning dataset/protocol for tool use are absent; GitHub repo access may be restricted.
- Substitutions with other models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude) will likely underperform due to model-specific tuning.

## Confidence
- **Decoupled generate-verify-revise loop:** Medium – Internal experiments show benefit, but reliance on same base model risks shared blind spots; external validation limited.
- **Inference-time scaling law for research math:** Medium – Correlation shown in plots, but generalizability outside Google's infrastructure and model family untested.
- **Tool integration necessity:** Medium – Paper asserts search is critical and Python marginal, but this may reflect specific problem distribution or model's pre-existing code ability.
