# HLER: Human-in-the-Loop Economic Research via Multi-Agent Pipelines for Empirical Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07444
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07444
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Dataset-aware hypothesis generation increased feasible research questions to 87% (vs 41% unconstrained), 86% of pipelines completed end-to-end, and automated reviewer scores improved from 4.8 to 6.3 across revisions at an average cost of $0.8–$1.5 per run.

## Executive Summary
Empirical economics research automation faces two key hurdles: the generation of infeasible hypotheses due to unconstrained LLM ideation, and the need for human judgment to assess identification strategies and scientific significance. HLER addresses these with a multi-agent pipeline that conditions hypothesis generation on dataset metadata and diagnostics, and employs a two-loop architecture: a human-screened question quality loop to select tractable and promising questions, and an automated revision loop that iteratively refines manuscripts based on critique. Evaluations across three datasets show high feasibility and completion rates, with substantive improvements in manuscript quality through revision cycles, all at low per-run cost.

## Method Summary
HLER orchestrates seven specialized agents. The DataAuditAgent validates dataset schema; the DataProfilingAgent computes variable distributions, missingness, and other diagnostics to produce a structured DataProfile. The QuestionAgent generates candidate hypotheses conditioned on this profile, which are then screened for basic feasibility. A human researcher selects the most promising hypothesis, applying domain expertise on significance and plausibility. The chosen hypothesis is executed via DataAgents (data retrieval), EconometricsAgent (model estimation using OLS, fixed effects, DiD), and PaperAgent (manuscript drafting). The ReviewerAgent then evaluates the draft, issuing requests for robustness checks or identification discussion. This triggers cycles of re-analysis (EconometricsAgent) and revision (PaperAgent) until convergence, after which a human publication approval gate decides on final output.

## Key Results
- Feasible research questions: 87% (dataset-aware) vs. 41% (unconstrained).
- End-to-end pipeline completion rate: 86%.
- Reviewer scores improved from 4.8 to 6.3 across revision iterations; average cost $0.8–$1.5 per run.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Dataset-aware constraint application
- **Claim:** Conditioning hypothesis generation on dataset metadata and diagnostics drastically reduces infeasible questions.
- **Mechanism:** DataAuditAgent and DataProfilingAgent create a structured inventory of variables, missingness, and distributions. The QuestionAgent uses this profile to avoid hypotheses requiring absent variables or incompatible designs.
- **Evidence:** Feasibility rose from 41% to 87%; main failure modes in unconstrained case were missing variables (42%) and design incompatibility (35%) [Section 4.2].
- **Break condition:** Incomplete or erroneous dataset metadata could lead to residual infeasibility.

### Human-screened question quality loop
- **Claim:** Human selection of hypotheses ensures pursuit of questions with economic significance and identification plausibility beyond mere feasibility.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple feasible hypotheses are generated; a human researcher selects one, applying tacit knowledge about novelty and identification that automated checks miss.
- **Evidence:** Pipeline places humans at question selection and publication approval gates [Section 3.4]; related work (IRIS) highlights interactive ideation value.
- **Break condition:** Human biases or time constraints may filter out unconventional but valid hypotheses.

### Automated research revision loop
- **Claim:** Iterative critique, re-analysis, and revision materially improve manuscript quality, especially clarity and identification discussion.
- **Mechanism:** ReviewerAgent evaluates drafts and requests robustness checks or identification elaboration; EconometricsAgent executes analyses; PaperAgent revises; cycle repeats.
- **Evidence:** Mean reviewer scores: v1=4.8 → v2=5.9 → final=6.3; largest gains in clarity (+2.1) and identification (+1.4); frequent requests: robustness checks (10/12), identification discussion (8/12) [Section 4.4].
- **Break condition:** ReviewerAgent feedback may be superficial or request unsupported analyses, causing stagnation or suboptimal convergence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dataset-aware constraint application**  
  - **Why needed:** Prevents LLMs from generating hypotheses that reference non-existent variables or designs incompatible with data structure, a primary failure mode.  
  - **Quick check:** Given a variable with 40% missingness, how would you rephrase a hypothesis to remain feasible?

- **Human-in-the-loop decision gates**  
  - **Why needed:** Economic research requires judgment on significance, novelty, and identification plausibility that automated systems lack; human gates ensure scientific responsibility.  
  - **Quick check:** What are two specific decisions in the HLER pipeline where human judgment is irreplaceable, and why?

- **Iterative critique-driven revision**  
  - **Why needed:** LLM-first drafts often weak on identification and clarity; structured feedback loops target these weaknesses programmatically.  
  - **Quick check:** If revision improves clarity but not novelty, what does that suggest about the role of the ReviewerAgent vs. initial hypothesis selection?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  DataAuditAgent → DataProfilingAgent → QuestionAgent → [Screening] → Human selection → DataAgents → EconometricsAgent → PaperAgent → ReviewerAgent → [if revisions: EconometricsAgent → PaperAgent → ReviewerAgent...] → Human publication gate.  
  Orchestrator manages shared RunState and sequential execution.

- **Critical path:**  
  As above; human gates at question selection and final approval; revision loop may iterate 2–4 times.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Cost vs. completeness: More iterations improve scores but increase API costs and runtime.  
  - Automation vs. oversight: More human gates raise reliability but lower scalability; HLER balances with two human gates.  
  - Generality vs. domain specificity: Agent roles generic but econometric methods limited (OLS, FE, DiD); adding IV/RD would expand applicability.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - EconometricsAgent convergence failure (e.g., sparse fixed-effects).  
  - ReviewerAgent requests unsupported analyses (e.g., IV when no instrument exists).  
  - Human rejects all generated questions, indicating constraint mismatch.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Compare dataset-aware vs. unconstrained hypothesis generation on a holdout dataset; measure feasibility rate and document failure modes.  
  2. Execute full pipeline with 2–3 revision iterations; track ReviewerAgent score deltas per dimension and categorize feedback types.  
  3. Vary number of initial candidate questions (e.g., 3 vs. 8) and measure human selection time and final paper quality to find optimal candidate set size.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extension to advanced econometric designs:** Current EconometricsAgent supports only basic models (OLS, FE, DiD). How can HLER incorporate IV, RDD, structural models? (Limitation: restricted toolkit; resolution: implement and evaluate new agent modules.)
- **Independent quality validation:** ReviewerAgent scores may be circular. Do human economists or a different LLM rate HLER manuscripts consistently? (Unresolved: risk of bias without external validation; resolution: blind review study comparing internal vs. external scores.)
- **Impact of human gates:** Could automated alternatives suffice? No ablation study measures marginal contribution of question selection and publication approval gates. (Unresolved: necessity of human oversight untested; resolution: A/B test replacing human gates with automated selection.)
- **Revision loop optimization:** Which components of the critique-re-analysis-revise cycle drive score gains? Efficacy per step unknown. (Unresolved: no decomposition of loop ingredients; resolution: ablation studies disabling selective steps.)

## Limitations
- Prompt templates, hyperparameters, and termination conditions unreported, hindering exact replication.
- Human selection criteria and biases unmeasured; variability in PI judgment may affect hypothesis steering.
- ReviewerAgent circularity: same LLM generates and evaluates manuscripts, risking inflated self-assessments.
- Dataset profiles may miss qualitative constraints (e.g., causal interpretations), limiting feasibility gains.
- Econometric scope limited to OLS/FE/DiD; IV/RD unsupported, reducing applicability.

## Confidence
- Dataset-aware constraint: High (strong ablation evidence, but contingent on profile completeness).
- Human-screened question quality: Medium (theoretically necessary but impact not isolated; human variability unquantified).
- Automated revision loop: Medium (internal score improvements documented, but external validation lacking).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a blind review study: compare ReviewerAgent feedback with human economist critiques on identical drafts to measure alignment and identify systematic gaps.
2. Perform A/B testing: runs with human selection vs. automated surrogate (e.g., highest feasibility score) to quantify human contribution to output quality.
3. Replicate the full pipeline on additional diverse economic datasets (e.g., PSID, LIS) to test generalizability of feasibility and completion claims.
