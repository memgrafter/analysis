# MUNIChus: Multilingual News Image Captioning Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10613  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10613  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Task-specific fine-tuning outperforms prompting by large margins; low-resource languages (e.g., Sinhala) remain challenging despite fine-tuning.  

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MUNIChus, the first multilingual benchmark for news image captioning, addressing a critical gap in multimodal evaluation. The dataset spans nine languages—including low-resource Sinhala and Urdu—with over 700,000 image-caption-article triplets. The authors evaluate more than 20 state-of-the-art multimodal models using both prompt-based generation and instruction fine-tuning. Key findings reveal that fine-tuning (e.g., Llama-3.2-11B, Aya-vision-8b) dramatically surpasses all prompting strategies, while traditional image captioning models fail entirely. Performance on low-resource languages, particularly Sinhala, lags significantly even after fine-tuning, highlighting persistent pre-training data biases. The work establishes that news image captioning remains a challenging, domain-specific task across linguistic contexts.  

## Method Summary
MUNIChus is constructed by collecting news articles containing images and associated captions across nine languages, followed by rigorous filtering to ensure high-quality alignment between visual content, article text, and captions. The dataset is split into training, validation, and test sets. Models are evaluated under two paradigms: (1) **prompting**, where frozen large multimodal models (e.g., GPT-4o) generate captions given instructions and few-shot examples, and (2) **instruction fine-tuning**, where base models are trained on the MUNIChus training set with task-specific prompts. Performance is measured using BLEU-4 and CIDEr, focusing on caption quality relative to reference captions. Ablations compare prompting strategies (random vs. retrieval-augmented few-shot) and model architectures (traditional vs. modern MLLMs).  

## Key Results
- **Fine-tuning dominates prompting:** Fine-tuned models achieve large gains—Llama-3.2-11B (BLEU-4 8.40, CIDEr 44.77), Aya-vision-8b (CIDEr 56.34)—while the best prompting (GPT-4o random few-shot) reaches only BLEU-4 3.57, CIDEr 36.17.  
- **Traditional models fail:** BLIP+NLLB and Paligemma yield BLEU-4 below 0.7, indicating they cannot handle the multimodal-news domain.  
- **Low-resource languages lag:** Sinhala consistently scores below CIDEr 12 under prompting and only reaches ~10–11.5 after fine-tuning, while other languages improve substantially.  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Fine-tuning adapts models to the specific demands of news image captioning: integrating visual cues with structured article context, adhering to journalistic style, and generating entity-rich captions. In contrast, prompting fails because pretrained MLLMs lack inherent capability to process the multimodal-news domain without task-specific optimization; their pretraining objectives do not emphasize article-grounded captioning. The mechanism behind fine-tuning’s success is likely the model’s exposure to domain-specific patterns during training, such as entity retention and event-context linking, which are absent in general-purpose pretraining.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Image -> Visual Encoder -> Visual Features -> Cross-Modal Fusion <- Text Encoder <- Article Text -> Caption Decoder -> Generated Caption  

**Critical path:**  
1. Image processed by visual encoder (e.g., ViT) to extract spatial features.  
2. Article text tokenized and encoded by text encoder (e.g., transformer).  
3. Visual and textual features fused via cross-attention or concatenation.  
4. Fused representation fed into autoregressive caption decoder.  
5. Caption tokens generated conditioned on both modalities.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Model size vs. efficiency:** Smaller models fine-tune more effectively on MUNIChus, while larger models underperform in prompting due to misalignment with news domain.  
- **Prompting vs. fine-tuning:** Prompting offers flexibility but fails to capture domain nuances; fine-tuning requires data/compute but yields specialized models.  
- **Multilingual tokenization:** Shared vocabularies disadvantage low-resource languages; dedicated tokens may help but increase complexity.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Extremely low CIDEr/BLEU on Sinhala/Urdu indicates pretraining data scarcity.  
- Poor performance of traditional models (BLIP+NLLB) shows inadequate fusion for article-grounded captioning.  
- Retrieval-augmented prompting fails when retrieved examples lack article context.  

**First experiments:**  
1. **Prompt optimization:** Systematically vary prompt templates (e.g., chain-of-thought, entity-focused instructions) for GPT-4o and Llama to quantify sensitivity.  
2. **Human evaluation:** Recruit annotators to rate captions on factual consistency with the article, visual grounding, and fluency across high- and low-resource languages.  
3. **Training data forensics:** Analyze pretraining corpora of Aya-vision and Llama to quantify news-domain text and Sinhala/Urdu content, correlating with performance drops.  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What specific factors cause Sinhala to exhibit drastically lower performance than other languages in multilingual news image captioning, even after task-specific fine-tuning?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly identify Sinhala as the worst-performing language across all models and conditions (F3), noting its CIDEr scores remain below 12 under prompting and only reach ~10–11.5 after fine-tuning, while other languages improve dramatically. They attribute this to "severe underrepresentation of Sinhala in the pre-training data."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze the composition of the pre-training corpora of the evaluated MLLMs to confirm the extent or nature of Sinhala's absence, particularly in news-related contexts. It also does not test whether targeted data augmentation or specialized tokenization could alleviate the gap.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A detailed analysis of the pre-training datasets of top-performing models (e.g., Aya-vision, Llama) to quantify the volume and domain (news vs. general) of Sinhala text. Additionally, controlled experiments where models are further pre-trained on a dedicated Sinhala news multimodal corpus before fine-tuning on MUNIChus would isolate whether data scarcity is the primary bottleneck.  

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does retrieval-augmented similar few-shot prompting fail to provide significant gains over random few-shot for news image captioning, despite retrieving visually similar examples?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors report (F5) that "few-shot prompting does not confer substantial benefits" and show in Figure 4 that retrieved training images, while visually similar, lack relevant contextual news information for caption generation. They hypothesize the retrieved examples provide "limited contextual information."  
- **Why unresolved:** The retrieval mechanism relied solely on visual similarity (via an image embedding model) without considering textual article content or semantic relevance to the test article's topic. The paper does not experiment with hybrid retrieval strategies that incorporate article text or entity overlap.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing caption quality when few-shot examples are retrieved using: (a) visual similarity only (as in the paper), (b) textual similarity of the news articles, (c) a multimodal combination of both. Human evaluation of whether retrieved examples provide useful journalistic context would also clarify the failure mode.  

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What architectural or training factors enable smaller models like Llama-3.2-11B to achieve comparable or superior news captioning performance to larger models after instruction fine-tuning, despite being weaker in zero/few-shot settings?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Finding F4 states that "model size does not guarantee superior performance," highlighting that Llama-3.2-11B becomes "highly competitive after fine-tuning" while the larger Aya-vision-32b underperforms its 8b variant in many prompting scenarios.  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not compare intrinsic model properties such as tokenizer efficiency for multilingual text, pre-training objectives (e.g., captioning vs. general instruction tuning), or parameter update dynamics during fine-tuning that might favor smaller models in this domain-specific task.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comparative analysis of the base models' pre-training data mixes (proportion of news-like text, multilingual coverage) and tokenization behavior on news article content. Measuring gradient norms or adaptation speed during the first fine-tuning steps could reveal if smaller models learn task-specific patterns more efficiently.  

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Are standard automatic evaluation metrics (BLEU-4, CIDEr) sufficient to measure progress in multilingual news image captioning, particularly for low-resource languages and entity faithfulness?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors restricted evaluation to BLEU-4 and CIDEr because "more advanced metrics such as BERTScore and BLEURT lack support for Sinhala and Urdu." They also explicitly forgo an entity-retrieval metric due to "limited availability and suboptimal performance of NER systems" for low-resource languages.  
- **Why unresolved:** By avoiding entity-focused metrics, the paper cannot assess whether models generate captions with accurate, specific named entities—a core requirement for news captioning. This leaves open whether metric improvements reflect genuine gains in factual accuracy or just fluency.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop and release robust NER tools for Sinhala and Urdu in the news domain, then compute entity precision/recall for model outputs. Correlate these with human judgments of factual correctness to validate if existing metrics are proxies for entity accuracy or if new metrics are needed.  

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How can multimodal architectures be better designed to leverage the structured context of news articles (e.g., entities, events, discourse) rather than treating them as unstructured text?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The persistent low performance of all models (even after fine-tuning) and the failure of prompting suggest that current MLLMs do not effectively integrate the rich, entity-centric context of news articles with visual information. The paper calls for "specialised architectures" but does not propose or test specific designs for news-specific fusion.  
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments use standard MLLM architectures that simply concatenate article text with image tokens. No ablation explores separate encoders for article sections (headline vs. body), explicit entity grounding, or hierarchical modeling of article structure.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and evaluate news-specific architectural modifications on MUNIChus, such as: (a) separate text encoders for headline and body with cross-attention to image features, (b) pre-training objectives that force entity-entity and entity-visual alignment, (c) incorporating article metadata (e.g., topic, date) as conditioning. Comparing these against the baselines would indicate what design choices yield meaningful gains.  

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies solely on BLEU-4 and CIDEr; human assessment of factual accuracy, bias, and news context fidelity is absent.  
- Prompt engineering for large models was not systematically optimized, possibly understating prompting potential.  
- Linguistic diversity is constrained by news domain availability, leading to noisier alignments for some languages.  

## Confidence
- **High:** Existence and scale of the MUNIChus dataset (700k+ triplets, 9 languages) as a new resource.  
- **Medium:** Relative performance ranking of models (fine-tuning > prompting; poor performance of traditional models) based on provided metrics.  
- **High:** Severe underperformance on low-resource languages (e.g., Sinhala) persists post-fine-tuning, indicating pre-training data bias.
