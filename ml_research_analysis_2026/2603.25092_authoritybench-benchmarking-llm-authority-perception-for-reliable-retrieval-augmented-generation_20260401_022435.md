# AuthorityBench: Benchmarking LLM Authority Perception for Reliable Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25092
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25092
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: LLMs can perceive source authority beyond textual semantics, and using ListJudge-PointScore to filter retrieved documents boosts RAG accuracy by up to 16.67%.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces AuthorityBench, a benchmark to evaluate whether large language models (LLMs) can perceive information authority—an intrinsic property of a source—distinct from semantic content. AuthorityBench comprises three subtasks: DomainAuth (10K web domains labeled by PageRank), EntityAuth (22K entities labeled by Wikipedia popularity), and RAGAuth (120 queries with retrieved documents for downstream RAG evaluation). Five LLMs are tested under three judgment paradigms (PointJudge, PairJudge, ListJudge) with and without webpage text. Results show that ListJudge and PairJudge using PointScore achieve the highest correlations with ground-truth authority (Spearman’s ρ up to 0.8594, Kendall’s τ up to 0.7762), while ListJudge is the most cost-effective. Notably, including webpage text degrades performance, indicating that authority is not encoded in textual style. In RAGAuth, filtering retrieved documents with ListJudge-PointScore significantly improves answer accuracy compared to relevance or utility baselines, demonstrating the practical utility of authority perception for reliable knowledge retrieval.

## Method Summary
The core of AuthorityBench consists of two evaluation tracks. The first track assesses authority perception by having an LLM judge evaluate items from DomainAuth or EntityAuth. The judge can either assign an absolute score (PointJudge), compare two items (PairJudge), or rank a list (ListJudge). Outputs may be direct rankings (ListRank/PairRank) or per-item scores (PointScore). The judgments are compared to continuous authority labels (PageRank 0–9, sitelink counts) using rank correlation metrics (Spearman’s ρ, Kendall’s τ). The second track evaluates RAG performance on RAGAuth: for each question, ten documents are retrieved (with URLs and domain PageRank). An authority filter, implemented via ListJudge-PointScore applied to the URLs, selects the top-k documents, which are then fed to the generator LLM to produce an answer. Answer accuracy (yes/no) is measured against the ground truth. Baselines include no filter, a relevance filter, and a utility filter. All experiments test five LLMs (Qwen3-8B/14B/32B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) with configurations that include or exclude the webpage text snippets.

## Key Results
- ListJudge and PairJudge with PointScore achieve the strongest rank correlations with ground-truth authority (Spearman’s ρ up to 0.8594, Kendall’s τ up to 0.7762).
- Incorporating webpage text consistently degrades judgment performance, indicating that authority perception is distinct from textual style.
- Authority-guided filtering using ListJudge-PointScore significantly boosts RAG answer accuracy (e.g., +16.67% for Qwen3-8B at k=1) compared to relevance or utility baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LLMs can infer authority from meta-information such as domain names (PageRank) or entity popularity (Wikipedia sitelinks) when prompted appropriately. PointScore yields fine-grained numeric ratings that align well with continuous authority labels, while ListJudge and PairJudge capture relative ordering, which is robust to scaling biases. The degradation when adding webpage text suggests that authority cues are not present in the content itself but in external signals like source reputation. In the RAG pipeline, the authority filter leverages this ability to prioritize high-authority documents, which are more likely to contain correct information, thereby improving the generator’s chance of producing accurate answers.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
AuthorityBench datasets (DomainAuth, EntityAuth, RAGAuth) → LLM Judge (Point/Pair/List) → Scores/Ranks → Metrics (ρ, τ, accuracy); RAG path: Query → Retriever → Documents → Authority Filter (ListJudge-PointScore on URLs) → Top‑k → Generator → Answer.

**Critical path:**  
- Authority perception: dataset item → LLM judgment → aggregation → correlation with labels.  
- RAG: query → retrieval → authority scoring → selection → generation → answer evaluation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Scoring vs. ranking:* PointScore is cheap and easily aggregated; ListRank directly orders but may be sensitive to list length.  
- *Text inclusion:* Adding webpage text increases context but introduces noise and length, hurting authority discrimination.  
- *Cost vs. accuracy:* ListJudge-PointScore offers a strong balance, achieving near-best correlation with lower complexity than BubbleSort aggregation for PairJudge.

**Failure signatures:**  
- JSON formatting errors from the LLM (extra text, missing braces) causing parsing failures.  
- Poor correlations when prompts are ambiguous or the model lacks authority heuristics.  
- No RAG improvement if authority filter misranks documents or if the selected top‑k still contain incorrect information.

**First experiments (exactly three):**
1. Run PointJudge on a 100‑sample subset of DomainAuth (without text) to verify baseline score correlation.
2. Compare ListJudge-PointScore and PairJudge-PointScore on a 10‑item list from EntityAuth; measure Kendall’s τ.
3. Apply authority filtering (ListJudge-PointScore) to RAGAuth with k=1; compute answer accuracy and compare to no‑filter baseline.

## Limitations
- Prompt and parsing details are only partially specified; minor variations (whitespace, JSON schema) could affect reproducibility.
- The sampling strategy for constructing 10‑item lists and the anchor‑based mapping to fine‑grained labels is underspecified, hindering exact replication of evaluation sets.
- Authority proxies (PageRank, Wikipedia sitelinks) are narrow and may not capture broader dimensions of trust or expertise; results are limited to the tested LLM families (Qwen, Llama).

## Confidence
- Authority perception (high ρ/τ): Medium  
- ListJudge cost‑effectiveness: Medium  
- Webpage text harms performance: Low  
- Authority filter boosts RAG: Medium
