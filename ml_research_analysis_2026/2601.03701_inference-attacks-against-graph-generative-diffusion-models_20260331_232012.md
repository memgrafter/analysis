# Inference Attacks Against Graph Generative Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03701  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03701  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Three black‑box inference attacks (graph reconstruction, property inference, membership inference) against GGDMs achieve high success (up to 0.99 F1, 0.999 AUC) and two new defenses provide better privacy‑utility trade‑offs than DP or random flips.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates privacy risks in graph generative diffusion models (GGDMs). It introduces three black‑box inference attacks: Graph Reconstruction Attack (GRA) that aligns multiple synthetic graphs to recover training graph structures; Property Inference Attack (PIA) that estimates global graph statistics directly from generated outputs; and Membership Inference Attacks (MIA) using shadow models and graph embeddings to determine if a specific graph was in the training set. Evaluations on three GGDMs across six real‑world datasets demonstrate attack effectiveness, with GRA reaching 0.99 F1, PIA achieving near‑exact property estimates, and MIA attaining up to 0.999 AUC. Additionally, the paper proposes defense mechanisms that perturb the least salient edges in training or generated graphs, offering stronger privacy‑utility trade‑offs than baselines like differential privacy.

## Method Summary
The authors design three black‑box attacks targeting GGDMs. GRA queries the target model to obtain many synthetic graphs, uses REGAL to produce permutation‑invariant node embeddings, aligns graphs by computing representation differences, selects the most similar pairs, and intersects their edge sets to reconstruct training graphs. PIA simply aggregates statistics (e.g., average degree, density) over a large set of generated graphs, assuming the generation distribution matches the training distribution. MIA employs shadow models trained on auxiliary data to generate labeled examples of member and non‑member graphs; features are derived from pairwise similarities using Anonymous Walk Embeddings (AWE) and a classifier is trained to predict membership. Two defense strategies are introduced: they compute saliency maps of edges/non‑edges via gradient back‑propagation and perturb the least salient ones either during training or on generated graphs.

## Key Results
- Graph reconstruction (GRA) achieves up to 0.99 F1 score and exactly recovers 36% of training graphs.  
- Property inference (PIA) estimates average degree with differences as low as 0.005 and density with differences as low as 0.001.  
- Membership inference (MIA) reaches AUC up to 0.999 in same‑dataset setting and 0.895 under dataset transfer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The graph reconstruction attack can recover training graph structures with high fidelity, reporting up to 0.99 F1 score and exact recovery of 36% of training graphs.
- **Mechanism:**  
  1. Query the target GGDM Φ to obtain many synthetic graphs Ĝ.  
  2. For each pair of graphs in Ĝ, apply REGAL representation learning to align nodes and compute the average node‑representation difference (Equation 5).  
  3. Identify the most similar counterpart for each graph (Equation 6).  
  4. Select the top‑k% (default 10%) pairs with smallest differences.  
  5. For each selected pair, intersect their edge sets to produce a reconstructed graph (Equation 7).  
  6. Return the set of reconstructed graphs.
- **Core assumption:** A memorizing GGDM will emit multiple synthetic graphs that are structurally similar to a given training sample; therefore, overlapping edges between distinct generations likely belong to the original training graph. Intersection amplifies these common edges while discarding spurious ones.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “First, we design a graph reconstruction attack, which can reconstruct graphs structurally similar to those training graphs from the generated graphs… achieving up to 0.99 F1 score and exact recovery of 36% of training graphs.”  
  - [section] Section 5.1 details the three steps (graph generation, graph alignment using REGAL, edge inference via intersection) and provides Equation 7.  
  - [corpus] Neighboring work such as “Backdoor Attacks on Discrete Graph Diffusion Models” explores vulnerabilities of diffusion models but does not address reconstruction; the use of graph alignment to extract training instances is novel in this context.
- **Break condition:** The attack fails if the target model does not overfit (e.g., due to strong regularization or differential privacy), if the number of generated graphs is insufficient to reveal overlapping edges, or if REGAL cannot produce consistent node representations (e.g., highly permutation‑variant graphs).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The property inference attack accurately estimates global statistics (average degree, density, arboricity) of the training set from generated graphs, achieving absolute differences as low as 0.005 in degree and 0.001 in density.
- **Mechanism:**  
  1. Query Φ to collect a large set of synthetic graphs Ĝ.  
  2. For each graph in Ĝ, compute the desired property (e.g., density, degree, number of triangles, arboricity).  
  3. Aggregate (mean or histogram) across all generated graphs and report this as the inferred property of the training data (Equation 9).
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of generated graphs matches the training distribution; hence, sample statistics of Ĝ converge to the true statistics of Φₜᵣₐᵢₙ.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “Second, we propose a property inference attack to infer the properties of the training graphs, such as the average graph density and the distribution of densities, from the generated graphs.”  
  - [section] Section 5.2 explicitly formalizes the attack as P(Φₜᵣₐᵢₙ) ← P(Ĝ) and shows experimental results with tiny absolute differences in Table 4 and Figure 5.  
  - [corpus] The related paper “Generated Distributions Are All You Need for Membership Inference Attacks Against Generative Models” studies membership leakage; the direct statistic‑based property inference for graphs is a distinct contribution.
- **Break condition:** The attack becomes ineffective if the generative model distorts key properties (e.g., via aggressive post‑processing, clipping, or limited sample size), causing the sample mean to deviate significantly from the true training mean.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Two black‑box membership inference attacks determine whether a given graph belongs to the training set, achieving AUC up to 0.999 in the same‑dataset setting and 0.895 under dataset transfer.
- **Mechanism:**  
  1. Train a shadow GGDM ΦΣ on a collection of shadow graphs Gˢ that mimics the target’s training conditions; split Gˢ into known member and non‑member subsets.  
  2. For each shadow graph, generate a set of synthetic graphs Ĝˢ.  
  3. Construct two attack feature types:  
     - **Atrain‑1:** pairwise similarity (dot, cosine, Euclidean, Jensen‑Shannon) between each synthetic graph and the input shadow graph, using Anonymous Walk Embeddings (AWE).  
     - **Atrain‑2:** pairwise similarity among all synthetic graphs within the same set (also via AWE). See Equations 11‑12.  
  4. Train a binary classifier (e.g., MLP) on the features with known membership labels.  
  5. For a target graph G, obtain its synthetic set from Φ, compute identical features, and run the classifier to predict membership.
- **Core assumption:** Member graphs cause the target model to generate outputs that are either more similar to the input graph (Atrain‑1) or more internally similar (Atrain‑2) compared to non‑member graphs.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “Third, we develop two membership inference attacks to determine whether a given graph is present in the training set… reaching AUC up to 0.999.”  
  - [section] Section 5.3 provides the full pipeline, including shadow‑model training, AWE‑based feature extraction, and the two feature definitions.  
  - [corpus] The work “Membership Inference on Text-to-Image Diffusion Models” demonstrates membership leakage in image diffusion; our adaptation to discrete graph structures and the AWE similarity framework are novel extensions.
- **Break condition:** The attack degrades when (a) the shadow model differs significantly from the target (different architecture, hyperparameters, or data distribution), (b) the target employs strong privacy‑preserving mechanisms (e.g., differential privacy), or (c) the similarity metrics fail to capture meaningful structural differences (e.g., very small or extremely noisy graphs).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Target GGDM (Φ): Black-box model that generates graphs.  
Attacker Module:  
  - Graph Generator – queries Φ to obtain Ĝ.  
  - Alignment Engine (for GRA) – computes REGAL node embeddings and differences.  
  - Property Calculator (for PIA) – aggregates statistics over Ĝ.  
  - Shadow Model (ΦΣ) – local copy mimicking Φ.  
  - Feature Extractor – builds Atrain‑1/Atrain‑2 using AWE and four similarity metrics.  
  - Attack Classifier – MLP/RF that predicts membership.  
Defense Module:  
  - Saliency Map Generator – back‑propagates GNN gradients to adjacency matrix.  
  - Perturbation Engine – flips least salient edges/non‑edges in training or generated graphs.

**Critical path**  
*Graph Reconstruction:*  
1. Generate Ĝ from Φ.  
2. For all pairs, run REGAL → node embeddings.  
3. Compute average representation differences (Eq 5) and find most similar counterpart per graph (Eq 6).  
4. Select top‑k% pairs with smallest differences.  
5. Intersect edge sets of each pair (Eq 7) to produce reconstructed graphs.

*Property Inference:*  
1. Generate Ĝ.  
2. Compute the property on each synthetic graph.  
3. Take the mean or histogram across Ĝ as the inferred value.

*Membership Inference:*  
1. Train shadow ΦΣ on shadow data split into members/non‑members.  
2. For each shadow graph, generate Ĝˢ.  
3. Using AWE, build Atrain‑1 (similarity to input) and Atrain‑2 (internal similarity) from the four metrics.  
4. Train binary classifier on (feature, membership label).  
5. For target graph G, generate its Ĝ, compute identical features, predict label.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *GRA:* Intersection reduces false positives but may miss edges present in only one generation; union increases recall but hurts precision. k% threshold balances precision‑recall. REGAL parameters (embedding dim, α) affect alignment quality vs runtime.  
- *PIA:* More generated samples N lowers variance but increases query cost. Property choice must be stable under generation (e.g., average degree is robust; higher‑order statistics may need larger N).  
- *MIA:* Atrain‑1 is sensitive to per‑graph similarity; Atrain‑2 captures distributional shift and often transfers better. Classifier type (MLP vs RF) trades capacity vs overfitting. Diversity of shadow dataset influences generalization to target.  
- *Defense:* Perturbation ratio r trades privacy (attack reduction) vs utility (downstream performance). Saliency map based on GCN gradients may be noisy if the model is poorly trained; alternative importance scores could improve the tradeoff.

**Failure signatures**  
- *GRA:* F1 < 0.2 or recall < 0.2 suggests alignment failure; examine pairwise similarity distribution – if all differences are similar, REGAL may not discriminate. Empty reconstructed graphs indicate threshold too low.  
- *PIA:* Absolute difference > 10% of true value signals distribution shift; compare property histograms of Ĝ vs expected.  
- *MIA:* AUC ≈ 0.55 indicates indistinguishable features; plot Atrain‑1/Atrain‑2 distributions for members vs non‑members – strong overlap usually means the shadow model does not mimic the target or the target applies heavy privatization.  
- *Defense:* Attack AUC remains > 0.8 even at r = 0.9 → saliency estimation may be inaccurate; consider increasing perturbation budget or using alternative importance metrics (e.g., gradient‑free perturbation).

**First 3 experiments**  
1. *Reconstructing graphs:* Run GRA on MUTAG with EDP‑GNN, query 1,000 synthetic graphs. Report precision, recall, F1, and exact‑recovery ratio. Expected F1 ≥ 0.75, exact‑recovery > 10%.  
2. *Property inference sanity check:* Use GDSS on IMDB‑M, generate 1,000 graphs, compute average degree and density. Compare to training‑set values; expect absolute difference < 0.2 for degree and < 0.01 for density.  
3. *Membership inference proof‑of‑concept:* On ENZYMES with Digress, split data into two halves; train shadow on one half, generate 100 samples per shadow graph, build Atrain‑2, train MLP. Evaluate on held‑out test graphs; target AUC ≥ 0.85.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is confined to 6 relatively small datasets and 3 GGDM architectures; generalizability to larger or more complex graphs and other diffusion models remains uncertain.  
- Graph reconstruction relies on REGAL alignment, which may fail on graphs with weak structural signals or high permutation variance.  
- Defense evaluations use only simple baselines (differential privacy, random flips) and lack downstream task utility metrics, limiting assessment of practical privacy‑utility trade‑offs.

## Confidence
- **Attack effectiveness (GRA/PIA/MIA):** Medium  
- **Defense superiority (vs. DP/random flips):** Low‑Medium

## Next Checks

2. Query budget sensitivity: Measure attack performance under varying numbers of queries (e.g., 10, 100, 1000) to determine feasible lower bounds and cost‑effectiveness.
