# Adaptive Retrieval for Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04618
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04618
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: REPAIR achieves nDCG@10 of 24.7 on BRIGHT (5.6 points above REARANK) and Exact Match of 32.2 on HotpotQA, demonstrating consistent improvements in reasoning-intensive retrieval.

## Executive Summary
Reasoning-intensive retrieval tasks often suffer from bounded recall, where critical bridge documents that connect query fragments are missed by conventional retrievers. REPAIR tackles this by integrating reasoning-based reranking with adaptive retrieval. The framework repurposes explicit reasoning plans—generated by a Planning-based Step Reranker—as dense feedback signals to selectively expand the candidate set. This adaptive process targets high-reward reasoning steps, improving the chances of retrieving essential bridge documents. Evaluated on BRIGHT and HotpotQA, REPAIR outperforms the best prior method (REARANK) by 5.6 nDCG points on BRIGHT and 1.8 Exact Match points on HotpotQA, confirming the approach’s effectiveness for complex queries.

## Method Summary
REPAIR comprises two core components. The Planning-based Step Reranker consumes an initial candidate pool and produces a sequence of explicit reasoning steps (e.g., sub-questions), each scored for its anticipated utility. These steps and scores are then fed into the Step-Adaptive Neural Retriever (NAR). The NAR treats step scores as dense feedback to identify a subset of high-reward steps. For the selected steps, the system performs adaptive query expansion—issuing supplementary retrieval calls to fetch additional documents that likely contain missing bridge information. The expanded corpus is subsequently reranked, yielding improved recall for multi-hop reasoning. This design avoids extra supervision by reusing the reasoning plan itself as a guidance signal, while focusing computational effort on promising reasoning paths.

## Key Results
- On BRIGHT, REPAIR reaches nDCG@10 = 24.7, a 5.6-point gain over REARANK (19.1).
- On HotpotQA, REPAIR obtains Exact Match = 32.2 versus 30.2 for REARANK.
- Improvements are consistent across both benchmarks, demonstrating the approach’s effectiveness for reasoning-intensive retrieval.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
REPAIR mitigates bounded recall by actively seeking documents pertinent to intermediate reasoning steps that initial retrieval may have overlooked. The reasoning steps serve as targeted queries that expose the system to bridge entities and supporting facts. The adaptive selection mechanism ensures that only steps with high estimated reward—derived from the reranker’s scores—trigger expansion, conserving compute while increasing the likelihood of retrieving missing information. By filtering out low-value steps, the system reduces noise in the candidate pool. The empirical superiority over baselines validates that reasoning plans provide a powerful, self-supervised signal for directing adaptive retrieval in complex, multi-hop settings.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**  
Initial Retriever → Candidate Pool → Planning-Based Step Reranker → Step Reward Calculator → Step-Adaptive NAR → Adaptive Query Expansion → Expanded Candidate Set

**Critical Path:**  
The generation of reasoning steps followed by reward‑based selection and targeted expansion is the most critical sequence; any degradation in step quality or reward estimation directly curtails final recall.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
The system trades off the computational overhead of generating and scoring steps against recall gains; excessive expansion may introduce noise, while overly selective expansion risks missing bridges. Additionally, the reliance on the step reranker introduces a single point of failure.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Sharp drop in nDCG on queries requiring multiple hops.  
- High latency due to uncontrolled expansion of candidate sets.  
- Degenerate reward distributions where all steps receive similar scores, leading to indiscriminate expansion.

**First Experiments:**  
1. Vary the number of high‑reward steps selected for expansion and measure impact on nDCG and latency.  
2. Replace the Planning-based Step Reranker with a random step generator to isolate its contribution.  
3. Test REPAIR with different initial retrievers (e.g., BM25 vs. dense retriever) to assess robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly identify any open questions or future research directions. However, the limitations highlighted above suggest several potential avenues for further investigation, such as evaluating on a broader set of reasoning-intensive benchmarks, analyzing the efficiency and scalability of adaptive retrieval, and conducting user studies to assess the interpretability and correctness of the generated reasoning steps.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to two datasets (BRIGHT, HotpotQA); generalizability unproven. This restriction means the approach might not transfer to other reasoning-intensive domains or query types.
- Efficiency costs of adaptive expansion and reranking are not measured. Without runtime or computational overhead analysis, the practical applicability of REPAIR in real-world settings remains uncertain.
- No human assessment of reasoning step interpretability or correctness. The lack of human evaluation makes it difficult to judge whether the generated steps are meaningful and reliable for guiding retrieval.

## Confidence
- Performance gain: Medium. Improvements on BRIGHT and HotpotQA are promising but lack statistical validation and are based on limited benchmarks.
- Interpretability of steps: Low. The paper does not include human evaluation of the reasoning steps, so their quality and utility remain uncertain.
- Adaptive retrieval effectiveness: Medium. The adaptive mechanism shows gains over baselines, but its contribution is not thoroughly isolated via ablations across varying conditions.

## Next Checks

2. Broaden evaluation: Test on additional reasoning‑intensive benchmarks (e.g., TEMPO, MRMR).
3. Human study: Evaluate reasoning step quality via expert ratings and correlate with retrieval outcomes.
