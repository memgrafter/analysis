# SubQuad: Near-Quadratic-Free Structure Inference with Distribution-Balanced Objectives in Adaptive Receptor framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17330  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17330  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Processes 1M sequences at 97.2k/sec with 1.4GB memory, achieves 92% cluster purity, equity score 0.91, reduces subgroup disparity to 12%, and maintains recall@100 of 0.96 while cutting storage by 58%.

## Executive Summary
SubQuad addresses the twin challenges of near-quadratic computational cost and severe dataset imbalance in large-scale immune repertoire comparison. It introduces an antigen-aware MinHash prefilter that dramatically reduces candidate comparisons, a differentiable gating controller that dynamically fuses multiple affinity signals, and a fairness-constrained spectral clustering objective that ensures proportional representation of rare antigen-specific subgroups. Evaluated on viral and tumor datasets, SubQuad delivers high throughput and memory efficiency alongside state-of-the-art clustering quality and equity.

## Method Summary
SubQuad’s pipeline integrates several novel components: an antigen-aware MinHash index shrinks the comparison space from quadratic to near-linear; a dual-phase meta-encoder (ImmunoBERT-style) produces contextual embeddings; a gating network computes per-pair dynamic weights to fuse alignment, embedding, and graph affinities; a graph is built with Random Matrix Theory-based thresholding; and a fairness-constrained optimization minimizes within-cluster variance while enforcing proportional coverage of antigen subgroups, with automated calibration of the fairness trade-off parameter.

## Key Results
- Throughput: 97.2k sequences/sec; memory: 1.4GB on 1M sequences  
- Cluster purity: 92%; equity score: 0.91; subgroup disparity: 12%  
- Recall@100: 0.96; storage reduction: 58% vs. dense baselines  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Antigen-aware MinHash prefiltering
- **Claim:** Reduces candidate comparisons from near-quadratic to near-linear while maintaining high recall for antigen-enriched pairs.  
- **Mechanism:** Sequences are hashed into MinHash sketches organized into antigen-centric blocks; candidate pairs are generated by intersecting sketches within the same block.  
- **Core assumption:** Antigen-specific sequences share k-mers and thus co-occur in the same block when blocking is guided by antigen information.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract mentions “antigen-aware, near-subquadratic retrieval” and Section 3.2 describes block-aligned storage reducing I/O.  
- **Break condition:** Highly cross-reactive antigens or noisy blocking keys could cause recall collapse.

### Mechanism 2: Differentiable gating controller for dynamic fusion
- **Claim:** Dynamically fuses alignment and embedding affinities per pair, capturing complementary similarity signals.  
- **Mechanism:** A small MLP scores the relevance of each affinity channel; softmax weights are applied to combine scores into a single affinity.  
- **Core assumption:** Optimal channel weighting varies per pair (e.g., edit distance for point mutations, embeddings for semantic similarity).  
- **Evidence:** Section 3.6 defines Equations (13)–(14); ablation in Table 2 shows purity drops when gating is disabled.  
- **Break condition:** If one channel consistently dominates, the gating may learn trivial weights, reducing to a single-channel system.

### Mechanism 3: Fairness-constrained spectral clustering with automated calibration
- **Claim:** Ensures proportional representation of rare antigen-specific subgroups, reducing disparity to ~12%.  
- **Mechanism:** Clustering loss combines within-cluster variance with a Jensen-Shannon divergence term penalizing deviation from ideal subgroup coverage; λ is tuned automatically to meet a target disparity.  
- **Core assumption:** Jensen-Shannon divergence between cluster-level and global subgroup proportions effectively enforces equity without collapsing clusters.  
- **Evidence:** Equation (15) defines objective; Table 4 shows JS disparity drops to 9–13%; Appendix D.2 provides a theoretical coverage guarantee.  
- **Break condition:** Noisy subgroup labels or pleiotropic antigen functions may cause mis-penalization and harm purity.

## Foundational Learning
- **MinHash and Locality-Sensitive Hashing:** Provides subquadratic candidate reduction; SubQuad adapts it with antigen-aware blocking to preserve antigenic affinity. Quick check: How does MinHash estimate Jaccard similarity, and why do fixed-size sketches bias recall for rare k-mers?  
- **Meta-learning and Learnable Fusion:** The differentiable gating controller (MetaNet) adapts fusion weights per pair; meta-training tunes the controller for downstream performance. Quick check: In Eq. (5), what does the sigmoid output α_m represent and how is it used in Eq. (9)?  
- **Spectral Clustering with Constraints:** Fairness-constrained objective modifies standard spectral clustering by adding a subgroup equity term, requiring solving a generalized eigenproblem with trade-off λ. Quick check: What is the role of the Laplacian matrix, and how does adding the fairness term change the optimization?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  

Preprocessing (trimming, MinHash sketches, antigen-aware indexing) → Embedding (ImmunoBERT encoder) → Affinity (multi-channel computation + gating fusion) → Graph (RMT thresholding) → Fair clustering (Eq.15 with automated λ tuning) → Output (clusters, UMAP, equity heatmaps)

- **Critical path:** Preprocessing must finish before affinity computation; the gating network runs on the critical path for each candidate pair; MinHash indexing is the first gatekeeper that determines the candidate set size.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - MinHash sketch size (k) vs. recall: larger k improves precision but increases index size and candidate set.  
  - Fairness λ vs. cluster purity: higher λ reduces disparity but may split cohesive clusters.  
  - GPU batch size vs. memory: larger batches improve throughput but risk OOM; Eq. (12) guides sizing.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Memory blowup despite MinHash: candidate set too large (k too small) or batch size misconfigured.  
  - Low cluster purity (<80%): gating undertrained or one channel dominating; check α_m distribution.  
  - High disparity (>20%): λ too low or subgroup labels inconsistent; inspect fairness heatmap.  
  - Recall@100 drop: MinHash blocking too aggressive (blocks too narrow); adjust granularity.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **MinHash parameter sweep:** Vary sketch size k ∈ {64,128,256} and similarity threshold τ; measure recall@10 vs. candidate set size.  
  2. **Ablation study:** Disable each component (gating, fairness, GPU kernel) and record throughput, memory, purity.  
  3. **Fairness calibration:** Run Algorithm 3 with different δ_max; plot disparity vs. purity trade-off to select λ.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **WCD vs. existing fairness metrics:** How does the proposed Weighted Coverage Divergence compare to JS, DP, EO in preserving rare clonotype coverage without harming clustering quality? The paper introduces WCD theoretically but does not implement or compare it empirically.  
2. **Clinical Adaptation Principle:** Does selecting a fairness metric by minimizing the gradient of clinical risk lead to better clinical outcomes than a fixed metric? The principle is formalized in Appendix B.8 but never evaluated.  
3. **Longitudinal extension:** How can SubQuad model longitudinal immune repertoire dynamics? The conclusion mentions future work; temporal coherence is not explored.  
4. **Unsupervised subgroup discovery:** Can SubQuad identify novel antigen-specific subgroups without prior labels? The current fairness constraint requires known antigenic subgroups, limiting use in exploratory analyses.

## Limitations
- Antigen-aware MinHash relies on k-mer overlap; cross-reactivity or noisy blocking may cause recall collapse.  
- The gating controller may learn trivial weights if one affinity channel dominates, losing multimodal benefits.  
- Fairness objective depends heavily on accurate subgroup labels; label noise or pleiotropy can harm purity.  
- Novelty claims (antigen-aware blocking, fairness spectral clustering) lack external validation in the literature.
