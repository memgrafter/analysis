# X-OPD: Cross-Modal On-Policy Distillation for Capability Alignment in Speech LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.24596  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24596  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** X-OPD reduces the average performance gap between speech and text LLMs on three audio benchmarks from 11.29% (speech) and 5.51% (text) to 3.43% and 0.97%, respectively, and mitigates catastrophic forgetting on MMAR to a 0.6–2.0% drop versus ~11% for baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
Speech large language models (Speech LLMs) consistently underperform text-only counterparts due to modality misalignment and limited paired speech-text training data. X-OPD introduces cross-modal on-policy distillation: the speech LLM student generates on-policy rollouts from both speech and text prompts; a strong text-only teacher evaluates these trajectories and provides token-level feedback via KL divergence, enabling fine-grained credit assignment. This approach avoids exposure bias and eliminates reliance on static ground-truth outputs. Experiments on Qwen3-Omni-A3B-Instruct show substantial closure of the modality gap on BIG Bench Audio, Audio Multi-Challenge, and VoiceBench, while drastically reducing catastrophic forgetting on the MMAR benchmark.

## Method Summary
Reproduction requires building a paired dataset of approximately 27k speech-text prompts. Text instructions from Tulu 3 and NaturalReasoning are rewritten into conversational style (originally with Gemini-3-Flash-Preview) and synthesized into speech using CosyVoice3, then filtered by SenseVoice ASR to keep utterances with word error rate <5%. The student model (Speech LLM, e.g., Qwen3-Omni-A3B-Instruct) has its audio encoder frozen and generates multiple rollouts per prompt using its current policy for both the text prompts (in-modal) and the synthesized speech prompts (cross-modal). A frozen teacher model (text-only LLM, e.g., Qwen3-A3B-Instruct) computes per-token log-probabilities. Two advantage functions are derived: in-modal advantage A_im compares teacher vs. student on text prompts; cross-modal advantage A_cm compares teacher (on text) vs. student (on speech). A policy gradient loss uses importance ratios r = π_θ(s)/π_old(s) to weight the advantages; the final loss is a weighted sum L = λ L_im + (1-λ) L_cm (λ=0.5 in the paper). Training runs for one epoch with Adam (lr=2e-6, batch=256), updating the student’s LM backbone while keeping the audio tower frozen.

## Key Results
- Reduced the average performance gap on BIG Bench Audio, Audio Multi-Challenge, and VoiceBench from 11.29% (speech) and 5.51% (text) to 3.43% and 0.97%, respectively.
- Mitigated catastrophic forgetting on the MMAR benchmark to a 0.6–2.0% drop, compared to ~11% for SFT, offline KD, and GKD.
- Achieved these improvements without any ground-truth outputs, relying solely on student rollouts and teacher token-level feedback.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: On‑Policy Rollouts Mitigate Exposure Bias
- **Claim:** Generating rollouts from the student’s current policy avoids exposure bias by training on self‑generated trajectories that reflect the student’s true error modes.
- **Mechanism:** For each prompt, the student produces *n* candidate trajectories (speech or text conditioned). The teacher scores these realistic outputs rather than ideal distributions, enabling a policy gradient update aligned with inference conditions [abstract].
- **Core assumption:** Self‑generated rollouts, even if flawed, yield a teachable signal when evaluated by a stronger teacher; the student can learn from its own mistakes via the teacher’s feedback.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “enables the Speech LLM to explore its own distribution via on‑policy rollouts… avoids exposure bias.” [section 3.2] notes that single‑sample rollouts are too stochastic, so multiple samples achieve broader coverage.
- **Break condition:** If the teacher’s scoring fails to provide a meaningful gradient (e.g., poorly calibrated or sparse), the student may not correct errors and could diverge.

### Mechanism 2: Dual‑Advantage Functions Balance Preservation and Alignment
- **Claim:** Separating in‑modal and cross‑modal advantages provides complementary reward signals that stabilize text capability retention while closing the speech‑text gap.
- **Mechanism:** A_im measures the KL divergence between teacher and student when both process *text* prompts, anchoring text performance. A_cm compares teacher (text) with student (speech) on *speech* prompts, directly optimizing speech‑to‑text reasoning transfer. Their weighted sum (L_im + L_cm) balances preservation and alignment.
- **Core assumption:** Text‑only performance is a stable proxy for general reasoning; optimizing A_im prevents catastrophic forgetting while A_cm builds speech alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] formally defines the two advantages. Table 2 shows ablations: λ=1 (text‑only OPD) improves speech slightly; λ=0 (speech‑only) improves text, confirming complementary effects.
- **Break condition:** If the advantages conflict and λ cannot compensate, the model may either forget foundational text skills or fail to align speech capabilities.

### Mechanism 3: Token‑Level KL Feedback Enables Fine‑Grained Credit Assignment
- **Claim:** Using per‑token teacher log‑probability differences as advantages distills reasoning patterns efficiently without needing ground‑truth outputs.
- **Mechanism:** Token‑wise KL divergence (teacher vs. student) serves as a dense reward, assigning credit for correct reasoning steps even when final output differs. This is integrated into a policy gradient framework via the expectation over advantages [section 3.4].
- **Core assumption:** Teacher token probabilities encode sufficient reasoning quality; the student’s multimodal encoder can map speech to the teacher’s logical token space.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states token‑level feedback distills capabilities into multi‑modal representations. The loss formulation (Eqs. 3‑4) explicitly uses per‑token advantages.
- **Break condition:** If teacher probabilities are miscalibrated or the modality encoder cannot align speech features to the teacher’s text space, token‑level signals may be misleading, causing unstable learning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Policy Gradient Methods (e.g., REINFORCE, PPO)**
  - *Why needed:* X‑OPD’s loss (Eqs. 3‑4) is a policy gradient algorithm where the “reward” is the teacher’s token‑level advantage. Understanding how the probability ratio r(θ) and baselines stabilize on‑policy updates is essential.
  - *Quick check:* How does using the ratio π_θ/π_old in the objective stabilize training compared to a vanilla gradient update?
- **Kullback‑Leibler Divergence as a Distance Metric**
  - *Why needed:* The advantages A_im and A_cm are explicit KL divergences between teacher and student token distributions, quantifying the capability gap per token.
  - *Quick check:* Why is symmetric KL not used? What does the direction D_KL(P_teacher || P_student) versus D_KL(P_student || P_teacher) imply about the optimization goal?
- **Catastrophic Forgetting in Multimodal Models**
  - *Why needed:* A core outcome is mitigating forgetting (Table 3). This requires understanding how new speech‑text alignment training can overwrite previously learned acoustic or textual knowledge in the shared LM backbone.
  - *Quick check:* Why does freezing the audio tower (section 4.3) help reduce forgetting, and what risks does it introduce if the adapter is non‑linear?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - Student: Speech LLM (e.g., Qwen3‑Omni‑A3B‑Instruct) with frozen audio encoder and trainable LM backbone + modal adapter.
  - Teacher: Text‑only LLM (e.g., Qwen3‑A3B‑Instruct), frozen, provides token distributions for prompts.
  - Rollout Engine: Generates *n* candidate trajectories per prompt using current student policy (π_old).
  - Advantage Calculator: Computes A_im and A_cm per token by comparing student vs. teacher log‑probs.
  - Policy Optimizer: Updates student backbone via Adam on the weighted loss L(θ).

- **Critical path:** Input pairing → Student rollout (multi‑sample) → Teacher scoring → Advantage computation → Policy gradient update → Loop.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Frozen audio tower vs. full finetuning:* Freezing reduces forgetting risk but limits acoustic feature adaptation; full finetuning may improve alignment but risk catastrophic drop in audio tasks.
  - *λ weighting:* λ=0.5 balances text preservation and speech alignment; λ=1.0 best for MMAR retention but may limit speech alignment depth.
  - *Teacher scale:* A smaller teacher (A3B) outperformed a larger one (A22B), suggesting the knowledge gap must be within the student’s capacity to mimic; an overly strong teacher may produce unreachable distributions.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Catastrophic forgetting on MMAR (>5% drop) → λ too low or audio tower not frozen.
  - No improvement on BIG Bench Audio → teacher fails to provide meaningful token‑level signal or rollout count *n* too low.
  - Training instability (loss spikes) → *n* too low (high variance), learning rate too high, or missing ratio clipping.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check:** Run X‑OPD with λ=1.0 (text‑only OPD) on a tiny subset (100 samples). Expect: speech scores improve slightly, text scores preserved. If text drops, advantage computation is broken.
  2. **Ablation n:** Vary multi‑sample rollouts *n* ∈ {1,4,8}. Expect: n=1 unstable, n=4 stable, n=8 diminishing returns. Monitor gradient norm variance.
  3. **Teacher capacity probe:** Swap teacher with a same‑architecture randomly initialized model. Expect near‑zero improvement, confirming teacher’s capability is essential.

## Limitations
- **Model/data accessibility:** Core components (Gemini‑3‑Flash‑Preview, Qwen3‑235B‑A22B judge) may be proprietary; substitutes could alter results.
- **Implementation gaps:** Specific verl integration details (π_old update frequency, audio embedding caching) are omitted, risking replication fidelity.
- **Scalability unknowns:** Findings may not generalize to larger student models or architectures with unfrozen audio towers.

## Confidence
- On‑policy rollouts mitigate exposure bias: **Medium‑High**
- Dual‑advantage functions provide complementary rewards: **Medium**
- Token‑level KL feedback enables fine‑grained credit assignment: **Medium**
- Primary outcome (gap reduction/forgetting mitigation) for the reported setup: **High**; broader generalization to other architectures: **Low**
