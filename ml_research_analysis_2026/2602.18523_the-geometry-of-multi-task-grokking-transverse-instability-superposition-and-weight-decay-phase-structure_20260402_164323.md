# The Geometry of Multi-Task Grokking: Transverse Instability, Superposition, and Weight Decay Phase Structure
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18523
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18523
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Multi-task grokking exhibits universal integrability, holographic incompressibility, and transverse fragility across weight decay regimes, with staggered generalization (mul → sq → add) and spectral symmetry‑breaking in attention layers.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the geometric properties of multi-task grokking in shared‑trunk Transformers trained on modular arithmetic tasks (addition, multiplication, squaring). Through systematic weight decay sweeps and analyses including trajectory PCA, commutator defects, Hessian eigenvalues, and gradient perturbations, the authors uncover several universal patterns across 42 experimental runs. Key findings include a staggered generalization order (multiplication first, then squaring, then addition); a low‑dimensional “center manifold” that constrains optimization trajectories, with orthogonal commutator defects preceding generalization; a weight‑decay‑dependent phase structure where non‑zero weight decay is essential for grokking; holographic incompressibility—solutions occupy only a few principal directions yet are distributed across full‑rank weights and destroyed by minimal perturbations; and a characteristic spectral symmetry‑breaking in query/key matrices that accompanies grokking. The study also reveals transverse fragility: removing less than 10% of orthogonal gradient components eliminates grokking, with dual‑task models showing partial recovery at 50% deletion but tri‑task models not, highlighting redundancy from overparameterization. These results provide a coherent geometric picture of how overparameterized models grok multiple tasks simultaneously.

## Method Summary
The authors trained shared‑trunk Transformers on two task combinations: addition+multiplication (dual‑task) and addition+multiplication+squaring (tri‑task). They performed a systematic sweep over weight decay coefficients and used a suite of geometric diagnostics: trajectory principal component analysis (PCA) to assess dimensionality; commutator defects to measure task operator non‑commutativity; Hessian eigenvalue analysis to characterize curvature; and targeted gradient perturbations (orthogonal deletion) to probe transverse stability. These tools were applied to optimization trajectories and learned weights across all conditions, enabling a comparative study of grokking dynamics.

## Key Results
- Staggered generalization and universal integrability: multi-task grokking shows a consistent order (mul → sq → add) with orthogonal commutator defects preceding generalization, confined to a low‑dimensional manifold (4–8 PCA directions).
- Holographic incompressibility and transverse fragility: generalizing solutions occupy few PCA directions yet are full‑rank and extremely sensitive; <10% orthogonal gradient deletion eliminates grokking, with dual‑task models partially recovering at 50% deletion but tri‑task not, indicating redundant center manifolds from overparameterization.
- Attention spectral symmetry‑breaking accompanies grokking, following a universal competition–instability–alignment loop; bottom transformer layers exhibit 1.5–2.4× stronger non‑commutativity than top layers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper attributes multi‑task grokking to a weight‑decay‑induced stabilization of a low‑dimensional center manifold in the loss landscape. When weight decay is non‑zero, optimization trajectories become confined to a manifold spanned by a few principal directions, as evidenced by trajectory PCA. The “defect” measured via orthogonal commutators between task operators serves as an early indicator of approach to this manifold; a declining defect precedes generalization across all tasks. This universal integrability explains why grokking emerges reliably despite the complexity of multi‑task learning.

Furthermore, the holographic incompressibility—that solutions occupy only 4–8 PCA directions yet are distributed across full‑rank weights—suggests that the generalizing parameters lie on a thin, high‑dimensional structure that cannot be compressed post‑hoc. The extreme transverse fragility (elimination by <10% orthogonal gradient deletion) indicates that the solution is perched on a ridge where any perturbation orthogonal to the manifold pushes the model out of the generalization basin. Overparameterization creates redundant directions within the manifold (center manifolds), allowing dual‑task models some resilience at 50% deletion but not tri‑task, implying that task interference increases the fragility.

Finally, the spectral symmetry‑breaking in query/key matrices—characterized by a shift from degenerate to distinct eigenvalues—maps the grokking transition onto a competition‑instability‑alignment loop. The bottom layer’s stronger non‑commutativity reveals that early transformer layers encode the core task interactions, while higher layers align to the emerging solution. Together, these mechanisms form a coherent geometric narrative: weight decay narrows the effective dynamics, commutator defects signal convergence to a center manifold, and spectral reorganization finalizes multi‑task grokking.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input tokens → Shared Transformer trunk (multiple layers) → Task-specific output heads (Addition, Multiplication, Squaring). The trunk consists of self‑attention and feed‑forward layers; each head is a linear classifier.

**Critical path** (training loop):
1. Sample batch from a specific task (e.g., a×b mod p).
2. Forward pass through trunk and corresponding head.
3. Compute cross‑entropy loss.
4. Backpropagate to obtain gradients.
5. Apply optimizer (e.g., Adam) with weight decay (λ) to all weights.
6. Repeat until training loss plateaus and generalization emerges.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Shared trunk reduces parameters and enables transfer but may cause task interference; observed staggered generalization hints at hierarchical task difficulty.
- Weight decay strength critically shapes grokking dynamics; λ=0 fails, while moderate values yield systematic phase structure.
- Overparameterization creates redundant center manifolds: dual‑task models show partial robustness to gradient deletion, while tri‑task does not, indicating a capacity vs. fragility trade‑off.
- Depth (not varied in this study) could alter the geometry but was left for future work.

**Failure signatures**:
- λ=0: absence of grokking despite convergence.
- Deleting >10% of orthogonal gradient components: complete loss of generalization.
- Tri‑task gradient deletion: no recovery even at 50% deletion.
- Any post‑hoc compression (SVD, pruning, scaling) destroys performance.
- Lack of spectral symmetry‑breaking in query/key matrices correlates with failure to grok.

**First experiments** (three to run when onboarding to this architecture):
1. Weight decay sweep across logarithmic values to reproduce phase structure (grokking timescale, Hessian depth).
2. Gradient orthogonal deletion experiment to confirm transverse fragility thresholds (≈10% deletion).
3. Trajectory PCA to verify low‑dimensional confinement (4–8 principal components).

## Limitations
- Generalizability: only shared‑trunk Transformers on synthetic modular arithmetic; extension to other architectures/tasks uncertain.
- Theoretical foundation: geometric metrics lack rigorous derivation; weight‑decay effects are correlational.
- Perturbation validity: artificial gradient‑deletion may not reflect real‑world failure modes.
