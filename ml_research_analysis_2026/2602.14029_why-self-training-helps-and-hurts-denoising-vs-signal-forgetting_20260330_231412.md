# Why Self-Training Helps and Hurts: Denoising vs. Signal Forgetting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14029
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14029
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Self-training in overparameterized linear regression yields a U-shaped risk curve due to early denoising followed by signal forgetting; optimal stopping time and soft feature selection emerge via spectral filtering, with iGCV enabling fully data-driven tuning.

## Executive Summary
Iterative self-training (self-distillation) repeatedly refits a model on its own pseudo-labels, trading off denoising early on against signal forgetting later. Using high-dimensional asymptotics, the authors derive deterministic recursions for prediction risk and effective noise in overparameterized linear regression. They identify a U-shaped risk curve arising from the interaction between stochastic error reduction and systematic signal degradation, with an optimal early-stopping point. Under spiked covariance, self-training acts as a spectral filter that preserves strong eigendirections and suppresses weak ones, implementing soft feature selection distinct from ridge shrinkage. An iterated generalized cross-validation (iGCV) criterion is proposed and proven to consistently estimate risk along the trajectory.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze self-training in overparameterized linear regression by leveraging high-dimensional Gaussian approximations. They derive deterministic recursions tracking prediction risk and effective noise across iterations, revealing a denoising-forgetting trade-off. Under spiked covariance, self-training is interpreted as a spectral filter that implicitly selects features based on eigendirection strength. To enable data-driven stopping, they propose iGCV and prove its uniform consistency over the training trajectory. Empirical validation is provided via synthetic covariance simulations and ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10.

## Key Results
- U-shaped risk curve emerges from early denoising (projection of noise) followed by signal forgetting (drift from ground truth).
- Self-training acts as soft spectral feature selection, preserving strong eigendirections and suppressing weak ones, distinct from ridge shrinkage.
- iGCV is uniformly consistent over the training trajectory, enabling fully data-driven stopping without cross-validation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Self-training's denoising phase removes noise-dominated components by projection, improving generalization early on. However, repeated refitting on imperfect pseudo-labels causes the model to drift away from the true signal, increasing systematic error in later iterations. The interaction between these opposing effects produces the U-shaped risk curve and optimal early stopping.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Extend deterministic recursions to data reuse and filtered pseudo-labels.
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state that extensions to reused data and filtered pseudo-labels are necessary to address finite-sample constraints and formalize heuristics.
- **Why unresolved**: Current theory assumes fresh independent covariates and noiseless pseudo-labels; real self-training often reuses data or filters.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Derive recursions for such settings and validate with simulations; identify conditions that prevent signal forgetting.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Determine whether the spectral filtering mechanism and deterministic equivalents extend to nonlinear models.
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "Investigating nonlinear models is also critical to determine whether evolving representations stabilize signal or exacerbate drift."
- **Why unresolved**: Nonlinearities may alter the spectral filtering and U-shaped risk dynamics.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extend high-dimensional asymptotics to nonlinear architectures; empirically verify on deep networks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Obtain an asymptotic characterization of ridge's optimal regularization as a function of spike strength and prove when self-training beats ridge.
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "While simulations show self-training can surpass ridge, a theorem-level comparison—specifying the asymptotic tuning rule for ridge as a function of s is left for future work."
- **Why unresolved**: No analytical result exists linking ridge tuning to spike strength; comparison remains empirical.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Solve optimal ridge risk in spiked model; derive explicit λ(s,ρ) and prove risk dominance conditions; produce a phase diagram.

## Limitations
- Analysis restricted to linear regression in proportional asymptotics regime, limiting applicability to deep nets.
- High-dimensional Gaussian approximations may not capture complex neural network behaviors.
- Signal forgetting depends on spectral structure of feature covariance, which may differ substantially in real datasets.

## Confidence

- iGCV uniform consistency: Medium-High (conditional on asymptotic assumptions)
- Soft feature selection interpretation: Medium (empirical support, theoretical justification limited to linear case)
- Optimal early-stopping generalization: Medium (empirical but not formally proven beyond linear case)
