# Success Conditioning as Policy Improvement: The Optimization Problem Solved by Imitating Success
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18175
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18175
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Success conditioning—improving policies by imitating successful trajectories—exactly solves a trust-region policy optimization problem with a χ² divergence constraint, yielding a statewise triple equality and guaranteeing conservative improvement without dangerous distribution shift.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes that success conditioning, the practice of training a policy on successful trajectory segments, is mathematically equivalent to solving a specific trust-region policy optimization problem. The optimization maximizes first-order performance gain under a χ² divergence constraint whose radius is automatically determined by the behavior policy's action-influence. This yields a statewise triple equality between relative advantage, χ² divergence, and action-influence, ensuring that the updated policy is conservative: it never decreases performance and limits distribution shift to safe regions. The analysis reveals that failures occur only when action-influence is negligible, leading to minimal policy change. The framework also clarifies trade-offs when using proxy rewards (e.g., return thresholds), which can amplify improvement but risk misalignment with the true objective.

## Method Summary
Success conditioning begins with a behavior policy π₀ that generates trajectories labeled by a binary success indicator. A new policy π⁺ is derived by conditionally imitating actions from successful trajectories: π⁺(a|s) = Pπ₀(At=a|St=s, R(τ)=1). The paper proves this procedure exactly solves the constrained optimization problem: maximize the first-order policy improvement objective Lπ₀(π) subject to Σₛ d⁺π₀(s) χ²(π(·|s)∥π₀(·|s)) ≤ Γ, where Γ is the total action-influence under π₀. The trust-region radius is automatic, avoiding hyperparameter tuning. In practice, π⁺ is approximated via supervised learning on successful trajectories, with error bounds provided. The method's conservatism stems from the χ² geometry, which penalizes increasing probability on rarely-taken actions.

## Key Results
- Success conditioning exactly solves a trust-region policy optimization problem: it maximizes Lπ₀(π) under a χ² divergence constraint with radius equal to total action-influence under the behavior policy.
- Statewise triple equality: For the exact π⁺, the relative advantage Aπ₀(s,π⁺)/Vπ₀(s) equals both χ²(π⁺(·|s)∥π₀(·|s)) and the action-influence Iπ₀(s) at every state.
- Conservative operator guarantee: ρ(π⁺) ≥ ρ(π₀) holds always; failures occur only when action-influence is very small, causing negligible policy change.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Success conditioning exactly solves a trust-region policy optimization problem.  
**Mechanism:** The Bayesian update defining π⁺(a|s) = Pπ₀(At=a|St=s, R(τ)=1) is mathematically equivalent to maximizing the first-order policy improvement objective Lπ₀(π) subject to a χ² divergence constraint. The constraint's radius equals Σₛ d⁺π₀(s) Iπ₀(s), the total action-influence under π₀.  
**Core assumption:** The behavior policy π₀ generates trajectories with sufficient coverage; success is possible from all non-terminal states (though possibly with low probability).  
**Evidence anchors:** Proposition 4.1 formally states the optimization problem, identifying importance weights (w(s)=d⁺π₀(s)), divergence (χ²), and radius. Neighbor papers focus on KL-based trust regions, underscoring the novelty of the χ² geometry and automatic radius.  
**Break condition:** Relies on exact computation of π⁺ and correct estimation of occupancy measures. Approximation errors in supervised training (Prop. 5.1) or non-Markian dynamics weaken the correspondence.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Relative improvement, policy change magnitude, and action-influence are exactly equal at every state.  
**Mechanism:** For any state s, Aπ₀(s, π⁺)/Vπ₀(s) = χ²(π⁺(·|s)∥π₀(·|s)) = Iπ₀(s). This "action-influence identity" emerges from the closed-form solution π⁺(a|s) = π₀(a|s)(1 + Aπ₀(s,a)/Vπ₀(s)) and properties of χ² divergence.  
**Core assumption:** The success-conditioned policy π⁺ is computed exactly from the true behavior policy and its value functions.  
**Evidence anchors:** Proposition 4.4 states the statewise triple equality. Action-influence Iπ₀(s) is defined as the squared coefficient of variation of Qπ₀(s,·) over π₀'s action draw.  
**Break condition:** Holds only for the exact π⁺. Deviations due to finite data or function approximation break the identity, making χ² divergence a diagnostic proxy only for the ideal case.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Success conditioning is a conservative operator; it never degrades performance and fails only via over-conservatism.  
**Mechanism:** The trust-region solution with χ² geometry and radius set to total action-influence ensures ρ(π⁺) ≥ ρ(π₀) (Corollary 4.5). The constraint is binding, so policy movement is bounded by achievable improvement. When action-influence Iπ₀(s) is very small across states, the solution π⁺ ≈ π₀, causing minimal change.  
**Core assumption:** π₀ has non-zero success probability everywhere; the χ² constraint is computed with d⁺π₀.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.5 compare with KL-based TRPO/PPO, highlighting χ²'s tolerance for dropping actions but penalty for exploring rare actions. Example 3.1 (bandit) demonstrates failure via near-zero policy change when action-influence is low.  
**Break condition:** Guarantees assume exact π⁺ and correct action-influence estimation. If the proxy reward is misaligned (Section 6), the "conservative" update might not improve the true objective despite moving the policy.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Behavior policy π₀ -> Success filter (labels trajectories with binary R(τ)) -> Supervised learner (trains on successful actions via cross-entropy loss) -> Deployment (trained policy ˆπ as π⁺ or approximation).  
Critical path: 1) Collect trajectories from π₀; 2) Filter by success criterion; 3) Train sequence model (e.g., transformer) on filtered trajectories via next-action prediction; 4) Deploy trained model; monitor policy shift (χ² divergence) as diagnostic.  
Design tradeoffs: χ² geometry permits dropping actions (concentration) but heavily penalizes increasing probability on rare π₀ actions, reducing exploration risk but limiting improvement when high-value actions are rarely taken. Automatic radius avoids manual tuning but may yield minimal change if action-influence is low. Proxy rewards (e.g., return thresholds) amplify action-influence but risk misalignment with true objective.  
Failure signatures: Low action-influence Iπ₀(s) → tiny χ² divergence → negligible policy change and improvement. High action-influence but poor proxy alignment → improvement on proxy but degradation on true objective. Function approximation error bounded by √(M·Δ/2) (Prop. 5.1); monitor offline loss Δ.  
First 3 experiments:  
1. **Bandit verification:** Implement the 2-arm bandit (Example 3.1) with q₁=0.495, q₂=0.505, π₀ uniform. Compute exact π⁺, Lπ₀(π⁺), χ² divergence, and Iπ₀. Verify triple equality (Prop. 4.4) and ρ(π⁺) ≥ ρ(π₀).  
2. **χ² vs. KL geometry:** Construct a 3-action MDP with π₀=(0.49,0.49,0.02). Plot feasible sets for χ² (ε=0.1) and KL (ε=0.1) constraints. Observe χ² allows concentration on common actions but prohibits mass on rare action; KL prohibits dropping any action.  
3. **Proxy reward sweep:** In the 100-arm Beta distribution example (Section 6.3), vary return threshold θ. For each θ, compute CV(proxy), alignment with true advantage, and improvement on true objective. Identify threshold range where proxy outperforms faithful conditioning and where it fails.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees assume exact success conditioning, infinite data, and perfect value estimates; finite-sample error and function approximation can violate the statewise triple equality and conservative improvement guarantee.
- χ² geometry leads to over-conservatism when action-influence is small, yielding negligible policy change regardless of data size or tuning.
- Requires every state to have non-zero success probability under the behavior policy π₀; otherwise the conditional policy π⁺ may be undefined or unstable.
- Proxy rewards (e.g., return thresholds) risk misalignment with the true objective, potentially degrading true performance even while improving on the proxy.
