# Time series forecasting with Hahn Kolmogorov-Arnold networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.18837
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18837
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: HaKAN achieves state-of-the-art forecasting performance, with best MSE in 18/32 cases and best MAE in 19/32 cases, and up to 8.98%/3.96% average error reduction over baselines.

## Executive Summary
HaKAN is a lightweight, interpretable framework for long-term multivariate time series forecasting that addresses key limitations of Transformer and MLP models. It replaces standard activations with learnable Hahn polynomial parameterizations within a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network architecture, combined with channel independence, patch processing, and a bottleneck output layer. This design efficiently captures hierarchical temporal patterns—global trends via inter-patch layers and local dynamics via intra-patch layers—while mitigating spectral bias and avoiding quadratic complexity. Extensive experiments on eight benchmarks show consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art methods, with superior average reductions in MSE (up to 8.98%) and MAE (up to 3.96%). Ablation studies validate the contributions of the dual-layer Hahn-KAN blocks and bottleneck structure.

## Method Summary
HaKAN processes each variable independently. Each univariate series is normalized with RevIN, then split into patches of length *P* with stride *S*. Patch embeddings combine a linear projection and learnable positional encoding. The core consists of a stack of *R* identical Hahn-KAN blocks. Each block contains: (1) an intra-patch KAN layer that mixes features within each patch (operating on the feature dimension *D*), and (2) an inter-patch KAN layer that mixes patches (operating on the patch dimension *N*), both with residual connections. KAN layers use Hahn polynomial basis functions (degree *d*) as learnable, grid-free activations. After the stacked blocks, the flattened representation (*ND*) is passed through a bottleneck: a down-projection to *H* followed by an up-projection to the prediction horizon *T*. Finally, RevIN denormalization yields the forecast.

## Key Results
- Outperforms SOTA on 8 benchmark datasets: best MSE in 18/32 cases, best MAE in 19/32 cases under optimized look-back.
- Average MSE/MAE reductions up to 8.98%/3.96% compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm the contributions of Hahn polynomials, dual-layer structure, and bottleneck design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Hahn Polynomial Parameterization**
- Claim: Hahn polynomial basis in KAN layers enables grid-free, efficient, learnable activations that mitigate spectral bias.
- Mechanism: Orthogonal polynomials on discrete domains replace B-splines, reducing parameters and complexity (O(d_in d_out d)) while maintaining approximation flexibility for low/high frequencies.
- Core assumption: Hahn polynomials can effectively parameterize the univariate functions required by the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation.
- Evidence anchors: Paper's complexity analysis; ablation shows Hahn outperforms Chebyshev/B-Splines (Table 3).

**Mechanism 2: Dual-Layer Hahn-KAN Block**
- Claim: Stacking inter-patch (global) and intra-patch (local) KANs hierarchically captures temporal patterns.
- Mechanism: Inter-patch KAN models cross-patch relationships for long-term trends; intra-patch KAN refines within-patch dynamics; residuals enable iterative refinement.
- Core assumption: Separating patch- and within-patch processing extracts multi-scale patterns better than monolithic layers.
- Evidence anchors: Ablation (Table 6)—removing intra-patch increases MSE most (0.559 vs 0.507), removing inter-patch also degrades (0.520).

**Mechanism 3: Channel Independence + Patching + Bottleneck**
- Claim: This combo yields a lightweight, robust architecture avoiding quadratic complexity and overfitting.
- Mechanism: Channel independence simplifies training; patching reduces inter-patch cost by lowering *N*; bottleneck compresses flattened features for large *T*.
- Core assumption: Per-variable modeling with patches and compressed features suffices for many tasks.
- Evidence anchors: SOTA performance with near-MLP parameter counts (~899K); patching optimization (optimal *P=16* in Figure 3).

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Input (multivariate X) -> RevIN normalization -> Patching (N patches of length P) -> Linear embedding + positional encoding -> Stack of R Hahn-KAN blocks (intra-patch KAN + inter-patch KAN with residuals) -> Flatten -> Bottleneck (down H, up T) -> RevIN denormalization -> Forecast

**Critical path:**
Process each channel independently: For each variable, apply RevIN, split into patches, embed, pass through stacked Hahn-KAN blocks, flatten, bottleneck, then denormalize. Inter-patch KAN's O(N²D) cost is mitigated by small N from patching.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Expressiveness vs Efficiency: Increasing R, polynomial degree d, or bottleneck H increases capacity but also parameters and overfitting risk.
- Local vs Global: Patch length P balances local detail vs global context per patch; stride S controls overlap.
- Channel Independence vs Multivariate Correlation: CI simplifies training but discards cross-channel signals, which may hurt performance on strongly correlated datasets.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Poor performance on datasets with strong inter-variable correlations (e.g., Traffic) due to channel independence.
2. Training instability or slow convergence possibly from poor initialization of Hahn polynomial coefficients or inappropriate learning rate.
3. Degradation with large prediction horizon T if bottleneck H is too small to compress features adequately.
4. High variance across random seeds indicating sensitivity to initialization or data splits.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce optimized look-back baseline:** On ETTh1 with L=336, P=16, R=5, H=336, verify HaKAN achieves best performance against PatchTST/64 and DLinear as in Table 1.
2. **Validate polynomial basis choice:** Compare Hahn, Chebyshev, and B-Splines on Weather and ETTh2 for T=96,192; expect Hahn to yield lowest MSE/MAE (Table 3).
3. **Verify dual-layer contribution:** Remove intra-patch KAN, remove inter-patch KAN, and use full model on 2-3 datasets; full model should perform best, intra-patch removal worst (Table 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can HaKAN be combined with frequency-domain transformations (e.g., Fourier, Wavelet) to improve modeling of periodic patterns?
- How can HaKAN model inter-variable correlations without sacrificing computational efficiency?
- Does increasing the degree of Hahn polynomials improve approximation capability, and what is the optimal degree for balancing expressiveness and efficiency?

## Limitations
- Unknown initialization details for Hahn polynomial coefficients may affect convergence stability and reproducibility.
- Channel independence limits performance on strongly correlated datasets (e.g., Traffic) by discarding cross-channel dependencies.
- The claim of mitigated spectral bias lacks direct Fourier analysis; it is supported only by ablation and theoretical arguments.
- Bottleneck structure may struggle with very long prediction horizons (>720) due to feature compression limits.

## Confidence
- Hahn Polynomials mitigate spectral bias: Medium (ablation and complexity support, but no direct spectral evidence)
- Dual-layer block captures hierarchical patterns: High (strong ablation evidence)
- Efficiency via channel independence/patching/bottleneck: Medium (parameter counts and SOTA comparisons, but scalability beyond tested ranges uncertain)

## Next Checks
1. **Initialization Sensitivity Test:** Train HaKAN across multiple seeds with different polynomial coefficient initialization schemes; measure loss stability and final MSE/MAE variance.
2. **Cross-Variable Dependency Stress Test:** Evaluate HaKAN against a channel-mixing variant (e.g., concatenate channels pre-patching) on Traffic dataset; performance drop confirms CI limitation.
3. **Scalability Stress Test:** Increase look-back L to 1536 and horizon T to 1152 on ETTh1; monitor GPU memory, training time, and MSE degradation relative to baselines to test bottleneck/patching scalability.
