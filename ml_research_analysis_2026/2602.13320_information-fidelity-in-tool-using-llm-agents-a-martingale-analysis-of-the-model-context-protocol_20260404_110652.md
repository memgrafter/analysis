# Information Fidelity in Tool-Using LLM Agents: A Martingale Analysis of the Model Context Protocol
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13320
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13320
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a theoretical framework proving cumulative distortion in MCP agents grows linearly with high-probability \(O(\sqrt{T})\) bounds, validated across Qwen2-7B, Llama-3-8B, and Mistral-7B.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the first formal analysis of error accumulation in Model Context Protocol (MCP) agents, which use tool calls within an LLM reasoning loop. The authors model each step’s distortion as a martingale with bounded increments under geometric temporal decay and branching assumptions, enabling Azuma–Hoeffding concentration bounds that rule out exponential failure modes. A hybrid distortion metric combines discrete fact matching (weighted Jaccard) and continuous semantic similarity (embedding cosine) to capture both factual drift and paraphrase variation. Empirical validation on three 7B-parameter models confirms linear distortion growth with deviations within theoretical envelopes, and shows an 80% reduction in distortion via semantic weighting (\(\lambda\)). The analysis also yields a practical re-grounding schedule: refreshing context every ~9 steps suffices for error control under fitted decay parameters.

## Method Summary
The theoretical method frames MCP agent interactions as an adaptive query chain where each step’s distortion \(\Delta_t\) depends on prior steps with geometrically decaying influence \(\phi(i,j)=\beta^{j-i}\). Cumulative distortion \(D(T)=\sum_{t=1}^T \Delta_t\) is shown to be a martingale with bounded differences under assumptions of bounded branching (\(\beta B < 1\)) and response stability. High-probability bounds follow from Azuma–Hoeffding. Experimentally, adaptive chains are generated: starting from a seed template, each subsequent query is produced with probability \(\beta\) by conditioning on the previous agent response, else a fresh seed is sampled. Deterministic tools provide ground truth responses; distortion is computed per step via a hybrid metric combining weighted fact extraction and MiniLM embeddings. Empirical \(D(T)\) trajectories are compared against the theoretical envelope calibrated by estimating decay \(\hat{\beta}\) from autocorrelation.

## Key Results
- Cumulative distortion concentrates around its expectation with deviations bounded by \(O(\sqrt{T})\), preventing exponential error blowup under the stated assumptions.
- Semantic weighting (\(\lambda\)) in the hybrid metric reduces distortion by up to 80% compared to pure fact matching.
- Periodic re-grounding every ~9 steps (for fitted \(\beta \approx 0.7\)) effectively truncates error chains and maintains bounded distortion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Under temporal decay and branching assumptions, cumulative distortion concentrates tightly with high-probability deviations bounded by \(O(\sqrt{T})\).
- **Mechanism:** Construct the distortion martingale \(Z_t = \mathbb{E}[D(T) \mid \mathcal{F}_t]\). Bounded increments \(|Z_{t+1} - Z_t| \leq c_\star\) arise from geometric influence decay (Assumption 3) and \(\beta B < 1\) (Assumption 1). Azuma–Hoeffding then yields the concentration.
- **Core assumptions:** Bounded branching (Assumption 1), response stability (Assumption 2), temporal decay (Assumption 3).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Theorem 1 state the \(O(\sqrt{T})\) bound; Lemma 1 proves bounded Doob increments; Section 4.2 provides the full proof. Related concentration results for MDPs exist, but adaptation to adaptive MCP querying is novel.
- **Break condition:** If \(\beta B \geq 1\), geometric series diverge, increments unbounded, concentration may fail. Large response variation (violating Assumption 2) also breaks the bound.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The hybrid distortion metric \(\Delta_t = (1-\lambda) d^\text{set}_w + \lambda d^\text{emb}\) captures both factual mismatch and semantic drift.
- **Mechanism:** Weighted Jaccard distance over extracted facts (\(d^\text{set}_w\)) is combined with normalized cosine distance of sentence embeddings (\(d^\text{emb}\)). The trade-off parameter \(\lambda \in [0,1]\) balances factual precision vs. semantic coherence.
- **Core assumptions:** Weight/embedding normalization (Assumptions 4–5), embedding regularity (Assumption 6), fact extraction stability (Assumption 7).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 defines \(\Delta_t\) (Equation 1); Claim 1 shows sensitivity to lexical variation; Table 1 shows embedding distances for paraphrases; Section 5 demonstrates up to 80% distortion reduction with higher \(\lambda\).
- **Break condition:** Brittle fact extraction (threshold \(\tau\) violated) or non-unit-normalized embeddings can cause discontinuities or metric values outside \([0,1]\).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Errors propagate with an effective information horizon \(H_\varepsilon = \lceil \log \varepsilon / \log \beta \rceil\); periodic re-grounding truncates error chains.
- **Mechanism:** Influence decays geometrically via \(\phi(i,j) = \beta^{j-i}\). Cumulative influence \(C^\star = \alpha/(1-\beta B)\) caps total error amplitude. Re-grounding resets dependencies, limiting horizon.
- **Core assumptions:** Temporal decay (Assumption 3) and \(\beta B < 1\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 defines horizon and re-grounding; Corollary 2 derives \(H_\varepsilon\); Section 6 recommends ~9-step interval for \(\beta=0.7\); Section 5 fits \(\hat{\beta} \in [0.68,0.71]\) via autocorrelation.
- **Break condition:** If \(\beta\) is near 1, \(H_\varepsilon\) becomes large; infrequent re-grounding may exceed operational thresholds despite linear growth.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  LLM Agent → MCP Server (JSON-RPC router, schema validator, context tracker) → Tool Suite (deterministic, cached) → Distortion Module (fact extractor, embedding encoder, hybrid aggregator with \(\lambda\)) → Monitor (track \(D(T)\), fit \(\hat{\beta}\), trigger re-grounding)
- **Critical path:**  
  Query → MCP routing → Tool call → Response → Extract facts & compute embedding → Update \(\Delta_t\) → Accumulate \(D(T)\) → Compare to theoretical envelope → Decide on re-grounding
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - \(\lambda\) trade-off: Lower (0.3–0.5) for factual safety; higher (0.6–0.8) for semantic coherence.
  - Re-grounding interval: Shorter intervals reduce error horizon but increase tool overhead; theoretical \(H_\varepsilon\) suggests data-driven scheduling (e.g., every 9 steps for \(\beta=0.7\)).
  - Branching factor \(B\): Limit parallel tool calls to tighten bounds; more children increase cumulative influence \(C^\star\).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Persistent violation of calibrated \(O(\sqrt{T})\) envelope across multiple chains.
  - Empirically fitted \(\hat{\beta} > 0.9\), indicating strong long-range dependencies and inflated envelopes.
  - Extreme \(\lambda\): \(\lambda=0\) yields high distortion from minor rewording; \(\lambda=1\) may overlook factual inaccuracies.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline validation:** Run \(T=10\) chains with \(\beta=0.7\), \(\lambda=0.5\); verify linear growth and that empirical \(D(T)\) stays within theoretical envelope.
  2. **Lambda sweep:** Vary \(\lambda \in \{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}\) at \(T=30\); measure distortion reduction; identify optimal \(\lambda\) for target domain.
  3. **Long-chain scaling:** Extend to \(T=60\) across \(\beta \in \{0.5,0.7,0.9\}\); confirm \(O(\sqrt{T})\) concentration and linear trend hold under high dependency and long horizons.

## Limitations
- The exact adaptive query generation algorithm (beyond probability \(\beta\)) is unspecified, critically affecting the influence decay assumption \(\phi(i,j)=\beta^{j-i}\) and effective branching factor \(B\).
- The "TF-based weights" for weighted Jaccard lack a precise definition (corpus vs. response frequency), impacting the hybrid metric's continuity and sensitivity.
- Theoretical assumptions (bounded branching, response stability) are validated on synthetic deterministic tools; real-world MCP deployments with stochastic APIs or non-deterministic tools may violate these, breaking the martingale structure.

## Confidence
- **Linear distortion growth & \(O(\sqrt{T})\) concentration:** High. Proofs rigorous under assumptions; empirical envelopes hold across three models. Primary risk is violation of Assumptions 1–3 in practice.
- **Hybrid metric (\(\Delta_t\)) effectiveness:** Medium. 80% distortion reduction from \(\lambda\)-tuning is empirically strong but relies on an uncalibrated fact extractor and embedding model; domain generalizability not fully established.
- **Re-grounding interval (~9 steps for \(\beta=0.7\)):** Low. Derived from a single fitted \(\beta\) in a constrained setting; small misestimates (e.g., \(\beta=0.75\) vs. 0.7) double the horizon, making fixed intervals brittle.
