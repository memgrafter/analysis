# WavSLM: Single-Stream Speech Language Modeling via WavLM Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05299
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05299
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** WavSLM-4k (307M parameters, 60k hours training) achieves competitive performance on acoustic and semantic consistency benchmarks (average 69.5% in Table 1) and generation quality (UTMOS 3.69, speaker similarity 91.6) compared to much larger text-pretrained models (7B–8B parameters), while supporting streaming inference with a real-time factor of 5.8.

## Executive Summary
WavSLM addresses the challenge of extending simple single-stream autoregressive pretraining from text to speech without relying on text supervision, hierarchical tokens, or complex architectures. It does so by distilling self-supervised WavLM representations into a single codebook via the streamable FocalCodec-Stream and training an autoregressive next-chunk predictor on the resulting token stream. Using only speech data, a 307M-parameter model trained on 60k hours achieves competitive results on acoustic and semantic consistency benchmarks (average 69.5%) and generation quality (UTMOS 3.69, speaker similarity 91.6), matching or exceeding much larger text-pretrained models. The approach also supports streaming inference with a real-time factor of 5.8.

## Method Summary
WavSLM’s pipeline consists of three stages: (1) Tokenization: raw audio is processed by a causally distilled WavLM-6 encoder, compressed, and quantized with FocalCodec-Stream into a single discrete token stream at 50 Hz, with each token representing a 4-token chunk. (2) Feature reconversion: the discrete tokens are decompressed and projected to feature vectors compatible with WavLM layers 7–24, enabling reuse of WavLM’s pretrained hierarchical representations. (3) Language modeling: the upper WavLM layers (7–24) are fine-tuned with a causal attention mask and a linear head to perform next-chunk prediction (predicting the next 4 tokens). Training uses the Libri-Light corpus (~60 k hours) with AdamW, mixed precision, batch size 16, and early stopping. Inference employs sliding‑window autoregressive generation with top‑k sampling, achieving real‑time streaming.

## Key Results
- **Benchmark performance:** Average 69.5% on acoustic and semantic consistency tasks (Table 1), competitive with text‑pretrained SLMs (e.g. sWUGGY 75.0, sBLiMP 63.9).
- **Generation quality:** UTMOS 3.69, speaker similarity 91.6%, both on par with much larger models.
- **Efficiency:** Streaming inference with real‑time factor 5.8 on a single H100 GPU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Distilling self-supervised WavLM representations into a single codebook yields tokens that jointly preserve semantic and acoustic information suitable for autoregressive modeling.
- **Mechanism:** FocalCodec-Stream processes raw audio via a causally distilled WavLM-6 encoder, compresses and quantizes the features into a single discrete stream, and decompresses them back into a continuous space compatible with upper WavLM layers (7–24). This creates a bridge: discrete tokens for efficient sequence modeling, while reconstructed features maintain alignment with WavLM's hierarchical representations.
- **Core assumption:** Mid-level WavLM representations (layer 6) contain a sufficient mixture of semantic and acoustic detail to be distilled into one codebook without requiring separate semantic/acoustic streams.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "distilling self-supervised WavLM representations into a single codebook"; [section 2.1] "We use the representations from the 6th transformer layer of WavLM-large... strike a balance between semantic richness and fine-grained acoustic detail"; [corpus] Similar works (TaDiCodec, VocalNet-MDM) explore distillation, but WavSLM's single codebook from WavLM-6 is novel.
- **Break condition:** If mid-level WavLM features prove insufficiently expressive, the single codebook would fail to capture one domain (e.g., speaker identity in Spk.↑ or semantics in sWUGGY↑), leading to poor performance on corresponding benchmarks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A next-chunk prediction objective enables efficient, high-resolution generation without sacrificing modeling fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of predicting one token at a time, the model predicts C=4 consecutive tokens per step using chunked causal attention. This matches FocalCodec-Stream's chunk size, reducing autoregressive steps while maintaining the tokenizer's 50 Hz resolution. Sliding-window attention bounds memory for streaming.
- **Core assumption:** Modeling dependencies at a 4-token chunk grain (200 ms) is sufficient to capture short-term temporal structure without losing the fine-grained information provided by the high-rate tokenizer.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.2] "To better align with the temporal resolution of the tokenizer and improve generation efficiency, we adopt a next-chunk prediction objective... C=4"; [section 2.2] "This formulation reduces the number of autoregressive steps required during generation, leading to faster inference while preserving high-resolution tokenization"; [section 4.3] Table 3 shows increasing chunk size to 8 or 16 degrades all metrics (UTMOS↓, Sim↓, PPL↑), supporting that C=4 is a sweet spot.
- **Break condition:** If linguistic or acoustic coherence critically depends on sub-chunk (sub-200ms) dependencies, larger chunks would cause severe drops in consistency metrics (e.g. sBLiMP↓, tSC↓) and generation quality (UTMOS↓), as observed in Table 3.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Autoregressive modeling on the single token stream, initialized from WavLM and trained speech-only, can learn both acoustic and linguistic patterns without text supervision.
- **Mechanism:** The upper WavLM layers (7–24) are made causal and fine-tuned via next-chunk prediction. The linear LM head maps features to token distributions. All training uses Libri-Light speech data only; no text or text-pretrained weights are involved. Linguistic structure emerges from the speech signal via the WavLM initialization.
- **Core assumption:** The hierarchical features from WavLM encode enough linguistic abstraction (e.g. phonetic, lexical, syntactic patterns) that an autoregressive decoder can model spoken language directly from speech, bypassing textual alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "without text supervision or text pretraining"; [section 2.2] "all linguistic structure in WavSLM emerges directly from speech data"; [section 4.1] WavSLM-4k achieves competitive sWUGGY (75.0) and sBLiMP (63.9) vs. text-pretrained models (e.g. LLaMA-Mimi 8B: 68.8, 55.1), suggesting learned linguistic knowledge.
- **Break condition:** If spoken language modeling fundamentally requires textual grounding, WavSLM would show large gaps on semantic tasks (sWUGGY, sBLiMP, tSC) compared to text-pretrained models, which it does not in Table 1. Failure would also manifest as poor alignment (Sent.↑) between acoustic and semantic cues.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-supervised speech representation (WavLM)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides continuous hierarchical features; early layers capture acoustics, later layers capture semantics. The tokenizer uses layer 6 outputs to balance both.  
  *Quick check:* Why might using only the first 6 layers of WavLM be preferable to using all layers? (Answer: Balances semantic richness and acoustic detail; deeper layers may over-abstract for acoustic preservation.)

- **Neural audio codec & vector quantization**  
  *Why needed:* FocalCodec-Stream converts continuous WavLM features into discrete tokens; the codebook size directly impacts capacity and stability.  
  *Quick check:* What is the trade-off when increasing the codebook vocabulary size from 4k to 65k in WavSLM? (Answer: Higher potential fidelity but increased modeling complexity; Table 2 shows PPL↑, UTMOS↓ for 65k variant.)

- **Autoregressive language modeling & next-token/chunk prediction**  
  *Why needed:* Core training objective; understanding causal masking, loss computation, and streaming inference is essential.  
  *Quick check:* How does the next-chunk objective differ from standard next-token prediction in attention pattern and generation speed? (Answer: Attention spans C tokens within a chunk; generation steps reduced by factor C; see section 2.2.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
1. Input: Raw waveform.
2. Tokenizer (FocalCodec-Stream): Causal WavLM-6 encoder → Compressor → Quantizer (single codebook) → Decompressor → Decoder. Output: discrete token stream (50 Hz, chunk size 4).
3. Feature reconversion: Decompressed tokens projected to features compatible with WavLM layers 7–24.
4. SLM backbone: WavLM layers 7–24 made causal (causal attention mask).
5. LM head: Linear layer predicting next-token distribution over codebook.
6. Training: Next-chunk prediction (input tokens left-shifted by C=4) with chunked causal attention and a context window of 512 tokens.
7. Inference: Sliding-window autoregressive generation with top-k sampling (k=30) and temperature 0.8.

### Critical path
Raw audio → FocalCodec-Stream tokenization → token stream → decompress to features → WavLM layers 7–24 (causal) → LM head → next token distribution → generate next chunk. The decompressor−WavLM feature compatibility is crucial; it reuses WavLM's pretrained knowledge.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Single vs. multi-stream codebook:** Single stream simplifies architecture but may struggle to disentangle semantics/acoustics. Multi-stream (e.g. LLaMA-Mimi) can specialize but requires complex interleaving.
- **Chunk size (C):** Larger C → faster inference but risks losing short-term dependency modeling (Table 3).
- **Context window:** Larger window improves long-range coherence (tSC↑, PPL↓) with mild compute cost; paper finds 512–2048 tokens sufficient.
- **Vocabulary size:** Larger vocab increases expressivity but may be harder to learn under fixed data; WavSLM-4k outperforms 65k.

### Failure signatures
- **Acoustic degradation:** Drop in speaker similarity (Sim↓) or UTMOS↓ indicates tokens lost fine-grained acoustic info.
- **Semantic degradation:** Drop in sWUGGY/sBLiMP/tSC indicates poor linguistic modeling.
- **Instability:** Large vocab (65k) shows higher PPL and worse generation metrics.
- **Streaming break:** Incorrect sliding-window masking causes context leakage.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablate chunk size:** Train WavSLM-4k with C=4 (default), 8, 16. Expect degradation in all metrics for C>4 per Table 3.
2. **Vary context window:** Evaluate with context lengths 256, 512 (default), 1024, 2048 tokens. Expect gradual improvement in tSC and PPL, but limited gain beyond 1024.
3. **Probe tokenization quality:** Compute reconstruction loss of WavLM-6 features from FocalCodec-Stream. Correlate with downstream SLM performance; worse reconstruction should predict lower consistency scores (Spk.↑, Sent.↑).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can better self-supervised speech representations alone match text-pretrained SLMs without scaling?
- **Basis:** Introduction questions if representation quality can replace scale/complexity.
- **Why unresolved:** Only WavLM tested; other SSL models may differ.
- **Resolving evidence:** Compare alternative SSL backbones (e.g. HuBERT, data2vec) in identical single-codebook SLM setups.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does codebook vocabulary size interact with data scale in single-stream SLMs?
- **Basis:** 65k vocab underperformed 2k/4k; larger vocab may need more data.
- **Why unresolved:** Not tested; unclear if more data or model capacity helps.
- **Resolving evidence:** Train models with varied vocab sizes (2k–65k) on different data amounts and compare metrics.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What chunk size optimizes the quality-speed trade-off in next-chunk prediction?
- **Basis:** Larger chunks (8,16) degraded quality but improved speed; only few sizes tested.
- **Why unresolved:** Intermediate and adaptive chunking unexplored; optimal may vary with constraints.
- **Resolving evidence:** Test chunk sizes 1–32, possibly dynamic, and measure quality, coherence, latency.

## Limitations
- **Tokenizer dependence:** FocalCodec-Stream’s architecture is not fully described; replication requires its external code and weights, which may not be readily available or compatible.
- **Training details opacity:** Exact schedule (epochs, early stopping, LR reset) is vague, potentially affecting convergence and final performance.
- **Single-dataset evaluation:** Models are trained and tested on read speech (Libri-Light/LibriSpeech); performance on spontaneous, multilingual, or noisy speech is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Single codebook sufficiency:** Medium. Competitive results but sensitive to vocabulary size; break condition not fully explored.
- **Next-chunk prediction (C=4):** High. Ablations (Table 3) show clear degradation for larger C, supporting the design.
- **Speech-only linguistic modeling:** Medium. Matches larger text-pretrained models on benchmarks, but size disparity and single dataset limit strength of claim.

## Next Checks
1. **Reconstruction fidelity:** Measure WavLM-6 feature reconstruction error from tokens and correlate with downstream performance to validate information preservation.
2. **WavLM layer ablation:** Distill from different WavLM layers (e.g., 3, 6, 12, 18) and train SLMs to test optimality of layer 6.
3. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Evaluate on non-read speech (e.g., dialogues, multilingual corpora) to assess robustness of emergent linguistic structure.
