# UNBOX: Unveiling Black-box visual models with Natural-language

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.08639  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08639  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A black-box interpretability method that uses LLM-driven textual refinement and diffusion models to recover semantic descriptors from classifier outputs, achieving competitive performance with white-box methods.

## Executive Summary
UNBOX addresses the interpretability gap for proprietary visual classifiers by reformulating activation maximization as a semantic search over natural language. Given only output probabilities, it iteratively refines text prompts via LLM agents, renders them with a diffusion model, and uses the classifier's feedback to guide the search. A dual-context mechanism stabilizes the optimization, and the process inherently exposes spurious correlations used by the model. Experiments show high semantic fidelity and effective bias slice discovery, demonstrating that black-box dissection is feasible without gradient access.

## Method Summary
UNBOX operates in a loop: a text prompt is converted to an image by a frozen diffusion model; the black-box classifier returns a probability for the target class. The probability's trend and intensity are transformed into discrete states that select instruction templates for two LLM agents—one provides critique, the other updates the prompt. Global and local contexts (accumulated high-scoring prompts and recent interactions) are fed to the agents to stabilize convergence and prevent drift. After optimization, high-confidence images are analyzed by a vision-language model to extract frequent visual descriptors, revealing the attributes the classifier associates with the class (including spurious cues). This entirely gradient-free approach requires only scalar feedback and textual manipulation.

## Key Results
- Semantic fidelity: mean descriptor-label similarity of 0.64 (ResNet-50) and 0.49 (ViT).
- Training data alignment: concept overlap scores of 0.63 and 0.45 respectively.
- Debiasing performance: 88.6% worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds and 90.01% on CelebA, competitive with white-box methods.
- Demonstrates effective slice discovery for bias auditing under strict black-box constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Gradient-free semantic search via textual refinement
Activation maximization is recast as an iterative search in text space, driven solely by the classifier's output probability. Each step: the current prompt is rendered to an image, the classifier outputs a scalar probability `s_t`. The temporal trend (`T_t`) and distance from a saturation threshold (`I_t`) are discretized into semantic states. These states index handcrafted natural-language templates that instruct two cooperating LLM agents: a Feedback Agent produces structured critique, and an Updater Agent revises the prompt. The loop navigates semantic space without gradients, relying on the assumption that textual edits can approximate gradient steps on the probability signal.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-context stabilization prevents drift and collapse
The optimization landscape is highly non-convex; pure signal-following leads to brittle behavior. UNBOX maintains two complementary contexts: (1) Global Optimization Context aggregates all prompts exceeding a high-confidence threshold (`τ_best`) and extracts persistent lexical units that individually activate the classifier strongly; (2) Local Optimization Context stores the last `k` prompt-feedback pairs. Both are supplied to the LLM agents as context, enabling them to reinforce high-reward concepts from global memory and avoid recently rejected directions from local history, ensuring coherent convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Inherent exposure of spurious correlations
During class-wise optimization, images that achieve high classifier confidence are fed to a vision-language model (LLaVA) to generate fine-grained textual descriptors. Aggregating these instance-level descriptors across iterations reveals the dominant contextual or attribute-based cues associated with the target class. Since the optimization converges on any visual pattern that maximally activates the classifier—including non-causal dataset artifacts—these aggregated cues naturally define coherent slices (e.g., "water" for "waterbird"), enabling bias discovery.

## Foundational Learning
- **Black-box gradient-free optimization**  
  *Why needed:* Only scalar output feedback is available; gradients, weights, and data are inaccessible. Understanding how to navigate a high-dimensional semantic space with a single number is essential.  
  *Quick check:* What risks arise from using a single scalar probability as the sole optimization signal in discrete, non-convex text space?

- **LLM agent coordination for textual editing**  
  *Why needed:* UNBOX relies on two LLMs (Feedback and Updater) with system prompts to perform meaningful, constrained linguistic edits. Grasping multi-agent prompt engineering is critical.  
  *Quick check:* How does separating critique generation and prompt updating in LLM agents prevent catastrophic forgetting or incoherent edits compared to a single-step approach?

- **Diffusion models as frozen perceptual proxies**  
  *Why needed:* The text-to-image generator translates text into perceptual space for evaluation; its fixed biases shape the optimization landscape.  
  *Quick check:* If the diffusion model has a strong prior (e.g., artistic style), how might that bias the discovered descriptors, and is this a bug or a feature for black-box dissection?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Black-box Classifier (ϕ) → Text-to-Image Generator (G) → Semantic Optimization Loop (Signal Transformer, Look-up Tables, Feedback Agent, Updater Agent) with Context Managers (Local Context, Global Context) → Slice Discovery Module (optional, using LLaVA).

**Critical path:** `p_t` → `G` → `x_t` → `ϕ` → `s_t` → `Signal Transformer` → `S_t` + `Contexts` → `A_f` → `e_t` → `A_u` → `p_{t+1}`. Any break (e.g., slow G, uninformative s_t) stalls progress.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Stability vs. exploration: Global Context stabilizes but risks narrow convergence; Local Context prevents cycling but may be myopic.  
- LLM cost vs. fidelity: Powerful LLMs yield coherent edits but are expensive and non-deterministic; cheaper models may produce repetitive prompts.  
- Diffusion step count: Single-step is fast but less detailed; more steps increase quality but amplify G's biases.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Oscillation: Frequent prompt undoings, unstable `P_best`/`D_best`.  
- Semantic collapse: Generic or irrelevant prompts.  
- Low activation plateau: `s_t` never exceeds high thresholds, indicating classifier relies on features not expressible in text.  
- Slice incoherence: Instance descriptors for a class are diverse and non-clustered, suggesting no stable spurious cue exploited.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Semantic Fidelity Test:** On a known subset (e.g., Salient ImageNet 30-class), compute similarity between top-5 UNBOX descriptors and ground-truth class labels.  
2. **Data Alignment Test:** Compare descriptors against keywords auto-extracted from training images (via captions) to validate they reflect the training distribution.  
3. **Slice Discovery & Debiasing Run:** On a bias benchmark (Waterbirds), use UNBOX to find slice-defining descriptors, convert to CLIP prototypes, label training data, retrain with DRO, and measure worst-group accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**How can UNBOX be extended to recover concepts that primarily depend on low-level visual features (e.g., textures, fine-grained patterns) that are challenging to capture with natural language?**  
*Basis:* The paper explicitly identifies "Sensitivity to low-level or non-semantic visual cues" as a limitation.  
*Why unresolved:* No remedy is offered; the method is restricted to high-level semantics.  
*Resolution evidence:* Develop hybrid approaches incorporating low-level descriptors; show improved fidelity on texture-driven categories with statistical significance.

### Open Question 2
**What architectural or algorithmic modifications can reduce the computational cost of UNBOX's iterative LLM-and-diffusion loop while maintaining descriptor quality?**  
*Basis:* The limitation section notes each step takes ~15 seconds, making large-scale use expensive.  
*Why unresolved:* No exploration of step budgets, distillation, or faster backbones.  
*Resolution evidence:* A cost-accuracy curve demonstrating >50% compute reduction with <5% drop in semantic fidelity, via minimal steps or model distillation.

### Open Question 3
**To what extent does UNBOX's output depend on the specific LLM, diffusion model, and handcrafted templates, and how can robustness be achieved?**  
*Basis:* Implementation relies on specific models (gpt-oss-120b, FLUX.1-schnell) and fixed templates; no sensitivity analysis.  
*Why unresolved:* Rapid model evolution could change behavior; without sensitivity understanding, generalizability is questionable.  
*Resolution evidence:* Cross-model experiments (multiple LLMs, diffusion models, templates) measuring variance in metrics; if high, propose learned policies or adversarial training to stabilize.

## Limitations
- **Narrow validation:** Evaluated on few classifier architectures (ResNet-50, ViT) and biased datasets (Waterbirds, CelebA); unknown generalizability to diverse models and real proprietary systems.
- **Signal reliability risk:** Scalar probability trend as optimization signal lacks theoretical grounding; may converge on classifier quirks rather than human-interpretable concepts.
- **Component biases:** Frozen diffusion model imposes strong priors that may confound whether discovered concepts reflect the classifier or the generator; LLM non-determinism harms reproducibility.

## Confidence
- Semantic fidelity & data alignment: **Medium** (strong on-paper metrics but limited cross-dataset/architecture tests)
- Slice discovery utility: **Medium** (ablations and qualitative support, but debiasing performance depends on external DRO retraining)
- Gradient-free optimization viability: **Low** (novel with compelling results but lacks white-box baselines and convergence guarantees)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture generalization:** Test UNBOX on at least three additional black-box classifiers (e.g., ConvNeXt, CLIP-based classifier, proprietary cloud API) and measure descriptor consistency.
2. **Controlled diffusion bias isolation:** Replace FLUX.1-schnell with a drastically different generator (e.g., pixel-art diffusion) and quantify shifts in descriptors; stable concepts strengthen evidence they come from the classifier.
3. **White-box comparative anchor:** Implement a white-box activation maximization method (e.g., differentiable generator optimization) for the same classes; compare resulting descriptors/images via human evaluation of semantic similarity and slice relevance.
