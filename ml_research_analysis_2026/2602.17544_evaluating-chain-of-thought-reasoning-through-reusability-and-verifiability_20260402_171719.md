# Evaluating Chain-of-Thought Reasoning through Reusability and Verifiability

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.17544
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17544
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: Reusability and verifiability metrics, measuring CoT quality for multi-model reuse and consistency, do not correlate with standard accuracy; general-purpose LLMs often match or exceed specialized reasoning models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical flaw in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) evaluation: reliance on final task accuracy alone ignores the quality of the reasoning process itself. The authors propose a Thinker-Executor framework and two new metrics—reusability (how well other models can adopt a CoT to change their answer) and verifiability (how consistently different models reach the same answer using the CoT). Evaluating four Thinker models across ten Executors and five benchmarks, they find these new metrics are uncorrelated with accuracy. Surprisingly, specialized reasoning models are not consistently more reusable or verifiable than general-purpose LLMs, exposing a major blind spot in current leaderboards that assess reasoning utility.

## Method Summary
The evaluation decouples reasoning generation (Thinker) from execution (Executor). For a given Thinker model and dataset, the system first generates CoTs and answers for all questions. Reusability is computed only on questions the Thinker got correct: it measures the proportion where an Executor’s answer flips after seeing the Thinker’s CoT—either correcting an error (helped) or introducing a new one (harmed) when given a corrupted CoT. Verifiability measures the frequency at which Executors produce the same final answer as the Thinker when provided with the Thinker’s CoT. Scores are averaged across a committee of ten Executors. Kendall’s τ is used to compare model rankings under accuracy, reusability, and verifiability.

## Key Results
- Reusability and verifiability scores show weak or negative correlation with standard accuracy across all datasets.
- General-purpose LLMs (e.g., Llama, Gemma) often achieve higher reusability and verifiability than specialized reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Phi4-reasoner), despite lower accuracy.
- The Thinker-Executor framework reveals that a model’s CoTs may be accurate but not necessarily clear, persuasive, or unambiguous for downstream use.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Reusability via Answer Flipping
- **Claim**: Reusability captures the persuasiveness of a Thinker’s CoT by measuring how often an Executor adopts the CoT to change its answer, either correcting a mistake (helped) or introducing an error (harmed).
- **Mechanism**: For questions where the Thinker is correct, two cases are evaluated: (a) If the Executor is wrong without CoT, appending the Thinker’s correct CoT should lead to a correct answer ("help"). (b) If the Executor is already correct, a plausible but corrupted CoT is substituted; if the Executor then answers incorrectly, this is a "harm" event. Reusability score is `R = (|helped| + |harmed|) / |Q_correct| × 100`.
- **Core assumption**: The Thinker’s correct CoT is a valid reasoning trace that other models can follow; the corrupted CoT is realistic enough to sway the Executor; Executors are neither omniscient nor completely incompetent.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract defines reusability; Section 2 formalizes with sets and equation; neighboring papers discuss CoT quality but not this flipping-based metric.
- **Break condition**: If `helped+harmed` is zero, the CoT is not reusable; if corruption never flips correct Executors, the corruption method may be flawed.

### Mechanism 2: Verifiability via Cross-Model Consistency
- **Claim**: Verifiability assesses how unambiguous a CoT is by checking whether different Executor models, given the same CoT, converge on the same final answer as the Thinker.
- **Mechanism**: For each question and Executor, the Executor is prompted with the Thinker’s CoT. The average match rate across the Executor committee is the verifiability score: `V = 100/|Q| Σ I(Ans(Executor, q, CoT_Thinker) == Ans(Thinker, q, CoT_Thinker))`.
- **Core assumption**: The CoT contains enough information to uniquely determine the answer; divergence is due to interpretational differences rather than missing information.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract defines verifiability; Section 2 gives equation; works on faithful reasoning aim to align reasoning with answer but do not measure cross-executor agreement.
- **Break condition**: Very low verifiability indicates ambiguity or reliance on model-specific knowledge; if some Executors always match while others never do, the CoT may be tailored.

### Mechanism 3: Accuracy-Utility Disconnect
- **Claim**: Standard accuracy leaderboards do not reflect reasoning utility captured by reusability and verifiability; these three evaluations produce uncorrelated rankings.
- **Mechanism**: Kendall’s τ between rankings of Thinker models based on accuracy, reusability, and verifiability shows weak or negative correlation (e.g., avg A vs. R = -0.53), proving that a model can be accurate yet produce CoTs that are not reusable or verifiable.
- **Core assumption**: The chosen datasets, Thinker models, and Executor committee are representative; Kendall’s τ is appropriate for ordinal association.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract notes the disconnect and surprise about specialized models; Section 3.1 Table 6 lists τ values; Table 5 shows per-model mismatches (e.g., Phi highest reusability on GSM8K despite not highest accuracy).
- **Break condition**: If future, larger-scale studies show strong positive correlations, the claim would be undermined. Inadequate committee diversity could also produce noisy correlations.

- **Why needed**: The paper evaluates CoT trace quality; CoT means eliciting intermediate reasoning steps to improve answer correctness.
- **Quick check**: What are the two main purported benefits of using CoT prompting, and how might those benefits be assessed beyond final accuracy?

#### Concept: Decoupled Reasoning Architecture
- **Why needed**: The Thinker-Executor framework separates generation from execution, enabling measurement of reasoning transferability.
- **Quick check**: How does a decoupled system (Thinker + Executor) differ from a single model generating its own CoT and answer, and what evaluation opportunities does it create?

#### Concept: Reusability vs Verifiability
- **Why needed**: These are the paper’s novel metrics; distinguishing them is essential to interpret results.
- **Quick check**: Provide a scenario where a CoT has high reusability but low verifiability, and another where it has high verifiability but low reusability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
Thinker service -> Orchestrator -> Executor pool -> Metric calculator

**Critical path**
1. Prepare Thinker outputs: generate CoT and answer for all questions; identify Q_correct.
2. Obtain baseline Executor answers (without CoT) for all questions.
3. Test reusability: For each (question, Executor) in Q_correct, if Executor wrong baseline, add Thinker’s CoT; if correct, use corrupted CoT. Record helped/harmed events.
4. Test verifiability: For each (question, Executor), prompt with Thinker’s CoT and check answer equality with Thinker’s answer.
5. Aggregate scores, compute Kendall’s τ, produce rankings.

**Design tradeoffs**
- *Executor committee strength*: More powerful executors raise absolute scores, but relative rankings may stabilize if some strong models are present. Including mixed strength reveals sensitivity.
- *Corruption method*: Must produce plausible yet wrong CoTs that flip answers. Too crude corruption may be detected; too subtle may not flip. Iterative testing required.
- *Cost scaling*: Reusability may require up to 2 calls per Executor per question; verifiability adds one more. Costs multiply with many Executors; mitigate by caching and batching.
- *Q_correct selection*: Only Thinker-correct questions used for reusability. Poor Thinker yields few questions, increasing variance; ensure sufficient size.

**Failure signatures**
- *Zero reusability*: All helped+harmed zero → either CoTs incomprehensible or Executors too weak/strong. Check baseline Executor accuracy distribution.
- *High helped but zero harmed*: Corruption ineffective. Inspect corrupted CoT plausibility; measure Executor accuracy on corrupted CoT.
- *Low verifiability*: CoTs likely ambiguous or rely on external knowledge. Inspect sample CoTs for vagueness.
- *Inconsistent rankings*: Kendall’s τ between Full and Strong committees significantly below 1 suggests committee composition heavily influences results; need stable capability spread.

**First 3 experiments**
1. Compute baseline scores: Thinker = Llama3.1-8B, Executor committee = Full, dataset = GSM8K. Expect reusability ~73, verifiability ~71.
2. Committee correlation: Same Thinker, compare rankings across Strong and Full committees via Kendall’s τ. Should be ~1.0 for reusability, ~0.8 for verifiability.
3. Accuracy vs utility mismatch: Pick Thinker with highest GSM8K accuracy (DeepSeek, 94%). Note its reusability (66.56) and verifiability (80.33) are not highest; Phi achieves higher despite lower accuracy (81%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can reusability and verifiability metrics be incorporated into LLM training and evaluation to enhance reasoning utility? Future research should integrate interaction-based metrics directly into the evaluation and training loops. Current evaluation relies on static accuracy benchmarks that fail to capture reasoning quality for multi-agent collaboration; operational integration remains unspecified. Evidence: algorithmic modifications (e.g., RL rewards based on these metrics), standardized benchmarks reporting both accuracy and these metrics, and demonstrations of improved multi-agent performance.

### Open Question 2
What specific characteristics of Chain-of-Thought outputs (e.g., length, step granularity, use of examples) explain why general-purpose LLMs sometimes generate more reusable and verifiable CoTs than specialized reasoning models? The paper finds specialized models not consistently superior but does not analyze CoT content. Without understanding features driving high reusability/verifiability, we cannot design models/prompts for multi-agent communication. Evidence: fine-grained linguistic/structural analysis of CoTs, correlations with scores, human evaluations of clarity/persuasiveness.

### Open Question 3
Does final answer agreement adequately capture verifiability, or might executors reach the same answer via different reasoning paths, leading to an overestimation of CoT clarity? Verifiability assumes same answer implies faithful execution, but executors could agree while ignoring or misinterpreting parts of the CoT. Thus, CoT may be ambiguous but appear verifiable. Evidence: require executors to output step-by-step reasoning; measure step-level consistency; or use adversarial perturbations to test robust following.

### Open Question 4
How robust and generalizable are the observed misalignments between accuracy and the proposed reusability/verifiability scores across different model families, scales, and tasks? Experiments involve four Thinker models, ten Executors, and five English reasoning benchmarks; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., encoder-decoders), larger scales, non-English, or non-reasoning tasks is unknown. Evidence: replicate with more diverse LLMs (including proprietary like GPT-4/Claude, varying sizes) and additional benchmarks (program-aided, multimodal); check if low correlations persist.

## Limitations
- Implementation details (prompt templates, corrupted CoT generation, answer extraction) are not fully disclosed, hindering exact reproduction.
- Results rely on a fixed committee of ten executors; rankings may shift with different compositions, especially if executors are uniformly strong or weak.
- Study covers only four Thinker models and five benchmarks; generalizability to broader model families and tasks remains uncertain.
- No direct human assessment of CoT quality exists to confirm that reusability/verifiability reflect meaningful reasoning utility.

## Confidence
- **C1 (metrics capture reasoning quality)**: Medium. Metrics are well-defined and behave as expected, but lack gold-standard validation.
- **C2 (metrics uncorrelated with accuracy)**: Medium. Kendall’s τ shows weak/no correlation across five datasets, but small model pool may inflate noise.
- **C3 (specialized models not superior)**: Medium. Result is consistent across datasets but could be artifact of limited specialized model selection.

## Next Checks
1. **Human evaluation**: Recruit experts to rate a stratified sample of CoTs for persuasiveness and clarity; compute correlation with reusability/verifiability scores.
2. **Scale up**: Extend to at least 10 Thinker models (various sizes/architectures) and 3 additional reasoning-heavy benchmarks; recompute correlations and rankings to test stability.
3. **Ablation on corruption**: Systematically vary corruption method (e.g., flipping intermediate steps, inserting subtle errors) and measure impact on reusability; ensure scores not overly sensitive to one strategy.
