# Robust Sequential Tracking via Bounded Information Geometry and Non-Parametric Field Actions
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13613  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13613  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** Resolves normalizability crisis in sequential inference by analytically truncating the parameter space into a finite probability droplet via bounded information geometry, yielding drastic error reductions (e.g., LiDAR tracking RMSE from 3.35m±0.34m to 0.67m±0.21m under severe ghost contamination).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a fundamental failure mode in Bayesian sequential tracking: when parameter spaces are unbounded, extreme outliers cause covariance inflation and mean divergence, leading to a loss of normalizability (the "normalizability crisis"). It introduces a mathematically grounded solution based on bounded information geometry. The core innovation anchors a non-parametric field action to a finite-volume base probability measure (the pre-prior) via the Radon‑Nikodym derivative, converting an ill‑posed infinite‑dimensional extremization into a well‑posed relative problem. This construction enforces a hard geometric constraint—derived from strictly invariant Delta (ν) Information Separations—that assigns exactly zero probability to points lying beyond a bounded droplet. The method natively enforces physical constraints (e.g., positive semi‑definiteness in quantum tomography) without post‑hoc projection, demonstrating robust performance across domains including LiDAR target tracking, cryptocurrency state estimation, and quantum state reconstruction.

## Method Summary
The method builds a meta‑prior π(*f*) over an infinite‑dimensional function space *F* by defining it as a Radon‑Nikodym derivative π = d*P*/d*P*⁰ relative to an integrable base measure *P*⁰ (the pre‑prior). This relativization circumvents the impossibility of a uniform volume element on *F*. The meta‑prior is derived by extremizing a (δ, ν)‑deformed action that combines a ν‑entropy term and (1−ν)‑deformed expectations. The extremum yields π(*f*) ∝ [1 − να *I*δ(*f*:*f*⁰)]^(1/ν), where *I*δ is a strictly invariant divergence and *f*⁰ is a reference in *F*. For π to be a valid density, the bracket must be non‑negative, imposing the hard constraint 1−να*I*δ ≥ 0. This geographically truncates the active parameter space into a finite droplet. For exponential family models (e.g., Gaussians), *I*δ simplifies via a closed‑form overlap integral *A*(δ), leading to a deterministic gating rule: a measurement contributes only if *A*(δ) ≥ 1 − δ(1−δ)/(να). The resulting algorithm iteratively re‑weights data, projects the state onto the bounded manifold, and fuses with a predicted state using a corrected Kalman gain.

## Key Results
- **LiDAR maneuvering target tracking:** Under severe +40m ghost contamination, mean RMSE dropped from 3.35m±0.34m (unconstrained) to 0.67m±0.21m; max error reduced similarly.
- **Cryptocurrency order flow:** Volatile state estimates and resulting turnover loss were structurally suppressed, indicating effective outlier rejection in high‑frequency financial data.
- **Quantum state tomography:** Reconstructions are strictly positive semi‑definite without any post‑hoc projection, eliminating unphysical negative eigenvalues that plague maximum‑likelihood estimation under noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Anchoring to a Finite Base Measure
The normalizability crisis stems from the lack of a translation‑invariant Lebesgue measure on an infinite‑dimensional space (Riesz’s Lemma). The method resolves this by defining the meta‑prior π(*f*) as a Radon‑Nikodym derivative with respect to a finite‑volume base measure *P*⁰ (the pre‑prior). This relativization converts the ill‑posed absolute problem into a well‑posed relative one on a space of finite total mass. The existence of a suitable *P*⁰ (e.g., via a reproducing kernel Banach space from a generalized Gaussian on the tangent space) is the core assumption; if *P*⁰ itself has infinite mass or is poorly regularized, the approach fails.

### Mechanism 2: Geometric Truncation via Hard Constraint
The constraint 1−να*I*δ ≥ 0 arises from the requirement that π(*f*) be a non‑negative density. The Delta Information Separation *I*δ is a strictly invariant divergence; when π is expressed as [1−να*I*δ]^(1/ν), the bracket must be ≥0 for all *f* to avoid negative densities. This creates a deterministic, topological cutoff: parameter states with *I*δ > 1/(να) are assigned exactly zero probability, regardless of tail decay. The truncation is therefore structural and not probabilistic. Its effectiveness depends on *I*δ properly reflecting "distance" from the core distribution; mis‑specification of the divergence metric δ or the reference *f*⁰ could either discard valid states or fail to remove true outliers.

### Mechanism 3: Closed‑Form Gaussian Gate
For exponential families (Gaussians), the overlap integral *A*(δ) and *I*δ admit a closed form involving a δ‑weighted mixture covariance Σδ. The truncation condition becomes *A*(δ) ≥ 1 − δ(1−δ)/(να). This yields a deterministic, analytic gating rule that assigns zero weight to points failing the inequality. The rule is implemented as a fixed‑point iteration: compute *A*(δ) per point, apply hard zeroing, re‑estimate mean and covariance with compression (adding a minimal covariance *R*ₘᵢₙ). This is a topological truncation, not a probabilistic χ² gate, and is computationally tractable. The closed form relies critically on the exponential family structure; for non‑Gaussian models the integral may become intractable, requiring approximations that could compromise the hard constraint.

## Architecture Onboarding
The algorithm consists of four conceptual components arranged in a critical path:

**Component map:**  
Prediction (Pre‑prior Generator) -> Geometric Gate (Δ*I*δ Evaluator) -> Manifold Projector (Fixed‑Point Iterator) -> Precision Corrector (Kalman Gain Calculator)

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate a predicted state (µ⁰, Σ⁰) that defines the pre‑prior *P*⁰ and reference *f*⁰.  
2. For each incoming measurement, compute the generalized overlap *A*(δ) (or *I*δ) against the current spatial prior (µₖ, Σₖ). Apply the hard truncation: if *A*(δ) < 1 − δ(1−δ)/(να) set weight = 0, else weight = *A*(δ).  
3. Iterate: normalize weights, compute weighted mean µₖ₊₁, compute weighted covariance and compress with minimal covariance *R*ₘᵢₙ to ensure positive definiteness. Repeat until convergence.  
4. Fuse the converged bounded spatial state (µₖ₊₁, Σₖ₊₁) with the full predicted state (µ⁰, Σ⁰) via a standard Kalman update using the bounded covariance in the innovation term.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Hyperparameters (δ, ν, α):** δ controls geometric deformation; ν controls entropy form (0≤ν≤1); α scales the energetic threshold. They must match the typical separation between valid measurements and the pre‑prior. Too small α → over‑truncation; too large α → under‑truncation.  
- **Base measure *P*⁰:** Must be broad enough to cover plausible states yet finite‑volume. Often a wide Gaussian from the kinematic model. Choice directly affects the truncation boundary and reference *f*⁰.  
- **Computational cost vs. accuracy:** Closed‑form *A*(δ) for Gaussians is cheap; for non‑exponential families numerical integration may be needed, potentially compromising the hard constraint and re‑introducing tail sensitivity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Empty droplet (W ≈ 0):** All points truncated; system retains only the strict prior. This is a correct response to total contamination but indicates possible model mismatch or severe outlier cluster.  
- **Non‑convergent iteration:** Weights oscillate or change slowly. Suggests parameter mismatch (α too large/small) or multi‑modal data where the bounded droplet attempts to split.  
- **Persistent non‑PD covariance:** After projection, Σ may lose positive definiteness due to numerical error; requires regularization (adding *R*ₘᵢₙ).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic 1D Gaussian truncation:** Sample N(0,1) plus a single outlier at x=100. Vary α and verify that for appropriate α the outlier receives zero weight and the posterior recovers the clean mean (0) and variance (1).  
2. **Replicate LiDAR Monte Carlo:** Implement Algorithm 1 with the parabolic maneuver and +40m ghost. Verify RMSE reduction to ~0.67m±0.21m over 1000 trials.  
3. **Quantum tomography single qubit:** Reconstruct a pure state |0⟩ from noisy Pauli measurements (σ=0.5). Confirm that the bounded reconstruction yields a positive semi‑definite density matrix without post‑hoc projection; vary α to show transition from unphysical (MLE‑like) to physical estimates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Automatic hyperparameter tuning:** How can δ, ν, α be set automatically across data regimes? The paper provides no selection method, blocking reliable deployment.
- **Convergence of fixed‑point iteration:** Does Algorithm 1 converge for arbitrary initial conditions and data? What is its convergence rate? No theoretical or empirical analysis is given.
- **Non‑Gaussian extension:** How to compute Δ*I*δ for non‑exponential family models? The closed‑form *A*(δ) is Gaussian‑specific; general likelihoods require approximations that may violate the hard constraint.
- **Principled base measure selection:** What is the principled way to choose *P*⁰? The paper uses ad hoc choices (e.g., maximally mixed prior, predicted state) without general justification.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters δ, ν, α are undisclosed for key experiments, preventing replication and obscuring sensitivity.
- Construction of the integrable base measure *P*⁰ for non‑Gaussian models is unspecified, undermining guarantees outside the exponential family.
- Closed‑form truncation relies on exponential family structure; for general likelihoods numerical integration may break the hard constraint and re‑introduce tail sensitivity.

## Confidence
- **Anchored Radon‑Nikodym action resolves normalizability:** Medium – mathematically motivated but depends on existence of a suitable *P*⁰; no full measure‑theoretic proof for infinite‑dimensional *F*.
- **Geometric truncation via 1−να*I*δ≥0:** Medium‑High – mechanism is explicit and enforces a hard cutoff, but effectiveness hinges on correct δ, ν specification.
- **Empirical dominance across domains:** Medium – results are striking but cannot be verified without hyperparameters; neighboring corpus shows alignment on the robustness problem but not on this specific solution.
