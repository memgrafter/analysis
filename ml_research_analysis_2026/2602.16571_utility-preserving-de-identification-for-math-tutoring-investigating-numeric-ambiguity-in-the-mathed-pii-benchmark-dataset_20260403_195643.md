# Utility-Preserving De-Identification for Math Tutoring: Investigating Numeric Ambiguity in the MathEd-PII Benchmark Dataset
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16571
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16571
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Domain-aware prompting (segment-aware) reduces numeric false positives by over 99.5% while maintaining recall, improving F1 from 0.379 to 0.821.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses a critical failure mode in de-identifying math tutoring transcripts: generic PII detectors misclassify mathematical expressions (e.g., "x=5", "3/4") as dates, IDs, or other sensitive data, severely degrading dataset utility. The authors construct the MathEd-PII benchmark—a large, annotated corpus of 1,000 tutoring sessions—through a human-in-the-loop LLM workflow that audits prior redactions and generates privacy-preserving surrogates. Using a density-based segmentation method, they demonstrate that 55.5% of false positives are concentrated in math-dense regions, which comprise only 36.6% of tokens. Their results show that incorporating domain awareness via prompting dramatically improves performance: segment-aware prompting reduces DATE false positives by >99.5% and achieves an F1-score of 0.821, versus 0.379 for a standard tool like Microsoft Presidio. The work establishes that effective de-identification in specialized domains requires explicit modeling of contextual ambiguity.

## Method Summary
The study’s methodology centers on creating and evaluating a domain-specific benchmark and detection strategies. First, the MathEd-PII dataset was constructed from already-redacted tutoring transcripts. Because original unredacted data was unavailable, a human-in-the-loop LLM workflow was used: an LLM initially audited upstream redactions and suggested synthetic surrogates; human annotators then validated and refined these, producing reliable token-level PII annotations and privacy-preserving replacements. Next, a density-based segmentation algorithm isolated math-dense transcript segments by computing a math density score per message (using a math vocabulary and regex patterns) and expanding from anchor messages via semantic similarity. Finally, four PII detection approaches were evaluated on the benchmark: a Presidio baseline, and LLM-based detectors with basic, math-aware, and segment-aware prompting. The segment-aware prompt incorporated a binary label (MATH/NON-MATH) from the segmentation to condition the LLM's decisions.

## Key Results
*   Generic detectors exhibit severe numeric ambiguity: 55.5% of false positives occur in math-dense segments, which cover only 36.6% of tokens.
*   Math-aware prompting substantially improves overall F1 from 0.379 (Presidio) to 0.821, demonstrating the value of domain knowledge.
*   Segment-aware prompting further reduces numeric false positives—DATE false positives drop by over 99.5%—while maintaining recall, achieving precision gains without sacrificing recall.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The human-in-the-loop LLM workflow produces a reliable benchmark dataset (MathEd-PII) even when original unredacted data is unavailable.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs first evaluate upstream redactions and suggest surrogates; human annotators then validate and refine these outputs, correcting false positives/negatives and ensuring contextually appropriate synthetic replacements. This iterative process yields a clean, annotated corpus.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can generate plausible surrogates consistent with dialogue context, and human oversight can effectively filter residual errors.

- **Break condition:** Inconsistent human annotations or incoherent LLM surrogates would introduce noise into the benchmark, compromising its utility for evaluation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Density-based segmentation isolates math-dense transcript regions, confirming numeric ambiguity as a structural failure mode and providing contextual signals for detection.
- **Mechanism:** Math density per message is computed via a weighted sum of math vocabulary and regex patterns; messages exceeding a threshold become anchors, and adjacent messages are added based on semantic similarity. This reveals that 55.5% of false positives occur in math-dense segments (36.6% of tokens).
- **Core assumption:** Mathematical discourse can be distinguished from conversational text using domain-specific terms and semantic coherence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Using a density-based segmentation method, we show that false PII redactions are disproportionately concentrated in math-dense regions..."
  - [section] "Section 5.1 describes the math density calculation, anchor thresholding, and similarity-based expansion."
- **Break condition:** Incomplete math vocabulary or poorly chosen thresholds would mis-segment messages, leading to inaccurate error attribution and suboptimal context for prompts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Segment-aware prompting, which conditions the LLM detector on the math segment label, dramatically reduces numeric false positives while preserving recall, achieving F1 up to 0.821.
- **Mechanism:** The detection prompt includes a "math label" (MATH/NON-MATH) derived from segmentation. The LLM uses this signal to treat numeric expressions in MATH messages as likely mathematical content, not PII, thus filtering false positives. DATE false positives drop by >99.5%.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can incorporate explicit contextual metadata into detection decisions, and the segmentation accurately reflects message nature.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "segment-aware prompting further reducing numeric false positives—e.g., DATE false positives drop by over 99.5%—while maintaining recall."
  - [section] "Section 6.2.3 shows that in MATH segments, precision improves from 0.142 (Presidio) to 0.819 (segment-aware) while recall remains comparable."
- **Break condition:** Incorrect segment labels (from segmentation failure) would mislead the LLM, potentially increasing false negatives or failing to reduce false positives.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Source corpus (already redacted transcripts) -> Human-in-the-loop surrogation module (LLM + validation UI) -> MathEd-PII benchmark -> Math segmentation module (density calculation + similarity expansion) -> PII detection module (Presidio baseline; LLM with basic/math-aware/segment-aware prompts) -> Evaluation engine (metrics, bootstrap confidence intervals)
- **Critical path:** Source corpus -> surrogation -> segmentation -> detection -> evaluation. The segmentation output (math label) is a required input for segment-aware detection.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Precision vs. recall: Domain-aware prompting gains precision with minimal recall trade-off.
  - Human effort vs. automation: Surrogation requires substantial human validation but ensures benchmark quality.
  - Computational cost: LLM inference is expensive; segmentation adds negligible overhead.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High false positive rate in math segments when using generic detectors (numeric ambiguity).
  - Low inter-annotator agreement in surrogation validation indicates ambiguous PII definitions.
  - Segmentation producing too many/too few math messages (adjust thresholds).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Validate the human-in-the-loop workflow: annotate 100 messages, compute agreement (e.g., Cohen's kappa) between LLM and humans, iterate prompt until >95% resolution of down-voted issues.
  2.  Optimize segmentation thresholds: grid search over anchor and similarity thresholds to maximize the harmonic mean of false positive capture and (1 - true positive capture) as described in Section 5.1.
  3.  Baseline comparison: run Presidio and LLM with basic prompt on the benchmark, compute overall F1 and per-category precision/recall to establish the numeric ambiguity problem.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How generalizable are the findings on numeric ambiguity and the effectiveness of domain-aware prompting to math tutoring datasets from diverse educational systems, curricula, and international contexts?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors explicitly state that MathEd-PII is based on 1,000 sessions from a single U.S.-based tutoring platform and note that expanding to more diverse contexts is a key future direction to improve generalizability.
- **Why unresolved:** The dataset’s limited scope may not capture the full variability of math tutoring dialogues globally, including different mathematical notations, PII types, and conversational styles, which could affect both the prevalence of numeric ambiguity and the performance of domain-aware methods.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Constructing or evaluating on additional benchmarks sourced from multiple tutoring platforms, different countries, and varied grade levels to test whether the observed performance gains from math-aware and segment-aware prompting persist universally.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does a performance gap between math and non-math segments persist even with segment-aware prompting, and can it be entirely closed?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 5 shows that segment-aware prompting improves precision in math segments but a gap in F1-score between math (e.g., 0.705) and non-math (0.860) segments remains for Gemini 3 Pro.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the residual gap is due to limitations in the segmentation method, insufficient contextual cues in the prompt, or inherent ambiguity in mathematical discourse that current prompting strategies cannot fully resolve.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting a fine-grained error analysis of remaining false negatives and positives within math segments to identify specific patterns; experimenting with alternative segmentation techniques or richer context injection (e.g., full dialogue history) to determine if the gap can be narrowed.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the use of LLM-generated surrogate text in the MathEd-PII benchmark bias the evaluation of PII detection models compared to a benchmark built from real, original transcripts?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The dataset construction relied on a human-in-the-loop LLM workflow to generate surrogates because original unredacted data was unavailable, which may alter the linguistic distribution and ambiguity patterns of the text.
- **Why unresolved:** Surrogates might not perfectly mimic the surface forms, contextual coherence, or numeric ambiguity present in real math tutoring dialogues, potentially leading to over- or under-estimation of model performance in practical deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** If ethically feasible, comparing model performance on a small, privacy-protected held-out set of real annotations against the surrogate-based benchmark; alternatively, rigorous human evaluation of surrogate realism and correlation with model error rates.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Generalizability:** The MathEd-PII benchmark's reliability depends on the unquantified inter-annotator agreement from the human-in-the-loop surrogation process. Its construction from already-redacted data (no original unredacted access) may embed upstream biases.
*   **Methodological Scope:** Results are tied to specific LLM versions (e.g., "Claude 4.5 Opus"), prompting strategies, and segmentation thresholds (`T_anchor=0.05`, `T_sim=0.3`). Performance on other tutoring domains (e.g., algebra vs. calculus) or with different student demographics is unknown.
*   **External Validity:** The study demonstrates effectiveness on a curated, high-quality benchmark. Transfer to real-world, noisier tutoring transcripts with varied redaction pipelines remains unvalidated.

## Confidence
*   **Benchmark Utility (MathEd-PII creation):** Medium. Strong internal process description, but lack of agreement metrics and reliance on a single domain limit confidence in its universal reliability.
*   **Numeric Ambiguity Concentration:** High. The density-based segmentation's finding—55.5% of false positives in 36.6% of tokens—is empirically supported and statistically significant within the study's context.
*   **Segment-Aware Prompting Efficacy:** High for demonstrated setup. The >99.5% reduction in DATE false positives with maintained recall is a robust result on the benchmark, though generalizability to other models/pipelines is Medium.

## Next Checks

2.  **Test Segmentation Robustness:** Re-run the density-based segmentation on an external, unannotated math tutoring corpus (e.g., from UPchieve). Manually inspect 100 randomly sampled "MATH" segment messages to verify >90% contain authentic mathematical discourse, confirming the method's domain portability.
3.  **Replicate with Heterogeneous Models:** Evaluate the segment-aware prompt on at least two distinct, state-of-the-art LLM families (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) not used in the paper. Successful replication (F1 >0.80, DATE FP reduction >95%) would validate the approach beyond the specific models tested.
