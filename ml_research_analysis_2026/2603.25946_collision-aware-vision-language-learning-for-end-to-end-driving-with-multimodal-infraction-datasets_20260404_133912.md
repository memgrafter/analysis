# Collision-Aware Vision-Language Learning for End-to-End Driving with Multimodal Infraction Datasets

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25946  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25946  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** VLAAD, a lightweight vision-language anomaly detector, yields a 14.12% relative improvement in CARLA Town 13 driving score with minimal fine‑tuning and outperforms a multi‑billion‑parameter VLM by 23.3% AUC on the Real‑Collide benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Collisions remain the dominant bottleneck in end‑to‑end autonomous driving, yet learning to anticipate them is under‑studied. This paper introduces VLAAD, a vision‑language anomaly detector that leverages Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) to produce temporally localized, stable collision risk signals. To enable training and evaluation, the authors contribute two multimodal datasets: CARLA‑Collide (simulated collisions across diverse scenarios) and Real‑Collide (annotated dashcam videos). VLAAD is designed as a plug‑in module for a pretrained TransFuser++ driving agent, requiring only minimal fine‑tuning. In closed‑loop evaluation on CARLA Town 13, VLAAD achieves a 14.12% relative improvement in the official leaderboard driving score. On the held‑out Real‑Collide benchmark, the 0.6B‑parameter VLAAD surpasses a multi‑billion‑parameter vision‑language model by 23.3% in AUC, underscoring the efficacy of its MIL‑derived, temporally precise signals for real‑world safety‑critical driving.

## Method Summary
VLAAD consists of a frozen XCLIP video encoder, a lightweight trainable MLP video adapter, and a binary collision detector head. During training, it uses two objectives: (1) a cosine embedding loss that aligns the adapted video embedding with a frozen text embedding from a caption describing the collision context, and (2) a binary cross‑entropy loss. To localize collision risk within a video clip, VLAAD employs a Multiple Instance Learning formulation where each clip is divided into short snippets (e.g., 1‑second segments). Snippet‑level logits are pooled via Log‑Sum‑Exp with temperature γ to produce a clip‑level score; gradients emphasize high‑scoring snippets, yielding sharp temporal peaks aligned with collision events. For driving integration, the pretrained VLAAD (frozen) processes a causal buffer of past frames and outputs a scalar risk score each timestep. This score is concatenated with vehicle velocity and discrete navigation commands to form an augmented global state for the TransFuser++ agent’s transformer decoder. The decoder then conditions on the risk token to modulate waypoint and speed predictions. The entire system is fine‑tuned for a few epochs on collision‑rich data, enabling the driving policy to exploit the safety‑critical signal without relearning low‑level perception.

## Key Results
- VLAAD‑MIL improves CARLA Town 13 driving score by **14.12%** relative to the baseline TransFuser++ agent after minimal fine‑tuning.
- On the Real‑Collide test set, VLAAD (0.6 B parameters) achieves an **AUC of 0.766**, outperforming a multi‑billion‑parameter VLM by **23.3%**.
- Ablations show that removing the vision‑language alignment loss degrades Real‑Collide AUC from 0.766 to 0.721, highlighting the importance of multimodal training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: MIL Concentrates Collision Evidence
- **Claim:** Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) concentrates collision evidence within temporally precise snippets, producing a stable, localized risk signal that improves closed‑loop robustness.
- **Mechanism:** MIL treats each video clip as a “bag” of short snippets (e.g., 1‑second segments). The model learns to assign high collision probability only to snippets containing the collision event (or its precursors), while keeping normal snippets near zero. A temperature‑controlled Log‑Sum‑Exp pooling aggregates snippet logits into a video‑level score, but gradients emphasize high‑scoring snippets, sharpening temporal focus. This yields a risk signal that peaks around the collision moment rather than diffusing over the entire clip.
- **Core assumption:** Collision‑relevant visual cues are temporally localized within a short, identifiable window; non‑collision frames provide negative supervision to suppress false positives.
- **Evidence anchors:**

  [section 3.2] Detailed MIL formulation and LSE pooling; Figure 4 shows MIL produces sharp peaks aligned with collisions while the non‑MIL baseline is diffuse.  
  [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., VLM‑AD) use VLMs for driving but not MIL for temporal localization; this is a novel application to collision risk.

### Mechanism 2: Vision‑Language Alignment Steers Features
- **Claim:** Vision‑language alignment during training steers the visual encoder toward semantically and safety‑relevant features, improving collision detection beyond video‑only models.
- **Mechanism:** VLAAD uses a frozen XCLIP backbone with a lightweight video adapter. Training combines a cosine embedding loss (aligning adapted video embeddings with frozen text embeddings from captions) and binary cross‑entropy. The textual captions describe collision contexts (e.g., “a car collides with a pedestrian”), providing a high‑level semantic scaffold that helps the model ignore irrelevant visual variations (e.g., lighting, background) and focus on actors and interactions indicative of risk.
- **Core assumption:** Textual descriptions of collisions capture invariances that purely visual learning might miss; language acts as a regularizer that emphasizes causal elements of dangerous scenes.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “VLAAD, a lightweight vision‑language model … leveraging multimodel cues … to detect collision events.”  
  [section 3.1] Architecture with dual encoders and joint losses; Table 3 shows removing text (w/o Text) drops AUC from 0.766 to 0.721.  
  [section D.2] Loss function uses homoscedastic uncertainty weighting of L<sub>cos</sub> and L<sub>bce</sub>.

### Mechanism 3: Risk Token Conditions Pretrained Policy
- **Claim:** Embedding VLAAD’s collision risk score as a conditioning token in a pretrained end‑to‑end driving agent’s global state improves closed‑loop safety with minimal fine‑tuning because it provides an explicit, high‑level safety prior that the driving policy can exploit without relearning low‑level perception.
- **Mechanism:** The VLAAD module (frozen after training) processes a short clip buffer to output a scalar collision risk score each timestep. This score is concatenated with the vehicle’s velocity and discrete navigation commands, forming an augmented global state that is fed to the driving model’s transformer decoder. The decoder then attends to this risk token when predicting waypoints and speed, allowing the policy to modulate actions (e.g., decelerate, plan evasive paths) based on anticipated collisions.
- **Core assumption:** The driving model’s architecture can effectively incorporate a single scalar risk token into its decision‑making; the pretrained weights already encode sufficient driving competence that only a small number of fine‑tuning steps are needed to adapt to the new signal.
- **Evidence anchors:**

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
- **VLAAD Module:** Frozen XCLIP video encoder → Video Adapter (MLP) → Collision Detector (MLP). During training: also uses frozen text encoder and MIL snippet bagging. During inference: processes a buffer of past frames (8 frames at 4 Hz) to output a single risk score per timestep.
- **Driving Agent (TransFuser++):** Multi‑sensor encoders (RGB, LiDAR) → Global Encoder (processes ego state + VLAAD risk token) → Transformer Decoder → Waypoints and speed predictions.
- **Data Pipeline:** CARLA‑Collide (simulated collision clips with auto‑generated captions) and Real‑Collide (real dashcam clips) for training VLAAD. SimLingo normal driving clips for non‑collision samples. Infraction logs for clip labeling.

### Critical Path
1. At each simulation timestep, collect the last K frames (e.g., 8 frames) into a buffer.
2. Pass buffer through VLAAD’s video encoder and adapter to get snippet embeddings (if using MIL) or directly to detector.
3. With MIL: compute snippet logits, pool via LSE to a clip‑level risk score. Without MIL: direct clip‑level score.
4. Concatenate risk score with velocity and discrete navigation commands.
5. Feed augmented global state to driving model’s global encoder.
6. Transformer decoder fuses global state with sensor features to predict waypoints and speed.
7. Execute control command in CARLA simulator.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Frozen backbone vs. full fine‑tuning:** Freezing XCLIP preserves general multimodal knowledge and reduces overfitting/compute, but limits adaptation to synthetic (CARLA) domain. Evidence: Table 4 shows baseline (w/o FT) performs near‑random on Town 13; fine‑tuning backbone helps.
- **MIL granularity:** Smaller snippets give finer localization but may miss context; larger snippets risk diluting the signal. The paper uses 1‑second snippets (4 frames at 4 Hz).
- **Integration depth:** Adding risk token to global state is minimally invasive but may not fully exploit spatial features. Alternative: injecting into decoder cross‑attention, but that would require more fine‑tuning.
- **Dataset composition:** CARLA‑Collide uses diverse collision scenarios from TF++ failures, but these are still simulated; Real‑Collide is small (1k clips) and curated, limiting real‑world diversity.

### Failure Signatures
- **False positives:** VLAAD fires on normal aggressive maneuvers (e.g., fast U‑turns, close passes), causing the driving model to be overly cautious, reducing route completion. Evidence: Figure 6 shows small bumps on U‑turns but below threshold; threshold mis‑setting could amplify this.
- **False negatives:** Missed collisions due to unusual viewpoints (e.g., RetroTrucks ego‑view) or rare collision types not in training, leading to unchanged baseline collision rates.
- **Temporal misalignment:** Risk signal peaks too early or too late relative to collision onset, giving the driving model insufficient reaction time. Check snippet‑level plots (Figures 4,7).
- **Domain gap:** Simulator‑to‑reality gap: VLAAD trained on CARLA‑Collide may not generalize to Real‑Collide without real‑data fine‑tuning (Table 3 shows Real‑Collide AUC 0.766 vs. CARLA‑Collide AUC 0.672).
- **Training instability:** MIL’s LSE pooling with inappropriate temperature γ could cause gradient saturation or vanishing. The paper uses γ=10.0.

1. **Reproduce VLAAD training on CARLA‑Collide:** Train the MIL variant on the training split, validate on the held‑out Town 13 clips. Report AUC, F1, Accuracy. Ensure loss decreases and snippet‑level localization visualizations show sharp peaks on collision examples.
2. **Closed‑loop integration test:** Load pretrained TF++ checkpoint, add VLAAD‑MIL token, fine‑tune for 5 epochs on CARLA‑Collide training data. Evaluate on Town 13 routes (20 routes, 3 seeds). Compare Driving Score, Route Completion, Collisions/km to baseline TF++ and TF++ fine‑tuned without VLAAD. Expect DS improvement ~14% relative.
3. **Real‑world generalization check:** Evaluate the same VLAAD model (trained only on simulated CARLA‑Collide + normal SimLingo) on the Real‑Collide test set. Report AUC. Expect performance drop compared to CARLA‑Collide but still above random. If AUC is below 0.6, investigate domain gap (e.g., Real‑Collide visual styles, caption mismatch).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does training the driving policy from scratch with the VLAAD‑MIL signal reduce collision rates but increase non‑collision infractions (e.g., traffic rule violations), and how can this trade‑off be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** Appendix A.3 reports that from‑scratch training with VLAAD‑MIL achieves lower collisions/km but a lower Infraction Score due to more non‑collision infractions, suggesting “causal confusion” where the model overfits to the collision‑avoidance signal at the expense of other driving rules.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper speculates on causal confusion but lacks analysis of its root cause (e.g., whether the collision signal introduces spurious correlations or masks other supervisory cues) and does not propose remedies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies varying the strength of the VLAAD signal during training, probing model attention on non‑collision scenarios, or architectural modifications (e.g., multi‑task heads for different infraction types) to disentangle collision avoidance from rule compliance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How well does VLAAD generalize to entirely unseen CARLA towns (beyond Town 13) and to diverse real‑world geographies not represented in the curated Real‑Collide dataset?
- **Basis in paper:** Evaluation is limited to Town 13 (unseen during training) and Real‑Collide (curated from four sources). The paper notes Real‑Collide emphasizes quality/diversity over scale, but does not test geographic generalization (e.g., to Towns 1–11 in CARLA or to cities with different road layouts/regulations).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper’s generalization claim is based on a single held‑out town and a small real‑world benchmark. Robust autonomy requires broader generalization across environments.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across all CARLA towns (including those with distinct topologies like Town 10’s urban sprawl vs. Town 4’s interchanges) and on larger, geographically diverse real‑world dashcam datasets (e.g., from different countries or rural areas).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the proposed MIL‑based temporal localization framework be effectively extended to detect and localize non‑collision safety‑critical infractions (e.g., red‑light running, stop‑sign violations) using similar multimodal cues?
- **Basis in paper:** The method is designed exclusively for collision detection, despite the paper acknowledging that other infractions (e.g., red‑light, yield) exist and sometimes follow collisions. The MIL formulation is tailored to collision events via snippet‑level risk concentration.
- **Why unresolved:** The framework’s ability to capture rule‑based violations—which may have different temporal signatures (e.g., gradual approach to intersection vs. sudden impact)—is unexplored. Extending it could improve overall infraction scores.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Retraining VLAAD‑MIL on a dataset annotated with diverse infraction types (e.g., using CARLA‑Collide’s scenario metadata or new real‑world labels) and evaluating snippet‑level localization accuracy for non‑collision events against baselines.

## Limitations
- **Architecture ambiguities:** Exact detector head structure, risk token projection, and fine‑tuning details are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- **MIL hyperparameters:** Snippet duration/count, buffer size *K*, and LSE temperature γ sensitivity are not thoroughly explored; choices may be suboptimal.
- **Dataset constraints:** Real‑Collide is small (1 k clips) and curated; CARLA‑Collide is synthetic. Domain shift remains a barrier to real‑world deployment.

## Confidence
- **VLAAD detection on simulated data:** High – closed‑loop improvement and ablations are consistent.
- **MIL temporal localization:** Medium – qualitative figures show sharper peaks but no quantitative localization metric; break conditions not fully tested.
- **Real‑world generalization:** Low – Real‑Collide performance drops relative to simulated data and may not reflect broader real‑world diversity.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate MIL design:** Systematically vary snippet length (0.5–2 s) and number of snippets; evaluate snippet‑level F1 and closed‑loop driving score to identify robust settings.
2. **Test transferability:** Integrate VLAAD into other end‑to‑end agents (e.g., LAV) and evaluate on multiple CARLA towns (e.g., 05, 12) to confirm improvement generalizes beyond TransFuser++ on Town 13.
3. **False positive audit:** Run VLAAD on diverse normal driving logs (e.g., nuScenes, human‑driven data); compute alert rate under thresholds; adjust training or threshold to avoid excessive caution.
