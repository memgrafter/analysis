# Where Bits Matter in World Model Planning: A Paired Mixed-Bit Study for Efficient Spatial Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11882
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11882
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Mixed-bit allocation preserving encoder precision improves 4-bit world model planning success on the Wall task, revealing encoder as a primary bottleneck in the sensitive 4-bit regime.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how bit allocation between encoder and predictor modules affects world model planning reliability under quantization. Using DINO-WM on the Wall planning task, a paired-goal mixed-bit evaluation compares uniform, mixed (encoder FP16, predictor quantized), and asymmetric quantizations under two planner budgets. Results show a consistent three-regime pattern: 8-bit and 6-bit settings maintain performance near FP16, 3-bit settings collapse completely, and 4-bit settings are highly sensitive to allocation. In the 4-bit transition regime, mixed INT4 and encoder-heavy asymmetric variants (e.g., E6/P4) achieve higher success than uniform INT4 at comparable model sizes, indicating encoder precision is a primary bottleneck. The direction of mixed-versus-uniform INT4 gains is robust across planner budgets but remains partially confounded by total precision and exhibits budget-conditioned reversals in low-sample replications, underscoring the fragility of the 4-bit frontier.

## Method Summary
The study applies post-training weight-only quantization symmetrically per output channel to all `nn.Linear` layers in DINO-WM’s encoder and predictor modules, without calibration or retraining. A paired-goal evaluation framework tests each variant (uniform, mixed, asymmetric) on identical goal sets across two planner budgets (bA: H=9, opt steps=2, max iter=2; bB: H=12, opt steps=3, max iter=3), with 10 episodes per seed. Success rates are compared via paired deltas using bootstrap confidence intervals and sign-tests. This design isolates bit allocation effects while controlling for total precision and planner constraints, using a pretrained DINO-WM checkpoint and the Wall environment.

## Key Results
- **Three-regime pattern:** 8-bit and 6-bit quantized models perform near FP16 baseline; 3-bit models fail entirely; 4-bit models show high sensitivity to bit allocation.
- **Encoder bottleneck in 4-bit regime:** Mixed INT4 (encoder FP16, predictor INT4) and encoder-heavy asymmetric variants (e.g., E6/P4) achieve higher success rates than uniform INT4 at similar model sizes (e.g., mixed INT4: 0.267 vs uniform INT4: 0.067 at budget bA).
- **Robustness with fragility:** Mixed-bit advantages hold across planner budgets but reverse in low-sample replications, indicating sensitivity to sampling variance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The encoder extracts visual features critical for accurate latent dynamics predictions. In the 4-bit regime, uniform quantization degrades encoder precision, corrupting features and causing planning failures. By preserving encoder bitwidth (in mixed or asymmetric setups), feature quality is maintained, enabling reliable planning even with a quantized predictor. This identifies the encoder as a precision-critical component for spatial reasoning in world models like DINO-WM, where small errors propagate catastrophically through the planning horizon.

## Foundational Learning
- **World Models:** Simulate environment dynamics to enable planning without direct interaction. *Why needed:* Allow agents to imagine outcomes and select actions efficiently. *Quick check:* Can the model predict next latent state given current state and action?
- **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ):** Reduce weight bitwidth without retraining to shrink model size. *Why needed:* Deploy models on memory-constrained devices. *Quick check:* Are weights converted to lower precision (e.g., INT4) using per-channel scaling?
- **Bit Allocation:** Distribute total bit budget across model modules. *Why needed:* Different modules have varying sensitivity to precision loss; optimal allocation improves task performance under size constraints. *Quick check:* Does assigning more bits to the encoder increase planning success compared to uniform allocation?
- **Paired Evaluation:** Test variants on identical goal sets to control for goal difficulty variance. *Why needed:* Isolate the effect of bit allocation from environmental stochasticity. *Quick check:* Are success outcomes compared for the same (seed, episode ID) pairs across all variants?
- **Planning Budget:** Constraints on rollout horizon and optimization steps during planning. *Why needed:* Reflect computational limits in real-time decision-making. *Quick check:* Do budgets bA and bB differ in horizon length and internal optimization iterations?
- **Spatial Reasoning:** Ability to understand and predict spatial relationships in environments. *Why needed:* Core to tasks requiring navigation or obstacle avoidance, like the Wall maze. *Quick check:* Does the task involve predicting collisions or paths in a 2D grid?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Visual Input -> Encoder (ViT with Linear layers) -> Predictor (Transition model with Linear layers) -> Latent Dynamics -> Planner (uses latents to rollout actions)
- **Critical path:** Image encoding via ViT produces features; predictor generates next latent; planner iteratively selects actions based on latent rollouts to reach goal.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Bitwidth allocation between encoder and predictor balances model size and planning reliability; higher encoder bits preserve feature fidelity but increase memory usage.
- **Failure signatures:** Misaligned paired units (different goals per variant), incorrect PTQ (per-layer vs. per-output-channel scaling), budget misconfiguration (wrong H/opt steps), checkpoint mismatch (non-native DINO-WM version).
- **First experiments:** (1) Reproduce FP16 baseline success rates for budgets bA and bB. (2) Apply uniform quantization at INT8, INT6, INT4, INT3 to confirm three-regime pattern. (3) Test mixed-bit variants (e.g., E16/P4) against uniform INT4 to validate encoder bottleneck.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can mixed precision be optimized directly for planning success rather than model reconstruction error?
- Which encoder blocks are precision-critical, and does that set change across environments?
- Does the three-regime pattern (8/6-bit safe, 4-bit sensitive, 3-bit collapse) generalize beyond DINO-WM on the Wall task?

## Limitations
- Checkpoint and environment fidelity depend on repository-specific implementations; undocumented variations may alter results.
- Quantization scoping is limited to weight-only PTQ on linear layers; effects on biases or activations are unexplored.
- Findings are specific to DINO-WM on Wall task; generalizability to other architectures and tasks is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Three-regime pattern (8/6-bit stable, 3-bit collapse, 4-bit sensitive):** Medium-High
- **Encoder precision as primary bottleneck in 4-bit regime:** Medium
- **Robustness of mixed-bit direction across budgets:** Medium
