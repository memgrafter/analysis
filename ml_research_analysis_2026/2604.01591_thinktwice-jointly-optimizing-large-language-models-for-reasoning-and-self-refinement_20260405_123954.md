# ThinkTwice: Jointly Optimizing Large Language Models for Reasoning and Self-Refinement
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01591
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01591
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A two-phase RL method using only binary correctness rewards improves both reasoning and self-refinement, yielding +5pp on AIME for Qwen3-4B and converging 16% faster with only 3% overhead.

## Executive Summary
ThinkTwice introduces a self-refinement training framework that jointly optimizes an LLM for solving reasoning problems and refining its own solutions, without relying on external supervision such as critique annotations or process labels. It employs a two-phase reinforcement learning approach based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). In each iteration, the model first optimizes for solving a batch of problems (Phase 1). It then uses a randomly selected solution from that batch as the base for a refinement step (Phase 2), where it optimizes to improve the same solutions using a generic multi-turn prompt and the same binary correctness reward. Across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks and two model families (Qwen3-4B, OLMo3-7B), ThinkTwice improves performance on both the initial reasoning pass and after one self-refinement step compared to standard GRPO. The method reveals an emergent “rectify-then-fortify” curriculum during training and adds minimal computational overhead while converging faster in wall-clock time.

## Method Summary
ThinkTwice uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as its reinforcement learning backbone. For each training iteration on a batch of problems, Phase 1 generates multiple reasoning rollouts per problem, computes a binary correctness reward (1 for exact match, 0 otherwise), and performs a GRPO update. One rollout per problem is randomly selected as the base solution. Phase 2 constructs a multi-turn refinement prompt by appending a generic instruction (without correctness hints) to the problem and the selected base solution. The updated policy generates refinements for these prompts, and the same binary reward is computed on the refined final answer. A second GRPO update is applied. This joint optimization allows the refinement phase to provide a learning signal even when the base solution is incorrect, as a correct refinement still yields a positive reward. The entire process uses only verifiable outcome rewards, avoiding any external process supervision.

## Key Results
- On Qwen3-4B, ThinkTwice achieves 44.11% pass@4 on AIME before refinement vs. 39.06% for GRPO, and 60.43% after one self-refinement step vs. 48.91% for GRPO.
- Consistent average gains are observed across five benchmarks (AIME, AMC, MATH500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench) and two model families.
- Training adds only 3% wall-clock overhead and reaches the best checkpoint 16% faster (220 vs. 280 steps) due to shorter refinement responses over time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** ThinkTwice induces an implicit *rectify-then-fortify* curriculum where refinement first corrects errors in failed solutions and later preserves/polishes already-correct ones as the base policy improves.
- **Mechanism:** The refinement phase uses the same binary correctness reward but conditions on the model’s own prior solution. Early in training, many base solutions are incorrect; refinement thus learns to *fix* these. As the base policy strengthens, most base solutions become correct; refinement naturally shifts to *fortify* concise, correct solutions without altering the answer.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s refinement behavior dynamically adapts to the distribution of base solution correctness, driven solely by the final-answer reward.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Analysis of the training dynamics... reveals an implicit rectify-then-fortify curriculum." [section 5.1] Figure 4a shows *fix-wrong* rate peaks early and *damage-correct* drops near zero later—opposite to GRPO’s consistently higher damage.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Jointly optimizing reasoning and refinement with a shared binary correctness reward yields a more rectified learning signal, as the refinement phase recovers gradient information from problems where the base attempt fails.
- **Mechanism:** In Phase 2, the policy generates refinements conditioned on its own Phase 1 outputs. Even when a base solution is wrong, a correct refinement produces a positive reward, providing a learning signal for problems that would otherwise yield zero reward. This "second chance" signal rectifies the sparse outcome-based reward landscape.
- **Core assumption:** The model can generate diverse refinements that sometimes correct base errors, and the binary reward is sufficient to guide this without process supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] Table 1 shows ThinkTwice improves *reasoning* performance over GRPO (e.g., +5 pp on AIME for Qwen3-4B). [corpus] Prior refinement training typically uses extra signals; ThinkTwice’s lack thereof is a key differentiator.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ThinkTwice’s two-phase design adds minimal training overhead and converges faster in wall-clock time because the refinement phase produces progressively shorter responses, offsetting longer initial contexts.
- **Mechanism:** While Phase 2 initially has higher per-step cost due to longer context (base solution prepended), refined responses shorten over training. This narrowing cost gap, combined with faster convergence (fewer steps to best checkpoint), yields net time savings.
- **Core assumption:** The model learns to produce more concise refinements as it fortifies correct solutions, reducing token-generation cost.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.2] Total wall-clock time at step 300 is only 3% higher (9.42h vs 9.15h). Best checkpoint reached in 16% less time (7.2h vs 8.6h). [section 5.2] Panel (b): refinement response length decreases markedly over training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
  - *Why needed here:* ThinkTwice uses GRPO as its RL backbone. Understanding group-normalized advantages and the absence of a separate critic is essential.
  - *Quick check:* How does GRPO compute advantages without a value network, and why does grouping samples reduce variance?
- **Concept: Outcome-based Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**
  - *Why needed here:* The framework relies on a binary correctness reward from a verifier (Math-Verify). This shapes learning from final answers only.
  - *Quick check:* What are the limitations of using only outcome-based rewards versus process-based rewards for complex reasoning?
- **Concept: Multi-turn Conversation Format for Refinement**
  - *Why needed here:* The refinement phase is implemented as a multi-turn chat (problem → base solution → review instruction). This format natively supports conditioning without architectural changes.
  - *Quick check:* Why is the refinement instruction kept generic (no correctness hints), and how does that affect what the model learns?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Policy πθ (LLM) -> GRPO updater (group-relative advantage, clipped PPO) -> Refinement prompt constructor (formats [user: x][assistant: y_base][user: I_refine]) -> Reward module (Math-Verify) -> Base solution sampler (random rollout selection)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Sample batch of problems.
  2. **Phase 1:** Generate G reasoning rollouts per problem → compute rewards → GRPO update → obtain π'θ.
  3. From Phase 1 rollouts, randomly pick one base solution per problem.
  4. Construct refinement prompts with base solutions + generic instruction.
  5. **Phase 2:** Generate G refinement rollouts per prompt using π'θ → compute same rewards → GRPO update → obtain π''θ.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Simplicity vs. curriculum control:* No explicit scheduling; curriculum emerges from base correctness distribution. Might be less predictable if base policy plateaus.
  - *Training stability vs. overhead:* Longer contexts in Phase 2 could cause instability, but KL penalty and GRPO’s group normalization mitigate this. Token cost is manageable due to response shortening.
  - *Single policy vs. specialized refiners:* Shared policy is efficient but may not reach peak refinement capability of a separate fine-tuned refiner.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *No rectify-then-fortify transition:* Monitor *fix-wrong* and *damage-correct* rates. If *damage-correct* stays high (>5%) and *fix-wrong* does not peak, refinement is harming correct solutions.
  - *Degraded reasoning:* If reasoning performance drops below GRPO baseline, refinement interference may be destabilizing the base policy.
  - *No length reduction:* Track average refinement length; if it grows or stays constant, the *fortify* phase isn’t activating.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run GRPO on MATH train set for same steps; verify base reasoning performance matches reported Table 1 numbers.
  2. **Transition tracking:** During ThinkTwice training, periodically evaluate on a held-out set: compute *fix-wrong* (incorrect → correct after refinement) and *damage-correct* (correct → incorrect) rates. Expect to see *fix-wrong* peak early then decline, and *damage-correct* drop near zero later.
  3. **Overhead measurement:** Measure average tokens per Phase 1 vs. Phase 2 step across training. Confirm Phase 2 cost decreases over time and total wall-clock time is within 5% of GRPO at same step count.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can ThinkTwice’s joint training framework generalize to other outcome-based tasks beyond mathematical reasoning, such as code generation or scientific QA?
- What precise factors (e.g., base solution sampling strategy, phase ordering) cause the emergent “rectify-then-fortify” curriculum in ThinkTwice training?
- How does ThinkTwice’s training efficiency (overhead, convergence speed) scale to much larger models (e.g., 70B+ parameters) given the increased context length from prepending base solutions?
- Does extending ThinkTwice to multiple iterative refinement steps (beyond the single step used) yield further gains or lead to diminishing returns/error propagation?
- How sensitive is ThinkTwice to the phrasing of the generic refinement instruction? Would domain-specific or structurally different instructions alter the balance between error correction and solution fortification?

## Limitations
- The emergent curriculum relies on the base policy improving; if learning plateaus early, refinement may not shift to “fortify” mode.
- Random selection of base solutions could sometimes pick overly easy or hard instances, affecting stability.
- The generic refinement prompt (no correctness hints) might not generalize beyond math domains.

## Confidence
- *Rectify-then-fortify*: Medium. Supported by Figure 4a but not guaranteed across all datasets/models.
- *Rectified reward signal*: Medium. Reasoning gains (Table 1) are consistent, but could also stem from better exploration rather than the two‑phase design.
- *Efficiency (low overhead, faster convergence)*: High. Wall‑clock measurements (Figure 5d) are direct and reproducible.
