# ROSA: Roundabout Optimized Speed Advisory with Multi-Agent Trajectory Prediction in Multimodal Traffic
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14780
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14780
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** ROSA reduces energy consumption by ~10%, fuel/CO2 by 5%, travel time by 3%, waiting time by 66%, and stops by 63% in conflict-prone roundabout scenarios, even with prediction uncertainty.

## Executive Summary
ROSA tackles safe and efficient speed coordination for automated and connected vehicles at roundabouts in mixed traffic with vulnerable road users (VRUs). It integrates a Transformer-based multi-agent trajectory predictor (forecasting vehicles and VRUs) with a proactive speed advisory that reacts to predicted conflicts at crosswalks and roundabout entries. Evaluated on real-world drone data and in simulation, ROSA achieves state-of-the-art trajectory prediction accuracy and significant efficiency gains while reducing waiting times and stops, demonstrating the value of linking multi-agent forecasting to eco-driving at complex intersections.

## Method Summary
ROSA uses the openDD roundabout dataset, processing 3-second histories of all agents (vehicles, VRUs) at 1 Hz. Features include position, velocity, accelerations, heading, class, and optional exit intention. A masked Transformer predicts single-step future kinematic states (position, velocity, etc.), deployed autoregressively for a 5-second horizon. The speed advisory computes arrival times at conflict zones (crosswalk, entry) and, if occupancy is predicted within the horizon, applies a kinematic formula to adjust speed, first ensuring crosswalk clearance then entry clearance. Performance is validated in SUMO simulation across 6,600 scenarios derived from real-world trajectories.

## Key Results
- Trajectory prediction: ADE 1.10m, FDE 2.36m at 5s (with exit intention), outperforming baselines.
- Efficiency gains in simulation: ~10% lower energy, 5% lower fuel/CO2, 3% shorter travel time.
- Conflict reduction: 66% lower waiting time, 63% fewer stops in conflict-prone scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Conflict-Triggered Advisory
- **Claim:** Speed advisories are only issued when predicted trajectories indicate a conflict zone (crosswalk or entry) will be occupied within the 5-second horizon.
- **Mechanism:** The system estimates time-to-arrival at each zone. If within the horizon, it predicts all agents’ positions for each future step and maps them to predefined zone polygons. An advisory is generated only if occupancy is predicted at the arrival time; otherwise, the vehicle maintains speed. This avoids unnecessary deceleration.
- **Core assumption:** Predicted trajectory-derived occupancy reliably proxies imminent conflict risk over ≤5s.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states advisories are "based on predicted conflicts"; Section III.A details the occupancy inference process; corpus shows no prior work linking multi-agent occupancy to roundabout speed advice.
- **Break condition:** Low occupancy recall (false negatives) causes missed conflicts, eliminating safety/efficiency gains.

### Mechanism 2: Two-Stage Optimization
- **Claim:** A sequential two-stage speed optimization first ensures conflict-free crossing at the VRU crosswalk, then re-optimizes for the roundabout entry, prioritizing VRU safety.
- **Mechanism:** Algorithm 1 first computes an optimal speed `v_c_opt` to arrive at the crosswalk one second after predicted clearance. This speed sets a new baseline for calculating arrival time at the entry `t_e`. If `t_e` is within the horizon and entry occupancy is predicted, a second optimal speed `v_e_opt` is computed. The cascade prevents optimizing for the entry at the expense of the crosswalk.
- **Core assumption:** Crosswalk safety is the primary constraint; entry conflicts are secondary but must be addressed sequentially to avoid cascading unsafe states.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III.A explicitly describes the two-stage process; abstract mentions "response to both prioritized crossing VRUs and intersecting vehicles"; corpus lacks direct precedent for separate zone optimization.
- **Break condition:** False negative at crosswalk risks VRU collision; false negative at entry after crosswalk optimization risks collision with circulating vehicles.

### Mechanism 3: Deterministic Autoregressive Predictor
- **Claim:** A deterministic, single-step Transformer predictor, trained on motion dynamics and optional route intention and deployed autoregressively, provides sufficiently accurate and low-latency occupancy forecasts.
- **Mechanism:** The Transformer uses masked self-attention to separately model temporal dynamics and spatial interactions, predicting full kinematic state one second ahead from a 3-second history. During deployment, predictions are recursively fed back for multi-step generation. Deterministic output simplifies occupancy checks compared to sampling multimodal distributions.
- **Core assumption:** Error accumulation from autoregressive rollout remains tolerable for binary occupancy classification over 5s.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section II.C describes the single-step design and autoregressive inference; Section II.E and Table III show >76% recall for entry and >64% for crosswalks at 5s, supporting usable forecasts; several neighbors use autoregressive RNNs/GNNs, but the masked Transformer for joint vehicle-VRU roundabouts is unique.
- **Break condition:** Catastrophic error buildup (e.g., poor VRU maneuver modeling) invalidates occupancy checks, causing excessive false positives or negatives.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-Agent Trajectory Prediction with Heterogeneous Dynamics**
  - *Why needed:* Roundabouts involve tightly coupled interactions between fast vehicles and slow, erratic VRUs. A joint predictor captures these cross-agent influences; an ego-centric model would miss critical interactions.
  - *Quick check:* Why is a single-agent predictor insufficient for this roundabout scenario?
- **Concept: Occupancy Estimation from Trajectories**
  - *Why needed:* The advisory operates on binary conflict decisions (zone clear/occupied), not precise paths. Continuous predicted (x,y) must be mapped onto discrete, topologically defined conflict zones.
  - *Quick check:* How are predicted (x,y) coordinates translated into a "crosswalk occupied" decision, and what geometric information is required?
- **Concept: Kinematic Speed Optimization under Constraints**
  - *Why needed:* The advisory computes a physically feasible speed (`v_opt = 2*d / t - v`) to achieve a desired arrival time under a maximum deceleration constraint.
  - *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the `2*d / t - v` formula, and what does the "2" represent in the context of constant deceleration/acceleration?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Processor -> Transformer Trajectory Predictor -> Autoregressive Rollout Module -> Occupancy Mapper -> Speed Advisory Engine
- **Critical path:** Raw trajectory data → Predictor → 5s future states → Zone occupancy per timestep → Arrival time calculation → Speed advisory output. The loop repeats every second.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Deterministic vs. Multimodal prediction: Chose deterministic for actionable, single-valued advisories; trade-off: cannot express uncertainty, may miss correct modes.
  - Single-agent vs. Multi-agent predictor: Chose multi-agent Transformer to capture interactions; trade-off: higher computational complexity vs. ego-centric models.
  - Route intention inclusion: Added as optional input; trade-off: requires V2X data vs. improved long-horizon accuracy (FDE reduction 2.99m → 2.36m).
  - Two-stage optimization: Prioritizes crosswalk then entry; trade-off: may sub-optimize fuel economy if entry conflict is more severe but crosswalk is clear.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - False negative occupancy: Missed VRU → no deceleration → safety risk. Evidenced by 5s crosswalk recall as low as 71% (model 2) in Table III.
  - False positive occupancy: Predicted VRU when absent → unnecessary deceleration → increased travel time/stops. Evidenced by precision drop at 5s (model 2: 80% crosswalk, 56% entry).
  - Autoregressive drift: Cumulative error causes agents to "slide" unrealistically, invalidating occupancy checks over longer horizons.
  - V2X latency/loss: Missing/stale input states degrade predictor accuracy immediately.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Prediction model validation on openDD test set: compute ADE/FDE at 1-5s (Table II) and verify ablations (dynamics, exit intention) improve over position-only baseline.
  2. Occupancy classification check: using predicted trajectories, compute precision/recall for each zone type against ground truth (Table III); confirm >80% recall at 3s for practical advisory window.
  3. Advisory simulation - Perfect Foresight Baseline: in SUMO, use ground truth trajectories to compute occupancy; run 6600 scenarios; measure efficiency/safety metrics (Table IV, right).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can ROSA be extended to coordinate speed advisories for multiple vehicles cooperatively? (Conclusion suggests RL for high-dimensional optimization.)
- How does ROSA perform under diverse traffic conditions (different roundabout geometries, volumes, VRU behaviors)? (Evaluation used only one dataset; generalizability unknown.)
- What is the impact of perception source (infrastructure vs. FCOs), V2X communication uncertainty/latency, and human driver compliance? (Assumes ideal perception/compliance.)
- Can ROSA's simulated benefits be validated in real-world deployments, and what practical challenges arise? (All evaluations are simulation-based.)

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter opacity: Exact transformer architecture (layers, heads, dimension), training schedule, and loss weighting are undisclosed, hindering replication.
- V2X assumption fragility: System depends on low-latency, high-availability infrastructure/FCO data; real-world noise, delays, and non-compliance are unaddressed.
- Safety validation gap: Safety claims rely on simulated conflict reduction; real-world interaction with unpredictable VRU behavior lacks empirical validation.

## Confidence
- **Prediction accuracy (ADE/FDE):** High. Anchored by standard benchmark numbers on openDD test set with clear ablations.
- **Efficiency gains in simulation:** Medium. Results from 6600 SUMO scenarios are specific to tested topology and traffic mixes; transferability uncertain.
- **Safety via conflict avoidance:** Low-Medium. Depends critically on occupancy prediction recall, which degrades at 5s (entry recall 0.47 for model 2); false negatives remain a key unquantified risk.
- **Two-stage optimization mechanism:** Medium. Logic is explicated but lacks direct comparative validation against alternative strategies; corpus evidence is weak.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Re-train predictor across a reasonable range (e.g., 4–8 layers, 4–12 heads, learning rates 1e-4 to 1e-3) on openDD rdb1 split. Quantify variance in ADE/FDE and 5s zone occupancy recall to establish robust performance bounds.
2. **Occupancy-metric stress test:** Using trained predictor, inject synthetic errors (e.g., shift predicted positions by ±0.5m, randomly drop 10% of VRUs) into occupancy mapping. Measure changes in advisory frequency, waiting time, and simulated conflict rate to establish operational tolerance thresholds.
3. **V2X degradation simulation:** In SUMO loop, introduce controlled latency (0.5–2.0s) and random state dropouts (5–20%) to input agent state stream. Evaluate degradation in ROSA’s efficiency gains and pinpoint failure points where advisory safety (e.g., crosswalk conflict) is compromised.
