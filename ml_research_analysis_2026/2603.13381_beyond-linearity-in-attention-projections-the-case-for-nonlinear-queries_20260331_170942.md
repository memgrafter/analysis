# Beyond Linearity in Attention Projections: The Case for Nonlinear Queries

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13381
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13381
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Nonlinear query projections reduce validation loss by 1.40% (2.915 vs 2.956) and enable higher learning rates (up to 3e-3) and lower weight decay (down to 0.03) without instability, in a 124M-parameter GPT‑3 small model trained on OpenWebText.

## Executive Summary
The paper reveals that the linear Query projection matrix \(W_Q\) in self‑attention is algebraically redundant due to a basis invariance, which permits setting it to the identity matrix without altering the model's function. To exploit this, the authors replace \(W_Q\) with a nonlinear residual bottleneck MLP \(f_\theta\) while preserving the original parameter count: \(Q(X) = (X + f_\theta(X))/2\). The identity term acts as a stabilizer, allowing more aggressive optimization.

In experiments using a 124‑million‑parameter GPT‑3 small architecture trained on OpenWebText for 60k steps (∼29 B tokens), the nonlinear query achieves a validation loss of 2.915 compared to the baseline's 2.956—a 1.40% improvement—and matches the performance of a baseline with 12.5% more non‑embedding parameters. Furthermore, the model tolerates a learning rate of \(3\times10^{-3}\) and weight decay as low as 0.03 without diverging, whereas the baseline fails under these settings.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the transformer self‑attention block by replacing the linear Query projection \(W_Q\) with a residual bottleneck MLP. For an input token matrix \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times s\times d}\), the query is computed as \(Q = \frac{1}{2}(X + f_\theta(X))\), where \(f_\theta = \text{LN}(\text{GELU}(\text{RMSNorm}(X)W_1)W_2)\) with \(W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d/2}\) and \(W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{d/2\times d}\). Keys and Values are computed independently with standard linear projections. The total number of parameters added by \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) matches the original \(W_Q\) (approximately \(d^2\)), maintaining model size.

The architecture is implemented in a 12‑layer, 12‑head GPT‑3 small model (embedding dimension 768, MLP hidden size 3072). Training uses AdamW (\(\beta_1=0.9\), \(\beta_2=0.95\)), gradient clipping 1.0, a 2000‑step linear warmup, and cosine decay over 60 000 steps on the OpenWebText dataset. Hyperparameter exploration identifies a best‑performing configuration with maximum learning rate \(3\times10^{-3}\) (final \(3\times10^{-5}\)) and weight decay \(2^{-5}\) (≈0.03). This configuration yields the reported validation loss improvement and enhanced training stability.

## Key Results
- Validation loss decreases from 2.956 (baseline) to 2.915 (nonlinear query), a 1.40% relative improvement.
- Training stability: the nonlinear query model withstands learning rate \(3\times10^{-3}\) and weight decay 0.03 without divergence; the baseline diverges at weight decay 0.05 and cannot tolerate \(3\times10^{-3}\).
- Parameter efficiency: the gain matches that of a baseline model with 12.5% more non‑embedding parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The linear Query projection \(W_Q\) is algebraically redundant due to a basis invariance in the attention computation.
- **Mechanism:** For any invertible matrix \(\Theta\), the transformation \((X, W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O) \rightarrow (X\Theta, \Theta^{-1}W_Q, \Theta^{-1}W_K, \Theta^{-1}W_V, W_O)\) leaves the multi-head attention output unchanged. Setting \(\Theta = W_Q\) yields \(W_Q \rightarrow I\), eliminating the learned linear query projection without altering the function.
- **Core assumption:** Adjacent linear layers can absorb the basis change; this propagation holds for standard transformers with layer norm and residuals.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States the invariance renders \(W_Q\) redundant and can be set to identity.
  - [section 1, page 2] Details the invariance equation and propagation through the network.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Attention Projection Mixing with Exogenous Anchors" discuss projection reuse but do not directly evidence this invariance; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** Non-invertible \(W_Q, W_K, W_V\) (measure zero) or architectures where basis change cannot propagate (e.g., certain non‑residual or non‑normalized setups).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The identity term in \(Q(X) = (X + f_\theta(X))/2\) anchors the nonlinearity to a stable prior, improving optimization.
- **Mechanism:** The identity provides a direct gradient path and implicitly regularizes the transformation, making training robust to higher learning rates and lower weight decay.
- **Core assumption:** Identity is a good prior for queries, as shown by stability when \(W_Q = I\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Notes the identity term anchors nonlinearity to a known‑good prior.
  - [section 1, page 2] Links stability to the identity anchor and cites [12] showing \(W_Q = I\) is stable.
  - [section 3.2] Reports the nonlinear variant tolerates weight decay \(2^{-5}\) and LR \(3 \times 10^{-3}\) while baseline diverges.
- **Break condition:** Removing the identity skip or using a different anchor that lacks the stabilising effect.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Nonlinear queries decouple the query pathway from keys/values, alleviating a linear bottleneck in the per‑token representation.
- **Mechanism:** Standard attention produces four linear outputs (\(q, k, v\), residual) from a single token embedding \(x\). Nonlinear \(q = x + f_\theta(x)\) adds independent variation, freeing the residual to specialize for keys/values.
- **Core assumption:** The four outputs in standard attention are linearly dependent, limiting representational flexibility.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1] Explicitly describes the bottleneck: all four outputs are linear functions of \(x\), and nonlinearity partially decouples \(q\).
  - [section 2.2] Cites empirical observations (Hu et al., Li et al.) that \(W_Q, W_K, W_V\) have distinct learning dynamics, supporting specialization.
  - [corpus] Neighbors mention nonlinearities in QKV but not this specific bottleneck argument; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If the residual pathway already has sufficient capacity or if nonlinearity introduces interference that outweighs decoupling.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Self‑attention computation (queries, keys, values)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper modifies the query projection; understanding Q, K, V generation and their role in attention logits is essential.
  - *Quick check question:* If \(Q = XW_Q\) and \(K = XW_K\), what algebraic property allows \(W_Q\) to be replaced by identity without changing attention outputs?
- **Concept: Basis invariance in linear layers**
  - *Why needed here:* The core mechanism relies on the invariance under \(X \rightarrow X\Theta\) and \(W \rightarrow \Theta^{-1}W\).
  - *Quick check question:* Given a linear layer \(Y = XW\), show that \(Y\) is unchanged if we simultaneously premultiply \(X\) by \(\Theta\) and postmultiply \(W\) by \(\Theta^{-1}\).
- **Concept: Residual connections and gradient flow**
  - *Why needed here:* The design \((X + f_\theta(X))/2\) uses a residual to maintain stability; understanding why residuals help is key.
  - *Quick check question:* How does adding an identity skip connection affect the gradient magnitude during backpropagation through \(f_\theta\)?
- **Concept: Normalization layers (RMSNorm, LayerNorm)**
  - *Why needed here:* The \(f_\theta\) MLP includes normalization; these layers interact with learning rate and weight decay stability.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might normalization layers contribute to scale‑invariance and allow higher learning rates?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input \(X\) → identity path \(X\) and bottleneck path \(f_\theta(X) = \text{LN}(\text{GELU}(\text{RMSNorm}(X)W_1)W_2)\) → average \((X + f_\theta(X))/2\) → scaled queries \(Q\) → attention with standard \(K, V\) → output.

**Critical path:** The nonlinear query computation \(f_\theta\) must complete before attention logits can be computed; this serial dependency is the main throughput concern.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Stability vs. speed:* Identity anchor enables aggressive LR/WD but adds MLP computation.  
- *Parameter efficiency:* Bottleneck MLP matches \(d^2\) parameters of \(W_Q\) but adds negligible overhead from norms.  
- *Generality:* Works with RoPE, GQA, MoE; extending nonlinearity to \(K, V\) may break symmetry or stability.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training divergence at moderate weight decay (e.g., 0.05) or high LR without the identity anchor.  
- No validation loss improvement over baseline if bottleneck is too weak (e.g., \(r < d/2\)) or norms are misconfigured.  
- Asymmetric performance degradation if applied to \(K\) or \(V\) without careful design.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train the standard NanoGPT model (linear \(W_Q\)) with given hyperparameters to confirm reported 2.956 validation loss at step 59k; ensure identical data batches for fair comparison.  
2. **Hyperparameter sweep for nonlinear variant:** Fix architecture \(Q = (X + f_\theta(X))/2\) with \(r = d/2\); sweep weight decay (\(2^{-5}\) to \(0.1\)) and learning rate (\(2 \times 10^{-4}\) to \(3 \times 10^{-3}\)) to reproduce stability gains and find optimal LR/WD pair.  
3. **Ablation of identity term:** Train with \(Q = f_\theta(X)\) (no identity) under the same LR/WD; expect divergence or worse performance, confirming the anchor's role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What hyperparameter configurations (learning rate, weight decay, bottleneck dimension, normalization scheme) maximize the performance and stability of nonlinear query projections?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The hyperparameter landscape (learning rate schedules, weight decay, bottleneck architecture, LayerNorm epsilon) remains largely unexplored" (Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents results from a limited set of hyperparameters and admits the 1.40% improvement is likely a floor, not a ceiling.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic, multi‑seed hyperparameter search (e.g., grid or Bayesian optimization) across learning rates, weight decay values, bottleneck sizes, and normalization variants to identify optimal settings and performance bounds.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the actual inference‑time computational overhead (throughput, latency, memory) of the nonlinear query bottleneck, and can custom kernel implementations eliminate its serial dependency?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The nonlinear query introduces a serial dependency... Custom kernel implementations may be needed for practical deployment" (Efficiency and deployment).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper analyzes FLOPs (negligible) but measures no actual speed; serial computation may hinder real‑world performance despite parameter parity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Profiling benchmarks on modern hardware (GPUs/TPUs) comparing end‑to‑end tokens/sec and memory footprint of the nonlinear query against a linear baseline, using both standard and custom fused kernels.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the validation loss advantage of nonlinear queries persist, increase, or diminish at significantly larger model scales (e.g., >1B parameters) under Chinchilla‑optimal token budgets?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "These results motivate investigation at larger scales" (Abstract); "Larger scales... remain for future work" (Discussion).
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments are confined to a 124M‑parameter model trained far beyond Chinchilla‑optimal (29B tokens vs. ~2.5B); scaling behavior is unknown and may interact with architectural inductive biases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training a suite of models (e.g., 1B, 10B parameters) with compute‑optimal token counts and identical data, comparing validation loss curves between nonlinear query and linear baseline with matched non‑embedding parameters.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do nonlinear queries impact downstream task performance and generalization (e.g., few‑shot learning, reasoning) compared to a standard linear projection baseline?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Downstream evaluation remains for future work" (Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** Results are limited to OpenWebText validation loss; no evaluation on benchmarks like MMLU, HellaSwag, or GSM8K to assess practical utility.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting zero‑shot/few‑shot evaluations and fine‑tuning studies on standard NLP benchmarks using models pretrained with and without nonlinear queries, controlling for training compute.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can the residual nonlinearity design be extended beneficially to Key and Value projections without destabilizing training or introducing attention biases (e.g., excessive \(XX^\top\) self‑similarity), and what architectural modifications (e.g., asymmetric residuals, gating) are required?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "One might ask whether the same residual structure could apply to K and V. This is non‑trivial... We leave extensions to K and V as future work" (Section 2.2).
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical concerns about symmetric skip connections creating \(XX^\top\) terms and ill‑conditioning are raised but not empirically investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing nonlinear K/V projections (with and without identity anchors), measuring attention entropy, symmetry (\(||QK^\top - KQ^\top||_F\)), and validation loss across training runs to determine feasibility and necessary design tweaks.

## Limitations
- The basis invariance mechanism is theoretically derived but lacks direct empirical validation or strong support from neighboring literature.
- Implementation details of \(f_\theta\) (ordering of RMSNorm and LayerNorm) are ambiguously specified, risking incorrect replication.
- Results are limited to a single scale (124M parameters) and a narrow hyperparameter sweep; the optimal hyperparameters and generalizability to larger models or different datasets remain uncertain.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (basis invariance redundancy): Medium
- Claim 2 (identity anchor stabilizes training): Medium
- Claim 3 (decoupling linear bottleneck): Low
