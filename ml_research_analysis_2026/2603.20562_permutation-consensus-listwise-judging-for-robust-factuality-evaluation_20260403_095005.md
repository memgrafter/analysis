# Permutation-Consensus Listwise Judging for Robust Factuality Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20562
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20562
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** Permutation-consensus judging (PCFJudge) yields significant accuracy gains in listwise factuality evaluation, with absolute improvements of +5.17 (GPT-5.4) and +7.00 (Claude Sonnet 4.6) on RewardBench 2 Factuality.

## Executive Summary
Listwise factuality evaluation using LLM-as-a-judge is sensitive to the order in which candidate answers are presented, leading to unstable selections when multiple plausible responses differ in subtle hallucination risks. This paper introduces PCFJudge, a method that runs a factuality-focused listwise judge over multiple fixed permutations of the candidate set and aggregates the resulting scores, ranks, and uncertainty signals into a consensus score for each candidate. On RewardBench 2 Factuality, PCFJudge produces statistically significant accuracy gains for leading proprietary models, demonstrating that aggregating over order perturbations effectively reduces a key source of noise in multi-candidate factuality selection.

## Method Summary
PCFJudge (Permutation-Consensus Factuality Judge) operates in three stages: permutation, judging, and aggregation. First, for a given prompt and set of candidate responses, the system applies a small fixed number (K=7) of predetermined orderings. For each ordering, a single LLM judge executes a standard “factuality-first” listwise prompt, outputting a numeric factuality score (0–100), a rationale, and three binary flags (major error, hallucinated specificity, calibrated uncertainty) per candidate, from which a ranking is derived. Second, all outputs are parsed and remapped to the original candidate identifiers. Third, for each candidate, four summary statistics are computed across the K runs: mean score, Borda rank (normalized inverse rank sum), top-set frequency (how often the candidate is in the top-scoring set), and calibrated uncertainty frequency. These are combined via a fixed weighted average (50% mean score, 25% Borda rank, 20% top-set frequency, 5% uncertainty frequency) to produce a final consensus score. The candidate(s) with the highest consensus score are selected as the winner(s). For pairwise evaluation (JudgeBench), a related protocol (APOCJudge) evaluates both answer orders and uses an override mechanism based on a keyed judge.

## Key Results
- On RewardBench 2 Factuality (300-sample slices), PCFJudge achieves absolute accuracy gains of +5.17 (GPT-5.4, p≈0.023) and +7.00 (Claude Sonnet 4.6, p≈0.0015), with a weighted average gain of +6.08 over 600 examples.
- Transfer to JudgeBench (pairwise, objective correctness) yields smaller but positive gains (+2.70 to +3.24), indicating the method's strength is specifically in multi-candidate factuality selection where order instability is a primary nuisance.
- Ablation studies show the weighted aggregation—especially the mean score and Borda rank components—recovers nearly all the benefit, while heavier overlay logic adds little.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Re-running the judge over multiple permutations and aggregating outcomes reduces order-sensitivity error by amplifying a stable underlying preference signal. Each permutation provides a noisy estimate of the true best candidate. Under the assumption that the probability `q` of a single random permutation correctly ranking the true best candidate first is greater than random chance (`q > 1/2`), Hoeffding’s inequality guarantees that the probability of a majority error decays exponentially as the number of permutations `K` increases. The consensus score aggregates multiple statistics (mean score, Borda rank, top-set frequency, uncertainty frequency) to suppress noise inconsistent across orderings.

### Mechanism 2
The specific aggregation weights (0.50, 0.25, 0.20, 0.05) emphasize the most stable signals: within-permutation factuality scores (absolute quality) and order-robust relative rank (Borda), while downweighting uncertainty to avoid over-penalizing cautious but correct answers. These weights were tuned on development data to maximize recovery of the direct judge’s correct decisions. The mean and Borda rank capture absolute and relative ordering stability; top-set frequency identifies consistently top-ranked candidates; a small uncertainty weight only boosts candidates marked as “calibrated uncertainty” across many runs, treating caution as a weak positive when it prevents over-specificity.

### Mechanism 3
PCFJudge is particularly effective in listwise factuality evaluation because the nuisance variable (candidate order) is most damaging when multiple plausible answers differ mainly in hallucination risk rather than broad correctness. In 4-candidate settings, small stylistic effects can tip the judge toward a polished but inaccurate response. Permutation averaging makes it harder for any candidate to win based on a lucky first position; the winner must consistently outperform others across orderings. This targets the “within-prompt ranking quality” failure mode, whereas transfer to pairwise objective tasks (JudgeBench) yields smaller gains, confirming the method’s alignment with multi-candidate factuality selection.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Order sensitivity (position bias) in LLM-as-a-judge**
  - Why needed: The entire method targets this specific instability. Understanding that a judge’s decision can change arbitrarily with candidate order is prerequisite to seeing why permutation consensus is a viable corrective.
  - Quick check: If you shuffle the order of four candidate answers to the same question, why might an LLM judge produce different winners, and what does that imply about using a single-order judge for best-of-N selection?

- **Concept: Listwise vs. pairwise evaluation**
  - Why needed: PCFJudge is designed for listwise ranking (multiple candidates shown at once). The aggregation components assume a full ranking per run. Transfer to pairwise (JudgeBench) requires a different protocol (APOCJudge).
  - Quick check: How does the information available to a judge differ between listwise and pairwise prompts, and why might order instability be more consequential in listwise factuality selection?

- **Concept: Consensus aggregation as variance reduction**
  - Why needed: The core statistical idea is that averaging over a nuisance variable reduces error if each run is at least weakly informative. This connects the method to standard ideas in meta-analysis or ensemble learning.
  - Quick check: If a judge’s probability of picking the true best candidate in a random ordering is `q > 0.5`, how does the probability of a majority error across `K` independent runs change as `K` increases? (See Proposition 1.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Judge backbone (frozen LLM) -> Permutation sampler (K fixed orderings) -> Output parser (extracts scores, ranks, flags per candidate) -> Aggregation module (computes ¯s_i, B_i, v_i, u_i; weighted sum to C_i) -> Decision logic (select max C_i)
- **Critical path:** 1) Receive prompt x and candidate set Y. 2) For each of K permutations, construct listwise prompt with candidates in that order. 3) Call judge backbone API and parse structured output. 4) Remap all outputs back to original candidate IDs. 5) For each candidate, compute the four summary statistics and final consensus score. 6) Select winner(s) and return.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Fixed K=7 balances cost and variance reduction; increasing K yields diminishing returns under weak independence. Fixed heuristic weights (0.5/0.25/0.20/0.05) were tuned on a development slice and may not generalize across domains or judge models. The factuality-first prompt is central; changing it would require retuning.
- **Failure signatures:** High tie rate indicates judge inconsistency or uniformly reliable candidates. Consensus score lower than direct judge’s score for its winner suggests over-penalization of order-dependent candidates. Domain mismatch (e.g., JudgeBench) yields minimal gains; uncertainty flag may be poorly calibrated in new domains.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Replicate main result on RewardBench 2 Factuality slice for a new backbone (e.g., GPT-4o). 2) Ablate K (1,3,5,7,9) to confirm most gain by K=7 and diminishing returns. 3) Component ablation: zero out each aggregation weight in turn to reconfirm dominance of mean score and Borda rank.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
To what extent do the improvements from PCFJudge generalize when evaluated on the full, unfixed slices of RewardBench 2 Factuality and other large-scale factuality benchmarks?
- Basis: First, the main evidence comes from fixed API-budgeted slices rather than full benchmark sweeps, so larger runs would better characterize variance across slices and domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates clear gains on 300-example slices due to API budget constraints. Full benchmark performance could differ due to slice-specific variance or domain distribution effects not captured in the fixed subset.

### Open Question 2
Why does permutation consensus yield substantially larger gains on the multi-candidate listwise factuality task (RewardBench 2) compared to the pairwise objective task (JudgeBench), and what specific properties of a judging task predict the magnitude of this gain?
- Basis: The paper contrasts the settings, noting RewardBench 2 Factuality involves "several plausible answers" differing in hallucination risk, while JudgeBench focuses on "objective task solving." The gains are positive but smaller on JudgeBench, but the precise task characteristics that modulate effectiveness are not formally identified.
- Why unresolved: Authors provide qualitative patterns (e.g., "unsupported specificity") but lack a formal framework to predict when order-robustness will be a primary bottleneck versus a minor nuisance.

### Open Question 3
Are the fixed aggregation weights (50% mean score, 25% Borda rank, 20% top-set frequency, 5% calibrated uncertainty frequency) optimal across different judge model architectures, prompting styles, and candidate set sizes?
- Basis: The weights in Equation 5 are presented as a final design choice based on development ablations. The paper states: "The weighting intentionally concentrates mass on two signals... The uncertainty term is small by design." However, it does not claim global optimality and notes earlier variants regressed.
- Why unresolved: The development process identified that permutation consensus itself contributed the dominant gain, and simpler rules performed better than "heavier overlay logic." However, the weight tuning was likely heuristic and specific to their prompt, judge backbone, and 4-candidate setting.

### Open Question 4
Does the assumption of conditional independence across permutation runs (Proposition 1) hold sufficiently in practice for the majority-vote analogy to explain the full benefit, or are other factors like judge self-consistency within a run more critical?
- Basis: The paper acknowledges: "We do not claim that exact independence holds in practice; the result is an intuition pump for why repeated order perturbations can amplify a stable preference." Proposition 1 provides a theoretical bound under a strong independence assumption.
- Why unresolved: The empirical setup aggregates scores and ranks (not just majority vote on top-1), and the benefit likely arises from a complex interaction of stabilizing ranks, penalizing inconsistent error flags, and aggregating uncertainty.

### Open Question 5
Can the permutation-consensus paradigm be effectively combined with other judge-stabilization techniques (e.g., chain-of-thought, self-critique, heterogeneous judge panels) without diminishing returns, or does it primarily address a distinct source of noise?
- Basis: The paper positions PCFJudge as an alternative to "heavier arbitration layers" and "diverse judges" (citing PoLL). Development ablations show that adding extra logic on top of permutation consensus yielded minimal extra gain. The abstract states gains come from "averaging over this nuisance variation."
- Why unresolved: The ablations compared a "robust overlay" variant but did not systematically combine PCFJudge with orthogonal methods like using a judgment distribution or a panel of diverse models. It remains open whether these methods tackle orthogonal error sources and can thus be stacked.

## Limitations
- Exact replication is blocked by non-disclosure of the precise factuality-first prompt text, output schema, the 7 predetermined permutations, and the exact 300/100 example indices used.
- The claimed gains are demonstrated on small, fixed slices due to API budget constraints; performance on full benchmarks or with different judge models may vary.
- The fixed aggregation weights and the calibration of the "calibrated uncertainty" flag were developed on a specific dataset (4-candidate, factuality-focused) and may not generalize to other settings (e.g., different numbers of candidates, other domains).

## Confidence
- **Core efficacy on multi-candidate factuality (+5-7% gains):** Medium (evidence is strong with statistical significance and ablation, but unreplicable exact setup and possible slice-specific overfitting reduce certainty).
- **Mechanism (variance reduction via permutation consensus):** Medium for the principle; Low for the precise quantitative model (the independence assumption is likely violated in practice).
- **Generalization to pairwise (JudgeBench):** Medium (transfer gains are positive but smaller; supported by APOCJudge adaptation but limited by reproducibility gaps).
- **Optimality of fixed aggregation weights:** Low (tuning was heuristic and context-specific; no evidence of cross-context optimality).
- **Claim that method targets order instability as primary nuisance:** Medium (qualitative evidence aligns, but lack of a formal predictive framework for task boundaries).
