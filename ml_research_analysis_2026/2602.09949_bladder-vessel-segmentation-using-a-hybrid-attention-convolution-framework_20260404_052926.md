# Bladder Vessel Segmentation using a Hybrid Attention-Convolution Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09949
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09949
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieved 0.94 accuracy and 0.66 clDice on BlaVeS, outperforming SOTA in precision and topological connectivity while suppressing false positives from mucosal folds.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of segmenting bladder vasculature from cystoscopic video for intraoperative navigation. Key difficulties include sparse labels, imaging artifacts, tissue deformation, and mucosal folds that mimic vessel structures. The proposed Hybrid Attention-Convolution (HAC) framework combines a Vision Transformer to capture global vessel topology with a U-Net to refine thin, fragmented vessels. Additionally, a physics-aware self-supervised pretraining strategy uses clinically relevant augmentations on abundant unlabeled videos. Evaluated on the BlaVeS dataset, HAC achieves high accuracy and connectivity metrics, demonstrating superior precision and temporal stability across surgical frames by effectively reducing false positives from dynamic folds.

## Method Summary
HAC employs a two-stream architecture: a Transformer backbone processes low-resolution patch embeddings to model long-range dependencies and output a coarse vessel topology prior, `P_A`. This prior is concatenated with the original input image and fed into a standard U-Net decoder, which predicts a residual refinement map, `P_U`, to recover fine vessel details and sharpen boundaries. The final segmentation is the element-wise sum `P_HAC = P_A + P_U`. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) **physics-aware pretraining**: the full HAC model is trained end-to-end as a denoising autoencoder on unlabeled cystoscopic videos, where synthetic corruptions (bubbles, vignetting, contrast reduction) model real artifacts; (2) **global topology learning**: the attention backbone is trained in isolation on optimized ground truth `M*` derived by pruning short branches (<100px) and terminal endpoints from full annotations, forcing it to focus on navigational trunks; (3) **HAC refinement**: the attention backbone is frozen, and the U-Net decoder is trained on full labels to add residual details.

## Key Results
- Achieved 0.94 accuracy, 0.61 precision, and 0.66 clDice on the BlaVeS test set.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art medical segmentation models in precision and clDice, indicating better topological connectivity.
- Demonstrated temporal stability across surgical video frames by successfully suppressing false positives from dynamic mucosal folds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Architecture Additive Fusion
**Claim:** The additive combination of a Transformer global prior and a U-Net residual refinement separately captures global topology and fine-scale details.  
**Mechanism:** The Transformer backbone processes patch embeddings to model long-range dependencies, producing a coarse vessel prior `P_A`. This prior is concatenated with the original image and fed to a U-Net decoder, which predicts a residual map `P_U` to recover thin vessels and sharpen edges. The sum `P_HAC = P_A + P_U` preserves the global structure while adding precision.  
**Core assumption:** Global vessel connectivity and fine boundaries are separable, additive tasks; the attention backbone is not hindered by ignoring high-resolution details.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the framework "combines Transformers to capture global vessel topology prior with a CNN that learns a residual refinement map"; Section II.B details the additive fusion design.  
**Break condition:** If `P_A` is systematically incorrect (e.g., misses major trunks), the U-Net's residual learning may be unable to correct global topology, leading to fragmented segmentation.

### Mechanism 2: Physics-Aware Self-Supervised Pretraining
**Claim:** Pretraining on unlabeled data with clinically grounded augmentations yields features robust to cystoscopic artifacts.  
**Mechanism:** The full HAC model is trained as a denoising autoencoder to reconstruct a clean cystoscopic image `I` from a corrupted version `˜I` with synthetic bubbles, photometric variations (vignetting, gain), and local contrast reduction.  
**Core assumption:** Learning to reverse these specific corruptions forces the model to learn representations disentangled from non-anatomical noise and stable across surgical conditions.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "physics-aware pretraining... using clinically grounded augmentations"; Section II.C.1 lists the three augmentations and objective.  
**Break condition:** If synthetic corruptions do not accurately match real cystoscopic artifact distributions, pretrained features may not transfer and could harm supervised performance.

### Mechanism 3: Optimized Ground Truth for Attention
**Claim:** Training the attention backbone on pruned labels (removing short branches and endpoints) focuses it on navigationally stable major trunks, reducing false positives from mucosal folds.  
**Mechanism:** The attention network is trained in isolation on modified ground truth `M*`, created by removing all vessels shorter than 100px and all terminal branches (via 8-neighbor connectivity) from the full annotation `G`. This teaches the backbone to ignore short, disconnected structures typical of folds.  
**Core assumption:** Mucosal folds and spurious noise create short, disconnected structures; pruning them from attention targets suppresses such patterns.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes the Transformer is "trained on optimized ground truth data that exclude short and terminal branches"; Section II.C.2 describes the isolation training on `M*`.  
**Break condition:** If pruned branches include navigational landmarks (e.g., short but stable features), the attention prior may omit necessary connectivity, and the U-Net may not recover them without introducing noise.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (512x512x3) → Patch Embedding (8x8 patches, 384d) → Transformer Encoder (8 blocks of MSA+MLP) → Prior Decoder (3 transposed convs) → P_A (512x512) → Concatenation with Input (4-channel) → U-Net Decoder (4-stage encoder-decoder with skip connections) → P_U (512x512x1) → Summation P_HAC = P_A + P_U → Sigmoid.

**Critical path:** Three-stage training: (1) Pretrain full HAC for image reconstruction on unlabeled data using physics-aware corruptions; (2) Train only attention backbone on pruned labels M*; (3) Freeze attention, train only U-Net decoder on full labels G.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Transformer patch size (p=8) trades global context (smaller patches = more tokens) for computational cost.
- Additive fusion assumes the residual is small and localized; concatenative fusion might allow aggressive rewriting but risks losing global coherence.
- Pruning aggressiveness (removing all <100px branches and terminals) may discard navigationally useful short landmarks but is intended to suppress fold mimics.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Over-segmentation of folds: U-Net overactive, adding many false tiny branches; may indicate Tversky loss weight `λ_T` too low in Stage 3.
2. Fragmentary global structure: Attention prior `P_A` weak or broken; major trunks missing; indicates Stage 2 training failed.
3. Loss of fine vessels: U-Net fails to add thin branches; could be insufficient capacity, poor initialization, or `P_A` too strong leaving no residual.
4. Temporal instability: Predictions change drastically frame-to-frame; likely due to `P_A` not robust to deformation/viewpoint or `P_U` overfitting to frame-specific artifacts.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablation of Physics-Aware Pretraining:** Train HAC with and without Stage 1 (random init vs. pretrained) on a hold-out cystoscopic video with strong artifacts. Expected: Pretrained model has higher precision and fewer fold false positives.
2. **Visualize the Vessel Prior (P_A):** Output `P_A` from the frozen attention backbone after Stage 2. Expected: A sparse, connected skeleton of major trunks, with small branches and fold regions absent.
3. **Temporal Consistency Test:** Process consecutive frames from an unseen surgery video (not in 81 unlabeled or 50 labeled). Compute frame-to-frame Dice in a fixed anatomical region. Expected: HAC shows higher stability than CNN-only (U-Net) or Transformer-only (Swin-UNet) baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
How can robust feature descriptors be extracted from segmented bladder vessels to achieve reliable matching across frames and surgeries, enabling a complete patient-specific vascular map? The paper explicitly states this as a necessary next step toward building a digital twin with a patient-specific vascular fingerprint, but the current work stops at segmentation.

## Limitations
- Small labeled dataset (50 frames) raises risk of overfitting and limits statistical power.
- Underspecified implementation details for physics-aware augmentations and optimized ground truth generation hinder reproducibility.
- Evaluation confined to a single dataset (BlaVeS); external validation on diverse cystoscopic videos is absent.

## Confidence
- Hybrid architecture improves segmentation and connectivity: **Medium** (promising results but rely on single dataset, no independent replication).
- Physics-aware pretraining yields artifact robustness: **Low** (augmentation specifics vague, no ablative evidence beyond full model).
- Pruned attention prior suppresses fold false positives and ensures temporal stability: **Medium** (shows stability but pruning aggressiveness may discard navigational landmarks).

## Next Checks
