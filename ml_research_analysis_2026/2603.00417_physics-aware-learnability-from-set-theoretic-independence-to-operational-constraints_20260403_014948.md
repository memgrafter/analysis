# Physics-Aware Learnability: From Set-Theoretic Independence to Operational Constraints

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00417
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00417
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Finite-precision coarse-graining reduces the ZFC-independent EMX class to a countable problem, making it learnable with explicit sample complexity; quantum PL learners are exactly POVMs on d copies, yielding Helstrom-type bounds; finite no-signaling/quantum models yield decidable PL feasibility via LP/SDP.

## Executive Summary

Learnability theory often quantifies over all conceivable set-theoretic mappings from data to hypotheses, which can lead to logical inconsistencies such as ZFC-independence results (e.g., the class of all finite subsets of [0,1] in EMX is undecidable). This paper introduces **physics-aware learnability (PL)**, a framework that restores robustness by restricting learners to physically admissible protocols defined through an explicit access model L. The authors demonstrate three central achievements. First, they show that finite-precision coarse-graining reduces the continuum EMX problem to a countable one via a pushforward/pullback reduction, proving that the previously undecidable class is learnable with sample complexity O((1/ε) ln(1/δ)). Second, in the quantum setting they prove that the only admissible learners are POVMs on d copies, translating sample size into copy number and recovering Helstrom-type lower bounds for state discrimination. Third, for finite no-signaling and quantum models, PL feasibility becomes a linear or semidefinite programming problem, thereby placing it in a computationally decidable regime. Collectively, PL bridges abstract learning theory with physical constraints, converting undecidable tasks into algorithmic ones.

## Method Summary

The PL framework augments a standard learning task—specified by a set of environments Θ, a representable hypothesis set H (at most countable), and a utility function U—with an access model L = {L_d}. For a given resource budget d, L_d defines the set of admissible Markov kernels Q(·|θ) that can be realized by physical protocols (e.g., finite-precision measurements, d-copy quantum measurements, no-signaling correlations). A task is PL-learnable if there exist d and Q ∈ L_d such that for all θ, the probability (over H∼Q(·|θ)) that U(θ,H) is within ε of the optimum is at least 1−δ.

Technical pillars include: (i) **Pushforward/pullback reduction**: A coarse-graining map π: X→Y (with Y countable) induces a pushforward distribution Q = π#P and a pullback hypothesis class π⁻¹(G). Any EMX learner on Y, when composed with g↦g∘π, yields a PL learner on X that preserves the utility values and opt. (ii) **Quantum characterization**: For d copies of an unknown state ρ_θ, the most general measurement is a POVM on the d-fold tensor product, derived from the quantum-to-classical channel; this shows that admissible PL learners in the quantum setting are exactly such POVMs. (iii) **Convex feasibility**: When Θ and H are finite and L_d is defined by linear inequalities (no-signaling polytope) or semidefinite constraints (quantum POVMs), the PL condition translates into a linear feasibility or semidefinite feasibility (SDP) problem, which is algorithmically decidable.

## Key Results

- Finite-precision coarse-graining renders the ZFC-independent EMX class learnable with explicit (ε,δ)-sample complexity O((1/ε) ln(1/δ)).
- For quantum data, admissible PL learners are exactly POVMs on d copies; sample complexity becomes copy complexity with Helstrom-type lower bounds d ≥ log(1/δ)/|log γ| for binary state discrimination with overlap γ.
- For finite no-signaling and quantum models, PL feasibility reduces to linear or semidefinite programming and is therefore algorithmically decidable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Finite-precision coarse-graining

- **Claim:** Finite-precision coarse-graining makes the EMX class (previously ZFC-independent) learnable.
- **Mechanism:** A coarse-graining map π: X → Y (with Y countable) induces a pushforward distribution Q = π#P and a pullback hypothesis class π⁻¹(G). For any EMX learner on Y that uses d samples, composing it with g ↦ g∘π yields a PL learner on X. This reduction preserves utility: P(f) = Q(g) and opt values remain unchanged.
- **Core assumption:** The learner’s interface reveals only π(x), not x itself, and hypotheses are restricted to π⁻¹(G) (i.e., they are representable by finite strings). This excludes set-theoretically problematic hypotheses that rely on infinite-precision distinctions.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 11 formalizes the pushforward/pullback reduction; Corollary 3 applies it to the class of finite subsets F(π)_fin, yielding explicit sample complexity d ≥ ln(1/δ)/(−ln(1−ε)) = O((1/ε) ln(1/δ)).
- **Break condition:** If the access model were the identity on an uncountable X (infinite precision), the reduction collapses and the original ZFC independence reappears.

### Mechanism 2: Quantum POVMs as admissible learners

- **Claim:** In the quantum setting, the admissible PL learners are exactly POVMs on d copies of the unknown state.
- **Mechanism:** Access to d copies of a state ρ_θ means only the d-fold tensor product ρ_θ^{⊗ d} is available. Any quantum protocol that outputs a hypothesis h can be represented by a POVM {M_h} on the d-copy Hilbert space K^{⊗ d}, with Q(h|θ) = Tr[M_h ρ_θ^{⊗ d}]. This follows from the quantum-to-classical channel and its adjoint.
- **Core assumption:** The no-cloning principle prevents generating additional copies; measurement back‑action limits the information extractable per copy. The hypothesis set H must be representable (finite or countable).
- **Evidence:** Theorem 10 proves the POVM representation; Theorem 13 uses it to derive a Helstrom‑type necessary bound on copy complexity for binary discrimination: d ≥ log(1/δ)/|log γ|, where γ = |⟨ψ_0|ψ_1⟩| for pure states.
- **Break condition:** If the access model allowed more general operations (e.g., adaptive measurements with feedback between copies or use of auxiliary systems), the POVM‑on‑d‑copies characterization would no longer suffice; PL admissibility would need to be extended.

### Mechanism 3: Decidability via convex feasibility

- **Claim:** When Θ and H are finite and the access model L_d is described by rational linear or semidefinite constraints (e.g., no‑signaling polytopes, quantum POVMs), PL feasibility becomes algorithmically decidable.
- **Mechanism:** The PL condition ∀θ: ∑_{h∈G_θ(ε)} Q(h|θ) ≥ 1−δ translates into linear constraints on the kernel variables Q(h|θ). For no‑signaling, L_d is a rational polytope; for quantum, L_d is defined by POVM constraints (M_h ⪰ 0, ∑ M_h = I). In both cases, feasibility reduces to a linear feasibility or SDP problem.
- **Core assumption:** Θ and H are finite, and L_d is given by a finite set of convex constraints with a rational or semidefinite representation. This avoids undecidability arising from infinite domains or implicit sets.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 14 establishes decidability for polytope access models (e.g., no‑signaling); Theorem 15 does so for quantum models via SDP. These results rely on the fact that LP and SDP feasibility are in NP∩co‑NP (or polynomial‑time under standard assumptions).
- **Break condition:** If Θ or H are infinite, or if L_d lacks a finite convex representation (e.g., arbitrary quantum channels beyond a fixed number of copies), the feasibility problem may become undecidable or at least not reducible to a standard convex program.

## Foundational Learning

- **Representable hypothesis class**  
  *Why needed:* PL requires hypotheses to be finitely nameable (e.g., at most countable) to exclude non‑operational entities that cause set‑theoretic fragility.  
  *Quick check:* If H contains all subsets of [0,1], can every set be encoded by a finite string? Restricting to finite subsets makes the class representable.

- **Admissible protocol family (L)**  
  *Why needed:* L explicitly encodes physical constraints (e.g., coarse‑graining, POVMs, no‑signaling) instead of quantifying over all set‑theoretic maps. Learnability becomes relative to (Θ,H,U) and L.  
  *Quick check:* How would you describe L for classical i.i.d. sampling? It would be the set of kernels that arise from d independent draws from the underlying distribution. For d-copy quantum access, L_d consists of all POVMs on the d-copy Hilbert space. For a no‑signaling distributed protocol, L_d is the set of conditional distributions consistent with no‑signaling constraints.

- **Pushforward/pullback reduction**  
  *Why needed:* It transfers EMX learnability from a discrete alphabet Y to a coarse‑grained space X while preserving utility and optimal values, effectively collapsing continuum problems to countable ones.  
  *Quick check:* Given π: X→Y and a distribution P on X, the pushforward Q = π#P satisfies Q(g) = P(g∘π). If G ⊆ {0,1}^Y is learnable with sample size d, does f = g∘π achieve P(f) = Q(g)? Yes, so the same d works for the original space via the reduction.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Task layer: (Θ, H, U) defines environments, hypotheses (representable), and utility.  
Access layer: L = {L_d} describes, for each budget d, the set of admissible Markov kernels Q(·|θ) that can be realized physically.  
PL condition: ∃ d and Q ∈ L_d such that ∀θ ∈ Θ: Pr_{H∼Q(·|θ)}[U(θ,H) ≥ opt_H(θ)−ε] ≥ 1−δ.  
Concrete instantiations: classical i.i.d. (L_d = all kernels from d samples), quantum (POVMs on ⊗ᵈ), coarse‑grained (π‑revealing kernels).

**Critical path:**  
1. Define learning task: Choose Θ, ensure H is representable (finite or countable), specify utility U(θ,h).  
2. Specify access model L: e.g., choose a coarse‑graining π, or adopt quantum d‑copy access, or define a no‑signaling polytope.  
3. For given ε,δ, search for the smallest d and construct a kernel Q ∈ L_d satisfying the PL condition.  
4. If Θ and H are finite and L_d is convex with a finite description (polytope or SDP), formulate the feasibility as an LP/SDP and solve; otherwise use specialized learners.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Resolution vs. learnability:* Coarser coarse‑graining (smaller |Y|) simplifies L_d but loses fine details, potentially increasing ε or required d.  
- *Physics vs. resources:* Quantum constraints (no‑cloning) often force larger d compared to classical i.i.d.; no‑signaling may restrict correlations and affect the achievable Q.  
- *Generality vs. decidability:* Allowing unrestricted set‑theoretic maps leads to undecidability; constraining L to explicit convex families yields decidability but may also exclude some learnable tasks.

**Failure signatures:**  
- ZFC independence reappears if L_d is too permissive (e.g., identity on an uncountable domain, arbitrary functions).  
- PL feasibility fails if physical constraints are too strong for the given accuracy (ε,δ) and budget d.  
- Algorithmic failure in LP/SDP when constraints are ill‑conditioned, dimensions too large, or representation of L_d is not genuinely convex.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Coarse‑graining EMX:** Implement the quantile learner for the class of finite subsets with a chosen π and injection idx. Verify empirically that for d ≥ ln(1/δ)/(−ln(1−ε)), the success probability meets the (ε,δ) guarantee.  
2. **Quantum state discrimination:** For two pure states with overlap γ, compute the Helstrom POVM on d copies and check the necessary copy lower bound d ≥ log(1/δ)/|log γ|. Use an SDP to find the minimal d that actually achieves both success probabilities ≥ 1−δ.  
3. **No‑signaling feasibility:** Encode a small no‑signaling polytope (e.g., 2 parties, 2 inputs, 2 outputs). With a random binary utility and ε=0, set up the linear feasibility LP from Theorem 14 and solve for increasing d to find the smallest feasible d.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Operational compression characterisation:** Does an operational analogue of monotone compression characterise PL learnability for broad task families? (Based on the PL compression concept; unresolved because set‑theoretic characterisations exist but an operational one is lacking; evidence would be a proof of equivalence between PL‑learnability and existence of a compression scheme for a wide class of tasks.)
2. **Dimension‑like invariants:** Can PL learnability be captured by dimension‑like invariants that depend on the access model L within restricted physical regimes? (Ben‑David et al. showed no absolute finite dimension characterises EMX; PL conjectures relative invariants tied to physics; resolution would define and prove such invariants, e.g., based on coarse‑graining granularity or quantum copy complexity.)
3. **Coarse‑graining trade‑offs:** How does the required sample complexity in PL scale with the coarse‑graining resolution, and what is the minimal resolution needed for a target accuracy? (The paper treats coarse‑graining as a reduction tool but does not analyse the trade‑off explicitly; evidence would be explicit bounds relating the number of bins or measurement precision to sample complexity for canonical tasks like EMX.)
4. **Quantum vs. no‑signaling gaps:** Are there explicit learning tasks where PL feasibility differs between quantum and no‑signaling access models, and what information principles explain the gap? (Quantum correlations are a strict subset of no‑signaling correlations; it is unknown if this separation manifests in learnability; evidence would be a concrete task learnable under one model but not the other, linked to principles like information causality.)
5. **Decidability frontier:** What is the decidability frontier for PL feasibility? Which classes of access models L preserve algorithmic decidability (or efficient decidability) of the existence question? (Theorem 15 covers polytope and SDP models; beyond that, the status is unknown for many natural physics‑inspired L; evidence would be a classification of L based on properties like finite description and convexity that guarantee (un)decidability.)

## Limitations

- PL requires **representable (countable) hypothesis classes**, which excludes many natural continuous classes and may limit applicability.
- **Coarse‑graining loses resolution**: the trade‑off between physical accessibility (finite precision) and statistical utility is not fully quantified beyond sample complexity bounds.
- **Decidability relies on finite Θ, H and explicit convex descriptions** (polytopes or SDP). Infinite or implicitly defined cases remain open and potentially undecidable.
- The choice of injection `idx: Y → ℕ` is arbitrary in theory and may influence empirical sample complexity, leaving a degree of freedom that could affect implementations.
- **Quantum lower bounds** (Theorem 13) are necessary but not known to be sufficient; the tightness of Helstrom‑type bounds for PL remains an open question.
