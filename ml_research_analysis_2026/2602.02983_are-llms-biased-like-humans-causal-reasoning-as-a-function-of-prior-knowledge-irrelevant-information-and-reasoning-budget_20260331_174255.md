# Are LLMs Biased Like Humans? Causal Reasoning as a Function of Prior Knowledge, Irrelevant Information, and Reasoning Budget

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02983
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02983
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: LLMs display more normatively correct causal reasoning (stronger explaining away, no Markov violations) than humans and become more robust to content manipulations when using chain-of-thought prompting.

## Executive Summary
This study compares over twenty large language models (LLMs) to a human baseline on eleven causal judgment tasks structured as colliders (C1→E←C2). By fitting LLM probability responses to a noisy-OR causal Bayes net, the authors show that LLMs adhere more closely to the explicitly stated causal rules—exhibiting higher Background-Adjusted Causal Strength (BACS) and lower attribution to unmentioned factors—while failing to replicate human biases: they demonstrate strong explaining away (EA-level ≈0.3–0.5 vs. ≈0.1 in humans) and near-zero Markov violations (≈0 vs. ≈0.1). Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting improves model fit and robustness to abstraction and irrelevant text, with state-of-the-art models like Gemini-2.5-pro showing invariant performance across conditions.

## Method Summary
The benchmark consists of eleven binary-causal inference tasks, each presenting a scenario with two independent causes and a common effect. Participants (humans or LLMs) are asked to provide a numeric probability (0–100) for the effect given specific evidence. The experiment manipulates three factors: (1) prior knowledge (real-world cover stories vs. abstract variable names), (2) information load (clean text vs. text overloaded with irrelevant content), and (3) prompting (direct numeric answer vs. chain-of-thought reasoning). For each model and condition, the collected probabilities are normalized and fit to a constrained noisy-OR model: Pr(E=1|C1,C2) = 1 − (1−b)(1−m1)^C1(1−m2)^C2. From the fit, the authors compute global error (MAE) and out-of-sample R² (LOOCV R²). Derived metrics include BACS = mean(m1,m2)−b (rule-following index), explaining-away level (EA = Pr(C1=1|E=1,C2=0)−Pr(C1=1|E=1,C2=1)), and Markov violation level (MV = |Pr(C1=1|C2=1)−Pr(C1=1|C2=0)|). Human baseline data from Rehder & Waldmann (2017) for RW17 conditions enable direct comparison.

## Key Results
- **Rule fidelity**: Most LLMs show higher BACS than humans, indicating tighter adherence to given causal rules and weaker reliance on unmentioned background factors.
- **Bias pattern**: LLMs exhibit strong explaining away (EA-level ≈0.3–0.5) and nearly zero Markov violations, contrasting sharply with humans' weak explaining away (≈0.1) and moderate Markov violations (≈0.1).
- **Robustness**: CoT prompting improves CBN fit (lower MAE, higher LOOCV R²) and reduces sensitivity to content abstraction and irrelevant text; advanced models (e.g., Gemini-2.5-pro) maintain consistent metrics across all conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: LLMs exhibit more rule-like reasoning than humans.** LLMs interpret the prompt in a closed-world manner, treating the stated causal links as exhaustive. This leads to higher Background-Adjusted Causal Strength (BACS) from noisy-OR fits, reflecting high causal strengths (m) and low background leak (b). Evidence: Most LLMs show higher BACS than humans (Section 3.3). The mechanism fails if an LLM's BACS consistently falls below the human benchmark.

**Mechanism 2: LLMs demonstrate stronger explaining away in collider structures.** LLMs approximate normative Bayesian updating, sufficiently discounting the alternative cause when the effect is already explained by the other cause. This yields a positive explaining-away level (EA ≈0.3–0.5), whereas humans underweight this dependence (EA ≈0.1). LLMs also show near-zero Markov violations, while humans violate conditional independence. Evidence: EA and MV metrics from Figure 7 (Section 3.4). The mechanism breaks if EA-level becomes near zero or negative.

**Mechanism 3: Chain-of-thought prompting enhances robustness and normativity.** By generating intermediate reasoning steps, CoT forces LLMs to apply the causal structure systematically, making them less susceptible to irrelevant content or abstraction. This improves noisy-OR fit (lower MAE, higher LOOCV R²) and reduces dispersion of BACS, EA, MV across conditions. Evidence: Figures 9a,b and Section 3.5. The mechanism fails if CoT does not significantly shrink the variance between RW17 and Abstract-Overloaded conditions.

## Foundational Learning

- **Noisy-OR Causal Bayes Net Parameterization**
  - **Why needed**: Provides a parametric model to fit LLM probability judgments and extract reasoning strategy metrics.
  - **Quick check**: In the equation Pr(E=1|C1,C2)=1−(1−b)(1−m1)^C1(1−m2)^C2, what does a high *b* imply? *Answer: The agent believes the effect can occur without the stated causes (strong background attribution).*

- **Collider Graph Biases (Explaining Away & Markov Violation)**
  - **Why needed**: These are the characteristic human biases in collider reasoning; comparing LLM metrics (EA, MV) reveals whether models replicate flawed intuition.
  - **Quick check**: In a collider, observing E=1 should render C1 and C2 conditionally independent. What is a Markov violation? *Answer: When agents still perceive dependence between C1 and C2 after conditioning on E.*

- **Background-Adjusted Causal Strength (BACS)**
  - **Why needed**: Summarizes rule-following as mean(m)−b; higher values indicate heavier reliance on stated causes and less background attribution.
  - **Quick check**: If an agent has BACS ≈ 1.0, what does that say about their reasoning? *Answer: They treat the stated causes as nearly sufficient (m≈1) and ignore background (b≈0), i.e., extreme closed-world reasoning.*

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Prompt generation (11 tasks × 3 domains × 8 conditions) -> LLM inference (20+ models, temp=0, CoT when applicable) -> Response parsing (numeric extraction) -> Aggregation per model/condition -> Noisy-OR CBN fitting (constrained least squares) -> Metric computation (BACS, EA, MV, MAE, LOOCV R²) -> Comparison to human baseline -> Robustness assessment (dispersion across conditions).

**Critical path**:
1. Generate full set of prompts for a given condition (e.g., RW17-Direct).
2. Query each LLM for all 11 tasks across domains, store normalized probabilities.
3. Fit noisy-OR model to each model's pooled judgments; compute MAE and LOOCV R².
4. Derive BACS, EA, MV; repeat for all conditions.
5. Compare to human baseline; analyze robustness via cluster tightness.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Prompt fidelity vs. generalizability**: Real-world cover stories test transfer of prior knowledge; abstract strings isolate pure rule extraction but sacrifice ecological validity.
- **Direct vs. CoT**: Direct is cost-effective but may capture superficial associations; CoT improves robustness and normativity at the expense of latency and parsing complexity.
- **Metric selection**: MAE/LOOCV R² assess CBN fit globally; BACS offers a compact rule-following index but collapses m and b into a single number.

**Failure signatures**:
- High MAE (>0.2) or negative LOOCV R² indicates the CBN is a poor model of the agent's reasoning.
- Low BACS (<0) signals strong background attribution (open-world) or confusion, opposite to rule-like behavior.
- Sensitive dispersion: EA or MV vary by >0.2 between RW17 and Abstract-Overloaded, indicating fragility to content.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline alignment**: Correlate LLM and human judgments (Spearman ρ) across tasks for direct vs. CoT; expect CoT to increase alignment for models that differ from humans.
2. **Rule-fidelity & bias characterization**: For each LLM in RW17-Direct, compute BACS, EA, MV; compare distributions to human baseline (bootstrap CIs); identify models with high BACS, strong EA, low MV.
3. **Robustness stress test**: For selected models (e.g., gemini-2.5-pro vs. flash-lite), plot metrics across the eight conditions; quantify cluster tightness and the effect of CoT; categorize models as robust or sensitive.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to other graphs**: Do LLMs' rule-like reasoning strategies generalize beyond collider structures? The study focused solely on colliders.
- **Prompt framing effects**: How do explicit instructions about unmentioned causes (consider vs. ignore) affect the reasoning gap between open-world humans and closed-world LLMs?
- **Ecological validity**: Are these underspecified collider tasks valid for studying real-world causal reasoning under uncertainty?
- **Robustness mechanisms**: What factors (architecture, scale, training) drive differences in robustness across LLMs, and why does chain-of-thought often improve it?

## Limitations
- **Model specification ambiguity**: The exact LLM variants (e.g., `gpt-5-nano-v`) are not standard, hindering precise replication.
- **Missing human robustness data**: Human baselines exist only for real-world prompts; performance under abstract/overloaded conditions is unreported.
- **CBN assumption**: Fitting a single noisy-OR to all pooled judgments assumes uniform reasoning across tasks; this may mask within-model heterogeneity.

## Confidence
- LLMs exhibit more rule-like reasoning than humans: **Medium**
- LLMs show stronger explaining away and no Markov violations: **Medium**
- Chain-of-thought improves robustness: **Medium**
- Exact replication using public APIs: **Low**
- Noisy-OR fit adequately captures reasoning strategies: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Alternative CBN validation**: Compare noisy-OR fit against more complex CBNs (e.g., adding direct C1→C2 links) via likelihood or cross-validation to confirm the collider structure is optimal.
2. **Open-source replication**: Run the core RW17-Direct condition using publicly available models (e.g., Llama 3.1 70B, Mistral Large) via local inference to verify findings beyond proprietary APIs.
3. **Human robustness pilot**: Conduct a human experiment (N≈30) on the Abstract-Overloaded condition to test whether humans' BACS and EA shift toward LLM-like values, which would indicate content-sensitivity rather than fundamental strategy differences.
