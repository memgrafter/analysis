# Comparing Natural and Synthetic Structured Data: A Study of the Passive Verb Alternation in French and Italian

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25227
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25227
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Models trained on synthetic BLMs achieve near-perfect test performance on synthetic data but catastrophically fail on natural data (F1 ~0.28), while models trained on natural data generalize robustly to both natural and synthetic test sets.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how the nature of structured training data—synthetic versus natural—affects a large language model's ability to learn a specific linguistic generalization: the passive verb alternation in French and Italian. Using Blackbird Language Matrices (BLMs) as controlled evaluation templates, the authors generate two dataset variants: one from natural sentences in Universal Dependencies, and another synthetically via DeepSeek. Models are trained and evaluated across four crossed conditions (synthetic/natural train/test). The core finding is a stark generalization gap: synthetic-trained models overfit to surface patterns and collapse on natural test data, whereas natural-trained models acquire more abstract knowledge that transfers across data sources. The work underscores that authentic linguistic corpora are crucial for evaluating whether models learn true generalizations versus dataset-specific artifacts.

## Method Summary
The experimental pipeline constructs BLMs—structured templates with lexicalized slots—for the passive alternation in French and Italian. "Natural" BLMs are instantiated by extracting and formatting sentences from the Universal Dependencies treebanks. "Synthetic" BLMs are generated by prompting the DeepSeek model to produce sentences matching the BLM template constraints. These two datasets serve as both training and test sets in a fully crossed design: SynSyn (synthetic train, synthetic test), NatNat (natural train, natural test), SynNat (synthetic train, natural test), and NatSyn (natural train, synthetic test). Model performance is measured via F1 score on the binary classification of whether a given sentence exhibits the passive alternation correctly. Error analysis further categorizes mistakes into "sentence-type" errors (wrong BLM pattern) versus other errors.

## Key Results
- Synthetic-trained models (SynSyn) achieve near-ceiling performance (F1 ~1.00) on synthetic test sets but exhibit a severe drop on natural test sets (SynNat: F1 ~0.28–0.29).
- Natural-trained models (NatNat) maintain robust performance on natural test sets (F1 0.62–0.77) and also perform well on synthetic test sets (NatSyn: F1 ~1.00).
- Error analysis reveals SynNat models fail predominantly on passive structures (sentence-type errors), while NatSyn models commit few sentence-type errors, indicating the knowledge from natural training transfers well to the synthetic domain.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The divergent generalization outcomes stem from the data's intrinsic properties. Synthetic data, while perfectly matching the BLM template surface forms, lacks the full distributional diversity and subtle lexical-semantic constraints of authentic language use. Models trained on such data effectively memorize template-specific co-occurrences (e.g., specific verb-preposition combinations) rather than the underlying argument-structure alternation rule. This leads to overfitting to spurious correlations present only in the generated set. Conversely, natural data from UD, despite its own annotation conventions, presents the alternation embedded in real-world usage with varied vocabulary, adjuncts, and less canonical examples. Learning from this forces the model to abstract away from surface form to the core structural property—the promotion of the implicit object to subject—which generalizes broadly, even to the more uniform synthetic instances.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Blackbird Language Matrices (BLMs)**
    *   *Why needed:* Provide a controlled, minimal-pair style framework to isolate and evaluate a specific linguistic generalization (the passive alternation) from confounding lexical and pragmatic factors.
    *   *Quick check:* Can you generate a BLM template for a different alternation (e.g., dative shift) and instantiate it with 5 natural sentences from a UD treebank?
*   **Synthetic Data Overfitting**
    *   *Why needed:* Explains the core failure mode where models latch onto superficial, dataset-specific patterns (e.g., frequent co-occurrence of a particular preposition with a verb) that do not represent the true rule.
    *   *Quick check:* For a SynNat model's errors, manually inspect 10 failed natural sentences. Do the errors cluster around specific lexical items or structural configurations absent or different in the synthetic training set?
*   **Natural Data Abstraction Pressure**
    *   *Why needed:* Natural language's inherent variability and noise forces learning systems to identify invariant, high-level properties (like grammatical function promotion) to achieve good performance.
    *   *Quick check:* Take a NatNat-trained model and test it on natural sentences where the passive construction is embedded in a complex clause with multiple adjuncts. Does performance degrade gracefully or catastrophically?
*   **Train-Test Distribution Shift**
    *   *Why needed:* The SynNat condition is a dramatic instance of a domain adaptation problem; the training and test distributions differ in ways not captured by the primary label (passive/non-passive).
    *   *Quick check:* Quantify the distributional shift between synthetic and natural BLMs using simple metrics: lexical diversity (type-token ratio), average sentence length, or frequency of adjunct types.
*   **Rule-Like Behavior vs. Surface Correlation**
    *   *Why needed:* The study contrasts acquiring an abstract, generative rule (the alternation) with capturing statistical regularities tied to surface forms.
    *   *Quick check:* Can a NatNat model correctly judge a novel, never-before-seen passive construction that uses entirely new vocabulary but matches the alternation pattern? Can a SynSyn model do the same?

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component Map*
Data Source (UD / DeepSeek Prompt) -> BLM Instantiator -> Training Dataset (Synthetic/Natural) -> Model (e.g., Transformer) -> Evaluator (BLM Test Sets) -> Performance Metrics (F1, Error Type)

*Critical Path*
The pipeline's critical path runs from the choice of data source and generation method (natural extraction vs. synthetic prompt) through to the model's learned representations. A decision at the data generation step (e.g., prompt wording for DeepSeek) directly determines the distributional properties of the training set, which in turn dictates whether the model can learn a generalizable rule or a dataset-specific heuristic.

*Design Tradeoffs*
- **Synthetic Data:** Offers perfect control, perfect label alignment with the BLM template, and infinite scalability. **Tradeoff:** Lacks the natural distributional "challenges" (noise, variation) that promote abstraction.
- **Natural Data:** Provides authentic linguistic distribution and constraints. **Tradeoff:** Limited by corpus size, annotation inconsistencies, and potential mismatches between the BLM template's idealized structure and naturally occurring constructions.
- **Evaluation Design:** Using BLMs as test sets controls for lexical/semantic variation. **Tradeoff:** May oversimplify the phenomenon, potentially not capturing all nuances of alternation use in free text.

*Failure Signatures*
- **SynNat Model Collapse:** Very high training accuracy on synthetic data coupled with very low test accuracy on natural data. Errors concentrated on passive structures that look "natural" but deviate from synthetic pattern biases.
- **NatSyn Robustness:** High test accuracy on synthetic data despite being trained on natural data, indicating the learned representation captures a rule that covers the simpler synthetic cases.
- **Model Calibration:** A model that assigns high confidence to its incorrect SynNat predictions (overfitting) vs. calibrated or lower-confidence errors for NatNat models.

*First 3 Experiments*
1.  **Mixed Data Training:** Train models on a mixture of synthetic and natural data at varying ratios. Does a small amount of natural data rescue the generalization of a primarily synthetic-trained model?
2.  **Probing for Abstraction:** Use a trained NatNat model to predict on a suite of carefully constructed minimal pairs that isolate the specific grammatical relation change (object-to-subject promotion) from all other sentence properties. Does the model's internal representation (e.g., in a middle layer) differentiate these pairs consistently?
3.  **Synthetic Data Perturbation:** Systematically alter the synthetic data generation process (e.g., add noise, introduce lexical variety, include common natural language errors) and re-run the SynNat condition. Which perturbations most effectively reduce the generalization gap?

## Limitations
- **Linguistic Scope:** Findings are restricted to two closely-related Romance languages with similar passive constructions. Generalizability to typologically diverse languages (e.g., with head-marking, non-periphrastic passives) is unknown.
- **Data Source Validity:** The "natural" dataset is from Universal Dependencies, a curated corpus with its own annotation guidelines. Its status as fully "authentic" versus a specific standardized subset is debatable. The synthetic generation process (single DeepSeek prompt) lacks transparency on potential biases introduced.
- **Model & Scale Unknown:** The exact model architecture, size, and pre-training regimen are unspecified. The interaction between model capacity and the observed overfitting/generalization dynamic remains unexplored; results may not hold for state-of-the-art large-scale LLMs.

## Next Checks
1.  **Multilingual Replication:** Replicate the exact experimental pipeline on a typologically diverse set of languages (e.g., English, Icelandic, Japanese) with different passive implementations to test the robustness of the synthetic/natural generalization gap.
2.  **Model Scaling Analysis:** Train models of varying parameter scales (from small to SOTA LLM sizes) on the same natural and synthetic BLMs. Quantify how the performance difference between SynNat and NatNat conditions changes with model capacity.
3.  **Synthetic Generation Ablation:** Systematically vary the prompt used for DeepSeek (e.g., adding few-shot examples, describing the rule explicitly, controlling for lexical diversity) and the BLM template complexity. Identify the specific factors in the synthetic data pipeline that most strongly predict the SynNat failure mode.
