# Not a fragment, but the whole: Map-based evaluation of data-driven Fire Danger Index models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25469
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25469
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** The ConvLSTM model achieves superior operational recall with perfect fire detection on 40% of fire days, and an ensemble of seven models yields a more positively skewed Fire Danger Index distribution, indicating robust rejection of false positives while maintaining high recall.

## Executive Summary
Standard machine learning evaluation metrics for wildfire forecasts rely on curated, balanced test samples that do not reflect the extreme class imbalance and spatial heterogeneity of full operational Fire Danger Index (FDI) maps. This paper introduces a map-based evaluation framework that simulates real-world deployment by analyzing entire daily FDI maps. Key findings show that spatiotemporal ConvLSTM models outperform CNN architectures in both recall and false positive control, with a more positively skewed FDI distribution on no-fire days. Furthermore, ensembling multiple models by averaging their FDI maps further increases positive skewness, enhancing robustness against false alarms without sacrificing recall.

## Method Summary
The study trains CNN and ConvLSTM models on a multi-year, 1km resolution data cube of climate and land cover variables to predict daily FDI maps. Instead of evaluating on balanced test patches, the authors perform full-map inference across the entire spatial domain for held-out years. Operational performance is assessed via (a) daily recall quantiles across fire days and (b) the skewness of the FDI value distribution on no-fire days as a proxy for false alarm prevalence. An ensemble is created by training seven model instances with different random seeds and averaging their pixel-wise FDI predictions.

## Key Results
- ConvLSTM achieves perfect fire detection on 40% of fire days, significantly surpassing CNN baselines in recall quantiles.
- CNN architectures generate noisier false-positive maps; ConvLSTM yields a more positively skewed FDI distribution on no-fire days, indicating fewer moderate-risk false alarms.
- The ensemble-average FDI map is more positively skewed than any individual member, demonstrating robust rejection of false positives while preserving high recall.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Distribution Shift Between Test Patches and Full Maps
Claim: Standard ML test-set metrics (Recall, Precision, F1) provide a misleading sense of operational performance because they are computed on curated, balanced samples that do not reflect the extreme class imbalance and spatial heterogeneity of a full daily FDI map.
Mechanism: A model trained on balanced, patch-based samples learns a decision boundary in a curated feature space. During full-map inference, it encounters a vastly larger number of "no-fire" samples from diverse, non-susceptible land covers. Test-set metrics fail to penalize the model for generating false alarms across these widespread low-risk areas.
Evidence: [section 3.2] notes that full-map inference encounters a wider distribution of samples, so test performance may not reflect operational behavior.

### Mechanism 2: FDI Distribution Skewness as False-Alarm Proxy
Claim: Evaluating the skewness of the FDI value distribution on no-fire days is a robust map-based proxy for operational false-alarm cost. A positively skewed distribution (mass at low FDI) indicates successful rejection of false positives.
Mechanism: On a no-fire day, any pixel with FDI > threshold is a false alarm. Instead of counting individual false positives, distribution shape summarizes overall caution. A model that consistently assigns low FDI to most pixels generates fewer actionable false alerts than one with a flat distribution issuing moderate alarms everywhere.
Evidence: [section 4.1] shows ConvLSTM generally has a more positively skewed distribution than CNNs, indicating fewer false positives.

### Mechanism 3: Ensemble Averaging Reduces Spatial Noise
Claim: Averaging independently trained model FDI maps produces a more robust operational forecast by reducing noise in the spatial prediction, leading to a more positively skewed distribution while maintaining high recall.
Mechanism: Individual models are noisy estimators; their errors are partially uncorrelated due to different random initializations. Averaging smooths idiosyncratic high-FDI "hotspots" (false alarms) not consistently supported by the majority, compressing the FDI distribution toward lower values. True fire pixels, which consistently receive high FDI across models, remain above threshold.
Evidence: [section 4.2] and Figure 8 show the ensemble-averaged FDI map is more positively skewed; the approximation D⟨FR(Θ̂ᵢ(t))⟩ᵢ ≈ FR(⟨Θ̂ᵢ(t)⟩ᵢ) holds empirically.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Spatiotemporal Deep Learning (ConvLSTM)  
  **Why needed:** FDI depends on both static spatial features (topography, land cover) and dynamic temporal drivers (weather, vegetation greenness). ConvLSTM explicitly models spatiotemporal coupling, which pure CNNs cannot.  
  **Quick check:** Why does adding an LSTM component to a CNN potentially improve wildfire forecasting compared to a CNN alone?

- **Concept:** Sampling Strategy for Imbalanced Spatial Data  
  **Why needed:** The "no-fire" class is vastly larger and more heterogeneous than "fire." The sampling strategy shapes what the model learns as "normal" vs. "fire-prone," directly impacting full-map false alarms.  
  **Quick check:** How might sampling "no-fire" pixels from urban areas (non-susceptible) differently impact a full-map false positive rate versus sampling from forested areas?

- **Concept:** Operational vs. Statistical Model Validation  
  **Why needed:** A model optimized for high F1-score on a balanced test set may still be unusable operationally if it generates too many moderate-risk false alarms. Understanding false-alarm cost shifts evaluation from point-wise accuracy to map-level properties (skew, spatial noise).  
  **Quick check:** Why is a model with 95% test-set recall but a flat FDI distribution on no-fire days potentially worse operationally than a model with 85% recall but a sharply peaked FDI distribution on no-fire days?

- **Concept:** Ensemble Methods for Spatial Predictions  
  **Why needed:** Individual models are noisy spatial estimators; ensembling can smooth idiosyncratic false positives while preserving true signals, improving robustness.  
  **Quick check:** How does ensembling reduce false positives in FDI maps, and under what conditions might it fail?

- **Concept:** False Alarm Management in Hazard Forecasting  
  **Why needed:** False alarms incur high operational costs (resource mobilization, alert fatigue). Model evaluation must explicitly account for false-alarm prevalence, not just hit rate.  
  **Quick check:** Why is the cost of false alarms in wildfire forecasting particularly high compared to other classification tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Cube -> Sampling Engine -> Model Zoo (CNN/ConvLSTM) -> Training Loop -> Ensemble Manager -> Full-Map Inference -> Map-Based Evaluator
- **Critical path:** Data Preparation → Patch Sampling → Single Model Training (x7 for ensemble) → Full-Map Inference (per model) → Ensemble Averaging → Map-Based Metric Calculation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Depth vs. False Positives:** Deeper CNNs may capture noise, increasing false positives; an intermediate depth (Deeper CNN 1) balances feature capture and confidence.  
  - **Temporal Depth:** ConvLSTM uses a 10-day history, increasing parameters (~371K vs. ~100K) but explicitly models slow-moving vegetation trends.  
  - **Operational Recall vs. Precision:** Fixed threshold (FDI > 0.5) forces a trade-off; the paper prioritizes high recall *and* low false alarms (positive skew).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High-test F1 but noisy full maps:** Indicates overfitting to curated patch distribution (seen in Basic CNN).  
  - **Low daily recall quantiles (e.g., <75% on 40% of days):** Model fails on a persistent subset of fire days, possibly due to unrepresented land cover or weather patterns.  
  - **Negatively skewed FDI on no-fire days:** Model issues moderate-risk warnings everywhere, useless for operational triage.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Replication:** Train Basic CNN, compute standard test metrics (expect F1~0.82). Run full-map inference on 2020 JAS. Compare daily recall quantiles and plot FDI distribution on 6 no-fire days; expect poor skewness.  
  2. **Architecture Swap:** Replace Basic CNN with ConvLSTM. Repeat full-map inference. Expect improvement in recall quantiles and FDI skewness.  
  3. **Ensemble Test:** Train 7 models (e.g., Deeper CNN 1 or ConvLSTM). Compute ensemble-average FDI map. Compare FDI distribution on no-fire days to individual members; expect more positive skew.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does ConvLSTM's temporal modeling reduce false positives vs. CNNs in full-map inference? The internal mechanisms (e.g., multi-timescale integration, memory) are not analyzed.
2. What negative sampling strategy minimizes false positives while preserving recall? The heuristic used is not systematically compared.
3. Why does ensemble averaging yield more positive FDI skew and reduce false positives, and when does average recall ≈ average of individual recalls? No theory links ensemble diversity to skewness or justifies the recall approximation.
4. Are the reported advantages (ConvLSTM, optimal CNN depth, ensemble) generalizable to other regions? Evaluation is restricted to Greece/Balkans/Turkey; results may be region-specific.

## Limitations
- The assumed substantial distribution shift between balanced test patches and full operational maps is not directly quantified; actual pixel-level class counts across land covers are not reported.
- The proposed skewness metric as a proxy for false-alarm cost lacks empirical validation linking it to real-world decision outcomes (e.g., resource mobilization, alert fatigue).
- Ensemble averaging benefits depend on partially independent errors, but error correlation among the seven models is not measured; systematic biases could persist.

## Confidence
- **Standard metrics are operationally misleading:** High confidence. Supported by clear discussion of distribution shift (Section 3.2) and contrasting full-map results.
- **Skewness reflects false-alarm robustness:** Medium confidence. Empirical evidence shows ConvLSTM yields more positive skew (Section 4.1), but causal link to operational cost remains untested.
- **Ensemble averaging reduces noise/improves skew:** Medium confidence. Averaged maps are more positively skewed (Figure 8), but independence assumption is not verified and systematic error cancellation is uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Direct distribution shift measurement:** Compute the empirical spatial class distribution (fire/no-fire and land cover fractions) of the full daily FDI maps and compare it to the curated test-set distribution across multiple years and regions.
2. **Proxy validation via human/simulation study:** Correlate FDI distribution skewness with operational response metrics (e.g., number of alerts generated, simulated resource dispatch) to confirm its predictive value for false-alarm cost.
3. **Ensemble error correlation analysis:** Calculate pairwise pixel-wise error correlations among the seven models; if high correlation, test alternative ensembling (e.g., stacking, bias correction) to ensure claimed noise reduction is achievable.
