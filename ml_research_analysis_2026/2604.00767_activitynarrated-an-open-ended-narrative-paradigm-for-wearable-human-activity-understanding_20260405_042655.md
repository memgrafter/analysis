# ActivityNarrated: An Open-Ended Narrative Paradigm for Wearable Human Activity Understanding
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00767
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00767
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduced a language-grounded, open-ended narrative paradigm for wearable HAR, achieving 65.3% Macro-F1 on a 23-class task and 38.5% Recall@1 for open-vocabulary retrieval, with robustness to placement shifts and missing sensors.

## Executive Summary
Wearable human activity recognition (HAR) has traditionally relied on closed-set classification of fixed-duration sensor windows, which fails to capture the open-ended, naturalistic behaviors encountered in real-world use. This paper introduces a paradigm shift: modeling activities as open-ended narratives aligned with natural language. The authors contribute (1) the ActivityNarrated dataset, collected via unscripted behavior with multi-position IMUs and time-aligned free-form narrations; (2) a retrieval-based evaluation framework that measures sensor–language semantic alignment; and (3) the ActNarrator architecture, which uses a spectral VQ-VAE to discretize IMU streams into motion tokens and a Q-Former to condition a frozen large language model for open-vocabulary generation. In cross-participant evaluation, ActNarrator reaches 65.3% Macro-F1 on a 23-class expert taxonomy, far surpassing conventional windowed HAR baselines (31–34%). For open-vocabulary retrieval, it achieves 38.5% Recall@1 (cross-subject) and 34.0% (cross-position). The model also shows robustness to missing sensors and placement variability, demonstrating that language-grounded, discretized representations can yield more deployable HAR systems.

## Method Summary
The ActivityNarrated dataset records up to 15 body-worn IMU sensors while participants move freely in a room with activity hotspots. Their continuous self-narrations are captured via audio and synchronized with the sensor streams, providing open-ended descriptions rather than fixed labels. Data is continuous; during preprocessing each sensor stream is segmented into 2‑second windows (50% overlap). Each window is transformed into three spectral views: time‑domain signal, STFT magnitude, and continuous wavelet transform (CWT) magnitude.

ActNarrator has two stages. First, a spectral VQ-VAE tokenizer encodes each 2‑s segment from its three views using lightweight 1D and 2D convolutional encoders. The fused embedding is vector‑quantized with a codebook of 128 tokens, yielding a motion‑token sequence per sensor position. Second, a Q‑Former ingests the concatenated token sequences from all active positions (augmented with dropout/swap for robustness) and applies cross‑attention with learnable queries to produce a fixed‑size set of “sensor prompt” vectors that match the embedding space of a pretrained LLM (e.g., Qwen‑2.5‑7B). A textual header specifies which sensors contributed and for how long. The frozen LLM is trained via next‑token prediction to generate the narration text. At inference, the pipeline generates or retrieves the most likely narration, which can be mapped to a closed label set if desired.

## Key Results
- **Closed-set classification:** ActNarrator attains 65.3% Macro‑F1 on a 23‑class expert taxonomy under cross‑participant evaluation, more than double the 31–34% achieved by strong windowed HAR baselines (DeepConvLSTM, TinyHAR).
- **Open-vocabulary retrieval:** The model reaches 38.5% Recall@1 for cross‑subject splits and 34.0% for cross‑position splits, substantially outperforming prior methods like IMU2CLIP and OVHAR.
- **Robustness:** ActNarrator maintains performance under missing‑sensor conditions and across varied placements, validating the benefit of discretized, placement‑invariant motion tokens.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Naturalistic, multi-position data with open-ended narrations exposes deployment-relevant variability (placement, semantics, compositionality) that closed-set datasets hide, forcing models to learn robust, placement-invariant motion primitives.
- **Mechanism:** By collecting unscripted behavior across 15 body positions and pairing continuous IMU streams with time-aligned free-form narrations (not predefined labels), the dataset structure makes sensor placement shift, missing sensors, and open-vocabulary semantics unavoidable training and evaluation conditions. The model cannot rely on spurious correlations tied to a fixed window or placement.
- **Core assumption:** Real-world deployment robustness emerges from training on data that systematically includes the distribution shifts (placement, long-tail semantics) expected at test time.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ActivityNarrated dataset, collected via unscripted behavior with multi-position IMUs and time-aligned free-form narrations; [...] robust to missing sensors and placement variability."
  - [section 3.1] "We [...] allow participants to act freely [...] Data is collected continuously, without imposing fixed window lengths."
  - [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers discuss HAR challenges but do not provide direct evidence for this specific dataset's design. Assumption: dataset design principles are sound based on problem framing.
- **Break condition:** If the dataset still relies on scripted repetitions, limited placements, or hard labels that collapse open-ended descriptions into a small taxonomy, it fails to encode the targeted variability.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A retrieval-based evaluation framework directly measures sensor-language semantic alignment, which generalizes closed-set classification and remains meaningful without fixed classes.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of accuracy on a fixed label set, the framework evaluates whether a model can retrieve the correct natural-language description (or a paraphrase) for an IMU segment from a candidate pool. Metrics like R@1, MRR, and nDCG@K penalize models that force sensor evidence into narrow label slots and reward those that capture open-vocabulary semantics. This subsumes classification as a special case (when descriptions are mapped to a fixed taxonomy).
- **Core assumption:** Semantic alignment between sensor signals and language descriptions is a necessary and sufficient intermediate objective for robust, open-ended activity understanding.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "retrieval-based evaluation framework measuring sensor–language semantic alignment; [...] subsuming closed-set classification as a special case."
  - [section 4.2] "In open-vocabulary wearable understanding, a single sensor segment may correspond to multiple semantically valid descriptions [...] R@K evaluates whether the model retrieves any correct description."
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on closed-set or self-supervised HAR; this retrieval paradigm for wearables is novel per paper's claims.
- **Break condition:** If retrieval metrics do not correlate with downstream performance (e.g., closed-set classification) or if the description pool is artificially constrained (e.g., only templated sentences), the framework fails to measure open-vocabulary generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Discretizing IMU signals into spectral motion tokens via a VQ-VAE, followed by cross-attention fusion (Q-Former) into a frozen LLM, yields a sensor-language alignment that is robust to placement shift and missing sensors.
- **Mechanism:** The Spectral VQ-VAE learns a compact codebook of reusable micro-action primitives by jointly modeling time, STFT, and wavelet domains. These tokens are placement-invariant (low JS divergence across positions) and compositional. The Q-Former then fuses variable-length, heterogeneous token sequences into a fixed set of LLM-compatible prompts, explicitly conditioning on sensor positions and segment duration. This avoids overfitting to specific placements or window sizes.
- **Core assumption:** Discrete, spectral motion tokens provide a semantic bottleneck that encourages invariance to amplitude, timing, and placement variations, while the frozen LLM supplies open-vocabulary reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "spectral VQ-VAE for discretizing IMU signals into motion tokens and a Q-Former to condition a frozen LLM for open-vocabulary generation."
  - [section 5.1] "Multi-view spectral encoding [...] captures both transient and periodic motion [...] quantized via a shared codebook."
  - [section 6.2] Table 4: Spectral VQ-VAE achieves lowest reconstruction error and lowest token divergence (JS) under cross-position (XSP).
  - [section 6.3] Table 5: Spectral tokens + Q-Former substantially outperform raw features and baselines on retrieval.
- **Break condition:** If tokenization fails to produce reusable codes (high JS divergence) or if the Q-Former/Llama fusion does not improve over simple pooling under missing-sensor evaluation, the architecture does not achieve its intended robustness.

## Foundational Learning
**Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)**
- Why needed here: Converts continuous, high-frequency IMU streams into discrete sequences of motion tokens, forming a finite "motion vocabulary" that can be aligned with language tokens.
- Quick check question: How does a VQ-VAE's codebook learning encourage the emergence of reusable motion primitives invariant to amplitude and slight temporal shifts?

**Cross-Attention Fusion (Q-Former) for Multimodal Alignment**
- Why needed here: Integrates variable-length token sequences from an arbitrary subset of 15 sensor positions into a fixed-size representation that a frozen LLM can consume, while making sensor positions and duration explicit.
- Quick check question: Why is cross-attention with learnable queries preferred over mean/attention pooling for fusing heterogeneous, partially observed sensor token sequences?

**Open-Vocabulary Retrieval Metrics (R@K, MRR, nDCG@K)**
- Why needed here: Evaluates semantic alignment without fixed classes, measuring whether correct (or paraphrased) descriptions are retrieved and ranked highly, which is critical for real-world usability.
- Quick check question: Why is nDCG@K a more suitable metric than plain accuracy for open-ended activity descriptions, where multiple valid descriptions may exist for the same sensor segment?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
1. **Spectral VQ-VAE Tokenizer:** Input raw multi-channel IMU segment → multi-view (time, STFT, CWT) encoding → vector quantization → discrete token sequence.
2. **Position & Duration Header:** Textual string encoding which sensor positions are active and for how long.
3. **Q-Former:** Takes concatenated token embeddings from active positions → cross-attention with learnable queries → fixed number of "sensor prompt" vectors in LLM embedding space.
4. **Frozen LLM (e.g., Qwen):** Takes `[Header] + [Sensor Prompts]` → autoregressive text generation (for retrieval) or mapping to fixed labels (for downstream classification).
5. **Token-based Augmentations (training only):** Random dropout, insertion, swap of tokens in the sequence to encourage robustness.

**Critical path**
IMU segment → Spectral VQ-VAE → token sequence → Q-Former (fuses active position sequences) → LLM → text output. The header must accurately state active sensors and segment duration for correct conditioning.

**Design tradeoffs**
- **Token sequence length vs. window size:** Longer Δ𝑡 improves local reconstruction but blurs motion phases; shorter Δ𝑡 increases fragmentation. Optimal Δ𝑡=2s found empirically.
- **Codebook size K:** Larger K reduces reconstruction error but harms token invariance (JS divergence). K=128 best trade-off.
- **LLM scale:** Performance improves up to ~7B parameters, then plateaus; alignment quality (training) matters more than raw linguistic capacity.
- **Full-sensor training vs. subset training:** Training on all 15 positions yields better robustness under missing-sensor inference than training only on the expected subset.

**Failure signatures**
- High **token JS divergence** under XSP indicates poor placement invariance in the codebook.
- Sharp drop in **Retrieval R@1** under cross-position (XP) vs. cross-subject (XS) suggests the model overfits to placement-specific signal patterns.
- **Closed-set Macro-F1** under XP < 40% indicates failure to generalize beyond the training placement configuration.
- Poor performance with **single-sensor input** (e.g., only wrist) even when trained on all sensors suggests inadequate learning of position-invariant primitives.
- **VLM annotation yield** low retrieval performance (Table 11), indicating a mismatch between vision-centric language and motion semantics.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Tokenizer Ablation:** Evaluate Spectral VQ-VAE against Raw VQ-VAE, STFT-only, Wavelet-only on reconstruction error (time/spectral ℓ1) and token JS divergence (XS/XSP). Expected: spectral multi-view wins on all.
2. **Retrieval Core:** Test `Spectral tokens + Q-Former` against `Raw + Q-Former` and prior baselines (IMU2CLIP, OVHAR, PaLM-E) on R@1/MRR under XS and XP splits. Expected: large gain for spectral tokens.
3. **Missing-Sensor Robustness:** Train on all 15 sensors, evaluate on subsets (wrist-only, thigh+head, etc.). Compare against models trained only on the subset. Expected: full-sensor training outperforms subset training across all inference subsets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can ActivityNarrated's naturalistic data collection paradigm be scaled to more diverse populations (e.g., elderly, children, individuals with motor impairments) and real-world environments beyond the controlled room setting?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors acknowledge: "The dataset comprises 22 participants collected from a limited number of locations. Certain populations, including elderly users and individuals with motor impairments, are not represented. As a result, reported performance should not be assumed to generalize universally without validation on more diverse cohorts and behavioral profiles."
- **Why unresolved:** The current data reflects a narrow demographic (age 21-33, mostly university-affiliated) and a structured indoor environment with predefined hotspots. Real-world deployment requires understanding activity patterns across age groups, physical capabilities, cultural contexts, and uncontrolled settings (homes, public spaces, workplaces). Without such data, claims about generalizability remain limited.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic expansion of the dataset protocol to recruit participants spanning age 65+, diverse cultural backgrounds, and varying mobility levels, coupled with deployments in natural living environments. Analysis would need to identify population-specific activity patterns, failure modes under such diversity, and whether architectural changes (e.g., adaptive tokenization, personalized narration alignment) are needed.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What model compression and efficiency techniques can preserve the semantic alignment benefits of language-grounded HAR while enabling real-time inference on resource-constrained wearable devices?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The limitation section states: "Our strongest results rely on discrete tokenization pipelines and large pretrained language backbones (up to 8B parameters). While smaller models remain competitive, computational and memory requirements currently limit direct on-device deployment. Future work on model compression, efficient tokenization, and lightweight language alignment will be critical for translating narrative-based HAR to resource-constrained wearable platforms."
- **Why unresolved:** ActNarrator's proof-of-concept uses server-grade GPUs (NVIDIA A6000). Wearables typically operate under <100mW power budgets and <100MB memory. The frozen LLM component alone exceeds these constraints. Without efficient deployment strategies, the paradigm remains academic.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of compressed variants (quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation) or alternative lightweight architectures (e.g., small transformer adapters instead of frozen LLMs) that achieve ≤95% of full model's R@1/Macro-F1 under cross-position evaluation while meeting wearable constraints (inference <100ms, <50MB memory, <200mW). Systematic benchmarking across compression techniques with real hardware.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can language supervision be personalized and adaptively refined over time through continual learning from in-the-wild user narrations, while avoiding catastrophic forgetting of cross-user semantic alignment?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The future directions section proposes: "Continual learning from in-the-wild narrations offers a path toward personalization and long-term adaptation." The paper also notes that user narrations showed lower performance than expert annotations due to variability.
- **Why unresolved:** Current training treats all users equally and relies on offline expert or user-provided narrations. Real deployment would involve a device learning from its specific user's way of describing activities over months, potentially incorporating unique terminology, routines, and phrasing. However, continual adaptation risks overwriting shared semantic knowledge and degrading cross-user generalization.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A lifelong learning framework that (a) separates shared motion primitives (token space) from user-specific language mappings, (b) employs elastic weight consolidation or replay buffers to preserve general alignment, and (c) demonstrates improved retrieval accuracy for the target user without degrading performance on other users or the closed-set taxonomy. Evaluation should include longitudinal studies with repeated narration collection.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the discrete token representation and sensor-language alignment framework be extended to handle truly multimodal wearable inputs (e.g., audio, physiological signals, environmental context) to generate more comprehensive activity narratives?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors suggest: "Extending discrete, token-based representations to additional sensing modalities—such as audio, vision, or physiological signals—could enable richer multimodal activity narratives." The current ActNarrator processes only multi-position IMU.
- **Why unresolved:** Activity understanding could benefit from audio (speech, ambient sounds), physiological (heart rate, skin conductance), and environmental (light, proximity) data. However, the tokenization and alignment pipeline is designed for homogeneous IMU streams. Integrating heterogeneous modalities with different sampling rates, semantics, and reliability characteristics is non-trivial and may require fundamentally different discretization strategies or cross-modal fusion mechanisms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An extended architecture that can accept arbitrary wearable modalities as input, either by modality-specific tokenizers or a unified multimodal token space, and demonstrate improved narrative generation (e.g., adding "talking on phone" from audio, "strenuous exercise" from heart rate) compared to IMU-only. Evaluation should include ablation of each modality and robustness to missing modalities.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What evaluation protocols and metrics best assess open-world capabilities like compositional generalization (recognizing unseen combinations of known primitives), multi-label activities, and fine-grained temporal localization in continuous streams?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "Finally, developing evaluation protocols that better reflect open-world, multi-label, and temporally overlapping behaviors remains essential for advancing wearable HAR beyond closed-set benchmarks." Current evaluation uses predefined segments and either retrieval or single-label classification.
- **Why unresolved:** Natural behavior frequently involves overlapping activities ("walking while eating"), hierarchies ("cooking dinner" contains "chopping", "stirring"), and variable durations. The current framework doesn't explicitly test whether models can decompose complex activities into primitives, recognize concurrent actions, or localize activity boundaries in untrimmed streams—key capabilities for real-world deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** New benchmark tasks such as: (a) compositional generalization test sets with novel combinations of motion primitives, (b) multi-label retrieval where multiple valid descriptions exist per segment, (c) temporal boundary detection for overlapping activities, and (d) hierarchical activity recognition. Metrics should go beyond R@1 to measure precision/recall of primitive sequences and overlap handling.

## Limitations
- Dataset unreleased: ActivityNarrated is not publicly available, so critical details such as annotation alignment, segment boundaries, and placement strategies remain unknown, preventing independent verification.
- Retrieval framework novelty: The sensor-language retrieval evaluation lacks precedent in HAR; its correlation with practical utility and open-vocabulary understanding is not demonstrated.
- Architecture and results: ActNarrator's strong performance relies on unreleased data and incompletely specified hyperparameters, with no external validation; thus confidence in reproducibility and generalizability is low.
