# To Mix or To Merge: Toward Multi-Domain Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.12566  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12566  
- **Reference count**: 21  
- **Primary result**: Mixed multi-task RL achieves comparable performance to merged models with 63.7% less GPU time, showing minimal cross-domain interference and synergy among reasoning domains (math, coding, science).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates multi-domain reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for language models, comparing mixed multi-task training against separate domain-specific training followed by model merging. Using Qwen3-4B and Nemotron data across five domains (math, coding, science, instruction following, agent), the authors find that mixed multi-task RL attains performance on par with merging while reducing GPU consumption by 63.7%. The study reveals minimal cross-domain interference and identifies policy neighborhood transfer and overlapping weight update footprints as key mechanisms enabling synergy. However, extended multi-task RL degrades process-level verification capabilities, whereas merging preserves them. The work highlights trade-offs in scaling multi-domain RL and provides guidance on when to mix versus merge.

## Method Summary
The authors base their experiments on Qwen3-4B-Base. First, they perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a blended Nemotron 3 Nano dataset (14M samples). Then, for each domain (math, coding, science, instruction following, agent), they conduct RLVR using the GRPO algorithm (400 steps, lr=2e-6) with binary correctness rewards and domain-specific verifiers (e.g., QwQ-32B for math, SandboxFusion for code). Multi-domain training is approached in two ways: (1) mixing all domains from the start (1000 steps), and (2) merging separately trained domain-specific RL models via techniques such as TIES, average, SCE, DARE, and on-policy distillation (OPD, 200 steps). Evaluation covers nine benchmarks (AIME, LCB, GPQA-Diamond, IFEval, IFBench, BFCL, etc.), measuring outcome accuracy, process verification, and computational efficiency. Underspecified details that could hinder exact replication include the precise implementation of reward verifiers, exact data splits and preprocessing, and the random projection seed used for weight-overlap analysis.

## Key Results
- Mixed multi-task RL achieves comparable performance to merged models while using 63.7% less GPU time.
- Weight update footprints across domains overlap substantially (Jaccard ~0.47), enabling stable multi-task training.
- Multi-task RL improves outcome verification but degrades process-level verification; merging preserves both capabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Policy Neighborhood Transfer
- **Claim**: Multi-domain RLVR gains occur when domain policies occupy KL-neighboring regions, enabling beneficial transfer during merging or joint training.
- **Mechanism**: Policies with low forward KL divergence (e.g., coding→math) occupy similar parameter-space neighborhoods; training on one domain shifts the policy toward a basin beneficial for neighboring domains, creating synergy.
- **Core assumption**: KL divergence between expert policies predicts transfer compatibility more reliably than weight overlap.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "neighborhood policy transfer during multi-task RLVR or model merging shapes the domain policies toward the optimal policy"
  - [section 3.4]: "experts from other domains can also exhibit relatively low KL divergence with the resulting multi-domain policy model" and policy neighborhood identification
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; corpus shows related multi-domain RL work but not this specific analysis; paper's empirical KL study is primary evidence
- **Break condition**: If KL(π_domainA || π_combined) exceeds threshold (e.g., >0.1 based on Figure 4 patterns), transfer turns to interference.

### Mechanism 2: Overlapping Weight Update Footprints
- **Claim**: RLVR domains modify largely coincident subsets of weights (Jaccard ~0.47-0.48 vs random ~0.18), reducing gradient conflict and enabling multi-task training.
- **Mechanism**: Significant overlap in changed weight masks means domains adjust similar functional modules; positive cosine similarity in overlaps (Figure 3) indicates compatible directional shifts rather than opposing forces.
- **Core assumption**: Weight change masks reflect meaningful functional specialization; measured overlap correlates with training stability.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3]: "weight update footprints in reinforcement learning across different domains have significant overlap" and Table 5 showing ~0.47-0.48 overlap across attention/FFN weights
  - [section 3.3 figure 3]: cross-domain cosine similarity remains positive after LSH projection
- **Break condition**: Overlap approaches random baseline (~0.18), indicating high interference; multi-task training would likely fail.

### Mechanism 3: Verification Capability Trade-off
- **Claim**: Extended multi-task RL collapses process-level verification while preserving outcome verification; decoupled merging retains both capabilities.
- **Mechanism**: Multi-task RL optimizes final accuracy but gradient interference misaligns the model's internal critic; merging combines already-aligned specialist critics, preserving robustness.
- **Core assumption**: Process verification (reasoning trace evaluation) is more sensitive to training dynamics than outcome verification (final answer check).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "RLVR improves outcome verification but extended multi-task RL degrades process-level verification"
  - [section 3.6 figure 8]: RL-Multi achieves generation ~73 but process judging ~27.5 (IFEval) vs Merging ~73 and ~80
  - [section 3.6 table 7]: RL-Agent (agentic training) achieves highest process judging scores across domains (95.3 on AIME, 99.4 on LCB)
- **Break condition**: Process judging score >20 points below outcome judging on reasoning tasks indicates collapse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards)  
  - *Why needed*: Core training paradigm; binary correctness rewards replace preference-based signals to stimulate explicit reasoning.  
  - *Quick check*: Why might verifiable rewards scale more cleanly across domains than human preference rewards?

- **Concept**: Model Parameter Merging (TIES, Average, SCE)  
  - *Why needed*: Primary alternative to multi-task RL; methods like TIES resolve conflicts via sign agreement pruning before merging.  
  - *Quick check*: What is the computational advantage of weight merging versus training a multi-task model from scratch?

- **Concept**: Policy Neighborhood via KL Divergence  
  - *Why needed*: Analytical framework to explain when domain combinations yield synergy vs interference.  
  - *Quick check*: Why is forward KL (π_old || π_new) used instead of symmetric KL in this analysis?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Qwen3-4B-Base → SFT → Domain-Specific RL (math/coding/science/IF/agent) → {Multi-Task RL OR Weight Merging OR MT-OPD}

- **Critical path**: First establish single-domain RL baselines; validate weight shift overlap (~0.47 expected) and KL patterns before attempting merging or multi-task experiments.

- **Design tradeoffs**: Multi-task RL saves 63.7% GPU time but risks process verification collapse; separate+merging preserves specialists but costs more; MT-OPD adds distillation overhead but captures behavioral alignment.

- **Failure signatures**: (1) Process judging score >30% below outcome judging on math/coding benchmarks, (2) >3 point drop when adding a new domain to multi-task mix, (3) Weight change Jaccard overlap <0.25.

- **First 3 experiments**: (1) Reproduce single-domain RL results for all 5 domains using Nemotron data; (2) Compare TIES vs average merging on math+coding combination, checking for gain consistency in Figure 5 pattern; (3) Measure weight shift overlap on 17th layer to confirm ~0.47 Jaccard baseline before scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors do not identify specific open questions in the paper. The study concludes with empirical findings and does not enumerate future research directions.

## Limitations
- Underspecified reward verifier implementations (e.g., QwQ-32B, SandboxFusion) risk inconsistent reward signals.
- Exact Nemotron data splits, filtering, and prompt formatting are not disclosed; domain balance may drift.
- Weight-change analysis depends on unreported random projection seed and threshold η=1e-3; generalizability is unknown.
- The experiments are restricted to a single model architecture (Qwen3-4B), limiting the generalizability of the findings to other model sizes or families.

## Confidence
- Policy Neighborhood Transfer: Medium (empirical evidence from KL measurements; theoretical underpinning and cross-domain validation are limited)  
- Overlapping Weight Update Footprints: High (consistent weight overlap across layers and domains; directly measured and reproducible)  
- Verification Capability Trade-off: Medium (strong correlation observed; mechanistic explanation remains speculative without ablation studies)  

## Next Checks
1. Reproduce single-domain RL baselines and compute weight-change masks across all layers to confirm the ~0.47 Jaccard baseline.  
2. Ablate the weight-change magnitude threshold η (e.g., 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2) to test robustness of overlap and KL-neighborhood results.  
3. Run zero-shot transfer experiments: after training math-only RL, evaluate on coding and science benchmarks; compare outcomes with KL predictions to validate neighborhood utility.
