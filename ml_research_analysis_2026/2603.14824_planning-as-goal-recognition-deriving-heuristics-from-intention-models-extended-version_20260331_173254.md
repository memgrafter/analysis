# Planning as Goal Recognition: Deriving Heuristics from Intention Models -- Extended Version

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14824  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14824  
- **Reference count:** 7  
- **Primary result:** The `OL_rp_r` heuristic increases coverage by up to 5% on the IPC satisficing benchmark suite and outperforms several state-of-the-art planners, while `op_rp` variants reduce node expansions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework that transforms planning heuristics into goal-recognition tools. The core idea is to assess a trajectory’s “goal-intentionality” by measuring its divergence from a distribution over goal-intended plans. From this framework, two efficiently computable heuristics are derived: `ol_rp`, based on the likelihood of observed facts, and `op_rp`, based on a Bayesian posterior ratio. Both are estimated from sampled delete‑relaxed plans and integrated into the BFWS planner as tie‑breakers. On the IPC satisficing benchmark suite (1831 problems), the best variant (`OL_rp_r`) improves coverage by up to 5% over the baseline and surpasses several state‑of‑the‑art planners. The `op_rp` variants achieve fewer node expansions, and the results empirically validate theoretical properties linking the heuristics to guided search and the exploration‑exploitation trade‑off.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three steps. First, offline sampling of delete‑relaxed plans is used to estimate Bernoulli fact occurrence probabilities under the goal (`P_rel(q|G)`) and under non‑goal conditions (`P_rel(q|¬G)`). Second, during forward state‑space search, for each state the heuristic value is computed as either `ol_rp` (sum of log `P_rel(q|G)` for facts observed along the trajectory) or `op_rp` (sum of log‑likelihood ratios `P_rel(q|G)/P_rel(q|¬G)`). A restart variant (`*_r`) resets the observed facts whenever the primary heuristic `h_GC` improves. Third, these heuristics are injected as a tertiary tie‑breaker in BFWS’s best‑first ordering: nodes are sorted primarily by `h_GC`, secondarily by `g` (path cost), and tertiarily by the new heuristic value. The offline precomputation enables O(1) per‑state evaluation, and the approach leverages a divergence‑based likelihood model that connects goal recognition to planning heuristics.

## Key Results
- The `OL_rp_r` variant increased coverage by up to 5% over baseline BFWS on the IPC satisficing benchmark suite (1831 problems).
- `op_rp` variants significantly reduced node expansions, demonstrating improved search efficiency.
- The best heuristic outperformed several state‑of‑the‑art planners, and the results empirically validated theoretical properties linking the heuristics to guided search and the exploration‑exploitation trade‑off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Divergence‑based likelihood**  
The framework defines a likelihood function `˜P(O|G) = e^(-D_KL(d_O,G || d_G))` where `d_G` models goal‑intended outcomes (from sampled plans) and `d_O,G` is the posterior after observing facts. Theorem 4 proves this equals `-log ρ(y*|G)`, the negative log‑likelihood of the observed facts under the goal model. Two heuristics are derived: `ol_rp` uses `˜P(O_F|G)` (product of fact probabilities) and `op_rp` uses the posterior ratio `˜P(G|O_F) ∝ P(O_F|G)/P(O_F|¬G)`. The core assumption is that goal‑directed behavior can be captured by a distribution over solution trajectories (e.g., sampled delete‑relaxed plans). If the sampled plans are poor approximations, the heuristic may misrank trajectories.

**Efficient fact probability estimation**  
Fact occurrence probabilities are estimated from sampled delete‑relaxed plans using a Bernoulli model: for each fact `q`, `P_rel(q|G)` is the probability it appears in any sampled plan. `ol_rp` multiplies these for observed facts; `op_rp` also requires `P_rel(q|¬G)` estimated from non‑goal sequences. These tables are computed offline, enabling O(1) per‑state evaluation during search. The assumption is that delete‑relaxed plans sufficiently capture goal‑intended behavior and that fact‑level statistics correlate with trajectory‑level intentionality. Break condition: in domains where delete relaxation yields many unsupported facts or complex interactions, the estimates become noisy.

**Guided search and exploration‑exploitation trade‑off**  
The IRPL model proves that expanding nodes by `max P(G|O)` (greedy) guarantees finding a minimal‑cost plan in the sample with bounded expansions, while `max P(O|G)` (exploratory) minimizes a lower‑bound on node generations. Empirically, `ol_rp_r` (exploratory, with restart) improved coverage, while `op_rp_r` (greedy) reduced expansions. This validates the theoretical link between the heuristics and guided search, and demonstrates the exploration‑exploitation trade‑off. The break condition arises when the sampled plan library omits critical solution paths or when the weighting scheme mismatches actual agent behavior.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Offline Fact Probability Estimator → Heuristic Calculator → BFWS Integrator → Search Engine  

Critical path: Sampling → fact probability tables → per‑state fact set extraction → heuristic value lookup → tie‑breaking during node expansion. The offline phase is separable; online phase must be O(|observed facts|).  

Design tradeoffs:  
- Sample size: More samples improve estimate quality but increase pre‑processing time.  
- Restart vs. global: `ol_rp_r` prevents degradation from early irrelevant facts but loses long‑term negative bias.  
- Weighting scheme: UTP re‑weighting (OL_rp_r‑UTP) prioritizes optimistic relaxed plans, improving speed on some domains but reducing coverage.

Failure signatures:  
- Heuristic provides no signal (values uniform) due to poor sampling or uninformative fact probabilities.  
- Excessive overhead from sampling and per‑node computation, reducing expansions per second.  
- Incorrect restart logic causing heuristic to reset too frequently or never.  
- `op_rp` sampling fails to cover non‑goal facts, yielding many zero probabilities.

First 3 experiments:  
1. Baseline ablation: Run `BFWS(f5)` vs. `+ol_rp_r` and `+op_rp_r` on the IPC satisficing suite to confirm coverage and expansion gains.  
2. Restart validation: Compare `ol_rp` (global facts) vs. `ol_rp_r` (restart) to measure impact of losing cumulative negative bias.  
3. Weighting scheme test: Compare `OL_rp_r` (uniform plan weights) vs. `OL_rp_r-UTP` (UTP‑weighted plans) to validate theoretical predictions about optimistic sampling.

## Limitations
- Non‑goal sampling procedure for `op_rp` is underspecified, risking biased estimates of `P_rel(q|¬G)`.
- Tie‑breaking and restart details are not precisely documented; small implementation changes could alter results.
- The approach depends heavily on delete‑relaxed plans and may not extend to domains with richer semantics (e.g., metrics, time, conditional effects).

## Confidence
- Divergence‑based likelihood: High  
- Fact probability efficiency: Medium  
- Guided search and exploration‑exploitation trade‑off: Medium
