# Optimal Regret for Policy Optimization in Contextual Bandits
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.13700
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13700
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves information-theoretically optimal high-probability regret bound $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{K|\mathcal{A}|\log|\mathcal{F}|})$ for stochastic contextual bandits with general offline function approximation.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents OPO-CMAB, a novel policy optimization algorithm for stochastic contextual bandits that leverages general offline function approximation. By integrating counterfactual exploration bonuses into an exponential-weight update rule, the algorithm attains an optimal regret bound of $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{K|\mathcal{A}|\log|\mathcal{F}|})$, matching the known lower bound and thereby providing the first rigorous justification for policy optimization methods in this setting. Empirical evaluations on the VowpalWabbit benchmark suite demonstrate competitive performance against state-of-the-art approaches, illustrating the practical effectiveness of the theoretical approach.

## Method Summary
OPO-CMAB operates by iteratively updating a policy based on a weighted exponential loss. Over a fixed offline dataset, a regression oracle provides reward estimates for all arms given contexts. The algorithm then constructs counterfactual exploration bonuses that quantify uncertainty in these estimates, augmenting the loss to encourage exploration. At each round, the policy is updated by multiplying weights exponentially according to the penalized loss, ensuring low regret relative to the best policy in the hypothesis class $\mathcal{F}$. The reliance on an offline regression oracle yields computational efficiency, as the heavy lifting is delegated to standard regression calls.

## Key Results
- Optimal high-probability regret bound $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{K|\mathcal{A}|\log|\mathcal{F}|})$, matching the information-theoretic lower bound.
- First theoretical justification for policy optimization in contextual bandits with offline function approximation.
- Competitive empirical performance on the VowpalWabbit benchmark suite.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm’s success stems from two key innovations. First, counterfactual exploration bonuses are derived from the variance of reward estimates under the current policy, ensuring sufficient exploration even when the data is purely offline. Second, the exponential-weight update rule is known to achieve no-regret in adversarial settings, and here it is carefully combined with the bonuses to maintain the optimal dependence on $K$, $|\mathcal{A}|$, and $\log|\mathcal{F}|$. The offline oracle assumption allows the method to scale to rich function classes while keeping per-round computation low.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Offline Dataset → Regression Oracle (F) → Reward Estimates for All Arms → Counterfactual Bonus Computation → Penalized Loss → Exponential Weight Update → Policy (π)

**Critical path**: The algorithm’s correctness relies on the proper construction of the exploration bonus from the uncertainty of the reward estimates, followed by the exponential weight update, which together ensure optimal regret.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Choosing the bonus magnitude trades exploration for regret; too large may hinder exploitation, too small may cause insufficient exploration.
- Relying on an offline oracle reduces computational burden but introduces potential errors if the oracle is inaccurate.
- The exponential-weight rule is simple and effective but may be suboptimal for certain structured losses.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Misspecification: if the true reward function is not in $\mathcal{F}$, the regret bound may degrade.
- Oracle instability: noisy or biased reward estimates lead to incorrect bonuses and poor performance.
- Large action spaces: the $\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|}$ factor can dominate regret if not carefully managed.

## Limitations
- Relies on realizability; performance may degrade under model misspecification.
- Computational cost is tied to the offline regression oracle, which may be prohibitive for very large or complex function classes (e.g., deep neural networks).
- Empirical evaluation is limited to the VowpalWabbit benchmark without statistical significance testing, leaving generalizability uncertain.
