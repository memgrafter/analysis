# Learning User Interests via Reasoning and Distillation for Cross-Domain News Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15005  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15005  
- Reference count: 15  
- Primary result: RL-trained teacher (Qwen2.5-32B) outperforms baselines; distilled student (Qwen2.5-0.5B) retains most gains with 30× throughput, yielding +0.95% DAU and +0.22% CTR online.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles cross-domain news recommendation by inferring reusable user interests from heterogeneous behavioral signals beyond direct news consumption. It uses a reinforcement learning framework (GRPO) to train a large language model (Qwen2.5-32B) to generate interest-driven news search query lists, optimized by five reward signals that tie query quality to retrieval performance. The learned policy is then distilled via on-policy token-level KL minimization to a compact student model (Qwen2.5-0.5B) suitable for high-throughput production. The approach demonstrates substantial offline improvements over strong baselines (Recall@10: 0.271 vs. 0.250; NDCG@10: 0.241 vs. 0.228) and maintains most gains in the distilled model while achieving a 30× speedup. Online A/B tests confirm statistically significant lifts in daily active users (+0.95%), click-through rate (+0.22%), and cold-start user engagement (+4.38%).

## Method Summary
The system treats the generation of user interest queries as a policy optimization problem. A teacher policy implemented by a 32-billion-parameter LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) is trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). For a given user’s cleaned multi-behavior log, the policy samples a group of candidate query lists and receives a composite reward computed as a weighted sum of five components: retrieval alignment (embedding similarity to retrieved articles), interest coverage and specificity (LLM-evaluated rubrics), intra-list diversity, and structural validity. The GRPO objective updates the policy to prefer query lists that outperform the group average, with a KL penalty relative to a reference model. After convergence, the teacher’s policy is transferred to a 500-million-parameter student (Qwen2.5-0.5B) via on-policy distillation, where the student learns to mimic the teacher’s next-token distribution on sequences it generates itself. This on-policy approach better aligns the student’s own distribution with the teacher’s reasoning, leading to significant improvements over supervised distillation. The final student model is deployed online: it takes the cleaned user behavior as input, produces a list of interest queries, which are used for approximate nearest-neighbor retrieval of news candidates.

## Key Results
- Offline: Teacher model achieves Recall@10 = 0.271 and NDCG@10 = 0.241, surpassing the HSTU baseline (0.250, 0.228). Student model retains most gains (Recall@10 0.261, NDCG@10 0.235) while increasing inference throughput 30× (67 vs. 2 users/sec).
- Online: A/B testing shows statistically significant lifts in daily active users (+0.95%), click-through rate (+0.22%), and cold-user DAU (+4.38%).
- Scaling: Both model size (0.5B→32B) and inference-time sampling (N=1→32) yield consistent performance improvements, with diminishing returns at higher compute budgets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

The paper presents three key mechanisms that together enable effective cross-domain interest modeling and efficient deployment.

**Mechanism 1: GRPO trains an LLM to generate high-quality query lists from heterogeneous signals.**  
The query-list generation is formalized as a policy optimization problem. The policy πθ samples a group of candidate lists from an old policy πθ_old. Each list receives a composite reward R. The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective (Dr.GRPO) updates the policy by maximizing the advantage of a sampled list relative to the group average, with a KL penalty to avoid large deviations from a reference model. This encourages the model to produce lists that consistently outperform random samples from its recent behavior across all reward dimensions. Evidence: The paper details the policy formulation, group sampling, advantage calculation (Ag = r_g - group_avg), and composite reward R(u, Z_u). Ablation studies (Table 5) confirm the necessity of multiple rewards. Break condition: If the reward signal fails to capture a necessary aspect (e.g., R_align too weak), the policy may exploit reward hacking.

**Mechanism 2: A multi-reward design ensures query lists are retrieval-useful and holistically represent interests.**  
The total reward R = Σ λ_m R_m combines five components: R_align ensures queries retrieve relevant articles; R_cov and R_spec (LLM-evaluated rubrics) ensure coverage of major inferred themes and avoid generic terms; R_div promotes semantic diversity; R_struct enforces output format. Ablation in Table 5 shows removing any component significantly degrades downstream metrics, proving their complementary and non-redundant roles. Break condition: Misaligned reward weights or calibration may cause offline gains that do not translate to online engagement.

**Mechanism 3: On-policy distillation transfers the teacher’s reasoning to a compact student for production.**  
During distillation, the student π_ψ generates token sequences conditioned on its own prefixes (y_<t ~ π_ψ). The loss minimizes reverse KL divergence between the student’s next-token distribution and the teacher’s distribution for the same prefix. This on-policy approach uses the student’s own rollouts for supervision, providing better coverage of the student’s error modes compared to supervised distillation that uses teacher-generated prefixes. Evidence: Table 3 shows on-policy distillation (Recall@10 0.261) significantly outperforms supervised distillation (0.245) and no distillation (0.187), closing the gap to the teacher (0.271). Break condition: The capacity gap between teacher (32B) and student (0.5B) may be too large for faithful approximation, leading to catastrophic forgetting or inability to capture nuanced patterns, evident by a sharp performance drop or high KL divergence.

## Foundational Learning

This section outlines core concepts required to understand the paper’s contributions.

- **Reinforcement Learning from Reward Models (RL from RM)**  
  *Why needed:* The system lacks ground-truth ideal query lists. It must optimize a black-box, non-differentiable reward function R composed of diverse sub-rewards (some LLM-based). Policy gradient methods like GRPO directly optimize the LLM’s generative policy against this composite reward.  
  *Quick check:* In the GRPO objective (Eq. 6), what is the purpose of the clip(ρ_g, 1-ε, 1+ε) term, and how does the group-relative baseline (Ag) stabilize training compared to using a fixed baseline?

- **On-Policy Knowledge Distillation**  
  *Why needed:* To deploy a high-quality but slow 32B model in a high-throughput production system. The student must mimic the teacher’s policy (mapping behaviors → query lists), not just its predictions on a fixed dataset. On-policy distillation uses rollouts from the student itself to compute the distillation loss, which aligns the student’s own distribution with the teacher’s.  
  *Quick check:* How does the distillation loss L_distill (Eq. 10) ensure the student learns from the teacher’s reasoning process, and why is using y_<t ~ π_ψ (student-generated prefix) potentially better than using y_<t ~ π_θ (teacher-generated prefix)?

- **Cross-Domain Multi-Behavior Signal Fusion**  
  *Why needed:* User interests are inferred from a cleaned, multi-source set of behaviors (browsing, search, news clicks). The LLM must synthesize these heterogeneous signals into a coherent, reusable set of interest queries, requiring modeling beyond simple concatenation.  
  *Quick check:* Before the RL stage, what is the purpose of the noise cleaning classifier f_φ, and what kind of behaviors is it likely designed to filter out based on the prompt in Appendix B?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Data Ingestion & Cleaning → Teacher Policy (Offline Training) → Student Policy (Distillation) → Online Serving  
  More concretely: Raw logs → RoBERTa classifier f_φ → Cleaned behavior set ˜B_u → GRPO-trained Qwen2.5-32B policy π_θ → List of interest queries Z_u; On-policy distillation with Qwen2.5-0.5B policy π_ψ → Deployed π_ψ for online serving (˜B_u → Z_u → ANN retrieval → ranking).

- **Critical path:** The core value chain is Cleaning → Teacher RL Optimization → Student Distillation → Online Serving. A failure in cleaning propagates noise to the teacher, which then distills poor behavior to the student. The RL reward design is the central tuning point linking interest quality to retrieval utility.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Teacher Quality vs. Student Efficiency: Larger teacher (32B) captures complex patterns but is too slow (2 users/sec). Smaller student (0.5B) meets throughput needs (67 users/sec) but loses some nuance (Table 3 gap).  
  - Reward Complexity vs. Training Stability: Five rewards improve quality but complicate credit assignment and risk reward hacking; ablations (Table 5) show interdependencies.  
  - Compute Scaling: Both model size (Fig 2) and inference-time sampling (Fig 3) improve metrics but increase cost; production uses N=1 for latency.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Offline: Sudden drop in Recall@10/NDCG@10 after a training epoch; high variance in reward components (e.g., R_div near zero indicates redundant queries); KL divergence during distillation remains high.  
  - Online: Negative or negligible lift in DAU/CTR in A/B test; disproportionate drop in cold-user metrics; increased latency or error rates in retrieval service following query generation.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Reward Ablation:** Train teacher models with one reward component removed each time (as in Table 5) to verify each reward’s contribution and identify potential hacking behaviors.  
  2. **Student Capacity Sweep:** Distill policies into student models of varying sizes (e.g., 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B) to quantify the performance-efficiency Pareto frontier and validate the chosen 0.5B/32B pairing.  
  3. **Noise Cleaning Threshold Analysis:** Vary the classification threshold of f_φ to control the aggressiveness of behavior filtering. Measure the impact on teacher policy reward and student final metrics to find the optimal precision-recall tradeoff for ˜B_u.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **What are the trade-offs and interactions between the five reward components during policy optimization?**  
   The paper designs a composite reward and performs an ablation, but does not analyze how these rewards might conflict or synergize during training, nor does it justify the fixed weights.

2. **What is the empirical scaling breakdown point for model capacity and inference-time compute in this interest modeling task?**  
   Scaling trends are shown up to 32B and N=32, but the point of diminishing returns or degradation for larger models or higher sampling is unknown.

3. **Which specific reasoning or abstraction capabilities of the teacher LLM are preserved versus lost during on-policy distillation to the 0.5B student?**  
   The student preserves most gains, but it is unclear what aspects of the teacher’s reasoning survive distillation, limiting understanding of the student’s failure modes.

4. **Why do cross-domain signals yield a substantially larger DAU lift for cold-start users (+4.38%) compared to the overall population (+0.95%)?**  
   The hypothesis is that heterogeneous signals help when news history is limited, but it is unclear which signals are most impactful or whether the improvement stems from better coverage, specificity, or simply more signals.

5. **Does the RL-based policy optimization lead to more robust or generalizable user interest representations compared to supervised fine-tuning with synthetic interest labels, especially under distribution shifts?**  
   The paper contrasts GRPO only indirectly via distillation outcomes; a direct comparison with an SFT baseline trained on high-quality synthetic queries is missing.

## Limitations
- Exact prompts for query generation, LLM-judge rubrics, and the cleaning classifier are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- Hyperparameters (group size G, KL penalty β, reward weights λ_m, learning rates) are not specified; optimal values likely differ across datasets.
- The extreme capacity gap (32B→0.5B) raises concerns about distillation fidelity; the student’s reasoning capabilities are only assessed via aggregate metrics.

## Confidence
- GRPO Training Mechanism: Medium
- Multi-Reward Design: High
- On-Policy Distillation Efficacy: Medium
- Online Engagement Lifts: Medium-High
