# DDCL-INCRT: A Self-Organising Transformer with Hierarchical Prototype Structure (Theoretical Foundations)

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01880
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01880
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** A self-organising transformer architecture that grows attention heads and prototype clusters based on directional residual information, achieving minimal hierarchical structure with theoretical guarantees and reduced information loss compared to fixed MLP blocks.

## Executive Summary
Standard transformers require fixed architectural decisions (number of heads, layers, width) before training, often leading to systematic redundancy as many components prove unnecessary after training. DDCL-INCRT addresses this by replacing each MLP feedforward block with a prototype-based competitive learning layer (DDCL) that self-organises via an anti-collapse separation force, and by incrementally adding attention heads (INCRT) only when the residual directional content—measured by the largest eigenvalue of the antisymmetric attention matrix—exceeds a threshold. The two mechanisms mutually reinforce each other: each new head amplifies prototype separation, which raises the signal for further growth. Theoretically, the authors prove stability, convergence, minimality, and pruning safety under multi-timescale stochastic approximation. Empirically, synthetic experiments confirm spectral ordering of growth, coverage guarantees, temperature divergence, and monotonic separation force. On the SST-2 task, the model reaches 69.4% accuracy with six heads while λ_max strictly decreases at each growth event, indicating that the growth history objectively measures the task’s directional complexity.

## Method Summary
DDCL-INCRT is a self-organising transformer that autonomously determines its architecture during training. Each MLP feedforward block is replaced by a DDCL layer—a prototype-based competitive learning module. The loss decomposition L_q = L_OLS + V yields a separation term V whose gradient ∇_P V = 2P Σ_q automatically pushes prototypes apart when they collapse, eliminating the need for explicit regularisation. Concurrently, an INCRT gate monitors the antisymmetric attention matrix A and computes the residual A = P_⊥ X M_a X^T P_⊥; when its largest eigenvalue λ_max exceeds a threshold θ_w, a new attention head is added in the dominant residual direction u_+. The two subsystems interact: adding a head perturbs token embeddings along u_+, increasing the spread of embeddings relative to prototypes and thereby amplifying the separation force, which in turn boosts future residual signals. Training employs three learning rates (η_θ for encoder, η_P/η_T for prototypes/temperature, η_+ for gate) with η_T ≪ η_P ≪ η_+ to ensure stable co-evolution. Theoretical analysis guarantees that the resulting architecture is minimal and safe for pruning. Experimental validation on synthetic data demonstrates the predicted spectral ordering, coverage ≥1−ε_θw, temperature divergence (Spearman ≤−0.9), and monotonic separation force; SST-2 results further confirm adaptive growth.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantees: stability, convergence, minimality, and safe pruning.
- Self-organisation into a unique minimal hierarchical structure: heads added in decreasing order of residual eigenvalue, partitioning into local (hard assignments, low temperature) and global (soft assignments, high temperature) regimes, with finer-grained heads exhibiting larger separation force.
- Synthetic validation confirms spectral ordering, coverage ≥1−ε_θw, temperature divergence (Spearman ≤−0.9), and monotonic separation force; SST-2 training yields 69.4% accuracy with 6 heads and strictly decreasing λ_max at growth events.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The DDCL-INCRT architecture self-organises through two complementary, interacting dynamics that replace fixed architectural decisions with task-driven growth and organisation.

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The DDCL prototype layer automatically separates its prototypes without explicit regularisation, driven solely by the training objective.
- **Mechanism:** The loss L_q decomposes into a clustering fidelity term and a non-negative separation term V. The gradient of V with respect to prototypes is ∇_P V = 2P Σ_q, where Σ_q is the aggregated soft assignment covariance matrix. This gradient acts as a separation force: whenever two prototypes coincide, Σ_q becomes positive semi-definite, pushing them apart. Prototype collapse is a first-order unstable equilibrium.
- **Core assumption:** The prototype bank is non-degenerate initially (S(P) > 0) and the temperature is sufficiently high for soft assignments. Under these, the anti-collapse force is guaranteed.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The prototypes spread apart automatically, driven by the training objective, without explicit regularisation."
  - [section 3.1] Formal loss decomposition L_q = L_OLS + V and derivation of ∇_P V = 2P Σ_q.
  - [corpus] Weak direct relevance; nearest neighbors discuss general competitive learning or prototype methods but not this specific algebraic anti-collapse guarantee.
- **Break condition:** If all tokens become hard-assigned to a single prototype (tr(Σ_q)=0), the separation force vanishes. This is prevented by the temperature annealing dynamics and initial non-degeneracy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The INCRT mechanism increments the number of attention heads only when the residual directional content in the antisymmetric attention matrix exceeds a threshold, ensuring growth is triggered by measurable information insufficiency.
- **Mechanism:** After K heads, the residual directional content is measured by λ_max(A_res), the largest eigenvalue of the antisymmetric attention matrix projected onto the subspace orthogonal to directions already captured. When λ_max > θ_w, a new head is added in the direction v₁(A_res). Growth stops when λ_max ≤ θ_w.
- **Core assumption:** The attention signal's antisymmetric part has a non-zero eigenvalue spectrum that decays. The threshold θ_w is set below the smallest task-relevant eigenvalue.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Starts from one head and adds a new one only when the directional information uncaptured by existing heads exceeds a threshold."
  - [section 3.2] Definition of A_res and the λ_max-based growth criterion.
  - [corpus] Directly inherited from INCRT paper [3] (cited); neighbor papers discuss incremental or dynamic transformers but with heuristic, not spectral, growth criteria.
- **Break condition:** If θ_w is set too high, growth stops prematurely, leaving residual directional content uncaptured. If too low, overgrowth may occur before noise dominates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** DDCL and INCRT dynamics mutually reinforce each other: each INCRT growth step amplifies the DDCL separation force, and increased separation amplifies the residual signal that triggers subsequent growth.
- **Mechanism:** When INCRT adds a head along u* = v₁(A_res), the token embeddings are perturbed in that direction. This increases the spread of embeddings relative to prototypes, which increases tr(Σ_q) and ∇_P V. The amplification gain scales with λ_max(A_res) and inversely with temperature T. The amplified separation then helps subsequent heads capture more directional content, raising future λ_max values.
- **Core assumption:** The new direction u* is orthogonal to existing prototype subspaces (ensured by INCRT's P_⊥ projection) and prototypes have non-zero projection onto u* (A2').
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Each new head amplifies prototype separation, which in turn raises the signal triggering the next addition."
  - [section 4] Theorem 2 proves F_sep amplification: F_sep(P, Z^(K+1)) ≥ F_sep(P, Z^(K)) + ϕ'(0) + ½ϕ''(0), with ϕ''(0) ∝ λ_max(A_res)·Var[p*].
  - [corpus] No direct neighbor studies this specific synergy; related work examines attention-MLP interactions or hierarchical representation but not co-emergence of growth and competitive learning.
- **Break condition:** If u* is orthogonal to all prototype differences (⟨p_k - p_k', u*⟩ = 0 for all k,k'), then ϕ'(0)=0 and amplification fails. This is precluded by A2' (distinct prototype projections on u*).

## Foundational Learning
Prerequisites for understanding and implementing DDCL-INCRT:

**Concept: Competitive Learning Dynamics**
- **Why needed here:** The DDCL layer relies on soft nearest-neighbor assignments and the derived separation force. Understanding how q_nk evolves with prototypes and how Σ_q captures assignment diversity is essential to predict anti-collapse behaviour.
- **Quick check question:** Given a fixed set of token embeddings and prototypes, how does decreasing temperature T affect the Gini diversity H²(q_n) and the separation term V?

**Concept: Spectral Analysis of Antisymmetric Matrices**
- **Why needed here:** INCRT's growth criterion is spectral: λ_max(A_res) measures uncaptured directional content. One must understand why the antisymmetric part M_a encodes directed attention and how eigenvalues correspond to "directional variance" in token interactions.
- **Quick check question:** If A = P_⊥ X M_a X^T P_⊥, what does λ_max(A_res)=0 imply about the directional information remaining after existing heads?

**Concept: Multi-Timescale Stochastic Approximation**
- **Why needed here:** The system has three coupled learning rates (η_θ, η_P, η_T) and a discrete growth process. Stability requires η_T ≪ η_P ≪ η_+ and sufficient dwell times between growth events. Ignoring this can cause oscillations or divergence.
- **Quick check question:** What happens if η_P is too large relative to η_+? (Hint: the gate direction u_+ may not converge to v₁(A_res) before prototypes over-update.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map, critical path, and practical guidance for a new engineer.

**Component map:**
Encoder f_θ → DDCL prototype bank per head → INCRT gate per head → growth/pruning events (discrete additions/removals of heads).

**Critical path:**
1. Forward: Compute z_n = f_θ(x_n). For each head h, compute soft assignments q_nk^(h) via (1) and centroids μ_n^(h).
2. DDCL update: ∇_P L_q spreads prototypes (increases separation), ∇_T L_q lowers temperatures (sharpen assignments). Encoder update ∇_θ L_q adapts embeddings.
3. INCRT recompute: With updated z_n and p_k^(h), recompute A_res. If λ_max > θ_w, add head: initialise prototypes along u_+ = v₁(A_res) (orthogonal to existing subspaces), set T = T_initial.
4. Pruning check: If S(P^(h)) < φ_g (prototype bank collapsed), remove head h.
5. Repeat until λ_max ≤ θ_w and no pruning triggered.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **θ_w vs. capacity:** Lower θ_w → more heads, finer-grained hierarchy, higher risk of overfitting on small data. Higher θ_w → fewer heads, risk of underfitting.
- **Temperature schedule:** η_T controls speed of local/global specialisation. Too fast → all heads become local (T→T_min) prematurely, losing global representational capacity. Too slow → insufficient specialisation.
- **Prototype count K per head:** Larger K increases separation force capacity but risks redundancy within a head. Must satisfy K ≤ embedding dim d for orthogonality (B2).
- **Step-size ratios:** Must enforce η_T ≪ η_P ≪ η_+ (Corollary 9). Violation risks gate misalignment, destabilising growth.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Prototype collapse:** S(P^(h)) → 0 for any head h. Monitor S(P) during training; if it drops near zero, reduce η_P or increase λ_reg (barrier strength).
- **Non-stop growth:** λ_max never falls below θ_w. Plot λ_max over growth events; if monotone decrease stalls, θ_w may be too low or data too complex. Could indicate heavy tail of attention signal—consider raising θ_w.
- **No temperature specialisation:** All T^(h) remain near T_initial. Compute σ^(h)(0) (initial assignment diversity). If all σ^(h)(0) are similar, specialisation may be weak; ensure (B3) holds (distinct initial gate activities).
- **Violation of spectral ordering:** λ_max at later growth events exceeds earlier ones. Means INCRT gate did not fully project out previous subspaces (P_⊥ not orthogonal). Verify orthogonality of u_+ vectors (dot product should be ~0).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Spectral ordering verification:** On synthetic data with prescribed eigenvalue decay λ_k = λ₁ ρ^{k-1}, verify that heads are added with λ^(h) matching the true eigenvalues and strictly decreasing. Metric: λ^(h) vs. h plot; Spearman rank correlation ≤ -0.9.
2. **Temperature divergence test:** Verify that heads with larger λ^(h) reach T_min faster. Metric: Plot T^(h)(t) for all heads; rank correlation between λ^(h) and convergence time should be ≤ -0.9.
3. **Separation force monotonicity:** At convergence, measure fractional separation force F^(h) = F_sep^(h)/F_total_sep. Verify F^(1) > F^(2) > ... > F^(K*). Metric: Bar chart of F^(h) vs. h; all ratios F^(h)/F^(h+1) > 1, with lower bound λ^(h)/λ^(h+1) · (T_∞^{(h+1)}/T_∞^{(h)})².

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes ideal conditions (non-degenerate prototypes, exact step-size hierarchy, continuous eigenvalues); real training may violate these, causing growth errors or collapse.
- INCRT’s λ_max threshold is sensitive to batch noise; inaccurate eigenvalue estimates could trigger unnecessary or missed head additions.
- Empirical evidence is restricted to synthetic data and a single SST-2 experiment; generalisation to larger models, other domains, and more complex tasks is unproven.
