# Graph Signal Processing Meets Mamba2: Adaptive Filter Bank via Delta Modulation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22333
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22333
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HADES reinterprets Mamba2's multi-head recurrence as an adaptive graph filter bank, achieving comparable or superior performance with 58.9% of Mamba2's parameters.

## Executive Summary
HADES introduces a hierarchical architecture that structures Mamba2's multi-head recurrence into shared global low-pass filters and expert local high-pass filters. Expert selection is driven by a spectral residual signal and delta modulation, improving interpretability and parameter efficiency. Trained on 200B tokens from the Pile, HADES reduces parameters to 58.9% of Mamba2 while matching or exceeding its performance on WikiText, LAMBADA, zero-shot commonsense reasoning, and long-context retrieval.

## Method Summary
HADES modifies the Mamba2 block by fixing total filters H=16, with S=8 shared filters always active and an expert pool of size E (unspecified). For each token, a router computes scores from a linear projection of concatenated Δ_base and residual r_t = u_t - cummean(u_t), selects the top-Q experts (Q not specified), and combines their outputs with shared filters. Experts modulate their step size as Δ = Softplus(Δ_base + γ·f_b(...)), γ=0.25, while shared filters use Δ_base only. Training uses the Pile dataset (200B tokens), AdamW with peak LR 48e-4 (ambiguous), weight decay 0.1, cosine schedule, warmup 375M tokens, batch size 222, and auxiliary losses L_balance (variance of selection probabilities) and L_diversity (mean squared output correlation deviation) with λ=1e-3 each.

## Key Results
- Parameter efficiency: HADES uses 58.9% of Mamba2's parameters.
- Language modeling: WikiText-103 perplexity 31.48 (vs Mamba2 31.34); LAMBADA perplexity 21.74 (vs 24.38).
- Zero-shot commonsense: average accuracy 42.91 vs 41.63 across 8 tasks.
- Long-context retrieval: superior passkey retrieval accuracy at large depths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HADES achieves efficiency and performance by separating filters into global low-pass (shared) and local high-pass (experts). The residual r_t, computed as the difference between the input and its causal cumulative mean, serves as a proxy for high-frequency content and biases the expert delta values upward, shifting their frequency response toward high-pass. The router dynamically allocates experts based on scores derived from Δ_base and r_t, enabling sparse, content-aware specialization. Auxiliary losses (balance and diversity) encourage uniform expert usage and decorrelated outputs, preventing collapse and fostering a diverse filter bank.

- **Delta Modulation (Δ)**: Controls time-varying dynamics of the SSM, determining its frequency selectivity. *Why needed*: Adjusting Δ shifts the filter's magnitude response, enabling adaptive frequency capture. *Quick check*: For a fixed SSM, increase Δ and observe the magnitude response moves to higher frequencies.
- **Spectral Residual (r_t)**: The residual between input and its causal cumulative mean. *Why needed*: Acts as an indicator of local high-frequency content to bias expert delta values. *Quick check*: Compute r_t for a synthetic signal with varying local variance and verify correlation with variance magnitude.
- **Router with Top-Q Selection**: Dynamically selects a subset of experts per token based on computed scores. *Why needed*: Enables sparse expert usage and parameter efficiency. *Quick check*: Implement router and track selection entropy; it should be >0 and increase with temperature.
- **Balance and Diversity Losses**: Regularization terms to promote uniform expert usage and decorrelated filter outputs. *Why needed*: Prevents router collapse and ensures experts specialize. *Quick check*: During training, monitor expert usage histogram (should be roughly uniform) and output correlation matrix (off-diagonals near zero).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input u_t -> compute r_t = u_t - cummean(u_t) and Δ_base -> router concatenates [Δ_base || r_t], projects to scores, selects top-Q experts -> shared SSM with Δ_base -> for each selected expert, compute Δ = Softplus(Δ_base + γ·f_b(...)) and apply SSM -> weighted sum of shared and selected expert outputs -> output.

**Critical Path**: (1) Causal cumulative mean computation, (2) router projection and top-Q selection, (3) SSM recurrences for shared and selected experts. Delta modulation can be computed in parallel with selection. The order must respect data dependencies: r_t and Δ_base needed before router; selection needed before expert SSMs; all SSMs before aggregation.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Increasing shared filter count S improves global smoothing but reduces parameter budget for experts.
- Larger expert pool E increases capacity but may dilute selection and increase router overhead.
- Higher selection count Q improves expressivity but raises computational cost per token.
- Auxiliary loss weights λ_balance and λ_diversity require tuning; too high may dominate task loss, too low may not prevent collapse.
- Gamma γ in expert delta modulation controls bias strength; higher values enhance high-pass bias but risk numerical instability.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Router collapse: one expert dominates selection histogram; low entropy; fix by increasing λ_balance or router temperature.
- Delta instability: Δ values explode or become NaN; clip Δ, reduce γ, or adjust learning rate.
- Auxiliary loss dominance: total loss spikes or CE loss plateaus; reduce λ values or scale them separately.
- Weak long-context performance: passkey retrieval accuracy degrades sharply with depth; check causality of router and cummean computations.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. **Baseline verification**: Replicate official Mamba2 370M configuration and confirm reported WikiText/LAMBADA results to establish a reference.
2. **Router distribution profiling**: Early in training, log per-expert selection counts and entropy per batch. If entropy is too low (<1.0 nats), increase λ_balance or add temperature to router scores.
3. **Delta modulation sanity**: Monitor mean and variance of Δ_base and expert Δ values across batches. Ensure Δ stays positive (Softplus) and within stable ranges (e.g., not exceeding 16). Clip if necessary and adjust γ.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: What theoretical mechanism links the spectral residual (r_t) to frequency-specific modulation in the delta bias? *Basis*: The paper uses r_t to modulate Δ via a learned projection and shows increased Δ shifts filter response higher, but lacks theoretical justification. *Why unresolved*: It is unclear whether residual magnitude reliably proxies high-frequency components across data distributions. *Resolution evidence*: A theoretical derivation or controlled experiments correlating r_t with Fourier spectrum and showing systematic filter response changes.

**Open Question 2**: Does the router explicitly select filters based on their spectral specialization? *Basis*: Router uses scores from fe([Δ_base, r_t]), not direct frequency metrics; filter selection patterns correlate with semantics but not proof of frequency-based routing. *Why unresolved*: Inputs are not frequency-domain; frequency specialization may emerge independently. *Resolution evidence*: Ablate with band-limited signals and verify router activates different filter subsets per band, or show correlation between filter scores and measured input frequency metrics.

**Open Question 3**: What are the interactions between balance and diversity losses in preventing filter collapse, and could one substitute for the other? *Basis*: Both losses are used; removing either degrades performance, but synergy is unexplained. *Why unresolved*: Non-linear effects; unclear if one could induce the other's effect over time. *Resolution evidence*: Track filter usage and output similarity throughout training under different loss combinations, including staged removal.

**Open Question 4**: Can HADES be generalized beyond line graphs to arbitrary graph structures? *Basis*: Paper positions HADES in GSP but only experiments on sequences; reliance on temporal ordering and unidirectional recurrence may not transfer. *Why unresolved*: Unknown if same routing and bias mechanisms benefit non-sequential graphs. *Resolution evidence*: Extend HADES to other graph types (e.g., social networks, molecular graphs) and compare against standard GNNs/graph SSMs.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter ambiguity**: Expert pool size (E), selected experts per token (Q), and total filters (H) are not explicitly stated, affecting exact parameter count and model capacity.
- **Training detail vagueness**: Learning rate value (48e-4 could be 0.0048 or 0.00048) and batch size (222 tokens or sequences?) are ambiguous, potentially impacting training stability and resource planning.
- **Auxiliary loss specification**: The exact implementation of diversity loss (batch vs token scope, normalization) and balance loss tuning is underspecified, risking suboptimal router behavior if not carefully replicated.
