# Night Eyes: A Reproducible Framework for Constellation-Based Corneal Reflection Matching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv**: 2604.01909
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01909
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Identity-preserving accuracy 0.74, precision 0.81, median localization error 1.41 pixels.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical gap in pupil-corneal reflection (P-CR) eye tracking: the lack of reproducible, hardware-agnostic methods for matching multiple corneal reflections (glints) to their corresponding light sources. Existing approaches often rely on heuristic, embedded rules that are tied to specific hardware. Night Eyes introduces a modular, 2D geometry-driven pipeline that treats glints as structured constellations. Its core innovation is a Similarity-Layout Alignment (SLA) procedure that robustly recovers glint identities by matching the detected pattern to a known LED template, even with missing or extra detections. By explicitly separating detection from geometric correspondence, the framework generalizes across datasets. Evaluated on a public multi-LED dataset with a frozen configuration, it achieves 0.74 identity-preserving accuracy and 0.81 precision, with a median localization error of 1.41 pixels. The authors release full code, presets, and evaluation scripts to enable transparent replication.

## Method Summary
Night Eyes processes a single-channel eye image through a sequence of stages. First, image enhancement (top-hat, DoG, CLAHE) amplifies glints. Candidate detection intentionally over-generates bright, blob-like structures using low percentile thresholds; if too few candidates are found, an adaptive fallback relaxes thresholds and uses larger kernels, then merges results. Detections are scored by appearance (brightness, compactness) and geometric support (voting). The top N candidates are retained after spatial gating (border exclusion, optional pupil-annulus). A pre-defined LED constellation template serves as the reference. The SLA matcher forms candidate triplets, checks distance ratios against template triplets, and initializes a 2D similarity transform hypothesis. This hypothesis is grown by greedily assigning nearby glints to template points while refitting the transform. The best hypothesis is chosen by inlier count, residual, and optional semantic/mirror constraints. Output consists of assigned glint IDs and locations. The modular design allows new hardware to be supported simply by providing a new template.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Controlled over-detection with adaptive fallback ensures sufficient candidate glints for matching even when some true glints are missing or faint.
- **Mechanism**: The pipeline intentionally over-detects plausible blobs using a low percentile threshold, scores them by appearance (brightness, compactness) and geometric support (voting among top candidates). If fewer than the target number of candidates are found, it relaxes the threshold and runs additional passes with larger kernels, merging clusters. This generates a superset of plausible glints that likely covers all true reflections.
- **Core assumption**: True glints are relatively bright and blob-like; spurious detections can be filtered later by geometric matching. Also, missing glints can be compensated by revisiting the image with more aggressive detection.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "brings together controlled over-detection, adaptive candidate fallback, appearance-aware scoring"
  - [Section 3]: "Candidate Extraction and Scoring... This stage intentionally over-detects plausible blobs... Adaptive Fallback and Spatial Gating... If fewer than the target number of candidates are detected, additional passes are run with relaxed percentile thresholds and larger enhancement kernels."
  - [corpus]: Weak; corpus neighbors focus on deep learning glint detection, but do not directly address over-detection and fallback. (No direct contradictory evidence.)
- **Break condition**: When true glints are extremely dim or heavily occluded such that they never rise above the threshold even with relaxed parameters, or when over-detection floods the pool with many spatially ambiguous blobs that confuse geometric matching.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Constellation-based matching using Similarity-Layout Alignment (SLA) robustly assigns identities by leveraging the fixed geometric layout of the LED array, tolerating missing or extra detections.
- **Mechanism**: SLA uses the known LED constellation as a template. It generates candidate triplets from detected glints and checks if their distance ratios match template triplets within tolerance. Valid triplets initialise a similarity transform hypothesis, grown by greedily assigning nearby glints to template points while refitting. The best hypothesis is selected by inlier count, residual, and optional semantic/mirror constraints.
- **Core assumption**: The projection of the LED constellation onto the cornea can be approximated by a 2D similarity transformation (uniform scale, rotation, translation) for typical viewing angles. Relative distance ratios among glints are sufficiently distinctive.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we treat glints as structured constellations rather than independent blobs... propose a Similarity-Layout Alignment (SLA) procedure which adapts constellation matching to the specific constraints of multi-LED eye tracking."
  - [Section 2]: "inspired by lost-in-space star identification... recover the identity of stars by matching geometric constellations, even when some stars are missing or false detections appear."
  - [Section 3]: "Constellation Matching via SLA... Starting from high-scoring candidates, SLA forms candidate triplets and checks whether their distance ratios are consistent with the template geometry... The final match is selected based on inlier support and geometric consistency."
- **Break condition**: If geometric distortion deviates significantly from similarity (e.g., extreme off-axis viewing causing perspective warping), or the LED layout is highly symmetric (e.g., equally spaced circle) causing ambiguous constellations, SLA may produce incorrect matches. Also, when fewer than three candidates are detected, SLA cannot run.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Explicit separation of detection and correspondence stages makes the pipeline modular, interpretable, and hardware-agnostic, enabling reproducible evaluation across different devices.
- **Mechanism**: The pipeline separates candidate generation/detection (which may over-generate) from geometric correspondence (which consumes a template). This decoupling means matching logic does not depend on specific detection thresholds; it operates on a candidate pool. Changing hardware only requires a new template and possibly tuning detection parameters, while the SLA matcher remains unchanged.
- **Core assumption**: The two stages can be optimised independently; the matching stage is robust to detection errors as long as the candidate pool contains the correct glints.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "keeping detection and correspondence explicitly separated."
  - [Section 1]: "We design Night Eyes as a modular pipeline in which detection, scoring, and geometric correspondence are explicitly exposed as separable components."
  - [Section 5]: "For the OpenEDS experiments, this prior was disabled, yet the rest of the pipeline still performed consistently, suggesting that the detection–alignment design transfers across datasets even without dataset-specific layout assumptions."
- **Break condition**: If detection consistently fails to include true glints in the candidate pool (e.g., due to extreme noise or unusual glint appearance), separation cannot recover. Also, if hardware induces non-similarity distortions that violate SLA assumptions, separation alone does not automatically adapt.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Pupil-Corneal Reflection (P-CR) eye tracking  
  - *Why needed here*: The paper targets multi-glint matching within the P-CR gaze estimation framework. Glints correspond to known light sources; identity-preserving matching is needed to disambiguate pupil center and handle head movement.  
  - *Quick check question*: In P-CR eye tracking, how do the pupil center and corneal reflections combine to estimate gaze direction, and what problem arises with multiple LEDs?

- **Concept**: Constellation identification (lost-in-space star tracking)  
  - *Why needed here*: Night Eyes adapts star identification techniques for glint matching. The core idea—matching a pattern of points to a known catalogue under noise and missing data—is central to SLA.  
  - *Quick check question*: How do lost-in-space star identification algorithms recognise a star pattern when only a subset of stars is visible and some false detections are present? What geometric invariants (e.g., distance ratios) are typically used?

- **Concept**: 2D similarity transformation  
  - *Why needed here*: SLA assumes the mapping from LED template to image glints follows a 2D similarity transform. Understanding this model explains why distance ratios are preserved and how matching is performed.  
  - *Quick check question*: What is a 2D similarity transformation and which geometric quantities (distances, angles, ratios) are invariant under it? Why is it a reasonable model for a fixed LED array projecting onto the cornea?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
1. **Input**: Raw eye image, optional pupil center, parameter preset, template file (LED layout coordinates).
2. **Preprocessing**: Grayscale conversion, optional denoising/contrast adjustment, pupil-centered ROI extraction if available.
3. **Enhancement**: Apply white top-hat, Difference-of-Gaussians, or high-pass filter to amplify small bright glints; optional CLAHE.
4. **Candidate Detection**: Percentile thresholding on enhanced image, morphological cleanup, connected component extraction.
5. **Candidate Scoring**: Heuristic score combining intensity, compactness, and optionally geometric support (voting among top candidates).
6. **Adaptive Fallback**: If candidate count < target, iterate with relaxed percentile thresholds and larger enhancement kernels; merge and deduplicate clusters.
7. **Pooling & Gating**: Keep top N candidates by score; optionally filter by image border and pupil-centered annulus.
8. **Template Matching (SLA)**: Given a template (or bank), run SLA to find best similarity transform and assign identities to candidates. Includes semantic/mirror constraints optionally.
9. **Output**: Predicted glint IDs and locations; optional metrics if ground truth provided.
10. **Utilities**: Template creation UI, annotation review UI, evaluation scripts.

### Critical path (per image)
Load image → grayscale → (ROI) crop → enhancement → detection_one_pass → while (candidates < target AND fallback passes remain): run another detection pass with relaxed parameters → merge all candidates → pool top N → apply gates → run SLA matcher → if match found: output assigned glints; else return none.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Over-detection vs. speed**: Larger candidate pools increase SLA computation (triplet generation). Pooling top N restricts runtime but risks discarding true glints.
- **Template reliance vs. flexibility**: A single template works for a fixed LED layout; changing hardware requires a new template but does not alter SLA logic.
- **2D similarity vs. distortion**: Similarity assumption simplifies matching but may fail under strong perspective warping (extreme gaze angles).
- **CPU vs. GPU**: Pipeline is CPU-oriented; SLA is non-learned and portable but may be slower than deep learning alternatives.

### Failure signatures
- **No candidates**: Check enhancement parameters, ROI validity, image brightness.
- **SLA returns no match**: Candidate pool too small (<3) or geometry inconsistent; inspect candidate distribution.
- **Rotated/mirrored matches**: Ensure semantic/mirror constraints are enabled; template may be symmetric.
- **High localization error**: Template accuracy, ROI alignment, or similarity model violation; examine residuals from SLA.
- **Subject-specific variability**: May indicate need for per-subject detection tuning or unusual illumination.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Main replication**: Using the frozen configuration (presets in repo), run on Chugh et al.’s dataset. Verify reported metrics: identity-preserving accuracy ~0.74, precision ~0.81, median error ~1.41 px via provided evaluation script.
2. **Template stability**: Build templates from several different reference images of the same LED layout (e.g., different subjects). Run pipeline; results should vary minimally, confirming template insensitivity.
3. **Cross-dataset transfer**: Apply the same frozen config to OpenEDS2019 and OpenEDS2020 (disable layout prior for OpenEDS2020). Expect consistent detection/matching behavior despite different cameras and illumination; note any systematic failures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Under what specific low-contrast or adverse illumination conditions does the 2D geometry-driven, non-learned Night Eyes pipeline fail to match the performance of deep learning-based glint correspondence methods, and what are the precise failure modes?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors explicitly state a limitation: "it may limit performance in very low-contrast conditions where deep learning models may excel better in."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper compares Night Eyes to other methods only indirectly via literature and does not provide a head-to-head empirical evaluation under systematically varied low-contrast conditions. The claimed interpretability and portability advantage is presented without quantifying the performance gap in these specific scenarios.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A controlled experimental study that varies image contrast, signal-to-noise ratio, or illumination uniformity on a common dataset, measuring the identity-preserving accuracy and precision of Night Eyes against representative deep learning models (e.g., Chugh et al., Byrne et al.). Detailed failure mode analysis (e.g., missed detections vs. incorrect matches) under each condition would pinpoint the trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: For what ranges of optical distortion (e.g., caused by extreme off-axis camera positioning or significant corneal curvature effects on reflection location) does the planar similarity transformation model in SLA break down, and can a learned or higher-order geometric model (e.g., a homography or thin-plate spline) robustly handle such distortions while maintaining the framework's reproducibility?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors identify a key assumption: "assumes a fixed template structure and similarity-based alignment, which may be less suitable when reflections become highly distorted (e.g., in eye tracking setups with highly off-axis cameras, the constellation may warp considerably when it hits the sclera)."
- **Why unresolved**: The framework's evaluation uses datasets with presumably modest off-axis angles. The paper does not test or characterize the failure threshold for the similarity model under increasing geometric distortion, nor does it explore modifications to the alignment model itself.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Generate or collect a dataset with known, graded levels of off-axis camera angle or corneal curvature. Evaluate Night Eyes' SLA performance (inlier count, residual error, identity accuracy) against variants using different transformation models (similarity, affine, homography). A clear performance cliff would indicate the model's operational boundary.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What specific geometric properties of an LED constellation (e.g., inter-glint angle distributions, ratio distributions, overall spread relative to expected pupil-glint distance) maximize the robustness and accuracy of the SLA correspondence algorithm when some glints are missing or spurious detections are present?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The results note that "the geometric layout of the glints matter more than how many are detected" and that performance varies with glint visibility. The SLA algorithm relies on distance ratios and triplet consistency from a fixed template, but the paper does not analyze which template geometries are inherently more discriminative or stable under missing data.
- **Why unresolved**: The study evaluates a few fixed templates from existing datasets. There is no systematic ablation or synthetic study varying the constellation geometry to derive principles for optimal layout design for this matching paradigm.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A large-scale simulation or controlled experiment using synthetic templates with systematically varied geometric properties (e.g., equilateral vs. skewed triangles, presence of near-collinear points). For each template, run the Night Eyes pipeline with simulated missing/spurious glints to measure correspondence accuracy and derive statistical relationships between geometric features and robustness metrics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can the adaptive fallback and candidate pooling mechanisms be formally optimized (e.g., via Bayesian decision theory or reinforcement learning) to minimize computational cost while maintaining a guaranteed lower bound on correspondence success probability, especially for real-time applications?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper describes a heuristic adaptive fallback with fixed percentile thresholds and pass limits. It notes runtime as a practical concern (~2.5 FPS on CPU) but does not model the cost-benefit trade-off of each fallback pass or candidate threshold relaxation. The mechanism is rule-based, not optimized for efficiency under uncertainty.
- **Why unresolved**: The fallback parameters were tuned via hyperparameter sweep for accuracy, not jointly for speed and accuracy. There is no analytical or learned model that predicts the marginal benefit of an additional detection pass or candidate threshold relaxation in terms of improved matching probability versus added computational load.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Frame the fallback decision as a sequential decision problem. Collect data on how candidate count and quality change with each fallback pass across many frames. Fit a model (e.g., success probability as a function of candidate set statistics). Then design and evaluate a policy that stops fallback when the predicted marginal gain is below a cost threshold, comparing its speed-accuracy trade-off to the fixed heuristic.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: To what extent does the semantic layout prior (e.g., "mirror constraint") in the SLA matcher generalize beyond the specific 5-LED Pupil Labs configuration it was tuned for, and can a more abstract, learnable prior (e.g., a probabilistic model of valid LED layouts) be derived from multiple datasets to maintain performance without dataset-specific tuning?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors disabled the SLA semantic prior for OpenEDS datasets, noting it was "tuned to the dataset of Chugh et al. [2021]" (which uses a specific LED layout). They state that the rest of the pipeline transferred well, implying the prior is dataset-specific.
- **Why unresolved**: The need to disable a key matcher constraint for other datasets reveals a lack of generalization in a core component. The paper does not investigate what makes the prior specific to one layout or propose a method to automatically acquire or adapt such a prior for new, unseen hardware configurations.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Collect multiple public (or synthetic) datasets from different eye trackers with varying LED counts and arrangements. Analyze the consistency of the "implausible configurations" the prior prevents (e.g., mirrored matches). Develop a generalized prior by learning a distribution over valid constellation geometries from pooled data or by defining layout-agnostic geometric invariants. Evaluate if this generalized prior allows the SLA matcher to be used without modification across all datasets.

## Limitations
- Exact dependency versions and full hyperparameter sets are only in the repository, not fully documented in the paper, risking replication errors.
- SLA's 2D similarity assumption may break under extreme off-axis viewing; failure envelope uncharacterized.
- Over-detection may produce large candidate pools, increasing computation; candidate pool size is a practical limiting factor.
