# GR-SAP: Generative Replay for Safety Alignment Preservation during Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10243
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10243
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: GR-SAP reduces harmful response ratios (e.g., Llama3: 6.28% → 0.58%) while preserving downstream task accuracy, matching the efficacy of original alignment data.

## Executive Summary
Fine-tuning large language models on downstream tasks frequently degrades their safety alignment because the original alignment data is typically inaccessible. GR-SAP solves this by using generative replay to synthesize high-quality, domain-specific safety alignment data directly from the model itself. The method involves extracting candidate safety queries via tailored prompting, rigorously filtering and revising them, and then mixing a small proportion (r=0.1) of this synthetic dataset with the downstream task data during supervised fine-tuning. Both theoretical analysis and extensive experiments across multiple models (Llama3, OLMo2, Qwen2.5) and tasks (GSM8K, MATH) show that GR-SAP effectively preserves safety without sacrificing utility, outperforming open-source safety datasets and approaching the performance of fine-tuning with the original alignment data.

## Method Summary
GR-SAP is a three-stage pipeline. First, it **extracts** synthetic safety data by prompting the model with domain-specific safety subdomains to generate queries and corresponding responses, aiming to elicit the model's memorized refusal behaviors. Second, it **refines** this raw data through multi-stage query filtering (perplexity thresholding, semantic deduplication, domain relevance) and response revision, where unsafe responses are rewritten into safe refusals using a guardrail model. Finally, it **mixes** a balanced subset of the refined synthetic examples (r=0.1) with the downstream task data for supervised fine-tuning. The mixing ratio is theoretically justified to bound the safety gap, and empirically shown to be robust across models and tasks.

## Key Results
- **Safety Preservation**: Dramatically lowers Harmful Score (HS) on benchmarks like WildJailbreak; e.g., Llama3-8B-Instruct HS drops from 6.28% (vanilla SFT) to 0.58% (GR-SAP).
- **Utility Maintenance**: Downstream task accuracy (e.g., on GSM8K, MATH) remains statistically indistinguishable from models fine-tuned without safety mixing.
- **Proxy Efficacy**: Synthetic alignment data achieves high semantic similarity to original alignment data (MAUVE: queries 0.455, responses 0.646) and outperforms mixing with open-source datasets like AEGIS and Beavertails.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Synthetic safety data can be extracted from the model itself via tailored prompting, mimicking the original alignment distribution.
- **Mechanism**: A prompt template asks the model to repeat a refused request and explain why, triggering memorized alignment behaviors. The generated queries and model responses form query-response pairs.
- **Core assumption**: The model's post-alignment parameters retain sufficient signal of safety boundaries; appropriate prompts can elicit this.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "synthesizes domain-specific safety alignment data from the model itself using generative replay, with tailored prompting"
  - [section] "Section 3.1: Extraction Setup" describes the prompt template and generation process.
  - [corpus] Corpus: Neighboring safety preservation papers (average FMR 0.449) emphasize different strategies; GR-SAP uniquely leverages generative replay for proxy data creation.
- **Break condition**: If the model has overfitted to downstream data or suffers catastrophic forgetting, extracted data may not reflect original alignment.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Multi-stage query filtering and response revision refine synthetic data, ensuring high quality and including challenging boundary cases to strengthen safety.
- **Mechanism**: Queries undergo perplexity thresholding (extreme percentiles removed), semantic deduplication (cosine similarity >0.85), and domain relevance filtering (similarity to keywords >0.5). Responses are evaluated by a guardrail; unsafe ones are revised to safe refusals via a simple prompt.
- **Core assumption**: Raw synthetic data contains noise and boundary failures; filtering improves representativeness, and revising failures provides additional safety signal beyond "easy" cases.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "query filtering (perplexity, deduplication, relevance), and response revision"
  - [section] "Section 3.2: Data Post-Processing" details each step; Table 3 shows revision reduces Harmful Score drastically compared to exclusion.
  - [corpus] Corpus: No direct corpus evidence; this module is based on standard data filtering principles (e.g., Wang et al., 2023).
- **Break condition**: Overly aggressive filtering may discard valid safety examples; guardrail misclassification could introduce incorrect refusals.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Mixing a small proportion (r=0.1) of refined synthetic data with downstream data during fine-tuning bounds the safety alignment gap, preserving safety while maintaining task performance.
- **Mechanism**: During SFT, sample rN synthetic examples and (1-r)N downstream examples. The regularization coefficient λ = r/(1-r) balances objectives. Theorem 2 bounds the safety gap G_s in terms of λ and distribution mismatches (D_KL, TV).
- **Core assumption**: The synthetic dataset is a reliable proxy for original alignment (small D_KL(C_s || Ĉ) per Theorem 1).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "integrate them during downstream adaption" and "theoretical and empirical analyses demonstrate this synthetic data serves as a reliable proxy"
  - [section] "Section 3.3: Safety-Augmented Fine-tuning" and Theorem 2; Figure 2 shows consistent HS reduction; Table 1 compares with original data.
  - [corpus] Corpus: Neighboring works like "Safety-tuned llamas" (Bianchi et al.) explore mixing but require original alignment data; GR-SAP's proxy approach is novel and shows high semantic similarity (MAUVE 0.455/0.646).
- **Break condition**: Excessive r causes distributional drift (Figure 3 U-shape); if proxy quality is poor, theoretical bounds may not hold empirically.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Generative Replay**
  - **Why needed here**: Core technique to synthesize alignment data without original dataset, preventing catastrophic forgetting of safety during fine-tuning.
  - **Quick check**: How does generative replay differ from experience replay in reinforcement learning? (Answer: It generates pseudo-samples from a learned generative model rather than storing and replaying real experiences.)

- **Concept: Kullback-Leibler Divergence**
  - **Why needed here**: Quantifies distribution mismatch between original alignment and synthetic proxy; appears in Theorem 1 decomposition and Theorem 2 bounds.
  - **Quick check**: If D_KL(P||Q) = 0, what relationship does that imply? (Answer: P and Q are identical almost everywhere.)

- **Concept: Safety Alignment**
  - **Why needed here**: The target property to preserve; understanding alignment helps evaluate what constitutes harmful output and why fine-tuning can degrade it.
  - **Quick check**: Name two common methods for achieving safety alignment in LLMs. (Answer: Supervised Fine-Tuning on safety data; Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - QueryGenerator -> QueryFilter -> ResponseGenerator -> ResponseReviser -> DataMixer -> FineTuner -> Evaluator
  - QueryGenerator uses 38 safety subdomains; QueryFilter uses Qwen2.5-0.5B and BGE embeddings; ResponseReviser uses WildGuard.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize base instruction-tuned model.
  2. For each safety subdomain, generate queries via QueryGenerator.
  3. Filter queries with QueryFilter (perplexity, deduplication, relevance).
  4. Generate responses via ResponseGenerator.
  5. Run ResponseReviser: evaluate each response; if unsafe, replace with revised refusal.
  6. Aggregate all processed pairs into synthetic_dataset.
  7. DataMixer samples r*N from synthetic_dataset and (1-r)*N from downstream dataset.
  8. FineTuner performs SFT on the mixed dataset.
  9. Evaluator tests fine-tuned model on safety benchmarks and downstream tasks.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Mixing ratio r**: Small r (0.1) suffices; larger r introduces distributional drift (U-shaped HS in Fig 3). Tradeoff: more safety data vs. risk of over-regularization.
  - **Response handling**: Revision vs exclusion. Revision improves safety significantly (Table 3) but depends on guardrail accuracy; exclusion simpler but misses boundary cases.
  - **Prompt template**: Avoids system prompts to prevent sensitivity; uses chat template with bolded "specific request". Tradeoff: May yield lower extraction quality than system prompt methods but is more robust across models.
  - **Filtering thresholds**: Perplexity percentiles (5th, 95th), similarity thresholds (0.85 for dedup, 0.5 for relevance). These are heuristics; need tuning for different models.
  - **Guardrail choice**: WildGuard used; could substitute others. Tradeoff: coverage vs. computational overhead.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Safety degradation**: Harmful Score after fine-tuning exceeds unmixed baseline by >50%.
  - **Utility loss**: Downstream accuracy drop >2% relative to unmixed fine-tuning.
  - **Data quality issues**: High duplicate rate (>20%) in synthetic set; >10% of responses still unsafe after revision.
  - **Theoretical mismatch**: Large MAUVE score (<0.3) between synthetic and original alignment (if available) indicates poor proxy.
  - **Training instability**: Loss spikes or NaN during mixing, often when r is too high or synthetic data quality is low.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Data sanity check**: Extract 100 synthetic examples; manually verify queries are safety-relevant and responses are refusals or safe. Adjust prompts if many non-safety queries appear.
  2. **Proxy validation**: If original alignment data is available (e.g., OLMo2), compute MAUVE score; target >0.4 for both queries and responses. If not, compare to known safety datasets.
  3. **Pilot fine-tuning**: Run SFT on a small downstream task (e.g., 500 GSM8K samples) with r=0.1; measure HS on WildJailbreak and accuracy. Should see HS drop vs. r=0 baseline with <1% accuracy change.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can GR-SAP preserve alignments beyond safety (e.g., truthfulness, fairness)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Conclusion states: "Owing to its generality, beyond safety, GR-SAP may be extended in future work to preserve other forms of alignment acquired during training."
- **Why unresolved**: Experiments exclusively target safety alignment; no evidence for other alignment dimensions.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Apply GR-SAP to datasets targeting other alignments (e.g., TruthfulQA for truthfulness, BBQ for bias) and compare preservation against vanilla fine-tuning.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What mechanism explains the U-shaped relationship between mixing ratio r and safety (Figure 3)?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Authors hypothesize "distributional drift" at high r but provide no empirical analysis of the phenomenon.
- **Why unresolved**: The underlying cause (e.g., overfitting to synthetic data, dilution of task-specific safety signals) remains speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analyze model representations (e.g., probing, clustering) at different r; measure diversity/novelty of synthetic vs downstream data; test alternative mixing schedules.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How sensitive is GR-SAP to the choice of guardrail model used for response revision?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method relies on WildGuard exclusively; no ablation on guardrail selection is reported.
- **Why unresolved**: Guardrail models can be biased or inaccurate; sensitivity could affect synthetic data quality and downstream safety.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Repeat experiments with multiple guardrails (e.g., LlamaGuard, OpenAI Moderation); measure inter-guardrail agreement and resulting safety performance.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic data scaling**: Unknown handling when filtered "difficult" examples are scarce (< r*N). Paper states balancing "provided sufficient" but no fallback strategy is specified.
- **Baseline replication details**: Implementation of open-source safety datasets (AEGIS, Beavertails) as mixing sources is ambiguous—filtering criteria, mixing ratio, and sampling strategy are not fully described.
- **Technical ambiguities**: Preprocessing for perplexity filtering (chat template application?) and exact WildGuard checkpoint version used for revision/evaluation are unreported.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Self-extraction viability)**: Medium. Theoretical grounding and MAUVE scores support proxy quality, but core assumption may fail under severe catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism 2 (Filtering & revision efficacy)**: Medium. Strong empirical evidence (Table 3) for revision benefit, but thresholds are heuristic and model/task-dependent.
- **Mechanism 3 (Mixing ratio & theoretical bounds)**: Medium-High. Consistent empirical U-shape and comparison to original data support r=0.1, but bounds rely on accurate D_KL estimation.
- **Overarching claim (GR-SAP preserves safety & utility)**: Medium. Results are strong across models/tasks, but reproducibility is jeopardized by the above unknowns.

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset balance protocol**: Intentionally create a synthetic pool with very few "difficult" examples post-filtering. Test whether the balancing step fails (pool size < r*N) and document any implicit upsampling or threshold relaxation.
2. **Baseline reconstruction**: Replicate AEGIS/Beavertails mixing exactly as described (or contact authors). Compare HS reduction against GR-SAP with same r=0.1; a large gap would indicate missing methodology.
