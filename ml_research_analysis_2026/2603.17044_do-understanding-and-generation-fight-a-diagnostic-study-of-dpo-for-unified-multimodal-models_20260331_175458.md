# Do Understanding and Generation Fight? A Diagnostic Study of DPO for Unified Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17044
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17044
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) fails to improve image generation in unified models (no significant CLIPScore gain at 7B, degradation at 1B) while yielding only modest understanding gains.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) can simultaneously align image understanding and image generation in a unified multimodal model, using the Janus-Pro architecture. Through extensive experiments across seven training strategies and two post-hoc methods, the core finding is negative: generation quality, measured by CLIPScore, shows no statistically significant improvement at the 7B scale and degrades at 1B. In contrast, understanding-only DPO yields a modest but statistically significant improvement on VQA tasks. Gradient analysis reveals that interference between the two tasks stems not from conflicting directions but from a severe magnitude imbalance—generation gradients are 11–14× larger due to the 576-token VQ generation sequence versus ~30–100 text tokens for understanding—while the two gradient sets are near-orthogonal. Magnitude-balancing techniques preserve the understanding gains in multi-task training but fail to improve generation. Further analysis shows the generation DPO loss stagnates at ln(2), indicating that preferred and rejected images are indistinguishable at the per-token level under discrete VQ tokenization. The study concludes that VQ-based autoregressive generation presents a structural bottleneck for offline DPO alignment in this unified architecture.

## Method Summary
The study uses Janus-Pro models (1B and 7B parameters) which share a DeepSeek-LLM backbone with LoRA adapters applied to all transformer projection layers. Two task-specific preference datasets are constructed from COCO val2017: for understanding, 1,300 pairs of (image, question, chosen_response, rejected_response) are created by generating two responses per instance, scoring them via entity overlap with COCO captions, and keeping pairs with a margin ≥0.5; for generation, 288 pairs of (prompt, chosen_image, rejected_image) are formed using real COCO images as chosen and Janus-Pro-generated images as rejected, filtered by CLIP-ViT-B/32 text-image similarity. Models are trained with the DPO loss (β=0.1) for 1,000 steps using AdamW (lr=1e-6). Seven training strategies are tested, including single-task DPO, naive joint DPO, PCGrad, gradient-weighted dynamic balancing, static weight ablations, and two post-hoc combination methods (Rewarded Soups, separate LoRA oracle). Evaluation on 200 held-out COCO samples uses VQA content overlap (0–6) for understanding and CLIPScore (CLIP-ViT-L/14 cosine ×100) for generation, with statistical significance assessed via Welch’s t-test across three seeds. Gradient analysis computes cosine similarity and norm ratio between understanding and generation gradients on shared LoRA parameters across 200 mini-batches.

## Key Results
- Generation quality does not improve: CLIPScore changes are statistically insignificant at 7B (|Δ| < 0.2, p > 0.5) and become negative at 1B.
- Understanding-only DPO yields a modest but significant VQA gain (+0.26).
- Understanding and generation gradients are near-orthogonal (cosine ≈ 0) but exhibit an extreme magnitude imbalance (generation gradients ~11–14× larger), driven by sequence length asymmetry (576 VQ tokens vs. ~30–100 text tokens).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Magnitude Imbalance Dominates
The dominant interference mechanism in multi-task DPO is an extreme gradient magnitude imbalance (generation gradients ~11–14× larger), not directional conflict, because gradients are near-orthogonal. Understanding DPO aggregates over short text sequences (~30–100 tokens); generation DPO aggregates over long VQ sequences (576 tokens). This token-count asymmetry gives generation gradients much larger norm. The two gradients occupy orthogonal subspaces (cos≈0), so the combined update is essentially the larger generation vector when losses are naively summed. Evidence: abstract and section 4.2 show mean magnitude ratio ρ=0.073 (7B) and 0.093 (1B); inter-task cosine = −0.0001, while intra-task cosines are significantly positive. Break condition: if token counts were balanced or gradients became positively aligned (cos>0), magnitude imbalance would matter less; if gradients turned negatively aligned (cos<0), projection methods like PCGrad would be needed.

### Mechanism 2: VQ Tokenization Makes DPO Uninformative
Generation quality cannot improve under offline DPO because discrete VQ tokenization renders chosen and rejected images indistinguishable at the token level, causing the DPO loss to stagnate at the uninformative baseline ln(2). DPO optimizes an implicit reward derived from the difference in log-probabilities between preferred and rejected sequences. In VQ-based generation, image quality is a global property of the 576-token combination, but individual token distributions are nearly identical for high- and low-quality images, yielding ∆≈0. Consequently the logistic loss −logσ(β∆) ≈ ln(2), providing no learning signal. Evidence: abstract and section 5.1 show generation-only DPO loss remains at ~0.691 vs. ~0.219 for understanding; KL divergence shows generation policy barely moves (0.041 nats/seq) vs. understanding (3.84 nats/seq). Break condition: if a reward model could back-propagate sequence-level scores to token-level gradients (e.g., policy gradient), or if generation used continuous latents (diffusion), DPO might become effective.

### Mechanism 3: Magnitude-Balancing Preserves Understanding
In multi-task DPO, reweighting task losses to equalize gradient norms prevents the larger generation gradient from erasing the understanding improvement. Set weights w_U = ||g_G||/(||g_U||+||g_G||) and w_G = ||g_U||/(||g_U||+||g_G||). Since generation gradients dominate (||g_G|| ≫ ||g_U||), this amplifies the smaller understanding signal. Because gradients are orthogonal, the weighted sum updates both tasks productively. Evidence: abstract and section 4.3 show naive joint DPO erases understanding gain (−0.01 VQA); gradient-weighted (+0.01), length-normalized (+0.04), and fixed-weight (+0.03) sustain positive VQA deltas. Dynamic weights stabilize at w_U≈0.93, matching the analytical sequence-length ratio N/(N+T)=576/(576+~65)=0.92. Break condition: if gradients developed significant negative correlation (cos<0), magnitude balancing alone would be insufficient; projection methods like PCGrad would be necessary.

## Foundational Learning
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
  *Why needed:* DPO is the alignment algorithm used; its implicit reward formulation explains why generation fails (loss at ln(2)).  
  *Quick check:* Write the DPO loss function. What does it imply when the reward margin ∆ = 0?

- **Multi-task Gradient Interference**  
  *Why needed:* The paper’s core question is whether understanding and generation gradients "fight" when combined.  
  *Quick check:* Given gradients g_U and g_G with norms 0.1 and 1.4 and cosine similarity 0, what fraction of the combined norm comes from g_U?

- **VQ Tokenization for Images**  
  *Why needed:* The 576-token asymmetry originates from VQ-VAE; understanding this discrete representation explains the magnitude imbalance.  
  *Quick check:* How many VQ tokens does Janus-Pro generate per image? What is the codebook size?

- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**  
  *Why needed:* LoRA adapters on the LLM are the trainable parameters whose gradients are analyzed; they provide a focused intervention.  
  *Quick check:* In LoRA, what matrices are typically updated? How does rank affect model capacity?

- **Cosine Similarity as Alignment Metric**  
  *Why needed:* The paper uses cosine similarity to diagnose conflict; interpreting near-zero values requires a null baseline (intra-task).  
  *Quick check:* If two random high-dimensional vectors are independent, what is their expected cosine similarity? Why is intra-task cosine higher than inter-task?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Shared DeepSeek-LLM backbone (30 layers) with LoRA adapters on q,k,v,o,gate,up,down projections → two branches: (1) Understanding: frozen SigLIP encoder → ~30–100 visual tokens concatenated with text prompt → LLM → text response; (2) Generation: text prompt → LLM → 576 VQ tokens → frozen VQ-VAE decoder → image.

Critical path: 1) Understanding forward: SigLIP produces visual tokens; LLM processes them with text to generate autoregressive response; DPO loss gradients computed on shared LoRA. 2) Generation forward: LLM generates 576 VQ tokens one by one; each token contributes to loss sum, generating large gradients. 3) In multi-task training, gradients from both losses are computed on the same LoRA params; magnitude ratio ρ≈0.07–0.09 means generation contributes ~14× more to combined norm. 4) Because gradients are orthogonal (cos≈0), combined update is essentially the sum of two independent vectors; without balancing, understanding component is negligible.

Design tradeoffs: Decoupled encoders (SigLIP semantic, VQ-VAE reconstruction) cause near-orthogonal gradients but share LLM bottleneck. Discrete VQ tokenization enables unified autoregressive model but causes DPO bottleneck; continuous latents (e.g., diffusion) may align better with holistic preferences. LoRA (rank 64) provides efficient PEFT; capacity is sufficient (understanding moves, generation doesn’t), so bottleneck not LoRA-specific. Separate LoRA adapters (oracle) avoid interference but require task-specific routing; a single unified adapter is simpler but needs balancing.

Failure signatures: Generation-only DPO loss converges to ~0.691 (ln(2)). CLIPScore changes statistically insignificant (|t|<0.7, p>0.5) at 7B and negative at 1B. KL(π_G||π_ref) ≈ 0.04 nats/sequence vs. ~3.84 for understanding. Gradient magnitude ratio ρ stable at ~0.07–0.09 across layers and scales.

First 3 experiments: 1) Understanding-only DPO (1,300 text pairs): VQA +0.26, CLIPScore −0.05 → alignment works for understanding. 2) Generation-only DPO (288 image pairs): CLIPScore −0.13, loss ≈ ln(2) → offline DPO fails for VQ generation. 3) Naive joint DPO (α=0.5): VQA −0.01 (erases gain), CLIPScore +0.06 → understanding improvement lost due to magnitude domination.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do masked diffusion-based unified multimodal models exhibit the same resistance to offline DPO alignment for image generation as autoregressive VQ-based models?
2. Does increasing the scale of preference data beyond the tested 150–288 pairs improve generation quality when applying DPO to VQ-based unified models?
3. Can alternative offline preference optimization algorithms (e.g., IPO, KTO) successfully align image generation in VQ-based unified models where DPO fails?
4. Does the gradient magnitude imbalance and DPO generation failure generalize to unified multimodal models with different architectural couplings (e.g., Chameleon's early fusion, BLIP3-o's continuous diffusion)?

## Limitations
- Data construction details (entity extraction, novelty bonus, CLIP filtering) are underspecified, risking variability in reproduction.
- Gradient measurement protocol (exact LoRA parameter grouping, exclusion of generation head) is architecture-specific and may affect results.
- Scope limited to Janus-Pro on COCO; conclusions may not hold for continuous latent generation or other datasets/tasks.
- Study considers only offline DPO with static reference policy; online/iterative variants not explored.

## Confidence
- Generation DPO fails: High
- Understanding DPO succeeds: High
- Magnitude imbalance dominates interference: High
- VQ tokenization causes uninformative DPO loss: Medium
- Magnitude-balancing preserves understanding gains: High
- Generality to other architectures is low: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Continuous generation test:** Replace VQ decoder with diffusion, apply DPO using CLIP-based reward; assess if sequence-level gradients become informative and CLIPScore improves.
2. **Policy gradient approach:** Implement REINFORCE or GRPO for VQ generation to directly optimize sequence reward; compare with DPO.
3. **Gradient projection ablation:** Apply PCGrad/GradDrop to multi-task DPO; also experiment with shorter VQ sequences to reduce token-count imbalance and observe any change in interference pattern.
