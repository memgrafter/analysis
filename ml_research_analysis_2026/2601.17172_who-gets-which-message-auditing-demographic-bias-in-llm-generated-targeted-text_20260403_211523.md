# Who Gets Which Message? Auditing Demographic Bias in LLM-Generated Targeted Text

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17172
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17172
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit systematic gender and age stereotypes in targeted messaging, with male and youth-directed outputs being more agentic and persuasive, while female and senior-directed outputs stress warmth and tradition; these biases are amplified by contextual prompts.

## Executive Summary
This study audits demographic bias in LLM-generated targeted persuasive text, focusing on gender and age. Using a framework with Standalone Generation (minimal demographic cues) and Context-Rich Generation (adding thematic/regional context) for climate communication, it evaluates GPT-4o, Llama-3.3, and Mistral. The authors introduce the Persuasion Bias Index (PBI) to measure rhetorical framing asymmetry. Results show intrinsic biases exist even in minimal prompts, but contextual information significantly magnifies disparities. Male and younger audiences receive more agentic, certain, and directive messages, while female and older audiences receive messages emphasizing warmth and tradition. The findings reveal latent stereotypes that intensify with realistic microtargeting cues.

## Method Summary
The study employs a controlled two-setting generation framework. In Standalone Generation (SG), prompts specify only a demographic group (e.g., "a message for women") and a stance. In Context-Rich Generation (CRG), prompts add thematic (e.g., patriotism) and regional context. Messages are generated from three LLMs without fine-tuning. Bias is measured through: (1) Lexical Content Bias via odds ratios of words matching curated lexicons (agentic, warmth, etc.); (2) Language Style Bias via formality classifiers and emotion detection; (3) Persuasion Bias Index (PBI), a composite of agency framing, modal certainty, and imperative frequency. Statistical tests (t-tests, ANOVA, WEAT) compare demographic groups. SG isolates intrinsic bias; CRG evaluates bias under realistic targeting conditions.

## Key Results
- Systematic gender and age stereotypes are present across all models in both settings: male/youth-targeted messages are more agentic and persuasive, female/senior-targeted messages stress warmth and tradition.
- Contextual prompts amplify disparities; for example, GPT-4o shows a significant male vs. female PBI difference (t=-3.15, p=0.0017) in CRG, with larger effect sizes than in SG.
- Formality varies significantly by age (ANOVA p<0.001), and lexical odds ratios confirm overrepresentation of agentic/masculine terms for males and personal/feminine terms for females.
- The Persuasion Bias Index successfully isolates rhetorical framing bias, correlating weakly with sentiment (|r| ≤0.13), and captures amplification effects in CRG.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit demographic conditioning in prompts triggers latent stereotype activation in LLMs, altering lexical and stylistic outputs.
- **Mechanism:** Models internally map demographic attributes to associative networks learned from training data. When these attributes appear in prompts, they activate correlated linguistic patterns (e.g., agentic verbs for males, warmth terms for females), reflecting stereotypical "prototype" representations.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs encode societal demographic stereotypes as statistical regularities in token distributions; conditioning on demographic tokens navigates the model toward regions of the distribution associated with those stereotypes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract conclusion on agency vs. warmth; SG lexical analysis showing bias via odds ratios; neighbor papers on LLM messaging bias.
- **Break condition:** If SG prompts produced lexically identical outputs across demographics, the stereotype activation claim would be falsified. Systematic differences were found.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Persuasion Bias Index (PBI) successfully isolates rhetorical framing bias (agency, certainty, imperatives) from affective sentiment.
- **Mechanism:** PBI composites three orthogonal features: agency framing, modal certainty, imperative frequency. High PBI indicates directive, agentic persuasion; low PBI indicates hedged, deferential framing. It operationalizes persuasion as a multidimensional rhetorical stance.
- **Core assumption:** Persuasive force is separable into these dimensions; demographic biases manifest as systematic shifts along this composite scale.
- **Evidence anchors:** Formal definition grounded in connotation frame theory; significant gender PBI differences primarily driven by modal certainty; low correlation (|r| ≤0.13) with VADER sentiment.
- **Break condition:** If PBI correlated strongly (|r| >0.7) with sentiment, it would not capture a distinct persuasion dimension. Low correlations prevent this.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Contextual information (thematic, regional) in prompts amplifies latent demographic biases.
- **Mechanism:** Thematic and regional descriptors interact with demographic cues, activating more specific stereotype scripts (e.g., "patriotism" + "male" → agentic nationalistic framing). Context provides semantic scaffolding that retrieves elaborate, culturally stereotypical associations, increasing lexical salience and persuasive intensity disparities.
- **Core assumption:** Stereotype representations are context-sensitive; additional semantic constraints narrow the generation space to pathways where demographic stereotypes are more strongly encoded.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement on amplification; CRG showing larger effect sizes than SG across metrics; more extreme lexical odds ratios.
- **Break condition:** If CRG produced smaller or reversed bias magnitudes compared to SG, the amplification claim would fail. Consistent amplification was observed.

## Architecture Onboarding
Prompt Engineering Module -> Generation Engine -> Bias Metrics Pipeline -> Statistical Testing Layer -> Visualization Module
- **Critical path:** To replicate core findings: (a) Generate SG prompts for all gender/age/stance combinations; (b) Compute lexical ORs; (c) Generate CRG prompts with theme/region; (d) Compute PBI, formality, emotion; (e) Run t-tests/ANOVA on CRG data. SG lexical analysis is prerequisite for establishing baseline; CRG analysis is the main evaluation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - SG sample size vs. isolation: SG uses small N (16/model/demographic) to isolate intrinsic effects but limits statistical power. Tradeoff: purity of signal vs. reliability of OR estimates. Solution: Aggregate across models for SG lexical analysis.
  - Lexicon-based vs. embedding bias: Lexicon categories are manually curated, ensuring interpretability but may miss neologisms. Embedding-based WEAT complements this but can be sensitive to word list choice.
  - Domain specificity: Climate communication is a salient testbed, but biases may differ in other domains. Framework is domain-agnostic, but results are not generalizable beyond studied stances/themes.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Lexical ORs near 1 across demographics → No detectable SG bias; check prompt clarity and lexicon coverage.
  - PBI strongly correlated (|r|>0.5) with sentiment → PBI not capturing distinct persuasion; revisit component definitions.
  - CRG effect sizes smaller than SG → Prompt engineering failure; context may not have been sufficiently constraining.
  - Model-specific anomaly (e.g., Mistral showing opposite age trend) → Could indicate architectural differences; requires deeper probing.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate SG gender lexical bias: For each model, generate 16 messages per gender/stance using SG prompts. Extract nouns/adjectives via spaCy. Compute ORs for 9 lexicon categories. Expect: Male-targeted OR >1 for Agentic/Masculine; Female OR >1 for Feminine/Personal.
  2. CRG gender PBI comparison: Generate 440 CRG messages/model (full factorial: gender × age × stance × region × theme). Compute PBI per message. Run Welch’s t-test (male vs. female PBI). Expect: Significant negative ΔGender (male PBI > female) for GPT-4o/Llama.
  3. Age formality ANOVA (CRG): Classify formality of all CRG messages using GYAFC classifier. Run one-way ANOVA across 4 age groups. Follow with Tukey HSD. Expect: Significant F-statistic (p<0.001); younger groups significantly less formal than seniors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do LLMs handle non-binary gender and intersectional identities in targeted messaging? Basis: Limitations state focus on binary gender and no intersectional analysis. Why unresolved: No data on diverse identities; intersectional effects could worsen bias. Evidence needed: Systematic audit across non-binary genders and demographic intersections using the same framework.
- **Open Question 2:** Are the identified biases generalizable across domains (health, politics) and other demographic groups (race, region)? Basis: Limitations note study only on climate change; may differ across topics and demographics. Why unresolved: Domain and demographic scope limited; cross-context validity unknown. Evidence needed: Replicate framework in multiple domains and with varied demographic attributes to compare bias patterns.
- **Open Question 3:** Can fine-tuning mitigate bias in targeted generation without harming persuasiveness? Basis: Limitations cite resource constraints preventing fine-tuning exploration. Why unresolved: Fine-tuning might reduce bias but could degrade message quality; trade-off unexplored. Evidence needed: Apply fine-tuning with bias mitigation objectives and measure changes in bias metrics and message persuasiveness.
- **Open Question 4:** Do the linguistic bias metrics (PBI, lexical ORs) correlate with real persuasive impact on human audiences? Basis: Limitations state evaluation through linguistic measures, not real-world impact. Why unresolved: Metrics may not capture actual persuasion; human validation needed. Evidence needed: Behavioral experiments measuring attitude/behavior change after exposure, correlated with bias scores.

## Limitations
- Unspecified API endpoints/parameters for Llama-3.3 70B and Mistral-Large 2.1 risk replication variability.
- Unclear fine-tuning details of the GYAFC formality classifier, potentially affecting style bias metrics.
- Small Standalone Generation sample size (n=16 per demographic) limits statistical robustness.
- Domain specificity to climate communication may constrain generalizability to other persuasive contexts.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (demographic conditioning activates stereotypes): **High**—consistent lexical biases across models in SG.
- Mechanism 2 (PBI captures distinct persuasion framing): **Medium**—low sentiment correlation shown, but orthogonality may vary across domains.
- Mechanism 3 (context amplifies biases): **High**—CRG effect sizes uniformly exceed SG.
- Foundational concepts (bias in NLG, persuasion theory): **High**—well-established in prior literature.
