# SynPlanResearch-R1: Encouraging Tool Exploration for Deep Research with Synthetic Plans

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07853
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07853
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SynPlanResearch-R1 achieves average score improvements of up to 6.0% on Qwen3-8B and 5.8% on Qwen3-4B over state-of-the-art baselines across seven multi-hop and open-web benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Research agents trained with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) often suffer from poor exploration, leading to premature termination and biased tool usage. SynPlanResearch-R1 addresses this by introducing a plan-guided framework that synthesizes trajectories with randomized tool sequences and soft cue-injected thoughts during cold-start supervised fine-tuning. This creates a stronger prior for subsequent reinforcement learning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and stabilization tricks. Evaluated on seven multi-hop and open-web benchmarks, SynPlanResearch-R1 achieves average score improvements of up to 6.0% on Qwen3-8B and 5.8% on Qwen3-4B over state-of-the-art baselines. Analysis confirms that the method significantly increases average tool calls, maintains higher policy entropy during training, and avoids shallow exploration patterns, directly contributing to the observed gains.

## Method Summary
SynPlanResearch-R1 employs a two-stage training pipeline. First, during cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT), synthetic trajectories are generated using a large reasoning model (LRM) with randomized tool sequences and soft cue-injected thoughts, encouraging diverse and deep tool interactions. This synthetic data yields a stronger prior for the target model. Second, the SFT model is further trained with reinforcement learning via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) combined with stabilization tricks (e.g., entropy regularization) to refine the policy while preserving exploration. The synthetic prior mitigates the weak initialization problem common in RLVR, preventing premature termination and tool bias.

## Key Results
- Up to 6.0% average score improvement on Qwen3-8B and 5.8% on Qwen3-4B across seven multi-hop and open-web benchmarks.
- Significantly higher average tool calls and policy entropy during training, indicating deeper and more diverse exploration.
- Avoidance of shallow exploration patterns, directly leading to superior final accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RLVR agents often begin with a weak policy that collapses to suboptimal behavior (e.g., early stopping or overusing a single tool) due to sparse rewards and poor exploration. SynPlanResearch-R1’s synthetic plan-guided SFT injects a strong prior by exposing the model to varied tool sequences and structured reasoning cues, which teaches the agent to use tools more deeply and flexibly. This prior then guides the GRPO phase, where stabilization tricks maintain higher entropy and prevent collapse, allowing the agent to continue exploring effectively and ultimately achieving higher accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**: Training paradigm where rewards are computed from task success (e.g., exact match). *Why needed*: Provides scalable supervision for complex tasks without human labeling. *Quick check*: Ensure reward function aligns with evaluation metric and is deterministic.
- **Tool-augmented reasoning**: Agents use external tools (e.g., web search, crawl) to gather information. *Why needed*: Grants access to up-to-date knowledge beyond model parameters. *Quick check*: Verify tool API integration and response parsing.
- **Synthetic trajectory generation**: Using a large reasoning model to produce example trajectories with tool calls. *Why needed*: Bootstraps exploration when human data is scarce. *Quick check*: Sample trajectories and validate correctness and format.
- **Plan-guided fine-tuning**: Conditioning on a high-level plan and injecting soft cues at each step. *Why needed*: Structures exploration and prevents random, inefficient tool usage. *Quick check*: Confirm plans are diverse and cues are properly placed.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: A PPO variant that optimizes relative advantage within a group of responses. *Why needed*: Stabilizes RL training by reducing variance. *Quick check*: Monitor KL divergence and advantage normalization.
- **Stabilization tricks**: Techniques like entropy regularization and learning rate scheduling. *Why needed*: Prevents policy collapse and promotes sustained exploration. *Quick check*: Track policy entropy and loss curves during training.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
LRM (QWEN3-32B) -> Synthetic Planner -> Trajectory Sampler -> SFT Dataset -> Cold-start SFT -> SFT Model -> GRPO Agent -> Stabilization Module -> Final Agent

**Critical path**:  
1) Use LRM to generate synthetic trajectories with randomized tool sequences and soft cues.  
2) Perform supervised fine-tuning on the target model using these trajectories.  
3) Apply GRPO reinforcement learning with stabilization tricks to the SFT model.  
4) Deploy the resulting final agent.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Larger LRM improves synthetic data quality but increases computational cost and limits accessibility.  
- Randomization encourages exploration but may produce invalid trajectories, requiring filtering.  
- Handcrafted soft cues aid plan adherence but may not generalize across domains; learnable conditioning could be more flexible.  
- Stabilization tricks maintain exploration but can slow convergence.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Premature termination (agent stops after few tool calls).  
- Tool bias (over-reliance on a single tool).  
- Collapse of policy entropy (becoming deterministic and stuck).  
- Poor generalization to unseen benchmarks.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Synthetic trajectory diversity audit**: Sample 100 synthetic trajectories; compute distribution of tool call lengths and combinations; compare against human-authored data.  
2. **SFT model tool usage analysis**: After cold-start SFT, run on a validation set and measure average tool calls and success rate; compare to baseline SFT without synthetic plans.  
3. **RL convergence check**: Train with GRPO and monitor policy entropy and reward curve; compare with and without stabilization tricks to verify their effect on stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How sensitive is SynPlanResearch-R1 to the scale of the large reasoning model used for synthetic data generation?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors use QWEN3-32B as the LRM for trajectory synthesis while training 4B/8B target models, but do not investigate the impact of LRM size or capability on downstream performance.
- **Why unresolved**: Relying on a much larger LRM increases computational cost and limits accessibility; determining whether smaller or equally sized models suffice is important for practical deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A systematic study varying the LRM size (e.g., same scale as target, smaller, larger) and measuring synthetic trajectory quality (correctness, format validity, diversity) and final RL performance on the target agent.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does the plan-guided exploration approach generalize to agents with more than two tools?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The experiments only use two tools (web search and crawl webpage) and do not evaluate scenarios with a richer toolset.
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world research agents often require many different tools; the method's ability to encourage exploration in larger action spaces remains unknown and could be a limiting factor for broader application.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extend the framework to include additional tools (e.g., calculator, database query, code execution) on a benchmark that necessitates multi-tool coordination, then compare exploration depth and task accuracy against baseline agents.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Are manually crafted cue templates essential for effective plan adherence, or can their role be replaced by automated conditioning methods?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method injects handcrafted cues at each thought step, and an ablation shows that removing cues significantly hurts performance; however, the paper does not explore alternative conditioning mechanisms.
- **Why unresolved**: Manual cue design may not generalize across domains and could be brittle; finding more flexible and learnable ways to guide plan execution would enhance robustness and reduce engineering effort.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Replace cue injection with alternatives (e.g., structured plan prefixes, dense reward shaping for plan compliance, or a small prompt-tuning network) and evaluate plan fidelity (e.g., percentage of steps matching the plan) and final task performance.
