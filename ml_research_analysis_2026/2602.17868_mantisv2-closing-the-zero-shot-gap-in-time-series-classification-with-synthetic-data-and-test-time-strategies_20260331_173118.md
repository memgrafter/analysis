# MantisV2: Closing the Zero-Shot Gap in Time Series Classification with Synthetic Data and Test-Time Strategies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17868
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17868
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** MantisV2 achieves 84.94% zero-shot accuracy on UCR with self-ensembling and fusion, surpassing prior foundation models and matching fine-tuned Mantis while using only 2.2M parameters.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MantisV2, a frozen time series encoder that closes the zero-shot performance gap on classification tasks. It achieves this through three innovations: pre-training exclusively on synthetic CauKer data to avoid leakage and improve generalization, architectural refinements that yield a lightweight yet powerful encoder, and a suite of test-time optimization strategies that boost performance without additional training. The approach matches fine-tuned state-of-the-art models across multiple benchmarks (UCR, UEA, HAR, EEG) with dramatically fewer parameters and fast inference, demonstrating that careful design in synthetic pre-training, architecture, and test-time refinement can eliminate the need for fine-tuning in time series classification.

## Method Summary
MantisV2 is pre-trained via contrastive learning (NT-Xent loss) on a large synthetic corpus (CauKer 2M) generated using Gaussian processes and structural causal models. The encoder architecture consists of a token generator with three parallel branches (raw signal convolution, first-order difference, and patch-based statistics) that produce fixed-length tokens, followed by a 6-layer Transformer with RoPE positional encoding, RMSNorm, and SwiGLU activations. For zero-shot classification, test-time strategies are applied: an intermediate Transformer layer (selected based on pre-training scale) is used, its CLS token is concatenated with the mean-pooled patch tokens, and optional self-ensembling via multi-scale interpolation (128/256/512/1024) on original and differenced series is performed. Finally, embeddings can be fused with those from other foundation models. A linear classifier (Random Forest or Logistic Regression) is trained on the extracted features.

## Key Results
- On UCR (128 datasets), MantisV2 reaches 84.94% accuracy with self-ensembling and cross-model fusion, outperforming TiViT-H (82.88%) and TS2Vec (82.54%).
- The model has only 2.2M parameters and maintains fast inference despite test-time ensembling.
- Performance is competitive with fine-tuned Mantis (85.24%) without any task-specific training.
- Gains hold on UEA, HAR, and EEG, though improvements on EEG are smaller, indicating domain-specific limitations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Synthetic Data Pre-training
**Claim:** Pre-training exclusively on synthetic time series (CauKer) avoids dataset leakage and yields more generalizable representations than real data.  
**Mechanism:** Synthetic data generated via Gaussian process kernel composition and structural causal models provides massive, diverse, out-of-distribution samples that cover a broad range of temporal patterns. The contrastive objective (NT-Xent) promotes uniformity in the embedding space, which benefits from this diversity.  
**Core assumption:** The synthetic distribution sufficiently approximates the manifold of real time series across domains.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "pre-training exclusively on synthetic CauKer data to avoid leakage and improve generalization."  
- Section 3.1 (Table 1): 100K synthetic subset (78.81%) matches/exceeds equally-sized real subsets (74.73%, 78.29%) and is comparable to full real dataset (79.21%).  
**Break condition:** If generation fails to capture domain-specific dynamics (e.g., rare physiological patterns in EEG), zero-shot performance degrades on those domains.

### Mechanism 2: Architectural Refinement
**Claim:** Controlled ablations refine the architecture into a lightweight (2.2M parameters) yet more performant encoder by adjusting convolutional kernel size, Transformer head dimension, and modernizing components.  
**Mechanism:** Increasing kernel size to 41 extends receptive field without parameter growth; reducing head dimension to 32 cuts parameters while maintaining capacity; upgrading to RoPE, RMSNorm, and SwiGLU improves stability and representation quality.  
**Core assumption:** Original hyperparameters (e.g., large head dim) and legacy components limited efficiency and performance.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "architectural refinement via controlled ablations yielding a lightweight encoder (conv kernel 41, head dim 32, RoPE/RMSNorm/SwiGLU)."  
- Section 3.2 (Figs 4–5): Accuracy peaks at kernel size 41 and head dimension 32; RoPE+RMSNorm+SwiGLU combination outperforms baselines.  
**Break condition:** Extremely short/long sequences or highly multivariate data may require different kernel sizes or token counts.

### Mechanism 3: Test-Time Optimization Suite
**Claim:** Inference-time strategies—using intermediate layers, concatenating CLS and mean tokens, multi-scale interpolation self-ensembling, and cross-model fusion—substantially boost zero-shot performance without additional pre-training or fine-tuning.  
**Mechanism:** Intermediate layers retain richer, less overfitted features; CLS+mean concatenation combines global and local statistics; self-ensembling over multiple scales and first differences creates robust embeddings; fusion exploits complementary representations from different models.  
**Core assumption:** The frozen encoder's intermediate representations are discriminative and amenable to input perturbations; different models capture orthogonal aspects.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "test-time optimization suite using intermediate layers (best layer varies), concatenating CLS and mean tokens, multi-scale interpolation self-ensembling, and cross-model fusion."  
- Section 3.3 (Figs 6–7): Intermediate layers outperform final layers, especially with more pre-training data.  
- Section 3.4 (Fig 9a): Concatenation improves Mantis+ by 0.1% and MantisV2 by 0.8% on UCR.  
- Section 5.1 (Fig 14): Multi-scale interpolation (+0.66%) and first-difference embeddings (+0.67%) yield gains.  
- Section 5.2 (Fig 15a): Fusion with TiConvNext gives highest UCR accuracy (84.97%).  
**Break condition:** Self-ensembling multiplies inference cost ~8×; best layer may be dataset-dependent; very short sequences may not benefit from multi-scale interpolation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Self-Supervised Learning**  
  *Why needed:* The pre-training objective is NT-Xent. Understanding positive/negative pair construction (random crop-resize) and how the loss shapes embedding space is key to interpreting why synthetic data helps.  
  *Quick check:* How does augmentation choice affect invariance properties, and why is it suitable for classification rather than forecasting?
- **Time Series Tokenization Strategies**  
  *Why needed:* MantisV2 uses a custom token generator with three branches (raw, differential, statistics) and convolution-based patching, differing from common patch-based approaches.  
  *Quick check:* What is the role of the first-order differential branch, and why is mean pooling preferred over max pooling or non-overlapping patches?
- **Transformer Variants (RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU)**  
  *Why needed:* The architecture upgrade includes these modern components; their advantages (relative position, stability, capacity) explain design choices.  
  *Quick check:* How does SwiGLU compare to GELU in parameter efficiency and gradient flow, and why might RMSNorm be beneficial for time series?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (resized to 512) -> Token Generator (InstanceNorm + 3 branches: Conv+meanpool on raw, Conv+meanpool on diff, patch-based stats) -> Transformer Encoder (6 layers, 8 heads, head_dim=32, RoPE/RMSNorm/SwiGLU) -> Feature Extraction (select best intermediate layer, concatenate CLS and mean tokens, optionally self-ensemble and fuse) -> Embedding (512-D).
- **Critical path:** Resize input -> Apply token generator -> Forward through all Transformer layers -> Extract output from chosen layer -> Aggregate tokens (CLS + mean) -> Final embedding.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Fixed token count (32) limits self-attention cost but requires input lengths divisible by 32, potentially distorting extreme lengths.  
  - Larger convolution kernel (41) increases receptive field but may blur fine details; chosen value balances both.  
  - Intermediate layer selection improves zero-shot but may be dataset-dependent.  
  - Self-ensembling improves accuracy but multiplies inference cost ~8×; fusion adds further overhead.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Memory error: Batch size too large for GPU (though model is small).  
  - Poor performance on a new domain: Could indicate synthetic distribution mismatch; try fine-tuning or adjust best layer.  
  - Worse than random: Input length not divisible by 32, or token generator misapplied (e.g., forgetting instance norm).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation on pre-training data:** Pre-train Mantis (original architecture) on 100K synthetic vs. 100K real subset (Anomaly UCR) vs. full Mantis V1 real (1.89M). Evaluate zero-shot on UCR with Random Forest. Expect synthetic ≥ real subsets and comparable to full real dataset with less data.  
  2. **Architectural ablation:** Starting from original Mantis, vary one component at a time: kernel size (9,17,25,33,41,49), head dimension (32,64,128,256), Transformer components (sinusoidal vs. RoPE, LayerNorm vs. RMSNorm, GELU vs. SwiGLU). Evaluate on UCR zero-shot. Expect kernel 41 and head 32 to peak; RoPE+RMSNorm+SwiGLU best.  
  3. **Test-time optimization:** For a fixed pre-trained MantisV2, compare (a) final layer CLS only, (b) final layer CLS+mean, (c) best intermediate layer CLS+mean, (d) add self-ensembling (multi-scale + diff), (e) add cross-model fusion with TiConvNext. Measure UCR accuracy. Expect concatenation and intermediate layer to improve; self-ensembling and fusion to add further gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What intrinsic properties of synthetic time series data (CauKer) enable them to outperform real data of the same size in contrastive pre-training? The paper shows empirical superiority but doesn't analyze whether broader distribution coverage, absence of noise, or controllable diversity is key.
2. Why do intermediate Transformer layers provide stronger zero-shot representations than the final layer, and why does this advantage disappear during fine-tuning? The paper hypothesizes final-layer overfitting to the contrastive objective but lacks probing analysis.
3. How do the proposed test-time strategies (multi-scale interpolation, first-order difference, cross-model fusion) synergistically interact, and is there a principled way to combine them without exhaustive search? The paper reports additive gains but not interactions.
4. To what extent does MantisV2's success on UCR/UEA generalize to highly multivariate, high-frequency, or non-stationary time series (e.g., EEG, financial ticks)? EEG results are weaker, suggesting limitations, but broader real-world applicability is untested.
5. Can the zero-shot performance gains from test-time optimization be formally linked to properties of the pre-trained embedding space (e.g., manifold smoothness, Lipschitz constants)? The paper lacks theoretical grounding for why interpolation and fusion work.

## Limitations
- Pre‑training hyperparameters (learning rate, optimizer, batch size) are undisclosed, preventing exact reproduction from scratch.
- The CauKer synthetic data generation process is only referenced externally; without code or kernel parameters, verifying its diversity and coverage is difficult.
- The “best layer” for test‑time feature extraction appears dataset‑dependent; using a single layer (e.g., layer 3) across all domains may degrade performance, especially on EEG or HAR.
- Self‑ensembling (~8× inference cost) and cross‑model fusion contradict the claim of fast inference; real‑world deployment may require trade‑offs.
- Synthetic pre‑training yields smaller gains on EEG, suggesting potential distribution mismatch for physiological time series.

## Confidence
- **Synthetic Data Pre‑training:** Medium (supported by Table 1 but limited independent validation and domain-specific inconsistencies)
- **Architectural Refinement:** High (systematic ablations align with best practices and show clear gains)
- **Test‑Time Optimization Suite:** High (multiple controlled experiments confirm consistent improvements)
