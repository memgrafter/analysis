# Domain-Specific Quality Estimation for Machine Translation in Low-Resource Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07372
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07372
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Intermediate Transformer layers (e.g., Layers −9, −11) consistently yield higher Spearman correlations than final layers across Healthcare, Legal, Tourism, and General domains; ALOPE with LoRA/LoRMA improves performance for smaller open-weight models, especially in complex domains.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles sentence-level Quality Estimation (QE) for English-to-Indic machine translation under domain-specific low-resource conditions. The authors evaluate prompt-only strategies across closed- and open-weight large language models (LLMs) and propose ALOPE, which attaches regression heads to intermediate Transformer layers and fine-tunes them with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and its multiplicative variant LoRMA. Key findings show that intermediate layers, particularly layers −9 and −11, consistently outperform final layers across Healthcare, Legal, Tourism, and General domains. ALOPE significantly boosts performance for smaller open-weight models, with LoRA achieving peak domain-average Spearman correlations up to 0.404 (General) and 0.415 (Healthcare), while LoRMA offers smoother layer-wise behavior. Although closed-weight models with guideline-anchored prompting remain strongest overall, ALOPE presents a lightweight alternative for resource-constrained settings, particularly in semantically demanding domains such as Legal.

## Method Summary
The study explores two complementary tracks. First, prompt-only baselines employ zero-shot and few-shot prompting (with and without domain guidelines) on closed-weight (Gemini) and open-weight (LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma) models to predict Direct Assessment (DA) scores. Second, ALOPE (Attaching Regression Heads to Intermediate Layers with Low-Rank Adaptation) targets the LLaMA-3.2-3B Instruct model. ALOPE selects intermediate Transformer layers (e.g., layers −1, −7, −9, −11) and attaches a lightweight two-layer feed-forward regression head (with ReLU activation) to the hidden states of the chosen layer. The base model is kept frozen while the regression head and optionally LoRA or LoRMA adapters are trained quantised (4-bit) using mean squared error (MSE) loss on domain-specific training data. Evaluation uses sentence-level Spearman’s ρ (primary) and Pearson’s r across four domains.

## Key Results
- Intermediate layers (−9, −11) achieve substantially higher Spearman correlations than the final layer across all domains.
- ALOPE with LoRA reaches peak domain-average Spearman ρ of 0.404 (General) and 0.415 (Healthcare); LoRMA yields smoother layer-wise trends.
- For open-weight models, ALOPE robustly improves QE performance, especially in the semantically complex Legal domain, narrowing the gap with closed-weight guideline-anchored prompting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Intermediate Transformer layers (e.g., Layers −9, −11) encode more robust Quality Estimation (QE) signals for English→Indic translation than the final layer.  
**Mechanism:** The regression head attached to an intermediate layer extracts representations that have undergone sufficient cross-lingual and semantic processing but have not yet been specialised for next-token prediction, which dominates the final layer’s objective.  
**Core assumption:** The QE task (predicting human Direct Assessment scores) aligns more with intermediate-layer semantic representations than with the final layer’s instruction-following output format.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “intermediate layers (e.g., Layers −9, −11) consistently yield higher Spearman correlations than final layers across Healthcare, Legal, Tourism, and General domains.”  
- [section 4.1] “Across all domains, the averaged results reveal a clear and consistent pattern: intermediate Transformer layers, particularly Layers −9 and −11, yield substantially higher Spearman correlations than the final layer (Layer-1).”  
- [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., “Toward domain-specific machine translation and quality estimation systems”) corroborate that QE degrades under domain mismatch, motivating targeted architectural adaptations.  
**Break condition:** If the base model has been extensively instruction-tuned on QE-like tasks, the final layer might become competitive; this was not observed here, as even closed-weight models showed variability without guideline anchoring.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Parameter-efficient adaptation via LoRA/LoRMA applied to selected intermediate layers (ALOPE) stabilises and improves QE performance for smaller open-weight models where prompt-only approaches fail.  
**Mechanism:** Low-rank adapters inject task-specific QE signals into the feed-forward or attention weights of frozen intermediate layers, tuning the model towards the regression objective without catastrophic forgetting or high computational cost.  
**Core assumption:** The base model’s representations contain QE-relevant information, but the output head and layer norms are not optimised for direct score regression; lightweight adaptation can re-purpose these representations.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “ALOPE notably improves performance for smaller open-weight models… ALOPE provides a lightweight alternative for resource-constrained settings.”  
- [section 3.3] “ALOPE attach regression heads to selected intermediate Transformer layers and updates only a minimal parameter subset using LoRA.”  
- [section 4.3] “Across the majority of the language pairs and domains, ALOPE consistently achieves higher Spearman correlations than prompt-only prompting strategies.”  
**Break condition:** If domain-specific terminology is already well-covered in the base model’s pre-training (e.g., General domain), adaptation yields marginal gains; seen in Healthcare where closed-weight prompting remained strong.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The benefit of intermediate-layer adaptation is domain-dependent, with the largest gains in semantically complex domains (Legal) where precise terminology and modality matter, compared to descriptive domains (Tourism).  
**Mechanism:** Complex domains require deeper semantic processing that intermediate layers capture better; surface-level fluency (adequate for Tourism) can be prompted directly, while nuanced legal constraints need adapted representations.  
**Core assumption:** Domain complexity correlates with the depth of linguistic analysis required for accurate QE; Legal texts involve strict semantics, while Tourism is entity-heavy and descriptive.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “gains in semantically complex domains… Legal.”  
- [section 4.4] “Legal remains the most challenging domain overall. ALOPE shows selective improvements… Legal domain, ALOPE substantially improves performance.”  
- [section 4.4] “Tourism exhibits an irregular pattern where zero-shot prompting with open-weight models achieves surprisingly strong performance… entity-heavy and descriptive nature of Tourism content.”  
**Break condition:** If domain complexity is low (e.g., simple descriptive text), prompting alone may suffice, making adaptation unnecessary. Conversely, if the domain is highly technical but the base model has seen similar data during pre-training, adaptation might not add value.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base LLM (frozen) → Layer Selection Module (choose intermediate layer, e.g., layer −9) → Regression Head (2‑layer FFN) attached to that layer’s hidden states → Adapter Module (LoRA/LoRMA) applied to the selected layer’s weights → DA score prediction.  
For baselines: Prompting Pipeline → LLM (closed‑ or open‑weight) → direct score output (no regression head).

**Critical path:**  
1. Select target intermediate layers based on prior analysis (L−9, L−11) or via grid search.  
2. Attach a regression head and optionally an adapter (LoRA/LoRMA) to each selected layer (single‑layer configuration per run).  
3. Train with MSE loss on gold DA scores using quantised base model (QLoRA, 4‑bit).  
4. Evaluate on the domain-specific test set; pick the layer/adapter configuration with highest Spearman’s ρ.  
5. Compare against prompt‑only baselines on the same backbone.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **LoRA vs. LoRMA:** LoRA tends to produce higher peak correlations but can be unstable at shallower layers; LoRMA yields smoother layer‑wise curves with modestly lower peaks.  
- **Layer depth:** Deeper intermediate layers (e.g., −9, −11) generally outperform shallower ones (e.g., −7), though optimal depth may be model‑specific.  
- **Rank (R) and scaling (α):** Higher rank (e.g., 128) increases capacity but risks overfitting/instability; rank=64 with α=32 offered the best balance in experiments.  
- **Prompting vs. Adaptation:** For closed‑weight models, guideline‑anchored prompting is simplest and strongest; for open‑weight models, ALOPE is necessary for robust performance.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Near‑zero or negative Spearman ρ on open‑weight models with zero‑shot prompting.  
- Large performance drop when switching from General to Legal/Healthcare domains with prompt‑only.  
- Extreme variance in performance across different intermediate layers without ALOPE (some layers yield ρ<0.1).  
- LoRMA underfitting in highly semantic domains (Legal) where LoRA peaks.  
- Regression head failing to converge if learning rate is too high or adapter rank too low (R=32).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate layer‑wise analysis:** Using LLaMA‑3.2‑3B, run ALOPE with LoRA (R=64, α=32) on the General domain test set across layers L−1, L−7, L−9, L−11. Confirm that intermediate layers outperform the final layer by at least 0.1 ρ.  
2. **Adapter comparison:** On Legal domain (English→Tamil), compare LoRA (R=64, α=32) vs. LoRMA (same config) at the best layer identified in Experiment 1. Expect LoRA to achieve higher peak correlation while LoRMA shows smoother layer‑wise behavior.  
3. **Prompting vs. ALOPE head‑to‑head:** On Healthcare (English→Gujarati), evaluate the best guideline‑anchored prompt (closed‑weight proxy: Gemini‑2.5‑Pro) against ALOPE (LoRA, best layer). ALOPE should match or surpass open‑weight prompts but may still trail the closed‑weight baseline.

## Limitations
- Pooling strategy and complete training hyperparameters (LoRA target modules, LR schedule, epochs) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- The study is restricted to English→Indic language pairs; it is unclear whether findings generalise to other low‑resource scenarios or language families.
- ALOPE requires task‑specific training (albeit lightweight), whereas closed‑weight prompting is truly zero‑shot; the claimed efficiency advantage holds only when open‑weight models are accessible.

## Confidence
- **Intermediate‑layer superiority:** High  
- **ALOPE gains for small open‑weight models:** Medium  
- **Domain‑dependent variability (Legal vs. Tourism):** Medium
