# Rigidity in LLM Bandits with Implications for Human-AI Dyads

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07717
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07717
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit robust rigidity in two-arm bandit tasks, characterized by low learning rates (0.09–0.33) and near-ceiling inverse temperatures (~5), leading to amplified initial biases and poor adaptation across decoding conditions.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the decision-making biases of large language models (LLMs) by framing them as participants in sequential two-arm bandit tasks. Across 20,000 trials per condition and four decoding configurations, LLMs (DeepSeek, GPT-4.1, Gemini) displayed striking rigidity. Under symmetric rewards, they amplified initial positional biases into stubborn, near-exclusive preferences for one arm. Under asymmetric rewards, they rigidly exploited the superior arm but underperformed an oracle and failed to adjust after losses. Hierarchical Rescorla-Wagner-softmax modeling attributes these patterns to low learning rates and extremely high inverse temperatures, suggesting over-deterministic policies that resist updating. The findings imply that current LLMs lack the adaptive exploration-exploitation balance seen in human learners, with implications for human-AI collaborative systems.

## Method Summary
The experimental design treated each LLM as a virtual participant in multi-trial two-arm bandit tasks. For each run, the model was prompted with a history of previous choices and rewards and asked to select the next arm (X or Y). Rewards were generated from Bernoulli distributions with specified probabilities (symmetric: 0.25/0.25; asymmetric: 0.75/0.25). The study manipulated decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) and ran 200 independent trials per experimental cell, yielding over 20,000 total trials. Choice sequences were analyzed using behavioral metrics (Stubbornness, Rigidity Index, Loss-Shift) and fitted with a hierarchical Bayesian Rescorla-Wagner-softmax model to infer per-run learning rates (A) and inverse temperatures (τ), alongside group-level hyperparameters (μ_A, μ_τ).

## Key Results
- In symmetric reward conditions, LLMs amplified initial positional biases into extreme one-arm policies (Stubbornness up to 0.97) with high bias amplification (0.62–0.67).
- In asymmetric conditions, LLMs rigidly exploited the higher-reward arm but showed negative Adjusted Choice Bias (-0.04 to -0.09 relative to an oracle) and minimal reaction to losses (Loss-Shift near 0).
- Hierarchical modeling revealed consistently low learning rates (μ_A ∈ [0.09, 0.33]) and near-ceiling inverse temperatures (μ_τ ∈ [4.99, 5.00]) across decoding settings, indicating over-deterministic value updating.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit rigidity due to a persistently low learning rate, which slows value updating and causes early choices to persist.
- **Mechanism:** In the Rescorla-Wagner-softmax model, the learning rate \( A_i \) scales the prediction error term \( (r_t - V_t(a)) \). A low \( A_i \) means new feedback has little influence on action values \( V_t \), so the policy relies heavily on initial conditions (e.g., first-trial positional bias) and resists change.
- **Core assumption:** The hierarchical Rescorla-Wagner-softmax model is a valid descriptive model for LLM choice sequences in bandit tasks, and the inferred per-run parameters \( A_i \) reflect a stable, underlying "learning tendency" rather than noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "low learning rates (0.09–0.33) and near-ceiling inverse temperatures (~5), which together explain both noise-to-bias amplification and rigid exploitation."
  - [section 3.2] "Across the symmetric settings, the group learning rates were uniformly low \( \mu_A \in [0.09,0.22] \). [...] For the asymmetric counterparts, learning rates increased \( \mu_A \in [0.17,0.33] \) [but remained low]."
- **Break condition:** If future work with different LLM architectures, longer horizons, or richer feedback shows \( \mu_A \) approaching human-like ranges (~0.2–0.5), this mechanism may not generalize. A break would also occur if increasing reward magnitude or saliency significantly raised \( A \).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit over-deterministic policies due to very high inverse temperatures, which suppress exploratory choice even when valuable.
- **Mechanism:** The softmax policy \( P(Y_t=1) = \text{logit}^{-1}(\tau_i [V_t(Y)-V_t(X)]) \) uses inverse temperature \( \tau_i \) to scale value differences. A near-ceiling \( \tau_i \) (here ~5, the imposed bound) makes choices nearly greedy, minimizing stochastic switching even after losses (low Loss-Shift).
- **Core assumption:** The bounded-\( \tau \) softmax is an appropriate lens for LLM decision stochasticity under fixed decoding, and saturation at the upper bound indicates genuine over-determinism rather than a misspecification (e.g., missing perseverance term).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "very high inverse temperatures (~5)" and "near-deterministic policies that resist updating."
  - [section 3.2] "The group inverse temperatures were effectively at the ceiling \( \mu_\tau \in [4.9984,4.9991] \)." and "Inverse temperatures again clustered near the ceiling \( \mu_\tau \in [4.991,4.998] \)."
- **Break condition:** If future work removes the \( \tau \) upper bound or uses models with more calibrated uncertainty, inferred \( \mu_\tau \) might fall substantially. A break would also occur if manipulations that increase output entropy (e.g., higher temperature) systematically lowered \( \tau \) in the hierarchical fit, suggesting decoding parameters decouple from the "intrinsic" policy temperature.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) primarily affect surface-level output variability (e.g., invalid tokens, minor shifts in bias) but do not alter the core strategy of low learning + high determinism.
- **Mechanism:** Changing temperature/top-p alters the stochasticity of the final token sampling step but leaves the internal value update process (captured by \( A_i \)) and the fundamental preference sharpness (captured by \( \tau_i \)) largely unchanged. Thus, the same underlying cognitive-style policy manifests with different failure modes (e.g., format errors at high temp).
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's "decision policy" can be separated from its "output distribution" via a fixed internal value representation that decoding parameters act upon secondarily.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The observed patterns were consistent across manipulations of temperature and top-p [...] indicating that the qualitative behaviours are robust to the two decoding knobs typically available to practitioners."
  - [section 3.1] Behavioural metrics (Stubbornness, Rigidity Index, Loss-Shift) show similar trends across decoding configurations, except where extreme decoding (e.g., Temp=2.0, Top-p=1.0) caused collapse in Gemini via invalid outputs.
- **Break condition:** If a systematic manipulation shows that increasing temperature *within the model's internal generation process* (not just final sampling) significantly lowers the hierarchical \( \mu_A \) or raises \( \mu_\tau \), this would challenge the separation. Also, if different prompting styles (beyond decoding) change \( \mu_A \), the "robustness" claim fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Two-arm bandit tasks as a minimal probe of exploration-exploitation.**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire study rests on bandits to disentangle learning rate (exploration/integration of new info) from choice determinism (exploitation). Without understanding this trade-off, the metrics (Loss-Shift, Stubbornness) are meaningless.
  - *Quick check question:* In a symmetric 0.25/0.25 bandit, what pattern of choices would indicate *high* exploration? What would indicate *high* exploitation (rigidity)?
- **Concept: Hierarchical Bayesian modeling of cognitive parameters.**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper’s core inference about "low learning rates" and "high inverse temperatures" comes from fitting a hierarchical Rescorla-Wagner-softmax model. Understanding that individual \( (A_i, \tau_i) \) are drawn from group distributions \( (\mu_A, \mu_\tau) \) is key to interpreting the posterior summaries.
  - *Quick check question:* If the group-level \( \mu_\tau \) is 4.999 but the per-run \( \tau_i \) estimates have wide uncertainty, what does that say about the *reliability* of the "very high inverse temperature" claim?
- **Concept: Rescorla-Wagner learning rule.**
  - *Why needed here:* This rule defines how action values are updated based on prediction errors, with the learning rate controlling the speed of integration. It is the engine behind the inferred low \( A_i \) and consequent rigidity.
  - *Quick check question:* If \( A = 0 \), how would a sequence of 10 trials with a rewarding first choice evolve? What if \( A = 1 \)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Bandit Simulator -> LLM API Wrapper -> Metrics Engine -> Hierarchical Model (Stan) [Config Manager oversees parameters and decoding settings]

**Critical path:** Define experiment cell (reward structure + decoding) → Launch N=200 parallel runs via simulator + wrapper → Aggregate run metrics → Fit hierarchical model to *all* runs in cell → Extract group posteriors \( \mu_A, \mu_\tau \).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Fixed prompt vs. natural language variability: The fixed game-themed prompt controls for wording effects but may not generalize to natural interactions.
- Bounded τ: Capping τ at 5 via probit transform avoids non-identifiability but compresses variation in high-determinism policies.
- Invalid response handling: Coding invalids as reward=0 may conflate format failures with exploratory choices; alternatives include exclusion or separate modeling.

**Failure signatures:**
- High invalid-rate (>5%) in a cell, especially with high temperature, indicating prompt-compliance issues rather than strategic exploration.
- \( \mu_A \) posterior overlapping 0 or 1, suggesting the model is not meaningfully updating (either pure perseveration or immediate perfect learning).
- \( \mu_\tau \) hitting the upper bound (5) with a sharp posterior mass, indicating the model is too deterministic for the softmax to be informative.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Symmetric baseline replication:** Run DeepSeek/GPT-4.1/Gemini at `temp=0.0, top-p=0.5` with symmetric rewards. Expect high Stubbornness (>0.9) and low Loss-Shift (<0.05).
2. **Decoding robustness check:** Replicate with `temp=2.0, top-p=1.0`. Expect similar \( \mu_A, \mu_\tau \) but higher invalid rates (especially Gemini) and slightly lower Stubbornness.
3. **Asymmetric exploitation test:** Run with `pX=0.75, pY=0.25`, `temp=0.0, top-p=0.5`. Expect high Target-arm Rate (~0.95) but Adjusted Choice Bias negative (~-0.07), and near-zero Loss-Shift.

## Limitations
- **Generality:** Tested only on two-arm Bernoulli bandits with fixed prompts; unknown if rigidity extends to other architectures (Claude, Llama), multi-arm tasks, or longer horizons.
- **Model validity:** RW-softmax may be misspecified; high τ saturation might reflect deterministic policy or bounded-parameter artifact rather than intrinsic overconfidence.
- **Prompt effects:** Fixed game-themed prompt could interact with positional bias; alternative framings might alter rigidity magnitude or learning rates.

## Confidence
- **Rigidity patterns (High):** Robust across 20k+ trials per condition, three LLMs, and decoding variations (except extreme temp).
- **Mechanistic attribution (Medium):** Hierarchical fits indicate low μ_A and ceiling-bound μ_τ, but model assumptions may be violated.
- **Decoding robustness (Medium):** Behavioral consistency holds for temp ≤1.0, but extreme decoding causes Gemini collapse.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-LLM replication:** Replicate symmetric/asymmetric bandit experiments on at least two additional model families (e.g., Claude 3, Llama 3.1) with temp=0, top-p=0.5; confirm low μ_A and high μ_τ.
2. **Model comparison:** Fit alternative behavioral models (e.g., with perseveration, win-stay/lose-shift) to the same choice sequences; compare via posterior predictive checks and WAIC to validate RW-softmax.
3. **Prompt manipulation:** Systematically vary prompt wording (neutral vs. strategy-explicit, numeric vs. narrative) while keeping task identical; measure changes in stubbornness and inferred μ_A/μ_τ to test dependency.
