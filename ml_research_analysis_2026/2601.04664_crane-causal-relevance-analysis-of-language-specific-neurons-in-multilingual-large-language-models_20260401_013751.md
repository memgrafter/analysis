# CRANE: Causal Relevance Analysis of Language-Specific Neurons in Multilingual Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.04664
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04664
*   **Reference count:** 10
*   **Primary result:** CRANE uses causal attribution (LRP) and kurtosis to isolate functionally necessary, language-specific neurons, achieving significantly higher LangSpec-F1 (e.g., 0.475 for Vietnamese NLU) than correlation-based baselines like LAPE.

## Executive Summary
CRANE introduces a causality-driven framework to identify language-specific neurons in multilingual Large Language Models (LLMs). Moving beyond prior correlation-based methods (e.g., LAPE) that rely on activation heuristics, CRANE employs Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) to assign causal responsibility for model outputs to individual neurons. By conditioning relevance on specific languages and analyzing the kurtosis (peakedness) of these relevance distributions, it isolates neurons that are consistently and distinctly important for a given language. Causal intervention—masking these neurons—produces selective degradation on language-specific benchmarks (LangSpec-F1), empirically validating their functional necessity. Evaluated on LLaMA2-7B across five languages, CRANE demonstrates substantially more precise targeting than LAPE.

## Method Summary
CRANE proceeds in four stages. First, for a target language, it collects a dataset of input prompts. Second, for each prompt, it performs a forward pass through the model and then uses LRP to backward-propagate relevance scores from the final output token to every neuron in every layer, yielding a relevance vector per neuron. Third, for each neuron, it aggregates relevance scores across all prompts of that language and computes the kurtosis of this distribution. High kurtosis indicates the neuron’s relevance is consistently extreme (very high or very low) for that language, suggesting a language-specific functional role. Fourth, it selects neurons above a kurtosis threshold, masks (zeroes) them, and evaluates the resulting model on a language-specific NLU task to compute LangSpec-F1—the drop in F1 score relative to the original model.

## Key Results
*   On LLaMA2-7B, CRANE achieves LangSpec-F1 scores up to **0.475 for Vietnamese NLU**, whereas LAPE’s scores are near zero.
*   Masking neurons identified for one language causes minimal performance drop on other languages, demonstrating precise language-selective targeting.
*   The high LangSpec-F1 scores following causal intervention provide direct evidence that the identified neurons are functionally necessary for language-specific processing.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CRANE’s effectiveness stems from its combination of **causal attribution** and **distributional statistics**. LRP assigns each neuron a relevance score that accounts for its contribution to the final output, providing a more theoretically sound measure of functional importance than raw activation magnitude. By computing relevance over many language-conditioned examples, CRANE captures stable patterns of importance. Kurtosis then acts as a filter for neurons with a binary-like importance profile: either consistently crucial or consistently irrelevant for that language. Such binary on/off switching is a strong indicator of specialization. Masking these high-kurtosis neurons therefore disrupts the model’s ability to process that specific language, while leaving other languages relatively unaffected.

## Foundational Learning
1.  **Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP)**
    *   *Why needed:* Provides a theoretically grounded, input-specific attribution method to assign causal responsibility for outputs to individual neurons, moving beyond correlation.
    *   *Quick check:* Implement LRP for a simple CNN on MNIST and verify that relevance sums to the output probability for the correct class.
2.  **Kurtosis as a Selectivity Metric**
    *   *Why needed:* Quantifies the peakedness of a neuron's relevance distribution across examples. High kurtosis indicates the neuron's role is consistently strong or weak for a language, a sign of specialization.
    *   *Quick check:* Generate synthetic distributions (e.g., a spike at 0.9 vs. a uniform [0,1]) and confirm kurtosis distinguishes them.
3.  **Language-Conditioned Analysis**
    *   *Why needed:* Ensures attribution is performed separately for each language, allowing the discovery of language-specific patterns without cross-language interference.
    *   *Quick check:* Compute average activation distributions for a set of neurons in an mBERT model and verify they differ between English and Arabic inputs.
4.  **Neuron Masking as Intervention**
    *   *Why needed:* The gold standard for testing functional necessity. Masking candidate neurons and measuring performance drop tests causality.
    *   *Quick check:* In a small trained model, ablate (zero) a known important neuron and observe a performance cliff on a simple task.
5.  **LangSpec-F1 Metric**
    *   *Why needed:* Quantifies the language-selective impact of masking. It is the ratio of the masked model’s F1 to the original model’s F1 on a language-specific test set.
    *   *Quick check:* Randomly mask 10% of neurons and compute LangSpec-F1; expect a moderate, non-selective drop.
6.  **Cross-Lingual Transfer in LLMs**
    *   *Why needed:* Context for the debate on whether language-specific neurons exist and facilitate transfer.
    *   *Quick check:* Probe a multilingual LLM (e.g., mBERT) on XNLI for high- and low-resource languages and compare performance gaps.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Input (Language L) -> LLaMA2-7B (Transformer Decoder) -> LRP Attribution Engine -> Relevance Distributions (per neuron, per L) -> Kurtosis Analyzer -> Neuron Selector (for L) -> Masking Module -> LLaMA2-7B (with masked neurons) -> Evaluation (LangSpec-F1)

Critical path:
1.  **Data Collection:** Gather a representative corpus for a target language.
2.  **Attribution:** For each example, run forward pass, then LRP backward from the final token’s output to all neurons, storing relevance.
3.  **Aggregation & Selection:** For each neuron, collect all relevance scores for that language, compute kurtosis, and select top-k neurons (or above threshold).
4.  **Intervention & Evaluation:** Create a copy of the model with the selected neurons permanently zeroed. Run the model on a held-out, language-specific NLU test set and compute the F1 score. LangSpec-F1 = (Masked F1) / (Original F1).

Design tradeoffs:
*   **Kurtosis vs. Mean Relevance:** Kurtosis ignores the average level of relevance. A neuron could be consistently but moderately important (low kurtosis, medium mean) yet still be functionally necessary. CRANE prioritizes binary on/off specialization.
*   **LRP Cost:** LRP requires a full backward pass per example, which is computationally expensive for large LLMs and large corpora. The method trades scalability for precision.
*   **Selection Threshold (k):** A higher k selects more neurons, increasing the chance of capturing necessary ones but also adding noise (non-specific neurons), diluting LangSpec-F1. The optimal k is language-dependent.

Failure signatures:
*   **Low LangSpec-F1 for all k:** The kurtosis criteria fails to identify functionally relevant neurons; perhaps relevance distributions are not peaked for specialized neurons in this model.
*   **High LangSpec-F1 for non-target languages:** Selected neurons are not truly language-specific; masking them harms multiple languages, indicating they serve shared functions.
*   **High variance across random seeds:** The neuron selection process is unstable, suggesting the relevance distributions are noisy or the corpus is insufficient.

First experiments:
1.  **Replication:** Run CRANE on LLaMA2-7B for Vietnamese with the exact hyperparameters from the paper and confirm LangSpec-F1 ≥ 0.45.
2.  **Ablation on Kurtosis vs. Mean:** Replace kurtosis with the mean of absolute relevance for neuron selection. Compare LangSpec-F1 curves; expect CRANE’s kurtosis to yield higher peak LangSpec-F1.
3.  **Cross-Language Generalization Check:** Select neurons for Vietnamese and French, mask them, and evaluate on English. Expect LangSpec-F1 for English to be close to 1.0 (no degradation), confirming selectivity.

## Limitations
*   **Theoretical contradiction:** A key related work (*Language-specific Neurons Do Not Facilitate Cross-Lingual Transfer*) directly challenges CRANE’s foundational premise by arguing language-specific neurons do not meaningfully aid transfer.
*   **Scope constraints:** Evaluation is limited to LLaMA2-7B and only five languages (mostly Latin-script). Generalizability to other architectures (e.g., mT5, XLM-R) or non-Latin scripts (e.g., Chinese, Arabic) is unknown.
*   **Metric sensitivity:** LangSpec-F1, while effective, may not capture nuanced aspects of language-specific functionality beyond a single NLU benchmark (e.g., syntactic generation, cultural nuances).

## Confidence
*   **CRANE outperforms LAPE on LangSpec-F1 for tested languages:** **High** (clear empirical results on five languages).
*   **Identified neurons are functionally necessary for language-specific processing:** **Medium** (causal intervention supports this, but contradicted by parallel work questioning the existence/role of such neurons).
*   **Findings generalize to other multilingual LLMs and languages:** **Low** (severe scope constraints; only one decoder-only model tested).

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-architecture replication:** Apply CRANE to encoder-decoder models (e.g., mT5) and smaller multilingual LLMs (e.g., 360M parameter models) to test architectural robustness and scalability.
2.  **Contradiction resolution:** Directly compare CRANE-identified neurons with those from competing frameworks (*Isolating Culture Neurons*, *Cross-Lingual Activation Steering*) using the same model, languages, and evaluation tasks to reconcile differing conclusions.
3.  **Metric triangulation:** Evaluate masked models on diverse multilingual benchmarks (e.g., XNLI for cross-lingual NLU, FLORES-101 for translation) and compute alternative specificity metrics (e.g., KL divergence of output distributions between languages) to validate LangSpec-F1.
