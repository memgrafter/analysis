# A unified foundational framework for knowledge injection and evaluation of Large Language Models in Combustion Science

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04452
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04452
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: A three-stage framework for combustion LLMs shows naive RAG tops at 58.24% accuracy due to 56.3% retrieval miss and context contamination, requiring knowledge graphs and continued pretraining.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes an end-to-end infrastructure for Large Language Models in combustion science, addressing the field’s lack of dedicated tools. It introduces a 3.5-billion-token multimodal knowledge base, a new benchmark (CombustionQA), and a staged knowledge injection pathway. Validation of the first stage (naive RAG) reveals a hard performance ceiling: even with optimal retrieval, accuracy reaches only 58.24% against a theoretical upper bound of 87.3%. The 29-point gap is caused by a 56.3% retrieval miss rate and degradation from irrelevant retrieved context (contamination). These results demonstrate that naive RAG is insufficient and motivate the advanced stages (knowledge-graph-enhanced RAG and continued pretraining) for building a true combustion domain foundation model.

## Method Summary
The authors construct an AI-ready multimodal knowledge base from over 200,000 peer-reviewed articles, 8,000 theses, and 400,000 lines of CFD code, totaling 3.5 billion tokens. They develop the CombustionQA benchmark through an agentic question-generation pipeline, difficulty filtering, and manual curation, yielding 436 verified questions across eight subfields. To diagnose Stage 1 (naive RAG), they conduct controlled experiments: Zero-Shot (no context), Theoretical Upper Bound (direct source citation), Optimal RAG (retrieval from a condensed 2,982-segment corpus), and Noise RAG (irrelevant domain content). The evaluation uses FAISS for vector search, BGE-M3 for embeddings, and multiple LLMs. Results isolate retrieval recall limits and context contamination effects, guiding the need for Stage 2 (KG-RAG) and Stage 3 (continued pretraining).

## Key Results
- Optimal RAG accuracy peaks at 58.24% vs. theoretical upper bound 87.3%.
- Retrieval miss rate: 56.3% of queries fail to retrieve the exact source segment.
- Context contamination degrades performance even on hits; Noise RAG lowers accuracy to ~21.1%, below zero-shot.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Large-scale multimodal knowledge base enables comprehensive coverage**  
- Claim: The curated corpus of papers, theses, and code provides a substrate that captures the breadth of combustion science, allowing retrieval to access both explicit and tacit knowledge.  
- Mechanism: Parsing and embedding ~3.5B tokens into a vector store ensures representativeness; the scale approaches cumulative journal output.  
- Evidence: Abstract and Section 2.1 describe corpus construction; neighboring work corroborates domain-specific KB necessity.  
- Break condition: Missing recent or proprietary knowledge leads to systematic retrieval failures.

**Mechanism 2: Rigorous benchmark diagnoses retrieval vs. generation bottlenecks**  
- Claim: CombustionQA’s multi-stage generation (bootstrapping, validation, curation) yields questions that are answerable from source text yet challenging, enabling controlled experiments to isolate retriever performance.  
- Mechanism: By ensuring question-context alignment, the benchmark allows comparison of Optimal RAG (perfect corpus) and Theoretical Limit (direct citation) to quantify gaps attributable to retrieval.  
- Evidence: Abstract and Section 2.2 detail the pipeline; neighbors indicate community need for domain benchmarks.  
- Break condition: Misaligned question distribution reduces generalizability.

**Mechanism 3: Three-stage injection pathway is necessitated by inherent RAG limitations**  
- Claim: The bottlenecks identified in Stage 1 (retrieval miss and context contamination) are structural to naive vector-similarity RAG, requiring architectural changes in Stages 2 and 3.  
- Mechanism: Stage 1’s diagnostic shows 56.3% miss rate and contamination (accuracy drop even when correct chunk retrieved). Stage 2 (KG-RAG) adds structure to improve precision; Stage 3 (continued pretraining) embeds knowledge directly.  
- Evidence: Abstract and Section 3.2 quantify bottlenecks; neighboring work on structured retrieval and fine-tuning supports staged approach.  
- Break condition: KG construction fails to scale or pretraining causes catastrophic forgetting.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Substrate (Multimodal Knowledge Base: vector store of parsed tokens/chunks from papers, theses, CFD code) -> Evaluation (CombustionQA: 436 Q&A pairs with citations, difficulty-filtered) -> Pipeline Stage 1 (Naive RAG: Query -> Vector Search top-k -> Prompt with chunks -> LLM) -> Pipeline Stage 2 (KG-RAG: Query -> KG entity/relation extraction -> Subgraph retrieval -> Context assembly -> LLM) -> Pipeline Stage 3 (Continued Pretraining: Domain corpus -> Full model or LoRA fine-tuning -> New foundational model).

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Validate Stage 1 fully using CombustionQA in controlled scenarios (Optimal RAG, Noise RAG).  
  2. Diagnose bottleneck split (retrieval miss vs. contamination) from results.  
  3. Use this diagnosis to prioritize Stage 2 (if miss rate high) or Stage 3 (if contamination high) or both.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - RAG (Stages 1/2) vs. Pretraining (Stage 3): RAG is dynamic but suffers retrieval bottlenecks; pretraining internalizes knowledge but is static and compute-intensive.  
  - KG-RAG Complexity: Building a combustion knowledge graph from 3.5B tokens is a major ontology engineering task vs. simpler vector DB.  
  - Evaluation Scope: CombustionQA is high-quality but limited (436 Qs); full capability requires more diverse tasks.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Stage 1: Plateaus at ~60% on Optimal RAG; high miss rate (>50%); accuracy drops when increasing retrieved chunk count (contamination).  
  - Stage 2 (KG-RAG): KG construction fails to scale; subgraph retrieval too slow/narrow; no improvement on cross-document synthesis.  
  - Stage 3 (Pretraining): Catastrophic forgetting; no improvement if training data misaligned; prohibitive compute cost.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Replicate Optimal RAG: Using the 2,982-segment corpus and CombustionQA, run top-5 FAISS/BGE-M3 retrieval; confirm ~58.24% ceiling and 56.3% miss rate.  
  2. Error stratification analysis: For all “Miss” cases, categorize why correct segment wasn’t top-5; for “Hit” cases, analyze if wrong answer contradicted by other retrieved chunks.  
  3. Prototype KG-RAG retrieval: On a subset of CombustionQA, manually extract entities/relations; test if KG-based retrieval increases hit rate for “Miss” cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How much do Stages 2 (knowledge-graph-enhanced RAG) and 3 (continued pretraining) improve accuracy over naive RAG, and which is more cost-effective?  
  Basis: The paper states Stage 1 hits a ceiling and requires Stages 2 and 3, but does not validate or compare them.

- **Open Question 2**: What explains the remaining ~29 percentage-point gap between Optimal RAG (58.24%) and Theoretical Upper Bound (87.3%) after eliminating retrieval misses?  
  Basis: Even on “Hit” cases, accuracy is ~70%, still 17 p.p. below limit; attributed to context contamination but not dissected.

- **Open Question 3**: How well does the framework handle domain knowledge expressed in non-textual forms (equations, CFD code, tables) given CombustionQA is text-only?  
  Basis: The knowledge base includes CFD code and multimodal data, but benchmark only evaluates textual QA; multimodal comprehension untested.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility blocked by unreleased assets: CombustionQA dataset and the 2,982 Optimal RAG source segments are not public.
- Unknown model identities, inference settings, and prompt templates prevent exact replication.
- Generalization uncertain: benchmark size (436 questions) may not represent full domain; knowledge base coverage unverified for recent/proprietary work.
