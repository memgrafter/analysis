# PQuantML: A Tool for End-to-End Hardware-aware Model Compression
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26595
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26595
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves accuracy comparable to QKeras (~76%) while reducing FPGA LUT usage by up to 29% and latency by 55% through unified pruning and quantization.

## Executive Summary
PQuantML is an open-source library that simplifies the compression of deep learning models for deployment on FPGAs under strict latency constraints, such as those in high-energy physics triggers. By unifying pruning (structured and unstructured) and fixed-point quantization—including High-Granularity Quantization (HGQ)—into a single, configurable training workflow, it enables hardware-aware trade-offs between accuracy, compression, and resource usage. Evaluated on jet substructure classification tasks, PQuantML matches the accuracy of existing quantization tools while significantly lowering hardware resource consumption, demonstrating its effectiveness for real-time embedded AI applications.

## Method Summary
PQuantML provides a unified, YAML-configurable interface for applying pruning and quantization, either jointly or separately, during model training. The library abstracts compression strategies via declarative data models and supports both direct layer definitions and automatic layer replacement to inject compression operators into standard architectures. Hardware-aware metrics like Effective Bit Operations (EBOPs) are incorporated into the loss function to guide models toward FPGA-friendly implementations. The end-to-end workflow consists of defining a model, specifying compression parameters in a YAML config, and invoking the training loop, which automatically handles pre-training, training, and fine-tuning stages. The resulting model can be exported to HLS via hls4ml for synthesis on target FPGAs.

## Key Results
- Achieves ~76% accuracy on jet substructure classification, comparable to QKeras.
- DST pruning with per-tensor quantization reduces LUT usage by 29% and latency by 55% relative to QKeras.
- PQuantML’s HGQ implementation yields performance identical to native HGQ training, confirming its reliability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PQuantML simplifies end-to-end model compression by unifying pruning and quantization into a single, configurable training workflow.
- **Mechanism:** It abstracts compression strategies via YAML-based configuration objects that define pruning methods, quantization parameters, and training stages. The library orchestrates multi-round training and supports both direct layer definitions and automatic layer replacement to inject compression operators into standard architectures.
- **Core assumption:** Joint optimization of pruning and quantization within one framework yields better hardware-aware trade-offs than sequential or separate toolchains.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "simplifies training of compressed models by providing a unified interface to apply pruning and quantization, either jointly or individually."
  - [section 3] "The library is organized into three main components: the core, data models, and pruning methods... declarative approach allows users to specify... compression strategies."
  - Corpus signals indicate related tools like QKeras and HGQ focus on quantization only, highlighting PQuantML's integrated approach as a gap filler.
- **Break condition:** If configuration complexity overwhelms users or integration with downstream tools (e.g., hls4ml) fails, the unified workflow may increase rather than reduce engineering effort.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** PQuantML embeds hardware-awareness during training via metrics like Effective Bit Operations (EBOPs) and fine-grained quantization to ensure deployed models meet FPGA constraints.
- **Mechanism:** EBOPs estimates hardware resource consumption (LUTs, DSPs) and is minimized as part of the loss function, particularly in HGQ mode. Pruning methods consider hardware-aware patterns (e.g., N:M sparsity), and quantization granularity (per-tensor, per-channel, per-weight) is chosen to align with FPGA arithmetic efficiency.
- **Core assumption:** Training with surrogates for hardware costs leads to models whose post-synthesis resource usage is predictable and within limits.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 2.3] "HGQ supports fine-grained precision control... designed to align with FPGA implementation requirements."
  - [section 5.1] "EBOPs may also be used together with fixed-point quantization to estimate resource usage on FPGA hardware."
  - [section 6.2] Empirical results show DST and PDP with per-tensor quantization reduce LUT usage by 29% and latency by 55% relative to QKeras.
  - Corpus includes papers on hardware-aware compression, confirming the importance of such metrics.
- **Break condition:** If EBOPs or other surrogates poorly correlate with actual FPGA place-and-route results for specific architectures, models may fail to meet latency or resource budgets.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** PQuantML's support for diverse pruning granularities and quantization modes enables flexible adaptation to varying hardware and accuracy requirements.
- **Mechanism:** It implements multiple pruning algorithms (DST, PDP, MDMM) with unstructured, structured, and N:M patterns, and offers quantization from layer-wise fixed-point to per-weight and High-Granularity Quantization (HGQ). Users configure granularity per layer or globally.
- **Core assumption:** No single granularity is optimal across all deployment scenarios; flexibility allows navigating the accuracy-resource Pareto frontier.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 5.2] "Table 1 summarizes all pruning methods supported... including unstructured, N:M, and structured."
  - [table 2] Results show DSTt (per-tensor) vs. DSTw (per-weight) yield different LUT/FF trade-offs; PDPc (per-channel) improves accuracy over PDPt.
  - Corpus trends toward mixed-precision and diverse sparsity patterns underscore the need for granularity options.
- **Break condition:** Misconfiguration of granularity (e.g., using unstructured pruning on FPGAs) can lead to inefficient hardware utilization or excessive accuracy loss.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Core library divided into `core/` (with `torch/` and `keras/` submodules for layers, activations, quantizers, training), `data_models/` (Pydantic config schemas), `pruning_methods/` (algorithms like DST, PDP), `configs/` (default YAMLs), `utils/`, and `examples/`. Key classes: `PQConv2d`, `PQDense`, `PQActivation`, `Quantizer`, `add_compression_layers`, `train_model`.

### Critical path
1. Model definition: instantiate PQuantML layers directly or apply `add_compression_layers` to a standard model.
2. Configuration: create a YAML config specifying pruning method, quantization granularity, training epochs, and hyperparameters.
3. Training: call `train_model` with the model, config, and custom epoch functions; this handles pre-training, training, fine-tuning stages automatically.
4. Deployment: convert trained model to HLS via `hls4ml` (bit-accurate if using PQuantML layers).

### Design tradeoffs
- Unified backend (PyTorch/TensorFlow) vs. feature parity (TensorFlow layer replacement limited).
- Compression aggressiveness (sparsity/bit-width) vs. accuracy.
- Pruning choice (e.g., DST for high sparsity vs. PDP for stability) vs. hardware pattern alignment.
- Quantization granularity (per-weight for max compression vs. per-tensor for simpler synthesis).

### Failure signatures
- After `hls4ml` conversion, resource reports exceed FPGA limits.
- Validation accuracy drops >2-3% from baseline.
- Training diverges with HGQ due to unbalanced `hgq_beta`/`hgq_gamma`.
- Hyperparameter optimization fails to improve objective.
- TensorFlow models with skip connections fail during layer replacement.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline establishment:** Train a standard model (e.g., HLF JSC network) without compression, record accuracy and hls4ml resource usage. Validate the end-to-end pipeline.
2. **Single-method compression:** Apply DST pruning with per-tensor quantization using default config. Compare resource reduction and accuracy drop to baseline; adjust `alpha` to tune sparsity.
3. **HGQ validation:** Train with `use_high_granularity_quantization: true` and vary `hgq_beta`. Compare accuracy and resource estimates to native HGQ results to confirm integration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Question 1
How to constrain weight magnitude growth during FITCompress pre-training to avoid inflated quantization bit-widths? (Sec. 7 identifies weight magnitude growth as a limitation; potential solution: apply regularization or high-bit-width quantization in pre-training; measure bit-width distribution on HLF.)

### Question 2
Can FITCompress be modified to allow users to specify a target EBOP budget directly instead of using BOP percentage? (Sec. 7 notes BOP estimation difficulty; evidence would be implementing an EBOP budget parameter and validating it on benchmarks.)

### Question 3
How can the TensorFlow frontend be enhanced to support layer replacement in non-sequential architectures with branching (e.g., residual connections, attention)? (Current TensorFlow only supports simple sequential networks; evidence would be a layer replacement mechanism for skip connections tested on ResNet.)

### Question 4
What is the impact of integrating knowledge distillation and low-rank factorization into PQuantML on the accuracy-compression trade-off for FPGA deployment? (Sec. 8 proposes adding these; evidence would be benchmarking with distillation/low-rank on HLF/PLF.)

## Limitations
- Exact model architectures for HLF/PLF benchmarks are not fully specified (layer sizes, activation placements).
- QKeras baseline configurations are not detailed, making direct comparison replication difficult.
- Some HGQ training hyperparameters (e.g., `hgq_beta` schedules) are partially described.
