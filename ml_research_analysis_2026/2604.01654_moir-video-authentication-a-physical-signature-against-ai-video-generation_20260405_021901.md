# Moiré Video Authentication: A Physical Signature Against AI Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2604.01654
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01654
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Real videos (n=87) exhibit mean absolute Pearson correlation |ρ|=0.87 (σ=0.14) between fringe phase and translational displacement, while AI-generated videos (n=92) from Veo, Grok, and LTX-2 show mean |ρ|=0.57 (σ=0.21). The separation yields an AUC of 0.8453, with optimal classification threshold at |ρ|≈0.927.

## Executive Summary
The proliferation of AI-generated video content threatens the trustworthiness of visual media. This paper introduces a passive, physics-based authentication method that reliably distinguishes authentic camera-captured videos from synthetic ones. The core innovation is a two-layer grating structure that produces Moiré fringes; the derived "Moiré motion invariant" mathematically couples fringe phase shifts with the grating's translational image displacement, independent of camera geometry and viewing distance. In real videos, the invariant holds with near-unity correlation (mean |ρ|=0.87), whereas AI video generators—trained on pixel statistics without explicit optical constraints—fail to preserve this coupling (mean |ρ|=0.57). A simple threshold on |ρ| achieves an AUC of 0.8453, offering a robust, hardware-rooted signature that is difficult for generative models to replicate.

## Method Summary
The system comprises a physical assembly (a lenticular front sheet and a printed rear grating with four ArUco markers) placed in the scene. During video capture, relative motion between the camera and the assembly generates Moiré fringes. The verification pipeline tracks the assembly via ArUco corners, warps the grating region to a canonical view, and extracts a one-dimensional intensity profile along the fringe orientation. The phase of the dominant spatial frequency is computed via FFT and accumulated over time, yielding a temporal phase signal Φ(t). Simultaneously, camera pose (rotation R and translation t) is estimated using a PnP solver on the ArUco markers. By re-projecting a known 3D centroid with a reference orientation and each frame's translation, the rotation component is cancelled to isolate the translational displacement Δu_trans along the fringe-sensitive axis. Finally, the absolute Pearson correlation between Φ(t) and Δu_trans(t) is measured over sliding 30-frame windows; the maximum absolute correlation across windows decides authenticity. The theoretical derivation shows that for authentic videos, |ρ|≈1, independent of unknowns like distance D.

## Key Results
- Real videos (n=87) achieve mean absolute correlation |ρ|=0.87 with standard deviation 0.14.
- AI-generated videos (n=92) from Veo, Grok, and LTX-2 yield mean |ρ|=0.57 (σ=0.21).
- Statistical testing confirms strong separation (Welch's t=11.6, p<10⁻²⁰, Cohen's d=1.71); classification AUC reaches 0.8453, with optimal threshold at |ρ|≈0.927.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**: Real videos obey the Moiré motion invariant with near-perfect linear correlation because the deterministic optics of the two-layer grating couple fringe phase shifts Δϕ directly to translational displacement Δu_trans, cancelling unknown parameters like distance D (Eq. 3). Generative models do not solve the underlying wave-optics equations and thus cannot enforce this precise frame-to-frame coupling. Evidence: [abstract] states the invariant; [section 3.2] predicts |ρ|≈1 for authentic videos; related physics-based methods (e.g., VeriLight [33]) also rely on physical laws. Break condition: If future generators incorporate full ray-tracing of the two-layer geometry, they could reproduce the invariant.

**Mechanism 2**: AI-generated videos yield significantly lower correlation because video diffusion/transformer architectures synthesize pixels based on statistical patterns without constraint by the optical equations linking camera motion to fringe displacement. Even when conditioned on a real first frame, accumulated errors in fringe position or shape violate the linear relationship. Evidence: [abstract] reports mean |ρ|=0.57; [section 5.3] shows this holds across Veo, Grok, and LTX-2; prior work (e.g., [44]) exploits physical inconsistencies. Break condition: Training with a differentiable physics simulator that penalizes invariant violation could enable models to satisfy it.

**Mechanism 3**: The signature is passive and robust because the invariant cancels viewing distance, grating periods, and focal length. Thus it remains valid across diverse real-world conditions (indoor/outdoor, 1–3 m) and resists removal unlike digital watermarks. The correlation measure is unaffected by mild video transformations as long as fringes are visible and motion exists. Evidence: [abstract] emphasizes independence; [section 3.2] shows cancellation of D; [section 5.1] validates across 29 scenes. Break condition: Severe compression or blurring may destroy fringes; absence of the assembly makes the method inapplicable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Moiré effect**: The beat pattern arising when two periodic gratings are overlaid with a slight mismatch. It is the foundational phenomenon that creates the fringes used for authentication. *Quick check*: "What is the beat period when two gratings with spatial periods p_f and p_r are overlaid with a slight mismatch?"
- **Pinhole camera model**: The simplified geometric camera model that projects 3D points onto a 2D image via a perspective projection. It is used to derive the invariant and to estimate camera pose for translation isolation. *Quick check*: "How does a pure translational motion of a pinhole camera affect the image coordinates of a stationary object at distance D?"
- **Pearson correlation coefficient**: A measure of linear correlation between two variables, ranging from -1 to 1. The authentication decision is based on the absolute Pearson correlation between phase and displacement signals. *Quick check*: "What does a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.0 indicate about the relationship between two variables?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Physical substrate (two-layer grating with ArUco markers) -> Input video (standard capture of assembly with relative motion) -> Tracking & extraction (ArUco detection, homography warp, fringe orientation detection, vertical alignment, 1D profile averaging) -> Phase extraction (1D FFT, track dominant frequency bin, phase unwrapping to cumulative Φ(t)) -> Pose estimation (PnP solver on marker correspondences yields rotation R_i and translation t_i per frame) -> Translation isolation (re-project 3D centroid with reference orientation using each frame's translation to cancel rotation; project onto fringe-sensitive axis) -> Correlation analysis (Pearson correlation between Φ(t) and Δu_trans(t) over 30-frame sliding windows) -> Decision (threshold on maximum |ρ|).

**Critical path**: Success requires (a) clear visibility and tracking of ArUco markers throughout the video; (b) sufficient relative motion to produce measurable fringe phase shifts; (c) accurate fringe extraction and stable phase tracking; (d) accurate pose estimation to isolate pure translation; (e) a high absolute correlation value.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Grating design*: Lenticular sheets provide high magnification but require precise alignment; printed gratings are simpler but less sensitive.
- *Marker choice*: ArUco markers ensure robust pose estimation but are visually obtrusive; natural feature tracking is less reliable.
- *Window size*: Larger correlation windows stabilize the signal but may smooth motion details; smaller windows are more noise-sensitive.
- *Threshold*: A high threshold (0.927) minimizes false positives but may reject noisy real videos; a lower threshold increases true positives at the cost of false positives.

**Failure signatures**:
- ArUco detection failure (markers out of view, blur, occlusion) breaks PnP pipeline.
- Grating region tracking drift due to non-planarity or lens distortion yields inaccurate homography.
- Weak fringe signal from poor lighting, reflections, or layer misalignment.
- Pose estimation error from insufficient markers, motion blur, or rolling shutter.
- Absence of relative motion (static scene) yields no phase change.
- AI-generated video that accidentally satisfies the invariant (unlikely now but possible with future ray-tracing).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline real video test**: Record a video with the physical assembly, moving the camera linearly horizontally. Verify that the pipeline yields |ρ|>0.9 across sliding windows.
2. **Physics rendering validation**: Render a synthetic video in Blender (Cycles) with known camera translation. Confirm the pipeline recovers |ρ|≈1.0.
3. **Adversarial I2V test**: Condition an image-to-video model (e.g., LTX-2) with a real first frame and a motion prompt. Verify the generated video produces |ρ|<0.7.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How effective will the Moiré authentication remain against future video generators that explicitly simulate the underlying optics via ray tracing?
- *Basis in paper*: The Limitations state: "Should future models incorporate full ray-tracing of the two-layer grating geometry, they could in principle reproduce the correct fringe-displacement coupling."
- *Why unresolved*: Current security relies on generators not modeling Moiré physics; accurate ray-tracing could yield near-real correlation values, potentially breaking the detector.
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Build a generator that correctly simulates the Moiré optical equations (with proper parallax) and test if its outputs achieve |ρ| comparable to real videos (e.g., >0.8). If so, the invariant is vulnerable.

**Open Question 2**: How can the system authenticate videos without relative camera-grating motion, which is currently required to generate a measurable phase signal?
- *Basis in paper*: Limitations note: "the method requires relative motion between the camera and the grating assembly to generate a measurable phase signal; authentication is not possible in the case where both remain static."
- *Why unresolved*: Without motion, no fringe phase shifts occur, so the correlation test cannot be performed; static recordings are unverifiable.
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Identify and validate alternative signatures extractable from static frames (e.g., spatial texture properties, spectral fingerprints) or combine with other static physical phenomena that do not require motion.

**Open Question 3**: What is the optimal multi-directional grating design to ensure strong correlation signals regardless of camera motion direction and under real-world degradations like compression and blur?
- *Basis in paper*: The Discussion (Sec. D.2) suggests multi-directional patterns to "strengthen the signature against degradation" and handle arbitrary motion, but no specific designs or evaluations are provided.
- *Why unresolved*: Trade-offs between number of grating orientations, spatial frequencies, hardware complexity, and robustness to environmental factors are unexplored; the current 1D design only works for motion along one axis.
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Fabricate and test grating assemblies with varying multi-axis configurations, measure correlation performance across diverse synthetic and real motions, and under compression/blur to determine the design that maximizes robustness and practicality.

**Open Question 4**: How can the grating assembly embed a unique, unforgeable identity (Moiré ID) to link the physical object to a specific person, preventing face-swap attacks that leave the Moiré region untouched?
- *Basis in paper*: Section 5.5 proposes "Mitigation 1: Personalized Moiré identity..." as a concept but leaves it for future work; no concrete encoding/verification scheme is developed.
- *Why unresolved*: The encoding method (e.g., unique spatial frequencies, non-uniform pitch patterns), extraction algorithm, and security against copying via imaging/printing are not specified or tested.
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Design a specific Moiré ID scheme (e.g., a spectral "fingerprint" via multi-band gratings), implement a verifier that reliably recovers the ID from video, and demonstrate resistance to forgery attempts under realistic attack conditions.

**Open Question 5**: What other deterministic optical phenomena besides Moiré can serve as physics-based video authentication signatures with comparable or better robustness against AI generation?
- *Basis in paper*: The Conclusion encourages "future exploration of other physics-based signatures" beyond Moiré, hinting at a broader research direction.
- *Why unresolved*: Only the Moiré effect has been validated; other phenomena (e.g., polarization, diffraction, chromatic effects) may offer different advantages (e.g., higher entropy, simpler hardware) but their theoretical invariants and practical discriminative power are unknown.
- *What evidence would resolve it*: For candidate phenomena, derive a correlation-like invariant, build a prototype hardware, extract signals from real and AI-generated videos, and measure separation (AUC) to assess viability; identify the most promising ones for further development.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility**: The AI-generated video dataset (92 videos) used in the paper is not publicly released, and generation parameters (prompts, seeds, model settings) are only partially specified. Additionally, several pipeline parameters (e.g., focal length factor α, RANSAC thresholds, CLAHE settings) are approximate, hindering exact replication.
- **Device & condition coverage**: Real videos were captured only with an iPhone 15 at 1080p, 60 fps. Performance on other cameras, codecs, and under conditions like blur, reflections, or non-planar assembly remains untested.
- **Marker dependency & adversarial risk**: The method relies on robust ArUco detection; failure due to occlusion, blur, or out-of-plane motion breaks pose estimation and thus authentication. Future generative models that incorporate accurate Moiré physics could potentially produce videos that satisfy the invariant, nullifying the detector.

## Confidence
- **Real videos obey the Moiré motion invariant**: High confidence (strong theoretical foundation and consistent empirical results across 87 real videos).
- **AI-generated videos violate the invariant**: Medium confidence (limited to three I2V models; possible cherry-picking; lack of raw video access).
- **Method is passive and robust to typical transformations**: Medium confidence (validated only at 1–3 m distances; compression and blur effects not fully evaluated; marker reliance not addressed).
