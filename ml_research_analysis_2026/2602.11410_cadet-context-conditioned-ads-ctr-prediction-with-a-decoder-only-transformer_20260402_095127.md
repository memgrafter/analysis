# CADET: Context-Conditioned Ads CTR Prediction With a Decoder-Only Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11410
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11410
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: 11.04% CTR lift vs. LiRank baseline in online A/B test at LinkedIn.

## Executive Summary
CADET introduces a decoder-only transformer for click-through rate (CTR) prediction in online advertising, specifically addressing the challenge of post-scoring contextual signals like ad position. Its core innovation is a context-conditioned decoding block with multi-tower prediction heads, which generates multiple CTR estimates for different potential rendering contexts within a single forward pass. Complementary techniques—self-gated attention for training stability, timestamp-based rotary positional encoding (RoPE) for temporal dynamics, and session masking for train-serve consistency—ensure robustness. Production optimizations, including tensor packing and a custom Flash Attention kernel, enable efficient scaling. Deployed at LinkedIn, CADET achieved an 11.04% CTR improvement over the existing LiRank system in an online A/B test.

## Method Summary
CADET processes user session sequences with a decoder-only transformer backbone. To incorporate context-dependent factors (e.g., ad position) that are unknown during training, it employs a context-conditioned decoding block. This block takes a context embedding (position, layout) and the session representation to produce context-specific features, which are then fed into multiple parallel prediction towers—one for each context—allowing a single forward pass to yield predictions for all considered contexts. Self-gated attention stabilizes deep transformer training by modulating attention logits. Timestamp-based RoPE injects relative temporal information based on interaction times, while session masking enforces causality during training, ensuring consistency with the serving environment where future interactions are invisible. On the systems side, tensor packing and a custom Flash Attention kernel reduce memory footprint and accelerate attention computations.

## Key Results
- Context-conditioned decoding block enables simultaneous multi-context prediction without multiple forward passes.
- Self-gated attention improves training stability for deep transformer models.
- Online A/B test at LinkedIn shows an 11.04% CTR lift versus the LiRank baseline.
- Production deployment demonstrates scalability via tensor packing and optimized Flash Attention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CADET resolves the chicken-and-egg problem of using post-scoring signals (like ad position) by decoupling the session representation from the context. The context-conditioned decoding block first generates a base representation from the session and then adapts it to each specific context at inference time, making position information available when the actual position is known. This eliminates the circular dependency that would arise if position were inferred during training. Self-gated attention stabilizes gradients in deep transformers by scaling attention scores based on their variance, preventing exploding or vanishing gradients. Timestamp RoPE provides a continuous, relative encoding of time that respects the chronological order of user actions, enhancing temporal generalization. Session masking ensures that the model only leverages past interactions during training, aligning perfectly with the serving scenario where future clicks are unavailable. Together, these mechanisms yield a robust, context-aware CTR model that scales to production workloads.

## Foundational Learning
- **Decoder-only Transformer**: Enables sequential modeling of user sessions with self-attention. *Why needed*: Captures complex patterns and dependencies within variable-length session histories. *Quick check*: Does it handle long sessions without quadratic complexity? (Yes, via Flash Attention)
- **Position Bias in Ads**: CTR varies significantly with ad placement (e.g., top vs. bottom). *Why needed*: Ignoring position leads to biased training and serving mismatch. *Quick check*: Are position features included and properly conditioned?
- **Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE)**: Injects relative positional information via rotation matrices. *Why needed*: Provides a differentiable, wavelength-decaying positional signal that generalizes to unseen sequence lengths. *Quick check*: How are timestamps incorporated? (Timestamp differences used to compute rotation angles)
- **Self-Gated Attention**: Attention mechanism with a gating projection to modulate the attention scores. *Why needed*: Stabilizes training in deep transformers by controlling the scale of attention weights. *Quick check*: Does gating reduce gradient explosion? (Reported to improve convergence)
- **Multi-Tower Prediction Heads**: Multiple parallel output layers each conditioned on a specific context. *Why needed*: Supports one-pass inference for multiple contexts (e.g., positions), reducing latency. *Quick check*: Are predictions for different positions generated without additional forward passes? (Yes)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input Features (user, ad, session) -> Token Embedding -> Decoder-only Transformer (self-gated attention, timestamp RoPE, session masking) -> Session Representation -> Context Embedding (position, layout) -> Context-Conditioned Decoding Block (cross-attention) -> Multi-Tower Prediction Heads -> CTR Scores.

**Critical Path**: During inference, the user’s session history passes through the transformer to produce a session representation. For each candidate context (e.g., ad position), the corresponding context embedding is combined with this representation in the decoding block to create a context-specific representation, which is then fed to the prediction tower to output a CTR probability. All context-specific predictions are computed in parallel within a single forward pass.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- *Multi-tower vs. single-tower*: Multi-tower enables parallel context-specific predictions but increases memory due to duplicated layers; however, towers are lightweight and share the heavy transformer backbone, making it efficient.
- *Self-gated attention*: Adds gating parameters and computation but yields more stable training, allowing deeper networks.
- *Custom Flash Attention kernel*: Requires significant engineering effort but reduces memory and speeds up attention for long sessions.
- *Session masking*: Simple to implement and crucial for train-serve consistency, but slightly reduces the effective context window during training.

**Failure Signatures**:
- High variance in CTR predictions across contexts indicates poor conditioning by the decoding block.
- Training instability (loss spikes) suggests self-gated attention may be incorrectly configured or omitted.
- Degradation on recent data indicates timestamp RoPE may not generalize well to future timestamps.
- Unexpected performance drops for new positions not seen during training reveal overfitting to a fixed context set.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. **Context Conditioning Validation**: Process a single session with multiple position embeddings and verify that predictions differ as expected (e.g., lower CTR for lower positions) and that all outputs are produced in one forward pass.
2. **Self-Gated Attention Ablation**: Replace self-gated attention with standard multi-head attention and compare convergence speed, training stability (loss variance), and validation AUC on a public CTR dataset.
3. **Timestamp RoPE Generalization**: Train on data from an early time period and test on a later period, with and without timestamp RoPE, to measure its impact on temporal generalization.

## Limitations
- No ablation studies isolating the contributions of the context-conditioned decoding block, self-gated attention, or timestamp RoPE.
- Limited dataset statistics (e.g., sparsity, session length distribution) are not disclosed, hindering reproducibility.
- Potential overestimation of the reported CTR lift due to unclear details of train-serve alignment in session masking.
- Reliance on proprietary LinkedIn data prevents external validation and comparison on public benchmarks.

## Confidence
- **High**: Online A/B test result (11.04% CTR lift vs. LiRank) – direct production metric.
- **Medium**: Efficacy of the context-conditioned decoding block – theoretically sound but not ablated; production optimizations (tensor packing, custom Flash Attention kernel) – plausible but not independently benchmarked.
- **Low**: Claim of fully resolving the "chicken-and-egg" problem with post-scoring signals – conceptual framing not formally defined or compared against alternative position bias handling methods.
