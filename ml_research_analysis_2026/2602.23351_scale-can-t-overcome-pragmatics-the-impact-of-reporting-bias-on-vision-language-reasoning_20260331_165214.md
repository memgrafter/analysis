# Scale Can't Overcome Pragmatics: The Impact of Reporting Bias on Vision-Language Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23351  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23351  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** Systematic omission of reasoning details (spatial, temporal, negation, counting) in image captions causes poor VLM performance; scale fails to close gaps, but intentional instruction during data collection significantly improves reasoning.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies *reporting bias*—the tendency of human annotators to omit tacit information such as spatial relations, temporal events, negations, and exact counts in image captions—as a fundamental cause of poor reasoning in vision-language models (VLMs). Analysis of large training corpora (LAION, LLaVA-1.5, Molmo) shows extremely low true occurrence rates for reasoning-related keywords (e.g., spatial prepositions estimated at 0.1% in LAION). Evaluations on curated benchmarks reveal stark performance gaps: contrastive VLMs like CLIP perform near random on negation (~11%) and poorly on spatial tasks (~27%), while generative VLMs also fall short of human ability. Scaling experiments demonstrate that increasing model size or data volume does not efficiently bridge these gaps; achieving human-level performance would require impractically large compute. Multilingual training does not alleviate the bias. However, a controlled user study shows that specific annotator instructions can dramatically increase the occurrence of reasoning keywords (e.g., counting from 6.9% to 39%). Finetuning a VLM on data collected with such instructions improves counting performance by 4.6 percentage points, proving that intentional curation—not scale—is the key to better VLM reasoning.

## Method Summary
The authors combine corpus analysis, model evaluation, scaling experiments, and a human annotation study to diagnose and address reporting bias. First, they quantify the prevalence of reasoning-related keywords (spatial prepositions, numerals, negation markers, temporal terms) in major vision-language training sets (LAION-2B, LLaVA-1.5, Molmo) by searching for keywords and manually estimating true positive rates. Next, they assess the impact of this bias by evaluating a range of VLMs (contrastive models like OpenCLIP and generative models like LLaVA) on four targeted benchmarks: spatial relations, counting, negation, and temporal reasoning. Scaling laws are investigated by training OpenCLIP models of varying sizes (ViT-B/32 to ViT-H/14) on different data volumes (80M to 2B image-text pairs) and tracking performance on the reasoning benchmarks. To test whether human annotators can be steered to produce richer data, a controlled study on Prolific instructs participants to caption 100 COCO images under four different instruction sets (COCO baseline, LLaVA, PixMo, and a custom set explicitly prompting for all four reasoning types). Finally, the authors finetune LLaVA-1.5-13B on a mixed dataset enriched for counting (39% prevalence) and measure gains on the counting benchmark.

## Key Results
- **Reporting bias is severe:** Estimated true occurrence of spatial reasoning keywords in LAION is ~0.1%; similar scarcity across all four reasoning types in multiple corpora.
- **VLMs perform poorly on reasoning:** CLIP scores near random on negation (~11%) and low on spatial (~27%); generative VLMs like LLaVA-1.5-13B achieve 61.7% spatial and only 33.4% negation, far below human levels.
- **Scaling does not help:** Increasing data size or model capacity yields negligible improvement on reasoning benchmarks; reaching human performance would require intractable compute.
- **Instructions mitigate bias:** Annotator instructions raise reasoning keyword occurrence (e.g., counting from 6.9% in COCO to 39% with custom prompts). Finetuning on such data boosts counting accuracy by 4.6 points (49.8% → 54.4%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Reporting bias—systematic omission of tacit reasoning information in image captions—leads to poor VLM performance on reasoning tasks.  
**Mechanism:** Humans adhere to Gricean maxims (conciseness, relevance), omitting "obvious" details like exact counts, negations, or spatial relations unless context demands them. Consequently, training corpora contain extremely low frequencies of reasoning-related tokens, providing insufficient supervision for VLMs to learn these relations; the loss is dominated by high‑frequency perceptual features.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Estimated true spatial occurrence in LAION: 0.1% (abstract, Table 1).  
- Low estimated true occurrence across LAION, LLaVA-1.5, and Molmo for all four reasoning types (section 3.2).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Simply scaling model parameters, training data size, or multilingual diversity cannot overcome the reasoning gaps caused by reporting bias.  
**Mechanism:** Scaling laws improve performance when the limiting factor is representational capacity; for reasoning, the limiting factor is the absence of training signal. The underlying human communicative behavior (reporting bias) remains unchanged regardless of scale, so larger models see more of the same biased distribution and do not emergently acquire missing skills.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Scaling experiments on OpenCLIP show flat or slowly descending loss curves on reasoning benchmarks (section 5.3, Figure 3).  
- Multilingual CLIP underperforms English‑only CLIP on reasoning tasks (section 5.4).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Intentional curation via specific annotator instructions can mitigate reporting bias and improve VLM reasoning.  
**Mechanism:** By making the implicit "Question Under Discussion" explicit in instructions (e.g., "include spatial relations between objects"), annotators are guided to produce captions that violate default Gricean maxims of quantity/relevance. This increases the frequency of reasoning‑related keywords in the dataset, and finetuning a VLM on such data provides a stronger learning signal for the targeted skill.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Controlled user study shows custom instructions raise counting occurrence from 6.9% to 39% (section 6.1, Table 3).  
- Finetuning LLaVA-1.5-13B on a dataset with 39% counting prevalence improves counting performance by 4.6 points (section 6.3, Table 4).

## Foundational Learning
- **Reporting Bias (Pragmatics)**  
  *Why needed:* It is the hypothesized root cause of poor VLM reasoning. Understanding that human communication omits "obvious" information is essential to diagnose the data problem.  
  *Quick check:* Why would a caption like "a photo of 37 people" be less likely than "at the game today!"?

- **Gricean Maxims**  
  *Why needed:* Provides the theoretical foundation for *why* reporting bias occurs (maxims of quantity, quality, relevance, manner). It predicts which information types are systematically omitted.  
  *Quick check:* Which Gricean maxim explains why "a cat and a dog" is preferred over "a cat left of a dog" in a neutral caption?

- **Keyword-Based Corpus Analysis & True Positive Estimation**  
  *Why needed:* The paper's core method to quantify the absence of reasoning in massive datasets. Searching for keywords is insufficient; one must estimate how many true positives exist because many hits are false positives (e.g., "right of way" is not a spatial reasoning instance).  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between "Occurrence" and "Estimated True Occurrence" in Table 1, and why is the latter more meaningful?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Corpus Analysis → Benchmark Evaluation → Scaling Experiments → Controlled Data Collection → Finetuning  
**Critical path:** Diagnose bias (corpus analysis) → Correlate with performance (benchmark eval) → Test scale ineffectiveness (scaling laws) → Test curation efficacy (user study + finetuning)  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Keyword selection* balances recall and precision; narrow keywords reduce false positives but risk missing valid expressions (addressed by manual true positive estimation).  
- *Benchmark contamination* is mitigated by templating questions (e.g., simplified CountBench) to avoid exact image‑text pair overlap.  
- *Finetuning scale* uses a small dataset (26K samples) as a proof‑of‑concept; full pre‑training‑scale curation is left for future work.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- High keyword occurrence but low true positive rate indicates annotator/LM misinterpretation.  
- Poor scaling law fits or negligible slope suggest no compute‑reasoning relationship.  
- Multilingual model underperformance on reasoning tasks hints at cross‑linguistic bias.  
- No improvement when finetuning baseline data (without increased keyword prevalence) would disprove the curation effect.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate corpus analysis for LAION‑2B on spatial reasoning: implement keyword search, manually verify 100 hits, compute estimated true occurrence.  
2. Evaluate OpenCLIP ViT‑B/32 on all four reasoning benchmarks; verify performance aligns with Table 2(a).  
3. Compare LLaVA‑1.5‑13B base vs. finetuned on its own instruction data vs. finetuned on the 39% counting dataset; reproduce the +4.6 pt counting gain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Automating gap identification** in large image‑text corpora (Future work 1).  
- **Synthesizing high‑quality data** to fill reasoning gaps without introducing artifacts (Future work 2).  
- **Comparing finetuning methods** (e.g., LoRA, different objectives) on augmented data (Future work 3).  
- **Eliciting natural reasoning‑rich captions** without explicit instructions, perhaps by identifying underlying communicative intents (Future work 4).

## Limitations
- **Corpus representativeness:** Exact LAION subset used for OpenCLIP training is unspecified; Molmo's audio‑to‑text pipeline may affect keyword frequencies.  
- **Benchmark validity & contamination:** Simplified benchmarks reduce but may not eliminate overlap; human performance baselines lack inter‑annotator agreement reporting.  
- **Curation scalability:** The finetuning experiment uses a small dataset (26K samples) and does not validate full pre‑training‑scale curation.
