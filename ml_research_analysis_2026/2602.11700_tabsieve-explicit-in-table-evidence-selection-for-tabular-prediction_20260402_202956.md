# TabSieve: Explicit In-Table Evidence Selection for Tabular Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11700
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11700
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Average gains of 2.92% in classification accuracy and 4.45% in regression NMAE over the second-best baseline across 75 classification and 52 regression tables.

## Executive Summary
TabSieve addresses the brittleness of tabular in-context learning by making evidence selection explicit. Given a table and query row, it first selects a small set of informative rows as evidence, then predicts the target conditioned solely on that selection. The system is trained via supervised fine-tuning on TabSieve-SFT-40K (40K trajectories synthesized from 331 tables) and reinforcement learning with TAB-GRPO—which jointly optimizes selection and prediction rewards and stabilizes mixed regression/classification training via dynamic task-advantage balancing. Extensive experiments on 127 tables demonstrate significant improvements over prior methods and robust attention focusing on selected evidence.

## Method Summary
TabSieve operates in two stages: evidence selection and conditional prediction. The selection stage produces a list of row indices that are most relevant to the query. The prediction stage then generates a reasoning trace and final answer using only the selected rows. Training combines supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on synthetic trajectories with reinforcement learning (RL). The RL component uses TAB-GRPO, which assigns separate rewards for format compliance, evidence quality (F1 against gold evidence), and prediction correctness. Advantages are computed group-relative and adjusted per classification/regression task using an exponential moving average to prevent regression objectives from dominating early updates. This joint optimization encourages both accurate selection and prediction while maintaining stability across mixed tasks.

## Key Results
- **Classification:** Average gain of 2.92% accuracy over the second-best baseline across 75 tables.
- **Regression:** Average gain of 4.45% NMAE over the second-best baseline across 52 tables.
- **Robustness:** Attention analysis shows increased focus on selected evidence rows (from 0.210 to 0.254 proportion), improving robustness to noisy context.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit evidence selection forces the model to concentrate attention on informative rows, reducing distraction from noisy context.
- **Mechanism:** The model first generates a `<select>` block listing row indices. This intermediate decision acts as a soft constraint, steering subsequent reasoning and attention layers toward those tokens during prediction.
- **Core assumption:** Attention allocation correlates with influence on the output; focusing attention on selected rows improves robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "explicitly selects a small set of informative rows as evidence...improves robustness to noisy context."
  - Section 2: Layer-wise attention analysis shows increased proportion on evidential rows (0.210→0.254) after inserting a `<select>` declaration.
  - Section 4.4: Figure 7 visualizes more concentrated attention on selected rows versus broad distribution without selection.
- **Break condition:** If the selection mechanism is poorly trained (e.g., random indices), the attention constraint becomes harmful, amplifying noise instead of filtering it.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint optimization of evidence selection and prediction correctness via separate rewards stabilizes and improves both sub-tasks.
- **Mechanism:** TAB-GRPO uses three reward components: format (`r_f`), evidence quality (`r_e` via F1 against gold evidence), and prediction correctness (`r_c`). Group-relative advantages encourage outputs that satisfy all criteria simultaneously.
- **Core assumption:** Separating rewards prevents the model from sacrificing selection quality to maximize answer accuracy, especially under noisy context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.3.1: Equations (1)-(6) define separate rewards and group-relative advantage computation.
  - Section 4.3: Ablation in Figure 5 shows removing the evidence reward degrades performance, confirming its role in strengthening context-filtering.
- **Break condition:** Misaligned reward scaling (e.g., `λ_e` too low) could cause the model to ignore selection quality, reverting to black-box prediction.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Dynamic task-advantage balancing prevents regression tasks from dominating early gradient updates during mixed-task training, enabling stable joint optimization.
- **Mechanism:** TAB-GRPO tracks EMA of mean absolute correctness advantage for classification (`m_cls`) and regression (`m_reg`). It rescales regression advantages by factor `η_t = clip(m_cls/(m_reg+ε), η_min, η_max)`, reducing their effective update magnitude.
- **Core assumption:** Regression correctness rewards initially exhibit larger normalized advantage magnitudes, creating an optimization imbalance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.3.2: Describes the early-stage observation and the EMA-based rescaling formula (Eqs. 7-9).
  - Section 4.3: Figure 5 shows classification improves significantly when replacing vanilla GRPO with TAB-GRPO (balancing), while regression remains stable.
- **Break condition:** If `η_min` is set too low, regression learning may be excessively suppressed, harming overall regression performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Encoder -> Selection Head -> Prediction Head -> Reward Aggregator -> Policy Optimizer

**Critical path:** Input → Selection generation → Prediction generation → Reward parsing → Advantage balancing → Policy update.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Selection budget vs. noise:** More selected rows provide more evidence but increase risk of including noise. The budget is implicitly derived from teacher synthesis.
- **Reward weighting:** `λ_c` (correctness) is highest (0.7) to prioritize answer accuracy; lowering `λ_e` risks degrading selection quality.
- **Balancing aggressiveness:** `η_min=0.8` caps suppression of regression; tighter clipping could destabilize regression learning.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Selection degradation:** Evidence reward (`r_e`) plateaus or drops → model ignores `<select>` or outputs arbitrary indices.
- **Reward hacking:** High format reward (`r_f`) but low `r_e`/`r_c` → syntactically valid but meaningless outputs.
- **Task imbalance:** Regression NMAE improves while classification accuracy stagnates or falls → `η_t` scaling insufficient or EMA decay (`β`) too low.

**First 3 experiments (for validation):**
1. Ablation - Evidence Reward: Train without `r_e` (w/o Evidence Reward in Figure 5). Expect degradation in both tasks, confirming selection’s role.
2. Ablation - Task Balancing: Replace TAB-GRPO with vanilla GRPO. Expect classification to lag behind regression in early epochs.
3. Attention Probing: Run inference with/without `<select>` in prompt (Section 2.1). Validate attention shift toward selected rows in final layers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling to larger models:** How does TabSieve scale to larger language model backbones beyond 8B parameters? The authors only explored 8B scale due to computational constraints; larger models may behave differently.
2. **Training corpus diversity:** To what extent does expanding the training corpus with more diverse tables improve generalization to unseen schemas and domains? The current SFT dataset uses only 331 tables; broader coverage is needed.
3. **Evidence selection heuristic:** Can evidence selection be learned without relying on embedding similarity as a proxy for ground truth evidence? The current pipeline uses cosine similarity of row embeddings, which may not align with human-intuitive or causal relevance.
4. **Alternative balancing methods:** Is the dynamic task-advantage balancing in TAB-GRPO the most effective approach for joint optimization of classification and regression? Simpler heuristics could be supplanted by more sophisticated multi-task RL balancing techniques.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic training data:** TabSieve-SFT-40K is auto-generated from only 331 tables, potentially limiting diversity and causing overfitting to synthetic patterns.
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluation covers 127 tables primarily from UCI/OpenML; performance on unseen schemas, high missing rates, or extreme cardinalities remains unknown.
- **Reward dependence:** Evidence reward (`r_e`) requires gold row indices during RL, which may not be available in practice, restricting applicability.

## Confidence
- **Explicit evidence selection improves robustness:** High (supported by attention analysis and ablations).
- **Joint optimization via TAB-GRPO stabilizes mixed-task training:** Medium (ablations show benefit, but sensitivity to reward weights and KL penalty not fully mapped).
- **Dynamic task-advantage balancing prevents regression dominance:** Medium (empirical improvement, but limited exploration of hyperparameters and break conditions).

## Next Checks
- **Cross-domain generalization:** Test TabSieve on external benchmarks (e.g., healthcare, finance tables) with different preprocessing and measure accuracy drop relative to in-domain.
- **Reward-free selection evaluation:** Train without `r_e` using only `r_c`, then measure selection F1 on a held-out set to assess whether selection emerges implicitly.
- **Efficiency profiling:** Compare inference latency and memory usage of TabSieve vs. standard LLM ICL across varying context lengths to quantify overhead of explicit selection.
