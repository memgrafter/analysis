# The Dual Role of Abstracting over the Irrelevant in Symbolic Explanations: Cognitive Effort vs. Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03467
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03467
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Clustering irrelevant details in symbolic explanations improves human transfer accuracy, while removing them reduces cognitive effort (answer time).

## Executive Summary
This study examines how two formal abstraction operations—clustering (grouping irrelevant features) and removal (mapping irrelevant features to truth)—affect human reasoning when interpreting symbolic AI explanations. Using Answer Set Programming (ASP) justifications across three biological domains (flowers, mushrooms, cacti), a between-subject experiment with 99 participants measured classification accuracy, response time, and confidence. Results show clustering significantly improved accuracy in transfer tasks, indicating better understanding, while removal significantly reduced answer time, indicating lower cognitive effort. Confidence ratings were unaffected. The findings suggest abstraction strategies can be tuned to optimize either understanding or efficiency in human-AI interaction.

## Method Summary
The study employed a between-subject design with four conditions: default (no abstraction), clustering, removal, and combined clustering+removal. Stimuli were binary classification problems derived from ASP programs encoding domain rules. For each domain, participants first learned from three positive examples accompanied by an explanation (a justification trace from the ASP solver `xclingo`), then classified six unseen test instances. Explanations were manipulated by applying the abstraction operations to χ-irrelevant atoms—features that never affect the classification outcome across all problem instances. Dependent variables were test accuracy, time per decision, and confidence ratings (0–100). Data were analyzed using ANOVAs and post-hoc tests.

## Key Results
- Clustering explanations significantly improved transfer accuracy compared to default (p=0.009) and cluster_removal (p=0.016) (F=2.72, p=0.049).
- Removal explanations (in cluster_removal) significantly reduced answer time compared to default and cluster (Welch F=6.41, p=0.001).
- No significant effect on confidence was found (F=1.58, p=0.19).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Clustering → Understanding
- **Claim:** Grouping irrelevant details into higher-level clusters improves human *understanding* as measured by transfer accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Clustering imposes an organizational structure on explanation content, reducing the need to track low-level distinctions that do not affect the outcome. This aligns with cognitive theories of chunking and schema formation, enabling learners to build a more coherent and generalizable mental model of the rule.
- **Core assumption:** Humans benefit from hierarchical information organization; the presented clusters create meaningful functional equivalences.
- **Evidence anchors:** Significant accuracy gain for *cluster* vs. *default* (T=2.75, p=0.009); *cluster* vs. *cluster_removal* (T=2.52, p=0.016).
- **Break condition:** If the base explanation is already very simple, or clusters do not represent functionally meaningful groupings, the accuracy benefit may not materialize. A significant negative interaction with simpler tasks would challenge this.

### Mechanism 2: Removal → Effort Reduction
- **Claim:** Eliminating irrelevant details (mapping to ⊤) reduces *cognitive effort* as measured by faster answer times.
- **Mechanism:** Removal shortens the explanation text by filtering out features constant across all instances. This directly reduces visual scanning and working memory load during parsing, speeding up integration of decisive information.
- **Core assumption:** Cognitive load is proportional to extraneous information volume; redundant details compete for attention and must be suppressed.
- **Evidence anchors:** *cluster_removal* was fastest; significant effects for *cluster_removal* vs. *cluster* (T=3.25, p=0.017) and vs. *default* (T=−3.23, p=0.018). The speed advantage is primarily driven by the removal component.
- **Break condition:** If removed details were needed for verification (e.g., to confirm absence), or if base task complexity is low, removal may not improve time or could cause confusion. Non-significant time effects in a high-load condition would contradict.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy of Clustering + Removal
- **Claim:** Combining clustering and removal maximizes effort reduction without sacrificing understanding gains.
- **Mechanism:** Removal first filters non-variant features, decreasing text volume. Clustering then organizes remaining variant features into meaningful groups. The combined abstraction yields a concise yet structurally informative explanation.
- **Core assumption:** Cognitive effort and understanding are orthogonal dimensions; operations target these independently and can be composed.
- **Evidence anchors:** *cluster_removal* matched *cluster* in accuracy (no significant difference) but was faster than both *cluster* and *default*. The marginal time difference between *cluster_removal* and *cluster* (p=0.017) suggests additive rather than synergistic effects.
- **Break condition:** If removal eliminates features necessary for interpreting clusters, combined accuracy could drop below *cluster*-only. A significant Accuracy(*cluster* > *cluster_removal*) would break this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Answer Set Programming (ASP) & Logical Explanations**
  - **Why needed:** The study’s stimuli and explanations are derived from ASP programs; explanations are justification traces of why an atom appears in an answer set.
  - **Quick check:** Given ASP rule `p(X) :- q(X), not r(X).` and instance with `q(a)` true, `r(a)` false, what atoms appear in the default justification for `p(a)`?
- **Concept: Formal Abstractions (Removal vs. Clustering)**
  - **Why needed:** These are the independent variables; removal maps irrelevant atoms to ⊤, clustering maps multiple irrelevant atoms to a single representative.
  - **Quick check:** If features `A` and `B` are always true in the training set, which abstraction likely applies, and what does the abstracted explanation show?
- **Concept: Cognitive Measures (Accuracy, Answer Time, Confidence)**
  - **Why needed:** These operationalize the hypothesis: accuracy indicates understanding/transfer; time indicates effort; confidence is subjective.
  - **Quick check:** In a transfer task, fast but inaccurate responses suggest what about the explanation’s effect on effort vs. understanding?
- **Concept: χ‑Irrelevance**
  - **Why needed:** The formal basis for identifying irrelevant atoms; an atom is χ‑irrelevant if its truth value never influences the classification across the defined problem instance set χ.
  - **Quick check:** If an attribute value never changes across all training and test instances, is it χ‑irrelevant? What if it changes but never flips the classification label?
- **Concept: Transfer Task**
  - **Why needed:** The dependent variable "understanding" is measured by accuracy on unseen test instances, requiring generalization beyond learning examples.
  - **Quick check:** Why is accuracy on novel instances a better measure of understanding than accuracy on the same learning instances?

## Architecture Onboarding
ASP Program Library -> Problem Instance Set (χ) -> Irrelevance Detector -> Explanation Generator (xclingo) -> Experiment Harness -> Analysis Pipeline

**Critical path:** Define domain ASP programs & χ → Compute χ-irrelevant atoms & define mappings (removal/clustering) → Generate explanation corpora for learning instances → Build/deploy online experiment → Recruit participants & collect data → Clean data (attention checks, outliers) → Run statistical tests (ANOVA/Welch) → Interpret results.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Abstraction definition:** Relaxed χ-irrelevance may abstract decisive features, harming understanding; stricter definition may yield fewer abstractable atoms, reducing observable effects.
- **Explanation presentation:** Textual tree format (xclingo) may interact with abstraction type (e.g., clusters may be less salient in text vs. graphics).
- **Task complexity:** Effects may only appear at intermediate complexity; too simple masks benefits, too complex masks them.

**Failure signatures:**
- No significant effects on any DV → Task too easy/hard or abstractions miscomputed (check m mappings).
- Removal harms accuracy → χ-irrelevance computation likely wrong; removed feature was sometimes discriminative.
- Clustering slows responses → Clusters may be semantically confusing or ambiguous.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate the cognitive effect with a new sample (N>99) using identical ASP programs and χ sets to confirm the double dissociation.
2. Dose‑response: Vary degree of abstraction (e.g., cluster 2 vs. 4 vs. all irrelevant features) to map the accuracy/time trade‑off curve.
3. Generalize to new domains (e.g., legal or financial rules) to test if effects hold outside biology.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** At what level of default explanation complexity does removal abstraction begin to significantly improve accuracy, rather than just reducing cognitive effort?
- **Basis:** Conclusion states they plan to explore how default explanation complexity interacts with removal’s accuracy effect.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiment used a single fixed complexity; it is unknown if removal’s non‑significant accuracy effect is due to the default being below a threshold or an inherent limitation.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the formal notion of χ‑irrelevance for removal primarily capture categorical specificity (removing common features) rather than abstracting generalizable concepts, and how does this distinction influence its cognitive effects?
- **Basis:** Discussion hypothesizes removal may align with "specificity" (removing ubiquitous features) and that this might not be clear to participants.
- **Why unresolved:** The operationalization of removal was fixed; the psychological impact of removing "common" vs. "abstractable" details is not disentangled.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does symbolic abstraction fail to increase participants’ subjective confidence despite improving objective performance (accuracy/speed)?
- **Basis:** Results show no confidence effect; Discussion notes this dissociation.
- **Why unresolved:** The reason for the null confidence effect is not investigated; possible explanations include: confidence driven by unrelated factors, abstraction making explanations less verifiable, or poor calibration.

## Limitations
- Unknown stimulus specifics: Exact ASP programs for flowers/mushrooms and full instance definitions are unreported, blocking full reconstruction of χ‑irrelevant mappings.
- Generalizability concerns: Effects are tested only on three Midwestern US biology domains with a specific binary classification task; transfer to other domains/tasks is unknown.
- Internal validity threats: Uncontrolled factors like explanation presentation format (textual tree) and participant pool (likely students) may confound abstraction effects.

## Confidence
- **Clustering → Improved Understanding (Accuracy):** Medium (effect significant but marginal, p=0.049; replication needed).
- **Removal → Reduced Cognitive Effort (Time):** High (strong, significant effect, p=0.001; mechanism parsimonious).
- **Synergy of Cluster+Removal:** Low (evidence indirect; could be additive rather than synergistic).

## Next Checks
1. **Stimulus Reconstruction & Abstraction Audit:** Re-derive complete ASP programs and instance sets from domain descriptions; recompute χ‑irrelevant atoms and verify abstractions preserve all answer sets for training and test instances.
2. **Statistical Reanalysis with Reported Data:** Using only summary statistics (means, F/Welch, p‑values), independently recompute ANOVAs/Welch tests, post‑hoc comparisons, and effect sizes to validate the primary analysis.
3. **Break Condition Test - Base Task Simplicity:** Run a follow‑up experiment with a significantly simpler base rule (e.g., 2–3 relevant attributes) to test whether clustering’s accuracy gain disappears while removal’s time reduction persists.
