# VisionCoach: Reinforcing Grounded Video Reasoning via Visual-Perception Prompting
## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.14659 | URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14659 | References: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art video reasoning with visual prompting: +27.6% V-STAR VQA accuracy over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (61.1% vs 33.5%), plus large gains in mAM/mLGM.

## Executive Summary
VisionCoach tackles poor spatio-temporal grounding in video reasoning by introducing an input-adaptive reinforcement learning framework that leverages visual prompting during training. The system uses a Visual Prompt Selector to choose question-relevant prompts (e.g., red circle, darken) for hard samples, and a Spatio-Temporal Reasoner that is trained with object-aware rewards to enforce identity consistency and precise localization. Self-distillation then internalizes these improvements, enabling efficient inference without prompts. Extensive evaluations show substantial gains across multiple benchmarks, notably V-STAR, VideoMME, and Charades-STA.

## Method Summary
VisionCoach comprises two main components: (1) a Visual Prompt Selector (VP-SELECTOR), a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B model that predicts the optimal visual prompt from a predefined pool for a given video-question pair, trained on proxy-generated data where prompts are labeled by their ability to improve answer accuracy and spatial grounding of larger models; (2) a Spatio-Temporal Reasoner (ST-REASONER), initialized via SFT on a mixture of grounding datasets and then fine-tuned with reinforcement learning using the GSPO algorithm. During RL, samples with low initial reward are deemed hard and receive the VP-SELECTOR’s prompt; new rollouts under the prompt that outperform the baseline are distilled into the model via next-token prediction. After RL, the final policy operates on raw videos at inference with a single forward pass.

## Key Results
- V-STAR: VQA accuracy 61.1% (+27.6 vs Qwen2.5-VL-7B), mAM 34.3 (+15.0), mLGM 47.5 (+25.1).
- Strong performance on general video QA: VideoMME 63.3, WorldSense 43.8, VideoMMMU 54.4, PerceptionTest 70.3.
- Charades-STA temporal grounding: R@0.5 45.8.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- Visual prompting forces the model to focus on question-relevant spatio-temporal evidence, mitigating the inherent bias of VLMs to ignore fine-grained details. The input-adaptive selector ensures that only samples that benefit from extra guidance are prompted, preserving efficiency.
- Object-aware rewards (rspa) explicitly penalize inconsistent object identity and imprecise bounding boxes, directly optimizing for grounding quality rather than just answer correctness.
- Self-distillation transfers the knowledge gained under prompted conditions to a model that must reason without prompts, effectively internalizing improved attention patterns and spatial reasoning capabilities.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning from Policy Optimization (GSPO)**: Used to optimize the reasoner beyond supervised learning; quick check: verify that reward components increase during training and that KL divergence stays bounded.
- **Visual Prompting**: Applying transformations (e.g., red circle, darken) to frames to highlight regions; quick check: visualize prompted frames to ensure prompts are correctly applied and that they focus on annotated objects.
- **Self-Distillation**: Using the model’s own high-reward rollouts as supervision; quick check: compare cross-entropy loss on distilled samples before/after to confirm learning signal is meaningful.
- **Object-Aware Rewards**: Combines answer accuracy, format compliance, temporal alignment, and spatial IoU with identity matching; quick check: manually compute components for a few samples to validate formulas.
- **Input-Adaptive Training**: Selectively applying prompting only to hard samples; quick check: monitor the fraction of samples marked hard and the distribution of selected prompts.
- **Proxy Reasoners**: Large models (Gemini, GPT-4o) used to label training data for the selector; quick check: assess agreement among proxies on prompt selection and answer quality.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: VP-SELECTOR -> ST-REASONER (SFT) -> RL loop (with VP-SELECTOR guidance on hard samples) -> Self-Distillation -> Final Model
- **Critical Path**: Cold-start SFT on STGR-Co T-30k -> RL on STGR-RL-36k (with adaptive prompting) -> Distillation of top rollouts -> Deployment.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: 
  - Separate selector vs. end-to-end: reduces RL complexity but requires high-quality proxy data.
  - Fixed prompt pool: limits expressiveness but simplifies selection; learned prompts could generalize better.
  - Reward composition: depends on GT annotations; may not generalize to domains without such supervision.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - RL rewards plateau or decrease: check reward parsing or hard sample selection logic.
  - VP-SELECTOR always predicts "raw": indicates poor proxy labeling or insufficient capacity.
  - Spatial metrics (mAM/mLGM) do not improve: likely rspa component not properly implemented or mismatched object matching.
- **First Three Experiments**:
  1. Train VP-SELECTOR on a small subset (e.g., 500 samples) and evaluate accuracy; verify that the selector prefers non-raw prompts for hard questions.
  2. Run SFT of ST-REASONER on a small STGR-Co T-30k split; confirm that it generates reasoning with required `<obj><box><t>` tags.
  3. Execute RL on a few STGR-RL-36k samples for two iterations; log reward components and check that hard samples indeed trigger VP prompting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can VisionCoach be extended to video reasoning tasks lacking spatio-temporal grounding annotations (bounding boxes, timestamps)? Basis: The framework relies on training signals derived from grounding annotations; scaling to annotation-scarce domains may require additional strategies.
- **Open Question 2**: Can learned generative visual prompts improve upon the fixed candidate pool (e.g., circle, darken, frame numbering)? Basis: The prompt candidate pool is predefined and relatively simple; learned prompt generation may further improve the training signal.
- **Open Question 3**: Through what internal mechanism does self-distillation enable the model to replicate the benefits of visual prompting at inference without prompts? Basis: Self-distillation lets the model "internalize the grounding improvements" but the exact mechanism (e.g., attention changes, feature representations) remains unclear.

## Limitations
- Unknown temporal tolerance τ and Gaussian σ for temporal reward; assumed 0.5s may differ.
- Key frame selection criteria for prompting frames unspecified; depends on dataset annotations.
- Object matching thresholds (substring/Jaccard) are vague, affecting reward and selector training.
- Core datasets STGR-Co T-30k and STGR-RL-36k may be inaccessible, hindering exact replication.
- Hard threshold k (mean initial reward) may be static but not explicitly described.

## Confidence
- VP-SELECTOR prompt prediction: Medium
- RL with object-aware rewards: Medium
- Self-distillation removes prompt dependency: Low
- Reported SOTA results across benchmarks: Low-Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt Distribution Check**: Train VP-SELECTOR on the provided data and verify that the predicted prompt distribution on a held-out set aligns with Figure 7 (e.g., raw prompts ≤50%, others non-trivial). Misalignment indicates issues in proxy simulation or reward.
2. **Spatial Reward Ablation**: Remove rspa (set to 0) and re-run RL on a small subset. Expect a substantial drop (>5 points) in spatial grounding metrics (mAM/mLGM) on V-STAR, confirming the reward's contribution.
3. **Self-Distillation Candidate Selection**: Compare the default top-2 racc selection with top-2 by (racc + rspa). Improvement with the latter would validate rollout robustness and mitigate reward hacking.
