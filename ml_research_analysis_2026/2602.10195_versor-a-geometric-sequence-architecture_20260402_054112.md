# Versor: A Geometric Sequence Architecture

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10195  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10195  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Native SE(3)-equivariant sequence model via Conformal Geometric Algebra, achieving orders-of-magnitude parameter efficiency, robustness, and hardware speedups on geometric, dynamic, vision, and language tasks.

## Executive Summary
Versor reimagines sequence modeling by embedding states in Conformal Geometric Algebra (Cl₄,₁) and evolving them via geometric rotations. This yields native SE(3)-equivariance without explicit structural encoding, addressing the Euclidean bottleneck. Its core mechanisms are Geometric Product Attention (GPA), which decomposes interactions into proximity and orientational components, and the Recursive Rotor Accumulator (RRA), which compresses temporal history into a single rotor for O(L) scaling. Versor outperforms Transformers and geometric baselines across chaotic dynamics, topological reasoning, image classification, and language modeling, while using far fewer parameters and demonstrating exceptional out-of-distribution robustness and energy conservation.

## Method Summary
Versor embeds inputs as multivectors in Cl₄,₁ (optionally via conformal lifting for Euclidean points) and processes them using either GPA or RRA. GPA computes attention scores by decomposing the geometric product of queries and keys into a scalar (linearized distance) and a bivector norm (relative orientation), then applies softmax over the combined score. RRA maintains a rotor state Ψ ∈ Spin(4,1) that is updated each step by a locally predicted rotor ΔR via the Cayley map, followed by manifold normalization. This recursive update compresses the entire history into a constant-memory state. Training uses standard optimizers (AdamW) with manifold normalization preventing numerical drift. The architecture achieves SE(3)-equivariance because all operations (geometric product, rotor multiplication) are isometries of the conformal model.

## Key Results
- 200× fewer parameters than Transformers with superior accuracy on N-body dynamics and CIFAR-10.
- Zero-shot scale generalization MCC 0.993 vs. Transformer 0.070 on the Broken Snake task.
- Out-of-distribution robustness: −63.9% error change on 10× mass vs. +1933.7% for Transformers.
- 2.9× better energy conservation in chaotic rollouts versus neural ODE baselines.
- Hardware-optimized Clifford kernels achieve 1.05 ms per step and >100× cumulative speedup.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Native SE(3)-Equivariance via Conformal Lifting
- **Claim:** Embedding states in Cl₄,₁ and evolving them via rotors achieves native SE(3)-equivariance without explicit structural encoding.
- **Mechanism:** Euclidean points x are lifted to 5D null vectors X = x + ½x²e∞ + eₒ. The inner product X·Y = –½‖x–y‖² linearizes distance. Rotors R ∈ Spin(4,1) (even normalized multivectors) encode SE(3) transformations and act via sandwich product X' = R X ˜R, guaranteeing isometry.
- **Core assumption:** Physical symmetries (rotations, translations) are fundamental and should be algebraically enforced rather than learned from data.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2 confirm conformal lifting linearizes distance; Section 4.2 defines the RRA update ΔR_t Ψ_t with manifold normalization; indirect corroboration from geometric deep learning (GATr) with different temporal modeling.
- **Break condition:** The 32-dimensional Cl₄,₁ overhead is unnecessary for tasks lacking geometric structure (e.g., pure symbolic reasoning), where Euclidean models may be more efficient.

### Mechanism 2: Geometric Product Attention (GPA)
- **Claim:** GPA decomposes the geometric product Q ˜K into interpretable scalar (proximity) and bivector (orientational torque) components, capturing both distance and angular relationships.
- **Mechanism:** The geometric product S = Q ˜K is grade-decomposed: ⟨S⟩₀ (scalar ≈ –½ distance²) and ⟨S⟩₂ (bivector = oriented area). Attention scores combine: α_ij = softmax(⟨Q_i ˜K_j⟩₀ + γ·‖⟨Q_i ˜K_j⟩₂‖/√d_in).
- **Core assumption:** Physical interactions depend on both proximity and relative orientation; scalar-only dot-product attention is insufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.1 define the score combination; Section 6.1.1 Figure 2 shows scalar matching distance laws and bivector highlighting torque; no direct external evidence—neighboring papers use scalar-only attention.
- **Break condition:** The bivector term adds computational cost (≈32 ops per pair vs. 1 for dot product). On tasks where orientation is irrelevant, γ may downweight it, but overhead remains. Higher-grade components (grade-4) are omitted, potentially missing complex interactions.

### Mechanism 3: Recursive Rotor Accumulator (RRA)
- **Claim:** RRA compresses sequence history into a single rotor state Ψ_t ∈ Spin(4,1), achieving O(L) time and O(1) memory while maintaining stability via manifold constraints.
- **Mechanism:** Local rotor ΔR_t is predicted from input via bivector projection B_t and Cayley map ΔR_t = (2 – B_t)(2 + B_t)⁻¹. State update: Ψ_{t+1} = ΔR_t Ψ_t / √⟨Ψ_t ˜Ψ_t⟩₀ (normalization enforces Ψ ˜Ψ = 1). This recursive product accumulates transformations without storing past states.
- **Core assumption:** Dynamical system history can be summarized by a composite rotation; gradient flow is preserved because rotor multiplication is orthogonal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions O(L) scaling; Section 4.2 details Algorithm 2; Section 6.1.2 Figure 3 shows linear latency; Appendix B.2 proves Lyapunov stability (λ=0) due to norm preservation; similar efficiency goals in RNN variants (RWKV) but without geometric constraints.
- **Break condition:** The rotor compression is lossy; for sequences requiring fine-grained historical details (e.g., exact memory of past tokens), RRA alone may underperform attention. Cayley map approximation error grows with ‖B_t‖, though initialization and small learning rates mitigate this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Clifford Algebra (Cl₄,₁)**
  - *Why needed:* All operations (geometric product, grade extraction) rely on its Cayley table and graded structure.
  - *Quick check:* What is the result of e₁e₂ + e₂e₁ in Cl₄,₁? How many basis blades exist? (Answer: anti-commute for distinct basis vectors; 2⁵=32 blades.)

- **Concept: Rotors and the Spin Group**
  - *Why needed:* RRA updates and state evolution are defined on Spin(4,1). Understanding rotor multiplication and normalization is essential for stability.
  - *Quick check:* Given a bivector B, how do you compute a rotor via Cayley map? Why must R ˜R = 1? (Answer: R = (2–B)(2+B)⁻¹; ensures isometry.)

- **Concept: Conformal Lifting**
  - *Why needed:* GPA’s scalar component relies on the linearized distance from conformal embedding.
  - *Quick check:* Lift points x=(1,0,0) and y=(0,1,0) to CGA. Compute X·Y and show it gives –½‖x–y‖².

- **Concept: Grade Decomposition**
  - *Why needed:* GPA explicitly uses scalar (⟨·⟩₀) and bivector (⟨·⟩₂) parts.
  - *Quick check:* For A = a₀ + a₁₂e₁e₂ + ..., what is ⟨A⟩₂? (Answer: sum of all bivector components.)

- **Concept: Manifold Normalization**
  - *Why needed:* Prevents numerical drift off the Spin manifold during RRA recurrence.
  - *Quick check:* If Ψ drifts so ⟨Ψ ˜Ψ⟩₀ ≠ 1, why does dividing by its square root restore manifold membership? (Answer: rotor norm must be 1 for isometry.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input -> Linear projection to 32-coeff multivector -> GPA (Q,K,V) with geometric product and grade extraction -> softmax -> weighted sum of V -> output; OR Input -> Linear to bivector B -> Cayley map to ΔR -> rotor multiply ΔR Ψ -> manifold normalize -> repeated for recurrence (RRA).
- **Critical path:** GPA path: Input → Linear (Q,K,V) → Geometric product (bit-masked kernel) → Grade extraction → Softmax → ∑ α_ij V_j → Output. RRA path: Input → Linear (to bivector) → Cayley map → Rotor multiply → Manifold normalize → (repeat). GPA is O(L²) spatially; RRA is O(L) temporally.
- **Design tradeoffs:** GPA vs. RRA: GPA for full relational reasoning (static sets); RRA for long sequential dynamics (e.g., N-body rollouts). Hybrids trade speed vs. global context. Channel count K increases capacity but linearly increases compute. Bivector projection (10D from 32D) loses information but is necessary for rotor generation. Normalization frequency: every step is stable but costly; less frequent risks drift.
- **Failure signatures:** NaNs during rollout: likely missing manifold normalization or Cayley denominator near zero. Poor scale generalization: conformal lifting misapplied; scalar part should be distance-based. High variance across seeds: poor rotor initialization or optimization on manifold. Quadratic latency: GPA with long sequences; switch to RRA or sparse attention.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Conformal distance linearity: generate random 3D points, lift to CGA, verify X·Y = –½‖x–y‖². 2. Rotor stability roll: initialize Ψ₀=1, apply 10k random small bivectors B_t ~ N(0,0.01), track |⟨Ψ_t ˜Ψ_t⟩₀ – 1| ≈ 0. 3. GPA decomposition sanity: create two points with known distance/orientation; vary positions: scalar decreases with distance; bivector varies with orientation change; verify gradients flow through both terms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can optimization directly on the Lie algebra spin(4,1) using the exponential map eliminate the need for explicit manifold normalization without compromising numerical stability or gradient flow? *Basis:* Explicitly identified as a limitation; exponential map noted as "prohibitively" expensive (O(D³)) and unproven at scale. *Why unresolved:* Current retraction-based normalization is an approximation; efficiency and stability of exponential map in large models untested. *What evidence would resolve:* Comparison of exponential vs. Cayley maps on N-body tasks at long horizons (L>10,000) showing no increase in NaNs or gradient explosion with reduced overhead.
- **Open Question 2:** How can Versor be extended to strictly enforce physical energy conservation (Hamiltonian mechanics) beyond the hybrid Ham-Versor, while retaining its geometric inductive biases and parameter efficiency? *Basis:* Explicitly noted; base Versor shows 133% energy drift vs. HNN's 10.7%. Proposed Hamiltonian-Versor hybrid uses external energy parameterization, not integrated update. *Why unresolved:* Geometric isometry does not imply symplecticity; integrated symplectic constraints are missing. *What evidence would resolve:* Modified RRA where ΔR_t is constrained by a learned Hamiltonian via symplectic splitting, achieving HNN-level energy drift (<15%) on long-horizon N-body with fewer parameters than HNN.
- **Open Question 3:** Does Versor's claimed scale generalization to out-of-distribution system sizes (e.g., N=5→N=7) hold for more complex topological or geometric tasks beyond the "Broken Snake," and what are the precise failure modes if it breaks? *Basis:* Inferred from Table 3; null-vector invariance (X·L=0) is proposed mechanism; high chaotic variance noted. *Why unresolved:* Evidence limited to one synthetic topology task; unclear if conformal embedding's orthogonality generalizes to arbitrary graphs, 3D meshes, or non-manifold topologies. *What evidence would resolve:* Systematic evaluation on scale-variant tasks: 3D mesh connectivity across resolutions, graph property prediction for variable node counts; analysis of failure on non-manifold inputs; consistent MCC>0.9 across scales.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the precise computational trade-off between Versor's O(L) recurrent complexity (RRA) and its O(L²) attention (GPA) in multimodal settings, and can hybrid architectures prune GPA for tasks where temporal dynamics dominate? *Basis:* Explicitly notes overall O(L²) through GPA and suggests task-specific pruning. *Why unresolved:* No systematic ablation comparing pure RRA vs. pure GPA vs. hybrid across modalities; conditions under which quadratic GPA is necessary vs. overkill are not formalized. *What evidence would resolve:* Controlled study varying sequence length L and relational complexity (particle count, image patches), measuring accuracy, latency, memory; decision framework for automatic hybrid configuration.
- **Open Question 5:** Does Riemannian optimization (e.g., RSGD) on Spin(4,1) reduce training variance and accelerate convergence compared to the current retraction-based Adam, especially for deep Versor networks (L>50)? *Basis:* Explicitly suggested under "Optimization on Lie Manifolds"; current "Manifold Normalization" is a forward-pass approximation; training uses Euclidean gradients. *Why unresolved:* Impact of true Riemannian gradients on sample efficiency and final performance unexplored. *What evidence would resolve:* Implement RSGD with Cayley/exponential retraction; compare training curves and final accuracy on N-body, CIFAR-10 against Adam baseline, focusing on seed variance and convergence speed for L=100 layer networks.

## Limitations
- Implementation gaps: Bivector encoding, manifold normalization details (ε, frequency), and rollout protocol (teacher-forcing, renormalization) are unspecified.
- Dataset vagueness: Integrator, timestep Δt, mass distribution, and train/test splits are ambiguous; variations could shift baselines.
- Statistical opacity: Seeds and splits for multi-run averages are not provided, hindering direct replication.
