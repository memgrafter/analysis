# Who Benefits from RAG? The Role of Exposure, Utility and Attribution Bias

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24218
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24218
- **Reference count**: 26
- **Primary result**: RAG amplifies accuracy disparities between query groups; accuracy improvement ranges (R∆) reach 36.7 points and accuracy ranges (Rrag) up to 26.1 points, with group exposure, utility, and attribution showing strong correlations with fairness outcomes.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates fairness in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by defining and measuring group exposure (retrieved document prevalence), utility (accuracy gain from documents), and attribution (LLM reliance on documents). Using TREC 2022 Fair Ranking Track datasets for article and title generation, the study finds that RAG often widens accuracy gaps between groups compared to LLM-only baselines. Strong Spearman correlations between group-level metrics and accuracy highlight that disparities stem from retrieval bias and the generator's interaction with retrieved documents. The work calls for fairness-aware interventions in future RAG systems.

## Method Summary
The authors construct balanced query sets from TREC 2022 Fair Ranking Track documents (Cities, Geography, Military History) with four predefined fairness categories (Age of Topic, Popularity, Age of Article, Alphabetical). For each query (e.g., title→article), they retrieve top-10 documents using BM25, SPLADE, or Contriever. Generators (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B) produce outputs with beam search. Accuracy is measured by ROUGE-L against ground truth. Group exposure is the average count of a group's documents in the retrieved set. Group utility is the aggregated per-document accuracy gain when that document is added to the LLM-only baseline. Group attribution is the fraction of retrieved documents entailed by the generated output (using roberta-large-mnli). Fairness is quantified by the range of per-group accuracy (Rrag) and accuracy improvements (R∆). Correlations between group metrics and accuracy are computed via Spearman rank correlation.

## Key Results
- RAG increases accuracy disparities: R∆ up to 36.7 points, Rrag up to 26.1 points across topics and tasks.
- Group exposure, utility, and attribution exhibit strong positive or negative Spearman correlations with group accuracy and accuracy improvements, varying by fairness category and topic.
- Disparities are amplified relative to LLM-only baselines, indicating that retrieval introduces or exacerbates unfairness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Higher group exposure leads to higher accuracy because more retrieved documents from a group increase opportunities for the generator to use relevant information. Higher group utility correlates with greater accuracy improvements since documents that individually boost LLM accuracy also contribute in multi-document settings. Higher group attribution (generator output entailing a group's documents) indicates reliance on that group’s content, improving accuracy when the content is relevant. Strong correlations observed across multiple settings confirm these mechanisms drive fairness outcomes.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data source -> Dataset builder -> Retrievers -> Generators -> Evaluation -> Aggregation

**Critical path**:
1. Load and filter TREC documents; sample balanced set per topic with one document per group intersection.
2. For each query (title→article or article→title): retrieve top-k, generate RAG and LLM-only outputs.
3. Compute ROUGE-L accuracy vs. ground truth.
4. Compute per-document utility (single-doc vs LLM-only) and attribution (NLI entailment).
5. Aggregate per-group statistics (E, U, A) and compute AC_rag, AC_llm, Δ[AC].
6. Calculate fairness ranges Rrag and R∆.
7. Compute Spearman correlations between group factors and accuracy/improvements.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Retrieval k: larger k increases exposure but may add noise.
- Retriever type: lexical vs neural trade-off between recall and group balance.
- LLM size: larger models better utilize documents but cost more; prompt ordering critically affects few-shot performance.
- NLI model choice influences attribution reliability; domain adaptation may help.
- Decoding: beam search (deterministic) vs sampling (diverse outputs).

**Failure signatures**:
- ROUGE-L near zero → prompt or input truncation error.
- Constant group exposure across groups → dataset not diverse or retriever deterministic.
- All utilities zero → bug in single-doc augmentation or LLM-only baseline.
- NaN correlations → constant group metric.
- All entailment false → NLI mismatch or generator not using retrieved docs.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Verify overall RAG accuracy gain over LLM-only for at least one retriever/LLM combo.
2. Check that group exposure values vary (non-uniform) within a fairness category.
3. Confirm expected correlation direction between group utility and Δ[AC] for a known case (e.g., Geography Pop: positive).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does query group fairness in RAG generalize to natural user queries and diverse tasks beyond document completion (e.g., QA, summarization)?
2. What factors determine the sign (positive/negative) of correlations between group-level metrics (exposure, utility, attribution) and accuracy in RAG systems?
3. How can RAG systems be modified to achieve equitable accuracy (EA) and equitable accuracy improvements (EAI) across query groups?
4. How does the attribution method impact measured group fairness in RAG systems?

## Limitations
- Query set construction: exact method for selecting one document per group intersection is unreported, risking selection bias.
- Prompt specification: full template (few-shot order, separators, truncation) is missing, possibly deviating from intended LLM behavior.
- Attribution reliability: NLI-based attribution (roberta-large-mnli) is not validated against human judgments; entailment may not reliably indicate document use.
- Experimental scope: Evaluation covers only two LLMs, three retrievers, and a single dataset, limiting generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Disparity amplification** (R∆ up to 36.7, Rrag up to 26.1): High – directly observed across all settings.
- **Exposure–accuracy link**: Medium – strong Spearman correlations, but exposure may be influenced by ambiguous query set sampling.
- **Utility–Δ link**: Medium – conceptually plausible, yet single-document utility may not fully represent multi-document interactions.
- **Attribution–accuracy link**: Medium – hinges on NLI reliability, which requires human verification.

## Next Checks
1. Replace roberta-large-mnli with a different NLI model (e.g., DeBERTa-v3-base) and recompute group attribution correlations to test robustness.
2. Conduct a human attribution study: sample 200 query-document-output triples; have annotators judge entailment; compare with automatic scores and recalc A(gi) using human labels.
3. Run the full pipeline on a new fairness ranking dataset (e.g., TREC 2023 Fair Ranking Track) to evaluate portability of the exposure/utility/attribution framework.
