# Beyond Scalars: Evaluating and Understanding LLM Reasoning via Geometric Progress and Stability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10384
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10384
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces TRACED, a framework that evaluates LLM reasoning via geometric trajectories, achieving superior detection of hallucinations compared to scalar-based methods.

## Executive Summary
Evaluating LLM reasoning traditionally relies on scalar probabilities such as perplexity or final-answer confidence, which ignore the temporal structure of thought. This paper presents TRACED, a novel framework that treats chain-of-thought generation as a geometric trajectory in a semantically aligned space. By deriving two key kinematic descriptors—Progress (normalized net displacement) and Stability (trajectory curvature)—TRACED reveals a clear topological pattern: correct reasoning proceeds with high progress and low curvature, while hallucinations exhibit low progress and high-curvature oscillations. These features feed into a Bayesian classifier that consistently outperforms scalar, probing, and alternative trajectory baselines across six diverse benchmarks and four LLMs. The approach also uncovers scaling laws linking displacement to reasoning quality and maps geometric signatures to cognitive concepts like “Hesitation Loops” and “Certainty Accumulation,” providing a physical lens to decode machine thought.

## Method Summary
TRACED processes a chain-of-thought trace by first extracting the final-layer hidden state after each generated token. To ensure geometric distances reflect semantic change, it whitens these states using the induced metric \(G = W_U^\top W_U\) derived from the unembedding matrix \(W_U\). From the whitened sequence, it computes per-token differences and aggregates them into an empirical covariance matrix \(C_n\) for each trace. A contrastive subspace learning step then uses the covariance difference between correct and incorrect traces to extract a low‑dimensional basis \(B\) that maximizes separation. Projecting whitened states onto \(B\) yields a quality‑aligned trajectory \(z_{n,t}\). Two scalar descriptors are computed: (1) normalized displacement \(M_n\) measuring net progress between start and end, and (2) average curvature \(K_n\) capturing directional instability along the path. Finally, a Bayesian classifier—assuming Gaussian class‑conditional distributions over \((M,K)\)—learns means and covariances from a calibration set and assigns a correctness probability via a uniform prior and likelihood ratio.

## Key Results
- TRACED achieves superior detection performance (AUROC, AUPR, lower FPR@95) compared to scalar‑based (MSP, perplexity), probe‑based, and other trajectory methods across six benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, TheoremQA, GPQA, Social I‑QA, Fables) and four LLMs (GPT‑4, Claude‑3, LLaMA‑3, Mistral).
- Correct reasoning shows high progress with low curvature; hallucinations show low progress and high curvature. These geometric signatures obey theoretical scaling laws: linear vs. sub‑linear displacement growth, respectively.
- The framework is robust to reasoning complexity, class imbalance, and cross‑domain transfer (with centroid alignment). Its features map onto cognitive states (“Hesitation Loops” ↔ high curvature, “Certainty Accumulation” ↔ high displacement).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Raw hidden states are geometrically misaligned with semantic meaning; TRACED first transforms them into a vocabulary‑aligned space using the induced metric \(G = W_U^\top W_U\).
- **Mechanism:** The unembedding matrix \(W_U\) maps hidden states to vocabulary logits. Its induced metric \(G\) weights dimensions by their impact on output probabilities, filtering non‑semantic noise. States are whitened via \(G^{1/2}\) before computing dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic evolution must be measured in the space that directly influences token probabilities; raw Euclidean distances conflate syntactic or high‑frequency noise with genuine meaning shifts.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “By utilizing this induced metric, we filter out non‑semantic artifacts.”; [section 2.2] Full derivation of \(G\) and motivation for semantic whitening.
- **Break condition:** If \(W_U\) is poorly estimated or the model’s output distribution is highly anisotropic, \(G\) may not cleanly separate semantic directions, causing displacement/curvature to reflect noise rather than thought progression.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Correct and incorrect reasoning traces exhibit distinct topological signatures in the semantic manifold: high displacement with low curvature vs. low displacement with high curvature.
- **Mechanism:** Displacement \(M_n = \frac{1}{T}\|z_{n,T} - z_{n,0}\|_2\) measures net semantic distance. Curvature \(K_n\) averages \(\kappa_{n,t} = \frac{\sqrt{\|v_t\|^2 \|a_t\|^2 - (v_t\cdot a_t)^2}}{\|v_t\|^3}\), quantifying directional instability.
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning quality is encoded in the temporal geometry of hidden‑state trajectories, not static snapshots; valid deduction moves steadily toward a conclusion, while hallucination loops or stalls.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “correct reasoning shows high progress and stable, low‑curvature trajectories, while hallucinations exhibit low progress and high‑curvature oscillations.”; [section 3.2] Formal definitions and Figure 1 showing consistent separation across datasets/models; [section 4.4] Domain‑specific differences support that geometry captures quality beyond task semantics.
- **Break condition:** If correct reasoning sometimes requires backtracking (e.g., self‑correction) without hallucination, the low‑curvature invariant may fail. The paper assumes such backtracking still yields net high displacement; excessive curvature without displacement indicates unproductive loops.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A Bayesian classifier using displacement and curvature as features leverages their class‑conditional distributional separation to reliably label reasoning quality without task‑specific supervision.
- **Mechanism:** Under a Gaussian assumption (justified by CLT for aggregated dynamics), TRACED estimates class means/covariances from a reference set and computes posterior \(P(y=1|x)\). The decision boundary is derived from likelihood ratio.
- **Core assumption:** The joint distribution of \((M, K)\) for correct and incorrect traces is approximately Gaussian and separable in the discriminative subspace \(B\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “A Bayesian classifier then leverages these features.”; [section 3.3] Probabilistic formulation and uniform prior assumption; [table 1] Superior AUROC/AUPR vs. scalar methods and probes; robustness analyses support distributional stability; [appendix E] Theoretical scaling laws provide a generative rationale for separate distributions.
- **Break condition:** If the Gaussian assumption is violated (e.g., multimodal feature distributions) or the subspace \(B\) fails to maximize separation, the Bayesian model may miscalibrate. The paper shows robustness to prior imbalance but extreme skew still hurts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) Trajectories**  
  Why needed: TRACED operates on multi‑step reasoning traces, not single‑token predictions. Understanding CoT’s token‑by‑token generation is prerequisite to interpreting state sequences.  
  Quick check: Why does analyzing the entire token sequence provide more signal than only the final token’s hidden state?

- **Concept: Induced Metric from Unembedding**  
  Why needed: The metric \(G = W_U^\top W_U\) defines the geometry in which "distance" equals semantic change (probability shifts). Without this, Euclidean norms in raw hidden space are meaningless.  
  Quick check: How does \(\|v\|_G = \|W_U v\|_2\) relate to the model’s output distribution?

- **Concept: Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) Models of Reasoning**  
  Why needed: The paper’s theoretical scaling laws (Appendix E) model reasoning as \(d z_t = v_{\text{logic}} dt + \sigma dW_t\). This links curvature/displacement to SNR (\(\|v_{\text{logic}}\|/\sigma\)).  
  Quick check: What geometric signature does each regime (high‑SNR vs. low‑SNR) produce, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** State extractor → Semantic whitening → Differential calculator → Covariance aggregator → Contrastive subspace learner → Projector → Geometric signature engine → Bayesian classifier
- **Critical path:** Whitening (step 2) → Dynamics (step 3) → Projection (step 6) → Metrics (step 7). Skipping whitening corrupts all downstream geometry.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Subspace dimension \(k\): Low \(k\) risks losing discriminative signals; high \(k\) increases noise and covariance estimation cost. Paper finds \(k=8\) stable.
  - Reference set size \(N\): Small \(N\) yields noisy covariance estimates; paper shows plateau at \(N \approx 400\).
  - Prior uniformity: Using uniform \(P(y)\) simplifies deployment but hurts under extreme class imbalance.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Overlapping \(M\)–\(K\) scatterplots for a model/dataset indicate geometric signatures are not discriminative.
  - Performance collapse when reducing reference set below ~200 samples.
  - Large AUROC drop (>5%) on out‑of‑domain tasks without centroid alignment.
  - Curvature distributions similar for correct/incorrect suggest the "hesitation loop" hypothesis fails for that model.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Visual sanity check:** Plot \(M\) vs. \(K\) for a small balanced sample of correct/incorrect traces; expect clear separation (Figure 1).
  2. **Sensitivity to \(k\):** Sweep \(k \in [2,10]\) on a held‑out set; AUROC should improve then plateau (Figure 4).
  3. **Baseline comparison:** Compute scalar baselines (MSP, Perplexity) on the same traces; TRACED should show AUROC gains of 5‑15% (Table 1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Under what precise mathematical conditions does the reasoning trajectory's net displacement scale linearly (O(T)) versus sub-linearly (O(√T)) as claimed by the theoretical scaling laws?  
*Basis:* The paper states in Section 4.3 and Appendix E that correct reasoning follows linear scaling while incorrect follows sub-linear scaling, deriving this from a stochastic differential equation framework.  
*Why unresolved:* While the paper provides intuitive arguments and empirical fits, a fully rigorous proof linking the SDE assumptions to the asymptotic scaling exponents is not formally established. The empirical slopes are close but not exactly 1 and 0.5, leaving room for deeper theoretical characterization.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A formal theorem with clearly stated assumptions (e.g., on dimensionality, properties of the logical velocity field, noise covariance structure) that derives the almost-sure or in-probability scaling limits D(T) ∝ T^α with α=1 for coherent reasoning and α=0.5 for noise-dominated cases.

**Open Question 2:** Is the proposed geometric‑cognitive correspondence—high curvature mapping to "Hesitation Loops" and high displacement to "Certainty Accumulation"—a causal mechanism or a correlated artifact of the model's internal state evolution?  
*Basis:* Sections 4.5 and 4.6 explicitly map geometric features to cognitive state transitions and interpret them as physical manifestations.  
*Why unresolved:* The evidence is correlational: the paper shows that high curvature coincides with certain state transitions and that correct reasoning has low curvature. However, it does not establish that modifying curvature would alter cognitive states, or that the geometric properties are the cause rather than a byproduct of a deeper process.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Causal intervention experiments (e.g., activation steering to artificially increase/decrease curvature) and measuring subsequent textual markers of hesitation/certainty, or a mechanistic interpretability analysis tracing the same neural circuits involved.

**Open Question 3:** To what extent are the geometric signatures (progress/stability) truly domain‑invariant, and what fundamental limitations prevent perfect cross‑domain transfer even after centroid alignment?  
*Basis:* Section 4.2 reports that a "Global Fit" model derived from aggregated data works across domains but lags behind in‑domain upper bounds, and that centroid alignment recovers some performance, suggesting distributional shifts.  
*Why unresolved:* The paper posits that tasks occupy different absolute regions in latent space but their relative topological structure remains isomorphic. The persistent gap after alignment indicates that other factors (e.g., curvature distribution scale, relationship between M and K) may not be fully domain‑invariant. The nature of these residual factors is not deeply investigated.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A systematic ablation study comparing geometric features across tasks after various normalization techniques (translation, scaling, affine transforms). If even adaptive normalization fails to close the gap, the shape of the quality manifold itself may differ, challenging universality.

**Open Question 4:** Does the Bayesian classifier's assumption of class‑conditional Gaussianity for the (M, K) features hold empirically under different model architectures, reasoning complexities, and domains, and how do violations affect robustness?  
*Basis:* Section 3.3 justifies the Gaussian likelihood by the Central Limit Theorem, as M and K are cumulative aggregations. The classifier is central to TRACED's decision rule.  
*Why unresolved:* The CLT justification is plausible but not guaranteed, especially for curvature which is a ratio of norms and may have heavy tails. The paper shows robustness to class imbalance but does not validate the distributional assumption (e.g., via Q‑Q plots, normality tests, or comparing to non‑parametric classifiers).  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Empirical analysis of (M, K) distributions across models/datasets: visual inspection (histograms, Q‑Q plots), statistical tests for normality and shared covariance, and sensitivity comparison to non‑parametric classifiers (kernel density, random forests). Significant deviations would suggest the need for a more flexible probabilistic model.

**Open Question 5:** Is the induced semantic metric \(G = W_U^\top W_U\) the optimal or even a sufficient metric for capturing "semantic evolution" in the context of reasoning quality assessment, or could alternative metrics (e.g., based on attention patterns, gradient norms, or learned similarity functions) provide a more discriminative geometry?  
*Basis:* Section 2.2 adopts \(G\) from prior work, arguing it weights hidden states by their impact on the vocabulary distribution and filters out non‑semantic anisotropy.  
*Why unresolved:* The choice of \(G\) is taken as a given theoretical grounding. However, the "vocabulary space" might not be the only space where reasoning dynamics are meaningful. The paper does not compare TRACED's performance when using alternative metrics, so it's unclear whether \(G\) is minimally sufficient or if a task‑specific or learned metric could further sharpen separation.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* An ablation study where the metric \(G\) is replaced or augmented—e.g., using Fisher information, layer‑wise cosine similarity, or a learned linear transformation that maximizes separation in a supervised way. Significant improvement would indicate \(G\) is suboptimal.

## Limitations
- Whitening via \(G^{1/2}\) is numerically fragile; ridge not specified.
- Gaussian assumption for \((M,K)\) and covariance form (full/diagonal) unclear; may fail under imbalance.
- Curvature averaging indices and short‑trace handling ambiguous.
- Possibly breaks if correct reasoning includes backtracking (high curvature).
- Domain shift requires centroid alignment; otherwise performance drops.
- Scaling laws rely on SDE approximations; not directly validated.

## Confidence
- Geometric separation: Medium (empirical but may not cover all reasoning styles).
- Semantic whitening: Medium (theoretical, no ablation).
- Bayesian classifier: High (consistent gains across 6 benchmarks, 4 LLMs).
- Scaling laws: Low (correlational, theoretical).
