# DiscoverLLM: From Executing Intents to Discovering Them

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.03429
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03429
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A framework using a hierarchical user simulator and tailored rewards trains LLMs to help users with ambiguous intents, improving intent discovery by ~10% and interactivity by 83% in human and automated evaluations.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles a gap in current LLM assistance: models fail when user requests are ambiguous or unformed, requiring the user to discover their own goal through exploration. To address this, the authors propose DiscoverLLM, a framework that shifts the objective from executing a fixed intent to actively helping users surface latent intents. It employs a user simulator that models a cognitive hierarchy of progressively concretizing goals. The model is rewarded for making progress in this hierarchy (discovery) and for converging to a satisfying leaf node (satisfaction). Evaluated on three interactive benchmarks and a user study, DiscoverLLM demonstrates significant gains in intent discovery, conversational interactivity, and user satisfaction compared to baselines.

## Method Summary
DiscoverLLM introduces a user simulator that represents a user's evolving intent as a tree of abstract-to-concrete goals. This tree is automatically constructed for each task artifact (e.g., a story, article, or SVG) via a three-stage LLM process (synthesis, abstraction, organization). During simulated multi-turn dialogue, the simulator tracks its position in the tree and updates its state based on the assistant's responses. The reward signal combines a discovery term (rewarding movement down the tree to new subtrees) and a convergence penalty (discouraging overly long or tangential conversations). This reward guides model training via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-reward synthetic dialogues, followed by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on turn-level pairs, and optionally by an online RL step (GRPO). The model is encouraged to diverge conversationally to explore intents and later converge to refine them.

## Key Results
*   **Intent Discovery:** Improves by ~10% on three interactive benchmarks (WritingPrompts, news article summarization, SVG drawing) compared to baselines like prompting or standard SFT.
*   **Interactivity:** Increases by 83% as judged by an LLM evaluator, indicating more exploratory and guiding conversational behavior.
*   **Efficiency:** Reduces average conversation length by 32%, suggesting more effective guidance toward satisfying conclusions.
*   **Human Validation:** A user study (n=75) confirms higher satisfaction and task efficiency compared to baseline models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by explicitly training the model to optimize for a *process* (intent discovery) rather than just a final outcome (intent execution). The hierarchical intent tree provides a structured, measurable proxy for a user's latent cognitive state. The dual-component reward (`R = Rd + Re`) creates a pressure to both explore broadly (increase `|I_t|` via `Rd`) and efficiently reach a concrete, satisfying goal (avoid long/tangential paths via `Re`). Training via SFT then DPO/RL aligns the model's policy to generate utterances that maximize this reward signal within the simulated environment, effectively teaching adaptive conversational strategies (divergence for exploration, convergence for refinement).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Hierarchical Intent Modeling:** Why needed: Provides a structured, gradual representation of an ill-formed user goal, enabling measurable progress. *Quick check:* Can you traverse a generated intent tree from a general root (e.g., "write a story") to specific leaves (e.g., "write a sci-fi story about a time-traveling librarian")?
*   **Reward Shaping for Process:** Why needed: Standard outcome-based rewards are sparse and misaligned for discovery tasks; shaping with `Rd` and `Re` provides dense, meaningful feedback per turn. *Quick check:* Does a response that introduces a new, relevant subtree get a positive `Rd`? Does a response that adds irrelevant detail trigger `Re`?
*   **Simulation-Based RL:** Why needed: Real human data for ambiguous intents is scarce and costly; a simulator allows scalable, controlled data generation for SFT and online RL. *Quick check:* Is the simulator's user response generation conditional on its current node in the intent tree and conversation history?
*   **Divergent-Convergent Behavior:** Why needed: Effective discovery requires balancing exploration (divergence) and exploitation (convergence); the reward and training must explicitly encourage this biphasic pattern. *Quick check:* Does the trained model produce sequences of turns that first branch into multiple intents before narrowing down?
*   **LLM-as-Judge Evaluation:** Why needed: Evaluating open-ended conversational processes (discovery, interactivity) is subjective; an LLM judge provides a scalable, consistent proxy. *Quick check:* Are the judge prompts (E.7, E.8) designed to score based on process criteria (e.g., "helps surface options") rather than just artifact quality?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Intent Tree Construction (LLM stages) -> User Simulator (State Tracker + Response Generator) + Base Model (Assistant) -> Reward Calculator (`Rd`, `Re`) -> SFT/DPO/GRPO Trainer -> Fine-Tuned Model

**Critical Path:** 1) For each training artifact, build a hierarchical intent tree. 2) Generate synthetic dialogues: simulator initializes state from a root node, base model responds, simulator updates state and replies based on tree progress. 3) Compute turn-level rewards. 4) Train: SFT on chosen full dialogues → DPO on turn pairs → (optional) GRPO online. 5) Deploy fine-tuned model as new assistant.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
*   **Tree Fidelity vs. Automation:** Automatic tree construction is scalable but risks low-quality or trivial hierarchies, misguiding the reward. 
*   **Reward Term Balance:** The `Rd` vs. `Re` tradeoff controls exploration vs. efficiency; incorrect weighting leads to endless divergence or premature convergence. 
*   **Simulator vs. Judge Model:** Using different models for simulation (Gemini Flash) and evaluation (judge) may cause distribution shift; using the same model might reduce diversity.

**Failure Signatures:** 
*   **Rewardcollapse:** Mean reward per dialogue plateaus near zero, indicating simulator is not recognizing progress.
*   **Over-Divergence:** Model repeatedly introduces new intents without converging (>70% consecutive divergent patterns).
*   **Tree Triviality:** Generated trees have shallow depth or leaf nodes that are not genuinely concrete/specific.
*   **Judge-Reward Mismatch:** High simulated reward but low LLM-judge satisfaction/interactivity scores.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Tree Quality Audit:** Manually inspect 20 randomly generated intent trees per task. Validate that non-leaf nodes are true abstractions of children and that leaves correspond to distinct, concrete artifact variations.
2.  **Reward Distribution Sanity Check:** Simulate 100 dialogues with the base model. Plot histogram of per-turn and per-dialogue rewards. Expect predominantly positive `Rd` on early turns, with `Re` penalties only on long/tangential responses.
3.  **Baseline Replication sanity:** Implement a "CollabLLM-like" baseline by fine-tuning a base model on synthetic dialogues but using a future-turn lookahead reward (instead of the tree-based `Rd`). Confirm it underperforms the reported DiscoverLLM scores, validating the hierarchical reward design.

## Limitations
*   **Simulator Fidelity:** Automatic tree construction and state update heuristics (thresholds `θv`, score accumulation) are not fully specified, risking reward misalignment.
*   **Benchmark Validity:** Key metrics (Satisfaction, Interactivity) rely on LLM-as-judge without reported human validation, introducing potential bias.
*   **Baseline Uncertainty:** The CollabLLM baseline is re-implemented approximately due to lack of original code, weakening comparative claims.

## Confidence
*   **Hierarchical intent concept:** Medium. Clear framing, but dependent on simulator fidelity.
*   **Quantitative gains (~10% discovery, 83% ITR):** Medium. Results on three benchmarks, but based on LLM judges and unspecified simulation details.
*   **User study outcomes:** High. Direct human evaluation (n=75) provides strong, though moderate-scale, evidence.
*   **Reproducibility of core method:** Medium. Key training details are provided, but critical simulator parameters are opaque.

## Next Checks
1.  **Sensitivity Analysis of State Updater:** Systematically vary the threshold sampling range for `θv` and the tangential probability `p` in simulation. Measure impact on final discovery scores and convergence patterns.
2.  **Human Evaluation of LLM Judges:** For a stratified sample (n=100) of final conversations from the best model, have human raters score Satisfaction and Interactivity. Compute correlation with the paper's LLM judge (Prompts E.7, E.8).
3.  **Ablation of Reward Components:** Train model ablations using only `Rd` (discovery) or only `Re` (convergence) on the same data. Compare against full reward to quantify each term's contribution to the reported gains.
