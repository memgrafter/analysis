# Synaptic Activation and Dual Liquid Dynamics for Interpretable Bio-Inspired Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13017
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13017
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** The LRC-SA model achieves the lowest turn-weighted validation loss, highest absolute correlation with road trajectory (0.766 in winter), and SSIM robustness closest to 1, demonstrating superior accuracy and interpretability.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the interpretability challenge in dense recurrent neural network policies for safety-critical tasks like autonomous driving. It proposes a unified framework comparing bio-inspired models, introducing Liquid-Capacitance (LC) models derived from electrical synapses and enhancing Liquid-Resistance Liquid-Capacitance (LRC) models with chemical synapses. The core innovation combines dual liquid dynamics (gating via liquid-resistance and liquid-capacitance) with synaptic activation, where each synapse learns distinct dynamics. Evaluated on a lane-keeping task, the LRC-SA model significantly outperforms baselines in metrics aligned with interpretability—correlating neural activity with road geometry and maintaining robust saliency maps under noise—suggesting that integrating chemical synapses and synaptic activation yields RNNs that are both more accurate and more interpretable.

## Method Summary
The method involves training and evaluating several variants of bio-inspired recurrent neural networks on an autonomous driving task. Models are derived from electrical equivalent circuits (EECs): LC models use fixed electrical synapses, while LRC models incorporate chemical synapses with state-dependent time constants. A key ablation compares neural activation (NA, shared per neuron) versus synaptic activation (SA, per-synapse parameters). Training uses open-loop imitation learning on front-camera images paired with steering commands, followed by closed-loop deployment in the VISTA simulator. Interpretability is quantified via three metrics: (1) turn-weighted validation loss to emphasize performance on sharp turns, (2) absolute Pearson correlation between individual neuron activities and the road trajectory, and (3) structural similarity (SSIM) of VisualBackProp saliency maps under Gaussian noise to assess robustness.

## Key Results
- LRC-SA achieves the lowest turn-weighted validation loss (0.011) and highest absolute correlation with road trajectory (0.766 in winter, 1.5× better than LTCs).
- Saliency maps from LRC-SA are most robust to Gaussian noise, with SSIM values closest to 1.
- Synaptic activation combined with chemical synapses (LRC-SA) outperforms all other configurations, including LRC-NA and LC variants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Dual Liquid Dynamics (Liquid-Resistance + Liquid-Capacitance)
Combining state/input-dependent liquid-resistance (forget gate) with liquid-capacitance yields smoother, more regularized internal dynamics that align closely with environmental features like road trajectory. Chemical synapses provide a liquid time-constant via a saturating forget gate (`σ(f_i)`), while symmetric elastance (`ϵ_i,t`) provides capacitance. This dual gating controls the integration of new state against forgetting, producing continuous trajectories that vary predictably with inputs. Ablations show disabling liquid-resistance (LC models) degrades interpretability metrics toward traditional gated RNNs.

### Synaptic Activation (Per-Synapse Dynamics)
Assigning distinct activation functions and parameters to each synapse (vs. per neuron) enhances interpretability by enabling finer-grained, input-dependent information routing. This heterogeneity allows the network to allocate different synapses to distinct, interpretable sub-tasks (e.g., responding to specific road features). Evidence shows LRC-SA learns visibly different sigmoid parameters for different outgoing synapses of the same neuron, leading to higher trajectory correlation and SSIM robustness compared to LRC-NA.

### Saturation via Biological Plausibility
Using saturating nonlinearities (`σ` for forget gate, `τ` for update gate) on conductance terms, as in saturated EECs, improves interpretability by preventing unbounded dynamics and aligning with biological membrane potential constraints. This regularization yields smoother, bounded state trajectories that correlate better with external variables. Comparisons with CT-RNNs (fixed time-constant) and vanilla gated RNNs confirm that saturation contributes to more interpretable activity patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Input (front-camera image 48×160) -> CNN head -> Recurrent core (19-neuron fully-connected RNN, variants: LSTM, GRU, MGU, CT-RNN, LTC, LC-NA/SA, LRC-NA/SA) -> Output (steering angle). Evaluation and interpretability modules take RNN hidden states and input images to compute metrics.

Critical path:
1. Train CNN+RNN via open-loop imitation learning (sequence length 32, AdamW, lr=5e-4) on summer/winter dataset; select best model by turn-weighted validation loss.
2. Deploy selected model in VISTA simulator for closed-loop 1 km drives; record neuron activities and generate VisualBackProp saliency maps.
3. Compute interpretability metrics: absolute correlation (neuron activity vs. road trajectory), SSIM of saliency maps under Gaussian noise (σ²=0.1,0.2).

Design tradeoffs:
- Parameter count vs. interpretability: LRC-SA has more parameters due to per-synapse activation; increasing neuron count in other models to match parameters may reduce interpretability by forcing less meaningful per-neuron features.
- Biological plausibility vs. performance: LC-SA (non-biological per-synapse activation on electrical synapses) underperforms LRC-SA, showing chemical synapses combined with SA are key.
- Task complexity: Lane-keeping is simple enough for analysis but may not generalize to more nuanced tasks.

Failure signatures:
- Low trajectory correlation (<0.5): Neural activity does not follow road curvature; likely due to missing liquid-resistance (LC) or synaptic activation (NA).
- SSIM drop with noise (values <<1): Saliency maps are fragile; common in LSTM/GRU/LC.
- Scattered neural activity in time: State trajectories are not continuous; indicates poor gating/liquid dynamics.
- High weighted validation loss: Model fails on sharp turns; suggests insufficient liquid-capacitance or synaptic activation.

First 3 experiments:
1. Train all model variants for 100 epochs; record best epoch validation loss and weighted loss. Expect LRC-SA/LRC-NA to converge faster and achieve lower weighted loss.
2. For each trained model, run closed-loop simulation in summer/winter; generate saliency maps on clean and noisy inputs; compute SSIM between them. Expect LRC-SA to have SSIM closest to 1 with smallest variance.
3. During closed-loop drive, record neuron activities and road curvature; compute absolute Pearson correlation per neuron. Expect LRC-SA (and LRC-NA) to have mean correlation >0.65 in winter, significantly higher than others.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1  
How do the interpretability benefits of LRC-SA models transfer to other safety-critical domains beyond autonomous driving, such as robotic manipulation or medical decision-making?  
*Basis:* The authors state they would like to test in diverse, safety-critical settings.  
*Why unresolved:* Evaluation is limited to a single lane-keeping task; generalizability is unknown.

### Open Question 2  
Can interpretable bio-inspired RNNs be designed in a modular fashion, where components (e.g., liquid-resistance, liquid-capacitance, synaptic activation) can be mixed and matched while preserving or enhancing interpretability and performance?  
*Basis:* The authors propose building networks modularly as future work.  
*Why unresolved:* No exploration of modular frameworks; trade-offs of component recombination are unclear.

### Open Question 3  
Why does the interpretability advantage of LRC-SA over LRC-NA, measured by absolute correlation with road trajectory, increase in winter conditions but vanish in summer?  
*Basis:* Table 1 shows similar correlation in summer (LRC-SA 0.645 vs. LRC-NA 0.652) but a larger gap in winter (0.766 vs. 0.693).  
*Why unresolved:* No analysis of seasonal factors (e.g., visual clutter, lighting) that might interact differently with synaptic activation.

### Open Question 4  
What functional or biological meanings can be attributed to the distinct synaptic activation parameters (e.g., sigmoid offsets and slopes) learned by LRC-SA models, and how do they map to specific driving maneuvers?  
*Basis:* Figure 5 shows LRC-SA learns separate sigmoids per synapse, but parameters are not interpreted.  
*Why unresolved:* Parameters are treated as black-box; their relationship to driving concepts is unknown.

## Limitations
- Single-task evaluation in a simulator; unknown if results generalize to other tasks or real-world conditions.
- Simulator dependency: VISTA's simplified dynamics may not reflect true vehicle physics or diverse environments.
- Ambiguous metric definitions (turn-weighted loss, correlation target, VisualBackProp parameters) hinder reproducibility.
- LRC-SA's parameter count is higher; interpretability gains could be due to capacity rather than per-synapse activation.
- Biological plausibility claim remains qualitative; no empirical test linking plausibility directly to interpretability.

## Confidence
- Dual liquid dynamics (chemical synapses) improves interpretability: Medium (supported by within-task ablations but external validity unclear).
- Synaptic activation improves interpretability: Medium (LRC-SA > LRC-NA, but parameter count not fully controlled).
- LRC-SA achieves best reported metrics on this task: High (for this setup), Low for broader claims due to task/simulator limits.
- Biological plausibility leads to interpretability: Low (asserted without direct validation).

## Next Checks
- **Cross-task generalization:** Evaluate LRC-SA on at least two additional safety-critical tasks (e.g., robotic manipulation, medical sequencing) and compare against relevant baselines.
- **Parameter-controlled ablation:** Train LRC-NA with increased neuron count to match LRC-SA's parameter budget; compare interpretability metrics to isolate the effect of synaptic activation.
- **Real-world transfer:** Test LRC-SA in a physical vehicle platform under varied conditions, correlating neural activity with human driver patterns and real road geometry.
