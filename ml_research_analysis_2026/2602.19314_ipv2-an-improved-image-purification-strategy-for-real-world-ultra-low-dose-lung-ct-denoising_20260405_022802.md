# IPv2: An Improved Image Purification Strategy for Real-World Ultra-Low-Dose Lung CT Denoising

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.19314  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19314  
- **Reference count:** 33  
- **Primary result:** IPv2 consistently outperforms IPv1 across multiple denoising models on a real 2% dose lung CT dataset, reducing FID by up to 52% and achieving an FID as low as 27.02.

## Executive Summary
Ultra-low-dose CT (uLDCT) reduces radiation but introduces severe noise, often correlated with anatomical structures due to physical factors and potential misalignment between noisy uLDCT and clean normal-dose CT (NDCT) pairs. The original image purification strategy (IPv1) failed to denoise background and lung parenchyma because it masked out these regions during training. This paper introduces IPv2, which adds three modules—Remove Background, Add Noise, and Remove Noise—to improve both training data construction and test label refinement. By preserving background noise in training inputs and injecting controlled simulated noise into lung regions, along with a refined test label that isolates lung denoising evaluation, IPv2 enables denoising models to learn effective mappings. Experiments on a real-world 2% dose lung CT dataset show substantial improvements in distribution-based metrics (FID, KID, CLIP-FID) for several state-of-the-art models, including Flow Matching (FM), Cold Diffusion, and FFM.

## Method Summary
IPv2 modifies the data pipeline for supervised uLDCT denoising. First, it processes paired uLDCT and NDCT images: Otsu thresholding and flood fill from the four corners labels pure background, which is then excluded from the common anatomical mask. For training, the *Add Noise* module simulates uLDCT lung noise by applying Poisson-Gaussian noise to NDCT sinograms and inverse transforming; the training input (*IPv2(uLDCT)*) fuses this simulated noisy lung with the real uLDCT background (using the mask), thus providing explicit lung denoising supervision while preserving background noise distribution. For evaluation, a *weak denoiser* (trained on simulated data) is applied to the real uLDCT; the *Remove Noise* module then fuses the original NDCT’s chest/bone/background with the weak denoiser’s lung output to form the test label (*IPv2(NDCT)*). The main denoiser is trained on (*IPv2(uLDCT)*, NDCT) pairs and evaluated against *IPv2(NDCT)* using distribution metrics.

## Key Results
- Flow Matching (FM) reduces FID by 52% (81.78 → 39.33).
- Cold Diffusion reduces FID by 54% (72.55 → 33.67).
- FFM achieves the best FID of 27.02.
All models show significant improvements in FID, KID, and CLIP-FID compared to IPv1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Remove Background Module**
- **Claim:** Enables background denoising by retaining background noise in training inputs.
- **Mechanism:** Before computing the common mask, Otsu thresholding and flood fill from image corners identify and label pure background (value 0) as background. During synthesis, background pixels are copied directly from the input uLDCT, preserving its noise distribution. This forces the model to learn a mapping from noisy to clean background rather than an identity.
- **Core assumption:** Background pixels in uLDCT are structurally irrelevant and were previously masked out, preventing the model from learning background denoising.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the flood-fill algorithm and weighted fusion formulation that retains uLDCT values in background.
- **Break condition:** If flood fill incorrectly segments anatomical structures as background, critical regions may lose structural alignment or retain unwanted noise, causing training instability or degraded performance.

**Add Noise Module**
- **Claim:** Provides explicit supervision for lung-specific denoising by injecting synthetic noise into the lung parenchyma of training inputs.
- **Mechanism:** Applies the Radon transform to NDCT, adds Poisson-Gaussian mixed noise calibrated to the 2% dose level in the sinogram domain, and inverse Radon transforms to create a simulated uLDCT. The lung region of this simulated image contains noise intensity matching real uLDCT.
- **Core assumption:** Real uLDCT lung noise is approximately additively modelable via Radon-domain noise injection; a model trained on such data generalizes to real lung denoising.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states IPv1 “lacks a dedicated mechanism for denoising the lung parenchyma.” Section 3.2 details the Radon transform and noise addition, noting it provides “explicit supervision for learning lung-specific denoising.”
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic noise distribution poorly matches real 2% dose noise, the model may learn a mismatched mapping, harming generalization.

**Remove Noise Module**
- **Claim:** Constructs a near-ideal test label (*IPv2(NDCT)*) by fusing NDCT chest/bone/background with weakly denoised lung parenchyma from uLDCT, enabling fair evaluation of lung denoising.
- **Mechanism:** A weak denoiser (same architecture as the main model) is trained on simulated noisy images (from Add Noise). This denoiser removes simulated noise in lung tissue but fails in chest/bone regions. At test time, it is applied to real uLDCT; then the common mask guides fusion: chest/bone/background from original NDCT, lung from the weak denoiser output.
- **Core assumption:** A denoiser trained on simulated noise generalizes to denoise real uLDCT lung parenchyma (due to additive noise similarity) but not chest/bone (due to complex structural noise), allowing selective use.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “provide a more reasonable evaluation protocol through refined label construction at the testing stage.” Section 3.3 explains the two-stage training and spatial limitation exploitation.
- **Break condition:** If the weak denoiser fails to remove real lung noise or inadvertently alters chest/bone textures, the fused label will contain residual noise or structural errors, misrepresenting model performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **CT noise formation (Poisson-Gaussian in projection, structural coupling in image domain).** Why needed: Understanding why real uLDCT noise is anatomically coupled and not simply additive motivates the Add Noise module’s design. Quick check: Why does adding simple Gaussian noise to an NDCT image not adequately simulate real 2% dose uLDCT noise?
- **Spatial misalignment in paired medical images (respiratory motion, organ deformation).** Why needed: The entire purification strategy exists to correct misalignment; grasping why direct (uLDCT, NDCT) pairs are flawed for supervised learning is essential. Quick check: How does spatial misalignment between uLDCT and NDCT pairs cause a denoising CNN to learn incorrect mappings?
- **Diffusion/flow-based generative models (Cold Diffusion, Flow Matching, FFM).** Why needed: The experiments apply IPv2 to these models; understanding their iterative sampling helps see why aligned training data is crucial. Quick check: Why might structural misalignment in training data increase uncertainty in the sampling paths of diffusion models?
- **Evaluation metrics for distribution alignment (FID, KID, CLIP-FID) vs. pixel-wise (PSNR, SSIM).** Why needed: The paper avoids PSNR/SSIM due to misalignment; feature-space distances are more appropriate. Quick check: Why are FID/KID more robust than PSNR for evaluating denoising on real misaligned uLDCT/NDCT pairs?
- **Image segmentation and morphological operations (Otsu thresholding, flood fill, mask operations).** Why needed: Core to Remove Background module’s implementation; ensures correct structural mask computation. Quick check: What is the risk of using the image corners as flood fill seeds for background removal in thoracic CT?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Paired (uLDCT, NDCT) -> Otsu & flood fill -> Common mask -> Training: Add Noise + mask fusion -> IPv2(uLDCT) -> Main denoiser -> output; Testing: Weak denoiser (trained on simulated data) + mask fusion -> IPv2(NDCT) -> evaluation metrics  

**Critical path:**  
1. Compute accurate anatomical mask via Otsu thresholding and flood fill from image corners, ensuring chest wall and lung are not erroneously labeled as background.  
2. Calibrate synthetic noise in Add Noise to match real 2% dose lung noise statistics.  
3. Train the weak denoiser on simulated noisy images and confirm it effectively suppresses lung noise while leaving chest/bone largely unchanged.  
4. Fuse images precisely according to the common mask during both training data construction and label refinement.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Synthetic noise realism vs. controllability:* The Radon-based Poisson-Gaussian model is simplified; over-simplification may limit generalization.  
- *Label purity vs. authenticity:* *IPv2(NDCT)* combines different sources (NDCT and weak denoiser output); fusion seams may introduce artifacts if mask boundaries are inaccurate.  
- *Computational overhead:* Pre-processing (Radon transforms, flood fill) and auxiliary weak denoiser training increase total cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No background improvement → background pixels in *IPv2(uLDCT)* likely taken from NDCT (mask error).  
- Oversmoothed lung textures → Add Noise injected too much/too little noise or weak denoiser over-fitted.  
- Metrics do not improve over IPv1 → test labels *IPv2(NDCT)* may still contain residual lung noise or misalignment.  
- Visible sharp boundaries at fusion seams → mask computation error or flood fill over-segmentation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablate Remove Background: reconstruct training inputs with IPv1 masking + Add Noise; expect background noise persists and FID increases.  
2. Validate weak denoiser spatial limitation: apply trained weak denoiser to real uLDCT; measure noise reduction separately in lung vs. chest/bone masks. Expect strong reduction only in lung.  
3. Verify noise calibration in Add Noise: generate simulated uLDCT lung region; compute noise variance and compare to real uLDCT lung variance (target <5% relative difference).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to other organs:** Does IPv2 extend to uLDCT of abdomen, brain, or other anatomies? The current evaluation is limited to lung CT.  
- **Noise distribution accuracy:** Is the synthetic noise from the Add Noise module distributionally accurate across different tissue types? No quantitative comparison to real noise statistics is provided.  
- **Clinical relevance of metrics:** Do FID/KID/CLIP-FID, which are natural-image metrics, correlate with radiologists’ quality assessments of denoised CT scans? The paper lacks clinical validation.

## Limitations
- Exact noise calibration parameters for the Add Noise module (Poisson‑Gaussian weights, dose conversion) are unspecified, relying on an unvalidated assumption of perfect match to real noise.  
- The weak denoiser’s training protocol (loss, optimizer, convergence) is not detailed; its success is pivotal yet opaque.  
- The precise pixel‑level fusion logic for constructing *IPv2(NDCT)* is ambiguous; unclear how “lung parenchyma” is delineated within the common mask, risking boundary artifacts.

## Confidence
- **Remove Background Module:** High (algorithm is explicit and testable).  
- **Add Noise Module:** Medium (mechanism described but confidence limited by unvalidated noise realism assumption).  
- **Remove Noise / Label Refinement:** Low (critically depends on an unverified spatial generalization gap of the weak denoiser).

## Next Checks
1. Quantify synthetic noise realism: compare noise variance in simulated lung region of *IPv2(uLDCT)* to that in real uLDCT lung parenchyma.  
2. Characterize weak denoiser’s spatial effect: measure noise reduction (standard deviation) separately within lung and chest/bone masks on real uLDCT after weak denoising.  
3. Audit mask and fusion quality: visualize the common mask and *IPv2(NDCT)* labels, inspecting for seam artifacts and confirming correct region separation.
