# Beyond Optimization: Intelligence as Metric-Topology Factorization under Geometric Incompleteness

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07974
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07974
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Metric-Topology Factorization (MTF) eliminates catastrophic forgetting under topological shifts (e.g., parity flips) by separating stable topological indexing from plastic metric warping, achieving rapid adaptation and orthogonal task routing.

## Executive Summary
The paper argues that catastrophic forgetting in continual learning arises from the geometric incompleteness of fixed-metric optimization on semantically complex manifolds. It introduces Metric-Topology Factorization (MTF), which decouples a task's topological identity (captured by spectral signatures) from its metric geometry (handled by memory-amortized metric inference). A core theoretical result, the Geometric Incompleteness Theorem, proves that any fixed Riemannian metric must introduce unnecessary saddles on manifolds with non-trivial intermediate homology, forcing a stability-plasticity tradeoff. The proposed Topological Urysohn Machine (TUM) implements MTF, enabling a system to switch between pre-learned metric geometries instead of re-optimizing. Empirical results on synthetic Möbius-world and Permuted MNIST show orders-of-magnitude reduction in adaptation latency, near-zero subspace overlap between tasks, and sustained plasticity where methods like EWC stiffen.

## Method Summary
MTF decomposes learning into two stages: (1) **Topological condensation**, where a lightweight module Ψ_top extracts a discrete spectral signature Σ from input data (e.g., parity bit or graph Laplacian eigenvalues) that indexes the task's manifold class. (2) **Memory-amortized metric inference (MAMI)**, where Σ retrieves a pre-learned pair (P_Σ, ˜W_Σ) from memory: an orthogonal projector P_Σ defining an active subspace and a whitening warp ˜W_Σ that isotropizes features within that subspace. During training, gradients are routed via a gating operator Π_Σ to update only parameters influencing the active subspace of the current task, enforcing orthogonal routing (P_A P_B ≈ 0) and preventing interference. After an initial learning phase that populates memory with metric geometries for each topology, new tasks with a previously seen topology can be solved nearly instantly by geometric switching rather than gradient descent.

## Key Results
- MTF reduces adaptation latency after task switches (e.g., parity flips) by orders of magnitude compared to SGD, achieving near-instant recovery.
- Learned task-specific metric warps are nearly orthogonal (|W_A W_B^⊤| ≈ 0), confirming effective subspace separation.
- On sequential Permuted MNIST, MAMI-MTF maintains high accuracy across many tasks where Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) plateaus due to Fisher information stiffness.
- Convergence time for single-metric learners scales super-linearly with manifold complexity (measured by intermediate Betti numbers βₖ), while MTF's adaptation remains constant.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Geometric Incompleteness Demands Factorization
Fixed-metric optimization fails on semantically complex manifolds (those with non-zero intermediate Betti numbers βₖ > 0). The Geometric Incompleteness Theorem proves that any Morse function (loss landscape) on such a manifold must have critical points of intermediate index, creating saddles that hinder stable convergence and force a tradeoff between remembering old tasks and learning new ones. MTF resolves this by factorization: topological indexing (discrete, stable) handles which manifold class we are on, while metric warping (continuous, plastic) operates within a topologically simple fiber where no intermediate saddles exist, allowing global contraction.

### Mechanism 2: Spectral Switching Enables Instant Adaptation
Topological shifts (e.g., a Möbius band vs. cylinder) change the manifold's global structure but not its local geometry. MTF uses spectral signatures (small non-zero Laplacian eigenvalues) as discrete "addresses" invariant under local metric deformations but sensitive to global topology. When a task switch occurs, the new signature retrieves a pre-learned metric geometry from memory (P_Σ, ˜W_Σ). The system therefore adapts instantly by applying the correct pre-shaped warp, avoiding slow gradient-based re-optimization. This is demonstrated by near-zero adaptation latency in Möbius parity alternation experiments.

### Mechanism 3: Orthogonal Routing Prevents Forgetting
MAMI ensures that parameter updates for task B affect only the subspace of task B, leaving task A's representation invariant. This is achieved by (a) condensing each task's features into a low-dimensional isotropic subspace via P_Σ ˜W_Σ and (b) routing gradients through Π_Σ to alter only parameters that influence the active subspace. Theorem 14 guarantees first-order invariance of past tasks when subspaces are mutually orthogonal and warps are subspace-supported. Empirically, the product of task-specific warps W_A W_B^⊤ is near-zero, confirming orthogonal routing and explaining catastrophic forgetting avoidance.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Morse Theory & Handle Decompositions**  
*Why needed here:* The Geometric Incompleteness Theorem uses Morse inequalities to link the number of critical points of a given index to Betti numbers. Understanding why complex topologies (βₖ > 0) force saddles requires knowing that attaching k-handles creates index-k critical points.  
*Quick check:* If a manifold has β₁ = 2 (two independent loops), what is the minimal Morse index of critical points that must appear in any Morse function?

**Concept: Riemannian Metrics & Gradient Flow**  
*Why needed here:* MTF's metric contraction operates in Riemannian geometries g_τ where gradient dynamics ˙z = -∇_g E depend on the metric tensor. The paper contrasts fixed-metric descent with topology-dependent metric switching.  
*Quick check:* How does changing the metric tensor g affect the direction of steepest descent compared to the Euclidean gradient?

**Concept: Graph Laplacian Spectral Signatures**  
*Why needed here:* Topological condensation Ψ_top uses the k-smallest non-zero eigenvalues of the normalized graph Laplacian as invariant task signatures. These must be stable under metric warps but discriminative across topologies.  
*Quick check:* Why are the smallest non-zero Laplacian eigenvalues (not the largest) used as topological signatures for Möbius vs. cylinder discrimination?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Φ_θ (shared plastic backbone) -> z_t -> Ψ_top (topological condenser) -> Σ_t -> M_mem.lookup(Σ_t) -> (P_Σ, ˜W_Σ) -> RU (Riemannian Unit: ˆz_t = P_Σ ˜W_Σ z_t) -> G (task readout) -> ˆy_t

**Critical path:**  
1. Input x_t passes through shared backbone Φ_θ to produce representation z_t.  
2. Topological condenser Ψ_top processes x_t (or its statistics) to compute spectral signature Σ_t.  
3. Memory M_mem uses Σ_t to retrieve active subspace projector P_Σ and whitening warp ˜W_Σ.  
4. Riemannian Unit applies metric collapse: ˆz_t = P_Σ ˜W_Σ z_t.  
5. Readout G produces prediction ˆy_t.  
6. Loss ℓ_t computed; gradients ∇_θ ℓ_t are routed via Π_Σ to affect only parameters influencing active subspace of current task.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Signature resolution:* Finer Σ increases memory fragmentation but reduces interference; coarser Σ risks collisions.  
- *Subspace rank k_Σ:* Larger rank preserves variance but raises overlap risk; smaller rank forces compression, potentially losing discriminability.  
- *Condensation interval K:* Frequent whitening updates keep ˜W_Σ accurate but increase compute; infrequent updates risk metric drift.  
- *Memory update policy:* Incremental vs. batch updates to ˜W_Σ; forgetting in memory could cause catastrophic switching errors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Signature collision:* Different topologies yield similar Σ → wrong metric retrieved → performance drop.  
- *Subspace overlap:* |⟨vec(P_A), vec(P_B)⟩| > ε for A≠B → violates orthogonal routing → forgetting.  
- *Whitening instability:* ˜W_Σ fails to satisfy E[ˆz ˆz^⊤] ≈ (1/k)P_Σ → metric collapse ineffective.  
- *Memory overwrite:* New signature overwrites old entry or signature space exhausted → past metrics lost.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Möbius parity alternation:** Train on alternating parity tasks; MTF shows near-zero adaptation latency vs. SGD's spikes; verify W_A W_B^⊤ ≈ 0.  
2. **Betti complexity scaling:** Generate manifolds with β₁ ∈ {0,1,2,4}; single-metric convergence time scales super-linearly with β₁, MTF remains flat.  
3. **Permuted MNIST sequence:** 5 sequential permuted tasks; MAMI-MTF maintains accuracy while EWC plateaus; analyze subspace orthogonality across tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can Metric-Topology Factorization be extended to discrete or non‑manifold state spaces, and what combinatorial analogs of Morse‑theoretic concepts (critical points, indices, handle attachments) are required?**  
  *Basis:* Many environments are discrete or only approximately manifolds, requiring discrete Morse theory analogs. *Why unresolved:* Current theory assumes smooth manifolds; without discrete extensions, MTF cannot apply to graphs or hybrid systems. *Resolution evidence:* A discrete Morse theory framework for MTF with analogous invariants; validation on graph-based continual learning.

- **Under what conditions are spectral signatures derived from finite samples stable enough to reliably trigger topological switching, and how should uncertainty in Σ influence exploration‑exploitation decisions?**  
  *Basis:* Signatures are estimated from approximate neighborhood graphs, sensitive to sampling density, noise, and hyperparameters. *Why unresolved:* MTF relies on Σ as a discrete index; noisy signatures could cause erroneous switching. *Resolution evidence:* Theoretical bounds on signature stability; principled uncertainty measures and switching decision rules; robust performance across sampling regimes.

- **How do design choices in memory bank size, routing mechanism, and subspace allocation affect the trade‑off between representational expressivity, computational efficiency, and catastrophic fragmentation in MTF systems?**  
  *Basis:* Larger banks and richer routing improve expressivity but increase computation and fragmentation risk. *Why unresolved:* No systematic analysis; practitioners must guess hyperparameters; over‑partitioning may degrade performance. *Resolution evidence:* Empirical studies varying bank size, routing complexity, and subspace rank, measuring accuracy, overhead, and switching stability; a theoretical fragmentation threshold model.

- **What are the relative merits and limitations of using learned/quantized keys, approximate eigensolvers, and finite‑rank projectors in implementing MTF, and how do they impact expressivity, noise robustness, and computational cost?**  
  *Basis:* Practical instantiations require approximations (learned keys, approximate eigensolvers, finite-rank projectors). *Why unresolved:* Idealized MTF theory; approximations may degrade guarantees and introduce new failure modes. *Resolution evidence:* Controlled experiments comparing approximation choices; error‑propagation analysis; a quantified cost‑expressivity frontier.

## Limitations
- The core theory assumes smooth manifolds with well-defined homology; applicability to discrete or highly non-manifold state spaces is open.
- Spectral signature extraction for high-dimensional data (e.g., images) is underspecified; without a robust unsupervised extractor, switching cannot be validated on standard benchmarks.
- The assumption that diverse tasks can be condensed into mutually orthogonal low-rank subspaces may fail for semantically correlated tasks, potentially harming within-task performance.

## Confidence
- **Geometric Incompleteness Theorem (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Mathematical proof is sound, but mapping "semantic complexity" to βₖ > 0 in real-world manifolds is untested beyond synthetic tasks.
- **Spectral Switching (Mechanism 2):** Low. Reliance on graph Laplacian eigenvalues as stable, discriminative signatures is the largest empirical gap; method for high-D data is unspecified.
- **Orthogonal Routing (Mechanism 3):** Medium. Orthogonality demonstrated on controlled tasks, but may degrade for correlated tasks requiring overlapping features.
