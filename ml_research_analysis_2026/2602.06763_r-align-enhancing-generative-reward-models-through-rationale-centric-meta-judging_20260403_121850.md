# R-Align: Enhancing Generative Reward Models through Rationale-Centric Meta-Judging

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.06763
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06763
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** R-Align mitigates Spurious Correctness in generative reward models, yielding downstream policy gains and an F-Score metric that strongly predicts RLHF performance (r=0.947).

## Executive Summary
Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) are promising for reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) because they provide both preference labels and natural language rationales. However, GenRMs often exhibit *Spurious Correctness*—producing correct labels with flawed reasoning—which undermines the stability of RLHF. The R-Align method addresses this by two key innovations: (1) augmenting training with *golden rationales* (high-quality reference explanations), and (2) introducing a *Meta Reward Model (MetaRM)* that supervises the alignment between the GenRM’s rationales and the golden ones via a combined reward signal. Across benchmarks in STEM, coding, and instruction following, R-Align reduces Spurious Correctness and leads to consistent improvements in downstream policies. Moreover, the proposed *F-Score*—which captures rationale-aligned correctness—correlates strongly (r=0.947) with actual RLHF performance, unlike standard label accuracy, demonstrating that rationale integrity is crucial for reliable reward models.

## Method Summary
R-Align modifies the training of generative reward models in two complementary ways. First, the training dataset is enriched with *golden rationales*—high-quality, human- or expert-curated explanations for why one preference is correct over another. These serve as a target for the GenRM’s rationale generation. Second, a *MetaRM* is trained to score the alignment between the GenRM’s generated rationales and the corresponding golden rationales. During GenRM training, the total reward combines the usual label accuracy (e.g., cross-entropy for the preference decision) and the MetaRM’s alignment score. This combined reward encourages the GenRM to produce both correct labels *and* sound rationales. The resulting GenRM is then used in the RLHF loop to provide rewards for policy optimization. By explicitly optimizing for rationale quality, R-Align reduces cases where the model is right for the wrong reasons, yielding more stable and effective learning signals for downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- R-Align reduces Spurious Correctness on standard reward modeling benchmarks, demonstrating improved rationale quality alongside label accuracy.
- Downstream policies trained with R-Align-augmented GenRMs achieve consistent gains in STEM problem-solving, code generation, and instruction-following tasks.
- The F-Score (rationale-aligned correctness) shows a strong correlation (r=0.947) with RLHF performance, whereas label accuracy alone does not; this validates F-Score as a predictive metric for reward model quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
R-Align tackles Spurious Correctness by forcing the GenRM to internalize *why* a preference is correct, not just *what* the correct label is. Golden rationales provide a gold-standard reasoning pattern for each training example. The MetaRM acts as an automatic judge that quantifies how closely the GenRM’s rationale matches the golden one, providing a dense reward for alignment. The combined reward ensures that the GenRM cannot achieve high training loss by merely copying label patterns; it must also generate rationales that are semantically aligned with the reference. This dual optimization cultivates reward models whose decisions are underpinned by coherent reasoning, which in turn produces more stable gradients for policy learning. The high correlation of F-Score with downstream RLHF outcomes confirms that rationale integrity translates to better real-world performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Generative Reward Models (GenRMs)**  
  *Why needed:* They extend traditional reward models by outputting natural language rationales, offering interpretability and potential for richer feedback.  
  *Quick check:* Does the model produce coherent, on-topic rationales for its preference predictions?

- **Spurious Correctness**  
  *Why needed:* Identifies the risk that a model is correct for the wrong reasons, which can mislead RLHF and cause instability.  
  *Quick check:* Are there instances where the label is right but the rationale contains factual errors or logical flaws?

- **Golden Rationales**  
  *Why needed:* Serve as a high-quality reference for what constitutes a good explanation; they anchor the alignment training.  
  *Quick check:* How are these rationales collected? Are they produced by experts or high-performing models, and what ensures their reliability?

- **Meta Reward Model (MetaRM)**  
  *Why needed:* Provides automated, scalable supervision for rationale alignment, removing the need for manual evaluation at every step.  
  *Quick check:* Does the MetaRM generalize to unseen GenRM rationales and produce meaningful alignment scores?

- **Combined Reward**  
  *Why needed:* Marries the objectives of label accuracy and rationale quality, preventing the model from sacrificing one for the other.  
  *Quick check:* How are the two components weighted? Is there a trade-off, and how is the balance determined?

- **F-Score (Rationale-Aligned Correctness)**  
  *Why needed:* A holistic metric that predicts RLHF success better than label accuracy alone, guiding model selection and development.  
  *Quick check:* Does F-Score correlate with downstream policy performance across diverse tasks and model scales?

## Architecture Onboarding
In the R-Align training pipeline, the following components interact:

Dataset (preference pairs + golden rationales) → GenRM (generates label & rationale) → MetaRM (scores alignment between GenRM rationale and golden rationale) → Combined Reward Calculator (merges label accuracy & alignment score) → GenRM Parameter Update.

After training, the static GenRM is used in the RLHF loop: Policy → GenRM (provides reward) → Policy Optimization.

Critical path: (1) Gather high-quality golden rationales, (2) train MetaRM to assess alignment, (3) train GenRM with combined reward, (4) deploy GenRM in RLHF.

Design tradeoffs:
- Golden rationales: Higher quality improves alignment but increases annotation cost and may embed annotator bias.
- Combination weighting: Overemphasizing label accuracy may neglect rationale quality; overemphasizing alignment may degrade label correctness.
- MetaRM overhead: Adds computational load during GenRM training; efficiency vs. accuracy trade-off.
- Generalization: Golden rationales may reflect a narrow reasoning style, limiting adaptability to diverse contexts.

Failure signatures:
- Persistently high label accuracy but low alignment scores (F-Score) after training.
- Downstream policy shows no improvement or regresses despite GenRM label accuracy.
- MetaRM scores become uninformative (e.g., ceiling/floor effects) indicating poor discrimination.
- GenRM rationales become overly templated or copied from golden rationales without true understanding.

First experiments:
1. Baseline comparison: Train GenRM with standard cross-entropy vs. R-Align; measure Spurious Correctness (gap between label accuracy and rationale quality) and downstream RLHF win rates.
2. Ablation: Evaluate conditions with (a) only golden rationales (no MetaRM), (b) only MetaRM (no golden rationales), and (c) full R-Align to isolate each component’s contribution.
3. Quality scaling: Vary the source (human vs. model-generated) and quantity of golden rationales; assess impact on F-Score correlation with RLHF performance and policy gains across domains.

## Limitations
- Generalizability may be limited to specific GenRM architectures and narrow domains (STEM, coding, instruction following); extension to other models or vision-language tasks is unclear.
- The reliance on high-quality golden rationales raises scalability concerns and potential bias from the rationale source.
- The strong F-Score vs. RLHF correlation is based on a constrained set of benchmarks; broader validation is needed to confirm its predictive power.

## Confidence
- Spurious Correctness exists and harms RLHF stability: **Medium**
- R-Align reduces Spurious Correctness and improves downstream policies: **Medium**
- F-Score is a superior predictor of RLHF performance compared to label accuracy: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. Replicate Spurious Correctness across diverse GenRM backbones (GPT-4, Claude, Llama) and preference datasets (HH-RLHF, APPS, UltraFeedback) to test robustness.
2. Ablate R-Align: compare full method against (a) golden rationales only and (b) MetaRM only to quantify each component's impact on correctness and downstream gains.
3. Prospective validation: manipulate rationale quality in training, compute F-Score, and measure actual RLHF outcomes (policy win rates, human eval) across multiple domains to confirm predictive power.
