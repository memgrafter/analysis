# Learning to Remember, Learn, and Forget in Attention-Based Models

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2602.09075  
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09075  
* **Reference count:** 40  
* **Primary result:** Palimpsa achieves 52.27% average accuracy on commonsense reasoning at 760M parameters, a 0.8-point gain over Gated Deltanet, with superior long-context retention as evidenced by LAMBADA perplexity improvement.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the fixed memory capacity and catastrophic interference in gated linear attention models like Mamba2 during in-context learning on long sequences. It introduces Palimpsa, a self-attention model that reformulates this as a continual learning problem solved via Bayesian metaplasticity. Palimpsa employs a dual-state update where per-synapse plasticity is dynamically controlled by an importance state tied to a prior, enabling adaptive learning and forgetting. Theoretically, it unifies several gated linear models, showing Mamba2 as a special case with dominant forgetting. Empirically, Palimpsa consistently outperforms baselines: on the Multi-Query Associative Recall (MQAR) benchmark, performance gaps widen with sequence length; on commonsense reasoning at 760M parameters, Palimpsa-D achieves a 0.8-point gain over Gated Deltanet, with notable LAMBADA perplexity improvements indicating superior long-context retention.

## Method Summary
Palimpsa reframes in-context learning in fixed-memory attention as a continual learning problem with a stability-plasticity dilemma, solved via Bayesian metaplasticity. The core update derives from minimizing a variational free energy objective, introducing a dual-state mechanism: an importance state \(I_t\) (precision) modulates the learning rate per memory element, protecting consolidated knowledge while allowing new learning. A forgetting gate \(\alpha_t = \exp(-A d_t)\) controls an effective memory window \(N_t\), preventing catastrophic remembering. Two variants are proposed: Palimpsa-D (with a gated MLP for decoupled plasticity) and Palimpsa-M (Mamba2-like, with coupled forgetting and input gating). Training uses a parallel associative scan to efficiently compute the recurrence. The framework theoretically unifies gated linear models like Mamba2 and Deltanet as special cases under specific posterior approximations, and enables upgrading pre-trained non-metaplastic models via fine-tuning with a reparameterization that gradually introduces metaplasticity.

## Key Results
* On the Multi-Query Associative Recall (MQAR) benchmark, Palimpsa’s performance advantage over baselines grows with sequence length, demonstrating effective long-context handling.
* At 760M parameters, Palimpsa-D achieves 52.27% average accuracy on commonsense reasoning tasks, a 0.8-point improvement over Gated Deltanet.
* Palimpsa shows lower LAMBADA perplexity than baselines, confirming better retention of long-range dependencies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** ICL in fixed-memory models is reframed as a continual learning problem with a stability-plasticity dilemma, solved via Bayesian metaplasticity.  
**Mechanism:** The attention state update is derived as an optimization of a variational free energy objective. This introduces a second "importance" state \(I_t\) (precision) that modulates the learning rate per memory element, protecting consolidated knowledge while allowing new learning.  
**Core assumption:** The attention mechanism's inner product loss can be modeled as a Bayesian linear regression with Gaussian priors and posteriors.  
**Evidence anchors:** Derived in Section 2.2; abstract explicitly mentions Bayesian metaplasticity tying plasticity to an importance state grounded by a prior.  
**Break condition:** The diagonal covariance approximation (mean-field) may under-estimate parameter interactions, limiting expressiveness in highly correlated data regimes.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The forgetting gate \(\alpha_t\) is input-dependent and controls an effective memory window \(N_t\), preventing catastrophic remembering.  
**Mechanism:** A weighted posterior approximates a truncated posterior with sliding window \(N\). The forgetting factor \(\alpha_t = (1 - 1/N_t) = \exp(-A d_t)\) dynamically discards stale information, where \(d_t\) is input-derived and \(A\) is learnable.  
**Core assumption:** The influence of historical data can be discounted geometrically via \(1/N_t\) at each step, simulating a finite memory capacity without explicit replay.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section A.2 formalizes the weighted posterior and its equivalence to a truncated posterior; Section 2.2 shows how \(\alpha_t\) emerges and maps to gated model parameters; Section 3.2 empirically tracks \(N_t\) growth during MQAR training.  
**Break condition:** If \(N_t\) diverges uncontrollably or collapses, the model may forget too much or too little; stability depends on \(d_t\) initialization and learned \(A\).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Mamba2 and other gated linear models are special cases of Palimpsa under specific posterior approximations, enabling metaplastic upgrades via fine-tuning.  
**Mechanism:** Different architectural choices (e.g., scalar vs. vector \(\beta_t\), full vs. diagonal covariance) correspond to different variational inference approximations. Mamba2 arises when forgetting is so strong that \(I_t \approx I_{\text{prior}}\) (constant importance).  
**Core assumption:** The stability term's matrix \(\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,i}\) can be approximated as diagonal (\(I_{t-1,i}\)) for tractability, enabling per-synapse plasticity.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 (Table 1) explicitly derives update rules for Mamba2, Deltanet, etc., from the framework; Section 2.4 describes the fine-tuning reparameterization \(v^*_t = \beta_t \odot v_t\) to fix \(\beta_t \approx 0\) initially.  
**Break condition:** The diagonal approximation may impair performance if true parameter covariances are critically non-diagonal; fine-tuning assumes pre-trained Mamba2 has unsaturated memory capacity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational Inference & Free Energy** – Needed because Palimpsa’s dual-state update derives from minimizing \(D_{KL}[q_{\theta_t} \| p_w]\), requiring understanding of ELBO/Free Energy decomposition. *Quick check:* How does the KL term between \(q_{\theta_t}\) and \(q_{\theta_{t-1}}\) enforce stability?
- **Linear Attention as Associative Memory** – Needed because the base model treats the state \(S_t\) as a key-value memory matrix updated by Hebbian rule \(S_t = S_{t-1} + v_t \otimes k_t\), which is the fixed-capacity memory being metaplasticity-augmented. *Quick check:* Why does the Hebbian update \(v_t \otimes k_t\) cause catastrophic interference if keys \(k_t\) are not orthogonal?
- **Stability-Plasticity Dilemma in Continual Learning** – Needed because the entire premise is balancing retaining old information (stability) against learning new (plasticity) within a fixed buffer. *Quick check:* What does catastrophic remembering refer to in the context of ICL, and how does the forgetting gate prevent it?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Dual-State Core (maintains \(\mu_t\) and \(I_t\)) ← Forgetting Gate (\(\alpha_t\)) & Input Gating (\(\beta_t\)) ← Input features (\(k_t, v_t, d_t\)); Scan Operation parallelizes the recurrence across sequence length.  
**Critical path:** Forward pass: compute \(k_t, v_t, \beta_t, d_t\) → compute \(\alpha_t\) → update \(I_t, \mu_t\) via Eq. (5) → output \(y_t = \mu_t q_t\). The recurrence \(\mu_t\) depends on \(\mu_{t-1}, I_{t-1}\), requiring sequential or scanned computation.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Diagonal vs. full covariance: Diagonal \(I_t\) enables vector \(\beta_t\) and efficiency but loses parameter correlation modeling (vs. full-covariance Mesanet). Register pressure: Parallel scan’s intermediate state materialization consumes many registers, forcing small chunk sizes (\(B_T\)) and hurting throughput (~3× slower than Simple GLA at 760M). Forgetting-input coupling (Palimpsa-M): Uses \(d_t\) for both forgetting and input gating, tying them; Palimpsa-D decouples with separate \(\beta_t\) and \(\alpha_t\) but adds parameters.  
**Failure signatures:** Memory collapse: \(N_t\) (or \(\alpha_t\)) too small → \(I_t\) never builds, metaplasticity vanishes, model behaves like Mamba2 with limited capacity. Plasticity loss: \(N_t\) too large → \(\alpha_t \rightarrow 1\), model stops forgetting, leading to catastrophic remembering. Training instability: Poor \(A\) or \(d_t\) initialization causes \(\alpha_t\) saturation (0 or 1), breaking gradient flow. Fine-tuning failure: \(\beta_t\) does not start near zero or gradients fail to reach \(\beta\) parameters.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **MQAR Curriculum:** Synthetic recall task with increasing sequence lengths (L=128→1024). Validates that metaplasticity (w/o Meta ablation) improves performance *especially* on longer sequences where interference is worse. Start here to isolate memory mechanism.  
2. **Learning Dynamics Tracking:** During MQAR training, log \(N_t\) and metaplasticity ratio \((I_{\text{max}}-I_{\text{min}})/I_{\text{min}}\). Expect \(N_t\) to grow early as model learns to retain, followed by loss decrease. Confirms the meta-learner adjusts memory window.  
3. **Fine-tuning vs. Full Training:** Compare Palimpsa-D/M fine-tuned from non-metaplastic checkpoints vs. fully trained from scratch on Commonsense Reasoning. Fine-tuning should match or beat full training, proving the "Mamba2→Palimpsa" upgrade path is viable and efficient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Diagonal covariance impact:** Does the diagonal approximation of the covariance matrix significantly degrade performance relative to a full-covariance metaplastic model, and under what conditions (e.g., sequence length, key orthogonality) does this degradation become most pronounced? The paper adopts diagonal for tractability, but all experiments use it; impact on correlated data is unknown.  
- **Modality generality:** Can the Bayesian metaplasticity framework be seamlessly transferred to non-text modalities (e.g., vision, audio) where key-value associations differ, and does the same forgetting/plasticity mechanism remain effective? All evaluations are on text and synthetic MQAR; generality beyond language is unverified.  
- **Fine-tuning robustness:** For large-scale models, does fine-tuning from a non-metaplastic pre-trained network to Palimpsa consistently yield improvements, or are there scenarios (data distribution shifts, task mismatches) where fine-tuning harms performance or fails to converge? Two-stage pipeline shows gains at 170M/760M but depends on careful hyperparameters; large-scale and diverse data behavior is unknown.  
- **Uncertainty calibration:** Are the uncertainty estimates derived from Palimpsa’s Bayesian posterior (e.g., via \(I_t\) or \(\Sigma_t\)) well-calibrated and useful for downstream applications like confidence scoring or OOD detection? The paper notes potential but provides no empirical validation.

## Limitations
* Theoretical reliance on diagonal covariance approximation may miss parameter correlations, potentially limiting expressiveness on highly correlated data.
* Parallel scan’s register pressure forces small chunk sizes, hurting throughput (~3× slower than Simple GLA at 760M) and may impede scaling.
* Key hyperparameters (e.g., \(I_{\text{prior}}\) initialization, \(\beta\) projection details, \(b_{\text{scale}}\) in fine-tuning) are underspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
* Fine-tuning upgrade assumes pre-trained Mamba2 has unsaturated memory capacity, which may not hold for all checkpoints or data distributions.
* Empirical validation focuses on short-to-moderate contexts (≤1k) and synthetic MQAR; long-context and diverse real-world scenarios remain underexplored.

## Confidence
- **Unification of gated linear models:** Medium – Derivation is clear but relies on approximations; fine-tuning bridge to Mamba2 is only partially validated.
- **Metaplasticity mechanism:** Medium-High – Free-energy foundation and ablations support it, but behavior on correlated data is uncertain.
- **Empirical gains (including fine-tuning):** Medium – Improvements on MQAR and commonsense are modest (+0.8 avg. at 760M) and not statistically quantified; fine-tuning from Mamba2 matches full training but depends on careful choices.

## Next Checks
1. **Long-context scaling:** Train Palimpsa on MQAR up to 4096 tokens; verify \(N_t\) growth and sustained accuracy advantage over Mamba2.
2. **Full-covariance test:** Build a full-covariance variant and compare to diagonal Palimpsa on MQAR with correlated keys to measure expressiveness loss.
3. **Fine-tuning robustness:** Conduct a hyperparameter sweep (learning rate, \(\beta\) init) when converting Mamba2→Palimpsa; report success rate of metaplasticity activation and final accuracy across seeds.
