# Current LLMs still cannot 'talk much' about grammar modules: Evidence from syntax

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20114
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20114
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** ChatGPT-5 accurately translated only 25% of 44 core English generative syntax terms into Arabic, with 38.6% incorrect and 36.4% partially appropriate.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated the proficiency of ChatGPT-5 in translating specialized generative syntax terminology from English to Arabic. By comparing the model's outputs against human translations of 44 core syntactic terms, the research quantified significant accuracy issues. The findings reveal that large language models struggle with the nuanced syntactic and semantic properties of technical linguistic vocabulary, often producing literal or morphologically inappropriate translations. The authors conclude that current LLMs cannot reliably handle such domain-specific tasks without substantial human post-editing and advocate for closer collaboration between AI developers and linguists.

## Method Summary
The study employed a descriptive, analytical, comparative approach using qualitative and quantitative methods. The task involved generating Arabic translations for 44 English generative syntax terms using a single session of the free version of ChatGPT-5 on September 28, 2025. These outputs were evaluated against a "human translation" benchmark across three categories: fully accurate, inappropriate (incorrect), and partially appropriate. The analysis focused on lexical, syntactic, and semantic divergences, identifying patterns such as literal translation and morphological derivation errors. No model training or fine-tuning was performed; the assessment used the pre-trained model as an off-the-shelf tool.

## Key Results
- Only **25%** of ChatGPT-5's translations were fully accurate compared to human benchmarks.
- **38.6%** of outputs were classified as incorrect, often due to literal or terminologically inappropriate renderings.
- **36.4%** were deemed partially appropriate, capturing some meaning but missing key nuances.
- The model exhibited consistent error patterns like word-for-word translation and failure to use established Arabic linguistic jargon.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose or test a specific mechanism for why the observed failures occur. It presents empirical results showing poor performance but does not investigate the underlying model behaviors, training data gaps, or architectural limitations responsible for the translation errors. The conclusion that LLMs cannot reliably capture nuanced terminology is based solely on output accuracy, not on a causal analysis of the model's internal processes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Tokenization:** Breaks text into subword units; needed because LLMs process tokens, not words. *Quick check:* Verify if complex syntax terms (e.g., " Merge ") are split into recognizable subwords or fragmented.
- **Embeddings:** Convert tokens into dense vectors capturing semantic relationships; needed to represent meaning. *Quick check:* Test if embeddings for synonyms (e.g., " probe " vs. " search ") are close in vector space.
- **Attention Mechanism:** Weights input tokens dynamically; needed to focus on relevant parts of a term. *Quick check:* For a compound term like "X-bar theory," check if attention focuses equally on "X" and "bar."
- **Pre-training:** Learns patterns from vast corpora; needed for general language competence. *Quick check:* Assess if the model has seen generative syntax texts during pre-training by probing related contexts.
- **Context Window:** Limits the amount of text processed at once; needed for long inputs. *Quick check:* See if providing example sentences within the window improves term translation.
- **Morphological Generativity:** Ability to derive forms; needed for creating accurate Arabic terms. *Quick check:* Ask the model to derive a noun from an Arabic root for a concept like "movement" and check appropriateness.

Design tradeoffs: Model scale (parameters) vs. inference cost; training data diversity vs. depth in specialized domains; context length vs. computational load; autoregressive decoding speed vs. output coherence.

Failure signatures: 1. Literal translation of multi-word terms without conceptual integration. 2. Morphological derivation that violates Arabic root-and-pattern conventions. 3. Retention of English loanwords or symbols (e.g., X-bar) instead of Arabized equivalents. 4. Inconsistent terminology across runs for the same term.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- To what extent can LLMs capture the syntactic and semantic nuances of English generative syntax terms during Arabic translation? The study reports only 25% full accuracy, but generalizability is limited by the narrow term set and single-model evaluation.
- What are the precise linguistic error patterns LLMs exhibit when translating specialized grammatical terminology? Error analysis is illustrative rather than exhaustive; a systematic taxonomy across a comprehensive syntax lexicon is lacking.
- How can AI-linguist collaboration be operationalized to enhance LLM translation accuracy for domain-specific terminology? The proposal remains conceptual without a concrete methodology for integrating expertise into training or inference.
- Does contextual embedding (e.g., within syntactic examples or discourse) improve LLM translation accuracy for isolated grammatical terms compared to term-only translation? The study tested only isolated terms; context-rich translation may yield different results.
- How do translation performance and error profiles vary across different state-of-the-art LLMs for the same set of syntactic terminology? The study evaluated only ChatGPT-5; comparative data across models is needed.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete term list:** Only 20 of 44 terms are provided; the full list is essential for replication and to assess sampling bias.
- **Undefined human benchmark:** The methodology for creating the human translations (translator qualifications, consensus process) is unspecified, undermining ground-truth validity.
- **Single-model, single-session evaluation:** Results are tied to one ChatGPT-5 interaction; no testing across prompts, model versions, or runs to gauge consistency or variability.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** "Only 25% of outputs were fully accurate." → **Low** (Unreproducible without full term list and human benchmark details).
- **Claim:** "LLMs cannot reliably capture nuanced syntactic/semantic properties of specialized terminology." → **Medium** (Supported by clear error patterns in examples, but sample representativeness is uncertain).
- **Claim:** "Outputs require human post-editing and closer AI-linguist collaboration." → **Medium** (Inferred from error analysis, but cost-benefit is not quantified).
