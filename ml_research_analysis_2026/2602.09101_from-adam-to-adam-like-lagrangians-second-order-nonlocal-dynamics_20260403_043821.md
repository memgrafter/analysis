# From Adam to Adam-Like Lagrangians: Second-Order Nonlocal Dynamics
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09101
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09101
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Derives a second-order inertial integro-differential equation for Adam, proves recovery of the first-order nonlocal Adam flow with explicit error estimates, and establishes stability/convergence under β₁≤√β₂ and PL/KL conditions, validated on Rosenbrock benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
The paper provides a rigorous continuous-time interpretation of the Adam optimizer as a second-order inertial system with nonlocal memory effects. By deriving an integro-differential equation (IDE) from discrete Adam via Taylor expansion, it bridges optimization algorithms and dynamical systems. The authors prove the IDE converges to the known first-order nonlocal Adam flow as step size vanishes, and they establish convergence guarantees under smoothness conditions and a stability condition β₁≤√β₂. A nonlocal Lagrangian formulation is introduced, though strict Adam causality prevents an exact variational principle, positioning the Lagrangian as an ideal blueprint. Numerical experiments on Rosenbrock functions confirm the second-order model's superior fidelity to discrete Adam for small step sizes and reveal basin-selection behaviors consistent with discrete Adam. This work lays foundations for a systematic dynamical analysis of adaptive optimizers.

## Method Summary
The paper’s methodology centers on constructing a continuous-time model that faithfully captures discrete Adam’s behavior. Starting from Adam’s update formulas for the parameters θ, first moment m, and second moment v, the authors perform a second-order Taylor expansion in the step size α. This yields an inertial integro-differential equation (IDE) where the acceleration term (α/2)θ̈ appears and the moments are expressed as causal convolution integrals with exponential kernels K_{β₁} and K_{β₂} derived from the discrete moving averages. The shift t+α in the forcing term accounts for discrete-time causality. The IDE is shown to be an O(ρ) perturbation of the first-order nonlocal flow, with ρ = max(α, α²/(1-β₁)², α/(1-β₂)). Stability and convergence are analyzed using Lyapunov functionals under Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) or Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) conditions, leading to exponential decay to a small neighborhood or KL-rate profiles. A nonlocal Lagrangian is constructed, but strict Adam causality violates the time-symmetric reciprocity required for a variational principle, so the Lagrangian serves as an ideal blueprint. Numerically, a Picard iteration scheme solves the IDE, projecting v to nonnegative values in underdamped cases. The methodology is validated by comparing trajectories with discrete Adam on Rosenbrock functions across hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- **Second-order inertial IDE:** Derived a continuous-time second-order model for Adam as an inertial integro-differential equation with causal convolution kernels representing exponential moving averages of gradients and squared gradients.
- **Asymptotic recovery and convergence guarantees:** Showed that as step size α→0, the second-order model converges to the first-order nonlocal Adam flow with explicit O(max(α, α²/(1-β₁)², α/(1-β₂))) error bounds; under β₁≤√β₂ and PL/KL conditions, established exponential convergence to a plateau of size O(ρ²) or KL-profile up to O(ρ^{1/2}) neighborhood.
- **Lagrangian insight and numerical validation:** Demonstrated that an Adam-inspired nonlocal Lagrangian exists but strict causality prevents satisfaction of the reciprocity condition; numerically, the second-order model matches discrete Adam more accurately than first-order surrogates for small α, reproduces basin-selection transitions, and confirms the β₁≤√β₂ stability regime with projection ensuring v≥0 in underdamped cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Second-order Taylor expansion yields inertial IDE.
**Claim:** Discretized Adam with small step size α corresponds to a second-order inertial integro-differential equation (IDE) when expanded via second-order Taylor approximation.  
**Mechanism:** The discrete updates for θ, m, v are Taylor-expanded. The moments m and v obey linear ODEs with damping β₁, β₂; solving gives causal convolution kernels K_{β₁}, K_{β₂}. The parameter equation becomes (α/2)θ̈ + θ̇ = –η(t+α)·[m(t+α)/√(v(t+α)+ε)], with shift accounting for discrete-time causality.  
**Core assumption:** α small enough for second-order accuracy; initial moments zero.  
**Evidence:** Proposition 1 (p.5–6) gives IDE and kernel formulas; Appendix A bounds drift shift.  
**Break condition:** If α not small relative to Lipschitz constants or (1–β)⁻¹, truncation error dominates and IDE mismatch occurs.

### Mechanism 2: Perturbative link to first-order nonlocal flow.
**Claim:** The inertial IDE is an O(ρ) perturbation of the first-order nonlocal Adam flow on fixed horizons away from initial transients, with ρ = max{α, α²/(1–β₁)², α/(1–β₂)}.  
**Mechanism:** Compare bias-corrected continuous moments m^{(2)}, v^{(2)} with first-order convolutions m^{(1)}, v^{(1)}. Kernel differences bounded by C·(α/(1–β_a))² due to exponential tails. Inertial acceleration term (α/2)θ̈ is O(α) under uniform bound on ‖θ̈‖. Combine to obtain θ̇ = –μ·M₁ + R_ρ with ‖R_ρ‖ ≤ C_{δ}ρ.  
**Core assumption:** θ∈C³, g∘θ∈C³, uniform bound on ‖θ̈‖ over [δ–α₀,T]; M₂(t)≥0, ε≥ε₀>0.  
**Evidence:** Proposition 2 (p.9–11); Theorem 1 proof constructs perturbed Lyapunov inequality.  
**Break condition:** Requires smoothness along trajectory and uniform acceleration bound; near discontinuities or if α₀ ≤ δ, O(ρ) estimate may fail.

### Mechanism 3: Nonlocal Lagrangian blueprint conflicts with causality.
**Claim:** An Adam-inspired nonlocal Lagrangian exists, but strict Adam causality violates the weighted reciprocity needed for an exact variational principle; thus the Lagrangian is an ideal blueprint.  
**Mechanism:** The Lagrangian depends on translated trajectory T^tθ. Euler–Lagrange equations under reciprocity condition W(ξ) δF_i(T^ξθ,ξ)/δθ_j(σ) = W(σ) δF_j(T^σθ,σ)/δθ_i(ξ) yield inertial equation (α/2)θ̈ + θ̇ + a(t)F^i = 0. Adam’s force F^i uses strictly one-sided causal convolutions, so for ξ≠σ reciprocity fails because future perturbations do not affect past forces.  
**Core assumption:** Nonlocal force functional sufficiently smooth and satisfies reciprocity for all ξ,σ.  
**Evidence:** Section 4.2 and Proposition 5 (p.19–22) derive Lagrangian (22) and reciprocity (20); p.23 explains causality breaking reciprocity.  
**Break condition:** If Adam’s force were modified to be time-symmetric (e.g., symmetric kernels or include future), reciprocity might hold; but standard Adam’s strict causality inevitably breaks it.

## Foundational Learning

- **Causal convolution representation of exponential moving averages.** Adam’s moments are exponential moving averages; in continuous time they become convolution integrals with kernels K_β(s)∝ e^{-s/α}·f(s), encoding memory and causality. *Quick check:* For β<1/2 (underdamped), what property of K_β(s) can cause v(t) to become negative, and what sufficient condition (Lemma 1) prevents it?
- **Lyapunov stability and convergence via integral dissipation.** For the nonlocal IDE, Lyapunov functionals (e.g., V(t)=f(θ)–f_⋆ + (μ/(2λ₁))‖M₁‖²) provide dissipation estimates yielding convergence rates under PL/KL geometry. *Quick check:* In the PL case, what is the structure of the bound on Φ(t)=f(θ(t))–f_⋆ (exponential decay plus what plateau), and how does the plateau size scale with ρ?
- **Variational principles and functional derivatives in kinematic space.** The nonlocal Lagrangian uses translations T^tθ in function space; Euler–Lagrange equations involve functional derivatives δL/δθ(σ) and the reciprocity condition is a functional-analytic analogue of symmetric mixed partials. *Quick check:* In the proof of Proposition 5, why does a factor τ appear in the nonlocal part, and how does the reciprocity condition turn a τ–integral into a total τ–derivative?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Discretized ADAM → Taylor expansion → Continuous IDE with kernels → Causal convolution integrals → Inertial equation → Numerical solver (Picard iteration) → Stability analysis → Nonlocal Lagrangian construction.

**Critical path:**  
1. Implement IDE solver for chosen (β₁,β₂),α; validate kernel evaluation and memory convolution.  
2. Verify correctness by comparing discrete Adam trajectories at t_k.  
3. Check stability condition β₁≤√β₂ by sweeping parameters.  
4. Monitor v(t)≥0; apply projection if needed.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Memory vs. stability: Smaller β’s increase memory horizon but risk violating β₁≤√β₂ (instability). Use derived condition to choose hyperparameters.
- Step size α: Smaller α reduces ρ and improves match to discrete Adam but increases computational cost; affects kernel shape.
- Initial velocity u₀: Sensitive for large α but synchronizes quickly for small α; suggestion u₀=0 or momentum heuristic.

**Failure signatures:**
- Numerical instability: large oscillations in ˙θ or ¨θ, especially if β₁>√β₂.
- Moment negativity: v(t)<0 during iterations; must project v←max(v,0).
- Discrepancy with discrete Adam not decreasing with α: check α/(1-β₁)² term.
- Basin selection failure for c>2: ensure long enough integration.

**First experiments:**
1. **α-refinement validation:** For fixed (β₁,β₂)=(0.99,0.999), run discrete Adam and both continuous models on convex (c=0) and Rosenbrock (c>0) losses; compute errors E_T, E_{f,T} vs. α; confirm second-order error decays faster.
2. **Stability regime sweep:** Fix small α; vary β₁ across [0.5,0.99] and β₂ just above/below β₁²; monitor ‖θ̈‖∞ and kinetic energy to observe sustained oscillations when β₁>√β₂.
3. **Moment positivity check:** In underdamped β<1/2 regime, solve IDE with and without projection v←max(v,0); plot v(t) to verify projection maintains v≥0; also verify Lemma 1 condition along trajectory.

## Limitations
- Theoretical constants (e.g., c₀, C₀) are omitted, hindering quantitative validation of error plateaus and rates.
- Numerical solver details (tolerance, quadrature nodes, relaxation schedule) and initial velocity selection remain vague, jeopardizing reproducibility.
- Experiments confined to Rosenbrock functions; applicability to modern deep architectures remains speculative.
