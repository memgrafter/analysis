# On the Stability of Nonlinear Dynamics in GD and SGD: Beyond Quadratic Potentials

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14789
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14789
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Exact stability conditions for GD (stable period‑2 oscillations) and SGD (moment convergence) are derived using flip bifurcation analysis and Koopman theory, respectively, improving upon prior approximations.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines the stability of gradient descent (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD) near minima, emphasizing nonlinear effects beyond the usual quadratic approximation. For GD, the authors analyze a flip bifurcation to obtain a precise condition for stable period‑2 oscillations, showing that earlier conjectured hypotheses are only sufficient. For SGD, Koopman operator theory is employed to derive linear dynamics for moments; this leads to two key results: SGD diverges in expectation if any minibatch’s GD diverges superlinearly, and all even moments converge when the step size is below a bound involving the maximum Hessian eigenvalue across minibatches. The work unifies dynamical systems theory with optimization, providing new theoretical guarantees.

## Method Summary
For GD, the deterministic update \(x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta\nabla L(x_t)\) is studied near a critical point. By linearizing the update map, the authors examine when the leading eigenvalue of the Jacobian crosses –1, triggering a flip bifurcation. They compute the normal form using third‑ and fourth‑order directional derivatives along the top eigenvector, yielding an explicit inequality that distinguishes stable period‑2 orbits from instability. For SGD, the stochastic update \(x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta\nabla\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{B_t}(x_t)\) is analyzed via the Koopman operator, which linearly evolves observables (e.g., moments) of the state. This yields finite‑dimensional linear recursions for even moments; spectral analysis of the resulting matrix gives a step‑size condition for convergence and a divergence criterion when any minibatch’s GD behaves superlinearly.

## Key Results
- **GD period‑2 condition**: \(D^3L(v_{\max})^2 q > D^4L(v_{\max})^4\), where \(q = (\nabla^2 L)^{-1}\nabla_v D^3L(v)^3\) evaluated at the top eigenvector \(v_{\max}\); this is both necessary and sufficient, and it generalizes prior univariate sufficient conditions.
- **SGD divergence**: Expected iterates of SGD diverge if there exists any minibatch whose GD update diverges superlinearly (Theorem 5).
- **Even‑moment convergence**: For an interpolating minimizer, if \(\eta < \min_B 2/\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2\hat{\mathcal{E}}_B(x^*))\), then all even moments of SGD converge in a neighborhood of the optimum (Theorem 6).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The GD mechanism relies on the flip bifurcation: as \(\eta\) grows, the dominant eigenvalue of the GD Jacobian crosses –1, destroying the fixed point’s stability and creating a stable period‑2 orbit. The derived inequality compares the oscillatory effect of third‑order terms (which tend to create period‑2 cycles) against the stabilizing influence of fourth‑order terms (which can suppress them). For SGD, the Koopman operator linearizes the nonlinear stochastic dynamics by evolving expectations of observables. The moment recursions form a linear system; its spectral radius dictates stability. The divergence result holds because a superlinearly unstable batch forces the expected update to grow unbounded, regardless of averaging over other batches.

## Foundational Learning
- **Gradient Descent (GD)**: First‑order method using full‑batch gradients. *Why needed*: Baseline deterministic optimizer; stability analysis starts here. *Quick check*: For a quadratic \(L(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^TAx-b^Tx\), GD converges iff \(\eta < 2/\lambda_{\max}(A)\).
- **Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD)**: Uses random minibatch gradients; introduces noise. *Why needed*: Captures practical large‑scale training; analysis must account for batch variability. *Quick check*: In linear regression, SGD converges in expectation to the least‑squares solution when the design matrix is positive definite and \(\eta\) is small.
- **Flip Bifurcation**: A local bifurcation where a fixed point loses stability and a stable period‑2 orbit appears when an eigenvalue crosses –1. *Why needed*: Explains onset of stable oscillations in GD beyond linear stability. *Quick check*: The 1D map \(x_{t+1}=\mu x_t - x_t^3\) undergoes a flip bifurcation at \(\mu=-1\).
- **Koopman Operator**: Linear operator \(\mathcal{K}\) acting on observables via \(\mathcal{K}g = g\circ F\). *Why needed*: Transforms nonlinear dynamics (SGD) into linear dynamics on an infinite‑dimensional space, enabling spectral analysis of moments. *Quick check*: For a linear system \(x_{t+1}=Ax_t\), the Koopman operator on quadratic monomials corresponds to \(x_tx_t^\top \mapsto A x_tx_t^\top A^\top\).
- **Moment Dynamics**: Evolution of statistical moments (mean, variance, etc.) under SGD updates. *Why needed*: Provides finite linear system for SGD when considering even moments; stability determined by eigenvalues. *Quick check*: For constant‑step SGD on a linear model with squared loss, weight mean and variance obey linear recursions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Loss \(L\) → Gradient \(\nabla L\) → GD update map \(x\leftarrow x-\eta\nabla L\) → Linearization (Jacobian \(J\)) → Eigenvalue analysis (\(\lambda_{\max}\)) → Top eigenvector \(v_{\max}\) → Directional derivatives \(D^3L(v_{\max})\), \(D^4L(v_{\max})\) → Bifurcation condition. For SGD: Batch losses \(\hat{\mathcal{E}}_B\) → Stochastic update → Koopman operator \(\mathcal{K}\) on moment observables → Linear moment system \(M_{t+1}=A M_t\) → Spectral radius \(\rho(A)\) → Step‑size bound.
- **Critical path**:  
  GD: (i) Identify non‑degenerate critical point \(x^*\). (ii) Compute Hessian \(\nabla^2 L(x^*)\) and its eigendecomposition. (iii) Isolate top eigenvector \(v_{\max}\). (iv) Evaluate third‑ and fourth‑order directional derivatives along \(v_{\max}\). (v) Derive inequality \(D^3L(v_{\max})^2 q > D^4L(v_{\max})^4\) for stable period‑2. (vi) Show prior hypothesis is only sufficient.  
  SGD: (i) Assume an interpolating minimizer common to all minibatches. (ii) Construct Koopman operator on even moments. (iii) Obtain matrix representation \(A\). (iv) Relate eigenvalues of \(A\) to step size \(\eta\) and Hessian eigenvalues \(\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2\hat{\mathcal{E}}_B(x^*))\). (v) Derive convergence condition for even moments. (vi) Prove divergence if any batch GD diverges superlinearly.
- **Design tradeoffs**: The analysis assumes high smoothness (GD: \(C^4\); SGD: \(C^2\) plus Lipschitz gradients) and a positive‑definite Hessian at the optimum, enabling Taylor expansions and inversion. GD also requires a distinct largest eigenvalue (non‑degenerate) for a clean normal form; SGD requires all minibatches to admit the same interpolating minimizer and i.i.d. sampling. These assumptions trade realism for analytical tractability; many real‑world scenarios (flat directions, non‑interpolating solutions, nonsmooth activations) violate them, but the results offer a valuable theoretical baseline.
- **Failure signatures**: If the Hessian has repeated eigenvalues, the GD bifurcation may involve higher‑dimensional normal forms and the simple condition may no longer apply. Nonsmooth losses (e.g., ReLU) can break the existence of directional derivatives. For SGD, a minibatch whose loss induces a region of negative curvature can cause GD to diverge superlinearly; then the expected SGD iterates diverge. Exceeding the step‑size bound \(\eta < \min_B 2/\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2\hat{\mathcal{E}}_B(x^*))\) leads to growth of even moments, indicating instability.
- **First experiments**:  
  1. **GD bifurcation test**: Use a 2D loss \(L(x,y)=\lambda_1 x^2 + \lambda_2 y^2 + \alpha x^3 + \beta x^4\) with \(\lambda_1>\lambda_2>0\). Compute the condition along the top eigenvector (x‑axis), vary \(\eta\), and verify stable period‑2 cycles appear exactly when \(D^3L^2 q > D^4L^4\).  
  2. **SGD divergence test**: Construct two minibatches: one from a strongly convex quadratic (stable GD) and one with an added quartic term that makes GD diverge for certain \(\eta\). Run SGD by randomly sampling batches and track the expected distance from the optimum; confirm divergence when the unstable batch is present and its GD diverges superlinearly.  
  3. **Even‑moment convergence**: In an overparameterized linear regression setting (one data point per batch), compute \(\eta^* = \min_B 2/\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2\hat{\mathcal{E}}_B(x^*))\). Run SGD with \(\eta\) just below and above \(\eta^*\) and measure the variance of the iterates (an even moment); observe convergence for \(\eta<\eta^*\) and divergence for \(\eta>\eta^*\).

## Limitations
- Assumptions of smoothness and non‑degenerate Hessian may not hold in complex deep learning landscapes.
- The GD stability condition requires evaluating high‑order derivatives and the top eigenvector, which is intractable for large models.
- SGD analysis relies on i.i.d. minibatches and an interpolating minimizer, both often unrealistic in practice.

## Confidence
- GD stable period‑2 criterion: Medium
- SGD divergence if any batch GD diverges superlinearly: Low
- SGD even‑moment convergence under step‑size bound: Medium
- Koopman linear moment dynamics: Medium
