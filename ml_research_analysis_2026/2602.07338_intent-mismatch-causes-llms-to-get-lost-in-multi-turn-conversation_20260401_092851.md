# Intent Mismatch Causes LLMs to Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07338  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07338  
- **Reference count:** 17  
- **Primary result:** A Mediator-Assistant framework recovers roughly 20 percentage points of average performance loss in multi-turn dialogues by reconstructing latent user intent from ambiguous follow-ups.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies the "Lost in Conversation" (LiC) phenomenon, where large language models (LLMs) exhibit significant performance drops in multi-turn dialogues due to misinterpreting vague user follow-up queries. It argues that the root cause is an **intent alignment gap**—a structural ambiguity in mapping noisy conversational context to the user's true intent—rather than a deficit in model capability. Consequently, scaling model size alone cannot resolve this issue. To address it, the authors propose a **Mediator-Assistant framework**. An experience-driven **Refiner** distills historical contrastive conversation pairs (failed vs. successful trajectories) into concise pragmatic guidelines. These guidelines steer a **Mediator** to rewrite ambiguous multi-turn contexts into explicit, fully-specified instructions before execution by an **Assistant**. Experiments across Code, Database, Actions, and Math tasks with GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5.2, and DeepSeek-v3.2-Thinking show substantial recovery: average performance improves by ~20 percentage points and reliability by 15 points over a sharded baseline. For example, GPT-5.2’s performance rises from 48.5% to 72.6%. The method also outperforms memory-based approaches like Mem0 by 17.4% on average, demonstrating that **reconstructing latent intent** is more critical than merely recalling facts.

## Method Summary
The core method introduces a two-stage Mediator-Assistant architecture. First, an **experience-driven Refiner** analyzes a small set of contrastive multi-turn conversation pairs—each consisting of a failed trajectory and a successful correction—to extract high-level pragmatic guidelines (e.g., "disambiguate referential pronouns," "explicitly restate incomplete constraints"). These guidelines are derived in a few-shot, non-parametric manner without model fine-tuning. Second, a **Mediator** LLM uses the distilled guidelines to rewrite the user’s ambiguous multi-turn context (which may include vague follow-ups like "change it to the other one") into a clear, self-contained instruction that explicitly states the inferred true intent. This rewritten instruction is then passed to an **Assistant** LLM for task execution. The approach does not rely on memory retrieval of past turns; instead, it focuses on **intent reconstruction** to resolve the structural ambiguity at the root of LiC. The Refiner’s guidelines are task-agnostic in form but tailored per task cluster (Code, Database, etc.) using domain-specific examples.

## Key Results
- **Large performance recovery:** Across three models (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5.2, DeepSeek-v3.2-Thinking) and four task clusters, the framework recovers an average of ~20 percentage points in accuracy and improves execution reliability by ~15 points compared to a sharded baseline that processes each turn independently.
- **Significant absolute gains:** For GPT-5.2, accuracy jumps from 48.5% to 72.6% on the LiC benchmark.
- **Superior to memory augmentation:** The method outperforms state-of-the-art memory-augmented techniques (e.g., Mem0) by an average of 17.4%, highlighting that intent reconstruction is more effective than factual recall for handling ambiguous follow-ups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper posits that LiC stems from an **intent alignment gap**: the user’s true intent \( I_t \) is not a deterministic function of the noisy context \( C_t \) (the conversation history with ambiguous follow-ups), leading to misalignment \( P(I_t | C_t) \). Scaling model size does not close this gap because it is a **structural ambiguity** in the input distribution, not a lack of parametric capacity. The Mediator directly addresses this by **reconstructing \( I_t \)** from \( C_t \) using distilled pragmatic guidelines. These guidelines encode how successful trajectories resolved similar ambiguities, effectively teaching the Mediator to "fill in the blanks" of user intent. This transforms the problem from one of capability (executing ambiguous instructions) to one of alignment (creating explicit instructions), which is solvable even with smaller models. The success over memory-based methods confirms that simply recalling past context is insufficient; the latent intent must be **explicitly inferred and stated**.

## Foundational Learning
- **Intent Gap vs. Capability Deficit:** Distinguishes between performance loss due to misaligned input interpretation (structural) versus insufficient model knowledge or reasoning (parametric).  
  *Why needed:* Correctly diagnosing LiC as an alignment issue redirects solutions toward intent reconstruction rather than scaling.  
  *Quick check:* If scaling alone fixed LiC, larger models would show monotonic gains on the LiC benchmark; the paper shows this is not the case.

- **Mediator-Assistant Decoupling:** Separates the roles of intent reconstruction (Mediator) and task execution (Assistant).  
  *Why needed:* Isolates the alignment subproblem, allowing each component to be optimized independently.  
  *Quick check:* Does adding a mediator layer that rewrites inputs consistently improve downstream execution across model sizes?

- **Experience-driven Refiner:** A module that distills pragmatic guidelines from contrastive conversation pairs (failed vs. successful).  
  *Why needed:* Automates the extraction of human-like heuristics for disambiguation without requiring labeled intent data.  
  *Quick check:* Do guidelines generated from a small set of pairs capture patterns that generalize to unseen ambiguous queries?

- **Contrastive Pragmatic Guidelines:** Concise, actionable rules (e.g., "always specify the target entity explicitly") derived from comparing negative and positive trajectories.  
  *Why needed:* Encodes tacit knowledge about how users successfully convey intent in multi-turn settings.  
  *Quick check:* Are these guidelines more effective than generic dialogue act taxonomies for ambiguous follow-up resolution?

- **Sufficient Statistic Assumption:** The theoretical simplification that true intent \( I_t \) is sufficient for task execution, i.e., \( P_\theta(R|I_t) \approx P_\theta(R|I_t, C_t) \).  
  *Why needed:* Justifies focusing reconstruction solely on intent rather than full context.  
  *Quick check:* For which tasks does execution remain dependent on contextual history beyond inferred intent?

- **Benchmark Diversity Need:** Recognition that current multi-turn benchmarks (like LiC) have homogeneous user logic, limiting evaluation of real-world generalization.  
  *Why needed:* Rigorous assessment of intent alignment frameworks requires varied user expression patterns.  
  *Quick check:* Do models trained on one benchmark’s ambiguity types fail on others with different ambiguity structures?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  User (ambiguous multi-turn context) -> Refiner (distills guidelines from contrastive pairs) -> Mediator (rewrites context using guidelines) -> Assistant (executes rewritten instruction)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Receive ambiguous user input with follow-up history.  
  2. Refiner extracts pragmatic guidelines from stored contrastive pairs.  
  3. Mediator applies guidelines to generate an explicit, self-contained instruction.  
  4. Assistant executes the instruction to produce final response.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Latency vs. accuracy:** Adding Mediator introduces computational overhead but significantly improves correctness.  
  - **Guideline specificity vs. overfitting:** Few-shot guidelines may be too coarse for nuanced ambiguities or too narrow if derived from limited examples.  
  - **Non-parametric vs. parameterized:** Current Refiner is in-context; moving to fine-tuning could improve scalability but risks losing interpretability.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Mediator output remains ambiguous or introduces new errors.  
  - Guidelines fail to cover novel ambiguity types (e.g., cultural references, sarcasm).  
  - High latency in real-time systems due to sequential processing.

- **First experiments:**  
  1. **Cross-model validation:** Test the framework on architectures beyond GPT and DeepSeek (e.g., Claude 3.5, Gemini 2.0) to check robustness to model-specific quirks.  
  2. **Ablation of Refiner:** Replace experience-driven guidelines with hand-crafted dialogue act taxonomies to quantify the unique value of learned contrastive patterns.  
  3. **Human-intent alignment study:** Have human raters judge whether Mediator-rewritten instructions match the latent user intent in 200 ambiguous follow-ups, comparing against raw context and memory-augmented baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the experience-driven Refiner be scaled to utilize larger datasets for parameterized training, enabling it to internalize more nuanced alignment strategies beyond heuristic guidelines?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations section (Page 7) states: "Future work could leverage larger-scale datasets to transition towards parameterized training, enabling the Mediator to internalize more nuanced alignment strategies via fine-tuning rather than relying solely on in-context summaries."
- **Why unresolved:** The current Refiner operates in a few-shot, non-parametric manner, extracting only coarse-grained interaction patterns from limited contrastive pairs, which may not capture subtle or diverse user pragmatics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments with scaled-up contrastive pair datasets, implementing fine-tuning for the Refiner or Mediator, and evaluating performance gains on multi-turn benchmarks with varied user behaviors to assess improvements in intent reconstruction.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the essential characteristics of comprehensive multi-turn benchmarks that accurately reflect complex and heterogeneous user behaviors, and how can such benchmarks be constructed to rigorously evaluate intent alignment frameworks?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations section (Page 7) notes: "the multi-turn settings in current benchmarks exhibit relatively homogeneous user logic. Developing more comprehensive benchmarks that mirror complex user behaviors remains a critical direction to further validate and evolve our framework."
- **Why unresolved:** Existing benchmarks, like the LiC benchmark, have limited diversity in user expression patterns and intent revelation strategies, potentially failing to capture real-world variability and edge cases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Designing new benchmarks with diverse user personas, ambiguous query types, and longitudinal interaction logs; using these to test the Mediator's adaptability, generalizability, and robustness across different user groups and domains.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the assumption that user intent (It) is a sufficient statistic for task execution (i.e., Pθ(R|It) ≈ Pθ(R|It, Ct)) universally valid across all multi-turn dialogue tasks, or are there scenarios where contextual history beyond intent is indispensable?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 3.1 (Page 3) assumes: "assuming that the true intent It is a sufficient statistic for the task such that R becomes conditionally independent of the noisy context Ct given It." This underpins their theoretical decomposition.
- **Why unresolved:** This simplification may not hold for tasks requiring dynamic context integration, such as maintaining state across turns, handling referential dependencies, or tasks where execution depends on iterative refinements not fully captured by intent alone.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies on tasks known to rely heavily on context history (e.g., long-form storytelling, multi-step planning), comparing performance when only reconstructed intent is provided versus full context; analyzing failure cases where intent alignment succeeds but execution fails due to missing contextual cues.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent do the distilled pragmatic guidelines from the Refiner generalize across different user populations, domains, and model architectures, and how can potential biases in the few-shot examples be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** The method uses a Refiner to distill guidelines from a small set of contrastive pairs (Pages 5-6), with only 5 instances per task for few-shot learning (Page A.2), risking overfitting to specific patterns or lacking diversity.
- **Why unresolved:** Guidelines derived from limited data may not capture the full spectrum of user behaviors or may be biased toward the particular examples used, affecting transferability to new users, tasks, or models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Cross-validation with diverse user cohorts, testing on unseen domains, measuring performance consistency when applying guidelines from one user to another, and analyzing guideline variability across multiple Refiner runs to assess robustness and bias.

## Limitations
- **Domain and model scope:** Evaluation confined to four task clusters (Code, Database, Actions, Math) and three specific models; generalizability to open-domain social dialogues, creative tasks, or newer architectures (e.g., Claude 3.5, Gemini 2.0) is unknown.
- **Refiner data limitations:** The few-shot Refiner uses only 5 contrastive pairs per task, which may not capture the full spectrum of real-world intent ambiguity, especially in culturally nuanced or highly subjective conversations.
- **Computational overhead:** Latency and resource costs introduced by the Mediator layer in production systems are unreported, leaving practical deployment feasibility uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Root Cause (Intent Gap over Capability Deficit):** Medium – Supported by ablation showing size scaling fails, but causal isolation from other factors (e.g., prompt engineering) is not definitive.
- **Performance Recovery (~20 p.p. avg.):** High – Consistent gains across three models and four task clusters with clear baselines.
- **Generalizability Beyond Tested Tasks:** Low – No evidence of transfer to non-tool-use or purely social contexts.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Domain Benchmarking:** Replicate on at least two additional multi-turn benchmarks (e.g., MultiWOZ 2.4 for task-oriented dialogues, Social-IQa for social reasoning) to test scope limitations.
2. **Guideline Transfer Ablation:** Remove the experience-driven Refiner and substitute with hand-crafted dialogue act taxonomies to quantify the unique value of learned contrastive guidelines.
3. **Human-Intent Alignment Study:** Conduct a human evaluation where raters judge whether Mediator-rewritten instructions match the latent user intent in 200 ambiguous follow-ups, comparing against raw context and memory-augmented baselines.
