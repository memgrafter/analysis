# Netflix Artwork Personalization via LLM Post-training

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2601.02764
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02764
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: SFT with reasoning distillation from Qwen-32B yields a 5.21% improvement in inverse propensity score (IPS) over the Netflix production model on a 5K held-out user-title evaluation set.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of selecting personalized artwork for Netflix titles, where user preferences for visual representations vary widely. The core method post-trains Llama 3.1 8B models using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), with inputs comprising user interaction histories and text captions of candidate artwork images. A key innovation is reasoning distillation: a larger model (Qwen-32B) generates natural language explanations for why the optimal artwork suits a given user, and these explanations are used to augment the training data. This approach yields a 5.21% IPS gain over the production baseline, with consistent 3–5% improvements across post-trained variants. Although accuracy gains are more modest, IPS is the primary evaluation metric as it properly accounts for the varying number of artwork candidates per title.

## Method Summary
The system converts each candidate artwork image into a detailed textual caption (~200 tokens) using a fine-tuned vision-language model (Llama-3.2-11B). The user’s interaction history, the title description, and all candidate captions are concatenated into a prompt for a base LLM (Llama 3.1 8B). Two post‑training strategies are explored: (1) standard SFT on tuples of (user history, title, optimal caption), and (2) SFT augmented with reasoning, where the prompt prefix includes an explanation generated by Qwen-32B for why that optimal caption matches the user. Additionally, DPO is applied to contrast the optimal caption against randomly sampled negative captions from the same candidate set. At inference, the model generates text output; the selected artwork is identified via n‑gram matching between the generated text and the list of candidate captions.

## Key Results
- The main model (SFT with reasoning distillation) achieves **5.21% IPS improvement** over the Netflix production baseline on a 5,000‑example held‑out set.
- All post‑trained variants show consistent gains of **3–5% in IPS** compared to the baseline.
- Accuracy improvements are smaller; IPS is prioritized because it weights correct predictions by the inverse of the candidate set size, reflecting real‑world difficulty.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Reasoning Distillation from a Larger Model
**Claim**: Post‑training with reasoning distillation from Qwen‑32B improves personalized selection by teaching the smaller model to generate explanations that align with user preferences.  
**Mechanism**: For each training example, the ground‑truth optimal artwork is provided to Qwen‑32B, which generates a rationale explaining why that artwork suits the user based on their history and title. These rationales are prepended to the model’s output during SFT, so the smaller model learns to produce a justification followed by the prediction. The core assumption is that these high‑quality, human‑like explanations encode preference logic that the smaller model can emulate, even if its raw prediction ability is lower.  
**Evidence**: The abstract and Sec. 4.2 describe the augmentation of training data with “Reason: …” prefixes from Qwen‑32B and report the main 5.21% IPS gain from SFT‑with‑reasoning.  
**Break condition**: If the smaller model merely mimics the reasoning format without internalizing the preference logic, or if the reasoning introduces spurious correlations not present in the raw preference labels.

### Mechanism 2: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
**Claim**: DPO improves performance by explicitly training the model to distinguish between preferred and rejected artwork options through a contrastive objective, rather than only maximizing the likelihood of positive examples.  
**Mechanism**: For each user‑title pair, a preference pair is constructed where the chosen response is the ground‑truth optimal caption and the rejected response is a randomly selected alternative caption from the same candidate set. The DPO loss increases the relative likelihood of the chosen caption over the rejected one, pulling the model’s policy away from suboptimal options. The assumption is that random negatives, while not guaranteed to be truly disliked, are sufficiently distinguishable from the optimal choice to provide a useful learning signal.  
**Evidence**: Sec. 4.2 outlines the DPO loss formulation; Table 1 shows DPO yields +2.82% IPS improvement, less than the +5.40% from SFT‑with‑reasoning.  
**Break condition**: If the randomly selected negatives are not meaningfully worse for the user (e.g., they are also highly appealing), the contrastive signal is weak.

### Mechanism 3: Multimodal‑to‑Text Representation
**Claim**: Representing artworks via text captions generated by a fine‑tuned vision‑language model enables scalable, language‑based personalization by converting a multimodal preference problem into a text‑only one that standard LLMs can process.  
**Mechanism**: Each artwork image is passed through a VLM (Llama‑3.2‑11B) to produce a detailed textual caption. The LLM’s prompt then concatenates the user’s interaction history, the title, and these captions (delimited by <option> tags). The model’s task reduces to selecting the caption that best matches the user profile, leveraging its inherent text comprehension and world knowledge.  
**Evidence**: Sec. 4.1 highlights that caption representation “enables all multimodal inputs to be expressed in text” and scales to 40+ candidates.  
**Break condition**: If caption generation fails to capture key visual attributes relevant to user preference (e.g., color palette, composition), or if the LLM cannot effectively map caption semantics to the user’s taste dimensions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Inverse Propensity Score (IPS)**  
  *Why needed*: IPS accounts for the varying number of artwork candidates per title (4 to 40+). It weights a correct prediction by the inverse of the probability that the chosen artwork would be shown (assumed uniform), thus properly penalizing easy selections from small sets and rewarding difficult selections from large sets.  
  *Quick check*: Why does a correct pick from 40 candidates contribute more to IPS than a correct pick from 2 candidates? Because the inverse propensity weight is larger (1/40 vs. 1/2), reflecting greater difficulty and business impact.

- **Supervised Fine‑Tuning (SFT) with Reasoning Distillation**  
  *Why needed*: Standard SFT trains the model to mimic (user, title, optimal caption) pairs. Reasoning distillation augments each example with an explanation from a larger model (Qwen‑32B), training the smaller model to generate a justification before its prediction. This encourages internalization of preference rationale, not just the outcome.  
  *Quick check*: Why might SFT with reasoning outperform standard SFT even if the larger model’s raw prediction accuracy is lower? Because the reasoning provides a structured, human‑like explanation of the preference logic that the smaller model can learn to emulate, improving its decision‑making beyond simple imitation.

- **Multimodal‑to‑Text Grounding for Personalization**  
  *Why needed*: The task requires comparing user preferences (from text‑based interaction history) against visual content. Using captions as a textual proxy allows leveraging powerful text‑only LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B) without needing expensive multimodal architectures at inference.  
  *Quick check*: What is a key risk of relying on automatically generated image captions for this personalization task? Captions may lose fine‑grained visual attributes (e.g., color, composition, specific actors), limiting the LLM’s ability to reason about user‑relevant visual cues.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
User History Encoder → Prompt Formatter → Artwork Caption Generator (offline) → Post‑training Engine (SFT/DPO with LoRA) → Inference Scorer (generation + n‑gram match)

**Critical path**  
Raw user logs and artwork images → Caption generation (offline, pre‑computed) → Prompt construction (per user‑title pair) → Post‑training (SFT/DPO) → Validation/checkpoint selection → Inference (prompt → generation → n‑gram match to candidate captions). Caption generation is a separate offline pipeline; its quality directly impacts all downstream stages.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Caption fidelity vs. length*: Detailed captions preserve nuance but increase prompt length and may include irrelevant details; simpler captions are efficient but risk losing key preference signals (e.g., “hero shot” vs. “group scene”).  
- *Output format*: Text caption prediction vs. integer option number. Text is semantically direct but sensitive to n‑gram matching errors; integer output may suffer from positional bias (model predicts low numbers), as observed in Fig. 2.  
- *Post‑training strategy*: SFT with reasoning gives best IPS (5.21%) but requires a powerful teacher and filtering; DPO is conceptually cleaner for contrast but showed lower gains (+2.82%) and may suffer from noisy negative examples.

**Failure signatures**  
- *Systemic output bias*: Model consistently predicts artworks with lower option numbers or certain themes regardless of user history.  
- *Poor generalization on high‑variation sets*: Performance drops significantly on titles with >20 artwork candidates, as IPS weighting amplifies errors.  
- *Reasoning degradation*: During SFT+Reasoning, if the model generates plausible‑sounding but preference‑inconsistent reasons, accuracy may degrade despite better training loss.  
- *DPO plateau*: DPO fails to improve over SFT, indicating negative examples are not sufficiently informative (Table 4: DPO from SFT‑reasoning yields +2.82% IPS, less than SFT‑reasoning’s +5.40%).

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Replicate SFT baseline**: Train Llama 3.1 8B on the 110K (user, title, optimal caption) tuples with the standard prompt (no reasoning). Validate IPS gain vs. production model; expect ~+2.45%.  
2. **Output format ablation**: For a fixed 8B model (zero‑shot or SFT), compare performance when trained/evaluated with text caption output vs. integer option number output. Measure bias via accuracy breakdown by ground‑truth label number (expect text format avoids the low‑number bias seen in Fig. 2).  
3. **Reasoning data quality check**: For the SFT+Reasoning pipeline, analyze the 2% filtered‑out Qwen generations (where reasoning didn’t match the optimal caption). Qualitatively assess whether failures are due to nonsensical reasons or logically sound but incorrect preferences; this informs whether the teacher’s reasoning is reliably aligned with ground truth.

## Limitations
- Reasoning generalization: The smaller model may mimic the reasoning format without truly internalizing the preference logic, risking spurious correlations.  
- Negative example quality: DPO uses random rejections that may not be truly suboptimal, weakening the contrastive signal.  
- Caption fidelity dependency: The multimodal‑to‑text conversion might lose fine‑grained visual attributes, limiting the LLM’s reasoning capacity.

## Confidence
- **Reasoning Distillation Efficacy**: Medium  
- **DPO Improvement**: Low  
- **Caption‑Based Representation**: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Reasoning Generalization Test**: Replace Qwen‑32B rationales with rule‑based or paraphrased nonsense; measure SFT performance drop relative to full reasoning data. A significant drop confirms reliance on semantic quality.  
2. **Negative Example Audit**: Sample 500 DPO rejected artworks, have human annotators score alignment with user history (1–5). Compute correlation between human scores and model’s DPO loss; low correlation confirms noisy negatives.  
3. **Caption Fidelity Analysis**: For a subset of artworks, compute alignment between fine‑tuned VLM captions and ground‑truth human annotations of key visual attributes (e.g., “action intensity”, “character proximity”). Correlate attribute retention with model accuracy to isolate caption loss impact.
