# Multimodal Learning for Arcing Detection in Pantograph-Catenary Systems
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08792
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08792
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MultiDeepSAD achieves AUROC 93.49% on SBB-AD and 95.03% on Open-AD, significantly outperforming unimodal and classical methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the detection of rare, transient electrical arcing in pantograph-catenary systems—a challenge compounded by noisy operational conditions and severe scarcity of labeled anomalies. The authors introduce MultiDeepSAD, a semi-supervised anomaly detector that fuses synchronized high-speed camera images and contact force measurements. Key innovations include an exponential loss variant for stable optimization and modality-specific pseudo-anomaly generation: arc patches are pasted onto normal images, and force signals are synthesized by mixing normal and abnormal patterns. Evaluated on two datasets (SBB-AD from Swiss Federal Railways and Open-AD from public sources), the model achieves near-perfect AUROC scores, demonstrates robustness to visual corruptions, and maintains high performance with minimal real anomaly supervision.

## Method Summary
The framework leverages two datasets: SBB-AD (3,107 training, 328 test) and Open-AD (3,800 training, 484 test). Each sample comprises a 1-second image sequence and a concurrent 1-second force signal sampled at 500 Hz. The force signal is preprocessed via FFT to a 250-dimensional magnitude spectrum. Training uses all available normal data and a small set of labeled anomalies, augmented with pseudo-anomalies: for images, arc regions cropped from real anomalies are pasted onto normal images; for force, synthetic anomalies are created by linearly mixing normal and abnormal signals (λ · a_abnormal + (1–λ) · a_normal). The architecture employs a ResNet-18 image encoder and a 2-layer MLP force encoder; their features are concatenated and passed through a fusion MLP to produce an embedding ϕ(x). The loss encourages normal embeddings to cluster tightly around a learnable center c while pushing anomaly embeddings outward using an exponential term exp(–‖ϕ(x)–c‖²) for stabilization. At inference, the anomaly score is the squared Euclidean distance to the final center c.

## Key Results
- MultiDeepSAD achieves AUROC 93.49% on SBB-AD and 95.03% on Open-AD, substantially outperforming image-only (89.43%/91.64%) and force-only (70.66%/83.30%) variants.
- The exponential loss improves over the original DeepSAD loss (91.16% vs. 93.49% AUROC on SBB-AD), confirming more stable optimization.
- Ablations validate the critical role of multimodal fusion, robustness to image corruptions (e.g., fog), and effective pseudo-anomaly generation even with as few as one real anomaly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Smoothing Gradient Dynamics via Exponential Loss
Replacing the original DeepSAD anomaly penalty (‖ϕ(x)–c‖²)⁻¹ with exp(–‖ϕ(x)–c‖²) prevents gradient explosion from anomalous samples mapped very near the center c. The original penalty is unbounded as distance approaches zero, causing large gradients that can dominate training. The exponential term is bounded (≤ 1) and yields gentle gradients for samples near c, naturally down-weighting “easy” anomalies while focusing learning on boundary samples. This yields a more balanced hypersphere. Evidence: [Section 4.3] states the exponential formulation provides smoother optimization and prevents single hard samples from dominating; [Section 5, Table 1] shows MultiDeepSAD (exponential) outperforms MultiDeepSAD* (original loss) on both datasets. Break condition: If pseudo-anomalies are generated with feature vectors extremely close to the normal cluster, the exponential penalty may provide insufficient pushing force.

### Mechanism 2: Modality-Specific Pseudo-Anomaly Synthesis
Generating pseudo-anomalies by pasting real arc patches onto normal images and mixing normal/abnormal force signals effectively augments the scarce real anomaly set. For images, cropping the luminous arc region from a real anomaly and pasting it onto a normal pantograph preserves the key visual signature. For force, linear interpolation between normal and abnormal signals blends their temporal‑frequency characteristics, simulating the continuous spectrum of arcing severity. Core assumptions: arc appearance is localized and transferable across backgrounds; force anomalies are linearly mixable after FFT. Evidence: [Section 4.3] details the generation strategies; [Section 5, Table 4] shows robust performance with only one real anomaly; [Section 5, Table 6] outperforms generic Mixup. Break conditions: (Image) if arc appearance is highly context‑dependent, pasting may create unrealistic artifacts; (Force) if arcing involves non‑linear dynamics, linear mixing may fail.

### Mechanism 3: Complementary Information Fusion in Latent Space
Fusing image (spatial/luminous arc signature) and force (temporal/mechanical disturbance) modalities in a late‑fusion architecture captures complementary failure modes, reducing false positives from unimodal noise. Each modality encodes distinct physical aspects; normal samples from both project near a shared center, while anomalies deviate in at least one modality. Evidence: [Abstract] notes fusion provides complementary information; [Section 5, Table 2] shows multimodal significantly outperforms unimodal; [Section 5, Table 3] demonstrates robustness to image corruptions when force is available. Break condition: If a dominant noise source (e.g., mechanical fault or camera artifact) consistently affects both modalities during normal operation, separation may degrade.

## Foundational Learning
- **Deep Semi‑Supervised Anomaly Detection (DeepSAD):** Extends DeepSVDD by jointly learning a deep representation and a hypersphere center that encloses normal data while pushing labeled anomalies outward. *Why needed:* MultiDeepSAD is a direct extension to multimodal inputs; understanding the hypersphere objective is prerequisite. *Quick check:* What is the role of the center c in the DeepSAD objective, and how does the anomaly term modify optimization compared to DeepSVDD?
- **Pseudo‑Anomaly Generation for Low‑Shot Detection:** Strategy of synthesizing anomalous samples from limited real examples to augment training. *Why needed:* The framework’s effectiveness hinges on this augmentation; it requires understanding the rationale (augmenting D_a) and risks (unrealistic samples). *Quick check:* Why might pasting arc patches be more suitable than Gaussian noise injection for this task?
- **Modality‑Specific Feature Engineering:** Transforming raw sensor data into representations suited for deep models (e.g., FFT for force signals). *Why needed:* Raw 1‑D force signals are not directly fed to a CNN; FFT aligns better with anomaly characteristics. *Quick check:* According to Table 5, why did FFT outperform raw signal and other time‑series encodings for the force modality?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Image -> ResNet-18 -> F_img ; Force -> FFT -> Force MLP -> F_force ; [F_img; F_force] -> Fusion MLP -> ϕ(x) ; ϕ(x) -> ‖ϕ(x)-c‖² -> Loss
- **Critical path:** Training: Inputs processed by modality‑specific encoders, features concatenated, final embedding computed, distance to center c calculated, loss applied, network updated, center updated via running mean of normal embeddings. Inference: identical forward pass; anomaly score = squared distance to final c; threshold set via validation quantiles.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Fusion strategy: Late fusion (concatenation + MLP) beats weighted, gated, and attention fusion (Table 7). Simplicity wins; complex gating may overfit on limited anomalies.
  - Force representation: FFT chosen over raw, wavelet, recurrence plots (Table 5). FFT is simple, fast, captures dominant frequency bands linked to arcing vibrations, but discards phase information.
  - Pseudo‑anomaly source: Using real arc patches (image) and real abnormal patterns (force) is superior to generic Mixup. Requires at least a few real anomalies to seed generation; performance drops sharply if only one is used for force mixing.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Force modality collapse: If FFT input is misconfigured (not normalized, wrong half‑spectrum), force encoder learns nothing; AUROC drops to ~70%.
  - Pseudo‑anomaly dilution: If Beta α for Mixup is biased, generated samples are too similar to normal or real abnormal, failing to create informative boundary samples.
  - Center c instability: If c is not re‑initialized periodically or if anomaly batch has extreme outliers, the hypersphere may drift. Monitor average ‖ϕ(x_normal)–c‖ on held‑out normal batch.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Unimodal baselines & ablation: Train image‑only, force‑only, and full multimodal variants. Expected: multimodal > unimodal (replicate Table 2).
  2. Pseudo‑anomaly generation ablation: Compare no pseudo‑anomalies, Mixup for both, proposed method. Expected: proposed > Mixup > none (replicate Table 6 logic).
  3. Corruption robustness check: Apply Level‑3 fog corruption to test images only. A robust model will see less than ~25% drop from clean (Table 3 shows ~26% drop on SBB‑AD).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the proposed MultiDeepSAD framework be efficiently deployed for real‑time, on‑train monitoring given its current computational requirements and the need for synchronized multi‑sensor data?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "Future work will explore real‑time deployment on operational trains... and develop lightweight cumulative damage modeling for use on resource‑constrained embedded platforms."
- **Why unresolved:** The current model (ResNet‑18 + MLP) and processing pipeline (FFT for force) are not explicitly optimized for edge deployment. Real‑world constraints include latency, power consumption, and sensor synchronization robustness on moving trains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarking inference latency and memory footprint on embedded hardware (e.g., Jetson Nano), testing robustness to imperfect sensor synchronization, and developing a compressed/pruned model variant that maintains >90% AUROC.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do more sophisticated multimodal fusion mechanisms (e.g., cross‑attention, transformers) offer significant advantages over the simple late fusion (concatenation + MLP) found optimal in this study, particularly under domain shift or with noisier modalities?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablation studies (Tab. 7) show late fusion outperforms weighted, gated, and attention‑based fusion. However, the tested attention fusion was a simple sample‑weighting mechanism, not a full cross‑modal attention architecture.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluated a basic attention fusion. More complex, learned interactions between image and force feature spaces might capture subtler complementary cues, especially when one modality is degraded.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and evaluate state‑of‑the‑art multimodal fusion techniques (e.g., Transformer encoders, modality‑specific query/key/value attention) on both datasets, including corrupted‑image and synthetic‑domain test sets, to measure AUROC improvements and analyze learned attention maps.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the model's performance depend on the realism and diversity of the pseudo‑anomalies, and could cross‑modal pseudo‑anomaly generation (e.g., forcing a spatial correspondence between synthetic arc patches and force disturbances) further improve robustness?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses modality‑specific pseudo‑anomaly generation and shows strong results with few real anomalies. However, it does not generate pseudo‑anomalies where image and force signals are synthetically correlated to mimic a true physical arc event.
- **Why unresolved:** Current pseudo‑anomalies are generated independently per modality. Real arcs produce coupled visual and mechanical signatures. Independent synthesis might create unrealistic training samples.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design and implement cross‑modal pseudo‑anomaly generation where a pasted arc region's location/intensity is linked to a generated force disturbance pattern. Compare detection performance against independent generation under low‑shot regimes.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the specific factors driving the domain shift performance differences between the SBB‑AD and Open‑AD datasets, and can domain adaptation techniques mitigate the drop when moving from one railway system/geography to another?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper notes the framework shows "enhanced sensitivity to real arcing events even under domain shifts" but does not analyze the shift. Open‑AD uses internet videos (different countries, weather) and synthetic force, while SBB‑AD is from Swiss operational data.
- **Why unresolved:** High performance on both datasets suggests robustness, but the paper does not measure or analyze the covariate shift (camera angles, lighting, pantograph models) or label shift between the two sources.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform domain shift analysis: (1) Quantify visual feature differences (e.g., Fréchet Inception Distance); (2) Systematically corrupt one dataset's test set to mimic the other's conditions; (3) Apply unsupervised domain adaptation (e.g., ADDA, DANN) and measure AUROC change on target‑domain test set.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Are there alternative time‑series representations for force data beyond FFT that could capture discriminative temporal or spectral patterns of arcing more effectively, perhaps by preserving time‑localized information?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Tab. 5 ablates several representations (Raw, Wavelet, STFT, GAF, MTF, RP, FFT) and finds FFT best. However, STFT (time‑frequency) was worse than FFT (global frequency), and Wavelet also underperformed.
- **Why unresolved:** Arcing events are transient. Global FFT might lose timing information. The poor STFT result may be due to suboptimal parameter choices; other representations like continuous wavelet transform (CWT) scalograms or learnable filter banks were not tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re‑evaluate STFT with a grid search over window sizes and overlap. Test CWT scalograms as input images to the force encoder. Experiment with learnable filter banks (e.g., SincNet‑inspired layers). Compare AUROC and analyze which features the model attends to (e.g., via Grad‑CAM on force representations).

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters: Beta α for force Mixup and pseudo‑anomaly‑to‑real ratio are unspecified, affecting reproducibility.
- Undetailed center update: EMA decay rate and windowing strategy for updating hypersphere center c are not provided.
- Image pasting specifics: Details on resizing, blending, or mask usage for arc patch insertion are omitted.

## Confidence
- **Exponential loss stabilization:** Medium (ablation shows improvement, but gradient dynamics not fully diagnosed).
- **Modality‑specific pseudo‑anomaly synthesis:** Medium (effective in experiments, but reliant on realism of synthetic samples and seed anomaly quality).
- **Multimodal latent fusion:** High (consistent large gains over unimodal, robust to corruption).
- **Overall AUROC results:** High (validated on two datasets with comprehensive ablations).
