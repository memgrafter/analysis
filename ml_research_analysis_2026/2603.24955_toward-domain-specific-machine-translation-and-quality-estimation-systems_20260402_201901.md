# Toward domain-specific machine translation and quality estimation systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24955
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24955
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Domain-specific data selection and quality estimation–guided adaptation significantly improve machine translation and quality estimation performance in specialized fields while optimizing computational and environmental costs.

## Executive Summary
This dissertation addresses performance degradation in machine translation (MT) and quality estimation (QE) caused by domain mismatch. It proposes a similarity-based method to extract high-quality in-domain parallel sentences from generic corpora, yielding a 5.5 BLEU point gain with only 1M versus 31M generic sentences. For QE, a staged adaptation pipeline with data augmentation improves average Pearson correlation by 14.36% and enables robust zero-shot performance. An analysis of subword tokenization reveals that in-domain BPE and vocabulary maximize in-domain BLEU but at higher computational cost, while hybrid configurations offer a practical trade-off. Finally, a QE-guided in-context learning approach for large language models surpasses retrieval baselines by up to 3.5 BLEU points with far lower CO₂ emissions than fine-tuning. Collectively, these contributions demonstrate that intelligent data selection, adaptation, and efficient QE integration can enhance domain-specific MT while reducing resource consumption.

## Method Summary
The work introduces four interconnected methodological contributions. First, a data selection technique uses Sentence-BERT embeddings and PCA to rank generic parallel sentences by semantic similarity to in-domain monolingual text, extracting a targeted subset for MT training. Second, a three-stage QE fine-tuning pipeline for XLM-R begins with out-of-domain data, mixes in authentic and synthetic in-domain examples, and concludes with in-domain-only tuning, optionally using domain tags. Third, a systematic investigation of BPE tokenization and vocabulary construction compares in-domain, out-of-domain, and hybrid sources for MT fine-tuning, measuring their impact on in-domain performance and compute. Fourth, an iterative in-context learning framework combines BM25 retrieval, a generative LLM (XGLM), and the domain-specific QE model to select and combine translation examples without any reference translations. Reproduction notes are not provided by the authors.

## Key Results
- Similarity-based data selection achieves BLEU 31.3 with 1M sentences versus 25.8 using 31M generic sentences.
- Staged QE adaptation increases average Pearson correlation by 14.36% and generalizes to zero-shot language pairs.
- In-domain BPE and vocabulary yield the highest in-domain BLEU (54.8) but with greater computational cost; hybrid configurations balance performance and efficiency.
- QE-guided in-context learning improves BLEU by up to 3.5 points over BM25 retrieval baselines and reduces CO₂ emissions relative to model fine-tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Similarity-based data selection
Sentences in a generic parallel corpus are embedded with Sentence-BERT, dimensionality-reduced via PCA, and ranked by cosine similarity to embeddings of in-domain monolingual text. The top K pairs form a high-quality, compact training subset. This assumes that semantically close sentences will produce relevant translations for the target domain. Evidence shows a substantial BLEU improvement with only 1M selected sentences compared to 31M generic ones. The method may fail if the generic corpus lacks sufficiently similar sentences or if SBERT does not capture domain-specific semantics.

### Mechanism 2: Staged QE training with augmentation
A three-step pipeline fine-tunes XLM-R: (1) on large out-of-domain QE data, (2) on a mixture of out-of-domain and in-domain data (authentic concatenated across languages or synthetic generated via MT with quality labels), and (3) solely on target in-domain data. Optional domain tags are used. The gradual introduction of in-domain data while retaining out-of-domain knowledge prevents catastrophic forgetting and leverages limited labeled in-domain data. Synthetic data approximates real in-domain quality distributions. This yields a 14.36% average Pearson correlation gain and robust zero-shot performance. Risks include low synthetic data quality or cross-lingual noise during mixing.

### Mechanism 3: QE-guided in-context learning
For a test sentence, BM25 retrieves K candidate in-context examples (ICEs) from the training set. A search algorithm iteratively selects subsets of candidates, translates with the LLM, estimates quality with the domain-specific QE model, and retains the best combination under a patience condition. No references are needed. The QE model’s accuracy in ranking candidate ICE sets by resulting translation quality is central. This approach improves BLEU by up to 3.5 points over retrieval-only baselines and is far more carbon-efficient than fine-tuning. Failures occur if the QE model misranks due to domain shift or if the BM25 candidate pool lacks relevance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Domain adaptation in MT**: Generic models underperform in specialized contexts due to vocabulary, style, and terminology mismatch. Understanding this underpins all proposed solutions.  
  *Quick check*: What distinguishes a generic from a domain-specific MT system, and why does the former fail in specialized contexts?
- **Quality estimation (QE)**: Reference-free assessment of MT output, crucial when gold translations are unavailable. It differs from metrics like BLEU by not requiring references.  
  *Quick check*: How does sentence-level QE differ from BLEU, and why is it particularly useful for domain-specific scenarios?
- **In-context learning (ICL)**: LLMs adapt to tasks via prompt examples without parameter updates. Success depends on example relevance, number, and order.  
  *Quick check*: What key factors (e.g., number, order, relevance) govern ICL success for machine translation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Data selection module → Selected parallel data → MT model training  
  Generic QE data + In-domain data (authentic/synthetic) → QE training pipeline → Fine-tuned QE model  
  BPE/vocabulary source → Tokenization config → Applied to MT training data  
  BM25 index + QE model + LLM → ICL engine → Translations
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Data selection: prepare corpora → embed (SBERT) → PCA → compute similarities → rank → select top K → train MT.  
  2. QE: prepare triplets → Stage 1 (OOD fine-tune) → Stage 2 (mix with augmentation) → Stage 3 (ID fine-tune) → evaluate.  
  3. ICL: index training set with BM25 → for each test: retrieve K candidates → iterative search loop (select, translate, estimate) → output translation.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Data selection: precision vs. recall (K size).  
  - QE pipeline: cost vs. gain (additional stages, synthetic data quality).  
  - ICL: search depth vs. inference time (more ICEs, deeper search).  
  - Tokenization: in-domain BPE/vocab maximizes BLEU but increases compute; hybrid balances cost.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Data selection: low similarity of selected set to in-domain corpus; MT worse than random baseline.  
  - QE: no correlation improvement after Stage 2; zero-shot near random.  
  - ICL: QE scores not increasing during search; output length divergence.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Compare SBERT+cosine vs. random selection on small in-domain test; measure MT BLEU gap.  
  2. Train QE: only Stage 1 → add Stage 2 → add Stage 3; observe incremental Pearson gains.  
  3. Run ICL with patience=0 (greedy) vs. patience=∞ (brute force); verify search matches oracle when using BLEU as QE.

5. What metrics beyond BLEU and which additional features (e.g., bigrams, type/token ratio, length) can further improve the quality of in-context example selection for machine translation with LLMs?

## Limitations
- Data selection depends on SBERT capturing domain semantics; may fail for highly specialized jargon or corpora with low similarity.
- Synthetic in-domain data used in QE augmentation may be noisy, introducing harmful patterns.
- ICL search is limited by QE model accuracy and BM25 candidate pool relevance; domain-shifted QE or sparse pools degrade results.
- Tokenization trade-offs (in-domain BPE/vocab) improve BLEU but increase compute; hybrid ratios lack theoretical justification.
- External validation is weak; neighboring papers show low citation counts and moderate similarity, indicating limited corroboration.

## Confidence
- **Medium** for all claim clusters. Evidence is primarily internal (paper’s own experiments); external replication or cross-study validation is minimal.
