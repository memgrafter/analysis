# Explainable AI: Context-Aware Layer-Wise Integrated Gradients for Explaining Transformer Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16608
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16608
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CA-LIG improves explanation faithfulness across NLP and vision tasks, achieving higher Token-F1 on rationale benchmarks and superior insertion/deletion AUC compared to baselines.

## Executive Summary
Transformer explainability methods often suffer from final-layer bias, lack unified local-global reasoning, and insufficient context awareness. This paper introduces Context-Aware Layer-wise Integrated Gradients (CA-LIG), which computes Integrated Gradients at every Transformer block and fuses them with class-specific attention gradients to produce signed, context-sensitive attribution maps. The framework traces relevance evolution across layers and aggregates it via a multiplicative rollout. Evaluations on NLP tasks (sentiment, hate speech, long document classification) and vision (image classification with ViT) show consistent improvements: higher Token-F1 scores on human rationale benchmarks and better perturbation faithfulness (higher insertion AUC, lower deletion AUC). CA-LIG provides more coherent, semantically aligned explanations.

## Method Summary
CA-LIG extends Integrated Gradients (IG) to a hierarchical per-layer computation. For each Transformer block, it first computes Layer-wise Integrated Gradients (LIG) by integrating gradients between a baseline representation and the actual hidden state at that block’s input, yielding a token-level relevance vector. Simultaneously, it obtains the gradient of the target class score with respect to the block’s attention matrix. These two signals are fused by element-wise multiplication after normalizing the LIG relevance, effectively weighting the attention gradient by token importance. The fused matrices are then row-normalized and multiplied across all blocks in sequence (rollout) to produce a final context matrix. The first row of this matrix gives token attributions, which can be decomposed into positive (supporting) and negative (opposing) components. This design unifies local token importance (from LIG) with global structural dependencies (from attention gradients) while preserving the flow of evidence through the network.

## Key Results
- **NLP:** CA-LIG achieves higher Token-F1 scores on rationale benchmarks (e.g., Movie Reviews) compared to baseline explainability methods.
- **Vision:** On CIFAR-10 and ASIRRA, CA-LIG attains higher insertion AUC (0.617 vs. 0.567) and lower deletion AUC (0.215 vs. 0.237), indicating more faithful explanations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Capturing hierarchical relevance evolution
- **Claim:** Computing Integrated Gradients at every Transformer block captures the hierarchical evolution of token relevance, addressing final-layer bias.
- **Mechanism:** For each block, compute LIG between baseline and actual hidden state for the target class score, then sum over hidden dimensions to obtain token relevance \(R_t^{(l)}\). This yields a sequence of signed attribution maps.
- **Core assumption:** Token relevance is dynamically formed and refined through successive Transformer layers.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.1 define LIG; prior work does not apply IG hierarchically across blocks.
- **Break condition:** If the model’s decision is formed predominantly in the final layer, layer-wise IG may be redundant or noisy.

### Mechanism 2: Fusing with class-specific attention gradients
- **Claim:** Fusing layer-wise token relevance with attention gradients creates context-sensitive attributions that unify local and global reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** For each block, compute gradient of target class score w.r.t. attention matrix \(\nabla A^{(b)}\). Then fuse with normalized LIG relevance via element-wise product: \(\bar{A}^{(b)} = M_h[\nabla A^{(b)} \odot \text{Norm}(R^{(b)})] + I\).
- **Core assumption:** Attention gradients provide a class-sensitive signal of structural token interactions that complements local token gradients.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.3 describe fusion as a relevance-gated sensitivity mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If attention gradients are uninformative or saturated, fusion could add noise.

### Mechanism 3: Context-aware rollout
- **Claim:** Aggregating fused matrices via multiplicative rollout preserves relevance flow and yields a final map reflecting cumulative evidence propagation.
- **Mechanism:** Row-normalize each \(\bar{A}^{(b)}\), then multiply sequentially: \(C = \bar{A}^{(1)} \cdot \bar{A}^{(2)} \cdots \bar{A}^{(B)}\). Positive/negative decomposition separates supporting vs. opposing evidence.
- **Core assumption:** Transformer information flow is sequentially compositional; multiplicative rollout approximates evidence accumulation along residual pathways.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.4 describes rollout; Figure 8 shows relevance peaks aligning with functional stages.
- **Break condition:** If the model relies on non-sequential interactions (e.g., strong skip connections), multiplicative rollout may distort relevance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Integrated Gradients:** Understanding IG’s axioms (completeness, sensitivity) is essential because CA-LIG extends IG to per-layer computation. *Quick check:* What does the completeness axiom imply about the sum of attributions relative to the output difference?
- **Transformer Architecture Hierarchy:** Knowledge of Transformer components (self-attention, FFN, residual connections, LayerNorm) and their sequential flow is necessary to interpret layer-wise signals. *Quick check:* What are the two main sub-layers in a standard Transformer encoder block and how does data flow through them?
- **Attention as a Structural Signal vs. Explanation:** Raw attention weights are not explanations; their gradients can indicate class-sensitive dependencies. *Quick check:* Why might the raw attention weight \(A_{i,j}\) not correlate with token \(j\)’s importance for predicting class \(c\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** LIG Extractor -> Attention Gradient Module -> Fusion Engine -> Rollout Composer -> Decomposition
- **Critical path:** Input → Embedding → [For each block \(b\) (in order): compute LIG on \(x^{(b)}\) and \(\nabla A^{(b)}\) → Fuse → \(\bar{A}^{(b)}\) → Row-normalize → Multiply into cumulative \(C\)] → Output \(C\), \(C^+\), \(C^-\).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Granularity vs. Cost:* More layers and IG steps increase fidelity but scale as \(O(L \times m)\).
  - *Faithfulness vs. Readability:* Signed outputs provide richer evidence but may require thresholding for simple visualizations.
  - *Context Window:* Normalization is per-sequence; very long documents may need sliding windows.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Noisy/blurry attributions: likely due to mis-specified \(\lambda\) or insufficient IG steps.
  - Special token dominance ([CLS]/[SEP]): caused by poor baselines or missing normalization.
  - Relevance non-conservation: if row-normalization is omitted or faulty, \(\sum C\) may not approximate output difference.
  - Language/domain mismatch: using a baseline not matching data distribution.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Token-F1 on Movie Reviews: Use BERT-base on IMDB test split, compute top-p% tokens from \(C^+\) or \(|C|\), compare to human rationales. Expect CA-LIG > IG/LRP/Attn-Rollout.
  2. Layer-wise relevance tracing: For a fixed sentence, plot \(R_t^{(l)}\) per layer. Expect early uniform, mid-layer emergence of sentiment words, final sharp peak on key token.
  3. Ablation on \(\lambda\) and fusion: Run CA-LIG with \(\lambda \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}\) on a sample. Visual inspection should show \(\lambda=1\) yields most coherent, context-aware maps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Decoder-based models:** The framework is designed for encoder-only models; extending to decoder-based language models (e.g., GPT) requires handling causal masking and autoregressive generation.
2. **Adaptive fusion coefficient:** The fixed \(\lambda=1\) may not be optimal across tasks; an adaptive or learnable mechanism could improve generalizability.
3. **Wider vision evaluation:** Current vision experiments are limited to one model (MAE) and two datasets; more extensive tests across ViT variants and benchmarks are needed.
4. **Multimodal Transformers:** Cross-modal attention pathways in multimodal models (e.g., CLIP) pose distinct challenges not addressed by CA-LIG.

## Limitations
- Missing implementation details (baseline construction, normalization method, exact layer indexing) hinder exact reproduction and theoretical guarantees.
- Computational cost scales linearly with number of layers and IG steps, limiting applicability to long sequences or very deep models.
- Generalizability beyond the evaluated NLP and vision models (BERT, XLM-R, MAE) remains unverified; the method might not perform as well on other architectures or tasks.

## Confidence
- Layer-wise IG addresses final-layer bias: Medium
- Fusion of LIG and attention gradients yields context-sensitive attributions: Medium
- Multiplicative rollout aggregates evidence faithfully: Medium
