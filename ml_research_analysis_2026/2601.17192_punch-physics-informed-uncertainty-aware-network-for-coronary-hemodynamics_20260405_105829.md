# PUNCH: Physics-informed Uncertainty-aware Network for Coronary Hemodynamics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17192
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17192
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Non-invasive estimation of coronary flow reserve (CFR) from standard angiography with uncertainty quantification, achieving Spearman ρ=0.89 versus invasive thermodilution in 20 patients.

## Executive Summary
PUNCH is a per-patient trained physics-informed neural network (PINN) that estimates coronary flow reserve (CFR) from standard angiographic sequences without requiring ground-truth flow data or population-level training. It models contrast agent transport via an advection-diffusion equation and uses variational inference to quantify predictive uncertainty. On 1,000 synthetic angiograms, model uncertainty strongly correlates with estimation error (Pearson r=0.997). In a clinical validation with 20 patients, PUNCH-derived CFR agrees well with invasive thermodilution (Spearman ρ=0.89), and the generated confidence intervals are narrower than the variability of repeated invasive measurements. The entire pipeline runs in approximately three minutes per patient on a single GPU.

## Method Summary
The task is to predict CFR—the ratio of hyperemic to rest mean coronary flow—from rest and hyperemic fluoroscopy sequences. Preprocessing converts sequences into kymographs (spatiotemporal intensity maps along the left anterior descending artery centerline). The core method is a dual-branch PINN (one branch per state) with variational inference. Each branch contains three subnetworks modeling contrast intensity I(s,t,z), velocity u(s,z), and dispersion D(s,t,z), governed by the advection-diffusion PDE: ∂I/∂t + u∂I/∂s − D∂²I/∂s² = 0. A shared latent variable z ∼ q(z)=N(μ,diag(σ²)) captures unknowns. Training minimizes a composite loss: data mismatch, PDE residual, and a KL divergence term λ_KL KL(q(z)||p(z)) with prior p(z)=N(0,σ_prior²I). Per-patient training uses Adam (10k epochs, cosine annealing) with 2k collocation and 2k observed points per state. Inference samples z 100 times to produce a posterior CFR distribution (mean ± 95% CI). Physiological plausibility is enforced via output bounds (sigmoids).

## Key Results
- Uncertainty–error correlation on synthetic data: Pearson r=0.997, Spearman ρ=0.998.
- Clinical agreement with invasive thermodilution: Spearman ρ=0.89 (n=20).
- Computational efficiency: ~3 minutes per patient on a single GPU.
- Model confidence intervals are narrower than the variability of repeated invasive measurements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PUNCH succeeds by tightly integrating physical laws with data-driven modeling and uncertainty quantification. The advection-diffusion PDE embeds known hemodynamic principles, ensuring predictions are physically plausible even with limited or noisy data. Variational inference over a low-dimensional latent variable z allows the model to express epistemic uncertainty—arising from incomplete knowledge of patient-specific parameters—without requiring a large training set or ground-truth flow measurements. Per-patient training tailors the model to individual anatomy and imaging conditions, avoiding biases from population heterogeneity. The resulting posterior distribution naturally inflates uncertainty for noisy inputs or fast flows (where data is sparse), yielding calibrated confidence intervals that reflect estimation reliability.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**  
   *Why needed*: Enforce solution consistency with governing PDEs, stabilizing training and guaranteeing physical plausiability.  
   *Quick check*: Monitor PDE residual during training; it should decrease and remain finite.

2. **Variational Inference (VI)**  
   *Why needed*: Approximate the posterior over latent variables to quantify epistemic uncertainty without Markov chain Monte Carlo.  
   *Quick check*: Posterior samples of u(s) should exhibit meaningful diversity correlated with data noise.

3. **Advection-Diffusion Equation**  
   *Why needed*: Describe contrast transport in coronary arteries, capturing both convective flow and molecular dispersion.  
   *Quick check*: Simulate with known u(s), D(s,t); synthetic I(s,t) should match expected transport dynamics.

4. **Per-Patient Training**  
   *Why needed*: Avoid inter-patient variability and personalize to individual vessel geometry and imaging conditions.  
   *Quick check*: Train on a single synthetic case; recovered CFR should match the ground truth within uncertainty.

5. **Uncertainty Calibration**  
   *Why needed*: Ensure reported confidence intervals reflect true error probability, critical for clinical trust.  
   *Quick check*: On a held-out synthetic set, empirical coverage of 95% CI should be near 95% (e.g., 90–98%).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input kymograph I_obs(s,t) → Dual-branch PINNs (subnetworks I(s,t,z), u(s,z), D(s,t,z)) + shared latent z ∼ q(z) → Solve PDE via autodiff → Sample z (N=100) → Integrate u(s) along centerline → CFR = (mean hyperemic velocity)/(mean rest velocity) → Posterior distribution (mean ± 95% CI).

**Critical path**:  
1. Preprocess fluoroscopy to kymograph (centerline extraction, intensity sampling).  
2. Initialize q(z); train dual-branch PINN with Adam (10k epochs) using data loss, PDE residual, and KL divergence.  
3. Sample latent z from learned posterior; for each sample compute u(s) and CFR.  
4. Aggregate samples to obtain CFR posterior statistics.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Latent dimensionality (Z_dim)**: Low (e.g., 1) simplifies inference but may miss complex uncertainty sources; higher Z_dim improves flexibility but risks overfitting and increases computational cost.  
- **KL weight (λ_KL)**: Balances data fit versus prior regularization; too low yields under‑confident intervals, too high yields over‑confident or biased predictions.  
- **Per‑patient vs. population training**: Per‑patient ensures personalization and avoids Population shift but requires retraining for each new case (~3 min); population training would be faster but may not generalize across imaging protocols.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Velocity u(s) becomes negative or exceeds physiological bounds (>80 cm/s).  
- Empirical coverage on synthetic data is far below 95% (e.g., <80%) or CI widths are excessively large.  
- PDE residual fails to converge or produces NaN values during training.  
- CFR posterior mean is inconsistent across random seeds for the same input.

**First three experiments**:  
1. **Boundary sensitivity**: Train with different velocity bounds ([0,60], [0,80], [0,100] cm/s) and dispersion bounds; evaluate impact on synthetic uncertainty–error correlation (target r≥0.95) and coverage.  
2. **Calibration check**: Compute 95% CI coverage and mean width on the 1,000‑case synthetic test set; tune λ_KL and σ_prior to achieve coverage within 90–98%.  
3. **Latent space ablation**: Vary Z_dim (1, 2, 5) and λ_KL (1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2) in a grid search; select configuration that maximizes uncertainty–error correlation while maintaining plausible u(s).

## Limitations
- Preprocessing steps (SAM2-UNet for vessel segmentation, LocoTrack for centerline tracking) are not algorithmically detailed, preventing end‑to‑end reproduction from raw angiography.  
- Clinical validation involves only 20 patients, limiting statistical power and generalizability; selection bias cannot be ruled out.  
- Physiological bounds for velocity and dispersion, kymograph spatial/temporal resolution, and synthetic corruption parameters are not quantitatively specified, hindering faithful replication of the training and evaluation pipeline.
