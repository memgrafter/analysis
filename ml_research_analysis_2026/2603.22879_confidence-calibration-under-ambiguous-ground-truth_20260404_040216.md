# Confidence Calibration under Ambiguous Ground Truth

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.22879
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22879
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Standard confidence calibration methods fail under label ambiguity; ambiguity-aware post-hoc calibrators (Dirichlet-Soft, Monte Carlo Temperature Scaling, Label-Smooth Temperature Scaling) significantly improve true-label calibration without model retraining.

## Executive Summary
Standard confidence calibration assumes a unique ground truth per input, but real-world tasks often involve genuine annotator disagreement. This paper shows that calibrators fitted on majority-voted labels appear well-calibrated by standard metrics yet remain miscalibrated against the true annotator distribution. The authors introduce ambiguity-aware post-hoc calibrators that optimize proper scoring rules against the full label distribution. Across four benchmarks, Dirichlet-Soft reduces true-label expected calibration error (ECE) by 55–87% relative to Temperature Scaling, while Label-Smooth Temperature Scaling (LS-TS) reduces ECE by 9–77% without any annotator data. Notably, Monte Carlo Temperature Scaling with just one annotation per example matches full-distribution calibration within 0.6 percentage points ECE, demonstrating that pre-aggregated distributions are unnecessary.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes post-hoc calibration methods that account for ambiguous ground truth by targeting the annotator distribution rather than a majority vote. Dirichlet-Soft extends temperature scaling with a Dirichlet parameterization to directly model the full categorical distribution. Monte Carlo Temperature Scaling (MCTS) uses individual annotations in a Monte Carlo objective, requiring only a single annotation per example to approximate full-distribution calibration. Label-Smooth Temperature Scaling (LS-TS) operates with only voted labels by constructing soft targets from the model's own confidence, applying instance-specific smoothing based on the complement of the predicted probability. All methods adjust a temperature parameter (or more generally a calibrator) by minimizing cross-entropy against distributional targets, without retraining the backbone model.

## Key Results
- Dirichlet-Soft reduces true-label ECE by 55–87% compared to standard Temperature Scaling.
- MCTS with S=1 annotation per example matches the performance of full-distribution calibration within 0.6 pp ECE.
- LS-TS reduces true-label ECE by 9–77% without requiring any annotator data, though its effectiveness depends on the correlation between model confidence and annotation ambiguity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Standard Temperature Scaling fails under label ambiguity because it optimizes toward a one-hot majority-vote target, which exerts a downward pull on the temperature parameter for ambiguous inputs, leading to overconfidence relative to the true annotator distribution.
- **Mechanism:** The TS loss (minimizing cross-entropy against one-hot votes) decreases as temperature T decreases on ambiguous examples (where the voted class has majority probability q < 1), because lower T increases confidence on the voted class. This creates a bias toward T < T*_soft, where T*_soft would match the true annotator distribution's confidence q. The global optimum T*_TS is thus systematically lower than the ambiguity-aware optimum, causing overconfidence on ambiguous inputs.
- **Core assumption:** The model's pre-softmax logits for ambiguous examples are such that the voted-class confidence exceeds the majority probability (p_i > q). This holds for reasonably accurate models.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Temperature Scaling is biased toward temperatures that underestimate annotator uncertainty, with true-label miscalibration increasing monotonically with annotation entropy." [section 5] Proposition 1 formalizes the directional bias: T*_TS < T*_soft for ambiguous clusters. [corpus] Neighbor papers on soft-label training and ambiguous data support the principle that distributional targets improve calibration.
- **Break condition:** If the model's predicted confidence on ambiguous examples is *lower* than the majority probability (p_i < q), the gradient direction reverses, but this regime is atypical for trained classifiers.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** True-label miscalibration (|p_ˆc - π_ˆc|) for voted-label calibrators increases monotonically with annotation entropy H(x), because the voted-label target fixes the confidence goal at 1 for the majority class regardless of π_ˆc(x).
- **Mechanism:** Voted-label calibration aims to set p_ˆc(x) ≈ 1 for the majority class across all examples. For unambiguous inputs (H=0), π_ˆc(x)=1, so error is near zero. As H increases, π_ˆc(x) decreases below 1 while p_ˆc(x) remains pushed toward 1, creating an increasing gap |p_ˆc - π_ˆc|. This holds under the assumption that p_ˆc is approximately independent of H(x).
- **Core assumption:** (i) Model confidence p_ˆc(x) does not strongly correlate with H(x); (ii) π_ˆc(x) decreases as H(x) increases. Both are empirically valid.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5] Proposition 2 states the monotonicity formally. [section 4] Figure 3 shows empirical validation: TS's pointwise error rises with normalized entropy on CIFAR-10H and ChaosNLI.
- **Break condition:** If p_ˆc(x) *decreases* with H(x) strongly enough to offset the drop in π_ˆc(x), the gap could shrink. However, experiments show this does not occur in practice.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Ambiguity-aware calibrators (Dirichlet-Soft, MCTS, LS-TS) succeed because they minimize a strictly proper scoring rule (cross-entropy) against the full annotator distribution (or an approximation), aligning the optimization target with the true deployment objective.
- **Mechanism:** By replacing one-hot targets with ˆπ(x) in the loss, the calibrator's temperature (or parameters) is tuned so that output probabilities match the annotator distribution. For MCTS, even S=1 annotation per example suffices because the Monte Carlo objective converges to the KL divergence between ˆπ and the calibrated distribution, and a single sample provides an unbiased gradient direction. LS-TS constructs pseudo-soft targets from model confidence, approximating ˆπ when model uncertainty correlates with annotation ambiguity.
- **Core assumption:** (Dirichlet-Soft/MCTS) The annotator distribution ˆπ(x) is available (or can be sampled). (LS-TS) The model's complement confidence (1 - p_ˆc) is a reliable proxy for average annotator disagreement across the calibration set.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Dirichlet-Soft reduces true-label ECE by 55–87% relative to Temperature Scaling... MCTS with S=1 matches full-distribution calibration within 0.6 pp ECE." [section 6] Proposition 3 shows cross-entropy against ˆπ is strictly proper. [corpus] Proper scoring rules literature provides theoretical foundation.
- **Break condition:** Dirichlet-Soft/MCTS fail if ˆπ(x) is massively misestimated. LS-TS fails if model confidence is uncorrelated with true ambiguity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Proper scoring rules**
  - **Why needed here:** They define the correct objective for distributional calibration; cross-entropy against ˆπ(x) is strictly proper, meaning it uniquely incentivizes the calibrator to output the true annotator distribution. Voted-label cross-entropy is improper for ambiguous data.
  - **Quick check:** If you minimize cross-entropy against a one-hot label on data where the true label is actually 70% class A, 30% class B, what distribution will your model converge to? (Answer: It will push toward a degenerate peak at class A, misrepresenting the ambiguity.)

- **Concept: Annotator distribution vs. majority vote**
  - **Why needed here:** The annotator distribution π(·|x) captures irreducible aleatoric uncertainty; majority vote collapses this into a possibly misleading point estimate. Calibration must target π, not the vote.
  - **Quick check:** For an image with human votes [cat: 4, dog: 3, bird: 3], what is the majority vote and what is the annotator distribution? Which should be the calibration target for true-label calibration? (Answer: vote = cat; distribution = [0.4, 0.3, 0.3]; target = distribution.)

- **Concept: Temperature scaling as a post-hoc parametric family**
  - **Why needed here:** All proposed methods operate within the temperature-scaling family (or extensions), meaning they adjust logits without retraining the backbone. Understanding that T > 1 typically reduces overconfidence, while T < 1 increases it, is key to interpreting the bias.
  - **Quick check:** If TS yields T = 0.6 on a dataset with ambiguous examples, what does that imply about the model's pre-calibration confidence relative to the voted labels? (Answer: It was underconfident on clear examples, but TS is now overcorrecting on ambiguous ones because the target is wrong.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Pre-trained model f(x) produces logits z(x) -> Calibration dataset (full distribution ˆπ(x) or individual annotations {a_i^s} or voted labels y*_i) -> Calibrator g(z; θ) (with parameters θ) -> Optimized by loss (cross-entropy against target) -> Cached logits avoid repeated forward passes.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Cache model logits on the calibration set.
  2. Prepare targets: aggregate annotations into ˆπ(x_i) for Dirichlet-Soft, keep raw for MCTS, or compute LS-TS pseudo-targets using mean complement confidence.
  3. Optimize calibrator parameters θ by minimizing the chosen loss (e.g., L_soft for SLTS, L_MCTS for MCTS).
  4. At test time, apply g(z_test; θ*) to get calibrated probabilities.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Annotation cost vs. performance: Dirichlet-Soft (full distribution) > MCTS S=1 ≈ SLTS (individual annotations aggregated) > LS-TS (no annotations). MCTS S=1 is nearly as good as full distribution.
  - Model capacity vs. target: Expressive calibrators (Dirichlet-Hard) underperform simple TS when using wrong (voted) targets; the target matters more than capacity.
  - Stability: LS-TS is sensitive to the correlation between model confidence and true ambiguity; if low confidence does not reflect high disagreement, it may over-smooth and underconfidently calibrate.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Reliability diagram: Voted-label methods show residual overconfidence on ambiguous examples even after TS (red residuals in middle-confidence bins).
  - ECE_true vs. entropy: TS error increases with annotation entropy; ambiguity-aware methods' error is flat or decreasing.
  - Temperature anomaly: LS-TS yields T_LS > T_SLTS on datasets where model confidence poorly tracks ambiguity (e.g., ISIC ViT-S/16: T_LS=3.98 > T_SLTS=2.68).
  - Dirichlet-Hard underperformance: Despite having K²+K parameters, it often yields higher ECE_true than TS, indicating target mismatch.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate toy example (Section 4): Train a 2-layer MLP on synthetic data with an ambiguous cluster. Apply TS and SLTS. Verify TS increases ECE_true while SLTS reduces it. Plot reliability diagrams stratified by cluster.
  2. MCTS annotation efficiency (Appendix D): On CIFAR-10H, subsample annotations per example (m = 1, 2, 5, 10, 50). Plot ECE_true vs. m for MCTS and SLTS (which uses full distribution). Confirm MCTS S=1 matches SLTS within 0.6 pp.
  3. LS-TS smoothing ablation (Section 7.7): Implement Fixed-LS (ε=0.1), Ent-LS (per-instance entropy), CC-LS (per-class ε). Compare ECE_true to LS-TS on CIFAR-10H. Verify Fixed-LS degrades below TS, Ent-LS is inconsistent, CC-LS ≈ LS-TS, confirming global mean smoothing is key.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do ambiguity‑aware calibration methods perform on real multi‑reader medical datasets with per‑image annotator distributions?
- **Basis:** The authors write that “ISIC 2019 and DermaMNIST rely on synthetic annotators derived from clinician‑reported agreement. ... Real multi‑reader medical datasets with per‑image reader distributions would provide stronger validation and would capture the instance‑level variation our synthetic model does not; concrete candidates include VinDr‑CXR and CheXpert.”
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluations use class‑conditional synthetic annotations, which ignore instance‑specific difficulty and may not reflect natural annotator disagreement patterns; real datasets could reveal different robustness properties.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions does the assumption that model confidence is a reliable proxy for annotation ambiguity break down, and how can LS‑TS be improved without additional annotations?
- **Basis:** The authors note that LS‑TS “does not fully match Dirichlet‑oft, particularly on datasets where model confidence diverges from annotation ambiguity (e.g., ISIC 2019 ViT‑S/16).” LS‑TS’s effectiveness relies on the assumption that “model uncertainty is a reliable proxy for annotation disagreement.”
- **Why unresolved:** LS‑TS constructs pseudo‑soft targets from the model’s confidence; if the model is overconfident on ambiguous inputs, the smoothing weight becomes inaccurate.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What should future calibration benchmarks and deployment standards look like to properly evaluate confidence calibration when ground truth is ambiguous?
- **Basis:** In the conclusion the authors state: “Together, these results suggest that future calibration benchmarks and deployment standards should move beyond the voted‑label paradigm and account for the distributional nature of human annotation.”
- **Why unresolved:** Existing benchmarks typically treat majority‑vote labels as ground truth; as the paper shows, a model can appear well‑calibrated under such metrics while being miscalibrated with respect to the true annotator distribution.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the theoretical analysis explaining why Temperature Scaling fails under ambiguity be extended beyond the simplifying assumptions (e.g., independence of model confidence and annotation entropy)?
- **Basis:** The proofs of Propositions 1 and 2 assume (i) the model’s predicted confidence is approximately independent of the annotation entropy and (ii) the majority‑class probability decreases monotonically with entropy; the paper acknowledges these are simplifying assumptions.
- **Why unresolved:** Real‑world datasets often violate both assumptions, so the monotonic relationship may not fully capture TS behavior; a more general theory is needed.

## Limitations
- Mechanisms assume model confidence exceeds majority probability on ambiguous inputs (p_i > q) and does not decrease with label entropy; these assumptions are not universally verified.
- LS-TS requires model complement confidence to correlate with true ambiguity; empirical results are mixed (e.g., higher temperature on ISIC ViT), raising robustness concerns.
- Evaluation limited to four datasets and standard architectures; generalizability to other tasks and model families is unknown.

## Confidence
- **TS bias under ambiguity** (Mechanism 1): Medium. Supported by theory and benchmarks, but assumption coverage uncertain.
- **Miscalibration increases with entropy** (Mechanism 2): Medium-High. Empirical monotonicity is strong, yet theory relies on independence assumptions.
- **Ambiguity‑aware calibrators improve true‑label ECE** (Mechanism 3): High for Dirichlet‑Soft/SLTS; Medium for MCTS S=1 and LS-TS due to limited external validation.

## Next Checks
1. Quantify prevalence of p_i > q across diverse models/datasets; if >20% of ambiguous examples violate it, revisit the directional bias claim.
2. Stress‑test LS‑TS by introducing engineered calibration mismatches (e.g., extreme label smoothing); if performance falls below TS, its robustness is questionable.
3. Extend MCTS annotation‑efficiency study to ≥5 additional datasets with varied ambiguity levels; determine whether S=1 consistently matches full‑distribution calibration or requires more samples.
