# Kirin: Improving ANN efficiency with SNN Hybridization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08817  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08817  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Achieves near‑FP16 accuracy on LLMs (e.g., Llama2‑7B perplexity 5.71 vs. FP16 5.68) while reducing energy by up to 84.66% and latency by 93.75% (time window from 256 to 16 timesteps) under W4A4&8 quantization.

## Executive Summary
Kirin addresses the fundamental challenge of high latency and energy consumption in converting large artificial neural networks (ANNs) to spiking neural networks (SNNs). The primary issue arises from outlier parameters that force long time‑to‑first‑spike (TTFS) windows, and the general trade‑off between accuracy and efficiency in spiking encodings. Kirin proposes an integer‑spike hybrid SNN architecture: it selectively retains outlier values as integers while encoding normal low‑bit parameters as binary spikes, thereby minimizing integer operations. Additionally, the Silence Threshold mechanism modifies TTFS firing by delaying spikes after threshold crossing, preserving mathematical equivalence to the original ANN output without resorting to energy‑intensive rate encoding. Evaluated on Llama2 and OPT models under mixed W4A4&8 quantization, Kirin achieves near‑FP16 accuracy (e.g., Llama2‑7B perplexity 5.71 vs. FP16 5.68) while reducing energy consumption by up to 84.66% and shortening the inference time window by 93.75% (from T=256 to T=16).

## Method Summary
Kirin’s method combines Spike Matrix Hybridization and a Silence Threshold mechanism. For each linear or attention operation in an LLM, the system first performs outlier detection using median absolute deviation (MAD) on both operands (e.g., activation and weight matrices). It then selects the matrix with fewer outliers as the spike matrix, encoding its non‑outlier (4‑bit) values as TTFS spikes (spike time equals the quantized value) and retaining outliers (8‑bit) as integers; the other matrix remains fully integer. During SNN inference, an integrate‑and‑fire neuron with a Silence Threshold processes the spike train. The threshold V_th is set to 1/(S1·S2), where S1 and S2 are the quantization scales of the two matrices. When membrane potential V(t) reaches V_th, a silence counter increments instead of firing. After all spikes are received (within T_low=16 timesteps), the output is computed as (silence count + residual potential/V_th) plus the integer product of outliers and bias. This yields exact equivalence to the dequantized ANN output. By bounding the spike‑based operations to T_low, Kirin eliminates the long TTFS windows required for outliers, achieving drastic latency and energy savings while preserving accuracy. The approach is applied successively to every layer (embedding, attention projections, MLP) of models like Llama2 and OPT, with mixed‑precision quantization (4‑bit normal, 8‑bit outliers). Energy consumption is simulated using a 22nm process model, showing up to 84.66% reduction compared to conventional TTFS SNNs.

## Key Results
- Near‑FP16 accuracy: Llama2‑7B perplexity 5.71 vs. FP16 5.68 on WikiText‑2.  
- Energy reduction: Up to 84.66% lower consumption.  
- Latency reduction: Inference time window shortened by 93.75% (from T=256 to T=16).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Latency Reduction via Selective Integer Retention
- **Claim:** Kirin reduces inference latency by avoiding long TTFS time windows for outlier parameters through selective integer retention in matrix multiplications.  
- **Mechanism:** For any two matrices A and B in a linear or attention layer, the system computes C(M) — the count of quantized values that would require a time window exceeding T_low (2^4=16) if encoded as TTFS spikes. The matrix with the smaller C(M) is designated the spike matrix; its values exceeding T_low are retained as 8‑bit integers instead of being encoded into long TTFS spike trains. The multiplication is then decomposed into an SNN accumulation (using TTFS spikes for low‑bit values) plus a sparse integer MAC operation (for retained outliers). This bounds the overall latency by T_low, as the spike‑based part finishes within 16 timesteps regardless of outliers.  
- **Core assumption:** Outliers constitute a small, manageable fraction of total parameters (e.g., ~2.68% in Llama2‑7B), so the overhead of integer MACs is negligible compared to the latency savings from eliminating long TTFS windows.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "selectively retains outlier values as integers while encoding normal low‑bit parameters as binary spikes, minimizing integer operations."  
  - [section 3.2] Equations (9)-(11) formalize C(M) and the hybrid decomposition Y = IF(A(S), B) + A(I)×B.  
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related works on outlier‑aware quantization (SmoothQuant, Atom) but Kirin’s specific hybridization for SNN latency reduction is novel—no direct parallel in retrieved papers; evidence is weak.  
- **Break condition:** If outlier ratio exceeds ~5%, the integer MAC overhead grows quadratically, potentially increasing total execution time and eroding energy savings, especially on hardware where MACs are not orders of magnitude costlier than ACCs.

### Mechanism 2: Mathematical Equivalence via Silence Threshold
- **Claim:** The Silence Threshold mechanism enables TTFS‑based SNNs to produce layer‑wise outputs mathematically equivalent to the original ANN’s dequantized outputs, preserving accuracy without resorting to energy‑intensive rate encoding.  
- **Mechanism:** The firing threshold V_th is set to 1/(S1·S2), where S1 and S2 are quantization scales for the input and weight matrices. During TTFS accumulation, when membrane potential V(t) ≥ V_th, the neuron does not fire; instead, a silence counter (st) is incremented. This continues until all input spikes have arrived (t ≤ T_low). The final output is computed as (st + V_res / V_th) + bias, which equals the integer part plus fractional residue of the ANN’s dequantized matrix product. This effectively captures multiple “virtual” threshold crossings that standard TTFS would ignore.  
- **Core assumption:** Quantization scales S1, S2 are accurately calibrated (e.g., via MAD for outliers) and static during inference; TTFS spike times perfectly represent quantized values.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Silence Threshold mechanism modifies TTFS firing by delaying spikes after threshold crossing to maintain mathematical equivalence with the original ANN output."  
  - [section 3.3] Equations (15)-(16) derive equivalence: Output_IF = st + V_res/S_th = Output_ANN.  
  - [corpus] SpikeQuant (retrieved) uses rate encoding and a different threshold scheme for equivalence; Kirin’s TTFS+delay approach is distinct—no direct support.  
- **Break condition:** If quantization scales are miscalibrated (e.g., due to activation distribution shift across layers), V_th becomes incorrect, leading to cumulative error and perplexity degradation. The mechanism also assumes no spike loss in the spike matrix; missing spikes would break the accumulation sum.

### Mechanism 3: Overall Energy and Accuracy Gains
- **Claim:** The synergy of Spike Matrix Hybridization and Silence Threshold yields near‑lossless ANN‑to‑SNN conversion with drastic energy (up to 84.66%) and latency (93.75%) reductions by exploiting sparse additions for most parameters while minimally handling outliers.  
- **Mechanism:** Hybridization confines the spike‑based part to low‑bit (4‑bit) values with T_low=16 and very low spike rate (S_low^r = 1/T_low), enabling energy‑efficient ACC operations. The integer‑retained outliers (8‑bit) incur MACs but are so few (β ~ 2%) that their energy cost is offset by the massive saving from converting the majority of operations from MACs to ACCs and avoiding long T_high (256) waits. Silence Threshold ensures that despite TTFS’s single‑spike constraint, the layer‑wise outputs match ANN dequantization exactly, avoiding accuracy loss.  
- **Core assumption:** On target hardware (22nm), ACC energy is significantly lower than MAC (e.g., 4‑4‑4 ACC = 1.0×, 4‑8‑32 MAC = 10.94× as per Table 5); memory access energy dominates, but sparsity reduces data movement.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "reducing energy consumption by up to 84.66% and shortening time steps by 93.75% (from T=256 to T=16)."  
  - [section 4.3] "energy consumption increases by a mere 0.283 μJ compared to a pure spike matrix. However, ... saving 17.458 μJ in a single matrix multiplication."  
  - [section 4.2, Table 2] Near‑FP16 zero‑shot accuracy on Llama2/OPT under W4A4&8.  
  - [corpus] Related SNN works (SpikeLLM, SpikeQuant) achieve accuracy but with T≥32 or hybrid components not fully spiking; Kirin’s combined approach is unique—corpus lacks direct evidence.  
- **Break condition:** If the ACC/MAC energy ratio on deployed hardware is low (<3–4×), the energy benefit shrinks. If outlier detection is too aggressive (high β), integer MACs could become the dominant cost. Also, if T_low must be increased (e.g., for ultra‑low bit quantization), latency gains diminish.

## Foundational Learning

### Quantization in ANN‑to‑SNN conversion
- **Why needed here:** Kirin relies on mixed‑precision quantization (4‑bit normal, 8‑bit outliers) to map floating‑point LLM parameters to discrete values that can be encoded into TTFS spike times. The bit‑width directly determines the maximum spike time (T = 2^bit). Without proper outlier handling, a few 8‑bit values force T=256, causing latency explosion.  
- **Quick check:** If a weight matrix has a few values quantized to 8‑bit (range 0‑255) while most are 4‑bit (0‑15), what is the minimum time window T needed for TTFS encoding to represent all values without clipping, and why does this hurt latency?

### TTFS vs. Rate encoding trade‑offs
- **Why needed here:** Kirin chooses TTFS for its ultra‑low spike rate (1 spike per neuron per window), which minimizes energy per operation. However, TTFS’s single‑spike constraint causes information loss across layers. The Silence Threshold adapts TTFS to preserve information, bridging the gap to rate encoding’s accuracy without its spike‑rate penalty.  
- **Quick check:** In a T=32 window, how many spikes does a neuron emit on average under Rate encoding (if its quantized value is 10) versus TTFS? How does this difference scale energy consumption in an IF neuron that accumulates weighted spikes?

### Matrix operations in Transformer‑based LLMs
- **Why needed here:** Kirin applies hybridization to all matrix multiplies: linear projections (activation × weight) and attention (Q × K). Understanding tensor shapes (e.g., activations: [B, S, H_in]; weights: [H_in, H_out]) is essential to reason about outlier distribution (per‑channel vs. per‑token) and to compute energy formulas (e.g., linear: B·S·H_in·H_out MACs; attention: B·S²·H_out MACs).  
- **Quick check:** In the attention mechanism, what are the dimensions of the Q, K, and V matrices after linear projection? Which matrix multiplication (Q×Kᵀ or K×V) typically dominates compute, and why might Kirin’s hybridization be more impactful on one versus the other?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
The architecture consists of three key elements. The Spike Matrix Hybridization module processes each matrix multiplication (e.g., in linear layers or attention) by comparing outlier counts of the two operand matrices and selecting the one with fewer outliers as the spike matrix. It encodes non‑outlier values (4‑bit) as TTFS spikes (spike time = quantized value) and retains outliers (8‑bit) as integers. The IF neuron with Silence Threshold receives the spike train, accumulates membrane potential, and increments a silence counter whenever the potential reaches V_th = 1/(S1·S2) instead of firing. After processing all spikes within T_low=16, it outputs (silence count + residual/V_th) plus bias. The Hybrid Execution Flow sequentially applies these steps to every layer of the LLM (embedding, attention projections, MLP), with the output of one layer feeding into the next. Integer products from outliers are summed and added to the final output before passing to the next layer.

### Critical Path
1. **Quantization & outlier detection:** Offline MAD detects outlier channels/values in weights and activations. Online, per‑layer, compute γ (outlier channels) and β (retained integers after hybridization).  
2. **Matrix selection:** For current layer’s two operands (e.g., input activation A and weight W, or Q and K), compute C(A) and C(W). Choose operand with smaller C as spike matrix S; the other as fixed matrix F (may contain integers too, but only outliers in S are retained as integers to minimize β).  
3. **Encoding:** Encode low‑bit values in S as TTFS spikes; create integer mask for outliers.  
4. **Spike accumulation:** Run IF with Silence Threshold on S × F (using TTFS spikes and low‑bit weights). This yields V(T_low).  
5. **Integer accumulation:** Compute sum of outlier products (from mask) and add to V(T_low).  
6. **Output:** Final layer output = (st + V_res/V_th) + integer_sum + bias. Pass to next layer.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Outlier threshold sensitivity:** MAD’s threshold (e.g., 3×MAD) controls γ. Lower threshold → more outliers → higher β → more integer MACs, less latency gain. Must balance accuracy vs. efficiency.  
- **Matrix selection criterion:** Choosing the operand with smaller C minimizes integer ops, but if the other operand has many zeros, the spike rate might be even lower, suggesting a different selection could yield lower total energy. Current heuristic is simple but not necessarily optimal.  
- **Silence Threshold calibration:** Requires precise knowledge of quantization scales S1, S2. If scales are learned/changed post‑training, re‑calibration is needed; otherwise equivalence fails.

### Failure Signatures
- **Accuracy collapse:** Perplexity jump >0.5 compared to FP16 indicates either outlier misclassification or Silence Threshold miscalculation.  
- **Energy inefficiency:** If β > 5%, measured energy savings may drop below 50% because integer MAC energy (E_MAC ~8–11× ACC) outweighs spike ACC savings.  
- **Latency worse than expected:** Effective T > 32 suggests either hybridization failed (high C(M) for both matrices) or spike arrival times exceed T_low due to encoding error.  
- **Numerical instability:** NaNs/Infs in outputs usually mean V_th set incorrectly or integer overflow in mask accumulation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can Kirin’s hybrid approach scale to extremely large models (e.g., 70B parameters) and to Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) architectures without losing accuracy or efficiency?  
- **Basis:** Evaluation only included Llama2 up to 13B and OPT up to 2.7B; MoE and larger models were not tested.  
- **Why unresolved:** Outlier distributions and computational dynamics in larger/MoE models could increase the fraction of high‑bit values, reducing the effectiveness of the integer‑spike split.  
- **Evidence needed:** Comprehensive experiments on state‑of‑the‑art large models (e.g., Llama2‑70B, Mixtral) under the same W4A(4&8) setting, reporting perplexity, zero‑shot accuracy, latency, and energy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How accurate are the theoretical energy savings of Kirin when implemented on real neuromorphic or edge hardware, considering practical overheads like spike routing and memory access?  
- **Basis:** Energy consumption is estimated using a 22nm commercial process model; no actual hardware implementation is presented.  
- **Why unresolved:** Real systems incur additional costs (e.g., clock distribution, interconnect, buffering) that might erode the claimed 84% reduction.  
- **Evidence needed:** Implementation of Kirin on an FPGA or ASIC platform, measuring actual power and energy during inference, and comparing to both theoretical predictions and baseline quantization methods.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Could a dynamic, input‑aware outlier detection further minimize the number of integer operations in Spike Matrix Hybridization while preserving lossless accuracy?  
- **Basis:** The paper states they “offline detect outliers” using the MAD algorithm and then fix the spike/integer split after conversion.  
- **Why unresolved:** Activation outliers may vary with input; a static selection might retain more integers than necessary or cause accuracy loss under distribution shift.  
- **Evidence needed:** A runtime mechanism that monitors activation outlier counts and selects the spike matrix per batch, followed by evaluation showing reduced integer proportion (β) and maintained or improved accuracy.

## Limitations
- Outlier detection sensitivity: The exact MAD threshold multiplier is not specified; this strongly influences γ and β. Different values may break the assumed small outlier fraction (2–3%).  
- Silence Threshold implementation details: The timing of adding integer‑component products relative to silence counter computation is unclear; incorrect ordering yields non‑equivalent outputs.  
- Hardware dependence: Energy reductions assume a large ACC vs MAC energy gap (e.g., 10×) on 22nm; on other nodes or architectures the benefit may shrink or reverse.
