# Can LLMs Truly Embody Human Personality? Analyzing AI and Human Behavior Alignment in Dispute Resolution
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07414
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07414
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: LLMs show significant misalignment with human personality-driven behavior in dispute resolution, particularly in trait-behavior mapping, temporal adaptation, and strategy distribution.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether LLMs can simulate human personality-driven behavior in dispute resolution dialogues. It compares 248 human-human conversations (KODIS) with simulations of three LLMs (GPT-4o mini, Claude Sonnet 3.7, Gemini 2.0 Flash) using matched scenarios and Big Five personality profiles, analyzing strategies via the Interests-Rights-Power (IRP) framework and regression models. The results reveal substantial misalignment: humans rely on neuroticism as a key predictor of outcomes, while LLMs show stronger effects for extraversion and agreeableness; LLMs exhibit rigid, polarized strategy distributions and poor temporal adaptation, with only partial mirroring by Claude and Gemini. The findings challenge the use of personality-prompted LLMs as reliable behavioral proxies in socially impactful applications.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a parallel dataset: human dialogues from KODIS (with BFI personality scores) and LLM-LLM simulations (L2L) that match the same dispute scenarios and personality profiles. Each LLM agent receives a prompt describing its Big Five traits (using bipolar adjectives) and importance weights for issues. Conversations proceed turn-by-turn until resolution or a maximum length. All dialogues are annotated with IRP strategic moves using a GPT-4o-based coder validated on human labels. Derived metrics include IRP ratios, reciprocity, escalation, and final outcomes. Regression models (linear or logistic) with self/partner BFI traits and role (buyer/seller) as predictors are fitted separately for humans and each LLM, and coefficients are compared to assess behavioral alignment.

## Key Results
- In human dialogues, neuroticism is the strongest predictor of lower acceptance and competitive escalation, whereas LLMs show dominance of extraversion and agreeableness in predicting outcomes.
- LLMs over-rely on transactional strategies (Proposal, Concession) and under-use Facts, resulting in a more polarized strategy distribution than humans' balanced mix.
- Humans dynamically shift from early Facts to later Interests/Concessions across dialogue stages; LLMs exhibit rigid, flat temporal patterns with persistent Proposal use.
- Claude and Gemini partially replicate human competitive reciprocity patterns; GPT-4o mini shows the least alignment across metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Trait-Behavior Mapping Divergence**  
   LLMs do not replicate the human personality–behavior mapping in conflict; they exhibit a different latent strategy-personality linkage. When prompted with equivalent Big Five traits, LLMs generate strategic behaviors whose statistical association with traits differs systematically from humans (e.g., humans: neuroticism → lower acceptance; LLMs: extraversion/agreeableness dominate). This indicates a misalignment in the decision policies underlying trait expression.

2. **Adaptive Temporal Strategy Shifts vs. Static Distribution**  
   Humans dynamically adapt their IRP strategy portfolio across dialogue phases (heavy early Facts usage shifts toward Interests, Concessions, and Proposals later). LLMs, in contrast, start with high Proposal and maintain flat trajectories, showing limited early fact-grounding and poor late-stage adaptation.

3. **Over-reliance on Transactional Strategies & Polarization**  
   LLMs (except Claude partially) over-use Proposal and Concession (transactional moves) while under-using Facts (grounding). They also show elevated Residual and Power/Rights volatility, indicating a rigid, less fluid interaction style compared to humans' opportunistic alternation between cooperation and competition.

## Foundational Learning
**Big Five Inventory (BFI) Personality Traits**  
- *Why needed:* The study matches LLM prompts to human BFI scores and tests if trait-induced behaviors align. Understanding BFI is prerequisite to interpreting trait effects.
- *Quick check:* If an LLM is prompted as "highly agreeable" and "low in neuroticism," what behavioral tendencies does human psychology predict in a conflict (e.g., more concessions, less competitive escalation)?

**Interests-Rights-Power (IRP) Framework**  
- *Why needed:* This is the core behavioral coding scheme; all strategic analysis depends on correct classification into Cooperative (Interests, Proposal, Concession), Neutral (Facts, Procedural), Competitive (Power, Rights), and Residual.
- *Quick check:* Is "According to the store policy, sales are final" an example of a `Rights` or `Facts` move? What about "I need this refund because my nephew is sick"?

**Regression Analysis for Causal Inference (with controls)**  
- *Why needed:* The primary evidence comes from regressing strategic outcomes and behaviors on personality traits, controlling for role. Interpreting beta coefficients and significance is essential.
- *Quick check:* In a regression where `Accept` is the DV, a significant negative coefficient for `SELF_NEU` in humans means what? What about a significant positive coefficient for `PARTNER_EXT` in an LLM?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Data Parallelization Engine → LLM Persona Configurator → Dialogue Simulator → IRP Annotator → Feature Engineering Module → Regression Analysis Suite

**Critical Path:**  
The process begins with the KODIS dataset and BFI trait sampling, proceeds to L2L prompt generation, then dialogue simulation, followed by IRP annotation, feature computation, regression fitting, and concludes with coefficient comparison between LLMs and humans.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Prompt Variation:* Minimizing prompt variation tests inherent representation but reduces generalizability.
- *Annotation Scalability:* Using LLM (GPT-4o) for IRP labeling enables scale but risks annotation bias; validated on a human subset.
- *Metric Granularity:* Analyzing aggregated cooperative/competitive categories boosts statistical power but loses nuance (e.g., distinguishing Proposal from Concession).

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Annotation drift: LLM-based IRP labels deviate >15% from human gold standard on critical classes (`Power`, `Rights`).
- Simulation collapse: L2L dialogues show >80% `Proposal` or >50% `Residual` usage across traits, indicating prompt failure.
- Regression failure: >50% of personality-IV coefficients are non-significant (p>.05) in L2L for strategies that are significant in KODIS, or vice versa.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. *Reproduce IRP annotation reliability:* Run the GPT-4o annotation prompt on the 25-dialogue human subset, compute A-Kappa against human judgments, target ≥0.80 for all categories.
2. *Replicate human regression baseline:* Using only KODIS data, fit the regression model for `Accept` and `Competitive Ratio`; verify that `SELF_NEU` has a significant negative coefficient for `Accept` and that `SELF_EXT`/`PARTNER_EXT` are significant for `Competitive Ratio`.
3. *Single-model L2L pilot:* Configure and run 50 simulations for one LLM (e.g., Claude) with personality profiles sampled from the KODIS distribution; compute strategy distribution for high Extraversion cases; expect elevated `Proposal` vs. human `Facts` dominance and flatter temporal curves.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across LLM architectures** – The study only evaluated three closed-source models. It remains unknown whether the observed divergences (e.g., neuroticism effects in humans vs. extraversion/agreeableness in LLMs) hold for open-source models with different training data and sizes.
2. **Impact of multimodal conflict cues** – Real-world disputes involve emotional tone and facial expressions; text-only prompts may omit critical contextual factors. Incorporating audio-visual inputs into LLM simulations could improve personality simulation fidelity.
3. **Architectural modifications for temporal adaptation** – LLMs show rigid temporal patterns (e.g., early Proposal overuse). Stage-aware prompting, memory augmentation, or fine-tuning on temporally annotated human dialogues might enable human-like strategy progression.

## Limitations
- The study is limited to a single dispute scenario (jersey sales), restricting generalizability to other conflict domains.
- IRP annotations rely on GPT-4o, which may introduce systematic bias despite validation; personality prompts may not fully capture human psychological mechanisms.
- Evaluation uses LLM-LLM self-play rather than human-LLM interaction, potentially overestimating misalignment in real-world deployments.

## Confidence
- Trait-behavior mapping divergence: Medium
- Temporal rigidity: Medium
- Transactional over-reliance: High

## Next Checks
1. **Human-label audit:** Re-annotate a stratified sample of L2L dialogues with human coders; compute Cohen’s κ against GPT-4o labels; assess how discrepancies affect regression conclusions.
2. **Domain shift:** Run simulations on additional dispute scenarios (e.g., rental negotiations, software licensing) to test whether the misalignment patterns persist across contexts.
3. **Prompt sensitivity:** Systematically vary prompt wording (synonyms, ordering) and temperature; measure stability of key regression coefficients; report variation to gauge robustness.
