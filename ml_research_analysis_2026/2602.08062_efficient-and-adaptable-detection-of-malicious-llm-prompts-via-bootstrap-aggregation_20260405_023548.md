# Efficient and Adaptable Detection of Malicious LLM Prompts via Bootstrap Aggregation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08062
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08062
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** BAGEL achieves an F1 score of 0.92 using only 430M total parameters (5 ensemble members), with an attack success rate of 0.095 and false positive rate of 0.066, outperforming billion-parameter baselines.

## Executive Summary
Existing LLM defenses face trade-offs between performance, efficiency, and adaptability. BAGEL introduces a bootstrap-aggregated ensemble of small (86M) fine-tuned classifiers with a random forest router for dynamic selection. It achieves state-of-the-art detection with minimal compute and adapts incrementally to new attacks, maintaining robust performance across updates while offering interpretable routing features.

## Method Summary
BAGEL constructs an ensemble of specialized classifiers (promptcops), each fine-tuned from a base 86M-parameter model (Prompt Guard 2) on a distinct attack subtype dataset. A random forest router, trained on nine handcrafted structural features, selects the most relevant specialist for a given prompt. Inference aggregates predictions from the router-chosen specialist and a random subset of other ensemble members. The decision threshold is calibrated on a global set to maximize F1. When new attacks emerge, the system updates by adding a new fine-tuned promptcop, retraining the router, and recalibrating the threshold—avoiding full retraining.

## Key Results
- F1 score: 0.92 with 5 ensemble members (430M total parameters).
- Attack success rate (ASR): 0.095.
- False positive rate (FPR): 0.066.
- Outperforms billion-parameter baselines (OpenAI Moderation, ShieldGemma).
- Maintains F1 ≥0.92 across nine incremental updates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Specialization via distinct training datasets gives each promptcop unique expertise, enabling the ensemble to cover a broad spectrum of malicious prompts.
- **Mechanism:** Each promptcop is fine-tuned on a specific dataset containing one attack subtype (e.g., jailbreaks). The router sends the prompt to the most relevant specialist, and the final decision aggregates predictions from a small subset that includes that specialist and random others.
- **Core assumption:** Different attack categories exhibit stable, discriminative syntactic/lexical patterns captured by fine-tuning a small classifier.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “BAGEL employs a bootstrap aggregation and mixture of expert inspired ensemble of fine‑tuned models, each specialized on a different attack dataset.” [section 4.2] Formal definition of datasets and fine-tuning. [corpus] Neighboring work on prompt-injection classifiers provides indirect support.
- **Break condition:** A novel attack sharing no features with existing datasets causes router misrouting and random exclusion of the correct detector, increasing ASR.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A lightweight random forest router, trained on structural features, reliably selects the most suitable promptcop, and routing errors are compensated by stochastic inclusion of additional members.
- **Mechanism:** Nine handcrafted features (prompt_length, whitespace_proportion, etc.) are extracted. A random forest is trained on a global calibration set to predict the best dataset index. At inference, the router outputs an index; n‑1 other promptcops are drawn randomly. Their probability outputs are averaged.
- **Core assumption:** The chosen structural features correlate strongly with attack type and remain stable.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “BAGEL uses a random forest router to identify the most suitable ensemble member, then applies stochastic selection.” [section 4.3] Lists features and training. [section 6.1] Router accuracy ≈0.806; predicted-expert strategy nearly matches oracle.
- **Break condition:** Router misroutes and random draw misses the correct specialist (especially with small n), raising ASR or FPR.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** New attacks are incorporated incrementally by fine‑tuning a fresh 86M promptcop and updating the router and threshold, avoiding costly full‑system retraining.
- **Mechanism:** Upon a new dataset, a base model is fine‑tuned and added. The global calibration set is extended, the random forest is retrained, and the threshold is re‑calibrated via a two‑stage coarse‑to‑fine search.
- **Core assumption:** The base architecture is expressive enough that fine‑tuning on a new attack set yields a competent specialist without forgetting.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “BAGEL updates incrementally by fine‑tuning a small prompt‑safety classifier (86M parameters) and adding the resulting model.” [section 4.5] Threshold search details. [section 6.2] F1 never falls below 0.92 across nine additions.
- **Break condition:** New attack is fundamentally different; fine‑tuning yields a poor specialist, or router retraining degrades accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
### Bootstrap Aggregation (Bagging)
- **Concept:** Ensemble method that reduces variance by aggregating predictions from models trained on different data samples.
- **Why needed here:** BAGEL adapts bagging by training each promptcop on a distinct attack dataset rather than bootstrap samples, creating diverse expertise.
- **Quick check:** How does using entirely separate datasets affect ensemble diversity compared to classic bootstrap samples?

### Mixture of Experts (MoE)
- **Concept:** A gating network routes inputs to specialized models.
- **Why needed here:** The random forest acts as a learned router; stochastic selection of additional experts provides robustness even when routing is suboptimal.
- **Quick check:** Why combine a deterministic router with random selection instead of always using the top‑ranked expert?

### Threshold Calibration
- **Concept:** Tuning the decision boundary to balance precision and recall.
- **Why needed here:** As the ensemble grows, the distribution of aggregated probability scores shifts; recalibrating τ after each update preserves optimal F1.
- **Quick check:** Why employ a coarse‑to‑fine search instead of an exhaustive grid scan for τ?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Feature extractor -> Random Forest router -> Selection controller -> n promptcops -> Probability aggregator -> Threshold

### Critical path
1. Receive prompt x.
2. Extract structural features f(x).
3. Random forest predicts index i*.
4. Build subset Sx = {Mi*} ∪ random n‑1 others.
5. Each Mj ∈ Sx returns malicious probability p_j(x).
6. Avg = (1/n) Σ p_j.
7. If Avg > τ → Malicious; else → Benign.

### Design tradeoffs
- *n*: Larger n improves robustness but costs more compute; saturation near n=5.
- Base model size: Smaller (86M) allows many promptcops; larger models reduce per‑member latency but limit ensemble breadth.
- Feature set: More features may boost router accuracy; pruning to 5 features caused negligible drop (0.806→0.794).
- Router retraining: Must be done on every update but incurs modest cost vs. full system retraining.

### Failure signatures
- ASR spikes while FPR stays low ⇒ router misrouting and unlucky random draws; increase n.
- High FPR with low ASR ⇒ τ too low; recalibrate threshold.
- Router accuracy falls below ~0.75 ⇒ feature distribution shift; need more recent calibration data.
- New promptcop shows poor validation (high ASR) ⇒ dataset too small/noisy; monitor before adding.

### First 3 experiments
1. *Ensemble/Selection Efficiency Analysis:* Fixed k=9, varied n, measured ASR/FPR; found n=5 yields near‑optimal F1 with 45% fewer active members.
2. *Adaptability Analysis:* Sequentially added datasets (k=3→9), re‑calibrated each time; F1 remained ≥0.92 across nine updates.
3. *Interpretability Analysis:* Computed Spearman correlations among router features, performed hierarchical clustering, and pruned to 5 features with negligible accuracy loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can BAGEL detect malicious prompts in multi-turn conversations without major changes?
- **Basis:** Section 7.2 states multi-turn effectiveness is untested and needs extension.
- **Why unresolved:** Evaluations used single-prompt datasets; temporal dynamics may break routing/aggregation.
- **What evidence:** Test on multi-turn dialogue datasets with malicious intents, measuring detection and latency.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Will BAGEL adapt to fundamentally novel attack categories beyond harmful, jailbreak, and injection?
- **Basis:** Section 7.2 says it "remains an open question" whether novel paradigms could break fine-tuning-only adaptation.
- **Why unresolved:** Base model may lack features for truly new attacks, possibly requiring full retraining.
- **What evidence:** Introduce synthetic attacks with new patterns; see if fine-tuning a promptcop suffices for detection.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can BAGEL support multi-class policy violation labeling while keeping modular updates?
- **Basis:** Section 7.2 suggests hierarchical classification as future work beyond binary detection.
- **Why unresolved:** Current design outputs binary score; multi-class may need extra classifiers or redesign.
- **What evidence:** Add a second-stage lightweight classifier per violation; evaluate accuracy and update cost.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How to reduce BAGEL's dependency on large labeled datasets for new attacks?
- **Basis:** Section 7.2 notes data collection bottleneck for novel attacks and suggests few-shot/zero-shot methods.
- **Why unresolved:** Fine-tuning needs many examples; early emergence of attacks yields few samples.
- **What evidence:** Explore data augmentation, meta-learning, or synthetic data; test detection with <100 examples per new attack.

## Limitations
- Unknown fine-tuning hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) and random forest configuration (tree count, max depth) block exact replication.
- Corpus lacks direct validation that attack subtypes exhibit stable discriminative syntactic patterns; support is indirect via related work.
- Evaluations used single-prompt datasets; multi-turn and truly novel attack categories remain untested.

## Confidence
- Specialization via dataset diversity: Medium (evidence from [section 4.2] but no direct ablation).
- Router + stochastic selection: High (0.806 accuracy, near-oracle performance in [section 6.1]).
- Incremental updates: High (F1 ≥0.92 across nine steps in [section 6.2]).

## Next Checks
