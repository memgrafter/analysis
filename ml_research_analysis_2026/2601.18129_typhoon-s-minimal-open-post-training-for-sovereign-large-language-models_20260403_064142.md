# Typhoon-S: Minimal Open Post-Training for Sovereign Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.18129
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18129
- **Reference count**: 36
- **Primary result**: A minimal post-training recipe (SFT + on-policy full-logits distillation + small-scale InK-GRPO) enables an 8B model to achieve state-of-the-art Thai legal reasoning (78.02% on NitiBench) while preserving general capabilities (~49% average) and strong Thai code-switching (93.4%), all within academic-scale compute (~2 days).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of developing sovereign large language models for regions like Thailand, where resource access is limited. The proposed Typhoon-S approach requires only minimal post-training—comprising supervised fine-tuning on mixed English/Thai data, on-policy full-logits distillation, and a tailored reinforcement method (InK-GRPO)—to simultaneously achieve high competence on region-specific tasks (e.g., Thai legal reasoning) and maintain general assistant capabilities. Under practical compute constraints, the method demonstrates that sovereign LLM development need not involve massive, costly retraining, offering a path for resource-constrained regions to build capable local models.

## Method Summary
The Typhoon-S recipe consists of three sequential stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a curated mix of English and Thai data to establish bilingual foundation; (2) on-policy full-logits distillation, where the SFT model generates responses that are distilled into a student model using the complete output distribution (not just token predictions); and (3) small-scale reinforcement fine-tuning with InK-GRPO, an extension of GRPO that incorporates next-token prediction to improve agentic performance on sovereign tasks. All steps are designed for efficiency, requiring only about two days of compute for an 8B-parameter model.

## Key Results
- Sovereign capability: The final model achieves 78.02% on the Thai legal benchmark NitiBench, exceeding a comparable agentic GPT-5 setup (75.34%).
- Code-switching: Thai-English code-switching performance reaches 93.4%, dramatically outperforming a Top-K distillation baseline (69.8%).
- General preservation: Average score across general benchmarks remains ~49%, indicating no catastrophic forgetting despite intensive sovereign adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method succeeds by balancing two competing needs: general capability retention and sovereign specialization. Mixed-language SFT provides a stable bilingual foundation. Full-logits distillation preserves the teacher model’s nuanced output distribution, avoiding the severity of knowledge loss seen in simpler distillation (e.g., Top-K). InK-GRPO’s integration of next-token prediction likely stabilizes reinforcement learning for agentic tasks, allowing targeted optimization on Thai legal reasoning without extensive compute.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Adapts a pretrained model to a specific data distribution; needed to initialize bilingual competence. *Quick check*: Evaluate on a held-out mix of English/Thai tasks.
- **Knowledge Distillation**: Transfers knowledge from a teacher to a smaller student model; needed to retain general knowledge during specialization. *Quick check*: Compare logit distributions between teacher and student on general queries.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) / GRPO**: Optimizes model outputs against a reward signal; needed for agentic sovereign performance. *Quick check*: Track reward improvement over training steps.
- **On-Policy Training**: Generates training data using the current model policy; needed to avoid distribution shift in distillation/RL stages. *Quick check*: Monitor diversity of generated responses.
- **Code-Switching**: Handling mixed-language input/output; needed for natural Thai assistant interaction. *Quick check*: BLEU/chrF on manually annotated code-switched utterances.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting**: Loss of previously learned knowledge; needed to preserve general capabilities. *Quick check*: Performance drop on pre-training or general benchmarks after adaptation.

## Architecture Onboarding
```
Mixed-Data SFT -> Full-Logits Distillation -> InK-GRPO
```
**Critical path**: Data curation (English/Thai mix) → SFT model training → Distillation data generation (on-policy sampling) → InK-GRPO reinforcement tuning → Multi-benchmark evaluation (general + sovereign).
**Design tradeoffs**: Compute efficiency vs. peak performance (minimal steps limit potential gains); data mix balance vs. specialization (Thai-heavy mix risks forgetting English); distillation fidelity vs. training stability (full logits are expensive but precise).
**Failure signatures**: Sharp decline in general benchmark scores (catastrophic forgetting); poor Thai legal performance (<60% on NitiBench); unstable RL training (reward oscillations or collapse); low code-switching quality (<80%).
**First three experiments**:
1. Replicate SFT stage only; evaluate general and Thai baselines to establish starting point.
2. Ablate full-logits distillation (replace with Top-K) and measure impact on code-switching and general scores.
3. Run InK-GRPO with varying reward scales/iteration counts on NitiBench to find optimal settings.

## Limitations
- The comparison showing superiority over GPT-5 on Thai legal tasks lacks independent verification due to unclear evaluation setup and limited API access.
- Critical implementation details (exact data composition, distillation/RL hyperparameters) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation covers only two sovereign tasks; generalizability to other Thai domains or different languages is unknown.
- No statistical reporting (e.g., standard deviation across random seeds) limits assessment of result robustness.

## Confidence
- **General capability preservation** (average ~49% without forgetting): Medium (supported by aggregate score, lacking task-specific breakdown).
- **Thai code-switching** (93.4% vs. 69.8% Top-K): Medium (large gap is compelling but depends on benchmark specifics and baseline implementation).
- **Agentic InK-GRPO on Thai legal** (78.02% > GPT-5 75.34%): Low (extraordinary claim with insufficient evidence on GPT-5 evaluation conditions).
- **Academic-scale compute** (2 days for 8B): Medium (plausible but hardware/data pipeline specifics are missing).

## Next Checks
1. **Independent replication of Thai legal results**: Run the identical InK-GRPO pipeline on NitiBench using openly available models (e.g., Llama-2 8B, GPT-4) and, if possible, direct GPT-5 API queries under the same prompt template to verify the reported performance gap.
2. **Ablation study**: Systematically remove full-logits distillation and InK-GRPO components to quantify their individual contributions to sovereign performance and catastrophic forgetting on both Thai and general benchmarks.
3. **Domain diversity evaluation**: Test the Typhoon-S recipe on additional Thai-specific tasks (e.g., local legal document analysis, medical record interpretation, dialect translation) and report performance to establish the scope of sovereign capability transfer.
