# Inducing Epistemological Humility in Large Language Models: A Targeted SFT Approach to Reducing Hallucination

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17504  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17504  
- **Reference count:** 39  
- **Primary result:** Training on validated non-existent terms via HypoTermInstruct reduces hallucination (HypoTerm Score +0.19% to +25.91%) with minimal impact on general knowledge (MMLU -0.26% to -0.35%), but effects vary by architecture.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses hallucination in LLMs, partly caused by standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) that rewards always-respond behavior. It introduces HypoTermInstruct, an SFT dataset of questions about rigorously validated non-existent ("hypothetical") terms, to teach models to recognize knowledge limits and express uncertainty. Through 800 controlled LoRA SFT runs on Llama3.1-8B and Gemma3-4B, replacing generic instruction data with HypoTermInstruct significantly reduced hallucination on a dedicated benchmark while preserving general knowledge performance. The approach demonstrates that targeted SFT can instill meta-cognitive humility without reinforcement learning, though architectural differences lead to varying trade-offs in capability retention.

## Method Summary
The study constructs HypoTermInstruct (11,151 Q-A pairs) from a validated set of 676 non-existent terms (HypoTermQA-Enhanced). In a controlled experiment, 8,961 samples from a 68,856-sample control dataset (seven public instruction blends) are replaced with HypoTermInstruct, keeping total size constant. Models are fine-tuned via LoRA across diverse hyperparameter configurations (100 random seeds per model-checkpoint pair). Evaluation uses multiple metrics: HypoTerm Score and FactScore for hallucination, MMLU for general knowledge, IFEval for instruction following, and Safety scores. Paired statistical tests (Wilcoxon) assess significance against control runs.

## Key Results
- HypoTerm Score (hallucination measure) increased by median +0.19% to +25.91% across configurations.
- FactScore improved by +0.39% to +0.86%.
- MMLU showed minimal decreases: -0.26% to -0.35%, indicating preserved general knowledge.
- Gemma3-4B exhibited substantially larger gains and smaller MMLU trade-offs than Llama3.1-8B.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Abstract Heuristic for Uncertainty Recognition
- **Claim:** Targeted SFT on hypothetical terms teaches a transferable meta-cognitive behavior by decoupling knowledge-boundary recognition from factual content.
- **Mechanism:** The model learns an abstract heuristic to signal uncertainty when encountering lexical/semantic patterns associated with absent parametric knowledge, rather than memorizing specific terms.
- **Core assumption:** Validated non-existent terms are uniformly unfamiliar, forcing reliance on learned meta-cognitive rules.
- **Evidence anchors:** Paper states training fosters "generalized pattern of uncertainty recognition"; section 3 notes domain-independent behavior; linear probes show disentangled uncertainty signal.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonal Uncertainty Vector in Residual Stream
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning induces a separable internal signal for distinguishing real vs. hypothetical terms, concentrated in later layers, enabling targeted intervention.
- **Mechanism:** Linear probes reveal an "Uncertainty feature" nearly orthogonal (|cos|<0.02) to Safety and Knowledge vectors, allowing modulation without erasing factual knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs linearly represent abstract concepts; fine-tuning can carve new, disentangled directions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 6.2 reports orthogonality; section 6.1 shows Logit Lens late-stage intervention; related to representation engineering.

### Mechanism 3: Architectural Differences in Adaptation Efficiency
- **Claim:** Gemma3-4B adapts surgically via output-projection updates, while Llama3.1-8B undergoes systemic rotation across modules, causing greater interference.
- **Mechanism:** SVD of LoRA weights shows Gemma adapters concentrate in `o_proj`, leaving internal circuits intact; Llama adapters show low similarity (<0.4) across all modules, suggesting broader trajectory shift.
- **Core assumption:** LoRA rank is limited; distributed updates crowd out other task vectors more than localized ones.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 6.3 contrasts "Surgical" vs. "Systemic" adaptation; section 5 shows Llama's smaller gains and larger MMLU/IFEval drops.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Term validation (HypoTermQA-Enhanced) -> Dataset assembly (Control/Experimental) -> LoRA SFT on Llama3.1-8B/Gemma3-4B -> Evaluation (HypoTerm Score, FactScore, MMLU, IFEval, Safety)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Validate hypothetical terms against current corpora (Dolma, multi-engine search).
  2. Assemble Control and Experimental datasets with identical total size (68,856), swapping 8,961 generic instructions for HypoTermInstruct.
  3. Run minimal LoRA SFT (1 epoch, rank 8, lr 1e-4) on one architecture-checkpoint pair.
  4. Evaluate on HypoTermQA-Enhanced, FactScore, MMLU to confirm effect direction.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Data: Synthetic generation may inject biases; manual validation recommended.
  - Metrics: HypoTerm Score domain-specific; MMLU trade-offs suggest overcorrection risk.
  - Architecture: Gemma shows large gains/minimal drop; Llama shows modest gains/noticeable drops.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Over-refusal on valid queries: Monitor "Silent Refusal" rate spike.
  - Verbosity inflation: Gemma shows +42–49% verbosity on benchmark.
  - Safety degradation: Base models show significant drops; plan downstream alignment.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce HypoTermQA-Enhanced validation: Check 100 terms against latest sources.
  2. Minimal training run: Fine-tune Gemma3-4B Base with specified config; expect ~+5–10% HypoTerm gain, ~0.3% MMLU drop.
  3. Adapter module ablation: Train Gemma with LoRA only on `o_proj` vs. all layers; test for surgical update benefits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why does MMLU consistently drop? Hypothesized competition for LoRA capacity in final layers, but causal link unclear.
2. Why do safety scores degrade, especially in base models? Attributed to shift toward maximal helpfulness when certain, but recovery and safety-uncertainty interaction untested.
3. Why larger hallucination reduction in Gemma vs. Llama? Linked to architectural adaptation differences (surgical vs. systemic), but generalizability beyond two models unknown.
4. Do standard evaluations misalign with trustworthy AI? Speculative based on MMLU trade-off; requires uncertainty-aware benchmarks to test.

## Limitations
- Dataset validity: HypoTermInstruct/QA not public; potential term leakage or semantic closeness to real concepts.
- Evaluation fidelity: Judge model (Llama3.3-70B) prompts and human agreement unreported; automated scoring bias possible.
- Generalization scope: Hallucination reduction may not extend to open-domain or creative tasks; benchmark-specific effect.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (HypoTermInstruct reduces hallucination): High (consistent gains across 800 runs)
- Claim 2 (Meta-cognitive mechanism via abstract heuristic): Medium (supported by probes but causal generalization inferential)
- Claim 3 (Architectural differences drive side-effects): Medium (SVD evidence robust for studied models, not generalizable)
- Claim 4 (Minimal MMLU impact): High (small average drops, but model-dependent trade-offs)
