# RVR: Retrieve-Verify-Retrieve for Comprehensive Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18425
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18425
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RVR achieves at least 10% relative and 3% absolute improvement in MRecall@100 over strong baselines on QAMPARI, with efficient execution and consistent gains on out-of-domain datasets.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of retrieving comprehensive document sets for queries that admit many valid answers. Standard retrievers often fail to cover all answers within a fixed top-k. To this end, the authors propose Retrieve-Verify-Retrieve (RVR), a multi-round framework that iteratively expands coverage. An initial retriever fetches a candidate set; a verifier filters relevant documents; subsequent retrievals are conditioned on the verified set to target missing answers. RVR is compatible with off-the-shelf retrievers and verifiers; fine-tuning retrievers within the loop yields additional gains. Evaluations on the QAMPARI benchmark show substantial improvements in multi-answer recall compared to state-of-the-art agentic search systems, and the approach generalizes to out-of-domain datasets with favorable efficiency.

## Method Summary
RVR operates in a loop. First, a retriever (e.g., BM25 or dense retriever) fetches the top-100 documents for the query. A verifier—implemented as a zero-shot LLM or a binary classifier—assesses each candidate’s relevance to the query and retains those deemed pertinent. The verified documents then inform the next retrieval round: the query is either augmented with terms from these documents or the retriever is explicitly conditioned on them, encouraging discovery of documents covering answers not yet captured. This retrieve-verify-retrieve cycle repeats for a fixed number of iterations T. After each round, the newly retrieved candidates are verified and merged into the growing set of relevant documents. The process stops when T is reached or a coverage threshold is met. The framework allows plug-and-play of various retrievers and verifiers; fine-tuning the retriever on data generated by RVR’s own iterations further improves performance.

## Key Results
- On QAMPARI, RVR improves MRecall@100 by at least 10% relative and 3% absolute over a range of baselines, including iterative retrieval and agentic search systems.
- Gains transfer to out-of-domain datasets QUEST and WebQuestionsSP, demonstrating robustness.
- Efficiency analysis shows RVR is faster and less memory-intensive than agentic baselines, while achieving higher recall.
- Oracle verifier experiments reveal significant headroom: with perfect verification, recall continues to improve for up to five rounds, indicating that better verifiers could yield further gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RVR’s iterative design directly tackles the coverage problem. The initial retrieval casts a wide net but typically captures only the most frequent answers. The verifier acts as a quality filter, ensuring that subsequent operations focus on genuinely relevant material. Conditioning the next retrieval on the verified set steers the system toward documents that likely contain missing answers, effectively exploring complementary regions of the document space. This loop gradually fills gaps in answer coverage. Moreover, using the verified set as context helps disambiguate the query and retrieve diverse supporting documents. The framework’s modularity permits leveraging strong pre-trained retrievers and verifiers, while fine-tuning aligns the retriever with the iterative objective, further boosting performance.

- **Document Verification**  
  *Why needed:* Candidate sets contain noise; a verifier filters irrelevant documents to focus effort.

- **Conditional Retrieval**  
  *Why needed:* Conditioning retrieval on already verified documents directs the search toward missing answers.  
  *Quick check:* Are new retrieved documents semantically related to the verified set (e.g., high embedding similarity)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: User query → Initial Retriever → Candidate Set C0 → Verifier → Verified Set V0 → Conditional Retriever (conditioned on V0) → Candidate Set C1 → Merge with V0 → New Verified Set V1 → (repeat until stopping condition).  

Critical path: The core loop consists of (1) retrieve candidates, (2) verify relevance, (3) condition retrieval on verified set, (4) merge and repeat.  

Design tradeoffs:  
- Off-the-shelf retrievers are easy to deploy but may be suboptimal; fine-tuning improves in-domain recall but risks domain shift.  
- Number of rounds T: More rounds increase coverage but also latency; the LLM verifier shows diminishing returns after T=2.  
- Verifier choice: LLM-based verifiers are flexible and require no training data but are slow; cross-encoders are faster but need labeled examples.  

Failure signatures:  
- MRecall plateaus after two iterations when using the LLM verifier, indicating redundant document selection.  
- Fine-tuned retriever underperforms on out-of-domain datasets, evidencing domain shift.  
- Verifier may eliminate relevant documents, harming recall if too strict.  

First experiments:  
1. Replicate RVR with BM25 as retriever and a simple lexical overlap verifier to establish baseline behavior.  
2. Ablate the number of rounds (T=1–5) to quantify recall growth and identify plateau points.  
3. Compare two verifier types (LLM-based vs. cross-encoder) on recall, precision, and wall-clock time.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can we design a verifier that selects diverse, non-redundant documents across rounds to avoid performance plateau?  
- **Basis in paper**: Performance with the LLM verifier plateaus after the second iteration... indicating substantial headroom for better verification mechanisms. The LLM verifier tends to select redundant documents.  
- **Why unresolved**: The off-the-shelf LLM verifier lacks explicit diversity promotion, limiting gains from additional rounds.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A fine-tuned verifier with diversity constraints showing continued improvement beyond two rounds on MRecall.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How to mitigate domain shift when fine-tuning retrievers for RVR on out-of-domain datasets?  
- **Basis in paper**: We find that fine-tuned fi underperforms the baseline on these datasets, potentially due to domain shift. The authors resort to base retrievers for initial retrieval.  
- **Why unresolved**: Domain shift reduces the benefit of fine-tuning, hindering generalization.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Domain adaptation methods for retrievers evaluated on QUEST and WebQuestionsSP, showing improved performance over base retrievers.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can verifier and retriever be trained jointly to optimize overall coverage?  
- **Basis in paper**: We do not train the verifier, and use off-the-shelf LLMs. Retrievers are fine-tuned with a fixed verifier.  
- **Why unresolved**: Separate training may lead to suboptimal coordination; joint training could improve synergy.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An end-to-end differentiable or RL framework training both components, with results surpassing staged training.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What is the optimal stopping point for iterative retrieval, and can it be determined dynamically?  
- **Basis in paper**: With LLM verifier, gains plateau after T=2; with oracle verifier, gains continue to T=5. Fixed T is used.  
- **Why unresolved**: A fixed number of rounds is inefficient; queries vary in answer coverage needs.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analysis of coverage curves and a learned stopping criterion that adapts T per query, improving efficiency/recall trade-off.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation: Primary results on QAMPARI; out-of-domain tests on only two datasets, raising questions about broad applicability.  
- Efficiency claims: Lack details on hardware, software stack, and reproducibility; comparisons against agentic baselines may be cherry-picked.  
- Verifier analysis: The paper does not thoroughly characterize the verifier’s errors or its sensitivity to architecture/hyperparameters, leaving the core filtering mechanism opaque.
