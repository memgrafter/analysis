# How to Label Resynthesized Audio: The Dual Role of Neural Audio Codecs in Audio Deepfake Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16343
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16343
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Labeling strategy for codec-resynthesized audio (bonafide vs. spoof) significantly impacts deepfake detection performance; optimal choice depends on whether the neural audio codec is compression-oriented (bonafide better) or synthesis-oriented (spoof may be better).

## Executive Summary
Neural audio codecs (NACs) serve a dual role: they compress audio for transmission (producing codec-resynthesized, CoRS, audio that should be considered bonafide) and they decode tokens in codec-based speech generation (CoSG) attacks, where the output is spoof. This creates ambiguity in labeling CoRS data during training for audio deepfake detectors. The paper introduces the CodecDeepfakeDetection (CDD) dataset, extending ASVspoof 5, and evaluates two labeling strategies: treating CoRS as bonafide or as spoof. Results show that the optimal strategy depends on the NAC's primary design: compression-oriented codecs (e.g., DAC) benefit from bonafide labeling to avoid false positives on legitimate compressed audio, while synthesis-oriented codecs (e.g., Mimi) may require spoof labeling to focus on generative artifacts. The work highlights the need to consider codec functionality when constructing training data for robust detection.

## Method Summary
The study uses the CDD dataset, which includes raw bonafide speech, CoRS samples (created by resynthesizing bonafide audio with four NACs: EnCodec, Mimi, DAC, XCodec2), and CoSG spoof samples from various TTS systems using those NACs. Two anti-spoofing models are employed: X-AASIST (cross-entropy) and LWBN (one-class), both using a frozen wav2vec2-XLS-R frontend for feature extraction. The core experimental manipulation is a training-time augmentation: with 50% probability, a raw bonafide sample is replaced by its CoRS version; the label is either kept as bonafide or flipped to spoof, representing the two strategies. Models are trained on the CDD training set with these augmentations and evaluated on three test configurations: T-CoSG (raw bonafide + CoSG spoof), T-CoRS (CoRS bonafide + CoSG spoof), and All (combined). Performance is measured by Equal Error Rate (EER).

## Key Results
- Treating CoRS as bonafide reduces EER on T-CoRS test sets by up to 17.92% for compression-oriented NACs like DAC.
- Treating CoRS as spoof improves detection of some CoSG attacks but increases EER on T-CoRS for compression-oriented NACs (by up to 14.08% for DAC).
- For synthesis-oriented NACs like Mimi, bonafide labeling can hurt CoSG detection (e.g., CSM), suggesting spoof labeling may be preferable.
- The optimal labeling strategy is contingent on the NAC's primary design objective: compression-oriented codecs favor bonafide labeling, synthesis-oriented ones may favor spoof labeling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Artifact-spoof correlation from spoof labeling
When CoRS is labeled as spoof during training, the detector learns to associate codec-specific artifacts (e.g., spectral distortions) with the spoof label because those artifacts appear exclusively in spoof samples (since only CoSG uses that NAC). At test time, when bonafide samples resynthesized with the same NAC appear in T-CoRS, these benign artifacts trigger false positives, inflating EER. This effect is pronounced for compression-oriented NACs whose artifacts are subtle but consistent.

### Mechanism 2: Codec-invariant learning from bonafide labeling
When CoRS is labeled as bonafide, the model sees identical codec artifacts in both bonafide (CoRS) and spoof (CoSG) samples. This forces the detector to rely less on codec-specific distortions and more on other cues, such as artifacts from the TTS generation process. Consequently, performance on T-CoRS improves because codec artifacts are no longer spoof indicators. However, if the NAC is synthesis-oriented, CoRS may already contain generative-like artifacts that overlap with CoSG, causing confusion and potentially harming CoSG detection.

### Mechanism 3: Dependence on NAC design objective
Compression-oriented NACs (e.g., DAC) prioritize perceptual fidelity, producing CoRS artifacts that are acoustically plausible and dissimilar to typical spoof cues. Labeling such CoRS as spoof misleads the detector. Synthesis-oriented NACs (e.g., Mimi) are optimized for tokenization efficiency in TTS; their artifacts may resemble those in CoSG, so labeling CoRS as spoof helps the model treat those artifacts as generative. Hybrid NACs (e.g., EnCodec) exhibit mixed behaviors, indicating no universal strategy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Raw waveform → Augmentation (RIRNoise/RawBoost + optional CoRS replacement with label flip) → wav2vec2-XLS-R frontend → X-AASIST or LWBN backend → detection score.

**Critical path:** The data loading pipeline's conditional substitution (replacing raw bonafide with CoRS and possibly flipping the label) is the pivotal design choice; it directly shapes the artifacts the model learns to associate with classes.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Bonafide labeling improves robustness to legitimate compressed audio (lower T-CoRS EER) but may reduce sensitivity to CoSG attacks for synthesis-oriented NACs.
- Spoof labeling may improve CoSG detection for some NACs but increases false positives on T-CoRS for compression-oriented NACs.
- Using a single NAC for augmentation limits generalization to other NACs; multi-NAC strategies are unexplored.

**Failure signatures:**
- EER >25% on T-CoRS for a specific NAC when its CoRS was labeled as spoof during training.
- >5% EER increase on T-CoSG when using bonafide labeling for a synthesis-oriented NAC.
- Poor generalization to unseen TTS systems even when they share the same NAC tokenizer (e.g., CBX vs. CV2).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline transfer: Train on ASVspoof5, test on CDD; and train on CDD without augmentation, compare T-CoSG vs. T-CoRS performance.
2. Bonafide labeling ablation: For a chosen NAC (e.g., Mimi), set `CoRS_as_bonafide=True` during training; observe EER changes on T-CoRS (should improve) and T-CoSG_CSM (may worsen).
3. Spoof labeling ablation: For a chosen NAC (e.g., DAC), set `CoRS_as_spoof=True`; observe EER changes on T-CoRS (likely worsens) and T-CoSG_MS5/CSM (may improve for some).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Disentangling codec artifacts from spoof cues:** For hybrid NACs like DAC, codec artifacts and spoof signals are entangled; no method yet separates them to guide labeling.
2. **Generalization of labeling effects:** Results are based on two detectors and one dataset; it's unclear if findings hold for other architectures or data sources.
3. **Codec properties determining labeling strategy:** The paper broadly categorizes NACs but doesn't identify which architectural features (e.g., token rate, quantization) predict optimal labeling.
4. **Automating labeling decisions:** Relying on manual assessment of NAC design intent is error-prone and doesn't scale; an algorithmic approach to choose labels for novel NACs is needed.

## Limitations
- Limited to four NACs and a few TTS systems; broader generalizability is unknown.
- Dataset splits and speaker coverage details are not publicly available, risking hidden biases.
- Mechanistic explanations (artifact correlation, invariant learning) are inferred, not causally validated.
- Results are tied to wav2vec2-XLS-R frontend and specific backends; other feature extractors may behave differently.
- The boundary between compression- and synthesis-oriented codecs is ambiguous for hybrid NACs like EnCodec.

## Confidence
- Impact of labeling strategy on EER: **High** (consistent across detectors and NACs).
- Bonafide labeling improves T-CoRS performance for compression-oriented NACs: **High** (up to 17.92% EER reduction).
- Spoof labeling improves CoSG detection for synthesis-oriented NACs: **Medium** (evidence for Mimi but limited TTS coverage).
- Optimal labeling depends on NAC design: **Medium** (supported by DAC vs. Mimi, but hybrid cases are ambiguous).
- Artifact-based mechanisms: **Low–Medium** (plausible but not proven).
