# ARLArena: A Unified Framework for Stable Agentic Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21534
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21534
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SAMPO achieves consistently stable training and superior performance across multiple agentic tasks, including 92.72% success on ALFWorld, 74.08% on WebShop, and 88.86% on Sokoban, representing an average 25.2% improvement over the GRPO baseline.

## Executive Summary
Agentic reinforcement learning (ARL) with large language models suffers from severe training instability and collapse, limiting its scalability to long-horizon tasks. This paper introduces ARLArena, a unified framework that establishes a standardized testbed and systematically decomposes policy gradient methods into four design dimensions: loss aggregation, importance sampling (IS) clipping, trajectory filtering, and advantage design. Through controlled experiments, the authors identify key stabilizing choices: sequence-level clipping prevents token-level instability, fine-grained advantage design alleviates reward sparsity, and dynamic filtering removes degenerate gradient batches. Guided by these insights, they propose SAMPO, a stable agentic policy optimization method that integrates these components. Empirically, SAMPO demonstrates robust training stability and state-of-the-art performance on ALFWorld, WebShop, Sokoban, and TIR Math, with average gains of 25.2% over the GRPO baseline.

## Method Summary
ARLArena provides a common foundation for agentic RL research by defining a reproducible training pipeline. Starting from a base LLM, the process begins with behavior cloning initialization using self-generated high-quality trajectories, followed by reinforcement fine-tuning. The framework allows systematic variation of four algorithmic dimensions: how loss is aggregated across tokens (token-mean vs. sequence-mean), how IS clipping is applied (token-level with symmetric or tolerant thresholds vs. sequence-level), whether dynamic filtering removes groups with homogeneous rewards, and how advantage is constructed (global-only vs. global plus local step-level information). By isolating each dimension, the analysis reveals that sequence-level clipping, in combination with fine-grained advantage estimation and dynamic sampling, yields consistently stable updates. SAMPO combines these stabilizers—sequence-level clipping, GiGPO-style fine-grained advantages, and DAPO-style dynamic filtering—alongside the recommended training recipe (BC init, format penalty, KL regularization). This integrated method achieves stable improvement where baselines like GRPO, SAPO, or CISPO collapse.

## Key Results
- SAMPO avoids training collapse and exhibits monotonic improvement across all evaluated tasks (ALFWorld, WebShop, Sokoban, TIR Math).
- It achieves 92.72% success on ALFWorld, 74.08% on WebShop, and 88.86% on Sokoban, with an average 25.2% improvement over GRPO.
- The study identifies sequence-level clipping as the primary factor preventing instability, while fine-grained advantage design and dynamic filtering provide additional performance boosts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Sequence-Level Clipping Mitigates Off-Policy Drift
- **Claim:** Token-level IS clipping causes training collapse in agentic RL, while sequence-level clipping ensures stable improvement.
- **Mechanism:** In multi-turn agentic environments, individual token-level IS weights accumulate variance across long horizons. A few extreme token ratios can destabilize an entire trajectory's policy update. Sequence-level clipping (as in GSPO) computes a trajectory-level IS ratio from the average log-probability ratio across all tokens, then applies clipping once per sequence. This bounds the total update magnitude for any interaction, preventing pathological updates from outlier tokens.
- **Core assumption:** The unit of credit assignment and reward in agentic RL is the full interaction trajectory, not individual tokens.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 shows that CISPO and SAPO with tolerant clipping collapse around step 130, while GSPO (sequence-level) remains stable. Token-level analysis reveals that negative-advantage samples with low IS ratios are the main contributors to collapse; sequence masking (SAMOSM) that removes such samples also stabilizes training. Table 1 contrasts clipping designs.
- **Break condition:** Token-level clipping with asymmetric, tolerant thresholds (separate ε_low/ε_high) on sequences with high variance in token rewards leads to collapse. Sequence-level clipping may fail only if the aggregated log-ratio itself becomes extremely biased, but using exp(mean(log)) aggregation mitigates this.

### Mechanism 2: Fine-Grained Advantage Design Alleviates Reward Sparsity
- **Claim:** Incorporating environment-level information into advantage design (e.g., GiGPO's state-level grouping) improves performance by providing denser, more stable learning signals.
- **Mechanism:** Standard GRPO uses sequence-level normalized returns as the sole advantage, which is sparse for long-horizon tasks. GiGPO decomposes advantage into a global component (trajectory return) and a local component (step-level relative return within shared anchor states). This finer temporal resolution improves credit assignment across interactions with the same environment state, helping the policy distinguish useful steps within a trajectory.
- **Core assumption:** The environment's state provides auxiliary structure that can be exploited to reshape advantages more informatively than a single scalar per trajectory.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 reports that GiGPO generally outperforms GRPO (average score 49.71 vs 48.08). Section 5.1 emphasizes that increasing advantage diversity across finer scales is essential to overcoming reward sparsity. EMPG’s step-level entropy modulation shows task-dependent gains, suggesting that environmental information (like anchor states) is more broadly beneficial than purely statistical shaping.
- **Break condition:** If the environment lacks recurring states or the grouping function is noisy, fine-grained advantages may introduce more variance than signal. Anchor-state grouping must remain stable across rollouts.

### Mechanism 3: Dynamic Filtering Removes Degenerate Gradient Batches
- **Claim:** Dynamic resampling of trajectories with non-informative advantages (where all group rewards are identical) stabilizes training by ensuring gradients come from meaningful comparisons.
- **Mechanism:** In early agentic training, many rollout groups contain exclusively correct or exclusively incorrect trajectories due to format errors or policy collapse. These produce near-zero or constant advantages, yielding uninformative policy gradients. Dynamic filtering measures the equivalence set size for each sample: if all trajectories in a group have the same reward, the sample is filtered out and additional rollouts are generated until a threshold of informative diversity is met.
- **Core assumption:** Policy gradient methods require within-group variance in returns to compute meaningful relative advantages.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 introduces DAPO’s dynamic filtering, which adaptively removes degenerate groups. Finding 4 states that dynamic filtering with GiGPO benefits stability and performance. Section G.1 (Figure S7) shows DAPO+GRPO degrades format validity, while DAPO+GiGPO maintains stability, indicating synergy with diverse advantages.
- **Break condition:** If groups naturally produce homogeneous rewards (e.g., binary success/failure without partial credit), filtering may discard too much data and slow learning. A non-trivial fraction of groups must contain reward variance that can be captured by resampling.

## Foundational Learning

- **Policy Gradient Theorem & REINFORCE**  
  *Why needed:* ARLArena builds on the policy gradient objective ∇θL = E[wt ∇ log πθ A]. Understanding how the expectation over trajectories decomposes into token-level contributions is essential to grasp the four design dimensions.  
  *Quick check:* How does the expectation over trajectories relate to token-level gradients when a trajectory is decomposed into multiple turns?

- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Importance Sampling**  
  *Why needed:* All methods in ARLArena are PPO-style: they use importance sampling to reuse off-policy trajectories and clip the surrogate objective to bound policy updates. The clipping design (token vs. sequence) is the core stability lever.  
  *Quick check:* Why is clipping the importance ratio or the surrogate objective necessary for multi-turn agentic RL? What breaks without it?

- **Multi-Turn Agent-Environment Interaction Loops**  
  *Why needed:* Agentic RL differs from single-step RL because each "episode" consists of K turns where the policy conditions on history and the environment state transitions. This introduces compounding off-policy effects and format constraints not present in standard benchmarks.  
  *Quick check:* What specific challenges (e.g., invalid actions, sparse rewards) arise from segmenting a multi-turn trajectory into single-turn policy updates?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Testbed Constructor → Dimension Decomposer → Stability Analyzer → SAMPO Synthesizer

- **Testbed Constructor:** Applies behavior cloning, format penalties, KL regularization, and hyperparameter search to create a stable baseline.
- **Dimension Decomposer:** Categorizes existing and new PO methods into four axes: Loss Aggregation, IS Clipping, Dynamic Filtering, Advantage Design.
- **Stability Analyzer:** Tracks success rate, KL divergence, gradient norm, format ratio to identify collapse modes.
- **SAMPO Synthesizer:** Integrates the stabilizing components—sequence-level clipping (GSPO), fine-grained advantage (GiGPO), dynamic filtering (DAPO)—into a unified update rule.

### Critical Path
1. Initialize policy via behavior cloning on self-generated SFT data.
2. Add format penalty and KL regularization.
3. Run baseline GRPO to establish instability baseline.
4. Systematically vary one dimension at a time (e.g., IS clipping variants).
5. Identify stabilizing choices (sequence-level clipping, fine-grained advantage, dynamic filtering).
6. Combine stabilizers into SAMPO.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Loss aggregation:* Token-mean favors long trajectories; sequence-mean favors short ones. SAMPO uses token-mean after sequence-level clipping, avoiding sequence-mean’s length bias.
- *Clipping scope:* Token-level is sensitive to hyperparameters; sequence-level is more robust but may under-update on correct long sequences if the average ratio is low.
- *Advantage detail:* Fine-grained advantages (GiGPO) add environment-specific computation but improve credit assignment; global-only (GRPO) is simpler but sparse.
- *Dynamic filtering:* Requires careful group size calibration; too aggressive filtering reduces sample efficiency, too lenient leaves degenerate gradients.

### Failure Signatures
- Format collapse: Valid output ratio plummets.
- KL explosion: Off-policy KL between current and old policy diverges.
- Gradient norm spike: Indicative of unstable updates.
- Success rate degradation after initial rise: Classic collapse pattern.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Reproduce GRPO baseline instability on ALFWorld/Sokoban:** Train GRPO with default hyperparameters (ε=0.2) and observe collapse around 100-150 steps; record success rate, KL divergence, gradient norm.
2. **Isolate IS clipping effect:** Compare GRPO (token-level), GSPO (sequence-level), SAPO (tolerant), CISPO (stop-gradient) under identical conditions. Use sequence masking on SAPO/CISPO to verify the negative-advantage token hypothesis.
3. **Test advantage design and dynamic filtering:** Run GiGPO and EMPG alone, then apply DAPO to both. Monitor whether dynamic filtering interacts positively (with GiGPO) or negatively (with GRPO) with format validity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Minimal necessary and sufficient components:** What are the minimal necessary and sufficient components of training recipes to ensure stable ARL across diverse tasks? The paper’s recipe is task-specific; generalizable principles remain unknown.
- **IS clipping sensitivity:** What causes the high sensitivity of IS clipping hyperparameters in agentic RL, and can we develop clipping strategies with provable stability guarantees? The empirical findings lack theoretical grounding.
- **Scaling laws:** What are the empirical scaling laws for stable ARL with respect to interaction horizon, environment complexity, and data volume, and how do they compare to supervised pretraining scaling? The paper only evaluates up to moderate horizons, leaving scaling relationships unexplored.

## Limitations
- Critical implementation details are missing: behavior cloning data thresholds, dynamic filtering group size (G), anchor-state hashing, and the exact format penalty values.
- Evaluation is restricted to Qwen3-4B on four agentic tasks; no evidence for other model scales or domains.
- Mechanistic claims rely on correlational evidence from ablations without fully controlled single-factor isolation.
- SAMPO has not been compared to external RL stabilizers (e.g., PPO with KL control), limiting claims of superiority.

2. **Advantage Variance Correlation:** For GiGPO vs GRPO, compute per-group advantage standard deviation within anchor states. Correlate with learning speed on ALFWorld and Sokoban; higher variance should predict faster learning.
3. **Dynamic Filtering Sensitivity:** Vary group size G (e.g., 5, 10, 20) in SAMPO on WebShop. Measure filtered group fraction, rollouts per update, and final performance; expect an optimal G balancing diversity and efficiency.
