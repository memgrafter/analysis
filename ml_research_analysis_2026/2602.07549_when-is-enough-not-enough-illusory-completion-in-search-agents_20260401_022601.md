# When Is Enough Not Enough? Illusory Completion in Search Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07549
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07549
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LiveLedger, an inference-time constraint-tracker, reduces underverified answers by up to 26.5% and improves accuracy by up to 11.6% on multi-constraint QA.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates *illusory completion* in search agents—where agents prematurely believe they have satisfied all constraints in multi-constraint problems, leading to underverified answers. To diagnose this, the authors propose the Epistemic Ledger, an evaluation framework that separately tracks evidential support (`E`) and agent belief (`B`) for each constraint throughout reasoning. This reveals four failure patterns: bare assertions, overlooked refutations, stagnation, and premature exit. Building on this insight, they introduce LiveLedger, an inference-time intervention that provides real-time updates on constraint satisfaction. Experiments across multiple search agents and multi-constraint QA datasets show that LiveLedger consistently reduces underverified answers and improves accuracy, especially for larger models, demonstrating the value of explicit constraint-state monitoring for reliable multi-hop reasoning.

## Method Summary
The core method revolves around the Epistemic Ledger, a data structure that for each candidate answer and constraint records two states: evidential support `E` (SATISFIED, REFUTED, UNKNOWN) derived from search results, and agent belief `B` (AFFIRM, DENY, UNADDRESS). The ledger is updated incrementally after each agent step using a two-prompt scheme with an annotator model (`gpt-oss-120b`). LiveLedger is an inference-time wrapper that, after every action, invokes the *Objective Evidence Update* prompt to refresh `E` based on the latest observation, then feeds the updated ledger back to the agent to inform its next step. For prompt-based agents (e.g., ReAct), the ledger is included in the context; for RL-trained agents (e.g., TongyiDR), the ledger is concatenated to the observation. Evaluation uses a curated set of 215 multi-constraint QA instances from five benchmarks, measuring accuracy (via `gpt-5` judge) and underverified answer rate (UAR), and diagnosing failure patterns by analyzing `(E,B)` trajectories.

## Key Results
- LiveLedger reduces underverified answer rate (UAR) by up to 26.5% and increases accuracy by up to 11.6% across various search agents.
- The intervention mitigates all four failure patterns (bare assertion, overlooked refutation, stagnation, premature exit) for sufficiently capable models (e.g., 120B), but may worsen some patterns for smaller models (20B).
- Extent of candidate exploration (ECE) increases with LiveLedger, indicating more thorough search behavior.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies three mechanisms through which LiveLedger improves performance:

**Mechanism 1: Explicit Constraint Completion Awareness**
- **Claim:** LiveLedger reduces premature exits by making the agent explicitly aware of which constraints remain unsatisfied.
- **Mechanism:** After each search, the ledger updates show the agent the verification status (`E`) for each constraint. Seeing that some constraints are not SATISFIED, the agent continues searching rather than terminating early.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's policy can interpret the ledger and adjust its termination decision based on the constraint states.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "explicit constraint-state tracking during execution mitigates these failures"; Section §4: "By making constraint states explicit, we investigate whether LIVELEDGER can steer agents away from premature commitment"; Corpus: Weak support; neighboring paper "To Search or Not to Search..." (arxiv:2602.03304) highlights the importance of deciding when to stop searching.
- **Break condition:** If the agent ignores the ledger or remains biased toward early termination, the mechanism fails.

**Mechanism 2: Evidence-Guided Exploration**
- **Claim:** For models with sufficient search capabilities, LiveLedger reduces stagnation by revealing unverified constraints and directing new searches toward them.
- **Mechanism:** The ledger identifies which constraints are still UNKNOWN (`E=UNKNOWN`). The agent, conditioned on this information, formulates targeted queries for those constraints instead of repeating ineffective searches.
- **Core assumption:** The agent can generate search queries that specifically target the unverified constraints indicated by the ledger.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "explicit constraint-state tracking ... mitigates ... stagnation"; Section §5, Figure 7 shows LiveLedger increases the extent of candidate exploration (ECE) across models; Corpus: Weak; neighboring papers do not address constraint-driven exploration directly.
- **Break condition:** If the agent cannot generate effective queries for the unverified constraints, stagnation may persist despite ledger updates.

**Mechanism 3: Objective Evidence Belief Revision**
- **Claim:** For sufficiently capable models, LiveLedger reduces bare assertion and overlooked refutation by providing objective evidence (`E`) that can override erroneous internal beliefs (`B`).
- **Mechanism:** The ledger presents the agent with objective evidence (`E`) determined from search results. When `E=REFUTED` or `E=UNKNOWN` conflicts with the agent's assumptions, the agent can revise its beliefs and avoid making unsupported claims or ignoring contradictions.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's reasoning process conditions on the ledger and is influenced by the presented objective evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "explicit constraint-state tracking during execution mitigates these failures"; Section §5, Figure 5 shows reductions in bare assertion and overlooked refutation for ReAct-120B (29%→15% and 13%→7%), but increases for ReAct-20B (7%→11% and 10%→13%); Corpus: Weak; neighboring papers do not investigate belief-evidence alignment in multi-constraint settings.
- **Break condition:** If the model lacks capacity to integrate external evidence, the ledger may be misinterpreted or ignored, potentially increasing these failures.

## Foundational Learning
The analysis relies on several key concepts:

**Concept: Multi-constraint problems**
- **Why needed here:** The paper's analysis targets questions requiring simultaneous satisfaction of multiple conditions; this setting exposes illusory completion by making individual constraints independently verifiable.
- **Quick check question:** Given "Name a tech company founded before 1990, headquartered in Japan, and listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange," what are the three constraints?
  - *Answer:* (1) founded before 1990, (2) headquartered in Japan, (3) listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

**Concept: Epistemic ledger**
- **Why needed here:** The ledger separately tracks evidential support (`E`) and agent belief (`B`) for each candidate-constraint pair, enabling detection of underverified answers and diagnosis of failure patterns.
- **Quick check question:** What does it mean if for a candidate-constraint pair, `E=SATISFIED` but `B=UNADDRESS`?
  - *Answer:* The evidence supports that the constraint is satisfied, but the agent has not explicitly addressed (believed) that the constraint holds for the candidate. This could indicate the agent is unaware of the satisfied constraint, possibly leading to premature abandonment or underverification.

**Concept: LiveLedger**
- **Why needed here:** LiveLedger is the inference-time intervention that updates `E` after each search, providing the agent with real-time constraint verification status to steer its reasoning.
- **Quick check question:** What component of the ledger does LiveLedger update (`E` or `B`), and how is this update provided to the agent?
  - *Answer:* LiveLedger updates the evidential support component `E` after each step. The updated ledger (showing the new `E` values) is then fed back to the agent as part of its context (for prompt-based) or concatenated to the observation (for RL-trained).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**
Agent (π) -> Action (a) -> Environment -> Observation (o) -> LiveLedger -> Updated Ledger (L) -> Agent (π)

**Critical path:**
1. Agent initializes with question and empty ledger `L₀`.
2. For step `t`: agent uses `Hₜ₋₁` and `Lₜ₋₁` to generate `τₜ` and `aₜ`.
3. Execute `aₜ` to get `oₜ`.
4. LiveLedger updates using `(τₜ, aₜ, oₜ)` to produce `Lₜ`.
5. Append `Lₜ` to history and repeat until answer.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Using the same model for reasoning and ledger updates (prompt-based) is simpler but may produce inconsistent ledger states.
- Using a separate model for ledger updates (post-trained) avoids destabilizing the agent’s learned policy but adds computational cost.
- Updating only evidential support (`E`) keeps the ledger objective and leaves belief formation to the agent, but relies on the agent to integrate `E` effectively.

**Failure signatures:**
- Persistent failure patterns (bare assertion, overlooked refutation, stagnation, premature exit) even with LiveLedger.
- Incorrect ledger updates (e.g., marking `E=SATISFIED` without evidence) due to evaluator errors.
- Agent ignoring or misinterpreting the ledger, especially in smaller models.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline evaluation:** Run search agents (e.g., ReAct, TongyiDR) on the multi-constraint dataset without LiveLedger. Measure accuracy (Acc) and underverified answer rate (UAR). Use the epistemic ledger to categorize failure patterns.
2. **LiveLedger ablation:** Apply LiveLedger to selected agents and compare Acc, UAR, and failure pattern distributions to baseline. Test both prompt-based (ReAct) and post-trained (TongyiDR) models.
3. **Efficiency and exploration analysis:** Compare number of turns and extent of candidate exploration (ECE) with and without LiveLedger. Verify that reductions in UAR accompany more efficient and exhaustive search behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**
- **Question:** How can Epistemic Ledger–annotated trajectories be used to train search agents that fundamentally avoid illusory completion?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "For future work, we plan to leverage the Epistemic Ledger to collect high-quality agent trajectories for post-training optimization."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not implement or evaluate any post-training method using the ledger; it only proposes the idea.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training experiments where the ledger’s verification signals are used as supervision (e.g., via fine-tuning or RL) to penalize underverification, followed by evaluation on multi-constraint benchmarks to measure reductions in illusory completion and improvements in fully verified accuracy.

**Open Question 2**
- **Question:** Why does LiveLedger’s effectiveness depend so strongly on model scale, and what specific capabilities (e.g., instruction following, evidence integration) are required for it to consistently reduce all four failure patterns?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results show LiveLedger with a 120B model reduces multiple failures, while with a 20B model it increases bare assertion and overlooked refutation.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze the underlying model behaviors or capacities that mediate this interaction.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies probing model performance on ledger interpretation tasks, correlated with failure pattern shifts; error analysis to identify which constraint types smaller models mismanage when provided ledger updates.

**Open Question 3**
- **Question:** Is the Epistemic Ledger’s model-based evaluation reliable across diverse evidence types and domains, or does its reliance on an LLM evaluator introduce systematic biases that misclassify verification states?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The ledger uses gpt-oss-120b for updates; human validation was limited to 30 instances and primarily confirmed correct classifications, leaving potential edge cases unexplored.
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluator may misinterpret ambiguous or truncated search snippets, especially in domains with nuanced evidence, risking false positives/negatives in underverification detection.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale, multi-domain human evaluation (e.g., across web, academic, and specialty sources) comparing ledger classifications against expert judgments; stress-testing the evaluator on adversarially constructed examples with subtle evidence violations.

**Open Question 4**
- **Question:** Do the four identified failure patterns generalize beyond web-search agents to other multi-step reasoning paradigms (e.g., code generation, database querying, or tool-augmented mathematical reasoning)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The study exclusively uses web-based search agents on QA benchmarks; other tool-use contexts may involve structured outputs or different reasoning constraints.
- **Why unresolved:** Illusory completion might manifest differently (e.g., as logical oversights or execution errors) in non-web domains, and the ledger’s design assumes textual evidence.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Applying the Epistemic Ledger to agents using non-web tools (e.g., Python interpreters, SQL engines) on multi-constraint tasks in coding or data analysis, then analyzing whether the same failure patterns emerge or new ones arise.

## Limitations
- **Missing details:** Exact prompts, sampling parameters, and ledger update schemas are omitted, hindering replication.
- **Dataset closure:** The 215-instance multi-constraint subset and its decomposition method are unreleased, preventing verification on identical data.
- **Annotator bias:** Ledger states (`E`,`B`) rely on `gpt-oss-120b`; its error rates and systematic skew are unevaluated.

## Confidence
- **LiveLedger reduces underverified answers:** Medium confidence (results consistent but depend on unvalidated ledger updates and GPT-based metrics).
- **Four failure patterns are correctly identified:** Medium-High confidence (definitions are clear, but classification uses same annotator model; human validation is limited).
- **Mechanistic explanations for why LiveLedger works:** Medium confidence (evidence is indirect; counter-examples show capacity-dependence, leaving causality unproven).
