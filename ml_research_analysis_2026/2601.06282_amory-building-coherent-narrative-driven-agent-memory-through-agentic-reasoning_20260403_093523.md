# Amory: Building Coherent Narrative-Driven Agent Memory through Agentic Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06282
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06282
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Amory reduces response time by 50% while matching full-context accuracy and improves overall accuracy by 27.8% over Mem0 on the LOCOMO benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Long-term conversational agents suffer from high computational costs when processing full dialogue histories repeatedly. Amory introduces an agentic working memory framework that constructs structured episodic narratives during offline time, consolidates memories with momentum, and extracts peripheral facts into a semantic graph. At retrieval, coherence-driven reasoning over narrative structures replaces embedding similarity, yielding performance comparable to full-context reasoning with half the latency and significant gains over existing memory systems.

## Method Summary
Amory comprises five core processes operating on a conversational history: `MemInit` segments the initial dialogue (first 20 turns) into hierarchical episodic narratives; `MemBinding` assigns new utterances to existing narratives or spawns new ones based on LLM-judged continuity; `Consolidation` (triggered when a narrative becomes inactive) summarizes recent fragments into subplots and updates the main plot headline; `Semanticization` extracts peripheral fact triples into a Neo4j graph; and `MemRetrieving` combines an LLM coherence-based selection of narrative headlines with graph queries to augment the agent's context. The system targets the LOCOMO benchmark, aiming to reduce computational cost while preserving or improving answer accuracy, measured by LLM-as-judge scores and response latency.

## Key Results
- 50% reduction in response time compared to full-context reasoning while maintaining comparable accuracy.
- +27.8% absolute improvement in overall accuracy over Mem0.
- Inactive consolidation improves temporal reasoning by 4.6% over rapid consolidation.
- Coherence-driven retriever achieves higher memory coverage than embedding methods, especially for multi-hop queries.
- Context compression exceeds 96.3% at top-k=2.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Episodic Narrative Organization
**Claim:** Organizing fragmented conversational turns into coherent, hierarchical episodic narratives preserves contextual relationships and temporal logic, enabling more accurate recall for multi-hop and temporal queries than isolated embeddings.
**Mechanism:** During offline processing, an LLM segments the history into distinct narratives. Each narrative possesses a `topic` (headline), an `owner`, and a tree of `memory fragments`. New turns are bound to existing narratives or create new ones based on LLM-judged logical and chronological continuity.
**Core assumption:** An LLM can reliably infer narrative continuity and thematic coherence from conversational text, mimicking human episodic memory organization.
**Evidence:** Abstract states "organizes conversational fragments into episodic narratives"; Section 3.2 describes prompting to segment and group logically/chronologically connected events; related work (TraceMem, Memoria) explores narrative structures, suggesting community validation.
**Break condition:** Narrative fragmentation or mis-binding (e.g., forcing unrelated fragments into one narrative) degrades coherence and recall, especially for multi-hop reasoning.

### Mechanism 2: Momentum-Aware Consolidation
**Claim:** Consolidating narratives only after they become inactive (no recent updates) improves temporal reasoning and overall memory quality by aligning summarization with natural conversational topic completion.
**Mechanism:** The system monitors a narrative's active state. When no new fragments bind to it for a period, a consolidation step analyzes the last N=10 fragments to generate subplot headlines and potentially revise the main plot headline, forming a dynamic "plot → subplots" structure.
**Core assumption:** Conversational momentum—periods of focused topic discussion followed by shifts—provides an implicit signal that a narrative arc is complete, making consolidation safer and more coherent.
**Evidence:** Abstract mentions "consolidates memories with momentum"; Section 3.2 explains that inactivity avoids concurrent conflicts with memory binding and premature summarization; Table 2 shows inactive consolidation yields +4.6% absolute gain in temporal reasoning over rapid consolidation.
**Break condition:** Incorrectly identifying inactivity (e.g., marking a paused but continuing narrative as inactive) leads to premature and inaccurate consolidation, harming later recall.

### Mechanism 3: Coherence-Driven Retrieval
**Claim:** Using an LLM to reason over narrative headlines and characters to select relevant episodic memories superiorly covers relevant evidence for complex queries compared to embedding similarity, particularly for multi-hop questions requiring logical linkage.
**Mechanism:** At query time, an LLM is given the list of narrative headlines (and subplots) and selects the top-k most relevant based on narrative coherence with the query. Semantic memory (fact graphs) is queried in parallel via translated graph queries.
**Core assumption:** Human-like reasoning over story structure ("which story might discuss X leading to Y?") is more effective for recall than shallow semantic token overlap, especially for non-factoid, relational queries.
**Evidence:** Abstract notes "employs coherence-driven reasoning over narrative structures"; Section 4.3 Figure 4 (Left vs. Middle) shows coherence-driven retriever achieves significantly higher memory coverage than embedding-based, especially for multi-hop; Table 3 indicates agentic retriever selects narratives based on logical connection, not keyword overlap.
**Break condition:** If coherence reasoning is too slow or the LLM consistently misjudges narrative relevance (e.g., due to ambiguous headlines), the benefit diminishes and latency increases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Episodic vs. Semantic Memory (Cognitive Science)**
  - *Why needed:* Amory directly maps these human memory systems: episodic memory stores context-rich narratives (events tied to time/place), semantic memory stores decontextualized facts. Understanding this distinction explains why Amory splits memories and treats peripheral facts differently.
  - *Quick check:* Why might a fact about "Caroline's rainbow bridge painting" be stored in semantic memory, while "Caroline's process of creating that painting during a difficult time" be kept in episodic memory?

- **Narrative Cognition**
  - *Why needed:* The entire episodic memory structure (plots, subplots, characters) is built on the principle that humans understand and recall experiences as structured stories. This justifies the tree-based format and coherence-driven retrieval.
  - *Quick check:* How does representing a conversation about "movie preferences" as a narrative with subplots (e.g., genre debates, specific film recommendations) aid recall for "What movies did both Joanna and Nate see?" versus a flat list of movie mentions?

- **Conversational Momentum & Topic Inactivity**
  - *Why needed:* This is the trigger for consolidation. Recognizing that conversations have natural rhythms of focus and shift is core to the system's timing logic. It assumes topic shifts correlate with narrative completion.
  - *Quick check:* If a user discusses "planning a trip" over turns 5-10, then at turn 11 asks about "favorite foods," when would Amory consider the "trip" narrative inactive, and what consolidation might occur?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** `MemInit` → `MemBinding` → (`Consolidation`, `Semanticization`) → `MemRetrieving` → `Agent`.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Dialogue history exceeds threshold T → `MemInit` creates initial episodic narratives.
  2. Each new turn → `MemBinding` routes to existing narrative or spawns new one.
  3. Inactive narratives detected → async `Consolidation` summarizes (subplots, headline update).
  4. Concurrently, `Semanticization` extracts facts to graph DB.
  5. On query → `MemRetrieving` selects narratives via coherence reasoning and queries graph; retrieved context sent to agent.
  6. Agent generates response.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Latency vs. Coherence: Coherence retriever slower but better coverage; top-k compression lever.
  - Consolidation Timing: Inactive (higher quality, async compute) vs. Rapid (faster, risk of premature summarization).
  - Memory Structure Depth: Deeper narrative trees improve organization but increase retrieval reasoning complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Fragmented narratives: Same topic split across multiple narratives due to poor binding.
  - Poor multi-hop recall: Coherence retriever missing linked evidence; check narrative headline quality.
  - High retrieval latency: LLM-based coherence selection too slow; consider distillation or pruning.
  - Semantic noise: Graph populated with trivial conversational facts; tighten extraction prompt.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Binding Accuracy Audit:** Annotate 100 `MemBinding` decisions with human judgment on narrative continuity; measure agreement.
  2. **Consolidation Impact Ablation:** Compare inactive vs. no consolidation on a temporal-heavy query subset; expect +4.6% gain.
  3. **Retriever Head-to-Head:** On multi-hop questions, compare memory coverage and latency of coherence-driven vs. embedding-based retriever.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to real-world conversations:** The evaluation uses only synthetic LOCOMO data; performance on diverse, noisy, natural long-term interactions remains unknown. Resolution requires testing on collected human-agent dialogues across scenarios.
2. **Neural representations for memory:** Amory relies on LLM prompting for memory construction; learned neural (e.g., embeddings, GNNs) may be more efficient and biologically plausible. Resolution: implement and compare neural modules.
3. **Optimal inactivity detection:** The heuristic (no binding in previous turn) and window size N=10 are ad-hoc; sensitivity unexplored. Resolution: vary N and test alternative signals (e.g., topic coherence shift).
4. **Episodic-semantic boundary:** The criterion for extracting "peripheral" facts is vague and LLM-dependent; misclassification may harm coherence. Resolution: analyze extraction outputs and systematically adjust boundary.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility uncertain due to unspecified prompts and LLM hyperparameters.
- Evaluation limited to synthetic LOCOMO benchmark; generalizability to real-world, longer conversations unclear.
- Latency and quality of coherence-driven retrieval may differ in production; no human validation of narrative binding correctness.
