# Watching, Reasoning, and Searching: A Video Deep Research Benchmark on Open Web for Agentic Video Reasoning

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.06943
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06943
*   **Reference Count:** 6
*   **Primary Result:** The top model (Gemini-3-pro-preview) achieves 76% accuracy in the Agentic paradigm, significantly exceeding the human average of 50.4%, but the Agentic paradigm is not consistently superior to a two-stage Workflow approach.

## Executive Summary
VideoDR is a novel benchmark for "video deep research," where models must answer open-domain questions by extracting multi-frame visual cues from a video, conducting iterative web searches, and reasoning over combined video-web evidence. The paper compares two agentic paradigms: **Workflow** (a two-stage process with an intermediate textual summary of video cues) and **Agentic** (an end-to-end loop where the model manages state internally). Experiments reveal that the Agentic paradigm's performance is conditional on a model's ability to retain initial video anchors over long retrieval chains; its gains are offset by critical bottlenecks in goal drift and long-horizon consistency. Performance strongly correlates with question difficulty and video duration, with numerical reasoning remaining a persistent weakness across all models.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates six multimodal models (Gemini-3-pro-preview, GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B, InternVL3.5-14B, MiniCPM-V 4.5) on the VideoDR benchmark of 100 human-annotated samples spanning six domains. Two operational paradigms are implemented:
1.  **Workflow:** Stage 1 uses a multimodal LLM (MLLM) to extract cross-frame visual anchors from the video and generate a structured intermediate text summary. Stage 2 feeds this summary (without video re-access) into an iterative agent loop (`think` and `search` tools) to retrieve web evidence and synthesize the final answer.
2.  **Agentic:** A single MLLM processes the raw video and question in an end-to-end loop, autonomously deciding when to `search`, reflecting (`think`), and integrating evidence while internally maintaining state. The video is not revisited after initial processing.
Primary evaluation metric is binary answer accuracy, judged by DeepSeek-V3-0324, with secondary stratification by difficulty (based on human success rate), video duration, and domain.

## Key Results
*   The **Agentic paradigm is not universally superior**; its effectiveness depends on the model's capacity for long-horizon consistency. Weaker models (e.g., MiniCPM-V 4.5) perform worse in Agentic mode.
*   **Core bottlenecks** are **goal drift** and the inability to maintain **long-horizon consistency** of initial video anchors, especially on high-difficulty and long-duration questions.
*   The top model, Gemini-3-pro-preview, achieves **76% accuracy** in Agentic mode, substantially exceeding the **human average of 50.4%**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The Workflow paradigm's intermediate textual representation reduces goal drift during long retrieval chains.
*   **Mechanism:** By externalizing multi-frame visual anchors into a structured text summary before web search, the system provides a persistent, reviewable reference that downstream steps can consult, preventing misalignment between original video clues and evolving search queries.
*   **Core Assumption:** The model can generate a sufficiently precise and comprehensive textual summary of relevant video cues in one pass.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Agentic is not consistently superior to Workflow: its gains depend on a model’s ability to maintain the initial video anchors over long retrieval chains."
    *   [section 4.2] "Workflow externalizes video cues into an intermediate textual representation, effectively providing a repeatedly accessible external memory that reduces drift risk in long-horizon decision-making."
    *   [corpus] Neighbor papers on retrieval-augmented QA identify drift as a core bottleneck.
*   **Break Condition:** If the initial textual summary misses critical visual anchors or introduces inaccuracies, the Workflow system will propagate these errors.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Agentic mode’s performance gains are conditional on the model’s capacity for long-horizon consistency in retaining and utilizing initial video anchors.
*   **Mechanism:** In the end-to-end Agentic setting, the model must internally maintain the state of extracted visual clues from the first video viewing and use it to guide all subsequent search queries and evidence filtering without revisiting the video. Success requires robust internal state management over the evidence chain.
*   **Core Assumption:** The model’s internal state (e.g., context window, attention) can stably encode and recall specific, fine-grained visual details from a single processing pass.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [section 4.2] "Agentic preserves details more faithfully but relies more on long-term consistency."
    *   [section 4.2] "Agentic places higher demands on a model’s ability to retain and continuously leverage initial video cues over the long run, leading to strong polarization" in results by video duration.
    *   [corpus] Corroborated by neighbor work on long-context video understanding highlighting temporal evidence tracking as a challenge.
*   **Break Condition:** If the model’s internal state degrades or distracts over the long sequence of tool calls, the search process will drift.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The core bottleneck for both paradigms scales with question difficulty, as harder questions require longer, more fragile multi-hop evidence chains.
*   **Mechanism:** Difficulty stratifies the required length and dependency strength of the reasoning chain. Higher difficulty implies more steps, greater interdependence between clues, and less margin for error, amplifying the effects of perceptual grounding errors and long-horizon consistency failures.
*   **Core Assumption:** Human success rate is a valid proxy for the structural complexity and fragility of the required evidence chain.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [section 3.3.2] Difficulty is explicitly defined based on human success rate.
    *   [section 4.2] "All evaluated models exhibit a highly consistent decline in performance as the difficulty level increases... suggesting that our difficulty definition... indeed corresponds to longer and more fragile evidence chains."
    *   [table 1] Performance drops sharply from Low to High difficulty for all models.
*   **Break Condition:** If a question’s difficulty is misestimated (e.g., due to ambiguous annotation), the stratified analysis may not cleanly isolate chain length/fragility.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Cross-frame visual anchor extraction.**
    *   **Why needed here:** The task requires identifying and composing cues distributed across many video frames to form a coherent search query. Failure to associate information across time breaks the pipeline.
    *   **Quick check:** Given a video where an object appears only in frames 10s and 45s, can your model generate a single query that meaningfully connects observations from both moments to search for a related fact on the web?
*   **Concept: Multi-hop retrieval-reasoning over heterogeneous evidence.**
    *   **Why needed here:** The answer is not present in any single webpage or video frame. The model must plan a sequence of searches, filter results, and synthesize information that bridges video-originated queries and the final answer, often requiring 3+ distinct information pieces.
    *   **Quick check:** For a question requiring (Video clue A) + (Web fact B) + (Web fact C) where B and C are on different pages, can your system design and execute a search plan that discovers B, then uses B to refine the search for C, before synthesizing with A?
*   **Concept: Goal drift in long-horizon tool-use.**
    *   **Why needed here:** As the agent performs multiple `search` and `think` cycles, the original intent derived from the video can be lost due to irrelevant results, incorrect filtering, or context limits, leading the final answer astray.
    *   **Quick check:** Simulate an agent that, after two successful searches, starts generating queries that ignore a key constraint from the original video. Is this detectable from its tool call logs before the final answer?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    1.  Video Frame Sampler/Encoder -> processes input video, extracts frame features.
    2.  Visual Anchor Extractor (MLLM) -> takes sampled frames + question, outputs structured text summary of relevant multi-frame clues. *Critical for Workflow.*
    3.  Query Generator / Agent Controller -> for Agentic mode, this is the full MLLM; takes raw video+question (or intermediate summary) and decides when to call `search`, what query to use, and when to `think`.
    4.  Browser Search Tool -> executes queries, returns snippets/URLs.
    5.  Evidence Synthesizer (MLLM) -> aggregates information from all search results and initial video context/summary to produce final answer.
    *   **Workflow path:** Video -> Extractor -> Text Summary -> Synthesizer (with iterative search/think) -> Answer.
    *   **Agentic path:** Video + Question -> Agent Controller (internally manages state, issues search/think) -> Answer. Video not re-accessed.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Workflow:** More controllable, debuggable (summary is inspectable), reduces drift risk. Cost: potential information loss in one-shot summarization; summary quality is a single point of failure.
    *   **Agentic:** More flexible, potentially captures nuances missed by a fixed summary; preserves raw details. Cost: severely tests internal state management; harder to debug; amplified goal drift.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Categorical Error:** Answer is in wrong domain/entity type. Often indicates early visual misclassification or query mis-targeting.
    *   **Incomplete/Not Found:** Search chains terminate without acquiring necessary evidence. Points to poor query formulation or evidence filtering.
    *   **Numerical Error:** Correct entity identified but wrong numeric attribute (date, count). Suggests weak fine-grained reasoning over retrieved text.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Difficulty stratification replication:** Run both paradigms on Low/Mid/High splits. Expect Agentic performance equal or worse on High for weaker models, validating the "long-horizon consistency" bottleneck.
    2.  **Video duration ablation:** Test on videos <30s vs. >2min. Expect stronger Agentic gains *only* for top models on longer videos, and sharper drops for others. Validates the "anchor retention over time" requirement.
    3.  **Summary quality ablation (Workflow):** Manually inject perfect summaries for a subset of Agentic-failing cases. If Workflow performance jumps to Agentic levels, it confirms the summary is the key differentiator.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   How can video deep research agents be designed to mitigate goal drift and maintain long-horizon consistency across multi-hop retrieval and reasoning?
*   To what extent does the narrowness of human-annotated search trajectories in VideoDR limit the generalizability of models trained or evaluated on this benchmark?
*   Why do numerical errors remain a persistent bottleneck across all models and paradigms in video deep research, and how can they be specifically addressed?
*   Can a hybrid paradigm dynamically switching between Workflow and Agentic execution improve overall performance and robustness?

## Limitations
*   **Reproducibility blockers:** Exact prompt templates for the Workflow's intermediate text and the precise search tool specification (engine, snippet policy, state management) are not disclosed.
*   **Human-model performance gap:** Top model (76%) substantially exceeds human average (50.4%), raising questions about benchmark difficulty calibration or whether humans fully exploited web search.
*   **Causal isolation failure:** The paper does not disentangle whether Workflow's advantage stems solely from the external summary or from other implicit design choices.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1** (Workflow reduces drift via external text): **Medium**. Evidence is suggestive but depends on unimplemented prompt quality; a poor summary would break it.
*   **Mechanism 2** (Agentic gains require long-horizon consistency): **Medium**. Supported by duration/difficulty results, but the internal state variable is unobserved; alternative explanations exist.
*   **Mechanism 3** (Difficulty scales with evidence chain fragility): **High**. Directly validated by consistent performance drops across all models as human success rate decreases.

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate difficulty stratification:** Run both paradigms on the published Low/Mid/High splits. Expect to see Agentic underperform Workflow on High-difficulty samples for weaker models, confirming the long-horizon bottleneck.
2.  **Summary quality ablation (Workflow):** For a subset of Agentic-failing cases, replace the model-generated intermediate text with a human-curated "perfect" summary. If Workflow performance jumps to near-Agentic levels, it confirms summary quality as the key differentiator.
3.  **Search log drift analysis:** Manually inspect trajectory logs from Agentic runs on High-difficulty, long-duration videos. Quantify keyword/constraint retention over sequential `search` calls; expect a strong correlation between early query relevance and final accuracy.
