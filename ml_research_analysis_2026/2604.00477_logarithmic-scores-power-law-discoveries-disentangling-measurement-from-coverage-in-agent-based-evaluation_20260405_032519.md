# Logarithmic Scores, Power-Law Discoveries: Disentangling Measurement from Coverage in Agent-Based Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00477
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00477
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLM agent judges yield human-comparable evaluation reliability (p=0.38); scoring reliability grows logarithmically with panel size, while unique issue discovery grows sublinearly (power law), both contingent on structured persona conditioning.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the reliability and coverage of LLM-based agent judges for evaluating conversational AI. Using 32 persona-based agents conditioned on Big Five personality traits across 960 multi-round sessions, the authors measure scoring reliability (via intraclass correlation) and breadth of issue discovery as panel size increases. A human Turing test (n=50) shows that agent-human score differences are statistically indistinguishable from human-human differences (p=0.38), supporting the trustworthiness of such agents. The core finding is a dissociation: scoring reliability improves logarithmically with panel size (ICC from 0.29 to 0.93 as N grows from 1 to 32), while unique issue discovery follows a sublinear power law (y=7.8·N^0.69). A controlled ablation confirms that structured persona conditioning (Big Five profiles) is necessary to achieve these scaling properties; simple prompting or no persona fails. The results suggest that diverse agent panels can serve as scalable, controllable alternatives to human evaluation, with clear trade-offs between reliability and coverage.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework employs 32 LLM-based agent judges, each conditioned on a structured persona defined by Big Five personality traits (O/C/E/A/N scores), expertise level (Expert/Intermediate/Novice), age, and geographic region. Agents engage in multi-turn conversations with target conversational AI systems across 15 tasks spanning five domains and three complexity levels. Each agent simultaneously acts as a conversationalist (interacting with the target) and an evaluator (recording per-turn scores, rationales, and categorized issues in a diary). Emotional state (trust, frustration, engagement, patience, fatigue) evolves across turns based on response quality and personality modulation. Panels of size N (1 to 32) are formed by a coordinator that ranks agents per task by task fitness (expertise match, personality suitability, expertise level). For each panel, the authors compute intraclass correlation (ICC(2,k)) of session scores to assess reliability and count semantically unique issues after embedding-based deduplication (text-embedding-3-small, agglomerative clustering, cosine threshold 0.65). Human validation involves 50 raters rating a subset of sessions to compare agent-human vs. human-human score differences.

## Key Results
- Human validation: Agent-human score differences are statistically indistinguishable from human-human differences (p=0.38), supporting agent judges' trustworthiness.
- Scaling dissociation: Scoring reliability (ICC) scales logarithmically with panel size (e.g., ICC 0.29 at N=1 → 0.93 at N=32); unique issue discovery follows a sublinear power law (y=7.8·N^0.69, R²=0.999). Scores saturate roughly twice as fast as discoveries.
- Persona necessity: Structured Big Five conditioning is required for these scaling properties; simple prompts or no persona yield higher score variance and fewer insights per session.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Panel scoring reliability improves logarithmically because diverse agent probes cancel probe-specific noise while the shared quality signal accumulates.
**Mechanism:** Each agent evaluates from a distinct persona-driven perspective. Variance decomposition shows near-zero between-judge variance (0.3–1%) and high residual interaction (70–75%), indicating probe-specific noise that averages out. The between-task variance (24–29%) represents the common quality signal that strengthens with more judges.
**Core assumption:** Agent probes are independent enough for noise cancellation, and a common quality dimension exists that all agents respond to.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "The mechanism traces to ensemble diversity—Big Five personality conditioning makes agents probe different quality dimensions..."
- [section 4.3]: Variance decomposition table and interpretation: "each agent probes from a different angle; when averaged, probe-specific noise cancels while the shared quality signal accumulates."
- [corpus]: Weak support; "Evaluative Fingerprints" finds judges consistent with themselves but not each other, aligning with low between-judge variance but not directly addressing panel averaging.
**Break condition:** If agents share correlated biases (e.g., sycophancy from backbone model), noise cancellation fails, between-judge variance rises, and ICC improvement flattens.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Unique issue discovery follows a sublinear power law because the finding space has a power law distribution: critical issues (head) are discovered by small panels, while corner cases (tail) require larger panels.
**Mechanism:** Persona diversity causes each agent to traverse different interaction paths. Issues have a long-tail distribution of discoverability: common issues appear in many conversations (head), rare issues only in specific probing (tail). Adding judges yields progressively rarer issues, hence sublinear scaling.
**Core assumption:** Issues are not uniformly discoverable; there is a heavy-tailed distribution where many issues are very hard to encounter.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "We hypothesize this reflects a power law distribution of the finding space: critical issues are discovered first by small panels, while corner cases require progressively larger panels, analogous to species accumulation curves in ecology."
- [section 4.2]: Discovery fits y=7.8·N^0.69 (R²=0.999); exponent b<1 across all deduplication thresholds (θ=0.50–0.80).
- [corpus]: No direct support; corpus neighbors discuss coverage but not power law scaling.
**Break condition:** If issue discoverability is uniform or agents probe identically, discovery scales linearly (b=1) or saturates quickly.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The scaling properties require structured persona conditioning (Big Five), not simple prompting; without it, score variance increases and insights per session decrease.
**Mechanism:** Structured conditioning creates systematic diversity along orthogonal personality dimensions, leading to distinct evaluation angles. This controls between-judge variance (enabling logarithmic ICC) and maximizes breadth of issues discovered. Simple prompts (e.g., "act as an expert") fail to create such controlled diversity.
**Core assumption:** Big Five traits reliably produce distinct, consistent evaluation behaviors in LLMs.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "A controlled ablation confirms that structured persona conditioning, not simple prompting, is required to produce these scaling properties."
- [section 4.5]: Ablation: structured vs. simple score SD (0.087 vs. 0.160); insights/session (13.2 vs. 9.0); expertise effect moderated.
- [corpus]: Indirect support from "PersonaLLM" and "Generative agents" showing LLMs can embody personality traits, but no direct evidence for scaling properties.
**Break condition:** Replacing structured conditioning with unstructured prompts or random traits destroys the logarithmic ICC and sublinear discovery patterns.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Intraclass Correlation (ICC)**
- **Why needed here:** ICC measures scoring reliability across multiple judges; the paper uses ICC(2,k) to quantify how panel scores converge with size.
- **Quick check question:** If between-judge variance is very low and within-judge variance is high, would ICC be high or low? (Answer: low, because ICC requires consistency across judges.)

**Concept: Species Accumulation Curves**
- **Why needed here:** The paper analogizes issue discovery to species accumulation in ecology, where new species discoveries slow down as more samples are collected – explaining sublinear power law.
- **Quick check question:** If each new sample independently discovers new species with constant probability, what would the accumulation curve look like? (Answer: linear, not sublinear.)

**Concept: Ensemble Diversity**
- **Why needed here:** Ensemble learning theory explains how diverse members can cancel errors and improve aggregate performance; here it explains panel reliability despite individual noise.
- **Quick check question:** In an ensemble, if all models are identical, does diversity help? (Answer: No, diversity is necessary for error cancellation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Persona Engine -> Agent Judge (provides persona)
Agent Judge (conversationalist) <-> Target System (dialogue)
Agent Judge (evaluator) -> Diary (score/issues) -> Scoring Module & Discovery Module
Coordinator -> ranks Agent Judges -> selects panel (size N)
Scoring Module -> ICC, variance decomposition
Discovery Module -> unique issue count
Human Validation Module -> human ratings -> comparison

**Critical path:**
1. Define tasks and target models (two pairs).
2. Build persona pool (32 agents with Big Five, expertise, backgrounds).
3. For each panel size N, use coordinator to select top-N agents per task.
4. For each agent and task, run multi-turn session: agent converses with target model (temperature 0.7) until task completion or turn limit, generating diary per turn.
5. Aggregate panel scores → compute ICC and fit curve.
6. Extract issues from diaries, embed, cluster, count unique → fit power law.
7. Run variance decomposition (ANOVA) to confirm ensemble mechanism.
8. Conduct ablation (structured vs. simple vs. no persona) and human Turing test.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Panel size: Larger panels improve reliability (logarithmic) and discovery (sublinear) but with diminishing returns; cost scales linearly.
- Persona design: Structured Big Five yields controlled diversity; simple prompts are cheaper but lose scaling properties.
- Semantic deduplication threshold: Lower θ merges more paraphrases but risks over-merging distinct issues; higher θ preserves distinctness but overcounts same issue. The paper uses θ=0.65 with band θ=0.60–0.70.
- Task-aware selection: Ensures smaller panels are fit for task but may inflate ICC relative to random composition; generalizability may be lower.

**Failure signatures:**
- High between-judge variance (>5% of total) → poor persona conditioning or agents not following personas.
- ICC curve not logarithmic (e.g., linear) → agents too similar or too noisy; check if personality conditioning is applied.
- Discovery exponent b≥1 → no long-tail issue distribution; likely all agents probing same aspects (low diversity).
- Expertise effect absent or reversed (experts not scoring lower) → personas not affecting probing depth; may need to adjust expertise levels.
- Human validation fails (agent-human diff significantly > human-human) → agents not human-like; revisit persona design or interaction dynamics.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Human validation: Run Turing test with 50 humans on same tasks; confirm agent-human score differences within human-human range (p>0.05).
2. Ablation study: Compare structured persona agents against simple prompt agents (e.g., "act as an expert") and no-persona agents on score variance and insights per session; expect structured to have lower variance and higher insights.
3. Scaling verification: For panel sizes N=1,2,4,8,16,32, compute ICC and unique issues; fit logarithmic and power law models; expect R²>0.97 for ICC log-fit and b<1 for power law.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What shared biases from the backbone LLM affect agent judges and how do they impact reliability?
- Basis: The paper notes in Limitations that agent judges may inherit shared biases (e.g., sycophancy, positional bias) invisible in variance decomposition.
- Why unresolved: These biases could systematically skew scores and discoveries yet remain hidden because all agents share the same backbone.
- Needed evidence: Design benchmark tasks isolating specific biases, measure effects on ICC and discovery, compare to human baselines.

**Open Question 2:** Does the agent–human score equivalence hold when human ratings are balanced across tasks?
- Basis: The human study covers all 15 tasks but with unequal per-task sample sizes; balanced recruitment would strengthen task-level claims.
- Why unresolved: Unbalanced human data may mask task-specific disagreements between agents and humans.
- Needed evidence: Recruit an equal number of human raters per task (e.g., 10 each) and repeat the Turing-style pairwise difference analysis to verify stability.

**Open Question 3:** Is the distribution of issues discovered across sessions truly a power law?
- Basis: The authors hypothesize that the sublinear discovery scaling (y ∝ N^0.69) reflects a power law distribution of the finding space; they fit scaling but do not directly test the issue frequency distribution.
- Why unresolved: Observed scaling does not guarantee the underlying issue frequencies follow a power law; other heavy-tailed distributions could produce similar curves.
- Needed evidence: Compile all unique issues, count their occurrence frequency across sessions, and perform goodness-of-fit tests (e.g., Clauset et al.) comparing power law vs. alternatives; estimate tail exponent and locate the head/torso/tail breakpoint.

**Open Question 4:** How does random panel composition compare to task-adaptive selection for score reliability and discovery?
- Basis: The paper acknowledges that task-adaptive panel selection may inflate ICC relative to random composition.
- Why unresolved: The reported logarithmic ICC improvement and sublinear discovery scaling are based on task-fitness ranking; random assignment might yield different patterns, affecting practical guidance.
- Needed evidence: For each panel size, randomly sample agent subsets, compute ICC(2,k) and semantically deduplicated issue counts, and compare the scaling curves to those from adaptive panels. Also score variance under both schemes.

## Limitations
- Underspecified emotional state update rules and task-fitness ranking algorithm require assumptions for faithful replication.
- Shared backbone biases (e.g., sycophancy) may systematically affect evaluations but are undetectable in variance decomposition.
- Discovery scaling exponent may vary with embedding model and clustering parameters.

## Confidence
- Human validation result (p=0.38): High confidence.
- Scaling laws (logarithmic ICC, sublinear discovery): Medium confidence.
- Mechanistic explanations (ensemble diversity, power-law distribution): Low-Medium confidence.

## Next Checks
1. Replication of Ablation: Re-run the structured vs. simple persona ablation to confirm the necessity of Big Five conditioning for achieving the reported logarithmic ICC and sublinear discovery patterns.
2. Deduplication Robustness: Systematically vary the semantic clustering threshold (θ=0.60–0.70) and embedding model to verify that the power-law exponent b remains reliably sublinear (b<1).
3. Human Tail Validation: Conduct a targeted human evaluation on issues discovered only by the largest panels (N≥16) to confirm these "corner cases" are genuinely valid and meaningful, not artifacts.
