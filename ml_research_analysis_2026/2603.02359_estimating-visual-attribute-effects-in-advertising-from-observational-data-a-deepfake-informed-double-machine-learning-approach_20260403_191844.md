# Estimating Visual Attribute Effects in Advertising from Observational Data: A Deepfake-Informed Double Machine Learning Approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02359
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02359
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DICE-DML estimates that darker skin tone reduces Instagram engagement by 522 likes (p=0.062), a substantially less biased estimate than the -1,455 likes from standard DML.

## Executive Summary
Causal inference from observational image data faces the challenge of visual treatment leakage: standard vision encoders embed treatment attributes (e.g., skin tone) alongside confounding visual factors, invalidating Double Machine Learning (DML) and producing biased estimates. This paper introduces DICE-DML, a method that uses deepfake-generated image pairs to isolate treatment variation from confounders. By combining adversarial learning on paired difference vectors with orthogonal projection, DICE-DML produces representations free of treatment fingerprints while preserving outcome-predictive confounding. In simulations with known ground truth, DICE-DML reduces root mean squared error by 73–97% compared to standard DML. Applied to 232,089 Instagram influencer posts, DICE-DML achieves valid confounding control (positive outcome R² = 0.63) where standard DML fails (negative R²). The estimated causal effect of darker skin tone on likes is -522 (p=0.062), far smaller in magnitude than the biased standard DML estimate of -1,455.

## Method Summary
DICE-DML operates in two stages. First, a weak supervision signal is created by generating for each original image a deepfake counterpart with the treatment flipped (e.g., skin tone via LAB color space manipulation) while preserving non-treatment visual factors. Both images are processed by a pretrained ResNet-50 and a trainable MLP encoder. The core mechanisms are: (a) DICE-Diff adversarial learning—a discriminator tries to predict the treatment direction from the difference vector between the paired embeddings; the encoder is trained to fool it, removing treatment fingerprints; (b) orthogonal projection—for each pair, the treatment direction is estimated and subtracted from each embedding, guaranteeing geometric removal of any residual treatment component. The encoder is trained with a composite loss that also includes outcome prediction and contrastive terms to retain confounding information. Second, the cleaned embeddings are fed into a standard DML pipeline with 5-fold cross-fitting; nuisance models (outcome and propensity) are trained using Random Forests, and cross-fitted residuals yield an average treatment effect estimate.

## Key Results
- Simulated data: DICE-DML reduces RMSE by 73–97% versus standard DML, with a 97.5% improvement at the null effect.
- Real-world application: DICE-DML achieves valid confounding control (outcome R² = 0.63) where standard DML fails (negative R²).
- Estimated causal effect of darker skin tone on Instagram likes: -522 (p = 0.062), about one-third the magnitude of the biased standard DML estimate (-1,455).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Deepfake pairs isolate treatment**  
For each image, a deepfake with flipped treatment (e.g., skin tone) is generated while preserving other visual factors. The difference between embeddings of the pair cancels confounders and primarily reflects treatment-related features ("fingerprints"). This provides a directional signal for removal. Assumes deepfake manipulation alters only the target attribute. Evidence: high difference norm in standard ResNet features (~3.6) confirms strong treatment signal. Break condition: artifacts introduced by deepfakes that correlate with outcome would contaminate supervision.

**Mechanism 2: Adversarial removal of fingerprints**  
A discriminator (MLP and linear) predicts treatment direction from the pair difference Δ; the encoder is trained to fool it via gradient reversal, pushing treatment information out of the representation. An outcome predictor on averaged embeddings preserves confounding. Ablation shows adversarial-only reduces but does not eliminate fingerprint norm; both mechanisms are necessary. Break condition: if the discriminator exploits irrelevant Δ variations, the encoder may over-suppress variance.

**Mechanism 3: Orthogonal projection**  
Residual treatment components along the estimated treatment axis \(\hat{\delta} = \Delta/\|\Delta\|\) are removed by projection: \(Z_{\text{clean}} = Z - \alpha (Z \cdot \hat{\delta}) \hat{\delta}\) (α=0.95). This guarantees zero predictability along that axis. Evidence: projection alone eliminates treatment but also outcome signal; combined with adversarial training it yields low fingerprint norm and high outcome predictability. Break condition: if treatment and confounding are not orthogonal in the learned space, projection discards confounding, biasing the estimate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Double Machine Learning (Neyman Orthogonality)**: Why needed: DML requires control variables that capture confounding without embedding treatment; standard vision encoders fail because they encode treatment as well, leading to biased estimates. Quick check: If your control representation has high treatment R² (>0.2) but negative or very low outcome R² (<0.1), what does that suggest?
- **Adversarial Domain Invariance**: Why needed: To achieve invariance to the treatment, an adversarial game is used where a discriminator tries to predict treatment from the representation difference; this forces the encoder to remove treatment fingerprints. Quick check: Why operate the discriminator on difference vectors Δ rather than on individual embeddings Z?
- **Orthogonal Projection**: Why needed: Even after adversarial training, residual treatment components may remain; orthogonal projection provides a geometric way to exactly remove any component along the estimated treatment axis. Quick check: Why does projection alone fail (as shown in ablation) and require prior adversarial disentanglement?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Original image & deepfake → ResNet-50 backbone → DICE Encoder (MLP) → Difference Module (computes Δ and δ) → Projection Module → Clean embeddings (Z_clean). Side connections: Δ → Adversarial Heads; (Z_clean, Z'_clean) → Auxiliary Heads (outcome predictor, contrastive loss). Clean embeddings → DML stage.

**Critical path**  
1. Generate deepfake pairs for all images (offline).  
2. Extract ResNet-50 features for original and deepfake images.  
3. Train the DICE encoder with adversarial, projection, and auxiliary losses using paired batches.  
4. After training, apply projection to all original images to obtain final clean embeddings.  
5. Run DML estimation on clean embeddings with 5-fold cross-fitting using Random Forest nuisance models.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Projection strength α: higher α removes more treatment but risks discarding confounding if treatment/confounding not fully disentangled; tuned via validation Y_R² and ‖Δ‖.  
- Loss weights (λ_adv, λ_Y, etc.): balance treatment removal vs. preserving confounding; ablation shows both are necessary—over-weighting λ_Y leaves residual leakage, over-weighting λ_adv collapses outcome variance.  
- Deepfake fidelity: Simple LAB manipulation is efficient but may not preserve all non-treatment factors for complex attributes; more advanced generative models could improve supervision but introduce new biases and computation.

**Failure signatures**  
- Negative outcome R² after DML: indicates remaining treatment leakage in Z_clean; check mean ‖Δ‖ > 1.0.  
- Very low outcome R² (<0.3) with low ‖Δ‖: suggests over-projection destroyed confounding; reduce α or increase λ_Y.  
- High treatment R² (>0.2) on Z_clean: projection ineffective; verify δ direction stability.  
- Unstable DML estimates across nuisance specifications: may indicate residual confounding or insufficient encoder capacity.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Baseline difference norm**: Compute ‖Δ‖ for original ResNet features; expect ~3.6. After DICE training, target <1.0.  
2. **Projection-only sanity check**: Apply only orthogonal projection to ResNet features; ‖Δ‖ should drop dramatically but outcome R² in DML should become negative, confirming projection needs disentanglement.  
3. **DML diagnostic**: Before final effect estimation, train DML on Z_clean; expect positive outcome R² (0.5–0.7) and treatment R² <0.2. If not, revisit encoder training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can alternative weak supervision strategies be developed for visual treatments that cannot be convincingly manipulated using deepfake techniques?
2. How can sensitivity analysis methods be adapted to the DICE-DML framework to address concerns about unobserved confounders?
3. To what extent do the findings from DICE-DML generalise beyond Instagram, skin tone, and likes?
4. How can DICE-DML be extended to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects across subgroups?

## Limitations
- Deepfake manipulation may introduce artifacts correlated with outcome, contaminating the weak supervision.
- Orthogonal projection assumes treatment and confounding are fully disentangled and orthogonal; if not, it discards useful confounding information.
- Findings are limited to a single platform (Instagram), treatment (skin tone), and outcome (likes); generalisability is unknown.

## Confidence
- Overall effectiveness: Medium (simulations strong, real-world only marginal significance)
- Deepfake pair weak supervision: Medium (novel but vulnerable to artifacts)
- Adversarial disentanglement: Medium-High (ablation supports contribution)
- Orthogonal projection: Medium (geometrically sound but relies on orthogonality)

## Next Checks
1. Leakage quantification: Compute treatment R² on Z_clean (target <0.1) and mean ‖Δ‖ (target <1.0) on held-out data.
2. Orthogonality stress test: Correlate the projected-out component α(Z·δ)δ with known confounders (e.g., image sharpness, background complexity); significant correlations indicate confounding removal.
3. Deepfake fidelity audit: Conduct a blind human study comparing original and deepfake pairs on non-treatment attributes; expect >95% agreement that only skin tone changed.
