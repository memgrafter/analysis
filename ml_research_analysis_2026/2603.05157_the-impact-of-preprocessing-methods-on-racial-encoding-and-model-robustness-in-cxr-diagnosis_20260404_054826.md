# The Impact of Preprocessing Methods on Racial Encoding and Model Robustness in CXR Diagnosis
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.05157
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05157
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: Simple bounding box lung cropping reduces racial encoding in external validation (race AUROC 0.593 vs 0.623) while maintaining diagnostic performance (diagnostic AUROC 0.738 vs 0.742).

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates racial shortcut learning in chest X-ray (CXR) diagnosis models, where AI can infer patient race from images, risking biased diagnoses. It evaluates whether common preprocessing techniques—lung masking, lung cropping, and CLAHE—can reduce racial encoding without sacrificing diagnostic accuracy. Using DenseNet-121 on MIMIC-CXR (internal) and CheXpert (external) datasets, the authors find that simple bounding box-based lung cropping significantly lowers racial encoding externally (race AUROC 0.593 vs baseline 0.623) while preserving disease prediction performance (diagnostic AUROC 0.738 vs baseline 0.742). Lung masking also reduces racial encoding but harms external diagnostic accuracy, while CLAHE shows no notable effect. The study demonstrates a viable method to mitigate bias without a fairness-accuracy trade-off.

## Method Summary
The study trained DenseNet-121 models on the MIMIC-CXR dataset for multi-label disease classification, with the internal dataset used for training and the separate CheXpert dataset for external validation. Preprocessing methods included: (1) lung masking (replacing lung regions with a constant value), (2) lung cropping (extracting bounding boxes around segmented lungs), and (3) CLAHE (contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization). Baseline used no preprocessing. To quantify racial encoding, a linear race classifier was trained on frozen features from the disease model, and its AUROC was measured (lower values indicate less race predictability). Diagnostic performance was assessed via AUROC for disease detection. All models were trained with standard augmentations and view selection, and evaluated on both internal and external sets to compare robustness. The race classifier used binary labels (White vs. non-White) derived from metadata, and the disease classifier targeted 14 common conditions.

## Key Results
- Lung cropping reduced external racial encoding (race AUROC 0.593) compared to baseline (0.623) while maintaining diagnostic AUROC (0.738 vs 0.742).
- Lung masking lowered racial encoding but degraded external diagnostic performance (diagnostic AUROC 0.696 vs 0.742).
- CLAHE had no significant effect on either racial encoding or diagnostic accuracy.
- Internal validation showed minimal changes across preprocessing methods, highlighting the importance of external testing for robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper posits that racial cues in CXRs are often concentrated in peripheral areas outside the lung fields, such as breast tissue, skin folds, and positioning devices. Lung cropping directly removes these peripheral regions, eliminating many race-associated features while preserving the lung parenchyma where most pathology manifests. Lung masking, although also removing peripheral areas, replaces lung interiors with uniform values and introduces artificial boundaries, leading to loss of diagnostic texture and artifacts that hurt generalization to external datasets. CLAHE enhances local contrast but does not remove extraneous cues, explaining its negligible impact. The success of cropping suggests that spatial cutoff of non-lung regions is a simple but effective bias intervention.

## Foundational Learning
- **Racial Shortcut Learning**: Models exploit spurious correlations with race present in training data, leading to biased predictions.  
  *Why needed*: To define the bias problem.  
  *Quick check*: Train a linear probe on frozen features to predict race; AUROC > 0.5 confirms encoding.
- **Preprocessing for Bias Mitigation**: Techniques like cropping, masking, and CLAHE alter input images to test removal of biased cues.  
  *Why needed*: To assess whether input-level interventions can reduce bias without harming accuracy.  
  *Quick check*: Apply each method and compare race AUROC and diagnostic AUROC.
- **Evaluation Metrics (AUROC)**: Area Under the ROC Curve; lower values indicate less racial encoding, higher values indicate better diagnostic performance.  
  *Why needed*: Provides a quantitative measure for both bias and accuracy.  
  *Quick check*: Compute AUROC for race classifier (bias) and disease classifier (accuracy) on held-out data.
- **External Validation**: Testing on a dataset (CheXpert) different from the training set (MIMIC-CXR).  
  *Why needed*: Ensures findings are not overfitted to a single distribution and reflects real-world generalization.  
  *Quick check*: Compare internal (MIMIC-CXR) and external (CheXpert) metrics; large drops signal domain shift issues.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
CXR Image → Preprocessing (None / Cropping / Masking / CLAHE) → DenseNet-121 backbone → (a) Disease classification head (multi-label sigmoid) (b) Feature extractor for race probe.

**Critical Path**:  
Preprocessing choice influences the features learned by DenseNet-121 during disease classification training. These features then determine (i) diagnostic AUROC and (ii) the performance of a separately trained linear race classifier (race AUROC). Thus, the pipeline is: Preprocessing → Model weights → (Disease AUROC, Race AUROC).

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Cropping is simple but may discard peripheral disease signs (e.g., pleural effusion).  
- Masking removes more information, risking accuracy loss due to boundary artifacts.  
- CLAHE preserves all pixels but may amplify noise; minimal effect observed.  
- All methods depend on segmentation quality if applied using masks.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Sudden drop in external diagnostic AUROC compared to baseline indicates over-aggressive preprocessing.  
- High race AUROC after preprocessing suggests biased cues remain.  
- Inconsistent segmentation errors across racial groups could introduce new biases.

**First Experiments**:  
1. **Baseline replication**: Train DenseNet-121 on MIMIC-CXR without preprocessing; evaluate race and diagnostic AUROCs on internal and external sets.
2. **Preprocessing comparison**: Apply each method (cropping, masking, CLAHE) and measure changes in both metrics on CheXpert.
3. **Segmentation sanity check**: Verify lung segmentation accuracy across demographic subgroups to ensure cropping does not introduce systematic errors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does lung cropping equally reduce racial encoding across all disease subsets, particularly for conditions with peripheral manifestations (e.g., pneumothorax, pleural effusion)?
- How stable are the results to variations in segmentation algorithm quality, and could segmentation biases themselves create new fairness issues?
- Would the observed trade-offs (or lack thereof) generalize to other model architectures (e.g., Vision Transformers) or larger pretraining regimes?
- Can the reduction in racial encoding be precisely localized to specific image regions, and are there residual biases within the cropped lung fields?
- Does cropping affect model robustness to other distribution shifts (e.g., different scanners, patient positions) beyond racial encoding?

## Limitations
- The study relies on lung segmentation to define cropping and masking; segmentation errors could disproportionately affect certain demographic groups and undermine the bias reduction.
- Experiments are limited to a single model architecture (DenseNet-121) and two datasets (MIMIC-CXR for training, CheXpert for external test); findings may not generalize to other contexts.
- Race labels are derived from structured metadata, which may contain inaccuracies or reflect coarse categorizations that do not capture genetic or social heterogeneity.
- The analysis uses a binary race classifier (White vs. non-White), which may obscure subgroup-specific biases and intersectional effects with other factors like age or sex.
- The study does not evaluate clinical decision-making impact (e.g., treatment recommendations), focusing solely on prediction metrics.
- Internal validation showed small differences, but the external test was limited to CheXpert; other external datasets could yield different robustness patterns.

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the core finding that simple lung cropping reduces external racial encoding without harming diagnostic accuracy: the experiment is well-controlled, uses standard datasets, and the effect size is meaningful (race AUROC drop from 0.623 to 0.593).
- **Medium confidence** in generalizability: while the method is straightforward, the study’s scope is narrow (one architecture, two datasets). The lack of internal validation changes suggests the effect is primarily robustness-related, but long-term clinical utility remains unknown.
- **Uncertainty** about whether cropping fully eliminates biased shortcuts or merely shifts them; the linear probe may not capture all forms of encoding, and residual cues could persist in lung texture or artifacts.

## Next Checks
- **Replicate with diverse architectures**: Test cropping on ResNet, EfficientNet, or vision transformers to confirm architecture-independence.
- **Multi-dataset external validation**: Evaluate on additional datasets like NIH ChestX-ray14 or a geographically distinct hospital cohort.
- **Per-disease analysis**: Disaggregate diagnostic AUROC by condition to see which diseases benefit or suffer from cropping.
- **Segmentation error audit**: Quantify segmentation accuracy across racial groups and simulate error impacts on cropping efficacy.
- **Intersectional fairness probes**: Extend race classification to multi-class or intersectional labels (e.g., race × sex) to uncover residual biases.
- **Combine with other debiasing methods**: Layer cropping with adversarial training or label smoothing to see if further bias reduction is possible.
- **Visual attribution**: Use saliency maps or feature ablation to pinpoint exactly which image regions contribute to racial encoding pre- and post-cropping.
