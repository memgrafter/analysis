# EcoFair-CH-MARL: Scalable Constrained Hierarchical Multi-Agent RL with Real-Time Emission Budgets and Fairness Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.14625  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14625  
- **Reference count**: 39  
- **Primary result**: Achieves up to 15% lower emissions, 12% higher throughput, and 45% fair‑cost improvement over MARL baselines, with stronger equity (lower Gini, higher min‑max) than SOTO and FEN.

## Executive Summary
EcoFair-CH-MARL tackles constrained hierarchical multi‑agent reinforcement learning for maritime logistics, where a fleet of vessels must operate under a cumulative emission budget while ensuring fairness in cost distribution and maximising throughput. The framework introduces a high‑level policy that sets routes, arrival windows, and per‑vessel budgets every τ_H steps, and a low‑level policy that executes vessel control actions each environment step. A primal‑dual layer injects Lagrange multipliers (λ for emissions, µ/ν for port capacities) into the reward to enforce stochastic constraints in expectation, guaranteeing O(√T) regret. Fairness is shaped via a reward transformer that adds a dynamic penalty β(t)·φ(c), where φ measures Gini or min‑max equity. Centralised training with decentralised execution (CTDE) allows the critic to use global state while actors rely on local observations. Evaluated on a digital twin of 16 ports and 50 vessels with stochastic weather, demand, and congestion, the method reduces emissions by up to 15%, increases throughput by up to 12%, and improves fair‑cost metrics by up to 45% compared to hierarchical and constrained MARL baselines, outperforming fairness‑specific methods like SOTO and FEN.

## Method Summary
EcoFair-CH-MARL combines hierarchical reinforcement learning with primal‑dual constrained optimisation and fairness shaping. The high‑level macro‑actor selects strategic decisions (route, arrival window, per‑vessel emission budget) at intervals τ_H. The low‑level micro‑actor chooses per‑step vessel control actions conditioned on local observations and the current macro‑action. A centralised critic accesses the full global state during training. Constraint satisfaction is achieved by a primal‑dual layer that augments the reward with Lagrange multipliers: λ for cumulative emissions (versus budget B), µ and ν for berth and crane capacity constraints. These multipliers are updated with a diminishing stepsize η_t = 0.1/√(t+1) to achieve sub‑linear O(√T) regret. Fairness is enforced by a transformer that adds β(t)·φ(c) to the reward, where φ(c) is either the Gini coefficient or 1 minus the min‑max ratio of cumulative vessel costs. The scalar β increases according to a schedule (β_max, η_β) to gradually emphasise equity. The entire system is trained with standard policy gradient algorithms (PPO/MAPPO/QMIX) unchanged, leveraging CTDE.

## Key Results
- Up to **15% reduction in emissions** compared to hierarchical and constrained MARL baselines.  
- Up to **12% higher throughput** (port operations processed).  
- Up to **45% fair‑cost improvement** (lower Gini, higher min‑max) over fairness‑specific methods like SOTO and FEN.  
- Provides **theoretical O(√T) regret bounds** for cumulative constraint and fairness violations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical decoupling separates long‑term strategic planning from low‑level control, mitigating the curse of dimensionality and enabling coherent coordination over extended horizons. The primal‑dual layer dynamically reweights rewards to steer the policy toward constraint satisfaction in expectation, while the diminishing stepsize ensures stable convergence with sub‑linear violation. The fairness transformer gradually incorporates equity considerations, preventing abrupt policy disruptions and allowing the system to first optimise efficiency before enforcing fairness. CTDE allows the critic to exploit global information for credit assignment without requiring central execution at test time. Together, these elements yield strong empirical performance and theoretical guarantees.

## Foundational Learning
- **Constrained RL**: needed to respect emission caps during learning; quick check: verify λ > 0 after training and that constraint violations are bounded.  
- **Hierarchical RL**: needed for temporal abstraction and decision decoupling; quick check: identify macro‑action interval τ_H and confirm high‑level decisions are executed at that frequency.  
- **Primal‑Dual Optimisation**: needed to enforce constraints in expectation with O(√T) regret; quick check: ensure stepsize η_t = 0.1/√(t+1) (or similar diminishing schedule).  
- **CTDE**: needed for coordination in MARL while keeping execution decentralised; quick check: critic input contains global state while actor inputs are local.  
- **Fairness Metrics (Gini, Min‑Max)**: needed to quantify equity of cost distribution; quick check: compute Gini coefficient and min‑max ratio from per‑vessel cost returns.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Global State → Centralised Critic → Primal‑Dual updates (λ, µ, ν) → Reward Shaping (including β(t)·φ(c)) → High-level Macro‑Actor → Macro‑action (route, window, budget) → Low‑level Micro‑Actor → Vessel actions → Environment. Observations per vessel flow to Low‑level Micro‑Actor; emission e_t and port congestion feed back to primal‑dual layer.

**Critical path**: At each macro step (every τ_H), the high‑level actor selects macro‑actions for all vessels. Between macro steps, the low‑level actors act per environment step using current macro‑action and local observations. The environment returns new observations, emission e_t, port queue lengths, and per‑step rewards. The reward is augmented by primal‑dual terms (λ·e_t, etc.) and fairness penalty (β·φ). Dual variables are updated instantly using the newest constraint data. β is updated per episode based on the episode’s cost distribution. Transitions are stored and used for PPO updates with a centralised value function that conditions on global state.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Macro‑period τ_H*: smaller τ_H offers more frequent strategic reassessment but increases high‑level decision frequency; larger τ_H reduces computational load but may lead to sub‑optimal routing if conditions change rapidly.  
- *Fairness schedule*: β_max and η_β control the equity‑throughput trade‑off; overly aggressive β can over‑equalise vessel costs and degrade overall efficiency.  
- *Primal‑dual stepsize*: too large risks oscillation; too small slows constraint satisfaction. The diminishing η_t balances this but requires careful tuning of initial scale.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Dual variables (λ, µ, ν) collapse to zero → constraints ignored, emission budget violated.  
- β increases but Gini/min‑max do not improve → fairness penalty ineffective, possibly due to insufficient β_max or problematic φ computation (e.g., division by zero).  
- High variance in returns or PPO clipping excess → learning instability; may need entropy regularisation or learning rate adjustment.  
- Training time grows super‑linearly with fleet size → scalability bottleneck; consider parameter sharing or factorised critics.

**First experiments** (exactly 3):
1. **Baseline reproduction**: train with default τ_H=10, β_max=1.0, η_β=0.01; verify emissions ≤ B, Gini ≤ 0.2, throughput improvement ≥10% compared to baselines.  
2. **Macro‑period sensitivity**: run with τ_H ∈ {5, 10, 20} keeping other hyper‑parameters fixed; compare total return, emission compliance, and wall‑clock time.  
3. **Fairness ablation**: set β=0 (no fairness penalty); confirm Gini rises and min‑max ratio drops significantly relative to default, demonstrating the transformer’s impact.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified hyperparameters**: macro‑action interval τ_H and fairness schedule (β_max, η_β, target ζ) are not concretely given; they affect reproducibility and the efficiency‑equity trade‑off.  
- **Theoretical assumptions**: Regret analysis assumes bounded losses, convexity, and i.i.d. data; real maritime dynamics may violate these, potentially loosening the O(√T) bounds.  
- **Evaluation scope**: Experiments limited to a 16‑port, 50‑vessel digital twin; scalability to larger fleets and real‑world uncertainties is unproven.  
- **Fairness definition**: Cost‑based Gini/min‑max may not align with stakeholder equity concerns; guarantees are probabilistic and depend on appropriate β scheduling.  
- **Centralised training reliance**: Requires accurate global state during training; partial observability at deployment could degrade performance.
