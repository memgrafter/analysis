# Selecting Hyperparameters for Tree-Boosting

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.05786
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05786
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SMAC achieves the best hyperparameter optimization performance for LightGBM/GPBoost across 59 regression and classification datasets with a fixed budget of 135 trials, significantly outperforming TPE, GP-BO, Hyperband, random/full grid search, and default settings.

## Executive Summary
Tree-boosting models like LightGBM are highly sensitive to hyperparameter choices. This paper presents a large-scale empirical comparison of seven hyperparameter optimization (HPO) methods—SMAC, TPE, GP-BO, Hyperband, random grid, full grid, and default parameters—on 59 regression and classification datasets. Each method is allocated a fixed budget of 135 trials (with Hyperband receiving a comparable total training budget). The evaluation uses ADTM-normalized scores, ranks, and relative differences derived from repeated 5-fold cross-validation. Results show that SMAC clearly outperforms all other methods, with TPE typically second. Using default hyperparameters leads to very inaccurate models, and achieving accurate tuning generally requires more than 100 trials. For regression tasks, selecting the number of boosting iterations via early stopping is superior to including it in the search space, while for classification including iterations can be competitive. All tested hyperparameters have a material effect on accuracy. The authors suggest extending comparisons to other boosting libraries and exploring hybrid HPO methods.

## Method Summary
The study uses 59 datasets from Grinsztajn et al. (2022) after standardized preprocessing: removal of high-cardinality categoricals (>20 levels), one-hot encoding, subsampling to at most 100,000 instances, and binarization of multi-class responses. Each dataset undergoes 5-fold outer cross-validation; within each outer fold, an inner 80/20 split creates training and validation sets. Tree-boosting models are trained using GPBoost v1.4.0 (LightGBM-compatible). Seven HPO methods are evaluated:
- SMAC (v2.3.1)
- TPE via Optuna (v3.5.0)
- GP-BO via scikit-optimize (v0.9.0)
- Hyperband (Optuna, $R=2150$, $\eta=2.8$)
- Random grid search
- Deterministic full grid search
- Default parameters (no optimization)
All methods except Hyperband run for exactly 135 trials; Hyperband’s budget is adjusted so the total number of boosting iterations is comparable (~128,816). Search spaces are defined in Tables 1–4. For each trial, hyperparameters are sampled, the model is trained on the inner training set, and validation performance (RMSE or accuracy) is recorded. After HPO, the best configuration found is retrained on the combined inner training and validation set and evaluated on the outer hold-out test fold. This process is repeated 20 times with different random seeds. Final results are aggregated across seeds and folds, then normalized per-dataset using the ADTM method (10%/90% quantile scaling), and averaged separately for regression and classification tasks.

## Key Results
- SMAC achieves the highest ADTM-normalized scores, significantly outperforming TPE (usually second), GP-BO, Hyperband, random/full grid search, and default parameters.
- Default hyperparameters yield very inaccurate models; accurate tuning typically requires more than 100 trials.
- For regression, early stopping for boosting iterations is superior to including iterations in the search space; for classification, including iterations can be competitive.
- All tested hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, max depth) have a material effect on model accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper is primarily an empirical study and does not delve into the underlying mechanisms that make SMAC more effective than other HPO methods. It reports the performance differences without providing a theoretical or diagnostic analysis of why SMAC’s random forest surrogate or its handling of conditional hyperparameters lead to better outcomes. Similarly, the advantage of early stopping for regression iterations is observed but not explained. These mechanistic questions are left for future research.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO)**: Automated search over a configuration space to find hyperparameters that maximize model performance. *Why needed*: The paper’s core objective is to compare different HPO algorithms. *Quick check*: Ensure each HPO method is implemented according to its standard algorithm and respects the defined search spaces.
- **Tree Boosting (LightGBM/GPBoost)**: Gradient boosting with decision trees, optimized for speed and memory efficiency; GPBoost v1.4.0 is used. *Why needed*: The models being tuned. *Quick check*: Confirm GPBoost version 1.4.0 is used and that objectives (regression/binary classification) match the dataset tasks.
- **SMAC**: A sequential model-based Bayesian optimization method using random forests to model the objective function. *Why needed*: The top-performing HPO method in the study. *Quick check*: Verify SMAC v2.3.1 is called with the correct parameter configuration space (Table 1).
- **Early Stopping**: Monitoring validation loss during training and halting when performance degrades. *Why needed*: The paper compares using early stopping to automatically determine boosting iterations versus treating the number of iterations as a tunable hyperparameter. *Quick check*: Check that for the early stopping condition, `n_estimators` is set high and `early_stopping_rounds` is enabled; for the search-space condition, `n_estimators` is given a range and early stopping is disabled.
- **ADTM Normalization**: A per-dataset scaling technique that maps raw scores to a normalized range based on the 10% and 90% quantiles. *Why needed*: To aggregate results across datasets with different performance scales. *Quick check*: For each dataset, collect all method-seed-score values, compute quantiles, and apply the scaling formula exactly as described.
- **Hyperband**: A multi-fidelity HPO algorithm that allocates budgets via successive halving. *Why needed*: One of the compared methods; its budget is defined in terms of maximum resource (boosting iterations) and reduction factor. *Quick check*: Ensure Hyperband’s $R=2150$ and $\eta=2.8$ yield 9 rungs and a total of ~128,816 boosting iterations; also verify scores are recorded after each rung, not only the final configuration.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data Loader -> Preprocessor -> Outer Splitter -> Inner Splitter -> HPO Engine -> (Sampler -> Trainer -> Validator) -> Best Config Selector -> Retrainer -> Tester -> Score Recorder -> Normalizer -> Aggregator
- **Critical path**: The longest execution chain involves loading a dataset, preprocessing, splitting, and then for each outer fold, inner split, and HPO seed: running HPO (135 trials or Hyperband brackets), retraining the best model, evaluating on test, and storing the score. The HPO loop dominates runtime due to many sequential model fits.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Fixed trial budget (135) ensures fair comparison across most methods but may favor methods that use trials quickly; Hyperband uses a different resource allocation to match total iterations.
  - Early stopping vs. including `n_estimators` trades adaptive stopping for a discrete search dimension; early stopping may overfit the validation set if not careful.
  - Use of GPBoost (a LightGBM fork) guarantees compatibility with LightGBM v2 but introduces reproducibility risks due to discontinuation; a future switch to LightGBM may alter results.
  - 5-fold outer CV provides robust estimates but multiplies computation by 5 and interacts with HPO seeds.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Version mismatch: Using LightGBM instead of GPBoost, or newer/older library versions, changes defaults and potentially algorithm behavior.
  - Hyperband misconfiguration: Incorrect $R$ or $\eta$ leads to wrong number of rungs or total iterations; recording only the final incumbent instead of all rung scores underestimates Hyperband’s curve.
  - Dataset/preprocessing deviation: Not using the exact 59 OpenML IDs or applying different preprocessing (e.g., different cardinality threshold, no binarization) invalidates the benchmark.
  - ADTM applied globally instead of per-dataset: This would mix heterogeneous performance scales and distort normalized scores.
  - Seeds not replicated: Without the original 20 seeds, random variation may differ from reported confidence intervals.
- **First experiments**:
  1. **Single-dataset SMAC sanity check**: Run SMAC on one small dataset (e.g., `california_housing`) with 1 seed, 135 trials. Verify that validation scores improve over trials and that the best hyperparameters lie within the prescribed ranges.
  2. **ADTM normalization validation**: Collect dummy scores from two HPO methods on 5 synthetic datasets; manually compute ADTM normalization and confirm that normalized scores are within [0,1] and reflect relative performance.
  3. **Hyperband resource audit**: Run Hyperband on a trivial dataset with $R=2150$, $\eta=2.8$. Compute the total number of boosting iterations across all brackets (should be close to 128,816) and ensure that 9 intermediate scores are saved (one per rung).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Do HPO method rankings generalize to other boosting libraries like XGBoost?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Authors suggest extending comparisons to additional boosting libraries.
- **Why unresolved:** Paper used only LightGBM/GPBoost; other libraries may behave differently.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run the same experiment with XGBoost and compare rankings.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How should computational budget be allocated across folds/seed repetitions for reliable HPO method comparison?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Authors propose investigating "principled ways to allocate budgets across folds and seeds."
- **Why unresolved:** The study used 5-fold CV with 20 seeds; optimal allocation is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary numbers of folds, seeds, inner splits while keeping total budget constant, and evaluate ranking stability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do hybrid HPO methods combining multi-fidelity and model-based search outperform SMAC for tree-boosting?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Authors suggest exploring "hybrid approaches that combine multi-fidelity resource allocation with strong model-based search and meta-learning/warm-starting."
- **Why unresolved:** Hyperband performed poorly alone; hybrid methods may leverage benefits of both.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and test hybrids (e.g., BOHB) in the same benchmark, compare performance to SMAC.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why is early stopping superior for number of iterations in regression, but including iterations competitive for classification?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Paper reports that early stopping beats including iterations for regression, while including iterations can be competitive for classification.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper provides no explanation for this difference; understanding could improve tuning practices.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze validation loss curves, study hyperparameter interactions involving iterations, across multiple datasets.

## Limitations
- The exact list of 59 OpenML dataset IDs and the 20 random seeds are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- The use of a discontinued GPBoost fork and potential subtle preprocessing differences introduce reproducibility risks.
- Findings are limited to LightGBM/GPBoost and may not generalize to other tree-boosting libraries (e.g., XGBoost).
