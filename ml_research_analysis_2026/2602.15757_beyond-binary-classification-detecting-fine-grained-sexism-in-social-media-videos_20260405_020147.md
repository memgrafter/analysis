# Beyond Binary Classification: Detecting Fine-Grained Sexism in Social Media Videos

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15757
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15757
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Multimodal LLMs achieve binary accuracy up to 85.8% and fine-grained Macro F1 up to 72.9, approaching human performance but failing on visually-conveyed objectification.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the limitation of binary sexism detection by introducing FineMuSe, a Spanish multimodal dataset of 828 short videos annotated with a hierarchical taxonomy covering multiple overlapping sexist types and rhetorical devices. The study evaluates a range of LLMs and multimodal models, finding that vision-language models (e.g., Claude-3.7-Sonnet V+L) perform best on both binary and fine-grained tasks, yet exhibit high failure rates in fine-grained detection and struggle with objectification conveyed through visual cues. Model-generated explanations are judged comparable to human-written ones in relevance and groundedness.

## Method Summary
FineMuSe comprises videos from TikTok, BitChute, and YouTube Shorts, each processed to extract frames (2–10 per video, filtered to exclude black frames) and audio transcripts using Whisper. Expert annotators apply a hierarchical, multi-label taxonomy in three passes, marking sexist content across four categories (stereotypes, inequality, discrimination, objectification) and two rhetorical devices (irony, humor), with span-level timestamps. Final labels are derived by majority vote. Models are evaluated in a zero-shot setting with Spanish prompts instructing JSON-formatted outputs for binary and fine-grained predictions. Metrics include Accuracy, Macro F1, Failure Rate, and Format Error Rate; a subset of outputs is human-evaluated for explanation quality.

## Key Results
- Best multimodal model (Claude-3.7-Sonnet V+L) achieves 85.8% binary accuracy on Part 1 and 83.6% on Part 2, with fine-grained Macro F1 of 67.6 and 72.9 respectively.
- Objectification remains a severe challenge: Macro F1 for objectification falls below 30 on Part 1 even for top models.
- Models show high failure rates (up to 34%) in predicting fine-grained labels on sexist instances, and exhibit low correlation with human labels for objectification (r=0.15). Model explanations rated on par with human ones for relevance and groundedness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A hierarchical, multi-label taxonomy allows modeling overlapping and nuanced sexist expressions that binary schemes miss.
- **Mechanism:** By decomposing sexism into non-exclusive sub-types (stereotypes, inequality, discrimination, objectification) and adding rhetorical device labels (irony, humor), the annotation framework captures co-occurring manifestations. This structure forces models to consider multiple, simultaneous facets rather than collapsing distinct forms.
- **Core assumption:** Sexist content is inherently multi-faceted; representing this complexity in labels improves model learning and diagnostic capability.
- **Evidence anchors:** Dataset annotation scheme; multi-label approach; extension of MuSeD to fine-grained multimodal labels.
- **Break condition:** If co-occurring labels are rare in practice, added complexity may not yield performance gains and could increase annotator burden without return.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multimodal processing (text + audio + video) provides complementary cues for sexism detection, but visual information is critical for identifying certain types (like objectification) and is under-utilized by models.
- **Mechanism:** Video frames contain visual cues (gestures, setting, body portrayal) that text/audio transcripts miss. Models that process images (V+L) can integrate these, but results show they still struggle with visually conveyed objectification, suggesting a gap in multimodal fusion or visual reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** Some sexist signals are primarily visual; text-only models are fundamentally limited for these cases.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement on visual struggles; Section 6.3 shows multimodal consistently outperforms text-only; correlation analysis reveals low model-human agreement for objectification (r=0.15) with failures on visual-only instances.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Expert annotators produce higher-quality, more consistent fine-grained labels than crowd-sourcing, but annotation remains subjective for nuanced rhetorical devices (irony/humor), limiting the gold standard's reliability.
- **Mechanism:** Using annotators with academic backgrounds and specific training reduces noise and aligns interpretations with theoretical frameworks. However, irony/humor perception is inherently subjective, leading to low Fleiss' Kappa for these categories, which creates noisy training/evaluation targets.
- **Core assumption:** Expert judgment improves annotation quality for complex social constructs, but subjective categories will always show lower agreement.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.4 describes expert annotators; Section 5.2 reports Fleiss' Kappa: high for binary/main types (up to 0.73), poor to moderate for Irony/Humor; Section 5.1 shows scarcity and inconsistency of these labels.

## Foundational Learning
### Hierarchical Multi-Label Classification
- **Why needed:** The task requires predicting a set of non-exclusive labels (sexist types) conditional on a binary root. Engineers must design output heads or loss functions that handle label co-occurrence (e.g., binary cross-entropy per label) rather than mutually exclusive softmax.
- **Quick check:** How would you modify a standard binary classifier architecture to output four independent probability scores for the sexist types, and what loss function would you use?

### Multimodal Fusion Strategies
- **Why needed:** Models must combine information from text transcripts, audio, and video frames. Engineers need to understand early fusion (concatenating features) vs. late fusion (separate encoders + joint layer) and their trade-offs for capturing cross-modal interactions (e.g., visual objectification matching textual stereotypes).
- **Quick check:** Given a video model that processes frames and text separately, what are two architectural ways to fuse their representations before classification, and which might better capture a sexist gesture accompanying a stereotypical statement?

### Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) Metrics for Multi-Label Tasks
- **Why needed:** Evaluating dataset quality requires understanding Fleiss' Kappa for multi-label scenarios. Low agreement on irony/humor indicates inherent ambiguity; engineers should not expect models to perfectly predict these noisy labels.
- **Quick check:** If Fleiss' Kappa for a label is 0.25 (fair), what does this suggest about using human annotations as a ground truth for that category, and how should model performance be interpreted?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Video URL → download → frame extraction (2–10 frames, black frame filter) → audio extraction → transcription (Whisper with diarization) → annotation pipeline (three passes with hierarchical labels + span selection, expert majority vote) → model interface (zero-shot Spanish prompt expecting JSON) → evaluation engine (computes Accuracy, Macro F1, Failure Rate, Format Error Rate, plus correlation and human evaluation).

### Critical path
Annotation quality → Label consistency → Model performance interpretation.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Zero-shot vs. Fine-tuning:** Zero-shot ensures generalizability; fine-tuning on FineMuSe would likely improve fine-grained F1, especially for low-frequency types.
- **Modality inclusion:** Text-only is cheaper; adding vision improves performance but increases compute cost and complexity. Video-input models (e.g., Gemini Video) underperform V+L, suggesting current video-LLMs inefficiently exploit temporal cues in short-form content.
- **Output structure:** Strict JSON ensures parseability but may constrain natural justification phrasing.

### Failure signatures
- **Category-specific failure:** Very low F1 on Objectification (<30 in P1) even for best models, indicating a data scarcity and/or visual understanding gap.
- **High failure rate:** Up to 34% of sexist instances missed at binary step (Gemini V+L P2), meaning the model never attempts fine-grained prediction.
- **Platform shift:** Performance on Stereotypes drops 10–15 points from P1 (TikTok/BitChute) to P2 (YouTube Shorts), suggesting platform-specific expression patterns not captured by zero-shot prompting.
- **Format errors:** Qwen-32B shows up to 35% malformed JSON on binary task, disrupting evaluation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** What specific visual features distinguish objectification from other sexism types, and why do multimodal models fail to detect them?  
**Basis in paper:** Objectification had the lowest F1 scores (below 30 in Part 1); models struggle when objectification is conveyed through visual cues.  
**Why unresolved:** Scarcity prevents models from learning robust visual patterns; models may over-rely on textual cues where objectification is less explicit.  
**What evidence would resolve:** A larger dataset with diverse visual objectification examples; Grad-CAM/attention visualization; human evaluation of visual-only instances.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Why do models over-associate Stereotypes with Inequality compared to human annotations, and how does this bias affect multi-label predictions?  
**Basis in paper:** Correlation analysis shows model Stereotypes-Inequality correlation r=0.29 vs human r=0.22; model label associations differ from human co-occurrence patterns.  
**Why unresolved:** This may reflect biased co-occurrence assumptions from training data or hierarchical classification forcing associations, leading to systematic over-prediction of certain category pairs.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Error analysis on instances where humans assigned only one category; probing embeddings to measure category entanglement; comparing independent multi-label training versus hierarchical cascades.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How can annotation consistency and model detection of irony and humor be improved given their subjective interpretation and severe scarcity in current datasets?  
**Basis in paper:** Fleiss’ Kappa for irony/humor ranges from poor (<0) to moderate (0.41–0.60); these categories are extremely scarce (e.g., sexist humor only 0.5% in Part 2).  
**Why unresolved:** Subjective perception leads to low inter-annotator agreement; scarcity limits model learning; models likely lack cultural/pragmatic knowledge needed for sarcasm detection.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Larger annotated dataset with detailed pragmatic guidelines; cross-cultural studies on irony/humor perception; evaluation on established sarcasm/humor benchmarks.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How do individual modalities (text, audio, video) uniquely contribute to detecting specific fine-grained sexism types, and what fusion strategies optimally integrate multimodal information?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper states future work may further analyze modality contributions. While multi-level annotation across modalities was performed, experiments lack systematic modality-ablation.  
**Why unresolved:** Without controlled experiments, it is unclear whether improvements stem from visual confirmation, complementary cues, or modality-specific importance per category.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Modality-ablation studies (text-only, audio-only, video-only, combinations); attention/explanation analysis showing modality-driven predictions per category; synthetic videos with independently controlled modalities.

## Limitations
- Dataset accessibility and exact structure are uncertain (GitHub repository may be unavailable or incomplete), blocking reproduction.
- Annotation subjectivity for irony/humor yields low inter-annotator agreement, producing noisy labels that cap model performance for these categories.
- Multimodal models consistently fail to detect visually-conveyed objectification (Macro F1 <30), indicating a gap in visual reasoning and/or data scarcity.
- Performance drops significantly on out-of-platform data (YouTube Shorts vs. TikTok/BitChute), limiting generalizability of zero-shot prompts.
