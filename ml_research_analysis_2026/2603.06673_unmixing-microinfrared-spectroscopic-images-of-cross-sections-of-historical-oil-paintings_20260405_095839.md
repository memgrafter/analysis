# Unmixing microinfrared spectroscopic images of cross-sections of historical oil paintings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06673  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06673  
- **Reference count:** 12  
- **Primary result:** A CNN autoencoder with a weighted spectral angle distance (WSAD) loss enables blind hyperspectral unmixing of ATR-µFTIR painting cross-sections, improving spatial coherence of abundance maps and suppressing atmospheric CO₂ contamination.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents a novel approach for blind hyperspectral unmixing of ATR-µFTIR cross-section images of historical paintings. The method uses a convolutional neural network autoencoder that models local spatial structure through patches and introduces a weighted spectral angle distance (WSAD) loss to downweight unreliable spectral bands automatically. Applied to a cross-section from the Ghent Altarpiece, the WSAD-based method yields abundance maps with improved spatial coherence and chemical interpretability compared to using the standard SAD loss, and effectively suppresses contamination from atmospheric CO₂ around 2350 cm⁻¹. The number of endmembers was qualitatively set to 10.

## Method Summary
The proposed unmixing framework consists of a patch-based CNN autoencoder. The encoder processes small spatial patches (p×p) to produce per-pixel abundance maps for a fixed number of endmembers, enforcing non-negativity and sum-to-one constraints via a softmax activation. The decoder reconstructs each patch by convolving the abundance maps with a set of endmember spectra, implementing a linear mixing model. The reconstruction loss is a weighted spectral angle distance (WSAD) that multiplies both target and reconstructed spectra by band-specific reliability weights before computing the angular error. These weights are computed automatically from the hyperspectral cube using three diagnostics: spatial flatness (identifying uniform regions like atmospheric features), neighbor-correlation deficit (spotting isolated spikes), and spectral roughness (detecting high-frequency noise). The entire system is trained end-to-end in an unsupervised manner, as no ground truth abundances are available. The number of endmembers K is chosen heuristically by visual inspection of the resulting abundance maps, with the study identifying 10 distinct materials in the Ghent Altarpiece cross-section.

## Key Results
- Patch-based CNN autoencoder captures spatial-spectral regularities, leading to coherent abundance maps.
- WSAD loss automatically downweights unreliable bands, suppressing atmospheric CO₂ contamination (~2350 cm⁻¹) and improving chemical interpretability.
- Visual evaluation shows improved spatial coherence of specific components (e.g., metal soaps) compared to standard SAD loss.
- The method identifies 10 endmembers for the analyzed cross-section.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Modeling local spatial patches with a CNN autoencoder captures spatial-spectral regularities, improving abundance map coherence compared to pixel-wise methods.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder processes small spatial patches (`p×p` neighborhoods) to generate per-pixel abundance maps that respect local contextual smoothness. The decoder reconstructs each patch via a convolution over these abundances, enforcing that spectra in a neighborhood share a consistent linear mixing model (Eq. 2). This spatially-constrained reconstruction biases the learned representation toward material distributions that vary smoothly across the painting cross-section.
- **Core assumption:** Adjacent pixels in a painting cross-section are likely to share similar material compositions (spatial homogeneity) and thus similar spectral mixtures.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "exploiting local spatial structure through patch-based modelling"
  - [section II.B]: Introduces the patch-wise LMM `bXc = W * A_c` and states the goal is to "exploit spectral–spatial regularities."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on different applications (medical, fluorescence microscopy) but confirm patch-based CNNs are a known paradigm for spectral-spatial unmixing.
- **Break condition:** If the painting cross-section contains highly discontinuous material interfaces (e.g., sharp cracks, delamination) where neighbors are spectrally dissimilar, the patch model may blur boundaries or force artificial smoothness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Weighted Spectral Angle Distance (WSAD) loss reduces the influence of unreliable spectral bands (e.g., atmospheric CO₂, noise spikes) during training, leading to endmember spectra and abundance maps that are less corrupted by artifacts.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of treating all `B` spectral bands equally in the reconstruction loss, WSAD multiplies both the ground truth and reconstructed spectra by a band-specific reliability weight `w` before computing the angular similarity (Eq. 6). Bands deemed unreliable by the automatic estimator (Section II.E) receive low weights (`w_b ≈ w_min`), thus contributing minimally to the loss gradient. This prevents the autoencoder from fitting spurious signals in those bands.
- **Core assumption:** Not all spectral bands carry equal information about the sample's material composition; some are dominated by atmospheric, instrumental, or acquisition artifacts that should be ignored for chemically meaningful unmixing.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "To reduce sensitivity to atmospheric and acquisition artefacts across >1500 bands, we introduce a weighted spectral angle distance (WSAD) loss with automatic band-reliability weights"
  - [section II.D]: Explicitly defines the WSAD loss `WSAD(x, ˆx; w) = arccos(...)` and states the goal is to "reduce the influence of unreliable/nuisance spectral regions."
  - [section III]: Visually demonstrates that WSAD suppresses the ~2350 cm⁻¹ CO₂ peak in endmember spectra compared to SAD.
- **Break condition:** If the artifact bands are spectrally indistinguishable from genuine material absorption bands using the proposed flatness/neighbor/roughness diagnostics, they will not be downweighted and will still bias the results.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Automatic band-reliability weights derived from spatial flatness, neighbor-agreement deficit, and spectral roughness effectively identify and downweight artifact-dominated bands without manual intervention.
- **Mechanism:** For each spectral band `b`, three complementary statistics are computed from the entire HSI: (1) `d_flat` measures abnormally low spatial variance (e.g., common-mode atmospheric features). (2) `d_corr` measures low correlation with adjacent bands (e.g., isolated spikes or sharp instrumental artifacts). (3) `d_rough` measures high curvature in the median spectrum (e.g., single-band noise spikes). These are converted into outlier scores `s_b` (Eq. 12) and mapped to weights `w_b` via a sigmoid (Eq. 13). High outlier scores yield low weights.
- **Core assumption:** Artifacts (atmospheric H₂O/CO₂, detector noise) exhibit statistical signatures—spatial homogeneity, spectral isolation, or high-frequency roughness—that are distinct from the spectra of chemically heterogeneous painting materials.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section II.E]: Provides the full formulation for `d_flat`, `d_corr`, `d_rough`, scoring `s_b`, and weight mapping `w_b`. States the goal is to "downweight unusually noisy bands."
  - [section III]: Notes that WSAD "effectively suppressed contamination from atmospheric CO₂ around 2350 cm⁻¹" and that endmember spectra show "reduced sensitivity to the CO₂ region."
- **Break condition:** If artifacts do not exhibit one of these three signature patterns—e.g., if a noisy band is also spatially varying and spectrally smooth—it will not be penalized. Conversely, a genuine but narrow, isolated chemical absorption peak could be misclassified as "rough" and downweighted, losing real information.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear Mixing Model (LMM)**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire unmixing framework assumes pixel spectra are linear combinations of pure material (endmember) spectra (Eq. 1). This is the foundational physical model (Beer-Lambert law for absorbance) that the decoder's linear layer (`W * A_c`) aims to approximate.
  - *Quick check question:* If an HSI pixel contains a material at 20% concentration and another at 80%, under the LMM what is the relationship between the observed spectrum and the two pure endmember spectra?
- **Concept: Spectral Angle Distance (SAD)**
  - *Why needed here:* SAD is the baseline reconstruction loss (`arccos(x·x̂/(‖x‖‖x̂‖))`), measuring spectral similarity. WSAD is a direct modification of SAD, so understanding its unit-norm, angle-based interpretation is key to seeing how weighting modifies the optimization objective.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might SAD be preferred over Euclidean distance for FTIR spectra, where overall intensity can vary due to thickness but spectral *shapes* are more indicative of composition?
- **Concept: Autoencoder Architecture for Unmixing**
  - *Why needed here:* The encoder produces abundance maps (interpreted as nonnegative coefficients summing to 1), and the decoder's weights are interpreted as endmember spectra. This architecture learns the LMM parameters in an unsupervised, end-to-end differentiable way.
  - *Quick check question:* In this autoencoder, what constraints are placed on the encoder output (`A_c`) and decoder weights (`W`) to make them physically plausible as abundances and endmembers?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Input:** HSI cube `X` (H×W×B).
  2.  **Patch Extractor:** Extracts all `p×p` patches (with overlap) for training.
  3.  **Encoder (`E`):** Two conv layers (3×3, then 1×1) with BN, dropout, leaky-ReLU, output scaled by `softmax(α·)` to produce abundance maps `A_c` (K×p×p) per patch, enforcing ANC/ASC.
  4.  **Decoder (`D`):** Single linear conv layer with softplus on weights `U` → `W` (B×K×p×p), ensuring ENC. Reconstruction: `bXc = W * A_c`.
  5.  **Endmember Estimator:** `bE = sum_{u,v} W_{u,v}` (B×K matrix).
  6.  **Band-Weight Estimator (offline):** Computes `w` from input cube `X` using flatness, neighbor-correlation, and roughness diagnostics (II.E).
  7.  **Loss Function:** `LWSAD = average(arccos((w⊙x)ᵀ(w⊙x̂) / (‖w⊙x‖‖w⊙x̂‖)`).
- **Critical path:** Data input → offline compute `w` → for each training epoch: sample patch → `E` → `A_c` → `D` → `bX_c` → compute per-pixel WSAD loss using fixed `w` → backpropagate to update `E` and `D` weights → periodically aggregate decoder weights to update `bE`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Patch size `p`:** Larger `p` captures broader spatial context but increases memory (decoder weights ∝ `p²`) and may oversmooth fine details.
  - **Weight hyperparameters (`α`, `τ`, `γ_rough`, `γ_flat`):** Control aggressiveness of downweighting. Too aggressive (`τ` low, `γ` high) risks discarding informative bands; too conservative leaves artifacts.
  - **Number of endmembers `K`:** Must be set heuristically (they used visual inspection). Overestimation yields redundant/noisy maps; underestimation forces merged materials.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Training loss plateaus at a high value: possible misconfigured weights `w` (over-downweighting), poor learning rate, or architecture mismatch.
  - Endmember spectra show strong residual peaks at known artifact regions (e.g., 2350 cm⁻¹): `w` failed to downweight those bands (check diagnostic calculations).
  - Abundance maps are noisy or scattered: patch size too small, or `α` too low (abundances not sparse enough).
  - Duplicate or empty abundance maps for `K>10`: indicates `K` overestimated; check for repeated spatial patterns.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline comparison:** Train FTIR-unmixer with `w = 1` (SAD loss) and with auto `w` (WSAD). Visually compare endmember spectra around 2350 cm⁻¹ and spatial coherence of known components (e.g., metal soaps Map 8).
  2.  **Ablation of weight components:** Re-run WSAD but disable one diagnostic at a time (set `γ_flat=0`, `γ_rough=0`). Observe if CO₂ suppression degrades, indicating which statistics are most critical for this dataset.
  3.  **Sensitivity to `K`:** Train with `K=8,10,12` using WSAD. Inspect abundance maps for merging (K too low) or splitting/duplication (K too high) to confirm chosen `K=10` is near a stability point.

## Limitations
- Endmember count (K=10) is heuristically determined without a robust statistical criterion, risking over/under-estimation.
- The band-weighting diagnostics are validated only on a single dataset; their generalizability to other painting systems or artifact types is uncertain.
- The patch-based model assumes local spatial homogeneity, which may blur sharp material interfaces or cracks.
- Absence of ground truth limits quantitative validation.

## Confidence
- WSAD Artifact Suppression (e.g., CO₂): High
- Improved Spatial Coherence (vs. SAD): Medium
- Automatic Band-Weighting Diagnostics: Medium-Low
- Optimal K=10: Low
