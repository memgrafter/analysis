# AgenticShop: Benchmarking Agentic Product Curation for Personalized Web Shopping

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12315  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12315  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Current search-augmented LLMs and web agents achieve only 30–35% curation scores on personalized product selection, with high failure rates in Open Exploration and aesthetic domains.

## Executive Summary
AgenticShop is the first benchmark for evaluating agentic systems on personalized product curation in open-web shopping. It constructs realistic user profiles from Amazon review histories, defines three shopping intents (Target Finding, Alternative Selection, Open Exploration), and uses a checklist-driven evaluation framework with an LLM-as-a-judge. Experiments on state-of-the-art search-augmented LLMs and autonomous web agents reveal overall low performance (30–35% for top models, lower for agents), particularly on Open Exploration and aesthetic-driven categories. Key deficiencies include inability to handle dynamic pricing, scarcity of review-rich products, and high rates of invalid product links (24–59% failure). The benchmark highlights significant challenges in building effective personalized shopping agents.

## Method Summary
The benchmark builds user profiles by filtering Amazon users with ≥100 reviews and identifying a “target product type” as the most frequent subcategory within a domain. For each user, GPT-5-mini generates: a narrative context from purchase history and reviews, an intent-specific query (three variants for Target Finding and Alternative Selection; none for Open Exploration), and a six-dimensional checklist (e.g., budget, features, brand loyalty). Agents receive the query and must return product URLs from the open web. Retrieved product pages are rendered via Playwright (capturing screenshots and extracted text). An LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-5-mini) evaluates each product against checklist criteria, producing binary satisfaction scores. The primary metric is the Curation Score: the average proportion of checklist criteria satisfied across recommended products.

## Key Results
- Top search-augmented LLMs achieve 30–35% curation scores; autonomous web agents perform worse.
- Performance drops significantly on Open Exploration tasks and aesthetic-driven product domains.
- High rates of invalid product links (24–59% failure) and frequent mismatches on dynamic pricing and review scarcity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AgenticShop’s design yields a realistic and rigorous evaluation by grounding personalization in authentic user histories and testing across diverse intents. The checklist framework provides fine-grained alignment measurement, while the LLM-as-a-judge with visual context captures both textual and aesthetic product attributes. The open-web setting introduces natural complexities (dynamic content, noisy links) that mirror real shopping challenges, revealing limitations that controlled simulations miss. Moreover, the separation of intent types isolates specific capabilities—targeted search, comparative reasoning, and exploratory discovery—enabling targeted analysis of agent strengths and weaknesses.

## Foundational Learning
Understanding AgenticShop requires several key concepts:

- **Realistic User Profiling**: Profiles derived from Amazon users with ≥100 reviews capture long-term preferences and purchase contexts. *Why needed*: Personalization must be grounded in genuine behavior. *Quick check*: Verify that the target product type is the most frequent subcategory within the selected domain.
- **Shopping Intent Taxonomy**: The three intents (Target Finding, Alternative Selection, Open Exploration) cover distinct curation scenarios. *Why needed*: Different intents probe different agent capabilities. *Quick check*: Ensure query generation follows intent-specific formats (explicit title, attribute query, or no query).
- **Checklist-Based Evaluation**: A six‑dimension checklist (e.g., budget, features, brand, review sentiment) is scored per product by an LLM judge. *Why needed*: Provides interpretable, granular alignment metrics. *Quick check*: Confirm checklist criteria align with the user’s narrative and historical spending.
- **LLM-as-a-Judge with Visual Rendering**: Product pages are rendered via Playwright; GPT‑5‑mini evaluates both text and images. *Why needed*: Captures visual attributes (e.g., aesthetics) essential for certain domains. *Quick check*: Validate that scraped pages include prices, review counts, and product images.
- **Agent Paradigms**: Two classes are evaluated—search‑augmented LLMs (retrieval‑augmented generation) and autonomous web agents (interactive navigation). *Why needed*: Highlights different failure modes. *Quick check*: Compare invalid URL rates between paradigms.

## Architecture Onboarding
The benchmark pipeline comprises the following components (plain text flow):

Amazon Review Dataset -> Profile Builder -> (User Narrative, Query, Checklist) -> Agent System (LLM + Search/Navigation) -> Product URLs -> Playwright Renderer (screenshots + text extraction) -> LLM-as-a-Judge -> Curation Score Aggregator

**Critical Path**: The most crucial and failure‑prone stages are (1) the agent’s URL generation (often producing invalid or non‑product links) and (2) the LLM judge’s ability to correctly interpret checklist criteria from rendered pages. Errors directly affect the final score.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- *LLM judge vs. human evaluation*: LLM offers scalability but may misalign with human preferences, especially for aesthetics.
- *Checklist granularity*: Six dimensions improve interpretability but may oversimplify complex preferences.
- *Open web vs. curated e‑commerce*: Open web increases realism but introduces noise (dynamic prices, broken links).

**Failure Signatures**:
- High proportion of invalid or non‑product URLs (>24%).
- Selected products with zero or very few reviews, indicating poor handling of review scarcity.
- Price mismatches (product exceeds user budget or outdated dynamic pricing).
- Systematic underperformance on Open Exploration and aesthetic‑driven categories.

**Three First Experiments**:
1. Baseline replication: Run provided agent implementations on a fixed subset of 100 profiles to establish baseline scores, failure rates, and error logs.
2. Prompt sensitivity test: Vary prompts for checklist generation and LLM judging to assess metric robustness; measure score variance.
3. Agent comparison: Directly compare search‑augmented LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT with search) against autonomous agents (e.g., WebVoyager) across all three intents to map relative strengths/weaknesses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How effectively can agentic systems curate personalized products in open‑web shopping?** Current systems achieve only 30–35% scores, with severe failures in Open Exploration and aesthetic domains. A resolution would require systems that consistently exceed 80% across all intents and domains, robustly handling dynamic pricing, review scarcity, and invalid links.
2. **What are the primary factors making personalized curation challenging?** Identified factors include dynamic pricing, review scarcity, URL hallucination, and promotional distractions, but their relative impacts and mechanisms are unclear. Ablation studies isolating each factor and targeted improvements would clarify.
3. **How can we enhance personalized curation capabilities?** The paper notes correlations with information‑seeking and balanced exploration but offers no concrete enhancements. Significant progress (e.g., >50% absolute improvement) would require novel agent architectures, grounding mechanisms, or search strategies, validated by thorough component analysis.

## Limitations
- Reproduction requires reconstructing unreported details (exact LLM prompts, Amazon filtering thresholds, agent interaction specs), introducing variability.
- Performance claims (30–35% scores) depend on these reconstructions, so confidence is medium.
- The benchmark relies solely on Amazon review data, limiting domain diversity and exacerbating challenges in aesthetic categories where review‑rich products are scarce.
