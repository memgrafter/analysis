# RBCorr: Response Bias Correction in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12445
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12445
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** RBCorr reduces response bias (TVD) and improves or maintains accuracy across 12 models and three question types via a simple LogProb mean-subtraction using a small class-balanced calibration set; correction effects are highly specific to model, dataset, and prompt format, and transfer generally fails.

## Executive Summary
Language models often exhibit response bias—systematic preferences for certain answer tokens—when evaluated on fixed-response tasks. This bias inflates Total Variation Distance (TVD) and can mask latent knowledge, leading to inaccurate performance estimates. RBCorr is a post-hoc correction method that subtracts per-token mean log-probabilities estimated from a small class-balanced calibration set. Across 12 models and multiple datasets, RBCorr consistently reduces TVD and either improves or maintains accuracy, sometimes recovering up to ~29% of lost performance. However, the correction is highly configuration-specific; transfer of calibration terms between models, datasets, or prompt formats generally fails.

## Method Summary
RBCorr operates on a model’s log-probabilities (LogProbs) for predefined response tokens (e.g., “Yes”, “No”). First, a small class-balanced calibration set is sampled from the training data. For each response token, the mean LogProb over the calibration items is computed. During testing, these mean values are subtracted from the corresponding LogProbs of each test item. The final prediction is the token with the highest corrected LogProb. This simple mean-subtraction effectively centers the LogProb distribution, mitigating the model’s inherent token preference. The method requires no extra inference compute and works with any open-weight language model.

## Key Results
- **Bias reduction:** RBCorr significantly lowers TVD across 12 models and three question types (2/3/4-choice).
- **Accuracy maintenance/recovery:** Accuracy is either improved or remains stable; some datasets (e.g., bAbI) show ~29% improvement.
- **Configuration specificity:** Correction terms do not transfer across model, dataset, or prompt changes; transfer success rates are low (≈10–22%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Mean-subtraction of option-specific LogProbs using a class-balanced calibration set reduces measured response bias (lower TVD).
- **Mechanism:** The calibration set estimates the model’s inherent, dataset-independent prior preference for each response token. Subtracting these means from test item LogProbs centers the distributions, aligning the model’s relative scoring with a uniform prior. Bias is operationalized as TVD between the uniform ground-truth label distribution and the model’s response distribution. Centering aims to make the response distribution more uniform.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s option-specific bias is stable across items within a dataset/prompt configuration and can be captured by the mean LogProbs over a small, class-balanced sample.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states RBCorr “effectively eliminates bias”; section 2.3 details the procedure; section 4/5 figures show pre/post bias; corpus provides context but weak direct evidence.
- **Break condition:** Unbalanced calibration set yields skewed mean estimates; bias not stable across items makes a single mean inadequate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The correction can recover or maintain accuracy by removing a confound (option preference) that may distort the model’s signal for the correct answer.
- **Mechanism:** Response bias causes selection of a wrong but preferred option even when underlying knowledge favors the correct answer. Neutralizing the constant bias offset allows corrected LogProbs to better reflect content-specific evidence. If latent correct knowledge exists, de-biasing lets that signal dominate the argmax decision, improving accuracy; otherwise accuracy remains stable.
- **Core assumption:** For many misclassifications, the error is due to a constant offset rather than lack of knowledge; the bias is separable from item-specific signal in final-layer LogProbs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes “improves or maintains accuracy”; section 5 argues bias removal uncovers hidden performance; figure 3 shows many models moving to higher accuracy; table 1 shows specific large recoveries.
- **Break condition:** Errors primarily due to lack of knowledge/reasoning; correction could subtract from correct answer signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The efficacy of a specific correction term (mean LogProb adjustments) is highly contingent on the exact configuration of model, dataset, and prompt format, and does not transfer reliably.
- **Mechanism:** LogProb values depend on the entire input context (prompt + question + options). Changing model weights, dataset properties, or prompt template alters activation patterns and thus the bias pattern. The calibration mean for one configuration is an inaccurate estimate for another.
- **Core assumption:** Model LogProbs are sensitive to model, data, and prompt; no universal bias vector exists.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “highly specific … transfer … fails”; table 2 quantifies transfer failure (cross-dataset ~22%, cross-model/prompt ~10%); figure 4 heatmaps show poor transfer.
- **Break condition:** Transfer would succeed only if a dominant, configuration-invariant bias factor exists, which evidence contradicts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Token LogProbability (LogProb):** Pre-softmax logit for a token; RBCorr uses these raw scores. *Why needed:* Correction operates on LogProbs before normalization. *Quick check:* If a model assigns higher LogProb to “No” than “Yes” for balanced questions, this indicates a raw preference for the “No” token.
- **Total Variation Distance (TVD):** Metric measuring difference between the uniform label distribution and the model’s empirical response distribution. Lower TVD means less bias. *Why needed:* Primary bias evaluation metric. *Quick check:* For 4-way balanced data, TVD = 0.5 if model always picks “A”; TVD = 0 if responses are perfectly uniform.
- **Class-Balanced Calibration Set:** A small sample with equal numbers of examples per label. *Why needed:* Ensures mean LogProb estimates per token are not skewed by label frequency. *Quick check:* If the calibration set for a binary task contains 80 “B” and 20 “A”, the mean for “B” will be underestimated relative to its true bias, leading to over-correction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Processor -> Model Inference Engine -> Calibration Sampler -> Bias Estimator -> Correction Applier -> Response Decoder -> Evaluator

**Critical path:**  
1. Baseline measurement (without correction) → 2. Sample class-balanced calibration set → 3. Compute per-token mean LogProbs → 4. Subtract means from test LogProbs → 5. Predict via argmax → 6. Evaluate TVD & accuracy.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Calibration set size: Larger sets improve stability (gains plateau ~100 items) but require more labeled data.  
- Zero compute overhead: Correction is simple subtraction on already-extracted LogProbs.  
- Configuration specificity: A unique correction term must be stored per (model, dataset, prompt) tuple; no sharing.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High TVD after correction (bias not reduced).  
- Significant accuracy drop post-correction.  
- High variance in metrics across random calibration seeds.  
- Unexpectedly successful transfer to a new configuration (indicates a bug or non-generalizable finding).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline characterization:** For a given (model, dataset, prompt), compute accuracy and TVD; confirm presence/direction of bias.  
2. **Single-configuration RBCorr:** Apply RBCorr with fixed calibration size (e.g., 100). Verify TVD reduction and accuracy stability over many random calibration iterations.  
3. **Cross-method comparison:** Implement Contextual Calibration and Batch Calibration; run all three on identical configurations (e.g., Llama3.1-8B across datasets) to reproduce Table 1 and confirm RBCorr’s advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can models be fine-tuned on corrected probabilities to inherently reduce response bias and eliminate post-hoc correction?
2. What neural mechanisms encode response bias, and can targeted interventions (e.g., early layers, weight editing) mitigate bias at its source?
3. Does RBCorr generalize to open-form generation tasks with unbounded output spaces?
4. Why do correction terms fail to transfer across configurations, and what factors (training data, architecture, prompt design) drive this specificity?

## Limitations
- **Token aggregation ambiguity:** The method for combining multiple token variants per label is underspecified; aggregation choice may affect results.
- **Calibration plateau unknown for all models:** The ~100-sample plateau observed may not hold for smaller models or highly imbalanced datasets.
- **Causal mechanism unproven:** The assumption that bias is a constant offset separable from item-specific knowledge is inferred, not directly isolated; alternative explanations exist.

## Confidence
- **Bias reduction (TVD):** High
- **Accuracy maintenance/recovery:** Medium‑High
- **Configuration specificity / transfer failure:** High
- **Latent knowledge recovery mechanism:** Medium

## Next Checks

3. Create a synthetic dataset with known truth (e.g., parity of random numbers) and inject a constant bias via a preference‑inducing prompt; test whether RBCorr recovers the underlying signal, directly validating the constant‑offset assumption.
