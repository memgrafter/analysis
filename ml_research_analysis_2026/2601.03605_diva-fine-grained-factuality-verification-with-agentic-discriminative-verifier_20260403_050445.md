# DiVA: Fine-grained Factuality Verification with Agentic-Discriminative Verifier
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03605
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03605
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** DiVA significantly outperforms prior verifiers on fine-grained factuality benchmarks via an agentic-discriminative framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DiVA, a framework for fine-grained factuality verification that moves beyond binary correct/incorrect judgments to predict continuous factuality scores. It combines a generative agent that performs tool-augmented search (using WebSearch and a local Wikipedia dump) to gather evidence with a discriminative module trained via margin ranking loss on pairwise preferences. The authors also release FGVeriBench, a new benchmark with ranking-based annotations for general and multi-hop QA. DiVA achieves state-of-the-art performance on FGVeriBench, demonstrating the value of granular scoring and the hybrid agentic-discriminative design. Ablations confirm the importance of context compression and the benefit of dual knowledge sources. The work also shows promising adaptability to related tasks like binary verification and best-of-N selection.

## Method Summary
DiVA operates in two stages. First, a generative agent (based on Qwen-2.5-7B/Qwen-3-8B) reasons about a claim and uses external search tools (WebSearch and LocalSearch) to retrieve relevant evidence, generating a search trajectory. This raw trajectory is then compressed using the same LLM into a shorter, focused context. Second, a discriminative model (DeBERTa-v3-base) takes the claim and compressed context and produces a continuous factuality score. The discriminator is trained using a margin ranking loss on pairwise preference data constructed via LLM-as-a-judge and human verification, where one answer is judged more factual than another. This dual design leverages the generative model's reasoning and retrieval capabilities for evidence collection while the discriminative model provides stable, fine-grained scoring.

## Key Results
- DiVA achieves significant improvements in Kendall’s τ and Precision@1 on FGVeriBench compared to generative and discriminative baselines.
- Ablations show that context compression is crucial, especially for multi-hop queries, and that combining WebSearch and LocalSearch yields the best performance.
- The framework demonstrates strong adaptability, outperforming baselines in binary verification, long-form factuality, and best-of-N selection tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The agentic search module gathers diverse, tool-augmented evidence, addressing the knowledge cut-off and hallucination issues of standalone LLMs. The subsequent context compression step condenses often lengthy and noisy search trajectories into a form suitable for a discriminative verifier, preventing the discriminator from being overwhelmed by irrelevant details. The discriminative module, trained with a ranking objective on pairwise preferences, learns to distinguish subtle differences in factuality, producing a continuous score rather than a binary label. The use of two distinct knowledge sources (web and local) increases coverage, ensuring that both current and well-established facts can be verified. This separation of concerns—evidence gathering via generative search and fine-grained scoring via discriminative ranking—creates a robust pipeline for nuanced verification.

## Foundational Learning
- **Agentic Search:** Uses an LLM with tool-use (search APIs) to iteratively gather evidence. *Why needed:* To retrieve up-to-date or specific information beyond the model's parametric knowledge. *Quick check:* Audit search queries and retrieved documents for relevance to the claim.
- **Margin Ranking Loss:** A loss function that optimizes a model to score a preferred instance higher than a non-preferred one by a margin. *Why needed:* To train the discriminator on relative factuality judgments from pairwise annotations. *Quick check:* Test if synthetic preference pairs are learned correctly on a held-out set.
- **Context Compression:** Summarizing or extracting key information from a long retrieval trajectory. *Why needed:* Discriminative models have limited context windows; compression retains only verification-critical evidence. *Quick check:* Compare performance with and without compression on long-trajectory inputs.
- **Dual Knowledge Sources:** Utilizing both a dynamic web search and a static knowledge base (Wikipedia). *Why needed:* To balance coverage of current events with depth on established facts. *Quick check:* Ablate each source independently and measure performance drop per query type.
- **LLM-as-a-Judge:** Using a large language model to generate preference labels for training. *Why needed:* Scalable creation of fine-grained training data without exhaustive human annotation. *Quick check:* Sample LLM-generated preferences for human validation and measure agreement.
- **Pairwise Preference Construction:** Building training data from ranked annotations by forming ordered pairs. *Why needed:* To train a ranking-based scorer rather than an absolute classifier. *Quick check:* Ensure transitivity (or near-transitivity) in the derived pairwise dataset.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Generative Agent (Qwen) with Tools (WebSearch, LocalSearch) -> Raw Search Trajectory -> Context Compression (LLM-prompted) -> Compressed Context -> Discriminative Module (DeBERTa) -> Continuous Factuality Score

**Critical Path:**  
Claim -> Agentic Search (tool calls, reasoning steps) -> Trajectory -> Compression -> Discriminator -> Score

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Generative vs. Discriminative:* The agent provides flexibility and tool-use but is slow and variable; the discriminator is fast and stable but depends on the quality of the compressed context.
- *Compression:* Simple LLM-based summarization is lightweight but may lose subtle evidence; a learned compressor could be more precise but adds complexity.
- *Pairwise Training:* Ranking loss handles ordinal judgments but is sensitive to noise in the preference pairs (from LLM-as-a-judge).
- *Tool Cost:* WebSearch incurs latency and API costs; LocalSearch is free but static. Combining both improves accuracy but increases overall system cost.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Agent retrieves irrelevant or low-quality documents, leading to poor evidence.
- Compression discards key evidence or retains noise, confusing the discriminator.
- Discriminator confuses closely ranked examples, especially on ambiguous or multi-hop claims.
- System degrades on time-sensitive facts if LocalSearch is over-relied, or on obscure topics if WebSearch returns no results.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Evaluate DiVA on established factuality benchmarks (e.g., FactCC, HOVER) to test cross-dataset generalization.
2. Conduct a human evaluation of FGVeriBench: measure inter-annotator agreement and compare DiVA’s scores to human rankings.
3. Perform error analysis categorizing failures by hop count, domain (e.g., current events vs. historical), and tool usage pattern.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the agentic search module be specifically optimized or fine-tuned for factuality verification tasks beyond its general reasoning and tool-use capabilities?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Limitation" section states: "While we leveraged the inherent agentic search capabilities of Qwen-2.5-7B and Qwen-3-8B, we have not yet specifically optimized this behavior for factuality verification. Future work will focus on further enhancing and fine-tuning these search abilities."
- **Why unresolved:** The current framework uses off-the-shelf agentic search, which may not be optimally efficient or effective for the specific goal of gathering precise verification evidence. Unoptimized searches could retrieve unnecessary or irrelevant information, increasing cost and potentially harming the discriminative module's performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing DiVA's performance when the generative agent is (a) used as-is, (b) supervised fine-tuned on a dataset of fact-checking search trajectories, or (c) reinforcement learning-tuned with a reward signal based on final verification accuracy or retrieval relevance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does the performance of the pairwise ranking-trained discriminative module depend on the quality and consistency of the pairwise preference data constructed via LLM-as-a-judge, given the observed 64.8% inter-annotator agreement?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper constructs training pairs using "LLM-as-a-judge" with human verification (Section 3.2, "Pairwise Data Construction"). The FGVeriBench construction notes a 64.8% human inter-annotator agreement rate, "showing that fine-grained factuality annotation is non-trivial." This implies the preference labels used for training may contain noise or subjectivity.
- **Why unresolved:** The margin ranking loss assumes pairwise preferences are mostly correct. Noise in the preference labels (from the LLM judge or even human disagreement) could lead the discriminator to learn incorrect scoring boundaries, especially for "intermediate" factuality cases. The paper does not analyze the robustness of the ranking loss to label noise.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments injecting synthetic noise into the pairwise training data (e.g., flipping a percentage of preferences) to measure the degradation in Kendall's τ on FGVeriBench. Alternatively, analyzing if performance gaps between DiVA and baselines are larger on samples with lower human-LLM judge agreement.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the specific trade-offs and optimal use cases for the dual knowledge sources (WebSearch vs. LocalSearch) within the agentic search framework?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.6 states: "WebSearch significantly outperforms LocalSearch... Nonetheless, combining both sources yields the best performance." It also shows a correlation between retrieval relevance and verification accuracy.
- **Why unresolved:** While combining sources is best, the paper does not investigate scenarios where one source might be preferable (e.g., cost, latency, privacy, or domain-specificity). LocalSearch uses a static Wikipedia dump; its utility for time-sensitive or niche factual claims is unknown. The "adaptive" retrieval strategy is not characterized.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A breakdown of DiVA's performance per question/answer type, tagged by whether the ground truth is best supported by web-indexed information (current events, very specific facts) vs. encyclopedic/local knowledge (historical, well-established facts). Analysis of search trajectory logs to see if the agent's choice of tool is correlated with final accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the proposed context compression step quantitatively contribute to performance, and could a learned compression or extraction method further improve results?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The ablation "w/o compression" in Tables 2 & 3 shows a "substantial drop" in performance, "particularly in multi-hop scenario." The paper describes compression as using the generative LLM to "condense the agentic search trajectory."
- **Why unresolved:** The compression is rule-based/LLM-prompted, not learned. It is a fixed preprocessing step. It is unclear if this simple compression is near-optimal or if a trainable compression/retrieval-augmented approach could better distill the most relevant evidence for the discriminator, especially for complex multi-hop reasoning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the hand-crafted compression prompt with a parametric model (e.g., a small Transformer trained to extract salient sentences from the raw trajectory) and compare performance. Alternatively, analyze compression quality by measuring the overlap between compressed context and gold-standard evidence sets (if available) or by correlating compression length with final score accuracy.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can the fine-grained factuality scores predicted by DiVA be reliably used as reward signals or feedback for LLM post-training (e.g., preference optimization or RLHF) to improve model factuality, and does this outperform binary or LLM-as-a-judge feedback?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Conclusion states: "A direct utility of fine-grained verifier is to provide more granular feedback for the post-training of LLMs... we will integrate our verifier directly into LLM preference optimization pipelines." Section 5.5 shows DiVA improves best-of-N selection, implying utility as a scorer.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates DiVA as a *verifier* (scoring model outputs). Its utility as a *trainer* is proposed but not tested. Key questions remain: Are the continuous scores well-calibrated and meaningful as reward magnitudes? Can they provide more stable and informative gradients than binary labels or sparse LLM-judge rankings? Does fine-grained feedback lead to models that are not only more factual but also maintain other qualities (helpfulness, fluency)?
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An experiment using DiVA's scores as reward models in an RLHF or DPO pipeline to fine-tune an LLM, then evaluating the fine-tuned model on factuality (using FGVeriBench or FactScore) vs. models trained with (a) binary correctness rewards, or (b) rewards from a standard LLM-as-a-judge. Measuring trade-offs in factuality vs. other alignment goals would be crucial.

## Limitations
- FGVeriBench's ranking annotations may suffer from subjectivity due to moderate inter-annotator agreement (64.8%).
- Evaluation is restricted to a single new benchmark; generalization to other factuality datasets is unproven.
- Claims about adaptability to binary verification, long-form, and best-of-N lack comprehensive experimental detail and error analysis.

## Confidence
- **Fine-grained verification effectiveness:** Medium (strong on-benchmark results but limited to one novel benchmark).
- **Component necessity (context compression, dual sources):** Medium (ablations supportive but not跨-dataset).
- **Task adaptability (binary, long-form, best-of-N):** Low (insufficient experimental evidence provided).
