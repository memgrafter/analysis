# Xray-Visual Models: Scaling Vision models on Industry Scale Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16918
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16918
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: SOTA on ImageNet (89.3% LP) and Kinetics (78.1%) with 4× efficiency; +8.1% on ObjectNet, +10.8% on cross-domain retrieval.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scarcity of large-scale visual data by curating 15B image-text and 10B video-hashtag pairs from social media. It introduces a three-stage training pipeline—self-supervised MAE, semi-supervised hashtag classification, and CLIP-style contrastive learning—combined with a ViT+EViT architecture and an LLM-based text encoder (LLM2CLIP). The resulting model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on ImageNet (89.3% linear probe) and Kinetics (78.1%) while being four times more efficient than competitors. It also demonstrates superior robustness on out-of-distribution benchmarks and significant gains on internal real-world retrieval tasks.

## Method Summary
The core method involves curating a massive dataset of 25B visual–text pairs from social media, applying semantic balancing via WordNet resampling and noise suppression through similarity filtering. A three-stage training pipeline is employed: (1) Masked Autoencoding (MAE) with high masking ratios (75% images, 90% video) to learn generic visual features; (2) semi-supervised hashtag classification on a large label set (~22k hashtags) to anchor semantics; (3) CLIP-style contrastive learning to align visual and textual representations. The architecture uses a ViT backbone enhanced with EViT for token pruning (enabling 336px input at only 288 tokens) and Registers for outlier handling. For text encoding, either a standard CLIP encoder or an LLM (LLaMA-1B) via LLM2CLIP (with a frozen LLM and a 4-layer adapter) is used. Additional components include synthetic caption generation, a Siamese loss to balance caption and hashtag objectives, and a progressive resolution schedule. The model is trained jointly on images and videos, achieving high efficiency and strong generalization.

## Key Results
- ImageNet: 89.3% (linear probe); Kinetics: 78.1% (SOTA) with 4× efficiency (288 tokens @ 336px).
- Out-of-distribution robustness: +8.1% on ObjectNet.
- Real-world retrieval: +10.8% on cross-domain ads-to-reels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Systematic data curation from raw social media (100B+ samples) to balanced, high-quality image-text/video-hashtag pairs (25B total) provides the scale and semantic diversity necessary for learning robust, generalizable visual representations.
- **Mechanism:** Applying semantic balancing via WordNet synset-based resampling mitigates long-tail distribution, while similarity-based filtering (using a pre-trained MetaCLIP) and label smoothing suppress noise. This transforms noisy, imbalanced web data into a reliable supervision signal for large-scale contrastive and classification objectives.
- **Core assumption:** High-quality, semantically diverse data at sufficient scale is a primary driver of model generalization, outweighing architectural innovations when moving from academic to industrial-scale benchmarks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "curating 15B image-text and 10B video-hashtag pairs... incorporating balancing and noise suppression strategies to maximize semantic diversity while minimizing label noise."
  - [section 2.1]: Tables 1 & 2 directly quantify the impact of resampling (7.3% ImageNet LP gain) and filtering (1.1% gain).
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., "Scaling Vision Transformers to 22B Parameters", "OReole-FM") emphasize data scaling as a co-requisite to model scaling, aligning with the paper's central thesis.
- **Break condition:** If the semantic balancing (WordNet resampling) or noise suppression (similarity filtering) steps are omitted, the model's performance on OOD benchmarks (Table 13, 14) and internal retrieval tasks (Table 16, 17) would degrade significantly, as the training signal becomes dominated by head-class repeats and noisy pairs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The three-stage training pipeline (MAE → Semi-supervised Hashtag Classification → CLIP) bootstraps representation quality by first learning generic visual patterns, then aligning them with structured semantic labels, and finally with free-form language.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 (MAE) learns dense, reconstruction-oriented features without label noise. Stage 2 (hashtag classification) anchors these features to a large, curated label set (~22k hashtags for nouns), providing semantic stability. Stage 3 (CLIP) aligns the stabilized visual features with rich, diverse textual descriptions, enabling zero-shot and retrieval capabilities.
- **Core assumption:** Progressive curriculum from self-supervision to structured weak-supervision to free-form contrastive learning avoids the optimization instability and semantic collapse that can occur when training contrastively on raw, noisy web data from scratch.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "three-stage training pipeline that combines self-supervised MAE, semi-supervised hashtag classification, and CLIP-style contrastive learning."
  - [section 4.2]: Table 6 shows additive gains from each stage (MAE→URU: 87.1% IN LP → +CLIP: 87.3% → +SLIP: 87.8%).
  - [section 4.2.4]: The addition of SLIP (self-supervised contrastive) further strengthens features, validating the benefit of multi-objective stability.
- **Break condition:** Removing Stage 2 (hashtag classification) and training directly from MAE initialization to CLIP on raw social media captions would likely lead to poorer convergence and lower final performance, as the model would be exposed to high label noise from the start, destabilizing the contrastive alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The EViT token reorganization mechanism, combined with LLM2CLIP (LLaMA-1B as text encoder), enables state-of-the-art accuracy at drastically reduced computational cost (288 tokens @ 336px) and superior real-world robustness.
- **Mechanism:** EViT prunes low-attention tokens during forward passes, allowing processing of higher-resolution inputs (336px) with fewer total tokens (288), reducing FLOPs quadratically. LLM2CLIP replaces the small, context-limited CLIP text encoder with a large LM, providing richer linguistic context and better alignment for complex, in-the-wild queries, which is critical for internal retrieval tasks.
- **Core assumption:** Efficiency gains from token pruning do not come at the cost of discriminative power for downstream tasks, and the linguistic sophistication of LLMs provides a superior text alignment space for vision models deployed on diverse, user-generated content.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "ViT+EViT architecture... using only 288 tokens at 336px resolution—4x more efficient than competitors... LLM-based text encoder (LLM2CLIP)... significant gains on internal real-world retrieval tasks (+10.8% on cross-domain ads-to-reels)."
  - [section 3.2]: Table 4 shows EViT at 336px/0.5 keep rate achieves comparable/higher accuracy (87.38% LC) vs. baseline at 224px, with higher throughput.
  - [section 4.6.2]: Table 18 shows LLM2CLIP variants outperform standard CLIP on internal FB search and Reels-to-Ads metrics, despite similar COCO/MSCOCO scores.
- **Break condition:** Aggressive EViT token pruning (e.g., keep rate <<0.5) would cause a steep drop in accuracy on fine-grained benchmarks like ObjectNet or internal retrieval. Using the standard CLIP text encoder instead of LLM2CLIP would maintain academic benchmark performance but regress on real-world, linguistically complex retrieval tasks (evidenced by the +10.8% gap in Table 16).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Self-Supervised Masked Autoencoding (MAE)
  - *Why needed here:* Provides a stable, noise-robust initialization for the vision backbone before exposure to noisy hashtag and caption data. It learns general visual features without semantic collapse, which is foundational for the subsequent supervised stages.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a high masking ratio (75% images, 90% video) used in MAE pre-training for this architecture?
- **Concept:** Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)
  - *Why needed here:* Aligns the stabilized visual representation space with a text encoder space, enabling zero-shot generalization and cross-modal retrieval. The three-stage design defers this alignment until after feature quality is improved by MAE and hashtag classification.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the functional role of the temperature parameter in the CLIP contrastive loss, and how might it interact with the scale of the ViSE dataset?
- **Concept:** Token Pruning / Efficient Inference (EViT)
  - *Why needed here:* Allows the model to operate at higher input resolutions (336px) and process more data with fewer FLOPs, making 2B parameter models feasible at industrial scale. It directly addresses the quadratic cost of ViT token counts.
  - *Quick check question:* On what signal (e.g., attention maps from CLS token) does EViT decide which tokens to prune, and at which layers is this typically applied?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Input Pipeline:** Native resolution support (variable aspect ratio) → Augmentations (blur, color jitter) → Tokenization (3D for video, repeated frames for images).
  - **Backbone:** ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) enhanced with EViT (Liang et al., 2022) for token reorganization and Registers (Darcet et al., 2023) for outlier features.
  - **Text Encoder:** Standard CLIP encoder (small) or LLaMA-1B via LLM2CLIP (frozen, with 4-layer adapter).
  - **Training Heads:** Stage 1: MAE decoder (pixel reconstruction). Stage 2: Hashtag classification head (linear). Stage 3: Projection heads for contrastive loss.
  - **Losses:** L_MAE (L2), L_class (CE), L_CLIP (contrastive), L_SLIP (SimCLR-style), L_den (denoising on projected embeddings).
- **Critical path:**
  1.  **Data Ingestion & Curation:** Raw social media → Clean captions/hashtags → Semantic balancing (WordNet resampling) → Similarity filtering → Synthetic caption generation/LLM rewrite.
  2.  **Stage 1 MAE Pre-training:** Train encoder-decoder on masked patch reconstruction (75%/90% mask) using curated *unlabeled* image/video data.
  3.  **Stage 2 Hashtag Finetuning:** Freeze encoder, train classification head on URU-Noun-5B hashtag labels. Builds semantic anchors.
  4.  **Stage 3 CLIP Pre-training:** Unfreeze encoder, add projection heads. Train with contrastive loss on image/video-text pairs (ViSE + URU-Action-5B), optionally with LLM2CLIP text encoder, SLIP, and denoising losses.
  5.  **Resolution Scaling:** Apply progressive resolution schedule (98→154→224→336→448) during Stage 3.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Resolution vs. Token Count:** Higher resolution (336px vs. 224px) captures finer details but increases tokens. EViT's token pruning (keep rate) trades minor accuracy loss for major efficiency gains (4x QPS). The paper's SOTA is achieved at 336px/288 tokens.
  - **Image-Only vs. Joint Training:** Joint image-video training improves video performance (+1.5% Kinetics over pure video models) with minimal image regression (-1.2% vs. image-only), but requires careful batch sampling to prevent modality bias (section 4.1).
  - **Text Encoder:** Standard CLIP text encoder is simple and fast but has limited context/world knowledge. LLaMA-1B (LLM2CLIP) improves real-world retrieval (+10.8% on Reels-to-Ads) but increases memory footprint and requires LoRA/adapter tuning to align output spaces.
  - **Synthetic Captions:** Improve retrieval (+4% on MSR-VTT) but hurt video classification (-4% on Kinetics); siamese loss (1:1 synthetic:hashtag) recovers the loss.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Poor OOD/Real-World performance:** Model performs well on ImageNet but poorly on internal retrieval (Ads/Reels). Likely cause: training data lacks real-world domain diversity or LLM2CLIP was not used.
  - **Video underperformance:** Kinetics accuracy lags behind image performance. Possible cause: insufficient video data in Stage 3 (URU-Action), or Stage 2 hashtags lacked action concepts.
  - **Training instability in Stage 3:** Contrastive loss diverges. Possible cause: inadequate noise suppression in data, incorrect temperature scaling, or missing denoising loss for stability.
  - **Degradation with EViT:** Aggressive token pruning (keep rate <0.5) or incorrect layer selection causes sharp accuracy drops on fine-grained benchmarks (ObjectNet).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **MAE Reconstruction Sanity Check:** Train Stage 1 on a small subset (e.g., 1M images). Compute pixel MSE on a held-out set to verify the autoencoder learns to reconstruct, establishing baseline feature learning.
  2.  **Hashtag Classification Linear Probe:** After Stage 2, freeze the encoder and train a linear classifier on ImageNet. *Expected:* Should significantly outperform random initialization and approach MAE-only LP (Table 6: 87.1% for MAE→URU), confirming hashtag stage adds semantic grounding.
  3.  **Token Pruning Efficiency-Accuracy Curve:** For a fixed model (e.g., ViT-H), run inference at 336px with varying EViT keep rates (1.0, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3). Measure throughput (images/sec) and ImageNet LP accuracy. *Expected:* Should show the 0.5 keep rate tradeoff (Table 4: 87.38% LC at 23.87 img/sec) as the efficient operating point.
