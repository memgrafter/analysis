# A Probabilistic Framework for LLM-Based Model Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18266
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18266
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Recasts LLM-based model discovery as Bayesian inference over executable programs, introducing ModelSMC to discover accurate and mechanistically interpretable models via Sequential Monte Carlo sampling.

## Executive Summary
Current LLM-based scientific model discovery lacks a formal probabilistic foundation, obscuring assumptions and hindering theoretical analysis. This paper addresses this gap by formulating model discovery as Bayesian inference over executable simulator programs. It introduces ModelSMC, an algorithm that uses Sequential Monte Carlo to maintain a population of candidate models (particles), iteratively refining them with LLM proposals and weighting them by marginal likelihood estimates from simulation-based inference. Evaluated on synthetic and real-world tasks (SIR epidemiology, kidney aldosterone regulation, Hodgkin-Huxley neuroscience), ModelSMC matches or outperforms baselines in predictive metrics while yielding a posterior distribution over model structures that reveals both supported mechanisms and non-identifiable ambiguities.

## Method Summary
ModelSMC operates by approximating the posterior distribution \( p(m|x_o) \) over mechanistic models \( m \) given observed data \( x_o \). It maintains a pool of \( N \) particles (candidate model programs). Each iteration involves: (1) resampling particles proportionally to their current weights (estimates of \( p(x_o|m) \)); (2) propagating each particle: with high probability \( \alpha \) it is cloned unchanged (exploitation), otherwise the LLM generates a new model conditioned on the ancestor’s history and feedback (exploration); (3) weighting each new model by estimating its marginal likelihood via simulation-based inference (NLE or NLE-PFN), which trains a surrogate density estimator on simulated data; (4) generating LLM-based feedback on model performance to guide future proposals. The process concentrates probability mass on high-likelihood models while preserving diversity.

## Key Results
- ModelSMC discovers accurate and mechanistically interpretable models across three scientific domains.
- It matches or exceeds baseline methods (FunSearch+, single-particle ModelSMC) in negative log-likelihood metrics.
- The resulting posterior distribution reveals both supported mechanisms and non-identifiable ambiguities, providing richer insight than point estimates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reformulating LLM-based model discovery as Bayesian inference over executable programs yields a unified objective that clarifies assumptions and enables theoretical analysis.
- **Mechanism:** The target posterior \( p(m|x_o) \propto p(x_o|m)p(m) \) assigns probability proportional to the marginal likelihood of the model. ModelSMC uses SMC to approximate this posterior: particles (candidate models) are resampled proportionally to weights \( \propto p(x_o|m) \), then propagated via LLM proposals that incorporate ancestry and feedback, iteratively concentrating on high-likelihood regions.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM proposal distribution has support covering all models with non-zero posterior mass (Assumption: LLM can generate scientifically meaningful models). Additionally, the likelihood estimator \( p_\phi(x|m,\theta,c) \) accurately approximates the true simulator likelihood.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We recast model discovery as probabilistic inference, i.e., as sampling from an unknown distribution over mechanistic models capable of explaining the data."
  - [section] Sec. 3.2 defines the posterior \( p(m|x_o) \) and its factorization; Sec. 3.3 details the SMC steps that target this posterior.
  - [corpus] Corpus signals show multiple recent LLM-based discovery systems (e.g., BioLunar, MADE, BoxingGym) that use heuristic workflows without explicit probabilistic goals, underscoring ModelSMC’s novel formulation.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM cannot propose models from the true posterior support (e.g., due to limited code generation capabilities), or if the likelihood surrogate is severely mis-specified, the particle distribution will converge to an incorrect region, failing to discover the true model.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The SMC resampling–propagation cycle, with a mixture kernel (cloning probability \( \alpha \)), balances exploitation of high-weight models and exploration of new structures, mimicking iterative human refinement.
- **Mechanism:** Resampling selects ancestors with probability proportional to their likelihood-based weights. Propagation then generates new models: with probability \( \alpha \), the particle is cloned unchanged (exploitation); with probability \( 1-\alpha \), the LLM proposes a new model conditioned on the ancestor and contextual feedback (exploration). Gradually, ancestry accumulates high-weight models, guiding the LLM towards promising regions.
- **Core assumption:** The mixture kernel maintains diversity enough to avoid premature convergence; contextual feedback effectively steers the LLM towards model refinements that improve likelihood.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ModelSMC represents candidate models as particles which are iteratively proposed and refined by an LLM, and weighted using likelihood-based criteria."
  - [section] Algorithm 1 (lines 2–5) implements resampling and propagation with mixture; Sec. 3.3 discusses the role of \( \alpha \) and feedback.
  - [corpus] Corpus indicates that prior LLM-based systems (e.g., FunSearch) lack population-based resampling, leading to less controlled exploration; ModelSMC’s SMC foundation addresses this.
- **Break condition:** If \( \alpha \) is too high, the population collapses to a few clones and loses diversity; if too low, the algorithm may wander randomly. Poor feedback (e.g., misleading diagnostics) can misdirect proposals.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Simulation-based inference (specifically NLE/NLE-PFN) provides tractable marginal likelihood estimates for black-box simulators, enabling weighting without differentiable parameters.
- **Mechanism:** For each candidate model \( m \), we train or condition a surrogate density estimator \( p_\phi(x|\theta,c,m) \) on simulated data (\( \theta \) sampled from prior, \( x \) simulated). The marginal likelihood \( p(x_o|m) \) is then approximated by Monte Carlo integration over \( \theta \). This allows computing weights even when the simulator is non-differentiable and parameters are high-dimensional.
- **Core assumption:** The surrogate model captures the simulator’s conditional distribution sufficiently well; the simulation budget (number of \( \theta \) samples) is large enough for stable estimates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "weighted based on marginal likelihood estimates from simulation-based inference."
  - [section] Sec. 3.3, Weighting step (Eq. 4–5); Appendix D.2 details NLE and NLE-PFN.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows that many recent methods rely on direct simulation evaluation (e.g., MMD) rather than likelihood-based weighting; ModelSMC’s use of NLE fills this niche.
- **Break condition:** If the surrogate is mis-specified (e.g., fails to capture multimodality), the marginal likelihood estimates become biased, misguiding resampling. Insufficient simulations lead to high variance, causing weight fluctuations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Bayesian inference over models
  - **Why needed here:** Provides the probabilistic objective that unifies model proposal and selection; without it, there is no principled way to weigh competing mechanistic hypotheses.
  - **Quick check question:** If you have two candidate models \( M_1 \) and \( M_2 \), and observed data \( X \), how do you compute the posterior probability that \( M_1 \) is correct? (Answer: \( p(M_1|X) \propto p(X|M_1)p(M_1) \), where \( p(X|M_1) \) is the marginal likelihood integrating over model parameters.)
- **Concept:** Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)
  - **Why needed here:** Enables iterative approximation of high-dimensional discrete posterior through resampling and propagation; crucial for handling large, non-differentiable model spaces where gradient-based methods fail.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the three core steps of an SMC update? (Answer: 1. Resample particles according to weights. 2. Propagate each particle to generate a new state. 3. Weight the new particles by likelihood.)
- **Concept:** Simulation-based inference (likelihood-free)
  - **Why needed here:** Many scientific simulators lack tractable likelihood functions; SBI techniques like NLE allow approximating likelihoods using only simulation capability, making \( p(x|\theta) \) accessible.
  - **Quick check question:** How can you estimate \( p(x|\theta) \) if you can only sample from the simulator? (Answer: Train a conditional density estimator on pairs \( (\theta, x) \) generated by simulation; then use it as a surrogate.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  LLM proposal module → Particle pool ← Resampling engine → Feedback generator ↑
                              ↓
                  Likelihood estimator (NLE/NLE-PFN)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize with a seed model (weight=1).
  2. For iteration \( k=1..K \):
     a. Resample \( N \) ancestor indices from current weights.
     b. For each ancestor:
        - With prob \( \alpha \): clone ancestor’s model unchanged.
        - With prob \( 1-\alpha \): call LLM to generate new model \( m' \) conditioned on ancestor’s ancestry, feedback, and \( c_{\text{task}} \).
     c. For each new model \( m' \): simulate on contexts; compute weight via likelihood estimator (NLE); generate new feedback.
     d. Update particle pool with \( (m', \text{weight}, \text{feedback}) \).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Cloning probability \( \alpha \): Controls exploration vs exploitation; typical 0.7–0.8. Higher \( \alpha \) speeds convergence but risks getting stuck; lower \( \alpha \) increases diversity but may be slower.
  - Particle pool size \( N \): Larger \( N \) improves posterior approximation but increases simulation cost linearly. Tradeoff: \( N=50 \) common.
  - Likelihood estimator: NLE requires training at each iteration (costly) vs NLE-PFN (training-free but limited capacity). Choice affects both bias and compute.
  - Summary statistics: Using summaries reduces dimension for NLE but may discard information; full data preserves detail but increases estimator complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Weight collapse: All weight concentrates on one model early → reduce \( \alpha \) or increase \( N \).
  - No weight improvement over iterations → Likelihood estimator may be inadequate; check simulation budget or consider summary statistics.
  - High rate of invalid models (code errors) → Improve LLM prompt or feedback content; consider more robust parsing.
  - Oscillating weights → Resampling variance too high; may need to smooth weights (e.g., temperature \( \tau \)) or increase \( N \).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **LLM-free validation (Sec. 4.1):** Run ModelSMC on a finite set of 20 GMMs with no LLM proposal. Verify that weights concentrate on the true model, confirming SMC logic.
  2. **Synthetic SIR model (Sec. 4.2):** Discovery with LLM proposals on a known epidemiological model. Check that ModelSMC recovers correct dynamics and outperforms single-particle baseline.
  3. **Real-world kidney aldosterone regulation (Sec. 4.3):** Apply to limited clinical data; assess whether discovered models capture missing feedback mechanisms and improve posterior predictive checks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Under what conditions do automated LLM-based model discovery systems generate genuinely novel scientific insights rather than merely rediscover known mechanisms?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "A natural question is whether automated discovery systems generate genuinely novel scientific insights or primarily rediscover known mechanisms." (Discussion, §5)
- **Why unresolved:** The paper focuses on methodological contributions and does not systematically evaluate the novelty of discovered mechanisms across applications.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Cross-domain benchmark suites with ground-truth novel mechanisms, expert evaluations, and quantitative measures of mechanistic divergence from existing literature.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the computational cost of ModelSMC be significantly reduced while preserving its ability to discover accurate and interpretable models?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors identify computational expense due to repeated simulation and likelihood evaluation as a primary limitation and suggest multi-fidelity simulation, adaptive particle budgets, and early-rejection strategies as possible alleviations (§5, Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** These strategies are proposed but not implemented or evaluated; no empirical evidence exists on their effectiveness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementations of multi-fidelity or adaptive schemes showing order-of-magnitude speed-ups with maintained or improved posterior approximation quality on benchmark tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can uncertainty in surrogate likelihood estimates be incorporated into ModelSMC's weighting scheme to reduce bias from likelihood misspecification?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors note that "posterior weights rely on surrogate likelihoods, which may introduce bias under misspecification" and call for "uncertainty-aware or robust likelihood estimation" (§5, Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** Current weights treat likelihood surrogates as ground truth; uncertainty propagation is missing.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Bayesian neural density estimators (e.g., Bayesian NLE) providing posterior predictive distributions of likelihoods, integrated into importance weights, with demonstrated robustness in synthetic misspecification experiments.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can the LLM-based proposal distribution in ModelSMC be improved to ensure better coverage of the space of mechanistically plausible scientific models?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The text states that discovery is "bottlenecked by the LLM’s ability to propose relevant model structures" and suggests domain-specific pretraining or retrieval-augmented generation as enhancements (§5, Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** Prompt design and the generative distribution are largely heuristic; no systematic study of alternative proposal mechanisms exists.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative experiments with retrieval-augmented prompting, fine-tuned domain-specific LMs, or learned proposal networks; measure improvements in proposal diversity and success rate of discovering high-quality models.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What principled similarity metrics can be defined over executable model programs to enable geometric reasoning and clustering of the posterior?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors observe that "models remain high-dimensional programs without an explicit geometry" and that "developing notions of similarity or clustering over model space would enable richer posterior analysis" (§5, Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** No existing method translates program differences into a metric reflecting mechanistic similarity; posterior analysis currently relies only on scalar weights.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduction of similarity measures based on program semantics, dynamical behavior, or abstraction of mechanistic components; validation by recovering known model families and revealing posterior structure in real discovery tasks.

## Limitations
- **LLM proposal coverage:** Assumes LLM can generate all models with non-zero posterior; coverage may be limited for complex systems.
- **NLE approximation bias:** Surrogate may mis-specify multimodal likelihoods, especially with limited simulations, biasing resampling.
- **Computational cost:** Each iteration requires many simulations and likelihood evaluation; may not scale to high-fidelity or computationally intensive simulators.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical formulation as Bayesian inference:** High (well-defined mathematical objective).
- **SMC algorithm convergence properties:** Medium (rely on LLM proposal adequacy; no formal guarantees).
- **Empirical performance on synthetic/real tasks:** Medium (competitive results but few benchmarks; potential overfitting).
- **Ability to reveal non-identifiabilities:** Low (only anecdotal evidence; needs broader study).
