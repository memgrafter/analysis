# Do We Need Bigger Models for Science? Task-Aware Retrieval with Small Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01965
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01965
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A fine-tuned 3B-parameter model with task-aware retrieval approaches an 8B baseline on a scholarly QA benchmark but trails on domain-shifted and complex tasks, showing retrieval can partially compensate for smaller scale.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether sophisticated retrieval design can offset the use of smaller, more accessible language models in scientific applications, aiming to improve reproducibility and reduce reliance on large proprietary models. The authors introduce a task-aware retrieval-augmented framework that classifies scholarly queries into types (e.g., general QA, summarization, KG-facts) and routes them to specialized retrieval strategies combining full-text papers with a knowledge graph. Using a 3B-parameter model for generation, evaluations on scholarly QA (ScholarQABench-Multi), biomedical QA (PubMedQA), and text compression (SciTLDR) show the 3B model with retrieval achieves performance close to an 8B baseline on in-domain QA but underperforms larger models under domain shift and on summarization. The findings suggest that retrieval and model scale are complementary: careful retrieval helps smaller models on grounded tasks, but model capacity remains crucial for complex reasoning and cross-domain scenarios.

## Method Summary
The core method is a task-aware retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system for scholarly queries. A lightweight classifier (fine-tuned Llama 3.2 3B Instruct) routes each input query to one of four task-specific retrieval pipelines: General/Simplified QA, Summarization, or KG-Fact. Retrieval combines unstructured evidence from a vector index of 165K arXiv papers (unarXive) and structured metadata queries to the SemOpenAlex knowledge graph via SPARQL templates. The retrieved context is composed into a task-specific prompt and fed to a fine-tuned small language model (Llama 3.1/3.2 3B Instruct), which generates an answer with inline citations. Models are fine-tuned using SFT on OpenScholar data with configurations varying LoRA/full fine-tuning, context length, and epochs.

## Key Results
- On the multi-paper scholarly QA benchmark (ScholarQABench-Multi), the fine-tuned 3B model with retrieval achieves a mean quality score of 4.00/5.0 and 24.53% citation F1, approaching the 8B Instruct baseline (4.04 mean).
- Under domain shift on PubMedQA (biomedical), the best 3B model reaches 68.0% accuracy in a retrieval setting, outperforming its base version (62.4%) but trailing the 8B model (75.6%).
- For text compression (SciTLDR), ROUGE-L F1 improves only modestly to 1.30 with retrieval, indicating limited benefit from the approach on this task.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Task-Aware Routing Reduces Retrieval Noise
A classifier predicts a task label for each query, which deterministically selects a specialized retrieval pipeline (e.g., vector search for QA, SPARQL templates for KG-Fact). This matches the retrieval method to the inferred information need, avoiding a one-size-fits-all approach that would yield irrelevant context. **Core assumption:** Scholarly queries have distinct intents that map cleanly to a small set of retrieval modalities. **Evidence:** The paper describes the four categories and routing mechanism as reducing unnecessary noise. **Break condition:** Misclassification leads to misaligned retrieval and degraded answers.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Retrieval Grounds Responses in Complementary Evidence Sources
The system retrieves from both unstructured full-text (unarXive) and structured scholarly metadata (SemOpenAlex KG). This union of evidence sources allows answering narrative questions with text passages while handling precise factual queries (e.g., author affiliations, metrics) via SPARQL. **Core assumption:** Scholarly knowledge bifurcates into narrative text and structured metadata. **Evidence:** The framework explicitly integrates both data sources, with task-dependent usage. **Break condition:** Semantic misalignment between the text corpus and KG (e.g., entity resolution failures) yields contradictory or incomplete evidence.

### Mechanism 3: Compact Model Capacity is Conserved for Synthesis, Not Memorization
A small (3B) instruction-tuned model, when fine-tuned and provided with high-quality retrieved context, can approach larger models on grounded tasks because its limited parameters focus on synthesizing and reasoning over supplied evidence rather than memorizing facts. **Core assumption:** Model scale is primarily needed for storing factual knowledge; external retrieval liberates capacity for context processing. **Evidence:** On ScholarQABench-Multi with retrieval, the fine-tuned 3B nearly matches the 8B baseline, while without retrieval both perform poorly. **Break condition:** On tasks requiring multi-hop reasoning, complex inference, or under severe domain shift (PubMedQA results), parametric limitations become a hard barrier that retrieval cannot fully overcome.

  *Why needed:* The entire framework relies on the LLM conditioning on retrieved evidence rather than recalling facts, central to the hypothesis that retrieval can compensate for smaller model scale.

  *Why needed:* The system’s novelty lies in pre-retrieval classification; understanding why a uniform retriever is suboptimal for heterogeneous scholarly queries is key.

  *Why needed:* The KG-Fact pathway uses template-based mapping from natural language to structured queries, a constrained method for accessing precise scholarly metadata.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a template-based KG approach (vs. a learned semantic parser) be preferable in a low-resource, high-precision scholarly setting?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
User Query → Task Classifier (fine-tuned Llama 3.2 3B Instruct) → Conditional Retrieval Subsystems (QA/Simpl/Summ: FAISS + Semantic Scholar; KG-Fact: rule-based + SPARQL templates) → Prompt Composer (task template + evidence + query) → Generator (fine-tuned Llama 3.1/3.2 3B Instruct) → Cited Answer.

**Critical path:** Query classification determines retrieval strategy; hybrid evidence is assembled into a prompt; the small generator synthesizes a cited response.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Routing accuracy vs. simplicity: complex classifiers may route better but add latency; simpler rules are interpretable but brittle.  
- Retrieval recall vs. precision: short chunks increase precision but may miss context; long contexts overload small models.  
- Fine-tuning data bias: training on OpenScholar multi-paper QA may bias performance and hurt generalization (e.g., zero-context PubMedQA).

**Failure signatures:**  
- System-level: High rate of "I don't know" answers → likely poor retrieval or misrouting.  
- Citation errors: Generated citations don’t correspond to any retrieved passage → generator not following instruction.  
- Task misclassification: Queries for author metrics routed to text QA → no/incorrect answer.  
- Domain mismatch: Performance collapse on PubMedQA → corpus (unarXive: CS/physics) lacks biomedical coverage.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Routing Validation:** Manually label 200 diverse scholarly queries; measure classifier accuracy and confusion matrix against the 4-category taxonomy.  
2. **Retrieval Isolation Test:** Compare the full pipeline with and without the retrieval step on a fixed QA set; quantify drop in citation F1 and answer quality to isolate retrieval’s contribution.  
3. **Context Length Sensitivity:** For a fixed query and retrieved set, truncate evidence to varying token lengths (1k, 2k, 4k); plot answer quality vs. context length to identify optimal window for the small generator.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can standardized evaluation benchmarks for question answering over large scholarly knowledge graphs (e.g., SemOpenAlex) be constructed to enable systematic progress?** A benchmark with validated SPARQL-question-answer triples covering common and edge-case patterns is needed but labor-intensive to create.
- **How can retrieval quality be improved to minimize irrelevant or noisy context passages that disproportionately degrade the performance of small language models?** Current embedding models may not capture domain-specific semantics; lightweight rerankers tuned for scientific corpora could help but require development and validation.
- **How can post-generation verification mechanisms be integrated to ensure that generated citations are truly supported by the retrieved evidence?** A lightweight fact-checking module cross-referencing statements against passages could reduce hallucinations but adds overhead; optimal design is unknown.
- **Under what specific task characteristics (e.g., reasoning depth, domain shift magnitude, multi-document synthesis complexity) does retrieval cease to compensate for smaller model capacity, making larger models necessary?** The study lacks systematic variation of task complexity and domain distance, leaving thresholds unclear.
- **Can task-aware routing be made adaptive, moving beyond a fixed taxonomy and rule-based classification, to better handle ambiguous or novel scholarly query types?** A dynamic mechanism learning latent task clusters from query embeddings or hierarchies could improve robustness but remains unevaluated.

## Limitations
- Generalizability limited to unarXive/SemOpenAlex; other domains may perform worse.
- Fixed four-category task taxonomy not empirically validated; misrouting risk not quantified.
- No direct ablation of task-aware routing vs. uniform retrieval; routing’s isolated contribution unknown.
- Significant domain shift observed on PubMedQA (3B 68% vs 8B 75.6%), limiting cross-domain claims.
- Reproduction hindered by missing details on training data splits, exact prompt templates, and classifier confidence threshold.

## Confidence
- **Task-aware retrieval compensates for smaller models:** High (3B with retrieval ≈ 8B on ScholarQA).
- **Hybrid retrieval improves grounding:** Medium (system integrates text and KG but lacks controlled comparison).
- **Routing reduces noise:** Medium (depends on classifier accuracy, which is unreported).
- **Model scale essential for complex/domain-shifted tasks:** High (consistent performance gap on PubMedQA and SciTLDR).
