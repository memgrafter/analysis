# Measuring What Matters -- or What's Convenient?: Robustness of LLM-Based Scoring Systems to Construct-Irrelevant Factors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25674
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25674
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** The LLM-based scoring system showed robustness to meaningless text padding, moderate spelling errors, and reading level adjustments, but heavily penalized off-topic responses and surprisingly lowered scores when responses were duplicated.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the robustness of an LLM-based automated scoring system for situational judgment tests (SJTs) against construct-irrelevant factors. Testing on 545 altered responses, the system proved resilient to padding with non-content text, spelling errors (up to 30% error rate), and reading level changes, indicating strong semantic focus. However, off-topic responses were severely penalized, and full response duplication unexpectedly reduced scores, contrasting with prior non-LLM systems. The findings suggest the system effectively targets intended competencies but may be sensitive to specific irrelevant factors.

## Method Summary
The study used a dual-architecture LLM-as-a-Judge system: an LLM (GPT-5.X) extracted interpretable, competency-related features from each response, which were then input to a clear-box regression model trained on human ratings to produce a 1–5 score. The system was applied to a dataset of 545 SJT responses spanning 30 items and four competencies. Robustness was tested by altering responses in controlled ways: appending meaningless text (duplication, competency statements, scenario rephrasing, formulaic sentences), introducing spelling errors at various character error rates, adjusting reading levels via LLM rephrasing, and pairing responses with off-topic items (different competency or same competency). Score changes were measured using paired Cohen’s d against original scores.

## Key Results
- Padding with meaningless text (competency statements, scenario rephrasing) had negligible effect on scores (|d|≤0.01), but duplicating entire responses decreased scores (d=-0.24).
- Spelling errors minimally impacted scores until exceeding 30% character error rate (d≤-0.11 at 5–30% CER).
- Adjusting reading level (up to 6 grade levels) produced small score changes (d≤0.10).
- Off-topic responses were heavily penalized: scores dropped by 0.92 points (d=-1.33) when matched to a different competency, and by 0.47 points (d=-0.62) when matched to a different item of the same competency.
- The system reliably distinguished between competencies, suggesting effective construct relevance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Semantic Focus Enables Noise Robustness
The system's robustness to meaningless padding and moderate spelling errors stems from its dual architecture. An LLM extracts semantic features (e.g., evidence of collaboration, problem-solving) from responses, ignoring surface-level text. A regression model, trained on human scores, weights only competency-relevant features. This design allows the system to disregard non-content additions and decode partially garbled text. Evidence includes negligible score changes from added meaningless text (|d|≤0.01) and spelling errors up to 30% CER (d≤-0.11). Robustness fails when text becomes semantically incoherent (CER >30–40%) or when padding accidentally matches high-value features (e.g., formulaic phrases).

### Mechanism 2: Item-Specific Feature Extraction Penalizes Off-Topic Content
Off-topic responses receive low scores because feature extraction is tightly coupled to each item’s scenario and competency. The LLM is prompted to look for specific behaviors (e.g., "suggesting approaches to managing differences" for a collaboration item). A response that does not address the scenario fails to activate these item-specific features, resulting in low feature values and thus a low score. Evidence: off-topic responses matched to a different competency saw scores drop by 0.92 points (d=-1.33); even same-competency off-topic responses scored lower (d=-0.62), confirming item-specificity.

### Mechanism 3: Duplication Penalty Reflects Depth Assessment
Pure duplication reduces scores (d=-0.24), contrary to earlier non-LLM systems, because the scoring model rewards *original, relevant* content contribution. The feature extraction likely assigns positive weight to indicators of idea generation or depth. Duplication provides no new evidence for these features, effectively diluting the density of positive features per token or triggering a verbosity penalty. This indicates the system evaluates variety and relevance, not just keyword matches or length.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Processor -> Competency/Item Registry (lookup) -> LLM Feature Extractor (item-specific prompt) -> Feature Vector -> Item-specific Regression Model -> Predicted Score.

**Critical path:** Item ID + Response Text -> Retrieve prompt and model from registry -> LLM call with prompt -> Feature vector -> Regression model -> Score.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Robustness vs. Sensitivity: LLM provides noise robustness but may miss subtle cues.
- Interpretability vs. Cost: Feature extraction yields explainability but requires maintaining 30+ prompts and models.
- Construct Specificity vs. Generalization: Item-specific models maximize relevance but need more training data and don’t transfer.

**Failure signatures:**
- CIV Leakage: Scores correlate with response length or readability in validation.
- Off-Topic Blind Spot: System gives moderate scores to gibberish that accidentally triggers high-weight features.
- Duplication Blindness (opposite of finding): If scores inflated for duplicated text, it would indicate length-based proxy.
- Formulaic Phrase Gaming: Consistent use of specific high-scoring phrases across responses.

**First 3 experiments for new engineer:**
1. **Baseline Replication:** Rescore the 545 unaltered responses. Confirm mean ~3.11, SD ~1.17, distribution match.
2. **Minimal Perturbation Test:** Append a single neutral sentence (e.g., "This is my answer.") to 50 random responses. Expect |d| < 0.05.
3. **Competency Mismatch Test:** For 20 items, feed a high-scoring response from Item A to the scoring pipeline for a different Item B (ideally different competency). Expect score drop of ~1.0.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. To what extent do the robustness findings generalize to other LLM-based scoring systems and assessments beyond the studied SJT? The study's scope is limited to one dual-architecture system on one SJT; different architectures or constructs may yield different results.
2. How do formulaic phrases quantitatively affect scoring outcomes when used in varying frequencies and combinations? Only one formulaic phrase was tested; the broader impact of common formulaic expressions remains unknown.
3. Is the observed robustness to spelling errors and reading level adjustments consistent across LLMs of varying capabilities? The study used advanced GPT-5.X models; less capable models may show different robustness.
4. How can automated scoring systems reliably flag "unusual" responses (e.g., off-topic, adversarial inputs) to supplement score-based penalties? The system penalizes but does not flag off-topic responses; anomaly detection methods are needed.

## Limitations
- **Critical unknowns** block full replication: exact LLM prompts, model identifiers, regression details, and alteration implementations are unreported.
- **Sampling ambiguity**: Bin edges for selecting the 545 responses are unreported, risking sample mismatch.
- **Generalizability limits**: Findings apply only to this specific SJT, item-specific prompts, and GPT-5.X; behavior may differ with other LLMs or item types.

## Confidence
- Robustness to meaningless padding and moderate spelling errors: Medium (supported by effect sizes but dependent on unrevealed details).
- Heavy penalty for off-topic responses: High (large effect sizes and item-specificity tests provide strong evidence).
- Duplication penalty: Low (counterintuitive finding contradicts prior non-LLM research; requires independent verification).
