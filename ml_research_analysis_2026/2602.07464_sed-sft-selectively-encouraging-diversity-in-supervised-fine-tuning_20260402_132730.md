# SED-SFT: Selectively Encouraging Diversity in Supervised Fine-Tuning
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.07464
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07464
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** Selectively regularizing token diversity during SFT mitigates mode collapse and improves downstream RL performance by 1.20–2.06 points on average across eight math benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Cross-Entropy loss induces severe mode collapse, concentrating the model's probability mass on limited reasoning paths and harming the subsequent Reinforcement Learning (RL) phase's exploration efficiency. SED-SFT addresses this by introducing a selective entropy regularization term and a token-level masking mechanism. It encourages diversity only for tokens identified as having adequate exploration space (via cumulative top-k probability), preserving accuracy where alternatives are scarce. On eight mathematical benchmarks within an SFT-then-RL pipeline, SED-SFT yields consistent RL performance gains for both Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, while measurably increasing generation diversity with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
SED-SFT modifies the standard SFT loss by adding a token-specific entropy regularization term that is selectively applied. For each token position *t* in the target sequence, the method computes the cumulative probability of the *k* most likely tokens (*P*<sub>Top‑k</sub>(*t*)). A binary mask *M*<sub>*t*</sub> is then set to 1 if *P*<sub>Top‑k</sub>(*t*) falls below a threshold τ (the (1−*r*)-th quantile of all such values from a calibration subset), indicating sufficient exploration space; otherwise *M*<sub>*t*</sub> = 0. The final loss combines standard Cross-Entropy (CE) with a quadratic Diversity Encouragement (LDE) term: *L*<sub>SED‑SFT</sub> = Σ<sub>*t*</sub> [−log π<sub>θ</sub>(*y*<sub>*t*</sub><sup>*</sup>) + λ · *M*<sub>*t*</sub> · (*p* − 0.5)²], where *p* is the model's probability for the ground-truth token. This selectively flattens the distribution toward *p*=0.5 for masked tokens, enriching the set of valid reasoning paths without sacrificing accuracy on constrained tokens.

## Key Results
- SED-SFT improves average downstream RL performance by **2.06 points** for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and **1.20 points** for Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct over standard CE-based baselines.
- It significantly **increases generation diversity**, evidenced by lower Self-BLEU scores compared to CE and other diversity-promoting methods.
- The approach adds **negligible computational overhead** beyond standard SFT.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Selective Masking Preserves Accuracy
**Claim:** The masking mechanism (*M*<sub>*t*</sub>) prevents diversity encouragement on tokens with limited exploration space, preserving accuracy where alternative paths are scarce.  
**Mechanism:** For each token position *t*, compute cumulative probability of the top‑k tokens (*P*<sub>Top‑k</sub>(*t*)). If *P*<sub>Top‑k</sub>(*t*) < τ (where τ is the (1−*r*)-th quantile of all *P*<sub>Top‑k</sub> values over a calibration set), then *M*<sub>*t*</sub>=1; else *M*<sub>*t*</sub>=0. This masks tokens that already have high confidence (low exploration space).  
**Core assumption:** Tokens with high confidence (e.g., fixed structural tokens) offer little opportunity for alternative reasoning paths; pushing their probability away from the target harms accuracy more than it helps diversity.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states selective masking "encourages diversity only for tokens with adequate exploration space." Section 2.2 notes tokens with restricted space exhibit higher prediction confidence. Neighboring paper 66597 uses selective token updates, corroborating selective intervention.  
**Break condition:** If the cumulative top‑k metric fails to correctly identify restricted tokens (due to noisy/miscalibrated predictions), the mask may incorrectly suppress diversity where needed or allow it where harmful, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2: Entropy Regularization Increases Diversity
**Claim:** The entropy regularization term LDE(*p*) increases diversity for unmasked tokens by flattening their probability distribution toward maximum entropy.  
**Mechanism:** For each token with *M*<sub>*t*</sub>=1, compute LDE(*p*) = (*p* − 0.5)² where *p* = π<sub>θ</sub>(*y*<sub>*t*</sub><sup>*</sup> | …). Minimizing this quadratic penalty encourages *p* to approach 0.5, moving probability mass from the ground‑truth token to others.  
**Core assumption:** For tokens with sufficient exploration space, a more uniform distribution over plausible alternatives does not sacrifice correctness but enriches reasoning paths for subsequent RL.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes "selective entropy regularization term … encourages diversity only for tokens with adequate exploration space." Section 3 defines LDE and its minimum at *p*=0.5. Section 5.1 shows empirical Self‑BLEU reduction. Neighboring paper 84486 studies entropy preservation in SFT, aligning with the diversity goal.  
**Break condition:** If applied to tokens where accuracy is critical due to masking failure, LDE can force ground‑truth probability too low, causing factual errors and harming RL performance.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy Balances Diversity‑Accuracy Trade‑off
**Claim:** The synergy between selective masking and entropy regularization yields a balanced trade‑off that improves downstream RL performance.  
**Mechanism:** The combined loss *L*<sub>SED‑SFT</sub> = Σ<sub>*t*</sub> [−log π<sub>θ</sub>(*y*<sub>*t*</sub><sup>*</sup>) + λ·*M*<sub>*t*</sub>·LDE(π<sub>θ</sub>(*y*<sub>*t*</sub><sup>*</sup>))] extends CE with a diversity boost only where allowed by *M*<sub>*t*</sub>, avoiding CE's mode collapse and over‑diversification of methods like GEM.  
**Core assumption:** RL algorithms benefit from a diverse set of initial reasoning paths; by slightly flattening distributions in flexible regions, SED‑SFT creates a richer exploration space that RL can optimize more efficiently.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports RL improvements of 2.06 and 1.20 points. Section 4.4 (Table 1) shows SED‑SFT outperforms CE and other baselines on eight benchmarks after RL. Section 5.1 (Table 2) confirms lower Self‑BLEU than CE and DFT. Neighboring papers 3182 and 83243 discuss SFT‑RL inconsistency and distributional collapse, highlighting the need for such methods.  
**Break condition:** Poor choice of hyperparameters (*k*, *r*, λ) can tip the balance toward excessive diversity (hurting accuracy) or insufficient diversity (no RL benefit). Sensitivity tests (Table 3) show performance varies significantly with *r* and *k*.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cross‑Entropy Loss in Supervised Fine‑Tuning**  
  *Why needed:* It is the baseline method that induces mode collapse; understanding its effect on probability distributions is essential.  
  *Quick check:* What does minimizing cross‑entropy encourage about the model’s predicted probability for the target token at each position? (Answer: It pushes it toward 1.0.)

- **Concept: Token‑level Exploration Space**  
  *Why needed:* SED‑SFT’s core innovation is to assess and selectively act on tokens based on their exploration potential.  
  *Quick check:* How is the cumulative top‑k probability used to estimate the exploration space for a token? (Answer: It sums the probabilities of the *k* most likely tokens; a low sum indicates a concentrated distribution and thus limited exploration space.)

- **Concept: Entropy as a Measure of Diversity**  
  *Why needed:* The LDE term directly manipulates entropy; familiarity with entropy’s role in exploration is important.  
  *Quick check:* For a binary distribution with probabilities *p* and 1−*p*, why is *p*=0.5 the point of maximum entropy? (Answer: Because entropy = −*p* log *p* − (1−*p*) log(1−*p*) is maximized when *p*=0.5.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
DataLoader -> Model πθ -> Forward Pass -> Cumulative Top‑k Calculator -> Quantile Threshold τ -> Mask Generator *M*<sub>*t*</sub> -> Loss Aggregator (CE + λ·*M*<sub>*t*</sub>·LDE) -> Optimizer

### Critical path
For each batch: (1) run model to get token probabilities; (2) compute *P*<sub>Top‑k</sub>(*t*) for each token; (3) compute *M*<sub>*t*</sub> using precomputed τ; (4) compute per‑token CE and LDE; (5) sum for total loss; (6) backpropagate; (7) optimizer step.

### Design tradeoffs
- *k*: Smaller (e.g., 2) is more discriminative but noisy; larger (e.g., 5) smoothes but loses distinction. Typical: 2 or 3.
- *r*: Controls masking aggressiveness. Higher *r* (e.g., 0.7) masks fewer tokens (only very low *P*<sub>Top‑k</sub>), leading to conservative diversity. Lower *r* includes more tokens, increasing diversity risk.
- λ: Weight on LDE; paper fixes λ=1. Adjusting λ scales diversity encouragement.
- Overhead: Top‑k computation adds moderate cost; can be optimized with batched GPU ops. Masking step is negligible.

### Failure signatures
- No Self‑BLEU improvement: Mask may be all zeros → check τ (perhaps too high) or *k* too large.
- SFT CE loss spikes: LDE may be applied to too many tokens → check *r* (too low) or mask calculation bug.
- RL performance worse than CE: Possibly due to incorrect threshold or λ; verify mask ratio on training data matches expectation (e.g., ~30–50% masked).
- Training instability: Monitor *p* distributions for unmasked tokens; if they converge to exactly 0.5, consider reducing λ.

### First 3 experiments
1. Baseline: Train SFT with CE loss only, then RL; record Self‑BLEU and final RL scores as reference.
2. SED‑SFT with default hyperparameters (*k*=2, *r*=0.7, λ=1) from the paper; compare diversity (Self‑BLEU) and RL performance.
3. Ablation: SED‑SFT without masking (*M*<sub>*t*</sub>=1 for all tokens) to isolate the effect of selective masking; expect lower accuracy and possibly lower RL gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How does the optimal value of *k* in the cumulative top‑k probability metric vary across different model architectures and task domains, and what are the underlying factors that determine this variation?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper selects *k*=2 or 3 based on analysis in Section 2.3 and sensitivity tests in Table 3, but only evaluates on two models and mathematical tasks. The trade‑off between discriminative power and informativeness is noted.  
**Why unresolved:** The study is limited to math benchmarks; token distribution and exploration space characteristics likely differ in other domains (e.g., dialogue, code), potentially requiring different *k* values for effective masking.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments across diverse model scales (e.g., small to large LLMs) and multiple task domains (mathematics, conversational AI, creative writing) to measure performance (RL improvement, diversity metrics) as a function of *k*. Analysis of token probability distributions in these domains could identify patterns.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Can SED‑SFT's selective masking strategy, designed for mathematical reasoning tasks, be effectively adapted to domains where the notion of "restricted exploration space" (e.g., fixed conjunctions) is less prevalent, and how should the masking criteria be redefined?  
**Basis in paper:** The masking strategy targets tokens with high confidence and limited alternatives, justified by math problems having structural tokens. However, experiments are confined to math benchmarks (Section 4.1), and the assumption may not hold for open‑ended generation.  
**Why unresolved:** In domains like story generation or open‑domain QA, most tokens may have flexible alternatives, so masking based on top‑k probability might incorrectly suppress diversity where it's beneficial. The paper does not test such scenarios.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Apply SED‑SFT to non‑math tasks (e.g., summarization, dialogue) with adapted masking criteria (e.g., based on semantic entropy or part‑of‑speech). Compare generation diversity and task‑specific accuracy against baselines to validate adaptability.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** What is the joint effect of the entropy regularization weight λ and masking ratio *r* on the diversity‑accuracy trade‑off in SED‑SFT, and is there a principled method to set these hyperparameters optimally rather than through grid search?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper fixes λ=1 and varies *r* in sensitivity studies (Table 3), but does not explore λ or the interaction. Hyperparameters are tuned empirically without theoretical guidance.  
**Why unresolved:** The loss function combines two terms with hyperparameters; their interplay likely depends on data distribution and model capacity. Without understanding this, applying SED‑SFT to new settings requires costly tuning.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a controlled study varying both λ and *r* across multiple datasets, analyzing performance surfaces. Develop a heuristic or automated method (e.g., based on validation diversity metrics) to set them, possibly using token‑level uncertainty statistics.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Does the performance improvement from SED‑SFT in subsequent RL phases primarily result from enhanced exploration during RL training (e.g., higher policy entropy early on) or from a better initial policy that guides RL more effectively?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper claims SED‑SFT enhances diversity which boosts RL exploration efficiency, but only reports final RL scores (Table 1). No analysis of RL training dynamics or exploration metrics is provided.  
**Why unresolved:** Without tracking exploration during RL (e.g., entropy of sampled trajectories, novelty of solutions), it's unclear whether SED‑SFT's role is to provide a diverse starting point or to sustain exploration throughout RL. This distinction affects how to best integrate SED‑SFT with RL algorithms.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Run RL training with detailed logging: measure policy entropy, visit counts of reasoning paths, and learning curves for SED‑SFT vs. CE SFT. Use ablation studies to correlate initial diversity with early RL exploration and final performance.

## Limitations
- The masking threshold τ is computed from a static calibration subset; its update frequency (e.g., per epoch vs. once) is unspecified, potentially affecting stability.
- The definition of cumulative top‑k probability is ambiguous regarding whether the ground‑truth token is included if it ranks among the top‑k, which could alter masking behavior.
- The method's effectiveness is demonstrated only on mathematical reasoning tasks; its generalizability to other domains (e.g., open‑ended generation) is unknown.

## Confidence
- **RL performance improvement:** High (empirical results on eight benchmarks support).
- **Selective masking mechanism:** Medium (theoretical rationale and neighbor studies support, but break conditions exist).
- **Entropy regularization benefit:** Medium (diversity increase confirmed, but risk of over‑flattening if masking fails).
- **Synergy/balanced trade‑off:** Medium (observed in experiments, yet sensitive to hyperparameters).
