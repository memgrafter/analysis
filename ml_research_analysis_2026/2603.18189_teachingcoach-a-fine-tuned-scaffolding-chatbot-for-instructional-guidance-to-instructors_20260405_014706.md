# TeachingCoach: A Fine-Tuned Scaffolding Chatbot for Instructional Guidance to Instructors

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.18189
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18189
*   **Reference Count:** 24
*   **Primary Result:** A fine-tuned LLaMA-2-13B model, trained on synthetic pedagogical dialogues, significantly outperforms a GPT-4o mini baseline on expert-rated clarity, tone, encouragement of reflection, and acknowledgment, but reveals a trade-off where the baseline is preferred for efficiency while the fine-tuned model is credited with deeper learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents "TeachingCoach," a chatbot designed to provide scalable, pedagogically grounded instructional guidance to higher education instructors. The core innovation is a data-centric pipeline: it extracts 36 instructional rules from educational resources, uses GPT-4o to generate synthetic multi-turn dialogues aligned to those rules, and fine-tunes a LLaMA-2-13B model to produce step-scaffolded guidance (problem identification, diagnosis, strategy). Expert evaluations show the fine-tuned model surpasses a GPT-4o mini baseline on key pedagogical interaction metrics, though a user study uncovers a trade-off between interaction depth and efficiency.

## Method Summary
The method involves a three-phase pipeline. First, human experts extract 36 core instructional rules from authoritative texts and encode them as structured system prompts keyed to phases (Problem Identification, Explore Reasons, Develop Strategies). Second, these rules, combined with instructor profiles and teaching challenges, condition GPT-4o to generate 20–30 turn synthetic dialogues simulating instructional consultations. These dialogues undergo expert filtering for consistency and soundness, yielding a dataset of ~406k training examples. Third, LLaMA-2-13B-Chat is fine-tuned via full-parameter updates on this dataset. The training objective forces the model to first predict a step tag (`<step>Step m</step>`) and then generate a response, aligning its output with the pedagogical scaffold.

## Key Results
*   **Expert Evaluation:** TeachingCoach significantly outperforms GPT-4o mini on clarity (2.62 vs 2.01), respectful tone (2.55 vs 1.99), encouragement of reflection (2.59 vs 1.68), and acknowledgment of input (2.56 vs 1.85) on a 3-point scale across 200 dialogues.
*   **User Study Trade-off:** Among 41 instructors, the GPT-4o mini baseline is more often preferred for efficiency and breadth of suggestions (21 vs 13), while the fine-tuned TeachingCoach is more often credited with facilitating deeper learning (18 vs 13).
*   **System Design:** The fine-tuned model successfully internalizes the step-wise scaffolding structure, producing concise, supportive, and actionable guidance within each defined instructional phase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Rule-Grounded Pedagogy
Extracting and encoding 36 instructional rules from authoritative resources grounds the chatbot's responses in evidence-based pedagogy. These rules are structured as system prompts keyed to instructional phases, constraining responses to be pedagogically sound and phase-appropriate during both training data generation and inference. The core assumption is that a finite, curated set of principles can effectively scaffold diverse instructional challenges.

### Mechanism 2: Scalable Synthetic Data
Generating multi-turn dialogues with GPT-4o, conditioned on the rules, instructor profiles, and challenges, creates a scalable, high-quality training dataset that captures realistic pedagogical scaffolding. Expert filtering ensures consistency and soundness. The assumption is that a powerful LLM can generate dialectically coherent and pedagogically valid dialogues when precisely prompted, and this synthetic data is sufficient for fine-tuning a smaller model.

### Mechanism 3: Step-Aware Fine-Tuning
Fine-tuning LLaMA-2-13B on the curated dataset trains the model to implicitly learn the instructional step structure and generate phase-appropriate responses. The training objective explicitly optimizes for predicting the step token followed by the response, forcing alignment with the pedagogical scaffold. This assumes a smaller, open-weight model can internalize the reasoning and style from synthetic conversations, achieving superior performance on targeted metrics compared to a general-purpose baseline.

## Architecture Onboarding
1. Rule Extractor -> 2. Synthetic Data Generator -> 3. Data Curator/Filters -> 4. Fine-Tuning Engine -> 5. Deployment Server -> 6. Frontend

**Critical Path:** Rule Extraction -> Synthetic Generation -> Filtering -> Fine-Tuning -> Deployment -> User Interaction. Errors in rule definition or filtering propagate to model performance.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   Depth vs. Efficiency: The fine-tuned model prioritizes reflective, step-wise dialogue (depth), while the baseline offers rapid, broad suggestions (efficiency).
*   Synthetic vs. Human Data: Gains in scalability and privacy come at a potential cost of conversational authenticity and edge-case coverage.
*   Open-weight vs. Proprietary: Using LLaMA-2 allows full supervision and customization but may sacrifice raw capability compared to closed models.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   Step bleed: Giving strategy advice during problem identification.
*   Rule drift: Responses becoming generic and no longer reflecting extracted principles.
*   Inefficient dialogue: Conversations exceeding 30 turns without converging.
*   Low expert metrics: Scores on clarity, tone, reflection, or acknowledgment dropping below baseline.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Step Prediction Accuracy:** Manually annotate 100 model turns. Target: >85% accuracy against gold step labels.
2.  **Rule Adherence Probe:** For each rule, craft a scenario and check for correct rule citation in the correct step. Target: >75% activation rate.
3.  **Depth-Efficiency Balance A/B Test:** With 20 instructors, measure average turns to a strategy and perceived learning gain for TeachingCoach vs. baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  How can the depth-efficiency trade-off be systematically balanced in a single instructional chatbot system?
2.  Does step-aware, pedagogically scaffolded dialogue translate into measurable improvements in actual classroom teaching practices or student learning outcomes?
3.  To what extent does reliance on fully synthetic, LLM-generated training data affect the authenticity, cultural sensitivity, and generalizability of guidance compared to models trained on human dialogues?
4.  How robust is the step-aware training formulation to different pedagogical scaffolds or instructional design frameworks beyond the three-step model used?
5.  Can a multi-agent architecture effectively combine breadth-oriented and depth-oriented support in a seamless, user-controlled manner?

## Limitations
*   **Pedagogical Rule Set:** The exact 36 rules, their wording, and precise encoding are unpublished, making reproduction reliant on reverse-engineering.
*   **Synthetic Data Generation:** Specific GPT-4o prompts, constraints, and expert filtering criteria are omitted, hindering faithful dataset replication.
*   **Training Protocol:** Full fine-tuning hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs) and hardware details are unspecified.
*   **Evaluation Rubrics:** Operational definitions and rater instructions for the four expert metrics are not provided.
