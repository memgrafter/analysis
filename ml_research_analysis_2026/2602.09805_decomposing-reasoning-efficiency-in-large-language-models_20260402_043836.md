# Decomposing Reasoning Efficiency in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09805
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09805
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Token efficiency in LLM reasoning is exactly decomposed into interpretable factors; efficiency and accuracy rankings diverge (Spearman ρ=0.63), logic robustness explains most cross-model gaps, and verbalization overhead varies 9× with only weak correlation to model scale.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework to decompose the token efficiency of LLM reasoning into additive components in log space: robustness against token budget truncation (\(r_{\text{ctx}}\)), conditional correctness given no truncation (\(r_{\text{logic}}\)), and verbosity (inverse expected token count). When per-instance workload metadata (e.g., from CogniLoad) is available, verbosity further splits into mean verbalization overhead and a coupling coefficient capturing scaling with task complexity. With full reasoning traces, trace quality (grounding, repetition, prompt copying) separates degenerate loops from engaged verbosity. Evaluating 25 models on CogniLoad (32k token budget), the authors find efficiency and accuracy rankings only moderately correlated (\(\rho=0.63\)). Most efficiency differences are attributed to logic robustness; verbalization overhead varies ninefold and shows weak correlation with model scale. Efficiency drops sharply (70–90%) as task length increases, and the workload coupling is consistently sublinear. The decomposition yields distinct bottleneck profiles per model, indicating the need for targeted interventions rather than uniform token reduction.

## Method Summary
The core metric is \(E_0 = 1000 \cdot \mathbb{E}[S]/\mathbb{E}[T]\), where \(S\) is success (correct final answer) and \(T\) is output token count. Using truncation events, \(\log E_0\) decomposes exactly into \(\log r_{\text{ctx}} + \log r_{\text{logic}} - \log \mathbb{E}[T] + \log 1000\). For benchmarks providing a workload proxy \(W\) (e.g., clause counts in CogniLoad), verbosity factorizes as \(\mathbb{E}[T] = \mathbb{E}[W] \cdot \bar{V}O \cdot \kappa\), where \(\bar{V}O = \mathbb{E}[T/W]\) and \(\kappa\) captures covariance between work and overhead. When traces are available, deterministic heuristics (grounding to known entities, repetition detection, prompt copying) identify “signal” tokens; trace quality \(q_{\text{trace}} = \mathbb{E}[T_{\text{sig}}]/\mathbb{E}[T]\) and analogous signal-based verbosity factors are computed. Experiments run 25 models on CogniLoad with a fixed token budget, collecting success, token counts, truncation flags, and (for some) full traces.

## Key Results
- Efficiency and accuracy rankings diverge (Spearman ρ=0.63), demonstrating that token-efficient models are not necessarily more accurate.
- Cross-model efficiency gaps are largely explained by differences in logic robustness (\(r_{\text{logic}}\)), whereas verbalization overhead varies up to 9× and correlates only weakly with model scale.
- As task workload (per CogniLoad’s proxy) increases, efficiency declines sharply by 70–90%.
- The workload coupling coefficient \(\kappa\) is consistently sublinear (\(\kappa < 1\)) across all models, indicating that overhead grows slower than workload.
- Trace analysis reveals that low-efficiency models often exhibit degenerate looping (very low \(q_{\text{trace}}\)), while high-efficiency models maintain engaged reasoning with moderate overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Additive log decomposition
- **Claim:** Token efficiency \(E_0\) can be exactly decomposed into three additive log components: robustness against token budget truncation (\(r_{\text{ctx}}\)), conditional correctness given no truncation (\(r_{\text{logic}}\)), and verbosity (\(-\log \mathbb{E}[T]\)).
- **Mechanism:** The law of total probability gives \(P(S) = P(N)P(S|N) = r_{\text{ctx}} \cdot r_{\text{logic}}\). Substituting into \(E_0 = 1000 \cdot P(S)/\mathbb{E}[T]\) and taking logarithms yields \(\log E_0 = \log r_{\text{ctx}} + \log r_{\text{logic}} - \log \mathbb{E}[T] + \log 1000\). This additive form attributes cross-model efficiency differences to distinct terms.
- **Core assumption:** The ratio-of-expectations \(\mathbb{E}[S]/\mathbb{E}[T]\) correctly represents expected correct answers per generated token; the factorization is mathematically exact given the definitions.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Abstract] "decomposes token efficiency into interpretable factors: robustness against token budget truncation, conditional correctness given completion, and verbosity."  
  - [Section 3.1] Equation 4: \(\log E_0 = \log r_{\text{ctx}} + \log r_{\text{logic}} - \log \mathbb{E}[T] + \log 1000\).  
  - Prior efficiency metrics report aggregate token usage without isolating these components; the decomposition fills this gap.

### Mechanism 2: Workload-normalized verbosity
- **Claim:** With a per-instance workload proxy \(W\), verbosity factorizes into mean verbalization overhead \(\bar{V}O\) and a coupling coefficient \(\kappa\), separating constant overhead from how it scales with task complexity.
- **Mechanism:** Define \(VO = T/W\) per instance. Then \(\mathbb{E}[T] = \mathbb{E}[W \cdot VO]\). By setting \(\kappa = \mathbb{E}[W VO]/(\mathbb{E}[W]\mathbb{E}[VO])\) we obtain the exact identity \(\mathbb{E}[T] = \mathbb{E}[W] \cdot \bar{V}O \cdot \kappa\). \(\kappa\) captures covariance: \(\kappa < 1\) means overhead decreases with workload (sublinear), \(\kappa > 1\) means it increases (superlinear). Experiments find \(\kappa < 1\) for all models, indicating sublinear scaling.
- **Core assumption:** \(W\) is a deterministic lower bound on the work needed for a given instance (provided by benchmark metadata); the decomposition holds exactly for any random \(VO\).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Abstract] "When benchmark metadata provides per-instance workload proxies, we further factor verbosity into two components: mean verbalization overhead (tokens per work unit) and a coupling coefficient capturing how overhead scales with task workload."  
  - [Section 3.2] "This yields the exact factorization: \(\mathbb{E}[T] = \mathbb{E}[W] \cdot \bar{V}O \cdot \kappa\)."  
  - Empirical range \(\kappa \in [0.24, 0.60]\) confirms sublinearity in CogniLoad.

### Mechanism 3: Trace quality via deterministic heuristics
- **Claim:** When reasoning traces are available, deterministic heuristics—grounding, repetition, prompt copying—quantify trace quality \(q_{\text{trace}}\) (signal density) and separate degenerate generation from verbose-but-engaged reasoning, avoiding human annotation or LLM judges.
- **Mechanism:** Tokens are labeled grounded if near a benchmark entity with an anchor word, repetitive if part of repeated lines or \(n\)-grams, and prompt-copied if overlapping with the prompt. Signal tokens satisfy grounded \(\land \neg\)repetitive \(\land \neg\)prompt-copied. Then \(q_{\text{trace}} = \mathbb{E}[T_{\text{sig}}]/\mathbb{E}[T]\), \(\bar{V}O_{\text{sig}} = \mathbb{E}[T_{\text{sig}}/W]\), and \(\kappa_{\text{sig}}\) are defined analogously. Low \(q_{\text{trace}}\) identifies degenerate loops; high \(\bar{V}O_{\text{sig}}\) after filtering indicates engaged verbosity.
- **Core assumption:** The mask rules reliably detect reasoning waste: grounding requires a task-specific ontology; tokenization for analysis aligns with model tokens via scaling.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Abstract] "When reasoning traces are available, we add deterministic trace-quality measures (grounding, repetition, prompt copying) to separate degenerate looping from verbose-but-engaged reasoning, avoiding human labeling and LLM judges."  
  - [Section 3.3] Definition of signal and \(q_{\text{trace}}\).  
  - A compression-based variant \(q^{(c)}_{\text{trace}}\) correlates moderately (\(\rho=0.64\)) with the mask-based measure, providing cross-validation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Ratio of expectations vs. expectation of ratio.  
  **Why needed:** The efficiency definition \(\mathbb{E}[S]/\mathbb{E}[T]\) weights token expenditure correctly by total cost, unlike per-instance averaging which overemphasizes short failures.  
  **Quick check:** "Why does \(\mathbb{E}[S]/\mathbb{E}[T]\) better reflect deployment cost than \(\mathbb{E}[S/T]\)?"

- **Concept:** Workload normalization via extrinsic metrics.  
  **Why needed:** Raw token counts conflate task difficulty with model verbosity; a workload proxy normalizes by a lower bound on necessary reasoning effort.  
  **Quick check:** "Two models use 500 tokens on a 10-work-unit task and 5000 tokens on a 100-work-unit task. How does \(\bar{V}O\) compare their efficiencies?"

- **Concept:** Trace quality as signal density.  
  **Why needed:** To distinguish tokens that contribute to reasoning from waste without human judgment.  
  **Quick check:** "If a trace repeats the same grounded fact 20 times, how do \(q_{\text{trace}}\) and \(\bar{V}O_{\text{sig}}\) reflect its efficiency?"

- **Concept:** Additive log decomposition for attribution.  
  **Why needed:** The log transformation turns multiplicative efficiency factors into additive contributions, making it easy to see which component drives cross-model gaps.  
  **Quick check:** "If Model A has \(\log r_{\text{logic}} = -0.5\) and Model B has \(\log r_{\text{logic}} = -1.0\) (relative to reference), how much more efficient is Model A due to logic?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Outcome Aggregator → Robustness Calculator, Workload Normalizer, Trace Analyzer (optional) → Decomposition Composer  

**Critical path:**  
1. Run models on benchmark instances with 32,768 token budget.  
2. Extract outcomes: success flag, token count \(T\), truncation flag \(C\).  
3. Compute baseline \(E_0\), \(r_{\text{ctx}}\), \(r_{\text{logic}}\).  
4. If workload metadata exists, compute per-instance \(W\) and then \(\bar{V}O\), \(\kappa\).  
5. If full traces available, apply trace analysis to get \(q_{\text{trace}}\), \(\bar{V}O_{\text{sig}}\), \(\kappa_{\text{sig}}\).  
6. Compare models by subtracting a reference model’s log-space values.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Token counts are provider-specific; character normalization offers robustness but ignores FLOPs/energy.  
- Workload proxy \(W\) is benchmark-dependent; applying to other tasks requires analogous metadata.  
- Trace heuristics trade semantic depth for interpreter-free computation; they detect surface patterns but cannot judge correctness.  
- Ratio-of-expectations weights by actual token expenditure, aligning with deployment cost but obscuring per-instance variability.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Truncation misclassification when models stop early without answer.  
- \(W\) underestimates true work (e.g., ignoring condition checks) inflating \(\bar{V}O\).  
- Grounding fails if anchor words missing or ontology mismatched; repetition flags common phrases (false positives).  
- Division by zero in \(\kappa\) if \(\mathbb{E}[VO]\) near zero.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Outcome-level replication:** Compute \(E_0\), \(r_{\text{ctx}}\), \(r_{\text{logic}}\) for 5 representative models. Verify efficiency vs. accuracy divergence (\(\rho \approx 0.63\)).  
2. **Workload normalization validation:** Using CogniLoad’s \(W_{\text{poi}}\), calculate \(\bar{V}O\) for all 25 models; confirm the 9× spread and \(\kappa < 1\).  
3. **Trace pipeline test:** Run analysis on traces from two models with predicted high/low \(q_{\text{trace}}\); check grounded-span coverage, repetition rates, and signal fraction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Standardized work units:** How can we define universal units of reasoning work to enable cross-benchmark efficiency comparisons? Benchmarks expose workload in heterogeneous units (constraints, facts, path length), and no common scale exists.
2. **Causal interventions:** Which specific interventions (e.g., length penalties, loop detection, training improvements) causally improve token efficiency for different bottleneck profiles? The decomposition shows correlations but not causality.
3. **Generalization beyond CogniLoad:** Do the observed efficiency rankings and bottleneck profiles (logic-limited vs. verbosity-limited models) hold on other reasoning tasks? Evaluation is confined to CogniLoad; other benchmarks may reveal different patterns.
4. **Semantic-enhanced trace metrics:** Can trace-quality metrics incorporate semantic analysis to better distinguish engaged reasoning from degenerate loops, reducing reliance on heuristic surface patterns? Current measures (grounding, repetition) miss higher-level incoherence.

## Limitations
- **Replication blockers:** CogniLoad generation code, exact zero-shot prompt, and input formatting are undisclosed; model versions and decoding parameters are only partially specified.
- **Workload proxy generalizability:** The proposed \(W\) may not generalize to other benchmarks; its claimed lower-bound property remains unverified outside CogniLoad.
- **Heuristic trace analysis:** Deterministic masks cannot judge semantic correctness and may fail on creative reasoning patterns; they rely on task-specific ontologies.

## Confidence
- Mathematical decomposition (log \(E_0\) identity): High  
- Workload normalization (\(\mathbb{E}[T] = \mathbb{E}[W] \cdot \bar{V}O \cdot \kappa\)): Medium  
- Trace quality heuristics: Medium  
- Empirical divergence (\(\rho=0.63\), logic dominance, overhead spread): Medium  
- Sublinear \(\kappa\): Medium
