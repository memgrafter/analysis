# DeepDFA: Injecting Temporal Logic in Deep Learning for Sequential Subsymbolic Applications

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03486
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03486
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: A differentiable neurosymbolic layer encoding temporal logic automata achieves state-of-the-art performance on sequential classification and non-Markovian RL tasks.

## Executive Summary
DeepDFA introduces a neurosymbolic architecture that injects temporal logical knowledge into deep learning models for sequential subsymbolic applications. By representing Deterministic Finite Automata (DFA) or Moore Machines as differentiable probabilistic finite automata (PFA), the approach bridges symbolic and subsymbolic representations. The system combines a neural symbol grounder with a differentiable automaton layer, enabling end-to-end training while respecting temporal constraints.

Evaluations on image‑sequence classification and non‑Markovian reinforcement learning show consistent improvements over recurrent, transformer, and prior neurosymbolic baselines, demonstrating enhanced data efficiency and generalization to longer sequences.

## Method Summary
DeepDFA addresses sequential tasks where temporal rules are known a priori. For classification, a CNN processes each frame to produce symbolic probabilities, which are fed into a differentiable PFA layer initialized from a target automaton. The PFA maintains a belief distribution over automaton states, updating via learned transition matrices, and produces a final classification through a linear readout. For non‑Markovian RL, the automaton belief is integrated into the policy and value networks of an A2C agent, while the symbol grounder is trained online using environment rewards. The automaton parameters are typically frozen after initialization, with learning focused on the grounder and policy.

## Key Results
- Outperforms LSTM, GRU, Transformer, FuzzyDFA, and NeSyA on MNIST4Declare sequence classification, with higher accuracy on longer test sequences (lengths 10 and 15).
- Achieves macro F1 up to 0.85 on CA VIAR surveillance video activity classification.
- In non‑Markovian Minecraft RL tasks, DeepDFA attains cumulative rewards approaching the upper‑bound Reward Machine performance under dense rewards, with significant gains in data efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DeepDFA works because it leverages the complementary strengths of symbolic and neural methods. The differentiable PFA layer provides a continuous relaxation of automaton dynamics, allowing gradient‑based optimization while preserving the underlying logical structure. Initialization from a known automaton injects robust prior knowledge, ensuring that the model respects temporal constraints from the start. The symbol grounder maps raw inputs to the predefined logical alphabet, and backpropagation through the PFA enables the grounder to adapt to the task’s temporal requirements. This separation of concerns—symbolic temporal reasoning handled by the PFA and perceptual grounding by the neural module—yields models that are both data‑efficient and interpretable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Differentiable programming**: Enables gradient flow through discrete structures; necessary for end‑to‑end training of the PFA layer.
  - *Quick check*: Verify that the transition matrix gradients are non‑zero during training.
- **Probabilistic Finite Automata (PFA)**: The stochastic extension of DFA used for continuous relaxation; models state transitions probabilistically.
  - *Quick check*: Ensure that row sums of transition matrix T are 1 (softmax normalization).
- **Neurosymbolic AI**: Integrates neural perception with symbolic reasoning; DeepDFA follows the neurosymbolic paradigm by coupling a neural grounder with a symbolic automaton.
  - *Quick check*: The overall architecture has distinct neural (CNN) and symbolic (PFA) components with differentiable interfaces.
- **Temporal logic (LTLf/DFA)**: Provides formal specifications of sequential properties; the automaton is compiled from such formulas.
  - *Quick check*: The provided automaton transition tables match the intended logical formulas.
- **Reinforcement learning with non‑Markovian rewards**: RL tasks where rewards depend on history; DeepDFA automaton belief serves as a sufficient statistic for history.
  - *Quick check*: Belief state changes when similar histories lead to different outcomes, indicating history dependence.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input (image/frame) -> CNN Symbol Grounder -> Symbol Distribution -> DeepDFA Layer (belief state) -> Output (Class or RL Policy/Value)

### Critical Path
The forward pass proceeds from raw input through the CNN to obtain symbol probabilities. These probabilities are multiplied with the transition matrix of the PFA to update the belief state. The belief is then used either for classification (via a linear layer) or as part of the state representation in RL. The automaton parameters (T, O) are typically initialized from a symbolic automaton and may be fine‑tuned or kept fixed.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Discrete vs continuous relaxation**: Using a soft temperature τ allows gradient propagation but can lead to fuzzy state beliefs; lower τ sharpens but may hinder training.
- **Knowledge injection vs learning**: Initializing from a known automaton ensures correctness but may limit adaptability if the automaton is inaccurate.
- **Scalability**: The PFA state space size grows with automaton states; however, the matrix operations remain efficient for moderate sizes (e.g., <100 states).
- **Integration with RL**: On‑policy training (A2C) avoids non‑stationarity from changing symbol grounder; off‑policy methods would require careful handling.

### Failure Signatures
- Transition matrix rows remain near‑uniform (low max probability) after training, indicating the automaton did not specialize.
- Symbol grounder fails to produce meaningful probabilities (e.g., all symbols equally likely), leading to stagnant belief updates.
- In RL, the policy does not improve over random or baseline, especially under sparse rewards, suggesting grounding failure.
- DeepDFA performance degrades significantly on longer sequences compared to baselines, implying inadequate temporal modeling.

### First Experiments
1. **Classification sanity check**: Train DeepDFA on MNIST4Declare with a known automaton; expect >95% accuracy on short sequences and >90% on length‑15 sequences.
2. **Ablation on initialization**: Compare random initialization vs. automaton‑injection; measure convergence speed and final accuracy.
3. **RL dense vs sparse**: Evaluate cumulative reward on a simple Minecraft task under both dense and sparse reward settings; expect dense rewards to yield better grounding and higher rewards.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can DeepDFA be extended to autonomously induce temporal automata from raw sequential data without relying on prior symbolic specifications?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors identify automata induction as a key future direction, stating: "First, we aim to investigate automata induction techniques under the DeepDFA framework, allowing the system to learn temporal rules from both symbolic and raw sequences without relying on prior knowledge."
- **Why unresolved**: DeepDFA currently assumes access to a predefined automaton for knowledge injection; the capability to learn automata parameters and structure from data alone remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Empirical demonstrations where DeepDFA successfully learns automata from diverse sequential datasets (e.g., activity recognition videos, log data), achieving comparable performance to knowledge injection while scaling to realistic automata sizes and handling noise.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What architectural modifications are needed to integrate DeepDFA into autoregressive sequence generation models for enforcing temporal logical constraints during generation?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors plan to "explore integration with autoregressive sequence generation models, enabling constrained sequence generation in domains like natural language generation and suffix prediction in BPM."
- **Why unresolved**: DeepDFA has been applied to classification and RL, but its role in guiding step‑by‑step sequence generation—where outputs must satisfy temporal logic—is unaddressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implementations combining DeepDFA with models like GPT or LSTM‑based generators, evaluated on tasks such as generating LTL‑compliant text or business process sequences, showing improved constraint satisfaction without degradation in generation quality.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can DeepDFA be adapted for off‑policy reinforcement learning to manage the non‑stationarity introduced by a continuously evolving symbol grounding function?
- **Basis in paper**: In section 5.3.1, the authors explicitly state: "Although integrating our model with off‑policy or model‑based reinforcement learning algorithms is not inherently infeasible, it requires additional care and methodological refinements. We therefore leave this integration as an interesting direction for future work."
- **Why unresolved**: Off‑policy RL uses replay buffers storing past experiences, but DeepDFA’s automaton states depend on the current symbol grounder, which changes during training. This mismatch can corrupt learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Proposed solutions like recomputing automaton states during replay sampling or using importance weighting, validated on non‑Markovian RL benchmarks with comparison to on‑policy performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What techniques can enhance symbol grounding in neurosymbolic RL under sparse reward conditions, where feedback is insufficient for learning correct symbol mappings?
- **Basis in paper**: In section 5.3.2, the authors discuss that sparse rewards lead to poor symbol grounding, calling it "a fundamental problem in neurosymbolic AI." They compare sparse and dense rewards but do not offer solutions.
- **Why unresolved**: Sparse rewards are typical in RL, and symbol grounding relies on reward signals for supervision. Without dense feedback, the grounder fails to learn accurate symbol perceptions.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Introduction of auxiliary tasks (e.g., predicting future symbols), reward shaping tailored for grounding, or self‑supervised objectives. Evaluation on sparse‑reward RL tasks with temporal constraints, measuring grounding accuracy and cumulative reward improvements.
