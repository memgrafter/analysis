# How does fine-tuning improve sensorimotor representations in large language models?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03313
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03313
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Supervised fine-tuning with human ratings significantly enhances alignment between LLM representations and human sensorimotor experiences.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether fine-tuning can bridge the "embodiment gap" in large language models by improving their sensorimotor representations. Using Representational Similarity Analysis and dimension-specific correlation metrics, the research demonstrates that supervised fine-tuning with human ratings significantly enhances the alignment between LLM representations and human sensorimotor experiences. Fine-tuning leads to a targeted reorganization of the model's semantic space, evidenced by near-zero correlation between performance rankings of base and fine-tuned models, indicating non-uniform corrective recalibration.

## Method Summary
The research employs supervised fine-tuning on GPT-4o-mini using human sensorimotor ratings from Lancaster norms (English) and Dutch sensory norms. Three fine-tuning approaches are tested: direct rating prediction, QA-format training on PerceptualQA, and cross-lingual transfer from Dutch to English concepts. Evaluation uses RSA correlation, dimension-wise Spearman correlations, and word-level similarity scores. Training involves atomic prompts with temperature=0 for deterministic outputs, and statistical validation through bootstrap resampling.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning significantly improves RSA correlation between model and human sensorimotor representations
- Near-zero Spearman correlation between base and fine-tuned model performance rankings indicates targeted, non-uniform representational reorganization
- Cross-lingual transfer shows robust generalization from Dutch to English, but QA-format fine-tuning fails to transfer to rating prediction tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuning with human ratings induces targeted representational reorganization rather than global improvement.
- Mechanism: Supervised gradient updates selectively correct the largest errors, causing concepts with highest misalignment to receive the strongest learning signal, thus reshuffling performance rankings.
- Core assumption: Base model representations have heterogeneous alignment with human ratings, and gradient-based fine-tuning can differentially adjust concept-level embeddings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "near-zero correlation between the performance rankings of base and fine-tuned models, indicating that the corrective recalibration was not uniform across concepts"
  - [section] "A key theoretical insight of this work is that fine-tuning drives a process of targeted representational reorganization. The near-zero Spearman correlation between pre- and post-fine-tuning performance rankings rules out a simple 'global boost.'"
- Break condition: If performance rankings between base and fine-tuned models show high positive correlation, this mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning generalizes robustly across languages but is highly sensitive to learning objective/task format.
- Mechanism: Cross-lingual transfer occurs because fine-tuned models converge on shared abstract sensorimotor structures that can be accessed via different linguistic instantiations, but task format mismatch prevents effective knowledge transfer.
- Core assumption: Sensorimotor semantic space contains language-agnostic structural patterns that can be learned from any language, but task format constrains the type of learned representation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "improvements generalize robustly across languages... but are highly sensitive to the learning objective, failing to transfer across two disparate task formats"
  - [section] "Fine-tuning on Dutch ratings (Nl_FT) substantially improved performance on English concepts... However, this transfer is not complete... QA_FT model showed inconsistent and limited improvement"
- Break condition: If fine-tuning on one language fails to improve performance on related concepts in another language, or if task format transfer succeeds unexpectedly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning on sensory attributes can facilitate partial, indirect learning of related motor representations.
- Mechanism: Sensorimotor semantic space exhibits interconnectedness where learning about sensory features provides sufficient contextual information to improve motor feature representations, even without explicit motor training.
- Core assumption: Sensory and motor dimensions are not independent in semantic space; learning one dimension's representations provides information about related dimensions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "fine-tuning on sensory attributes can facilitate a partial, indirect learning of related motor representations"
  - [section] "Although the Nl_FT and QA_FT models were trained exclusively on sensory dimension ratings, their representations of motor features also showed significant improvement during testing"
- Break condition: If motor dimension improvements do not occur when only sensory attributes are fine-tuned.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA)
  - Why needed here: RSA provides the global structural alignment metric to assess whether fine-tuning reorganizes the model's semantic space toward human-like representations.
  - Quick check question: What statistical measure compares the similarity of representational dissimilarity matrices between model and human ratings?

- Concept: Spearman rank correlation
  - Why needed here: Spearman correlation is used to assess both the overall structural alignment (RSA) and dimension-specific alignment, capturing monotonic relationships in ordinal data.
  - Quick check question: When would Spearman correlation be preferred over Pearson correlation in this analysis?

- Concept: Bootstrap resampling
  - Why needed here: Bootstrap tests provide statistical validation of the improvements observed in RSA and dimension-specific analyses by assessing whether differences are significant or due to random variation.
  - Quick check question: How does bootstrap resampling help determine if observed improvements are statistically reliable?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base model (gpt-4o-mini) -> Fine-tuning service (OpenAI API) -> Prompt templates (Atomic task formatting) -> Analysis pipeline (RSA, dimension correlation, word-level distance) -> Evaluation datasets (Lancaster norms, Dutch sensory norms, PerceptualQA)

- Critical path:
  1. Generate prompts for each word-dimension combination
  2. Submit fine-tuning jobs via API
  3. Run evaluation with temperature=0 for deterministic outputs
  4. Compute RSA matrices and correlations
  5. Perform bootstrap validation
  6. Analyze dimension and word-level performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Temperature=0 ensures reproducibility but may limit model's natural variability
  - Atomic prompt structure increases training data size but provides clear supervision signals
  - Fine-tuning vs. training from scratch: fine-tuning is more efficient but may be limited by base model's representational capacity

- Failure signatures:
  - High correlation between base and fine-tuned model rankings indicates failure of targeted reorganization
  - No improvement in motor dimensions when only sensory data is fine-tuned suggests lack of cross-dimensional generalization
  - Successful QA_FT transfer would contradict established task format sensitivity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune base model on English ratings and verify RSA improvement vs. base
  2. Fine-tune on Dutch ratings and test cross-lingual transfer to English concepts
  3. Fine-tune on QA task and verify failure to improve rating prediction performance

## Limitations
- Study relies on a single base model (gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18), leaving generalizability to other architectures unclear
- Dutch sensory norms dataset construction process isn't fully specified, potentially introducing confounds
- Study doesn't address potential confounds from prompt engineering variations or impact of temperature settings during fine-tuning

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core finding that fine-tuning improves sensorimotor alignment (supported by multiple independent analyses)
- Medium confidence in the mechanism of targeted representational reorganization (strong theoretical framing but complex to fully validate)
- Medium confidence in cross-lingual generalization (robust within tested languages but limited scope)
- Low confidence in the universality of task format sensitivity (only two formats tested)

## Next Checks
1. Test whether fine-tuning-induced improvements transfer to novel sensorimotor tasks beyond rating prediction, such as embodied reasoning or physical reasoning tasks.

2. Evaluate whether the observed cross-lingual transfer extends to non-Indo-European languages or whether it's specific to Dutch-English pairs.

3. Conduct ablation studies varying the fine-tuning dataset size and composition to determine the minimum requirements for achieving robust sensorimotor improvements.
