# Towards Controllable Video Synthesis of Routine and Rare OR Events
## Quick Facts
-   **arXiv ID:** 2602.21365
-   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21365
-   **Reference Count:** 28
-   **Primary Result:** Introduces an ellipsoid-based video diffusion framework for controllable OR event synthesis, outperforming baselines and enabling downstream safety-critical event detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the data scarcity problem for rare, safety-critical events in operating rooms by proposing a controllable video synthesis framework. The core innovation lies in representing OR scenes with editable geometric ellipsoids that encode entity class, position, size, and depth. A conditioning module translates either ground-truth trajectories from routine events or user-drawn counterfactual paths into a sequence of these abstract scenes. This sequence, combined with an initial frame, conditions a fine-tuned video diffusion model (LTX-Video) to generate photorealistic, temporally coherent videos that adhere to the specified motion. The method demonstrably improves video quality and controllability metrics over baselines and shows promise for augmenting training data for downstream tasks like near-miss detection.

## Method Summary
The framework comprises three stages. First, a **geometric abstraction module** processes an initial video frame using SAM2 for segmentation and Video Depth Anything for depth estimation, fitting each entity (personnel, patient, equipment) to an ellipsoid with parameters (centroid, axes, rotation, normalized depth). These ellipsoids are rendered onto a canvas where RGB channels encode class and depth. Second, a **conditioning module** generates a target ellipsoid sequence: for routine events, it extracts trajectories from a template video; for counterfactuals, an interactive tool lets users sketch new 2D paths for selected ellipsoids, which are interpolated across frames. Third, a **diffusion module** fine-tunes a pre-trained latent video diffusion model (LTX-Video) using In-Context LoRA (IC-LoRA) to accept the concatenated initial frame and ellipsoid sequence as conditioning. Training employs a standard diffusion loss augmented by a PatchGAN adversarial loss to enhance local realism. The model is trained on 338 OR videos (97 frames, 1024x768) for 8000 steps.

## Key Results
-   The proposed method achieves superior video quality and controllability on in-domain (MMOR) and out-of-domain (4DOR) tests, with lower FVD/LPIPS and higher SSIM/PSNR than diffusion baselines.
-   Ablation studies confirm the contribution of key components: ellipsoid-based conditioning, depth encoding, and the PatchGAN adversarial loss.
-   A downstream model trained on synthetic data for sterile-field violation near-miss detection achieves 70.13% recall, demonstrating utility for safety-critical data augmentation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Ellipsoid Abstraction for Controllable Scene Representation
-   **Claim:** Representing OR entities as ellipsoids provides a manipulable abstraction that preserves essential spatial information while enabling trivial trajectory edits.
-   **Mechanism:** A semi-automated pipeline (SAM2, Video Depth Anything) extracts 2D centroids, 3D ellipsoid parameters, and normalized depth from an initial frame. Each entity is rendered as a colored ellipse on a canvas, with RG channels encoding class and B channel encoding depth.
-   **Core Assumption:** Ellipsoidal approximations sufficiently model entity extents and spatial relationships, and centroid manipulation yields smooth, editable motion paths.
-   **Evidence:** Section 3.1 details the parameterization and rendering pipeline. Neighbors (e.g., SAW) use different primitives; ellipsoids are novel for this control-fidelity trade-off.
-   **Break Condition:** Failures in upstream segmentation (SAM2) or depth estimation on atypical scenes (occlusion, unusual equipment) produce erroneous conditioning.

### Mechanism 2: Trajectory Conditioning via Abstract Scene Sequences
-   **Claim:** Conditioning the diffusion model on a temporally sequenced series of abstract ellipsoid scenes provides fine-grained per-frame control over entity motion.
-   **Mechanism:** The conditioning module outputs a target sequence of rendered ellipsoid scenes. For routine events, it follows ground-truth trajectories from a template video. For counterfactuals, a user interface allows selecting an ellipsoid and sketching a new 2D path; waypoints are interpolated to modify the centroid sequence across frames. This entire sequence, plus the initial frame, conditions the video diffusion.
-   **Core Assumption:** The fine-tuned diffusion model possesses a strong prior that maps sparse ellipsoid motion cues to coherent, plausible video with natural interactions.
-   **Evidence:** Section 3.2 describes both conditioning pathways and the interactive tool. Fig. 5 shows qualitative changes in entity interactions following modified paths.
-   **Break Condition:** User-drawn trajectories are physically implausible (e.g., teleportation) or violate learned spatial priors; the model may ignore conditioning or revert to a routine.

### Mechanism 3: Fine-Tuned Diffusion with Adversarial Loss for Realism
-   **Claim:** Fine-tuning LTX-Video with In-Context LoRA (IC-LoRA) and a PatchGAN loss enables the model to interpret ellipsoid sequences as structural guidance and generate locally realistic videos.
-   **Mechanism:** The model is adapted for video-video translation where the "source" is the concatenated initial frame and ellipsoid sequence. IC-LoRA injects conditioning capabilities. The PatchGAN loss (applied to spatial patches) encourages high-frequency detail and local realism, complementing the diffusion loss.
-   **Core Assumption:** The pre-trained LTX-Video's video understanding can be efficiently steered via LoRA to align with the novel geometric conditioning modality.
-   **Evidence:** Section 3.3 details IC-LoRA and PatchGAN integration. Table 4 ablation shows adding `L_g` (PatchGAN) improves FVD (487.20 vs. 532.05) and Seg IoU (0.91 vs. 0.90).
-   **Break Condition:** The abstract conditioning is too divergent from natural image distribution, causing the model to ignore it or generate incoherent content. PatchGAN imbalance may cause texture artifacts.

## Architecture Onboarding
1. **Input:** (a) Initial OR video frame, (b) Conditioning specification (routine event video OR user-drawn trajectories on ellipsoids).
2. **Geometric Abstraction Module:** SAM2 + Depth Anything → Ellipsoid parameters → Rendered abstract scene image (per frame). Output: Sequence of rendered images.
3. **Conditioning Module:** Takes initial frame + rendered sequence. For counterfactuals, integrates user trajectory modifications via interpolation.
4. **Diffusion Module:** Fine-tuned LTX-Video with IC-LoRA & PatchGAN loss. Input: Initial frame + full rendered sequence. Output: Synthesized video.
5. **Evaluation:** Compute standard video metrics (FVD, SSIM) against ground truth. Compute controllability metrics (BB IoU, Seg IoU) by tracking entities in both real and generated videos.

**Critical Path:** Success depends critically on the **Geometric Abstraction Module's** ability to produce clean, consistent ellipsoids. Errors here propagate unconditionally. The second critical dependency is the **diffusion model's learned prior** to respect the spatial conditioning; insufficient fine-tuning leads to ignored ellipsoids.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
-   *Ellipsoids vs. Segmentation Masks:* Ellipsoids offer trivial continuous control but lose fine-grained articulation. Masks preserve detail but are discrete and sensitive to segmentation errors (Ablation Table 4: masks yield slightly better FVD but less controllability).
-   *Depth Encoding:* Including depth in B channel improves layering cues but relies on accurate depth estimation; the paper finds it beneficial.
-   *PatchGAN Loss:* Adds cost and tuning but improves local realism (ablation evidence). Risk of over-emphasis on texture.

**Failure Signatures:**
-   *Geometric Abstraction Failure:* Inconsistent ellipsoids across frames (jittery size/position), missing entities, or poor entity coverage. Diagnose by visualizing rendered abstraction.
-   *Conditioning Ignorance:* Generated video does not reflect changed trajectories; entities follow original paths. Diagnose by comparing ellipsoid sequence vs. entity tracks in output.
-   *Temporal Incoherence:* Flickering, duplication, or motion stuttering, especially in later frames. Likely diffusion model temporal consistency limitation.
-   *Poor Out-of-Domain Generalization:* For new OR layouts/equipment (4DOR), ellipsoid extraction may fail if SAM2 isn't promptable, or generated content may be semantically wrong.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Geometric Abstraction Validation:** Run pipeline on 100 random MMOR frames. Inspect rendered ellipsoid sequences for entity coverage, temporal smoothness, and correct class/depth encoding.
2.  **Routine Event Reconstruction:** Generate videos for MMOR test set using ground-truth ellipsoid trajectories. Compute FVD, SSIM, BB IoU against ground truth.
3.  **Controllability Sanity Check:** For a simple routine clip, sketch a drastically different trajectory (e.g., person walking away). Generate video. Qualitatively assess path following; quantitatively compute centroid L2 error and BB IoU.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
-   **Open Question 1:** How to achieve fine-grained articulation control (e.g., arm extension) in the ellipsoid abstraction? Ellipsoids cannot encode joint angles or detailed poses.
-   **Open Question 2:** How can the model generalize to OR environments and surgeries not seen in training data? MMOR covers limited scenarios; distribution shift degrades realism for unseen configurations.
-   **Open Question 3:** Can the geometric abstraction pipeline be fully automated to eliminate manual SAM2 prompts? Manual annotation hinders large-scale curation.
-   **Open Question 4:** How to evaluate the clinical realism and downstream utility of the synthetic OR videos? Lack of formal domain-expert evaluation and real-data testing.
-   **Open Question 5:** How to improve temporal consistency in long video sequences? Resolution and clarity degrade as video progresses, calling for architectural modifications.

## Limitations
-   Dependence on SAM2 and Video Depth Anything: segmentation/depth failures on rare OR scenes (unusual equipment, heavy occlusion) may corrupt ellipsoid abstraction.
-   Generalization to new OR layouts (4DOR) assumes promptable segmentation; failure cases not analyzed.
-   Ellipsoid abstraction's inherent limitation: cannot enforce explicit fine-grained articulation control (e.g., finger movements, joint angles).
