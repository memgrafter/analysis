# When AI Navigates the Fog of War

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.16642
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16642
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** State-of-the-art LLMs, queried with contemporaneous news about a post-training-cutoff conflict, show strategic reasoning (avg. calibration consistency 0.72), with higher reliability in economic/logistical domains (0.79) than politically ambiguous ones (0.67), and exhibit a clear narrative evolution from optimism to sober attrition.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether large language models (LLMs) can perform genuine geopolitical reasoning free from hindsight bias. By anchoring the analysis to a fictional but temporally grounded 2026 Middle East conflict that occurred after all evaluated models' training cutoffs, the authors simulate the "fog of war." Models are queried at 11 sequential temporal nodes using only news available up to that precise moment, forcing reasoning under genuine uncertainty. Analysis of responses reveals that frontier LLMs demonstrate notable strategic insight, often focusing on structural incentives over rhetoric. However, this capability is domain-dependent, with significantly higher calibration in economically structured scenarios compared to politically ambiguous multi-actor environments. A longitudinal analysis of recurring questions captures a coherent narrative evolution in model responses, shifting from early expectations of rapid diplomatic resolution to a later consensus on a protracted "hurting stalemate."

## Method Summary
The core methodology constructs a temporally locked case study. Researchers built a detailed timeline (T0–T10) of the early 2026 conflict, validated by human experts. For each temporal node, they assembled a raw, unfiltered context corpus from 12 international news outlets containing *only* articles published before that node's timestamp. No summaries or editorial curation were applied, preserving noise and contradictions. Each model was queried once per node with a standardized prompt containing the context and a specific question (42 verifiable, 5 recurring exploratory). Probabilities for verifiable questions were manually extracted from free-text responses and compared to outcomes defined by a fixed later observation cutoff, using 1-MAE (calibration consistency) as the primary metric. The full set of raw model responses was archived for qualitative analysis of reasoning patterns and narrative shifts.

## Key Results
*   **Strategic Reasoning:** Models adeptly identified structural drivers (e.g., economic interdependence, military logistics) over surface-level diplomatic rhetoric, suggesting forms of strategic realism.
*   **Domain Gap:** Calibration consistency was significantly higher for questions on economic/logistical themes (0.79) versus politically ambiguous, multi-actor themes (0.67), revealing a key limitation.
*   **Narrative Evolution:** Models' analyses shifted systematically over time, transitioning from early optimism about containment to a mature narrative centered on attritional de-escalation and systemic stalemate, indicating coherent belief updating.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Temporal information constraints prevent training-data leakage from confounding the analysis of model reasoning.
*   **Mechanism:** The study anchors all queries to a conflict that occurred entirely after the training cutoffs of all evaluated models. For each temporal node, models receive only news published before that node's timestamp, forcing them to reason from a strictly contemporaneous information state.
*   **Core assumption:** If models cannot rely on parametric memorization of outcomes, their responses reflect genuine reasoning under uncertainty rather than pattern matching to known historical results.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "We construct a temporally grounded case study of the early stages of the 2026 Middle East conflict, which unfolded after the training cutoff of current frontier models."
    *   [section 1] "By anchoring the study to a conflict that postdates all current model training, we ensure that neither parametric recall nor simulated ignorance can substitute for genuine real-time reasoning."
    *   [corpus] The constructed timeline (T0–T10) and context corpus (growing from 158 to 1,787 articles) are explicitly filtered to exclude post-node information (Section 3.4).
*   **Break condition:** If models still demonstrate hindsight-like consistency across nodes despite the temporal constraint, it may indicate they are reconstructing plausible trajectories from general world knowledge rather than responding to the specific, time-bound context.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** A raw, unfiltered, multi-source news corpus simulates the "fog of war" information environment.
*   **Mechanism:** Context packages for each node are built by aggregating all available news articles from 12 international outlets without editorial summarization or duplicate removal beyond basic title normalization. This preserves redundancy, contradictory reports, and noise, mirroring the unstructured data an analyst would face.
*   **Core assumption:** The cognitive challenge of navigating ambiguity, conflicting signals, and information overload is essential to studying realistic geopolitical reasoning. Curated or summarized contexts would artificially simplify the task.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.4] "We apply no further filtering, summarization, or editorial curation... This is intentional; it simulates the raw, unstructured information environment a real-world forecasting agent would encounter."
    *   [section 3.4] "The cumulative article pool grows from 158 articles at T0 to 1,787 at T10 (totaling ≈889K words)."
    *   [corpus] The corpus includes diverse sources (Al-Monitor, Reuters, Fox News, etc.), explicitly retaining multiple perspectives on the same event.
*   **Break condition:** If model performance degrades precipitously when context is minimally cleaned (e.g., removing obvious duplicates), it would suggest their reasoning is fragile to noise rather than robust to realistic ambiguity.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Longitudinal probing with consistent exploratory questions captures narrative evolution and belief updating.
*   **Mechanism:** Five general exploratory questions (e.g., "Will the conflict escalate into a global war?") are asked at every temporal node. Analyzing how responses to the same question change over time reveals how models integrate new information and revise their mental models of the conflict.
*   **Core assumption:** The trajectory of a model's narrative—shifts in emphasis, probability, or causal framing—is a primary output of interest, not just a single-point accuracy score. This tracks reasoning dynamics under evolving partial information.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.2.2] "These questions... serve a different purpose: to record how language models interpret and narrate the evolving situation as the conflict unfolds."
    *   [section 5] "Our longitudinal analysis reveals a striking consensus... model analyses transitioned from optimistic diplomatic resolutions to a sobering consensus of a 'hurting stalemate'."
    *   [corpus] The released dataset includes full model responses for all five exploratory questions across all 11 nodes.
*   **Break condition:** If models simply repeat prior answers verbatim or show no coherent trend in narrative shifts, the longitudinal design fails to capture meaningful belief updating.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Temporal Grounding in Evaluation**
    *   *Why needed here:* The entire study design rests on using a post-training-cutoff event to eliminate leakage. Understanding why static benchmarks fail and how dynamic, time-locked evaluations provide cleaner signals is prerequisite.
    *   *Quick check question:* What specific methodological problem does using a 2026 conflict solve that a retrospective analysis of a pre-2024 conflict cannot?
*   **Concept: Fog of War as an Information-Theoretic Constraint**
    *   *Why needed here:* The "fog of war" is operationalized as strict information asymmetry (only pre-node data). This requires understanding partial observability, state estimation, and reasoning under uncertainty in multi-actor systems.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does providing only contemporaneous news differ from giving a model a perfect summary of events up to a point? Which aspect of reasoning does the former stress that the latter does not?
*   **Concept: Calibration Consistency for Open-Ended Geopolitical Probes**
    *   *Why needed here:* The paper uses 1-MAE to measure alignment between model probabilities and outcomes, but acknowledges that outcomes are operationalized with a fixed cutoff. Understanding this metric's limitations (e.g., sensitivity to window length, non-binary events) is key to interpreting quantitative results.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why is a high calibration score (e.g., 0.79) on economic shockwave questions not definitive proof that models "understand" economics?

## Architecture Onboarding
1.  **Timeline Builder** -> selects critical temporal nodes (T0–T10) from a news archive based on event significance and human interview validation.
2.  **Context Corpus Engine** -> for each node Ti, filters a master news archive (12 sources, Feb 1–Mar 7, 2026) to articles published before Ti's timestamp, formats them, and truncates at a 480K-char token budget.
3.  **Query Dispatcher** -> for each node, injects the context corpus and a specific node-specific or general exploratory question into a standardized prompt template and calls the LLM API.
4.  **Response Parser & Extractor** -> manually extracts probability judgments from free-text model responses for node-specific questions (due to non-standard formats).
5.  **Analysis Module** -> performs qualitative thematic analysis on full responses and quantitative scoring (1-MAE) against outcomes defined at a fixed observation cutoff.

**Critical path:**
1.  Define temporal scope & secure data: Establish the post-training-cutoff event and begin collecting contemporaneous news from multiple sources from day one.
2.  Construct timeline: Identify turning points, validate with human actors who experienced the event, finalize 11 nodes.
3.  Build corpus & context windows: Implement the non-curated aggregation and time-slicing logic. This must be completed *before* any model queries.
4.  Run model queries: Systematically query all models for all questions at all nodes, preserving the exact context and response.
5.  Analyze & archive: Perform qualitative coding of reasoning patterns and extract probabilities. Archive all raw responses.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Context completeness vs. noise:** Using all raw articles preserves realism but includes redundancy and irrelevant information. A curated summary would be cleaner but would remove a key challenge (noise filtering).
*   **Manual vs. automated node selection:** Human-informed selection improves ecological validity but reduces reproducibility and scalability.
*   **Single-run vs. multiple sampling:** The study uses a single run per question to focus on qualitative patterns, sacrificing reliability estimates for insight into reasoning trajectories.

**Failure signatures:**
*   Models produce highly similar narratives across all nodes, indicating they are ignoring time-sensitive context and falling back on prior knowledge or generic conflict scripts.
*   Probability estimates are consistently extreme (0 or 1) for most questions, suggesting the task is being treated as a knowledge recall rather than an uncertain forecast.
*   Narrative evolution analysis shows no coherent trend; models' answers to the same exploratory question jump erratically between nodes without clear informational justification.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Replicate with a different post-cutoff event:** Apply the identical protocol to a major economic crisis or natural disaster that occurred after model training to test domain generality of findings (economic vs. political reasoning).
2.  **Controlled context ablation:** For a single node, run queries with (a) raw corpus, (b) corpus with only one news source, (c) corpus with key contradictory reports removed. Measure impact on calibration and narrative coherence.
3.  **Vary the observation cutoff:** For the final node (T10), re-score all "verifiable" questions after 2 weeks, 1 month, and 3 months. Quantify how sensitive the MAE scores are to the arbitrary choice of "resolution" window.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**
*   **Question:** Why do LLMs show significantly lower calibration consistency in politically ambiguous multi-actor environments (0.67) compared to economically/logistically structured settings (0.79), and what architectural or training modifications could bridge this gap?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper states models are "more reliable in economically and logistically structured settings than in politically ambiguous multi-actor environments" and provides domain-specific calibration scores.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies this as a key limitation but does not analyze whether this stems from a lack of world models for political psychology, difficulty with multi-agent recursive reasoning, or insufficient representation of signaling games in training data.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies comparing model performance on matched economic vs. political scenarios, or probing internal representations for differences in how causal chains are constructed across domains.

**Open Question 2**
*   **Question:** What specific mechanisms cause some models (e.g., Claude, GPT-5.4) to produce more structurally consistent strategic reasoning that resists "diplomatic smokescreens" while others (e.g., Gemini) overweight domestic political rhetoric during pre-conflict analysis?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper details divergent model behaviors at T0, where some models maintained inferential consistency about military momentum while others shifted probabilities based on diplomatic reports.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper describes this divergence but cannot explain whether it results from differences in chain-of-thought prompting style, internal value alignment, or training objectives that affect the weighting of institutional vs. rhetorical signals.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative analysis of attention patterns and reasoning traces when models process conflicting evidence types (military deployments vs. diplomatic statements) in temporally constrained settings.

**Open Question 3**
*   **Question:** How do LLMs internally represent and update their "narrative models" of an unfolding conflict, and what triggers the observed shift from expectations of rapid containment to systemic accounts of attritional de-escalation?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper documents a clear narrative evolution across phases, with models transitioning from diplomatic to exhaustion-based de-escalation pathways as certain events (e.g., Strait closure, leadership decapitation) accumulate.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper captures the output trajectories but lacks access to the latent belief states or world model updates that produce this shift. It is unclear whether this represents genuine model updating or pattern-matching to stereotypical conflict escalation scripts.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal probing of model hidden states or belief vectors at each temporal node to track how internal representations of actor incentives, escalation ladders, and systemic risk change with new information.

**Open Question 4**
*   **Question:** Does the "decapitation paradox" observed in model outputs—where leadership elimination is framed as increasing rather than decreasing conflict duration due to fragmented command—reflect a correct strategic insight about modern asymmetric warfare, or an artifact of overgeneralizing from particular historical patterns in the training data?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper repeatedly notes models reasoned that killing Iran's leadership created a power vacuum that decentralized control, making ceasefire harder (T10 analysis).
*   **Why unresolved:** The authors present this as evidence of strategic reasoning but do not validate whether this pattern aligns with political science theories of state fragmentation or is an emergent interpolation from training examples (e.g., US operations in Iraq/Libya).
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Expert evaluation of the model-generated rationale against established theories of authoritarian resilience and command-and-control breakdown, followed by testing the same reasoning on synthetic conflicts where fragmentation outcomes are known.

**Open Question 5**
*   **Question:** For the ongoing 2026 conflict archived in this study, will the contemporaneous LLM reasoning trajectories ultimately prove predictive of the actual conflict trajectory, or will hindsight reveal systematic biases (e.g., overestimation of institutional continuity, underestimation of non-state actor agency)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper explicitly frames itself as a "snapshot" for future comparison, noting the conflict remains ongoing and inviting follow-up studies.
*   **Why unresolved:** At the time of writing, the conflict's endpoint is unknown. The archived model responses constitute a unique dataset for future retrospective analysis but cannot yet be evaluated for predictive accuracy.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Once the conflict reaches a stable conclusion (settlement, regime change, regional war, etc.), researchers can re-evaluate the probability estimates and narrative predictions against the realized outcome, measuring both point accuracy and structural insight (e.g., did models correctly identify the decisive variables?).

## Limitations
*   **Outcome Definition Arbitrariness:** The calibration metric (1-MAE) depends on a fixed "observation cutoff" for declaring a question resolved. Shorter or longer windows could alter MAE scores, especially for slower-moving events.
*   **Qualitative Interpretation Subjectivity:** Claims about "strategic realism" and narrative evolution rely on thematic analysis of free-text responses without reported inter-coder reliability, risking researcher bias.
*   **Single-Run Inference:** Each question is queried once per model, providing no measure of response variance or stability under sampling (temperature), limiting the generalizability of observed narrative trajectories.

## Confidence
*   **Leakage Resistance:** High. The post-cutoff 2026 conflict design is a strong, necessary condition for leakage-free evaluation. The break condition (hindsight-like consistency) is well-specified, though unverified in this study.
*   **Domain-Dependent Reasoning:** Medium. The calibration difference between economic/logistical (0.79) and political (0.67) themes is clear, but causal attribution is uncertain. Simpler outcome definitions for economic shock questions may inflate scores; political ambiguity may be harder to operationalize as binary outcomes.
*   **Narrative Evolution:** Medium. The longitudinal shift from "rapid containment" to "hurting stalemate" is a compelling qualitative observation. However, with single-run data, it's unclear if this reflects coherent belief updating or selective pattern-matching to later context snippets.

## Next Checks
1.  **Outcome Window Sensitivity:** Re-score all 42 verifiable questions using multiple, staggered observation cutoffs (e.g., 3 days, 2 weeks, 1 month post-T10). Plot MAE distributions to quantify how dependent the 0.72 calibration score is on the chosen resolution window.
2.  **Context Perturbation Ablation:** For a subset of nodes (e.g., T5), run the full query suite with three context variants: (a) raw corpus (original), (b) single-source only (e.g., only Reuters), and (c) de-duplicated + minimal noise filtering. Measure changes in calibration consistency and narrative coherence to test robustness to information noise/source bias.
3.  **Independent Replication with New Event:** Apply the identical protocol (timeline selection, context aggregation, question set) to a completely different post-cutoff geopolitical event (e.g., a 2026 South China Sea crisis). Success requires reproducing the core pattern: (a) higher calibration in economic/domain-structured sub-questions, and (b) a detectable narrative evolution phase shift.
