# Patient-Centered, Graph-Augmented Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Passive Surveillance for Early Stroke Risk Detection in High-Risk Individuals

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22228
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22228
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A passive surveillance system analyzing patient-reported symptoms achieved specificity 1.00 and prevalence-adjusted PPV 1.00 under conservative thresholds, with sensitivity up to 0.72 in a 90-day window and alert burden 0.16–0.35.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses delayed stroke recognition in high-risk diabetic patients by developing a passive surveillance system that analyzes free-text symptoms from secure patient messages. The core innovation is a patient-language symptom taxonomy constructed iteratively with an LLM, which structures unstructured patient narratives into a hierarchical ontology. This taxonomy feeds a dual machine learning pipeline (a heterogeneous graph neural network and elastic-net/LASSO regression) that identifies symptom patterns statistically and temporally associated with subsequent stroke. The resulting hybrid screening system, optimized for very high specificity, demonstrated in temporal EHR simulations the ability to flag at-risk patients 3–90 days in advance with extremely low false-alert rates, using patient-reported language alone.

## Method Summary
The method involved three main phases. First, an iterative LLM-based clustering process built a three-level symptom taxonomy (MAIN→SUB1→SUB2) from a corpus of secure messages, with human validation ensuring coherence (Gwet's AC1=0.93). Second, a dual ML pipeline was trained on historical data: a heterogeneous GNN (GraphSAGE-based) modeled complex relationships among patients, symptoms, and timestamps, while an EN/LASSO model provided an interpretable, complementary predictor. Their outputs were combined into a composite stroke risk score. Third, a hybrid screening algorithm, designed for high specificity, applied a rule-based trigger (high-risk symptom count) or a logistic threshold to generate alerts. The system was evaluated via retrospective simulation across multiple temporal windows (3–90 days) on hold-out data.

## Key Results
- Under conservative, high-specificity thresholds, the system achieved specificity = 1.00 and prevalence-adjusted PPV = 1.00.
- Sensitivity was highest at 0.72 in the 90-day screening window.
- The alert burden (fraction of monitored patients flagged) ranged from 0.16 to 0.35.
- LLM-guided taxonomy construction achieved high inter-rater reliability (Gwet’s AC1 = 0.93).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: LLM-Guided Taxonomy Uncovers Latent Symptom Signals
**Claim:** Patient-reported symptom language, when structured by an LLM-guided taxonomy, reveals clinically meaningful clusters associated with future stroke.
**Mechanism:** An iterative LLM clustering process creates a hierarchical symptom map (MAIN→SUB1→SUB2) from free-text messages. This transforms unstructured patient language into a structured ontology where symptom nodes can be analyzed for statistical and temporal patterns.
**Evidence:** [section] Identified 35 main topics, 109 sub1 topics, and 495 sub2 topics; high investigator agreement on LLM reasoning (Gwet’s AC1, 0.93).
**Break Condition:** LLM-generated clusters fail inter-rater reliability (e.g., AC1 < 0.8) or produce clinically incoherent groupings.

### Mechanism 2: Dual ML Pipeline Captures Statistical and Temporal Risk Patterns
**Claim:** A heterogeneous GNN and EN/LASSO identify symptom patterns whose statistical relevance and temporal proximity to stroke events are predictive.
**Mechanism:** The GNN models complex, tree-like relationships among symptoms, patients, and timestamps. EN/LASSO provides a complementary, interpretable predictor selection. Their combined output ("event association score") reflects both symptom-stroke statistical linkage and timing.
**Evidence:** [section] Three dual ML pipelines evaluated via 5-fold cross-validation; patient-level symptom importance derived from z-score averaging GNN event delta and EN permutation score.
**Break Condition:** GNN and EN/LASSO produce discordant top symptom rankings, or the composite score fails to improve temporal validation performance over a symptom-count baseline.

### Mechanism 3: Conservative Thresholding Enables High-Precision Alerting
**Claim:** A screening algorithm that prioritizes very high specificity can achieve a clinically useful trade-off, flagging at-risk patients with minimal false alerts.
**Mechanism:** The system optimizes the decision threshold for specificity >0.9, using an interpretable symptom-count rule plus a logistic risk score to generate alerts only for patients meeting strict criteria.
**Evidence:** [abstract] Achieved specificity 1.00 and PPV 1.00 under conservative thresholds; alert burden 0.16–0.35.
**Break Condition:** Specificity drops significantly below 0.95 or alert burden exceeds a manageable threshold (e.g., >5%) in prospective validation.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**
*Why needed here:* Patient-symptom-time data is naturally a heterogeneous graph (different node/edge types). A heterogeneous GNN (GraphSAGE-based) learns distinct embeddings and message-passing rules for each type, capturing complex dependencies a homogeneous model would miss.
*Quick check:* Why might a homogeneous GNN (all nodes/edges same type) fail to capture the predictive signal in this patient-symptom-time dataset?

**Concept: Symptom Taxonomy Induction from Patient Language**
*Why needed here:* Patient-reported symptoms are unstructured, idiomatic, and noisy. A structured, hierarchy-based taxonomy (built via LLM + human validation) is the prerequisite for reliable pattern detection, converting free text into computable features.
*Quick check:* What are two key risks of using an LLM alone (without iterative human review) to build a clinical symptom taxonomy from patient messages?

**Concept: Conservative Threshold Optimization for Clinical Deployment**
*Why needed here:* For passive surveillance, the cost of a false positive (alert fatigue) likely outweighs a false negative in early validation. The system explicitly optimizes for specificity, a critical operational constraint.
*Quick check:* If a model is optimized for maximum F1-score instead of high specificity, how would its utility likely change for a clinician receiving passive alerts?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Data Ingestion -> LLM Taxonomy Engine -> Graph Constructor -> Dual ML Pipeline (GNN & EN/LASSO) -> Symptom Scorer -> Hybrid Screening Simulator -> Evaluation Module

**Critical Path:** Message -> LLM Annotation -> Graph Construction -> GNN/EN Training -> Symptom Scoring -> Threshold Optimization -> Screening Simulation. The LLM annotation quality is the gating factor.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **Specificity vs. Sensitivity:** Explicitly chosen for high specificity (1.00) to ensure alert trustworthiness, accepting lower sensitivity (0.72 max at 90 days).
- **LLM Autonomy vs. Human Oversight:** Used LLM for scalable taxonomy building but required human validation (Gwet's AC1) to ensure clinical coherence; a fully automated pipeline was deemed too risky.
- **Graph Complexity vs. Overfitting:** Added more graph edges (semantic, comorbidity, similarity) but increased message pruning stringency and dropout rates to combat overfitting on sparse temporal data.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Taxonomy Collapse:** Low inter-rater agreement (Gwet's AC1 < 0.8) on LLM's topic clustering/annotation; clinically incoherent symptom clusters.
- **Model Discordance:** GNN and EN/LASSO produce significantly different top-ranked symptoms (low correlation), indicating one model is not capturing the true signal.
- **Temporal Drift:** Model performance degrades sharply in the third temporal validation block (04/2025-07/2025), suggesting concept drift.
- **Alert Burden Explosion:** Under conservative thresholds, alert burden exceeds ~0.35 in any window, indicating high-specificity constraint failure.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Validate LLM Taxonomy Pipeline:** Run iterative LLM clustering on a held-out set of 500 messages. Measure (a) Gwet's AC1 for topic assignment vs. two clinicians, and (b) semantic coherence of top 10 clusters via clinician review. Goal: AC1 > 0.85.
2.  **Ablate Dual Pipeline Components:** Compare ROC AUC of three pipelines (symptom-only, +demographics/comorbidities, +GNN-predicted risk) in 5-fold CV. Goal: GNN-enhanced model significantly outperforms (p<0.05) simpler models.
3.  **Threshold Sensitivity Sweep:** For the 90-day window, plot specificity vs. sensitivity across thresholds 0.01-0.99. Identify the threshold achieving >0.99 specificity. Goal: Confirm a threshold exists that meets the 1.00 specificity claim and quantify the associated sensitivity drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How will clinicians accept and act upon alerts from this passive surveillance system in real-world workflows, and what is the perceived cognitive and EHR burden?
- **Open Question 2:** Will the system's high specificity and performance hold when externally validated in geographically and demographically different healthcare systems?
- **Open Question 3:** Does incorporating patient-reported symptoms from secure messages provide unique predictive value beyond traditional structured EHR data?
- **Open Question 4:** How does the conservative, high-specificity threshold trade-off sensitivity in a way that is acceptable and clinically useful across different resource settings?
- **Open Question 5:** Is the LLM-guided, patient-language symptom taxonomy robust and generalizable to other diseases and patient populations?

## Limitations
- **Unknown LLM configuration:** Exact prompts, model version ("Gemini Pro 3"), and temperature for taxonomy induction and annotation are unreported, risking irreproducible symptom grouping.
- **Undisclosed model hyperparameters:** GNN architecture (layers, dimensions) and EN/LASSO regularization strengths are omitted, hindering faithful replication and raising overfitting concerns.
- **Simulation-only validation:** Claims rely on retrospective EHR simulations; real-world alert burden, clinician acceptance, and temporal drift remain unassessed.

## Confidence
- **LLM-driven taxonomy coherence:** *Medium*. High inter-rater reliability (AC1 0.93) is reported, but without prompt details, generalizability is uncertain.
- **Dual ML pipeline predictive signal:** *Low*. Evidence is internal; neighbor corpus shows limited direct support for this specific GNN application to stroke symptoms.
- **Conservative threshold utility in practice:** *Medium*. Simulation metrics are strong but may not hold prospectively; alert burden range suggests potential feasibility but lacks workflow validation.

## Next Checks
1.  **Reproduce LLM taxonomy stability:** Run the iterative clustering with documented prompts on a held-out message set. Target: Gwet’s AC1 > 0.85 between clinicians and LLM output across batches.
2.  **Ablate GNN contribution:** Compare ROC AUC of the full dual pipeline against (a) symptom-only EN/LASSO and (b) a homogeneous GNN baseline in 5-fold CV. Target: Full model significantly outperforms (p<0.05).
3.  **Threshold robustness test:** On an external temporal hold-out (e.g., last 6 months of data), sweep decision thresholds to locate the point where specificity ≥ 0.99. Report associated sensitivity and alert burden; confirm feasibility of high-specificity operation.
