# When Abundance Conceals Weakness: Knowledge Conflict in Multilingual Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.07041
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07041
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Cross-lingual knowledge conflict resolution in multilingual LLMs exhibits a task-dependent dichotomy: reasoning tasks are persuaded primarily by high-resource language evidence (resource-abundance hierarchy), while entity-centric factual tasks are dictated by linguistic affinity (script/morphology similarity), revealing an "abundance-weakness paradox" where resource-rich non-Latin languages falter in factual alignment.

## Executive Summary
Multilingual large language models encode knowledge unevenly across languages, leading to conflicts when external evidence in one language contradicts internal parametric memories activated by the query language. This paper introduces CLEAR, a comprehensive framework that decomposes conflict resolution into four progressive tasks—from parametric elicitation to multi-source arbitration—and evaluates models on two QA benchmarks (entity-centric PopQA and reasoning-intensive StrategyQA) across ten diverse languages. The results uncover a striking dichotomy: in reasoning-intensive tasks, persuasion follows a language resource abundance hierarchy, with high-resource languages (e.g., English, Chinese) dominating; in entity-centric factual tasks, linguistic affinity—particularly shared script and morphology—determines persuasion, enabling low-resource Latin-script languages to outperform distant high-resource ones. This reveals an "abundance-weakness paradox" where data-rich non-Latin languages excel in reasoning but suffer from fragile factual cross-lingual alignment, as measured by Stubborn Rate (SR) and Persuasion Rate (PR). The findings advance our understanding of multilingual knowledge dynamics and provide actionable insights for building more reliable multilingual retrieval-augmented generation systems.

## Method Summary
CLEAR (Cross-lingual Evidence Arbitration Framework) structures the analysis of knowledge conflict through four sequential tasks: (1) Parametric Memory Elicitation: obtaining closed-book answers to semantically equivalent queries in each language to establish baseline correctness; (2) Intra-lingual Conflict: providing contradictory evidence in the same language as the query to measure susceptibility to persuasion; (3) Cross-lingual Evidence: testing whether evidence in a different language can correct parametric errors; (4) Multi-source Arbitration: competing evidence in multiple languages to identify dominant sources. The authors construct multilingual datasets by translating PopQA and StrategyQA into ten languages while preserving entity mentions (PopQA) and ensuring semantic equivalence. Conflicting evidence snippets are automatically generated and validated. Models are evaluated using Stubborn Rate (SR), Persuasion Rate (PR), and Accuracy (ACC), with an AI-as-Judge (Gemini-2.5-Flash) for flexible multilingual semantic matching. This design isolates the roles of parametric memory, evidence language, and query language in conflict resolution.

## Key Results
- CLEAR reveals a task-dependent split: on StrategyQA, high-resource languages (en, de, zh) exhibit higher Stubborn Rate and dominate persuasion; on PopQA, linguistic affinity supersedes resource abundance, with Latin-script languages (including low-resource ones like af, is, sw) showing high Persuasion Rates even for non-Latin queries.
- The "abundance-weakness paradox" emerges: high-resource non-Latin languages (zh, ja, bn) perform strongly on reasoning persuasion but poorly on correcting entity-centric factual errors, indicating fragile cross-lingual factual alignment.
- Analysis of Task 4 multi-source arbitration identifies two dominant pathways: the Logic-Resource path (reasoning strength scales with training data volume) and the Representation-Affinity path (factual correction driven by script/morphological proximity).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1 – Parametric Memory Activation Bias**  
The query language selectively activates language-specific parametric memories, creating a baseline of uneven knowledge fidelity across languages. Task 1 differences in closed-book correctness reveal that the same fact may be stored more accurately in some linguistic subspaces than others. This activation pattern then biases subsequent evidence integration, causing the model to rely more heavily on memories from the query language and be more susceptible to correction by evidence from linguistically proximate languages.

**Mechanism 2 – Resource-Abundance Hierarchy in Reasoning**  
For reasoning-intensive tasks (StrategyQA), high-resource languages provide dense, resilient logical scaffolds due to abundant training data. Consequently, evidence from these languages can persuade or resist regardless of the query language. This Logic-Resource path explains why high-resource languages dominate persuasion and exhibit higher Stubborn Rate in Task 3/4.

**Mechanism 3 – Representation-Affinity Path for Factual Conflicts**  
For entity-centric factual tasks (PopQA), entity names and their factual associations are stored in a representation space strongly influenced by script and morphology. Languages sharing the target entity's script (typically Latin) have a proximity advantage, enabling them to correct factual errors more effectively. This script barrier means low-resource Latin-script languages can outperform distant high-resource non-Latin languages, decoupling persuasion from resource scale.

## Foundational Learning
- **Parametric vs. External Knowledge Reliance**  
  Why needed: The conflict framework hinges on tension between stored knowledge (parametric) and provided evidence (context). Grasping Stubborn/ Persuasion Rates requires understanding when the model defers to memory versus evidence.  
  Quick check: If a model answers correctly in closed-book (Task 1) but incorrectly when given contradictory context (Task 2), what does that indicate about its reliance on parametric memory?

- **Knowledge Conflict in RAG**  
  Why needed: CLEAR extends monolingual RAG conflict studies to the multilingual setting. Recognizing the two factors (memory strength vs. evidence convincingness) is essential to interpret cross-lingual persuasion patterns.  
  Quick check: In a monolingual RAG system, what two factors primarily determine whether a model is persuaded by a retrieved document? (Hint: strength of memory vs. convincingness of evidence).

- **Linguistic Resource Scales & Affinity**  
  Why needed: The core finding distinguishes training data abundance from linguistic proximity (family/script). Confusing these would obscure the abundance-weakness paradox.  
  Quick check: Why might Japanese (high-resource) be less effective than Swahili (low-resource) at correcting a factual error about a European football team's colors in a Swahili query?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Dataset Construction Engine → Task Orchestrator → Evaluation Metrics Module → Analysis Dashboard

**Critical path:** The core evaluation depends on three linked stages: Dataset Construction (translate benchmarks, generate conflicts) → Task 1 (elicit parametric memory baselines per language) → Task 3/4 (measure cross-lingual persuasion/resistance using the baseline metrics). Errors in Task 1 majority-vote elicitation propagate to all SR/PR calculations.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Translation vs. Native Data: Using translated benchmarks enables controlled cross-lingual comparison but may miss culture-specific nuances and native discourse patterns.  
- Judge Model Reliability: AI-as-Judge evaluation (Gemini) introduces potential bias; exact-match gaps indicate noise in semantic matching.  
- Task Isolation: Decomposing conflict into four tasks cleanly isolates mechanisms but does not capture the continuous, overlapping nature of real-world multilingual RAG.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Script Barrier Manifestation: In PopQA Task 3/4, consistently low Persuasion Rate for high-resource non-Latin sources (zh, ja, bn) when correcting Latin-script entity queries, contrasted with high PR for low-resource Latin sources (af, is, sw).  
- Resource Hierarchy Manifestation: In StrategyQA Task 3/4, Persuasion/Stubborn Rates strongly correlate with language resource level (en, de, zh high; tr, bn, sw low) irrespective of script.  
- Quadrant Collapse: In Task 4 quadrant analysis (Table 3), if accuracy in the "both wrong" (FF) condition is not the lowest, it suggests parametric memory is not the primary anchor, violating a core assumption.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1** – What underlying model mechanisms cause the task-dependent dichotomy? The paper observes the split but does not investigate root causes such as training data distribution, representation geometry, or architectural biases.
- **Open Question 2** – To what extent do the findings generalize beyond the 10 evaluated languages and translated benchmarks? The limitations note language coverage and translation artifacts; native multilingual contexts remain unexplored.
- **Open Question 3** – Why do high-resource non-Latin languages exhibit the abundance-weakness paradox? The paradox is described and attributed to script barriers, but specific training or representational factors are not isolated.
- **Open Question 4** – How exactly does query-language priming interact with source-language authority during multi-source conflict resolution? The effect is shown statistically but not mechanistically explained; a predictive model is lacking.

## Limitations
- **Translation artifacts:** Translated benchmarks may not capture native cultural or entity nuances, potentially distorting linguistic affinity measurements.
- **Judge model bias:** AI-as-Judge evaluation (Gemini) introduces unseen biases; exact-match gaps indicate noise in semantic matching.
- **Task decomposition simplification:** Isolating conflict into four progressive tasks may not reflect the continuous, overlapping nature of real-world multilingual RAG.

## Confidence
- **Dual-pathway claim (resource vs. affinity):** High
- **Script barrier mechanism for PopQA:** High
- **Abundance-weakness paradox generalizability:** Medium
- **Parametric memory unevenness as root cause:** Medium
