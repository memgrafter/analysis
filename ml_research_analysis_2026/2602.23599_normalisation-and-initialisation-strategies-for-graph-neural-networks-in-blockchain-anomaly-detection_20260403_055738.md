# Normalisation and Initialisation Strategies for Graph Neural Networks in Blockchain Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23599
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23599
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: GraphSAGE with Xavier initialization achieves AUPRC 0.6678; GAT best with GraphNorm+Xavier (AUPRC 0.6568); GCN baseline best (AUPRC 0.5993).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the impact of weight initialization and graph-specific normalization strategies on the training of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for blockchain anomaly detection. Using the Elliptic Bitcoin dataset with temporal splits, the authors conduct a systematic ablation study across three popular GNN architectures: GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE. Evaluation focuses on AUPRC due to extreme class imbalance (2% illicit transactions). Results reveal architecture-dependent optimal configurations: GraphSAGE performs best with Xavier initialization alone, GAT gains the most from combining GraphNorm with Xavier, while GCN shows limited sensitivity and its baseline configuration remains strongest. The study underscores the necessity of tailoring initialization and normalization choices to the specific GNN architecture and dataset characteristics, particularly in heterophilic, degree-skewed graphs like Elliptic.

## Method Summary
The authors employ the Elliptic Bitcoin transaction graph, comprising 166-dimensional node features and directed payment edges, which are undirected and augmented with self-loops. To prevent temporal leakage, the dataset is split by timesteps: first 29 for training, next 10 for validation, and last 10 for testing. Models are trained in full-batch mode (preserving multi-hop dependencies) using Adam with hyperparameters tuned via Optuna. The GNN architectures tested are GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE, each with 1–3 layers and hidden dimensions of 128–256. Stabilization techniques include GraphNorm (applied after each GNN layer before activation) and dropout. Weight initialization is varied: default framework resets versus Xavier uniform for the GNN layers. The loss function is weighted binary cross-entropy to address class imbalance. Performance is primarily measured by AUPRC on the test set, with bootstrapping for robust estimates. The experimental protocol systematically ablates initialization and normalization across architectures.

## Key Results
- GraphSAGE achieves its peak AUPRC of 0.6678 using Xavier initialization alone, representing a +0.013 improvement over its baseline.
- GAT shows the largest absolute gain, with AUPRC rising from 0.6022 (baseline) to 0.6568 (+0.055) when combining GraphNorm with Xavier initialization.
- GCN exhibits limited sensitivity; its best AUPRC is 0.5993 with the baseline configuration (no modifications), and both Xavier and GraphNorm+Xavier yield lower scores.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Xavier initialization stabilises training and improves convergence for GraphSAGE by maintaining variance propagation through its mean/max-pooling aggregators under the Elliptic dataset's degree-skewed heterophily.
- **Mechanism:** GraphSAGE's fixed-degree sampling and aggregation operators (mean/max) are sensitive to the scale of incoming signals. Xavier initialization (`uniform(-√6/fan_in, √6/fan_in)`) sets initial weights to preserve activation variance across layers, preventing early explosion or vanishing during the mean-aggregation steps. This yields a more stable gradient flow in the first epochs, allowing the optimiser to find a better basin before the influence of severe class imbalance (2% illicit) dominates the loss landscape.
- **Core assumption:** The variance-preserving property of Xavier is particularly beneficial for architectures without learned attention weights that might self-normalise, such as GraphSAGE's aggregators.
- **Evidence anchors:**

- **Break condition:** If the graph's neighbourhood degree distribution were homogeneous, or if GraphSAGE used LSTM-aggregators with gating, the variance-preserving effect of Xavier might be less critical, and Kaiming initialization could be preferable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** GraphNorm combined with Xavier initialization stabilises GAT's attention mechanism under high-degree node skew, preventing attention collapse and improving AUPRC.
- **Mechanism:** GAT's learned attention weights are computed per edge, making them highly sensitive to the scale and distribution of neighbour features. On the Elliptic graph, a few high-degree illicit nodes can dominate neighbouring feature statistics, causing attention weights to become uniform or saturated. GraphNorm normalises node features *per graph* (subtracting graph mean, dividing by graph std), which re-centres and rescales the aggregated neighbourhood representations before attention computation. This ensures that attention operates on features with a more consistent scale across nodes of differing degrees, allowing the learned attention to reflect relational relevance rather than degree artefacts. Xavier initialization provides a stable starting point for the attention weight parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The primary failure mode for GAT on this dataset is attention instability due to feature scale variance induced by heterogeneous node degrees, not the attention mechanism itself.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "GAT benefits most from combining GraphNorm with Xavier (AUPRC 0.6568)"
  - [section 4, Table 2] GAT's AUPRC increases from 0.6022 (baseline) to 0.6568 (+0.055) with GraphNorm+Xavier, the largest absolute gain.
  - [section 5] "GraphNorm combined with Xavier initialisation produced the largest gains for GAT... graph-level normalisation helps stabilise attention-based aggregation under the skewed degree distribution"
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; neighbor papers on GNNs for time series or subgraph learning do not address normalization for attention. Assumption: the mechanism is inferred from the reported performance uplift and the described purpose of GraphNorm.
- **Break condition:** If the transaction graph exhibited low degree skew (e.g., artificially regularised), or if attention were normalised internally (e.g., via softmax with temperature), the marginal benefit of GraphNorm would diminish.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Graph Heterophily & Degree Skew**
  - Why needed: The Elliptic Bitcoin graph is expected to be heterophilic (illicit nodes connect to both illicit and licit nodes) and highly degree-skewed (a few hubs). These properties destabilise vanilla GNN message passing, making training dynamics (initialization, normalization) critically important.
  - Quick check: How would you expect a GNN's performance to differ on a homophilic vs. heterophilic graph of similar degree distribution, and why might this affect the optimal initialization strategy?
- **Concept: AUPRC vs. AUC-ROC for Imbalanced Evaluation**
  - Why needed: The dataset has 2% illicit labels. The paper explicitly chooses AUPRC as the primary metric because it is sensitive to performance on the positive (minority) class across all thresholds, whereas AUC-ROC can be deceptively high.
  - Quick check: If a model achieves an AUC-ROC of 0.95 but an AUPRC of 0.30 on a 1% positive rate dataset, what does this discrepancy suggest about its practical utility for an analyst?
- **Concept: Full-Graph vs. Sampled Training**
  - Why needed: The paper uses full-graph training to preserve multi-hop structural and temporal dependencies. This design decision interacts with normalization: GraphNorm is applied globally across the entire graph in each epoch.
  - Quick check: What is a key risk of using neighbour sampling (like GraphSAGE's inductive training) with GraphNorm, compared to full-graph training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input (166-dim features + edges) → GNN layer (GCNConv/GATConv/SAGEConv) → GraphNorm → Dropout → ReLU → (repeat 1–3 layers) → Linear classifier (2 outputs) → Weighted BCE loss.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Data prep: Load Elliptic, enforce temporal split (train: first 29 timesteps, val: next 10, test: last 10). Never random-split.
  2. Model definition: Wrap chosen GNN layer in a module that applies GraphNorm and Dropout in sequence.
  3. Init policy: For experiments, manually override reset_parameters() of GNN layer's weight matrices to Xavier uniform if testing initialization effect. For baselines, use defaults.
  4. Training loop: Full-batch Adam (Optuna-tuned LR). Compute loss only on labeled nodes (masking unknown). Log validation AUPRC.
  5. Evaluation: On test set, compute AUPRC via sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score on illicit-positive predictions. Bootstrapping (50% subsample, 100 reps) optional.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Full-graph vs. Sampling: Full-graph preserves all dependencies but uses high memory (~16GB GPU for Elliptic). Sampling reduces memory but may lose multi-hop AML patterns and alters GraphNorm's global stats.
  - AUPRC focus vs. F1: AUPRC is threshold-free and imbalance-robust but less interpretable; F1 at high-confidence thresholds is actionable but requires threshold selection.
  - GraphNorm placement: Applied after GNN layer (before activation). Applying before message passing would normalize input features, a different untested variant.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Over-smoothing: All embeddings become nearly identical (vanishing AUPRC). Mitigate by reducing layers (1–3) or using GraphNorm (but only for GAT per paper).
  - Temporal leakage: Validation AUPRC > test AUPRC by >0.1. Check split uses time step strictly; no edges crossing splits.
  - Initialization sensitivity: Large variance in final AUPRC across random seeds (std > 0.02). May indicate unstable training; try Xavier init or lower LR.
  - GraphNorm collapse: AUPRC plummets when adding GraphNorm (as for GCN/GraphSAGE). Disable it.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline replication: Train each architecture (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) with default initialization and no GraphNorm. Use hyperparameters from Table 1. Confirm approximate AUPRCs: GCN ~0.60, GAT ~0.60, GraphSAGE ~0.655.
  2. Xavier initialization test: For GraphSAGE and GAT, replace GNN layer weight init with Xavier uniform (torch.nn.init.xavier_uniform_). Keep no GraphNorm. Observe if GraphSAGE AUPRC improves to ~0.667 and GAT changes modestly.
  3. GraphNorm addition: For the GAT model from Exp 2, add GraphNorm after each GNN layer. Retrain (seeded). Observe if AUPRC jumps to ~0.656. Also test adding GraphNorm to GCN/GraphSAGE to confirm degradation or minimal change.

## Limitations
- Results are restricted to the Elliptic Bitcoin dataset; generalizability to other blockchain networks or anomaly tasks is unknown.
- Only three GNN architectures were evaluated; other message-passing variants may behave differently.
- Proposed mechanisms are post-hoc interpretations without direct empirical validation (e.g., measuring activation variance or attention distributions).
- No statistical significance tests or multi-seed variance reporting, undermining confidence in improvement magnitudes.
- Full-graph training was used; the impact under neighbor sampling remains untested.

## Confidence
- Architecture-specific optima exist: Medium
- Xavier initialization stabilizes GraphSAGE: Medium-Low
- GraphNorm stabilizes GAT attention: Medium-Low
- GCN insensitivity due to built-in normalization: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Activation variance profiling: For each architecture and configuration (default/Xavier ± GraphNorm), compute mean and variance of node embeddings after every GNN layer on the training set. Verify whether Xavier preserves more stable variance for GraphSAGE.
2. Multi-seed significance testing: Repeat key experiments (GraphSAGE+Xavier, GAT+GraphNorm+Xavier) across 10 random seeds; report mean AUPRC ± 95% CI and perform paired t-tests versus baselines.
3. Cross-dataset replication: Run the same ablation on the Elliptic2 dataset or a synthetic graph with controllable degree heterogeneity to test generalizability of the identified architecture-initialization/normalization pairings.
