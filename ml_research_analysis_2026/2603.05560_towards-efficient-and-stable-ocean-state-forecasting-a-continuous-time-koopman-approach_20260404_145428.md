# Towards Efficient and Stable Ocean State Forecasting: A Continuous-Time Koopman Approach
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05560
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05560
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** CT-KAE achieves bounded error growth (λ=-0.0267) and ~300× speedup over a numerical solver in 2083-day quasi-geostrophic rollouts while preserving large-scale statistical invariants.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the instability of long-horizon ocean state forecasting when using autoregressive machine learning models, which suffer from error accumulation. It proposes the Continuous-Time Koopman Autoencoder (CT-KAE), a method that projects nonlinear ocean dynamics into a latent space governed by a linear ordinary differential equation. By solving this latent ODE via matrix exponentiation, CT-KAE enables temporally resolution-invariant forecasts with stable error growth. Evaluated on a two-layer quasi-geostrophic system, the model demonstrates bounded error (λ=-0.0267) versus diverging error in a Vision Transformer baseline, maintains key statistical invariants like energy spectra, and operates orders of magnitude faster than traditional numerical solvers. The work shows that continuous-time Koopman modeling can yield both efficient and stable ocean forecasting.

## Method Summary
CT-KAE uses two convolutional encoders to ingest the current and previous ocean state fields, merging their features into a 128-dimensional latent vector. The latent dynamics follow a learned linear operator **K**, decomposed into a skew-symmetric part (conservative) and a symmetric negative definite part (dissipative). Training employs short trajectory segments (10 steps) with a composite loss that combines reconstruction, prediction, latent whitening, and physical constraints (Sobelov and spectral losses) via RK4 integration. For long-term forecasts, the model computes the latent state at any future time exactly as **z(τ) = exp(Kτ) z(0)** and decodes it, avoiding recursive errors. This continuous-time formulation inherently supports prediction at unseen temporal resolutions and, combined with the operator decomposition, promotes stable statistical behavior.

## Key Results
- **Bounded error growth:** Over 2083-day rollouts, CT-KAE achieves a negative error growth rate λ=-0.0267, indicating stable error, while a Vision Transformer baseline shows positive λ=0.0217 (diverging error).
- **Statistical invariant preservation:** The model retains low-wavenumber kinetic energy spectra and enstrophy with controlled attenuation of fine-scale structures, preventing energy blow-up.
- **Efficiency:** Inference is approximately 300× faster than a pseudpectral quasi-geostrophic solver, enabling extensive long-horizon simulations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Bounded error growth via linear latent ODE.**  
CT-KAE embeds states into a latent space where dynamics obey dz/dt = Kz. The learned matrix **K** has eigenvalues constrained (by training and decomposition) to non-positive real parts, ensuring perturbations in latent space do not grow exponentially. The decoder translates this latent stability to physical space, assuming it does not introduce excessive sensitivity. *Evidence:* Reported negative λ vs. positive λ for ViT. *Break condition:* Decoder nonlinearities could amplify small latent perturbations into large physical changes, breaking the stability guarantee.

**Mechanism 2: Temporal resolution invariance from continuous-time formulation.**  
Training minimizes prediction error over short, fixed-Δt segments using RK4, but inference uses the exact solution **z(τ)=exp(Kτ)z(0)** for any τ. This works if **K** accurately approximates the continuous-time generator of the true dynamics; otherwise, overfitting to the training Δt’s integration errors may cause deviations for distant τ. *Evidence:* RMSE remains stable across 1h, 5h, and 10h predictions without retraining. *Break condition:* If **K** overfits the training step size, performance may degrade for τ far from the training Δt.

**Mechanism 3: Controlled dissipation via operator decomposition.**  
**K** is split into **K_skew** (skew-symmetric, preserves an energy-like norm) and **K_sym** (symmetric negative definite, introduces dissipation). This selectively attenuates high-wavenumber (fine-scale) modes while protecting low-wavenumber (large-scale) vortices, mimicking physical subgrid dissipation. *Evidence:* Kinetic energy spectra show bulk energy preservation at low wavenumbers and gradual attenuation at high wavenumbers; negative drift in KE/enstrophy. *Break condition:* If **K_sym** mistakenly classifies large-scale modes as dissipative, essential climatological features could be lost.

## Foundational Learning
- **Koopman Operator Theory:** The infinite-dimensional linear operator that evolves observables of a nonlinear dynamical system. *Why needed:* CT-KAE approximates this operator with a finite-dimensional linear ODE in a learned latent space. *Quick check:* Why can’t we compute the Koopman operator directly for high-dimensional systems like QG? (It is infinite-dimensional and requires an appropriate basis, which is impractical for high-D systems.)
- **Continuous-Time vs. Discrete-Time Dynamics:** The shift from discrete maps (z_{t+1}=K_d z_t) to continuous ODEs (dz/dt=Kz). *Why needed:* The continuous formulation enables prediction at arbitrary τ via matrix exponential and avoids error accumulation from fixed-step integration. *Quick check:* What mathematical property of exp(Kτ) allows prediction at any τ? (It is the exact solution to the linear ODE dz/dt=Kz, defined for all τ.)
- **Stability vs. Accuracy Trade-offs in Chaotic Systems:** Pointwise trajectory accuracy is impossible beyond the Lyapunov time; the meaningful goal becomes capturing invariant measures (statistics). *Why needed:* CT-KAE explicitly prioritizes stable statistics over short-term trajectory matching. *Quick check:* Why is anomaly correlation (ACC) near zero at 2083 days not necessarily a failure? (Because the forecast may still capture the climatological distribution, even if the specific trajectory is uncorrelated.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Dual CNN encoders (E_pres, E_hist) -> averaged latent z_t -> linear operator K (dz/dt=Kz) with skew-symmetric/symmetric decomposition -> decoder D -> reconstructed state.

**Critical path:**  
1. Train encoders and decoder to autoencode short sequences.  
2. Learn **K** such that RK4 integration of dz/dt=Kz matches next-state targets.  
3. Apply spectral and Sobolev losses to align decoded gradients/spectra with truth.  
4. At evaluation, compute z_τ = exp(Kτ) z_0 and decode.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Latent dimension d=128: lower d speeds inference but risks reconstruction error; higher d improves fidelity but increases exp(Kτ) cost (O(d³)).  
- Operator decomposition: constraining K_sym negative definite aids stability but may limit representation of growing modes.  
- Loss weights (α, β, γ): heavy L_phys (Sobolev/spectral) stabilizes spectra but may blur fine details if over-weighted.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Exploding latent norms: eigenvalues of **K** with positive real parts (inspect during training).  
- Spectral collapse: decoded KE spectrum overly concentrated at a single wavenumber (latent dimension too low or L_latent weight insufficient).  
- Resolution sensitivity: RMSE degrades significantly at τ=1h vs. τ=10h (possible overfitting to training Δt).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity check:** Train on a single short trajectory. Verify that exp(Kτ) rollout matches RK4 integration (close match) and autoencoder reconstruction error is low (<1% normalized MSE).  
2. **Resolution invariance test:** Train at Δt=5h, evaluate at Δt=1h and 10h. Compute RMSE difference; if >5% increase at 1h, K is not continuous-time generalizable.  
3. **Long-horizon stability:** Run a 10,000-step rollout. Plot MSE and KE drift. If MSE grows exponentially or KE drift is strongly positive (>10%), inspect K’s eigenvalues (should have max real part <0) and check L_phys activity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can CT-KAE be modified to better capture high-frequency turbulent structures while maintaining long-horizon stability? *Basis:* Conclusion states this as future work; current architecture and losses may inherently damp fine scales. *Why unresolved:* No modifications proposed. *Resolving evidence:* Architectural changes (e.g., higher latent dim, nonlinear corrections) or loss adjustments that reduce fine-scale dissipation without increasing λ.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent do the bounded error growth and statistical invariant preservation transfer to more complex ocean models or real-world data? *Basis:* Study uses only idealized two-layer QG. *Why unresolved:* Real oceans have additional physics (stratification, coastlines) and higher-dimensional attractors; finite-dimensional linear latent ODE may not generalize. *Resolving evidence:* Application to midsize realistic models (e.g., MITgcm) or reanalysis datasets (e.g., GLORYS) showing similar stability over multi-year rollouts.

**Open Question 3:** Does temporal resolution invariance extend to spatial resolution invariance? *Basis:* Paper tests temporal but not spatial invariance; encoders/decoders are convolutional and likely resolution-dependent. *Why unresolved:* Latent dynamics are resolution-independent, but encoding/decoding mappings are trained on a specific spatial grid. *Resolving evidence:* Train on coarse resolution (e.g., 32×32) and evaluate directly on higher-resolution data (e.g., 128×128) using the fixed decoder.

## Limitations
- Exact QG solver implementation and data generation pipeline are unspecified (e.g., stationary turbulence criteria, downsampling), blocking faithful dataset reproduction.
- Architectural details (number of ResBlocks, CBAM configuration, loss weights α/β/γ) are vague, risking implementation drift and hyperparameter sensitivity.
- Evidence is indirect: stability claims rely on λ without rigorous Lyapunov analysis; resolution invariance tested only up to 10h; no ablation on loss terms for statistical preservation.

## Confidence
- **Bounded error growth (λ < 0):** Medium (supported by reported λ vs. ViT but depends on unverified decoder stability and eigenvalue constraints).
- **Temporal resolution invariance:** High for tested Δt (1-10h), Medium for extrapolation beyond training horizon (potential K overfitting to Δt).
- **Statistical invariant preservation:** Medium (KE spectra positive but sensitive to L_phys weighting and correctness of K decomposition).

## Next Checks
1. **Eigenvalue Stability Audit:** After training, compute full eigenvalue spectrum of **K**. Verify all real parts ≤ 0 and max real part significantly negative (e.g., < -0.01). Cross-check with λ from Eq 5 for consistency.
2. **Resolution Stress Test:** Train with Δt=5h, evaluate rollouts at τ=1h, 5h, 10h, and τ=20h. RMSE difference at τ=20h should be minimal (<5%) to affirm continuous-time generalization.
3. **Dissipation Mechanism Probe:** Ensure K_sym is negative definite (e.g., via Cholesky). Plot wavenumber-resolved KE decay; confirm low-k preservation and monotonic high-k attenuation. Ablate L_phys loss to quantify its impact on spectra.
