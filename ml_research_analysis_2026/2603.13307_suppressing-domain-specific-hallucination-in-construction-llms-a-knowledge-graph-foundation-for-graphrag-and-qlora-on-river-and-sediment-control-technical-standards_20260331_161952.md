# Suppressing Domain-Specific Hallucination in Construction LLMs: A Knowledge Graph Foundation for GraphRAG and QLoRA on River and Sediment Control Technical Standards

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13307
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13307
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: An 8B QLoRA fine-tuned model achieves a judge score of 2.92/3 on a 100-question benchmark, surpassing a 20B plain baseline (2.29) and a 20B GraphRAG system (2.62), with 3× lower latency and 92% score-3 rate.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles domain-specific hallucination in LLMs when answering technical questions from Japan's River and Sediment Control Technical Standards. It constructs a manual Neo4j knowledge graph (200 nodes, 268 relations) to generate 715 QA pairs for QLoRA fine-tuning of an 8B model (Swallow-8B-Instruct). Evaluated on a 100-question benchmark, the fine-tuned 8B model (Case B) outperforms a 20B plain baseline (Case A) and a 20B GraphRAG system (Case C) in accuracy (judge score 2.92 vs 2.29 and 2.62), achieves 3× lower latency, and attains a 92% score-3 rate with zero low-quality responses. The work demonstrates that domain-specific fine-tuning can decisively outperform larger plain models and retrieval augmentation in this regulatory domain task.

## Method Summary
The authors manually extract a property graph from the technical standards Markdown documents, capturing both document structure (Standard→Chapter→Section→Item) and domain semantics (FacilityType, HazardType, etc.). This graph serves two purposes: (1) it seeds the generation of 715 domain-specific QA pairs by prompting a 20B model with graph edges, and (2) it provides the retrieval context for a GraphRAG system via Cypher queries. The QA pairs are used to fine-tune Swallow-8B-Instruct via QLoRA (4-bit quantisation, LoRA on 7 projection layers, 3 epochs). The fine-tuned model is exported to GGUF and served via Ollama. Three inference cases are evaluated: Case A (direct 20B baseline), Case B (fine-tuned 8B), Case C (20B with GraphRAG retrieval). Performance is judged by Qwen2.5-14B on a 100-question test set using a 0–3 rubric.

## Key Results
- Case B (8B QLoRA) achieves average judge score 2.92/3, significantly higher than Case A (20B plain, 2.29) and Case C (20B GraphRAG, 2.62).
- Case B’s score-3 rate is 92% with zero score-0/1 responses, while Case A has 28% low-quality and Case C has 13%.
- Case B runs at 3× lower latency (14.2s vs 42.2s for Case C) and demonstrates that domain alignment via fine-tuning outweighs scale for this task.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: QLoRA fine-tuning internalises regulatory knowledge
- **Claim**: QLoRA fine-tuning suppresses domain-specific hallucination by internalising the regulatory knowledge structure into model weights, enabling stable, concise, and accurate generation without runtime retrieval.
- **Mechanism**: The manually constructed Neo4j knowledge graph (200 nodes, 268 relations) generates 715 domain-specific QA pairs. These pairs fine-tune an 8B model via QLoRA, injecting low-rank adapters that align the model's output distribution with the patterns, terminology, and factual constraints of the technical standards. At inference, the fine-tuned model generates answers directly from its weights, bypassing retrieval latency and avoiding retrieval-induced context errors.
- **Core assumption**: The synthetic QA pairs sufficiently capture the diversity and factual specificity of the test questions, and the base model has adequate Japanese language capacity.
- **Evidence anchors**: Case B achieves judge score 2.92/3, surpassing both 20B baseline (2.29) and GraphRAG (2.62), with 92% score-3 rate and zero low-quality responses (Section 7.1, 9.1). The paper notes "Specialisation over Scale".
- **Break condition**: If the knowledge graph’s relation coverage is incomplete or synthetic QA generation fails to capture nuanced regulatory language (e.g., exception clauses), the fine-tuned model may still hallucinate on uncovered topics, as hinted by underperformance in the Planning category.

### Mechanism 2: GraphRAG grounds generation in verified facts but is bounded by graph recall
- **Claim**: GraphRAG reduces hallucination by grounding the LLM’s generation in retrieved, graph-verified factual context from the standards, but its effectiveness is bounded by graph recall and retrieval precision.
- **Mechanism**: At inference, five parallel Neo4j Cypher queries (fulltext, facility-hazard, maintenance flows, etc.) retrieve relevant subgraphs based on question keywords. Retrieved nodes/relations are scored, deduplicated, truncated to 2,000 characters, and prepended to the prompt. This constrains the LLM’s output space to graph-grounded facts, mitigating confident confabulation.
- **Core assumption**: The knowledge graph has sufficient recall for the test questions, and the Cypher queries effectively map natural language questions to graph structures without over-retrieving irrelevant nodes.
- **Evidence anchors**: GraphRAG (Case C) improves over baseline by +0.33, reducing low-quality responses from 28% to 13% (abstract). It converts many score-1 answers into score-3 by supplying specific thresholds and references, but 10% remain score-2 due to context truncation or marginal relevance (Section 7.2). Adaptive retry (doubling TOP_K on <25 hits) recovers context in 96/100 questions (Section 5.3).
- **Break condition**: Low graph coverage for a query (e.g., Planning category where C<A) leads to sparse/off-topic retrieval, causing partially correct or irrelevant answers. Over-retrieval of structural nodes can also mislead.

### Mechanism 3: Knowledge graph as dual-use foundation with scalability limits
- **Claim**: The knowledge graph serves as a unified foundational infrastructure that enables both data-efficient fine-tuning (via synthetic QA generation) and interpretable retrieval (via Cypher queries), but its manual construction limits scalability and recall.
- **Mechanism**: The property graph encodes both document structure and domain semantics with cross-links. For fine-tuning, graph edges seed LLM-generated QA pairs. For GraphRAG, the same graph is queried at runtime. This dual-use ensures both systems are grounded in the same curated schema.
- **Core assumption**: The manually extracted graph accurately and comprehensively represents the regulatory content, such that relations correspond to testable facts.
- **Evidence anchors**: Graph formalisation: 200 nodes, 9 types, 268 relations, 11 relation types (Section 4). QA pairs are generated per triple (Eq. 12) using the 20B model with graph context (Section 5.4.1). GraphRAG queries explicitly traverse structural and semantic relations (Section 5.3). The paper notes the limitation: manual construction would dramatically scale coverage via automation (Section 10.3).
- **Break condition**: Incomplete manual curation leaves gaps (e.g., PRECEDES relations only 7 pairs, 1% of training data), causing under-representation of certain reasoning patterns (e.g., process order) in fine-tuning and poor retrieval.

## Foundational Learning
- **QLoRA (Quantised Low-Rank Adaptation)**: *Why needed*: Enables domain adaptation of an 8B model on a single 16GB GPU by injecting trainable low-rank matrices into a frozen 4-bit quantised base, making fine-tuning feasible locally. *Quick check*: What hyperparameter controls the rank of the low-rank update matrices in LoRA, and why is a low rank (e.g., r=16) sufficient for domain adaptation?
- **GraphRAG**: *Why needed*: Augments a general LLM with structured, relational retrieval from a domain knowledge graph to inject specific regulatory facts and reduce hallucination without retraining. *Quick check*: How does the adaptive retry mechanism in this paper’s GraphRAG pipeline handle queries that yield insufficient graph hits?
- **Property Knowledge Graph Schema Design**: *Why needed*: The graph’s dual structure (document hierarchy + domain semantics) allows both fine-tuning data generation (from edges) and precise multi-hop retrieval (via Cypher), ensuring consistency between training and inference grounding. *Quick check*: What are the two main node categories (V_S and V_D) and how do cross-link relations (DESCRIBED_IN, DEFINED_IN) connect them?
- **Japanese LLM Adaptation (Swallow family)**: *Why needed*: The technical standards are in Japanese; general models lack domain vocabulary and cultural-regulatory context. Swallow models are continued-pre-trained on Japanese corpora. *Quick check*: Why might a general Japanese LLM still hallucinate on technical standards despite language capability?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Source Markdown documents → Knowledge graph builder (01_extract_entities.py, 02_load_neo4j.py) → Neo4j KG (200 nodes, 268 relations) → { (1) Training data generator → 715 QA pairs → QLoRA trainer (Swallow-8B-Instruct, unsloth) → GGUF exporter (llama-quantize.exe) → Ollama serve → Case B; (2) GraphRAG query engine (5 parallel Cypher queries, scoring, adaptive retry, truncation) → Case C } → Evaluator (FastAPI, Qwen2.5-14B judge). Case A: Base Swallow-20B → Ollama.

**Critical path**: The knowledge graph construction is the single point of failure; errors in its schema or coverage propagate to both Case B (training data) and Case C (retrieval).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Fine-tuning vs. Retrieval: Fine-tuning (B) gives higher accuracy and speed but requires retraining for updates; retrieval (C) allows dynamic updates but adds latency and depends on graph completeness.
- Model scale: Smaller fine-tuned (8B) beats larger plain (20B) in this domain, but larger models may still excel at precise numerical memorisation.
- Graph scope: Manual curation ensures high precision but limits recall (200 nodes). Automated extraction could scale but risks noise.
- Context length: 2,000-char truncation in Case C improves speed but discards specific clauses, causing score-2 failures.

**Failure signatures**:
- Data generation: `json.loads()` errors from extra text after JSON array (fix: `json.JSONDecoder().raw_decode`); LLM timeouts from `num_predict=-1` (fix: limit to 2048).
- Training: Triton hang on Windows with `packing=True` (set `packing=False`); unsloth GGUF export hang (use `llama-quantize.exe` directly).
- GraphRAG: Sparse hits (<25) trigger adaptive retry; low score in Planning category suggests over-retrieval of Chapter nodes.
- Inference: Without repeat penalty, Case A enters phrase repetition loops (mitigate with `repeat_penalty=1.2`).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Reproduce Case A baseline: Run 100 test questions through Swallow-20B via Ollama with `repeat_penalty=1.2`; verify average ~2.29 and 28% low-quality rate.
2. Test GraphRAG retrieval quality: Manually inspect top-5 retrieved nodes for 10 diverse questions; check if context contains specific standards citations and thresholds. Tune `GRAPH_TOP_K` and `GRAPH_RERANK_RATIO` to optimise precision/recall trade-off.
3. Validate QLoRA convergence: Train on a 100-pair stratified subset (Section 5.4.1); confirm final loss ~0.7958 and monotonic decrease across 100→250→500→715 pairs before full training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Hybrid architecture (Case D)**: Does combining QLoRA fine-tuning with GraphRAG retrieval achieve higher accuracy than QLoRA alone? The paper proposes this as the highest-priority future experiment.
2. **Automated knowledge graph extraction**: Can LLM-based automated extraction from Markdown standards match manual curation quality for downstream QA tasks?
3. **Adversarial benchmark**: Would a larger, adversarial benchmark (500 questions with paraphrasing and multi-hop reasoning) reveal different relative performance between QLoRA, GraphRAG, and plain LLMs?
4. **Generalisability**: Is the performance inversion (8B QLoRA > 20B plain) generalizable to other technical domains and languages? The evaluation is Japanese-only.
5. **Judge reliability**: Does reliance on a single LLM-as-judge (Qwen2.5-14B) introduce systematic bias in domain-specific answer evaluation?

## Limitations
- **Graph coverage & bias**: The manually constructed KG (200 nodes) is small; its completeness for the regulatory domain is unverified, with key gaps possibly affecting the Planning category.
- **Synthetic data fidelity**: The 715 training QA pairs are LLM-generated from graph edges; their quality, diversity, and alignment with the test set are unknown.
- **Benchmark scope & generalisability**: Evaluation uses a single 100-question test set from one Japanese standard; generalisability to other standards, domains, or languages is unestablished.

## Confidence
- **Primary result (B > A & C)**: High
- **Mechanism 1 (Fine-tuning suppresses hallucination)**: Medium
- **Mechanism 2 (GraphRAG's bounded effectiveness)**: Medium
- **Mechanism 3 (KG as dual-use foundation)**: Medium-High
- **Generalisability beyond this domain**: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Graph Recall Audit**: Systematically map the 100 test questions to the KG to quantify coverage and identify missing relation types or clause-level details.
2. **Training Data Ablation**: Re-train Case B using subsets of the synthetic QA (only high-degree nodes, random subset, human-written) to isolate impact of graph topology vs. data quality.
3. **Cross-Domain Transfer Test**: Apply the identical Case B pipeline to a different Japanese technical standard (e.g., Building Standard Law) to test if performance gains persist.
