# OSIL: Learning Offline Safe Imitation Policies with Safety Inferred from Non-preferred Trajectories

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv**: 2602.11018
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11018
- **References**: 40
- **Result**: OSIL achieves near-Constrained-RL safety with high return, outperforming baselines up to 2.8× in normalized cost on MuJoCo velocity and navigation tasks.

## Executive Summary
OSIL addresses offline safe imitation learning without explicit reward or cost annotations by leveraging non-preferred trajectories alongside a union dataset of high-return trajectories with varying costs. The method reformulates the problem as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), deriving a lower bound on reward performance using the union dataset policy to avoid reward labels. Safety is inferred by training a cost model on non-preferred trajectories via contrastive and preference losses, which estimates the likelihood of unsafe behavior. The policy is then optimized with a Lagrangian relaxation combining behavior cloning and a learned cost critic, using an adaptive penalty to enforce constraints. On MuJoCo velocity and navigation tasks, OSIL attains normalized cost comparable to oracle Constrained-RL while preserving high return, and outperforms baselines like SafeDICE and PPL by up to 2.8× in safety metrics.

## Method Summary
OSIL consists of three integrated steps. First, it theoretically lower-bounds the reward objective by the performance of the union dataset policy minus a KL-divergence term, enabling CMDP reformulation without reward labels. Second, it learns a cost model from non-preferred and union trajectories: an encoder maps state-action pairs to embeddings trained with contrastive loss (to capture temporal adjacency) and preference loss (to enforce higher cumulative cost for non-preferred trajectories). Third, it optimizes the policy via a Lagrangian loss that combines behavior cloning on the union dataset with a cost-critic term; an adaptive penalty increases when the policy's actions are riskier than the dataset actions. This approach effectively infers safety from weakly labeled data and balances performance with constraint satisfaction.

## Key Results
- OSIL attains normalized cost comparable to Constrained-RL (oracle) while maintaining high normalized return.
- Outperforms baseline methods (SafeDICE, PPL) by up to 2.8× in safety (normalized cost).
- Validated across diverse MuJoCo locomotion and navigation tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Lower Bounding Reward Performance via Union Dataset
- **Claim**: The reward performance \(J_r(\pi)\) of any policy \(\pi\) is lower-bounded by the performance of the union dataset policy \(\pi_U\) minus a term involving KL divergence, enabling optimization without reward labels.
- **Mechanism**: Using Lemma 1 (performance decomposition into sum of advantages), bounding advantages with policy coupling and maximum advantage (Lemma 2), and relating total variation to KL divergence (Lemma 3), the bound \(J_r(\pi) \geq J_r(\pi_U) - \frac{2\epsilon}{1-\gamma} \sqrt{D_{\text{max\_KL}}(\pi_U, \pi)}\) is derived. This allows reformulating the CMDP objective to minimize KL divergence to \(\pi_U\).
- **Core assumption**: \(\pi_U\) generates high-return trajectories, and the advantage function is bounded (\(\epsilon\) finite).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "reformulates the CMDP problem by deriving a lower bound on reward maximizing objective"
  - [section 4.2]: Theorem 1 and proof in Appendix A detail the bound derivation.
  - [corpus]: Corpus indicates SafeDICE assumes union dataset contains only low-cost or high-cost trajectories, while OSIL relaxes this, supporting the necessity of a looser bound.
- **Break condition**: If \(\pi_U\) has low return or the union dataset lacks state coverage, the bound becomes loose or invalid, leading to suboptimal policies.

### Mechanism 2: Cost Model via Contrastive and Preference Learning
- **Claim**: A cost model estimating non-preferred likelihood can be trained from non-preferred and union trajectories without explicit cost annotations, using contrastive and preference-based losses.
- **Mechanism**: An encoder \(f\) maps state-action pairs to embeddings; contrastive loss \(L_{\text{cost}}^{\text{cont}}\) pulls temporally adjacent pairs in the same trajectory together. A linear model \(g\) outputs cost \(\tilde{c} = g(f(\cdot))\). Preference loss \(L_{\text{cost}}^{\text{pref}}\) uses Bradley-Terry to enforce \(\sum \gamma^t \tilde{c}(\tau_N) > \sum \gamma^t \tilde{c}(\tau_U)\) for non-preferred \(\tau_N\) vs union \(\tau_U\).
- **Core assumption**: Non-preferred trajectories have higher cumulative cost than union trajectories on average, and temporal adjacency correlates with safety-relevant features.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "learning a cost model that estimates the likelihood of non-preferred behavior"
  - [section 4.1]: Equations 3 (contrastive loss), 4-5 (preference loss), and 6 (joint loss) specify the mechanism.
  - [corpus]: Corpus shows PPL uses preferences for reward learning, but OSIL adapts this for cost inference without reward labels.
- **Break condition**: If non-preferred and union cost distributions overlap significantly or temporal proximity does not capture safety dynamics, the cost model may fail to discriminate, leading to high-cost policies.

### Mechanism 3: Lagrangian Policy Optimization with Adaptive Penalty
- **Claim**: The CMDP constraint is handled via Lagrangian relaxation, combining behavior cloning with a cost-critic term, and an adaptive penalty \(\alpha\) that increases when policy actions are costlier than dataset actions.
- **Mechanism**: Reformulate to minimize average KL divergence: \(\min_\pi -\mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim D_U}[\log \pi(a|s)]\) s.t. \(\mathbb{E}_{s_0\sim D_U}[Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}(s_0, \pi(s_0))] \leq b\). Relax with Lagrangian: \(L_{\text{policy}} = -\mathbb{E}[\log \pi] + \alpha \mathbb{E}[Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}]\). Adaptive \(\alpha = \bar{\alpha} / \frac{1}{N} \sum \exp(Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}(s_0,a_0) - Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}(s_0,\pi(s_0)))\) increases when policy is riskier.
- **Core assumption**: Average KL divergence approximates the max KL constraint, and adaptive \(\alpha\) effectively balances safety and performance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "optimized with a Lagrangian penalty using behavior cloning and the learned cost critic"
  - [section 4.2]: Equations 12-14 define the reformulation and adaptive \(\alpha\).
  - [corpus]: TD3+BC uses similar structure with reward critic; OSIL replaces it with cost critic for safety, as noted in section 4.2.
- **Break condition**: If union dataset state coverage is poor, the KL approximation fails; adaptive \(\alpha\) may oscillate with noisy Q-estimates, causing unstable training or over-penalization.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: The system comprises four main components: (1) Cost Model (encoder \(f\) and linear head \(g\)) trained on partial trajectories from \(D_N\) (non-preferred) and \(D_U\) (union) with contrastive and preference losses; (2) Cost Critic (Q-network \(Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}\)) trained via TD loss on \(D_U\) using the cost model’s \(\tilde{c}\) values; (3) Policy (actor \(\pi\)) trained with a Lagrangian loss combining behavior cloning (negative log-likelihood on \(D_U\)) and the cost-critic term weighted by an adaptive \(\alpha\); (4) Adaptive \(\alpha\) computed from batch statistics of Q-differences between dataset actions and policy actions. Data flow: Cost Model supplies \(\tilde{c}\) to Cost Critic; Cost Critic guides Policy updates; Adaptive \(\alpha\) modulates the penalty; both \(D_N\) and \(D_U\) feed Cost Model, while \(D_U\) feeds Cost Critic and Policy.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Load \(D_N\) (non-preferred) and \(D_U\) (union) without reward/cost labels.
  2. Cost model training: Sample batches from \(D_N\) and \(D_U\), compute joint contrastive and preference loss, update \(f\) and \(g\).
  3. Critic training: Use \(\tilde{c}\) from the cost model to compute TD targets on \(D_U\), update \(Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}\) and its target network.
  4. Policy training: Sample from \(D_U\), compute Lagrangian loss using current \(Q^\pi_{\tilde{c}}\) and adaptive \(\alpha\), update \(\pi\).
  5. Periodically update target Q-network; iterate until convergence.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Partial trajectory length \(H\)**: Shorter \(H\) (e.g., 5) captures local temporal correlations efficiently but may miss long-term safety patterns; longer \(H\) improves modeling but increases computational cost.
  - **Adaptive \(\alpha\)**: Dynamically adjusts the safety-performance tradeoff and responds to policy changes, but requires tuning \(\bar{\alpha}\); a fixed \(\alpha\) is simpler but less responsive.
  - **Contrastive loss inclusion**: Enhances representation learning for the cost model and improves generalization, but adds computational overhead; ablations show cost increases significantly without it.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **High normalized cost**: Cost model fails to assign higher cost to non-preferred trajectories (check contrastive/preference losses), or Q-function underestimates true costs.
  - **Low normalized return**: Over-penalization from \(\alpha\) or insufficient behavior cloning signal (e.g., union dataset lacks high-return examples).
  - **Training instability**: Adaptive \(\alpha\) causing oscillations (monitor \(\alpha\) values), or Q-function divergence (check TD error magnitudes).
  - **Poor generalization**: Union dataset \(D_U\) has limited state coverage, leading to high KL divergence in unseen states.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Performance Comparison** (Section 5.1): Compare OSIL with BC-Union, DWBC, PPL, SafeDICE on six tasks (MuJoCo velocity, navigation). Metrics: normalized return and cost. Result: OSIL achieves cost near Constrained-RL while maintaining return.
  2. **Impact of Non-Preferred Dataset Size** (Section 5.2, Figure 5): Vary \(|D_N|\) in Point-Circle2. Smaller \(|D_N|\) degrades performance, but OSIL outperforms baselines across sizes, indicating reliance on non-preferred coverage.
  3. **Impact of Contrastive Loss** (Section 5.2, Figures 6, 10, 11): Ablate contrastive loss; cost increases significantly across tasks, showing its role in learning effective cost representations.

## Limitations
- The theoretical lower bound may become loose if the union dataset has limited state coverage or suboptimal returns, leading to suboptimal policies.
- The cost model relies on non-preferred trajectories having higher cumulative cost; overlapping cost distributions between non-preferred and union data could cause the model to fail to discriminate.
- Empirical evaluation is limited to six MuJoCo and navigation tasks and lacks independent replications; generalization to more complex domains (e.g., image-based) remains uncertain.

## Confidence
