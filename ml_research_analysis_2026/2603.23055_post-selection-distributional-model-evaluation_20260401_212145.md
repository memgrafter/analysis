# Post-Selection Distributional Model Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.23055
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23055
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PS-DME provides valid post-selection inference for CDFs, controlling FCR at a target level while often achieving narrower confidence bands than sample-splitting baselines, especially with limited data.

## Executive Summary
Post-selection bias occurs when the same data is used both to select models and to evaluate their performance distributions. This paper introduces post-selection distributional model evaluation (PS-DME), a method that constructs confidence bands for the cumulative distribution function of a key performance indicator (KPI) for each selected model. PS-DME leverages e-values to control the false coverage rate (FCR) even under arbitrary data-dependent selection while reusing the full dataset for both selection and evaluation. Theoretical guarantees show FCR ≤ δ, and empirical results on synthetic data, LLM text-to-SQL decoding, and O-RAN network configurations demonstrate that PS-DME maintains nominal FCR and yields tighter bands than the sample-splitting baseline, especially in limited-data regimes. Variants using Berk-Jones statistics further adapt to the tails of the distribution for additionally tighter guarantees.

## Method Summary
PS-DME constructs distributional confidence intervals for selected models after an arbitrary selection rule has been applied to the full dataset. For each selected model, it computes the empirical CDF from all available data and inverts e-value tests based on the Kolmogorov (or Berk-Jones) statistic. The e-value is obtained by applying an e-calibrator to a p-value derived from the DKW inequality. The resulting confidence bands are those for which the e-value does not exceed a threshold \(K/(\delta |\mathcal{K}|)\), thereby accounting for the selection multiplicity and controlling the false coverage rate (FCR) at level δ. A sample-splitting baseline (SS-DME) uses disjoint halves for selection and evaluation, which avoids post-selection correction but suffers from reduced effective sample size. PS-DME’s theoretical analysis shows that when the benefit from full-data reuse outweighs the extra correction factor introduced by the e-calibrator, the resulting bands are narrower than those of SS-DME. The method is applicable to i.i.d. KPI data; extensions to correlated data and multivariate KPIs are left for future work.

## Key Results
- PS-DME achieves valid post-selection inference: the false coverage rate (FCR) is theoretically guaranteed ≤ δ for any selection rule.
- PS-DME produces narrower confidence bands than SS-DME when the gain from full data reuse outweighs the correction term, a condition most often met in small-sample regimes.
- Empirical studies on synthetic ridge regression, LLM text-to-SQL decoding, and O-RAN network configurations confirm FCR control and demonstrate improved (lower) guaranteed KPI values, particularly for extreme outage probabilities. Berk-Jones variants further tighten tail bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: FCR control via e-values
**Claim:** PS-DME achieves valid post-selection inference (FCR ≤ δ) by leveraging e-values, which allow the significance level to be adjusted after seeing the data while maintaining statistical validity.
**Mechanism:** For each selected model, PS-DME constructs confidence bands by inverting e-value tests based on the Kolmogorov statistic. The p-value under the null is derived from the DKW inequality, then converted to an e-value via an e-calibrator. The confidence set includes all CDFs whose e-value is below a threshold K/(δ|K|). This post-hoc thresholding accounts for the data-dependent selection.
**Core assumption:** The p-value constructed from the DKW inequality is valid under the null hypothesis that the candidate CDF equals the true CDF, and the e-calibrator satisfies Definition 1 (nonincreasing, upper semicontinuous, integral ≤1).
**Evidence anchors:** Theorem 1 (Section 3.2) proves FCR ≤ δ for any selection rule and any valid e-calibrator.
**Break condition:** Guarantee fails if the e-calibrator violates Definition 1 (e.g., integral >1) or if the DKW-based p-value is invalid due to unaccounted dependencies (e.g., non-i.i.d. across models).

### Mechanism 2: Narrower bands than sample-splitting
**Claim:** PS-DME can produce narrower confidence bands than sample-splitting (SS-DME) when the gain from full data reuse outweighs the cost of the post-selection correction factor.
**Mechanism:** PS-DME uses the full sample size \(n_k\) for inference, while SS-DME uses only a reduced evaluation sample size \(n_k^{eval}\). The band width for PS-DME includes a correction term \(f^{-1}(K/(\delta|\mathcal{K}|))\) from the e-calibrator, whereas SS-DME uses a simpler \(\sqrt{\log(K/(\delta|\mathcal{K}|))}\) term. PS-DME is narrower when \(n_k^{eval}/n_k\) is small relative to the ratio of the logarithmic terms.
**Core assumption:** The comparison in Lemma 2 holds for a fixed selected set size \(|\mathcal{K}|\); the e-calibrator is from the power family for Corollary 2.
**Evidence anchors:** Lemma 2 gives the exact condition (15) for PS-DME width < SS-DME width; Corollary 2 provides a necessary condition (16) and optimal τ (17). Figure 3 in experiments shows PS-DME yields uniformly lower guaranteed KPI, especially for small n.
**Break condition:** The advantage disappears if the evaluation split in SS-DME is extremely small but the correction factor \(f^{-1}\) is extremely large, e.g., when \(|\mathcal{K}|\) is large relative to δ and K.

### Mechanism 3: Berk-Jones statistics yield adaptive tails
**Claim:** Using Berk-Jones (BJ) statistics instead of DKW within PS-DME yields input-dependent confidence bands that can be tighter in the tails of the CDF.
**Mechanism:** BJ bands are derived by inverting a likelihood ratio test based on Bernoulli KL divergence. The acceptance region leads to asymmetric bands: tighter where the empirical CDF is near 0 or 1, wider near 0.5, thus adapting to the underlying distribution shape.
**Core assumption:** The true CDF \(F\) is continuous, enabling the probability integral transform for the null distribution of the BJ statistic.
**Evidence anchors:** Appendix F details the construction and shows that compared to DKW, the BJ bands are typically tighter in the tails. Figure 7(b) demonstrates better (lower) guaranteed KPI for extreme outage probabilities γ.
**Break condition:** Guarantee may not hold if \(F\) has point masses (discontinuities), as the probability integral transform fails. Computational cost also increases due to root-finding per quantile.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: E-values and e-calibrators.**
- *Why needed:* E-values enable post-hoc significance level adjustment after data-dependent selection, which is central to PS-DME’s FCR control. They are multiplicative supermartingales under the null.
- *Quick check:* How does an e-calibrator transform a p-value into an e-value, and what property must it satisfy to ensure \(E[E] \leq 1\) under the null? (Answer: It is a nonincreasing, upper semicontinuous function with \(\int_0^1 f(u)du \leq 1\).)

**Concept: False Coverage Rate (FCR) vs. Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER).**
- *Why needed:* PS-DME controls FCR (expected proportion of miscovering selected models), which is less stringent than FWER and more appropriate for multiple post-selection inference.
- *Quick check:* Why is FCR a suitable error metric for distributional inference after model selection, as opposed to per-model coverage? (Answer: It allows some errors while bounding the expected fraction, which is more realistic when many models are selected.)

**Concept: DKW inequality and uniform confidence bands for CDFs.**
- *Why needed:* The DKW inequality provides the initial p-value for the Kolmogorov statistic, forming the basis of both SS-DME and PS-DME band constructions.
- *Quick check:* What does the DKW inequality guarantee about the supremum distance between empirical and true CDF, and how is the constant 2 derived? (Answer: \(P(\sup_x |\hat{F}_n(x) - F(x)| > \epsilon) \leq 2 e^{-2n\epsilon^2}\); the factor 2 comes from the union bound over lower and upper tails.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Datasets \(D_k\) for each model → Selection rule \(S(\cdot)\) yields selected set \(\mathcal{K}\) → Empirical CDF estimator \(\hat{F}_k(x)\) (full data for PS-DME or split for SS-DME) → E-value generator: compute p-value via DKW/BJ → apply e-calibrator \(f(\cdot)\) → Band constructor: invert threshold \(K/(\delta|\mathcal{K}|)\) → Output confidence bands \([L_k(x), U_k(x)]\) and guaranteed KPI \(X_k^*\).

**Critical path:** Data \(D\) → Selection \(S(D)\) yields \(\mathcal{K}\) → For each \(k \in \mathcal{K}\), compute \(\hat{F}_k\) → Compute Kolmogorov statistic for candidate CDF → Derive p-value \(p_k(F)\) from DKW → Apply e-calibrator to get e-value \(E_k(F)\) → Invert via threshold to define band \([L_k, U_k]\).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Full reuse vs. correction:* PS-DME uses all data and an e-calibrator correction; SS-DME avoids correction but loses sample size. Choose PS-DME when data is scarce or selection is not overly aggressive.
- *E-calibrator choice:* The power family \(f_\tau(p) = (1-\tau)p^{-\tau}\) with optimal \(\tau\) (derived from selection size) balances width. In practice, \(\tau=1/2\) is a default.
- *DKW vs. Berk-Jones:* DKW gives simple uniform bands; Berk-Jones provides adaptive tails (tighter extremes) but requires continuity and more computation.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Invalid FCR control:* Using a non‑valid e‑calibrator (integral >1) or ignoring dependencies leads to FCR > δ.
- *Overly wide bands:* Extremely small evaluation split in SS‑DME or excessively large correction factor in PS‑DME (when \(|\mathcal{K}|\) ≈ K and δ small).
- *BJ band breakdown:* Presence of point masses in the true CDF can cause under‑coverage for Berk‑Jones bands.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Synthetic linear‑Gaussian ridge regression:** Compare PS‑DME and SS‑DME on confidence band width and best guaranteed KPI across calibration sizes \(n=20\) and \(n=100\).
2. **LLM text‑to‑SQL decoding:** Apply to 43 LLM configurations; PS‑DME yields lower guaranteed loss across outage levels.
3. **O‑RAN network configurations:** Evaluate 18 O‑RAN configs on URLLC buffer occupancy; PS‑DME provides consistently tighter guarantees, especially with limited calibration data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extending to multivariate KPIs:** The current framework handles only a single scalar KPI; extending to multiple correlated performance measures is an open challenge.
- **Sharper confidence bands:** Developing constructions that improve upon DKW and Berk‑Jones bounds, possibly by exploiting problem‑specific structure, remains future work.
- **Integration into downstream decisions:** How to incorporate PS‑DME’s distributional guarantees into automated model deployment or further selection pipelines is not yet addressed.

## Limitations
- The exact e‑calibrator hyperparameter (τ) used in real‑world experiments (LLM, O‑RAN) is not reported, hindering exact reconstruction of the bands.
- Implementation details for Berk‑Jones bands (e.g., root‑finding tolerances, handling of discrete KPIs) are omitted, potentially affecting tail tightness and computational cost.
- Theoretical assumptions (i.i.d. data, continuous CDF for BJ) may be violated in practice, risking coverage failures for BJ variants.

## Confidence
- **PS‑DME controls FCR under arbitrary selection:** High confidence (theorem with proof, grounded in e‑value theory).
- **PS‑DME yields narrower bands than SS‑DME under the identified condition:** Medium confidence (theoretical condition clear but advantage depends on τ and selection set size; empirical support limited to reported settings).
- **Berk‑Jones bands adaptivity:** Medium confidence (derivation assumes continuity; Figure 7 shows tail gains but robustness to discreteness uncertain).
