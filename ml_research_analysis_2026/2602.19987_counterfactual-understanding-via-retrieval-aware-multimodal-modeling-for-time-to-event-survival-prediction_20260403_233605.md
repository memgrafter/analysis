# Counterfactual Understanding via Retrieval-aware Multimodal Modeling for Time-to-Event Survival Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19987
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19987
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: CURE achieves state-of-the-art counterfactual survival prediction with Ctd 0.669/IBS 0.165 on METABRIC and Ctd 0.752/IBS 0.164 on TCGA-LUAD.

## Executive Summary
CURE is a novel framework for counterfactual time-to-event survival prediction that integrates heterogeneous multimodal data—clinical, paraclinical, demographic, and multi-omics—via a cross-attention fusion mechanism and a mixture-of-experts adapter for omics signals. It employs implicit latent subgroup retrieval to disentangle baseline survival risk from treatment-specific effects, enabling individualized counterfactual outcomes without explicit patient matching. Evaluated on METABRIC and TCGA-LUAD, CURE sets new state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating significant gains especially in highly censored TCGA-LUAD data. Ablation studies validate the importance of cross-modal fusion and subgroup retrieval.

The method's two-phase training separately optimizes multimodal representation and survival-specific gating. Key design choices include pairwise attention across modalities and top-k MoE routing, which allow CURE to handle high-dimensional, incomplete omics data and model treatment heterogeneity effectively. Strong empirical results highlight robustness in challenging, real-world survival settings.

## Method Summary
CURE follows a two-phase architecture. First, each modality is encoded into embeddings: omics data passes through a linear bottleneck and a mixture-of-experts (MoE) module to adaptively select relevant molecular features; clinical, paraclinical, and demographic features are embedded via MLPs. Then a cross-attention fusion computes pairwise attention across the three core modalities (clinical, paraclinical, omics), averages the six outputs, and concatenates with demographics to form a patient representation. Second, this representation is fed into two independent gating networks producing categorical distributions over K treatment-response subgroups and M baseline-survival subgroups. A baseline hazard network and treatment-effect parameters combine with the subgroup weights to yield the final survival function under a given treatment. Training uses AdamW for the multimodal phase (lr=5e-4, 200 epochs) and Adam for the survival phase (lr=0.01, 20 epochs), with hyperparameters such as bottleneck dimension 256, MoE experts=4 (top-2 routing), and Transformer 2 layers/4 heads.

## Key Results
- On METABRIC: Ctd 0.669, IBS 0.165, outperforming prior SOTA SA-DGNet (Ctd 0.668, IBS 0.194).
- On TCGA-LUAD: Ctd 0.752, IBS 0.164, substantially beating CMHE (Ctd 0.616, IBS 0.198).
- Ablation studies confirm that removing cross-attention fusion or latent subgroup retrieval significantly degrades performance, validating their essential contributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Cross-modal attention fusion creates a unified patient representation that captures complex, non-linear interactions between heterogeneous clinical, paraclinical, and multi-omics data.
- **Mechanism:** Separate modality-specific embeddings are projected to a shared space. Pairwise cross-attention (e.g., Clinical querying Paraclinical keys/values) is computed for all six modality pairs. The six resulting attention outputs are averaged to produce a fused embedding, which is concatenated with static demographic features.
- **Core assumption:** Survival-relevant information is distributed across modalities and can be discovered through mutual attention, rather than being isolatable within a single modality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...integrate clinical, paraclinical, demographic, and multi-omics information, which are aligned and fused through cross-attention mechanisms."
  - [section 3.1b]: "...employ a pairwise cross-attention fusion mechanism...for each modality, we compute its cross-attention to the other two modalities."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring works focus on survival modeling and interpretability, not this specific multimodal fusion architecture. The mechanism is novel to this paper.
- **Break condition:** If one or more modalities are completely uninformative or severely corrupted, the pairwise attention may fail to produce a coherent fused signal, as suggested by the ablation study showing performance degradation when cross-attention is removed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The mixture-of-experts (MoE) adapter on multi-omics data dynamically filters and refines high-dimensional molecular signals, assigning higher weights to the most prognostically relevant omics components for each patient.
- **Mechanism:** Each omics modality is first compressed via a linear bottleneck. The low-dimensional embeddings are then routed through an MoE module with multiple experts. A gating network produces sample-dependent weights over experts, and the final omics representation is a weighted sum of expert outputs.
- **Core assumption:** Different molecular pathways or biological mechanisms (experts) are relevant for different patients, and a learned gating function can identify this relevance from compressed embeddings.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1a]: "Not all patients have complete omics information, and among available data, not all features are equally informative...a mixture-of-experts approach...to select the most relevant omics information...The gating mechanism dynamically selects and weights experts based on the latent structure of each sample."
  - [section 4.2b (ablation)]: Removing the MoE component ("w/o multi-omics MoE") causes a performance drop, indicating its contributory role beyond simple concatenation.
  - [corpus]: Limited. Neighbors discuss survival models and multi-modal learning generally but not this specific MoE-for-omics-refinement design.
- **Break condition:** If the gating network fails to learn a meaningful mapping (e.g., due to insufficient training data or expert collapse), the model may default to equal expert weighting, reducing its ability to denoise and specialize.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Implicit latent subgroup retrieval disentangles baseline survival risk from treatment-specific response effects, enabling counterfactual prediction without explicit patient matching.
- **Mechanism:** The patient embedding is fed into two separate networks to produce two categorical distributions: one over *baseline survival subgroups* (Φ) and one over *treatment-response subgroups* (Z). These are assumed independent and multiplied to form weights over all (k,m) subgroup pairs. Each pair has a dedicated survival kernel. The final survival function is a weighted sum of these kernels.
- **Core assumption:** Patient heterogeneity in survival outcomes can be explained by a finite set of latent subtypes, where one latent variable governs inherent baseline risk and another governs differential response to treatment. The joint distribution over subtypes is factorable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...implicitly retrieves patient-specific latent subgroups that capture both baseline survival dynamics and treatment-dependent variations."
  - [section 3.2a]: "P(Z=k, Φ=m|x) = P(Z=k|x)P(Φ=m|x)" and "The latent retrieval gate Φ encodes the matching probability...effectively retrieving the subgroup whose latent characteristics best align with the patient’s multimodal profile."
  - [section 4.2c]: PCA visualizations show that the predictions of Φ (treatment response) and Z (baseline risk) form distinct clusters in their respective spaces, supporting the disentanglement claim.
  - [corpus]: Strong conceptual support from CMHE [20], which also uses a latent mixture for counterfactual phenotyping. CURE extends this with retrieval-awareness and multimodal fusion.
- **Break condition:** If the latent subtype structure is too complex or not well-separated in the data, the independent factorization assumption (P(Z,Φ|x)=P(Z|x)P(Φ|x)) may be violated, leading to poor counterfactual separation. The efficacy also depends on the chosen numbers of subgroups (K, M).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Representation Learning**
  - **Why needed here:** The model's core innovation is fusing four heterogeneous data types. Understanding the challenges of modality gaps, missing data, and the purpose of cross-attention vs. simple concatenation is crucial.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is pairwise cross-attention between all modalities preferred over a single shared attention mechanism in this context?
- **Concept: Survival Analysis with Censoring**
  - **Why needed here:** The task is time-to-event prediction with right-censored data. One must understand why standard regression/classification fails, the role of the hazard/survival functions, and metrics like Ctd and IBS that account for censoring.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the Cox partial likelihood handle censored observations differently from a standard mean-squared error loss?
- **Concept: Counterfactual Inference & Treatment Effects**
  - **Why needed here:** The goal is to predict *what would have happened* under alternative treatments. This requires understanding the conceptual shift from predicting a single outcome to predicting multiple potential outcomes and the assumptions (e.g., conditional unconfoundedness) needed for causal identification.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is predicting S(t|X, A=1) and S(t|X, A=0) for the same patient X considered a counterfactual prediction, and what key assumption makes this estimable from observational data?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Raw Modalities -> Omics MoE -> Modality Embedding -> Cross-Attention Fusion -> [g_k, g_m] Gates -> [f_θ, ω_m] -> Weighted Sum -> Survival Function

### Critical Path
The fusion step is pivotal as it affects the quality of `X`, which drives all subsequent gates.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Cross-Attention Complexity:** Pairwise attention is computationally heavier (O(R^2)) than a single shared attention but is argued to better capture modality-specific nuances. Tradeoff: performance vs. memory/compute.
- **MoE Routing:** Top-k routing (k=2) balances expert specialization and training stability. A higher k increases capacity but risks training instability (expert collapse).
- **Subgroup Count (K, M):** Too few subgroups underfit heterogeneity; too many risk overfitting, especially on smaller datasets like TCGA-LUAD. The paper searches [1,5] for M.

### Failure Signatures
- **Ablation patterns:** A large performance drop when removing both cross-attention and MoE suggests failure in multimodal integration. A drop primarily from removing subgroup retrieval points to poor counterfactual disentanglement.
- **Training instability:** High variance in validation Ctd/IBS, especially with large K/M or high auxiliary loss coefficient λ_aux, indicating MoE or gating network collapse.
- **Poor calibration:** High IBS relative to Ctd suggests the predicted survival probabilities are poorly calibrated, often a sign of flawed hazard aggregation or mis-specified subgroup kernels.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Sanity Check:** Train on a small, held-out subset of METABRIC. Verify that loss decreases and that the gating distributions P(Z|x) and P(Φ|x) are not degenerate (e.g., one-hot for all samples).
2. **Component Isolation:** Run the ablation from Fig. 2. Expected outcome: Removing cross-attention causes the largest drop on TCGA-LUAD; removing subgroup retrieval causes a larger drop on METABRIC. This validates the design rationale.
3. **Subgroup Plausibility:** After training on METABRIC, compute the mean predicted risk difference (A=1 vs A=0) for the top-10% and bottom-10% of patients by P(Φ=high_responder|x). Expect a significantly larger positive difference in the high-responder group, confirming the treatment-response subgroups capture meaningful variation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Longitudinal extension:** How can CURE be extended to incorporate longitudinal patient data (e.g., treatment changes over time) for dynamic treatment recommendations? *Basis:* The conclusion states that future work will incorporate longitudinal data and graph-based patient representations to capture temporal evolution. *Why unresolved:* The current model uses static multimodal snapshots; real-world treatment decisions require updating predictions as new data arrives, which is not addressed.
2. **Missing modalities:** What specific strategies can mitigate performance degradation when modalities are missing or incomplete, without relying on imputation? *Basis:* The conclusion identifies addressing modality gaps, missing data, and feature imbalance as necessary for robustness in real-world clinical settings. *Why unresolved:* The model assumes all modalities are available during training/inference; missing data is common but the paper does not propose or test solutions.
3. **Subgroup interpretability:** Do the latent subgroups (treatment-response and baseline survival) discovered by CURE correspond to clinically or biologically meaningful phenotypes, or are they dataset-specific statistical artifacts? *Basis:* Figure 3 visualizes subgroup separation in latent space but provides no external validation against known clinical markers (e.g., cancer subtypes, genomic signatures). *Why unresolved:* Without correlation to established phenotypes, the interpretability and generalizability of subgroups remain unclear, limiting clinical utility.
4. **Fairness:** Does the implicit latent retrieval mechanism amplify biases if certain demographic groups are underrepresented, and how can fairness be ensured? *Basis:* The "Potential Negative Externalities" section warns of bias amplification from systematic disparities but the model contains no explicit fairness constraints. *Why unresolved:* Retrieval operates in a learned latent space; underrepresented patients may be mis-assigned to subgroups, leading to inequitable predictions.

## Limitations
- Performance claims: High confidence (exact numbers reported and benchmarked).

- Generalizability & causal assumptions: Low confidence (evaluation on only two datasets; counterfactual identification relies on unverifiable observational assumptions such as no unmeasured confounding).
