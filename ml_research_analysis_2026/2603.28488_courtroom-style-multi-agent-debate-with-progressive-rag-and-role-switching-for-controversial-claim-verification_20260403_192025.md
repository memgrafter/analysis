# Courtroom-Style Multi-Agent Debate with Progressive RAG and Role-Switching for Controversial Claim Verification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28488
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28488
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PROCLAIM achieves 81.7% accuracy on Check-COVID, a 10.0 percentage point improvement over standard multi-agent debate.

## Executive Summary
PROCLAIM introduces a courtroom-style multi-agent framework to mitigate LLM unreliability in high-stakes claim verification. It combines adversarial roles (Plaintiff, Defense, Judge), progressive retrieval-augmented generation (P-RAG) for dynamic evidence refinement, evidence negotiation, self-reflection, and heterogeneous multi-judge aggregation. Ablations confirm the contribution of P-RAG (+7.5 pp), role-switching (+4.2 pp), and a three-judge panel (+3.3 pp). The system enhances robustness and confidence calibration at a significant computational cost.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with claim decomposition into atomic premises. Evidence negotiation retrieves initial documents, scores admissibility via relevance × credibility (threshold >0.5), and forms a shared pool. A structured debate then proceeds with specialized agents (Plaintiff, Defense, Critic, Expert Witness, The Court) over up to 10 rounds. The Court drives a Progressive RAG loop, refining queries using a novelty filter (η(d)=1−maxₑ∈P cos(e,d), threshold 0.2) and halting on redundancy >70%, relevance gain <0.05, or max rounds. After the primary debate, roles are switched for a second pass to test consistency. Finally, three heterogeneous LLM judges evaluate the full record; their majority vote yields the final verdict, with confidence adjusted by role-switching consistency.

## Key Results
- PROCLAIM attains 81.7% accuracy on the Check-COVID benchmark, improving over standard multi-agent debate by 10.0 percentage points.
- Ablation studies show Progressive RAG contributes the largest single gain (+7.5 pp), followed by role-switching (+4.2 pp) and a heterogeneous three-judge panel (+3.3 pp).
- The system uses ~210k tokens per claim, approximately 11× more than standard MAD, but with improved robustness and calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Progressive RAG (P-RAG)
**Claim:** P-RAG dynamically expands and refines the evidence pool during debate, preventing stagnation and contributing the largest ablation gain (+7.5 pp).  
**Mechanism:** Counsel identify evidential gaps; The Court refines queries; retrieval applies a novelty filter (η(d)=1−maxₑ∈P cos(e,d)) with threshold 0.2; the loop stops if redundancy >70%, relevance gain <0.05, or after 10 rounds.  
**Core assumption:** Static, one-pass retrieval cannot satisfy evolving evidential needs in adversarial reasoning.  
**Break condition:** Novelty scores remain below 0.2 for consecutive queries or redundancy exceeds 70%, risking missed critical evidence; ineffective Court refinement yields irrelevant documents.

### Mechanism 2: Courtroom Roles and Evidence Negotiation
**Claim:** Specialized roles and structured evidence negotiation create an adversarial process that surfaces conflicting evidence and prevents echo chambers.  
**Mechanism:** Premise decomposition yields atomic units; stance-conditioned retrieval generates proponent/opponent pools; admissibility scoring (relevance × credibility, threshold >0.5) filters; structured debate with Plaintiff, Defense, Judge, Critic, and Expert Witness enforces adversarial dynamics.  
**Core assumption:** Unstructured debate without explicit roles and evidence filtering leads to premature convergence and shared bias reinforcement.  
**Break condition:** Low role-play consistency (<5/10) indicates collusion or position-anchoring; overly strict admissibility thresholds may exclude crucial disputed evidence.

### Mechanism 3: Heterogeneous Judges and Role-Switching
**Claim:** Heterogeneous multi-judge aggregation and role-switching consistency mitigate systematic biases, contributing +3.3 pp (judges) and +4.2 pp (role-switching).  
**Mechanism:** Three independent judges from different model families evaluate the full case record; majority vote yields verdict. Role-switching swaps primary debate roles; a consistency analyzer scores logical stability (γ) and adjusts confidence (δrs).  
**Core assumption:** Homogeneous LLMs share biases; diversity yields complementary errors; role-switching tests whether arguments are evidence-driven rather than position-anchored.  
**Break condition:** Judges are not truly independent (e.g., similar training data), majority vote may amplify correlated errors; low consistency score (γ<5) suggests arguments not robust to role reversal.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input → Premise Decomposer → Premises → Evidence Negotiation (retrieval + admissibility scoring) → Shared Pool → Debate Orchestrator (Plaintiff, Defense, Critic, Expert Witness, Court) ↔ P-RAG Loop (query refinement + novelty filter) → Role-Switcher → Judicial Panel (3 heterogeneous judges) → Verdict + Confidence. All LLM calls are stateless; state is maintained by the orchestrator.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Claim → Premise decomposition.  
  2. Initial evidence retrieval and negotiation → admitted pool.  
  3. Primary debate rounds (max 10) with P-RAG, self-reflection, critic, and optional expert.  
  4. Role-switched debate (second pass).  
  5. Judicial panel evaluation and majority vote.  
  6. Confidence adjustment and output.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Token cost vs accuracy: Full pipeline ~210k tokens/claim vs Standard MAD ~18.9k; ~10× cost for +10 pp gain.  
  - Heterogeneity vs coordination: Different models increase diversity but require careful prompt engineering.  
  - Adaptive stopping: Reflection plateau and novelty thresholds control length but risk early termination if too aggressive.  
  - Evidence pool size: Larger pools improve coverage but increase retrieval cost and novelty dilution.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low inter-judge agreement (κ) with low accuracy → epistemic bubble.  
  - Rapid novelty decay (<0.2) before round 4 → evidence saturation or poor query refinement.  
  - Role-switching consistency γ<5 → position-anchored arguments.  
  - Self-reflection plateau reached in <3 rounds with unstable trajectories → likely incorrect convergence.  
  - Judge-specific bias (e.g., one judge always INCONCLUSIVE) → panel imbalance.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Single-claim validation: Run a known claim; verify evidence pool contains relevant documents and verdict matches ground truth.  
  2. P-RAG ablation: Disable P-RAG; expect ~7.5 pp accuracy drop, smaller evidence pool (~37.5 vs 67.5 docs), longer debates (6.0 vs 5.47 rounds).  
  3. Judge homogeneity test: Replace three-judge panel with three copies of same model; expect ~3.3 pp accuracy loss and amplified bias (e.g., over-refutation).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What is the precise cost-accuracy trade-off threshold at which PROCLAIM's deliberative overhead becomes unjustified for real-world deployment in low-stakes domains?
2. To what extent does role-switching alter final verdicts versus merely serving as a consistency diagnostic, and what evidence characteristics most trigger a verdict change post-switch?
3. Is there an optimal panel composition strategy for heterogeneous judges (e.g., balancing over-refuting vs. over-abstaining models) that maximizes accuracy more reliably than simple majority voting?
4. Are the observed termination conditions (reflection plateau, novelty stabilization, critic resolution) equally valid stopping criteria across different claim types, or do some prematurely halt deliberation on complex claims?
5. Does the "burden of refutation" standard (classifying INCONCLUSIVE as SUPPORT) introduce domain-specific bias, and would a calibrated probability threshold over the judicial confidence score be more robust?

## Limitations
- **Corpus construction ambiguity:** The PubMed corpus is described as “constructed for this study” without reproducible search/filter/chunking details, risking outcome variability.
- **Model identity stability:** Specified models (e.g., `gpt-5-mini`, `deepseek-v3.2`) may be unavailable, renamed, or updated, breaking heterogeneity assumptions.
- **Random effects reproducibility:** Seeds for retrieval, sampling, or debate initialization are undisclosed; reported numbers are averaged over three runs but variance is unreported.

## Confidence
- **High:** Ablation gains (P-RAG +7.5 pp, role-switching +4.2 pp, judges +3.3 pp) are directly reported.
- **Medium:** Mechanism claims (e.g., novelty filter halting retrieval) are described but assume ideal implementation.
- **Low:** Generalizability beyond Check-COVID or to other domains; robustness to corpus/model substitutions.
