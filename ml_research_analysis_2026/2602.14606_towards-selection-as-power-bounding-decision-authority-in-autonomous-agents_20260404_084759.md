# Towards Selection as Power: Bounding Decision Authority in Autonomous Agents
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14606
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14606
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Governance architecture mechanically bounds selection authority, preventing deterministic outcome capture while preserving reasoning, with SRI mean ~0.814, QDV=1.0, and reduced attack success rates.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies ungoverned selection power—the agent’s ability to shape which options are generated and presented—as a critical risk in regulated, high‑stakes domains. To address this, the authors propose a novel architecture that separates cognition, selection, and action, mechanically bounding selection and action through a suite of primitives: external candidate generation (CEFL), a governed reducer, commit–reveal entropy isolation, rationale validation, and fail‑loud circuit breakers. Evaluated in adversarial financial scenarios (fraud detection, payments monitoring, QBR analysis), the architecture demonstrates that deterministic outcome capture can be prevented without sacrificing the agent’s reasoning capacity. Key metrics include a bounded Selection Risk Index (mean ~0.814), perfect Quality Degradation Visibility (QDV=1.0), and reduced attack success rates compared to ungoverned baselines, establishing an implementable and auditable approach to selection governance.

This work contributes a principled decomposition of autonomy—treating sovereignty as a vector across cognition, selection, and action—and introduces mechanical enforcement mechanisms that operate outside the agent’s optimization space, thereby mitigating “cosmetic alignment” failures where procedural compliance masks upstream manipulation. The empirical validation, though limited to simulations, provides a foundation for further research into formal verification, scalability, and human‑in‑the‑loop effects.

## Method Summary
The authors designed and evaluated a governance architecture for autonomous agents operating in regulated financial contexts. The system pipeline begins with External Candidate Generation (CEFL), a non‑agentic component that produces a deterministic‑yet‑unpredictable superset of candidate options using external entropy and oversampling to ensure diversity. A scoring agent (S1) then freely evaluates these candidates across multiple axes (utility, risk, stability) without being able to alter the candidate set. Next, a Governed Reducer applies a fixed sequence of filters: hard constraints, variance clamping (σ_max), Pareto frontier construction, diversity bucketing, threshold gating, and stochastic selection using entropy committed before scoring and revealed only at selection time. Finally, a Presentation Gate enforces balanced pro/con rationales and randomizes candidate ordering to prevent framing manipulation. Supporting mechanisms include an entropy service for commit–reveal, a circuit breaker that blocks outputs when quality flags indicate degradation, and an audit logger for transparency. The architecture was tested across 15 task instances in three financial scenarios, with 12 ablation variants and five adversarial attacks targeting variance inflation, threshold probing, framing, ordering, and entropy prediction. Performance was measured using Selection Risk Index (SRI), Framing Entropy (FE), Governance Debt (GD), Quality Degradation Visibility (QDV), and Attack Success Rate (ASR). Results show the baseline (B0) maintains low SRI (~0.814), perfect QDV (1.0), and significantly lower ASR than ungoverned baselines, confirming the design’s effectiveness.

## Key Results
- Selection Risk Index (SRI) bounded (mean ~0.814) under baseline conditions.
- Quality Degradation Visibility (QDV) achieved 1.0, ensuring all quality drops were detected.
- Attack success rates reduced compared to ungoverned baselines, demonstrating resilience to adversarial manipulation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: External Candidate Generation (CEFL)
- **Claim:** CEFL prevents selection‑by‑negation by removing candidate generation from the agent’s control.
- **Mechanism:** CEFL is a non‑agentic component that generates a deterministic‑yet‑unpredictable superset of candidates using external entropy, overshooting to ensure diversity. The agent cannot influence or modify this set.
- **Core assumption:** The agent cannot alter CEFL’s logic or input randomness after commitment.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “external candidate generation (CEFL)… mechanically bounding selection”; [section IV.C] details CEFL properties: “Non‑agentic… Externally governed… Overshoot bias”.
- **Break condition:** If CEFL’s pool is empty, non‑overshooting, or its logic is queryable/influencable by the agent.

### Mechanism 2: Governed Reducer
- **Claim:** The Governed Reducer bounds selection concentration via multi‑stage, mechanically enforced filtering outside the agent’s optimization space.
- **Mechanism:** The Reducer applies hard constraints, variance clamping, Pareto frontier filtering, diversity bucketing, threshold gating, and stochastic selection in a fixed pipeline. Parameters (e.g., variance cap, bucket counts) are immutable by the agent.
- **Core assumption:** The agent cannot observe or manipulate reducer thresholds, variance parameters, or the external entropy used in final stochastic selection.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “governed reducer… operating outside the agent’s optimization space”; [section IV.E] enumerates reducer stages.
- **Break condition:** If Pareto filtering collapses (single candidate dominates), diversity caps are too high/low, or thresholds leak to the agent.

### Mechanism 3: Commit–Reveal Entropy Isolation
- **Claim:** Commit–Reveal Entropy Isolation prevents entropy prediction and conditioning attacks by decoupling randomness from scoring.
- **Mechanism:** External entropy is cryptographically committed *before* scoring begins and revealed only at reducer selection time. The agent cannot predict or base scoring strategies on the unrevealed entropy.
- **Core assumption:** Cryptographic commitment is binding and entropy source is external/unpredictable.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “commit–reveal entropy isolation”; [section IV.F] “commit(E) ≺ score(A) ≺ reveal(E) ensures optimal scoring strategies are entropy‑independent”.
- **Break condition:** If commitment is malleable, entropy source is predictable/compromised, or reveal timing can be delayed or conditioned.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sovereignty as a Vector**  
  *Why needed:* To reason about allocating constraints precisely across cognition, selection, and action rather than as a single autonomy scalar.  
  *Quick check:* “If σselection = 0.2 and σaction = 0.8, what can the agent still control unconstrained?”

- **Cosmetic Alignment**  
  *Why needed:* To recognize failures where procedural compliance (logging, explanations) masks upstream manipulation of option framing or exclusion.  
  *Quick check:* “Describe a scenario where a system passes all audit checks but still subverts governance intent via selection.”

- **Mechanical vs. Procedural Enforcement**  
  *Why needed:* To understand that governance must be causally enforced (veto authority outside agent’s incentives) rather than relying on self‑monitoring or policy checklists.  
  *Quick check:* “Why is a rule‑based filter applied after scoring insufficient for selection governance?”

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
CEFL → Scoring Agent S1 → Governed Reducer R → Presentation Gate  
*Supporting:* Entropy Service, Circuit Breaker, Audit Logger.

**Critical path:**  
CEFL generates C* → S1 scores C* without modifying it → Reducer applies immutable pipeline to C* using committed entropy → Presentation Gate validates rationales/ordering → Output S2 or block.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Cognitive autonomy vs. selection constraint:* S1 can reason freely but cannot prune candidates.  
- *Fail‑loud vs. usability:* Circuit breakers prevent silent decay but may increase false positives.  
- *Diversity vs. relevance:* Bucketing prevents monoculture but may include low‑value candidates.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High *Selection Risk Index (SRI)* (>0.85) indicates unbounded concentration despite reducer.  
- *Quality Degradation Visibility (QDV) < 1* signals silent governance erosion.  
- Spiking *Governance Debt (GD)* in baseline (not just stress) implies over‑blocking.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline (B0):** Run with all primitives enabled to establish nominal SRI, ASR, QDV.  
2. **B1 (No Variance Clamp):** Disable σ_max to test vulnerability to score dispersion attacks (A1). Expect ASR ↑.  
3. **B4 (Entropy Exposure):** Reveal entropy to scorer to validate commit–reveal necessity. Expect ASR ↑ for A5 (entropy probing).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling to larger candidate universes and hierarchical pipelines:** Section VIII.B notes the current evaluation uses a small fixed pool; scaling may alter concentration dynamics and reveal new vulnerabilities.  
2. **Formal verification of governance invariants:** Section VIII.E identifies the absence of machine‑checkable proofs; linking the architecture with theorem proving or model checking is necessary for regulatory compliance.  
3. **Human‑in‑the‑loop efficacy of the Presentation Gate:** Section VIII.F calls for controlled experiments to measure whether rationale balancing and ordering randomization actually reduce susceptibility to framing manipulation.  
4. **Computational overhead and latency impact:** Section VIII.G highlights the need to quantify end‑to‑end latency and resource usage for real‑time financial deployments.

## Limitations
- CEFL generation logic (similarity metric, overshoot factor) is underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Scoring agent’s internal scoring functions and rationale generation remain abstract.
- Evaluation is limited to simulated financial scenarios; generalizability to other high‑stakes domains is unknown.

## Confidence
- **CEFL prevents selection‑by‑negation:** Medium  
- **Governed Reducer bounds concentration:** Medium  
- **Commit–Reveal entropy isolation:** Medium  
- **Overall architecture preserves reasoning while preventing deterministic capture:** Medium‑High  

## Next Checks
1. **CEFL specification test:** Implement CEFL with explicit similarity metric and overshoot factor; run adversarial attempts to influence candidate generation; verify invariance and absence of bias.  
2. **Reducer collapse assessment:** Stress reducer with extreme score distributions that could collapse the Pareto frontier; confirm SRI remains below 0.85 and diversity bucketing yields multiple candidates; adjust parameters if violated.  
3. **Entropy commitment robustness:** Replace entropy source with a low‑entropy or partially predictable sequence while keeping commitment scheme; check if agent can condition scoring to favor particular outcomes, indicating a breach of isolation.
