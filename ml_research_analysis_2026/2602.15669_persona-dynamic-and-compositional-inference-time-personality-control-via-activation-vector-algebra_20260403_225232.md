# PERSONA: Dynamic and Compositional Inference-Time Personality Control via Activation Vector Algebra

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15669  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15669  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: PERSONA achieves a PersonalityBench mean score of 9.60, nearly matching supervised fine-tuning (9.61), and up to 91% win rates on PERSONA-EVOLVE.

## Executive Summary
PERSONA is a training-free method that enables dynamic and compositional personality control in large language models at inference time. It extracts orthogonal personality vectors from model activations using contrastive prompting and steers generation via vector arithmetic (scaling, addition, subtraction). Evaluations on PersonalityBench and the proposed PERSONA-EVOLVE benchmark show near–fine-tuning performance without gradient updates and high win rates for context-aware adaptation across multiple model families.

## Method Summary
PERSONA consists of three modules. PERSONA-BASE extracts trait vectors for OCEAN dimensions by contrasting residual activations from prompts that elicit versus suppress each trait; an effective steering layer is selected via validation. PERSONA-ALGEBRA then applies algebraic operations: scalar multiplication adjusts intensity, addition combines traits, and subtraction suppresses traits by injecting the resulting vector into the residual stream. PERSONA-FLOW dynamically predicts per-turn coefficient adjustments (from –2 to +2) for each dimension based on conversational context, composes the composite steering vector, and injects it at the chosen layer. The entire pipeline operates without model retraining.

## Key Results
- PersonalityBench mean score: 9.60 (SFT upper bound 9.61), demonstrating near–fine-tuning performance.
- Up to 91% win rates on PERSONA-EVOLVE across Qwen, Llama, and Mistral models, indicating effective dynamic adaptation.
- Linear modulation of trait expression (R² > 0.9) and successful compositional control of multiple personality dimensions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**: Personality traits are approximately linearly encoded as distinct directions in the model's activation space. Contrastive activation analysis—pairing trait-expressing and trait-suppressing prompts and computing the mean difference in residual activations—yields a trait vector. Evidence includes negative cosine similarity between opposing pole vectors (Figure 2) and monotonic trait expression scores when steering with these vectors (Table 2).

**Mechanism 2**: Trait vectors support algebraic composition. Scalar multiplication controls intensity, addition combines traits, and subtraction suppresses traits, relying on linear superposition in activation space. Near-linear scaling of BFI scores with steering coefficient (Figure 3) and expected increases in both traits when combining complementary vectors (Figure 4, Appendix A.11) confirm this property.

**Mechanism 3**: Dynamic, context-aware adaptation is achieved via a predict-then-steer loop. A context analyzer predicts per-trait coefficient deltas for each turn; weak adjustments are gated out; the signed vectors are composed and injected. The model’s ability to self-assess appropriate adjustments without training is supported by PERSONA-FLOW’s up to 91% win rates on PERSONA-EVOLVE (Table 3) and case studies showing context-appropriate emotional shifts (Figure 5).

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Activation Analysis**: Needed to extract a directional trait vector by contrasting activations under eliciting vs. suppressing conditions. Quick check: Given high‑compassion and low‑compassion response sets, compute the mean difference in their residual stream activations to obtain the compassion vector.
- **Residual Stream Steering**: Needed to inject personality vectors into the forward pass without gradients. Adding vector *v* to residual activation *h<sub>l</sub>* at layer *l* biases subsequent predictions toward the trait. Quick check: How does adding *v* to *h<sub>l</sub>* affect output? It shifts activations along *v*, biasing generation toward associated representations.
- **Approximate Orthogonality**: Needed to minimize interference when combining multiple trait vectors. Vectors are not perfectly orthogonal but exhibit low-to-moderate cosine similarity. Quick check: If two vectors have cosine similarity 0.6, combining them may cause unintended effects on traits correlated with both.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: PERSONA-BASE → PERSONA-ALGEBRA → PERSONA-FLOW → PERSONA-EVOLVE

Critical path:
1. Choose base model (e.g., Qwen2.5‑7B).
2. Run PERSONA‑BASE to extract and validate 10 OCEAN pole vectors.
3. Verify scalar multiplication linearity and compositionality.
4. Implement PERSONA‑FLOW’s coefficient predictor and sparsity gate.
5. Evaluate on PERSONA‑EVOLVE with pairwise judging.

Design tradeoffs:
- Single‑layer vs. multi‑layer steering: Paper uses one layer for simplicity, sacrificing potential distributed gains.
- Continuous vs. discrete coefficients: Continuous provides fine‑grained control; discretization harms performance.
- Evaluation judge: Uses GPT‑4.1‑mini; open‑source substitutes may produce score shifts.

Failure signatures:
- Vectors fail validation: low BFI scores or non‑monotonic trends.
- High cross‑trait leakage: single‑trait steering unexpectedly changes opposing traits.
- PERSONA‑FLOW degrades general capability or safety.
- PERSONA‑EVOLVE win rate near random (≈50%).

First 3 experiments:
1. Replicate extraction of `v_Inventive` and `v_Consistent`; test steering with α ∈ {‑1,0,1,2}; verify monotonic O scores (r > 0.95).
2. Compose `v_Outgoing + v_Compassionate`; verify both E and A increase without weakening opposite poles.
3. Run PERSONA‑FLOW on a PERSONA‑EVOLVE subset; confirm win rate >80%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization across architectures** – The method is evaluated only on decoder‑only models (Qwen, Llama, Mistral). It remains unknown whether PERSONA works on encoder‑decoder or multimodal architectures.
2. **Scalability of dimensions** – While pairwise combinations are successful, the limit on simultaneously controlling arbitrary subsets of the ten OCEAN poles (or more) is unclear; interference may rise with more vectors.
3. **Why linearity suffices** – The paper shows linear modulation works empirically but offers no theoretical explanation for why a single direction captures a trait; non‑linear interactions across layers might be missed.
4. **Robustness of dynamic adaptation** – PERSONA‑FLOW is tested on 8‑turn scenarios with clear emotional arcs; performance in long, ambiguous conversations with shifting contexts is unknown.
5. **Safety constraints** – Some traits (e.g., Careless, Inventive) increase adversarial attack success rate; the paper does not provide a way to guarantee safety when steering.

## Limitations
- Precomputed vectors and optimal layer parameters are not publicly released, forcing manual extraction that may diverge from the original.
- Evaluation relies on proprietary LLM judges (GPT‑4.1‑mini/GPT‑4o), risking bias and limited accessibility.
- PERSONA‑EVOLVE benchmark remains unreleased, blocking independent verification of dynamic adaptation claims.
- Non‑orthogonal trait vectors may cause cross‑interference during composition.
- PERSONA‑FLOW’s prompt‑based coefficient predictor may not generalize beyond curated scenarios.

## Confidence
- Linear encoding of personality: **Medium** – strong internal evidence but external reproducibility uncertain.
- Algebraic compositionality: **Medium** – figures show near‑linear scaling and expected combinations, but interference noted; independent tests needed.
- Dynamic context‑aware adaptation: **Low–Medium** – high win rates on unreleased benchmark; confidence hampered by dataset opacity and judge reliance.
