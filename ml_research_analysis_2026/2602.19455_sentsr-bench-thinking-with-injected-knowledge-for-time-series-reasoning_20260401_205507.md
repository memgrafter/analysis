# SenTSR-Bench: Thinking with Injected Knowledge for Time-Series Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19455  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19455  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** Hybrid knowledge injection improves time-series reasoning, outperforming TSLMs by 9.1%-26.1% and GRLMs by 7.9%-22.4%.

## Executive Summary
General reasoning LLMs (GRLMs) lack domain-specific knowledge, while fine-tuned time-series LLMs (TSLMs) lack robust general reasoning for diagnostic tasks. This paper introduces a hybrid framework that directly injects a specialist TSLM's analysis into the GRLM's reasoning trace. On the newly released SenTSR-Bench and two public datasets, the method yields consistent gains of 9.1%-26.1% over standalone TSLMs and 7.9%-22.4% over zero-shot GRLMs. Furthermore, training the TSLM with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) produces 1.66×–2.92× larger improvements than supervised fine-tuning (SFT), demonstrating the value of eliciting analysis-first reasoning without human-labeled traces.

## Method Summary
The framework employs a specialist TSLM (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B) trained on time‑series diagnostic QA. The TSLM processes the raw time series **X** and query **q** to generate a concise analysis snippet. At inference, this snippet is injected at the beginning of the GRLM’s reasoning trace (early injection), immediately after a `⟨think⟩` token. The frozen GRLM (e.g., Claude, Qwen3‑32B) then continues autoregressively to produce the final answer. The TSLM can be trained via supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) or via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), where rewards consist of a format compliance score and a binary correctness indicator on the final answer. This RL setup eliminates the need for human‑written reasoning chains. The injection approach avoids modifying the GRLM’s parameters, enabling effective collaboration between a generalist and a small specialist. Results across multiple benchmarks confirm significant performance improvements, with RLVR‑trained TSLMs providing notably larger gains than SFT‑trained TSLMs.

## Key Results
- Hybrid injection surpasses standalone TSLMs by **9.1%-26.1%** across all benchmarks.  
- It also exceeds zero‑shot GRLMs by **7.9%-22.4%**.  
- RLVR‑based TSLM yields **1.66×–2.92×** larger improvements compared to SFT‑based injection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Injecting TSLM-generated insights directly into the GRLM’s reasoning trace combines domain‑specific pattern recognition with general causal reasoning, steering the GRLM toward accurate diagnoses.  
- **Mechanism:** The TSLM processes the time‑series input **X** to produce a concise, domain‑grounded analysis snippet **KT** (e.g., key observations about anomalies). This snippet is prepended to the GRLM’s internal reasoning trace **r** at a designated injection point (e.g., immediately after `⟨think⟩`). The GRLM then conditions its subsequent reasoning steps on this injected knowledge, allowing it to incorporate domain facts while still applying its strong general reasoning to integrate, critique, and build upon them.  
- **Core assumption:** The TSLM’s analysis is sufficiently reliable and relevant to provide a useful starting point for the GRLM’s reasoning, and the GRLM can effectively critique and build upon it.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "injects TSLM-generated insights directly into GRLM's reasoning trace, thereby achieving strong time‑series reasoning with in‑domain knowledge."  
  - [section 2.2] "Given the current reasoning state of the general reasoner πG… we form an injection–oriented token sequence… the specialist then generates the knowledge snippet KT… we then inject… πG continues generation conditioned on the updated prefix."  
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on diagnostic reasoning or time‑series forecasting but not hybrid injection.  
- **Break condition:** If the TSLM’s snippet is systematically misleading or hallucinated, the GRLM may be steered incorrectly. The paper’s ablation in Figure 5 (a) shows performance drops when GRLM lacks direct access to the raw time series **X**, indicating reliance on a flawed **KT** can be damaging.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) aligned the TSLM to produce analysis‑first thinking traces without human‑annotated reasoning chains, solving the task shift problem between QA and knowledge injection.  
- **Mechanism:** The TSLM is trained with a group‑relative RL objective (GRPO) using two binary rewards: a *format reward* enforcing the structure `⟨think⟩r⟨/think⟩ ⟨answer⟩y⟨/answer⟩`, and a *hard reward* for correct final answer `y == y*`. This incentivizes the model to generate an analysis trace **r** that leads to correct answers, implicitly learning to produce useful intermediate reasoning for injection. No ground‑truth traces **r*** are needed.  
- **Core assumption:** Correctness of the final answer **y*** is a sufficient proxy for the quality of the intermediate analysis **r** in the context of diagnostic tasks.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "leverage a reinforcement learning‑based approach with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to elicit knowledge‑rich traces without human supervision."  
  - [section 2.4] "Let z = [r, y]… we optimize with the group‑relative objective… R(z) = r_fmt(z) + r_hard(z)… no labeled traces are required."  
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Time Series Forecasting as Reasoning: A Slow‑Thinking Approach with Reinforced LLMs" (arxiv:2506.10630) explore RL for reasoning but not specifically for injection; evidence for mechanism is strong within the paper itself.  
- **Break condition:** If the task requires a specific reasoning style not captured by end‑task accuracy (e.g., certain causal chains), RL may produce traces that are correct in outcome but not faithfully grounded in the time series, making them poor for injection. The paper’s own analysis suggests RL yields larger gains than SFT, implying SFT suffers more from task shift.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Early knowledge injection (inserting TSLM analysis immediately after `⟨think⟩`) is superior because it aligns with the TSLM’s strength in producing short, focused analyses and avoids errors from revising long, out‑of‑distribution reasoning traces.  
- **Mechanism:** In early injection, the TSLM is queried with a simple help‑instruction (`"produce a step–by–step analysis…"`) on the raw **X, q** before any GRLM reasoning occurs. Its output **KT** is injected right after the thinking start token. This places domain‑grounded observations at the root of the reasoning chain. Intermediate/late injection requires the TSLM to critique or correct partial/full GRLM reasoning traces, which lie outside its QA‑trained distribution, leading to drift.  
- **Core assumption:** The TSLM is optimized for direct analysis of **X, q** but not for metacognitive tasks like critiquing arbitrary reasoning drafts.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 2.3] "early injection yields the strongest gains… mid/late injection requires the specialist to read and revise long reasoning traces, which lie outside the distribution of QA‑style SFT and lead to drift or hallucination."  
  - [section 4.3] Table 4 shows early injection consistently outperforms intermediate and late strategies across benchmarks.  
  - [corpus] No direct corpus support; mechanism is derived from the paper’s empirical comparison.  
- **Break condition:** If the GRLM’s initial reasoning is deeply flawed, early injection may be insufficient; a later correction point could be more effective. However, the paper finds early injection robust even when TSLM alone performs poorly, suggesting the GRLM’s reasoning strength compensates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) Reasoning**  
  *Why needed here:* The framework explicitly manipulates the GRLM’s internal reasoning trace (`r`). Understanding how CoT decomposes problems into steps is essential to grasp why injecting knowledge at the start shapes the entire subsequent reasoning process.  
  *Quick check question:* If a reasoning trace is a sequence of logical steps, what is the likely effect of inserting a factual observation at the very first step versus near the end?

- **Concept: Multimodal Representation (Time‑Series to Text)**  
  *Why needed here:* Both TSLM and GRLM must interpret the same time‑series **X**, but encoded differently (e.g., as plots or JSON). The injection mechanism assumes the TSLM’s analysis of **X** is transferable to the GRLM’s representation of **X**.  
  *Quick check question:* How might encoding a multivariate time series as a line‑plot image versus a JSON string affect the type of "domain knowledge" a TSLM extracts?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) / GRPO**  
  *Why needed here:* The core methodological innovation is using RL to train the TSLM to produce analysis traces without supervision. Understanding group‑relative policy optimization and reward shaping (format + correctness) is key to seeing how the task shift is resolved.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is a *group‑relative* objective (normalizing rewards within a batch) used instead of absolute reward values, and how does a binary format reward help?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):  
Time-Series Encoder → TSLM → Injection Controller → GRLM  

Critical path:  
TSLM Training (SFT + RLVR) → Inference: For each query (X,q) → 1. Generate r_T from π_T(X,q) → 2. Form r_inj = Inject([], r_T) → 3. Continue π_G(X, q, [⟨think⟩, r_inj]) → 4. Get final y.

Design tradeoffs:
- *Token efficiency vs. completeness:* Early injection uses a compact snippet but may omit context; longer snippets risk exceeding GRLM context or including noise.
- *TSLM reliability vs. GRLM correction:* Heavy reliance on TSLM quality; however, GRLM’s strong reasoning can partially correct weaker snippets, but not severe hallucinations.
- *Frozen GRLM vs. full fine‑tuning:* Injection avoids expensive GRLM fine‑tuning but cannot adapt the GRLM’s core knowledge; limits improvement ceiling if GRLM’s time‑series perception is fundamentally poor.

Failure signatures:
- **TSLM hallucination:** Snippet contains patterns not in **X** (detect by comparing **KT** to ground‑truth annotations if available).
- **Injection misalignment:** **KT** is too generic or off‑topic (e.g., TSLM returns a direct answer instead of analysis). Check prompt adherence.
- **GRLM ignoring injection:** GRLM reasoning trace shows no engagement with injected content; may indicate poor prompting or model capability. Inspect generated **r_G**.
- **Encoding mismatch:** GRLM’s representation of **X** differs from TSLM’s (e.g., text vs. image), causing **KT** to reference features the GRLM cannot perceive.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Ablate injection position:** Compare early, intermediate, late injection using a strong TSLM (e.g., ChatTS‑14B) on a held‑out set. Measure accuracy and inspect reasoning traces for TSLM critique quality (especially for late injection).
2. **Test GRLM’s direct access necessity:** Run injection with GRLM input = `(q, r_inj)` only vs. `(X, q, r_inj)`. Significant drop in the "w/o TS" condition confirms the GRLM needs its own view of **X**.
3. **Compare RL vs. SFT TSLM for injection:** Train two TSLMs (one SFT, one RLVR) on same data. At inference, inject their traces into the same GRLM. Expect RL‑trained TSLM to yield higher gains, validating the thinking transfer design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**  
  *Question:* How robust is the injection framework to low-quality or misleading reasoning traces from the TSLM?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The approach assumes the TSLM provides accurate domain knowledge, but the paper does not evaluate performance when the injected analysis contains errors or hallucinations.  
  *Why unresolved:* Experiments only report average gains; there is no analysis of failure modes or the GRLM's ability to correct incorrect injections.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematically perturb TSLM outputs (e.g., introduce factual errors, noise) and measure the impact on final accuracy; qualitatively examine traces where injection leads to incorrect diagnoses.

- **Open Question 2**  
  *Question:* Can the RLVR‑based thinking transfer method be effectively applied to non‑time‑series domains, such as textual QA or image reasoning?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper exclusively evaluates on time‑series benchmarks; no experiments explore generalization to other modalities or task types.  
  *Why unresolved:* The RL training objective and reward design may be tailored to time‑series diagnostics; their broader applicability remains unvalidated.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Re‑train a TSLM using the same RLVR pipeline on a different domain (e.g., medical report generation) with verifiable rewards and assess the quality of elicited reasoning traces.

- **Open Question 3**  
  *Question:* What is the precise mechanism behind early injection's consistent superiority over intermediate and late injection?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The paper reports early injection yields the strongest gains and speculates that mid/late injection forces the TSLM to handle long reasoning traces, which is out‑of‑distribution. However, this hypothesis is not directly tested.  
  *Why unresolved:* No empirical evidence is provided to confirm the distribution shift explanation or to understand how the GRLM integrates knowledge at different positions.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate TSLM's performance when generating critiques for drafts of varying lengths; perform fine‑grained analysis of GRLM's reasoning traces to see when and how injected content is referenced.

## Limitations
- Exact TSLM help‑oriented query prompt and reflection trigger token string are not specified, making precise replication difficult.
- The SenTSR‑Bench dataset and its preprocessing details are not publicly released, preventing verification on the primary benchmark.
- The synthetic data generation pipeline (VLM code synthesis + LLM diversification) is not provided, hindering reproduction of TSLM training.
