# Multimodal Optimal Transport for Unsupervised Temporal Segmentation in Surgical Robotics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24138
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24138
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** TASOT achieves significant gains in unsupervised surgical phase and step segmentation over zero-shot baselines across multiple datasets via a multimodal optimal transport framework.

## Executive Summary
TASOT introduces an unsupervised method for temporal segmentation in surgical videos by combining visual and textual cues through a multimodal optimal transport formulation. It leverages off-the-shelf encoders (DINOv3 for frames, CLIP for LLM-generated captions) and a temporally consistent unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein objective to produce pseudo-labels for self-training. Evaluated on three surgical datasets, it substantially improves phase and step segmentation F1 scores compared to zero-shot baselines, with ablations confirming the benefit of fusing visual and text costs at the transport level.

## Method Summary
The method operates in an iterative self-training loop. First, videos are split into windows and processed by an LLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash) to generate temporal captions. Visual features are extracted per frame using DINOv3, and text features per caption segment using CLIP; each frame is assigned the text embedding of its containing caption. Separate projection heads map vision and text features to a shared latent space, where L2-normalized embeddings are compared to a fixed set of *K* learned prototypes via cosine distance, yielding modality-specific cost matrices. These are fused via a weight β into a multimodal cost matrix, which is further regularized with a temporal prior to encourage contiguous assignments. An unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein solver (based on ASOT) then finds an optimal transport plan from frames to prototypes. This soft plan serves as pseudo-labels to train the projection heads with a cross-entropy loss, iteratively refining the embeddings and transport assignments. At inference, the final transport solution yields frame-wise cluster assignments, mapped to ground-truth classes via Hungarian matching for evaluation.

## Key Results
- Outperforms zero-shot baselines by large margins in phase segmentation: +16.5 F1 (Cholec80), +19.6 F1 (AutoLaparo), +23.7 F1 (Stras), and +4.5 F1 (Bern).
- For step recognition on MultiBypass140, achieves 30.7 F1 (Stras split) and 23.0 F1 (Bern split).
- Ablations confirm that weighted fusion of visual and text cost matrices (at the OT level) significantly surpasses visual-only, text-only, and feature concatenation baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Multimodal cost fusion improves segmentation by combining complementary visual appearance and semantic textual cues. The model computes separate cosine-distance cost matrices from visual (DINOv3) and textual (CLIP) embeddings for each frame against learned prototypes. A weighted sum `C_i,k = β C_img + (1-β) C_text` integrates these signals before optimal transport. The core assumption is that visual and textual modalities provide non-redundant information for distinguishing surgical actions, and their integration at the cost level is more effective than post-hoc fusion (e.g., concatenation). Evidence includes the multimodal cost definition in the paper and its superior performance in ablation studies. A break condition occurs if either modality is highly noisy or misaligned, causing degradation relative to a unimodal signal.

### Mechanism 2
Temporal consistency enforced via unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein prevents fragmented, incoherent segment boundaries. The OT formulation includes a temporal regularization term that penalizes violations of monotonic alignment between frame indices and cluster assignments, encouraging contiguous segment blocks. The "unbalanced" aspect allows flexible mass transport, accommodating videos where not all phases/steps appear. The core assumption is that surgical procedures have an inherent sequential structure; enforcing temporal smoothness yields more realistic segmentations. Evidence is drawn from the paper's temporal prior injection and strong foundational support from the ASOT framework. A break condition arises if the temporal regularization weight is too high (over-smoothing) or too low (noisy segmentation).

### Mechanism 3
Self-training with OT pseudo-labels bootstraps representation learning without manual annotations. The soft transport plan *T* from the OT solver provides probabilistic pseudo-labels for each frame. These are used with a cross-entropy loss to train the modality-specific projection heads, iteratively refining embeddings and transport. The core assumption is that the initial OT assignment, while noisy, is sufficiently accurate to start a virtuous cycle of representation improvement. Evidence includes the self-training loop description and performance gains over zero-shot baselines. A break condition is if initial pseudo-labels are too poor due to a bad cost matrix, potentially causing representation divergence or convergence to a poor local optimum.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT)**
  - *Why needed here:* To align frame embeddings with a fixed number of prototype clusters while allowing for non-uniform mass distribution (i.e., some clusters may be empty in a given video), which is common in surgical procedures where not all phases occur.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the "unbalanced" KL regularization term in the OT objective differ from standard balanced OT in handling missing classes?

- **Concept: Vision-Language Feature Alignment**
  - *Why needed here:* To make visual features from DINOv3 and textual features from CLIP comparable in the same latent space for cost computation, requiring learned projection heads and normalization.
  - *Quick check question:* Why are separate projection heads for vision and text used instead of a single shared head?

- **Concept: Temporal Segmentation Structure**
  - *Why needed here:* Surgical videos have long, sequential phases; the model must output contiguous segments, not just frame-wise classification. The temporal prior in the cost matrix and OT regularization encode this structural constraint.
  - *Quick check question:* What would happen if the temporal regularization were removed from the cost matrix?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Video input -> (Split into 300s windows) -> Gemini 2.0 Flash captioning -> Temporal text segments -> Per-frame text alignment -> DINOv3 frame encoding & CLIP caption encoding -> Separate projection heads -> L2 normalization -> Cosine distance to *K* prototypes (C_img, C_text) -> Weighted fusion (β) + Temporal prior -> Unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein solver -> Transport plan (pseudo-labels) -> Cross-entropy training of projection heads -> Iterate.

Critical path: Caption generation creates aligned textual supervision; multimodal cost fusion combines visual and text signals; temporal-regularized OT produces coherent pseudo-labels; self-training loop refines embeddings.

Design tradeoffs: Fixed *K* (dataset-wide class count) simplifies but may force empty segments; video-specific *K* adapts but requires knowing true per-video class counts. β tuning balances modality contributions; weighted fusion generally outperforms unimodal or concatenation. Encoder choice (DINOv2/3, CLIP vs. Gemma) impacts quality; CLIP superior for text.

Failure signatures: (1) Poor temporal coherence: fragmented predictions despite decent F1, indicating insufficient temporal regularization. (2) Mode collapse: most frames assigned to few clusters, suggesting cost matrix or OT solver failure. (3) Text misalignment: performance drops when captions are inaccurate; check caption quality. (4) β sensitivity: large performance swings with β changes, indicating modality conflict.

First 3 experiments: (1) Ablate multimodal fusion: compare TASOT (weighted cost) vs. visual-only, text-only, and feature concatenation baselines. (2) Vary cluster number *K*: compare fixed *K* (total classes) vs. video-specific *K* (actual classes per video). (3) Text encoder swap: replace CLIP with Gemma to validate CLIP's superiority.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Is large-scale surgical pretraining truly necessary for effective temporal segmentation, or can an unsupervised approach achieve competitive performance? The work shows gains using general pretrained encoders (DINOv3, CLIP, Gemini), but the necessity of any large-scale pretraining remains open.
- How can the number of action clusters (segments) be automatically determined for each video in an unsupervised setting? TASOT requires predefined *K*; oracle experiments with video-specific *K* show large gains, motivating automatic estimation.
- Does TASOT maintain its effectiveness on non-surgical procedural video domains, and does it generalize across different types of aligned textual cues? Claims of generality lack validation outside surgery; evaluation on domains like cooking or assembly is needed.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters (β, OT ε/τ, temporal weight), ASOT solver details, Gemini prompt, and specific encoder checkpoints are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Reliance on Gemini-generated captions introduces potential misalignment and quality variance across videos.
- Fixed cluster number *K* may not match per-video class sets, causing empty clusters; video-specific *K* requires ground-truth class counts, which may be unavailable in practice.
- β sensitivity risks modality domination if not properly tuned.

## Confidence
- Multimodal cost fusion: **Medium** (ablation evidence present but limited neighbor support).
- Temporal consistency via unbalanced GW: **High** (strong foundation in ASOT and clear contribution).
- Self-training with OT pseudo-labels: **Medium** (gains over zero-shot suggest efficacy but bootstrap validity depends on initial pseudo-label quality).
