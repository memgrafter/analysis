# When Exploration Comes for Free with Mixture-Greedy: Do we need UCB in Diversity-Aware Multi-Armed Bandits?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21716
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21716
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Mixture-Greedy, a simple greedy algorithm without UCB, achieves sublinear regret and often outperforms Mixture-UCB on diversity-aware metrics (FID, Vendi) by leveraging implicit exploration from interior mixtures.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of online selection and combination of multiple generative models, where sampling from suboptimal models is costly and diversity-aware metrics (e.g., FID, Vendi) may favor a mixture over a single best model. The proposed Mixture-Greedy strategy optimizes the empirical objective over the simplex at each step and samples from the resulting mixture, without any UCB exploration bonus. Diversity-aware objectives inherently favor interior mixtures, ensuring that all generators are sampled linearly. Theoretically, under structural conditions such as population innovation for log-Vendi and interiority margin for Fréchet, Mixture-Greedy achieves sublinear regret (e.g., $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$). Empirically, across datasets (FFHQ, LSUN-Bedroom, ImageNet) and metrics (FID, Vendi, RKE, KID), it converges faster and often outperforms Mixture-UCB, especially for non-quadratic metrics. These results challenge the necessity of explicit confidence bonuses in diversity-aware multi-armed bandit problems.

## Method Summary
The algorithm begins with a warm‑start of $M$ samples from each generative model to initialize sufficient statistics. Then, for each round $t$: (1) compute an empirical diversity‑aware loss (e.g., Fréchet distance, Vendi, or quadratic RKE) based on all collected samples; (2) solve a convex optimization over the probability simplex $\Delta^m$ to obtain mixture weights $\alpha_t$ (using exponentiated gradient descent); (3) sample an arm $I_t$ from the categorical distribution defined by $\alpha_t$; (4) observe a sample from the chosen model and update the relevant statistics. The key insight is that the objective’s geometry penalizes degenerate mixtures, forcing $\alpha_t$ to stay away from the simplex boundary and guaranteeing linear sampling of all arms. No explicit exploration bonus is used; exploration emerges implicitly from the optimization.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantee:** Under conditions (population innovation for log‑Vendi, interiority margin for Fréchet), Mixture‑Greedy attains sublinear regret, e.g., $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ for the studied objectives.
- **Empirical superiority:** Across FFHQ, LSUN‑Bedroom, and ImageNet datasets, and using FID, Vendi, RKE, and KID metrics, Mixture‑Greedy converges faster and often achieves better final performance than Mixture‑UCB, particularly for non‑quadratic metrics like FID and Vendi where UCB bounds are loose.
- **Implicit exploration:** Diversity‑aware objectives induce a natural exploration effect by favoring interior mixtures, eliminating the need for explicit confidence‑based bonuses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Diversity‑aware objectives (e.g., FID, Vendi) inherently favor **interior mixtures**, causing the greedy empirical minimizer to assign non‑zero weight to all arms, which ensures linear sampling without an explicit UCB bonus.
- **Mechanism**: The objective landscape penalizes degenerate mixtures (weights on a single arm). For instance, maximizing Vendi (von Neumann entropy) is equivalent to minimizing negative log‑Vendi, which attains its optimum at a fully mixed state when arms provide complementary features. When greedy optimization (`argmin_α bL_t(α)`) is applied to the empirical objective, the solution stays uniformly away from the simplex boundary under conditions like population innovation (log‑Vendi) or interiority margin (Fréchet). Consequently, each arm’s sampling probability remains bounded below, leading to linear sampling rates.
- **Core assumption**: The population objective has an **interior optimum** or the empirical minimizers are **uniformly interior** with enough samples.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: “diversity‑aware objectives induce implicit exploration by favoring interior mixtures, naturally ensuring all generators are sampled.”
  - [section 5.1]: Assumption 2 (population innovation for log‑Vendi) and Lemma 6 show that empirical minimizers satisfy `α_min ≥ γ_min > 0`.
  - [corpus]: The neighbor paper “DAK‑UCB: Diversity‑Aware Prompt Routing for LLMs and Generative Models” (FMR 0.551) also deals with diversity‑aware prompt routing but uses an explicit UCB, indicating the field’s prior reliance on optimism bonuses.
- **Break condition**: If the population optimum lies on the boundary (e.g., one arm strictly dominates under the diversity metric) or early estimation error causes the empirical minimizer to collapse to a vertex, implicit exploration vanishes and regret may become linear.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Under structural conditions, the empirical objectives (e.g., `bL_{FD}`, `bL_{Vendi}`) are **convex** and exhibit **uniform concentration** around their population counterparts, enabling greedy optimization to track the true optimum with sublinear regret.
- **Mechanism**: Proposition 1 shows convexity for the three objective families. Combined with uniform deviation bounds (Lemma 4 for Vendi, analogous for Fréchet), the greedy iterate `α_t` stays close to the population minimizer. The regret `Reg_T` scales as `O(√T)` because the uniform deviation is `O(√t)` and the number of rounds is `T`.
- **Core assumption**: The objective function is smooth (convex for Vendi; Fréchet is smooth in the interior) and the arm distributions have bounded moments (e.g., `||Z||_2 ≤ B` for Fréchet).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4]: Proposition 1 proves convexity for `bL_{FD}`, `bL_{Vendi}`, and `bL_quad`.
  - [section 5.1]: Lemma 4 gives a Frobenius norm uniform bound for empirical covariances under normalized features.
  - [corpus]: The neighbor “Data‑Driven Upper Confidence Bounds with Near‑Optimal Regret for Heavy‑Tailed Bandits” (FMR 0.555) uses UCB for heavy‑tailed bandits, contrasting with the paper’s greedy approach.
- **Break condition**: Non‑convex objectives or unbounded moments would invalidate the concentration arguments, potentially causing greedy to diverge.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A **warm‑start** of `M` samples per arm, combined with the interiority property, yields **linear sampling rates** (`n_i(t) = Ω(t)`), which in turn ensures the uniform deviation decays as `O(1/√t)`, closing the loop to sublinear regret.
- **Mechanism**: Warm‑start provides initial statistics. The interiority guarantee (e.g., Lemma 6) means `α_{t,i} ≥ γ_min` for all `i`. Then Lemma 2 (a standard Azuma‑Hoeffding argument) shows that the count `n_i(t)` grows at least linearly: `n_i(t) ≥ M + γ_min t - O(√t)`. This implies `n_min(t) = Θ(t)`, so the maximal deviation `ε_t = max_i ||bS_i(t) - S_i||_F` scales as `O(1/√t)`. Summing the instantaneous regrets (`O(1/√t)`) gives `O(√T)`.
- **Core assumption**: The warm‑start size `M` is sufficiently large to establish the interiority condition in the first few rounds (Lemma 6 requires `η ≤ ν0/4`, which depends on `M`).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 5]: Theorem 3 (log‑Vendi) and Theorem 2 (Fréchet) explicitly state the warm‑start requirement and derive `Reg_T = O(√T)`.
  - [algorithm 1]: The algorithm begins with `M` warm‑start samples from each model.
  - [corpus]: The neighbor “Revisiting Social Welfare in Bandits: UCB is (Nearly) All You Need” (FMR 0.567) considers UCB for social welfare, but does not address mixture‑greedy; the contrast highlights the novelty of avoiding optimism.
- **Break condition**: If `M` is too small, the early empirical minimizer might assign zero weight to some arm, and that arm will never be sampled again (since greedy never forces exploration once `α_i=0`). The analysis breaks down.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Convex optimization over the probability simplex.
  - **Why needed**: Mixture‑Greedy requires solving $\alpha_t = \arg\min_{\alpha \in \Delta^m} bL_t(\alpha)$ each round. The loss functions (FD, Vendi, quadratic) are convex over $\Delta^m$ (Proposition 1), enabling efficient global optimization via methods like exponentiated gradient.
  - **Quick check**: Why does the Fréchet distance loss remain convex even though the square‑root matrix term is non‑linear? (Because the trace of the square‑root of a concave matrix function yields a concave term, and the overall loss is affine minus a concave term, hence convex.)

- **Concept**: Uniform deviation / concentration of empirical statistics.
  - **Why needed**: To bound $\sup_\alpha |bL_t(\alpha) - L(\alpha)|$, which drives regret. The analysis uses matrix Hoeffding bounds (Lemma 4) and continuity of the objective (e.g., Fannes‑Audenaert for Vendi).
  - **Quick check**: How does the $O(\sqrt{t})$ deviation rate arise from $n_{\min}(t) = \Omega(t)$ and matrix concentration?

- **Concept**: Interiority conditions in mixture optimization.
  - **Why needed**: They guarantee that greedy mixtures remain non‑degenerate, forcing linear sampling. Without them, greedy could collapse to a single arm.
  - **Quick check**: What is the difference between the population interiority margin for Fréchet (Assumption 5) and the population innovation condition for log‑Vendi (Assumption 2)?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Statistics Tracker → Optimization Solver → Sampler → Update Block  
- **Critical path**: The solver’s outcome (`α_t`) dictates which arm is sampled; the updated statistics affect the next round’s solver. Performance depends on solver accuracy and sufficient statistics fidelity.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Solver choice**: Exponentiated gradient (EG) is simple and works well for smooth objectives, but for very high‑dimensional $m$ one might consider projected gradient or Frank‑Wolfe. EG’s stepsize $\eta$ must be tuned; the paper uses a fixed small $\eta$.
  - **Kernel computation**: For Vendi with large pooled sample size $N$, computing $N×N$ kernel matrix and its log is expensive. The paper recommends using **random Fourier features** to reduce to a $D×D$ feature covariance matrix (with $D \ll N$), trading off approximation error for speed.
  - **Warm‑start size $M$**: Must be large enough to satisfy the theoretical conditions (e.g., $\eta \leq \nu_0/4$ for log‑Vendi). Too small $M$ risks early collapse; too large wastes budget. Empirically, $M=100$ in image experiments is adequate.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Degenerate mixture**: At some round $t$, the optimizer returns `α_t` with one or more weights extremely close to zero. Check: Is the empirical objective near‑linear? Verify that the objective truly favors diversity. If the population optimum is on the boundary, greedy will fail; consider switching to a Mixture‑UCB variant.
  - **Non‑convergence of EG**: If the EG loops do not decrease `bL_t` sufficiently, increase the number of EG steps $S$ or the stepsize. Monitor `α_t` changes.
  - **Memory blow‑up for Vendi**: The pooled Gram matrix grows with $t$. If $t$ is large, switch to random Fourier features or truncated eigenvalue approximation for the log operation.
  - **Numerical instability in FD**: The Fréchet distance involves a matrix square‑root and inverse‑square‑root. Ensure covariance estimates are regularized (e.g., add $\lambda I$ to $\Sigma$ during early rounds when $n_i$ is small).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Synthetic Gaussian mixture**: Create two arms with known Gaussians. The population Fréchet optimum is interior if covariances differ. Verify that `α_t` converges to the known `α*` and that all arms are sampled throughout.
  2. **Quadratic objective (RKE) sanity check**: Use a small set of arms with known feature covariances. The objective $\text{Tr}(S_\alpha^2)$ is convex. Compare Mixture‑Greedy against a uniform baseline and UCB (if implemented) to see that greedy converges faster and avoids over‑exploration.
  3. **Non‑quadratic (Vendi) on a toy dataset**: Use a few pretrained small diffusion models (or even different random seeds of the same model with diverse outputs). Verify that the mixture improves Vendi over any single arm and that the algorithm does not abandon any arm early.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on stringent structural conditions (e.g., interior population optimum) that may not hold universally; if the population optimum lies on the boundary, greedy can suffer linear regret.
- The warm‑start size $M$ is critical; too small risks early collapse to degenerate mixtures with no recovery, yet no automatic method for setting $M$ in new problems is provided.
- Empirical evaluation is restricted to image generation metrics (FID, Vendi) and a few datasets (FFHQ, LSUN‑Bedroom, ImageNet); generalization to other domains or non‑diversity objectives remains unverified.

## Confidence
- Implicit exploration via interior mixtures under diversity‑aware objectives: **High**
- Sublinear regret for convex objectives (Fréchet, log‑Vendi, quadratic): **Medium**
- Empirical outperformance of Mixture‑UCB: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. Test non‑convex diversity objectives: Implement Mixture‑Greedy with recently proposed non‑convex diversity metrics (e.g., from robust statistics) and verify if interior mixtures still emerge and regret scales sublinearly.
2. Ablation study on warm‑start $M$: Systematically vary $M$ (e.g., 10–1000) across synthetic and real tasks to identify thresholds where interiority guarantees hold and quantify regret increase when $M$ is too small.
3. Broad baseline comparison: Evaluate Mixture‑Greedy against Thompson Sampling, adaptive UCB variants, and pure greedy (no warm‑start) in diverse bandit settings (e.g., combinatorial, contextual) to test generalizability beyond the paper’s focused comparison.
