# UniFAR: A Unified Facet-Aware Retrieval Framework for Scientific Documents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23766
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23766
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** UniFAR achieves state-of-the-art performance on both q-doc (DORIS-MAE) and doc-doc (CSFCube) benchmarks, with improvements of up to +0.31 MAP and +0.54 Recall@5.

## Executive Summary
UniFAR introduces a unified retrieval framework to reconcile mismatches between existing document-centric scientific retrieval models and the emerging question-driven needs of systems like retrieval-augmented generation. The framework employs three core innovations: an adaptive multi-granularity aggregation mechanism that handles inputs of varying lengths, learnable facet anchors that align rhetorical structure with query intent, and a facet-aware joint training objective that unifies citation-based (doc-doc) and question-oriented (q-doc) supervision. Evaluations on standard benchmarks demonstrate consistent superiority across multiple base encoders, establishing UniFAR as a robust solution for scientific document retrieval.

## Method Summary
UniFAR processes inputs (documents or questions) through an Input Formatter that produces a unified `[SEP]`-delimited string. A replaceable Base Encoder (e.g., SciBERT, Contriever, MPNet) generates token embeddings, and sentence pooling yields sentence embeddings. A set of shared learnable facet anchors, conditioned on the input context (title for documents, `[CLS]` for questions), is transformed by an MLP with residual connection to produce facet queries. An adaptive selector chooses between sentence-level and token-level multi-head attention based on the number of sentences relative to the number of facets, producing facet-specific embeddings. For a query-candidate pair, a facet similarity matrix is computed; retrieval scores are derived by averaging the diagonal for q-doc or using facet-wise diagonals for doc-doc. Training uses three losses: contrastive InfoNCE losses for doc-doc (LDD) and q-doc (LQD) pairs, and a KL divergence loss (LKL) that aligns predicted facet–sentence attention with LLM-generated facet labels. This unified formulation enables strong performance on both retrieval settings.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art on DORIS-MAE (q-doc) with +0.31 MAP and +0.54 Recall@5 over prior methods.
- Improved facet-level nDCG and MAP on CSFCube (doc-doc) benchmark.
- Gains hold consistently across diverse base models (SciBERT, Contriever, MPNet).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Adaptive multi-granularity aggregation reconciles the input granularity mismatch between long scientific documents and short questions.
- **Mechanism:** A selector chooses between sentence-level and token-level multi-head attention based on the number of sentences |S| relative to the number of facets N_facet. If |S| ≥ N_facet, sentence-level attention aggregates over sentence embeddings; otherwise, token-level attention aggregates over token embeddings. This yields robust facet embeddings across varying input lengths.
- **Core assumption:** Sentence-level attention is stable and effective for longer inputs with sufficient sentences, while token-level attention provides finer granularity for short inputs where sentence-level signals are sparse.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "reconciles granularity differences through adaptive multi-granularity aggregation"
  - [section 3.2] Describes the selector and two attention branches with conditional equation.
  - [corpus] Neighbors such as MixGR (Cai et al., 2024) and CoRank explore granularity challenges, but UniFAR uniquely integrates an adaptive selection within a facet-aware framework.
- **Break condition:** If N_facet is poorly tuned or the attention heads fail to learn meaningful aggregations, the selector may consistently choose one branch in inappropriate contexts. Ablation (Table 3) shows performance drops when either branch is removed, especially on the task where that branch is critical (e.g., removing token attention harms q-doc retrieval on DORIS-MAE).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Learnable facet anchors align scientific discourse structure with specific question intent by producing input-specific facet queries.
- **Mechanism:** A set of shared anchor vectors A_facet is concatenated with an input context embedding (title for documents, [CLS] for questions), transformed via an MLP with residual connection and layer normalization to generate facet queries Q_facet. These queries are input-specific and guide the multi-granularity attention to extract facet-relevant evidence.
- **Core assumption:** Rhetorical facets (background, method, result) can be represented as latent prototypes, and conditioning these prototypes on the input yields queries that appropriately match the document structure and question intent.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "aligns document structure with question intent via learnable facet anchors"
  - [section 3.2] Details the anchor generation and the Q_facet construction process.
  - [corpus] Other facet-aware works (FLeW, FaBle) use fixed or separately trained encoders; UniFAR introduces unified learnable anchors, and ablation shows freezing or removing anchors degrades performance (Table 3, Figure 3).
- **Break condition:** If anchors are frozen or removed, facet queries become less discriminative, leading to collapsed facet embeddings (observed in w/o A_facet). Poor learning of anchor vectors may also cause all queries to converge to similar directions, eliminating facet disentanglement.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Facet-aware joint training unifies doc-doc citation-based supervision and q-doc question-oriented relevance within a shared semantic space, addressing training signal mismatch.
- **Mechanism:** Training units (FTU) combine a query document, its facet-specific positives/negatives (from citations), and facet-specific generated questions. For each facet, InfoNCE contrastive losses are applied to doc-doc (LDD) and q-doc (LQD) pairs. A KL loss (LKL) aligns the predicted facet–sentence attention with sentence-level facet labels, acting as a structural regularizer.
- **Core assumption:** Documents and questions can be embedded in the same space, and facet-level alignment provides a common supervision signal that benefits both retrieval settings.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "unifies doc-doc and q-doc supervision through joint training"
  - [section 4] Lays out FTU construction, forward pass, and the three losses (Equation 1, 2, 3).
  - [section 5.2] Results show UniFAR outperforms baselines trained separately for doc-doc (e.g., FLeW) or q-doc (e.g., FaBle). Ablation (Table 3) reveals that removing LKL or LQD hurts performance across both benchmarks.
  - [corpus] Approaches like SciRepEval and MixGR target either doc-doc or q-doc; UniFAR’s joint framework bridges both, as seen in its consistent gains across tasks.
- **Break condition:** If the gradients from LDD and LQD conflict, joint training could destabilize. The paper restricts LQD gradients to the question branch and uses a weight schedule for λ to mitigate. Noisy facet labels in LKL may also misguide learning; high KL loss without improvement signals such a failure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dense retrieval**
  - *Why needed here:* UniFAR’s base model produces dense embeddings for both documents and questions, forming the core matching space.
  - *Quick check question:* What distinguishes dense retrieval from traditional sparse retrieval, and why is it more suitable for semantic matching?

- **Contrastive learning (InfoNCE)**
  - *Why needed here:* The framework uses InfoNCE loss for both doc-doc and q-doc pairs, enabling multi-positive training and stable optimization.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the InfoNCE objective differ from triplet loss, and what advantage does it offer for retrieval?

- **Facet modeling in scientific documents**
  - *Why needed here:* UniFAR represents documents via discrete rhetorical facets (background, method, result) to align with question intent and structure.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the typical rhetorical facets of a scientific paper, and how do they reflect user information needs?

- **Multi-granularity representations**
  - *Why needed here:* Token and sentence levels provide complementary signals; the adaptive selector chooses the appropriate granularity.
  - *Quick check question:* Under what conditions would token-level attention be preferred over sentence-level in UniFAR?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Formatter → Base Encoder → Facet Anchor Module → Multi-Granularity Attention → Facet Similarity Matrix → Retrieval Strategy. Loss modules (LDD, LQD, LKL) act on the embeddings and attention outputs.

- **Critical path:** Input → Base Encoder → token & sentence embeddings → E_input → Q_facet → Selector → Attention → {e^{(f)}_q}, {e^{(f)}_c} → M_{q,c} → final score. During training: compute LDD, LQD, LKL from embeddings and attention maps; backpropagate total L_total.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Replaceable base model: flexibility across SciBERT/Contriever/MPNet but requires per-model hyperparameter tuning (LR, etc.).
  - Fixed N_facet: simplifies training and matches common rhetorical structure; however, it may not adapt to domains with more/less facets.
  - Joint training: unifies objectives and yields better generalization but complicates data construction (FTU) and loss balancing (λ schedule).
  - Adaptive granularity: handles variable input lengths effectively but introduces a heuristic threshold that might not generalize to extreme cases.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Sharp performance drop on short queries (DORIS-MAE) if token-level attention branch is disabled or under-trained.
  - Uniform facet similarity matrix (no diagonal pattern) indicates anchor collapse or poor conditioning.
  - KL loss remains high while retrieval metrics do not improve → noisy sentence-level facet labels.
  - Divergence between LDD and LQD curves during training suggests conflicting gradient directions; inspect gradient masking.
  - Attention visualizations show one facet dominating all input positions → facet disentanglement failed.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce main results:** Train UniFAR-SciBERT on the provided FTU corpus and evaluate on DORIS-MAE (q-doc) and CSFCube (doc-doc). Confirm MAP improvements over SciBERT/SPECTER baselines.
  2. **Ablation of multi-granularity:** Remove sentence-level (w/o MHAsent) and token-level (w/o MHAtok) branches separately; expect larger drop on CSFCube when sentence branch removed, and on DORIS-MAE when token branch removed.
  3. **Ablation of joint training:** Train with only LDD, only LQD, and without LKL; compare to full model to validate contribution of each loss term, especially for cross-task generalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can UniFAR dynamically adapt the number or granularity of facets (N_facet) to different retrieval scenarios, query complexities, or scientific domains, rather than using a fixed set of background/method/result anchors?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section states: "the number of facets in UniFAR is fixed during training... Dynamically adapting the number or granularity of facets to different retrieval scenarios... may further improve flexibility and retrieval effectiveness."
- **Why unresolved:** The current framework uses a static three-facet schema. Adaptive facet discovery could better handle diverse document structures (e.g., review papers with more sections) or user intents (e.g., nuanced queries requiring finer-grained facets), but no mechanism for automatic adjustment is proposed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments evaluating performance with variable N_facet (e.g., 4–6 facets) on benchmarks with heterogeneous document structures, or a clustering-based method to derive facet anchors from training data, with results compared to the fixed three-facet model.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does the facet–sentence attention alignment enforced by L_KL generalize across scientific domains and writing styles, and does it consistently yield interpretable attention maps on unseen corpora beyond the curated FTU training data?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The KL-loss supervises attention with LLM-generated facet labels (Section 4.1). The case study (Appendix F) shows coherent patterns on a LitSearch sample, but the paper does not analyze domain shift or whether the attention remains semantically meaningful on documents with atypical structures or from fields with different rhetorical conventions.
- **Why unresolved:** The label generation prompt (Table 6) is abstract-specific and may not generalize to full papers or disciplines like mathematics or philosophy with different discourse patterns. Unanswered is whether the learned attention is robust or overfits to the LLM’s labeling biases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Cross-domain evaluation of attention alignment scores (e.g., facet–sentence match accuracy) on held-out corpora from diverse fields, coupled with human evaluation of attention heatmaps for interpretability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the impact of freezing gradients from L_QD to the base model while allowing gradients from L_DD to update it? Could joint training with full gradient flow improve q-doc retrieval, or does the design truly prevent catastrophic forgetting of document structure?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Figure 2 notes: "gradients from L_QD are restricted to the question branch to preserve document representations." This design choice implies a trade-off: protecting doc-doc performance might limit q-doc adaptation. The paper does not ablate this gradient restriction.
- **Why unresolved:** The authors hypothesize that full gradient flow could distort document representations optimized for citation-based similarity, but no experiment compares frozen vs. unfrozen base model updates under joint training. The actual contribution of the gradient constraint to the reported gains is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation where L_QD gradients are allowed to update the base model, measuring changes in both doc-doc (CSFCube) and q-doc (DORIS-MAE) performance, especially on facet-level alignment and query understanding.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do LLM-generated facet-specific questions (used in FTUs) effectively approximate real user information needs in scientific retrieval, or do they introduce distributional shifts that limit UniFAR’s effectiveness on human-authored queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Training questions are synthetically generated via prompts (Table 5) from facet-positive documents. The evaluation uses human-written benchmarks (DORIS-MAE, LitSearch). The paper does not analyze whether synthetic questions cover the diversity and complexity of real user queries.
- **Why unresolved:** Synthetic questions may be overly canonical or simplify facet boundaries, creating a mismatch between training and deployment distributions. The strong results on human-query benchmarks suggest generalization, but the underlying reason (robust facet anchors vs. synthetic data adequacy) is untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare UniFAR trained on synthetic vs. human-annotated facet questions (if available) on q-doc benchmarks. Analysis could include embedding distribution comparisons (e.g., t-SNE of question vectors) and error case studies on out-of-distribution query types.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can the facet-aware aggregation mechanism be extended to incorporate other structural signals beyond rhetorical facets (e.g., citation contexts, figure/table references, or keyword phrases) to further improve retrieval for questions that target specific evidence types?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] UniFAR focuses on three rhetorical facets (bg/mt/rs) as semantic anchors. The paper notes in the Limitations that "facet-level representations" are key, but does not explore alternative or additional structural dimensions, despite the multi-granularity attention being capable of attending to any token/sentence units.
- **Why unresolved:** Scientific queries often target specific evidence like datasets, algorithms, or results. Rhetorical facets are coarse; aligning with more granular evidence types could enhance precision. The current design’s flexibility for other anchor definitions is mentioned but not investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace or augment facet anchors with evidence-type anchors (e.g., "dataset", "method name", "metric") and evaluate on subsets of queries that explicitly mention such evidence. Ablation studies would measure the contribution of non-rhetorical facets.

## Limitations
- Fixed number of facets (N_facet) may not generalize to all scientific domains or document types.
- Adaptive granularity selector relies on a simplistic heuristic (|S| ≥ N_facet) that may fail on extreme input lengths.
- Joint training with noisy sentence-level facet labels (LKL) and potential gradient conflicts complicates optimization and may limit applicability to new corpora.

## Confidence
