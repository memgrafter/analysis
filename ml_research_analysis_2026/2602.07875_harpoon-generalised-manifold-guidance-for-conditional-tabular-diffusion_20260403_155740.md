# Harpoon: Generalised Manifold Guidance for Conditional Tabular Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07875  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07875  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Harpoon improves imputation accuracy and constraint satisfaction with ≤2× diffusion runtime overhead.

## Executive Summary
Harpoon introduces a manifold-guided diffusion approach for conditional tabular generation. It leverages the theoretical insight that diffusion denoisers act as orthogonal projectors, ensuring that gradients of any differentiable test-time loss lie in the data manifold's tangent space. By interleaving standard denoising steps with tangential gradient updates, Harpoon steers samples toward diverse constraints while remaining on the manifold. Empirical evaluations on eight datasets show state-of-the-art imputation performance under MAR, MCAR, and MNAR, and reduce constraint violations from >70% to <8% for inequality constraints, all with modest computational overhead.

## Method Summary
Harpoon builds upon diffusion models for tabular data. The core innovation is to use the denoiser as an orthogonal projector onto the data manifold. At each denoising timestep, after obtaining a denoised sample, the algorithm computes the gradient of a differentiable constraint loss. Because the denoiser’s output is an orthogonal projection, this gradient is guaranteed to be tangent to the manifold. A small tangential step along the gradient is then taken before re-initiating the next denoising step. This interleaving permits arbitrary test-time conditioning without leaving the data manifold, making the method widely applicable to tasks like imputation and constrained generation.

## Key Results
- Imputation: Lower MSE and higher categorical accuracy than SOTA on MAR, MCAR, MNAR, especially under high missingness.  
- Constraint satisfaction: Violation rates drop from >70% to below 8% across range, category, conjunctions, and disjunctions.  
- Runtime: Overhead stays within 2× of unguided diffusion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The orthogonal projector property of denoisers (Theorem 3.1) ensures that any gradient of a loss function computed on the denoised sample lies within the tangent space of the data manifold. Consequently, taking a step along this gradient does not move the sample off the manifold. By interleaving these tangential updates with denoising, Harpoon gradually nudges the sample toward satisfying the desired constraints while preserving the generative quality inherent to diffusion. This synergy between geometry and optimization explains the method’s effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Initial noisy sample → Diffusion denoiser (εθ) → Denoised sample → Constraint loss gradient → Tangent‑space projection (implicit via denoiser property) → Tangential update (step size η) → New sample (re‑noised for next timestep)

### Critical Path
1. Sample from Gaussian at timestep T.  
2. For t = T down to 1:  
   a. Perform denoising step to obtain clean estimate x̂₀.  
   b. Compute gradient ∇L(x̂₀) of constraint loss.  
   c. Because the denoiser is an orthogonal projector, ∇L(x̂₀) is already in the tangent space.  
   d. Update x̂₀ ← x̂₀ – η ∇L(x̂₀).  
   e. Add noise according to diffusion schedule to obtain next sample at t−1.  
3. Return final sample at t=0.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Interleaving frequency**: More tangential steps per timestep increase constraint adherence but risk divergence and higher compute.  
- **Step size η**: Larger η speeds convergence but may overshoot; adaptive schemes trade stability for efficiency.  
- **Loss choice**: MSE vs. MAE; MAE aligns with sparsity (e.g., one‑hot features) and empirically works better at inference despite MSE training.  
- **Backbone flexibility**: The theory holds for Gaussian noising; extending to discrete diffusion (e.g., multinomial) remains an open challenge.

### Failure Signatures
- Constraint violations remain high after many steps.  
- Sample quality degrades (e.g., unrealistic values, loss of diversity).  
- Numerical instability: NaNs or exploding norms in gradients.  
- Oscillation: Constraint satisfaction alternates between feasible and infeasible across steps.  
- Excessive runtime: Overhead significantly exceeds 2× baseline.

### First Experiments
1. **Validate orthogonal projection**: On synthetic manifold data, compute the angle between the loss gradient and the residual (denoiser input – output); it should be near 90°.  
2. **Basic imputation test**: Apply Harpoon to a small dataset (e.g., Boston Housing) with 30% MAR missingness; compare MSE and categorical accuracy against vanilla diffusion (no guidance) and a simple baseline (mean imputation).  
3. **η scaling study**: Harpoon on a constrained generation task (e.g., range constraints) varying η from very small to large; record constraint violation rate and sample fidelity to identify a stable operating range.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does the orthogonal projection property (Theorem 3.1) hold for discrete diffusion processes (e.g., multinomial, masked) that use non-Gaussian noising?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "extending the theory itself to discrete noising mechanisms... would broaden applicability" (Sec. 5, Limitations).  
- **Why unresolved**: Theorem 3.1's proof relies on Gaussian continuity; discrete transitions break this continuity, so tangential gradient behavior may not be guaranteed.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A generalized theorem proving orthogonal projection under discrete noising; or empirical measurements of gradient angles for discrete-diffusion backbones showing near-tangential alignment.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Under disjunctive constraints (OR), why does Harpoon preferentially converge to certain feasible submanifolds, and can this bias be controlled to improve coverage?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper hypothesizes "gradient updates may prioritize one condition over another" causing skewed distributions (Sec. 4.3, Fig 4).  
- **Why unresolved**: No theory explains how initialization, η, or geometry determine which disconnected region is selected; this harms diversity under OR constraints.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Simulations mapping convergence basins; theoretical analysis of gradient flow on disconnected manifolds; coverage metrics for all feasible regions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does the interleaved sampling (Alg 1) converge for all t, η, and manifold curvatures? What stability conditions ensure it?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The algorithm interleaves denoising with tangential corrections but lacks any convergence analysis.  
- **Why unresolved**: Tangential steps could cause divergence or oscillation, especially on curved manifolds or with large η; no bounds on η exist.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Prove convergence under η ≤ η_max(t, curvature); empirical stability tests varying η and curvature metrics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Why does MAE loss at inference (even after MSE training) outperform MSE, despite Theorem 3.2 guaranteeing tangential gradients for both?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "ablations confirm that using a sparsity-inducing MAE loss at inference—even after MSE training—is optimal" (Abstract; Table 4).  
- **Why unresolved**: Theorem 3.2 applies to any differentiable loss, so both MAE and MSE should be valid. The empirical advantage likely stems from MAE's alignment with sparse one-hot features, but no theory explains this.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Study gradient norms and directional stability for MAE vs MSE; analyze how loss smoothness interacts with manifold geometry; test other sparsity-inducing losses.

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantee relies on the data manifold being smooth and low-dimensional, which may not hold for high-cardinality tabular data.  
- Evaluation on only eight datasets limits confidence in generalizability to very high-dimensional or extreme missingness scenarios.  
- The reported 2× runtime overhead lacks absolute scaling details; memory and time costs may be prohibitive for large tables.

## Confidence
- Denoiser orthogonal projection property: **Medium**  
- Empirical superiority (imputation, constraint satisfaction): **Medium**  
- Runtime overhead (≤2×): **Low**
