# Fact-Checking with Large Language Models via Probabilistic Certainty and Consistency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02574
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02574
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PCC reduces miscalibration (ECE) versus verbal confidence and improves fact-checking F1, especially on false claims, by adaptively routing claims to appropriate verification strategies.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses inefficiencies in LLM-based fact-checking, where models often retrieve external evidence unnecessarily, ignoring internal knowledge and introducing noise. It introduces Probabilistic Certainty and Consistency (PCC), a framework that estimates a claim's factual confidence by combining two signals: internal certainty (derived from the log-probability margin of the verdict) and reasoning consistency (coherence between rationales generated under opposing truth assumptions). These signals guide an adaptive pipeline that selects between direct answering, targeted search, reflection-guided retrieval, or deep iterative search. Across multiple LLM families and benchmarks, PCC achieves substantially better uncertainty quantification (lower Expected Calibration Error) and higher fact-checking F1 scores than strong baselines like FIRE, with notable gains on false claims.

## Method Summary
PCC first generates a verdict and two opposing rationales for a claim using an LLM. Internal certainty τ(c) is computed from the probability margin between "True" and "False" tokens in the verdict output. Reasoning consistency γ(c) is computed as 1 minus the average contradiction score, where an NLI model evaluates every pair of rationales (one assuming the claim is true, one assuming it is false). These two scores route the claim into one of four strategies: (1) high τ & γ → direct answer; (2) high τ & low γ → targeted search using queries derived from the most contradictory rationale pair; (3) low τ & high γ → reflection-guided retrieval; (4) low τ & low γ → deep iterative search. After retrieval, a final verification step produces the verdict.

## Key Results
- PCC significantly reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) compared to verbal confidence across diverse LLMs and datasets.
- It achieves superior macro-F1 scores, with a 15.2% relative improvement on false claims in the HoVER benchmark.
- The adaptive routing successfully reduces unnecessary retrievals while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- Gains are consistent across both proprietary (e.g., GPT-4) and open-source (e.g., Mistral) models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Signal Confidence Estimation
- **Claim:** PCC estimates factual confidence by combining internal certainty (log-probability margin) and reasoning consistency (NLI-based coherence between rationales generated under opposing assumptions).
- **Mechanism:** For a claim *c*, internal certainty τ(*c*) is derived from the absolute difference between aggregated class probabilities for "True" and "False" tokens, or set to 1 if the top two tokens agree on a verdict class. Reasoning consistency γ(*c*) is computed as 1 minus the average NLI contradiction score across rationales generated assuming *c* is true vs. false. These signals are used jointly to route claims.
- **Core assumption:** A model's factual reliability is reflected both in the decisiveness of its output distribution (certainty) and the logical stability of its explanations under adversarial framing (consistency).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...combining internal certainty (log-probability margin of the verdict) and reasoning consistency (NLI-based coherence between rationales assumed true vs. false)."
  - [section 2.1] "We define the internal certainty score τ(c) as: ..."
  - [section 2.2] "We define reasoning consistency γ as: γ(c) = 1 − ¯ϕcontr(c)"
- **Break condition:** If either signal is unreliable—e.g., log-probabilities are inaccessible via API, or NLI model introduces domain bias—the combined confidence estimate degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Retrieval Orchestration
- **Claim:** The adaptive verification pipeline uses PCC signals to select retrieval strategies, avoiding unnecessary searches while escalating effort for ambiguous claims.
- **Mechanism:** Claims are partitioned into four quadrants using thresholds α, β on τ(*c*) and γ(*c*). High certainty & high consistency → direct answer; high certainty & low consistency → targeted search based on contradictory rationale pairs; low certainty & high consistency → reflection-guided retrieval; low both → deep iterative search.
- **Core assumption:** Different uncertainty patterns (overconfidence vs. knowledge gaps) require distinct verification strategies; a one-size-fits-all retrieval approach is inefficient and noisy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] "These confidence signals enable an adaptive verification strategy: ... direct answer ... targeted search ... reflection-guided retrieval ... deep search."
  - [section 4.5] Table 3 shows PCC compensates for lower model capability by adaptively triggering retrieval.
  - [section 4.6] Table 4 demonstrates quadrant-specific effectiveness: targeted search helps when certainty high/consistency low; deep search helps when certainty low/consistency high.
- **Break condition:** Fixed thresholds α, β may not generalize across domains or model families, leading to misrouting (e.g., unnecessary deep search for simple claims).

### Mechanism 3: Superior Calibration via Grounded Confidence
- **Claim:** PCC provides better-calibrated factual confidence than verbal confidence, leading to improved fact-checking F1, especially on false claims.
- **Mechanism:** By grounding confidence in token probabilities and NLI consistency—both less prone to prompt manipulation than self-reported verbal confidence—PCC reduces overconfidence. This improves calibration (lower ECE) and allows the routing logic to avoid retrieving for confident/correct claims while retrieving for uncertain/error-prone ones.
- **Core assumption:** Verbal confidence is systematically miscalibrated and varies with prompt design; probability-based signals are more stable and model-agnostic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "PCC achieves superior uncertainty quantification, significantly reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) compared to verbal confidence ... with notable gains on false claims."
  - [section 2.3] Figure 3 shows consistently lower ECE for PCC vs. verbal confidence across datasets/models.
  - [section 4.2] Table 1 shows PCC outperforms baselines, e.g., 15.2% relative gain on false claims in HoVER.
  - [section 4.4] Figure 4 shows PCC (using FIRE's search module) beats verbal-confidence guided FIRE.
- **Break condition:** If log-probability extraction is inaccurate (e.g., due to sampling artifacts) or NLI models fail on domain-specific rationale pairs, PCC's calibration advantage may diminish.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Token-level log-probability as a certainty proxy**
  - Why needed here: Internal certainty τ(*c*) directly uses the margin between aggregated probabilities of verdict tokens. Understanding how to extract and interpret log-probabilities from autoregressive models is essential.
  - Quick check question: Given a claim and the LLM's top-2 tokens with probabilities p₁=0.6 (True), p₂=0.3 (False), and other tokens summing to 0.1 (True), what is τ(*c*)? Answer: Since top tokens are different classes, τ = |p_True - p_False| = |(0.6+0.1) - 0.3| = 0.4.

- **Concept: Natural Language Inference for reasoning consistency**
  - Why needed here: γ(*c*) requires an NLI model to score contradiction between pairs of rationales generated under opposing assumptions. This assumes NLI models can detect logical conflict in short explanations.
  - Quick check question: If NLI scores for a rationale pair are entailment=0.1, contradiction=0.8, neutral=0.1, what is the contradiction probability ϕ contr used, and what is γ(*c*) for that pair? Answer: ϕ contr ≈ 0.8; γ = 1 - average ϕ contr across pairs.

- **Concept: Adaptive retrieval orchestration based on confidence signals**
  - Why needed here: The pipeline's value lies in switching between direct answer, targeted search, reflection-guided retrieval, and deep search. Engineers must implement the routing logic and each strategy's prompts/agents.
  - Quick check question: For a claim with τ=0.8, γ=0.4, which verification strategy should be selected assuming thresholds α=0.6, β=0.6? Answer: High certainty, low consistency → targeted search.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Claim Processor -> PCC Estimator (Verdict Generator, Internal Certainty Calculator, Reasoning Consistency Evaluator) -> Routing Logic -> Verification Strategy (Direct Answer / Targeted Search / Reflection-guided Retrieval / Deep Search Agent) -> Verdict Aggregator. The Deep Search may iterate with Retrieval & Reflection.
- **Critical path:** Input claim → generate initial verdict & rationales → compute τ and γ → route via thresholds → execute selected retrieval/verification strategy → output final verdict. Deep search involves multiple retrieval-reflection cycles.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Threshold sensitivity vs. adaptability:** Fixed α, β simplify implementation but may not work across all datasets; learning thresholds per model/domain adds complexity.
  - **Log-prob accessibility vs. API constraints:** PCC needs token probabilities; proprietary APIs (e.g., OpenAI) support this, but others (e.g., standard Gemini API) require workarounds, limiting portability.
  - **NLI cost vs. consistency fidelity:** Using an external NLI model adds latency and cost; a smaller or quantized NLI model could reduce overhead but may hurt consistency accuracy.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Misrouting due to threshold mismatch:** Many claims fall into "low certainty, high consistency" region, triggering deep search unnecessarily, hurting efficiency.
  - **NLI model bias:** If NLI model struggles with domain-specific jargon (e.g., scientific claims in SciFact), γ(*c*) becomes noisy, leading to poor routing.
  - **Log-prob extraction errors:** Imperfect calculation of τ(*c*) from top-k tokens (e.g., if verdict tokens are not cleanly separable) causes miscalibration.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Calibration sanity check:** Compute ECE for PCC vs. verbal confidence on a held-out set of claims from one dataset (e.g., 100 SciFact claims). Verify PCC ECE is lower.
  2. **Routing correctness validation:** Manually inspect a sample of claims in each quadrant (e.g., 20 per quadrant). Confirm that high certainty/high consistency claims are factually straightforward and high certainty/low consistency claims show overconfident hallucinations.
  3. **Strategy effectiveness ablation:** On a subset (e.g., HoVER), run PCC with only direct answer, only targeted search, only deep search, and full adaptive. Compare macro-F1 to confirm adaptive routing yields gains over any single strategy.

## Limitations
- **Threshold sensitivity:** Fixed α, β thresholds for quadrant routing are not specified and may not generalize across datasets/model families, risking misrouting.
- **NLI model domain bias:** Consistency γ(c) relies on a single off-the-shelf NLI model; its performance on domain-specific rationale pairs is unvalidated and could inject noise.
- **Deep search algorithm opacity:** The referenced iterative agent is not detailed; simplified reimplementations may underperform the paper’s deep search, limiting comparability.

## Confidence
- **PCC signal combination (τ + γ):** Medium. Mechanically sound and supported by section definitions, but dependent on reliable log-prob extraction and NLI fidelity.
- **Adaptive routing efficacy:** Medium-Low. Evidence from tables shows quadrant-specific gains, but thresholds are tuned empirically without disclosed values; generalizability is uncertain.
- **Calibration superiority over verbal confidence:** High. Consistently lower ECE across models/datasets aligns with known verbal confidence miscalibration literature, though still mediated by τ/γ reliability.

## Next Checks
1. **Threshold sensitivity analysis:** Sweep α, β across [0.1, 0.9] on each dataset’s calibration split; identify ranges that maintain quadrant balance and maximize macro-F1. Report variance.
2. **NLI model domain generalization:** Evaluate the NLI model on a held-out set of rationale pairs per dataset (e.g., 50 pairs from SciFact, HoVER); measure contradiction prediction accuracy via human annotation. High error rates indicate γ(c) noise risk.
3. **Deep search contribution isolation:** Ablate the deep search module on claims flagged “low certainty/low consistency”; compare performance against a fallback to “reflection-guided retrieval” only. Quantify F1 drop.
