# LUCID-SAE: Learning Unified Vision-Language Sparse Codes for Interpretable Concept Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07311
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07311
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LUCID learns a unified vision-language sparse autoencoder with a shared dictionary and private modality-specific capacity, aligned via guided optimal transport, achieving cross-modal concept alignment without labels and improving reconstruction fidelity, spatial grounding, and reducing concept clustering degeneracy.

## Executive Summary
LUCID addresses the lack of cross-modal comparability in sparse autoencoders (SAEs), which are typically trained separately for vision and language, producing non-aligned, uninterpretable dictionaries. The core method is a unified vision-language SAE with a shared latent dictionary for image patches and text tokens, augmented with private modality-specific capacity. Shared codes are aligned via an entropy-regularized optimal transport objective with guided cross-modal transport (GCMT), eliminating the need for labeled data. Experiments on COCO and CC3M demonstrate improved reconstruction fidelity (vision self-R² ↑ from 0.405 to 0.461; text self-R² ↑ from 0.574 to 0.668), enhanced cross-modal predictability (text→vision cross-R² ↑ from 0.055 to 0.117), sharper object-level grounding (BBOX point@1 ↑ from 0.473 to 0.600), and reduced concept clustering degeneracy (maxfreq ↓ from 0.381 to 0.074). An automated interpretation pipeline reveals coherent shared features across semantic categories and generalizes spatially to ImageNet.

## Method Summary
LUCID operates on frozen vision-language model (SigLIP ViT-B/16) activations: image patch tokens (196 × 768) and text token embeddings (up to 77 × 768). For each modality, a sparse encoder produces two sets of latent codes: a shared component (size 1536, Top-*k* with *k*<sub>s</sub>=16) and a private component (size 4608, Top-*k* with *k*<sub>p</sub>=32). Decoders are linear: each modality has a shared decoder (reconstructing from shared codes) and a private decoder (reconstructing private details). The loss combines three terms: L<sub>self</sub> (MSE reconstruction of original activations), L<sub>align</sub> (Global Semantic Lock: pooled shared codes from one modality decoded via the other modality’s shared decoder vs. target pooled activations), and L<sub>cross</sub> (cross-modal reconstruction via barycentric mapping using an optimal transport plan Π). Alignment uses entropy-regularized OT (ε=0.1, 10 Sinkhorn iterations) on the cosine cost between shared codes, enhanced by Guided Cross-Modal Transport (GCMT) which incorporates a structural mask and global context reweighting (λ<sub>global</sub>=0.5). Total loss: L = α L<sub>self</sub> + β L<sub>align</sub> + γ L<sub>cross</sub> with α=1.0, β=0.5, γ=0.3. Training uses COCO (118k) for 200 epochs, batch 256, AdamW (lr=3e-4, wd=0.01), cosine LR (24h on 8×RTX8000). Evaluation metrics include self/cross R², object grounding (mass@obj, point@1, IoU@10), degeneracy (maxfreq), and automated neuron interpretation via cross-modal concept clustering.

## Key Results
- **Reconstruction fidelity:** Adding private capacity improves self-R² for vision (0.405 → 0.461) and text (0.574 → 0.668).
- **Cross-modal predictability:** Shared-private decomposition with alignment lifts text→vision cross-R² from 0.055 to 0.117.
- **Grounding and degeneracy:** GCMT significantly boosts spatial grounding (BBOX point@1 from 0.473 (OT only) to 0.600) and reduces concept clustering degeneracy (maxfreq for text-to-image ↓ from 0.381 to 0.074).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A shared-private decomposition improves reconstruction fidelity and cross-modal predictability by shielding the shared space from modality-specific noise.
- **Mechanism:** The shared latent dictionary captures cross-modal semantic concepts, while private dictionaries absorb modality-specific details. This prevents the shared space from learning idiosyncratic factors that cannot be aligned across vision and language, forcing it to specialize in genuinely comparable features.
- **Core assumption:** Factors of variation in vision and language activations can be decomposed into a set of common semantic concepts and separate sets of modality-specific factors.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.1 (motivation); Table 2 (shared-private ratio ablations show gains in both self-R² and shared cross-R²).
- **Break condition:** If private capacity ratio *r* is too low, the shared space remains contaminated, degrading cross-modal alignment as measured by cross-R² and maxfreq.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Guided Cross-Modal Transport (GCMT) sharpens patch-level grounding and reduces concept clustering degeneracy by biasing the optimal transport plan toward semantically plausible correspondences.
- **Mechanism:** GCMT augments the entropy-regularized OT cost matrix with structural (mask) and contextual (global compatibility) guidance. This steers the transport plan away from spurious matches, concentrating alignment on semantically meaningful patch-token pairs.
- **Core assumption:** The raw cosine similarity between shared codes is an insufficient signal for precise grounding; incorporating lightweight priors (e.g., noun-object compatibility, global scene consistency) provides necessary contextual disambiguation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2.2 (GCMT formulation); Table 3 (BBOX point@1 progressive improvement); Table 4 (maxfreq reduction).
- **Break condition:** If guidance signals (masks, context vectors) are miscalibrated (e.g., overly restrictive masks), transport may become biased toward incorrect regions, degrading grounding and degeneracy metrics.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Automated neuron interpretation via cross-modal concept clustering scales to large dictionaries without manual labeling, revealing coherent semantic specializations aligned with spatial grounding.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline associates each shared neuron with text concepts via spatial activation matches in the shared code space. These text concepts are then clustered by semantic similarity (cosine) to identify the neuron's dominant theme, leveraging cross-modal alignment as a weak supervision signal.
- **Core assumption:** Concepts that consistently co-activate with a neuron across images and text form a semantically coherent cluster, and the cluster's largest group represents the neuron's primary function.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.5 (interpretation pipeline); Table 1 & Figure 4 (example neurons with high cluster purity); Figure 5 (generalization to ImageNet).
- **Break condition:** If shared codes fail to align (high maxfreq, low cross-R²), concept-neuron associations become noisy, leading to incoherent clusters or low purity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**  
  *Why needed:* SAEs provide the core architecture for learning overcomplete, sparse dictionaries that disentangle polysemantic activations into interpretable features. LUCID builds on SAEs but extends them to multimodal alignment.  
  *Quick check:* What is the purpose of applying a Top-*k* sparsity constraint on the latent codes, and how does an overcomplete dictionary aid interpretability?
- **Optimal Transport (OT)**  
  *Why needed:* OT defines a differentiable, many-to-many alignment between variable-length patch and token sequences. It is the mathematical engine for the cross-modal consistency signal without labeled correspondences.  
  *Quick check:* Why is entropy regularization (ε>0) used in the OT formulation, and what would happen without it?
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Activations**  
  *Why needed:* LUCID operates on frozen VLM (SigLIP) patch and token embeddings. Understanding the modality-specific distributions (e.g., patch granularity vs. token semantics) is key to diagnosing reconstruction and alignment challenges.  
  *Quick check:* Why might text→vision cross-reconstruction be inherently harder than vision→text, based on the information density of the modalities?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
1. **Input:** Image patches and text tokens from a frozen VLM (SigLIP ViT-B/16).  
2. **Separate Encoders:** Two sparse encoders (Top-*k*) producing shared (size K<sub>s</sub>=16) and private (K<sub>p</sub>=32) codes per token/patch.  
3. **Shared Decoder Pair:** Linear decoders W<sub>s</sub><sup>img</sup> and W<sub>s</sub><sup>txt</sup> reconstruct from shared codes only.  
4. **Private Decoders:** W<sub>p</sub><sup>img</sup> and W<sub>p</sub><sup>txt</sup> reconstruct modality-specific details.  
5. **GCMT Module:** Computes guided transport plan Π using cost matrix from shared-code similarities, masks, and global context reweighting.  
6. **Cross-Reconstruction:** Barycentric mapping using Π to exchange shared codes between modalities.  
7. **Loss Aggregator:** Combines L<sub>self</sub> (reconstruction), L<sub>align</sub> (OT-based semantic lock), and L<sub>cross</sub> (barycentric consistency) with weights α, β, γ.

**Critical path:** Self-reconstruction first establishes basic fidelity in each modality. Alignment and cross-reconstruction losses then shape the shared space for cross-modal correspondence. Private codes naturally evolve to absorb residual modality-specific variance.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Shared vs. Private Capacity Ratio (*r*):** Higher *r* (more shared) risks shared-space contamination and worse cross-predictability; lower *r* preserves fidelity but reduces the shared dictionary's expressive power for common semantics.  
- **Sparsity Levels (*k*<sub>s</sub>, *k*<sub>p</sub>):** Higher *k* increases reconstruction but may reduce monosemanticity and increase clustering degeneracy (maxfreq).  
- **GCMT Guidance Strength (β, λ<sub>global</sub>):** Too weak → OT remains diffuse; too strong → transport becomes brittle and loses valid matches.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High maxfreq indicates shared dimensions are overused, suggesting failed alignment.  
- Low cross-R² (especially T→V) indicates shared codes do not carry transmissible semantics.  
- BBOX point@1 ≈ random (<<0.5) indicates failure at local grounding; transport plan is not focused on object regions.  
- Neuron clusters with purity <0.55 in the interpretation pipeline indicate incoherent features.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablate Shared-Private Decomposition:** Train LUCID with *r*=1.0 (fully shared) vs. *r*=0.25. Verify that adding private capacity increases both self-R² and shared cross-R² (Table 2). Failure mode: if cross-R² does not improve, private capacity may be insufficient or improperly regularized.  
2. **Evaluate Grounding with BBOX Metrics:** Compute heatmaps from OT transport mass and measure point@1 against COCO GT boxes (Table 3). Failure mode: if point@1 does not improve with GCMT, the guidance signals (mask, context) are likely ineffective.  
3. **Measure Clustering Degeneracy:** Compute maxfreq for text→image and image→text concept retrieval (Table 4). Failure mode: if maxfreq remains high (>0.3) despite GCMT, the shared codes are not becoming selective; check sparsity *k*<sub>s</sub> and alignment loss weight β.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Optimal shared-private capacity ratio**  
   *Basis:* The paper uses a fixed shared ratio of 0.25 without analyzing sensitivity.  
   *Why unresolved:* The trade-off between cross-modal alignment and reconstruction fidelity likely depends on model scale, data distribution, and task specifics; a single ratio may not generalize.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic evaluation across multiple VLMs (e.g., ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) and datasets (COCO, CC3M, domain-specific) with varied shared ratios, measuring self/cross reconstruction, grounding metrics, and computational cost.

2. **Automating GCMT structural guidance**  
   *Basis:* GCMT relies on predefined compatibility masks and global context reweighting.  
   *Why unresolved:* Manual heuristics may not generalize across domains or modalities; automating guidance could improve adaptability and reduce domain expertise requirements.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Replace handcrafted masks with a trainable guidance module (e.g., a small neural network) and compare alignment quality, grounding accuracy, and robustness on diverse datasets.

3. **Scaling to larger VLMs and additional modalities**  
   *Basis:* Evaluation is limited to SigLIP ViT-B/16 on image-text pairs only.  
   *Why unresolved:* SAE dynamics may change with model scale; additional modalities introduce new alignment challenges not addressed by the current shared-private decomposition.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Train LUCID on larger VLMs (e.g., CLIP-ViT-L/14) and multimodal datasets (e.g., video-text), analyzing reconstruction, alignment metrics, and computational efficiency.

4. **Downstream task performance**  
   *Basis:* The paper focuses on intrinsic metrics (reconstruction, grounding) without evaluating practical applications.  
   *Why unresolved:* While LUCID shows favorable interpretability, its utility for task performance, robustness, or explainability in real-world scenarios remains unproven.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Integrate LUCID’s shared codes into VQA or cross-modal retrieval models, comparing accuracy, failure modes, and human-evaluated explanations against baselines (e.g., separate SAEs, end-to-end VLMs).

## Limitations
- **Ambiguous SigLIP extraction protocol:** Unclear whether CLS token is included and exact patch token count, which may alter feature dimensionality and network inputs.
- **GCMT structural mask source unspecified:** Likely requires an external grounding model, raising reproducibility concerns.
- **Bounding-box evaluation metrics lack precise definition:** e.g., IoU@10 calculation not fully explicit, hindering direct replication.

## Confidence
- **Shared-private decomposition improves fidelity & cross-predictability:** High
- **GCMT sharpens grounding and reduces degeneracy:** High
- **Automated interpretation reveals coherent shared concepts:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Shared-private ratio sweep:** Train LUCID with private ratios *r* ∈ {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} over 5 random seeds each. Report self-R² and cross-R² distributions to confirm the optimum and assess stability.
2. **GCMT component ablation:** Remove mask (set to all-ones) and disable global reweighting (λ<sub>global</sub>=0) separately. Evaluate BBOX point@1 and maxfreq to quantify each guidance signal’s unique contribution.
