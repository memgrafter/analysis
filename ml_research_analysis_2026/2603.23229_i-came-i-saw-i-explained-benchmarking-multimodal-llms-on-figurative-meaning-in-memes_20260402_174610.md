# I Came, I Saw, I Explained: Benchmarking Multimodal LLMs on Figurative Meaning in Memes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23229
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23229
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** All tested MLLMs exhibit a strong bias to predict figurative meaning even in literal memes; larger models achieve higher automatic F1 scores but generate explanations with a faithfulness gap, primarily due to failures in visual information extraction.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the ability of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to detect and explain figurative meaning in memes. Using three datasets covering six figurative types, eight MLLMs were tested under zero-shot conditions with full-meme, text-only, and image-only inputs. Results show a universal bias toward figurative predictions, with modality reliance varying by type (e.g., irony/sarcasm depend heavily on images, while metaphor requires both). Although larger models attain higher label accuracy, human evaluation reveals that correct predictions often lack faithful explanations, particularly in visual completeness and correctness, highlighting deficiencies in visual grounding.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted zero-shot evaluations of eight instructed-tuned MLLMs (Aya-Vision, Gemma 3, Qwen2.5-VL) on three meme datasets: FigMemes (multi-label, 6 types), Memotion2 (binary sarcasm), and MET-Meme (binary metaphor). Inputs were processed in three modalities: original meme, text-only (OCR-extracted), and image-only (text inpainted via LaMa). Models generated labels and explanations using two prompt templates. Automatic metrics (F1/micro-F1) measured label accuracy, while human annotators rated 90 sampled explanations on seven criteria (relevance, consistency, correctness, length appropriateness, visual/textual completeness, faithfulness) using a 5-point scale.

## Key Results
- All models showed a strong bias to predict figurative meaning, even for memes with no figurative content (literal gold labels), leading to high recall but low precision.
- Modality ablation revealed type-specific reliance: irony and sarcasm performance dropped sharply without the image, while metaphor required both text and image.
- Larger models (e.g., Qwen-72B) achieved higher automatic F1 (avg. 64.78% on FigMemes), but human evaluation uncovered a faithfulness gap; explanations scored lowest on visual completeness and correctness due to poor visual extraction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Modality reliance varies by figurative type
- **Claim:** Different figurative types distribute meaning across modalities; irony/sarcasm rely heavily on visual incongruity, while metaphor requires integrated textual-visual mapping.
- **Mechanism:** Models process multimodal inputs but prioritize cues from the dominant modality for each type. Removing the primary modality causes steep performance declines.
- **Core assumption:** Figurative types have distinct primary carriers of meaning, and model integration can be lopsided.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “Modality reliance varies by type: irony/sarcasm rely heavily on images, while metaphor requires both text and image.”; [section] Table 3: Qwen‑72B irony F1 drops from 65.35% (full) to 22.95% (‑Image), while metaphor drops substantially for both ‑Image and ‑Text ablations.
- **Break condition:** If a type’s definition makes visual cues non-essential (e.g., purely textual irony), the pattern would not hold.

### Mechanism 2: Bias toward figurativity from training data spurious correlation
- **Claim:** MLLMs overgeneralize a correlation between meme format (image+embedded text) and figurative interpretation, leading to a prior favoring figurative labels.
- **Mechanism:** Training data likely contains many figurative memes, causing the model to associate the multimodal structure itself with non-literal meaning.
- **Core assumption:** Prevalence of figurative memes in training creates a spurious signal that the model exploits.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “All models exhibit a strong bias to associate a meme with figurative meaning, even when no such meaning is present.”; [section] Figure 2: on 495 literal memes, all models assign mostly 1–3 figurative labels; multi-label recall high but precision low (Table 4).
- **Break condition:** Balanced training data between literal and figurative memes would reduce the bias.

### Mechanism 3: Unfaithful explanations stem from visual extraction failures
- **Claim:** Explanation unfaithfulness arises primarily from the model’s inability to reliably extract and ground visual information, not from textual reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** The visual encoder provides a noisy or incomplete internal representation; thus explanations may hallucinate, omit, or misinterpret key image elements, creating a gap between label and explanation.
- **Core assumption:** Explanations are conditioned on flawed visual grounding.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “Human evaluation reveals a faithfulness gap… with models struggling particularly with visual completeness and correctness due to failures in visual information extraction.”; [section] Figure 3: lowest scores on visual completeness/correctness; Figure 4 shows missed gestures or objects.
- **Break condition:** A perfect visual encoder or a task requiring only textual reasoning would eliminate this failure mode.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Module (loads datasets, OCR, inpainting) -> Prompt Module (JSON instructions) -> Inference Engine (vLLM, 8 MLLMs) -> Evaluation Module (automatic metrics + human eval UI) -> Analysis Module (aggregation, figures, error analysis)

Critical path to reproduce: 1) Prepare datasets and splits; 2) Preprocess images: PaddleOCR + LaMa for text masking, manually fix low-res cases; 3) Run inference for each model, modality, and prompt template (temp=0.7, top-p=0.1, etc.); 4) Compute label accuracy (F1/micro-F1); 5) Sample 90 memes for human evaluation of explanations; 6) Analyze modality impact, bias, and faithfulness.

Design tradeoffs: Zero-shot evaluation chosen to gauge inherent ability (vs. fine-tuning), limiting peak performance. Human evaluation limited to 90 memes (depth over breadth). Two prompt templates used; more variations unexplored. Manual fixes for image-only ablations ensured data integrity but reduced scalability.

Failure signatures: - Sharp F1 drop for irony/sarcasm without image indicates visual incongruity reliance. - Systematic over-prediction on literal memes reveals format-based bias. - Low human scores on visual completeness/correctness point to flawed visual grounding. - Incoherent label-explanation pairs (e.g., label “sarcastic” but explanation denies sarcasm) show reasoning breakdowns.

First 3 experiments: 1) Binary/multi-label classification with original meme inputs (Section 4.1, Table 3). 2) Modality ablation comparing full, text-only, image-only (Section 4.1, analysis of Table 3/Figure 2). 3) Human evaluation of explanation quality (Section 4.2, Figure 3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

   Basis: Explicitly mentioned in text.

   Evidence needed: Systematic ablation across formats/wordings, measuring stability of detection and explanation.

2. **How can multimodal architectures ensure reasoning faithfulness?**  
   Basis: Explicit call for improved architectures.  
   Why unresolved: Large faithfulness gap found; visual grounding errors predominant.  
   Evidence needed: New models/objectives optimized for faithfulness, validated by human eval.

3. **How can MLLMs gain sensitivity to socio-psychological contexts in memes?**  
   Basis: Explicit call for deeper context sensitivity.  
   Why unresolved: Models fail on social/norm-based reasoning.  
   Evidence needed: Incorporate external knowledge or social data, evaluate on context-rich benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Exact definitions for the six figurative types (from Liu et al. 2022a) are not provided; must be sourced externally.
- Human evaluation annotation guidelines (rubrics, anchor examples) are insufficiently detailed.
- Human evaluation sample (90 memes) is small and may not generalize.
- Zero-shot evaluation may not reflect fine-tuned model performance.

## Confidence
- **Bias toward figurativity:** High — consistent over-prediction on literal memes across all models (Figure 2).
- **Modality reliance differences:** Medium — ablation results (Table 3) show clear patterns, but dataset-specific cues could contribute.
- **Visual grounding as primary failure:** Medium — low visual completeness scores (Figure 3) indicate this, though root cause not isolated.

## Next Checks
1. Fine-tune the same MLLMs on the training splits and re-evaluate to test whether bias, modality reliance, and explanation faithfulness patterns hold.
2. Expand human evaluation to ≥500 memes, balanced across types and modalities, for robust explanation quality estimates.
3. Correlate explanation failures with quantitative visual extraction metrics (e.g., OCR accuracy, object detection recall) to directly link grounding errors to faithfulness gaps.
