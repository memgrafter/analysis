# Learning Dynamic Belief Graphs for Theory-of-mind Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20170  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20170  
- **Reference Count**: 40  
- **Primary Result**: A dynamic belief graph model with LLM-derived potentials and amortized inference significantly improves action prediction and recovers interpretable belief trajectories in wildfire evacuation datasets, outperforming LLM-based baselines.

## Executive Summary
Theory-of-Mind (ToM) reasoning in high-stakes settings requires modeling latent, interdependent beliefs that evolve over time and drive sparse, delayed actions. Existing LLM-based approaches treat beliefs as static and independent, leading to incoherent mental models and poor predictive performance. This paper introduces a structured cognitive trajectory model that represents mental states as a dynamic belief graph. LLM-derived semantic evidence is projected into unary and pairwise potentials of an energy-based factor graph, with temporal transitions conditioned on past beliefs and observations. Actions are predicted via attention over belief-conditioned tokens. The model is trained end-to-end using an ELBO objective with an amortized inference network, requiring no belief-level supervision. Evaluated on real-world wildfire evacuation datasets, the approach improves action prediction accuracy and recovers interpretable belief trajectories consistent with human reasoning, demonstrating the value of structured belief dynamics.

## Method Summary
The model frames ToM reasoning as a latent dynamical system. A belief vector at each timestep is defined by an energy-based factor graph with unary and pairwise potentials. These potentials are computed by projecting frozen LLM embeddings of observations and belief relationships into a consistent probabilistic parameterization via a contrastive anchoring scheme. The belief transition prior models `p(bt | bt-1, ot)` using these potentials, with exact marginal computation feasible for small belief sets (K=6). An amortized inference network `q(bt | ot, at)` generates belief posteriors conditioned on the observed action. The action model maps belief marginals to action likelihoods using a two-stage process: mixing LLM-derived per-belief embeddings and applying action-specific self-attention to capture nonlinear belief interactions. The whole system is trained by maximizing an ELBO combining action log-likelihood and a KL divergence between the posterior and prior, using only action labels.

## Key Results
- The model significantly improves action prediction accuracy over multiple LLM-based baselines on two wildfire evacuation datasets.
- It achieves higher Spearman correlation for recovering individual belief trajectories and pairwise belief structure compared to prior work.
- Ablation studies confirm that both pairwise belief interactions and temporal conditioning are necessary for capturing belief dependencies and dynamics, with their removal causing substantial performance drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Semantic-to-Potential Projection
- **Claim**: The projection translates LLM-derived textual evidence into structured unary and pairwise potentials, grounding language semantics in a consistent factor graph while preventing sign ambiguity.
- **Mechanism**: For each belief *i*, the model queries a frozen LLM to produce two embeddings: `hYes` (belief present) and `hNo` (absent). These are mixed proportionally to the marginal probability of the previous belief to obtain a history-aware embedding `ht,i`. A base unary score `ϕbase` is formed by contrasting `ht,i` with `hYes` and `hNo`. A learned residual `wu·ReLU(ht,i)+bu` is added. Pairwise potentials use `hij = LLM(bi,bj)` followed by `ϕij = wp·ReLU(hij)+bp`. These potentials define an energy over the binary belief vector.
- **Core assumption**: The frozen LLM accurately encodes semantic relationships between observations, beliefs, and belief pairs; the contrastive anchoring resolves energy sign ambiguity.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract mentions "a novel projection from textualized probabilistic statements to consistent probabilistic graphical model updates." Section 3.2 details the projection (Eq. 4-8). Among related works, AutoToM and FLARE use LLMs to directly infer beliefs or actions without such a factor graph parameterization; no neighbor explicitly implements a contrastive anchoring scheme.
- **Break condition**: If the LLM fails to produce discriminative embeddings (e.g., due to ambiguous prompts or domain shift), the contrastive score collapses and potentials become uninformative, leading to erratic belief updates and poor action prediction.

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Belief Transition Prior
- **Claim**: The prior models beliefs as a dynamic factor graph, enabling evidence accumulation and belief interactions across time steps for coherent trajectories.
- **Mechanism**: At each time *t*, belief vector `bt` is a Markov Random Field with energy `Eθ(bt)= Σi logϕi(bt,i) + Σi<j logϕij(bt,i,bt,j)`. Potentials `ϕi, ϕij` depend on `(bt-1, ot)` via the semantic projection. The prior `pθ(bt | bt-1, ot) = exp(Eθ(bt))/Z` conditions the new belief configuration on past state and current observation. Marginals are computed by enumerating all `2^K` joint assignments (feasible for K=6).
- **Core assumption**: Belief dynamics follow a first-order Markov process; `bt-1` contains all relevant history.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract states "dynamic belief graph" and "temporal transitions conditioned on past beliefs and observations." Section 3.1 defines the generative factorization; Section 3.2 presents the belief transition prior (Eq. 3). Corpus includes temporal ToM works like TimeToM that address time but do not combine it with a factor graph of belief interdependencies.
- **Break condition**: If the Markov assumption is violated (e.g., long-range dependencies) or observations are sparse, the limited context can cause error accumulation. The ELBO’s KL term may mitigate drift but cannot fully recover lost information.

### Mechanism 3: Action Model with Belief-Conditioned Tokens and Attention
- **Claim**: The action model with belief-conditioned tokens and action-specific self-attention captures the nonlinear, context-dependent mapping from belief combinations to actions, improving prediction in high-stakes settings.
- **Mechanism**: For each action *j*, two LLM-derived embeddings per belief are precomputed: `e(1)_{j,i}` (belief *i* present) and `e(0)_{j,i}` (absent). These are mixed using marginal `pθ(bt,i=1)` to form token matrix `Xt,j`. A separate self-attention module operates on `Xt,j` to compute weights `At,j` that model how beliefs modulate each other for action *j*. The aggregated representation yields action likelihood `pθ(at|bt)`.
- **Core assumption**: Beliefs influence actions in a highly nonlinear, combinatorial fashion; attention can learn these interactions from data.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract says "Actions are predicted via attention over belief-conditioned tokens." Section 3.2 "Action Generation" (Eq. 10-12) describes the construction and attention. Baselines such as AutoToM rely on hypothesis sampling and averaging; none incorporate a learned attention layer that dynamically weights belief interactions per action.
- **Break condition**: If belief marginals are misestimated (e.g., due to inference errors), mixed tokens misrepresent the true state, causing attention to produce spurious patterns and degrading action prediction. The model may also overfit if attention capacity is too high relative to data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Variational Inference & ELBO  
  - **Why needed here**: Beliefs are latent and unlabeled; the model maximizes an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) to jointly learn the generative prior and an amortized inference network using only action supervision. This forces beliefs to be predictive of actions while remaining consistent with past beliefs and observations.  
  - **Quick check question**: "What are the two terms in the ELBO and how do they enforce different aspects of the model?" (Answer: `Lact` encourages beliefs that explain actions; `LKL` enforces agreement between inference posterior and temporal prior.)
- **Concept**: Energy-Based Models & Factor Graphs  
  - **Why needed here**: Structured dependencies among beliefs (reinforcement, suppression) are captured by unary and pairwise potentials in an energy-based factor graph, ensuring coherent joint belief configurations.  
  - **Quick check question**: "According to the ablation study, what is the effect of removing pairwise terms on pairwise belief structure recovery?" (Answer: Spearman correlation drops significantly, showing the necessity of explicit belief interactions.)
- **Concept**: Self-Attention for Belief-Action Mapping  
  - **Why needed here**: Beliefs interact in complex, nonlinear ways when determining actions; attention allows the model to flexibly weight and combine belief dimensions per action, rather than using a fixed scoring function.  
  - **Quick check question**: "Why apply attention separately for each action rather than a single attention over all actions?" (Answer: Different actions may rely on different belief interactions; separate attention heads allow action-specific weighting patterns.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Input observations `ot` and previous belief `bt-1` -> Frozen LLM (Qwen-8B) -> Semantic embeddings -> Trainable projections (unary/pairwise potentials, inference logits, action embeddings) -> Belief transition prior (exact marginals) & Inference network `q_ϕ` -> Sampled `bt` -> Action model (token mixing + action-specific self-attention) -> Action logits. ELBO trainer sums `Lact` and `LKL` across timesteps; test-time generator discards `q_ϕ` and iterates with prior.
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Initialise `b0` (e.g., all zeros).  
  2. For each timestep *t*:  
     - Compute prior `pθ(bt | bt-1, ot)` using LLM projections and exact enumeration.  
     - Compute posterior `qϕ(bt | ot, at)` via LLM inference embeddings and trained logits.  
     - Sample `bt` from `qϕ`.  
     - Evaluate `Lact_t` by feeding `bt` into action model to get log `pθ(at|bt)`.  
     - Compute `LKL_t = KL(qϕ(bt|ot,at) ∥ pθ(bt|bt-1,ot))`.  
  3. Sum losses across *t*, backpropagate.  
  4. At test time, run forward only with prior and action model.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Exact enumeration*: Guarantees exact marginals for small *K* (6) but does not scale; acceptable for limited belief sets.  
  - *Amortized inference conditioned on action*: Informative posteriors speed training but introduce asymmetry; may limit generalisation if test actions differ.  
  - *Frozen LLM*: Leverages high-quality semantics without pretraining cost; cannot adapt to domain-specific nuance and adds latency.  
  - *Action-specific attention*: Captures nuanced interactions but increases parameters; may need regularisation with limited data.  
  - *Unweighted KL*: No β parameter; risk of posterior collapse if prior is too strong, though experimental stability suggests anchoring mitigates this.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - KL divergence high or rising → posterior not matching prior; check LLM embedding quality, projection init.  
  - Action log-likelihood plateaus early → belief marginals not predictive; inspect belief–action correlation.  
  - Per-belief Spearman low → semantic projection misaligned; review prompts and contrastive anchoring.  
  - Pairwise ablation impact minimal → pairwise weights near zero; examine `wp` norms.  
  - Temporal metrics (Cohen’s *d*, DTW) degrade → transitions not learning meaningful updates; observation encoding may be insufficient.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Reproduce main results**: Train full model on Kincade and Marshall fire datasets (100 epochs, Adam, lr = 1e‑3). Report action accuracy and belief Spearman; compare against AutoToM, FLARE, and LLM‑prior baseline. Expect numbers similar to paper (e.g., Spearman ≈ 0.5–0.6).  
  2. **Ablation validation**: Train three variants: (a) remove pairwise potentials (NO‑PAIRWISE), (b) remove temporal conditioning (NO‑TEMPORAL), (c) full model. Evaluate pairwise belief Spearman (Fig.6b) and temporal metrics (Cohen’s *d*, DTW). Confirm both components are crucial.  
  3. **Training dynamics monitoring**: Plot per‑epoch action log‑likelihood and KL for train/validation sets. Verify action likelihood increases while KL stabilises near zero, indicating stable generative learning without posterior collapse. Also track belief recovery over epochs.

## Limitations
- Dependence on a frozen LLM for semantic anchoring may degrade performance under domain shift or ambiguous prompts.
- First-order Markov assumption for belief transitions may fail with long-range dependencies, risking error accumulation.
- Exact marginal enumeration limits scalability beyond ~6 beliefs.
- Amortized inference conditioned on observed actions may limit generalization to unseen action distributions.
- No direct belief-level ground truth; recovery metrics rely on indirect proxy evaluations.

## Confidence
- **Action prediction improvement**: High. Supported by significant gains on two real-world datasets vs. multiple baselines.
- **Belief trajectory interpretability**: Medium. Qualitative consistency claimed but without gold-standard belief labels; recovery metrics are correlational.
- **Necessity of pairwise/temporal components**: High. Ablations show substantial drops in pairwise and temporal metrics when removed.
- **Generalizability beyond wildfires**: Low. Evaluation restricted to evacuation contexts; unknown performance in other ToM domains.

## Next Checks
1. **Multi-domain evaluation**: Test on at least two additional ToM datasets (e.g., negotiation or collaborative tasks) to probe domain robustness.
2. **Scalability stress test**: Increase belief set size (K > 10) and measure degradation in action accuracy vs. approximate inference alternatives (e.g., sampling).
3. **Isolated factor-graph ablation**: Compare against a temporal model *without* pairwise potentials but with identical LLM+attention pipeline to decouple the contribution of the dynamic graph structure from other components.
