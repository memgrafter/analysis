# Sampling-Free Privacy Accounting for Matrix Mechanisms under Random Allocation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.21636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21636
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Deterministic privacy accounting for matrix mechanisms under random allocation via two sampling-free methods, overcoming Monte Carlo limitations and achieving polynomial complexity.

## Executive Summary
Traditional privacy accounting for matrix mechanisms with subsampling relies on Monte Carlo simulation, yielding only probabilistic guarantees or requiring computationally expensive random abstention. This paper introduces two sampling-free accounting techniques: a Rényi divergence-based method using dynamic programming for *p*-banded matrices, reducing complexity from exponential to polynomial; and a conditional composition accountant that delivers tighter bounds for small privacy parameters by decomposing loss via per-step dominating pairs and high‑probability reverse hazard bounds. The framework supports both banded and non‑banded matrices, enabling more efficient and reliable privacy budgeting in differentially private machine learning.

## Method Summary
The core contribution consists of two complementary accountants. The Rényi DP accountant targets *p*-banded matrix mechanisms and computes exact privacy bounds through a dynamic programming recurrence that efficiently aggregates divergence contributions across composition steps. Its complexity scales as *O*(*bα²p*), where *b* is the number of bins, *α* the Rényi order, and *p* the bandwidth. For general (non‑banded) matrices, the conditional composition accountant constructs per‑step dominating pairs of privacy loss distributions, then applies reverse hazard bounds to condition on rare events, yielding tighter guarantees especially when ε is small. Both methods avoid sampling and provide deterministic guarantees.

## Key Results
- The Rényi accountant matches Monte Carlo accuracy in low‑privacy regimes (ε≥2‑4) while being orders of magnitude faster.
- The conditional composition accountant achieves significantly lower calibrated noise multipliers in high‑privacy regimes (ε≤1), with noticeably tighter bounds under strong subsampling and multi‑epoch training.
- The framework extends to both banded and non‑banded matrix mechanisms, offering a unified privacy accounting approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Dynamic programming exploits the limited interaction (bandwidth *p*) between rows of the privacy loss matrix, enabling state compression and avoiding the exponential blow‑up of naïve composition. The DP recurrence aggregates Rényi divergences stepwise, preserving exactness. For the conditional method, per‑step dominating pairs capture worst‑case privacy loss; by conditioning on a rare but high‑impact event and using reverse hazard bounds, the accountant decouples composition and avoids overly pessimistic union bounds. Both approaches replace stochastic estimation with deterministic, analytically tractable computations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rényi Differential Privacy**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a flexible privacy measure that trades off utility and composition; foundational for the dynamic programming accountant.  
  *Quick check*: Confirm that Rényi DP with α→∞ reduces to standard (ε,δ)-DP.  

- **Matrix Mechanisms**  
  *Why needed*: Encodes correlated noise addition for multiple queries, enabling optimal utility‑privacy trade‑offs.  
  *Quick check*: Show that adding Gaussian noise with covariance Σ yields a matrix mechanism with that Σ.  

- **Balls‑in‑Bins Sampling**  
  *Why needed*: Models without‑replacement subsampling in private algorithms, central to the paper’s setting.  
  *Quick check*: Verify that placing *n* balls into *b* bins uniformly yields expected bin count *n/b*.  

- **Dynamic Programming for Privacy Accounting**  
  *Why needed*: Efficiently computes privacy bounds by reusing intermediate results, turning exponential state space into polynomial.  
  *Quick check*: Implement the DP recurrence for a 2‑step, 2‑banded matrix and compare with brute‑force composition.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Input (matrix spec, subsampling rate *b*, Rényi order α, bandwidth *p*, ε, δ) → Accountant Selector → {Rényi DP Engine (for *p*-banded) | Conditional Composition Engine (general)} → Privacy Bound (ε, δ)  

**Critical path**  
- For Rényi DP: DP table update across steps and divergence values; cost O(bα²p).  
- For Conditional Composition: generation of dominating pairs per step and evaluation of reverse hazard bounds; cost depends on number of steps and tail probability choices.  

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Rényi DP: exactness and speed for *p*-banded matrices but not applicable to arbitrary matrices.  
- Conditional Composition: general but may be looser for extremely small ε; parameterization (tail probability) trades off tightness vs. computational overhead.  
- Both avoid sampling, eliminating Monte Carlo variance at the cost of potentially higher polynomial complexity for large *b* or *p*.  

**Failure signatures**  
- Rényi DP: immediate failure if the input matrix is not *p*-banded (structure mismatch).  
- Conditional Composition: bounds become vacuous (ε grows large) if reverse hazard parameters are too conservative or if dominating pairs are too loose.  
- Scalability breakdown when *b* or number of epochs is very large due to polynomial scaling.  

**First experiments**  
1. Verify Rényi DP accountant by comparing its output with brute‑force composition on a tiny (*b*≤3, *p*≤2) instance.  
2. Benchmark both accountants against Monte Carlo estimates for a medium‑sized *p*-banded matrix across varying ε and δ.  
3. Profile runtime as a function of *b*, *α*, and *p* to empirically confirm the O(bα²p) claim and identify practical limits.

## Limitations
- The Rényi accountant is limited to *p*-banded matrices; non‑banded cases rely on the conditional method which may be looser.  
- The conditional composition accountant’s tightness for very small ε (<0.1) or extreme subsampling rates remains unexplored.  
- Empirical evaluation omits recent analytical accountants (e.g., Fourier, GDP), leaving the claim of state‑of‑the‑art utility only partially supported.

## Confidence
- Rényi DP accountant correctness & polynomial complexity for *p*-banded matrices: **High**  
- Conditional composition accountant tighter bounds for small ε: **Medium**  
- Framework applicability to banded and non‑banded matrices: **Medium**  
- Empirical results matching Monte Carlo and improved noise multipliers: **Medium‑High**

## Next Checks

2. Benchmark against a broader suite of privacy accountants (Fourier, GDP, RDP‑subsampling) across a wide grid of ε (including ε<0.1), δ, subsampling rates, and multi‑epoch settings to validate claimed utility improvements.
