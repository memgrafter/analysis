# Compliance-Aware Predictive Process Monitoring: A Neuro-Symbolic Approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26948
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26948
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** A neuro-symbolic pipeline using Logic Tensor Networks (LTNs) to inject domain knowledge into neural classifiers improves both predictive accuracy (F1-score up to +18.4%) and compliance with logical process constraints, especially on smaller datasets.

## Executive Summary
Predictive process monitoring traditionally relies on sub-symbolic neural models that ignore explicit domain constraints, leading to predictions that may violate fundamental business rules. This paper introduces a neuro-symbolic approach that integrates process knowledge—mined from event logs as logical rules—into a neural classifier via Logic Tensor Networks (LTNs). The method employs three knowledge injection strategies: Feature Expansion (adding derived inputs), Output Refinement (shaping the decision boundary), and Parallel Constraints (regularizing the shared representation). Evaluated on four real-world event logs, the LTN-based models consistently outperform LSTM and Transformer baselines in accuracy and F1-score while achieving significantly higher compliance with logical constraints, even when compliant training examples are scarce.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by extracting control-flow, temporal, and payload features from event log prefixes. Declarative mining (Declare), SLA analysis, and correlation techniques then automatically generate a set of first-order logic rules, which are categorized by dependence on the outcome class. These rules form a knowledge base. An LTN framework grounds the rules using a neural backbone (LSTM or Transformer). Three injection configurations wire the knowledge to the model: (A) Feature expansion concatenates deterministic features derived from rule antecedents to the input; (B) Output refinement adds outcome-oriented rule terms directly to the loss affecting the final classification layer; (C) Parallel constraints introduce auxiliary predicates evaluated in parallel, contributing jointly to the satisfaction aggregation (SatAgg). Training minimizes `1 – SatAgg` using Adam, balancing classification loss with logical constraint satisfaction.

## Key Results
- Neuro-symbolic LTN models achieve higher accuracy and F1-scores than pure LSTM/Transformer baselines and semantic loss variants across four datasets.
- Output Refinement (B) is the most effective standalone strategy for outcome prediction.
- Compliance with logical constraints is significantly higher for LTN models, maintaining strong satisfaction even with few compliant training examples.
- Largest F1 gains observed on the smallest dataset (Sepsis: +18.4% vs. Transformer).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Output Refinement as Direct Decision-Shaping**
Claim: Injecting class-dependent, outcome-oriented rules directly into the classifier’s output layer is the most effective strategy. Mechanism: Rules referencing the outcome predicate (e.g., `WaitTime(l,Surg,ATB)≤2→¬P(l)`) are added to the LTN knowledge base. During training, `SatAgg` aggregates their truth values along with the classification loss. Gradients from constraint satisfaction terms directly modify the final layers, imposing a soft regularizer that penalizes outcome predictions violating domain knowledge. Evidence: Section 4.4 describes Output Refinement’s direct impact; Section 5.3 ablation shows Configuration B delivering strongest improvements. Break condition: Noisy or contradictory outcome rules could force the model into a suboptimal parameter region.

**Mechanism 2: Parallel Constraints as Representation Regularization**
Claim: Encoding class-independent structural rules as parallel constraints regularizes the shared representation, improving generalization and compliance. Mechanism: Control-flow rules (e.g., `□(Rev⇒◊Exam)`) are formalized in FOL with auxiliary predicates (`HasAct`, `Next`). These predicates are often deterministic functions evaluated on all prefixes. Their satisfaction contributes to `SatAgg`, creating gradient pressure on the backbone’s layers to produce representations compatible with valid process structures, preventing overfitting to anomalous patterns. Evidence: Section 4.4 explains parallel constraints prevent overfitting; Section 5.3 notes they slightly improve performance when combined with Output Refinement; Figure 5 shows high compliance with Configuration ABC. Break condition: Irrelevant or erroneous structural rules could harm the predictor by suppressing useful signals.

**Mechanism 3: Feature Expansion as Limited Complementary Boost**
Claim: Expanding inputs with class-independent, non-outcome-oriented knowledge provides minor benefit only when combined with other strategies. Mechanism: Knowledge like “elderly diabetic patients require monitoring” generates deterministic features (e.g., `Age>60 ∧ HasCond(Diabetes)`). These are concatenated to the input vector before the classification predicate. The antecedent rule itself is also added to the KB. Evidence: Section 4.4 defines Feature Expansion as preprocessing; Section 5.3 ablation shows Configuration A alone is less effective than Output Refinement. Break condition: If generated features are uninformative for the outcome or cause feature distribution shift, the classifier may fail to incorporate them effectively.

## Foundational Learning
- **Logic Tensor Networks (LTNs)**  
  *Why needed:* Provide the differentiable embedding of first-order logic formulas into neural computation graphs, enabling gradient-based learning of both data and constraints. Without grasping `G_θ(ϕ)`, fuzzy connectives, and `SatAgg`, implementation is impossible.  
  *Quick check:* How does the `pMeanError` aggregator for universal quantification `∀x+ A(x+)` differ from a standard mean, and what does the parameter `p` control?

- **Declarative Process Mining & Rule Extraction (Declare, SLAs)**  
  *Why needed:* Source of the symbolic knowledge; engineers must understand how control-flow templates (response, precedence) are mined and translated to LTL/FOL, and how temporal SLAs become `IF-THEN` rules.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between a “control-flow rule” (mined via Declare) and a “temporal rule” (derived from SLA analysis) in terms of the features they operate on?

- **Feature Engineering for Predictive Process Monitoring**  
  *Why needed:* The pipeline starts with extracting features (control-flow flags, waiting times, payload) from prefixes; both rule extraction and the classifier depend on this shared vocabulary. Misalignment breaks the system.  
  *Quick check:* Given a trace prefix `l` ending with activity `X`, how would you compute the `WaitTime(l, Y, Z)` feature for activities `Y` and `Z` that have both occurred in the prefix?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (linear flow):
Event Log Input -> Feature Extraction Module -> Rule Extraction Module -> Knowledge Base Builder -> LTN Classifier Core (Grounded Inputs, Neural Predicate, Auxiliary Predicates, Aggregation) -> Injection Configuration (A/B/C wiring) -> Training Loop (Adam, minimize 1–SatAgg)

Critical path: Feature Extraction → Rule Extraction → KB Categorization → Injection Configuration Wiring → Training. A break in rule-to-feature mapping (e.g., a rule uses `HasAct(l,Rev)` but the feature extraction didn’t define it) is fatal.

Design tradeoffs:
- Output Refinement (B) vs. Feature Expansion (A): B directly shapes the decision boundary but requires careful rule formulation; A is simpler but less direct.
- Parallel Constraints (C): Adds computational overhead and potential for conflicting gradients if rules are noisy; strongest benefit when combined with B.
- LTN vs. Semantic Loss: LTN integrates constraints into the core satisfaction function, which the paper suggests is more effective, especially with scarce compliant data.

Failure signatures:
- Low compliance despite injection: Rules incorrectly translated to FOL, predicates buggy, or quantifier misuse.
- No accuracy gain/degraded performance: Injected rules irrelevant/contradictory to true signal; poor `p` tuning; feature expansion introduces noise.
- Training instability: Loss landscape becomes non-smooth; gradient magnitudes from formula terms dominate.

First three experiments:
1. **Unit Test Predicates:** For a single prefix, manually compute outputs of all grounded predicates (`HasAct`, `WaitTime`, `Next`) and verify against expected values.
2. **KB Sanity Check:** Construct a tiny synthetic KB (2–3 rules) and batch of 2–3 handcrafted prefixes; manually compute `SatAgg` to ensure it behaves as expected (near 1.0 for compliant batches, lower otherwise).
3. **Synthetic Rule Authority Ablation:** Generate a controlled log where one simple rule is the sole predictor. Train `LTN-B` with only that rule; model should achieve perfect test accuracy and 100% compliance, validating gradient flow from rule to output.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we formalize an explicit representation with specific templates to automatically connect extracted features with logical rules in the knowledge base?
- How effective is the neuro-symbolic approach in real-world case studies where domain knowledge is critical, such as in healthcare?
- Can the proposed LTN-based neuro-symbolic framework be effectively extended to other process mining tasks like next activity prediction or remaining time estimation?
- How does the quality and completeness of automatically extracted rules (from Declarative mining, SLA analysis, etc.) impact the overall performance and compliance of the neuro-symbolic model?

## Limitations
- Critical reproducibility gaps: exact rule sets per dataset and rule extraction parameters (Declare support thresholds, SLA bounds, correlation cutoffs) are undisclosed.
- Underspecified implementation details: semantics of key predicates (e.g., `Next`), functional forms for parallel constraints, and precise output refinement mechanics are missing.
- Dependency on high-quality, domain-valid rules is assumed; sensitivity to rule noise or contradictions is not thoroughly stress-tested.

## Confidence
- **Overall Claim (LTN models outperform baselines in accuracy & compliance):** High. Supported by consistent results across four datasets.
- **Claim (Output Refinement is the most effective standalone strategy):** Medium-High. Based on reported ablations, but untested across alternative rule sets limits certainty.
- **Claim (Reproducible implementation from paper alone):** Low. Missing extraction parameters and predicate definitions make perfect replication unlikely.
