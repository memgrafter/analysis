# Diagnosing Retrieval Bias Under Multiple In-Context Knowledge Updates in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12271
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12271
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit a widening Earliest-Latest Accuracy Gap (ELAG) as the number of in-context updates increases, caused by flattened internal signals that fail to reliably identify the latest fact among historical competitors.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates how large language models (LLMs) handle retrieval when a single fact is updated multiple times within the same context. Using the Dynamic Knowledge Instance (DKI) framework, which models updates as cue-value trajectories, the authors find that models maintain high accuracy on the earliest value but see substantial declines in latest-value accuracy as update count grows. Internal diagnostics reveal that attention distributions, hidden-state similarity, and output logits become flatter and less discriminative on errors, providing unstable retrieval signals. Cognitively inspired prompt interventions yield only modest improvements, suggesting the bias stems from fundamental representational competition during forward passes rather than superficial encoding weaknesses.

## Method Summary
The core method is the DKI framework, which constructs sequences of updates for a persistent cue (e.g., "A:B, A:C, ..."). Two query types are used: one asking for the earliest value and one for the latest. Models are prompted to output JSON with both answers. Accuracy is measured via exact match, and the Earliest-Latest Accuracy Gap (ELAG) quantifies bias. For internal analysis, the authors extract attention weights, hidden-state similarities, and logits at the answer position, computing alignment scores for each candidate value in the trajectory. Experiments run across multiple LLMs on synthetic (random word pairs) and real-world (evolving facts) DKI sets, with update counts ranging from 32 to 512 for synthetic data. Prompts are also modified with cognitive heuristics (rehearsal, integration, forgetting) to test intervention efficacy.

## Key Results
- Earliest-state accuracy remains near ceiling across all models and update counts, indicating no general retrieval failure.
- Latest-state accuracy drops sharply as the number of in-context updates increases, causing ELAG to widen rapidly (e.g., from ~0% to >40% for LLaMA3.1-8B between 32 and 512 updates).
- On error cases, internal signals (attention, hidden-state similarity, logits) flatten and lose a dominant peak for the correct latest candidate, making them weakly discriminative.
- Prompt-based cognitive interventions produce only small, inconsistent gains in latest accuracy and do not close the ELAG.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Internal signal collapse under competition.**  
*Claim:* Retrieval failure occurs because the model's internal signals (attention, hidden-state similarity, logits) flatten into non-discriminative distributions when the latest value competes with multiple historical candidates.  
*Mechanism:* As update count grows, activation spreads across all candidate positions, preventing a stable, peaked evidence gradient favoring the most recent trace. Successful retrieval requires a consistent, high-magnitude signal aligned with the target; when this flattens, the model guesses among competitors.  
*Core assumption:* A separable, peaked internal signal is necessary for reliable retrieval; its collapse leads to random or biased selection.  
*Evidence anchors:* [abstract] "...internal signals become flatter and weakly discriminative on errors..." [section 4.5] Figures 4–7 show flattened distributions on wrong cases. [corpus] *Weak evidence*; neighboring work focuses on conflict detection, not systematic signal collapse.  
*Break condition:* If latest-state retrieval remains high while internal signals are flat on errors, or if correct cases show flat signals, this mechanism is falsified.

**Mechanism 2: Cue-overload interference.**  
*Claim:* Bias intensifies due to cue-overload: binding multiple values to the same cue increases competition during retrieval, with no inherent recency weighting.  
*Mechanism:* Following cognitive theory (Watkins & Watkins, 1975), each update adds a memory trace linked to the identical cue. During retrieval, traces co-activate and compete equally; the model lacks a temporal decay or recency bias to privilege the latest. This resembles the AB-AC interference paradigm extended to multiple updates.  
*Core assumption:* LLMs treat all in-context associations as equally accessible; they do not implement recency-weighted retrieval.  
*Evidence anchors:* [abstract] Compares to AB-AC interference. [section 2.2] Explicitly links to cue-overload. [section 4.2] ELAG widens rapidly with T, consistent with overload. [corpus] *Weak evidence*; no direct study of sequential cue-overload in LLMs.  
*Break condition:* If ELAG does not increase with T, or if a model with strong recency bias (e.g., positional decay) shows no ELAG growth, this mechanism is undermined.

**Mechanism 3: Prompt interventions cannot fix architectural competition.**  
*Claim:* Modest gains from cognitive prompt interventions occur because they operate at the input level and cannot reshape the underlying cross-layer representational dynamics causing signal collapse.  
*Mechanism:* Interventions like rehearsal or integration alter encoding but do not change the fundamental competition during the forward pass. The instability in internal signals arises from model architecture and training objectives, which prompts alone cannot override.  
*Core assumption:* The failure is architectural/algorithmic, not merely an encoding weakness correctable by prompting.  
*Evidence anchors:* [abstract] Interventions yield only modest gains. [section 4.6] Table 1 and Appendix E.2 show limited, inconsistent improvements. [section 5] Calls for model-side mechanisms. [corpus] *Weak evidence*; RAG/editing work suggests architectural fixes are needed for persistent conflicts.  
*Break condition:* If a new prompting technique (not based on studied heuristics) eliminates ELAG across models and datasets, this mechanism is challenged. If architectural changes prove isomorphic to certain prompts, the distinct failure mode is questioned.

## Foundational Learning
**In-context learning (ICL)**  
*Why needed:* The DKI framework relies on the model treating context as a dynamic knowledge source. Understanding ICL explains why multiple contradictory updates cause interference rather than simple overwriting.  
*Quick check:* Why does providing multiple contradictory updates for the same fact not lead the model to simply use the last one, but instead cause competition among all?

**Attention mechanism and representational similarity**  
*Why needed:* Internal diagnostics depend on analyzing attention weights and cosine similarity of hidden states to candidate values. These probes how the model distributes focus and aligns representations.  
*Quick check:* In the paper's analysis, what does a "flatter" attention distribution or hidden-state similarity curve across candidate positions indicate about the model's internal evidence?

**Retrieval bias from interference (AB-AC paradigm)**  
*Why needed:* This cognitive psychology concept frames the problem, explaining why multiple valid historical values for the same cue lead to competition and systematic bias.  
*Quick check:* How does the AB-AC paradigm generalize to more than two updates (A:B, then A:C, then A:D), and what does cue-overload theory predict as update count increases?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
DKI Constructor -> Prompt Generation (with/without intervention) -> Model Inference -> Internal Signal Extractor -> Signal Scorer -> Metrics Calculator

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate synthetic or real-world DKI trajectories of length T.  
2. Create prompts for earliest/latest queries (baseline or with intervention).  
3. Run LLM inference, optionally extracting activations at answer position.  
4. For extracted signals, compute alignment scores (attention sum, hidden-state cosine, logit probability) for each candidate value.  
5. Calculate Accearliest, Acc latest, and ELAG; compare across T, models, and intervention conditions.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Synthetic vs. Real DKIs: Synthetic isolates interference but lacks ecological validity; real-world data introduces confounding factors like semantic strength and parametric priors.  
- Endpoint probing vs. full tracking: Endpoints offer low-ambiguity measurement but ignore intermediate state retrieval.  
- Black-box accuracy vs. internal diagnostics: Accuracy measures outcome bias; internal signals probe mechanism but require model access and careful activation extraction.  
- Prompt-based vs. architectural intervention: Paper tests only cognitive heuristics; architectural changes (e.g., modified attention) are costlier but potentially more effective.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. ELAG curve widens as update count T increases.  
2. Latest-state accuracy plateaus or declines while earliest accuracy stays near ceiling.  
3. Internal signal distributions flatten on error cases, lacking a clear peak for the latest candidate.  
4. Model-specific error patterns: e.g., LLaMA regresses to earlier positions, Qwen outputs near-end but out-of-field tokens.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate core phenomenon:** For LLaMA3.1-8B, run endpoint probing on synthetic DKIs with T ∈ {32,64,128,256,512}. Confirm Accearliest ≈ 100% and declining Acc latest, producing rising ELAG.  
2. **Internal signal profiling:** For Qwen2.5-7B at T=32, split samples by correct/incorrect latest queries. Plot layer-wise/head-wise attention scores, hidden-state similarity curves, and logit distributions. Verify flattening on wrong cases.  
3. **Intervention efficacy test:** Apply 2-shot and memory integration prompts on real-world DKIs. Measure change in Acc latest and ELAG; expect modest gains but persistent gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
- *Question:* What is the causal mechanism behind the degradation of internal signals when LLMs fail latest-state retrieval?  
- *Basis:* Internal-signal analyses are correlational and cover limited models, precluding strong causal claims.  
- *Why unresolved:* Without knowing the causal driver—e.g., attention head specialization or hidden-state interference—designing effective interventions is unclear.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Controlled ablation studies (e.g., patching layers/heads) combined with mechanistic interpretability to trace representational drift during forward passes.

**Open Question 2**  
- *Question:* Can model architectures with built-in temporal tracking mechanisms (e.g., modified attention or external memory) fundamentally eliminate retrieval bias?  
- *Basis:* Prompt interventions yield only modest gains and do not eliminate bias; authors call for model-side mechanisms.  
- *Why unresolved:* Only prompt-based strategies were tested; architectural solutions remain unexplored.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Compare modified architectures (e.g., recurrence, temporal masks, memory buffers) against baselines on DKI, measuring ELAG reduction without harming earliest accuracy.

**Open Question 3**  
- *Question:* Does retrieval bias persist in ecologically valid, long-form narrative contexts with real-world multi-update facts, and how does it interact with parametric prior knowledge?  
- *Basis:* Key limitation is the ecological gap between synthetic and real-world data; narrative experiment is preliminary.  
- *Why unresolved:* Unclear how robust ELAG is in natural, lengthy documents with complex structure and strong pre-training priors.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Large-scale benchmark of real-world multi-update facts embedded in diverse narratives, controlling update length, context coherence, and parametric overlap.

**Open Question 4**  
- *Question:* Can LLMs track and retrieve intermediate update states (not just endpoints) in long sequences?  
- *Basis:* Endpoint probing narrows observable scope; ability to retrieve arbitrary historical versions is unknown.  
- *Why unresolved:* Real-world applications may need access to any intermediate state (e.g., "value after third update").  
- *Resolving evidence:* Extend DKI to include intermediate-state queries (V^(2), V^(3), etc.) across T, measuring accuracy decay and comparing signal noisiness to endpoints.

**Open Question 5**  
- *Question:* Why do different LLM families exhibit distinct error patterns (e.g., LLaMA backward drift vs. Qwen tail clustering) under latest-state queries?  
- *Basis:* Error analysis shows qualitatively distinct patterns; failure mechanisms appear model-specific.  
- *Why unresolved:* Causes (e.g., positional encoding, pre-training data) are not investigated.  
- *Resolving evidence:* Cross-model analysis correlating error distributions with architectural attributes (RoPE vs. ALiBi, layer count, training corpus temporal diversity) and probing specific attention heads/MLPs.

## Limitations
- Exact synthetic DKI generation (word list, sampling) is unspecified, hindering precise replication.  
- Prompt templates for interventions are described briefly; reconstruction may introduce variance.  
- Internal signal extraction details (tokenization, span mapping) are ambiguous, potentially affecting reported signal shapes.  
- Endpoint probing only assesses earliest/latest states, ignoring intermediate retrieval dynamics.  
- Only prompt-based interventions were tested; architectural solutions remain unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Internal signal collapse):** Medium confidence. Strong observational correlation between flattened signals and errors, but no causal evidence.  
- **Mechanism 2 (Cue-overload):** Medium confidence. ELAG widening with T is consistent, but direct measurement of trace competition is absent.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Prompt limitations due to architecture):** Medium confidence. Modest gains from prompts are documented, but inference to architectural causes is indirect.

## Next Checks
1. **Sharpening attention intervention:** Design a prompt explicitly discounting earlier updates (e.g., "Only consider the last fact") and verify if latest accuracy improves and attention distributions peak on the final candidate.  
2. **Recency-biased positional encoding:** Fine-tune a model with decay-enhanced positional bias (e.g., ALiBi-style) and compare ELAG across T to baseline; reduced gap would support cue-overload.  
3. **Memory-augmented architecture:** Add a timestamp embedding and train a lightweight adapter to track the latest value; evaluate if this eliminates ELAG increase with T, indicating an architectural rather than ICL-based failure.
