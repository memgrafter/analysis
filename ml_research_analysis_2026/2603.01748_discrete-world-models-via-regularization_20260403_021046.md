# Discrete World Models via Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01748
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01748
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: DWMR, a regularization-based method for learning discrete Boolean world models without reconstruction, achieves state-of-the-art representation and one-step prediction accuracy on MNIST 8-puzzle and IceSlider benchmarks, even under additive noise, by using four regularizers (variance, correlation, coskewness, locality) and a two-step training scheme.

## Executive Summary
DWMR introduces a novel approach to learning discrete world models that avoids pixel reconstruction or contrastive objectives, thereby preventing latent collapse and encouraging symbolic state dynamics. The method combines a standard prediction loss with four statistical regularizers on the Bernoulli latent space: variance to ensure per-bit variability, correlation and coskewness to enforce bit independence, and locality to bias sparse action-induced changes. A two-step training procedure updates the predictor on hard bits first, then jointly refines encoder and predictor using soft probabilities, improving robustness. Experiments on two combinatorial benchmarks show significant improvements over autoencoding baselines in both clean and noisy conditions, with ablations confirming each regularizer's necessity. The work suggests a promising direction for learning interpretable, factorized world models for planning.

## Method Summary
DWMR learns an encoder `enc_ϕ` mapping observations to Bernoulli bit probabilities and hard bits, and a predictor `pred_ψ` mapping current state and action to predicted next-state probabilities. The target next-state bits are produced by a slowly updated EMA copy of the encoder (`enc_ϕ'`). The total loss is a weighted sum of: (1) binary cross-entropy prediction loss between predicted and target bits; (2) `L_var`, a hinge penalty on batch standard deviation per bit (target ≈0.5); (3) `L_cor`, mean absolute off-diagonal correlation of batch-normalized probabilities; (4) `L_cos`, mean absolute third-order cross-moments (coskewness) for distinct bit triplets; (5) `L_loc`, penalizing soft Hamming distance between current probabilities and target bits from falling outside a sparse window `[L, U]`. Training proceeds in two steps per batch: first update only the predictor with hard bits, then update both encoder and predictor with the full loss using soft probabilities. This avoids gradient issues from the discrete bottleneck while maintaining robustness.

## Key Results
- On clean MNIST 8-puzzle, DWMR achieves 91±1% encoding accuracy and 84±2% one-step imagination accuracy, surpassing DeepCubeAI (79±3%, 77±4%).
- Under additive Gaussian noise (`σ=0.5`), DWMR degrades minimally (87±2% encoding, 74±3% imagination) versus baselines which collapse; IceSlider shows similar robustness.
- Ablations reveal that removing any regularizer causes significant performance drops, with `L_var` removal leading to complete latent collapse (~12% accuracy, random chance).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: The variance regularizer (`L_var`) prevents latent collapse by ensuring each latent bit exhibits sufficient per-bit variability across the batch.  
**Mechanism**: `L_var` computes the batch standard deviation √*v_k* for each bit *k* and applies a hinge penalty when it falls below a threshold *γ* (close to 0.5, the std dev of Bernoulli(0.5)). This explicitly discourages the encoder from producing near-constant bit probabilities, forcing each bit to carry information.  
**Core assumption**: An informative Boolean latent space requires each bit to be approximately a fair coin flip (Bernoulli(0.5)) in the ideal case, providing maximum entropy per bit.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract states goal of maximizing entropy and independence; Section 3.2 details `L_var` formulation (Eq. 4); Ablation in Table 3 shows removal causes collapse to ~12% accuracy.  
**Break condition**: Removing `L_var` causes complete latent collapse, as shown in the ablation.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: The correlation (`L_cor`) and coskewness (`L_cos`) regularizers enforce statistical independence between latent bits, maximizing the overall information content and preventing redundancy.  
**Mechanism**: `L_cor` penalizes the mean absolute off-diagonal of the correlation matrix of batch-normalized probabilities, suppressing pairwise linear dependencies. `L_cos` extends this to third-order moments (coskewness), penalizing structured triplet co-activations that survive pairwise decorrelation.  
**Core assumption**: A factorized latent space where each bit encodes an independent state variable is optimal for symbolic dynamics and planning. Independence is proxied via low correlation and coskewness.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions maximizing independence; Section 3.2 gives formulas (Eqs. 5-6) and justification; Ablation (Table 3) shows large performance drops when either is removed (e.g., ~42% vs 93% for `L_cor` removal).  
**Break condition**: Removing either regularizer severely degrades representation quality, indicating dependency structures persist and harm downstream tasks.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: The locality regularizer (`L_loc`) instills a structural prior that actions induce sparse changes in the latent state, mirroring the true dynamics of combinatorial environments.  
**Mechanism**: For each transition, it computes a soft Hamming distance *d_n* between current encoder probabilities *p* and target bits *b'*, counting only differences crossing the 0.5 threshold. It then penalizes deviations of *d_n* from a desired window `[L, U]` (e.g., 1-6 flipped bits).  
**Core assumption**: In factored state spaces like puzzles, valid actions affect only a small, local subset of state variables. A good world model should reflect this sparsity in its latent dynamics.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions enforcing locality; Section 3.2 defines `d_n` and `L_loc` (Eqs. 7-8); Ablation (Table 3) shows `L_loc` is crucial for noise robustness (Imagination accuracy drops from 95% to 33% in noisy setting when removed).  
**Break condition**: Without `L_loc`, the model learns non-sparse transitions, which harms generalization under perceptual noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bernoulli Latent Space as Maximum Entropy Code**: DWMR assumes the ideal discrete state representation is a vector of independent Bernoulli(0.5) bits, providing a principled, high-entropy target for regularization. This avoids degenerate constant codes and aligns with capturing all state factors.  
  *Quick check*: If a latent bit has a batch variance near zero, what does that imply about its information content, and how does `L_var` address it?
- **Regularization as Collapse Prevention without Reconstruction**: Traditional world models use pixel decoders to retain information. DWMR replaces this with statistical regularizers on latents, necessitating understanding of how variance/covariance constraints substitute for reconstruction feedback.  
  *Quick check*: Why might minimizing only prediction loss (`L_pred`) lead to latent collapse, and how do `L_var` and `L_cor` jointly counteract it?
- **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) for Dynamics**: DWMR is a JEPA variant where the predictor maps (current latent, action) → next latent. This requires grasping the separation between encoder and predictor, and the role of EMA targets in stabilizing learning.  
  *Quick check*: What is the purpose of the EMA target encoder (`enc_ϕ'`), and how does its update rate *τ* differ from BYOL and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
The architecture consists of several interconnected components. The data flow is: observations → `enc_ϕ` (CNN/MLP) produces Bernoulli probabilities `p` and hard bits `b` (threshold 0.5) → `pred_ψ` (MLP) takes `b` or `p` concatenated with one-hot action to predict next probabilities `ˆp'` → `enc_ϕ'` (EMA copy) provides detached target bits `b'`. Loss components compute `L_pred`, `L_var`, `L_cor`, `L_cos`, `L_loc`; total loss `L_DWMR` weights them. Optimization uses a two-step loop per batch: first update only `pred_ψ` on `(b, b')` with `L_pred`, then update both `enc_ϕ` and `pred_ψ` on `(p, b')` with full `L_DWMR`. After each step, `enc_ϕ'` is updated by EMA with rate `τ`.

**Critical path**: Encoder produces bits and probabilities; predictor consumes them; losses calculated; gradients applied in two phases; EMA updates target encoder. The key loop stabilizes learning via EMA and two-step training.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Hard vs. soft inputs to predictor: Hard bits match test-time deployment but break gradients; soft probs enable end-to-end tuning. Two-step balances both.
- EMA decay `τ`: Slower (`τ≈0.996` as in BYOL) stabilizes representation but may slow adaptation; faster (`τ≈0.9`) improves predictive accuracy here.
- Regularizer weights: Must be tuned per-benchmark; `λ_var` is most sensitive (ablation shows collapse if near zero).

**Failure signatures**:
- Latent collapse: Encoder outputs near-constant bits; `L_var` goes to zero. Check batch variances.
- Bit dependency: High off-diagonal in correlation matrix; `L_cor` not decreasing.
- Non-sparse transitions: `L_loc` high, meaning predicted transitions flip many bits; locality window `[L,U]` may be mis-specified.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline check: Train AE (with decoder) and verify it learns some representation but likely collapses under noise (per Table 1, AE performs poorly).
2. Ablation of `L_var`: Remove `L_var` from DWMR; expect rapid collapse to constant codes (~12% accuracy, per Table 3).
3. Two-step vs. alternatives: Compare two-step training to "fully differentiable" and "straight-through" on MNIST 8-puzzle (per Table 2); two-step should show robustness, especially under noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can DWMR be extended to handle partially observable environments where latent states depend on history?  
  *Basis in paper*: Limitations state experiments "do not address partial observability, for which it may be necessary to incorporate some memory (e.g., with a recurrent architecture)."  
  *Why unresolved*: Current DWMR encodes single observations into stateless Boolean vectors, which cannot capture history-dependent states.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Incorporate recurrent/transformer memory, evaluate on POMDP benchmarks (e.g., masked observations).

- **Open Question 2**: Does the locality regularizer consistently yield more interpretable and modular transition functions across diverse domains?  
  *Basis in paper*: Future Directions suggest studying "the interpretability of the learned transition functions, and whether locality yields more modular, human-readable update rules."  
  *Why unresolved*: While locality improves performance (ablation), its effect on structural simplicity of transitions (e.g., isolated bit flips) is not analyzed.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Quantify modularity (bits flipped per action) or have experts assess readability of transition rules in symbolic domains.

- **Open Question 3**: Can the regularization-only approach of DWMR scale to environments with stochastic dynamics or continuous action spaces?  
  *Basis in paper*: Limitations note "a natural direction is to address stochastic environments and/or continuous actions."  
  *Why unresolved*: DWMR assumes deterministic transitions and discrete actions; stochasticity could violate the bit-flip locality assumption, and continuous actions require different conditioning.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Extend to stochastic variants (e.g., probabilistic tile movements) or continuous control tasks, measure robustness via transition prediction entropy.

- **Open Question 4**: How does DWMR's performance compare when integrated with downstream planning or reinforcement learning versus using it solely for representation learning?  
  *Basis in paper*: Future Directions propose integrating with "MBRL policy learning or with planning/heuristic search."  
  *Why unresolved*: Paper evaluates only representation quality (linear probes, one-step imagination), not long-horizon planning or control.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Combine DWMR latents with planning algorithms (e.g., A* with learned heuristics) or RL agents on puzzle-solving, compare sample efficiency and success rates.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (regularizer weights λ_var, λ_cor, λ_cos, λ_loc; EMA rate τ; locality bounds [L,U]; scheduling factors) and full IceSlider predictor architecture are not disclosed, requiring validation search for reproduction.
- Evaluation is limited to two combinatorial benchmarks (MNIST 8-puzzle, IceSlider); transfer to continuous, stochastic, or larger-scale environments is unknown.
- Metrics focus on per-cell F1-score for one-step imagination; long-horizon planning or closed-loop execution performance is not assessed.
- Two-step training heuristic lacks theoretical analysis; its superiority over end-to-end alternatives is empirical only.
- Regularizer sensitivity not fully explored; risk of benchmark-specific overfitting.

1. **Independence audit**: For held-out data, compute average pairwise mutual information between latent bits. DWMR should yield near-zero values; ablations without `L_cor`/`L_cos` should show significantly higher dependence.
2. **Transition sparsity profiling**: Measure distribution of soft Hamming distances between predicted and target latents. With `L_loc`, distribution should concentrate within `[L,U]`; without it, spread should be broad. Correlate sparsity with noise robustness.
3. **Cross-benchmark generalization**: Apply DWMR to (a) a larger puzzle (e.g., 15-puzzle) to test scalability, and (b) a stochastic domain (e.g., Atari Pong with discrete latent bottleneck) to test robustness. Compare against baselines under identical protocols.
