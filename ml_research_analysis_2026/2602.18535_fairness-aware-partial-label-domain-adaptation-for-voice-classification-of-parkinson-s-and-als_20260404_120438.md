# Fairness-Aware Partial-label Domain Adaptation for Voice Classification of Parkinson's and ALS

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18535
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18535
- **Reference Count:** 22
- **Primary Result:** Proposed FairPDA framework achieves highest external generalization (mean External MCC up to 0.19) and lowest gender disparity in most cross-cohort evaluations for three-class (HC/PD/ALS) voice classification.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a challenging real-world problem: classifying voice recordings into healthy, Parkinson’s disease (PD), and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) under severe distribution shifts. The shifts arise from using different recording devices and patient cohorts (domain shift), mismatched disease labels across source and target domains (partial-label problem), and inherent gender-based performance biases. To address these jointly, the authors introduce FairPDA, a hybrid deep learning framework. It combines MixStyle-based data augmentation for domain generalization, a conditional adversarial alignment method tailored for partial labels, and an adversarial branch to enforce gender invariance. Evaluated on four sustained vowel datasets under both domain generalization and unsupervised domain adaptation protocols, FairPDA demonstrates superior and more fair external test performance compared to prior methods, with ablations confirming the critical role of each component.

## Method Summary
The task is unified three-class (healthy/PD/ALS) voice classification under cross-device/cohort domain shift and partial-label mismatch. Source domains have non-overlapping disease labels (e.g., one has HC/PD, another HC/ALS). Four sustained vowel /a/ datasets are used: *mPower* (HC/PD), *VOC-ALS* (HC/ALS), *UAMS* (HC/PD), *Minsk* (HC/ALS). Inputs are log-Mel spectrograms after preprocessing (peak normalization, resampling to 8 kHz, VAD, segmentation, RMS equalization). Key metrics are patient-level Balanced Accuracy, MCC, macro-F1, and fairness metrics (Equal Opportunity Difference, Equalized Odds Gap) on external tests.

The proposed **FairPDA** uses a ResNet-18 backbone. MixStyle layers are inserted after the first and second residual blocks to mix style statistics (mean/variance) across samples, augmenting domain invariance. Three output branches are attached:
1.  **Task Classifier:** Linear layer for 3-way classification. Loss uses patient-normalized cross-entropy (weighting segments by the inverse of their patient's segment count in the batch) or standard CE.
2.  **Partial CDAN Domain Discriminator:** A binary classifier conditioned on the feature representation *f* and the model's *class prediction* *p* via a multilinear map *f ⊗ p*. Source samples are weighted by γ(ys), defined as the average predicted probability of class ys over the current target batch. Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) is applied.
3.  **Gender Discriminator:** A 3-layer MLP predicting gender (binary). A GRL enforces adversarial debiasing so features become gender-invariant.

Total loss: `L_y - λ_d * L_d - λ_fair * L_fair`. Loss weights λ_d (domain) and λ_fair (fairness) are linearly warmed up. Training uses 5-fold patient-level cross-validation on pooled source data. For unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), 30% of each target cohort's patients are added as unlabeled data; the remaining 70% form the held-out external test set. For domain generalization (DG), no target data is used during training.

## Key Results
- FairPDA achieves the highest mean External MCC (up to 0.19) and External Balanced Accuracy across both DG and UDA protocols compared to multiple competitor methods.
- It attains the lowest gender disparity (EOD/EOG) in three out of four tested source-target configurations, while no competitor shows statistically significant external performance gains.
- Ablation studies confirm that removing MixStyle, Partial CDAN, or the fairness branch each degrades external generalization and/or fairness, validating the integrated design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Domain Generalization via MixStyle:** By randomly mixing feature channel-wise mean and variance between samples within a batch during training, MixStyle exposes the model to synthetic domain shifts, encouraging it to learn domain-agnostic features that generalize better to unseen cohorts and devices.
- **Partial-Label Adversarial Alignment:** The conditional CDAN discriminator focuses alignment on features relevant to the *predicted* class distribution. The weighting scheme γ(ys) down-weights source samples from classes *not* present in the current target batch's predictions, mitigating negative transfer from source-only classes (e.g., aligning only on the shared "healthy" class when target has HC/ALS).
- **Adversarial Gender Debiasing:** The gradient-reversed gender branch forces the main feature extractor to discard information predictive of gender, reducing unfair performance gaps between male and female patients without significantly harming disease-related discrimination.

## Foundational Learning
- **Domain Generalization (DG):** Training on multiple source domains to perform well on a *new*, unseen target domain without any target data during training. *Why needed:* Real-world clinical models encounter varied recording setups; DG prepares for this. *Quick check:* Does performance drop significantly when evaluating on a completely held-out dataset vs. source validation?
- **Partial-Label Learning:** A form of semi-supervised learning where each training instance is labeled with a *set* of possible classes, not a single true label. *Why needed:* Source and target diseases don't fully overlap (e.g., source has PD, target has ALS but not PD). *Quick check:* Does the model collapse to predicting only classes common to all sources, ignoring source-only classes during alignment?
- **Adversarial Debiasing:** Using a gradient-reversed adversarial objective to remove sensitive attribute information (e.g., gender) from learned representations. *Why needed:* To meet fairness requirements and ensure equitable model performance across demographic groups. *Quick check:* Does the auxiliary gender discriminator's accuracy drop toward chance (50%) while main task accuracy is maintained?
- **MixStyle:** A style-based data augmentation that mixes channel-wise feature statistics (mean, variance) across samples. *Why needed:* To simulate diverse recording conditions and force the model to be robust to style variations (e.g., device effects). *Quick check:* Is MixStyle applied only during training? Does increasing its probability (p) improve external generalization past a point?
- **Conditional Domain Alignment (CDAN):** Aligning feature distributions conditioned on model predictions (multilinear map of feature and prediction). *Why needed:* Aligning *what matters* for the task, not all features blindly. *Quick check:* Does a standard, unconditional adversarial loss underperform the conditional version in partial-label settings?
- **Patient-Normalized Loss:** Weighting each training segment by the inverse of the number of segments from its patient in the current batch. *Why needed:* To prevent patients with more recordings from dominating the loss, ensuring learning is patient-centric rather than segment-centric. *Quick check:* Does removing this normalization overfit to patients with many segments on internal validation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**
ResNet-18 (backbone) -> [MixStyle (after block1, block2)] -> Feature Map -> {Task Head, Domain Head (f⊗p), Gender Head}
GRL before Domain Head & Gender Head.
Losses: Task CE (weighted) + (-λ_d * Domain BCE) + (-λ_fair * Gender CE).

**Critical Path**
1. Input spectrogram -> ResNet conv1/bn1/relu -> Residual Block1 -> MixStyle -> Residual Block2 -> MixStyle -> Residual Blocks3-4 -> Global Avg Pool -> 512-dim feature vector.
2. Feature vector branches to three heads.
3. For domain head, compute conditional input `h = f ⊗ p` (multilinear map of feature `f` and class prediction `p`), then pass through domain discriminator.
4. For gender head, feature directly into MLP.
5. GRLs invert gradients for domain/gender losses during backprop.
6. Total loss computed, gradients flow back through task head normally but through GRLs with scaled negative sign for other branches.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- **MixStyle Strength (α/p):** Higher values increase domain augmentation but may corrupt disease-relevant features, harming internal accuracy. Tradeoff between robustness and task performance.
- **λ_d (Domain Weight):** Too low: insufficient alignment, negative transfer. Too high: task signal overwhelmed, model may align to irrelevant domain features. Requires careful scheduling (warm-up).
- **λ_fair (Fairness Weight):** Similar tradeoff; strong pressure can hurt overall accuracy if gender information is inadvertently correlated with disease cues.
- **Partial CDAN Weighting (γ):** The target-based reweighting is adaptive but introduces noise if target predictions are inaccurate early in training. May need a confidence threshold.

**Failure Signatures**
- **Poor External Generalization:** High internal (source) BalAcc/MCC but near-chance external. Suggests overfitting to source artefacts or failed domain alignment. Check MixStyle application and λ_d value.
- **Unfairness Persists:** EOD/EOG remains high (>0.1). Indicates fairness branch ineffective. Monitor gender discriminator accuracy; if high (>60%), λ_fair may be too low or GRL misconfigured.
- **Training Instability:** Oscillating losses, exploding gradients. Common with adversarial training and warm-up schedules. Check gradient norms, warm-up length, and λ scaling.
- **Partial-Label Collapse:** Recall for source-only classes (e.g., PD when adapting to HC/ALS) drops to zero. γ weighting may be too aggressive or alignment applied indiscriminately. Consider restricting domain alignment to shared classes only.

**First Three Experiments**
1. **Ablation 1 – No MixStyle:** Train FairPDA without MixStyle layers. Expect significant drop in external generalization, confirming MixStyle's role in DG.
2. **Ablation 2 – No Partial CDAN:** Remove domain discriminator branch. Expect performance collapse, especially under UDA, showing necessity of conditional alignment for partial-label shift.
3. **Ablation 3 – No Fairness Branch:** Remove gender discriminator and λ_fair. Expect gender disparity (EOD/EOG) to increase substantially, validating the fairness mechanism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Imbalance-Aware Objectives:** How to design more effective objectives that better detect and down-weight source-only classes under partial-label shift? The current γ-reweighting is basic.
- **Scaling & Multiple Attributes:** How does FairPDA scale to larger, more diverse cohorts? Can the fairness framework extend to multiple sensitive attributes (age, ethnicity) and intersectional subgroups? Current study is limited to binary gender.
- **Identifying Cohort Artefacts:** What specific cohort-specific artefacts (device effects, recording protocols) cause the internal-external gap? Without pinpointing, improving DG is partly guesswork.
- **Acoustic Feature Robustness:** Which acoustic features are most robust for cross-device HC/PD/ALS discrimination, and how does the ResNet-18 architecture under FairPDA utilize them? Lacks interpretability.

## Limitations
- **Critical Hyperparameters Unreported:** Final values for λ_d, λ_fair, learning rate, batch size, MixStyle α/probability, warm-up epochs, and total training epochs are not provided, blocking exact replication.
- **Partial CDAN in DG Unclear:** The paper evaluates under DG (no target data) but the Partial CDAN formula requires target batch statistics. It is unspecified how the domain loss is computed in pure DG mode (e.g., omitted, or proxy target distribution?).
- **Patient-Normalized CE Ambiguity:** The exact implementation of segment weighting (per-batch vs. per-epoch, global vs. local patient counts) is not defined, affecting optimization dynamics.
- **External Evaluation Aggregation:** Whether metrics are averaged over target cohorts first or pooled predictions across all external patients is unclear, hindering comparison.

## Confidence
- **Superior External Generalization:** Medium. Relies on unreported hyperparameters and ambiguous DG implementation; reproducibility is partially blocked.
- **Lowest Gender Disparity:** Medium. Depends on correct tuning of λ_fair and GRL, which are unspecified; fairness gains may not replicate exactly.
- **Ablation Validity:** High. Ablation patterns (each component hurts performance when removed) are described and plausible, but exact replication of the drop magnitude depends on the ambiguous implementations.
- **Partial CDAN Mechanism:** High. The conditional alignment concept is clearly described and central to handling partial labels, though its DG implementation is the ambiguity.
- **MixStyle for DG:** High. Using style augmentation for domain generalization is a well-established concept; the specific insertion points are confirmed.
