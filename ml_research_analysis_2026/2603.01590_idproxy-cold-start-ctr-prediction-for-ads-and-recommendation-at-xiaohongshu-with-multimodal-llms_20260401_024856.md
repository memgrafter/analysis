# IDProxy: Cold-Start CTR Prediction for Ads and Recommendation at Xiaohongshu with Multimodal LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01590
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01590
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Cold-start item AUC improved by 0.23–0.32% (2× global gain), with online lifts in time spent (+0.22%) and advertiser value (+1.93%).

## Executive Summary
IDProxy addresses the item cold-start problem in CTR prediction at Xiaohongshu by leveraging a multimodal LLM (InternVL) to generate content-based proxy embeddings for new items. The two-stage system first aligns coarse proxies with existing ID embeddings via contrastive learning, then refines them end-to-end with the CTR ranker by reusing its feature interaction and sequence structures. Offline experiments show an AUC gain of +0.14% over a strong baseline, and online A/B tests confirm significant improvements, especially for items under 24 hours old.

## Method Summary
The system uses a frozen InternVL MLLM to encode multimodal item content. Stage 1 aggregates the final hidden state, projects it via an MLP, and aligns it with ℓ2-normalized warm-item ID embeddings using a contrastive loss (Proxy Alignment Loss). Stage 2 extracts multi-granularity representations from k-means-selected MLLM layer subgroups, fuses them with a lightweight adaptor, and combines the result with the coarse proxy via residual gating. The refined proxy is injected into the CTR ranker’s core modules (e.g., as an ID-like feature), and the adaptor plus ranker are trained end-to-end on the CTR objective. During serving, proxies are precomputed for new items and retrieved by the ranker.

## Key Results
- Offline AUC gain: +0.14% over production baseline.
- Content Feed online lifts: Time Spent +0.22%, Reads +0.39%, Engagements +0.5%.
- Display Ads online lifts: Advertiser Value +1.93%, COST +1.73%, CTR +0.23%.
- New items (<24h) achieved 2× higher AUC improvement (0.23–0.32%) vs. global items (0.12–0.15%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Contrastive Alignment
- **Claim:** Content-based proxy embeddings can be aligned with the existing collaborative ID embedding space via contrastive learning.
- **Mechanism:** MLLM encodes item to `z_i`, projected and ℓ2-normalized to `~h_i`. Contrastive loss pulls `~h_i` toward its corresponding normalized ID embedding `e_i` and pushes away from other in-batch IDs.
- **Core assumption:** Warm-item ID embedding distribution is a meaningful target; aligning to it allows seamless substitution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] aligns proxies with ID space; Section 3.1.3 defines `L_PAL`; corpus offers weak support.
- **Break condition:** If warm ID distribution is highly unstable or non-stationary, contrastive alignment to a static snapshot may fail.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-granularity Fusion
- **Claim:** Leveraging MLLM hidden states from multiple layers, fused via an adaptor and residual gating, refines coarse proxies with fine-grained CTR-relevant signals.
- **Mechanism:** Hidden states from k-means-selected layer subgroups are pooled, concatenated, and passed through an MLP `φ̃` to produce `p_raw_fine_i`. Gated residual fusion `p_fine_i = W_c p_coarse_i + r ⊙ p_raw_fine_i` combines coarse and fine signals.
- **Core assumption:** Hierarchical MLLM representations contain complementary semantics; a small adaptor can transform them without overfitting.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2.1 hierarchical partitioning; 3.2.2 adaptor and gating; corpus weak.
- **Break condition:** If MLLM hidden states are poorly calibrated for CTR or adaptor capacity is insufficient, fine signals may add noise.

### Mechanism 3: Structural Reuse
- **Claim:** End-to-end joint training of the refined proxy with the CTR ranker, while explicitly reusing its feature interaction and sequence modeling components, enables structural knowledge transfer.
- **Mechanism:** Final proxy `p_fine_i` is injected into the ranker’s core modules (e.g., as an additional ID-like feature). Ranker parameters `θ`, adaptor `φ̃`, and gating `W_g, W_c` are updated via CTR loss `L_CTR`; MLLM remains frozen.
- **Core assumption:** The ranker’s learned structural priors (feature crosses, attention patterns) are generalizable to new item representations if the proxy occupies the same architectural slot as a standard ID embedding.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes reuse; Section 3.2.3 details injection and training; Section 4.2.2 ablation shows v5 (with reuse) outperforms v4 (without).
- **Break condition:** If the ranker’s architecture is overly specialized to ID embedding quirks (e.g., popularity bias), structural reuse might inhibit adaptation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE Loss):** Needed to align MLLM content embeddings with warm-item ID embeddings without requiring interaction data. *Quick check:* Why ℓ2-normalize? To remove magnitude biases (e.g., popularity) and focus on directional alignment.
- **MLLM Hidden State Utilization:** Needed because final-layer outputs are often task-agnostic; intermediate layers capture more transferable, fine-grained semantics beneficial for aligning with collaborative signals. *Quick check:* What is the risk of using all MLLM layers directly? High dimensionality and noise; hierarchical partitioning mitigates by selecting representative layer groups.
- **Residual Gating for Multi-Granularity Fusion:** Needed to combine coarse (Stage 1) and fine (Stage 2) proxy signals adaptively, preventing fine signals from overwriting the established alignment. *Quick check:* What if gating (`r = σ(W_g[concatenated features])`) were omitted? Fine-grained adaptor output might dominate and destabilize Stage 1 alignment.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
MLLM (InternVL, frozen) -> Stage1 Pooling & Projector (g, φ) -> Coarse Proxy (p_coarse) -> Hierarchical Partitioner -> Stage2 Adaptor (φ̃) -> Residual Gating (W_g, W_c) -> Fine Proxy (p_fine) -> CTR Ranker (f_θ)

Critical path:
- Training: (1) Stage1: Train φ with `L_PAL` on warm items; freeze MLLM. (2) Stage2: Train φ̃, W_g, W_c, θ with `L_CTR` using p_coarse and p_fine; MLLM frozen.
- Serving: For new item, run MLLM → compute p_coarse → compute p_fine → store; ranker retrieves p_fine via item ID at inference.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Frozen MLLM vs. Full Tuning:** Freezing ensures stability and reuse of powerful priors; tuning risks catastrophic forgetting and high deployment cost.
- **Coarse-Fine Fusion:** Residual gating protects Stage 1 alignment but adds complexity; simpler concatenation might degrade performance.
- **Structural Injection:** Plugging into atomic ID slots maximizes reuse but limits flexibility; treating as a side feature might not fully utilize feature interaction modules.

Failure signatures:
- **Misalignment:** Stage1 ΔAUC << +0.05% (Table 1 v3). Diagnostic: t-SNE/UMAP of p_coarse vs. true IDs; average cosine similarity between matched pairs.
- **Ineffective Fine Stage:** No gain over coarse or overfits. Diagnostic: Ablate fine stage; check if p_fine contributes non-redundant info; monitor CTR vs. validation AUC.
- **Structural Mismatch:** Different injection points yield no gain. Diagnostic: Compare AUC when treating proxies as separate vs. replacing ID embedding.
- **Dataset Shift:** Public data yields overly optimistic gains (>0.5% AUC). Diagnostic: Compare embedding sparsity and cluster metrics.
- **Resource Constraints:** MLLM latency spike >5%. Diagnostic: Profile generation; consider distilled MLLM or caching.

First 3 experiments:
1. Ablate Stage1: Train Stage2 with randomly initialized coarse proxy → expect near-zero gain, confirming need for alignment.
2. Ablate structural reuse: Compare v4 (concatenate p_fine as side feature) vs. v5 (inject into ID slot) → expect significant drop if reuse is critical.
3. Analyze gating: Monitor average gate value `r` for new vs. warm items during training → low `r` on new items suggests fine signals not being used for cold-start.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does fine-tuning the MLLM during Stage2 improve proxy quality compared to the frozen approach?
2. Can the heuristic k-means layer selection be replaced by a learnable mechanism that automatically determines optimal layers and weights?
3. Does ℓ2 normalization of ID embeddings discard useful popularity signals? Could magnitude modeling enhance cold-start performance?
4. How well does IDProxy generalize to other content types (e.g., video, audio) and multilingual settings, or to different MLLM architectures?

## Limitations
- Production CTR model architecture is undisclosed, hindering exact replication of structural reuse.
- MLLM (InternVL) specifics, prompt design, aggregation function, and Stage1 hyperparameters are not fully detailed.
- Stage2 design (k-means parameters, adaptor architecture, injection points) remains ambiguous.
- Evaluation methodology (cold-split strategy, A/B test rigor) lacks transparency.
- Generalizability is only demonstrated on Xiaohongshu’s data; external validation is limited.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Contrastive Alignment): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (Multi-granularity Fusion): Low
- Mechanism 3 (Structural Reuse): High

## Next Checks
1. Ablate structural injection: Replace ID-slot injection with side feature concatenation; expect significant AUC drop if reuse is critical.

3. Monitor warm ID distribution drift: Compute cosine similarity shifts of warm-item ID embeddings across epochs; high drift challenges alignment stability.
