# The Impact of Post-training on Data Contamination

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.06103
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06103
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Post-training (SFT/GRPO) resurrects contamination gaps, with SFT causing local overestimation and GRPO partial generalization; scale amplifies these effects.

## Executive Summary
Data contamination—injecting test items into pre-training—poses a threat to accurate evaluation of language models. This paper explores how post-training via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) interacts with such contamination. Using controlled experiments with GSM8K/MBPP contamination and uncontaminated analog benchmarks (GSMPlus/HumanEval), the authors find that extended clean pre-training gradually suppresses but does not erase contamination effects. However, subsequent post-training revives the performance gap between contaminated and clean models, reaching up to ~4% on contaminated tasks. Notably, SFT leads to inflation only on the contaminated benchmarks, while GRPO also improves analogous uncontaminated tasks, suggesting partial generalization. Moreover, model scale amplifies these tendencies: larger SFT models memorize more, whereas larger GRPO models convert leakage into broader capabilities. The study concludes that contamination audits must be performed after post-training, as final benchmarks may reflect resurrected memorization rather than genuine skill acquisition.

## Method Summary
The study creates contaminated and clean variants of two model families (Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B, Gemma3 1B/4B). Contamination is introduced by inserting five copies of the GSM8K and MBPP test sets into the first 2 billion tokens of the pre-training stream. Both variants then undergo an additional 25 billion tokens of pre-training on a mixture of FineWeb-Edu, CodeParrots, and OpenMath-Instruct. After pre-training, models are adapted via either Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on GSM8K/MBPP training splits (with loss masked before the CoT prefix) or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using rule-based rewards for one epoch with 16 samples per prompt. Evaluation is conducted on the contaminated benchmarks (GSM8K, MBPP) and uncontaminated analogs (GSMPlus, HumanEval) using the lm-evaluation-harness and math-verify. The primary metric is the difference in performance between contaminated and clean models (Δ), which isolates the contamination effect. The experimental design tracks Δ immediately after extended pre-training (Base) and after post-training (SFT or GRPO).

## Key Results
- Contamination gaps shrink to near zero after extended clean pre-training but are resurrected by both SFT and GRPO, reaching up to ~4% on contaminated tasks.
- SFT inflates scores solely on contaminated benchmarks, while GRPO also yields gains on uncontaminated counterparts, indicating partial generalization from leaked data.
- Scale modulates the effects: larger SFT models exhibit greater Δ (more memorization), whereas larger GRPO models show reduced Δ (leakage converted to broader skills).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Post-training (SFT/GRPO) resurrects a performance gap that appeared negligible after extended clean pre-training.
- **Mechanism:** Extended pre-training on clean data may suppress (but not erase) the model's ability to retrieve memorized contamination. Task-specific post-training then provides a strong, targeted signal that re-activates or exploits these latent memories, causing measurable score inflation on the contaminated benchmarks.
- **Core assumption:** Memories formed during contamination remain accessible in the model's parameter space but are not preferentially activated by the general pre-training objective; a specialized post-training objective preferentially triggers them.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Contamination causes performance spikes that are gradually diminished with continued pre-training... Both SFT and GRPO resurface the leaked information."
  - [section 4, Figures 2-4] Show the gap between contaminated and clean models shrinking toward zero during the final 23B tokens of pre-training but widening again after both SFT and GRPO.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence weak/missing; no direct anchor from provided neighbors. Mechanisms inferred from paper's controlled experiments.
- **Break condition:** If post-training on a *truly* uncontaminated successor task (e.g., a novel task unrelated to GSM8K/MBPP) fails to resurrect a gap, it suggests the effect is specific to the contamination's domain.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** SFT and GRPO differ in the external validity of the resurrected gap: SFT causes local overestimation (inflation only on contaminated benchmarks), while GRPO causes partial generalization (inflation on both contaminated and clean benchmarks measuring similar abilities).
- **Mechanism:** SFT (imitation learning) directly teaches the model to replicate the specific solutions seen during contamination, leading to overfitting to the exact test distribution. GRPO (exploration-based RL) discovers and reinforces general reasoning patterns present in the contaminated data, which coincidentally improve performance on analogous, uncontaminated problems.
- **Core assumption:** The nature of the optimization signal (demonstration mimicry vs. reward-driven exploration) determines whether learned improvements are instance-specific or pattern-based.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "SFT inflates scores only on the contaminated tasks, whereas GRPO also inflates performance on uncontaminated counterparts (GSMPlus, HumanEval)."
  - [section 4.1, Figure 5] Shows SFT models have a large gap on GSM8K/MBPP but near-zero gap on GSMPlus/HumanEval; GRPO models show positive gaps on both.
  - [corpus] Weak anchor: [corpus id 60564] discusses SFT-RL post-training trade-offs but does not address contamination generalization.
- **Break condition:** If GRPO's gap on uncontaminated benchmarks vanishes when controlling for reward function formulation (e.g., using only correctness rewards without format rewards), it would indicate the generalization stems from superficial heuristics rather than deep pattern transfer.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Model scale amplifies these behaviors differently: larger SFT models extract more pure contamination benefit (greater local overestimation), while larger GRPO models convert leakage into more generalizable capability (smaller relative overestimation).
- **Mechanism:** Increased capacity allows SFT to more perfectly memorize the contaminated examples. For RL, increased capacity provides a richer hypothesis space where the patterns extracted from contamination can be integrated with broader knowledge, leading to wider applicability.
- **Core assumption:** Scale magnifies the dominant inductive bias of the training method (memorization for SFT, pattern discovery for RL).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "larger Supervised Fine Tuned models memorize more, while larger GRPO models translate leakage into more generalizable capabilities."
  - [section 4.2, Figure 6] Shows the contamination-gap difference (Eq. 1) increasing with scale for Base and SFT, but decreasing (even turning negative) for GRPO.
  - [corpus] Weak anchor: [corpus id 177936] discusses persistent poisoning but does not analyze scale interactions with post-training.
- **Break condition:** If the scale trend reverses for models beyond 4B parameters, or if different architectures (e.g., MoE) show divergent scaling laws, the assumed monotonic amplification would be invalid.

## Foundational Learning

### Pre-training vs. Post-training Distinction
**Why needed here:** The entire analysis hinges on comparing model states *after* extended pre-training (Base) versus after subsequent task-specific adaptation (SFT/GRPO). Misunderstanding these stages would obscure the mechanism of "resurrection."
**Quick check question:** What is the primary optimization objective during pre-training (predict next token) versus SFT (match demonstration outputs) versus GRPO (maximize rule-based reward)?

### Contamination Measurement via Delta Analysis
**Why needed here:** The study measures effect size as the *difference* in performance between a contaminated and a clean model (∆). This isolates contamination impact from base model capability.
**Quick check question:** If a clean model scores 60% on GSM8K and a contaminated model scores 64%, what is the contamination's apparent impact? What would you need to measure to see if it's a real skill gain or overestimation?

### Generalization vs. Memorization in Evaluation
**Why needed here:** The core diagnostic is comparing performance on the *contaminated* benchmark (GSM8K/MBPP) versus an *uncontaminated analog* (GSMPlus/HumanEval). This distinguishes localized recall from broader skill transfer.
**Quick check question:** If a model improves on both GSM8K and GSMPlus after GRPO, does that suggest the improvement is (A) purely memorization, (B) genuine generalization, or (C) a flawed evaluation?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
- Base Models: Clean, pre-trained checkpoints (Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B, Gemma3 1B/4B).
- Contamination Injector: Script to insert 5 copies of GSM8K/MBPP test items into the first 2B tokens of the pre-training stream.
- Extended Pre-training: Training loop for an additional ~25B tokens on a mixed corpus (FineWeb-Edu, CodeParrots, OpenMath-Instruct).
- Post-training Modules: SFT (supervised fine-tuning on GSM8K/MBPP training splits, masked CoT loss) and GRPO (RL with rule-based rewards, 16 samples, 1 epoch).
- Evaluation Harness: `lm-eval-harness` with task-specific prompt templates and `math-verify` for parsing. Benchmarks: GSM8K, MBPP (contaminated), GSMPlus, HumanEval (uncontaminated).
- Comparator: Computational pipeline to calculate `contaminated_score - clean_score` for each recipe/model/benchmark.

### Critical Path
The pipeline runs two parallel branches starting from a Clean Checkpoint:
- **Branch A (Contaminated):** Inject contamination → Extended Pre-training → Post-training (SFT/GRPO) → Evaluation.
- **Branch B (Clean):** No injection → Extended Pre-training → Post-training (SFT/GRPO) → Evaluation.
The critical comparison is the delta (Branch A – Branch B) at each stage (Base, SFT, GRPO). The key insight is that the delta may be near-zero after Extended Pre-training but becomes significant after Post-training.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Controlled Injection vs. Realism:** Injecting 5× exact test items into the first 2B tokens is a strong, easily detectable signal. Real contamination is often noisier (paraphrased, distributed). Tradeoff: clarity of mechanism vs. ecological validity.
- **Benchmark Selection:** Using GSMPlus/HumanEval as uncontaminated analogs assumes they measure the same underlying ability. If they differ in difficulty or style, the "generalization" signal could be confounded.
- **Post-training Scope:** Both SFT and GRPO use the *training* splits of the contaminated benchmarks. This ensures post-training itself is not "contaminated" by the test set. The risk is that SFT/GRPO might be optimizing for a distribution that is still "contaminated-adjacent."

### Failure Signatures
1. **No Resurrection:** The contaminated-clean gap remains near-zero after both SFT and GRPO. This would falsify Mechanism 1.
2. **SFT Generalizes:** SFT shows a positive gap on *both* contaminated and uncontaminated benchmarks, matching GRPO. This would falsify Mechanism 2's distinction.
3. **Scale Inversion for GRPO:** Larger GRPO models show a *larger* contamination-gap difference (Eq. 1) than smaller ones. This would falsify Mechanism 3's claim about GRPO diluting overestimation with scale.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replicate Base Findings:** Train one small model (e.g., Qwen2.5‑0.5B) through the full pipeline (clean vs. contaminated) and reproduce Figure 3 & 4. Confirm the gap closes after pre‑training and re‑opens after SFT.
2. **Test Uncontaminated Generalization:** For the same models, plot the performance difference on GSM8K vs. GSMPlus (analogous to Figure 5). Verify SFT's gap is only on GSM8K, while GRPO's is on both. This is the core test of Mechanism 2.
3. **Scale Variation Check:** Train both the 1B and 4B variant of one model family (Gemma3). Calculate the `contamination-gap difference` (Eq. 1) for SFT and GRPO. Verify it increases with scale for SFT and decreases for GRPO, as in Figure 6.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do different contamination patterns (e.g., multi-pass, paraphrased, or distributed throughout pre-training) affect the resurrection of contamination gaps during post-training with SFT and GRPO?
- **Basis in paper:** From the limitations and future work: "Real-world leakage can be multi-pass, paraphrased, or distributed throughout pre-training, and its effects may differ."
- **Why unresolved:** The study isolated a single "5-copy, late-injection" contamination scenario; real-world leakage varies in frequency, form, and placement, potentially altering how post-training exploits or mitigates contamination.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments injecting contamination in varied ways (e.g., at different pre-training stages, with paraphrased examples, or uniformly distributed) and compare post-training performance on contaminated vs. uncontaminated benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do human-annotated preference signals in RLHF (as opposed to synthetic rule-based rewards) change the contamination dynamics observed with GRPO, particularly regarding generalization to uncontaminated tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** "Finally, our RL setting uses synthetic rule-based rewards; human-annotated preference signals might have different effects."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper used GRPO with synthetic rewards for practicality, but real-world RLHF often relies on human preferences, which may introduce different biases or generalization properties for contaminated models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the experimental pipeline with RLHF using human preference data or high-fidelity proxies, measuring contamination gaps on both contaminated and uncontaminated benchmarks, and compare to the synthetic reward results.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do larger model scales (beyond 4B parameters) and different architectures (beyond Qwen2.5 and Gemma3) influence the interaction between data contamination and post-training methods like SFT and GRPO?
- **Basis in paper:** "we restrict model sizes to 4B parameters and focus on two open-weights families... larger proprietary models, different architectures, or alternative RLHF algorithms could behave differently."
- **Why unresolved:** The findings are limited to small models; scale was shown to amplify tendencies (e.g., larger SFT models memorize more), but this may not hold for much larger or architecturally diverse models, especially proprietary ones.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend experiments to larger models (e.g., 10B+ parameters) and varied architectures (e.g., decoder-only, encoder-decoder, or MoE models), evaluating contamination effects after pre-training and post-training.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What mechanistic factors explain why GRPO-based post-training leads to generalization from contaminated data to uncontaminated benchmarks, while SFT does not?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred from results: GRPO improved performance on both contaminated (GSM8K/MBPP) and uncontaminated (GSMPlus/HumanEval) benchmarks, whereas SFT gains were confined to contaminated tasks, indicating differing utilization of leaked information.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies this pattern but does not investigate underlying causes—whether due to GRPO's reward structure, exploration benefits, optimization dynamics, or inherent differences between RL and SFT.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform ablations on GRPO components (e.g., reward function terms, group size), analyze internal representations (e.g., probing for generalized reasoning patterns), or compare with other RL algorithms to isolate factors driving generalization versus memorization.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter specifics (SFT epochs, GRPO KL coefficient, learning rates) are unreported; these could significantly affect resurrection magnitude.
- Benchmark analog validity: assuming GSMPlus/HumanEval are uncontaminated proxies for GSM8K/MBPP; differences in difficulty or style may confound "generalization" signals.
- Ecological realism: injecting 5× exact test items into the first 2B tokens is an extreme contamination scenario; real-world leakage may be noisier and distributed.
