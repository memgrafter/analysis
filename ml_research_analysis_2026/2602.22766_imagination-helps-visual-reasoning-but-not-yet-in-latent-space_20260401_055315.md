# Imagination Helps Visual Reasoning, But Not Yet in Latent Space

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22766  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22766  
- **Reference count:** 13  
- **Primary result:** CapImagine replaces latent reasoning with explicit text-space imagination, improving accuracy by 3.44% on HR-Bench, 2.6% on V*, and 6.1% on TableVQA over Monet.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates latent visual reasoning (LVR) in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) using causal mediation analysis. The authors find that latent tokens—special continuous representations meant to encapsulate visual information—exhibit high similarity across different inputs and exert minimal causal influence on final answers. Interventions on these tokens lead to negligible performance changes, and probing confirms they encode little usable visual data. Consequently, they propose CapImagine, which converts visual manipulations into textual descriptions (e.g., captions of auxiliary images) to replace latent tokens. This shift to text-space imagination establishes a stronger causal chain, significantly outperforming latent-based baselines on multiple vision-centric benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The study first conducts a causal analysis of existing latent reasoning models (Monet, LVR, Mirage). It treats latent tokens \(Z\) as mediators between input \(X\) (image + question) and answer \(Y\). Key analyses include: (1) measuring cosine similarity of latent tokens across inputs to test \(P(Z|\text{do}(x))\); (2) applying interventions on \(Z\) (e.g., adding noise, replacing with constants) to test \(P(Y|\text{do}(z))\); and (3) probing whether latent tokens alone can answer derived visual questions. Results show latent tokens are homogeneous and causally disconnected.

To address this, the authors construct CapImagine by transforming the original Monet-SFT-125K dataset. For each auxiliary image in a reasoning chain, they generate a textual description using Qwen3-VL-4B (e.g., describing visual changes after a "zoom-in" or "draw" operation). The original latent segment is replaced with the caption wrapped in a special `<think_image>` tag. An automatic filtering step with Qwen3-VL-4B removes ambiguous or inconsistent examples, yielding ~17k high-quality chains. Finally, Qwen2.5-VL-7B is fine-tuned on this text-imagination data using supervised chain-of-thought.

## Key Results
- Latent tokens across different inputs show high cosine similarity (>0.9), indicating a collapse into nearly uniform representations.
- Interventions on latent tokens (noise, constant, zero) cause at most ~1% performance drop on benchmarks like V* and HR-Bench; in some cases, performance improves slightly.
- CapImagine achieves substantial gains over Monet: +3.44% on HR-Bench, +2.6% on V*, +6.1% on TableVQA.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Latent tokens fail to encode input-specific visual semantics, showing high homogeneity across different inputs.
- **Mechanism:** During autoregressive generation, the LLM backbone applies progressively smaller modifications to latent states, causing them to degenerate into uniform representations irrespective of the input image or question.
- **Break condition:** If latent tokens exhibit low similarity across varied inputs, the X→Z causal chain is intact.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Latent tokens exert minimal causal influence on the final answer; the model often bypasses them.
- **Mechanism:** The model relies on shortcuts (e.g., text-based heuristics) rather than processing latent tokens; thus, even drastic interventions on Z yield negligible performance changes.
- **Break condition:** Significant degradation (e.g., >10%) when latent tokens are zeroed or replaced.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Explicit text-space imagination (CapImagine) establishes a stronger causal chain because textual descriptions force the model to verbalize visual changes, preserving semantics and integrating with textual reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Convert auxiliary image manipulations into captions, turning latent abstraction into concrete textual tokens that the LLM can attend to and reason over directly.
- **Break condition:** If text descriptions become too ambiguous or noisy, performance would regress toward latent methods.

  *Why needed:* The proposed solution shifts the imagination modality; grasping this distinction explains CapImagine’s design.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input encoder (image + question) → LLM backbone → Latent token generator (special tokens) → Intervention module (modifies Z) → Probing module → CapImagine rewriter (converts manipulation images to captions) → SFT on text-only traces.

- **Critical path:**  
  For causal analysis: Input X → generate Z → measure Z similarity → intervene on Z → measure Y change.  
  For CapImagine: Original interleaved data with auxiliary images → caption generation → reasoning chain refinement → SFT on text-only traces.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  Latent reasoning: compact, higher information capacity but prone to collapse and untestable causality. Text-space: explicit, causally transparent but longer sequences and reliance on caption quality.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  High inter-instance cosine similarity (>0.9) in latent tokens; <1% performance drop under Z interventions; probing accuracy near random; CapImagine: rewritten captions conflicting with original questions.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Compute inter-instance and intra-instance cosine similarity of latent tokens from Monet/LVR/Mirage across 100 instances from V*, HR-Bench, etc.  
  2. Apply do(Z) interventions (constant, Gaussian noise, zero) on Monet and Mirage; compute accuracy deltas on target benchmarks.  
  3. Conduct probing by feeding latent tokens alone to the model for derived multiple-choice VQA; compare against text-only and image-grounded baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
What fundamental architectural or training mechanisms in current MLLMs cause latent tokens to exhibit high homogeneity and progressive collapse during autoregressive generation?

**Open Question 2**  
Given the demonstrated "Input-Latent Disconnect" and "Latent-Answer Disconnect," what specific modifications to the latent reasoning paradigm could establish a stronger causal pathway where latent tokens meaningfully mediate between input and answer?

**Open Question 3**  
Why does explicit text-space imagination (CapImagine) exhibit a stronger causal effect and better performance than latent-space imagination, and what are the precise linguistic or representational properties of text that enable this?

**Open Question 4**  
Do the failures observed in latent visual reasoning generalize to all current MLLM architectures and training paradigms, or are they specific to the particular models (Monet, LVR, Mirage) and datasets studied?

**Open Question 5**  
Can a hybrid approach that combines the representational capacity of latent space with the causal transparency and grounding of text-space imagination outperform pure text-space methods like CapImagine, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial or quantitative reasoning?

## Limitations
- Exact data filtering criteria (prompt, scoring threshold) for CapImagine are not provided.
- Training hyperparameters (learning rate, optimizer, early stopping details) are missing.
- Intervention specifics for latent tokens (tensor location, noise variance) and probing architecture remain vague.
- Findings are based on a few Monet/Mirage models; broader generalizability to other MLLM architectures is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Latent token homogeneity: Medium
- Minimal causal effect of latent tokens: Medium
- CapImagine superiority: Medium
