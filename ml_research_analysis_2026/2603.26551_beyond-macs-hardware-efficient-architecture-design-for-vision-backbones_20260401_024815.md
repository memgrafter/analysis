# Beyond MACs: Hardware Efficient Architecture Design for Vision Backbones
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26551
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26551
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** LowFormer matches SOTA accuracy (83.6% ImageNet top-1) with 3× higher GPU throughput and 36% lower edge latency versus FastViT-SA36; edge variants improve efficiency further.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the reliance on Multiply-Accumulate operations (MACs) as the primary metric for neural network efficiency, demonstrating that execution time varies dramatically across hardware for the same MAC count due to memory access patterns and parallelization capabilities. Through systematic hardware-aware experiments on convolution blocks and attention mechanisms, the authors derive design principles that prioritize actual latency and throughput. These insights culminate in LowFormer, a vision backbone family that combines a macro-level layout concentrating computation in low-resolution stages with a novel lightweight attention module, Lowtention. LowFormer achieves state-of-the-art speed-accuracy trade-offs on ImageNet and downstream tasks like detection and segmentation, highlighting the necessity of hardware-centric architecture design.

## Method Summary
The methodology comprises two phases: empirical hardware analysis and guided architecture design. First, the authors conduct controlled experiments measuring latency/throughput of toy models with equal MACs but varying designs (e.g., depthwise vs. standard convolutions, fused vs. unfused MBConv, resolution adjustments) across edge (Jetson TX2) and desktop (GPU) hardware. This reveals that MACs poorly predict real-world performance and identifies efficient patterns: ungrouped convolutions often outperform depthwise, fused MBConv is faster for channels ≤256, and lower-resolution operations yield higher throughput. Second, these insights inform LowFormer’s macro design (minimal early-stage layers, fused MBConv in high-resolution stages, concentrated late-stage computation) and micro design (Lowtention, which downsamples resolution and compresses channels before scaled dot-product attention). Models are trained on ImageNet-1K and evaluated on detection, segmentation, retrieval, and tracking benchmarks, with efficiency measured via throughput on A40/Titan RTX and latency on TX2/iPhone13/Pi5.

## Key Results
- LowFormer-B3 matches FastViT-SA36’s 83.6% ImageNet top-1 accuracy while achieving 3× higher A40 throughput and 36% lower Jetson TX2 latency.
- Edge-optimized LowFormer-E2 matches FastViT-T12 latency with +2.5% higher accuracy.
- In object detection (RetinaNet), LowFormer-B0 outperforms FAT-B0 by +1.0 AP with 93% higher throughput.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MAC count is an unreliable predictor of execution time (latency/throughput) across hardware because it ignores memory access costs and parallelism.
- **Mechanism:** The paper measures latency of toy models with identical MAC counts but different architectural choices (e.g., depthwise vs. ungrouped convolutions). It demonstrates that ungrouped convolutions achieve higher throughput and lower latency on some devices (Jetson TX2, ARM CPU) despite having 10× more MACs, due to better utilization of memory bandwidth and parallel compute units.
- **Core assumption:** Hardware efficiency depends on device-specific factors (memory hierarchy, core count, parallelization capability), not just arithmetic intensity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States that "execution time varies significantly across hardware... due to factors like memory access and parallelism."
  - [section 3.1] Table 1 and analysis showing ungrouped convolutions outperform depthwise in MACs/execution time ratio.
  - [corpus] Weak direct anchor; related work (e.g., CPUBone) echoes hardware-awareness but does not cite this paper.
- **Break condition:** If a hardware platform exhibits uniform memory access latency and perfect parallelization ( idealized ), MACs might correlate perfectly with latency.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Downsampling resolution and compressing channels before Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDA) in MHSA dramatically improves hardware efficiency with minimal accuracy loss.
- **Mechanism:** Lowtention applies two depthwise convolutions (down/up-sample) around SDA and halves channel dimension during Q/K/V projection. Experiments (Table 3) show combined "conv+low+chcompr." reduces latency by up to 98% on edge devices at 64×64 resolution versus vanilla MHSA, while ImageNet accuracy is maintained or improved (Table 8).
- **Core assumption:** Attention’s quadratic cost in resolution and linear in channels dominates latency; reducing either dimension yields near-linear speedups without harming representational capacity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] Table 3 latency reductions across devices and resolutions.
  - [section 4.1] Figure 5 design of Lowtention; [section 5.2] Table 8 ablation showing no accuracy drop when removing downsampling/channel compression.
  - [corpus] Not directly anchored; EfficientViT and similar works also reduce MHSA cost but via different means.
- **Break condition:** If the task requires fine-grained, high-resolution attention maps (e.g., dense segmentation), downsampling may harm accuracy beyond recoverable limits.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Concentrating computation in late, low-resolution stages using fused MBConv (early) and unfused MBConv (late), with minimal early-stage layers, optimizes hardware efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** Insights from Sections 3.2 and 3.3: fused MBConv (conv + pwconv) is faster than unfused (pwconv + dwconv + pwconv) when channels ≤256 (early stages). Lower-resolution convolutions (high channel, low spatial) achieve higher throughput than high-resolution ones (low channel, high spatial) for same MAC count. LowFormer thus places most layers in Stage 3/4 (14×14/7×7) and uses fused MBConv in Stages 0-2.
- **Core assumption:** Memory bandwidth pressure and parallelization efficiency favor operations with higher arithmetic density (more FLOPs/byte) and fewer sequential layers; early high-resolution layers are memory-bound.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] Figure 3: fused MBConv faster for channels ≤256 across resolutions.
  - [section 3.3] Table 2: low-resolution models have 1.8–3.3× higher GPU throughput than high-resolution ones at equal MACs.
  - [section 4.2] Text: "most computation is concentrated in the last two stages."
  - [corpus] ShuffleNetV2 and FasterNet also address grouped convolution inefficiency but do not propose this specific macro pattern.
- **Break condition:** If the backbone must extract strong low-level features (e.g., texture for fine-grained classification), reducing early-stage layers may harm accuracy beyond compensable gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Hardware-aware architecture design vs. metric-driven (MACs) design.
  - *Why needed here:* The paper’s core premise is that MAC optimization misleads; designers must consider actual device metrics (latency, throughput, power) and hardware traits (memory bandwidth, core count).
  - *Quick check question:* Given two models with equal MACs but one uses depthwise convolutions and the other uses standard convolutions, which is likely faster on a Jetson TX2 and why?

- **Concept:** Attention efficiency via dimension reduction.
  - *Why needed here:* MHSA’s quadratic cost in resolution makes it prohibitive on edge devices; Lowtention’s dual reduction (resolution + channels) is presented as a generalizable pattern.
  - *Quick check question:* How does halving both resolution and channels before SDA affect theoretical FLOPs and empirical latency on an ARM CPU?

- **Concept:** Macro-micro co-optimization for vision backbones.
  - *Why needed here:* LowFormer’s gains come from coordinating macro_layout (stage-wise depth/channel distribution) with micro_blocks (fused MBConv, Lowtention). Isolating either yields suboptimal results (Table 8 ablations).
  - *Quick check question:* Why might removing Lowtention but keeping the macro design still hurt efficiency on high-resolution downstream tasks (e.g., detection at 512×512)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Stages 0-2 (56×56→14×14, channels 16-128) -> Stages 3-4 (14×14→7×7, channels 128-512, with Lowtention) -> GlobalAvgPool -> Linear classifier.
- **Component map:**
  - Input (224×224) -> Stem (conv3×3, stride2) -> Stage0 (optional conv) -> Stage1 (fused MBConv) -> Stage2 (fused MBConv, resolution 56×56) -> Stage3 (unfused MBConv + Lowtention with 2× downsample) -> Stage4 (unfused MBConv + Lowtention) -> GlobalAvgPool -> Classifier.
  - Edge variants (E1-E3): Stages 3-4 use only unfused MBConv blocks (no Lowtention, no MLP), reduced depth (2 layers).
- **Critical path:** Early stages use minimal fused MBConv blocks to quickly reduce resolution; most computation (unfused MBConv + Lowtention) occurs in Stages 3-4 at 14×14 and 7×7 resolutions.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Fusion vs. accuracy:** Fusing MBConv reduces latency; paper finds accuracy improves or is maintained (Table 8), possibly due to reduced fragmentation.
  - **Attention presence:** Lowtention adds parameters but reduces effective resolution/channels for SDA. Removing it (edge E1/E2) hurts accuracy but improves latency.
  - **Depth vs. width:** Reducing depth (E1/E2) improves latency more than reducing width; accuracy drop is minimal (0.4-0.8% Table 9).
  - **Resolution scaling:** Higher input resolution improves accuracy but latency increases super-linearly; LowFormer’s late-stage concentration scales better to resolution than competitors.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Accuracy collapse:** Removing both downsampling and channel compression in Lowtention (vanilla MHSA) hurts accuracy; either reduction alone suffices for capacity, both needed for efficiency.
  - **Edge variant over-pruning:** Removing MLP and attention (E1/E2) yields >2× speedup vs. B1.5 but accuracy drops ~2% (Table 9); not suitable for accuracy-critical tasks.
  - **Channel threshold violation:** Using fused MBConv when channels >256 (e.g., Stage4 of B3) hurts latency; ensure unfused MBConv in late stages.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Table 1: Benchmark depthwise vs. ungrouped convolutions with equal MACs on target hardware (e.g., TX2). Expect ungrouped to be faster on memory-bound devices.
  2. MBConv fusion sweep: For channels 64–512 and resolutions 14–112, measure fused vs. unfused latency. Confirm fused wins only for channels ≤256 (Figure 3).
  3. MHSA resolution adaptation: Test vanilla MHSA vs. Lowtention on hardware at resolutions 16×16–64×64. Expect >90% latency reduction at 64×64 (Table 3). Validate accuracy on a toy task (e.g., CIFAR-10 with small MHSA blocks) does not drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Do LowFormer's hardware-efficiency gains generalize to architectures beyond the tested devices (e.g., TPUs, FPGAs, next-gen GPUs)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors state that "determining the extent to which a model’s architecture influences hardware efficiency... is complex and heavily dependent on hardware-specific implementation details" yet evaluate only five devices.
- Why unresolved: The paper’s conclusions rely on a narrow hardware set; untested platforms may exhibit different memory/parallelism characteristics, invalidating the design principles.
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmark LowFormer and SOTA baselines across a diverse hardware suite (e.g., Google TPU, Intel FPGA, AMD GPU, RISC-V CPU) to measure if speedups persist universally.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What exact channel dimension and resolution thresholds determine when fused MBConv becomes more efficient than unfused MBConv across hardware?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Figure 3 visualizes regions of efficiency but provides only qualitative thresholds (e.g., “channel dimension <256”); precise crossover points are not quantified.
- Why unresolved: Design decisions need exact break-even values; current heuristics may not hold for all hardware due to varying memory bandwidth and parallelization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct a fine-grained sweep over channel counts (32–512) and resolutions (14×14–224×224) on multiple devices, fit a response surface to identify universal and hardware-specific thresholds.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the marginal contributions of resolution reduction versus channel compression in Lowtention, and how should their balance vary with input resolution?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Table 3 tests both optimizations jointly and separately but does not isolate their effects or interactions on accuracy and latency across resolutions.
- Why unresolved: Lowtention always combines both; the trade-off space (e.g., is one more critical at low vs. high resolutions?) remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablate four variants (baseline, resolution-only, channel-only, both) across input resolutions (224–1024 pixels) on edge and GPU devices; measure accuracy and latency to model optimal balance.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Which hardware specifications (e.g., core count, memory bandwidth) optimally predict the inclusion and ratio of attention/MLP modules in vision backbones?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Edge variants (E1–E3) remove attention and MLP based on intuition about edge GPU parallelism but provide no quantitative correlation with hardware specs from Table 17.
- Why unresolved: Design rules for edge GPUs are qualitative; a predictive model linking hardware metrics to architecture choices is absent.
- What evidence would resolve it: Gather hardware specs (cores, bandwidth, FLOPs) for 20+ devices; for each, train/search models with varying attention/MLP proportions; fit a regression to predict optimal ratios from hardware parameters.

## Limitations
- **Hardware scope:** Evaluations limited to 5 devices (Titan RTX, A40, Jetson TX2, Pi5, iPhone 13); newer architectures (Ampere/Ada, NPUs) not tested.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Missing training details (weight decay, optimizer epsilon, multi‑scale range) and incomplete specs for edge variants (E1–E3).
- **Task boundaries:** Standard detection/segmentation only; fine-grained high-resolution tasks (>1024 px) unexplored; Lowtention’s aggressive reductions may hurt accuracy.

## Confidence
- **MACs unreliable predictor of latency:** **Medium** – Empirical evidence across few devices is solid but lacks universal derivation.
- **Lowtention efficiency (resolution/channel reduction):** **High** – Consistent >90% latency cuts on multiple platforms, ablations show no ImageNet drop; >64 px resolution boundary not fully tested.
- **Macro design (fused MBConv early, late unfused, computation concentration):** **High** – Controlled experiments confirm fused MBConv benefit and resolution advantage; edge variants slightly weaker but still positive.
- **Overall SOTA speed‑accuracy trade‑off:** **Medium** – Competitive on selected benchmarks but not exhaustive; newer models may close the gap.
