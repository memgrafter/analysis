# AD$^2$: Analysis and Detection of Adversarial Threats in Visual Perception for End-to-End Autonomous Driving Systems
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10160
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10160
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AD² achieves 100% test accuracy in detecting adversarial attacks on end-to-end autonomous driving systems, with a 20× reduction in parameters and 1.6× faster inference than CyberDet.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the adversarial robustness of end-to-end autonomous driving (AD) systems under black-box attacks targeting visual perception. Three attacks—Poltergeist (acoustic-wave-induced blur), SNAL (digital ghost objects), and ESIA (electromagnetic distortion)—are tested on Transfuser and Interfuser agents within the CARLA simulator. Results show driving scores drop up to 99%, exposing severe vulnerabilities. To mitigate these threats, the authors propose AD², a lightweight detector that enforces spatial‑temporal consistency across multi‑camera streams using transformer modules. AD² attains 100% detection accuracy on unseen test data while reducing parameters by 20× and improving inference speed by 1.6× over the CyberDet baseline. The work both highlights critical security gaps and offers a practical, efficient detection solution.

## Method Summary
The study comprises two phases: attack evaluation and detector design. The authors simulate three black‑box attacks in CARLA: Poltergeist manipulates camera stabilization via acoustic waves to create motion blur; SNAL adds ε‑bounded color patches that project ghost objects into images; ESIA injects electromagnetic interference to produce colored strip artifacts. These are applied at various intervals and severities while the end‑to‑end agents (Transfuser, Interfuser) execute closed‑loop driving tasks (Longest6 routes). Performance metrics (driving score, infraction penalty, route completion, lane deviation) suffer drastic declines, confirming attack potency. In response, AD² processes consecutive frames from three overlapping cameras. A shared ResNet backbone encodes each image to a 64‑dimensional feature vector. A spatial transformer then enforces cross‑camera coherence, followed by a temporal transformer checking frame‑to‑frame consistency. The combined embeddings are classified by an MLP into four categories (benign, Poltergeist, SNAL, ESIA). Trained on RouteScenario19 and tested on RouteScenario0, AD² achieves near‑perfect detection with minimal computational overhead.

## Key Results
- Poltergeist, SNAL, and ESIA reduce driving scores by up to 99% on both Transfuser and Interfuser.
- AD² achieves 100% test accuracy in identifying all three attack types.
- AD² uses 20× fewer parameters and runs 1.6× faster than the CyberDet baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Poltergeist (Acoustic Blur)**
- **Claim:** Acoustic wave‑induced blur disrupts feature extraction in the perception backbone, leading to erroneous control signals.
- **Mechanism:** The attack manipulates camera image stabilizer inertial sensors, producing motion‑blurred frames. Convolutional backbones rely on high‑frequency edges and textures; blur suppresses these, degrading latent representations fed to the fusion transformer and waypoint predictor.
- **Core assumption:** The backbone lacks deblurring invariance; blurred inputs yield out‑of‑distribution feature patterns.
- **Break condition:** If the system incorporates a robust deblurring preprocessor or is trained with synthetic blur augmentation, the blur may not propagate as a disruptive perturbation.

**Mechanism 2: SNAL (Digital Ghost Objects)**
- **Claim:** Bounded digital perturbations inject "ghost objects" that create false positive detections in latent space, causing unsafe trajectories.
- **Mechanism:** SNAL adds carefully crafted, ε‑bounded color‑manipulated patches that simulate non‑existent objects. These patches are optimized to activate neuron firing patterns associated with target classes in the perception backbone, making the end‑to‑end agent interpret them as real scene elements.
- **Core assumption:** The perception module is vulnerable to adversarial examples within human‑imperceptible ε‑bounds; latent class embeddings are linearly separable and attackable.
- **Break condition:** Adversarial training against such patches or a certified object detector that rejects atypical activations could mitigate the attack.

**Mechanism 3: ESIA (Electromagnetic Interference)**
- **Claim:** Electromagnetic interference introduces structured colored strip artifacts that corrupt spatial feature alignment across the multi‑camera rig, violating the spatial transformer’s consistency assumptions.
- **Mechanism:** EM injection distorts analog‑to‑digital conversion in camera circuits, generating fixed‑pattern colored bands. These introduce high‑frequency, non‑semantic edges. In multi‑camera setups, the spatial transformer expects correlated features from overlapping regions; stripes cause misaligned features across views, leading the transformer to attend to spurious correlations.
- **Core assumption:** The spatial transformer is sensitive to feature misalignment across camera views and lacks robustness to coherent within‑view but inconsistent across‑view noise.
- **Break condition:** Per‑camera artifact detection/masking before fusion or training the spatial transformer with synthetic EM distortions could break the attack.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Spatial‑Temporal Consistency in Multi‑Modal Streams**
- Why needed here: AD²’s detection core relies on the invariant that adjacent cameras (spatial) and consecutive frames (temporal) should produce coherent feature representations; attacks violate one or both.
- Quick check: Why would a blur attack (Poltergeist) be detectable via temporal consistency but potentially evade pure spatial checks between cameras at a single timestep?

**Concept: End‑to‑End Differentiable Driving Pipelines**
- Why needed: Understanding that control outputs (steer, throttle, brake) are direct functions of raw sensor inputs via a monolithic network (e.g., Transfuser) is key; there is no isolated "object detector" output to monitor; attack impact is latent.
- Quick check: How does closed‑loop evaluation differ from open‑loop evaluation in revealing adversarial vulnerabilities, according to the paper’s motivation?

**Concept: Black‑Box Threat Modeling for AD Systems**
- Why needed: The attacks assume no access to model parameters or gradients; this shapes AD²’s design—it must detect attacks from input/output behavior alone, not internal gradients.
- Quick check: What makes a "black‑box" attack on an end‑to‑end AD agent more realistic than a white‑box attack for assessing real‑world security?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Input Handler (x_{t‑1}^C, x_t^C) → Per‑camera Feature Backbone (3× ResNet, 2 residual blocks + linear layer → v_t ∈ R^64) → Spatial Transformer (single‑layer ViT, 4 heads, positional encoding) → Temporal Transformer (single‑layer ViT, 4 heads) → Classification Head (MLP, 4‑class logits)

Critical path: Raw multi‑camera images are encoded to feature vectors; the spatial transformer ensures cross‑camera coherence, then the temporal transformer checks cross‑time coherence; the concatenated embeddings are classified. Both transformers are sequential; failure in either propagates.

Design tradeoffs:
- Speed vs. Context: Using only two timesteps and one transformer layer per module minimizes latency (0.16 ms) but limits long‑term temporal modeling.
- Generality vs. Specificity: Training a 4‑class detector improves accuracy on known attacks but may fail against novel ones; a binary OOD detector would be more general.
- Multi‑camera dependency: Requires at least two cameras with overlapping fields of view; does not support monocular setups.

Failure signatures:
- High false positive rate on clean data when normal driving phenomena (sudden lighting, motion blur) mimic attack artifacts.
- Low true positive rate for SNAL if ε is very small, making perturbations subtler than natural variations.
- Complete failure on attacks that preserve both spatial and temporal consistency (e.g., uniform color tint applied continuously).

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline Attack Impact Replication: Run each attack (Poltergeist with d=1, SNAL with ε=8 and d=1, ESIA at high severity) on Transfuser and Interfuser in CARLA’s Longest6 route. Record driving score, infraction penalty, route completion, and lane deviation to confirm >90% performance drop.
2. AD² vs. Baselines on Test Set: Evaluate AD², CyberDet, KPCA, and LAP4 on a held‑out CARLA route (RouteScenario0). Report per‑class TPR, FPR, and AUC; verify AD² >99% overall accuracy, especially on SNAL/ESIA where others degrade.
3. Ablation – Consistency Modules: Disable the spatial transformer (use only temporal) and vice versa; measure accuracy drop to validate the joint design. Also test AD² with only a single camera input to demonstrate necessity of multi‑camera spatial checks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Can AD² maintain high detection accuracy against adaptive attackers who know the detector’s spatial‑temporal consistency mechanism and craft perturbations to evade it? *(Basis: Conclusion identifies extending to adaptive strategies as future work, acknowledging current evaluation is non‑adaptive.)*
**Open Question 2:** How well does AD² generalize to other state‑of‑the‑art end‑to‑end autonomous driving agents beyond Transfuser and Interfuser, especially those with different sensor fusion architectures (e.g., LiDAR‑only or multi‑modal without cameras)? *(Basis: Only two agents evaluated; assumptions about transferability remain untested.)*
**Open Question 3:** Does AD²’s detection capability hold in real‑world driving conditions where sensor noise, weather variations, and environmental factors differ from the simulator? *(Basis: Conclusion calls for real‑world assessability; CARLA limitations may affect transfer.)*
**Open Question 4:** Can AD² effectively detect continuous or highly stealthy adversarial attacks where perturbations are minimized to avoid triggering temporal inconsistencies (e.g., extremely low‑magnitude SNAL or targeted ESIA)? *(Basis: Attacks evaluated at fixed strengths; sensitivity curve unknown.)*
**Open Question 5:** How does AD²’s computational efficiency scale when deployed on embedded hardware typical of production autonomous vehicles, given its transformer modules and multi‑camera input processing? *(Basis: Reported 0.16 ms on NVIDIA Quadro RTX 5000, but automotive SoCs differ.)*

## Limitations
- Missing training hyperparameters and precise SNAL/ESIA implementations impede faithful reproduction.
- Evaluation restricted to two simulated end‑to‑end agents; no real‑world or monocular camera testing.
- Perfect 100% detection accuracy raises suspicion of potential overfitting or data split contamination.
