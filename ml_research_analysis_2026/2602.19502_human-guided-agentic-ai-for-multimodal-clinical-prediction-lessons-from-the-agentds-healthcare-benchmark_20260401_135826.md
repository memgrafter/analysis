# Human-Guided Agentic AI for Multimodal Clinical Prediction: Lessons from the AgentDS Healthcare Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19502
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19502
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Human-guided agentic AI achieved top-5/3 placements across three clinical prediction tasks, with multimodal feature engineering providing the largest single gain (+0.041 Macro-F1).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the role of human expertise in augmenting agentic AI systems for multimodal clinical prediction tasks. Facing the limitations of fully automated pipelines on heterogeneous data (clinical notes, PDF receipts, vital signs time-series), the authors implemented a workflow where domain experts intervened at critical junctures: feature engineering, model/ensemble selection, and validation design. On the AgentDS Healthcare benchmark, this human-guided approach yielded competitive leaderboard rankings (3rd–6th place) and demonstrated significant performance compounding through systematic ablation. The core finding is that human-directed multimodal feature extraction offers the largest marginal gain, underscoring the enduring value of clinical knowledge in structuring predictive signals from raw, unstructured data.

## Method Summary
The method is a hybrid human-agentic workflow. An automated agentic system handled baseline data loading, preprocessing, and hyperparameter tuning via Optuna for single models. Human analysts then intervened in three key areas: (1) **Multimodal feature engineering**, applying task-specific strategies like trigram TF-IDF for discharge summaries, regex-based CPT code extraction from PDFs, and statistical aggregation of vital sign sequences; (2) **Ensemble design**, selecting diverse base models and combination strategies (stacking vs. weighted averaging) based on dataset size to prevent overfitting; (3) **Clinically informed validation**, choosing appropriate metrics (Macro-F1, MAE) and stratified cross-validation schemes aligned with task imbalances. The process was iterative, with error analysis between rounds to refine features and models.

## Key Results
- **30-day readmission prediction**: Macro-F1 = 0.8986 (5th place).
- **3-year ED cost forecasting**: MAE = $465.13 (6th place).
- **Discharge readiness assessment**: Macro-F1 = 0.7939 (3rd place).
- **Ablation impact**: Cumulative human contribution was +0.065 Macro-F1 over automated baselines; domain-informed multimodal feature extraction alone provided the largest single improvement of +0.041 F1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Human-guided multimodal feature extraction
- **Claim**: Human experts engineer clinically meaningful features from heterogeneous unstructured data, capturing signals that generic automated pipelines miss, leading to compounding performance gains.
- **Mechanism**: Domain knowledge directs modality-specific extraction—e.g., trigram TF-IDF for discharge notes to capture phrase-level context, regex for CPT codes in PDFs, and statistical summaries for vital sign trends—rather than relying on uniform methods like unigram TF-IDF.
- **Core assumption**: Clinical prediction tasks involve unstructured data where a single automated strategy fails to extract task-relevant semantics or structures.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states multimodal feature extraction contributed the largest single improvement (+0.041 F1). Section II details task-specific engineering: trigrams for Challenge 1, CPT extraction for Challenge 2, vital statistics for Challenge 3. Ablation (Table II) confirms the gain.

### Mechanism 2: Human-directed model architecture and ensemble design
- **Claim**: Expert selection of diverse base models and ensemble combination strategies (weighted averaging vs. stacking) prevents overfitting on modest clinical dataset sizes (N=1,000–5,000), where automated meta-learners are unstable.
- **Mechanism**: Analysts choose ensemble diversity and combination methods based on sample size constraints, overriding generic hyperparameter search or stacking when N is small to ensure stability.
- **Core assumption**: Small-N healthcare data makes learned meta-learners (stacking) prone to high variance; manual configuration with clinical validity constraints yields more robust performance.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract notes deliberate ensemble diversity outperforms random hyperparameter search. Section II.B describes overriding stacking with fixed weights for Challenge 2 (N=2,000), improving CV MAE stability (±$19.93). Ablation (Section III.B) shows single-model degradations largest on smallest dataset (Challenge 3, N=1,000).

### Mechanism 3: Human-guided validation strategy selection
- **Claim**: Experts align validation (metrics, sampling) with clinical utility, preventing misleading performance estimates common with default automated validation.
- **Mechanism**: Task characteristics (e.g., class imbalance, skewed costs) drive choices like stratified CV and appropriate metrics (Macro-F1, MAE), rather than using default 5-fold without stratification.
- **Core assumption**: Standard default validation can overestimate performance on imbalanced clinical tasks, risking unrealistic deployment expectations.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions clinically informed validation. Section II specifies stratified CV for classification and hold-out + CV for regression. Ablation shows non-stratified CV degraded F1 by -0.005 to -0.007. Test scores closely matched or exceeded CV estimates, indicating proper alignment.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Clinical Domain Feature Engineering**
  - **Why needed**: Raw EHR data rarely contains direct predictive signals; experts must transform age, comorbidities, vitals, and text into hypothesis-driven features (e.g., age×Charlson interactions, mobility keywords) that reflect pathophysiology.
  - **Quick check**: What clinical knowledge would you encode into a "cost-per-visit ratio" feature for readmission prediction, and why might this be more informative than raw prior costs?

- **Concept: Multimodal Strategy Selection**
  - **Why needed**: Different unstructured data types (long clinical notes vs. short daily notes vs. PDF receipts) require distinct extraction methods (TF-IDF, keyword matching, regex); a one-size-fits-all approach fails.
  - **Quick check**: Why did trigram TF-IDF work for discharge summaries but fail for daily progress notes? What alternative did humans choose and why?

- **Concept: Small-N Modeling Constraints**
  - **Why needed**: Healthcare datasets are often modest (N<10,000); deep learning risks overfitting, favoring feature engineering and tree ensembles. Human guidance ensures sample-efficient designs.
  - **Quick check**: Given N=1,000 and 10-day vital sign sequences, why did humans choose statistical aggregation over an LSTM? What would change if N=50,000?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Raw multimodal data (structured EHR tables, clinical notes, PDF receipts, time-series vitals) → domain-guided feature engineering (modality-specific extraction, interaction terms, composites) → feature consolidation (single matrix) → deliberate ensemble design (diverse base models, stacking/weighted averaging) → clinically aligned validation (stratified CV, metric selection) → iterative error analysis.

- **Critical path**: `Raw multimodal data → domain-guided feature engineering → deliberate ensemble design → clinically aligned validation → iterative error analysis`. Highest-leverage interventions occur at feature engineering; model tuning follows.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Interpretability vs. performance: Tree-based ensembles + handcrafted features maintained interpretability (feature importance aligns with clinical factors) while reaching top-5 performance.
  - Generalization vs. optimization: Used consistent workflow across tasks (risked missing task-specific gains) to build transferable methodology.
  - Effort vs. gain: 10–15 hours per challenge invested in feature engineering; ablation shows this yields >4× the gain of hyperparameter tuning.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Stagnant CV improvement after adding features → likely wrong modality strategy (e.g., TF-IDF on short notes).
  - High fold-to-fold variance (e.g., MAE range $435–$478) → ensemble combination unstable; consider fixed weights over stacking on small N.
  - Test score >> CV estimate → validation misalignment (no stratification, data leakage).

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline replication: Run agentic system with default single-model + basic features. Expect CV F1 ~0.85 (Ch1) / MAE ~$467 (Ch2) / F1 ~0.49 (Ch3). Confirm gap to paper.
  2. Human-guided iteration 1: Apply one domain-informed change per modality (trigram TF-IDF, CPT features, vital statistics). Measure delta; expect largest gain from multimodal extraction.
  3. Ensemble design test: Compare stacking vs. weighted averaging on hold-out fold for Challenge 2. Expect weighted averaging more stable on N=2,000.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Q1**: Can transformer-based text models (e.g., BioClinicalBERT) for clinical notes and PDFs outperform manual keyword/TF-IDF feature engineering in multimodal prediction when training sample sizes exceed 5,000? *Basis*: Explicitly noted; study only evaluated manual NLP on N=1,000–5,000. *Unresolved* because sample size constraints prevented testing deep semantic models.

- **Q2**: To what extent do the reported human-AI collaboration gains generalize from synthetic AgentDS data to real-world EHRs with missing values, measurement errors, and data entry inconsistencies? *Basis*: Inferred from synthetic benchmark limitations. *Unresolved* due to lack of testing on noisy, real-world data.

- **Q3**: Does the observed superiority of domain-informed feature engineering over extensive hyperparameter tuning persist in very large EHR datasets (N>50,000), where deep multimodal transformers might capture representations automatically? *Basis*: Inferred from modest sample sizes (N=1,000–5,000). *Unresolved* because large-scale data may shift optimal trade-offs.

- **Q4**: What are the measurable impacts on patient outcomes and hospital operational efficiency when deploying these human-guided readmission or discharge readiness models in a live clinical setting with clinician oversight? *Basis*: Explicitly called for prospective trials. *Unresolved* as no real-world clinical utility evaluation was performed.

- **Q5**: Can automated multimodal feature learning (e.g., cross-modal transformers) match the performance of manual, domain-informed feature engineering while reducing human labor, and what is the minimum sample size required for such automation to be viable? *Basis*: Explicitly stated that multimodal data integration requires task-specific human judgment. *Unresolved* because the study attributes large gains to human extraction, but scalability of automation is unknown.

## Limitations
- Synthetic benchmark data may not reflect real-world EHR complexity and missingness, limiting generalizability.
- Code repository lacks explicit details (e.g., regex patterns, keyword lists), risking reproducibility gaps.
- Human analyst effort is unreported, preventing cost-benefit analysis.
- Ablation assumes independent effects; potential interactions between human interventions remain unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (multimodal feature extraction)**: Medium – Strong within-benchmark ablation but unverified externally.
- **Mechanism 2 (ensemble design)**: Medium – Supported by small-N tests; unknown for larger datasets.
- **Mechanism 3 (validation alignment)**: High – Consistent ablation and CV/test alignment across tasks.
