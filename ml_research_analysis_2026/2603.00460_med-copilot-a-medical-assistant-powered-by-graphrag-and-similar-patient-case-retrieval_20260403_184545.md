# MED-COPILOT: A Medical Assistant Powered by GraphRAG and Similar Patient Case Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00460
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00460
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Combining GraphRAG from WHO/NICE guidelines with hybrid similar-patient retrieval yields substantial gains (ROUGE-L 0.144→0.291; MedQA accuracy 0.846→0.912) over strong baselines.

## Executive Summary
MED-COPILOT addresses LLM hallucinations and poor integration of long medical documents by combining GraphRAG retrieval from authoritative guideline knowledge graphs with hybrid semantic-keyword retrieval from a large similar-patient database. The system constructs graph communities from WHO/NICE guidelines and maintains a SOAP-normalized repository of 36,000 cases derived from MIMIC-IV and Synthea. Evaluated on clinical note completion (MIMIC-IV) and medical QA (MedQA, MMLU-clinical), it consistently outperforms parametric LLM and standard RAG baselines. For example, using GPT-4.1-mini, ROUGE-L improves from 0.144 to 0.291 and MedQA accuracy from 0.846 to 0.912. An interactive HuggingFace Space enables evidence inspection and controllable reasoning.

## Method Summary
The pipeline has two parallel retrieval streams. The GraphRAG engine preprocesses WHO/NICE guidelines: documents are hierarchically segmented into TextUnits, entities (SNOMED-CT, ICD-10, ATC) and relations are extracted to form a knowledge graph, whose communities are summarized and all artifacts embedded with BioClinicalBERT. The similar-patient engine indexes 36,000 SOAP-normalized cases (real MIMIC-IV and synthetic Synthea trajectories). For a query, hybrid similarity scoring combines keyword matching (BM25 on clinical entities) and embedding cosine similarity over SOAP sequences, with a tunable weight λ. Retrieved evidence from both streams is aggregated (preserving provenance) and fed to an LLM (e.g., GPT-4.1-mini) to generate the final response. Benchmark results show significant improvements in note completion and QA tasks.

## Key Results
- ROUGE-L on MIMIC-IV note completion increased from 0.144 (standard RAG) to 0.291 (proposed) with GPT-4.1-mini.
- MedQA accuracy improved from 0.846 to 0.912.
- Ablation studies confirm both GraphRAG and similar-patient retrieval contribute independently, with the combination yielding the strongest gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Graph-structured guideline retrieval reduces hallucination
- **Claim:** Grounding generation in verifiable, relational clinical logic (guideline graphs) reduces LLM hallucinations compared to relying on parametric memory.
- **Mechanism:** Guidelines are segmented into semantically coherent TextUnits and transformed into a knowledge graph with entities (diseases, medications) and relations (indication, contraindication). At query time, graph-conditioned retrieval identifies relevant "communities" (subgraphs) representing multi-step clinical logic. Retrieved nodes/edges with explicit provenance are fed to the LLM, constraining outputs to documented pathways.
- **Core assumption:** Authoritative guideline logic can be effectively abstracted into a graph without losing critical decision constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "...combines guideline-grounded GraphRAG retrieval... to support transparent and evidence-aware clinical reasoning." Section 3.2 describes TextUnit metadata for provenance tracking and explicit encoding of relations/constraints.
- **Break condition:** If graph construction omits key conditional logic (e.g., pregnancy contraindications), retrieved evidence may be incomplete, allowing hallucinations to re‑emerge.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid similar-patient retrieval improves integration of long narratives
- **Claim:** Matching on both discrete clinical signals (keyword) and longitudinal trajectory patterns (semantic) provides context beyond guidelines and improves handling of unstructured clinical narratives.
- **Mechanism:** A repository of SOAP-normalized cases is indexed. Retrieval uses a weighted combination: (1) keyword alignment on clinical entities (diagnoses, interventions) to enforce eligibility constraints, and (2) embedding similarity of longitudinal SOAP sequences to capture progression patterns. This dual signal retrieves analogs sharing both static attributes and dynamic responses.
- **Core assumption:** Clinically meaningful patient similarity can be computed from structured SOAP notes using a hybrid of lexical and embedding-based signals.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions the 36,000-case database; Section 3.3 explains the keyword component (discrete signals) and semantic component (graded trajectory similarity).
- **Break condition:** Over-weighting keywords yields rigid retrieval (missing analogs with different terminology); under-weighting yields semantically similar but clinically irrelevant cases.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy of guideline‑grounded and analogical retrieval
- **Claim:** Combining normative (guideline) and empirical (similar patient) evidence yields superior reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Two retrieval streams run in parallel. GraphRAG returns guideline communities with explicit contraindications and escalation logic. The similar‑patient stream returns ranked cases with highlighted salient attributes. Both evidence sets are aggregated and presented to the LLM, enabling cross‑referencing of normative recommendations with observed outcomes (e.g., "Guideline recommends X, but similar patients often required Y due to comorbidity Z").
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can effectively synthesize evidence from structurally different sources when provenance is explicit.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.3 show combined framework achieves highest ROUGE‑L and QA accuracy. Section 4.4 ablation shows independent improvements and largest combined gain.
- **Break condition:** If one stream is consistently noisy (e.g., poor graph coverage for rare conditions), conflicting signals may mislead the LLM, potentially degrading performance below single‑source retrieval.

## Foundational Learning
### Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG)
- **Why needed here:** The system's core novelty is using a knowledge graph (not text chunks) for guideline retrieval. Understanding graph construction, community detection, and graph‑conditioned search is essential to grasp how relational clinical logic is retrieved.
- **Quick check question:** If a guideline states "For patients with condition A and symptom B, start medication X, but if kidney function <30%, use Y instead," how would this conditional logic be represented in the graph, and how might retrieval differ from a text‑slice RAG?

### SOAP Note Structure and Normalization
- **Why needed here:** The similar-patient database is built from SOAP‑formatted notes. SOAP provides a standardized temporal structure for patient trajectories. Normalization (converting free‑text MIMIC notes to SOAP) is a key preprocessing step that enables consistent similarity scoring.
- **Quick check question:** Why might converting free‑text clinical notes into rigid SOAP sections lose some information, and how could that affect the quality of "similar patient" retrieval?

### Hybrid Similarity Scoring for Patient Retrieval
- **Why needed here:** Patient similarity is not a single metric. The system explicitly combines keyword‑based matching (for strict clinical constraints like diagnosis codes) with semantic embedding similarity (for nuanced trajectory patterns). Understanding this trade‑off is critical to interpreting retrieved cases.
- **Quick check question:** What is a potential failure mode if the system retrieves a patient with high semantic similarity but low keyword alignment (e.g., similar narrative but different primary diagnosis)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Query Interface -> [GraphRAG Retriever, Similar‑Patient Retriever] -> Evidence Aggregator -> LLM Reasoner -> Interactive UI

**Critical path:** A query (free‑text question or SOAP case) triggers parallel retrieval: the GraphRAG engine searches the guideline knowledge graph communities, while the Similar‑Patient engine scores and retrieves top‑k cases using hybrid similarity. The Evidence Aggregator merges these results, applies saliency highlighting, and constructs a prompt for the LLM. The LLM generates the final response, which is displayed in the interactive UI with toggles to inspect evidence.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Guideline coverage vs. graph abstraction: Using WHO/NICE ensures authority but limits scope; graph extraction may simplify nuance.
- Data heterogeneity: Mixing real (MIMIC‑IV) and synthetic (Synthea) cases increases coverage but may cause distribution shifts.
- Retrieval latency vs. evidence breadth: Two parallel retrievals increase latency but provide richer evidence for clinical decision‑support.
- Interpretability vs. automation: Saliency highlighting and source links aid transparency but add UI complexity.

**Failure signatures:**
- GraphRAG failure: Retrieved guideline communities are clinically irrelevant (e.g., wrong disease chapter) due to poor query‑to‑graph alignment; symptom: LLM cites unrelated guidelines.
- Patient retrieval failure: Top‑ranked cases lack critical similarity (e.g., wrong comorbidity profile); symptom: highlighted attributes seem clinically peripheral.
- Synergy failure: Combined evidence is contradictory (e.g., guideline says "use X" but similar patients all failed on X); symptom: LLM output shows confusion or ignores one source.
- Systemic: Low QA accuracy on a specific disease subset indicates inadequate coverage in either knowledge graph or patient repository.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Component validation:** For 50 held‑out MIMIC‑IV notes, run only GraphRAG retrieval and manually check if top‑3 guideline communities logically support the gold‑standard Plan section. Target: >80% applicability.
2. **Similar‑patient retrieval sanity:** For 50 seed cases, retrieve top‑5 and have a clinician blind‑rate clinical similarity (1‑5 scale). Target median ≥4. Log cases where keyword and semantic scores disagree severely (>0.5 difference).
3. **End‑to‑end ablation replication:** Re‑run Table 2 ablation on 100 MedQA questions using fixed prompts, LLM temperatures, and a λ tuned on a held‑out validation set. Verify that each component independently adds lift and that combination superiority holds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can a gold‑standard dataset be constructed to reliably evaluate patient‑level similarity retrieval in clinical decision support?
- **Basis in paper:** "...no gold‑standard dataset currently exists for assessing patient‑level similarity retrieval." (Section 5, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** Absence of a benchmark prevents systematic, comparable evaluation of similar‑patient retrieval components.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and community adoption of a curated dataset with clinician‑validated similarity judgments for specific clinical scenarios.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent does the heterogeneity between MIMIC‑IV (real) and Synthea (synthetic) patient trajectories impact the quality and generalizability of similar‑patient retrieval?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitation states the combined repository has "heterogeneous data distributions that only partially reflect real‑world clinical documentation." (Section 5)
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not isolate or compare retrieval performance using solely real vs. synthetic data, leaving the contribution and potential bias of each source unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies comparing performance when indexed with (a) only MIMIC‑IV, (b) only Synthea, and (c) the mixed set.

**Open Question 3:** Does the abstraction of narrative guideline recommendations into a structured knowledge graph omit critical contextual nuances necessary for safe clinical reasoning?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitation acknowledges the graph "may omit contextual nuances present in full guideline texts." (Section 5)
- **Why unresolved:** The system's reliance on the graph for core logic means any omitted nuance could lead to incomplete or unsafe recommendations, but this risk is not quantified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Expert clinician review comparing graph‑extracted logic against original guideline text for a sample of complex, conditional recommendations, identifying discrepancies.

**Open Question 4:** What are the primary usability and workflow integration barriers when deploying such a dual‑retrieval system in real‑time clinical settings, as opposed to a research prototype?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states the system is a "research prototype intended for decision‑support" and future work includes "exploring deployment in real‑world clinical workflows." (Section 6)
- **Why unresolved:** Performance on offline benchmarks does not address factors like interface latency, interaction design in high‑stress environments, alert fatigue, or alignment with existing EHR workflows.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Formative user studies (e.g., contextual inquiry, simulated encounters) with clinicians to identify workflow friction points and requirements for integrated deployment.

**Open Question 5:** Do improvements on note completion and medical QA benchmarks correlate with improved clinical reasoning or patient outcomes in prospective, simulated, or real clinical decision‑making tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** Evaluation is "limited to note completion and medical QA benchmarks." (Section 5)
- **Why unresolved:** Standard NLP metrics do not directly measure quality of differential diagnosis, risk‑benefit analysis, or final treatment decisions in complex, ambiguous cases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on a held‑out set of realistic, complex clinical vignettes where expert panels produce reference decisions, with metrics assessing alignment with expert reasoning (e.g., checklist of considered factors, safety of final plan) rather than just text overlap or multiple‑choice accuracy.

## Limitations
- Graph community detection/summarization algorithm and parameters are unspecified, risking loss of conditional clinical logic.
- Hybrid similarity scoring details (keyword weighting, embedding nuances, candidate pooling) are vague, hindering tuning and replication.
- Retrieval hyperparameters (top‑k, λ tuning method) and LLM prompt templates are omitted, blocking faithful reproduction.
- Reliance on Synthea synthetic cases may introduce distribution shifts affecting trajectory realism and generalization.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (GraphRAG grounding):** Medium – Performance gains are shown, but the assumption that graphs retain all critical constraints is vulnerable to omitted contraindications; evidence anchors are weak.
- **Mechanism 2 (Hybrid retrieval):** Medium – Supported by ablation, but relies on precise λ balance; break condition (rigid vs. loose retrieval) is plausible; tuning details missing.
- **Mechanism 3 (Synergy):** Medium – Combined results are strong, but synergy mechanism is inferred, not isolated; risk of degradation if one stream is noisy; robustness not quantified.
