# Continual Multimodal Egocentric Activity Recognition via Modality-Aware Novel Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16970
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16970
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MAND improves novel activity detection AUC by up to 10% and reduces FPR95 by 33.9% over state-of-the-art baselines, while also increasing known-class accuracy by up to 2.8% and decreasing forgetting.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses open-world continual learning for multimodal egocentric activity recognition, where a model must incrementally learn new activity classes while simultaneously detecting novel (unseen) activities. Existing approaches rely primarily on the main fused classifier logits for novelty detection, neglecting complementary cues—especially from IMU streams—and suffering from catastrophic forgetting of modality-specific discriminative boundaries. The authors propose MAND, comprising two core components: Modality-aware Adaptive Scoring (MoAS) for inference and Modality-wise Representation Stabilization Training (MoRST) for training. MoAS adaptively weights per-modality logits based on reliability estimated from energy scores normalized by replay buffer statistics, enhancing separability between known and novel samples. MoRST introduces auxiliary heads per modality and distills modality-specific logits on replayed exemplars to preserve discriminability across tasks. Evaluated on the UESTC-MMEA-CL dataset, MAND achieves substantial gains in novelty detection (AUC +10%, FPR95 -33.9%) and known-class accuracy (+2.8%) with reduced forgetting. Ablations confirm the necessity of both components and the superiority of adaptive over fixed-weight fusion.

## Method Summary
MAND employs a multimodal architecture with separate encoders for RGB (BN-Inception, ImageNet-pretrained) and IMU (DeepConvLSTM for gyroscope and accelerometer). Each encoder feeds into a modality-specific classification head (H_m) producing auxiliary logits z_m. The embeddings are fused via a Temporal Binding Network (TBN) and passed to a main classifier C yielding primary logits z_main. During training on a sequence of class-incremental tasks, a replay buffer of size 320 stores exemplars together with their modality logits. The loss combines super‑vised components L_Sup = L_CE(z_main, y) + λ·(1/|M|) Σ L_CE(z_m, y) (λ=0.3) and a modality-specific knowledge distillation term L_KD = Σ ||˜z_m − z_m||²₂ (β=0.08) that enforces consistency with stored logits for replayed samples. At inference, MoAS computes an energy score E_m = −log Σ_c exp(z_m,c) for each modality, normalizes it using replay buffer statistics (mean µ_m, std σ_m) to obtain reliability r_m = −(E_m−µ_m)/σ_m, derives weights α_m = softmax(r_m), and combines logits as z_fused = z_main + Σ_m α_m · z_m. The final novelty score is max_c z_fused,c; thresholding this score determines novelty, otherwise the known class is argmax(z_main). This design simultaneously stabilizes modality discriminability and leverages complementary cues adaptively for open-world detection.

## Key Results
- Novel activity detection: AUC improved by up to 10%, FPR95 reduced by 33.9% compared to state‑of‑the‑art methods.
- Known‑class continual recognition: average accuracy increased by up to 2.8% and forgetting decreased.
- Ablations confirm that both MoAS and MoRST are essential; adaptive weighting substantially outperforms fixed‑weight strategies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Modality‑aware Adaptive Scoring (MoAS)
**Claim:** MoAS improves novelty detection separability by adaptively reweighting modality‑specific logits based on sample‑wise reliability.  
**Mechanism:** For each modality m, energy E_m = −log Σ_c exp(z_m,c) is computed from its logits. Normalizing with replay buffer statistics (mean µ_m, std σ_m) yields reliability r_m = −(E_m − µ_m)/σ_m. A softmax over {r_m} gives weights α_m. The final fused logit vector is z_fused = z_main + Σ_m α_m z_m, and the novelty score s(x) = max(z_fused).  
**Core assumption:** Per‑modality logits retain discriminative signal for known/novel separation, and their energy distributions on the replay buffer reliably indicate reliability for a given sample.  
**Break condition:** If all r_m are nearly equal (e.g., due to MoRST failing to preserve discriminability), α_m ≈ 1/|M|, collapsing to uniform fusion and losing adaptive benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Modality‑wise Representation Stabilization Training (MoRST)
**Claim:** MoRST mitigates catastrophic forgetting of modality‑specific decision boundaries via auxiliary heads and modality‑specific logit distillation on replayed exemplars.  
**Mechanism:** Each modality encoder F_m has a dedicated head H_m producing logits z_m. During training, the loss includes cross‑entropy on z_m alongside the main classifier loss. The replay buffer stores past modality logits ˜z_m for each exemplar; a distillation loss L_KD = Σ ||˜z_m − z_m||²₂ forces each modality’s output distribution to remain stable over time.  
**Core assumption:** Modality‑specific classifiers can be trained to preserve class‑discriminative boundaries independently, and matching historical modality logits (not just fused logits) retains useful evidence for reliability estimation.  
**Break condition:** If the replay buffer is too small or unrepresentative, stored ˜z_m become poor targets, degrading L_KD. Also, if modalities are highly correlated, separate stabilization may be redundant or conflicting.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy between MoRST and MoAS
**Claim:** The synergy between MoRST and MoAS enables robust open‑world learning: MoRST stabilizes modality‑specific evidence over time, which MoAS then leverages for reliable adaptive weighting.  
**Mechanism:** MoRST’s distillation ensures modality logits z_m stay calibrated and discriminative across tasks. This stability makes their energy scores E_m meaningful for reliability r_m. Without MoRST, forgetting degrades z_m, making r_m noisy and causing MoAS to weight inaccurately. Together, they maintain high separability between known and novel samples via consistent modality cues.  
**Core assumption:** Modality forgetting is non‑uniform (e.g., IMU degrades faster than RGB); stabilizing all modalities’ outputs is necessary for adaptive scoring to work continually.  
**Break condition:** If one modality becomes permanently uninformative (e.g., sensor failure), MoAS may over‑rely on the others, but MoRST’s stabilization of that modality becomes moot. Excessive memory overhead may also force a small buffer, weakening both components.

## Foundational Learning

### Energy‑based Out‑of‑Distribution (OOD) Detection
**Why needed:** MoAS uses negative log‑sum‑exp of modality logits (energy) to estimate sample reliability. Understanding energy’s relationship to model uncertainty is crucial for effective weighting.  
**Quick check:** Why is energy often better calibrated than softmax confidence for OOD detection, especially under distribution shift?

### Replay Buffers in Class‑Incremental Learning
**Why needed:** MoRST stores exemplars together with modality‑specific logits to enable fine‑grained distillation. Standard replay buffers store raw data or fused logits; storing per‑modality logits is a memory‑accuracy trade‑off.  
**Quick check:** What are the trade‑offs between storing modality logits (small, but may lose nuance) versus raw data (larger, but preserves full distribution)?

### Modality Dominance and Imbalance in Multimodal Fusion
**Why needed:** The core problem is RGB dominating the main fusion, suppressing IMU cues. Understanding why this occurs (e.g., feature scale differences, gradient magnitudes) explains the need for adaptive weighting.  
**Quick check:** If RGB features have larger norm than IMU, how might that affect the fused representation without normalization like MoAS’s reliability weights?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text):**  
Synchronized RGB, gyroscope, accelerometer streams → modality encoders (BN‑Inception for RGB, DeepConvLSTM for IMU) → modality heads H_m yielding auxiliary logits z_m. Encoder embeddings fused via Temporal Binding Network (TBN) → main classifier C producing fused logits z_main. Replay buffer stores exemplars {x, y, ˜z_rgb, ˜z_gyro, ˜z_acc}. Training (MoRST) adds auxiliary cross‑entropy on z_m and distillation loss against stored ˜z_m. Inference (MoAS) computes per‑modality energy, normalizes with buffer stats, derives weights, combines logits: z_fused = z_main + Σ α_m z_m; novelty score = max(z_fused).

**Critical path:**  
*Training*: For each task, sample a batch from current data ∪ replay buffer. Forward pass obtains z_main and all z_m. Compute L_Sup = L_CE(z_main, y) + λ·(1/|M|) Σ L_CE(z_m, y). For replay samples, compute L_KD = Σ ||˜z_m − z_m||²₂. Total loss L = L_Sup + β·L_KD; backpropagate and update parameters. After the task, update replay buffer (reservoir sampling) with new samples and their modality logits. Update buffer statistics (µ_m, σ_m) per modality.  
*Inference*: For a test sample, compute z_main and all z_m. Compute energy E_m = −log Σ_c exp(z_m,c) for each m. Retrieve µ_m, σ_m from replay buffer. Compute reliability r_m = −(E_m − µ_m)/σ_m and weights α_m = softmax(r_m). Compute z_fused,c = z_main,c + Σ_m α_m·z_m,c. Novelty score s = max_c z_fused,c. If s > threshold, label as novel; else known class = argmax(z_main).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Memory vs. Stability:** Storing modality logits increases buffer memory but enables fine‑grained distillation. Reducing buffer size weakens MoRST’s preservation effect.  
- **Inference complexity:** MoAS adds negligible computation (energy, normalization, weighting) but requires per‑modality buffer statistics that must be maintained after each task.  
- **Threshold selection:** The novelty threshold for s(x) is typically tuned on validation; MoAS aims to make score distributions more separable, simplifying threshold setting.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Degenerate weights:** All r_m become similar → α_m ≈ uniform → adaptive benefit lost.  
- **Buffer contamination:** Buffer fills with mis‑classified novel samples → noisy ˜z_m corrupt L_KD.  
- **Modality failure:** One encoder (e.g., IMU) consistently yields uninformative logits → its α_m may dominate or collapse, causing over‑reliance.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablate MoAS:** Run full MAND but set α_m = 0 (ignore modality logits). Expect novelty detection AUC to drop while known‑class accuracy remains unchanged.  
2. **Ablate MoRST:** Remove L_KD and modality CE (λ=0). Expect both novelty detection AUC and known‑class accuracy to degrade over task sequences, especially long ones.  
3. **Fixed‑weight baselines:** Replace adaptive α_m with uniform sum (α_m=1) or average (α_m=1/|M|). Expect significant novelty performance drop compared to adaptive MoAS, as reported in the paper.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What memory‑efficient alternatives can replace the storage of modality‑specific logits and replay statistics in MAND without degrading open‑world continual learning performance?  
- How does replay buffer degradation—such as bias or insufficiency—affect MAND’s modality‑aware scoring and stabilization over very long task streams, and can adaptive buffer management mitigate this?  
- To what extent can MAND generalize to multimodal egocentric settings with non‑IMU sensors (e.g., audio, depth) or under strong domain shifts (e.g., new environments or user biases), and what architectural adaptations are necessary?

## Limitations
- The memory overhead of storing per‑modality logits and replay statistics may limit scalability in long‑term or resource‑constrained deployments.
- The protocol for computing and updating buffer statistics (µ_m, σ_m) and the lifecycle of stored modality logits (˜z_m) are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Evaluation is confined to the UESTC‑MMEA‑CL dataset; generalizability to other egocentric multimodal domains remains unverified.
