# eTracer: Towards Traceable Text Generation via Claim-Level Grounding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03669
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03669
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Claim-level grounding improves contradictory evidence prediction F1 by up to 0.129 (15.8%) and reduces user verification time by 2.6× while achieving 100% accuracy vs. 91–96% for baselines.

## Executive Summary
eTracer is a plug-and-play framework for traceable text generation that addresses the limitations of coarse-grained citations in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. It decomposes each sentence of a generated response into atomic, independently verifiable claims, then grounds each claim against contextual sentences using a combination of embedding-based retrieval and natural language inference (NLI). By aggregating these fine-grained claim-evidence links, eTracer quantifies response-level faithfulness via metrics like Faithful Claim Rate (FCR) and Hallucinated Claim Rate (HCR). Evaluated on a human-annotated biomedical dataset, eTracer substantially improves grounding quality over sentence-level baselines and enables users to verify responses significantly faster and more accurately.

## Method Summary
The eTracer pipeline operates as follows: (1) The response is tokenized into sentences. (2) Each sentence is decomposed into atomic claims using a fine-tuned decomposition model (Qwen3-14B via LoRA). (3) Claims are verified against their source sentence with an entailment model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) in an iterative loop. (4) Context documents are tokenized into sentences and embedded with Qwen3-Embedding-8B. (5) For each claim, top contextual sentences are retrieved by cosine similarity exceeding a threshold τ. (6) An entailment model predicts the logical relationship (support/contradiction/neutral) for each retrieved pair, assigning a signed polarity score ψ. (7) Claim-level evidence is aggregated to sentence-level citations using an unspecified rule (likely threshold-based). (8) Response-level metrics (FCR, HCR, ACR, UCR) are computed from aggregated links. The system is trained on a small, human-verified biomedical dataset (182 sentence-claim pairs, 4,267 claim-evidence pairs) using Unsloth with LoRA.

## Key Results
- Claim-level grounding (eTracer, τ=0.0) achieves an F1 score of 0.946 for contradictory evidence prediction, outperforming strong sentence-level baselines by up to 0.129 (15.8% relative gain).
- A user study shows eTracer reduces verification time by 2.6× while achieving 100% accuracy, compared to 91–96% accuracy for other grounding methods.
- Dual-phase retrieval+entailment yields high semantic similarity (ECSS ≈ 0.753) and polarity consistency (PFCR ≈ 90.1%), validating the grounding mechanism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decomposing response sentences into atomic claims enables finer-grained alignment with contextual evidence than sentence-level grounding.
- **Mechanism:** The decomposition model transforms a multi-fact sentence into separately verifiable units, allowing each claim to match different context sentences.
- **Core assumption:** A sentence often contains distinct statements originating from different sources; atomic claims simplify matching.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "decomposes response sentences into atomic claims"; [section 5.1] Claim-level baselines substantially outperform sentence-level baselines in F1 (e.g., +0.148 for supportive evidence).
- **Break condition:** Non-atomic or semantically incomplete claims degrade alignment precision; may be less effective for extractive tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A dual-phase approach (embedding retrieval + NLI entailment) improves grounding by combining semantic similarity with logical relationship assessment.
- **Mechanism:** For each claim, top context sentences are retrieved via cosine similarity, then an entailment model predicts ENTAILMENT/CONTRADICTION/NEUTRAL to assign polarity ψ.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity identifies candidates; NLI determines logical support/contradiction; combining reduces false positives from surface similarity alone.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "combination of embedding-based retrieval and entailment evaluation"; [section B.2] ECSS=0.753; [section B.3] PFCR=90.1%.
- **Break condition:** Failure in retrieval (low recall) or entailment (low precision) degrades quality; performance varies with threshold τ.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Aggregating claim-level grounding into response-level metrics (FCR, HCR, etc.) quantifies faithfulness and supports efficient verification.
- **Mechanism:** Metrics are computed by counting claims with specific evidence patterns (e.g., FCR: ≥1 supporting, 0 contradictory evidence).
- **Core assumption:** The proportion of claims with clear evidence patterns correlates with overall reliability and helps users quickly assess verification burden.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "aggregates results to quantify response faithfulness"; [section A.2] S⇒C method achieved 100% accuracy and 2.6× faster verification.
- **Break condition:** Poor decomposition (overlapping claims) causes aggregation to misrepresent sentence-level faithfulness.

## Architecture Onboarding
Sentence Tokenizer -> Decomposition Model (M_dec) -> Entailment Model (M_ent) (iterative verification) -> Embedding Model (E) -> Retrieval (similarity > τ) -> Entailment (ψ) -> Evidence Aggregator -> Faithfulness Metrics Calculator

**Critical path:** Input response/context → Tokenization → Sentence decomposition into claims → Claim verification against source sentence → Embedding of claims and context sentences → Retrieval of candidate evidence sentences (cosine similarity > τ) → NLI-based entailment scoring (ψ) → Aggregation of claim links to sentence-level citations → Computation of FCR/ACR/HCR/UCR.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Decomposition improves grounding quality (+0.129 F1) but increases inference time (+22s vs. sentence-level).
- Evidence threshold τ trades quality for speed (τ=0.5 reduces time 35% with minimal drop vs. τ=0.25).
- Fine-tuning small models (4B/14B) retains plug-and-play flexibility but may underperform larger proprietary models.

**Failure signatures:**
- High Hallucinated Claim Rate (HCR): Claims with contradictory evidence but no support → likely model hallucination.
- High Unverified Claim Rate (UCR): Claims with no supporting/contradictory evidence → retrieval failure or out-of-domain context.
- High Ambiguous Claim Rate (ACR): Claims with both supporting and contradictory evidence → conflicting context or poor decomposition.
- Low Faithful Claim Rate (FCR): Overall response untrustworthy.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Decomposition quality validation:** Compute claim precision/recall against human annotations; target >0.90 as in Figure 3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Can the eTracer framework's claim-level grounding approach maintain its verification efficiency and accuracy gains when applied to non-biomedical, high-stakes domains (e.g., legal, clinical notes) with different linguistic and structural characteristics?
### Open Question 2
What is the optimal, potentially dynamic, evidence threshold (τ) for balancing grounding quality (F1, FCR) and inference efficiency across different LLM architectures, embedding models, and context lengths?
### Open Question 3
To what extent do the proposed reference-free grounding quality metrics (CER, ECSS, PFCR) reliably predict human-judged faithfulness (FCR/ACR/HCR) in scenarios without gold-standard annotations?
### Open Question 4
Does the claim decomposition step (Mdec) introduce brittleness or error propagation in eTracer for complex, multi-clausal, or abstractive sentences that resist decomposition into truly atomic, independent claims?
### Open Question 5
Can the eTracer pipeline be optimized for real-time or near-real-time applications (e.g., interactive search, clinical decision support) without significant fidelity loss, and what architectural modifications (e.g., caching, specialized retrievers, model distillation) are most effective?

## Limitations
- Aggregation logic from claim-level to sentence-level citations is unspecified, hindering exact reproduction of metrics.
- Fine-tuned decomposition and entailment model checkpoints may not be publicly available; retraining on limited internal data risks performance gaps.
- The optimal threshold τ is corpus-dependent and selected empirically without a clear selection criterion.
- Benefits may not generalize to extractive generation tasks or non-biomedical domains, as admitted by the authors.
