# Interp3R: Continuous-time 3D Geometry Estimation with Frames and Events

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.14528
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14528
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Leverages event streams to interpolate frame-based 3D pointmaps to arbitrary times, reducing absolute relative depth error by 20–37% on PointOdyssey and rotation error by up to 50% on Sintel for large frame skips (1–15 frames).

## Executive Summary
Interp3R addresses a key limitation of modern pointmap-based 3D vision models like Align3R: they estimate scene geometry only at discrete frame timestamps, leaving intermediate moments unmodeled. By incorporating asynchronous event camera data, the method interpolates frame-based pointmaps to any continuous time τ between two frames. Trained solely on synthetic data, it generalizes effectively to real-world benchmarks. A coarse-to-fine joint alignment optimizes both source and interpolated pointmaps together, yielding significant improvements in depth and pose accuracy over two-stage video frame interpolation plus 3D estimation baselines, especially under large frame skips.

## Method Summary
Interp3R builds on a frozen frame-based model (e.g., Align3R) that predicts source pointmaps and confidences at two frame times. It processes the bidirectional event stream between these frames via voxelization and a dedicated event encoder. Features from the source pointmaps, confidences, and event voxels are fused using zero-convolution layers within the decoder of the base model, allowing a gradual “transport” of the source predictions toward an arbitrary intermediate time τ. The interpolated pointmaps are then jointly aligned with the source pointmaps in a two-stage optimization: a coarse stage initializes poses/depth at the frame times, and a fine stage incorporates the interpolated measurements to refine all timestamps simultaneously, with confidence-weighted fusion.

## Key Results
- Reduces absolute relative depth error by 20–37% on PointOdyssey compared to video interpolation + 3D baselines.
- Improves rotation error (RRE) by up to 50% on Sintel for large frame skips (up to 15 frames).
- Maintains robust performance across varying interpolation times τ, thanks to explicit time conditioning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Event streams provide temporally dense motion cues that enable the interpolation of geometric pointmaps to continuous time.
- **Mechanism:** Events encode per-pixel brightness changes asynchronously. Interp3R voxelizes forward and backward event streams between two frames and processes them with an event encoder. These event-derived motion features are fused with source frame-based pointmap predictions to regress pointmaps at τ. Events supply temporal derivative information bridging geometry at discrete frame times.
- **Core assumption:** The spatiotemporal distribution of events correlates with scene 3D motion and geometry evolution; events primarily encode true motion, not illumination noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states leveraging asynchronous event data for interpolation; [section 3.2] details voxelization and feature fusion.
- **Break condition:** If events are dominated by non-motion triggers (e.g., flickering lights, sensor noise), motion cues become misleading, corrupting interpolated geometry.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Progressive, frozen-base pointmap transport via zero-convolution stabilizes training and prevents catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained frame-based model’s geometric knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** The Align3R backbone is frozen. New lightweight encoders for source pointmaps, confidence, and events feed into the decoder. Zero-convolution layers initially apply zero weights, so at training start Interp3R outputs exactly the source pointmaps. As training progresses, these weights learn to modulate how much event and confidence information influences the decoder, gradually adjusting the source predictions toward τ without abrupt changes to the base model.
- **Core assumption:** The base model provides a sufficiently accurate geometric prior at frame times; small, learned adjustments are safer than training from scratch.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] describes zero-convolution use and exact initial output identity; explains gradual injection without abruptly changing decoders.
- **Break condition:** If source pointmaps are highly erroneous (e.g., due to motion blur or textureless regions), the interpolation starting point is corrupt, potentially propagating errors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Joint coarse-to-fine global alignment of both source and interpolated pointmaps within a unified spatial framework enforces temporal consistency and refines poses/depth at all timestamps.
- **Mechanism:** A two-stage optimization. Coarse stage aligns only source pointmaps to compute initial poses/depth at t=0,1 (as in Align3R). Fine stage incorporates interpolated pointmaps at τ, aligning them together with source pointmaps. This forces all pointmaps—observed and interpolated—to be mutually consistent in one world coordinate system. Optimization is bidirectional (0→1 and 1→0) with confidence-weighted fusion, solving for Θ_τ while finetuning Θ_0 and Θ_1.
- **Core assumption:** The DUSt3R-style reprojection loss validly solves for geometry and poses from individually noisy but collectively constrained pointmaps.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] details coarse stage using only source maps, fine stage incorporating interpolated maps, and confidence-weighted fusion of four measurements.
- **Break condition:** Systematic biases or low confidence in interpolated pointmaps can degrade the global solution; assumption of scene rigidity or smooth motion may be violated by abrupt dynamic object motion.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Pointmap-based 3D reconstruction (DUSt3R family).**  
  *Why needed:* Interp3R extends models like Align3R; understanding that pointmaps are per-pixel 3D coordinates in a reference frame and that global alignment recovers poses and scale is essential.  
  *Quick check:* What is the core representation predicted by DUSt3R, and how does global alignment recover camera poses from pairwise predictions?
- **Concept: Event camera data and preprocessing.**  
  *Why needed:* The entire method hinges on converting asynchronous, sparse polarity changes into a spatiotemporal representation (voxel grid) processable by a neural network.  
  *Quick check:* Why are events voxelized, and what does the polarity sign represent in a typical event stream?
- **Concept: Differentiable global alignment optimization.**  
  *Why needed:* Final depth and pose estimates result from gradient-based optimization (e.g., 300 steps) over alignment parameters, using predicted pointmaps as pseudo-observations.  
  *Quick check:* In the DUSt3R alignment loss, what does minimizing the Euclidean distance between projected and target points achieve?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Base Model (Align3R, frozen) -> Event Processing (split, voxelize) -> Encoders (pointmap, confidence, event) -> Decoders (zero-conv fusion) -> Heads (interp. pointmaps) -> Aligner (coarse-to-fine optimization).
- **Critical path:** Input (I₀, I₁, Events) → Base Model (X₀₀, X₀₁, C₀₀, C₀₁) → Event split & voxelization → Multi-encoders → Zero-conv fusion in decoders → Interpolated pointmaps (X_τ, C_τ) → Global optimization → Final depth/pose at τ.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Freezing vs. fine-tuning base: Freezing ensures stability but limits adaptation; compatibility shown with MonST3R outputs without fine-tuning.  
  - Bidirectional vs. unidirectional interpolation: Bidirectional (0→τ and 1→τ) fused via four measurements offers robustness over unidirectional.  
  - Explicit time encoding: Conditioning heads on τ makes performance less sensitive to τ; ablation shows degradation for extreme τ without it.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Degradation with very large skips (e.g., 15 frames) due to complex motion exceeding implicit prior.  
  2. Edge artifacts on dynamic objects; baseline VFI+3D methods show worse artifacts, but Interp3R may still blur on very fast motion.  
  3. Reliance on source quality: Removing events causes sharp performance drop for large skips, proving events are crucial when frame motion cues are insufficient.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Ablation: Remove event encoder/input. Expect: Performance acceptable for small skips (1–3) but collapses for larger skips (7,15) on depth/pose.  
  2. Ablation: Remove time encoding from heads. Expect: Inconsistent performance across τ, especially poor near τ=0 or 1.  
  3. Compatibility test: Swap base model with MonST3R without fine-tuning Interp3R. Expect: Competitive depth and pose metrics, confirming plug-and-play capability.

## Limitations
- Performance depends on accuracy of source pointmaps from Align3R; errors may propagate.
- Assumes event streams reflect true motion; not robust to lighting flicker or sensor noise.
- Generalization beyond 15-frame skips and to highly dynamic scenes is untested.
- No real-event training or evaluation; synthetic events may differ from real-world data.

## Confidence
- Event-augmented interpolation: Medium (synthetic validation strong, real-world noise not tested).
- Zero-convolution progressive training: High (training procedure well-described and stable).
- Joint coarse-to-fine alignment: High (leverages proven DUSt3R alignment).
- State-of-the-art performance: Medium (outperforms baselines on benchmarks; synthetic-to-real gap persists).

## Next Checks
1. **Real-event fine-tuning & evaluation:** Train and test on real event datasets (e.g., E-CDF); compare to synthetic-only model.
2. **Event corruption ablation:** Synthetically add noise/flicker to event streams to measure robustness and failure thresholds.
3. **Long-horizon interpolation:** Test on frame skips >15 (e.g., 30, 60) to quantify performance decay and identify breaking point.
