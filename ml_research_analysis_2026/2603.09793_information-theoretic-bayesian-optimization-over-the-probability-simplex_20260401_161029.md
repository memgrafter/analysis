# Information Theoretic Bayesian Optimization over the Probability Simplex

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09793
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09793
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: α-GaBO, a geometry-aware Bayesian optimization method for the probability simplex, achieves lower median log-regret and faster convergence than constrained Euclidean BO on synthetic and real-world tasks.

## Executive Summary
Bayesian optimization (BO) over the probability simplex is essential for optimizing mixtures and probabilities, but standard BO methods ignore the simplex's intrinsic Riemannian geometry, resulting in suboptimal performance. This paper introduces α-GaBO, a novel approach that incorporates information geometry via the Fisher-Rao metric and α-connections. The method constructs kernels by pulling back spherical Matérn kernels through an isometry to the hypersphere, and optimizes acquisition functions using Riemannian trust regions with closed-form exponential maps for α = -1 (exponential) and α = 0 (Levi-Civita). Extensive experiments on synthetic benchmarks and diverse real applications—concrete mixture design, chemical photodegradation, classifier ensembles, and robotic multi-task control—demonstrate that α-GaBO consistently reduces median log-regret and converges faster than constrained Euclidean BO, including the state-of-the-art BORIS. Variants using α = 0 achieve particularly low loss in complex tasks, validating the benefit of aligning BO with the simplex's geometric structure.

## Method Summary
α-GaBO adapts the standard BO loop to the probability simplex by embedding two geometric components. First, it defines a kernel on the simplex via an isometric mapping φ(x)=2√x to the positive orthant of a hypersphere; the resulting kernel is the pullback of a spherical Matérn kernel, which is positive-definite and respects the simplex's Riemannian metric (Fisher-Rao). Second, acquisition function maximization is performed on the manifold using Riemannian trust-region optimization with exponential maps derived from the α-connection. For α = -1, the exponential map is Exp_x(η) = (exp(η) ⊙ x)/(xᵀexp(η)), offering an unconstrained tangent space but struggling near the boundary. For α = 0, Exp_x(η) = (√x cos(‖η‖/2) + (√x⊙η)/‖η‖ sin(‖η‖/2))² (Levi-Civita), which can reach the boundary but requires careful trust region radius control. The GP posterior is updated with this kernel, and the acquisition (EI or LCB) is optimized via the chosen exponential map; the resulting candidate is evaluated, and the loop repeats. This geometric alignment yields better data efficiency and convergence on simplex-constrained problems.

## Key Results
- α-GaBO reduces median log-regret and converges faster than constrained Euclidean BO (including BORIS) across synthetic benchmarks and real applications.
- Both α variants outperform Euclidean baselines; α0-GaBO achieves the lowest loss in the robotic multi-task control task, highlighting the importance of boundary-friendly optimization.
- The method demonstrates lower variance in performance across seeds, indicating robust and data-efficient optimization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Geometry-Aware Kernel via Sphere Isometry
- **Claim:** The probability simplex's intrinsic Riemannian geometry can be exploited via an isometry to the sphere, enabling the use of well-established spherical Matérn kernels.
- **Mechanism:** The sphere map φ(x)=2√x (element-wise square root) is a diffeomorphic isometry from the simplex Δ^d (endowed with the Fisher-Rao metric) to the positive orthant of a hypersphere S^d_{≥0}(2). Spherical Matérn kernels, defined via the spectral decomposition of the sphere's Laplace-Beltrami operator, are pulled back through this map to define valid, positive-definite kernels on the simplex.
- **Core assumption:** The sphere map preserves geometric distances and smoothness, allowing kernel properties (e.g., positive definiteness, differentiability) to transfer.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Kernels are constructed via an isometry to the hypersphere (sphere map φ(x)=2√x), pulling back spherical Matérn kernels."; [section 4.1] Details the sphere map as an isometry and defines the pulled-back kernel; [section A.2] Proves the isometry and its extension to the simplex boundary.
- **Break condition:** If the spherical kernel's eigenfunction expansion fails to converge sufficiently on the restricted positive orthant, the pulled-back kernel may not be sufficiently smooth or positive-definite on the full simplex. The paper assumes truncation provides adequate approximation.

### Mechanism 2: α-Connection-Based Acquisition Optimization
- **Claim:** Acquisition function optimization over the simplex is guided by the geometry-specific exponential map derived from the α-connection, with α ∈ {-1, 0} providing closed-form, numerically stable updates.
- **Mechanism:** The α-connection family on the simplex (from information geometry) defines distinct exponential maps. For α=-1 (exponential connection), Exp_x(η) = (exp(η) / (x^T exp(η))) ⊙ x, allowing unconstrained tangent vectors but making boundary convergence difficult. For α=0 (Levi-Civita connection), Exp_x(η) = (√x cos(‖η‖_F/2) + (√x ⊙ η)/‖η‖_F sin(‖η‖_F/2))^2, which can reach the boundary but has a constrained tangent domain.
- **Core assumption:** The Riemannian trust region algorithm using these exponential maps respects the manifold structure better than Euclidean projections, leading to more efficient acquisition maximization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Acquisition optimization uses Riemannian trust regions with exponential maps from the α-connection; two instances, α=-1 (exponential) and α=0 (Levi-Civita), provide closed-form operations."; [section 4.2] Provides the explicit update rules and Algorithms 1 & 2; [section 3.2] Defines the α-connection and its exponential maps.
- **Break condition:** For α=-1, numerical instability near the simplex boundary due to ‖η‖ → ∞ required to reach it. For α=0, generic tangent vectors may map outside the simplex, requiring constrained trust regions.

### Mechanism 3: Integrated Geometry-Aware BO Loop
- **Claim:** Integrating geometry-aware kernels and α-connection-based optimizers within the Bayesian optimization loop yields superior data efficiency and convergence on simplex-constrained problems compared to constrained Euclidean BO.
- **Mechanism:** The full α-GaBO algorithm replaces standard Euclidean GP kernels with the pulled-back spherical Matérn kernel and replaces Euclidean acquisition optimization with Riemannian trust region steps using the chosen α-exponential map. This geometric alignment influences the GP's posterior smoothness assumptions and the search trajectory's geometry, leading to more informed evaluations.
- **Core assumption:** The inductive bias introduced by the simplex's Riemannian structure is beneficial for the target objective functions (common in mixture/probability optimization), outweighing any added computational complexity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Extensive experiments... show that α-GaBO reduces median log-regret and converges faster than constrained Euclidean BO (including BORIS)..."; [section 5] Presents experimental results across benchmarks and real applications showing α-GaBO's consistent advantage; [section 1] Notes BORIS's failure to account for intrinsic geometry, leading to suboptimal performance.
- **Break condition:** If the target function's intrinsic geometry is poorly matched by the Fisher-Rao metric or if the problem's optimum lies precisely where the chosen α-connection's exponential map is numerically problematic (e.g., α=-1 and optimum on a simplex vertex), performance may degrade relative to simpler methods.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Sphere Map Module → Kernel Module → Geometry Optimizer Module → BO Loop Controller

### Critical path
The choice of α (e.g., -1 or 0) is the primary architectural decision. It simultaneously determines (a) which exponential map the optimizer uses and (b) implicitly defines the geometric prior through the connection used in the Riemannian operations. The kernel, while geometry-aware via the sphere map, is decoupled from α in the current design (uses standard spherical Matérn). The critical path is: α selection → Exp_α → acquisition optimizer; and sphere mapping → kernel → GP posterior.

### Design tradeoffs
- **α = -1 (Exponential):** Pros: Unconstrained tangent space, simpler Exp formula. Cons: Cannot practically reach the simplex boundary (requires infinite ‖η‖), risk of numerical overflow near boundary.
- **α = 0 (Levi-Civita):** Pros: Metric-compatible, boundary reachable, equivalent to sphere geometry. Cons: Exp domain is constrained (trust region needed), more complex formula with trigonometric functions.
- **Kernel smoothness:** Matérn ν parameter controls smoothness; higher ν (e.g., 5/2) used for Olympus but leads to more expensive eigen-sum computations.

### Failure signatures
- **Optimizer divergence:** For α=-1, iterations causing ‖η‖ explode near boundary; for α=0, if trust region step has norm near 2π, sin/cos become sensitive.
- **Poor convergence on boundary:** If true optimum lies on a simplex face/vertex and α=-1 is used, the optimizer will hover arbitrarily close but never reach it, leading to plateauing regret.
- **Kernel ill-conditioning:** If the spherical kernel series truncation is too low, the pulled-back kernel may not be sufficiently smooth or positive-definite, causing GP posterior instability (e.g., non-positive covariance matrices). Diagnose via Cholesky failure of K(D,D)+σ²I.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Synthetic benchmarks (Ackley/Rosenbrock/Griewank on Δ^d):** Validates basic functionality. Checks if geometry-aware methods match/surpass Euclidean BO in low/medium dimensions and if α choice matters when optimum is interior.
2. **Concrete Mixture (Δ^6 × [0,1]):** Tests performance on a product manifold where optimum is on the simplex boundary. Should see α=-1 struggle while α=0 and Euclidean BO (which can project to boundary) perform better, validating boundary handling.
3. **Robotic Multi-Task Control (Π_k ∈ Δ^{N-1} for K=10):** Most complex real-world test with a high-dimensional product of simplices. Tests scalability and robustness. α0-GaBO is expected to excel if optimum involves boundary components and smooth transitions are needed.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter settings (kernel lengthscale, variance, GP mean, trust region parameters) and spherical kernel truncation level are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction and potentially affecting performance.
- The α=-1 variant exhibits poor boundary convergence, requiring many iterations to approach simplex vertices/faces and may suffer numerical overflow.
- The relative contribution of the geometry-aware kernel versus the geometry-aware optimizer is not isolated; the paper lacks an ablation study.

## Confidence
- Kernel validity via sphere map isometry: **High**
- Effectiveness of α-connection-based acquisition optimization: **Medium**
- Overall superiority of α-GaBO across benchmarks: **Medium**
