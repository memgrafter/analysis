# GSVD for Geometry-Grounded Dataset Comparison: An Alignment Angle Is All You Need

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10283
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10283
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A per-sample alignment angle derived from GSVD disentangles dataset-specific and shared geometry, enabling interpretable binary classification based on simple thresholding (τ = π/4) on MNIST digit pairs.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a geometric method for comparing two datasets using the generalized singular value decomposition (GSVD). The factorization A = HCU and B = HSV with CᵀC + SᵀS = I yields a shared basis H and diagonal factors that separate directions preferentially explained by one dataset from those common to both. For a sample z, the alignment angle θ(z) = arctan(∥C⁺c(z)∥/∥S⁺c(z)∥) (with c(z) = H⁺z) quantifies its relative affinity: small angles align with A, large with B, and π/4 indicates balanced presence. Applied to MNIST digits, θ distributions separate visually distinct classes (e.g., 0 vs 7) more than similar ones (e.g., 4 vs 9). Extreme GSVD directions also reconstruct class-specific and blended image patterns, demonstrating interpretability. The approach offers a simple, theoretically grounded diagnostic for dataset relationship.

## Method Summary
Let two datasets be represented as mean-centered data matrices A ∈ ℝ^{d×p} and B ∈ ℝ^{d×q}. Compute the GSVD: A = HCU, B = HSV with invertible H, orthogonal U, V and diagonal (or block-diagonal) C, S satisfying CᵀC + SᵀS = I. The diagonal entries of C and S are ordered to reflect each shared direction’s relative strength for A (large c_ii) and B (large s_ii). For a test sample z, obtain its coordinates in the shared basis via c(z) = H⁺z. The costs of representing z using A and B are a(z) = ∥C⁺c(z)∥ and b(z) = ∥S⁺c(z)∥. The alignment angle is θ(z) = arctan(a(z)/b(z)). A default threshold τ = π/4 yields a binary classifier: predict A if θ(z) ≤ π/4 else B. The extreme columns of H corresponding to the blocks of C and S generate prototype images for each dataset and their combinations.

## Key Results
- θ(z) serves as a per-sample alignment score: small values indicate z is cheaper to reconstruct from A, large values from B, and π/4 indicates equal representation cost.
- Class-conditional θ histograms on MNIST separate well for visually distinct digit pairs (e.g., 0 vs 7, Fisher–Rao distance up to 2.77 rad) and show more overlap for similar digits (e.g., 4 vs 9, 2.34 rad).
- GSVD extreme directions reveal class-specific and shared image patterns, providing visual interpretability of the linear decomposition.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method rests on three pillars. **Mechanism 1:** The GSVD factorization provides a joint coordinate system where the block-diagonal C and S separate shared and dataset-specific directions. The condition CᵀC + SᵀS = I forces a partition of the unit sphere in the H basis: directions with large c_ii and small s_ii are primarily explained by A, the converse by B. This decomposition assumes the column spaces admit a clear linear hierarchy; if many entries are similar, separation blurs. **Mechanism 2:** θ(z) compares minimal ℓ₂ norms of coefficients needed to express z in the two subspaces. Under constraints x⊥Ker(A), y⊥Ker(B), the solutions to Ax = By = z are unique; those norms measure representation cost. The ratio a(z)/b(z) in the shared basis yields an angle. This assumes z lies in col(A)∩col(B); otherwise θ is undefined. Pseudoinverses may require truncation to avoid instability from vanishing diagonal entries. **Mechanism 3:** Thresholding at τ = π/4 provides a simple binary classifier. When class-conditional θ distributions are separated around π/4, misclassification is low. Evidence from digit pairs shows such separation; however, for highly overlapping distributions the rule degrades.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Datasets (two classes) -> Matrices A, B (mean-centered columns) -> GSVD (H, C, S, U, V) -> For test z: c(z)=H⁺z -> a(z)=∥C⁺c(z)∥, b(z)=∥S⁺c(z)∥ -> θ(z)=arctan(a/b) -> threshold τ=π/4 -> label.

Critical path: GSVD dominates preprocessing with O(d³) cost (d = ambient dimension). Per-sample inference involves projecting z onto H (O(d²) given precomputed H⁺) and two small norm operations (O(d)). Thus inference is cheap after the upfront decomposition.

Design tradeoffs: The rank implicitly set by the number of training columns (p,q) controls the granularity of subspace modeling—more samples preserve variance but may add noise; fewer risk discarding discriminative modes. Truncating tiny singular values in C⁺, S⁺ improves numerical stability at the cost of ignoring very weak directions; no guidance on tolerance is provided. Preprocessing (per-class mean subtraction) affects the subspace alignment; other feature representations (e.g., hidden layers) may behave differently.

Failure signatures: Class-conditional θ distributions heavily overlap, suggesting insufficient geometric distinction. θ(z) becomes erratic for small perturbations in z, hinting at near-degenerate C or S entries—consider stricter truncation. A majority of θ(z) cluster near π/4 across classes, possibly due to overly similar subspaces or mismatched preprocessing.

First 3 experiments:
1. Digit pair separation: Build A (900 training images) and B (800 training images) for two MNIST digits (e.g., 1 vs 5). Compute θ for test images and plot histograms per true class. Visual separation should appear for distinct pairs (0 vs 7) and diminish for similar ones (4 vs 9).
2. Extreme direction reconstruction: For a chosen pair, extract blocks from C and S to identify indices r (A-specific), k (shared), t (B-specific). Form z_min = h_{r+1} and z_max = h_{r+k}, reshape to 28×28. Expect z_min to resemble a prototype of the first digit, z_max a blend, and intermediate columns to transition between them.
3. Fashion-MNIST replication: Repeat experiment 1 on Fashion-MNIST class pairs without tuning. Seek analogous patterns (e.g., T-shirt vs Sneaker separated, Sneaker vs Ankle boot overlapped), confirming cross-dataset applicability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-dataset extension:** How to generalize the pairwise GSVD alignment angle to >2 datasets? Could involve aggregating pairwise angles or a multiway GSVD; no concrete proposal exists.
- **Robustness characterization:** What is the sensitivity of θ to preprocessing (centering, normalization), rank selection, pseudoinverse truncation, and additive noise? The paper acknowledges this as a critical unknown.
- **Feature embeddings:** How does θ behave when A and B are built from neural network embeddings (e.g., CNNs, transformers)? The linear subspace assumption may be more realistic, but empirical validation is lacking.
- **Scalability:** GSVD’s O(d³) cost is prohibitive for high-dimensional data (e.g., high-resolution images). Approximate or randomized variants are needed but not developed.

## Limitations
- The GSVD factor ordering and block detection (A-specific vs B-specific vs shared) are underspecified, increasing risk of implementation divergence from the paper.
- Preprocessing choices (e.g., whether test samples are centered with class-specific or global means) are not clearly stated, potentially affecting projections and θ.
- Classification claims are not supported by numerical accuracy; evidence for separation relies on histogram visualizations and Fisher-Rao distances, not error rates or baselines.
- The theoretical derivation requires z ∈ col(A)∩col(B), but the algorithm applies the formula to all samples; handling of non-relational cases is not discussed and may be unstable.

## Confidence
- GSVD factor ordering and reproduction accuracy: **Medium** (based on standard GSVD usage, but exact block structure may differ).
- Preprocessing specifications (test centering): **Low** (ambiguous in the paper).
- Utility of τ = π/4 as classifier threshold: **Low** (no quantitative results provided).
- Practical robustness to noise and out-of-intersection samples: **Medium** (empirically plausible but unanalyzed).
