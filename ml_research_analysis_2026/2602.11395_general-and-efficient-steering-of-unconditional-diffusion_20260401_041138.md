# General and Efficient Steering of Unconditional Diffusion
## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2602.11395
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11395
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: Achieves 96.6% guidance accuracy on CIFAR-10, with 16.4× faster inference and lower FID (41.4 vs. 73.9) compared to prior training-free guidance.

## Executive Summary
Unconditional diffusion models lack built-in control over generated content, and existing steering methods either require expensive retraining or per-step gradient computations (e.g., classifier guidance). This paper introduces a two-stage, gradient-free steering mechanism that combines Noise Alignment and RFM Steering. Noise Alignment uses offline-computed class-conditional PCA statistics to provide a coarse pixel-space guidance signal during early, high-noise diffusion steps, thereby avoiding any per-sample gradients. RFM Steering learns a single, transferable discriminative direction in the activation space of a U-Net block from clean forward-process activations via the Recursive Feature Machine (RFM); this fixed direction is injected at later, low-to-intermediate noise steps and amplified through a CFG-style update. Evaluations on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and CelebA demonstrate substantial improvements over state-of-the-art training-free guidance, achieving 96.6% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs. 77.1%), a 16.4× speedup, and a significant FID reduction (41.4 vs. 73.9). The approach also generalizes to multi-attribute CelebA and out-of-distribution fine-grained concepts.

## Method Summary
The proposed method eliminates gradient computation at inference by precomputing two types of guidance assets: (1) class-conditional PCA statistics (means and principal components) derived from clean training images, which define a linear PCA denoiser for each class; and (2) a single class-discriminative steering direction `vc` learned from U-Net activations at a low noise level using the RFM algorithm. During sampling, the diffusion loop consists of a standard DDIM step. For timesteps with high noise levels, the PCA difference between the target class and the unconditional denoiser is added to the denoised estimate `x̂0`. For timesteps within a lower noise window, the activations at a specific encoder block are perturbed by adding `w_RFM * ||h|| * vc`. This modification triggers a second forward pass to produce a conditional noise prediction, whose corresponding `x̂0_rfm` is combined with the unconditional `x̂0` via a CFG-style boost (`x̂0 ← x̂0 + s * (x̂0_rfm - x̂0)`). The two windows can overlap, providing a smooth transition from coarse to fine steering. The entire process requires no per-step gradients, drastically reducing computational overhead while maintaining or improving guidance quality.

## Key Results
- Guidance accuracy on CIFAR-10 reaches 96.6%, surpassing the previous training-free TFG baseline (77.1%).
- Inference speed improves by a factor of 16.4× relative to gradient-based methods, while FID drops from 73.9 to 41.4.
- Strong performance is also observed on ImageNet and CelebA, including multi-attribute steering and out-of-distribution fine-grained concepts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Coarse semantic steering is possible at high noise levels using only pre-computed dataset statistics, avoiding any per-step gradients.
- **Mechanism:** At early timesteps (high noise), the diffusion process's linear Gaussian structure dominates. Pre-computed class-conditional PCA statistics (mean and principal components) approximate the optimal linear denoiser for that class. The difference between the class-conditional and unconditional PCA denoisers provides a pixel-space guidance signal that nudges the noisy sample toward the coarse statistical manifold of the target class.
- **Core assumption:** The data distribution for a given class is approximately Gaussian in the PCA subspace, and the optimal linear denoiser for Gaussian noise is given by PCA shrinkage.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - The abstract states: "Noise Alignment: even in early, highly corrupted stages, coarse semantic steering is possible using a lightweight, offline-computed guidance signal, avoiding any per-step or per-sample gradients."
  - Section 3.2 presents the PCA denoiser `Dc(xt;σt) = µc + Vc diag(λj/(λj+σt^2)) Vc⊤ (xt - µc)` and notes it "requires no training – just precomputed class statistics – and works precisely where activation steering fails."
  - Ablations in sections 4.5 and D.2 show noise alignment alone can reach moderate accuracy (~80% at λ=10) but with degraded FID, demonstrating its function as a coarse anchor.
- **Break condition:** This mechanism fails at low noise levels where the data is no longer well-approximated by a linear Gaussian model; fine-grained structure is needed, which PCA statistics cannot provide.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A single, fixed discriminative direction in the activation space of a U-Net block, learned from clean forward-process activations, can be effectively applied across a range of intermediate timesteps during reverse sampling.
- **Mechanism:** RFM (Recursive Feature Machine) learns a direction `vc` in the activation space (e.g., the last encoder block before the bottleneck) that maximally discriminates the target class from others, using only activations from clean forward noising steps. This direction is then added to the activations during the reverse sampling process at later timesteps (low-to-intermediate noise). The guidance effect is amplified via a CFG-style update on the denoised estimate `x̂0`.
- **Core assumption:** (1) Semantically meaningful, class-discriminative directions exist in intermediate activation spaces (not just the bottleneck); (2) These directions are approximately invariant ("transferable") across timesteps in the later stage of the reverse process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - The abstract notes: "Transferable concept vectors: a concept direction in activation space once learned transfers across both timesteps and samples; the same fixed steering vector learned near low noise level remains effective when injected at intermediate noise levels... Such concept directions can be efficiently and reliably identified via Recursive Feature Machine (RFM)."
  - Figure 3 (section 3.1) shows high cosine similarity (>0.8) between RFM directions learned at a clean timestep (σ≈0.21) and directions at intermediate timesteps, especially in encoder blocks, demonstrating temporal transferability.
  - Table 2 (section 4.1) reports that RFM-only guidance achieves 94.8% accuracy, confirming that a single clean-time direction is highly effective when applied across the latter half of sampling.
- **Break condition:** This mechanism is ineffective at very high noise levels because forward activations at those stages are near-random (as shown in Figure 2 top-left), and the learned clean-time direction does not align with the activation geometry there.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The two-stage combination (Noise Alignment + RFM Steering) is synergistic and necessary; each stage addresses a regime where the other is ineffective, together achieving high accuracy and quality without gradient computation.
- **Mechanism:** Noise Alignment is applied only during the earliest, high-noise steps (e.g., steps 0-50) to establish a coarse class anchor. RFM Steering is applied during the later, low-to-intermediate noise steps (e.g., steps 30-99) to refine the image with fine-grained discriminative control. The overlap window (steps 30-50) provides continuity. Both mechanisms use pre-computed, static vectors; the first operates in pixel space using PCA statistics, the second in activation space using the RFM direction.
- **Core assumption:** Class information emerges hierarchically: coarse structure can be guided by dataset-level statistics early on, while fine-grained details require semantic directions from the model's internal representations later.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Figure 2 (section 3.1) probes show reverse activations have class signal early (~35% accuracy), while forward activations are strong only at clean timesteps (~80%+). This asymmetry motivates the hybrid approach: use noise alignment (pixel-space, early) and RFM (activation-space, late).
  - Ablations in sections 4.5, D.2, and Figure 8 demonstrate that noise alignment alone (λ=10) achieves 80% accuracy but poor FID (127), while RFM alone achieves 94.8% accuracy but requires >30 guided steps to be effective (Figure 7). The combined NA-RFM achieves 96.6% accuracy with FID 41.4, breaking the Pareto frontier of the individual methods.
  - Algorithm 1 (section 3.4) explicitly defines two non-overlapping guidance windows (`σt ≥ σ_end` for noise alignment; `σt ∈ [σ_lo_R, σ_hi_R]` for RFM) applied in sequence during the reverse diffusion loop.
- **Break condition:** Removing either stage significantly harms performance (Table 2: RFM-only 94.8% vs. NA-RFM 96.6%; Noise-align-only max 80%). The break condition would be if one stage could cover the entire trajectory as effectively as the two combined, which the probing results (Figure 2) and timing ablations (Figure 7) suggest is not the case.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion model reverse process and denoised estimate `x̂0`**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire guidance framework modifies either the pixel-space sample `xt` (Noise Alignment) or the internal activations that influence the noise prediction `ϵθ`, which is then used to compute `x̂0 = (xt - √(1-ᾱt)ϵθ)/√ᾱt`. The CFG-style boost in Eq. (10) operates on `x̂0`. Understanding this quantity is essential to see how steering vectors alter the predicted clean image at each step.
  - **Quick check question:** If you add a guidance vector `g` to the pixel-space sample `xt` before computing `x̂0`, how does that linear operation propagate through the denoiser's prediction of the clean image?

- **Concept: U-Net internal activations as a semantic latent space**
  - **Why needed here:** RFM steering operates on activations `h(ℓ)` from a specific U-Net block (typically the last encoder before the bottleneck). The paper's finding that these activations retain class-discriminative information even under noise (Figure 2) is foundational. The engineer must understand which block to hook and that skip connections make modifications in the encoder affect the decoder.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might activations in the very early encoder blocks (high spatial resolution, low semantic depth) or the very bottleneck (low spatial resolution) be less suitable for class steering than the chosen block?

- **Concept: Recursive Feature Machine (RFM) for direction discovery**
  - **Why needed here:** RFM is the core method to extract a single, transferable class direction from forward activations *without backpropagation*. It iteratively refines a Mahalanobis distance kernel and computes the AGOP matrix to find the top eigenvector. The engineer needs to grasp this bootstrapping loop (kernel -> regression -> gradients -> AGOP -> new kernel) and the dual formulation trick for high-dimensional activations.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the AGOP matrix `M_AGOP` in RFM, and why does its top eigenvector give a good steering direction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Offline Precomputation:**
    1. **PCA Statistics Module:** Computes class-conditional means `µc` and principal components `Vc, Λc` from clean training images. Also computes unconditional statistics over all data.
    2. **Activation Collection Module:** Runs a single forward noising pass on each training image at a chosen low noise level (e.g., σ≈1.0) to extract U-Net block `ℓ` activations `h`.
    3. **RFM Trainer Module:** Takes activations `h` and binary labels for a target class, runs the iterative RFM algorithm (kernel construction, KRR, AGOP update) to output the steering direction `vc` (top-k AGOP eigenvectors).
  - **Inference (per generation step):**
    1. **Diffusion Sampler Core:** Standard DDIM step: run `ϵθ(xt, t)` to get noise prediction, compute `x̂0`.
    2. **Conditional Activations Hook:** Before the `ϵθ` forward pass for RFM steering, modify the saved activations at block `ℓ` via `h_steered = h + w_RFM * ||h|| * vc`.
    3. **Dual Forward Pass:** First, a standard forward pass to get unconditional `ϵ` and `x̂0`. If within RFM window, a second forward pass with the hooked activations to get `ϵ_rfm` and `x̂0_rfm`.
    4. **CFG Boost:** Update `x̂0 ← x̂0 + s * (x̂0_rfm - x̂0)`.
    5. **Noise Alignment Boost:** If within high-noise window, update `x̂0 ← x̂0 + λ * (Dc(xt, σt) - D_all(xt, σt))`.
    6. **Final Update:** Recompute `ϵ` from boosted `x̂0` and run the standard DDIM update to `x_{t-1}`.
- **Critical path:** The inference-time path is: (a) check noise level `σt`; (b) if in RFM window, inject `vc` into activations → second forward pass → CFG boost; (c) if in noise-align window, compute and add PCA difference; (d) standard DDIM update. The offline path must complete: collect activations → train RFM per class → precompute PCA.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Noise Alignment vs. RFM:** Noise alignment is cheaper computationally (pixel-space vector ops) but coarse and harms sample quality at high strengths (high FID). RFM is more semantically precise but requires careful block selection, window tuning, and a second forward pass during its active window.
  - **RFM Block Choice:** Encoder blocks (especially later ones like 8x8) show high temporal transferability and accuracy (Figure 6, 97.9% for Encoder-9). Middle/bottleneck blocks perform poorly (47.4%). Decoder blocks work but slightly less well. Tradeoff: earlier encoder blocks may have higher spatial resolution but potentially lower semantic abstraction; later encoder blocks offer a sweet spot.
  - **Guidance Window Overlap:** The paper sets windows that overlap (e.g., RFM from step 30-99, noise-align from 0-50). The overlap region (steps 30-50) receives both signals. This is a design choice; non-overlapping windows could be used but might lose synergistic effects in the transition.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High-FID, low-accuracy samples:** RFM strength `w_RFM` too high, causing over-guidance and collapse to a single template (as seen with mean-difference baseline in Figure 14). FID spikes while accuracy may not improve proportionally.
  - **Random or wrong class at high accuracy:** Noise alignment strength `λ` too high, destroying sample diversity and structure (see Figure 8). The samples may be classified correctly but look unrealistic.
  - **No improvement over unguided:** RFM window start `σ_lo_R` too late (e.g., training at σ≈1.0 but applying only from σ≈0.5), so the steering signal never accumulates (Figure 7 shows ~30-50 steps needed). Or, RFM direction learned from a block with poor discriminative power (e.g., middle block).
  - **Slow inference:** Unexpected overhead if the activation hook implementation forces full recomputation of hooked layers or if the second forward pass is not properly cached/optimized. The overhead should be negligible (~1-2 matrix-vector ops per guided step).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate RFM direction transferability:** Train an RFM direction on airplane class at DDIM step 93 (σ≈0.21). Apply it *only* at that exact step during generation of 256 samples. Expect very low accuracy (Figure 7: ~13.4%). Then apply it from step 50 onwards. Expect high accuracy (>90%). This confirms temporal transfer is not automatic; accumulation over steps is key.
  2. **Isolate Noise Alignment effect:** Run inference with *only* noise alignment (λ tuned, RFM off) on CIFAR-10 airplane. Sweep `λ` from 0 to 10. Expect accuracy to rise then plateau (~80%), while FID degrades monotonically. This establishes the coarse guidance capability and its quality tradeoff.
  3. **Block selection sanity check:** Repeat the main NA-RFM experiment for one class (e.g., airplane) but extract the RFM direction from three different blocks: Encoder-9 (8x8), Middle block (4x4), Decoder-6 (8x8). Following Figure 6, expect Encoder-9 and Decoder-6 to give >95% accuracy, Middle block to give ~50%. This validates the architectural prior for steering.

## Limitations
- Noise alignment assumes Gaussianity of class manifolds; this may fail for complex, high-resolution, or highly non-linear data distributions.
- RFM direction transferability is empirically strong but lacks theoretical justification and may not generalize to other architectures (e.g., diffusion transformers).
- Guidance windows require manual tuning; an automatic, data-driven method for selecting them is not provided.

## Confidence
- **Noise alignment coarse guidance:** Medium
- **RFM direction transferability:** High
- **Combined NA-RFM:** High
- **Generalization beyond U-Net/32x32:** Low

## Next Checks
1. **Resolution scalability:** Test the method on 256×256 diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) to measure accuracy/FID trade-offs and computational overhead, particularly the impact of the second forward pass.
2. **Architecture invariance:** Apply RFM to diffusion transformers (e.g., DiT) by extracting directions from its layers; compare performance against U-Net to assess whether the encoder-block preference holds.
3. **Automatic window selection:** Derive a data-driven criterion (e.g., based on activation signal-to-noise ratio) to set the noise alignment and RFM windows automatically; validate against hand-tuned baselines.
