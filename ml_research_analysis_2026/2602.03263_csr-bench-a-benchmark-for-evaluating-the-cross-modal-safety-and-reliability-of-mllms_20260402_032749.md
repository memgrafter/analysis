# CSR-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating the Cross-modal Safety and Reliability of MLLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03263
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03263
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** No MLLM tested achieves consistent cross-modal safety robustness; multimodal inputs often degrade performance compared to text-only baselines, revealing a trade-off between safety/bias reduction and over-rejection.

## Executive Summary
CSR-Bench is a new benchmark designed to expose weaknesses in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) regarding safety and reliability when genuine cross-modal understanding is required. Existing benchmarks often allow models to rely on unimodal shortcuts; CSR-Bench circumvents this by constructing 7,405 image-text pairs where correct responses depend on joint interpretation, and by pairing each multimodal sample with a text-only control that fuses both modalities. Evaluation of 16 state-of-the-art MLLMs reveals systematic gaps: multimodal inputs lead to worse safety performance than text-only controls, models exhibit weak safety detection and language dominance, and improvements in safety and bias correlate with increased over-rejection. The trade-off suggests current alignment encourages refusal heuristics rather than robust cross-modal reasoning.

## Method Summary
CSR-Bench evaluates MLLMs on four stress-testing patterns—Safety (malicious intent split across modalities), Over-rejection (benign queries with sensitive text disambiguated by images), Bias (appearance-based social judgments), and Hallucination (textual interference conflicting with visual evidence). The dataset comprises 7,405 image-text pairs, each crafted to require integrated interpretation; for Safety, Over-rejection, and Bias, a unimodal control (IU) is created by semantically fusing the image description and query into a single text question. Alignment Accuracy (S) is the primary metric, scored by an LLM judge (GPT-5-Nano) on a binary scale. The cross-modal reliability gap ΔAlign = S(I_M) − S(I_U) quantifies reliance on unimodal shortcuts. Construction employed three pipelines: filtering and rewriting existing safety datasets, systematic scenario synthesis with LLM assistance, and manual collection, all followed by rigorous human and automated quality checks to ensure correct labeling and genuine cross-modal dependence.

## Key Results
- All 16 MLLMs show inconsistent cross-modal robustness; multimodal inputs often yield lower safety performance than text-only controls.
- Models demonstrate weak safety detection, strong language dominance, and increased hallucination under textual interference.
- A clear trade-off exists: reductions in safety/bias issues correlate with higher over-rejection rates, indicating reliance on refusal heuristics rather than true understanding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CSR-Bench works by forcing models to process and integrate information from both visual and textual modalities simultaneously. Its design ensures that the correct answer cannot be derived from a single modality alone; instead, the model must resolve contradictions or complementarities between image and text. The unimodal controls (IU) serve as a diagnostic baseline: if performance drops when moving from IU to multimodal, the model is relying on unimodal shortcuts. The ΔAlign metric directly measures this gap. By covering diverse failure patterns—Safety, Over-rejection, Bias, and Hallucination—the benchmark captures multiple dimensions of cross-modal reliability. The use of an LLM judge scales evaluation while maintaining consistency across thousands of samples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**: Models that accept both image and text inputs to generate responses. *Why needed:* CSR-Bench targets the safety and reliability of such models. *Quick check:* Can the model answer a question that requires describing an image and relating it to text?  
- **Safety Alignment**: Techniques to prevent models from producing harmful content. *Why needed:* The benchmark evaluates whether alignment transfers to multimodal contexts. *Quick check:* Does the model refuse unsafe requests consistently across modalities?  
- **Cross-modal Understanding**: The ability to combine evidence from different sensory inputs. *Why needed:* CSR-Bench tests this explicitly; unimodal shortcuts are a key failure mode. *Quick check:* If the image and text provide complementary clues, does the model synthesize them correctly?  
- **Unimodal Shortcuts**: When a model ignores one modality and bases its answer on the other. *Why needed:* The benchmark diagnoses this via ΔAlign. *Quick check:* Is there a significant performance difference between multimodal and unimodal versions of the same test?  
- **Benchmark Construction**: Systematic design of test data to measure specific capabilities. *Why needed:* Poorly designed benchmarks can leak signals or fail to isolate the target skill. *Quick check:* Are samples verified to ensure the answer depends on both modalities?  
- **LLM-as-a-Judge**: Using an LLM to automatically score model outputs. *Why needed:* Enables large-scale evaluation but may introduce biases. *Quick check:* Does the judge agree with human annotators on a sample?  

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data Collection (BeaverTails-V, Spa-VL, LLM-assisted generation) -> IU Baseline Generation -> Model Inference (target MLLM on multimodal & unimodal inputs) -> LLM Judge Evaluation -> Metric Computation (Alignment Accuracy, ΔAlign)

### Critical Path
The evaluation pipeline hinges on: (1) constructing samples with genuine cross-modal dependency; (2) generating unimodal baselines that preserve the original intent; (3) running the target MLLM with fixed decoding settings; (4) scoring with a reliable LLM judge; (5) computing metrics. A failure at any stage invalidates the results.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Static benchmark vs. dynamic generation**: Static data ensure replicability but may be overfitted; dynamic generation can cover more cases but risks leakage and reduces comparability.
- **Automated vs. human evaluation**: LLM judges scale but may diverge from human judgment; human annotation is accurate but costly.
- **Breadth vs. depth**: Four patterns provide a comprehensive view, but expanding patterns would dilute sample sizes and reduce statistical power.
- **IU fusion complexity**: Simpler prompts risk altering intent; more complex prompts may introduce variability in baseline generation.

### Failure Signatures
- **Cross-modal leakage**: Multimodal samples where the answer is deducible from either modality alone.
- **Judge drift**: LLM judge scores that systematically differ from human annotators (κ < 0.85).
- **Intent distortion in IU**: Unimodal baselines that change the original query's meaning (low semantic similarity).
- **Inference inconsistency**: Accuracy changes >5% when decoding parameters vary slightly.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Baseline sanity check**: Evaluate a well-known MLLM (e.g., GPT-4V) on CSR-Bench to confirm the pipeline yields reasonable scores and non-zero ΔAlign values.
2. **Judge calibration**: Collect human annotations for 100 multimodal and 100 unimodal samples per subset; compute Cohen’s κ between human and LLM judge. If κ < 0.85, refine the judge prompt or add few-shot examples.
3. **IU robustness test**: Generate unimodal baselines using two alternative fusion prompts; compute the absolute difference in ΔAlign. If the mean |ΔAlign difference| > 0.05, adopt a standardized, validated prompt for future runs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Circularity in cross-modal benchmark generation**: The process of verifying cross-modal dependence often involves MLLMs, which are the very systems being evaluated, creating a circular dependency. A fully automated, scalable validation method without target models remains an open challenge.
- **Transferring safety awareness to multimodal settings**: Why does multimodal input degrade safety performance compared to text-only controls? The root cause—architectural, data, or objective—is unknown, hindering the development of effective alignment techniques for MLLMs.
- **Trade-off between safety/bias reduction and over-rejection**: Current alignment appears to correlate lower safety/bias issues with higher over-rejection, suggesting models resort to refusal heuristics rather than nuanced understanding. The causal factors behind this trade-off and how to decouple them are not yet identified.

## Limitations
- CSR-Bench dataset and evaluation code are not yet publicly released, impeding independent verification.
- The exact prompt and settings used to generate unimodal baselines (IU) are undocumented, making exact replication difficult.
- The official judge model (GPT-5-Nano) is proprietary; substitutes may introduce systematic score drift.

## Confidence
- Cross-modal test design that exposes unimodal shortcuts: **Medium**
- Systematic reliability gaps observed across MLLMs: **Medium**
- Trade-off between safety/bias reduction and over-rejection: **Medium**
