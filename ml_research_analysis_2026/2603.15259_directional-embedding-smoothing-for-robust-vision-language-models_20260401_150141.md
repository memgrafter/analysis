# Directional Embedding Smoothing for Robust Vision Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15259  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15259  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Directional RESTA reduces jailbreak attack success rate on LLaVA-1.5-7B from 50.13% to 25.93% while minimally impacting ScienceQA accuracy (64.07% → 61.42%), outperforming isotropic noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles vision-language model (VLM) vulnerability to jailbreaking attacks by extending the Randomized Embedding Smoothing and Token Aggregation (RESTA) defense with directional embedding noise. Unlike isotropic Gaussian noise, directional noise aligns perturbations with original embedding vectors, preserving semantic geometry. Evaluated on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Gemma-3-4B using the JailBreakV-28K benchmark and ScienceQA for utility, the method achieves favorable security-utility tradeoffs. Results demonstrate that directional smoothing is a lightweight, inference-time defense that meaningfully reduces attack success rates with limited utility cost, while isotropic noise fails to provide effective protection.

## Method Summary
RESTA is an inference-time defense that injects noise into the embeddings of user-controlled tokens only (system/template tokens remain fixed). For each token generation step, it creates \(k\) noisy copies of the input embedding sequence by adding either isotropic (\( \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \)) or directional noise (\( e \leftarrow e + (\sigma\sqrt{d}) \cdot z \cdot (e/\|e\|_2), z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1) \)). Each copy generates the next token greedily; the final token is chosen by majority vote across the \(k\) predictions. This process repeats autoregressively. The directional variant constrains noise to the 1D subspace of each embedding vector, aiming to preserve angular relationships that encode semantics. The defense is applied only to user content tokens, avoiding disruption of system prompts or formatting.

## Key Results
- On LLaVA-1.5-7B, directional RESTA (\(k=10\), appropriate \(\sigma\)) reduces attack success rate (ASR) on JailBreakV-28K from 50.13% (undefended) to 25.93%, while ScienceQA accuracy drops only from 64.07% to 61.42%.
- Isotropic noise yields poor security-utility tradeoffs, often performing near the trivial interpolation baseline (no defense).
- Similar trends hold for Gemma-3-4B, confirming directional noise’s advantage across models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Directional embedding noise (aligned with original embedding vectors) preserves semantic content more effectively than isotropic noise during inference-time smoothing.
- **Mechanism**: Noise is added in the direction of the original embedding vector \(e\) as \(e + (\sigma\sqrt{d}) \cdot z \cdot (e/\|e\|_2)\). This constrains perturbations to the 1D subspace spanned by \(e\), minimizing disruption to the angular relationships between embeddings that encode meaning.
- **Core assumption**: Semantic information in dense embedding spaces is primarily encoded in direction (angular geometry) rather than magnitude (norm).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Explicitly states results show directional noise achieves "favorable security-utility tradeoffs" while isotropic noise yields "poor tradeoffs, often near trivial baselines."
  - [section] Section 2 formally defines "Hard directional noise" and motivates it with "the hypothesis that semantic meaning is primarily encoded in the direction of embedding vectors."
  - [corpus] Weak signal: Corpus contains "Smoothed Embedings for Robust Language Models" (paper_id 13622), which introduces RESTA, but no direct evidence about *directional* vs isotropic effects for VLMs is cited in neighbors.
- **Break condition**: If model representations encode significant semantic information in embedding magnitudes or non-linear interactions between magnitude and direction, directional noise could disproportionately harm utility even at moderate \(\sigma\).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Majority-vote aggregation across \(k\) perturbed forward passes (\(k=10\)) stabilizes output against embedding perturbations, leveraging the smoothing effect to mitigate jailbreak pathways.
- **Mechanism**: For each token generation step, \(k\) noisy embedding sequences \(\tilde{e}_i\) are decoded independently (greedy). The final token \(y\) is chosen by \(\text{mode}(\tilde{y}_1, ..., \tilde{y}_k)\). This aggregates predictions, requiring a majority of perturbed paths to agree on an unsafe token for a jailbreak to succeed.
- **Core assumption**: Jailbreak-successful tokens occupy a relatively narrow, fragile region of the embedding space; isotropic or directional noise effectively disperses mass from this region, making it unlikely for a majority of \(k\) noisy paths to land in it.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Mentions RESTA "injecting noise in the embeddings" and "aggregating across perturbations."
  - [section] Algorithm 1 explicitly details the majority-vote token aggregation.
  - [corpus] No direct anchors; this is the core RESTA mechanism from Hase et al. (2024), which is not in the provided neighbor list but is cited.
- **Break condition**: If jailbreak attacks are inherently multi-path or exploit high-probability regions of the output distribution resilient to embedding noise, majority vote may not reduce attack success rate. Also, if \(k\) is too small relative to noise scale \(\sigma\), the aggregation may lack statistical power.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The observed superior security-utility tradeoff for directional noise (vs. isotropic) stems from its ability to disrupt jailbreak-attack vectors while minimally impacting performance on benign, standard-distribution inputs.
- **Mechanism**: Directional noise, by moving along the vector direction, preserves the *direction* of benign input embeddings on average, maintaining their relational geometry. Jailbreak inputs, by construction, are out-of-distribution (OOD) and likely occupy atypical angular regions; directional noise may more effectively scatter these atypical directions because their norms may differ, leading to unequal effective perturbation magnitudes. Isotopic noise adds fixed-variance noise to all dimensions, which can more uniformly degrade both benign and attack semantics.
- **Core assumption**: Jailbreak-promoting embeddings are OOD in a way that makes them more sensitive to direction-aligned perturbations than in-distribution (benign) embeddings. Alternatively, the decision boundary between safe/unsafe outputs is more aligned with embedding directions for attacks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Key result: "For LLaVA, attack success rate drops from 50.13% to 25.93% while ScienceQA accuracy minimally decreases from 64.07% to 61.42%." Contrasts with isotropic noise's "poor tradeoffs."
  - [section] Figure 1 and Tables 1-2 visually and numerically demonstrate the tradeoff curves, with directional noise showing a steeper security improvement per utility loss.
  - [corpus] Weak signal: Neighboring papers like "Towards Adversarially Robust Vision-Language Models" (paper_id 157449) study design choices for robustness, but no direct comparison to directional smoothing is evident.
- **Break condition**: If benign inputs also have highly variable norms or the semantic preservation hypothesis fails for the specific model/dataset, directional noise could harm utility comparably to isotropic. The break condition is essentially when the utility drop curve for directional noise converges to that of isotropic noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: **Embedding Space Geometry (Direction vs. Norm)**
  - **Why needed here**: The core mechanism hinges on the hypothesis that semantics are encoded in direction. Understanding why \(e/\|e\|_2\) might preserve meaning while adding noise to all components (\(e + \varepsilon\)) might not is key to predicting when this defense works.
  - **Quick check question**: If two embeddings have high cosine similarity (small angular distance) but one has twice the norm of the other, are they semantically close? How might a norm-sensitive vs. direction-sensitive perturbation affect their relative positions?
- **Concept**: **Randomized Smoothing for Certification**
  - **Why needed here**: RESTA is inspired by randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019). The principle is that adding noise and aggregating can transform a locally non-robust classifier into a globally robust one. Here, it's metaphorically extended to generation, where "robustness" is against jailbreak *behaviors* rather than misclassification.
  - **Quick check question**: In standard image classification smoothing, what is the certified radius? Why is it challenging to define an analogous certified radius for preventing a language model from outputting *any* harmful content?
- **Concept**: **Automatic Jailbreak Evaluation with LLM-as-a-Judge**
  - **Why needed here**: The security metric (ASR) relies on Llama-Guard-3-8B. Understanding its limitations (e.g., its own safety alignment, prompt sensitivity) is crucial for interpreting the reported ASR numbers.
  - **Quick check question**: If the defense slightly alters phrasing to be less inflammatory but still conveys harmful intent, might the judge model miss it? How could this affect the measured ASR difference between undefended and defended models?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Vision Encoder (e.g., ViT) -> Projector (linear) -> LLM Core (Transformer) -> Tokenizer & Embedding Lookup (\(E\)) -> RESTA Layer (Perturbation Module \(H_\sigma\) + Sampling Loop + Autoregressive Update)

- **Critical path** (during generation of output token \(t\)):
  input tokens (user+system) -> embed -> [RESTA: apply \(H_\sigma\) to user-token embeddings only] -> LLM forward pass \(f(\tilde{e})\) for \(k\) copies -> \(\text{argmax}\) per copy -> \(\text{mode}\) -> token \(t\) -> append & repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - \(k\) vs. latency: \(k\) copies increase compute/memory linearly; paper uses \(k=10\), smaller \(k\) reduces cost but weakens aggregation.
  - \(\sigma\) vs. security/utility: Higher \(\sigma\) increases ASR reduction but degrades utility. Directional noise enables better tradeoff at given \(\sigma\).
  - Noise type choice: Directional requires per-embedding norm computation (small overhead) but yields better tradeoffs. Isotropic is simpler but often ineffective.
  - Token scope: Perturbing *only* user tokens is a design choice to avoid breaking system prompts/formats. Perturbing all tokens likely harms utility more.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Security failure**: ASR remains high (>40%) even at large \(\sigma\) for directional noise on a given model/attack set. This suggests jailbreak vectors are either not directionally fragile or the perturbation is insufficient.
  - **Utility failure**: ScienceQA accuracy drops sharply (>5% relative) at moderate \(\sigma\) where ASR is still high. This indicates semantic preservation hypothesis fails; noise is too disruptive for in-distribution tasks.
  - **Isotropic failure**: ASR barely decreases while utility drops linearly with \(\sigma\). Tradeoff curve hugs or falls below the "trivial interpolation" diagonal (Figure 1), indicating noise only adds randomness without meaningful security gain.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline establishment**: Run undefended model (\(\sigma=0\)) on JailBreakV-28K and ScienceQA to establish (ASR, Accuracy) starting point. Verify baseline ASR is substantial (>40%).
  2. **Isotropic vs. Directional sweep**: Systematically vary \(\sigma\) for both noise types. Plot tradeoff curves (ASR vs. Accuracy). Expect directional to show a clear Pareto improvement over isotropic.
  3. **Component ablation**:
     - Test \(k=1\) (no aggregation) with directional noise: Does security improve? (Tests if noise alone helps vs. aggregation effect).
     - Perturb *all* tokens (including system prompt): Measure utility drop to confirm design choice.
     - Test on another VLM (e.g., InstructBLIP): Is the directional advantage model-agnostic?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What are the fundamental reasons why directional embedding noise in RESTA is more effective than isotropic noise at reducing jailbreak success rates while preserving utility for VLMs?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper explicitly states: "This motivates further investigation of other variants of directional noise and exploring the underlying, fundamental reasons for these observations" and highlights the "major role" of directionality.
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper empirically demonstrates the superiority of directional noise but does not provide a theoretical or in-depth analysis explaining why aligning noise with embedding directions better preserves semantic content and disrupts attacks.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Theoretical analysis of embedding manifolds showing that directional noise remains within semantically coherent subspaces, or controlled experiments that isolate the impact of noise direction on semantic preservation metrics and attack susceptibility.

- **Open Question 2**: What is the theoretical mechanism that explains RESTA's effectiveness against jailbreak attacks, and can it be formally grounded beyond metaphorical connections to randomized smoothing?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper poses: "A broader open question that remains about RESTA is with understanding the mechanisms by which it seems to work at all" and discusses differences from classical adversarial robustness settings.
  - **Why unresolved**: RESTA is inspired by randomized smoothing for classification, but jailbreaks involve open-ended generation with different objectives; the paper speculates on mechanisms without rigorous justification.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Development of a formal model linking noise parameters to robustness guarantees in autoregressive generation, or empirical studies tracing how noise affects intermediate activations and token logits to avoid unsafe outputs.

- **Open Question 3**: Does RESTA maintain its security-utility tradeoff against adaptive attacks where adversaries explicitly optimize jailbreak prompts to bypass the defense?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper acknowledges: "it remains to be seen whether this defense would hold up against focused adaptive attacks with significant resources and careful human red-teaming."
  - **Why unresolved**: Evaluation used the static JailBreakV-28K benchmark; adaptive attackers could leverage knowledge of RESTA's stochastic process to find noise-robust jailbreak strategies.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting adaptive red-teaming experiments where attackers iteratively refine prompts with full knowledge of RESTA's implementation, measuring attack success rates under sustained optimization.

- **Open Question 4**: How well do RESTA's results generalize to other VLM architectures (e.g., differing vision encoders or LLM backbones) and a wider array of attack benchmarks beyond JailBreakV-28K?
  - **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The study is limited to two specific VLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B and Gemma-3-4B) and one benchmark; generalizability is an implicit assumption not tested.
  - **Why unresolved**: The effectiveness of directional noise may depend on model-specific embedding properties or attack characteristics; without broader evaluation, findings may not extend to other systems.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Replicating experiments across diverse VLMs (e.g., BLIP-2, Flamingo) and multiple attack datasets (e.g., MM-SafetyBench, VibeCheck) to assess consistency of the security-utility tradeoff.

## Limitations
- **Token masking specifics**: The exact mapping from input conversations to “user content tokens” for LLaVA-1.5-7B and Gemma-3-4B is unspecified, risking incorrect noise application.
- **Prompt format ambiguity**: The precise VLM input prompts (system messages, instruction wrappers, answer extraction) for JailBreakV-28K and ScienceQA are not provided, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Evaluation scope**: Results are limited to two VLMs, one utility dataset, and one attack benchmark; generalizability to other architectures and attacks remains unverified. Safety judgments also rely on Llama-Guard-3-8B, which may introduce bias.

## Confidence
- **Directional noise preserves semantics** (Hypothesis that semantics are encoded in direction): **Medium** (plausible but not directly validated)
- **Majority-vote aggregation stabilizes outputs** (Core RESTA principle): **High** (well-established from randomized smoothing)
- **Directional smoothing yields superior security-utility tradeoff** (Empirical superiority over isotropic): **Medium** (strong empirical support but narrow model/dataset coverage)

## Next Checks
1. **Token masking verification**: Extract user token positions from official VLM chat templates; ensure baseline (\(\sigma=0\)) output matches unmodified model and noise is applied only to intended tokens.
2. **Embedding similarity test**: Compute average cosine similarity between original and noisy embeddings (both types) on a benign sample at matched noise energy (\(\sigma\sqrt{d}\)); expect directional to preserve similarity better.
3. **Cross-model replication**: Repeat experiments on a third VLM (e.g., InstructBLIP) and an additional utility benchmark (e.g., MME); consistency of directional advantage would confirm robustness.
