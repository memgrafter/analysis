# ConFu: Contemplate the Future for Better Speculative Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08899
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08899
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** ConFu improves average token acceptance rates and end-to-end speedup over EAGLE-3 by 8–11% on SpecBench with Llama-3 3B and 8B models.

## Executive Summary
Speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by using a draft model to predict multiple tokens ahead, which are then verified in parallel by the target model. However, draft models conditioned only on their own generated tokens suffer from error accumulation, causing drift from the target distribution. ConFu addresses this by enabling the draft model to anticipate the target model's future generation direction. It introduces learnable soft prompt tokens and a "contemplate" pause token that extracts a future-oriented signal (`f`) from the target model's hidden states at minimal cost. A Mixture-of-Experts mechanism makes the contemplate token dynamic and context-aware. Training uses anchor token sampling for memory efficiency and future prediction replication for robustness. Experiments show consistent improvements in acceptance rates and speedup over the EAGLE-3 baseline across diverse tasks and sampling configurations.

## Method Summary
ConFu extends the EAGLE-3 speculative decoding framework. It prepends learnable soft prompt tokens to the target model's KV cache and inserts a learnable "contemplate" token (`<con>`) after the current prefix. The hidden state of `<con>` serves as a future prediction vector `f`, which is fed as an auxiliary token to the draft model during its autoregressive generation. Both the `<con>` embedding and `f` are generated by separate Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) modules, whose routers are conditioned on the hidden state of the most recently accepted token, making the future signal context-adaptive. During training, anchor token sampling randomly chooses positions to insert `<con>`, limiting sequence length growth. Future prediction replication reuses each anchor's `f` to train the draft model on nearby non-anchor tokens, encouraging robust and locally consistent future predictions. The draft model is initialized from a pretrained EAGLE-3 checkpoint and fine-tuned with this modified objective.

## Key Results
- ConFu achieves an 8–11% improvement in average token acceptance rate and end-to-end speedup over the EAGLE-3 baseline on SpecBench.
- Gains are observed across both Llama-3 3B and 8B models, multiple temperatures (0, 0.7, 1.0), and draft node budgets (30, 60).
- The MoE component and future prediction replication both contribute positively to performance in ablation studies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Future-Aware Drafting via Contemplate Tokens
- **Claim:** Conditioning the draft model on a continuous thought vector `f` extracted from the target model's intermediate processing reduces error accumulation by aligning draft predictions with the target's semantic trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** A learnable contemplate token is appended to the input prefix of the target model. The target model's final hidden state for this token (`f`) is provided as an additional token to the lightweight draft model. Shared access to `f` allows the draft to generate multiple candidate tokens that better anticipate the target's future path.
- **Core assumption:** The target model's hidden state after processing the contemplate token contains a compressed, forward-looking representation of its next-step reasoning, which is stable enough to guide the draft.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "enable draft models to anticipate the future direction... contemplate tokens and soft prompts"; Section 3.1 describes feeding `f` as an auxiliary token to the draft.
- **Break condition:** If `f` becomes trivial or noisy (e.g., correlates poorly with the target's next-token distribution), the draft receives no useful guidance and error accumulation persists.

### Mechanism 2: Context-Adaptive Future Prediction via MoE
- **Claim:** A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module dynamically generates the contemplate token's embedding and the future token `f` based on the most recently accepted token's hidden state, allowing the future signal to adapt to diverse generation contexts.
- **Mechanism:** A router computes expert weights from the last accepted token's hidden state. The final embedding for both the contemplate token (input to target) and `f` (input to draft) is a weighted sum of top-K learnable expert embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** Different contexts (e.g., coding vs. summarization) require different "instructions" to elicit an accurate future prediction; a single fixed embedding is insufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "dynamic contemplate token mechanism with MoE"; Section 3.2 explains parameterizing the token using MoE conditioned on the last accepted token.
- **Break condition:** If the MoE router collapses (always selects one expert) or expert embeddings fail to diversify, the mechanism degenerates to a static token, losing context-awareness.

### Mechanism 3: Robust Training via Anchor Sampling and Prediction Replication
- **Claim:** Training the draft model with anchor token sampling and future prediction replication learns robust future predictions `f` that are stable to minor positional changes, while drastically reducing memory overhead.
- **Mechanism:** 
    1. **Anchor token sampling:** From a training sequence, randomly sample `K_train` anchor positions. Only at these positions is a contemplate token inserted and a future prediction `f` computed.
    2. **Future prediction replication:** For `l` non-anchor tokens immediately following each anchor, reuse the anchor's `f` as the future signal when computing the draft's loss for those positions. This encourages `f` to be locally consistent.
- **Core assumption:** The target model's future prediction for a token is similar for nearby tokens; thus a single `f` can robustly guide draft predictions for a small local window.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites "training framework with anchor token sampling and future prediction replication"; Section 3.3 details the procedure.
- **Break condition:** If future prediction is highly position-sensitive, replication introduces noise. If anchor sampling misses critical boundaries, the model may not learn to handle context shifts.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Speculative Decoding Fundamentals**  
  *Why needed:* ConFu modifies the drafting phase. Understanding the draft-verify-correct cycle, tree-based verification, and acceptance rate explains why error accumulation is problematic.  
  *Quick check:* Explain how a draft tree increases expected accepted tokens compared to a linear draft sequence, and why a draft model that drifts from the target's distribution reduces the tree's effectiveness.

- **Concept: Error Accumulation in Autoregressive Draft Models**  
  *Why needed:* ConFu's primary motivation is mitigating drift from error accumulation in existing draft models (like EAGLE) that condition only on the current prefix.  
  *Quick check:* Why does conditioning a draft model solely on its own previously generated tokens (even if starting from target states) lead to increasing divergence from the target model's predictions over multiple steps?

- **Concept: Pause (Contemplate) Tokens and Latent Reasoning**  
  *Why needed:* ConFu's key signal (`f`) comes from a pause token. Knowing that pause tokens increase compute and can encode intermediate "thoughts" is prerequisite.  
  *Quick check:* How does inserting a special "pause" token into an LLM's input sequence affect its internal computation for the next output token, and why might the hidden state of that pause token contain a summary of the model's next-step plan?

- **Concept: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for Dynamic Embeddings**  
  *Why needed:* ConFu uses MoE to generate dynamic contemplate token embeddings. Understanding MoE routing is necessary to grasp context-dependence.  
  *Quick check:* In an MoE module used to generate a token embedding, what is the role of the router, and how does conditioning the router on a specific hidden state (e.g., last accepted token) make the generated embedding context-dependent?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Target Model (Mt) -> receives [Soft Prompts] + [Prefix] + [Contemplate Token] -> outputs next token logits + contemplate token hidden state `f`.  
MoE (for contemplate) -> generates contemplate token embedding from last accepted token's hidden state.  
MoE (for `f`) -> generates `f` embedding from last accepted token's hidden state.  
Draft Model (Md) -> receives [Prefix] + [Draft tokens so far] + [`f`] -> outputs next draft token distribution.  
Training Loop -> Anchor Sampler -> selects anchor positions -> computes `f` at anchors -> replicates `f` for nearby tokens -> computes KL loss between Mt and Md distributions.

**Critical path:**  
1. **Prefill:** Target model processes `[soft_prompt] + [initial_prefix] + [1 contemplate]`. Produces first real token and initial `f`.  
2. **Draft Loop Start:** `f` is appended to draft model input. Md autoregressively generates a draft tree.  
3. **Verification & Next `f`:** Target model receives `[prefix] + [draft_tree_nodes]` with a contemplate token inserted after *each* draft node. Verifies all draft nodes in parallel and produces a distinct `f` for each. The `f` from the last accepted draft token is selected for the next iteration's draft model.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Memory vs. Accuracy:** More soft prompt tokens or MoE experts increase capacity but also memory. Anchor sampling trades training signal density for memory savings.  
- **Static vs. Dynamic Contemplate:** A single static token is simpler and cheaper; MoE adds router overhead but allows adaptation. Gain depends on task diversity.  
- **Inference Overhead:** Each draft tree node requires a separate contemplate token processing in the target model (2T tokens total). This cost grows with tree size but is offset by higher acceptance rates. Tuning anchor/replication parameters balances training stability and robustness.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High draft rejection rate** despite ConFu: `f` may be uninformative or misaligned. Check MoE router entropy (collapsed?) and `f` similarity across anchors.  
- **Training instability/loss not decreasing:** Could be due to poor anchor sampling or too large replication window `l`.  
- **No speedup over EAGLE-3 on easy tasks:** Overhead of contemplate tokens may outweigh gains when error accumulation is naturally low. Check acceptance rate delta.  
- **MoE expert utilization heavily skewed:** Use expert selection histogram. If one expert dominates, MoE is not learning diverse modes.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline comparison:** Replicate EAGLE-3 and ConFu on SpecBench with Llama-3 3B/8B. Measure average accept length (τ) and speedup ratio (SR) across temperatures and draft node budgets. *Expected: ConFu > EAGLE-3 on both metrics.*  
2. **Ablation - MoE & Replication:** Run ConFu without MoE (static contemplate) and without both MoE & replication. Isolate contribution of each component. *Expected: Removing either reduces gains; removing both hurts most.*  
3. **Sensitivity - Temperature & Draft Budget:** Test ConFu at T=0, 0.7, 1.0 and with 30 vs. 60 draft nodes. *Expected: Relative gains largest at T=0 and with smaller draft trees.*

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the number of contemplate tokens be reduced without sacrificing draft acceptance rates? The current implementation uses one token per draft node; no technique to share or reduce tokens is proposed despite observed robustness of future predictions.
- **Open Question 2:** Why does ConFu's relative improvement diminish at higher sampling temperatures, and how can future prediction be made effective under stochastic decoding? Results show smaller gains at T=1.0; the authors attribute this to sharper distributions at low temperatures but provide no deeper analysis or solution.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent do ConFu's gains generalize to larger language models and different architectures (e.g., non-Llama models)? Experiments are limited to Llama-3 3B and 8B on SpecBench; generalizability to other scales and architectures is untested.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters for MoE (experts, top-K), soft prompt length, anchor sampling rate, and replication window are not disclosed; these affect memory/accuracy trade-offs.
- Precise tree-verification integration (masking, alignment of `f` tokens) is only qualitatively described; implementation errors could invalidate results.
- Training details (additional steps from EAGLE checkpoint, optimizer settings) are vague; reproducibility is at risk.
- Evaluation limited to SpecBench with Llama-3 3B/8B; unknown generalizability to other architectures or domains.
- No analysis of MoE routing stability or `f` vector quality; potential for collapse or noisy guidance.

## Confidence
- **Future-Aware Drafting (contemplate tokens):** Medium. Plausible mechanism but relies on unverified property of `f`.
- **Context-Adaptive MoE:** Medium. Adds capacity but hyperparameter sensitivity may undermine gains.
- **Anchor Sampling & Replication:** Medium. Robustness claim lacks evidence on appropriate window size.
- **Overall speedup (8–11% over EAGLE-3):** Medium. Strong empirical results but depend on unreported settings.
