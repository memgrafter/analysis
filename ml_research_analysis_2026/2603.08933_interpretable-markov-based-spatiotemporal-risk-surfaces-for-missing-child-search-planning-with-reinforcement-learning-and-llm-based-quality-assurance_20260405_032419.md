# Interpretable Markov-Based Spatiotemporal Risk Surfaces for Missing-Child Search Planning with Reinforcement Learning and LLM-Based Quality Assurance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08933  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08933  
- **Reference count:** 2  
- **Primary result:** Guardian produces calibrated, operationally useful search plans, with >50% probability mass concentrated in the Tidewater region across 24/48/72-hour horizons and containment radii expanding from ~20 to mid-20 miles.

## Executive Summary
Fragmented initial evidence and a lack of dynamic geospatial tools hinder early missing-child investigations. This paper presents Guardian, a three-layer system that addresses these gaps. First, a sparse, interpretable Markov chain forecasts spatiotemporal risk using road accessibility, seclusion, and corridor biases. Second, reinforcement learning translates these forecasts into prioritized, actionable search zone plans that respect operational resource constraints. Third, an LLM performs post-hoc quality assurance, reweighting zone priorities based on narrative plausibility. Evaluation on a realistic Virginia synthetic case (GRD-2025-001541) demonstrates structured spatial uncertainty propagation and actionable outputs.

## Method Summary
Guardian ingests fragmented case data (narratives, geocoded points) into a structured format. It then builds an initial risk distribution by blending a Gaussian around the last-known point with a historical hotspot prior (KDE). This distribution is propagated forward in time via a sparse, feature-weighted Markov transition matrix that varies by day/night. The cumulative belief map is fed to a reinforcement learning agent, which selects and ranks geometric search zones (circles, sectors, rings) to maximize coverage efficiency while minimizing overlap. Finally, a constrained LLM assesses the narrative plausibility of each proposed zone and multiplicatively adjusts its priority. The system outputs ranked zones and aggregated risk surfaces.

## Key Results
- Probability mass remained concentrated in the Tidewater region (>50%) across all forecast horizons (24, 48, 72 hours).
- Containment radii expanded from approximately 20 miles at 24 hours to the mid-20 miles range at 72 hours.
- The system generated calibrated, operationally useful products: ranked sectors, hotspots, and containment rings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Markov Forecasting**  
A seeded Gaussian around the IPP is blended with a KDE-based historical prior. This initial distribution is propagated via a sparse KNN transition matrix where edge weights are energy-based functions of geospatial features: road accessibility cost (lower cost → higher transition), seclusion score (higher → higher), and corridor score (higher → higher). Separate day/night matrices parameterize these features differently. After each step, survival-style decay reduces confidence, and boundary masking re-normalizes probability. This transforms fragmented evidence into a structured, evolving risk surface with conserved probability mass under a memoryless, feature-dependent random walk assumption. *Break condition:* True mobility exhibits long-range dependencies, strong non-Markovian goal-directed travel, or grossly misaligned feature layers.

**Mechanism 2: Reinforcement Learning for Zone Planning**  
The RL agent observes the cumulative belief map (or aggregated sector masses) and a time window. Its action space selects geometric zones. The reward function combines: (1) early capture incentive (mass covered early), (2) coverage efficiency penalty (redundancy/overlap), and (3) plausibility shaping (soft constraints from Markov features like corridor proximity). Policies are learned to maximize cumulative reward across 0–72h windows with decaying weights (1.0, 0.7, 0.5). This converts probabilistic maps into prioritized, actionable search zones that respect resource limits by balancing mass coverage against spatial efficiency. *Break condition:* Resource constraints are highly dynamic/non-linear, optimal policy depends on unmodeled sequential dependencies between zones, or reward shaping misaligns with investigator intuition.

**Mechanism 3: LLM-Based Quality Assurance**  
For each RL-proposed zone, a schema-constrained LLM receives a structured case summary (movement profile, timeline, zone geometry) and outputs a bounded plausibility score [0,1] with a rationale. This score multiplicatively adjusts the zone's original RL priority. The design is decoupled: Markov transitions and RL rewards remain unchanged; the LLM acts only as a final filter/reweighter. This introduces narrative coherence checks (e.g., "child on foot would not cross interstate") without altering the quantitative forecast. *Break condition:* LLM rationales frequently contradict clear evidence, reweighting systematically demotes later-correct zones, or case nuances exceed the structured summary's capacity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Markov Mobility Modeling:** Understands how feature costs (`c_road`, `s_sec`, `c_corr`) are translated into transition probabilities via energy-based models (`w_ij ~ exp(-∑β*feature)`) and normalized. *Why needed:* Core predictive engine; interprets risk surfaces. *Quick check:* How would increasing `β_corridor` change spatial diffusion from an urban IPP?
- **Reinforcement Learning for Constrained Resource Allocation:** Grasps the trade-off encoded in the RL reward function (mass vs. overlap vs. plausibility) that explains zone prioritization. *Why needed:* Bridges continuous probability maps and discrete, bounded search assets. *Quick check:* What happens if the "coverage efficiency" penalty term is set to zero?
- **LLM-as-Post-Hoc-Validator Pattern:** Recognizes the decoupling: LLM can flag inconsistencies but cannot override the mathematical forecast. *Why needed:* Introduces narrative/contextual reasoning separate from quantitative model; crucial for auditing. *Quick check:* Can an LLM plausibility score cause the Markov forecast's probability mass to be redistributed? If not, what does it actually change?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Parser Pack (Stage 1) → Core System (Stage 2): LLM Preprocessing, Clustering/Hotspots (K-Means/DBSCAN/KDE), Layer 1 (Markov), Layer 2 (RL), Layer 3 (LLM QA). Outputs `search_plan.json` (sectors, rings, grid forecasts) and `zones.jsonl` (ranked zones with QA scores).  
**Critical path:** Raw PDF → Parser Pack (structured case) → LLM preprocessing/clustering → **Markov forecast** → **RL zone generation** → **LLM QA reweighting** → Final search plan. The three predictive layers are sequential and mandatory.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Markov sparsity (KNN) vs. expressivity; RL geometric action space simplicity vs. flexibility; LLM efficiency (3B) vs. depth of plausibility cues.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- *Markov:* Unrealistically broad diffusion, absent corridor bias, `p72` not more diffuse than `p24`.  
- *RL:* All zones near IPP, excessive overlap (>70%), ignoring clear high-mass sectors.  
- *LLM QA:* Volatile scores for similar zones, rationales citing absent facts, systematic downranking of corridor-adjacent zones for "on-foot" profiles.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Markov feature ablation:** Run forecast with only `β_road`, only `β_seclusion`, only `β_corridor`; confirm Tidewater concentration persists with corridor on, diffuses with seclusion-only.  
2. **RL reward sensitivity:** Vary coverage efficiency penalty weight (0 to 1); measure change in average zone separation and number of zones selected.  
3. **LLM QA consistency:** Have Qwen and LLaMA score plausibility for 50 zones; compute Cohen's κ; low κ (<0.6) indicates unstable validation.

## Limitations
- Synthetic validation only: Core claims rely on a single non-public synthetic case; no real-world or cross-jurisdiction evaluation.
- Parameter sensitivity: Feature weights (β) and decay constants lack sensitivity analysis; optimality for different geographies (urban vs. rural) is unknown.
- LLM QA opacity: Plausibility reweighting lacks defined failure thresholds; risk of systematic bias from omitted narrative cues is unmeasured.

## Next Checks
1. **Markov sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary β_feature weights (±50%) and decay T1/2 on the Virginia case; quantify stability of the >50% Tidewater concentration claim and containment radius expansion.
2. **RL vs. expert A/B test:** For 5 diverse synthetic cases (urban, rural, corridor-heavy), compare Guardian's ranked zones against plans from experienced SAR coordinators; measure overlap and mass coverage efficiency.
3. **LLM inter-rater reliability:** Have two constrained LLMs (e.g., Qwen and LLaMA) score plausibility for 100 RL-proposed zones; compute Cohen's κ; κ < 0.6 indicates unstable validation requiring schema refinement.
