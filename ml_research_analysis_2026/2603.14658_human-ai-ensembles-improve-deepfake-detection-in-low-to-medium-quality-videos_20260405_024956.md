# Human-AI Ensembles Improve Deepfake Detection in Low-to-Medium Quality Videos

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14658
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14658
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Human-AI ensembles eliminate catastrophic deepfake detection failures on low-quality videos, where AI alone performs near chance.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates human and AI deepfake detectors across professional (DF40) and mobile-phone-quality (CharadesDF) videos. Humans consistently outperformed state-of-the-art AI systems on both datasets, with the performance gap widening significantly on lower-quality mobile videos where AI accuracy collapsed. Critically, human and AI errors were complementary: humans struggled most with high-fidelity deepfakes, while AI frequently misclassified authentic videos. Combining humans and AI via simple averaging ensembles—supplemented by quality-weighted judge selection—reduced high-confidence errors from 17–32% to below 2% and completely removed catastrophic failures (errors ≥0.7). The findings demonstrate that robust, real-world deepfake detection requires human-AI collaboration, especially for videos with suboptimal quality.

## Method Summary
The study compared 200 human participants and 32 AI detector architectures across two video datasets. DF40 comprises 1,000 professionally sourced YouTube videos (500 real, 500 fake). CharadesDF contains 1,000 mobile-phone recordings (500 authentic, 500 fake generated via FaceFusion using five face-swapping models). AI models were trained on established benchmarks (FaceForensics++, CelebDF-v2, DF40-train) using DeepfakeBench protocols. Human evaluators rated 60 videos each on a 5-point confidence scale, converted to continuous scores. Ensemble methods were implemented: a within-group quality-weighted voting (leave-one-out accuracy weighting) and a hybrid ensemble averaging human and AI group predictions equally. Performance was assessed via accuracy, AUC, F1, catastrophic failure rate (CFR; absolute error ≥0.7), and calibration (ECE).

## Key Results
- Human accuracy was significantly higher than AI on both datasets: DF40 (0.743 vs. 0.610) and CharadesDF (0.784 vs. 0.537).
- AI performance collapsed on mobile videos (CharadesDF), nearing random chance, while humans remained robust.
- Hybrid human-AI ensembles reduced high-confidence errors (≥0.7) from 17–32% (individual humans/AI) to under 2% and eliminated all catastrophic failures (CFR = 0%).
- Human and AI error patterns were highly complementary (low phi coefficient), with humans missing high-quality deepfakes and AI misclassifying real videos.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The ensemble success stems from complementary error profiles between humans and AI. Humans leverage holistic scene understanding and artefact detection (e.g., unnatural lighting, audio-visual sync) that AI models miss, especially in low-quality videos where technical artefacts are inconsistent. Conversely, AI systems are sensitive to subtle pixel-level patterns trained on high-quality benchmarks but fail under distribution shifts (e.g., mobile compression, variable lighting). Quality-weighted judge selection further improves robustness by upweighting reliable evaluators on a per-video basis. The hybrid averaging merges these orthogonal signals, where human intuition corrects AI’s false positives on real videos, and AI’s texture analysis catches deepfakes that evade human scrutiny. This synergy is most effective in low-quality regimes where AI’s signal degrades but human heuristics remain partially valid.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Raw Videos -> Quality Feature Extractor -> Quality Metrics; Raw Videos -> AI Detectors -> AI Video Scores; Raw Videos -> Human Evaluators -> Human Scores; Quality Metrics + AI Scores + Human Scores -> Ensemble Aggregator (Quality-Weighted Voting -> Within-group Ensembles; Equal-Weight Averaging -> Hybrid Ensemble) -> Final Predictions.

**Critical Path:**  
1. Preprocess videos (face alignment, frame extraction).  
2. Compute quality metrics (face size, blur, contrast).  
3. Run 32 AI models (trained on high-quality benchmarks) to generate frame and video-level fake probabilities.  
4. Collect human confidence ratings (5-point scale, mapped to [0,1]).  
5. For each dataset, form AI group by averaging model outputs (simple or quality-weighted).  
6. Form human group similarly from participant scores.  
7. Create hybrid ensemble by averaging human-group and AI-group scores equally.  
8. Compute binary predictions (threshold 0.5) and evaluation metrics.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Human vs AI scalability:** Humans provide high accuracy but are slow and costly; AI is fast but brittle to quality shifts.  
- **Ensemble complexity:** Quality-weighted voting requires reliable per-video accuracy estimates (leave-one-out), increasing computation but adapting to individual judge reliability. Simple averaging in hybrid ensemble sacrifices some optimality for robustness and simplicity.  
- **Confidence calibration:** AI models output probabilistic scores but are overconfident on mobile videos; human confidence scales (5-point) are subjective but better calibrated. The ensemble inherits the average calibration.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- AI-only failure: Accuracy near 50% on CharadesDF, high CFR (>17%), poor calibration (high ECE).  
- Human-only failure: Missing high-quality deepfakes (e.g., DF40), inconsistent across individuals.  
- Ensemble failure: If human and AI errors correlate (e.g., both struggle with same video subset), hybrid CFR remains high. The paper finds error complementarity (phi ≈ 0), preventing this.

**Three First Experiments:**  
1. **Replicate AI baseline:** Train Xception and EfficientNet-B4 on FaceForensics++, evaluate on DF40 test; verify accuracy ~0.60 as reported.  
2. **Simulate human ensemble:** Using paper’s mean human accuracy per dataset, generate synthetic binary predictions for 200 humans; compute within-human quality-weighted ensemble accuracy vs. simple average.  
3. **Test hybrid ensemble on CharadesDF:** Combine simulated human group (from Exp 2) with AI group (from Exp 1); measure CFR and accuracy drop relative to DF40. Confirm CFR reduction to <2%.

## Limitations
- CharadesDF dataset is not publicly released; exact deepfake generation parameters (e.g., FaceFusion model assignments, clipping rules) must be reverse-engineered, risking replication deviation.
- AI training details (specific data splits, augmentation strengths, hyperparameters per model) are not enumerated; reliance on DeepfakeBench defaults may not reproduce paper’s exact AI performance.
- Raw human response data is unavailable; simulating human predictions from aggregated statistics may misrepresent individual variance and confidence distributions, potentially skewing ensemble outcomes.
