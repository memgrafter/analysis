# Linguistically Informed Graph Model and Semantic Contrastive Learning for Korean Short Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03652
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03652
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: Achieves 84.03% accuracy and 82.69% F1 on KLUE YNAT, with average F1 improvement of 9.8% over baselines.

## Executive Summary
LIGRAM addresses the challenges of Korean short text classification—agglutinative morphology, flexible word order, and sparse context—by constructing a hierarchical heterogeneous graph that integrates morpheme, part-of-speech, and named entity subgraphs. It further refines semantic boundaries through Semantics-aware Contrastive Learning (SemCon) using pseudo-topic distributions. Evaluated on four Korean datasets, LIGRAM achieves state-of-the-art performance, notably 84.03% accuracy and 82.69% F1 on the multi-class KLUE YNAT dataset, and shows average F1 gains of 9.8% over baselines. Ablation studies confirm the importance of both the multi-level graph integration and SemCon. The approach demonstrates promise for low-resource, morphologically complex languages.

## Method Summary
LIGRAM processes raw Korean text via the Kiwi morphological analyzer, extracting morphemes, POS tags, and named entities (using KPF-BERT-NER). It builds three independent heterogeneous subgraphs: G_w (morpheme nodes with PMI edges), G_p (POS nodes with PMI edges), and G_e (entity nodes with cosine similarity edges). Each subgraph is encoded by a two-layer GCN, producing node embeddings. For each document, subgraph embeddings are obtained via attention pooling (TF-IDF weights for morphemes/POS, binary mask for entities) and concatenated into a document vector x_s^i. A document-document graph G_s is constructed by thresholding the dot-product similarities of these vectors (δ=2.7). A final GCN processes G_s, and a linear layer yields classification logits. SemCon forms pseudo-topic distributions via a softmax over a projection of x_s^i; contrastive loss (NT-Xent) groups documents sharing the same argmax pseudo-topic. The total loss combines cross-entropy and λ·contrastive loss (λ=0.7). The model is trained with AdamW on full batches, using early stopping.

## Key Results
- SOTA accuracy (84.03%) and F1 (82.69%) on KLUE YNAT (7-class news headlines).
- Average F1 improvement of 9.8% across four datasets over strong baselines.
- Ablations reveal that both hierarchical graph integration and SemCon are critical, with SemCon contributing especially to multi-class datasets (e.g., +21.1% F1 on YNAT when added).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A hierarchical heterogeneous graph explicitly captures Korean linguistic structures (agglutinative morphology, flexible word order, omitted particles) to compensate for sparse context in short texts.
- **Mechanism:** The model constructs three independent subgraphs—morpheme (G_w), part-of-speech (G_p), and named entity (G_e)—each encoding different linguistic cues. Morpheme nodes capture lexical semantics and compositionality. POS nodes model grammatical relations to restore omitted functional morphemes. Entity nodes provide semantic anchors. These subgraphs are processed via separate GCNs, then hierarchically pooled and concatenated into a final document embedding x_s^i. This multi-level integration allows reconstruction of missing contextual cues via cross-graph dependencies.
- **Core assumption:** Korean short texts lose critical grammatical meaning due to omitted particles/endings; explicitly modeling morpheme-level, syntactic, and entity relations jointly recovers this lost structure more effectively than a single homogeneous word-level graph.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "constructs sub-graphs at the morpheme, part-of-speech, and named-entity levels and hierarchically integrates them to compensate for the limited contextual information in short texts while precisely capturing the grammatical and semantic dependencies inherent in Korean."
  - [section 3.2] Details the construction of each subgraph with PMI-based edges and specific initialization.
- **Break condition:** If the hierarchical pooling fails to weight the three subgraphs appropriately, or if the inter-subgraph edges in the final document-document graph do not capture meaningful cross-linguistic relations, the integrated representation will not surpass a well-tuned single-graph baseline.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Semantics-aware contrastive learning (SemCon) based on pseudo-topic distributions establishes clearer decision boundaries for ambiguous short texts by grouping semantically related documents.
- **Mechanism:** After obtaining the document embedding x_s^i, a softmax layer produces a pseudo-topic distribution p_i ∈ ℝ^C. Documents sharing the same top predicted pseudo-topic are treated as positives; others as negatives. The contrastive loss (NT-Xent) maximizes similarity for positive pairs and minimizes it for negatives. This forces the embedding space to align with latent topical clusters rather than surface lexical patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Short texts with the same true class share a latent thematic structure that can be approximated by a pseudo-topic distribution derived from the model’s own (noisy) predictions; this distribution is a better proxy for semantic similarity than instance-level augmentations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "apply Semantics-aware Contrastive Learning (SemCon) to reflect semantic similarity across documents, enabling the model to establish clearer decision boundaries even in short texts where class distinctions are often ambiguous."
  - [section 3.3] Defines pseudo-topic and contrastive loss.
- **Break condition:** If pseudo-topic distributions are extremely noisy early in training, the positive/negative pair construction becomes arbitrary, potentially pushing semantically similar documents apart.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The unified loss function (cross-entropy + contrastive loss) jointly optimizes for classification accuracy and semantically cohesive representations, leveraging both labeled signal and latent structure.
- **Mechanism:** The total loss L = L_ce + λ L_con combines supervised classification (using gold labels for document nodes) with unsupervised semantic alignment (using pseudo-topics). L_ce ensures discriminative class boundaries on the labeled set. L_con, applied across all documents, smooths the embedding space by encouraging documents of inferred topics to cluster together, which is valuable in low-resource settings.
- **Core assumption:** In low-resource short-text scenarios, the model benefits from an auxiliary signal derived from its own topical clustering to regularize representations, preventing overfitting to sparse labeled examples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] Defines combined loss with hyperparameter λ.
- **Break condition:** If λ is poorly tuned (too high), the model may over-prioritize semantic clustering at the expense of classification accuracy; if too low, the contrastive regularizer provides no benefit.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (raw text) -> Kiwi analyzer & KPF-BERT-NER -> three subgraph builders (G_w, G_p, G_e) -> three 2-layer GCNs -> hierarchical pooling (attention by TF-IDF/binary) -> concatenated x_s^i -> document adjacency A_s (threshold dot-product) -> final 2-layer GCN on A_s -> linear classifier -> predictions. SemCon module: x_s^i -> projection -> softmax (pseudo-topic) -> contrastive loss. Loss combiner: L = L_ce + λ·L_con -> AdamW.
- **Critical path:** Preprocessing -> subgraph construction -> independent GCN propagation -> hierarchical pooling -> doc-doc graph construction -> contrastive pair generation -> joint loss -> backpropagation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Graph sparsity vs. expressivity: PMI window size and similarity threshold δ control edge density. Sparse graphs reduce noise but may miss subtle relations; dense graphs increase quadratic cost.
  - Contrastive weight λ: High λ enforces clustering but may dilute classification signal; low λ risks ineffective regularization. Requires tuning.
  - Subgraph independence vs. integration: Separate graphs improve interpretability but delay cross-level interactions until pooling.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. POS-only or Entity-only graphs perform near-random → hierarchical pooling fails to extract useful signals.
  2. w/o SemCon matches or exceeds full model → pseudo-topics too noisy or λ misconfigured.
  3. Document graph A_s extremely dense or sparse (threshold δ mis-set) → memory blowup or disconnected graph.
  4. Training loss diverges or contrastive loss dominates → λ too high or temperature mis-scaled.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate single subgraph performance (only G_w, only G_p, only G_e) to confirm necessity of hierarchical integration.
  2. Ablate SemCon (λ=0) to measure its impact on validation F1, expecting significant drop on multi-class datasets.
  3. Sensitivity analysis on document-doc threshold δ: vary δ and plot performance vs. graph density to find optimal range.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Can the linguistically informed graph framework be effectively adapted to other agglutinative languages beyond Korean? The conclusion suggests this as future work, but cross-lingual evaluation is lacking. Evidence would be systematic testing on Turkish, Finnish, or Hungarian short-text benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
Why do large language models (LLMs) underperform LIGRAM on multi-class short-text classification despite superior results on binary sentiment tasks? The paper notes LLMs are optimized for generation, but the discrepancy in multi-class settings is not analyzed. Evidence would be controlled experiments varying class number and comparing embedding space properties.

### Open Question 3
Why does the POS-only subgraph perform poorly compared to the morpheme graph, and which specific grammatical relations in Korean short texts are not captured by POS tags alone? The paper suggests POS alone is insufficient but does not dissect the failure modes. Evidence would be error analysis of POS-only predictions or augmenting POS features with morphological attributes.

### Open Question 4
How does using pseudo-topic distributions (from softmax outputs) compare to using ground-truth topic labels for contrastive pair formation? The paper claims pseudo-topics allow label-free learning but does not evaluate the trade-off with gold topics. Evidence would be an ablation comparing SemCon with gold topics vs. pseudo-topics on accuracy and clustering metrics.

### Open Question 5
What is the practical scalability limit of LIGRAM given its O(N²d) complexity from document-document graph construction, and can approximate methods mitigate this without performance loss? The paper notes the bottleneck but does not test scaling. Evidence would be empirical scaling experiments and evaluation of approximations (e.g., top-k neighbors).

## Limitations
- Several key implementation details are unspecified (e.g., entity graph edge threshold, exact projection head design, KPF-BERT-NER usage), hindering faithful reproduction.
- Model effectiveness is only validated on four Korean datasets; cross-lingual generalization to other agglutinative languages remains untested.
- The approach relies on high-quality morphemic and NER tools (Kiwi, KPF-BERT-NER), which may be unavailable or error-prone in other domains or low-resource languages.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical heterogeneous graph captures Korean linguistic structures: **Medium** (supported by ablation but limited by unspecified edge thresholds and integration details).
- SemCon with pseudo-topics improves decision boundaries: **Medium** (ablation shows benefit, but pseudo-topic reliability not directly validated).
- Overall SOTA performance on the presented Korean datasets: **High** (strong empirical results).
- Generalization to other morphologically complex languages: **Low** (no cross-lingual experiments).
