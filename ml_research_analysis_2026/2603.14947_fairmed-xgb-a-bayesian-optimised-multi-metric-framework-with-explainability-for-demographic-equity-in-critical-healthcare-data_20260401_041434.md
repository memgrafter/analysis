# FairMed-XGB: A Bayesian-Optimised Multi-Metric Framework with Explainability for Demographic Equity in Critical Healthcare Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14947  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14947  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: FairMed-XGB reduces gender-based prediction bias by 40–51% on MIMIC-IV-ED and 10–19% on eICU while maintaining predictive accuracy (AUC-ROC drop <0.02) and provides SHAP-based explainability.

## Executive Summary
FairMed-XGB tackles gender-based prediction bias in critical care machine learning, which undermines clinical trust and equity. The framework integrates a multi-metric fairness-aware loss—combining Statistical Parity Difference, Theil Index, and Wasserstein Distance—into an XGBoost classifier, with hyperparameters tuned via Bayesian optimisation. Evaluated on seven cohorts from MIMIC-IV-ED and eICU, it achieves substantial bias reductions (SPD down 40–51% and 10–19%, Theil Index collapsing to near‑zero, Wasserstein Distance down 20–72%) with negligible accuracy loss (AUC‑ROC drop <0.02). SHAP explanations confirm reduced reliance on gender‑proxy features, offering clinicians transparent insights into the bias correction.

## Method Summary
Data preprocessing uses MIMIC‑IV‑ED and eICU: adult patients with binary gender, label‑encoding of categoricals, standardization of continuous features, and an 80/20 stratified train‑test split by gender and outcome. The core method modifies XGBoost with a custom loss: ℒ_total = ℒ_log + λ·(w₁·SPD + w₂·Theil + w₃·W), where λ balances the fairness penalty. Hyperparameters (λ, w₁, w₂, w₃) are searched via Bayesian optimisation (e.g., scikit‑optimize) with cross‑validation, maximising J(θ) = α·AUC – (1–α)·ℒ_fair. The best configuration retrains the final FairMed‑XGB model. Post‑mitigation, fairness metrics (SPD, Theil, Wasserstein), AUC‑ROC, and SHAP values are computed to evaluate bias reduction and explainability.

## Key Results
- Fairness metrics improved: Statistical Parity Difference reduced by 40–51% (MIMIC‑IV‑ED) and 10–19% (eICU); Theil Index collapsed to near‑zero; Wasserstein Distance reduced by 20–72%.  
- Predictive accuracy preserved: AUC‑ROC drop less than 0.02.  
- SHAP analysis confirmed reduced influence of gender‑proxy features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The fairness‑aware loss directly penalises disparities in predictions across gender groups during training, forcing the model to learn representations less correlated with gender. Combining three complementary fairness metrics captures various aspects of bias (statistical parity, outcome equality, distributional similarity). Bayesian optimisation efficiently navigates the trade‑off space between accuracy and fairness, while XGBoost’s gradient boosting framework supports custom differentiable losses. SHAP then provides post‑hoc explanations to verify that gender‑proxy features are down‑weighted, enhancing transparency and clinical trust.

## Foundational Learning
**Statistical Parity Difference (SPD)**  
- Why needed: Quantifies the difference in positive outcome rates between demographic groups; a fundamental fairness metric.  
- Quick check: Compute SPD on baseline model predictions to establish initial bias level.

**Theil Index**  
- Why needed: Measures inequality in outcome distributions across groups, capturing fairness beyond simple rate differences.  
- Quick check: Calculate Theil Index on model predictions; ensure it decreases after mitigation.

**Wasserstein Distance**  
- Why needed: Assesses distributional divergence between group predictions, sensitive to shifts in the full predictive distribution.  
- Quick check: Compare Wasserstein distances before and after fairness adjustment.

**Bayesian Optimisation**  
- Why needed: Efficiently searches hyperparameter spaces (λ, weights) with expensive objective evaluations, balancing fairness and accuracy.  
- Quick check: Run a BO loop (e.g., 5‑fold CV) for 20 iterations; observe convergence of the objective.

**SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations)**  
- Why needed: Provides interpretable feature attributions to validate that gender‑proxy features are not driving decisions.  
- Quick check: Compute SHAP values for top features; compare absolute contributions of gender and proxies between baseline and FairMed‑XGB.

**Stratified Train‑Test Split**  
- Why needed: Preserves group proportions in splits, ensuring unbiased evaluation of fairness metrics.  
- Quick check: Verify that train and test sets have similar gender and outcome distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Raw Data (MIMIC‑IV‑ED, eICU) → Filtering/Splitting → Feature Encoding & Standardization → Baseline XGBoost → Custom Fairness Loss (SPD+Theil+Wasserstein) → Bayesian Optimisation (λ, w₁,w₂,w₃) → Optimal Hyperparameters → Retrained FairMed‑XGB → SHAP Analysis

**Critical path**  
1. Preprocess data (filter, encode, split).  
2. Define custom fairness loss and integrate with XGBoost training loop.  
3. Set up Bayesian optimisation to tune hyperparameters by maximising J(θ) on validation folds.  
4. Train final model with best hyperparameters on full training set.  
5. Evaluate fairness and accuracy metrics; generate SHAP explanations.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Multi‑metric fairness vs. simplicity: Combining three fairness metrics improves robustness but complicates hyper‑parameter tuning and requires careful balancing.  
- Bayesian optimisation cost vs. efficiency: BO is sample‑efficient but requires many evaluations, increasing computational time.  
- Accuracy constraint: The <0.02 AUC drop limit restricts the strength of fairness regularisation, potentially capping achievable fairness gains.  
- Explainability overhead: SHAP adds interpretability but scales poorly to very large datasets and may produce unstable attributions.

**Failure signatures**  
- Training instability: Custom loss gradients cause divergent updates; monitor loss components and gradient norms.  
- Over‑penalisation: Fairness hyperparameters too high lead to AUC drop beyond 0.02; track validation AUC during BO.  
- SHAP proxy ambiguity: Failure to show reduced gender‑proxy importance indicates incomplete bias mitigation; define proxies clearly.  
- Data leakage: Poorly stratified splits or preprocessing leakage inflate metrics; verify split integrity.

**First experiments**  
1. **Baseline replication**: Train standard XGBoost (binary logistic) without fairness constraints; record SPD, Theil, Wasserstein, and AUC‑ROC.  
2. **Fairness tuning**: Implement the custom loss and run a small BO (λ∈[0.1,10], equal weights); observe impact on fairness‑accuracy trade‑off.  
3. **Explainability check**: Compute SHAP values for baseline and FairMed‑XGB; compare feature importance, focusing on gender and plausible proxies (e.g., diagnosis codes, marital status).
