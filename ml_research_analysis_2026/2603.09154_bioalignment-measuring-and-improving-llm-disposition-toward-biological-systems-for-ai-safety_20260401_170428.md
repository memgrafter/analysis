# Bioalignment: Measuring and Improving LLM Disposition Toward Biological Systems for AI Safety

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09154
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09154
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: QLoRA fine-tuning on a biomedical corpus significantly increased biological preference in LLMs without degrading general capabilities.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces the Bioalignment benchmark to measure and correct LLM bias toward synthetic over biological solutions. Baseline tests on 10 models reveal a widespread pro-synthetic bias. Targeted fine-tuning with a modest biomedical corpus using QLoRA shifts the disposition toward biology while preserving performance on standard benchmarks, demonstrating that model preferences are malleable through curated data—a step toward aligning AI with biological preservation.

## Method Summary
The Bioalignment benchmark presents 50 prompts, each with six solution sources (three biological, three synthetic). Models output Kelly criterion parameters (e.g., `p_up`); the primary metric, `Δp_up`, is the difference in average "probability of discovery" scores between biological and synthetic sources. To correct bias, the authors performed QLoRA fine-tuning on a ∼22M-token corpus derived from PubMed Central, mixing 65% raw text (continued pretraining) and 35% instruction-formatted examples. Fine-tuning used 4-bit quantization, LoRA rank 16, a learning rate of 5e-5 (Llama) or 1e-5 (Qwen), and gradient clipping. After training on a 25% subset (∼5.5M tokens), models were re-evaluated on the benchmark and on MMLU, HellaSwag, and ARC to ensure no capability loss.

## Key Results
- Baseline tests on 10 models revealed a widespread pro-synthetic bias (negative `Δp_up` scores).
- QLoRA fine-tuning produced significant increases in biological preference: Llama 3B (+0.132, d=0.87, p<0.001) and Qwen 3B (+0.054, d=0.58, p<0.01).
- The shift generalized across domains (materials, energy, manufacturing, algorithms) and was achieved with minimal data (0.5–5.5M tokens) without degradation on MMLU, HellaSwag, or ARC.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Exposure to a corpus emphasizing biological problem-solving shifts model dispositions by altering the statistical representation of biological vs. synthetic solution pathways in the model's parameter space.  
**Mechanism:** Continued-pretraining and instruction fine-tuning on PMC papers increases the model's internal association between problem contexts and biological solution concepts, thereby raising estimated `p_up` values for biological sources.  
**Core assumption:** Biases are malleable through curated data exposure, and `Δp_up` captures a stable disposition shift rather than superficial prompt following.  
**Break condition:** If the corpus lacks viable biological examples or if pre-training biases are exceptionally strong, the shift may not occur.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The `Δp_up` metric provides a valid, scalar measure of a model's latent disposition toward biological vs. synthetic solutions.  
**Mechanism:** Prompting the model to output structured Kelly parameters forces explicit comparison of perceived probabilities of success; averaging biological vs. synthetic `p_up` isolates a domain-general preference bias.  
**Core assumption:** Elicited probability estimates reflect internalized knowledge and value judgments, not arbitrary numbers.  
**Break condition:** If models cannot parse the structured output format or if prompt options are imbalanced in plausibility, the metric becomes invalid.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) with a small, targeted corpus is sufficient to induce large shifts because it locally adjusts key conceptual representations without full retraining.  
**Mechanism:** Low-rank adapters modify attention and MLP weights; training on biology-centric text specializes these adapters to activate biological pathways, generalizing zero‑shot to the benchmark.  
**Core assumption:** The disposition resides in a low‑dimensional subspace amenable to targeted adaptation; core knowledge need not be changed.  
**Break condition:** If target modules are not primary loci for encoding preferences, or if architecture‑specific sensitivities cause instability (e.g., Qwen with mixed data), QLoRA may fail.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept:** Kelly Criterion Adaptation for LLM Evaluation  
- *Why needed:* The core metric (`Δp_up`) builds on the Kelly formula; understanding `p_up` as the probability of discovering a mechanism that outperforms the current best-in-class is essential to interpret what the model estimates and how it reflects perceived value of solution sources.  
- *Quick check:* If a model assigns a higher `p_up` to a biological source than a synthetic one for a given problem, what does this imply about its internal ranking of those solution pathways?

**Concept:** QLoRA (Quantized Low‑Rank Adaptation)  
- *Why needed:* The bias‑correction mechanism relies on this parameter‑efficient method; it freezes the base model and trains only small adapter matrices, making it feasible to shift dispositions with minimal compute and data.  
- *Quick check:* Why is QLoRA particularly suitable for an experiment aiming to change a model's disposition without a massive retraining corpus?

**Concept:** Disposition vs. Capability in LLMs  
- *Why needed:* The paper's safety motivation hinges on distinguishing a model’s capability to generate correct solutions from its disposition (innate preference) to value biological systems. The key result is shifting disposition without harming capability scores on MMLU/ARC.  
- *Quick check:* How could a model be highly capable in a domain like materials science yet still exhibit a "pro‑synthetic" disposition in how it weighs solution sources?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Corpus Engine → Benchmark Suite → Fine‑tuning Pipeline → Evaluation Harness → Capability Checker

**Critical path:**  
Corpus Preparation → Model Baseline Evaluation → QLoRA Configuration & Training → Bioalignment Re‑evaluation → Capability Benchmarking → Statistical Analysis

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Data Format:* Mixing continued‑pretraining (65%) with instruction (35%) may deepen concept integration but caused instability for Qwen; instruction‑only is simpler but risks surface‑level cloning.  
- *LoRA Rank (r):* Higher rank captures more complex shifts but increases memory and overfitting risk; the paper found moderate benefit with r=16.  
- *Token Budget:* Using only 25% of the corpus (~5.5M tokens) achieved near‑neutrality for Llama; additional data yielded diminishing returns.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. **Parse rate < 80%:** Model fails to output required table format. *Mitigation:* adjust prompt, use structured decoding, or switch model.  
2. **No shift in Δp_up (Δ < 0.02):** Fine‑tuning had no effect. *Check:* verify training loss decreased, data loaded correctly, and learning rate appropriate.  
3. **Capability degradation > 2.5% on MMLU:** General knowledge harmed. *Mitigation:* reduce corpus size, lower LR, increase LoRA dropout.  
4. **Training instability (NaNs):** Often due to 4‑bit issues, especially with mixed data. *Mitigation:* use instruction‑only data, lower LR, ensure BF16, apply gradient clipping (norm 1.0).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Parse‑Validation Run:** Pass 5 bioalignment prompts through the full evaluation pipeline for the base model. Confirm parse rate >90% and `Δp_up` within the reported baseline range.  
2. **Sanity‑Check Fine‑Tune:** Run a minimal QLoRA fine‑tune for 1 epoch on 100 instruction examples. Expect a small positive `Δp_up` shift (e.g., +0.02 to +0.05).  
3. **Ablation Mini‑Test:** Replicate one ablation: train with CPT‑only data on 1M tokens. Expected result: near‑zero or negative shift (around -0.008), confirming the importance of instruction formatting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling to larger models:** Does the bioalignment effect from QLoRA fine‑tuning scale to 70B‑parameter or frontier‑scale architectures, or is it limited to small open‑weight models? The authors list this as future work; it remains unresolved because only 3B models were tested. Evidence would come from replicating the protocol on larger models.  
2. **Persistence under further training:** Does bioalignment persist after subsequent safety‑oriented training (RLHF, instruction‑tuning)? This is an acknowledged limitation; the effect may be fragile and overwritten by later alignment signals. A follow‑up study applying RLHF to a bioaligned model and tracking `Δp_up` would be needed.  
3. **Benchmark bias and validity:** How does the involvement of Claude Opus in prompt development introduce systematic measurement bias, and how can a more construct‑valid, model‑agnostic benchmark be constructed? This threat to validity is noted; resolution would require a blinded regeneration of prompts by diverse human experts and cross‑model evaluation.  
4. **Domain‑specific variance:** What explains the stronger baseline pro‑synthetic bias and larger training correction in the Algorithms domain? The paper speculates but offers no causal mechanism. Evidence could be gathered by analyzing the training corpus’s representation of biological vs. synthetic solutions in Algorithms versus other domains.  
5. **Safety impact on downstream behavior:** Does a higher bioalignment score correlate with reduced propensity to recommend actions harmful to biological systems in agentic tasks? The link between `Δp_up` and consequential decisions is unestablished. Developing behavioral evaluations that simulate high‑stakes trade‑offs and correlating results with `Δp_up` would test this relationship.

## Limitations
- Incomplete reporting of training hyperparameters (e.g., data mixing, batch size, gradient accumulation) hampers exact replication.
- Potential benchmark bias from using Claude Opus in prompt development and possible imbalance between biological/synthetic option plausibility.
- Findings are limited to two 3B‑parameter models; generalizability to larger scales, frontier models, and after subsequent alignment training is unknown.
