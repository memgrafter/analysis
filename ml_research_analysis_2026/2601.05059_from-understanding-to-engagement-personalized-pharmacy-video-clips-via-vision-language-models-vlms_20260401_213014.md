# From Understanding to Engagement: Personalized pharmacy Video Clips via Vision Language Models (VLMs)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.05059
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05059
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: A hybrid ALM/VLM pipeline with Cut & Merge post-processing achieves 3–4× speedup and 4× cost reduction vs. Gemini 2.5 Pro while preserving clip quality, informativeness (0.721), low redundancy (0.339), and compliance.

## Executive Summary
Pharmaceutical video content (clinical trial interviews, seminars) requires efficient highlight generation for varied downstream uses. This paper introduces a domain-adapted Video-to-Video Clip Generation framework that combines Audio Language Models (Whisper) and Vision Language Models (Gemini, Claude, etc.) with a reproducible Cut & Merge algorithm to ensure smooth transitions and compliance. The system personalizes output via role‑based prompt injection (e.g., marketing, training). Evaluated on 900 Video‑MME videos and a proprietary dataset of 16,159 pharmacy videos, it delivers a 3–4× speedup and 4× cost reduction compared to state‑of‑the‑art VLM baselines like Gemini 2.5 Pro, while improving informativeness (0.721) and reducing redundancy (0.339). The approach preserves original audio/visual content, meeting regulatory standards.

## Method Summary
The pipeline starts with audio extraction from input videos using FFmpeg (or MoviePy fallback). Whisper (large‑v2 primary, turbo‑v3 safeguard) produces a timestamped transcript. For voiceover videos, a text‑only LLM (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro) selects highlight segments based on a structured prompt that injects user‑defined role, output length, agenda keywords, and speaker tone preferences, requiring verbatim text and precise timestamps. For videos without voiceover, a VLM directly analyzes frames. Selected segments then undergo the Cut & Merge algorithm: each segment is re‑encoded to a standardized codec (H.264/AAC) with 0.5 s audio/video fade‑in/out, and concatenated via FFmpeg’s concat demuxer to avoid codec/timestamp mismatches. Optional subtitles or TTS voiceover can be added. The design leverages cheap ALM transcription and fast text‑based selection, reserving expensive VLM calls only when necessary.

## Key Results
- Speed: 3–4× faster than Gemini 2.5 Pro (30–55 s vs. 120 s per video).
- Cost: ~4× reduction (e.g., Gemini Flash at $0.30/$2.50 per MT vs. higher‑cost baselines).
- Quality: Informativeness 0.721, redundancy 0.339, coherence 0.348 on proprietary datasets; outperforms state‑of‑the‑art VLM baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Post‑Processing for Transition Smoothness
**Claim:** The Cut & Merge algorithm with re‑encoding and fade‑in/out transitions eliminates visual and auditory discontinuities (jump cuts, frame freezing, audio glitches) that occur when directly concatenating video segments.  
**Mechanism:** Selected segments are individually re‑encoded with synchronized audio fade (±0.5 s) and video frame adjustments, then concatenated via FFmpeg’s concat demuxer. This contrasts with direct stream copy (`-c copy`) which preserves incompatible codecs or timestamp gaps between segments.  
**Core assumption:** Re‑encoding each segment to a standardized format (H.264/AAC) and applying controlled fades resolves codec/timestamp mismatches inherited from source video heterogeneity.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “…a reproducible Cut & Merge algorithm with fade in/out and timestamp normalization, ensuring smooth transitions and audio/visual alignment.”  
- [section 4] “Algorithm 1” details the ffmpeg commands for `ProcessClip` (re‑encoding with `afade`/`vf` filters) and `ConcatClips`.  
- [corpus] Qualitative evidence in Fig 2(e‑f) shows fewer visual artifacts vs. direct concatenation.  
**Break condition:** If source videos are already homogeneous in codec/framerate and segments are cut on I‑frames, direct concatenation may suffice. The algorithm’s benefit scales with source video diversity and segment boundary arbitrariness.

### Mechanism 2: Role‑Guided Segment Selection via Prompt Injection
**Claim:** Injecting user‑defined “roles” (e.g., marketing, training) and structured selection metrics (agenda coverage, keyword emphasis, speaker tone) into the VLM prompt tailors clip selection to downstream use cases.  
**Mechanism:** The system prompt (Appendix Table 15) dynamically includes `User‑Provided Role / Style` and `Additional Requirements`. The VLM (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro) then weights segment importance based on these constraints alongside general criteria (transitions, full‑video coverage).  
**Core assumption:** VLM reasoning chains can be steered by explicit natural‑language instructions to prioritize content attributes aligned with communicative goals (persuasion vs. education).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “…personalization mechanism based on role definition and prompt injection for tailored outputs (marketing, training, regulatory).”  
- [section 6, Fig 8] Ablation shows removing “Speaker tone” causes “visual‑voice mismatches”; “Agenda” ensures key information coverage.  
- [corpus] Table 14 shows divergent segment selections for same video under “Marketing/Promotion” vs. “Training/Educational” prompts.  
**Break condition:** Effectiveness degrades if role instructions conflict with low‑level visual/audio salience signals the VLM is fundamentally tuned to detect, or if the VLM’s internal biases override prompt cues (evidenced by persistent timestamp errors in Table 14).

### Mechanism 3: Modality‑Specialized Pipeline for Cost & Speed
**Claim:** Using an ALM (Whisper) for transcription followed by a text‑only LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro text‑to‑text) for segment selection is significantly faster and cheaper than processing raw video with a VLM, while maintaining comparable or better clip coherence/informativeness.  
**Mechanism:** Audio extraction → Whisper transcription → text‑based segment scoring/selection decouples semantic understanding from expensive video‑frame processing. The VLM is only invoked for videos without voiceover or for visual‑heavy validation.  
**Core assumption:** For pharmacy videos (speech‑centric), textual transcripts capture the majority of “informativeness” relevant to highlight selection, making full video understanding redundant.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “…utilizing ALM for accurate transcription, combined with Gemini 2.5 Pro (text‑to‑text), significantly reduces costs (up to 4‑4.5× less) and accelerates processing time (up to 4× faster)…”  
- [section 5, Fig 3‑4] Empirical timing data: our method ~30‑55 s/video vs. Gemini 2.5 Pro ~120 s/video. Table 4 shows our method’s informativeness (0.721) > Gemini Pro (0.674).  
- [corpus] Signals include papers on efficient video QA (“Self‑Adaptive Sampling”) and long‑video VLMs (“Video‑XL”), suggesting community recognizes computational cost as a key challenge.  
**Break condition:** Fails for videos where critical informativeness is purely visual (e.g., surgical procedure without narration). The paper notes a VLM fallback for “video has Not Voiceover” cases.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: Video Timestamp Normalization & Frame‑Accurate Cutting
**Why needed here:** The pipeline segments videos based on Whisper’s word‑level timestamps. Inconsistent timestamp precision (e.g., Whisper V2 vs. V3 differences) and variable keyframe spacing cause “jump cuts” if segments are naively concatenated. Normalization (rounding to 2 decimals) and re‑encoding ensure segment boundaries align with decodable frames.  
**Quick check:** Given Whisper timestamps `12.345s` and `15.678s` for a segment, why might direct `ffmpeg -ss 12.345 -to 15.678 -i input.mp4 -c copy output.mp4` produce a glitchy clip, and how does `ProcessClip` in Algorithm 1 resolve this?

### Concept 2: VLM Prompt Engineering for Extractive vs. Generative Tasks
**Why needed here:** Most VLM benchmarks are question‑answering or summarization (video‑to‑text). This task is *extractive*: selecting original video segments. The prompt must force verbatim text copying and precise timestamp output, counter to VLM’s generative tendencies.  
**Quick check:** The system prompt in Table 15 includes “You MUST preserve the exact wording…”. What failure mode does this instruction directly address, based on the paper’s observations of Gemini 2.5’s generative paraphrasing in direct video‑to‑text mode?

### Concept 3: Whisper Model Trade‑offs for Enterprise ASR
**Why needed here:** Whisper V2 vs. V3 selection impacts downstream segment quality. V3 is faster and better at language ID but fragments sentences; V2 has better sentence‑boundary timestamps but occasional language misidentification. The pipeline uses V3 as a “safeguard” for V2’s language errors.  
**Quick check:** In a pharmacy video with mixed English and medical jargon, why might Whisper V3’s aggressive endpoint detection produce more fragmented transcriptions than V2, and how could that affect segment merge decisions?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Input Handler → Audio Extraction → Whisper (ALM) → LLM Segment Scorer (role‑injected prompt) → Cut & Merge Engine → (Optional) Subtitle/TTS → Output Formatter  
Fallback (no voiceover): Input Handler → VLM Segment Scorer → Cut & Merge Engine → Output Formatter

### Critical path
Video → Audio Extract → Whisper Transcription → Prompted Segment Selection (VLM/LLM) → Cut & Merge → Final Clip. **Bottleneck:** VLM segment selection latency (Gemini 2.5 Pro avg 120 s/video). **Key dependency:** Whisper transcript quality directly constrains segment boundary precision.

### Design tradeoffs
- Speed vs. Voiceover Handling: Text‑only pipeline (ALM+LLM) is 3‑4× faster but fails for non‑voiceover content; fallback to VLM adds cost/time.
- Segment Count vs. Coherence: More segments (Gemini Flash tendency) increase coverage risk but may harm narrative flow; prompt metrics (agenda, transitions) mitigate.
- Cost vs. Model Sophistication: Using Gemini Flash ($0.30/$2.50 per MT) vs. Pro ($1.25/$10) cuts cost 4× but may reduce selection precision.

### Failure signatures
1. Whisper fragmentation: Short, disjointed transcript segments (V3) → excessive clip count, jerkier transitions.
2. VLM timestamp hallucination: Gemini outputs timestamps beyond video duration → invalid CLI args, script failure.
3. Role prompt override failure: Marketing vs. training prompts yield similar segments → VLM not respecting role cue; requires prompt tuning.
4. Codec incompatibility: Source video uses rare codec → ffmpeg extraction fails; moviepy fallback needed.

### First 3 experiments
1. Validate Whisper pipeline: Run 10 long pharmacy videos (30+ min) through Whisper V2 and V3. Compare sentence‑boundary alignment (segment count per minute) and language detection accuracy (spot‑check non‑English clips). Goal: quantify fragmentation vs. speed trade‑off.
2. Test Cut & Merge vs. Direct Concat: Take a 5‑minute video, select 3 arbitrary segments. Generate two clips: (a) `ffmpeg -ss… -c copy -concat` (b) `Cut & Merge` (Algorithm 1). Visually/auditorily compare for skips, audio pops, freeze‑frames.
3. Role Prompt A/B Test: On 5 identical videos, run segment selection with prompts: (a) generic “select highlights”, (b) “training: explain step‑by‑step”, (c) “marketing: include USP mentions”. Compare segment text overlap (via BERTScore) and role‑cue adherence (manual review of selected text for persuasive vs. explanatory language).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** Can a hybrid ALM/VLM pipeline generate high‑quality highlight clips from long medical videos under strict non‑synthetic, efficiency and cost constraints?  
**Basis in paper:** Page 2 lists RQ1.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates speed and cost gains on proprietary and Video‑MME data, but real‑world deployment in regulated pharmaceutical settings may introduce additional constraints (audit trails, data provenance, multilingual robustness) not fully captured by current benchmarks.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal studies measuring compliance adherence, user satisfaction, and ROI across diverse real‑world video types (multilingual clinical trials, multi‑speaker seminars) with explicit regulatory review.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How to improve temporal coherence and transition smoothness compared to direct video frame concatenation?  
**Basis in paper:** Page 2 lists RQ2.  
**Why unresolved:** The proposed Cut & Merge algorithm uses fixed fade durations (±0.5 s) and timestamp normalization, but its optimal parameters may vary with video genre (rapid Q&A vs. lecture pauses), speaker overlap, or visual complexity. The algorithm’s reproducibility is claimed but not systematically benchmarked against alternative transition models.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled ablation varying fade parameters, concatenation strategies, and normalization techniques across diverse pharmaceutical video genres, with human evaluations of smoothness and computational trade‑offs.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How do role‑based prompt personas influence clip selection behavior?  
**Basis in paper:** Page 2 lists RQ3.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper shows marketing vs. training prompts yield different segments (Table 14), but the mechanism (which prompt tokens drive divergence) and limits of manual role definition are unclear. Automatically inferring roles from video content beyond user‑provided labels remains unexplored.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Fine‑grained ablation of prompt components (role, agenda, keywords) and user studies with domain experts evaluating role alignment; development of models that infer user intent from video context without explicit role input.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** To what extent does the proposed framework generalize beyond pharmaceutical videos to other regulated domains (finance, manufacturing) with similar compliance and traceability requirements?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred from Limitations (page 8): “Future work should extend this line of research to other regulated domains, such as financial services and manufacturing, to further validate the generalizability of framework.”  
**Why unresolved:** The pipeline’s personalization metrics (agenda, keywords, speaker tone) and compliance focus (preserving original frames/audio) are tailored to medical contexts. Transfer to other domains may require different segment heuristics, role ontologies, or audit mechanisms.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replication studies applying the same pipeline to non‑pharma regulated videos (e.g., earnings calls, safety training), measuring whether efficiency gains and clip quality metrics hold without domain‑specific tuning.

## Limitations
- Prompt template ambiguity: The exact structure for role‑based injection (Appendix Table 15) is not fully provided, hindering replication of personalization.
- Visual‑only fallback: The specific VLM used for non‑voiceover videos and its prompt adaptations are unclear.
- Metric definitions: Code and thresholds for computing informativeness, redundancy, and coherence are not disclosed, preventing exact reproduction.
