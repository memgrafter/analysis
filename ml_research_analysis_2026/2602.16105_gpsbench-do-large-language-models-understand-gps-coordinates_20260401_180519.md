# GPSBench: Do Large Language Models Understand GPS Coordinates?

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.16105  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16105  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LLMs show a consistent 9.9% performance gap favoring applied geospatial tasks over pure GPS geometry, with hierarchical degradation in geographic knowledge (strong country-level, weak city-level <25% accuracy) and robustness to coordinate noise.

## Executive Summary
GPSBench introduces a large-scale benchmark with 57,800 samples across 17 tasks to evaluate LLMs’ intrinsic geospatial reasoning, separating pure GPS coordinate operations from applied geographic knowledge integration. Evaluation of 14 models reveals a persistent 9.9% advantage for applied tasks, except for flagship reasoning models like GPT‑5.1 and Gemini‑2.5‑Pro. Models demonstrate hierarchical geographic knowledge: they reliably identify countries from coordinates but fail at city‑level localization (<25% accuracy). Their robustness to added coordinate noise suggests genuine spatial understanding rather than memorization. Finetuning improves geometric computation (+4.3% on Pure GPS) but degrades world knowledge (−1.6% on Applied). Augmenting downstream benchmarks with explicit GPS coordinates yields 6–23% accuracy gains and eliminates specific spatial biases such as hierarchical and alignment biases.

## Method Summary
GPSBench builds on the GeoNames database (18,196 cities with >15k population) to generate 3,400 samples per task via quota‑based continental balancing and separation filters. Tasks are divided into Pure GPS (coordinate transformations, distance, bearing, polygon area) and Applied (place association, route geometry, spatial relationships) tracks. Ground truth is computed using geodetic formulas (Haversine, spherical bearing, L’Huilier’s theorem) or GeoNames metadata. Fourteen LLMs are evaluated zero‑shot (temperature 0) with standardized prompts requiring a FINAL ANSWER format. Metrics include accuracy for multiple‑choice and 1‑MAPE for numeric outputs. Additional analyses examine granularity (city/province/country), noise robustness (Gaussian perturbation), GPS augmentation on downstream benchmarks (MapEval, Hierarchical Spatial), and finetuning trade‑offs via LoRA on Qwen3‑30B.

## Key Results
- Applied tasks consistently outperform Pure GPS by 9.9% on average across 14 models; GPT‑5.1 and Gemini‑2.5‑Pro are notable exceptions excelling at Pure GPS.
- Geographic knowledge degrades hierarchically: country‑level identification remains strong, while city‑level accuracy collapses to 1–23%.
- Models are robust to coordinate noise (e.g., Gaussian jitter up to 1 km), indicating internal spatial reasoning beyond memorized coordinate‑city pairs.
- Finetuning with LoRA improves Pure GPS (+4.3%) but harms Applied (−1.6%), revealing antagonistic effects between geometric and factual knowledge.
- Adding GPS coordinates to downstream benchmarks yields 6–23% absolute accuracy gains and eliminates hierarchical and alignment spatial biases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Coarse‑grained geographic knowledge transfers to applied tasks but lacks fine‑grained precision.
LLMs’ inherent geographic knowledge is organized hierarchically; they easily recall country‑level facts but struggle with city‑level coordinate associations. Training on textual corpora embeds geographic entities at varying granularities—country names appear frequently with strong contextual signals, while city‑level coordinates are sparse and noisy. Consequently, models infer countries via broad heuristics (hemisphere, region) but cannot pinpoint exact cities without memorized precise pairs. Evidence includes hierarchical degradation in Place Association accuracy (city‑level 1–23%) and robustness to coordinate noise at country level. If future models achieve >90% city‑level accuracy consistently across regions, this hierarchy would flatten, indicating dense coordinate‑city mappings have been internalized.

### Mechanism 2: Geometric GPS computations form a distinct reasoning pathway independent of world knowledge.
Pure coordinate operations (distance, bearing, transformations) require algorithmic processing that does not automatically benefit from factual geographic memory. These tasks invoke symbolic manipulation and trigonometric formulations stored as procedural patterns in the model. Hence, performance on Pure GPS correlates with mathematical reasoning capacity, not with the richness of geographic knowledge. Evidence: the consistent 9.9% Applied‑Pure gap across 14 models, and antagonistic finetuning effects (+4.3% Pure GPS vs −1.6% Applied). If a model family shows no gap (or reversed gap) across scales and finetuning yields parallel improvements, it would indicate geometric reasoning is not independent but fully transferable.

### Mechanism 3: Prompt‑based GPS augmentation directly injects spatial precision, bypassing internal biases.
Supplying explicit latitude/longitude coordinates in the prompt reduces reliance on heuristics and eliminates systematic spatial biases (hierarchical, alignment). Coordinates provide an objective, low‑entropy representation that models can process using internal arithmetic to derive answers rather than defaulting to memorized crude relationships. Evidence: augmenting downstream benchmarks (MapEval, Hierarchical Spatial) yields 6–23% accuracy gains and eliminates both hierarchical (70%→100%) and alignment (50%→100%) biases. If adding coordinates yields no improvement or models ignore them in favor of heuristics, the mechanism fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Landmark‑Route‑Survey spatial cognition framework** – Provides theoretical foundation for classifying GPSBench tasks into L/R/S/G types; explains the cognitive nature of geospatial reasoning tested. *Quick check:* “What type of spatial knowledge is required for a ‘Relative Position’ task given two place names but no coordinates?” (Answer: Survey – metric global layout.)
- **Geodetic computations (Haversine, spherical bearing, interpolation, L’Huilier’s theorem)** – Ground truth for Pure GPS tasks relies on these formulae; understanding them explains expected numeric outputs and tolerances. *Quick check:* “How does the Haversine formula account for Earth’s curvature?” (Answer: Uses arcsin of sum of squared chord lengths derived from lat/lon differences.)
- **Hierarchical geographic granularity (country → province → city)** – Explains dramatic drop in Place Association accuracy and robustness to coordinate noise; reveals models store geographic information at multiple resolutions. *Quick check:* “Why might adding Gaussian noise to coordinates not affect country‑level accuracy but dramatically reduce city‑level accuracy?” (Answer: Country identification uses coarse signals insensitive to small changes; city identification requires precise matches.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
GeoNames database → Sampling module → Generator → Evaluator (API wrapper) → Analyzer

**Critical path:**  
1. Select city samples per task rules.  
2. Construct prompt (coordinate conversion if needed).  
3. Query model at temperature 0.  
4. Parse model output to structured answer.  
5. Compare against ground truth using numeric tolerances or exact matches.  
6. Store results and aggregate statistics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Intrinsic vs tool‑augmented: intrinsic reveals training gaps but may underestimate real‑world utility; tool‑augmented masks gaps.  
- Zero‑shot only: ensures fairness but may under‑represent capabilities that benefit from examples.  
- Balanced coverage vs population realism: quotas prevent regional bias in evaluation but deviate from true geographic distribution.  
- Tolerances: tighter numeric tolerances increase discriminative power but risk false negatives from rounding; chosen to reflect typical use‑case precision.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Route Geometry: very low accuracy (>0% for many models) indicates inability to execute RDP simplification algorithmically.  
- Polygon Area: high variance and extreme overestimates show failure in multi‑step spherical excess calculation.  
- Place Association: systematic substitution with large nearby cities (e.g., giving state capital instead of exact city) reveals lack of dense coordinate‑city mappings.  
- Coordinate Transformation: recurrent UTM southern‑hemisphere errors (missing false northing offset) point to weaknesses in handling nonlinear projection formulas.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Compute mean accuracy for Applied vs Pure GPS tracks across all 14 models; report the 9.9% gap and highlight outliers (GPT‑5.1, Gemini‑2.5‑Pro).  
2. Hierarchical degradation analysis using Place Association task; measure accuracy at country, province, and city levels; add Gaussian coordinate noise to probe memorization.  
3. GPS augmentation study: add coordinates to MapEval and Hierarchical Spatial prompts; measure delta in accuracy and observe elimination of hierarchical/alignment biases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What is the performance gap between multimodal LLMs (receiving maps/satellite images) and text‑only LLMs on GPSBench?  
*Basis:* The paper evaluates only text‑based LLMs; multimodal models that process visual inputs may exhibit different capability profiles.  
*Why unresolved:* No multimodal models have been evaluated; the extent of advantage from visual input is unknown.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Adapt GPSBench to accept image inputs, test state‑of‑the‑art multimodal models, and compare scores.

**Open Question 2:** Does prompting with chain‑of‑thought or external tools raise Pure GPS accuracy and close the performance gap with Applied tasks?  
*Basis:* Evaluation uses zero‑shot prompting without CoT or tool augmentation, which may underestimate achievable performance.  
*Why unresolved:* It is unclear whether latent GPS reasoning can be elicited through advanced prompting or tool use.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Run experiments with CoT prompts and calculator tools; record changes per task and track whether Applied vs Pure gap narrows.

**Open Question 3:** How can GPSBench be revised to reduce geographic sampling bias and address the limitation of point‑based city representations?  
*Basis:* Geographic coverage is constrained by GeoNames data availability; regions like maritime, polar, and deserts are absent. Point‑based city representation treats the GeoNames reference point as definitive, ignoring city extent ambiguity.  
*Why unresolved:* Current benchmark may overfit to urban, point‑located cities and ignore area‑level ambiguity, limiting real‑world applicability.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Incorporate polygon data for city extents, add samples from underrepresented biomes, and re‑evaluate model performance.

## Limitations
- Missing exact prompt templates for all 17 tasks; minor phrasing variations could cause performance variance, especially for subtle geographic reasoning.
- Undisclosed inference parameters for proprietary models (GPT‑5.1, Gemini‑2.5‑Pro) prevent exact replication of their results.
- Static GeoNames snapshot; coordinate‑city mappings evolve over time, potentially affecting long‑term benchmark validity.

## Confidence
- **Hierarchical geographic degradation (Mechanism 1):** Medium Confidence. Evidence is strong from reported data splits and noise robustness, but claim depends on specific city‑population thresholds and sampling quotas not fully detailed.
- **Independence of Pure GPS reasoning (Mechanism 2):** High Confidence. Consistent 9.9% Applied‑Pure gap across 14 models, coupled with antagonistic finetuning effects, provides robust empirical support.
- **GPS augmentation eliminates spatial biases (Mechanism 3):** Medium‑High Confidence. Gains are quantified (6–23%), but efficacy hinges on precise coordinate formatting and downstream task compatibility, which may vary.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt fidelity audit:** Re‑run 5–10% of tasks using community‑sourced prompt variations to quantify performance variance against the reported baseline; flag tasks where phrasing changes alter accuracy by >5%.
2. **Finetuning trade‑off replication:** Apply the reported LoRA setup (Qwen3‑30B, rank=64, lr=2e‑5) on an open model (e.g., Llama‑3.1‑70B) and measure Pure GPS (+Δ) vs Applied (−Δ) shifts; confirm antagonism holds with different base models.
3. **Coordinate noise stress test:** Systematically increase Gaussian noise (σ = 10m, 100m, 1km) on Place Association and Bearing tasks; verify country‑level robustness persists while city‑level collapses non‑linearly, confirming memorization vs understanding.
