# A Hybrid Federated Learning Based Ensemble Approach for Lung Disease Diagnosis Leveraging Fusion of SWIN Transformer and CNN

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17566
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17566
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: The hybrid fusion model achieves a peak validation accuracy of 97.0%, outperforming individual models.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a federated learning framework for privacy-preserving, distributed diagnosis of lung diseases, specifically COVID-19 and pneumonia, from chest X-ray images. The core approach ensembles four pre-trained deep learning models (VGG19, InceptionV3, DenseNet201, and SWIN Transformer) via a sum-weighted fusion strategy. Experimental results report a validation accuracy of 97.0% for the ensemble, significantly higher than any single model. However, the federated implementation faces substantial hardware constraints, and the SWIN Transformer model notably underperforms relative to the CNN-based counterparts.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a hybrid ensemble model that combines predictions from four pre-trained networks: three CNNs (VGG19, InceptionV3, DenseNet201) and one vision transformer (SWIN Transformer). The ensemble uses a sum-weighted averaging mechanism to aggregate outputs. The training is conducted within a federated learning paradigm to maintain data privacy, though specific FL details (e.g., number of clients, data distribution) are not provided. The evaluation is performed on a dataset of chest X-ray images, with a single train-validation split. The paper reports the validation accuracy of each individual model and the fused ensemble.

## Key Results
- The sum-weighted ensemble model achieves a peak validation accuracy of 97.0%.
- Individual model accuracies: VGG19 (94.4%), InceptionV3 (94.5%), DenseNet201 (94.1%), SWIN Transformer (82.5%).
- The federated learning setup requires approximately 35GB of RAM per run, indicating high resource demands.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The hybrid ensemble leverages complementary feature representations—CNNs capture local textures and edges, while the SWIN Transformer captures long-range contextual dependencies. The sum-weighted fusion averages predictions, reducing model-specific errors and variance. Unknown: The paper does not provide an ablation study to quantify each model's contribution, nor does it explain the SWIN Transformer's poor performance (82.5%)—possibly due to domain shift from natural images (ImageNet pre-training) to medical X-rays, or suboptimal fine-tuning. The federated learning aggregation mechanism (e.g., FedAvg) and privacy guarantees (e.g., differential privacy) are not detailed, leaving the privacy-preserving mechanism speculative.

## Foundational Learning
This work builds on several foundational concepts:
- **Transfer Learning**: All models are pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned on chest X-ray data, transferring general visual features to the medical domain.
- **Ensemble Learning**: The sum-weighted averaging is a simple ensemble technique that typically improves accuracy and robustness by reducing variance.
- **Federated Learning**: A distributed training framework that keeps data local, sharing only model updates to protect privacy. The paper mentions FL but does not discuss its challenges (e.g., non-IID data, communication costs).
- **Vision Transformers**: SWIN Transformer represents a modern architecture using self-attention in windows, contrasting with CNNs' local receptive fields.
The paper applies these concepts without comprehensive discussion of their theoretical foundations or prior applications in medical imaging, missing an opportunity to contextualize the approach.

## Architecture Onboarding
The architecture onboarding involves fine-tuning each pre-trained model on the chest X-ray dataset and then combining their outputs. Specifically:
- Each model processes input images and produces class probability vectors (via softmax).
- The ensemble aggregates these vectors using sum-weighted averaging; the weighting factors are not described (possibly uniform or learned?).
- The SWIN Transformer requires image patchification and window-based attention, differing from the CNNs' convolutional processing.
- During federated training, each client presumably trains all four models locally, then model updates are aggregated across clients. However, the paper does not specify: number of clients, data partitioning, aggregation frequency, or synchronization protocol.
- The high memory footprint (35GB per run) suggests that loading all four models simultaneously is demanding, possibly due to large batch sizes or model sizes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out any open questions or future work. However, based on the limitations and suggested next checks, several implicit open questions arise:
- How robust is the 97.0% accuracy across different data splits and external datasets?
- Why does the SWIN Transformer underperform so drastically, and can its architecture or training be adapted for medical X-rays?
- What are the practical scalability and communication costs of the federated learning system in real-world multi-institution settings?
- Does the sum-weighted fusion strategy optimally utilize each model, or would a learned weighting (e.g., via stacking) improve performance?
- How does the model handle class imbalance, which is common in medical datasets?

## Limitations
- The reported 97.0% accuracy lacks statistical validation (e.g., cross-validation) and is based on a single split, raising overfitting concerns.
- The SWIN Transformer's significantly lower accuracy (82.5%) compared to CNN models is unexplained, possibly indicating tuning or data suitability issues.
- The federated learning implementation is underspecified and demands high memory (35GB per run), questioning scalability and practical privacy guarantees.
