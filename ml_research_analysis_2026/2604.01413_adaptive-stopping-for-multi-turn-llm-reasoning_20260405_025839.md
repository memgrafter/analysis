# Adaptive Stopping for Multi-Turn LLM Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01413
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01413
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** MiCP achieves target coverage while reducing average turns on multi-hop QA benchmarks via adaptive stopping with conformal guarantees.

## Executive Summary
Multi-turn LLM systems (e.g., adaptive RAG, ReAct) lack formal stopping guarantees, risking incorrect answers or unnecessary computational cost. This paper introduces MiCP, a method that extends conformal prediction to multi-turn reasoning. By allocating a global error budget across turns and using normalized entropy of sampled answers as a turn-level confidence score, MiCP enables adaptive early stopping while preserving an overall (1-α) coverage guarantee. It also incorporates conformal retrieval filtering and optional abstention. Evaluated on five QA benchmarks with three LLMs, MiCP reliably achieves target coverage, reduces average turns (e.g., on HotpotQA from 3.0 to ~2.5–2.8), and optimizes the coverage–efficiency trade-off via a composite metric.

## Method Summary
MiCP adapts split-conformal prediction to multi-turn settings by distributing a user-specified error budget α across T turns (∑αₜ = α). At each turn, it first applies conformal retrieval filtering: using a calibration set, it computes a quantile threshold on the maximum relevance scores of gold passages across turns, then retains only passages scoring above this threshold. The LLM generates multiple answer samples for the current query; normalized entropy (NE) of these samples serves as a turn-level nonconformity score. Per-turn stopping thresholds are calibrated via grid search on a composite metric that balances coverage and efficiency (e.g., minimizing expected turns subject to coverage constraints). If NE falls below the turn-specific threshold, the system stops; otherwise, it continues to the next turn. An optional “Can’t Answer” abstention is triggered when no answer cluster meets a confidence penalty. This framework guarantees marginal coverage for both retrieval and final predictions while adaptively terminating reasoning early.

## Key Results
- Achieves target coverage (≥1-α) for retrieval and predictions across α ∈ [0.05, 0.25] on five QA benchmarks (single-hop and multi-hop) with three LLMs.
- Reduces average number of turns—for example, on HotpotQA, from 3.0 (no early stopping) to ~2.5–2.8—while preserving coverage.
- The composite metric successfully balances coverage and efficiency, yielding fewer prediction set elements and lower computational cost versus baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mechanism 1: Conformal retrieval filtering removes low-relevance passages at each turn while guaranteeing that a user-specified fraction of retrievable gold passages are retained. This uses a split-conformal approach on a calibration set: for each gold passage retrievable in any turn, its maximum relevance score across all turns is recorded. The quantile of these scores defines a threshold ̂q_ret. At test time, only passages with relevance scores above ̂q_ret are kept, ensuring that with high probability, all gold passages that would ever be retrieved are included in at least one turn’s filtered set. This controlled filtering prevents premature discarding of critical information and maintains coverage for the final answer set.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conformal Prediction:** Provides distribution-free coverage guarantees; needed to turn heuristic stopping into a statistically reliable process. Quick check: Can you explain why a split-conformal approach yields marginal coverage for prediction sets?
- **Multi-Turn Reasoning:** Involves sequential interactions (retrieve → reason → act); needed to model error accumulation and early termination. Quick check: How does the number of turns affect the overall error budget in MiCP’s allocation?
- **Error Budget Allocation:** Distributes the global α across turns; needed to ensure the sum of per-turn error probabilities stays within α. Quick check: If αₜ are per-turn significance levels, why must ∑αₜ ≤ α for overall coverage?
- **Normalized Entropy (NE):** Measures diversity of sampled answers; used as a turn-level confidence proxy. Needed to quantify uncertainty without access to model logits. Quick check: Why might low NE not always indicate high answer correctness?
- **Retrieval Filtering:** Applies conformal thresholds to passage relevance scores; needed to control false exclusions of gold passages. Quick check: What is the difference between filtering per turn vs. across all turns in MiCP?
- **Abstention Mechanism:** Allows the system to output “Can’t Answer” with a penalty η; needed to avoid low-confidence guesses. Quick check: How does the penalty η affect the stopping threshold calibration?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Retriever → Passage Filter (conformal threshold) → LLM Generator (answer sampling) → Confidence Scorer (normalized entropy) → Stopping Threshold Comparator → Decision (stop/continue/abstain). The final answer is derived from clustered samples if not abstained.
- **Critical path:** For each turn t: (1) retrieve top-k passages; (2) filter with ̂q_ret; (3) generate m answer samples using LLM; (4) compute NE; (5) compare NE to calibrated threshold qₜ; (6) if NE ≤ qₜ, stop and output answer cluster; else continue to turn t+1. Abstention occurs if no cluster passes confidence penalty.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Composite metric weights (coverage vs. efficiency) tuned via grid search; fixed penalty η for abstention; per-turn thresholds from calibration may be conservative; retrieval filtering relies on a reliable relevance scorer.
- **Failure signatures:** (1) Test coverage falls below 1-α; (2) average turns remain high (no early stopping); (3) abstention rate spikes (>20%), indicating over-conservative thresholds; (4) retrieval recall drops due to aggressive filtering.
- **First three experiments:** (1) Validate marginal coverage on a single-hop QA dataset (e.g., SQuAD) with α=0.1; (2) Sweep composite metric weight λ and measure trade-off between average turns and coverage; (3) Replace the retriever with a noisy version (e.g., BM25) and assess impact on retrieval coverage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Conditional coverage for subgroups:** Can MiCP provide conditional coverage guarantees for predefined subgroups (e.g., by question difficulty or domain), and how would group-conditional calibration be implemented in the multi-turn setting? The authors state that group-conditional calibration is left for future work, as marginal coverage does not ensure reliability for high-stakes subpopulations.
- **Optimal error budget allocation:** Is the proposed error budget allocation (Eq. 12) optimal for minimizing expected turns while satisfying the overall coverage constraint, or can a theoretically grounded optimization improve efficiency? The current heuristic balances answerable fractions without a minimization objective.
- **Generalizability of normalized entropy:** How generally effective is normalized entropy as a turn-level confidence score for early stopping across different model sizes, architectures, and task types? MiCP uses NE with a fixed penalty η but does not investigate alternative uncertainty metrics or their calibration.
- **Applicability beyond extractive QA:** Can MiCP’s framework be seamlessly applied to multi-turn LLM applications that do not involve short answer extraction, such as open-domain dialogue or creative writing, where prediction set construction and clustering are more ambiguous? The experiments focus on extractive QA with clear gold answers; extending to generative tasks requires redesigning nonconformity scores.

## Limitations
- The composite metric weighting (coverage vs. efficiency) is heuristically tuned via grid search; theoretical justification for robustness across α ranges is lacking.
- Retrieval filtering details are sparse; its performance under noisy or imperfect retrievers is not evaluated.
- Calibration and testing splits may introduce data leakage risks; the work lacks independent replication (citation count low, no external validation cited).

## Confidence
- **Coverage guarantee:** High — Theoretically grounded in conformal prediction, with empirical validation on standard benchmarks.
- **Efficiency gains (turn reduction):** Medium — Results are consistent but depend on composite metric tuning and are limited to QA domains.
- **Generalizability beyond benchmarks:** Low — No evidence for multi-modal, interactive, or long-horizon tasks; the field is nascent.

## Next Checks
1. **Out-of-distribution coverage test:** Apply MiCP to domain-shifted QA datasets (e.g., biomedical, legal) and verify that (1-α) coverage holds without recalibration.
2. **Sensitivity analysis of composite metric:** Systematically vary the weight λ balancing coverage and efficiency; measure coverage violations and variance in average turns reduction across α ∈ [0.05, 0.25].
3. **Runtime overhead benchmark:** Compare the calibration cost of grid search for thresholds versus using fixed heuristics under strict latency budgets; test scalability with increased maximum turn limits.
