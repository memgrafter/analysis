# Mitigating Position-Shift Failures in Text-Based Modular Arithmetic via Position Curriculum and Template Diversity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04283
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04283
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Character-level Transformers trained on text-based modular加法 achieve high in-distribution accuracy (96.8%) but catastrophically fail under absolute position shifts (14.9%) and out-of-distribution templates (1.2%). A full training recipe combining anchors, position curriculum, template diversity, and consistency training raises position-shift accuracy to 73.7% and no-anchor OOD to 80.5% while maintaining in-distribution accuracy at 96.0%.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a critical robustness failure in character-level Transformers solving modular arithmetic via text: models that perform perfectly on in-distribution prompts fail dramatically when the absolute character position of the expression changes or when presented with novel natural-language templates. The authors propose a training intervention—explicit expression boundary markers, a curriculum broadening absolute positions, diverse template mixtures, and consistency training across multiple textual variants—that substantially mitigates these failures. The full recipe (I1-002a) achieves strong robustness across position shifts and OOD templates without sacrificing in-distribution performance, demonstrating that explicit invariance training, rather than architectural changes like ALiBi, is key for this character-level parsing task.

## Method Summary
The task is to compute `(a + b) mod 97` from a character-level string. Data uses a disjoint-pair split to prevent memorization. Baseline models are trained on a single template with the expression at a fixed position. The full intervention (I1-002a) incorporates: (1) wrapping the expression in `<EXPR>`/`</EXPR>` anchors; (2) a position curriculum that gradually expands the allowed absolute position range during training (from [10,30] to [10,70]); (3) a mixture of padding-style, natural-language, and mixed templates; and (4) multi-variant training where each underlying `(a,b)` pair is rendered as K=4 distinct surface forms per batch, with a consistency loss (λ=1.0) penalizing logit disagreement across variants. Model: 2-layer Transformer encoder, d_model=128, 4 heads, learned absolute positional embeddings, CLS pooling, trained for 5000 steps with batch size 256.

## Key Results
- Baseline: 96.8% in-distribution (Eval-A) accuracy, but collapses to 14.9% under position shift (Eval-B) and 1.2% on no-anchor OOD templates (Eval-C0).
- Full intervention I1-002a: 96.0% Eval-A, 73.7% Eval-B, 80.5% Eval-C0, 94.5% anchor-based OOD (Eval-C1), and 93.9% consistency across variants.
- ALiBi-style positional encoding ablation fails completely (21.4% Eval-A), underscoring that robustness is achieved by explicitly training invariances, not by removing positional signals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Explicit Expression Boundary Markers
- **Claim:** Anchors (`<EXPR>`, `</EXPR>`) provide a structural prior that helps segment the arithmetic expression from surrounding text, reducing reliance on absolute position for parsing.
- **Mechanism:** They create a relative reference frame within the sequence, encouraging the model to attend to tokens *between* anchors rather than memorizing absolute character indices.
- **Break condition:** Anchors absent during training or mismatched at test time invalidate the segmentation prior, likely causing failure.

### Mechanism 2: Position Curriculum
- **Claim:** Gradually expanding the allowed absolute position range forces the model to learn a position-agnostic procedure rather than overfitting to a narrow positional band.
- **Mechanism:** Starting with a narrow range (e.g., [10,30]) lets the model first learn the core computation, then adaptation to wider ranges requires discarding position-specific features.
- **Break condition:** Curriculum expands too quickly or the final range does not cover evaluation positions; positions far outside (e.g., 0–8) remain OOD stress tests.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Variant Training with Consistency Loss
- **Claim:** Training on K distinct surface forms per arithmetic fact, with a loss penalizing logit disagreement, directly incentivizes invariance to superficial format changes.
- **Mechanism:** The consistency loss creates gradient pressure to discard features that vary across variants (exact token positions, template words) and retain invariant core (digit values, relative order).
- **Break condition:** K is too low (e.g., K=1), variants are insufficiently diverse, or consistency loss weight (λ) is too small.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Disjoint-pair split**
  - *Why needed:* Ensures test accuracy reflects learning the modular addition function, not memorizing specific `(a,b)` pairs.
  - *Quick check:* If some pairs appear in both train and test, high test accuracy could be due to lookup, not algorithmic generalization.
- **Concept: Character-level tokenization**
  - *Why needed:* Makes absolute position a dominant feature; shifting the expression changes every token's index, exposing the position-shift failure mode.
  - *Quick check:* Would subword tokenizers mitigate the position-shift problem? Possibly less severe because digit grouping could create position-invariant subwords, but relative span localization might still depend on absolute position.
- **Concept: Grokking (delayed generalization)**
  - *Why needed:* Provides a lens for understanding how models transition from memorization to algorithmic solutions on small, clean datasets. This paper shows that even after grokking (high disjoint-pair test accuracy), format invariance may not have emerged.
  - *Quick check:* Does improved robustness under I1 necessarily mean the model has "grokked"? Not necessarily; it may have learned an invariant heuristic rather than a correct multi-digit procedure. Evidence like generalization to longer operands would be needed.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (character sequence) -> Embedding layer -> Learned absolute positional embeddings -> Transformer encoder (2 layers, 4 heads) -> [CLS] token pooling -> Linear classifier (97 classes)
- **Critical path:** Training pipeline must correctly implement: (a) variant rendering (position sampling, template selection), (b) curriculum step scheduling, (c) multi-variant batch collation (K variants per pair), (d) joint loss computation (cross-entropy + λ * consistency loss).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Position curriculum steps vs. final range:* More steps per phase risks slow training; wider final range increases robustness but may need more variants/consistency pressure.
  - *K (variants per pair) vs. batch size:* Higher K improves consistency signal but reduces unique pairs per batch, potentially slowing convergence.
  - *Anchors:* Add vocabulary size and may bias toward anchored evaluation, but provide strong structural prior.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Position cliff:** High accuracy at training positions but near-chance at slightly shifted positions (e.g., >95% at pos16 but <5% at pos8).
  - **Template collapse:** High accuracy on anchor-based templates but near-zero on no-anchor OOD templates.
  - **ALiBi fail:** In-distribution accuracy remains ~20%, indicating failure to parse multi-digit spans without absolute position signals.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train `Baseline-001` (no padding, single template, no anchors, K=1). Expect high in-distribution accuracy but catastrophic failure on position-shift and OOD templates. Confirms the robustness cliff.
  2. **Position-only intervention:** Train `I1-001.1` (position curriculum + multi-variant consistency, K=4, no template diversity). Expect position-shift accuracy to rise to ~70%, no-anchor OOD to ~60%, in-distribution maintained. Isolates effect of position invariance.
  3. **Full intervention:** Train `I1-002a` (add template diversity + anchors). Expect no-anchor OOD to jump to ~80%, anchor OOD to ~95%, position-shift similar to I1-001.1. Tests synergy of all components.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How do the trained invariances mechanistically manifest in the model's internal representations and computations?
  - *Basis:* Future work should extend to mechanistic analyses of the learned invariances (Section 6).
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper demonstrates behavioral robustness but provides no interpretability study of how position- and template-invariant solutions are implemented.
- **Question 2:** Do the proposed interventions effectively induce robust procedural generalization for more complex algorithmic tasks beyond single-step modular addition?
  - *Basis:* Future work should extend to compositional expressions (Section 6).
  - *Why unresolved:* Evaluation is restricted to a narrow, single-operation task; it is unclear whether the same training recipe yields invariances for multi-step reasoning or deeper compositional structure.
- **Question 3:** Can explicit invariance training be productively combined with grokking-acceleration techniques to improve both convergence speed and robustness under noisy supervision?
  - *Basis:* It is natural to combine explicit invariance training with grokking-acceleration techniques [2,9] (Section 6).
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper treats these approaches as complementary but does not empirically test their integration; relative benefits and trade-offs remain unknown.
- **Question 4:** Why does replacing learned absolute positional embeddings with ALiBi-style relative biases completely prevent learning of the character-level modular addition task?
  - *Basis:* The ALiBi ablation (I1-002-ALiBi) fails catastrophically (21.4% in-distribution) despite using the same invariance training recipe (Section 5.3).
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper speculates that learned absolute positions aid span localization but offers no mechanistic explanation for why ALiBi cannot suffice even with explicit invariance pressures.

## Limitations
- The exact template strings and filler text generation details are not enumerated in the paper; replicability relies on unreviewed code.
- Experiments are confined to modulus 97 and a 2-layer Transformer; generalizability to other moduli, larger models, or more complex tasks is uncertain.
- No systematic analysis of hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., consistency loss weight λ, variant count K) or comparison to alternative robustness methods.

## Confidence
- **Baseline failure** (catastrophic position/template shift): High — replicated across seeds and aligns with theoretical expectation.
- **I1-002a robustness gains** (position-shift and OOD improvements): Medium — results are strong but may be specific to the chosen architecture and modulus; limited ablations leave uncertainty about necessity of all components.
- **ALiBi ablation failure** (complete inability to learn): High — stark contrast with absolute-position baseline, though ALiBi might be under-tuned for this character-level setup.

## Next Checks
1. **Test transfer to different modulus:** Train I1-002a with a different prime modulus (e.g., 67) to assess whether robustness gains generalize beyond mod 97.
2. **Scale model capacity:** Increase model size (e.g., 4 layers, d_model=256) to confirm that robustness gains persist with larger capacity and do not require the specific small architecture.
3. **Vary position curriculum schedule:** Systematically change the number of curriculum phases and final position range (e.g., expand to [10,90]) to measure impact on position-shift accuracy; also compare against uniform random position sampling without curriculum.
