# Beyond Manual Planning: Seating Allocation for Large Organizations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05875
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05875
- **Reference Count:** 6
- **Primary Result:** A PRM-RRT-based hierarchical seating allocation framework computes accurate walkable distances and, using a deferred office assignment strategy, produces near-optimal contiguous team allocations significantly faster than exact methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the Hierarchical Seating Allocation Problem (HSAP), which involves assigning teams with a formal organizational structure to office seats while minimizing total walkable distance to each team’s central seat. Manual processes are often suboptimal. The proposed solution uses a Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) with Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT) to compute realistic walkable distances that account for obstacles like walls, preventing underestimation from simple geometric metrics. The large-scale HSAP is made tractable by decomposing it into a sequence of smaller Seating Allocation (SA) subproblems solved via integer programming and heuristics. A key innovation is delaying office allocation until after all desks are placed, which improves proximity for higher-level teams. On benchmark instances, the Iterative Clustering Algorithm with Local Search (ICA+LS) achieves solution quality within a small gap of the optimal exact method but with orders-of-magnitude lower computation time.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two main stages. First, a **distance engine** approximates walkable paths between all seats by sampling collision-free points in the floor plan to build a Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM), connecting nearby nodes and linking each seat to the graph. Shortest paths in this graph (via Floyd-Warshall) yield the pairwise distance matrix. Second, a **hierarchical allocator** traverses the organization tree top-down (depth-first). At each parent node, it solves a Seating Allocation (SA) subproblem to assign a contiguous block of seats to each child team within the parent’s allocated seats. SA is solved using exact integer programming (IPSA) for small instances or heuristics like Iterative Clustering (ICA) or Greedy with Regret (GSA). After all desks are allocated, offices are assigned in a final bottom-up step (delayed office selection) via a dedicated IP that places each team’s office near its leaf teams’ central seats.

## Key Results
- PRM-RRT distances avoid splitting teams across walls, unlike Euclidean/Manhattan metrics.
- The heuristic ICA+LS method yields near-optimal central seat distances (e.g., 329.05 pixels for small, 961.95 for medium, 273.4 for large instances) with drastically reduced runtimes (9.9s, 8.3s, 956s) compared to the exact IPSA solver.
- Delayed office allocation improves proximity for higher-level teams in the hierarchy.
- The framework consistently generates human-preferred contiguous team allocations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: PRM-RRT approximates walkable distances, avoiding obstacle-induced underestimation.
- **Claim:** Using a Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) with Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT) to compute pairwise seat distances yields more accurate walkable path estimates than Manhattan or Euclidean metrics, preventing allocations that split teams across impassable walls.
- **Mechanism:** The PRM samples collision-free points and connects nearby nodes within a threshold `δ_s`, forming a graph approximating the traversable space. The shortest path in this graph between seat nodes estimates walkable distance. RRT’s rapid exploration aids connectivity in complex floor plans.
- **Core assumption:** A connected PRM graph with sufficient sampling provides a path length close to the true shortest walkable path, and connecting seat nodes to the nearest PRM node within `δ_s` is sufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...use a Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) with Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT) to compute accurate walkable distances between seats, avoiding underestimation from obstacles like walls."
  - [section 3.1] Detailed Algorithm 1 for PRM generation, discussion of `δ_s` connectivity, and Floyd-Warshall for all-pairs distances.
- **Break condition:** If the floor plan has many narrow corridors or obstacles requiring fine-grained sampling, the PRM may fail to connect all seats within a reasonable `K` (node limit), leading to disconnected components and undefined distances. The paper notes `K` may need increasing if seats remain unconnected.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical decomposition into sequential SA subproblems makes large HSAP instances tractable.
- **Claim:** Decomposing the NP-hard HSAP into a sequence of independent Seating Allocation (SA) subproblems solved via dynamic programming (depth-first traversal) avoids intractable monolithic optimization.
- **Mechanism:** The organizational hierarchy is traversed top-down. At each parent team, an SA subproblem is solved to allocate seats to its immediate child teams. Child teams are then recursively allocated only within the seats assigned to their parent, enforcing hierarchical proximity constraints locally.
- **Core assumption:** The global HSAP objective (sum of distances at all levels) can be optimized by greedily optimizing each parent-child level sequentially, and local optimality at each level contributes to a good global solution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...HSAP is decomposed into sequential Seating Allocation (SA) subproblems solved via integer programming and heuristics..."
  - [section 3.3] Formal definition of HSAP and Algorithm 4 (DF-HSA) showing the recursive, level-by-level allocation.
- **Break condition:** If early subproblem allocations severely restrict feasible seat choices for deeper hierarchy levels (e.g., a parent team’s poor allocation leaves no contiguous blocks for its children), the sequential approach may produce a globally suboptimal or infeasible solution for leaf teams. The "delayed office selection" is a patch for one symptom.

### Mechanism 3: Delaying office allocation until leaf-team desk allocation improves hierarchical proximity.
- **Claim:** Allocating offices only after all desks are placed (bottom-up within the top-down DF-HSA) yields office placements closer to their team’s central seat, improving solution quality as evaluated by humans.
- **Mechanism:** In the standard DF-HSA, offices are allocated at higher hierarchy levels without knowledge of which leaf teams will ultimately use them. Delaying office allocation means offices are assigned in a final step, post-desk-allocation, where each leaf team’s central seat (from its desk cluster) is known. A simple ICA-style IP then assigns nearby offices.
- **Core assumption:** The primary human preference is that an office for a team (even a branch team) should be close to the desks of its *leaf* team members, not just its immediate children’s desks. Early office allocation lacks this leaf-level information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...delayed office allocation improves proximity for higher-level teams."
  - [section 3.3] "Delayed Office Selection" subsection describing the two-phase approach (desks top-down, offices bottom-up) and the final IP formulation (Eq. 17-19).
- **Break condition:** If office requirements for high-level branch teams are substantial and must be satisfied early due to hard constraints (e.g., executive offices must be on a specific floor), the delay may be infeasible. Also, if the floor plan has very few offices clustered in one area, delay may not improve average distance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Capacitated p-Median Problem (CPMP)**
  - **Why needed here:** The SA problem is framed as a special case of CPMP where each "median" (central seat) serves a "demand" (team size) but has no capacity limit itself—all assigned seats are the demand. Understanding CPMP clarifies the NP-hardness and why direct MIQP is unscalable.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the SA objective (minimizing sum of distances to central seat) relate to the classic p-median objective, and what makes it "capacitated" in the SA context?

- **Concept: Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) for Motion Planning**
  - **Why needed here:** PRM is the core technique to approximate walkable distances in complex, obstacle-filled floor plans without requiring a fine, manually curated grid.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the roles of the parameters `δ_c` (connection distance) and `δ_s` (seat connection threshold) in Algorithm 1, and how do they trade off path accuracy vs. graph connectivity?

- **Concept: Integer Programming (IP) for Allocation**
  - **Why needed here:** The SA and final office allocation subproblems are formulated as binary IPs (e.g., IPSA, Eq. 1-6; delayed office IP, Eq. 17-19). Understanding variable design (`x_ik`, `y_kt`) and constraints is key to following the heuristic improvements.
  - **Quick check question:** In the IPSA formulation (Eq. 1-6), what does constraint (5) enforce, and why is it quadratic in variables if written naively (as in Appendix MIQP)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Floor Plan Parser -> PRM Distance Engine -> Hierarchical Allocator (DF-HSA) -> [SA Solver Suite] -> Delayed Office Allocator
  (SA Solver Suite includes IPSA, ICA/ICA++, GSA, LS)

- **Critical path:** Floor Plan Parser produces seat coordinates and obstacle map. PRM Distance Engine computes the distance matrix D. Hierarchical Allocator (DF-HSA) recursively invokes an SA Solver for each hierarchy level. If delayed offices are enabled, the Delayed Office Allocator runs after all desks are placed.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **PRM vs. Grid-based A*:** PRM avoids manual floor plan discretization but requires tuning `K`, `δ_c`, `δ_s` and may be slower for one-off queries. A* on a grid is exact but needs a manually created traversal graph.
  - **Heuristic (ICA+LS) vs. Exact (IPSA):** Heuristics scale to large `|S|` but sacrifice optimality guarantees. IPSA is optimal but hits time limits on large instances due to `|S|^2` variables.
  - **Delayed Offices:** Improves proximity for high-level teams but adds a final IP solve and may violate if offices have hard location constraints not modeled.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **PRM failure:** Algorithm 1 returns with some seats unconnected (`connected==false`). Symptom: `D` matrix has `∞` entries. Fix: increase `K`, adjust `δ_c`/`δ_s`.
  - **SA infeasibility:** The `allocation` IP (Eq. 7-10) is infeasible. Symptom: solver returns infeasible. Likely cause: team demands exceed available seats of required type in the subproblem’s seat subset.
  - **Poor hierarchical contiguity:** Leaf teams of a parent are scattered. Symptom: visual output shows non-contiguous clusters for child teams. Could be due to poor `IPSA`/`ICA` solution at parent level or PRM distance inaccuracies.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate PRM distances:** On a synthetic floor plan with known straight-line and obstacle-obstructed paths, compare PRM shortest path lengths to manually measured walkable lengths. Check if Euclidean underestimates across walls.
  2. **Benchmark SA solvers:** For a fixed small/medium floor plan and single-level team set, run `IPSA` (with time limit), `ICA`, `ICA++`, `GSA`, `ICA+LS`. Compare objective (central seat distance) and runtime. Expect `IPSA` best but slow; `ICA+LS` near-optimal faster.
  3. **Test delayed office impact:** Run `DF-HSA` with a 2-3 level hierarchy on a medium instance. Compare (a) standard DF-HSA (offices allocated at each level) vs. (b) delayed office variant. Measure average office-to-central-seat distance per hierarchy level. Expect lower distances for higher-level teams in (b).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can a more robust local search heuristic be designed for the capacitated seating allocation problem that scales to large instances while improving solution quality? (Basis: Section 5 notes current LS uses fixed neighbor size and suffers slow convergence.)
- **Open Question 2:** Can a dynamic algorithm selection pipeline automatically choose the best seating allocation method (IPSA, ICA, GSA, etc.) for a given subproblem based on its size and structural complexity? (Basis: Section 5 suggests adapting a pipeline from prior work.)
- **Open Question 3:** In which scenarios (floor plan layouts, hierarchy shapes, team sizes) do different distance estimation methods (PRM+RRT vs. Euclidean) and allocation heuristics produce solutions that best match human evaluator preferences? (Basis: Section 4.2 identifies a disconnect between constrained programming and human-evaluated quality.)
- **Open Question 4:** For densely packed office layouts with long rows of seats, would allocating teams by minimizing the bounding box area (instead of total distance to a central seat) yield more contiguous, human‑preferred solutions? (Basis: Section 4.2 proposes exploring smallest bounding box objectives for linear arrangements.)

## Limitations
- **External validation is weak:** All mechanistic claims rely on internal evidence; neighboring corpus shows no direct corroborating work.
- **Reproduction is blocked:** Critical hyperparameters (K, δ_c, δ_s), exact benchmark data, RRT step details, and MILP solver settings are unreported.
- **Scalability claims are unsupported:** Tests cover three instance sizes but not extreme layouts (many narrow corridors) or deeply nested hierarchies where decomposition could fail.
- **Human preference justification is qualitative:** The claim of "human-preferred" allocations lacks user study data, being inferred from office distance improvements and visual inspection.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (PRM-RRT distance accuracy):** **Low** – Internal proof-of-concept only; no comparison to ground-truth walkable paths in complex real floor plans.
- **Mechanism 2 (Hierarchical decomposition tractability):** **Low** – No ablation showing monolithic IP infeasibility beyond anecdotal time limits; greedy sequential optimality is assumed, not proven.
- **Mechanism 3 (Delayed office allocation benefit):** **Low** – Benefit shown only on paper’s instances; no controlled test isolating leaf-team centrality as the driver.
- **Quantitative results (ICA+LS vs. IPSA):** **Medium** – Benchmarks are internally consistent but lack independent verification or statistical testing across multiple random seeds.
