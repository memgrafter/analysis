# From Skeletons to Semantics: Design and Deployment of a Hybrid Edge-Based Action Detection System for Public Safety

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29777
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29777
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A hybrid edge system for real-time violent behavior detection combining a skeleton pipeline (2.54 s latency, 41.9 eFPS) with a vision-language model (5.49 s latency, 1.34 eFPS) to balance speed, privacy, and resource limits.

## Executive Summary
Real-time detection of violent behavior in public spaces under edge-computing constraints presents a trade-off between latency, accuracy, privacy, and resource usage. This paper introduces a hybrid architecture that marries a fast, privacy-preserving skeleton-based pipeline with a slower but context-aware vision-language model (VLM). The skeleton pipeline achieves low latency (2.54 s) and high throughput (41.9 eFPS) by processing pose keypoints, while the VLM provides zero-shot semantic reasoning at the cost of higher memory (101.7 GB) and latency (5.49 s). Deployed on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor, the system demonstrates complementary strengths and motivates a selective triggering scheme where the VLM is invoked only on skeleton-detected events to enhance precision without sacrificing overall responsiveness.

## Method Summary
The proposed system comprises two independent processing backends: a skeleton-based pipeline that extracts 2D poses via YOLO-Pose, tracks them with ByteTrack, buffers frames, lifts to 3D using MotionBERT, and classifies actions with ProtoGCN; and a vision-language pipeline that samples video frames at two rates (1 FPS for context, 6 FPS for recent action) and feeds them to Qwen3.5-VL to produce narrative descriptions and risk assessments via recursive inference. A planned agent layer would monitor skeleton outputs and trigger the VLM only when risk thresholds are exceeded, thereby combining continuous low-latency monitoring with high-cost contextual validation. The implementation uses two FastAPI servers (ports 8080/8090), a React frontend, and SQLite storage, with configuration via environment variables.

## Key Results
- Skeleton pipeline: 41.9 effective FPS, 2.54 s end-to-end latency, 14.5 GB peak memory.
- VLM pipeline: 1.34 effective FPS, 5.49 s latency, 101.7 GB peak memory.
- Results show complementary trade-offs: skeleton enables continuous low-latency monitoring; VLM provides richer scene understanding at higher computational cost, motivating selective hybrid triggering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The skeleton-based pipeline provides continuous, low-latency detection of physically violent actions (e.g., punches, kicks) under edge-computing constraints by processing compact pose keypoints instead of full-resolution video.
- **Mechanism:** Multi-stage processing: (1) YOLO-Pose estimates 2D body keypoints per frame; (2) ByteTrack associates keypoints across frames to maintain person identities; (3) a sliding buffer accumulates `clip_len` frames per person; (4) MotionBERT lifts 2D poses to 3D; (5) ProtoGCN classifies the 3D skeleton sequence into action classes. The stride-based emission (every ~1s at 30 FPS) amortizes inference cost.
- **Core assumption:** Physically violent actions produce distinctive kinematic patterns (e.g., fast arm extensions, reduced interpersonal distance) that are preserved in skeleton representations and distinguishable by a GCN trained on NTU RGB+D.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "skeleton-based processing enables continuous, privacy-aware monitoring with low computational overhead"
  - [section III.A, III.B, III.H] Details the five-stage pipeline, YOLO-Pose selection for constant per-frame cost, and ProtoGCN classification with risk thresholds.
  - [corpus] No direct anchor; neighbor papers [3, 4] cite skeleton-based anomaly detection in CCTV environments, providing contextual support for the paradigm.
- **Break condition:** Degraded pose estimation due to occlusions, crowds, or shadows (Section IV, Fig. 6), leading to incomplete/erroneous skeletons and GCN misclassification. Also, inability to distinguish contextually different but kinematically similar actions (e.g., handshake vs. pickpocketing).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The vision-language model (VLM) layer provides contextual, zero-shot semantic reasoning to interpret complex or previously unseen situations by analysing raw video frames with natural language prompts.
- **Mechanism:** Qwen3.5-VL processes dual video streams: a sparse context stream (1 FPS) for scene history and a denser action stream (6 FPS) for recent motion. The model generates narrative descriptions and risk assessments via recursive inference (feeding prior summaries as context), enabling detection based on object interactions and environmental cues beyond kinematics.
- **Core assumption:** Large-scale VLMs pretrained on multimodal data possess sufficient world knowledge and reasoning to identify threats (e.g., "aggressive behaviour") from visual cues without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "vision-language models provide contextual understanding and zero-shot reasoning capabilities for complex and previously unseen situations."
  - [section V.A, V.B] Describes object/context awareness, zero-shot deployment, dual-stream sampling, and recursive narrative inference.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers [7, 14, 15] demonstrate zero-shot and fine-tuned VLM performance on action recognition benchmarks (Table III).
- **Break condition:** Prohibitively high computational cost (1.34 eFPS, 5.49s latency, 101.7 GB memory on Jetson Thor, Table IV), making continuous operation infeasible. Also, potential for semantic drift or hallucination in unconstrained natural language generation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A selective hybrid trigger architecture combines the strengths of both layers: the skeleton layer runs continuously for fast, resource-efficient alerts, while the VLM is invoked only on skeleton-detected events to provide contextual validation and reduce false alarms.
- **Mechanism:** The (planned) agent layer monitors skeleton pipeline outputs. When a "DANGER" or "WARNING" class probability exceeds thresholds (0.3/0.5), the agent triggers the VLM to analyse the same or recent video segment. The final decision combines classifier confidence with VLM's semantic assessment.
- **Core assumption:** Most time is spent on benign scenes; selectively invoking the expensive VLM only on skeleton-positive events maintains overall system viability while improving precision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "motivate a hybrid architecture that selectively augments fast skeleton-based detection with higher-level semantic reasoning."
  - [section II.C] "The agent layer is planned as future work to integrate model coordination, confidence handling..."
  - [section VII] Discusses how hybrid approaches mitigate risks like false alarms from skeleton context blindness.
- **Break condition:** Poor coordination leading to missed events (skeleton false negative) or delayed VLM validation (if skeleton triggers too late). Incorrect VLM override of a valid skeleton alert (or vice versa) due to mismatched confidence thresholds. Assumption: agent logic not yet implemented; current system runs pipelines independently.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input: USB/RTSP camera → StreamCapture (producer thread).
  Skeleton Backend (port 8090): YOLO-Pose (TensorRT) → ByteTrack → Skeleton Buffer → MotionBERT (2D→3D) → Joint Remapping → Pairing → ProtoGCN/CTR-GCN → Risk classification → Alerts/SQLite.
  VLM Backend (port 8080): Frame sampler (dual-stream) → Qwen3.5-VL (via vLLM) → Narrative generation → Risk classification → Alerts/SQLite.
  Frontend: React dashboard (proxy to both backends), WebSocket for live stream/metrics, alert feed with video clips.
  Config: Pydantic settings (env vars) for model paths, thresholds, sampling rates.
- **Critical path (skeleton alert):** Frame capture → YOLO-Pose inference (13.2ms/frame) → ByteTrack association → Buffer fill (100 frames ≈ 3.3s at 30 FPS) → MotionBERT + GCN inference (148ms) → risk threshold check → alert record + video clip save. End-to-end: ~2.54s.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Latency vs. Accuracy: Skeleton low latency, limited semantics; VLM high latency, rich context; hybrid aims for best of both but adds coordination complexity.
  - Privacy vs. Context: Skeleton keypoints are privacy-preserving; VLM uses raw frames but processes locally.
  - Resource vs. Functionality: Running both pipelines consumes 14.5 GB (skeleton) vs. 101.7 GB (VLM) peak memory; VLM requires two vLLM server processes.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Skeleton: Sudden drop in detected skeletons, erratic track IDs, repeated "shadow" skeletons, consistently low GCN confidence across classes.
  - VLM: Memory exhaustion (OOM), increasing inference latency >10s, repetitive/nonsensical text outputs.
  - System: Alert flood or prolonged silence after obvious violence; backend disconnects; SQLite write failures.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline throughput: Run each backend standalone on sample video; measure eFPS, latency, memory via `tegrastats`. Verify skeleton ~41.9 eFPS / VLM ~1.34 eFPS as in Table IV.
  2. Trigger logic simulation: Feed pre-saved clips of violent/non-violent scenes into skeleton backend; adjust `yolo_confidence` and `danger` probability threshold to control alert rate. Record precision/recall against ground truth (hand-labelled).
  3. Resource contention test: Run both backends simultaneously; monitor GPU power draw and frame drop rates in StreamCapture queues. Identify configuration that prevents frame drops (e.g., reduce VLM `clip_len` or skeleton `max_persons`).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the context blindness of skeleton-based action detection be effectively mitigated in hybrid systems?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section IV explicitly identifies "context blindness" as a key limitation, where skeleton models fail to distinguish semantically different but kinematically similar actions (e.g., theft vs. handshake) due to discarding visual scene information.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper proposes a hybrid architecture to combine skeleton-based speed with VLM semantics but does not specify how the VLM's contextual reasoning should be integrated to resolve these ambiguities in practice.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation comparing false alarm rates and detection accuracy for context-dependent actions (e.g., wallet handover vs. punch) across standalone skeleton, standalone VLM, and various hybrid triggering strategies on edge hardware.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is an optimal and robust triggering mechanism for selectively invoking the high-cost VLM based on skeleton-based alerts?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The abstract and Section VI motivate a "selective hybrid trigger design" to offset the VLM's high latency (5.49 s) and memory (101.7 GB) against the skeleton layer's efficiency, but the agent-based coordination logic is unimplemented (Section II.C).
- **Why unresolved:** The current prototype runs both pipelines independently; the criteria (e.g., confidence thresholds, temporal patterns, multi-person interactions) for when and how to escalate from skeleton alerts to VLM analysis are unspecified and untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation and evaluation of candidate trigger policies (e.g., risk score thresholds, temporal hysteresis, consensus checks) measuring overall system latency, resource spikes, and reduction in false positives/negatives compared to both standalone modes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does skeleton-based action classification performance degrade with increasing crowd density and occlusion, and can hybrid systems maintain robustness?
- **Evidence in paper:** [explicit] Section IV and Fig. 6 document failures from shadows, partial occlusions, and background structures causing incorrect skeleton extraction, especially in crowded scenes. Section VII notes challenges in "crowded scenes with many interacting people."
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation uses demonstrator-based scenarios without quantifying performance across controlled levels of crowd density or occlusion severity. The potential for VLM context to compensate for degraded skeleton input is hypothesised but not measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments with synthetically varied crowd densities and occlusion patterns, reporting skeleton pipeline accuracy (e.g., on NTU-derived metrics) and hybrid system recall/precision for violent actions, analysing correlation between skeleton quality metrics and final hybrid decisions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What legal, governance, and operational factors most critically influence the real-world deployment and acceptance of edge-based hybrid video analysis for public safety?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section VII details evolving legal frameworks (e.g., Hesse vs. Berlin), the need for transparency, auditability, and human-in-the-loop operation, and states that "practical suitability emerges through real-world use rather than purely technical optimisation."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's demonstrator is a laboratory setup; the interplay between technical design choices (e.g., data retention, inter-device communication, explainability outputs) and regulatory/social acceptance in specific jurisdictions is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal case studies or structured interviews with operators, data protection officers, and legal authorities in pilot deployments, mapping system configuration parameters (e.g., alert logging, VLM prompt logging) to compliance checklists and stakeholder trust metrics.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can vision-language model inference pipelines be optimised sufficiently to achieve sub-second latency and reduced memory footprint on edge devices without sacrificing zero-shot semantic accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section VI reports the VLM layer at 1.34 eFPS, 5.49 s latency, and 101.7 GB memory on the Jetson AGX Thor. Section VIII lists "improving the feasibility and robustness of vision-language models on edge hardware" as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The current deployment uses a 35B model via dual vLLM servers with high overhead; potential optimisations (e.g., quantisation, distillation, sparsity, different model sizes, hardware-aware compilation) are not evaluated for their accuracy-latency-memory trade-offs in this specific public safety context.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark suite measuring a range of model compression and serving techniques (e.g., 4-bit quantisation, speculative decoding, CNN-based early exit) on the target edge platform, reporting end-to-end latency, peak memory, power, and zero-shot performance on a curated set of public safety-relevant video prompts.

## Limitations
- The hybrid trigger agent is only planned; coordination logic and performance are unknown.
- The VLM backend requires >100 GB memory and 5.49 s latency, making continuous operation infeasible on the target edge device.
- Skeleton-based action detection is vulnerable to occlusions and context ambiguity; no accuracy metrics are reported in the evaluation.
- Multi-camera scalability is not addressed; resource contention likely under simultaneous streams.
