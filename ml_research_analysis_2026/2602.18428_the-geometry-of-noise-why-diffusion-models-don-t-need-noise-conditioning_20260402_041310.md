# The Geometry of Noise: Why Diffusion Models Don't Need Noise Conditioning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18428
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18428
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Autonomous (noise-agnostic) diffusion models generate stably without explicit noise-level conditioning because the learned vector field is a Riemannian gradient flow on a "Marginal Energy", where a conformal metric from the posterior noise variance neutralizes the energy's singularity near the data manifold; noise-prediction parameterizations are structurally unstable, while velocity-based ones are inherently stable.

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves the mystery of why autonomous diffusion models—using a single time-invariant vector field—can produce high-quality samples without conditioning on noise levels. The authors formalize the implicit learning objective as gradient descent on a "Marginal Energy" defined by integrating noisy data distributions over a prior on noise levels. They prove the learned field corresponds to a Riemannian gradient flow on this energy, where a local conformal metric derived from the posterior noise variance exactly cancels the energy's geometric singularity near the data manifold, turning an infinitely deep potential well into a stable attractor. A stability analysis shows that noise-prediction schemes (DDPM/DDIM) suffer from a high-gain "Jensen Gap" that amplifies errors and causes divergence, while velocity-based approaches (Flow Matching, Equilibrium Matching) have bounded gain and are inherently stable. Empirical tests on CIFAR-10 confirm: autonomous noise-prediction fails catastrophically (FID 40.90), while autonomous velocity models achieve state-of-the-art results (FID 2.23–2.61), matching conditional counterparts.

## Method Summary
The study combines rigorous theory with targeted experiments. Theoretically, it derives the "Marginal Energy" landscape that an autonomous diffusion model implicitly minimizes by averaging over all noise levels according to a prior. The optimal autonomous vector field decomposes into: (i) a Riemannian gradient of the marginal energy scaled by an effective gain λ(u), (ii) a transport correction term involving the posterior covariance, and (iii) a linear drift. Under assumptions of low-dimensional data manifolds (d ≪ D) and smooth affine noise schedules, the posterior concentrates either globally (in high ambient dimensions) or locally (near the manifold), making the transport correction vanish and the gain perfectly cancel the energy gradient's singularity. This yields a stable, bounded flow. The analysis then compares parameterizations: noise-prediction introduces a gain factor ν(t) ∝ 1/b(t) that blows up as t→0 and amplifies the Jensen Gap (a constant posterior uncertainty), causing divergence; velocity-prediction uses ν(t)=1, keeping errors bounded; signal-prediction (EDM) decays exponentially, overcoming the singularity. Empirically, autonomous (blind) and conditional models are trained on CIFAR-10 for DDPM (noise-prediction) and Flow Matching (velocity-prediction), with sample quality measured by FID. As predicted, autonomous DDPM fails (FID 40.90), while autonomous Flow Matching succeeds (FID 2.23–2.61), matching conditional versions.

## Key Results
- Autonomous diffusion models are stable because they implement a Riemannian gradient flow on the Marginal Energy, with the posterior noise variance providing a conformal metric that neutralizes the energy's singularity near the data manifold.
- Noise-prediction parameterizations (DDPM/DDIM) are structurally unstable due to a high-gain "Jensen Gap" that diverges as t→0; velocity-based parameterizations (Flow Matching, Equilibrium Matching) have bounded gain and are inherently stable.
- Empirical verification on CIFAR-10: autonomous DDPM fails catastrophically (FID 40.90), while autonomous velocity models achieve state-of-the-art results (FID 2.23–2.61), identical to their conditionally-trained counterparts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper explains the success of autonomous models through three interconnected mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Riemannian Gradient Flow with Local Conformal Metric**  
The optimal autonomous field is a Riemannian gradient flow on the Marginal Energy. Near the data manifold (t→0), the posterior concentrates, causing a transport correction term to vanish. Simultaneously, the effective gain λ(u) scales as b(t), exactly canceling the 1/b(t)² divergence of the raw energy gradient. This yields a bounded, stable attractor. Assumptions: data manifold dimension d ≪ D, affine smooth schedule with b(0)=0, ḃ(0) finite.

**Mechanism 2: Posterior Concentration Enables Noise-Agnostic Inference**  
In high ambient dimensions (D ≫ d), the noisy observation u = x + n concentrates on a spherical shell of radius ≈ ||n|| = σ√D. Distinct noise levels produce non-overlapping shells, so t becomes globally retrievable from ||u||, collapsing the posterior p(t|u) to a delta. This eliminates the Jensen Gap. For lower dimensions, local concentration near the manifold still ensures the posterior sharpens as t→0, allowing a single network to handle all noise scales.

**Mechanism 3: Parameterization Determines Structural Stability**  
The sampling ODE drift is ν(t)(f*(u)−f*t(u)). For noise-prediction, ν(t) ∝ 1/b(t) and the error ∝ Jensen Gap (constant >0 without posterior collapse). Their product diverges as t→0 → instability. For velocity-prediction, ν(t)=1 → bounded error; for signal-prediction (EDM), error decays exponentially, overcoming the 1/b(t)² divergence. Thus, velocity-based parameterizations guarantee stability.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Network (time-invariant U-Net fθ(u)) → target r (noise/velocity)  
  Schedule a(t), b(t) defines forward noising ut = a(t)x + b(t)ϵ  
  Sampler solves du/dt = µ(t)u + ν(t)fθ(u) with schedule-derived coefficients  
  Training loss: MSE(fθ(ut), r(c(t)x + d(t)ϵ)) with t∼Uniform(0,1)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Choose parameterization (velocity recommended for autonomous).  
  2. Set schedule (e.g., linear a=1−t, b=t for Flow Matching; cosine for EDM).  
  3. Train on noisy ut; do not feed t into network (only used for target).  
  4. Validate stability: monitor ||fθ(u)|| during early sampling; should remain bounded.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Velocity: inherent stability (ν=1) but target is ˙u; noise-prediction: simpler target but unstable autonomously.  
  - Schedule: affine schedules analytically tractable; SNRs must decrease monotonically.  
  - Capacity: network must approximate conditional denoiser D*t(u) for all t; f*(u) is a posterior average of these.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **DDPM Blind:** Norm explosion in first sampling steps, white-noise images (FID >>30 on CIFAR-10).  
  - **Flow Matching Blind:** Slow convergence if underfitting; check fθ(u) ≈ (u−D*t(u))/t at high noise.  
  - **General:** Residual high-frequency noise; inspect ν(t) for singularities.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate CIFAR-10 stability test:** Train DDPM Blind and Flow Matching Blind (10k steps, EMA 0.999, batch 128). Generate 50k samples; compute FID. Expect DDPM Blind FID >30, FM Blind FID <3.  
  2. **Dimensionality toy test:** Train on 2D concentric circles embedded in ℝ^D (D∈{2,8,32,128}). Visualize projections. Expect both succeed at D=128, only FM Blind at D=8.  
  3. **Effective gain measurement:** For a trained autonomous model, compute fθ(u) for u with known added ϵ (varying b(t)). Plot ||fθ(u)|| vs. b(t). DDPM Blind: blow-up as b→0; FM Blind: plateau.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does posterior concentration and the stability guarantee extend to manifolds with codimension exactly 2 (where analysis requires D−d > 2)?
2. How large is the Transport Correction term in realistic regimes, and does it bias samples or affect convergence?
3. Beyond bounded drift error, what else is needed for high-fidelity generation with velocity-based autonomous models? Is boundedness sufficient?
4. Can the geometric framework be extended to non-affine forward processes (e.g., spatially correlated noise)?

## Limitations
- Analysis assumes data lies on a low-dimensional manifold (d ≪ D); validity for high-persistence manifolds or continuous-valued data is uncertain.
- The high-dimensional concentration mechanism (Mechanism 2) lacks direct empirical validation beyond CIFAR-10.
- The boundary between velocity- and noise-prediction stability for non-affine schedules near t=0 is not fully explored.

## Confidence
- **Parameterization-driven stability analysis (Mechanism 3):** High. Clear mathematics and strong CIFAR-10 dichotomy support it.
- **Riemannian gradient flow explanation (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Rigorous but hinges on manifold and schedule assumptions.
- **Geometric concentration as sole enabler (Mechanism 2):** Medium-Low. Plausible but not isolated from other factors empirically.

## Next Checks
1. **Scalability Test:** Train autonomous FM and DDPM on ImageNet 256×256. Does the stability divide hold with longer schedules and higher d? Failure of FM would challenge bounded-gain robustness.
2. **Posterior Collapse Measurement:** On synthetic data with controllable d (e.g., concentric spheres in ℝ^D), estimate p(t|u) during training. Does its variance collapse as D−d grows, correlating with autonomous success?
3. **Schedule Ablation:** Train autonomous DDPM with a non-affine, non-smooth schedule near t=0 (e.g., ḃ(0)=∞). Theory predicts instability; observing failure tests the smoothness assumption.
