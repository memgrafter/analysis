# SignalMC-MED: A Multimodal Benchmark for Evaluating Biosignal Foundation Models on Single-Lead ECG and PPG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.09940
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09940
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Domain-specific biosignal models (e.g., CSFM, xECG) and multimodal ECG+PPG fusion outperform general time-series models, with longer signals (10 min) providing additional predictive information.

## Executive Summary
SignalMC-MED is a new multimodal benchmark for evaluating biosignal foundation models (FMs) on single-lead ECG and photoplethysmography (PPG) data. It comprises 22,256 emergency department visits, each with a synchronized 10-minute ECG and PPG signal, annotated for 20 clinical prediction tasks (demographics, disposition, lab results, prior diagnoses). Using a frozen feature extraction and linear probing framework, the authors compare general time-series models, unimodal biosignal models, and a multimodal model (CSFM). Key findings show that domain-specific biosignal models consistently outperform general models; multimodal ECG+PPG fusion yields robust improvements; full 10-minute signals significantly outperform shorter segments; larger model variants do not reliably beat smaller ones; and hand-crafted ECG features remain a strong complementary baseline. The work underscores the value of domain-specific pretraining, extended temporal context, and multimodal integration for clinical biosignal analysis.

## Method Summary
SignalMC-MED is derived from the MC-MED dataset with chronological train/val/test splits and no patient overlap. Each 10-minute signal is resampled to the model-specific frequency (default 250 Hz), cleaned and Z-score normalized using NeuroKit2, then split into non-overlapping 10-second segments. For each foundation model, a frozen encoder extracts a feature vector from every segment. Segment features are mean-pooled across the entire visit to obtain a single visit-level vector per modality. For multimodal inputs, the ECG-only and PPG-only visit vectors are averaged. Downstream tasks are solved via ridge regression (continuous targets) or logistic regression (binary classification) on these aggregated features. A single regularization strength per FM is chosen on the validation set using age regression (ridge) and sex classification (logistic). Performance on the test set is averaged across 20 tasks and reported as mean±std over five random subsamples of the training set (10%, 25%, 50%, 100%).

## Key Results
- Domain-specific biosignal foundation models (CSFM-base, xECG) achieve top AUROC across nearly all classification tasks, consistently outperforming general time-series models (MOMENT, Chronos).
- Multimodal ECG+PPG fusion (via feature averaging) yields robust improvements over unimodal inputs for most models and tasks.
- Using the full 10-minute signal significantly improves performance compared to shorter segments (e.g., 10 seconds), indicating the value of longer temporal context.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Domain-specific pretraining on large-scale cardiovascular data encodes clinically relevant morphological and rhythmic patterns as linearly accessible features, enabling superior generalization on downstream cardiac tasks compared to general time-series models.
- **Mechanism**: Exposure to millions of ECG/PPG recordings from diverse patients teaches the model domain-specific statistical regularities (e.g., PQRST complex morphology, heart rate variability patterns, artifact signatures). Under frozen linear probing, these features remain aligned with clinically meaningful axes of variation.
- **Core assumption**: Cardiovascular physiology exhibits structure that generic temporal patterns cannot capture without domain-specific inductive biases introduced during pretraining.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] "domain-specific biosignal FMs consistently outperform general time-series models"
  - [page 2] "We find that domain-specific biosignal FMs consistently outperform general time-series models"
  - [page 3] Table 2 shows CSFM-base and xECG achieving top AUROC across virtually all classification tasks; MOMENT/Chronos-Bolt cluster lower

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Extended temporal context (10 minutes vs. 10 seconds) provides additional predictive information by capturing slow-moving physiological dynamics and allowing robust computation of time-varying metrics (e.g., heart rate variability, trend stability).
- **Mechanism**: Longer signals enable aggregation of repeated cardiac cycles to estimate variability measures, detection of transient events that may not appear in a single short window, and reduction of noise through temporal averaging while preserving meaningful fluctuations.
- **Core assumption**: Certain clinically relevant states (e.g., arrhythmia burden, hemodynamic trends, response to stress) manifest over minute-scale observations rather than second-scale morphology alone.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [page 4] "Using the full 10-minute signal consistently outperforms shorter segments"
  - [page 4] Figure 4 shows clear performance gains when moving from 10sec→10min for xECG, ECGFounder, MOMENT-base across both regression and classification
  - [page 5] "longer temporal context provides additional predictive information beyond short-window morphology"

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Multimodal ECG+PPG fusion yields robust improvements because the modalities provide complementary information about cardiac function (electrical vs. volumetric), and simple late feature-level fusion effectively combines these without requiring complex cross-modal alignment.
- **Mechanism**: ECG captures depolarization and conduction; PPG captures pulsatile blood flow. Their combination offers a more complete hemodynamic picture. Late fusion (averaging independently extracted unimodal features) preserves each modality's specialized representation before combination, allowing the linear downstream model to weight complementary evidence flexibly.
- **Core assumption**: ECG and PPG, while correlated through cardiac output, contain partially independent physiological information due to different sensing principles and noise characteristics.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [page 2] "multimodal ECG + PPG fusion yields robust improvements over unimodal inputs"
  - [page 3] Figure 3 shows ECG+PPG solidly above both unimodal curves for CSFM-base, xECG, MOMENT-base across nearly all train fractions
  - [page 5] "PPG provides complementary physiological information beyond what can be captured from ECG alone"
  - [page 25] Figure S9: Feature-level fusion (solid lines) consistently slightly exceeds CSFM's internal fusion (dashed)

## Foundational Learning
- **Frozen Feature Extraction**: Freezing foundation model weights isolates representation quality from downstream adaptation, enabling fair comparisons across heterogeneous models. *Quick check*: Are observed performance differences due to the learned representations themselves, or to how easily they adapt to new tasks?
- **Linear Probing**: Training a simple linear model on frozen features tests whether information is linearly accessible, a standard diagnostic for foundation model quality. *Quick check*: Would adding a single hidden layer change relative model rankings? If yes, representations may encode information requiring non-linear decoding.
- **Multimodal Fusion Taxonomy**: Distinguishing between internal (model-native) and external (late) fusion clarifies where multimodal integration occurs and how it interacts with frozen representations. *Quick check*: Does a model's internal fusion outperform simple feature averaging when both use the same unimodal encoders?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input acquisition → Preprocessing → Segmentation → Feature extraction → Aggregation → Fusion (optional) → Downstream

**Critical path**: Signal quality control → correct segmentation → proper sampling rate conversion → successful feature extraction (model loading, correct channel formatting) → regularization hyperparameter selection on validation split → downstream training. The earliest failure (preprocessing) invalidates all subsequent steps.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Frozen vs. fine-tuned*: Frozen ensures fair representation comparison but may underutilize model capacity; fine-tuning could improve performance but confounds architecture effects.
- *Segment length*: 10-second windows balance computational cost and morphological information; longer contexts require handling variable segment counts.
- *Aggregation*: Mean pooling is simple and stable but discards temporal order; max pooling or attention could preserve rare events.
- *Fusion strategy*: Late feature-level fusion preserves unimodal integrity; early/intermediate fusion may enable cross-modal interactions but risks modality collapse.

**Failure signatures**:
- High variance across resampling runs (large ± in Table S1): unstable feature extraction or insufficient signal duration.
- PPG-specific model (PaPaGei) ranking last even on PPG-only: suggests limited pretraining data (13.5K subjects vs. 1.7M) or architectural mismatch.
- Larger variant underperforms smaller (e.g., Chronos-Bolt-base vs. mini): representational bottleneck under linear probing or suboptimal pretraining.
- Domain features beating FMs: FMs failed to learn clinically fundamental patterns; likely pretraining mismatch or insufficient capacity.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline sanity check: Extract hand-crafted ECG domain features (54-D) and train downstream models. All FMs should surpass this baseline.
2. Modality ablation: For each FM, compare ECG-only, PPG-only, and ECG+PPG (late fusion) performance. Verify multimodal beats unimodal, and cross-modal generalization occurs.
3. Signal length scaling: For a representative FM (e.g., CSFM-base), evaluate performance at 10sec, 1min, 5min, 10min. Confirm monotonic improvement with longer signals.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Fine-tuning vs. frozen evaluation**: The paper evaluates models under frozen representations; how do rankings change with end-to-end fine-tuning? Resolution: Re-evaluate with full model fine-tuning.
2. **Multimodal fusion mechanisms**: Why does simple late fusion surpass CSFM's internal fusion? Resolution: Ablate CSFM's pretraining (more paired data, contrastive losses) and test alternative fusion (e.g., cross-attention).
3. **Model scaling bottleneck**: Under what conditions does increasing parameters fail to improve performance under linear probing? Resolution: Evaluate scaling trends with nonlinear downstream heads, larger pretraining data, or different probing metrics.
4. **Generalizability**: Do findings hold across institutions, devices, and populations? Resolution: Replicate on external multimodal ECG/PPG datasets from diverse settings.
5. **ECG vs. PPG feature complementarity**: Why do hand-crafted ECG features consistently complement learned representations while PPG features do not? Resolution: Ablate specific hand-crafted features and probe models for PPG morphological markers.

## Limitations
- All data originate from a single US healthcare system; generalizability across institutions, devices, and populations remains uncertain.
- Resampling and preprocessing details (e.g., interpolation method) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproducibility.
- A single regularization strength selected on age/sex tasks is used for all 20 downstream tasks; this may not be optimal for every task and could affect relative model rankings.

## Confidence
- Domain-specific biosignal FMs > general time-series FMs: **High**
- Full 10-minute signals > shorter segments: **High**
- Multimodal ECG+PPG fusion > unimodal: **Medium**
- Larger model variants do not reliably beat smaller ones: **Medium**
- Hand-crafted ECG features provide a strong complementary baseline: **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Resampling robustness test**: Evaluate performance using alternative interpolation methods (linear, cubic, Fourier) to quantify sensitivity.
2. **Hyperparameter grid sensitivity**: For selected models, perform per-task grid search for regularization and compare rankings to the single-hyperparameter approach.
3. **Fusion strategy ablation**: Compare late feature-level fusion against intermediate fusion (e.g., cross-attention) using frozen unimodal encoders.
