# Exact Functional ANOVA Decomposition for Categorical Inputs Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02673  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02673  
- **Reference count:** 33  
- **Primary result:** An exact closed‑form functional ANOVA decomposition for arbitrary dependent categorical inputs, achieved via a parity basis that ensures hierarchical orthogonality; the method recovers SHAP under independence and scales to high‑dimensional sparse data.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel exact functional ANOVA decomposition for models with categorical inputs under arbitrary dependence structures. By bridging functional analysis and extended discrete Fourier methods, the authors construct a basis of parity‑like functions that are intrinsically hierarchically orthogonal. The decomposition is unique under full support and seamlessly recovers the classical independent case and Fourier‑based SHAP values. For sparse empirical supports, a greedy rank‑selection algorithm yields an efficient low‑rank approximation. Experiments on synthetic and real tabular datasets demonstrate accurate reconstruction, perfect orthogonality in independent settings, and scalability to hundreds of features.

## Method Summary
The decomposition expresses a function \(f\) as a sum of ANOVA components \(f_A(X_A)\) over subsets \(A\) of features, with orthogonality \(\langle f_A, g\rangle = 0\) for any \(g\) depending on a proper subset of \(A\). A basis \(\phi^{(z)}_A(x) = \prod_{i\in A}(1_{\{x_i=z_i\}} - 1_{\{x_i=N_i-1\}})/p_A(x_A)\) is defined, where \(z\) ranges over categories excluding the last. The Gram matrix \(\Gamma\) of inner products and vector \(\mu\) of inner products with \(f\) yield coefficients via \(\Gamma c = \mu\). When the empirical support \(\mathcal{X}\) is sparse (\(|\mathcal{X}| \ll |\mathcal{E}|\)), a greedy basis selection chooses a linearly independent subset of the parity functions of size \(|\mathcal{X}|\), reducing computational cost. Shapley values are obtained by averaging \(f_A\) contributions across coalitions, extending SHAP to dependent categorical inputs.

## Key Results
- Exact closed‑form decomposition with hierarchical orthogonality under arbitrary dependence and non‑rectangular supports.
- Recovers classical functional ANOVA and Fourier‑based SHAP values when inputs are independent.
- Scales to high‑dimensional sparse data (e.g., 784 binary features): perfect orthogonality errors (\(\sim10^{-16}\)), fast global computation (seconds for moderate dimensions), and strong reconstruction under truncation (R² up to 0.86).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Closed‑form Fourier decomposition for categorical inputs
The method defines a basis of parity functions \(\phi^{(z)}_A\) that are centered and mutually orthogonal under the \(L^2\) inner product weighted by the joint distribution. Under full support, the Gram matrix \(\Gamma\) is invertible, yielding a unique expansion \(f(X)=\sum_{(A,z)\in\mathcal{I}} c^{(z)}_A \phi^{(z)}_A(X)\). The core assumptions are positive marginals \(p_A(x_A)>0\) for all \(x_A\) in the support and full support for uniqueness. Evidence includes Theorem 3.2 establishing orthogonality and the Fourier expansion.

### Mechanism 2: Greedy basis selection for sparse supports
When the empirical support \(\mathcal{X}\) is small relative to the full grid \(\mathcal{E}\), a greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) iterates through a fixed order of candidate basis functions, adding each if it increases the rank of the evaluation matrix on \(\mathcal{X}\). This yields a minimal basis \(B_{S_r}\) of size \(r=|\mathcal{X}|\), ensuring a solvable restricted linear system. The approach leverages the fact that any linearly independent subset of the span forms a basis. Evidence: Theorem 4.1 guarantees existence; experiments on high‑dimensional sparse datasets (Mushrooms, Poker, etc.) show successful reconstruction with low ranks.

### Mechanism 3: Bridge to SHAP values and additive explanations
Under independence, the Gram matrix becomes block‑diagonal, and the basis orthogonality across sets \(A\) leads to ANOVA components that coincide with the Möbius transform of conditional expectations. Shapley values then arise as averages of \(f_A(x_A)/|A|\). For dependent inputs, the same harmonic basis provides a decomposition that respects hierarchical orthogonality and yields a natural generalization of SHAP. Evidence includes Corollary 3.5 and Remark 3.6, plus experimental recovery of KernelSHAP in independent settings.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Data preprocessing (estimate marginals, support) → Greedy basis construction → Linear system assembly (Gram matrix, inner products) → Solve for coefficients → Evaluation on queries (reconstruct \(f_A\), compute Shapley)

Critical path: Greedy basis selection is the bottleneck; it requires evaluating many candidate functions and repeatedly checking rank of an \(|\mathcal{X}|\times\)growing‑basis matrix.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Full decomposition (\(|\mathcal{X}|=|\mathcal{E}|\)) is only feasible for small \(d\); for larger \(d\) a low‑rank approximation trades accuracy for speed.  
- Greedy selection may favor high‑order interactions over simpler ones, potentially harming interpretability.  
- Incorporating domain knowledge (e.g., spatial structure) can prune the candidate set but reduces generality.

Failure signatures:  
(i) Greedy rank fails to reach target \(r\) – halts prematurely.  
(ii) Ill‑conditioned Gram matrix (\(\kappa > 10^{12}\)) leading to unstable solutions.  
(iii) Excessively long runtime on high‑\(d\) datasets; indicates need for structured pruning or parallelism.

First 3 experiments:  
1. Synthetic analytical case with dependent variables (\(d=5\)) to validate decomposition structure.  
2. Independent dataset comparison with KernelSHAP on Car Evaluation and Nursery.  
3. Mushrooms dataset: ground‑truth validation and sparsity analysis (reconstruction vs. rank).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to continuous inputs** – The current discrete basis does not extend to continuous domains; a continuous orthonormal functional basis is needed.  
2. **Theoretical guarantees for greedy rank selection** – No optimality or approximation bounds are proven; greedy may miss important low‑order interactions.  
3. **Validation of generalized SHAP under dependence** – Standard Shapley axioms may not hold; ground truth for dependent SHAP is lacking.  
4. **Scalability to thousands of categorical features** – Greedy selection’s complexity grows rapidly; no analysis or benchmarks beyond a few hundred features are provided.

## Limitations
- Basis functions divide by \(p_A(x_A)\); empirical marginals can be zero for observed \(x_A\), causing undefined values – handling is unspecified.  
- Greedy basis selection is heuristic; ordering and lack of guarantees may produce non‑interpretable decompositions.  
- Uniqueness requires full support \(\mathcal{X}=E\); sparse real data yield non‑unique solutions, and reconstruction quality depends heavily on chosen basis.

## Next Checks
1. **Zero‑marginal stress test** – Generate data where some \(p_A(x_A)=0\) for observed \(x\); run the method to see crashes; then apply marginal smoothing (e.g., add‑ε) and assess impact on reconstruction and orthogonality.  
2. **Greedy vs. exhaustive basis selection** – For small \(d\) (e.g., \(d=4\), binary), enumerate all bases of size \(|\mathcal{X}|\). Compare greedy reconstruction error, interaction‑order distribution, and sensitivity to different fixed orders.  
3. **Conditioning analysis** – On full‑support synthetic data with increasing \(d\), compute condition numbers of \(\Gamma\) and record reconstruction errors under varying floating‑point precision to identify the dimension where numerical instability becomes unacceptable.
