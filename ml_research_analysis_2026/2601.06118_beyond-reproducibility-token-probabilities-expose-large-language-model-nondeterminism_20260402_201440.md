# Beyond Reproducibility: Token Probabilities Expose Large Language Model Nondeterminism

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.06118
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06118
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Token probability nondeterminism exhibits a U-shaped pattern: variations are negligible for extreme probabilities (p ≲ 0.1 or p ≳ 0.9) but significant for mid-range probabilities (0.1–0.9), a pattern consistent across models, hardware, and batch sizes.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates nondeterminism in Large Language Model (LLM) inference by analyzing variations in token probabilities across multiple repeated runs, rather than just comparing final generated text. The authors define metrics—standard deviation (σj) and range (Rj) of token probabilities for each token position—and conduct experiments on several models (Gemma3, Llama3.2, Qwen3-VL, DeepSeek) across different NVIDIA and Huawei GPUs with varying batch sizes, using MMLU prompts. They discover a consistent U-shaped pattern: probability variations are negligible when token probabilities are near 0 or 1 but become significant for probabilities in the 0.1–0.9 range. This pattern holds across all evaluated models, hardware, and batch sizes, suggesting it is inherent to the softmax function. The study also implies that nondeterminism's impact on text generation can be estimated from a single inference run by analyzing the probability distribution.

## Method Summary
The study employs a controlled experimental design to quantify token-level probability nondeterminism. For each model–hardware–batch size configuration, they generate the same target prompt within a batch of size B (including B−1 distractor prompts) and repeat inference 50 times. After each run, they extract the full token probability distribution (logits) for the target prompt's generation. They align generation steps by the longest common prefix (LCP) across all runs to ensure comparable positions. For each aligned token position, they compute the standard deviation (σj) and range (Rj) of the probability for every token in the vocabulary across the 50 runs. They then aggregate metrics across multiple evaluation prompts, binning tokens by their mean probability (p) to reveal the relationship between base probability and nondeterministic variation.

## Key Results
- A robust U-shaped relationship exists between a token’s base probability and its susceptibility to nondeterministic variation: small variation for extreme probabilities (p ≲ 0.1 or p ≳ 0.9), large variation for mid-range probabilities (0.1–0.9).
- This pattern is consistent across four LLM families (Gemma3, Llama3.2, Qwen3-VL, DeepSeek), three NVIDIA GPU architectures (A100, A6000, H200), one Huawei Ascend-910 GPU, and batch sizes from 2 to 16.
- Model parameter count (270M to 12B) does not significantly change the magnitude of probability variations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Token probability variability across repeated runs provides a systematic measure of nondeterminism, beyond final text differences.
- **Mechanism:** For each token position, compute standard deviation (σj) and range (Rj) of the probability assigned to each vocabulary token across many runs. These metrics capture how stable the model's internal distribution is under identical input conditions.
- **Core assumption:** Discrepancies in generated text arise from earlier, granular perturbations in token probability distributions during decoding steps.
- **Evidence:** The paper introduces these metrics in Section 3 and states they quantify the impact of nondeterminism.
- **Break condition:** If repeated runs yield nearly identical token probability distributions (σj ≈ 0, Rj ≈ 0) for all tokens, nondeterminism is negligible.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The characteristic U-shaped pattern of nondeterminism (high variation for mid-range probabilities ~0.1–0.9, low for near 0 or 1) arises from the softmax function's response to small logit perturbations, not hardware-specific effects.
- **Mechanism:** Non-deterministic operation ordering introduces small random variations (δz) in logits. The softmax function p_i = e^z_i / Σ_j e^z_j non-linearly maps these logit variations to probability variations. When p_i is near 0 or 1, the corresponding logit z_i is much larger or smaller than competing logits, so δz_i has minimal effect on p_i. When p_i is moderate (0.2–0.8), competing logits are similar in magnitude (z_i ≈ z_j), so δz_i causes a large shift in the relative exponentials and thus in p_i.
- **Core assumption:** Token probability variation is primarily driven by small, independent perturbations in the pre-softmax logits, and the softmax transformation dictates the sensitivity pattern.
- **Evidence:** The paper's analysis (Section 5.2) shows that logit variations are relatively flat across probability bins, while probability variations exhibit the U-shaped curve. This indicates the pattern emerges from the softmax nonlinearity.
- **Break condition:** If the U-shaped pattern disappears when analyzing logit values directly, the pattern is confirmed to be due to softmax.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The impact of nondeterminism on generated text can be estimated from a single inference run by analyzing the token probability distribution, avoiding 50 repeated runs.
- **Mechanism:** Because the magnitude of probability variation (σj, Rj) depends primarily on the base probability p of a token (as shown by the U-shaped curve), one can construct a mapping from p to expected variation from earlier experiments. By analyzing the histogram of p values from a single run, an aggregate estimate of potential output variability can be obtained, with tokens having p ∈ [0.1, 0.9] contributing most.
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between p and its susceptibility to variation is stable and generalizable across runs for a given model, hardware, and batch size, and the distribution of p values in a single-run generation is representative of the distribution across many runs.
- **Evidence:** The paper states this implication in the abstract and Section 6.
- **Break condition:** If the U-curve (variation vs. p) varies substantially across different prompts or contexts, or if the variation for a given p has high uncertainty across samples, a single-run estimate would be unreliable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Token Probability Distribution**  
  Why needed: The entire study hinges on analyzing the model’s output probability distribution over the vocabulary at each generation step, not just the sampled token. Understanding that p(v|context) is a vector of values summing to 1 is fundamental to the metrics (σj, Rj) which measure instability in these vectors.  
  Quick check: If the probability for token "apple" is 0.7 in one run and 0.65 in another, what does the 0.05 difference represent in terms of the model’s confidence and the softmax function’s sensitivity?

- **Softmax Function & Saturation**  
  Why needed: The central mechanistic finding—the U-shaped variation pattern—is explained by the mathematical properties of softmax. The saturation effect (outputs near 0/1) versus the linear region (outputs near 0.5) determines how logit noise propagates.  
  Quick check: Explain why a small random change in a logit value will cause a much larger change in the final probability if that token’s probability is 0.5 compared to if it is 0.99.

- **Batch Parallelism & Operation Ordering**  
  Why needed: Nondeterminism’s root cause is the variable order of arithmetic operations within parallel GPU kernels when processing a batch. This ordering depends on thread scheduling, which is non-deterministic. Batching is the primary experimental lever used to induce this effect.  
  Quick check: Why does increasing the batch size from 1 to 16 likely increase the measured σj for a mid-range token probability?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: LLM Inference Engine (e.g., Hugging Face Transformers) interfaces with GPU Backend (CUDA/CANN) to execute kernels; a Controlled Batch Construction script builds batches with B−1 distractor prompts; the Repeated Runner orchestrates N=50 runs per configuration; the Metrics Calculator aligns token positions using longest common prefix and computes σj and Rj; the Analysis Plotter aggregates by probability bins and produces U-curve plots.

Critical path: Token position alignment across runs is the most error-prone step. The analysis must compare probabilities for the same token index generated from the exact same context. The paper uses the longest common prefix method; misalignment would conflate nondeterminism with divergent generation paths.

Design tradeoffs:
- Full vocabulary vs. top-K: Capturing full vocab is ideal but memory-intensive; top-K (e.g., top-50) reduces memory but may miss low-probability tokens.
- Number of runs (N=50): Balances statistical precision (standard error ∝ 1/√N) with computational cost.
- Prompt diversity: Using MMLU provides broad coverage but may not probe edge cases like very short prompts, code, or non-English text.

Failure signatures:
1. Flat or no U-curve (all bins near zero): likely due to inadvertently enabled deterministic kernels or batch size 1.
2. Extreme, unbounded variation (σj > 0.5 for mid-range tokens): indicates a bug in probability extraction or alignment.
3. Inconsistent results across hardware (e.g., A100 vs. Ascend-910): could point to vendor-specific numerical formats or kernel implementations not captured by the softmax theory.
4. One model shows a fundamentally different curve: may indicate architectural differences affecting logit perturbation distributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How do nondeterministic variations propagate and accumulate across the layers of LLMs?**  
   The conclusion identifies a need for deeper theoretical understanding of the accumulation of probability perturbations across layers. The study measures variations at each token position but does not analyze how these variations compound through successive decoder layers. Without this, we cannot predict how local nondeterminism affects global output consistency. A layer-wise ablation study tracking probability variance propagation across multiple model architectures, combined with mathematical modeling of error accumulation under softmax nonlinearities, would resolve this.

2. **Do the reported U-shaped nondeterminism patterns hold for models beyond the evaluated 270M–12B parameter range, including state-of-the-art >100B parameter models, and for non-English languages or code generation?**  
   The limitations state that only 270M–12B models and English prompts were tested, leaving generalizability unverified. The paper claims the pattern is "inherent to the softmax function" and thus universal, but this assumes no architectural or data distribution effects. Replicating the token probability variance analysis on frontier models (>100B parameters), multilingual models, and code models across diverse hardware would verify this.

3. **Can a single inference run with token probability analysis reliably estimate the expected variance in generated text across multiple runs, especially for long outputs with many tokens?**  
   The abstract and conclusion propose that "we may be able to estimate the impact of nondeterminism by running a single inference," but this is not validated experimentally. The relationship between per-token probability variance and final text divergence (e.g., Levenshtein distance) is nonlinear and may accumulate over long sequences. A single-run estimate might not capture sequence-level dependencies. Correlating single-run probability variance metrics with actual text divergence metrics across thousands of prompts of varying lengths, using regression or ML models, would quantify predictive power.

4. **What are the practical efficacy and trade-offs of mitigation strategies targeting nondeterminism, such as precision switching based on token probability bins, and do they meaningfully improve reproducibility without degrading model quality?**  
   The conclusion suggests exploring mitigation strategies such as hybrid precision computation based on token probabilities. However, the paper does not implement or evaluate any mitigation. Hybrid precision might reduce nondeterminism for mid-range probabilities but could increase memory/computation costs or affect accuracy. Implementing a dynamic precision system (e.g., FP32 for tokens with p∈[0.1,0.9], BF16 otherwise) and measuring reduction in σj/Rj, throughput/peak memory, and accuracy on benchmarks compared to uniform BF16 would provide evidence.

## Limitations
- Unspecified experimental details (prompt template, stopping condition, concurrent prompt handling) may affect probability distributions and reproducibility.
- Evaluation is restricted to MMLU prompts and 8–12B parameter models, limiting generalizability to other tasks, languages, and extreme model scales.
- Token alignment relies on longest common prefix; hidden sampling nondeterminism could bias the measured probability variations.

## Confidence

- Softmax as root cause: Medium
- Model size insignificance: Medium
- Single-run impact estimation: Low
