# On the Loss Landscape Geometry of Regularized Deep Matrix Factorization: Uniqueness and Sharpness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27072
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27072
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: ℓ₂ regularization yields a unique end-to-end minimizer for almost all target matrices, and the Hessian spectrum in the scalar case is constant across all minimizers, with explicit eigenvalues.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies ℓ₂-regularized deep matrix factorization (deep linear networks) with squared-error loss. It proves that, for almost any target matrix (excluding a Lebesgue measure-zero set), the regularization forces the singular vectors of any minimizer to align with those of the target, and the per-singular-value subproblems become scalar ℓ₂-regularized factorizations with unique solutions. Consequently, all minimizers share identical layer Frobenius norms and produce the same end-to-end product. In the scalar case, the full Hessian spectrum is shown to be constant across all minimizers, with eigenvalues λ₁=…=λ_{L−1}=4λ/L and a depth-dependent λ_L. For general matrices, this yields a global lower bound on the Hessian trace. Moreover, a critical threshold on λ forces the unique minimizer to collapse to zero. Theoretical findings are supported by gradient descent experiments that illustrate the uniqueness and collapse phenomena.

## Method Summary
The theoretical analysis hinges on an equivalence between the ℓ₂-regularized deep factorization and a Schatten‑2/L regularized problem, which reduces the infinite non‑convex parameterization to a finite‑dimensional optimization over singular values. Using von Neumann’s trace inequality, the authors show that any minimizer must align singular vectors with the target, decoupling the problem into independent scalar subproblems. These scalar problems are solved analytically, revealing conditions for uniqueness and a balanced‑layer property (all layers have equal Frobenius norm). The Hessian is then examined directly in the scalar setting and, for matrices, a lower bound on its trace is derived from the constant layer norms. Empirically, the paper runs gradient descent on the original deep factorization for various depths and regularization strengths, confirming the predicted uniqueness, layer balance, and collapse threshold.

## Key Results
- The ℓ₂-regularized deep matrix factorization admits a unique end‑to‑end minimizer for almost all target matrices (excluding a measure‑zero set determined by depth and λ).
- In the scalar case, the Hessian spectrum at any minimizer is constant: λ₁=…=λ_{L−1}=4λ/L and λ_L = 2L w^{2L−2} + 4λ/L − 2λ, where w is the common layer magnitude.
- For matrices, all minimizers (when they exist uniquely) have constant Frobenius norms per layer, implying a global lower bound tr(∇²L(w*)) ≥ 2L ‖P*‖_F² / g*² + 2λ/L N. A sufficiently large λ forces the unique minimizer to collapse to zero.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** ℓ₂ regularization induces uniqueness of the end‑to‑end minimizer for almost all target matrices.
- **Mechanism:** Regularization forces all minimizers to share the same singular vectors as the target (via von Neumann’s trace inequality achieving equality). The per‑singular‑value subproblem becomes a scalar ℓ₂‑regularized factorization with a unique solution except for a measure‑zero set of target singular values. This extends to matrices by decoupling singular values and vectors.
- **Core assumption:** Target matrix avoids the Lebesgue measure-zero set defined by depth L and λ (Eq. 56). Balanced layers hold at all minimizers.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.2 (Theorem 10), and the scalar uniqueness result (Theorem 5).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** For regularized deep scalar factorization, the Hessian spectrum is constant across all minimizers.
- **Mechanism:** Layer balancing forces all |w_i| equal. The Hessian structure (Eq. 34) depends only on ρ*, w, λ, L. Since ρ* and w are constant across minimizers (by Theorem 5), the spectrum (λ₁,…,λ_L) is fixed (Eq. 29). The non‑trivial eigenvalue λ_L depends on w^{2L−2}.
- **Core assumption:** Target scalar m is not in the two‑point exceptional set (Theorem 5). Balanced layers and uniqueness of w magnitude hold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1.1 (Theorem 6), abstract.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Under ℓ₂ regularization, the Frobenius norm of each layer is constant across minimizers (almost always), yielding a global lower bound on Hessian trace.
- **Mechanism:** Uniqueness of end‑to‑end product P* (Theorem 10) plus balanced layers (Theorem 2) implies each ‖W_i‖_F is fixed. The Hessian trace expression (Eq. 91) includes terms ‖A_i‖_F² ‖B_i‖_F². Cauchy‑Schwarz with constant P* gives lower bound (Eq. 96).
- **Core assumption:** Target matrix avoids the measure‑zero set; balanced layers hold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 (Corollary 13), Section 3.4 (Theorem 15).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Target matrix M^♮ -> SVD (U_M, Σ_M, V_M) -> Solve scalar problem for each singular value (Eq. 65) -> Construct P* = U_M diag(x*) V_M^T -> Balanced layers: distribute singular values equally across L layers (g_i = (x*)^{1/L}) -> Full factorization W_i satisfying ΠW_i = P* and ‖W_i‖_F = g*.
- **Critical path:** The uniqueness proof (Theorem 10) relies on von Neumann’s inequality and the scalar uniqueness (Theorem 5). Once uniqueness and balancing are established, the Hessian analysis follows by direct computation in the scalar case (Theorem 6) and by Cauchy‑Schwarz in the matrix case (Theorem 15).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Depth L: Higher L increases non‑convexity but also enlarges the set of targets admitting unique minimizers? Actually the measure‑zero set size depends on L via the exponent q=2/L.  
  - Regularization λ: Controls the collapse threshold; too high forces P*=0 (Corollary 14). Must be below λ_τ = (‖σ(M^♮)‖_∞ / ((1−q/2)(1−q)^{(q−1)/(2−q)}))^{2−q} to avoid collapse.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Multiple distinct minimizers found with different end‑to‑end products: target likely in measure‑zero set or numerical error.  
  - Hessian eigenvalues not matching Eq. 29 in scalar case: either target at threshold or implementation error.  
  - Layer norms not constant across minimizers: target in exceptional set.
- **First experiments:**

  2. Verify Theorem 6: For random m not in exceptional set, solve scalar problem, compute Hessian eigenvalues from Eq. 29, check λ₁=4λ/L and λ_L matches formula.  
  3. Test matrix uniqueness: Random M^♮ (e.g., 25×30 Gaussian), run gradient descent from multiple initializations (T=5000 steps), check that all runs converge to same P* (relative error <1e‑5) and that ‖W_i‖_F are equal across layers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Is the lower bound on the Hessian trace achieved, and does it correspond to a flat minimum?** The paper provides a global lower bound (Theorem 15) but leaves open whether it is tight or whether the minimizer attaining it exhibits flatness (small eigenvalues).
- **Do the uniqueness and layer‑norm constancy generalize to deep nonlinear networks (e.g., with ReLU activations)?** The analysis is restricted to linear networks; extending to nonlinear settings is an open challenge.
- **What is the full Hessian eigenvalue spectrum for the matrix case?** The scalar case yields a constant spectrum; for matrices only a trace lower bound is known; the individual eigenvalues remain unknown.
- **What is the structure of the solution set when the target lies in the measure‑zero exceptional set?** The paper shows non‑uniqueness can occur but does not characterize the set of minimizers or their sharpness properties.

## Limitations
- The empirical results rely on unspecified gradient descent hyperparameters (step size, initialization scale, convergence tolerance), which may affect reproducibility.
- The measure‑zero exceptional set is defined analytically; in practice, target matrices with singular values arbitrarily close to the critical thresholds may cause numerical instability and apparent non‑uniqueness.
- The collapse threshold λ_τ appears in two slightly different forms (Corollary 14 vs. Figure 3 caption); without reconciliation, the exact transition point is ambiguous.

## Confidence
- **Uniqueness of end‑to‑end minimizer (Theorem 10):** High – proof via von Neumann’s inequality and scalar uniqueness is rigorous.
- **Scalar Hessian spectrum constancy (Theorem 6):** High – explicit eigenvalue derivation under non‑exceptional conditions.
- **Layer‑norm constancy & Hessian trace lower bound (Corollary 13, Theorem 15):** High – follows from balanced layers and global inequalities.
- **Collapse threshold λ_τ (Corollary 14):** Medium – proof is sound but empirical replication depends on precise formula; potential notation mismatch.
