# AmharicStoryQA: A Multicultural Story Question Answering Benchmark in Amharic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02774  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02774  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** Introduced AmharicStoryQA, revealing a severe Amharic narrative comprehension gap in LLMs (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B accuracy drops from 0.986 on English to 0.383 on Amharic MCQA) and showing supervised fine-tuning improves accuracy by up to 40% and reduces regional disparities.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the problem that LLM evaluations conflate language and culture, overlooking intra-language cultural variations. It introduces AmharicStoryQA, a benchmark of 571 training and 649 test QA pairs built from 244 culturally diverse Amharic stories across nine Ethiopian regions. Evaluation of seven LLMs reveals a severe narrative comprehension gap for Amharic; for instance, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct drops from near-perfect English MCQA accuracy (0.986) to 0.383 on Amharic. Performance also varies significantly by region, underscoring the impact of region-specific cultural content. Supervised fine-tuning on the dataset improves Amharic MCQA accuracy by up to 40 percentage points for Llama 3.1 8B and narrows regional gaps, demonstrating that culturally tailored data can significantly enhance model capabilities in low-resource settings.

## Method Summary
The AmharicStoryQA dataset was constructed from publicly available Ethiopian folktales. Stories were collected, and GPT-4.1 was used to generate multiple-choice questions, answers, and distractors. Three human annotators rated each item for correctness, linguistic quality, and comprehension depth, with inter-annotator agreement measured via Gwet’s AC1. Accepted items were translated into Amharic, and translation quality was assessed using SSA-COMET (threshold 0.65) supplemented by manual inspection. The final split respects regional diversity, yielding 571 training and 649 test examples. For evaluation, the lm-eval framework was employed: MCQA accuracy was computed via log-likelihood over answer choices, and open-ended generation was scored with chrF. Three prompt templates were tested in both Amharic and English. Supervised fine-tuning used LLaMA-Factory with LoRA (rank 8) on the Alpaca-formatted training data for 3 epochs, batch size 8, learning rate 1e-4, and cosine scheduling with warmup 0.1. Models were analyzed for regional performance differences and semantic density via PCA and KDE on multilingual MiniLM embeddings.

## Key Results
- LLMs exhibit a severe Amharic narrative comprehension gap: Llama 3.1 8B Instruct MCQA accuracy drops from 0.986 (English) to 0.383 (Amharic).  
- Regional performance varies significantly, with some Ethiopian regions proving much harder than others.  
- Supervised fine-tuning on AmharicStoryQA boosts Amharic MCQA accuracy by up to 40 percentage points (Llama 3.1 8B: 0.383 → 0.802) and reduces regional disparities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Intra-language cultural variation (regional narratives within Amharic) creates measurable semantic differences that affect evaluation. Region-specific content (e.g., Afar pastoralism vs. Amhara agriculture) produces distinct contexts that models must decode, exposing gaps from homogenized training. Evidence: abstract, Figure 2 KDE, gap in prior work. Break: diversified fine-tuning mitigates.

**Mechanism 2:** The Amharic narrative comprehension gap is severe and disproportionate to general multilingual ability. Pre-trained models lack exposure to Amharic narrative patterns and cultural schemas, causing a hard bottleneck where even large multilingual models perform near random. Evidence: dramatic accuracy drop (0.986→0.383), bimodal performance across models (Figure 6), minimal prompt language effect for low performers. Break: substantial Amharic narrative data during pre-training or fine-tuning.

**Mechanism 3:** Supervised fine-tuning on AmharicStoryQA improves comprehension and reduces regional disparities. The diverse QA pairs force the model to learn region-specific patterns, enriching its representations. Evidence: up to 40-point gain for Llama 3.1 8B (Table 1), more stable performance across regions (Figure 7). Break: fine-tuning dataset must be sufficiently diverse and large.

## Foundational Learning
### Low-resource language evaluation pitfalls
- **Why needed:** Recognize that "low-resource" encompasses not only data scarcity but also lack of cultural representation depth.
- **Quick check:** Why might a model score highly on a generic Amharic QA benchmark but fail on stories from the Afar region?

### Cultural semantics in narrative texts
- **Why needed:** Stories embed culture-specific knowledge (e.g., pastoral practices, social norms) that are not directly translatable; comprehension requires decoding these embedded cultural schemas.
- **Quick check:** How might a story about "coffee ceremony" from Sidama region differ in cultural presuppositions from a similar story in Amhara region?

### Long-context QA as a cultural probe
- **Why needed:** AmharicStoryQA uses long stories (up to 6k tokens) to test sustained narrative understanding, which is more culturally immersive than short passages.
- **Quick check:** Why is evaluating comprehension of a 5000-token folktale potentially more revealing of cultural understanding than a 200-token news excerpt?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Pipeline: Ethiopian folktales collection → GPT-4.1 question/answer/distractor generation → Human correctness/linguistic/comprehension rating (3 annotators, Gwet’s AC1) → Human translation to Amharic → SSA-COMET scoring + manual inspection → Region-aware train-test split.  
Evaluation Harness: lm-eval framework with log-likelihood (MCQA) and generation (chrF) modes; three prompt templates in Amharic/English.  
Fine-tuning: LLaMA-Factory with LoRA (rank=8), Alpaca format, 3 epochs, batch size 8.  
Analysis: Regional performance breakdown, semantic density visualization (PCA + KDE on multilingual MiniLM embeddings).

**Critical path:**  
Story sourcing → Question generation → Human validation → Translation quality control → Train-test split → Zero-shot benchmarking → Fine-tuning → Regional analysis.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MCQA vs. Generation: MCQA is reproducible but vulnerable to option-order bias; generation evaluates fluency but not necessarily comprehension (chrF improvements may not reflect true understanding).  
- Prompt language: Amharic prompt for Amharic task is theoretically ideal but often yields lower scores, suggesting models are more fluent in English narratives even when content is Amharic.  
- Split strategy: Sorting by SSA-COMET score prevents translation-quality imbalance but may introduce subtle corpus biases; randomizing answer choices reduces structural bias.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Baseline: >0.98 accuracy on English MCQA but <0.40 on Amharic MCQA indicates narrative comprehension gap, not just language gap.  
- Regional: High variance in chrF across regions for English narratives suggests some regions' stories are intrinsically harder or more culturally specific.  
- Translation: SSA-COMET scores <0.65 flag potential quality issues; human review needed because metric may produce false negatives in low-resource contexts.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate zero-shot evaluation: Run baseline models (Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Gemma-3-27b-it) on AmharicStoryQA test set with all three prompt templates; compute MCQA accuracy and chrF; verify the performance collapse in Amharic.  
2. Analyze regional variation: For a low-performing model (e.g., Llama 3.2 1B Instruct), plot MCQA and chrF per region; correlate with story length (Figure 3) and semantic density (Figure 2) to identify which regional characteristics correlate with difficulty.  
3. Conduct SFT ablation: Fine-tune Llama 3.1 8B on (a) English-only, (b) Amharic-only, (c) combined splits; evaluate on Amharic MCQA and generation; measure change in regional variance (Figure 7) to confirm disparity reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does supervised fine-tuning on AmharicStoryQA yield uneven improvements across Ethiopian regions and between Amharic and English tasks?
- **Basis:** Abstract notes uneven improvements; Figure 7 shows stabilization for Amharic tasks but not English.
- **Unresolved:** The paper documents the pattern but does not investigate underlying causes (e.g., region-specific feature difficulty, pretraining biases).
- **Resolution evidence needed:** Ablation isolating region-specific training subsets; model representation analysis pre/post fine-tuning; error breakdowns by question type and story attributes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do the effects of intra-language cultural variation on LLM performance observed in Amharic generalize to other low-resource languages with significant regional diversity?
- **Basis:** Limitations state inability to determine generalization; study confined to Amharic in Ethiopia.
- **Unresolved:** Other languages may have different dialectal variation, cultural homogeneity, or resource constraints that could modulate findings.
- **Resolution evidence needed:** Replication studies with other regionally diverse low-resource languages (e.g., Hausa in Nigeria, Tamil in India) using similar benchmarks; cross-linguistic meta-analysis of regional gaps versus linguistic/cultural distance metrics.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the AmharicStoryQA data collection and question generation pipeline be scaled to create culturally diverse benchmarks for multiple languages, given the manual effort and resource constraints?
- **Basis:** Future work aims to extend to a broader multilingual/multicultural setting; limitations cite scarcity of volunteers and high manual effort.
- **Unresolved:** Current reliance on manual story sourcing, translation, and validation is infeasible for scaling without compromising authenticity or quality.
- **Resolution evidence needed:** Development of semi-automated frameworks using local digital archives or community-sourcing with quality control; evaluation of multilingual LLMs for automated question generation against human-generated questions on cultural fidelity; cost-benefit analysis of scaling strategies.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What specific cultural and linguistic features within Amharic narratives (e.g., region-specific vocabulary, folktale structures, cultural references) are most predictive of LLM performance drops, and how do these vary across Ethiopian regions?
- **Basis:** Inferred; paper analyzes regional semantic density and notes region-specific topics but provides no fine-grained mapping of features to errors.
- **Unresolved:** Without identifying precise features, it is unclear whether performance issues stem from lexical gaps, narrative style differences, or unfamiliar cultural concepts.
- **Resolution evidence needed:** Feature extraction from stories (e.g., named entities, culturally specific terms, narrative complexity metrics) correlated with per-region accuracy; qualitative error analysis categorizing failures by feature type; expert annotation of cultural markers to model performance impact.

## Limitations
- Unspecified details (generation prompt template, truncation strategy for long stories, MCQA prompt variant, exact model identifiers, region metadata) hinder faithful reproduction.
- The claim of novel intra-language variation may be overstated, as related multicultural VLM benchmarks (e.g., VULCA-Bench) already address multicultural evaluation, though not for textual narrative QA in a single language.

## Confidence
- Intra-language variation impacts model evaluation: **Medium**  
- Narrative comprehension gap for Amharic is severe: **High**  
- Supervised fine-tuning improves comprehension and reduces disparities: **High**
