# Axis Decomposition for ODRL: Resolving Dimensional Ambiguity in Policy Constraints through Interval Semantics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19878
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19878
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: Resolved intrinsic ambiguity in ODRL 2.2 constraints for multi-dimensional operands via axis decomposition, enabling deterministic policy evaluation and sound conflict detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental ambiguity in ODRL (Open Digital Rights Language) where multi-dimensional operands (e.g., spatial dimensions) lead to multiple interpretations of constraints. The proposed axis decomposition refines five base operands into fifteen axis-specific ones, mapping constraints to intervals. This yields decidable per-axis conflict detection and sound box-level verdicts via Strong Kleene logic. Implemented as the ODRL Spatial Axis Profile and mechanically verified, the approach achieves 100% concordance on benchmarks, facilitating reliable policy enforcement in cultural heritage dataspaces like Datenraum Kultur.

## Method Summary
The method transforms ambiguous multi-dimensional operands into axis-specific scalar operands through projection. Each constraint is interpreted as an interval on its axis. Conflict detection reduces to interval intersection per axis, ensuring decidability. For multi-axis constraints, per-axis results are composed using Strong Kleene three-valued logic, producing Conflict, Compatible, or Unknown to soundly represent incomplete information. This conservative extension adds new operand IRIs without altering existing ODRL semantics, verified via Isabelle/HOL proofs.

## Key Results
- Eliminated axis-selection ambiguity by decomposing five base ODRL operands into fifteen axis-specific operands.
- Per-axis conflict detection is always decidable; box-level composition uses Strong Kleene conjunction for soundness under incomplete info.
- Evaluated 117 benchmark problems (TPTP FOF + SMT-LIB) with Vampire/Z3, achieving 100% concordance.
- Four key properties (deterministic interpretation, AABB completeness, projection soundness, conservative extension) mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework resolves ambiguity through three mechanisms:

1. **Axis Disambiguation via Operand Refinement**: Multi-dimensional operands with value space D₁ × ... × Dₙ (n≥2) are replaced by axis-specific operands ℓₐ₁, ..., ℓₐₙ via projection πₖ, reducing n+2 interpretations to one. Assumes independent axes and totally ordered, non-wrapping domains.

2. **Interval-Based Conflict Detection with Three-Valued Composition**: Constraints denote intervals in Dₖ; per-axis conflict is decidable via intersection (∅ → Conflict). Box-level verdicts compose per-axis results using Strong Kleene logic, yielding three-valued outcomes (Conflict, Compatible, Unknown) to soundly handle unconstrained axes.

3. **Conservative Extension and Mechanical Verification**: New operands are fresh IRIs with `skos:broader` links; profile-unaware engines ignore them safely. Profile-aware engines gain precision without altering original operand verdicts. Key properties proved in Isabelle/HOL guarantee correctness and backward compatibility.

## Foundational Learning
- **Interval Arithmetic**: Core denotation for dimensional constraints; conflict detection reduces to interval intersection. Quick check: For `absoluteSizeWidth lteq 600` and `absoluteSizeWidth gteq 800`, intervals are (-∞,600] and [800,∞); intersection empty → Conflict.
- **Three-Valued Logic (Strong Kleene)**: Composes per-axis verdicts while respecting incomplete information; prevents false exclusivity in disjunctive constraints like `odrl:xone`. Quick check: Why is `Conflict ∨ Unknown = Conflict` unsound? Because Unknown should not force Conflict when other axes might be Compatible; Strong Kleene preserves soundness via truth tables.
- **Conservative Extension**: Ensures new operands don’t alter existing ODRL policy meanings; backward compatibility is mandatory for adoption. Quick check: If `absoluteSize` remains in a policy, does the profile change its interpretation? No, axis-specific operands are distinct IRIs; the original ambiguous operand is safely ignored by unaware engines.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: ODRL Spatial Axis Profile comprises OWL ontology (15 operand IRIs + SKOS links) → SHACL shapes (validation) → TPTP axiom files (interval predicates) → Isabelle/HOL theories (verified meta-theorems) → Benchmark suite (117 TPTP FOF + SMT-LIB problems).
- **Critical Path**: Classify operand dimensionality → Decompose to axis-specific operands → Encode constraints as intervals → Compute per-axis verdicts via intersection → Compose box-level verdict with Strong Kleene → Cross-domain composition.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Strong Kleene chosen over Heyting/Bochvar to balance soundness in `or`/`xone`; interval representation limits to axis-aligned regions (AABB); profile adds 15 operands without syntax changes, preserving ODRL’s `(leftOperand, operator, rightOperand)` pattern.
- **Failure Signatures**: (1) Circular domains (e.g., longitude crossing ±180°) produce wrap-around intervals not handled; (2) Coupled axes (e.g., `totalPixels = width * height`) require cross-axis constraints outside scalar pattern; (3) Non-numeric domains (e.g., colour gamuts) break interval assumption.
- **First 3 Experiments**: 1. Single-axis conflict: Encode `absoluteSizeWidth lteq 600` vs `absoluteSizeWidth gteq 800`; expect unsat from Vampire/Z3. 2. Box composition: Combine width/height constraints forming an AABB; verify subsumption with tighter AABB yields `Confirmed`. 3. Cross-domain: Mix spatial (width), concept (region), scalar (percentage) operands; ensure `Conflict` propagates from any single operand.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Circular Domains**: How to extend the interval algebra to handle circular domains (e.g., longitude at the antimeridian) while preserving deterministic interpretation and conflict detection? Current framework assumes non-wrapping axes; future work needed.
2. **Cross-Axis Constraints**: How can cross-axis constraints (e.g., total pixel area, aspect ratio) be expressed and reasoned about within ODRL’s scalar constraint pattern? Derived operands (e.g., `oax:totalPixels`) are proposed but not formalized.
3. **Integration with ODRL-SA**: How can the ODRL Spatial Axis Profile be integrated with ODRL-SA to provide design-time conflict detection for policy authors? This would enable immediate feedback during composition.
4. **Production-Scale Validation**: Does the axis-decomposition approach scale and remain effective in real-world, production-scale dataspace deployments beyond synthetic benchmarks? Evaluation used 117 curated problems; validation on live deployments is future work.

## Limitations
- **Domain Coverage**: Assumes independent axes, total ordering, and non-wrapping domains; explicitly fails for coupled axes, circular domains, cross-axis constraints, and non-numeric domains.
- **Benchmark Scope**: Evaluated on 117 synthetic TPTP/SMT-LIB problems inspired by cultural heritage constraints; lacks real-world ODRL policy corpora from diverse domains.
- **Adoption Risk**: Conservative extension relies on ODRL profile mechanism; unknown if profile-unaware engines handle axis-specific operands correctly in practice.

## Confidence
- **High**: Formal properties (determinism, projection soundness, conservative extension) mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL; per-axis interval conflict detection mathematically decidable.
- **Medium**: 100% concordance on curated benchmarks; Strong Kleene composition sound for incomplete info, but real-world policy complexity may expose edge cases.
- **Low**: Expressiveness limited to axis-aligned regions (AABB over-approximation); applicability beyond spatial numerics domains remains speculative.
