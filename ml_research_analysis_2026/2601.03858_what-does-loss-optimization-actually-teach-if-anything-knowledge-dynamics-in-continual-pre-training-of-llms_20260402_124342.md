# What Does Loss Optimization Actually Teach, If Anything? Knowledge Dynamics in Continual Pre-training of LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03858
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03858
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Loss decreases monotonically during continual pre-training, while factual recall is unstable and non-monotonic; out-of-domain task accuracy degrades from the first epoch under most strategies, revealing a systematic misalignment between optimization and knowledge acquisition.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates a fundamental misalignment in continual pre-training (CPT): while loss decreases consistently, the acquisition of factual knowledge is unstable, non-monotonic, and heavily conditioned on entity frequency. Through controlled experiments with distribution-matched Wikipedia revisions and epoch-level probing across multiple instruction-tuned models, the authors show that loss is a poor proxy for learning. High-frequency entities exhibit transient acquisition-forgetting cycles, low-frequency entities show minimal durable learning, and out-of-domain capabilities (e.g., MMLU) degrade early. Mechanistic analysis reveals rapid reconfiguration of internal knowledge circuits, suggesting that gradient updates overwrite rather than consolidate factual pathways. A retrieval-augmented generation upper bound confirms that updated information is accessible but not reliably internalized. These findings necessitate task-based stopping criteria for CPT.

## Method Summary
The study uses a custom corpus of ~10k revised Wikipedia documents (100 controlled entities) to train three instruction-tuned models (OLMo 7B, LLaMA 3.1 8B, LLaMA 3.2 1B) via LoRA for up to 10 epochs. After each epoch, models are probed on factual recall (10 questions per entity, scored by ROUGE-1) and out-of-domain benchmarks (MMLU, SocialIQA). Loss and recall trajectories are compared to identify divergence. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) upper bound uses perfect retrieval from the training corpus. For the smallest model, knowledge circuits are extracted via EAP-IG and compared across epochs using Jaccard similarity to analyze structural reconfiguration. The design isolates entity frequency as a key variable and directly measures knowledge dynamics rather than inferring them from loss.

## Key Results
- Loss decreases monotonically throughout CPT, but factual recall peaks early (epochs 3–5) then collapses, showing systematic divergence.
- Entity frequency is the strongest predictor of acquisition: high-frequency entities show transient cycles, low-frequency entities rarely consolidate.
- OOD accuracy (MMLU, SocialIQA) degrades from epoch 1 under non-regularized CPT; regularization that preserves OOD suppresses factual learning entirely.
- Circuit analysis reveals rapid reconfiguration: Jaccard similarity between knowledge circuits drops sharply after epoch 1 and does not recover, indicating pathway replacement rather than refinement.
- RAG with perfect retrieval consistently outperforms the best CPT checkpoint by >10%, proving updated facts are accessible but not internalized.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Loss minimization during CPT does not reliably correlate with stable factual knowledge acquisition; optimization and learning systematically diverge.
- **Mechanism:** The model's gradient-driven parameter updates optimize for next-token prediction on the revised corpus, which can refine pattern-matching heuristics without consolidating specific factual updates. This leads to transient improvements in recall that are overwritten as optimization continues.
- **Core assumption:** Perplexity reduction reflects general distributional fit, not the durable integration of specific factual updates.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "loss decreases monotonically, while factual recall is unstable and non-monotonic." Section 3: "loss provides no indication of when learning succeeds or fails. Peaks in factual accuracy do not align with local minima in perplexity."
- **Break condition:** When factual recall peaks and then declines while loss continues to decrease, indicating the optimization trajectory has passed a consolidation window.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Factual acquisition during CPT is strongly conditioned on entity frequency in the pre-training corpus, leading to asymmetric learning outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** High-frequency entities receive sufficient contextual signal during updates to drive transient parameter shifts, while low-frequency entities lack representational support, resulting in minimal or no durable changes. This creates acquisition-forgetting cycles for common facts and near-zeros for rare ones.
- **Core assumption:** Pre-training frequency determines the model's prior sensitivity to an entity, which gates the effectiveness of subsequent updates.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "High-frequency entities exhibit transient acquisition-forgetting cycles, while low-frequency entities show minimal, short-lived, or no acquisition." Section 3: "Entity frequency is the strongest predictor of whether updated knowledge is ever acquired or retained." Section 2.1: Corpus construction explicitly separates high/low frequency entities.
- **Break condition:** When an entity's frequency is below a threshold that provides enough pre-activated pathways for gradient updates to meaningfully alter representations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CPT induces rapid reconfiguration of internal knowledge circuits, which explains transient acquisition and systematic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** Parameter updates repeatedly redirect attribution mass toward different subgraphs responsible for factual recall, rather than strengthening a stable set of edges. This continuous circuit turnover overwrites previously learned pathways.
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge is stored in sparse subgraphs; altering these subgraphs' structure changes functional recall even if behavioral metrics are stable temporarily.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3: "Circuit similarity drops sharply as soon as training begins... CPT does not selectively refine existing factual pathways. Instead, attribution mass is repeatedly redirected." Section 4.3: "Circuit-level performance oscillates across epochs, closely mirroring the non-monotonic behavioral trajectories."
- **Break condition:** When circuit overlap between checkpoints stabilizes, but behavioral recall does not—indicating structural changes have ceased but functional consolidation failed.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Controlled Corpus (revised Wikipedia) -> CPT Loop (LoRA/curriculum/etc.) -> Epoch Checkpoints -> Dual Probe System (Knowledge + OOD) -> Trajectory Analysis -> Circuit Extraction (EAP-IG) -> Structural Comparison.

Critical path: 1) Build distribution-matched factual corpus (revised Wikipedia). 2) Interleave fixed probes into CPT loop at each epoch. 3) Track entity-level acquisition/retention/forgetting and OOD distortion. 4) Compare probe trajectories to loss curves to detect divergence. 5) Run RAG upper bound and extended CPT as baselines. 6) Extract and compare knowledge circuits across epochs.

Design tradeoffs: Probe frequency vs. training overhead (epoch-level probes add evaluation cost but reveal trajectories). RAG as upper bound shows attainable performance but doesn't explain internalization failures. Circuit analysis (EAP-IG) is computationally heavy and limited to smaller models (LLaMA 1B), sacrificing scale for mechanistic insight.

Failure signatures: Non-monotonic factual recall despite monotonically decreasing loss. Immediate OOD degradation in first epoch for non-regularized strategies. Low-frequency entities never exceeding ~30% recall. Circuit Jaccard similarity dropping below 0.5 after epoch 1.

First 3 experiments: 1) **Baseline divergence check:** Run CPT for 10 epochs with standard LoRA; at each epoch compute perplexity and factual recall. Expect to see recall peak early (epoch 2~5) then drop while loss continues falling. 2) **RAG upper bound comparison:** For the best-performing CPT checkpoint (by recall), run RAG with retrieval from the full document pool. Expect RAG to exceed CPT recall by >10%, proving information is accessible but not internalized. 3) **Circuit reconfiguration validation:** For LLaMA 1B, extract knowledge circuits (via EAP-IG) at epochs 0, peak-recall epoch, and final epoch. Compute Jaccard similarity between C0 and Ct; expect sharp drop from epoch 0 to 1, with no recovery.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we build a robust framework to distinguish transient optimization gains from stable factual knowledge acquisition during CPT?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The introduction states there is "a lack of a framework for distinguishing learning from transient optimization, and measuring how knowledge is integrated during training."
- **Why unresolved:** Current practice relies solely on loss reduction, which systematically diverges from factual recall trajectories and fails to signal when knowledge is acquired, retained, or forgotten.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A suite of probe-based metrics (beyond factual recall) that correlate with long-term retention across diverse models and domains, validated against retrieval-based upper bounds and downstream task stability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What signals or criteria can reliably indicate when factual acquisition has stabilized or when continued CPT will cause degradation, enabling effective stopping?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The introduction notes CPT offers "no mechanism for identifying when factual acquisition stabilizes or when continued optimization induces degradation."
- **Why unresolved:** Perplexity curves improve monotonically even as factual recall peaks and collapses, so loss provides no early warning of impending forgetting or OOD harm.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical identification of task-level performance thresholds or circuit-level stability markers that consistently precede degradation, demonstrated across multiple CPT strategies and model scales.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does extended CPT (e.g., 100 epochs) amplify forgetting of newly introduced facts rather than promoting consolidation, even under noise-free, high-exposure conditions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 4.2 shows factual recall peaks early (epochs 3–5) then collapses to a low, oscillatory state despite monotonically decreasing loss and perfect data distribution.
- **Why unresolved:** This contradicts the expectation that repeated exposure should strengthen representations; instead, optimization actively overwrites previously acquired knowledge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained analysis of gradient magnitude/direction, weight update norms, and circuit reconfiguration dynamics across very long training runs to identify when and why updates become destabilizing.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What modifications to CPT objectives, regularization, or data presentation could align loss minimization with stable knowledge consolidation without catastrophically degrading OOD abilities?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] All evaluated strategies show a trade-off: methods that permit factual updates (e.g., LoRA) cause OOD degradation, while those that preserve OOD (pre-training regularization) suppress learning (Section 3, RQ4).
- **Why unresolved:** The fundamental misalignment persists even with distribution-matched data and varied techniques, suggesting a deeper incompatibility between next-token prediction and durable knowledge integration.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments with novel objectives (e.g., contrastive, modular, or retrieval-grounded losses) that explicitly reward retention of new facts while preserving pre-existing capabilities, evaluated via the paper’s probing framework.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Why is factual acquisition during CPT so strongly conditioned on entity pre-training frequency, and can low-frequency entities ever be reliably updated?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] RQ3 and Figures 3, 11–15 show high-frequency entities exhibit transient acquisition-forgetting cycles, while low-frequency entities show minimal, short-lived, or no learning across all models and strategies.
- **Why unresolved:** This frequency dependence persists even with distribution-matched corpora (e.g., OLMo), ruling out domain mismatch but leaving the representational mechanism unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies controlling for contextual diversity, entity embedding quality, and training data ordering to isolate whether frequency effects arise from pre-trained representation strength, data co-occurrence patterns, or optimization bias toward familiar patterns.

## Limitations
- The exact revised Wikipedia corpus and final probe question set are not released, hampering direct replication and entity-level validation.
- Circuit analysis was limited to LLaMA 1B due to computational constraints; findings may not generalize to larger models.
- CPT strategies (e.g., LoRA rank, learning rate) are specific; divergence and forgetting under full fine-tuning or other regularizers remain untested.

## Confidence
- Loss–recall divergence: **High** (consistent across three model families and multiple CPT variants).
- Frequency-driven asymmetric learning: **High** (controlled corpus experiment isolates frequency as primary predictor).
- Circuit reconfiguration as mechanism for forgetting: **Medium** (evidence from a small model; needs scaling confirmation and alternative attribution methods).
- OOD degradation from first epoch: **High** (observed across models and strategies).
- RAG upper bound superiority: **High** (consistent and substantial gap across checkpoints).

## Next Checks
