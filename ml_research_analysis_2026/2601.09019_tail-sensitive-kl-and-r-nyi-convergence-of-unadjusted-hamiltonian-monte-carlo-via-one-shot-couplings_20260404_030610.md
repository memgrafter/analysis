# Tail-Sensitive KL and Rényi Convergence of Unadjusted Hamiltonian Monte Carlo via One-Shot Couplings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.09019
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09019
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Exponential convergence in KL and Rényi divergences for unadjusted HMC (velocity Verlet) under smoothness/stability; gradient complexity O(d^{3/4} ε^{-1/4} log(d/ε)) for KL and O(d^{3/2} ε^{-1} log(d/ε)) for Rényi (strongly log-concave targets).

## Executive Summary
The paper studies convergence of unadjusted Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (uHMC) in tail‑sensitive divergences – Kullback–Leibler and Rényi – which are crucial for Bayesian inference and differential privacy but notoriously difficult to bound. Using one‑shot couplings, the authors prove a *regularization property* of the uHMC transition kernel: small Wasserstein distance between initial measures implies small KL/Rényi distance after one step. This lifts known Wasserstein‑2 contraction results to exponential decay in KL and Rényi divergences. They further control the *asymptotic bias* – the gap between the stationary distribution of uHMC and the target – by cross‑regularizing uHMC with exact Hamiltonian dynamics. For strongly log‑concave targets and the velocity Verlet integrator, explicit gradient complexity bounds are derived: O(d^{3/4} ε^{-1/4} log(d/ε)) for KL and O(d^{3/2} ε^{-1} log(d/ε)) for Rényi. The framework is modular: any base kernel with suitable Wasserstein contraction can be combined with a final uHMC step, offering flexibility (e.g., stratified uHMC for improved dimension dependence).

## Method Summary
The core technique is the construction of two deterministic coupling maps. For any positions *x*, *y*, the *regularization map* φ_{x,y} aligns initial velocities so that the uHMC trajectories starting from (*x*, v) and (*y*, φ_{x,y}(v)) meet exactly after integration time *T*. Under smoothness of the potential (Lipschitz Hessian and third derivative) and step‑size constraints L(T²+Th) ≤ 1/12 and LT² ≤ 2π²/5, φ_{x,y} satisfies ‖φ_{x,y}(v)−v‖ ≤ (3/2)T‖x−y‖ and ‖∇φ_{x,y}−I‖_op ≤ min{2/9, (11/2)M T²‖x−y‖}. This yields a local Lipschitz bound: KL(δ_x ˜P_{T,h} ∥ δ_y ˜P_{T,h}) ≤ C‖x−y‖² (and similarly for Rényi). The *cross‑regularization map* Φ_{x,y} aligns a uHMC step from *x* with an exact HMC step from *y*: ˜q_{T,h}(x,v)=q_T(y,Φ_{x,y}(v)). Its error is O(h²), giving KL(δ_x ˜P_{T,h} ∥ δ_y P_T) ≤ C‖x−y‖² + Δ_h with Δ_h→0. These local inequalities extend to arbitrary initial measures via convexity of the divergences. The overall analysis combines: (i) exponential Wasserstein‑2 (or Orlicz‑Wasserstein) contraction of a base kernel Q̃; (ii) the regularization property of the final uHMC‑v step to convert Wasserstein decay into KL/Rényi decay; (iii) the cross‑regularization plus a bias assumption on Q̃’s invariant distribution to bound the asymptotic bias. The modularity allows plugging in alternative base kernels (e.g., uHMC‑s) while the final uHMC‑v step universally upgrades the divergence. Gradient complexity is obtained by optimizing *h* and *T* under the stability constraints and balancing mixing speed against discretization error.

## Key Results
- **Exponential KL convergence:** Under strong log‑concavity and smoothness, KL divergence between the distribution of uHMC‑v after *k* steps and its stationary distribution decays exponentially (Theorem 1). The asymptotic bias (KL between the uHMC‑v stationary distribution and the target) is O(h⁴) (Theorem 2).
- **Exponential Rényi convergence:** Similar exponential decay holds for Rényi divergence of order *q*>1 (Theorem 3) with an asymptotic bias bound (Theorem 4), though the analysis is coarser, leading to worse dimension dependence.
- **Gradient complexity:** To achieve error ε in KL, O(d^{3/4} ε^{-1/4} log(d/ε)) gradient evaluations suffice; for Rényi, O(d^{3/2} ε^{-1} log(d/ε)) (Corollaries 2, 4, 5). Using composite kernels (e.g., uHMC‑s as base) can improve the dimension dependence in the bias term.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The analysis hinges on three interconnected mechanisms.

1. **Regularization of uHMC‑v**  
   For any *x*,*y* ∈ ℝ^d, there exists a deterministic map φ_{x,y} on velocities such that the uHMC‑v trajectories from (*x*,v) and (*y*,φ_{x,y}(v)) coalesce at time *T*. Under the smoothness assumptions (Lipschitz Hessian, bounded third derivative) and step‑size constraints L(T²+Th) ≤ 1/12, LT² ≤ 2π²/5, φ_{x,y} is unique and satisfies the pointwise bound ‖φ_{x,y}(v)−v‖ ≤ (3/2)T‖x−y‖ and Jacobian bound ‖∇φ_{x,y}−I‖_op ≤ min{2/9, (11/2)M T²‖x−y‖} (Lemmas 4, 5). Consequently, the KL (or Rényi) divergence between one‑step Dirac measures is bounded by the divergence between a standard Gaussian and a Gaussian whose mean/covariance perturbations scale with ‖x−y‖. This yields local Lipschitz continuity:  
   KL(δ_x ˜P_{T,h} ∥ δ_y ˜P_{T,h}) ≤ C‖x−y‖².  
   If the step‑size conditions are violated, φ_{x,y} may be ill‑behaved and the bound becomes vacuous.

2. **Cross‑regularization for bias control**  
   To compare uHMC with exact Hamiltonian dynamics, define Φ_{x,y} via ˜q_{T,h}(x,v)=q_T(y,Φ_{x,y}(v)). Under the same smoothness and step‑size assumptions, the pointwise and Jacobian errors of Φ_{x,y} are O(h²) (Lemmas 6–9). Hence  
   KL(δ_x ˜P_{T,h} ∥ δ_y P_T) ≤ C‖x−y‖² + Δ_h,  
   with Δ_h → 0 as h→0. For Rényi, similar bounds hold but require additional moment boundedness (Assumption 5) and a tighter step‑size condition ((q−1)c² ≤ 1/8).

3. **Modular combination**  
   The convergence analysis separates two concerns:  
   (i) The base Markov kernel Q̃ must contract exponentially in Wasserstein‑2 (for KL) or Orlicz‑Wasserstein (for Rényi).  
   (ii) The invariant distribution of Q̃ must be close to the target in the same Wasserstein metric.  
   The final step of the chain is a uHMC‑v transition. Its *regularization* property (point 1) upgrades the Wasserstein mixing of Q̃ into exponential KL/Rényi decay of the composite chain. The *cross‑regularization* property (point 2) together with the bias assumption on Q̃ controls the asymptotic bias of the whole chain. This modular design permits plugging in alternative base kernels (e.g., uHMC‑s) while retaining the universal regularization of the last uHMC‑v step. Breakdown occurs if Q̃ fails to contract exponentially or its invariant measure is far from ν; the final bound then degrades.

## Foundational Learning
- **Wasserstein distances and optimal transport.**  
  *Why needed:* The main results upgrade convergence in Wasserstein‑2 (or Orlicz‑Wasserstein) into KL/Rényi. The dual formulation shows that small W₂ implies small KL when the transport map is close to the identity – exactly what the one‑shot couplings provide.  
  *Quick check:* What is the Kantorovich‑Rubinstein dual of W₂ and why does a near‑identity coupling yield a KL bound proportional to ‖x−y‖²?

- **Mixing time vs. asymptotic bias for MCMC.**  
  *Why needed:* For unadjusted MCMC, two error sources exist: mixing to the chain’s stationary distribution (mixing time) and the gap between that stationary distribution and the true target (asymptotic bias). The paper controls both separately and combines them.  
  *Quick check:* For uHMC, the stationary distribution is biased. How do the mixing time to this biased distribution and the bias (e.g., W₂(˜ν_h,ν)) combine to bound the total error to ν?

- **One‑shot couplings for Markov kernels.**  
  *Why needed:* One‑shot couplings are the technical engine: they construct a coupling that often coalesces after one step, enabling conversion between different divergence measures.  
  *Quick check:* Given a kernel *P*, what is a one‑shot coupling and how can it be used to prove KL(μP ∥ νP) ≤ L·W₂(μ,ν)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Potential *f* (smooth) → Hamiltonian dynamics (exact flow HF_T, velocity Verlet discretization) → Transition kernels (eHMC *P_T*, uHMC‑v ˜P_{T,h}, base kernel Q̃) → One‑shot coupling maps (φ_{x,y} for regularization, Φ_{x,y} for cross‑regularization) → Divergence bounds (KL, Rényi, W₂, W_ψ) → Main theorems (exponential mixing, bias, complexity). Assumptions (smoothness, Wasserstein contraction, bias, step‑size stability) govern each layer.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Verify step‑size constraints: L(T²+Th) ≤ 1/12 and LT² ≤ 2π²/5 (ensures existence and control of φ, Φ).  
  2. For KL mixing: confirm Q̃ satisfies Assumption 2 (exponential W₂ contraction); use known results (e.g., Prop 1 for strongly log‑concave ν).  
  3. For asymptotic bias: verify Assumption 3 (W₂(˜ν_h,ν) ≤ Δ_h) for Q̃ (Prop 2).  
  4. For Rényi: check Assumptions 4 (W_ψ contraction) and 5 (ψ‑Wasserstein bias) plus moment condition ‖X‖_ψ ≤ K_ν; use first‑order bounds (Lemmas 8, 9).  
  5. Apply Theorems 1–4 to obtain exponential decay rates and bias bounds.  
  6. Optimize *h* and *T* to minimise total gradient complexity (≈ (T/h)·k).  
  7. For composite kernels, choose Q̃ ≠ uHMC‑v but ensure final step is uHMC‑v and that Q̃ satisfies the required assumptions.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Integrator: Velocity Verlet is second‑order; KL analysis exploits full second‑order accuracy (h⁴ bias) while Rényi uses only first‑order (h² bias) → worse dimension dependence O(d^{3/2} ε⁻¹) vs O(d^{3/4} ε^{-1/4}).  
  - Base kernel: Using uHMC‑s as Q̃ reduces bias dimension dependence from d³ to d^{3/2} (Corollary 5) but requires ψ‑Wasserstein bias bound (still open).  
  - Divergence choice: KL bounds are tighter; Rényi is cruder but composes better for privacy applications.  
  - Step‑size vs. stability: Smaller *h* improves accuracy but increases per‑step gradient cost; larger *T* accelerates mixing but may violate stability; optimization required.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Smoothness violation: If *f* is not C³ (needed for φ bounds) or not C⁴ (some Rényi bounds), the regularity lemmas fail.  
  - Step‑size too large: L(T²+Th) > 1/12 destroys control of φ, Φ → bounds become vacuous.  
  - Base kernel non‑contracting: If Q̃ does not satisfy exponential W₂ (or W_ψ) contraction, exponential decay in KL/Rényi is lost.  
  - Rényi with heavy‑tailed ν: Assumption 5 demands finite ψ‑moments; violations can make Rényi divergences infinite.  
  - Composite kernel mismatch: Using uLA with too large step η inflates cross‑regularization constants a₁, a₂ → bound fails.

- **First three experiments:**  
  1. **KL mixing for Gaussian:** Implement uHMC‑v with ν = N(0,I). Vary *h*, *T*. Plot KL(𝓛(X_k) ∥ ˜ν_h) vs *k*; verify exponential rate α T²/10 (Proposition 1).  
  2. **Asymptotic bias order:** For same Gaussian, compute average ∇f(X) at stationarity to estimate bias. Vary *h* and check scaling: KL bias ∝ h⁴ d³ (theoretical constant from Theorem 2).  
  3. **Composite kernel improvements:** Use uHMC‑s as base (known W₂ contraction) plus final uHMC‑v step. Compare bias and mixing against pure uHMC‑v; verify improved dimension dependence (e.g., Δ_h ∝ h⁴ d^{3/2}) as per Corollary 5.

## Limitations
- The smoothness and stability requirements (L(T²+Th) ≤ 1/12, LT² ≤ 2π²/5) are restrictive; they may exclude many practical targets (e.g., non‑smooth or poorly conditioned potentials).
- Rényi analysis provides only first‑order accuracy, leading to suboptimal gradient complexity O(d^{3/2} ε⁻¹) and tighter step‑size conditions ((q−1)c² ≤ 1/8). Achieving KL‑like scaling for Rényi remains an open challenge.
- The modular framework for composite kernels requires a ψ‑Wasserstein bias bound for the base kernel; while such bounds exist for uHMC‑v and uHMC‑s in W₂, the ψ‑Wasserstein version for uHMC‑s is not yet established.

## Confidence
- **Exponential KL convergence (Theorem 1):** High (rigorous one‑shot coupling + established W₂ contraction).  
- **Exponential Rényi convergence (Theorem 3):** Medium (coarser first‑order bounds, extra moment assumptions).  
- **KL asymptotic bias (Theorem 2):** Medium‑High (similar to mixing but relies on bias assumption that is verifiable).  
- **Rényi asymptotic bias (Theorem 4):** Medium (weaker constants, moment constraints).  
- **Gradient complexity bounds (Corollaries 2, 4, 5):** Medium (derived from optimized parameters but depend on abstract constants).  
- **Modularity for composite kernels:** Medium (conceptually solid but requires per‑kernel validation).
