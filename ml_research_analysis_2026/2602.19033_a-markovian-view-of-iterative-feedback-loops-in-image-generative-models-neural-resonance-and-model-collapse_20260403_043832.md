# A Markovian View of Iterative-Feedback Loops in Image Generative Models: Neural Resonance and Model Collapse

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19033
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19033
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Iterative feedback in generative models, modeled as a Markov Chain, leads to "neural resonance" (convergence to a low-dimensional invariant latent structure) when ergodicity and directional contraction hold, with dataset compressibility determining collapse speed.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the phenomenon of model collapse in generative AI, where models trained iteratively on their own outputs degrade in performance. The authors model the iterative feedback process as a Markov Chain and identify two necessary conditions for a stable convergent state called "neural resonance": ergodicity of the feedback process and directional contraction of latent representations. Through experiments with diffusion models on MNIST and ImageNet, CycleGAN, and audio, they demonstrate that when both conditions are satisfied, the feedback converges to a low-dimensional invariant latent structure. They introduce an eight-pattern taxonomy to characterize collapse behaviors using metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), intra-class spread, intrinsic dimensionality, and participation ratio. A key empirical finding is that dataset compressibility strongly modulates outcomes: highly compressible datasets (e.g., MNIST) retain semantic coherence over many generations, while diverse, less compressible datasets (e.g., ImageNet) undergo rapid semantic erosion. The work provides a theoretical framework and diagnostic tools for analyzing and potentially mitigating generational collapse.

## Method Summary
The study employs diffusion models (DDPM) with U-Net architectures across three feedback regimes: (1) latent-feedback, where a single pre-trained conditional diffusion model is frozen and conditioned on features from previously generated images; (2) label-guided retrained, where a fresh conditional diffusion model is trained from scratch each generation solely on synthetic data from the previous generation; and (3) unconditional retrained, similar but without conditioning. For CycleGAN, they apply a pre-trained model in a loop (A→B→A...). The core analysis treats each iteration as a step in a generational Markov chain (GMC). Stationarity of the chain is assessed via local and cumulative Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) drifts. Latent manifold geometry is characterized by intra-class spread (σintra), local intrinsic dimension (mLB), and global participation ratio (PRG). The eight-pattern taxonomy classifies collapse based on the sign of changes in these metrics. Experiments run on MNIST (using a locally trained classifier for features) and a 5-class ImageNet subset (using Inception-V3 features). The iterative loop proceeds: generate samples from current model/conditioning → compute metrics in fixed feature space → use synthetic data to train the next model or extract new conditioning → repeat until stationarity or collapse.

## Key Results
- Two necessary conditions for neural resonance: ergodicity of the feedback process and directional contraction of latent representations.
- Introduction of an eight-pattern taxonomy of collapse behaviors based on FID, σintra, mLB, and PRG.
- Dataset compressibility modulates collapse speed: highly compressible datasets (MNIST) retain semantics longer; diverse datasets (ImageNet) suffer rapid semantic erosion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Markovian Convergence via Ergodicity and Contraction**  
The iterative feedback is modeled as a time-homogeneous Markov chain with a positive one-step transition density. Ergodicity (irreducibility and aperiodicity in a bounded state space) guarantees convergence to a unique stationary distribution independent of initial conditions. Directional contraction means that successive applications of the feedback operator attenuate latent directions orthogonal to an emerging invariant subspace. Together, these properties cause the chain to settle onto a low-dimensional latent manifold—neural resonance. Evidence: formal derivations in Section 2 and empirical stationarity observed via plateauing FID curves. If ergodicity fails (e.g., deterministic cycles) or contraction ceases (variance expansion), resonance does not emerge; instead, the chain may cycle among attractors or drift unstably.

**Mechanism 2: Compressibility as a Modulator of Semantic Retention**  
Dataset compressibility—the number of latent dimensions needed to capture salient structure—strongly influences the resonance outcome. In highly compressible data (e.g., MNIST), the invariant subspace overlaps substantially with task-relevant semantics, allowing those semantics to persist even as the manifold contracts. In incompressible data (e.g., ImageNet), the invariant subspace captures only low-frequency, high-variance modes (e.g., color blobs, textures), discarding fine-grained semantics early. Evidence: MNIST latent-feedback shows gradual detail erosion while digits remain recognizable for many generations; ImageNet-5 label-guided retraining loses object semantics within ~5 generations. For datasets of intermediate complexity, compressibility heuristics may not predict collapse behavior.

**Mechanism 3: Geometric Taxonomy via Dimensional Metrics**  
The evolving geometry of the latent manifold under resonance is described by an eight-pattern taxonomy based on three metrics: intra-class spread (σintra) measures local cluster expansion/contraction; local intrinsic dimension (mLB) captures small-scale wrinkling; global participation ratio (PRG) quantifies overall subspace contraction. Their sign combinations (increase/decrease) define distinct regimes (e.g., coherent contraction, oblate expansion) that characterize how variance is redistributed during collapse. Evidence: Table 1 defines the eight patterns; Table 2 reports empirical observation of five patterns across experiments. The taxonomy assumes monotonic metric trends; it may not handle topological changes (e.g., manifold splitting) or highly fluctuating chains.

## Foundational Learning
**Markov Chain Theory (Ergodicity, Stationarity)**  
*Why needed here:* The entire analysis models iterative feedback as a Generational Markov Chain (GMC). Understanding irreducibility, aperiodicity, and positive Harris recurrence is essential to determine when the chain will converge to a unique stationary distribution—a prerequisite for neural resonance.  
*Quick check question:* Does a one-step positive transition density guarantee ψ-irreducibility in a continuous state space? (Yes, as argued in Lemma C.1: if K(x,y) > 0 for all x,y, then any set with ψ(A)>0 has K(x,A)>0 in one step.)

**Diffusion Model Sampling (DDPM Reverse Process)**  
*Why needed here:* The ergodicity proof for diffusion-based GMCs relies on properties of the DDPM reverse sampler: annealed Gaussian noise injection (ensuring positive density), smooth neural updates, and bounded pixel space. These properties establish the one-step positive density condition.  
*Quick check question:* In Algorithm 1 (DDPM sampling), which step ensures the Markov kernel has positive density everywhere? (Step 3: z ~ N(0,I) — the Gaussian noise has full support.)

**Latent Manifold Geometry Metrics**  
*Why needed here:* The eight-pattern taxonomy relies on accurately measuring σintra (cluster tightness), mLB (local dimensionality), and PRG (global dimensionality). Each captures a different aspect of latent space transformations under feedback.  
*Quick check question:* Why can mLB increase while PRG decreases (e.g., wrinkled expansion)? (mLB is sensitive to small-scale wrinkles/folds that add local degrees of freedom without significantly increasing the global variance captured by PRG.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Markov Chain Engine -> Feedback Orchestrator -> Conditioning Extractors -> Diffusion Backbone -> Generated Images -> Metrics Module

**Critical path**  
1. Choose dataset and feedback regime (e.g., MNIST latent-feedback). 2. Train the initial diffusion model (or load a pre-trained one). 3. Initialize generation n=0 with conditioning features from real data. 4. For each generation: generate N images using current model/conditioning; form synthetic dataset D_{n+1}; if retraining, train a fresh diffusion model on D_{n+1}; if latent-feedback, extract conditioning features from D_{n+1}. 5. After each generation, compute metrics (FID, σintra, mLB, PRG) in a fixed feature space. 6. Continue until both local and cumulative FID curves plateau (empirical stationarity) and classify the dimensional pattern from metric trends.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Conditioning granularity:* Latent-feature conditioning isolates feedback to the conditioning stream, while label-only or unconditional conditioning mixes feedback into model parameters.
- *Sample budget:* Fixed number of generated images per generation (e.g., 50k) ensures comparability but may under-represent rare modes.
- *Feature space choice:* Metrics are computed in Inception-V3 space for ImageNet, or a locally trained classifier for MNIST; absolute values depend on this choice, so focus on relative trends.
- *Noise injection:* For ergodicity, generative processes must include stochasticity (e.g., DDPM noise). Deterministic loops (CycleGAN) are non-ergodic by design.

**Failure signatures**  
- *Non‑ergodic chain:* Persistent large fluctuations in local FID (FID_{n,n-1}) without plateau; cyclical t‑SNE patterns; multiple absorbing states.
- *No directional contraction:* PR_G does not systematically decrease; latent variance expands or remains constant.
- *Unanticipated regime transition:* Dimensional pattern shifts mid‑experiment (e.g., from coherent contraction to wrinkled expansion), indicating transients or complex invariant subspace.
- *Metric divergence:* mLB and PR_G move in opposite directions consistently—might signal unstable estimation if sample size is too small.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **MNIST latent‑feedback (ergodic, oblate expansion):** Train one conditional diffusion on MNIST, freeze it. Use MNIST test set as initial conditioning. Iterate: generate images from conditioning features → use generated images as next conditioning. Expect slow semantic erosion (digits remain recognizable) with σintra ↑, mLB ↓, PR_G ↓.
2. **CycleGAN Horse↔Zebra (non‑ergodic, coherent expansion):** Apply pre‑trained CycleGAN in a loop (horse→zebra→horse→zebra). Start from separate horse and zebra image sets. Expect unstable FID, t‑SNE clusters settling into distinct attractors per start image; no neural resonance.
3. **ImageNet‑5 label‑guided retrained (ergodic, coherent contraction):** At each generation, train a fresh conditional diffusion from scratch on synthetic images from the previous generation, conditioned only on class labels (5 classes, 128×128). Use 5k images per class. Expect rapid drop in all metrics, semantic collapse to low‑entropy blobs within ≤10 generations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does neural resonance scale to large transformer‑based generative models?** The authors note that the interplay between resonance and transformer‑sized architectures remains an open question. Current experiments used smaller CNNs; transformers have different inductive biases and scaling behaviors, so the two necessary conditions may not yield resonance in the same way.
2. **Can we design effective methods to mitigate or delay generational collapse without sacrificing sample fidelity?** The paper highlights this as a key future direction but provides no concrete mitigation strategies. Practical interventions that preserve quality while delaying collapse are unknown.
3. **Does neural resonance occur in text‑only and vision‑language models, and how should directional contraction be defined semantically?** Extending the framework to text or multimodal models is challenging because directional contraction must be defined in semantic rather than pixel space, and latent geometries differ.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified implementation details:** The MNIST classifier architecture (layer depth, training hyperparameters) and exact ImageNet‑5 class mapping are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Missing diffusion sampling specifics:** The sampler schedule, number of steps, and any classifier‑free guidance scale are omitted, which affect the feedback kernel and ergodicity.
- **Taxonomy generalizability:** The eight‑pattern taxonomy is based on only three metrics and was empirically validated with only five of the eight patterns observed; its applicability to other datasets and models remains uncertain.

## Confidence

## Next Checks
1. **Ergodicity test:** Remove stochasticity (no noise injection) in the MNIST latent‑feedback loop. If convergence to a stationary distribution fails, this confirms the necessity of ergodicity.
2. **Compressibility correlation:** Compute PCA intrinsic dimensionality for multiple datasets (MNIST, CIFAR‑10, ImageNet‑5) and correlate with measured collapse speed (generations until semantic degradation). This directly tests the compressibility modulator hypothesis.
3. **Taxonomy stress test:** Vary feedback regimes (e.g., different noise schedules, guidance scales) and hyperparameters across diverse datasets to attempt to observe all eight patterns. If certain patterns never appear, refine the taxonomy or incorporate topological metrics (e.g., persistence diagrams) to capture manifold splitting.
