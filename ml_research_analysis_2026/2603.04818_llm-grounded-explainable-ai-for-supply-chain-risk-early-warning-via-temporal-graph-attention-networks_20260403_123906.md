# LLM-Grounded Explainable AI for Supply Chain Risk Early Warning via Temporal Graph Attention Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04818
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** A TGAT-LLM framework predicts maritime bottleneck risks (AUC 0.761) with LLM explanations that show 99.6% directional consistency with underlying model evidence.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework to generate operationally interpretable early warnings for supply chain bottlenecks. It couples a Temporal Graph Attention Network (TGAT), which processes daily maritime AIS data to predict risk, with a large language model (LLM) reasoning module. The LLM’s generation is constrained by structured, model-internal evidence (feature z-scores and attention weights), ensuring explanations are verifiable and grounded in the predictive model's logic. Evaluated on six months of real-world logistics data, the system achieves credible predictive performance while producing highly consistent natural language explanations.

## Method Summary
The method processes daily maritime AIS data into dynamic spatial graphs. A TGAT model ingests these graphs to predict the probability of port bottleneck formation. For predictions exceeding a risk threshold, an Evidence Extractor pulls relevant model-internal signals—specifically, normalized feature importances (z-scores) and aggregated attention scores from neighboring nodes. These structured signals are formatted into a prompt that constrains an LLM to generate a narrative explanation citing only this provided evidence, preventing ungrounded hallucinations.

## Key Results
*   Test AUC of 0.761, Average Precision (AP) of 0.344, and recall of 0.504 under a strict chronological split.
*   LLM-generated explanations demonstrate 99.6% directional consistency with the direction (positive/negative influence) of the underlying TGAT’s feature and attention evidence.
*   The coupling provides a pathway to translate complex temporal graph model outputs into human-readable, evidence-linked narratives for supply chain planners.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by **grounding** LLM explanations in concrete, extracted model evidence. This prevents the LLM from fabricating reasons and forces the explanation to reflect the actual statistical patterns the TGAT used for its prediction. The temporal graph structure is critical because supply chain risks propagate through physical networks over time; TGAT is specifically designed to model such evolving dependencies and spatial influences. The system operationalizes explainable AI by making the *reasoning process* transparent (via the evidence prompt) rather than just the final prediction.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Temporal Graph Attention Networks (TGAT):** *Why needed:* To model dynamic, spatially interconnected systems (like shipping networks) where relationships and risk signals evolve daily. *Quick check:* Can it capture a port's increasing risk due to declining vessel traffic from a key upstream hub over a two-week window?
*   **Large Language Model (LLM) Constrained Generation:** *Why needed:* To translate complex, multi-dimensional model evidence into fluent, domain-relevant natural language for end-users. *Quick check:* Does the LLM reliably reference only the provided z-scores and attention maps without adding unsupported claims?
*   **Evidence Extraction & Grounding:** *Why needed:* To create a verifiable, auditable link between a prediction and its supporting data. *Quick check:* Is the extracted evidence (feature/attention scores) a sufficient and non-misleading summary of the TGAT's decision for that specific node?
*   **Maritime AIS Data as a Proxy for Logistics Health:** *Why needed:* AIS provides fine-grained, global, real-time vessel movement data, serving as a direct sensor for port activity, congestion, and network disruptions. *Quick check:* Can AIS-derived metrics (e.g., port calls, berthing times) consistently correlate with known historical bottleneck events?
*   **Supply Chain Risk as Network Bottleneck Formation:** *Why needed:* Defines the prediction target operationally—identifying where and when capacity will be exceeded. *Quick check:* Is the binary label (bottleneck/no bottleneck) robustly defined from logistics KPIs (e.g., dwell time spikes)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Raw AIS Data -> Graph Builder -> Daily Spatial Graphs -> TGAT Encoder -> Risk Score + Internal States (Feature/Attention) -> Evidence Extractor -> Structured Evidence Prompt -> LLM -> Natural Language Explanation

**Critical Path:** AIS data ingestion and graph construction -> TGAT forward pass for risk prediction -> conditional triggering of explanation branch -> evidence extraction and prompt engineering -> LLM generation. The prediction path must complete within a daily operational cycle.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Predictive power vs. explanation fidelity. The TGAT uses complex attention mechanisms; simplifying the extracted evidence for the LLM may lose nuance but improves reliability of the generated explanation. Real-time operation vs. completeness: using only the most recent timesteps for the LLM prompt reduces latency but may miss longer-term evidence.

**Failure Signatures:** 1) LLM generates explanations citing evidence *not* in the prompt (failure of grounding). 2) High-risk predictions with low or conflicting extracted evidence scores (TGAT uncertainty not communicated). 3) System latency exceeds operational window due to TGAT or LLM processing time.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **End-to-End Pipeline Integrity:** Feed a small, labeled dataset through the full pipeline. Verify every high-risk prediction triggers an LLM call, and every LLM output references only provided evidence tokens.
2.  **Constraint Ablation Impact:** Compare explanations from (a) the full constrained pipeline, (b) an unconstrained LLM given only the risk score, and (c) a baseline (e.g., LIME/SHAP) on a sample set. Measure for hallucination rates and user-rated plausibility.
3.  **Noise & Gap Sensitivity:** Inject synthetic noise (e.g., missing AIS positions, jittered timestamps) into a clean validation set. Quantify the degradation in (a) TGAT AUC, (b) explanation consistency score, and (c) latency to identify failure thresholds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   How does the framework generalize to other logistics modes (e.g., air, rail) or diverse geographic regions?
*   Does directional consistency of explanations translate to factual correctness and practical utility for human planners?
*   How robust is the system under extreme disruption scenarios (e.g., port strikes, pandemic) or with noisy/incomplete AIS data?

## Limitations
*   Evaluation is confined to 6 months of maritime AIS data; generalizability to other domains is unknown.
*   Explanation quality metric (99.6% directional alignment) does not guarantee factual accuracy or decision-support value for planners.
*   No robustness testing under extreme disruption scenarios or sensitivity analysis to AIS data noise and gaps.

## Confidence
*   **Predictive performance (AUC 0.761, AP 0.344, recall 0.504):** Medium. Validated on a single-domain chronological split; risk of overfitting to short-term maritime patterns.
*   **LLM explanation consistency (99.6% directional alignment):** High. Rigorous internal constraint-based evaluation, but the metric is narrow and does not assess truthfulness.
*   **Framework viability (TGAT-LLM coupling for interpretable warnings):** Medium. Novel integration proven on one dataset, lacking external benchmarks and human-centered validation.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-dataset generalizability:** Replicate on at least two additional logistics datasets (e.g., global air cargo tracking, European rail freight) to assess performance stability across modes and regions.
2.  **Human-centered explanation validation:** Conduct a blind expert study where supply chain managers rank LLM-generated explanations against baselines (e.g., SHAP, attention heatmaps) for accuracy, relevance, and decision-support value.
3.  **Ablation of grounding constraints:** Compare (a) TGAT-only predictions, (b) unconstrained LLM explanations, and (c) full framework; quantify how much the evidence constraints degrade predictive metrics and whether they improve explanation reliability.
