# Bi-level RL-Heuristic Optimization for Real-world Winter Road Maintenance

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.24097
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24097
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Reduced makespan (122.14 → 118.81 min) and emissions (3,386.63 → 3,220.95 kg CO₂) with increased vehicle usage (20 → 28), yielding Pareto improvements over a static KDTree baseline.

## Executive Summary
Winter road maintenance for large-scale networks is currently inefficient and heavily reliant on human decisions. This paper proposes a bi-level reinforcement learning-heuristic framework to address this: an upper-level Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent partitions the network and allocates resources from depots, while a lower-level constrained vehicle routing solver optimizes within each cluster for minimal makespan and emissions. Validated on real UK motorway data (M25, M6, A1), the method reduces maximum route completion time from 122.14 to 118.81 minutes and total carbon emissions from 3,386.63 to 3,220.95 kg CO₂, while increasing vehicle usage from 20 to 28. All improvements represent Pareto gains over a static KDTree baseline, demonstrating better workload balance and constraint adherence.

## Method Summary
The problem is formulated as a bi-level optimization: strategic depot assignment (upper level) and tactical routing (lower level). The upper level employs a PPO agent that sequentially assigns each treatment-required road segment to a depot, using per-segment features (coordinates, length, speed limit, lane count, distances to depots). The lower level uses a constraint-aware nearest-neighbor (NN) heuristic to construct feasible vehicle routes within each depot’s cluster, strictly enforcing operational constraints (one-way roads, salt capacity, maximum route duration 120 minutes, maximum distance 630 km). The PPO agent is trained using a reward that combines the lower-level makespan and emissions, starting from a deterministic KDTree nearest-depot warm-start. The bi-level loop iteratively refines the assignment. Evaluation on a real UK network (M25, M6, A1) compares the final PPO-refined assignment against the one-shot KDTree+NN baseline.

## Key Results
- Makespan decreased from 122.14 to 118.81 minutes.
- Total CO₂ emissions reduced from 3,386.63 to 3,220.95 kg.
- Vehicle count increased from 20 to 28, indicating improved workload distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The upper-level Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent refines a spatial cluster assignment to reduce system-level makespan and emissions.
- **Mechanism:** The PPO policy observes per-segment features (e.g., coordinates, length, lane count, distances to depots) and sequentially assigns each treatment-required road segment to a depot. After a full assignment is proposed, the lower-level solver computes route objectives (makespan, emissions). The PPO agent is updated using a reward `r = -(w1*Z1 + w2*Z2)`, directly aligning policy improvement with the operator’s multi-objective goals.
- **Core assumption:** The depot assignment problem can be framed as a sequential decision process where the reward signal from the lower-level solver provides a meaningful, dense gradient for the PPO agent to learn improved high-level resource allocation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...upper-level PPO agent partitions the network and allocates resources from depots"
  - [section 4.2]: "We cast segment-to-depot assignment as a sequential decision problem...reward is r = -(Z1 + Z2)"
  - [corpus]: Neighbor corpus shows related work on RL for routing (e.g., "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Drone Route Optimization...", "Network-Constrained Policy Optimization..."), but no direct evidence for this specific bi-level *mechanism*. *Assumption: The bi-level reward-shaping approach is effective for this domain.*
- **Break condition:** If the lower-level solver's solution quality or constraint adherence varies dramatically for minor assignment changes, the reward signal becomes too noisy for stable PPO training.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The lower-level constraint-aware nearest-neighbor (NN) heuristic produces feasible, execution-ready vehicle tours within each depot's assigned cluster.
- **Mechanism:** For each depot's assigned segments, the solver iteratively builds vehicle routes. Starting from the depot, it greedily selects the farthest untreated feasible edge (respecting one-way, speed, salt capacity, max duration/distance constraints) until no further feasible insertion exists, then closes the route and starts a new one. This ensures full coverage and strict operational feasibility.
- **Core assumption:** A fast, greedy heuristic with strong feasibility enforcement can generate sufficiently high-quality, reproducible routes for a given cluster, making it a reliable oracle for the upper-level RL agent's reward.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3]: "Given the clusters...we solve each depot subproblem with a nearest-neighbor (NN) heuristic...augmented with feasibility checks."
  - [section 3]: The mathematical model defines constraints (Eqs. 3-11) that the heuristic explicitly enforces.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; corpus focuses on RL and large-scale routing generally. *Assumption: The NN heuristic's performance is stable across the diverse cluster shapes produced by PPO.*
- **Break condition:** If PPO generates highly fragmented or geographically dispersed clusters, the NN heuristic may fail to find feasible tours within the max duration constraint, leading to infeasible lower-level solutions or excessive vehicle usage.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The closed-loop bi-level workflow enables emergent, system-level Pareto improvements (lower makespan *and* emissions) that a single-level optimization cannot achieve at this scale.
- **Mechanism:** The iterative interaction between levels creates a feedback loop: the upper-level assignment shapes the complexity of the lower-level subproblems, and the lower-level solution quality informs the upper-level policy. This decomposition avoids the combinatorial explosion of a monolithic formulation and allows the RL agent to learn *strategic* partitioning (e.g., creating compact, balanced territories) that a static heuristic (KDTree) cannot.
- **Core assumption:** The problem structure is such that near-optimal system performance can be achieved by first making good high-level assignment decisions, then solving each resulting subproblem optimally (or near-optimally). The coupling between assignment quality and subproblem difficulty is learnable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.4]: "Our system forms a tight feedback loop between assignment and routing."
  - [section 5.3 Results]: Table 2 shows simultaneous improvement in makespan (122.14 → 118.81 min) and emissions (3,386.63 → 3,220.95 kg CO₂) with increased vehicle count (20 → 28), indicating a Pareto shift. Figure 4 visually shows more compact, depot-centric territories after PPO refinement.
  - [corpus]: "Bi-level" and "RL-heuristic" neighbors (e.g., "A bi-level framework for learning to solve combinatorial optimization on graphs") support the architectural paradigm but not this specific outcome.
- **Break condition:** If the two objectives (makespan, emissions) are fundamentally conflicting at the assignment level (i.e., improving one always degrades the other for all possible partitions), the bi-level loop may converge to a poor equilibrium or oscillate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
  - *Why needed here:* To stably learn a high-level assignment policy from a reward signal that is itself generated by a non-differentiable, constrained heuristic solver. PPO's clipped objective helps mitigate the instability of policy gradient methods when rewards are sparse or noisy.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a clipped surrogate objective (PPO) preferred over a vanilla REINFORCE algorithm for this reward signal?
- **Concept: Bi-level Optimization Decomposition**
  - *Why needed here:* The full multi-depot, constrained VRP for a strategic network (thousands of edges) is computationally intractable for a single solver. Decomposing into (1) strategic assignment and (2) tactical routing makes the problem tractable while preserving domain logic (operators first assign responsibility, then plan routes).
  - *Quick check question:* What specific property of the real-world problem (discussed in Section 1) makes a single-level model "computationally brittle"?
- **Concept: Constraint-Aware Greedy Construction (Nearest-Neighbor Heuristic)**
  - *Why needed here:* The lower-level must produce *feasible*, auditable routes that strictly adhere to operational rules (one-way, max 120 min, salt capacity). A simple greedy insertion with hard constraint checks guarantees feasibility and provides a consistent, fast oracle for the upper level.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a pure cost-minimizing insertion heuristic (ignoring constraints) fail as a lower-level solver in this domain?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Layer -> Upper-Level Module -> Lower-Level Module -> Reward & Loop Controller -> Warm-start Module
- **Critical path:** Input data → Edge feature encoding → PPO assignment → Cluster formation → NN routing per cluster → Compute (Z1, Z2) → Reward calculation → PPO policy gradient update → Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Scalability vs. Optimality:* The bi-level decomposition sacrifices global optimality for tractability. The NN heuristic is fast but myopic; PPO can only improve within the space of assignments that the NN can solve feasibly.
  - *Interpretability vs. Performance:* Using a KDTree warm-start and NN solver preserves human-interpretable depot territories and route structures, easing operator acceptance, but may limit the ceiling of performance gains compared to a fully end-to-end learned policy.
  - *Training Stability vs. Adaptivity:* The fixed lower-level solver provides a stable learning signal but cannot adapt its routing strategy to complement the evolving PPO assignments.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Constraint Violations:** Lower-level solver reports infeasible clusters (e.g., no tour respecting the 120-min limit). *Diagnostic:* Check cluster compactness and total assigned segment length vs. vehicle capacity.
  2. **Non-Pareto Improvement:** Makespan decreases but emissions increase (or vice-versa). *Diagnostic:* Inspect reward weighting (`w1`, `w2`); PPO may be overfitting to one objective.
  3. **Unstable Training:** Z1/Z2 metrics fluctuate wildly across PPO iterations without convergence. *Diagnostic:* Reward normalization; lower-level solver stochasticity (if any); PPO hyperparameters (clip range, learning rate).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Replication:** Implement KDTree+NN exactly as described. Record (Z1, Z2, NoV) to establish the baseline for all subsequent comparisons. Validate that all operational constraints are met.
  2. **Ablation - Lower-Level Fixed:** Run PPO *without* allowing it to update (i.e., keep the warm-start KDTree policy fixed) for 10 iterations. This confirms that the NN solver itself is deterministic and that any observed variation in the main experiment is due to PPO's assignment changes.
  3. **Sensitivity - Network Perturbation:** Apply small random geometric perturbations (±500m) to a subset of edge coordinates and re-run KDTree-PPO+NN. Measure degradation in (Z1, Z2). This tests the robustness of the learned assignment policy to minor real-world data inconsistencies (e.g., GPS mapping error).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the bi-level framework be scaled to entire national strategic road networks while preserving solution quality and computational feasibility?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states that future work will "fully leverage the scalability of bi-level approach ... on a national level strategic road network."
- **Why unresolved:** The present study validates only a 60×60 km area; scaling to national networks (thousands of segments, multiple depots) could overwhelm RL and routing components due to increased state/action space and combinatorial complexity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments on larger real-world instances (e.g., regional networks, multiple motorways) measuring makespan, emissions, training/planning time. If degradation occurs, demonstrate improvements (e.g., hierarchical decomposition, parallel solvers) that restore performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can dynamic factors like storm progression and priority queues be incorporated to enable real-time adaptation during a winter event?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion identifies "incorporating storm progression and priority queues" as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The current model assumes static road-treatment requirements; actual storms evolve, creating urgent hotspots that require re-prioritization after initial planning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a time-extended environment that simulates changing storm coverage and priority levels. Extend the upper-level RL to consider temporal features and produce adjustable assignments. Evaluate against a static baseline using metrics such as proportion of high-priority segments treated within deadline and overall makespan.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can depot salt stock levels and vehicle reload logistics be integrated without compromising route feasibility?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion mentions "depot stock/reload logistics" as a future direction.
- **Why unresolved:** Salt consumption and depot inventory are ignored; vehicles might run out of salt mid-route or depots might lack capacity for reloads, leading to infeasible plans during long shifts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the lower-level heuristic to track per-vehicle salt load and per-depot stock, and allow the upper-level assignment to schedule reload stops. Conduct experiments with realistic stock limits, comparing to the current approach in terms of makespan, emissions, and number of reload trips.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the bi-level framework support limited mid-shift replanning to handle unexpected disruptions (e.g., vehicle breakdown, road closure)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion cites "limited mid-shift replanning" as a target.
- **Why unresolved:** The pipeline is designed for pre-shift planning only; during execution, disruptions require quick re-optimization while considering already completed work and ongoing routes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement an incremental re-optimization module that ingests current vehicle locations and remaining untreated edges, invokes the bi-level process (or a faster approximation) to update assignments, then measures disruption impact (e.g., increase in makespan, additional deadheading) in simulated failure scenarios.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters (PPO settings, reward weights) hinders exact reproduction.
- The greedy lower-level heuristic may fail on poorly clustered assignments, risking infeasibility or excessive vehicle usage.
- Evaluation limited to a single UK corridor, raising concerns about generalizability to other networks.
