# RAMoEA-QA: Hierarchical Specialization for Robust Respiratory Audio Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06542
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06542
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 0.72 in-domain discriminative accuracy and 0.67 Macro F1, and reduces regression MAE to 2.29 on the RA-QA benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
RAMoEA-QA tackles the heterogeneity of respiratory audio data and diverse clinical query intents through a hierarchical two-stage routing model. The system dynamically selects a pre-trained audio encoder per recording (Audio-MoE) and a LoRA adapter per query intent/format (Language-MoA), enabling example-specific specialization with minimal parameter overhead. On the RA-QA benchmark, RAMoEA-QA demonstrates superior in-domain performance and robustness across domain, modality, and task shifts, establishing a new standard for respiratory audio question answering.

## Method Summary
The model employs a two-stage hierarchical routing mechanism. First, an Audio-MoE router selects one of several frozen pre-trained audio encoders (e.g., OPERA-CT, OPERA-GT) based on a lightweight spectrogram proxy. The chosen encoder processes the full spectrogram, and its output is aligned to the LLM dimension via a trainable MLP, then injected as a prefix into the LLM. Second, a Language-MoA router selects a LoRA adapter (specialized for question type and answer format) using the question and the audio prefix. The frozen LLM (GPT-2) generates the answer with the selected adapter. Only routers, aligners, and LoRA adapters are trained, using a masked LM loss with load-balancing regularization to prevent expert collapse.

## Key Results
- In-domain discriminative accuracy: 0.72 (vs. 0.61 and 0.67 for baselines), Macro F1: 0.67.
- Regression MAE: 2.29 on RA-QA benchmark.
- Strongest generalization under controlled domain, modality, and task shifts, consistently outperforming monolithic baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Audio-MoE for Acoustic Specialization
- **Claim**: The Audio Mixture-of-Experts selects dataset- and modality-appropriate pre-trained audio encoders per recording, producing acoustic representations better suited to heterogeneous respiratory conditions.
- **Mechanism**: A lightweight router computes a routing vector from a spectrogram proxy and performs hard top-1 selection among frozen audio encoders. The chosen encoder processes the full spectrogram; its output is aligned via an expert-specific MLP and injected as an audio prefix. Only a small LoRA-style adaptation layer and aligner are trainable.
- **Core assumption**: Different respiratory audio encoders have complementary strengths across recording conditions; the router can assign each example to the encoder yielding most discriminative features.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: "an Audio Mixture-of-Experts selects a pre-trained audio encoder per recording"
  - [section 3.3]: Detailed routing and expert activation pipeline; expert-specific aligners; frozen base with LoRA-style adaptation.
- **Break condition**: Routing collapse (one expert used >90% of traffic) due to poor load balancing, weak router representation, or insufficient training signals for minority datasets/modalities.

### Language-MoA for Intent/Format Specialization
- **Claim**: The Language Mixture-of-Adapters selects a LoRA adapter on a shared frozen LLM based on question intent and answer format, tailoring generation behavior without modifying the LLM backbone.
- **Mechanism**: After audio prefix formation, a router computes a vector from question tokens and/or the audio prefix, selects one LoRA adapter among specialists for different question types and task families. The adapter is applied during autoregressive generation while LLM parameters remain fixed.
- **Core assumption**: Different QA formats and clinical query intents require distinct language generation patterns; LoRA adapters capture these deviations efficiently, and the router maps question characteristics to the appropriate adapter.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "a Language Mixture-of-Adapters chooses a LoRA adapter on a frozen LLM per query intent and answer format"
  - [section 3.4]: Router input policies; adapter selection; shared frozen LLM with multiple LoRA experts.
- **Break condition**: Router input fails to encode format-specific cues, or adapters are too similar, leading to collapse. Insufficient examples per format can cause underfitting for rare formats.

### Hierarchical Staged Conditioning for Complementary Routing
- **Claim**: The two-stage routing (Audio-MoE first, then Language-MoA) creates complementary specialization: coarse acoustic/domain choice vs. generation/style/intent refinement.
- **Mechanism**: Stage A: Audio-MoE router selects an audio encoder *before* full audio processing, based on a cheap proxy. Stage B: After the chosen encoder runs, Language-MoA router selects a LoRA adapter using the question and the *selected* audio prefix. Staged conditioning reduces compute and enables co-adaptation.
- **Core assumption**: Audio router decision should be made with minimal compute and independent of the full encoder output; Language router benefits from knowing which audio representation is used. The two-stage design avoids redundant conditioning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "staged conditioning makes routing decisions complementary"
  - [section 3.5]: Explicitly contrasts pre-encoder vs. post-encoder conditioning; notes complementary fused policies.
- **Break condition**: Audio proxy too simplistic fails to capture nuanced patterns; audio prefix too short loses information; imbalance in training data causes one router to dominate.

  - Why needed: Language-MoA uses LoRA adapters as lightweight language experts; knowing LoRA's low-rank decomposition explains parameter efficiency and adapter swapping.
  - Quick check: Why might LoRA be preferable to adding new output heads when specializing a frozen LLM for multiple tasks without full fine-tuning?

  - Why needed: The problem stems from diverse recording devices, environments, modalities (cough, breath, speech), and labeling conventions, motivating two-stage routing.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. **Input pipeline**: Audio → log-mel spectrogram; Question → tokenized string.
  2. **Audio-MoE router**: Lightweight MLP takes routing vector from spectrogram proxy and/or question; outputs expert logits via Gumbel-Softmax (train)/argmax (eval).
  3. **Audio experts**: Frozen pre-trained encoders (OPERA-CT, OPERA-GT); each has trainable LoRA adaptation layer and expert-specific aligner MLP to project to LLM dimension.
  4. **Selected audio prefix**: Output of chosen expert after alignment, concatenated to LLM input.
  5. **Language-MoA router**: MLP takes vector from question tokens and/or audio prefix; selects one LoRA adapter.
  6. **LLM backbone**: Frozen autoregressive model (GPT-2); selected LoRA adapter applied during generation.
  7. **Output head**: Autoregressive generation with teacher forcing; loss on answer tokens only.

- **Critical path**: Minimum viable system: 2 audio experts + 2 LoRA adapters, one router per stage, frozen encoders/LLM, trained with load-balancing regularizers. Must maintain balanced expert usage (>~20% each) to avoid collapse. Requires dataset with multiple question formats and audio modalities.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Router input policy**: `audio`-only is cheapest but ignores question intent; `question`-only ignores acoustic context; `fused` most powerful but adds overhead. Paper finds `fused` generally best but staged conditioning makes two `fused` policies complementary.
  - **Number of experts**: More experts increase capacity but risk underutilization without strong diversity and sufficient data. Paper uses 2 audio experts and 2 language adapters.
  - **Load-balancing weight (λ)**: Too low → collapse; too high → router ignores task signal. Needs tuning.
  - **Hard vs. soft routing**: Hard top-1 efficient; soft mixing could improve robustness but increases compute. Paper uses hard routing.
  - **Audio proxy complexity**: Deeper proxy might improve decisions but erodes compute benefit of early routing. Paper uses shallow feature extractor.

- **Failure signatures**:
  1. **Routing collapse**: One expert/adapter used >90% of time. Diagnose by plotting routing distribution per dataset/question type. Symptom: performance similar to single-path model.
  2. **Format confusion**: High exact match on multiple-choice but low on open-ended. Indicates Language-MoA router not aligning adapters to formats.
  3. **Modality-specific failure**: Poor performance on held-out modality (e.g., breath sounds when trained only on cough). Suggests audio experts lack diversity or router cannot distinguish modalities from proxy.
  4. **High variance across seeds**: Unstable routing due to Gumbel-Softmax temperature or poor initialization. Check router entropy during training.
  5. **Numeric extraction failure in regression**: MAE high but parsing coverage low → model generates text without numbers. Could be no regression adapter selected or LLM not trained to output numbers.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Router sanity check**: After 1 epoch, log routing probabilities for balanced mini-batch. Expect non-degenerate distributions (each expert ≥10%). If not, increase load-balancing λ or add entropy regularization.
  2. **Forced-path ablation**: For each (audio expert, LoRA adapter) pair, create a single-path model by disabling routing and forcing that pair. Evaluate on validation slice. Compare against fully routed model. If routed model doesn't beat best forced pair, routing may be harmful or untrained.
  3. **Modality transfer probe**: Train on cough-only datasets, then test on held-out breath modality. Compare routed vs. single-path baseline. Clear improvement under modality shift validates Audio-MoE's ability to leverage appropriate experts for unseen acoustic conditions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can router uncertainty be quantified and used to trigger selective answering (abstention) in clinical settings?
- **Basis**: The Discussion identifies "exploiting router uncertainty as a confidence signal for selective answering (abstention)" as a future direction.
- **Why unresolved**: Current model uses hard top-1 routing without explicit uncertainty estimates. Clinical applications require safeguards against confident but incorrect answers.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Develop confidence metric from routing probabilities (e.g., entropy); evaluate on test sets with known difficult cases; measure trade-offs between abstention rate and accuracy; conduct clinician user studies.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does the hierarchical routing approach remain effective when scaled to larger language models and a larger number of audio experts?
- **Basis**: The Discussion notes that "studying how the proposed architecture scales with larger backbones, more experts, and additional data" is an important future direction.
- **Why unresolved**: Experiments used GPT-2 and two OPERA experts due to resource limits. Scaling may introduce routing imbalances, increased memory, and diminishing returns.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Systematic experiments varying LLM size (e.g., GPT-2, LLaMA-7B) and number of audio experts (2, 4, 8, 16). Track performance on in-domain and shifted tasks, router load balancing, and parameter efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Would soft routing (mixing multiple experts) improve robustness compared to hard top-1 routing, especially for ambiguous or multi-faceted audio inputs?
- **Basis**: Inferred; paper employs hard routing for efficiency but uses load-balancing regularization to prevent collapse. Soft routing could allow combining strengths of multiple experts for ambiguous examples.
- **Why unresolved**: Design choice between hard and soft routing not analyzed. Hard routing may discard useful information from non-selected experts, potentially harming performance on ambiguous examples.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Implement soft-routing variant (weighted sum of expert outputs using router probabilities); compare performance on in-domain and robustness benchmarks, focusing on cases with multiple pathologies; evaluate computational overhead.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can diversifying the pool of audio experts beyond the OPERA family improve generalization across different recording devices, environments, and patient populations?
- **Basis**: Inferred; Audio-MoE uses two similar OPERA-based experts. Routing patterns differ across datasets, suggesting acoustic representation diversity is valuable, but current experts may not capture full spectrum.
- **Why unresolved**: Paper does not explore whether including experts trained on different acoustic feature sets (e.g., raw waveforms) or broader audio domains (e.g., general sound events) would enhance robustness.
- **What evidence would resolve**: Curate diverse set of pre-trained audio encoders (e.g., wav2vec2, YAMNet, medical audio models) and integrate into Audio-MoE. Evaluate on held-out datasets and modality shifts; analyze expert activation patterns.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (LoRA rank, router λ/β) unreported; reproduction requires guesswork.
- Evaluation details (splits, early stopping, numeric parsing) insufficiently described.
- No router diagnostics; collapse risk not evaluated.
- Generalization only on predefined shifts; real-world robustness unknown.
- Audio expert pool limited to two OPERA variants; broader applicability unclear.

## Confidence
- **Audio-MoE**: Medium – Gains evident, but router's encoder selection validated only indirectly.
- **Language-MoA**: Medium – Adapter selection effectiveness assumed, not directly measured.
- **Hierarchical conditioning**: Low – No ablation isolating staged design; benefits conjectural.
