# Learning to Alleviate Familiarity Bias in Video Recommendation
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.07987
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07987
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: LAFB, a lightweight post‑ranking debiasing framework, reduces familiarity bias, increasing novel watch‑time share by 0.83 percentage points and emerging creator exposure by 0.03%, while maintaining overall watch time (+0.05%).

## Executive Summary
Video recommendation systems often over‑recommend familiar content, limiting diversity and hindering emerging creators. This paper introduces LAFB, a lightweight and model‑agnostic post‑ranking framework that mitigates familiarity bias by normalizing the user rating prediction score (URPS) with an estimated conditional expectation given user‑content familiarity features. Deployed on YouTube, LAFB demonstrably increases novel watch‑time share, improves emerging creator exposure, and reduces familiar‑content consumption without sacrificing overall engagement. The approach requires no changes to the base ranking model and has been successfully launched at scale.

## Method Summary
LAFB operates on top of an existing scoring function that produces a predicted satisfaction score (URPS) for each user‑item pair. For each pair, it extracts a vector of familiarity features (e.g., historical watch counts, recency). It then estimates a debiasing factor Adj_b = E[URPS | b] either by averaging scores within discrete buckets or by training a small neural network on continuous features. The debiased score is computed as s_debias = URPS / Adj_b (with clipping), which theoretically guarantees that the expected debiased score is constant across familiarity levels, thereby removing linear correlation. Finally, s_debias replaces the original URPS as input to the unchanged ranking function R. The entire framework is lightweight, requiring only the addition of the debiasing estimator and a simple arithmetic operation, making it suitable for production deployment.

## Key Results
- Novel watch‑time share increased by **+0.83 pp** and familiar‑content watch‑time share decreased by **-0.63 pp**.
- Emerging creator exposure improved by **+0.03%**.
- Overall watch‑time remained stable with a slight gain of **+0.05%**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Score Normalization via Conditional Expectation
- **Claim**: Decorrelating the URPS from user‑content familiarity by dividing by the conditional expectation reduces the system’s propensity to favor familiar items.
- **Mechanism**: For a pair (u,v) with familiarity features b, the debiased score is s_debias = s_u,v / E[s_u,v | b]. This normalization forces E[s_debias | b] = 1, eliminating linear correlation between the score and any familiarity feature.
- **Core assumption**: The familiarity bias in the original URPS manifests as a systematic dependence on b that can be captured and removed by this normalization.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: LAFB estimates personalized debiasing factors to adjust scores, reducing dominance of familiar content.
  - Section 2.1: Formal definition of Adj_b and the property Corr(b_i, s_debias) = 0.
  - Corpus: Related work on popularity/exposure bias mitigation provides contextual support but not direct evidence for this specific mechanism.
- **Break condition**: If the estimation of E[s|b] is highly inaccurate (e.g., due to sparse data or poor model specification), the normalization could amplify noise rather than remove bias.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Discrete and Continuous Feature Modeling
- **Claim**: Using both bucketed (discrete) and continuous representations of familiarity features allows LAFB to capture nuanced, multi‑dimensional familiarity patterns more effectively than single‑type approaches.
- **Mechanism**: Discrete features use empirical averages per bucket; continuous features are modeled by a small neural network f(b; θ) trained via MSE to predict URPS. This hybrid handles sparsity (via bucketing) and fine‑grained signals (via continuous modeling) in a unified framework.
- **Core assumption**: The set of provided familiarity features is a sufficient proxy for the latent familiarity driving the bias.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Section 2.1: Explicit description of “Discrete Feature Bucketization” and “Continuous Feature Modeling.”
  - Section 3.1: Figures 1 and 2 show distinct performance patterns; continuous model generally yields stronger and smoother effects.
  - Corpus: Weak direct evidence; other works on feature engineering for exposure exist.
- **Break condition**: If key familiarity signals are omitted or interaction effects between discrete and continuous features are not captured, debiasing will be incomplete.

### Mechanism 3: Post‑ranking Model‑agnostic Intervention
- **Claim**: Applying debiasing at the post‑ranking stage as a lightweight score adjustment allows seamless integration into existing production systems without retraining core ranking models or altering candidate generation.
- **Mechanism**: The debiased score s_debias replaces the original URPS as an input to the existing ranking function R: Score_u,v = R(s_debias, X_u,v). This isolates the intervention to a single arithmetic transformation, preserving all other model logic.
- **Core assumption**: The ranking function R is primarily sensitive to the relative ordering/scale of its URPS inputs, and multiplicative, sample‑specific normalization does not destabilize its learned non‑linearities or calibration.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: LAFB is lightweight and model‑agnostic, integrates seamlessly without modifying the base model.
  - Section 2.2: “We propose to replace each URPS with its debiased version…”
  - Section 3.4: Deployment success in YouTube demonstrates practical feasibility.
  - Corpus: Post‑ranking intervention work supports architectural viability.
- **Break condition**: If R relies critically on absolute magnitude or distributional properties of URPS (e.g., thresholding), the normalization could cause unexpected ranking volatility.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Conditional Expectation Estimation (E[Y|X])**  
  Why needed: The core debiasing formula (s / E[s|b]) requires a reliable estimate of the conditional expectation of the score given familiarity features.  
  Quick check: If you have a scatter plot of score vs. a continuous familiarity feature, what does the *mean* score in a vertical slice at a specific familiarity level represent?

- **Concept: Feature Engineering for Behavioral Signals**  
  Why needed: The efficacy of LAFB depends on the quality and coverage of the familiarity feature vector b_u,v (e.g., watch count, recency). These must capture the user’s historical exposure to an item/creator.  
  Quick check: Why might “number of watches in the last 7 days” be a more informative familiarity feature for a frequently updated creator than “total lifetime watches”?

- **Concept: Post‑ranking Intervention in Recommender Systems**  
  Why needed: LAFB operates after an initial score is generated, meaning it must work with a fixed input signal and cannot influence candidate selection or embedding learning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
URPS Generator → Familiarity Feature Extractor → Debiasing Model → Score Adjuster → Ranking Function

**Critical path**  
Compute URPS (s_u,v) and familiarity features (b_u,v); feed into Debiasing Model to obtain Adj_b; compute s_debias = s_u,v / Adj_b (with clipping); pass s_debias along with other features X_u,v to Ranking Function R which outputs the final item ranking.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Discrete vs. Continuous Modeling: Bucketing is simpler and robust to sparse data but coarse; continuous modeling is smoother and captures interactions but risks overfitting on sparse regions. Continuous model often performs better but needs regularization.
- Clipping/Smoothing: Adj_b must be bounded (e.g., clipped to [0.5, 2.0]) to prevent extreme adjustments that could destabilize ranking or user experience.
- Model Size vs. Expressivity: The debiasing network should be lightweight (e.g., 1–2 layer MLP) yet expressive enough to model E[s|b].

**Failure signatures**
- Calibration drift: The ratio s_debias / s_u,v no longer inversely correlates with familiarity features (check expected constant mean across buckets).
- Overall watch‑time drop: A significant decrease in the primary engagement metric indicates over‑debiasing or harm to relevance.
- High variance in debiasing factors: Adj_b values that are extremely noisy for similar b_u,v indicate poor estimation.
- Metric divergence: Novel watch‑time share rises but emerging creator exposure does not (or vice versa), suggesting mis‑targeted debiasing.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Bucketing sanity check**: For each discrete familiarity bucket, compute the mean original URPS and mean debiased score on held‑out data. The mean debiased score should be approximately constant across buckets (standard deviation <5% relative variation), confirming E[s_debias|b]≈1.
2. **Continuous model calibration**: For a continuous familiarity feature, compare the learned f(b;θ) against a non‑parametric smoothed estimate of E[s|b] (e.g., using kernel regression). Aim for R² > 0.95 to ensure accurate conditional expectation modeling.
3. **Clipping‑bound sensitivity sweep**: In a small A/B test, vary the clipping interval for Adj_b (e.g., [0.5, 2.0], [0.7, 1.3], [0.9, 1.1]). Plot the trade‑off curve between reduction in familiar‑content watch share and change in overall watch time; select an operating region where familiar share drops significantly without statistically significant overall watch‑time loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Uneven subgroup impact**: Are there user subgroups (e.g., heavy vs. light users) for whom LAFB reduces engagement or relevance? The paper reports only aggregate gains; fine‑grained cohort analysis is needed.
- **Long‑term ecosystem effects**: What are the long‑term effects on user retention and creator diversity? Short‑term novelty gains may not translate into sustained platform health; longitudinal A/B tracking is required.
- **Interaction with other biases**: Does LAFB’s debiasing affect other biases such as popularity or position bias? The paper focuses solely on familiarity; measuring correlation of scores with popularity/position before/after would reveal potential side effects.

## Limitations
- Exact familiarity feature definitions and bucketing boundaries are undisclosed, hindering precise replication.
- Neural network architecture, regularization, and clipping thresholds for the debiasing factor are unspecified.
- Generalizability beyond YouTube’s specific ranking pipeline and user base remains unclear.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness claims**: High – supported by large‑scale offline analysis and online A/B tests with statistically significant improvements in novelty and emerging creator exposure while maintaining overall watch time.
- **Mechanism (decorrelation via conditional expectation)**: Medium – theoretical guarantee relies on perfect estimation of E[s|b]; empirical evidence shows reduced correlation but sensitivity to model misspecification or data sparsity is not fully quantified.
- **Post‑ranking deployment viability**: High – production deployment at YouTube validates practical stability, though dependence on the base ranking function’s tolerance to multiplicative adjustments remains an assumption.

## Next Checks
