# Decoupling Skeleton and Flesh: Efficient Multimodal Table Reasoning with Disentangled Alignment and Structure-aware Guidance
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03491
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03491
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** DISCO+Table-GLS achieves competitive performance on 21 table benchmarks using only 10K training samples and no external tools, with robust out-of-domain generalization.

## Executive Summary
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) struggle with table reasoning because standard multimodal alignment entangles structural layout with semantic content. This work introduces DISCO, a disentangled alignment framework that separately trains on table structure (via anonymized layouts) and content (via global dimensions and coordinate-based cell queries). Combined with Table-GLS, a training-free global-to-local reasoning process that performs structured exploration and evidence-grounded inference, the system achieves strong performance across diverse table tasks with minimal annotation. On 21 benchmarks, DISCO significantly boosts table understanding metrics and, when paired with Table-GLS, matches or exceeds state-of-the-art LVLMs trained on far larger datasets, while generalizing effectively to unseen table layouts.

## Method Summary
DISCO modifies the multimodal alignment phase of an LVLM by splitting training into two streams. Structure alignment teaches the model to predict a table's layout by replacing all cell content with a placeholder token, forcing it to learn row/column organization independently. Content alignment separately trains the model to describe global table dimensions and to retrieve content for specific cell coordinates. This disentangled training uses only 10K table images and is implemented via LoRA fine-tuning on a base LVLM. At inference, Table-GLS applies a three-stage, training-free prompting protocol: (1) Global Structure Exploration identifies relevant rows/columns; (2) Self-refined Sub-table Extraction evaluates this plan and extracts a minimal, relevant sub-table; (3) Evidence-grounded Reasoning produces the final answer based strictly on the sub-table. The combination leverages DISCO's learned structural representations to guide accurate region selection during reasoning.

## Key Results
- DISCO substantially improves table understanding: for Qwen3-VL-32B, table size detection rises from 42.0% to 64.2% and cell location from 86.8% to 93.5%.
- The full DISCO+Table-GLS system averages 58.12 on 8 QA and fact verification benchmarks, matching optimized LVLMs trained on 20K–82K samples.
- It demonstrates robust out-of-domain performance, e.g., 76.71 on AIT-QA and 77.14 on PubHealthTab.
- Transfer gains are observed on general multimodal tasks, improving results on ScienceQA and TextVQA.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Disentangled structure–content alignment (DISCO)** enables data-efficient adaptation by isolating structural learning. By training on anonymized layouts (structure) and coordinate-based queries (content), the model builds an explicit internal representation of tabular logic (rows, columns, coordinates) without entangling it with specific cell text. This works because LVLMs already possess semantic grounding from pre-training; DISCO efficiently adds a structural “skeleton” layer. The break condition is tables with highly irregular or implicitly encoded layouts (e.g., nested tables) where anonymization may remove essential relational cues.

**Table-GLS** improves reasoning robustness by enforcing a structured, evidence-grounded process. The three-stage prompting (global exploration → sub-table extraction → evidence reasoning) constrains the model’s attention to a minimal, relevant region, reducing hallucination from the full table. This assumes the LVLM can follow multi-step instructions and self-evaluate plans. A break condition occurs when the required evidence is non-contiguous and cannot be captured by a single minimal sub-table.

**Synergy for OOD robustness** arises because DISCO’s structural abstraction is reusable across layouts. Table-GLS relies on this abstract model to accurately locate relevant regions even in unseen table formats, preventing overfitting to training distribution quirks. If OOD tables violate the learned structural conventions (e.g., non-standard grids), the synergy breaks down.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal alignment in LVLMs:** Tables require mapping a 2D visual grid to a symbolic representation. Standard alignment (e.g., image→HTML) entangles layout tokens with content, hindering structure learning. *Quick check:* Why might training an LVLM on raw HTML tables lead to poor generalization to tables with different visual styles?
- **Disentangled representation learning:** DISCO assumes structure and content can be supervised independently. Anonymizing cell content preserves structural tokens (e.g., `<tr>`, `<td>`, `||`), forcing the model to learn layout separately. *Quick check:* If you replace all cell text with `[MASK]` in a table’s HTML, what structural information remains? Why is this useful?
- **Chain-of-thought and structured prompting:** Table reasoning often needs multi-hop lookups. Table-GLS uses explicit multi-step prompts instead of end-to-end generation to reduce hallucination and make reasoning traceable. *Quick check:* How does forcing a "global structure exploration" step change the model’s error pattern compared to direct answering?
- **Coordinate-based grounding:** Content alignment uses row/column indices to query cells, teaching the model to map spatial coordinates to content. This mirrors how humans reference tables. *Quick check:* Why is a coordinate-based query more effective for learning table structure than a free-form description of a cell’s content?
- **Global-to-local reasoning:** Complex queries often require first identifying the relevant region (global) before detailed examination (local). This decomposition reduces cognitive load on the model. *Quick check:* What types of table questions would fail if only global or only local reasoning were used?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** DISCO Training Module -> Table-GLS Inference Module -> Base LVLM. The DISCO module produces a fine-tuned LVLM with disentangled structure-content understanding. The Table-GLS module applies three sequential prompts to this model at inference.

**Critical path:** 1) Prepare 10K table images from MMTab and construct DISCO triples (anonymized structure, global dimensions, local coordinate queries). 2) Fine-tune base LVLM with LoRA using combined losses. 3) For each new question, sequentially invoke the Table-GLS prompts (global exploration, sub-table extraction, evidence reasoning).

**Design tradeoffs:** DISCO requires custom data construction (anonymization) but uses ~10x less data than full textual alignment. Risk: Over-specialization to anonymized formats. Table-GLS is training-free but increases inference token count (~2× vs. CoT). Risk: Error propagation if early stages misidentify regions.

**Failure signatures:** DISCO failure: low TSD/TCL scores on validation; inspect if anonymized HTML preserves layout tokens. Table-GLS failure: sub-table omits correct cell; check Stage I JSON output for wrong rows/columns. Combined failure: low OOD scores despite good in-domain; likely structural abstraction didn’t capture universal grid logic; increase layout diversity in training.

**First 3 experiments:** 1) Alignment sanity check: Train DISCO on 1K samples, evaluate TSD/TCL on MMTab in-domain set; expect >10% absolute gain over Textual(10K) baseline. 2) Table-GLS integration: Apply prompts to DISCO-tuned model on WTQ; compare against direct answering; expect >5% gain. 3) OOD robustness: Test on AIT-QA and PubHealthTab; if DISCO+Table-GLS outperforms Textual-All by >5%, disentanglement transfers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extension to other structured visuals:** How can the framework be extended to charts, diagrams, or forms? The paper explicitly notes this as future work; evidence would be comparable gains on such benchmarks.
- **Handling irregular layouts:** How can DISCO work for tables with extremely irregular or implicitly encoded structures? The limitation notes current reliance on clean separation; resolution would involve robust structure extraction for complex tables and evaluation on such datasets.
- **Efficiency of Table-GLS:** Can the multi-stage process be streamlined to reduce token generation and latency without sacrificing accuracy? The limitation cites increased token cost; evidence would be compressed variants (e.g., iterative refinement) maintaining performance.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility gaps: exact sampling method/seed for 10K MMTab tables, full prompt templates for DISCO training, and Table-GLS inference parameters are unspecified.
- Dependency on strong base LVLMs: performance relies on pretrained semantic grounding; results may degrade for weaker models.
- Break conditions: DISCO may fail on highly irregular layouts; Table-GLS may omit critical evidence for non-contiguous reasoning patterns.

## Confidence
- **DISCO improves table understanding with minimal data:** Medium. Large reported gains but lack full ablations on data size and model variations.
- **Table-GLS enhances reasoning via structured exploration:** Medium. Improvements noted but error propagation risks and token overhead not thoroughly analyzed.
- **Combined system achieves competitive OOD performance:** Medium. Scores are promising but independent validation needed given reproducibility gaps.
- **Transfer to general multimodal tasks:** Medium. Positive results on ScienceQA/TextVQA but limited evaluation scope.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation on training data size:** Train DISCO with 1K, 5K, 10K, 20K samples to verify data efficiency curve and identify saturation.
2. **Stress test on irregular tables:** Evaluate DISCO+Table-GLS on tables with complex merging, nested structures, and non-grid layouts to quantify break conditions.
3. **Detailed error analysis:** Manually inspect Stage I/II outputs on failure cases to determine whether errors stem from structure misidentification or sub-table extraction.
