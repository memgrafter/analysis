# Scaling View Synthesis Transformers
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21341
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21341
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SVSM achieves a 3x more compute-optimal Pareto frontier than previous SOTA decoder-only model (LVSM) on RealEstate10K (VC=2) while maintaining equal scaling trends, with superior reconstruction metrics and 2-4x faster inference. For multiview (VC>2), integrating PRoPE restores favorable scaling, establishing new SOTA with reduced training FLOPs.

## Executive Summary
The paper challenges the belief that decoder-only architectures are necessary for state-of-the-art view synthesis. It introduces the Scalable View Synthesis Model (SVSM), an encoder-decoder architecture that encodes a scene once and decodes multiple target views via cross-attention. This design amortizes encoding costs and leverages an "effective batch size" hypothesis (product of scenes per batch and target views per scene) to enable compute-optimal scaling. SVSM achieves a 3× left-shifted compute-performance Pareto frontier compared to the prior SOTA decoder-only model (LVSM) on RealEstate10K (VC=2), with better reconstruction quality and 2-4× faster inference. For multiview settings (VC>2), relative camera pose embeddings (PRoPE) are necessary to restore favorable scaling. SVSM with PRoPE outperforms LVSM by significant margins on DL3DV (VC=4) and establishes new SOTA on real-world benchmarks with significantly less training compute.

## Method Summary
SVSM is an encoder-decoder transformer. The encoder processes all context views into a latent scene representation (set of tokens) using self-attention. The decoder renders multiple target views in parallel by using cross-attention: target camera queries attend to the scene latent. This amortizes the encoding cost over all targets. For context view counts >2, PRoPE (Projective Relative Positional Encoding) is applied to embed relative camera geometry into the attention mechanism (typically in the decoder). Training uses AdamW with a learning rate of 4e-4, MSE + perceptual loss (λ=0.5), and a residual scaling factor 1/√L for stability. The key hyperparameter is the effective batch size B_eff = B * V_T; performance depends on B_eff, not individual B or V_T.

## Key Results
- SVSM (VC=2) yields a Pareto frontier ~3× more compute-optimal than decoder-only LVSM on RealEstate10K.
- At matched compute, SVSM improves reconstruction: +0.34 PSNR, -0.002 LPIPS vs. LVSM on VC=2.
- SVSM inference is 2-4× faster than LVSM due to single encoding.
- For VC=4 on DL3DV, SVSM with PRoPE outperforms LVSM by +0.68 PSNR and -0.016 LPIPS at comparable compute.
- SVSM establishes new SOTA on RealEstate10K, DL3DV, and Objaverse, with significantly reduced training FLOPs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Effective Batch Size Amplification
**Claim:** Training with multiple target views per scene (V_T) multiplies the gradient signal per forward pass when the scene encoding is amortized.
**Mechanism:** SVSM encodes the scene once into a latent z; the decoder then renders all V_T targets in parallel. The total compute scales as χ(SVSM) ∝ B_eff + B·V_C, where B_eff = B·V_T. By holding B_eff constant and increasing V_T while decreasing B, one achieves the same performance with lower total FLOPs than LVSM, which must reprocess context for each target (χ(LVSM) ∝ B_eff (V_C+1)). This "effective batch size" hypothesis ties performance to B_eff.
**Break condition:** If V_T is forced to 1, SVSM loses its amortization advantage; effective batch size becomes B, negating compute benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Encoder-Decoder Unidirectional Scalability
**Claim:** An encoder-decoder with unidirectional cross-attention can scale as effectively as bidirectional decoder-only models when combined with high V_T.
**Mechanism:** LVSM's decoder-only architecture updates both context and target tokens bidirectionally in each layer, requiring V_T full forward passes. SVSM's encoder produces z once; the decoder uses unidirectional cross-attention (target queries attend to z), allowing all V_T decodings in parallel without re-encoding. This reduces inference FLOPs from O(V_T·(V_C+1)^2) to O(V_C^2 + V_C·V_T). With sufficient V_T (enabled by effective batch size hypothesis), SVSM achieves a superior compute-performance Pareto frontier.
**Break condition:** If the scene latent z is bottlenecked (e.g., fixed-size global token), scaling suffers; unbottlenecked designs (z = all context tokens) are best.

### Mechanism 3: Relative Camera Pose Embeddings (PRoPE) for Multi-View Generalization
**Claim:** For V_C > 2, SVSM's scaling saturates without relative camera pose embeddings; adding PRoPE restores favorable scaling by preventing pose information loss through the latent bottleneck.
**Mechanism:** In multiview settings, wider baselines require reconciling more diverse relative poses. PRoPE transforms context tokens into a target-related reference frame before attention and inverse-transforms after MLP, directly encoding relative geometry into attention scores. This ensures the decoder can extract view-specific information from z regardless of the original pose distribution. Evidence shows SVSM with PRoPE matches LVSM's scaling slope but with a left-shifted frontier.
**Break condition:** For V_C=2, PRoPE offers negligible benefit; it is essential primarily for V_C > 2.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Effective Batch Size in Multi-Target NVS**
  - *Why needed:* Understanding that B_eff = B·V_T is the true batch size for compute-normalized scaling explains why increasing V_T while decreasing B saves FLOPs without hurting performance.
  - *Quick check:* How would you adjust B and V_T to halve the training FLOPs of SVSM while maintaining the same B_eff? (Answer: Double V_T and halve B, because χ(SVSM) ∝ B_eff + B·V_C; the B·V_C term reduces when B is smaller.)
- **Concept: Amortized Encoding via Cross-Attention**
  - *Why needed:* The core architectural innovation is replacing LVSM's bidirectional self-attention decoder with a unidirectional cross-attention decoder that operates on a fixed scene latent, enabling parallel decoding and amortization.
  - *Quick check:* Why does SVSM's decoder use cross-attention instead of self-attention over target tokens? (Answer: Cross-attention allows each target token to attend only to the scene latent z, not other target tokens, enabling independent, parallel decoding after a single encoding.)
- **Concept: Relative Camera Pose Embeddings (PRoPE)**
  - *Why needed:* For V_C>2, PRoPE is necessary to prevent scalability saturation by embedding relative geometry into attention, compensating for the latent bottleneck.
  - *Quick check:* What is the operational difference between applying PRoPE only to the encoder versus only to the decoder? (Answer: The paper's ablations show decoder-only PRoPE works nearly as well as both; the critical stage is cross-attention where target queries attend to z, so canonicalizing z or the queries to a common frame is key.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Context Views -> Encoder (ViT with self-attention) -> Scene Latent z (set of tokens)
  Scene Latent z + Target Camera Parameters -> Decoder (Cross-Attention, optionally with PRoPE) -> Target Views (all V_T in parallel)
- **Critical path:** Encode context C → z (once). For each target pose (g_T, K_T): decode I_T = D(z, g_T, K_T) in parallel. Training: sample batch of B scenes, each with V_T targets; total FLOPs ≈ B·(encode_cost + V_T·decode_cost).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Unbottlenecked latent (z = all context tokens) scales best; fixed-size latent (e.g., global pool) hurts scaling but improves inference memory for large V_C.
  - PRoPE placement: Decoder-only PRoPE is sufficient; encoder-only is weak. GTA alternative works similarly, indicating relative pose embedding matters more than epipolar geometry.
  - Patch size: Smaller patches increase compute quadratically but may be needed for high resolution.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Saturation on V_C>2 without PRoPE: LPIPS plateaus quickly with compute.
  - Poor scaling if V_T is too small (≈ V_C): B·V_C term remains large, negating amortization.
  - Inconsistent B_eff results across (B, V_T) pairs: check implementation of parallel decoding and loss averaging.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Effective Batch Size Validation: Train SVSM (V_C=2) with same B_eff (e.g., 256) using (B=16, V_T=16) and (B=256, V_T=1). Expect similar final test LPIPS (±0.1).
  2. Scaling Law Sweep: For V_C=2 on RealEstate10K, train models across parameter counts (15M–740M) and FLOP budgets (100 petaflops to 100 exaflops). Plot LPIPS vs. FLOPs; confirm SVSM frontier ~3× left-shifted vs. decoder-only LVSM.
  3. PRoPE Ablation for V_C>2: Train SVSM (V_C=4) on DL3DV with and without PRoPE. Without PRoPE, scaling saturates early; with PRoPE, improvement continues. Also test decoder-only vs. both.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Optimal Encoder-Decoder Compute Allocation:** What is the optimal split of training compute between encoder and decoder across different context view counts? (The paper used heuristics and left systematic study for future work.)
2. **Extreme B_eff Validity:** Does the effective batch size hypothesis hold when B or V_T are extreme (e.g., V_T >> V_C or B >> V_T)? (Validated for B_eff up to 1024, but extreme values could change optimization dynamics.)
3. **Mechanism of PRoPE Necessity:** Why does PRoPE enable compute-optimal scaling in multiview (VC>2) but not stereo (VC=2)? (Inferred: the encoder bottleneck hurts more for VC>2; stereo needs less pose information, but no theoretical analysis.)
4. **Fixed Latent Scaling Penalty:** Why do fixed-size latent representations drastically reduce the compute-optimal scaling exponent compared to bottleneck-free designs? (Inferred: Section 7 shows fixed-latent designs scale poorly; cause could be information loss or optimization difficulty.)

## Limitations
- PRoPE mechanics underspecified; exact equations omit reference to [14].
- Exact hyperparameters for large models (420M/740M) not fully listed, hindering precise scaling reproduction.
- Effective batch size hypothesis assumes unlimited target views per scene; may break on datasets with sparse viewpoints.

## Confidence
- **Effective batch size (B_eff):** High (strong empirical evidence across (B, V_T) pairs).
- **Encoder-decoder compute-optimality:** High (clear Pareto frontier shift on VC=2).
- **PRoPE necessity for VC>2:** High (ablation shows saturation without it).
- **Overall SOTA with lower FLOPs:** Medium-High (relies on fair FLOP-matched baselines; minor risks of implementation mismatch).
