# Bio-Inspired Self-Supervised Learning for Wrist-worn IMU Signals
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.10961
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10961
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Bio-PM (1.4M params) achieves an average Macro-F1 of 0.65±0.07 across six subject-disjoint HAR benchmarks, significantly outperforming prior SSL methods and uniform-chunk baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a key limitation in self-supervised learning (SSL) for wrist-worn IMU human activity recognition (HAR): existing methods treat signals as unstructured time series, ignoring the underlying biological structure of movement. The core innovation is a bio-inspired tokenization that segments accelerometer signals at acceleration zero-crossings, grounded in motor control's submovement theory. This creates a sequence of "movement segments" as meaningful tokens. A Transformer is then pretrained on ~28k hours of NHANES data using a masked segment reconstruction objective, forcing it to model dependencies between these biologically grounded units. The resulting model, Bio-PM, demonstrates superior performance, label efficiency, and generalization to unseen movement transitions compared to strong SSL baselines and ablations that remove the biological prior.

## Method Summary
The method involves two main phases. First, a specialized tokenization pipeline processes raw 3-axis acceleration: a high-pass filter removes gravity; acceleration zero-crossings (with hysteresis) detect boundaries of movement segments, each corresponding to a velocity extremum. Segments are resampled to a fixed length and encoded by a 1D CNN, with embeddings concatenated with axis and duration metadata. Second, a Transformer encoder with time-aware positional embeddings (handling irregular segment timing) processes the variable-length token sequence. It is pretrained via masked segment reconstruction (ℓ1 loss) on NHANES windows. For downstream HAR, frozen token embeddings are pooled and fused with the low-pass gravity signal to form a feature vector for a linear probe (multinomial logistic regression) on six public benchmarks, using subject-disjoint splits.

## Key Results
*   Bio-PM achieves an average Macro-F1 of **0.65±0.07** across six HAR benchmarks, outperforming TF-C (0.59), AugPred (0.44), and a naive equal-chunk masking baseline (0.47).
*   Ablation confirms the tokenization is key: replacing bio-aligned segments with uniform chunks reduces average Macro-F1 by **0.18**.
*   The model shows superior **label efficiency** and better **generalization to unseen movement transitions** compared to non-contextual baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Biologically Grounded Tokenization
*   **Claim:** Segmenting at acceleration zero-crossings imposes a biologically grounded inductive bias, structuring input into semantically meaningful movement units (submovements) that enable the model to capture compositional activity patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** The zero-crossing detection (after gravity removal) yields segments where each corresponds kinematically to a velocity peak. The Transformer's attention then operates over these event-level units rather than arbitrary time points, aligning with motor control theory.
*   **Core assumption:** Acceleration zero-crossings reliably demarcate functional boundaries between wrist movement units across diverse activities and subjects.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] tokenization grounded in submovement theory; [section 3.2] kinematic justification linking acceleration zero-crossing to velocity peak.
*   **Break condition:** If zero-crossings are noise-induced or fail to correspond to meaningful boundaries in low-amplitude/oscillatory motions, the token sequence misrepresents movement structure, harming learning.

### Mechanism 2: Masked Segment Reconstruction Objective
*   **Claim:** The masked reconstruction objective forces the Transformer to model long-range temporal dependencies *between* movement segments, shifting learning beyond local waveform morphology.
*   **Mechanism:** Contiguous time bins are masked, and all overlapping segments are corrupted. The model must infer missing segments from the context of *other* segments in the same 10s window. Success requires understanding how different movement units (e.g., reaching → grasping) relate in time.
*   **Core assumption:** The temporal order and context of movement segments are predictive of each other in natural human activity.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] objective models temporal dependencies of movement segments; [section 3.3] hybrid masking policy; ablation showing uniform chunking (preserving local morphology but losing event alignment) degrades performance by 0.18 Macro-F1.
*   **Break condition:** If the masking strategy (rate, contiguity) is mismatched to typical segment density/duration in NHANES, the task becomes trivial or impossibly difficult, preventing meaningful learning.

### Mechanism 3: Time-Aware Positional Encoding
*   **Claim:** Explicit time-aware positional encoding allows the Transformer to correctly interpret the irregular temporal spacing between movement segments, which is crucial for modeling their sequential dependencies.
*   **Mechanism:** Since segment durations vary, midpoints are irregularly spaced. The model adds a learned MLP embedding of the normalized absolute midpoint time and a relative-position bias based on discretized offset between segment pairs. This injects precise timing info into attention.
*   **Core assumption:** Relative timing and duration between segments carry activity-relevant information not recoverable from waveform features alone.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] justification for time-aware encoding; [section F.1] ablation shows removing positional encodings reduces Macro-F1 by ~3 points (max drop 8.2).
*   **Break condition:** If segment midpoint metadata is noisy (poor zero-crossing detection) or activity semantics are invariant to precise timing, the added complexity may not help and could overfit.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Submovement Theory (Motor Control)**
    *   **Why needed:** Provides the theoretical foundation for defining "movement segments" as the fundamental compositional units of wrist motion. Without this biological prior, tokenization would be arbitrary and unlikely to align with true movement structure.
    *   **Quick check:** Does the velocity profile of a typical reaching movement in your dataset exhibit a single, clear peak, or multiple overlapping peaks? If multiple, how would that map to acceleration zero-crossings?
*   **Concept: Self-Supervised Pretraining Objectives for Time Series**
    *   **Why needed:** To learn robust representations from massive unlabeled upstream data (NHANES). The choice of objective (masked reconstruction) interacts critically with tokenization, providing dense token-level supervision that enforces understanding of segment-level context.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might contrastive learning (e.g., TF-C) be less effective than masked reconstruction when using movement-aligned tokens? (Hint: Consider what "views" of the signal preserve the segment structure.)
*   **Concept: Irregular Time Series Sequence Modeling**
    *   **Why needed:** Movement-segment tokenization produces sequences of irregular length and irregular temporal spacing. Standard Transformers assume uniform token spacing; this model must handle irregularity via explicit time embeddings and a flexible token sequence.
    *   **Quick check:** If you resampled all segments to a fixed length *and* fixed the time between token midpoints (e.g., by inserting dummy tokens), what information would be lost?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Raw 10s Window -> Gravity Separation (6th-order Butterworth HP/LP) -> Tokenization (Zero-crossing detection -> Segment extraction -> Resample to L=32) -> Segment Encoder (1D CNN, 60-d output) -> Token Assembly (CNN embedding + axis embedding + duration) -> Time-Aware Position Layer (absolute MLP time embedding + relative offset bias) -> Transformer Encoder (5 layers) -> Pretraining: Masked Reconstruction Decoder -> Upstream Loss (ℓ1, 100x weight on masked). Downstream: Pool token embeddings (mean/std) -> Concatenate with low-pass gravity vector -> Linear Probe.
*   **Critical path:**
    *   *Pretraining:* Raw window → Gravity separation → Token creation → Time-aware positional add → Transformer → Masked reconstruction loss.
    *   *Downstream:* Raw window → Gravity separation → Token creation → Transformer → Pooling → Add gravity → Linear probe.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Segment duration variability vs. CNN input size: Resampling to L=32 discards fine resolution but enables a fixed CNN; larger L preserves detail but increases compute.
    *   Hysteresis thresholds (50ms, 0.01g): Higher thresholds merge true segments (reducing token count but losing boundaries); lower thresholds increase noise tokens. Tuned on NHANES; may need adjustment for other datasets.
    *   Model capacity (1.4M vs. 13M): Larger models show marginal gains; 1.4M chosen for edge deployment feasibility.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   *Upstream:* High masked-region reconstruction loss (MAE > 0.2) after convergence; extreme token count per window (<5 or >100); segments show no correlation with activity labels.
    *   *Downstream (linear probe):* Macro-F1 collapse (<0.3) if gravity component is withheld on postural-dependent activities; shuffling token order causes large drop (>10 points) on order-sensitive activities.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Tokenization Validation:** Process a small labeled subset (e.g., one PAMAP subject). Plot acceleration with detected zero-crossings (vertical lines) overlaid on activity label boundaries. Check alignment with known transitions (e.g., walk→stand). Significant misalignment suggests threshold recalibration.
    2.  **Masking Policy Sanity Check:** For a fixed window, visualize masking patterns (random vs. contiguous 1s bins). Verify contiguous masking masks *all segments* whose midpoint falls within a masked bin. Ensure 50% masking rate yields a mix of fully/partially visible context.
    3.  **Positional Encoding Ablation:** Train a linear probe on frozen Bio-PM embeddings with `p(˜t)` and relative bias **disabled**. Compare Macro-F1 to reported value. A drop of >5 points on datasets with complex ordering (e.g., MHealth) confirms reliance on timing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   Does the performance advantage of Bio-PM’s movement-segment tokenization persist or widen when pretraining on larger, more diverse upstream datasets (e.g., UK Biobank)?
*   How sensitive is Bio-PM’s downstream performance to the specific parameters of its tokenization pipeline (high-pass filter cutoff, hysteresis thresholds, segment resampling length)?
*   Would alternative biologically grounded tokenizations—such as velocity zero-crossings (after drift correction) or direct submovement decomposition—further improve HAR performance compared to acceleration zero-crossings?
*   Do Bio-PM’s segment embeddings support true compositional generalization, enabling zero-shot or few-shot recognition of novel activity combinations that were never seen during pretraining?

## Limitations
*   **Architectural/training details missing:** CNN encoder layers, Transformer heads, dropout, learning schedule, and checkpoint criteria are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
*   **Tokenization hyperparameters not justified:** Thresholds (50 ms, 0.01 g) and activity-index filtering (AI < 50) appear tuned to NHANES; transfer to other sampling rates or noise levels is unclear.
*   **Biological premise unverified:** The assumption that acceleration zero‑crossings reliably mark movement boundaries is plausible but untested on low‑amplitude or highly oscillatory motions.

## Confidence
*   **Movement-aligned tokenization is critical for performance:** **High** (ablation: uniform chunks reduce Macro-F1 by 0.18).
*   **Time-aware positional encoding is essential:** **High** (ablation removes 3–8 Macro-F1 points, largest drop 8.2 on HAD).
*   **Bio-PM outperforms prior SSL methods:** **High** (reported avg Macro-F1 0.65 vs. TF-C 0.59, AugPred 0.44, equal-chunk masking 0.47).
*   **Masked segment reconstruction captures long-range dependencies:** **Medium** (mechanism sound but no direct dependency probing).
*   **Superior label efficiency and generalization:** **Medium** (claimed but quantitative evidence not provided in excerpts).

## Next Checks
1.  **Boundary alignment test:** On a video-annotated subset (e.g., PAMAP2 with synchronized video), compute precision/recall between detected zero-crossings and ground-truth activity transitions. Misalignment >20% would undermine the tokenization premise.
2.  **Threshold sensitivity analysis:** Vary hysteresis thresholds (min interval: 30–100 ms; amplitude: 0.005–0.05 g) on a validation split and measure downstream Macro-F1. Identify sharp performance drops indicating over-reliance on specific values.
3.  **Independent reproducibility:** Implement the pipeline with reasonable defaults for unspecified architecture (e.g., 3-layer CNN, 8-head Transformer). Pretrain on a 10% NHANES sample and verify that relative gains over uniform-chunk masking and TF-C remain statistically significant (p < 0.05) on at least two downstream benchmarks.
