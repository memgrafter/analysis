# AgentIR: Reasoning-Aware Retrieval for Deep Research Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04384  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04384  
- **Reference count:** 30  
- **Primary result:** AgentIR-4B achieves 68% accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus with the Tongyi-DeepResearch agent, outperforming a conventional retriever twice its size (52%) and BM25 (37%), while also improving recall and reducing search calls.

## Executive Summary
Deep research agents generate explicit reasoning traces before each search action, but conventional retrievers ignore this contextual information and rely solely on the short sub-query. This paper proposes Reasoning-Aware Retrieval, which jointly embeds the agent’s current reasoning trace and sub-query into a single vector to better capture the agent’s evolving intent. To train such a retriever, the authors introduce DR-Synth, a pipeline that synthesizes contrastive training data by rolling out an agent on QA datasets and using an oracle reranker to label relevant and irrelevant documents. Fine-tuning Qwen3-Embedding-4B on this data yields AgentIR-4B, which delivers significant gains in accuracy, recall, and efficiency on the BrowseComp-Plus benchmark and generalizes to other agents.

## Method Summary
The DR-Synth pipeline generates training data for reasoning-aware retrieval in three steps: (1) rollout a base deep research agent on QA datasets using a conventional retriever, capturing multi-turn trajectories with reasoning traces; (2) for each search turn, employ an oracle reranker (LLM-based) to identify a positive document and hard negatives based on the gold answer; (3) construct training instances pairing the joint input (reasoning trace + sub-query) with the labeled documents. A base embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-4B) is then fine-tuned via contrastive loss on these triples. The resulting retriever, AgentIR-4B, is deployed in place of the conventional retriever within the target agent’s loop, enabling context-aware search without modifying the agent’s internal reasoning process.

## Key Results
- AgentIR-4B achieves **68% accuracy** on BrowseComp-Plus with Tongyi-DeepResearch, compared to 52% with a conventional embedding model twice its size and 37% with BM25.
- It attains **78.86% recall** and reduces average search calls from **30.43 to 25.91**.
- Gains hold across different agents (gpt-oss-120B, GLM-4.7); both joint embedding and DR-Synth training contribute independently to performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Jointly embedding the agent's reasoning trace with the query reduces ambiguity by explicitly grounding the search in the agent's evolving intent.
- **Mechanism:** The reasoning trace acts as an implicit, agent-generated instruction that contextualizes the short, often ambiguous query. It provides disambiguating signals about the global task, unresolved gaps, and the agent's current hypothesis.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's reasoning trace contains accurate and relevant information reflecting the true search intent at that turn.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Deep Research agents generate explicit natural language reasoning before each search, revealing rich intent and contextual information that existing retrievers entirely ignore."  
  - [section 3.2] The "backroom studio" example clarifies intended meaning ("backroom of a studio" vs. studio named "Backroom") and connects it to the global objective (composer, Grammy, subgenre).  
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; related work focuses on benchmarks or other retrieval paradigms.
- **Break condition:** If the reasoning trace is irrelevant, misleading, or contains significant hallucinations, joint embedding degrades retrieval quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The current reasoning trace implicitly curates the interaction history, summarizing confirmed findings and filtering out outdated/incorrect hypotheses, providing a cleaner signal than the raw history.
- **Mechanism:** Because the agent's reasoning at turn *t* is conditioned on the full history *H*<sub>t-1</sub>, it consolidates relevant prior information and discards speculative lines. Embedding only this curated summary avoids noise from full-history embeddings, which can compound errors.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's internal reasoning correctly prioritizes and synthesizes information from the history.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 5.3] Analysis shows current reasoning covers >40% of all past clues (redundancy) and introduces fewer incorrect claims than longer histories; embedding full histories including documents harms performance.  
  - [section 5.3] Figure 4(a) shows reasoning after identifying "Otto Knows" reiterates confirmed facts (Sweden, Grammy) and does not revisit incorrect candidates (Finland, Jesper Kyd).  
  - [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers (e.g., SAGE) investigate retrieval for deep research agents but do not establish this specific curation mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If reasoning fails to summarize key prior findings or persists with incorrect beliefs, the curated signal becomes incomplete or corrupted.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The reasoning trace leverages the agent's parametric knowledge to generate specific, context-grounded hypotheses about promising search targets, more effective than generic query expansion.
- **Mechanism:** The trace contains the agent's inferred intermediate hops (e.g., "subgenre with euphoric finale is progressive house"). This grounds hypothetical document generation in the agent's interaction history, not just static knowledge, making it more targeted than methods like HyDE.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's parametric knowledge is accurate and its hypotheses are logically derived from the context.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.2] "The agent hypothesizes that a subgenre with a 'euphoric finale' is likely 'progressive house,' which turns out to be correct."  
  - [section 4.4] Reason-Rewriter (HyDE-style) hallucinates ("Backroom Studio" as a proper noun, "Los Angeles-based") and performs worse, demonstrating the value of history-grounded hypotheses.  
  - [corpus] Missing; no direct corpus evidence for this comparative mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the agent's parametric knowledge is outdated or reasoning jumps to incorrect conclusions, hypotheses become misleading noise.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Agent (trajectory generation) -> DR-Synth Pipeline (oracle reranking) -> Training Loop (contrastive fine-tuning) -> AgentIR-4B Retriever -> Target Agent System

- **Critical path:**  
  1. **Data Generation:** Run agent rollouts on QA datasets using a base retriever.  
  2. **Model Training:** Fine-tune an embedding model on DR-Synth-generated triples.  
  3. **Deployment:** Integrate AgentIR-4B into the target agent’s retrieval step.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Input Scope:** Using only current reasoning reduces noise vs. full history, which risks error propagation.  
  - **Training Data Source:** DR-Synth uses successful rollouts for quality but limits dataset diversity and scale.  
  - **Oracle Reranker Cost:** LLM-based labeling creates high-quality supervision but is expensive; noisier alternatives are cheaper.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low recall/accuracy: Reasoning traces missing, truncated, or low quality.  
  - Excessive search calls: Vague reasoning traces lacking specific, actionable clues.  
  - Poor cross-agent generalization: Overfitting to rollout agent’s reasoning style and source domain.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Component Ablation:** Evaluate "w/o Training" (frozen backbone, joint embedding) and "w/o Reasoning" (trained on query-only) to isolate contributions.  
  2. **Signal Ablation:** Train separate retrievers for alternative *f(H*<sub>t</sub>*) (e.g., Global Question, full history) to identify optimal input.  
  3. **Generalization Test:** Deploy AgentIR-4B with a different agent (e.g., GLM-4.7) without retraining to check zero-shot transfer.

## Limitations
- **Dependency on agent quality:** Relies on accurate, relevant reasoning traces; likely degrades with weaker or hallucination-prone agents.
- **Training data bias:** DR-Synth data from a single successful agent may overfit to its reasoning style and the source domain’s question distribution.
- **Reasoning trace integrity:** Traces sometimes contain incorrect claims; threshold where noise outweighs contextual benefit is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Reasoning-Aware Retrieval efficacy:** High (strong empirical gains across multiple agents and ablations).
- **DR-Synth data quality & scalability:** Medium (oracle labeling is costly and rollout-dependent, limiting scale and diversity).
- **Cross-agent generalization:** Medium (zero-shot transfer to gpt-oss-120B and GLM-4.7 worked, but broader testing needed).

## Next Checks
1. **Test with lower-quality reasoning agents:** Deploy AgentIR-4B with agents having shorter/less-reliable traces (e.g., smaller models). Measure accuracy drop vs. baselines to quantify the break condition for trace utility.
2. **Human audit of reasoning-document alignment:** Sample retrieval instances where AgentIR-4B succeeded/failed. Annotate whether the reasoning trace accurately reflected the true intent for the gold document. Correlate scores with retrieval success.
3. **Diversify DR-Synth rollout agent:** Re-train AgentIR-4B using data from multiple architecturally diverse agents (e.g., ReAct-based, Plan-and-Execute). Evaluate on a held-out novel agent; significant performance loss would confirm bias toward rollout agent’s pattern.
