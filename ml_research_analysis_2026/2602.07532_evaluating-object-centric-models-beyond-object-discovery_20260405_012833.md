# Evaluating Object-Centric Models beyond Object Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07532
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07532
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Object-centric models evaluated via VLM probing and AwGA show competitiveness with self-supervised models despite token efficiency, and object discovery metrics poorly predict VQA performance.

## Executive Summary
Object-centric learning (OCL) aims to decompose scenes into object-level representations, but current evaluation relies heavily on object discovery metrics and linear probes, which provide limited insight into representation usefulness for downstream tasks. This paper introduces two key advancements: (1) using instruction-tuned vision-language models (VLMs) as zero‑shot probes to assess how well OCL representations support complex reasoning across multiple VQA benchmarks, and (2) proposing attribution‑aware grounded accuracy (AwGA), a unified metric that jointly evaluates localization and representation by attributing answers to specific slots and measuring mask overlap. Experiments demonstrate that OCL models, even with as few as 7 tokens, achieve VQA performance comparable to dense self‑supervised models like DINOv2 (which uses 196 tokens). Surprisingly, standard object discovery metrics (mIoU, mBO) show poor correlation with VQA results, underscoring their inadequacy. AwGA reveals fragmentation—cases where models either localize poorly or produce correct answers from the wrong slots—even among models with high accuracy or strong object discovery. Additionally, a multi‑feature reconstruction baseline (mFRESA), which reconstructs pixels, DINOv2 features, and HOG, consistently outperforms image‑only reconstruction on both VQA and AwGA. The proposed framework and metric prove robust across different LLMs and connectors.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework integrates an object‑centric encoder (e.g., slot attention) with a vision‑language model. Given an image, the OCL encoder outputs a fixed set of slot vectors and their associated masks. A two‑layer MLP connector, pre‑trained on the LLaVA dataset, maps each slot vector into the token embedding space of an instruction‑tuned language model (e.g., Phi‑2, Qwen2). The LLM then autoregressively generates answers to questions in a zero‑shot setting, conditioned on the text prompt and the visual tokens derived from the slots. To compute attribution‑aware grounded accuracy (AwGA), gradient‑based attributions identify the slots most responsible for the predicted answer. The masks of the top‑K selected slots (where K equals the number of ground‑truth objects provided by the eGQA dataset) are combined, and their intersection‑over‑union with the ground‑truth masks is measured; this value is multiplied by an indicator of answer correctness and averaged over the evaluation set.

## Key Results
- OCL models achieve VQA scores comparable to DINOv2 while using only 7 tokens instead of 196, highlighting the efficiency of slot‑based representations.
- Conventional object discovery metrics (mIoU, mBO) exhibit weak correlation with VQA performance, demonstrating their insufficiency as proxies for representation quality.
- AwGA uncovers localization and representation fragmentation missed by accuracy‑only or mIoU‑only evaluations; feature‑reconstruction models (e.g., mFRESA) surpass image‑reconstruction models, and mFRESA further boosts both VQA and AwGA, with results consistent across LLMs and connectors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: VLM-based Zero-Shot Probing
- **Claim:** Replacing task‑specific linear probes with an instruction‑tuned VLM enables scalable, zero‑shot assessment of OCL representations across diverse VQA benchmarks, measuring their utility for complex reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** The OCL encoder produces slot vectors. A connector MLP (trained once on LLaVA data) projects slots into the LLM’s token space. The instruction‑tuned LLM processes these visual tokens alongside text questions to generate answers. Performance on multiple VQA datasets reflects how well the slots capture semantics useful for reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** The VLM’s reasoning capability is sufficiently strong and fixed; differences in VQA scores primarily reflect the quality of the slot representations, not the VLM itself.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.2; neighboring papers do not propose VLM‑based probing for OCL.
- **Break condition:** If the connector fails to align slot embeddings with the LLM space or the LLM’s instruction tuning is inadequate, VQA scores may not reflect OCL quality.

### Mechanism 2: Attribution‑Aware Grounded Accuracy (AwGA)
- **Claim:** AwGA jointly evaluates localization and representation usefulness by attributing answers to specific slots and measuring mask overlap, thereby penalizing both localization and representation fragmentation.
- **Mechanism:** For each VQA sample, compute gradient‑based attribution of each slot with respect to the predicted answer. Select top‑K slots (K = number of ground‑truth objects from eGQA). Compute mIoU between the union of these slots’ predicted masks and the ground‑truth masks.
- **Core assumption:** Gradient attributions reliably identify slots responsible for the answer, and eGQA masks accurately denote objects relevant to the question.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.3, Table 5; novelty in OCL evaluation.
- **Break condition:** If attribution scores are uniform across slots or K is misestimated, AwGA may yield noisy scores.

### Mechanism 3: Multi‑Feature Reconstruction (mFRESA)
- **Claim:** Reconstructing multiple features (image pixels, DINOv2 features, HOG) yields more robust object‑centric representations, improving both VQA performance and AwGA scores compared to single‑target reconstruction.
- **Mechanism:** Extend a slot‑attention model with two additional decoders: one for DINOv2 features and one for HOG features. Train with combined L2 loss on all three targets. DINOv2 decoder provides semantic features; HOG decoder emphasizes edge and boundary information, leading to slots that better capture object shape and semantics.
- **Core assumption:** The three reconstruction targets provide complementary supervisory signals that together produce more accurate and disentangled slot representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Appendix, Table 2 (VQA gains), Table 7 (ablation); combining HOG with feature reconstruction is not standard.
- **Break condition:** Conflicting reconstruction targets or optimization difficulties could reduce gains; increased computational cost may not be justified by minimal improvements.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** OCL encoder (backbone + slot attention) → slot vectors and masks → Connector (2‑layer GeLU MLP) → LLM token embeddings → LLM (instruction‑tuned) → answer generation.

**Critical path:**
1. Image → OCL encoder → slots S and per‑slot masks.
2. Each slot sᵢ → connector → token embedding tᵢ.
3. Text question → LLM tokenizer → text embeddings.
4. Concatenate text embeddings and {t₁,…,tₖ} as input sequence to LLM.
5. LLM autoregressively generates answer tokens.
6. For AwGA: after answer generation, compute loss on predicted answer; compute gradients ∂loss/∂tᵢ for each slot token; select top‑K slots (K = number of GT objects); union their masks; compute mIoU with GT mask.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- LLM choice: Larger LLMs (Qwen2‑7B) yield higher scores but require more memory; smaller (Phi‑2) is faster. Paper shows robustness across both.
- Connector depth: 2‑layer MLP vs. 1‑layer; deeper may improve alignment but risks overfitting.
- Number of slots: 7 fixed for COCO; more slots capture more objects but increase sequence length and may dilute semantics.
- Reconstruction targets: Image‑only vs. feature‑only vs. multi‑feature (mFRESA). Multi‑feature improves but adds training time and decoder complexity.
- Attribution method: Gradient (fast) vs. integrated gradients (more accurate, slower). Paper finds gradient sufficient.

**Failure signatures:**
- VQA scores near Blind‑VLM baseline: indicates OCL slots carry little semantic information.
- High accuracy but low AwGA: representation fragmentation—answers correct but from wrong slots.
- Low mIoU but high accuracy: localization fragmentation—slots localize poorly but semantics are present in aggregated representation.
- Inconsistent rankings across LLMs: suggests connector or slot representation instability.
- Training instability: loss diverging during Stage II instruction tuning may indicate connector misalignment.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Zero‑shot VQA: Train VLM (Stage I + II) with OCL encoder, evaluate on GQA/VQAv2/MME/MM‑Vet. Verify that OCL models outperform Blind‑VLM and approach DINOv2.
2. AwGA evaluation: On eGQA, compute AwGA for each model. Compare rankings to VQA and object discovery metrics to reveal fragmentation.
3. Ablation on reconstruction targets: For mFRESA, train variants without HOG decoder or without feature decoder, measure impact on VQA and AwGA to validate contribution of each target.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can VLM‑based OCL evaluation be made less computationally expensive and less prone to LLM biases?
2. Can AwGA be generalized to standard VQA datasets without human‑annotated segmentation masks?
3. Why do OCL models significantly underperform on compositional and natural adversarial VQA benchmarks compared to self‑supervised models like DINOv2?
4. What specific components of multi‑feature reconstruction (image pixels, DINOv2 features, HOG) contribute most to gains in VQA accuracy and AwGA?

## Limitations
- VLM‑based probing assumes a fixed, strong LLM; connector misalignment or VLM biases could confound OCL model comparisons.
- AwGA requires grounding masks and relies on gradient attributions, which can be noisy; its applicability is limited to datasets with masks (e.g., eGQA).
- Correlation between object discovery metrics and VQA is based on a limited set of OCL models; may not generalize broadly.
- mFRESA’s improvements are demonstrated on COCO‑style data; generalization to synthetic or domain‑shifted scenes remains unclear.
- Evaluation uses a fixed number of slots (7); models with dynamic slot numbers were not tested.

## Confidence
- VLM probing framework: Medium
- AwGA metric: Medium
- mFRESA superiority: High
