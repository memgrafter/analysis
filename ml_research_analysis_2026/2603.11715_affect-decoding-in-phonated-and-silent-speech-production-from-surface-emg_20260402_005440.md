# Affect Decoding in Phonated and Silent Speech Production from Surface EMG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11715  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11715  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** sEMG can reliably decode frustration from speech motor signals with intra-subject AUC up to 0.845 and generalizes to silent speech.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether affective states (frustration vs. non-frustration) are encoded in the motor execution of speech, as measured by facial and neck surface electromyography (sEMG) during both phonated and silent speech. Using a dataset of 2,780 utterances from 12 participants across controlled reading and spontaneous conversational tasks, the authors extracted handcrafted features and deep embeddings (BioCodec) from sEMG, comparing performance against acoustic features. For intra-subject decoding, sEMG models reliably discriminated frustration with a peak AUC of 0.845, significantly outperforming acoustic features. EMG-based decoding generalized well from phonated to silent speech within the same speaker. In inter-subject and spontaneous speech settings, performance declined but remained above chance for sEMG, while speech embeddings (Vox-Profile) achieved the highest cross-speaker spontaneous performance (AUC 0.743). Ablation analysis identified key discriminative channels in facial regions (e.g., depressor supercilii), confirming that affective motor signatures persist in the absence of phonation.

## Method Summary
The study leveraged a dataset of 2,780 utterances collected from 12 participants. Participants performed controlled reading tasks (phonated speech) and spontaneous conversational tasks, including silent speech where no vocalization occurred. Surface EMG electrodes were placed on facial and neck muscles to capture muscle activation patterns during speech production. From these signals, two types of features were extracted: handcrafted features (e.g., time-domain and frequency-domain statistics) and deep embeddings using a pretrained model called BioCodec. Additionally, acoustic features were extracted from the audio recordings for comparison. The feature vectors were used to train classifiers to discriminate between frustration and non-frustration states. Evaluation was conducted under three conditions: intra-subject (same speaker), inter-subject (different speakers), and spontaneous speech, with separate analyses for phonated and silent speech.

## Key Results
- Intra-subject frustration decoding using sEMG achieved a peak AUC of 0.845, significantly outperforming acoustic features.
- sEMG models generalized well from phonated to silent speech within the same speaker, maintaining high performance.
- In inter-subject and spontaneous speech settings, sEMG performance remained above chance but declined; Vox-Profile (speech embeddings) achieved the highest cross-speaker spontaneous performance (AUC 0.743). Ablation identified depressor supercilii and other facial channels as key discriminative.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Affective states influence motor commands, leading to distinctive muscle activation patterns that are captured by sEMG. Facial muscles involved in speech articulation also convey emotional expressions; even without sound (silent speech), the motor plan includes affective modulation, which remains detectable. The success of deep embeddings (BioCodec) suggests that sEMG signals contain complex temporal structures relevant to affect. The identification of key facial channels (e.g., depressor supercilii, associated with frowning) indicates that affective motor signatures are present in muscles that, while not directly involved in phonation, reflect emotional expression.

   - *Why needed*: To compare interpretable, domain-specific features with automatic representations that may capture complex patterns.

   - *Why needed*: Evaluates robustness of affect decoding models to individual variability, crucial for real-world deployment.  
   - *Quick check*: Does performance drop when training on one speaker and testing on another? (Yes, as reported)
4. **Frustration as an Affective State**  
   - *Why needed*: Frustration is a common emotion in human-computer interaction; decoding it can enable adaptive systems.

   - *Why needed*: Explores whether affective information is preserved without acoustic output, relevant for assistive technologies.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: sEMG Sensors -> Preprocessing -> Feature Extraction (Handcrafted/BioCodec) -> Classifier -> Predicted Affect  
- **Critical path**: Raw sEMG signal must be correctly preprocessed, features extracted, and classifier trained to enable reliable affect prediction; each step is essential.  
- **Design tradeoffs**: Handcrafted features are lightweight and interpretable but may miss complex patterns; deep embeddings capture richer representations but require more data and computational resources. Acoustic features provide a strong baseline but are unusable in silent scenarios. The choice of classifier also balances complexity and performance.  
- **Failure signatures**: Poor cross-speaker generalization indicates high speaker-dependent variance; performance drop in spontaneous speech suggests sensitivity to linguistic/ecological variability; overfitting in deep models with small datasets; removal of key facial channels (e.g., depressor supercilii) causing significant performance degradation.  
- **First experiments**:  
  1. Intra-subject decoding: Train and test on the same speaker to establish the performance ceiling.  
  2. Phonated-to-silent generalization within speaker: Train on phonated speech, test on silent speech to assess modality invariance.  
  3. Inter-subject decoding: Train on multiple speakers, test on a held-out speaker to evaluate generalization across individuals.

## Limitations
- Small inter-subject sample (n=12) limits generalizability of cross-speaker findings.  
- Spontaneous speech performance drop for sEMG suggests sensitivity to linguistic/ecological variability not fully captured.  
- Unclear relative contribution of handcrafted vs. deep (BioCodec) features; ablation on features not provided.

## Confidence
- Intra-subject frustration decoding (AUC 0.845): High  
- Phonated-to-silent generalization within speaker: High  
- Inter-subject/spontaneous sEMG decoding: Medium  
- Cross-speaker spontaneous decoding (Vox-Profile AUC 0.743): High  
- Identified key facial channels (e.g., depressor supercilii): Medium  

## Next Checks
- Recruit ≥50 speakers for inter-subject testing to quantify sEMG variability and identify speaker-independent signatures.  
- Run a controlled comparison of sEMG vs. EEG on identical frustration-spontaneous speech tasks to isolate muscle-specific affective signals from neural correlates.  
- Test sEMG decoding in real-world noisy environments (e.g., crowds, movement artifacts) to assess robustness beyond lab-controlled recordings.
