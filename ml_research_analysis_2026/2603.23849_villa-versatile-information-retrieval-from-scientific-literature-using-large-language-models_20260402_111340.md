# VILLA: Versatile Information Retrieval From Scientific Literature Using Large LAnguage Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.23849  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23849  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: VILLA achieves a mean F1-score of 0.53 ± 0.13 on extracting influenza A mutations that modify host interaction, significantly outperforming zero-shot prompting, single-stage RAG, and existing scientific QA tools.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the scarcity of high-quality benchmarks for complex, open-ended scientific information extraction (SIE) in virology. It introduces a novel task: extracting viral mutations that alter host interactions from literature. To address this, the authors develop VILLA, a multi-level retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework that first filters relevant publications via abstract embeddings, then retrieves detailed passages from full texts. Evaluated on a curated dataset of 239 influenza A papers and 629 mutations, VILLA substantially improves extraction performance (F1=0.53) over strong baselines, demonstrating the value of hierarchical retrieval for distributed scientific knowledge.

## Method Summary
The task involves extracting a list of mutations (e.g., "T156A") in influenza A virus proteins that modify host interaction, given a query like " mutations in PB1 that change host range." The authors curate a dataset from 239 publications, each with abstract and full text, and ground-truth mutation lists per protein. VILLA operates in two stages: (1) retrieve top *k*<sub>a</sub> abstracts using an embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding:8B); (2) for each retrieved abstract, retrieve top *k*<sub>c</sub> full-text chunks (1000 tokens, 100-token overlap) using the same embedder. The concatenated chunks for each publication are fed to a responder LLM (Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct), which outputs a JSON object containing a mutation list and reasoning. The final result is the union of all publication-level lists. The best configuration uses *k*<sub>a</sub>=160, *k*<sub>c</sub>=160, and no model fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- VILLA (F1=0.53 ± 0.13) outperforms zero-shot prompting (max F1 0.10), RAG with abstracts only (0.27), RAG with full-text only (0.41), and SOTA tools like OpenScholar (0.02), PaperQA2 (0.07), and HiPerRAG (0.10).
- Two-stage retrieval boosts recall: VILLA recall (0.51 ± 0.14) exceeds full-text RAG recall (0.42).
- Qwen3-Embedding:8B yields the best context F1 (0.29 ± 0.21) among embedders, surpassing PubMedBERT (0.23) and Llama-based models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1  
**Claim**: A two-stage retrieval process (abstracts then full-text) significantly improves recall of relevant mutations compared to single-stage retrieval.  
**Mechanism**: Abstract embeddings first identify a broad set of candidate publications (high recall). Full-text chunk retrieval from those publications then extracts detailed mutation information often absent in abstracts.  
**Core assumption**: Abstracts are effective filters for identifying relevant full-text documents despite insufficient detail alone.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [abstract] "VILLA, a multi-level retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework that first identifies relevant publications via abstract embeddings, then retrieves detailed chunks from those full texts."  
- [section 4.2] RAG with abstracts achieved F1=0.27±0.08, RAG with full-text achieved F1=0.41±0.08; VILLA combined them to reach F1=0.53±0.13.  
- [corpus] Neighboring tools (PaperQA, HiPerRAG) validate the research space but do not directly anchor this mechanism; evidence is from the paper's own experiments.

### Mechanism 2  
**Claim**: Using a domain-adapted embedder (Qwen3-Embedding:8B) is critical for retrieving scientifically relevant context.  
**Mechanism**: Embeddings from general or biomedical-pretrained models (e.g., Llama, PubMedBERT) underperform on domain-specific virology text; Qwen3-Embedding, though not explicitly virology-pretrained, better captures semantic similarity in this domain.  
**Core assumption**: The embedding space must align with domain terminology and conceptual relationships to retrieve correct passages.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [section 4.2.2] Qwen3-Embedding:8B achieved best context F1-score (0.29±0.21) among embedders in abstract retrieval, outperforming PubMedBERT (0.23) and generalist models.  
- [section 3.2] Notes that "general-purpose encoding LLMs underperform in tasks involving scientific text" and justifies evaluating domain-specific embedders.  
- [corpus] Corpus neighbors (e.g., SciNetBench) highlight need for relation-aware retrieval, supporting that generic embeddings are insufficient.

### Mechanism 3  
**Claim**: Aggregating responses from multiple retrieved publications (via iterative chunk queries) increases mutation recall, despite reducing conciseness.  
**Mechanism**: VILLA retrieves *k*<sub>a</sub> abstracts and *k*<sub>c</sub> chunks per abstract. Querying the responder for each publication's context yields a union of mutations, boosting recall. The trade-off is redundant or verbose reasoning.  
**Core assumption**: Relevant mutation information is distributed non-redundantly across many papers; a single context cannot capture all.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- [section 4.4.1] VILLA's recall (0.51±0.14) surpassed RAG full-text recall (max 0.42); Figure 6 shows VILLA identifies more mutations across proteins.  
- [section 4.4.2] Increasing *k*<sub>a</sub> from 5 to 160 steadily increased recall (0.09 to 0.50) while precision peaked at *k*<sub>a</sub>=20 then declined.  
- [section 6] Qualitative evaluation notes VILLA's lower "Conciseness" score due to concatenated outputs, linking the mechanism to the observed trade-off.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Query → Embedder (E) → Abstract Retriever (from DA) → For each abstract: Chunk Retriever (from DF) → Context concatenation → Responder (G) per publication → Union of responses → Output.  
**Critical path**: User query → E encodes query → R retrieves *k*<sub>a</sub> abstracts from DA → for each abstract, R retrieves *k*<sub>c</sub> chunks from DF → context chunks concatenated with query → G processes each (publication) → union of responses.  
**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *k*<sub>a</sub> (abstracts): Larger *k*<sub>a</sub> improves recall but adds noise, lowering precision. Optimal *k*<sub>a</sub>=20 for precision, *k*<sub>a</sub>=160 for recall.  
- *k*<sub>c</sub> (chunks per paper): Performance insensitive to *k*<sub>c</sub> (F1 stable from 5 to 160), suggesting mutation info is concentrated in few dense chunks.  
- Embedder choice: Domain-adapted embedders (Qwen3-Embedding:8B) outperformed generalist and biomedical-specific models, indicating scale/training data matters more than explicit biomedical pretraining.  
**Failure signatures**:  
- **Low precision**: Retrieved chunks contain many false positive mutations. Check: Are retrieved publications truly relevant? Inspect cosine distance distributions.  
- **Low recall**: Missing mutations from ground truth. Check: Is *k*<sub>a</sub> too small? Is chunk size (1000 tokens) too large, burying details? Is embedder's space poorly aligned?  
- **High hallucination**: LLM generates mutations not in context. Paper finds this rare; precision w.r.t. context ≈ overall precision, indicating LLM mostly extracts only from provided text.  
**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Baseline single-stage RAG**: Implement RAG with abstracts only and RAG with full-text only. Measure mutation F1. Expect: Full-text > Abstracts (0.41 vs 0.27), but both < VILLA.  
2. **Embedder ablation**: Swap Qwen3-Embedding:8B with Llama3.1-8B and PubMedBERT as embedder in VILLA. Measure context F1. Expect: Qwen3-Embedding:8B significantly higher (0.29 vs ~0.20).  
3. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Vary *k*<sub>a</sub> ∈ {5,10,20,40,80,160} with *k*<sub>c</sub>=160. Plot precision/recall/F1. Expect: Recall increases monotonically with *k*<sub>a</sub>; precision peaks around *k*<sub>a</sub>=20 then drops.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can VILLA’s retrieval be improved to better discriminate between abstracts relevant and non-relevant to a given viral protein?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] Section 4.2.2 and Appendix Figure 24 show significant overlap in cosine distance distributions for eight of ten proteins, and retrieval precision remains low (0.16–0.22) despite using the best embedder.  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper identifies this overlap as a key limitation but does not explore alternative embedding models, fine-tuning on virology text, or hybrid retrieval methods.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Evaluate domain-adapted embedders (e.g., contrastive learning on virology corpora) or lexical-semantic hybrid retrievers on held-out protein–publication relevance judgments, measuring precision–recall trade-offs and downstream extraction F1.

- **Open Question 2**: What strategies can reduce the number of LLM queries in VILLA’s two-stage pipeline (currently proportional to *k*<sub>a</sub>) without compromising extraction quality?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion poses this as a future direction; hyperparameter tuning (Section 4.4.2) shows F1 increases with *k*<sub>a</sub> up to 160, implying more queries yield better recall but at high computational cost.  
  *Why unresolved*: The concatenation of outputs from all retrieved abstracts is inherent to the design; no mechanism exists to prioritize or subsample abstracts that contribute unique information.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Implement an abstract re-ranking or clustering step after level‑1 retrieval (e.g., via cross‑encoder scoring or diversity maximization), then measure extraction F1 while systematically reducing the number of queries.

- **Open Question 3**: Does VILLA generalize to viral families beyond influenza A, and what domain adaptations are necessary?  
  *Basis in paper*: [inferred] The study uses only influenza A; Section 5 notes that literature for other zoonotic viruses is “more dispersed and siloed,” but generalization is not tested.  
  *Why unresolved*: Influenza A has a dense, well‑curated literature; other viruses may exhibit different publication patterns, mutation nomenclature, and textual descriptions, impacting retrieval and extraction.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Curate ground‑truth mutation datasets for 2–3 additional high‑priority viruses (e.g., SARS‑CoV‑2, Ebola) following the same task, run VILLA with identical configurations, and compare performance to identify domain‑specific failure modes and adaptation needs.

- **Open Question 4**: Why does high quantitative recall in mutation extraction not translate to superior perceived biological relevance in expert qualitative evaluation, and how can evaluation be aligned with expert expectations?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] Section 6 and Figure 7 show VILLA achieves highest F1 (0.53) but scores on par with baselines on ‘Contribution’ and ‘Correctness’; the authors hypothesize that experts cannot assess recall without knowing full ground truth and conciseness penalties create a bias.  
  *Why unresolved*: The current five‑point rubric may not reflect real‑world utility for hypothesis generation; a mismatch between quantitative metrics and expert intuition could misguide development.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Conduct a user study with virologists who rate outputs for research usefulness across controlled recall levels; use insights to design a more robust metric that balances completeness, correctness, and conciseness for open‑ended SIE tasks.

## Limitations
- The curated dataset of 239 papers and 629 mutation ground truths is not publicly released, hindering independent verification and community progress.
- Minor but critical details are unspecified: exact `marker` version, precise tokenization/chunking implementation, and method for enforcing JSON output, which could cause performance variance.
- Evaluation is restricted to influenza A virology; generalization to other scientific domains with different literature structures or terminology is untested.
