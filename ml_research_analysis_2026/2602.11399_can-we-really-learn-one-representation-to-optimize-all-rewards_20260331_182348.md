# Can We Really Learn One Representation to Optimize All Rewards?

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.11399
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11399
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Proposes one-step forward-backward RL, which resolves fundamental convergence issues in prior FB representation learning and achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across diverse control tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper critically examines the forward-backward (FB) representation learning framework, which aims to learn a single unsupervised representation enabling optimal control for any reward without further training. Through theoretical analysis, the authors reveal that the core FB objective suffers from an inherent circular dependency between the learned policy and the successor measure it is trying to fit. This invalidates standard convergence guarantees, as the FB Bellman operator is proven not to be a contraction. To overcome this, the authors introduce one-step FB, a simplified method that learns representations for a fixed behavioral policy and performs only one step of policy improvement at test time. Empirically, one-step FB exhibits stable convergence and superior zero-shot performance compared to both the original FB method and other unsupervised RL baselines.

## Method Summary
One-step FB pre-trains three components offline: a forward network \(F_\beta(s,a)\), a backward network \(B_\beta(s_f,a_f)\), and a latent-conditioned policy \(\pi(a|s,z)\). The forward and backward networks are trained to estimate the successor measure ratio \(M^{\pi_\beta}/\rho\) of a fixed behavioral policy \(\pi_\beta\) via a temporal difference (TD) loss, with an orthonormalization regularization on \(B_\beta\). The policy is trained separately using these representations. For a new reward function \(r\) at test time, a task latent \(z_r\) is computed as the expectation of \(B_\beta\) weighted by \(r\) under the future marginal \(\rho\). The pre-trained policy \(\pi(a|s,z_r)\) is then evaluated zero-shot, approximating the optimal policy for \(r\) after one step of policy improvement over \(Q^{\pi_\beta}\).

## Key Results
*   Theoretical analysis proves the original FB Bellman operator is not a contraction, explaining its practical convergence failure.
*   In didactic MDPs, FB failed to converge with high errors, while one-step FB achieved errors over 100,000 times smaller.
*   Across 10 state-based and image-based control benchmarks, one-step FB improved average zero-shot performance by 24% over FB and matched or exceeded prior unsupervised RL methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The original FB representation objective contains a circular dependency that prevents convergence.
*   **Mechanism:** FB attempts to jointly learn a forward representation \(F(s,a,z)\) and a backward representation \(B(s_f,a_f)\) such that their inner product equals the successor measure ratio \(M^\pi/\rho\). Critically, the forward representation also defines a latent-conditioned policy \(\pi(a|s,z)\). This creates a loop: the policy \(\pi\) determines the successor measure \(M^\pi\) that the representations are trying to fit, while the representations define the policy. This circularity breaks standard contraction-based fixed-point analysis.
*   **Evidence:** Proposition 3 formally proves the FB Bellman operator \(T_{FB}\) is not a \(\gamma\)-contraction under any \(L_p\)-norm. Didactic experiments show FB fails to reduce the Q-value equivariance error \(\epsilon_{\text{equiv}}\) to near zero.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** One-step FB breaks the circular dependency by decoupling representation learning from the latent-conditioned policy.
*   **Mechanism:** It learns \(F_\beta\) and \(B_\beta\) to fit the ratio \(M^{\pi_\beta}/\rho\) for a *fixed* behavioral policy \(\pi_\beta\) via a TD loss. The policy \(\pi(a|s,z)\) is trained separately using \(F_\beta\). At test time, it performs a single step of policy improvement: compute \(z_r = \mathbb{E}_{\rho}[B_\beta r]\) and execute \(\pi(a|s,z_r)\). The successor measure target is now static, eliminating the circularity.
*   **Evidence:** The one-step FB loss (Eq. 10) uses a fixed \(\pi_\beta\). The paper links this to a one-step policy improvement guarantee (Appendix C.7). Empirically, one-step FB converges to low \(\epsilon_{\text{equiv}}\) and high zero-shot returns.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Orthonormal regularization on \(B_\beta\) stabilizes learning and connects to a low-rank SVD of the behavioral successor measure.
*   **Mechanism:** The ortho-loss \(L_{\text{ortho}}\) encourages columns of \(B_\beta\) to be orthonormal. With proper scaling, the solution \((F_\beta^\star, B_\beta^\star)\) that minimizes both the TD and ortho losses approximates the singular value decomposition (SVD) of \(M^{\pi_\beta}\text{diag}(\rho)^{-1}\). This yields a well-conditioned low-rank approximation even when the latent dimension \(d < |S \times A|\), mitigating the rank issues inherent to FB.
*   **Evidence:** Appendix C.6 derives this connection formally. Didactic experiments show low successor measure ratio prediction error \(\epsilon_{\text{SMR}}\) when ortho-loss is used. Ablations in Table 4 indicate performance sensitivity to \(\lambda_{\text{ortho}}\).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Successor Measure**
    *   Why needed: Central object FB and one-step FB aim to represent. It is a discounted distribution over future state-action pairs under a policy, \(M^\pi(s,a|s_0,a_0)\). Any reward-induced Q-value is its inner product with the reward function.
    *   Quick check: How is the successor measure matrix \(M^\pi\) defined recursively in terms of the transition matrix \(P^\pi\) and discount \(\gamma\)? (See Eq. 1)

*   **Concept: Matrix Rank & Pseudoinverse**
    *   Why needed: Proposition 1 shows exact FB representations require \(\text{rank}(F^\star_Z) \geq |S \times A|\), an impossibility for finite \(d\) in large problems. The pseudoinverse \(F^+\) links forward/backward representations to \(M^\pi\).
    *   Quick check: What does the condition \(d \geq |S \times A|\) imply about the feasibility of learning a generally useful FB representation in large or continuous MDPs?

*   **Concept: Contraction Mapping**
    *   Why needed: Standard RL operators (Bellman) are contractions, ensuring convergence of iterative methods. Proving \(T_{FB}\) is *not* a contraction explains FB's failure.
    *   Quick check: Why is the \(\gamma\)-contraction property sufficient (though not necessary) for an iterative algorithm like value iteration to converge to a unique fixed point?

*   **Concept: Orthonormalization & SVD**
    *   Why needed: The ortho-loss regularizer in one-step FB is motivated by connecting the learned representation pair to an SVD of the target matrix, providing a stable low-rank factorization.
    *   Quick check: In an SVD \(U\Sigma V^\top\), what property do the columns of \(U\) and \(V\) have that the ortho-loss explicitly enforces?

*   **Concept: One-Step Policy Improvement**
    *   Why needed: One-step FB's test-time procedure is derived from this classic RL principle: given a policy \(\pi_\beta\), the greedy policy w.r.t. \(Q^{\pi_\beta}\) is a one-step improvement. The paper shows \(z_r\) estimation and \(\pi(a|s,z_r)\) approximate this.
    *   Quick check: If you have an accurate estimate of \(Q^{\pi_\beta}(s,a)\) for all \(a\), how do you obtain the greedy policy in one step?

## Architecture Onboarding
F_θ(s,a) -> B_ω(s_f,a_f) -> π_η(a|s,z)
With: Target networks F_¯θ, B_¯ω; Losses: L_td(θ,ω) + λ_ortho L_ortho(ω) and L_pi(η); Latent mixing (prior + B_ω samples).

**Critical Path:**
1.  Initialize networks \(F_\theta\), \(B_\omega\), \(\pi_\eta\) and target networks.
2.  For each pre-training step: sample batch \((s,a,s',a')\) from offline dataset \(D\) and latents \(z\) (50% prior, 50% from \(B_\omega\)).
3.  Compute TD one-step FB loss (Eq. 10): predict \(F_\theta(s,a)^\top B_\omega(s_f,a_f)\); target uses indicator + bootstrapped \(F_¯θ(s',a')^\top B_¯ω(s_f,a_f)\).
4.  Compute orthonormalization loss \(L_{\text{ortho}}\) on \(B_\omega\) (Eq. 11).
5.  Update \(F_\theta, B_\omega\) via Adam on combined loss.
6.  Update policy \(\pi_\eta\): maximize \(F_\theta(s,a)^\top z\) with behavioral cloning regularization on \(\pi_\beta\) actions (Eq. 13).
7.  Update target networks \(F_¯θ, B_¯ω\) via Polyak averaging.
8.  After pre-training, for a downstream reward \(r\): sample zero-shot data, compute \(z_r = \text{mean}_\rho[B_\omega \cdot r]\), fix \(z_r\), evaluate \(\pi_\eta(\cdot|s,z_r)\).

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Representation dimension \(d\):** Larger \(d\) better approximates the full successor measure but increases compute and overfitting risk. Proposition 1 suggests \(d \geq |S \times A|\) for exactness, but practical low-rank approximations work with \(d=50\) (ExORL) or \(128\) (OGBench).
*   **Orthonormalization weight \(\lambda_{\text{ortho}}\):** Balances fitting the TD loss vs. conditioning \(B_\omega\). Too high ignores TD loss; too low causes instability (ill-conditioned \(B_\omega\)). Tuned per domain (Table 4).
*   **Latent prior \(p_Z\):** Mixing a scaled von Mises-Fisher prior with latents derived from \(B_\omega\) improves coverage of potential \(z_r\). Affects exploration during policy training.
*   **Behavioral cloning weight \(\lambda_{\text{BC}}\):** Regularizes policy to stay close to \(\pi_\beta\). Required for OGBench (\(\lambda_{\text{BC}}>0\)), harmful for ExORL (\(\lambda_{\text{BC}}=0\)).

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Didactic:** High equivariance error (\(\epsilon_{\text{equiv}} > 10^{-4}\)), high KL divergence to optimal policy, or successor measure ratio prediction error (\(\epsilon_{\text{SMR}}\)) not decreasing.
*   **Practical:** Zero-shot returns near random or significantly below baseline methods (FB, HILP). Policy collapse (near-deterministic actions) or divergence (exploding losses).
*   **Diagnostic:** \(B_\omega^\top B_\omega\) far from identity (high ortho loss). \(z_r\) values have near-zero variance. Policy loss dominated by BC term or not decreasing.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Three-state CMP convergence:** Train FB and one-step FB for 100k steps; compute \(\epsilon_{\text{equiv}}\) and \(\text{KL}(\pi\|\pi^\star)\). Expect FB \(\epsilon_{\text{equiv}} > 0.01\), one-step FB \(\epsilon_{\text{equiv}} < 10^{-4}\).
2.  **Zero-shot evaluation on ExORL/OGBench:** Pre-train for 1M steps; infer \(z_r\) from 100k zero-shot transitions; evaluate on downstream tasks. Report normalized returns (ExORL) or success rates (OGBench) and compare against Table 1/2 baselines.
3.  **Offline-to-online fine-tuning:** Initialize TD3 with the zero-shot policy from one-step FB; fine-tune online for 1M steps. Compare sample efficiency against fine-tuning from scratch or from FB (Appendix E.1, Fig. 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Does the practical FB algorithm converge to ground-truth FB representations under typical training conditions?
    *   **Basis:** The FB Bellman operator is not a contraction (Proposition 3). Standard fixed-point theorems do not apply, and circularity creates multiple fixed points.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Proofs using advanced fixed-point theories (e.g., Lefschetz, Lyapunov) or extensive empirical convergence studies across diverse MDPs.
*   **Open Question 2:** What is a complete convergence theory for the FB Bellman operator beyond the Banch fixed-point theorem?
    *   **Basis:** The paper shows \(T_{FB}\) is not a contraction, invalidating standard RL convergence guarantees.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Deriving sufficient conditions (on policy parametrization, initialization, step sizes) for convergence of \(T_{FB}\) or proving divergence under common setups.
*   **Open Question 3:** Can the theoretical analysis be rigorously extended to continuous state-action spaces?
    *   **Basis:** The analysis uses finite matrices (successor measures). Continuous spaces require measure-theoretic formulations and handle function approximation errors.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Developing continuous-space operator theory and validating it on high-dimensional benchmarks.
*   **Open Question 4:** What are the sample complexity and coverage requirements for one-step FB?
    *   **Basis:** Performance depends on the behavioral policy's occupancy measure; limited coverage degrades results.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Theoretical bounds linking coverage (e.g., concentrability) to suboptimality gaps, or empirical studies varying dataset diversity.
*   **Open Question 5:** How does representation dimension \(d\) trade off between approximation error and feasibility in one-step FB?
    *   **Basis:** Proposition 1 implies a rank condition. The SVD connection suggests \(d\) governs approximation quality, but no explicit trade-off curve is provided.
    *   **Resolution evidence:** Empirically sweeping \(d\) across domains with theoretical justification based on rank of \(M^{\pi_\beta}\), or generalization bounds.

## Limitations
*   **Theoretical gaps:** Analysis assumes exact, finite successor measures; extension to continuous spaces and stochastic function approximation is not formalized.
*   **Practical unknowns:** Exact reward preprocessing, behavioral policy details, and image augmentation specifications are omitted, hindering exact reproduction.
*   **Rank condition impracticability:** The requirement \(d \geq |S \times A|\) for exact FB representations is infeasible for large problems; reliance on low-rank approximation is not theoretically bounded.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Original FB's non-contraction and rank impossibility proofs (Propositions 1 & 3) are formal and sound.
*   **Medium:** One-step FB's empirical superiority is supported by reported numbers but relies on unreported hyperparameters and dataset nuances.
*   **Low:** The orthonormalization regularization's role as an SVD proxy is theoretically suggested but not universally validated across diverse domains.
