# Bootstrapped Physically-Primed Neural Networks for Robust T2 Distribution Estimation in Low-SNR Pancreatic MRI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14084
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14084
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Bootstrapped-P2T2 achieves superior test-retest reproducibility and group separation (KS p=4.79e-4, Hellinger distance=0.427) compared to NNLS, MIML, and deterministic P2T2.

## Executive Summary
Estimating multi-component T2 relaxation distributions from multi-echo spin-echo (MESE) MRI of the pancreas is severely ill-posed, especially under low SNR and residual uncorrelated noise. Classical non-negative least squares (NNLS) and deterministic deep learning methods yield unstable estimates. This work introduces inference-time bootstrapping applied to a physically-primed neural network (P2T2). By repeatedly resampling small echo subsets (with the first echo fixed) and averaging predictions, the method marginalizes over noise and stochastic inference errors, producing variance-reduced, physically consistent T2 distributions. In test-retest reproducibility (N=7) and T1DM vs. healthy differentiation (N=8), Bootstrapped-P2T2 achieved the lowest Wasserstein distances and strongest statistical separation, outperforming prior approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed method integrates two key components: a physics-informed backbone (P2T2) and an inference-time bootstrap aggregation scheme. P2T2 is a fully connected network that takes as input the concatenated vector of echo signals and their corresponding echo times (TE), forcing the model to learn decay relationships independent of the specific MESE protocol. It is trained on synthetic data generated via the Extended Phase Graph (EPG) formalism, simulating mixtures of T2 components relevant to pancreatic tissue. During inference, for a given scan with a full echo train (e.g., 32 echoes), the algorithm repeatedly samples subsets of echoes (always including the first echo) of a fixed size *m*, feeds each subset through the same trained P2T2 model, and averages the resulting *B* output distributions (e.g., *B*=200). This bootstrapping aggregates over aleatoric noise and sporadic inference errors, yielding a robust final estimate.

## Key Results
- Bootstrapped-P2T2 significantly improves test-retest reproducibility, achieving lower Wasserstein distances between repeated scans compared to NNLS, MIML, and deterministic P2T2.
- In the T1DM vs. healthy differentiation task, it yields the strongest group separation (KS p-value = 4.79e-4, Hellinger distance = 0.427).
- The method demonstrates robustness to the choice of bootstrap subset size *m*, with stable performance across values from 14 to 24 echoes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Inference-time bootstrapping reduces variance in T2 distribution estimates by averaging over stochastically resampled echo subsets.
- **Mechanism:** For each bootstrap iteration, a random subset of echoes (with the first echo fixed) is fed into the P2T2 network. The final distribution is the average of *B*=200 predictions. This marginalizes aleatoric noise (uncorrelated between echoes) and sporadic inference errors (uncorrelated across samples), yielding a lower-variance estimate.
- **Core assumption:** Noise and inference errors are approximately independent across bootstrap samples, so averaging reduces variance without introducing bias.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "By repeatedly resampling small echo subsets (with first echo fixed) and averaging their predictions, the method marginalizes over noise and stochastic inference errors, producing variance-reduced, physically consistent T2 distributions."

- **Break condition:** If noise or errors are correlated across samples (e.g., systematic artifact affecting all echoes), averaging may not reduce variance effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The P2T2 backbone ensures each bootstrap prediction is physically plausible by encoding TE values directly, enabling protocol-agnostic generalization.
- **Mechanism:** The network input concatenates the measured signal vector with the corresponding TE schedule. This forces the model to learn decay relationships based on actual time constants rather than echo indices, making it robust to varying MESE protocols.
- **Core assumption:** The EPG forward model accurately describes the signal decay, and the network capacity is sufficient to approximate the inverse mapping given TE encodings.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "P2T2 architecture, which encodes the echo-time schedule directly into the network, allowing generalization across heterogeneous MESE protocols."
  - [section 2.3]: "Explicitly encoding TE values—rather than echo indices—allows the model to learn protocol-invariant decay relationships and generalize across heterogeneous acquisition designs."
  - [corpus]: Corpus includes physics-informed MRI works but not specific to TE encoding; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition:** If TE values are miscalibrated or the network underfits, individual predictions may violate physical decay laws (e.g., non-monotonic decay).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Treating the echo train as a distribution via resampling captures aleatoric uncertainty inherent to low-SNR acquisitions, leading to more robust biomarkers.
- **Mechanism:** Randomly selecting echo subsets mimics the variability in possible measurements under the same noise realization. The aggregated distribution thus represents an average over plausible input signals, reducing sensitivity to any single noisy echo.
- **Core assumption:** The resampling procedure adequately samples the space of noise-realized echo trains, so the aggregated output approximates the expected T2 distribution under the noise model.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "This strategy treats the acquisition as a distribution rather than a fixed input, yielding variance-reduced, physically consistent estimates."
  - [section 2.2]: "We hypothesize that this approach addresses two distinct sources of variability... residual uncorrelated noise... and occasional incorrect estimation... By aggregating predictions, the method effectively marginalizes over these error sources."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; this is an inferred interpretation of the method's rationale.
- **Break condition:** If the subset size is too small, information loss may bias the estimate; if noise is not exchangeable across echoes, resampling misrepresents uncertainty.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Backbone (P2T2) -> Bootstrap module -> Aggregator  
*P2T2 backbone:* 12-layer fully connected network (256 units/ReLU, SoftMax output over a T2 grid) that maps concatenated [signal, TE] to a distribution.  
*Bootstrap module:* At inference, samples *m*-1 additional echoes (first echo fixed) from the full echo train to form input subsets.  
*Aggregator:* Computes element-wise mean of *B* predictions to produce the final distribution.

### Critical Path
1. **Training:** Train P2T2 from scratch on synthetic EPG-generated data with a fixed input length *m* (e.g., 14). The network learns the inverse mapping *f*: (*s*, *TE*) → *p*(T2).
2. **Inference:** For a new scan with *N_total* echoes, loop *b*=1..*B*: sample a subset of size *m* (first echo fixed), run P2T2, store prediction *p̂_b*(T2). Then average: *p̂_final* = (1/*B*) Σ *p̂_b*.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Subset size *m*:** Smaller *m* increases bootstrap diversity but loses signal information; larger *m* retains more signal but reduces resampling effect. Sensitivity analysis (Fig. 6) shows stability across *m*∈{14,16,20,24} for *N_total*=32, but optimal *m* likely scales with SNR.
- **Bootstrap count *B*:** Higher *B* reduces aggregation variance linearly but increases inference cost. Paper uses *B*=200 as a practical balance.
- **Network capacity:** 12×256 layers may be overkill for simpler tissues; for abdominal pancreas, capacity seems adequate given synthetic training diversity.

### Failure Signatures
- **Test-retest instability:** High Wasserstein distance between repeats suggests either *m* too small (information loss) or network underfitting. Check individual bootstrap predictions for outlier shapes.
- **Poor group separation:** Low Hellinger distance/high KS p-value in T1DM task may indicate over-smoothing from bootstrapping (if *m* too large) or insufficient multi-component modeling. Compare scalar vs. distribution outputs.
- **Physical inconsistency:** Negative probabilities or non-normalized distributions in *p̂_b* indicate training failure or TE encoding ignored. Verify each *p̂_b* sums to 1 and is non-negative.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Test-retest reproducibility:** Scan 7 healthy subjects twice within 10 min; compute Wasserstein distance between repeated scans for body/tail ROIs. Expect Bootstrapped-P2T2 to show lower median and variance than deterministic P2T2 and MIML.
2. **Glucose challenge differentiation:** Scan 4 T1DM and 4 controls pre/post glucose; compute Wasserstein distances between timepoints. Expect Bootstrapped-P2T2 to show largest separation (higher W1 in T1DM) and lowest KS p-value.
3. **Subset size sensitivity:** Vary *m* (e.g., 14,16,20,24) and re-run test-retest; expect stable W1 across *m* (Fig. 6). If variance spikes at extremes, adjust *m* to balance information and diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **External validation as a biomarker:** Can Bootstrapped-P2T2 be validated as a noninvasive biomarker for early T1DM detection in larger, multi-center cohorts? The current study includes only 7–8 subjects from a single center, limiting statistical power and generalizability.
- **Lesion characterization:** Can Bootstrapped-P2T2 reliably distinguish malignant from benign pancreatic lesions, addressing an unmet need? The method has only been evaluated on healthy and T1DM cohorts, with no lesion data.
- **Optimal bootstrap iterations:** What is the optimal number of bootstrap iterations (*B*) to maximize stability while minimizing computational cost? The paper uses *B*=200 without systematic justification.
- **First echo constraint:** Is the constraint of always including the first echo in bootstrap subsets necessary, or could fully random subsets achieve comparable robustness? No alternatives are tested.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed training details (Dirichlet concentration, T2 grid resolution, loss function) hinder exact replication.
- Small cohorts (test-retest N=7, T1DM N=8) risk overfitting and limit generalizability.
- No external validation; performance may be scanner- or site-specific.

## Confidence
- Bootstrapping reduces variance: Medium (internal experiments show improved reproducibility, but independent validation and tests of noise correlation assumptions are missing).
- TE encoding ensures physical plausibility: Low (design is theoretically sound, but no ablation isolates its contribution; corpus support is indirect).
- Superior task performance: Medium (strong statistical separation on small data, but requires confirmation on larger, multi-center cohorts).

## Next Checks
1. **Variance-reduction mechanism:** On a held-out low-SNR test set, compute per-voxel variance across the *B* bootstrap predictions and correlate with test-retest Wasserstein distances across subjects to directly test the averaging effect.
2. **Physical priming ablation:** Train P2T2 with TE concatenation vs. echo indices; evaluate both on a test set with a different TE schedule (e.g., varied ΔTE) to quantify generalization gains from physics-based input.
3. **External replication:** Partner to acquire multi-center pancreatic MESE data from >30 subjects (including T1DM) and repeat the group differentiation analysis (KS test, Hellinger distance) to assess robustness across scanners and populations.
