# Evaluating Semantic and Syntactic Understanding in Large Language Models for Payroll Systems
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.18012
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18012
*   **Reference Count:** 28
*   **Primary Result:** LLMs achieve near-perfect accuracy on simple payroll tasks with minimal prompts, but only Perplexity handles the most complex multi-jurisdiction scenarios with 100% exactness when given explicit formulas.

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks five large language models (GPT, Claude, Perplexity, Grok, Gemini) on a synthetic payroll calculation task requiring cent-level numeric accuracy and strict schema compliance. Using datasets of escalating complexity and four prompt specificity levels, the research identifies a clear breakpoint. For simple to moderately complex payroll (linear arithmetic, short rule graphs), minimal prompting suffices for high accuracy. For complex scenarios involving branching rules and caps, explicit formula-level instructions are necessary. However, for the most challenging tier with deep dependency chains, multi-jurisdiction rules, and foreign exchange, only Perplexity maintains 100% exactness with detailed prompts, while other models plateau significantly. The findings suggest that while prompting can offload simple payroll computation, highly complex rule-based numeric tasks remain a significant challenge, with model architecture and adherence to provided computational scaffolds being critical differentiators.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses five synthetic payroll datasets (100 employee records each), tiered from "very basic" to "very complex" in schema and rule intricacy. Ground truth Net Pay is generated via deterministic Excel-style formulas. Five models are queried with four prompt levels, ranging from minimal semantic instructions to explicit pseudocode for intermediate fields. Model predictions are captured as tabular outputs, aligned by Employee ID, and evaluated against references using primary metrics of "Exact within tier-specific tolerance" (0.01 USD for simple, 0.05 USD for complex) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The methodology is a zero/few-shot prompting study without model fine-tuning, focusing on the models' ability to map payroll semantics to correct arithmetic and rule application.

## Key Results
*   Simple to moderate payroll tasks are solved with near-perfect cent-level accuracy using minimal prompting (Level 1) by most models.
*   Complex payroll scenarios (branching rules, caps, thresholds) require explicit formula-level prompts (Level 4) to achieve high accuracy, showing a significant improvement from Levels 1-3.
*   On the very complex tier (multi-jurisdiction, FX, deep dependencies), only Perplexity reaches 100% exactness with explicit formulas; other models' accuracy plateaus below 30%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** For linear arithmetic tasks with short dependency graphs, LLMs achieve near-perfect cent-level accuracy using minimal prompting because they correctly map header semantics to single-step operations.
*   **Mechanism:** The model interprets column names (e.g., `Rate`, `Hours Regular`) as direct operands in a simple multiplication, leveraging pre-trained knowledge of payroll-like terminology without needing explicit procedural guidance.
*   **Break condition:** Introduction of branching logic, pretax deductions, or capped taxes, where simple header-to-operation mapping fails to capture required ordering and conditional application.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** For complex payroll scenarios with multiple interacting rules, explicit formula-level prompts (Level 4) are necessary to achieve high accuracy because they provide a deterministic computational scaffold that reduces reasoning drift.
*   **Mechanism:** Supplying explicit Excel-style expressions or pseudocode transforms the task from open-ended semantic reasoning to structured execution following, allowing the model to substitute values and compute stepwise without misordering operations or omitting caps/thresholds.
*   **Break condition:** Very complex tiers where dependency chains are deep (e.g., blended rates, multi-state apportionment) and even explicit formulas may not be sufficient for all models due to stacking and context sensitivity.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Model architecture and training influence the ability to handle very complex, multi-layered dependency chains; Perplexity's consistency suggests optimization for formula adherence and minimal deviation in deeply nested arithmetic.
*   **Mechanism:** Assumption: Perplexity's inference or fine-tuning emphasizes faithful execution of provided computational templates, allowing it to maintain precision across long chains of dependent calculations where other models introduce cumulative rounding or ordering errors.
*   **Break condition:** Tasks requiring dynamic context management (e.g., retroactive adjustments, variable currency conversions) that exceed the prompt's explicit scope.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Schema Semantics Mapping**
    *   **Why needed:** Payroll fields have precise policy meanings (e.g., `Taxable Wages` vs. `Disposable Income`); misunderstanding leads to incorrect bases for subsequent calculations.
    *   **Quick check:** Given a column `PreTax 401k Pct`, what monetary field does it modify, and should its application occur before or after tax calculations?
*   **Concept: Cent-Level Arithmetic with Tolerance**
    *   **Why needed:** Payroll outputs must match exact cents; however, complex rule stacks can produce minor rounding variations. Understanding the tolerance policy is crucial for evaluating correctness.
    *   **Quick check:** If two valid rounding sequences produce results differing by $0.03, are both acceptable for the `complex` tier where tolerance is 0.05 USD?
*   **Concept: Rule Dependency Graph Traversal**
    *   **Why needed:** Accurate payroll requires applying operations in a specific order (e.g., pretax deductions before tax bases). Misordering yields cascading errors.
    *   **Quick check:** List the correct sequence of steps to compute `Net Pay` from `Gross Pay`, including where a `Garnishment Cap` is applied relative to `Disposable Income`.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Dataset Generator -> Prompt Manager -> Model Interface -> Evaluator
*   **Critical path:**
    1. Define tier-specific schema and ground-truth formulas.
    2. For each model and prompt level, stream all 100 employee rows.
    3. Compare model outputs to references using tier-specific tolerances.
    4. Aggregate results to identify breakpoints between prompt sufficiency and need for tooling.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Prompt detail vs. context overload: More explicit formulas help complex tasks but may confuse some models (e.g., Claude degrades at Levels 2–3).
    *   Auditability vs. flexibility: Level 4 prompts yield traceable computations but reduce the model's ability to generalize beyond the pseudocode.
    *   Model diversity vs. consistency: Using five models captures varied failure modes but introduces heterogeneity in interpreting prompts.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Schema drift: Model invents helper columns not in the input schema.
    *   Rounding bias: Consistent off-by-one-cent errors across rows, especially in `very complex` tier.
    *   Context sensitivity: Accuracy drops with longer prompts, indicating inability to parse dense instructions.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1. Baseline competency: Run Level 1 prompt on `very basic` tier for all models; expect >99% exactness.
    2. Prompt gradient test: On `complex` tier, run Levels 1–4 for each model; measure exactness and MAE to identify which models benefit from explicit formulas.
    3. Stress test: On `very complex` tier, run Level 4 prompt; verify if any model reaches 100% exactness (Perplexity expected) and note others' plateau rates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Why does Claude Sonnet 4's accuracy decrease with increased prompt detail on complex payroll?** The paper notes a drop from 22% to 0% exactness but attributes it only to "overload" without causal analysis.
*   **What explains the persistent cent-level errors in GPT and Grok on very complex tasks despite Level 4 prompts?** The paper notes "penny-scale offsets" without diagnosing the source, whether from rounding order, cumulative error, or formula misinterpretation.
*   **How can schema drift be mitigated in LLM-based payroll systems?** The conclusion calls for "schema-drift safeguards" as future work, with no specific methods proposed or tested.

## Limitations
*   **Data & Prompt Fidelity:** Exact synthetic data generation rules and precise model versions/API parameters are unspecified, risking reproduction variance.
*   **Corpus Support:** Neighboring work shows weak corroboration (average FMR 0.53, zero citations), so claims rest primarily on the paper's internal evidence.
*   **Generalization Scope:** Results are payroll-specific; validity for other rule-based numeric domains (e.g., tax, billing) is unvalidated.

## Confidence
*   **Prompt Gradient Effectiveness: High** – Strongly supported by consistent accuracy improvements from Level 1 to Level 4 across multiple models in Tables 4–5.
*   **Complex Tier Breakpoint: Medium** – Evidence is clear for the `complex` tier but relies on the paper's tier definitions; the `very complex` breakpoint shows Perplexity's unique success but others' plateaus lack independent replication.
*   **Architectural Mechanism (Perplexity's Advantage): Low** – Claim is speculative; no architectural details or ablations provided, only observed outcomes.
