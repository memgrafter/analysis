# Scalable Gaussian process modeling of parametrized spatio-temporal fields

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00290
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00290
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a scalable Gaussian process framework for high-resolution parametrized spatio-temporal fields via deep product kernels and Kronecker algebra, achieving near-linear scaling and accurate uncertainty quantification on datasets with millions of points.

## Executive Summary
Gaussian processes (GPs) provide probabilistic surrogate models with calibrated uncertainty but are computationally limited by cubic scaling with the number of training points. This paper overcomes that bottleneck for parametrized spatio‑temporal fields by introducing a deep product kernel (DPK) that factorizes across parameters, space, and time. Coupled with Kronecker matrix algebra, the method yields near‑linear computational complexity for rectilinear grids. For unstructured grids, a gappy‑grid embedding maps data onto a rectilinear background with missing values, preserving efficiency while enabling exact (or bounded) uncertainty quantification. Benchmarks on PDE problems (1D Burgers’, hyper‑elasticity, 2D airfoil flow, pipe flow) demonstrate accuracy competitive with state‑of‑the‑art operator learning methods (FNO, DeepONet) and superiority over traditional projection‑based reduced‑order models. Training sets scale up to 10.24 million points without sacrificing predictive performance or uncertainty estimates.

## Method Summary
The core is a deep product kernel \(k((\mu, x, t), (\mu', x', t')) = k_\mu(G_\mu(\mu), G_\mu(\mu')) \times k_x(G_x(x), G_x(x')) \times k_t(G_t(t), G_t(t'))\), where each component uses a neural network feature map \(G\) followed by a stationary base kernel (Matérn‑5/2 or squared exponential). For rectilinear spatial grids, the spatial kernel further factorizes per dimension, i.e., \(k_x = \prod_{\ell=1}^d k_{x\ell}(G_{x\ell}(x_\ell), G_{x\ell}(x_\ell'))\). This induces a Kronecker product structure in the full covariance matrix, allowing efficient eigendecompositions of small factor matrices (for parameters, each spatial dimension, time). Training minimizes the negative log marginal likelihood (NLML) using Kronecker‑accelerated computations for quadratic forms and determinants, with gradients backpropagated through the Kronecker structure. For unstructured grids, the gappy‑grid approach maps data onto a rectilinear grid with gaps; pseudovalues at gaps are computed via conjugate gradient so that the larger structured system matches observed coefficients, and posterior variance bounds are derived via eigenvalue interlacing. Parametrized domains are handled via a reference mapping. The framework thus delivers scalable GP inference with uncertainty quantification for large spatio‑temporal datasets.

## Key Results
- Achieves relative L² errors of 0.0033/0.0029 on 1D Burgers’ test cases with 10.24 M training points, far below POD‑Galerkin (~0.03) and competitive with Deep‑LSPG.
- On hyper‑elasticity, test error 0.0326 vs. Geo‑FNO’s 0.0363.
- Near‑linear computational complexity; posterior mean and variance cost comparable, enabling uncertainty quantification at scale.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Mechanism 1: Separability via Deep Product Kernel
The DPK factorizes correlations across parameter, spatial, and temporal dimensions, enabling a Kronecker covariance structure without losing expressivity thanks to learned neural feature maps. The core assumption is approximate separability; strong cross‑dimension couplings may cause error. Evidence: kernel definition in Eq. (7) and factorization in section 3; Kronecker covariance from rectilinear grids in section 4.1. Break condition: non‑separable interactions degrade accuracy.

#### Mechanism 2: Kronecker Algebra for Near‑Linear Scaling
Eigendecompositions of small factor matrices are used to compute matrix‑vector products and determinants via tensor‑mode multiplications and Kronecker identities, avoiding the cubic cost of the full covariance. Complexity drops from \(O((N M N_t)^3)\) to nearly linear in grid size. Core assumption: rectilinear grid enabling per‑dimension spatial factorization. Evidence: NLML derivation in section 4.2 (Eqs. 11–13) and posterior computation in section 4.3 (Eqs. 14–18). Break condition: eigendecompositions become costly for large factor sizes; unstructured grids require the gappy extension.

#### Mechanism 3: Gappy‑Grid Extension for Unstructured Meshes
Unstructured observations are embedded onto a rectilinear grid with gaps. Pseudovalues at gaps are solved via conjugate gradient to match observed coefficients, and variance bounds use eigenvalue interlacing. Core assumption: accurate interpolation onto the rectilinear background and gaps lying in physically irrelevant regions. Evidence: embedding in section 5.1; Lemma 1 and pseudovalue equations in section 5.2; variance bounds in section 5.4. Break condition: high interpolation error or extensive gaps reduce accuracy and bound tightness.

## Foundational Learning
#### Gaussian Process Regression
- **Why needed**: Gives probabilistic surrogates with calibrated uncertainty, crucial for downstream tasks; standard GP suffers cubic training cost.
- **Quick check**: What are the dominant \(O(n^3)\) operations in GP training, and how do they hinder scaling?

#### Kronecker Product Algebra
- **Why needed**: Enables efficient manipulation of factorized covariance matrices, turning exponential storage/computation into near‑linear scaling.
- **Quick check**: How does \((A \otimes B)(C \otimes D) = AC \otimes BD\) simplify matrix solves in separable systems?

#### Separable vs. Non‑Separable Kernels
- **Why needed**: Separability is the key inductive bias for scalability; DPKs balance expressivity (via neural nets) with separability to keep efficiency.
- **Quick check**: Under what conditions does a product kernel retain universal approximation on the input space?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data ingestion → Grid processing (embedding for unstructured grids) → Kernel specification (deep product kernel with neural networks) → Kronecker setup (eigendecompositions of factor matrices) → Training (NLML minimization via Kronecker‑accelerated gradients) → Inference (posterior mean and variance).

**Critical path**: Data → embedding (if needed) → kernel hyperparameter learning via NLML minimization → posterior computation.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Separability vs. accuracy*: Product kernel assumes independent dimension correlations; strong cross‑dimension interactions may require partial coupling at a scalability cost.
- *Interpolation error*: Gappy‑grid embedding introduces error; trade‑off between grid resolution and gap size.
- *Variance bounds*: For unstructured grids, exact variance is replaced by rigorous but potentially loose bounds.

**Failure signatures**:
- High test error when training data is sparse in parameter or spatial extremes.
- Memory overflow if spatial grid is too large for eigendecompositions.
- Poor uncertainty calibration if noise variance is misestimated or pseudovalues are inaccurate.
- Slow CG convergence for gappy‑grid pseudovalues if kernel is ill‑conditioned.
- Non‑PSD kernel due to poor initialization, causing NLML instability.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **1D Burgers’ problem**: Tests scalability (10.24 M points) and accuracy against POD‑Galerkin and Deep‑LSPG; demonstrates superiority of deep product kernel.
2. **Hyper‑elastic problem**: Tests parametrized domain handling via reference domain mapping; compares against Geo‑FNO and DeepONet on stress field prediction.
3. **2D Transonic flow**: Tests unstructured C‑grid handling with gaps (airfoil interior); shows competitive performance with Geo‑FNO on velocity field prediction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Relaxing separability**: How can cross‑dimension interactions be incorporated (e.g., low‑rank cross‑term corrections) while preserving near‑linear scaling?
2. **Gappy‑grid interpolation impact**: What is the effect of the interpolation step on surrogate accuracy, and can adaptive meshing minimize it?
3. **Preconditioning for gappy‑grid**: Can efficient preconditioners be constructed for the linear solves that exploit Kronecker structure and guarantee fast CG convergence?
4. **Parameter dimension sensitivity**: How does the latent parameter dimension (after PCA) affect accuracy and scalability, and is there a principled way to choose it?

## Limitations
- Separability assumption may fail for PDEs with strong cross‑dimensional interactions.
- Gappy‑grid interpolation error is unquantified and may degrade accuracy.
- Eigendecomposition of large spatial factor matrices could become a bottleneck for extremely fine grids.

## Confidence
- **Scalability via separability**: High
- **Complexity reduction**: High
- **Gappy‑grid method**: Medium
