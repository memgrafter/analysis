# EpiCaR: Knowing What You Don't Know Matters for Better Reasoning in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06786
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06786
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** EPICAR mitigates calibration collapse in iterative self-training by jointly optimizing accuracy and epistemic uncertainty, achieving Pareto-superior accuracy and calibration over STaR while enabling significant inference compute reduction via confidence-informed self-consistency.

## Executive Summary
Iterative self-training (e.g., STaR) improves LLM reasoning accuracy but causes models to become overconfident, losing the ability to represent uncertainty—a form of model collapse. This work introduces EPICAR, an epistemically-calibrated reasoning objective applied within iterative supervised fine-tuning. EPICAR uses a dual-task approach: it reinforces correct reasoning paths while simultaneously training on self-evaluation signals (verbalized confidence) for all generated outputs. Across Llama-3 and Qwen-3 models (1B–8B), EPICAR yields better accuracy and calibration (lower Expected Calibration Error, higher AUROC, lower Brier Score) than standard iterative SFT, with gains most pronounced for models ≥3B parameters. The method generalizes to out-of-distribution code generation and, when combined with confidence-weighted self-consistency, reduces required inference samples by 3× while matching the performance of standard self-consistency with many more samples.

## Method Summary
EPICAR augments iterative self-training with a dual-objective fine-tuning step. For each iteration, the model generates reasoning paths and attaches a verbalized confidence estimate (e.g., "I am 80% confident"). First, correct reasoning paths are selected (e.g., by answer match) for standard SFT to boost accuracy. Second, the model is trained on the confidence predictions for **all** generated outputs—both correct and incorrect—using a probability-based loss that encourages calibrated confidence. This joint training embeds epistemic uncertainty awareness into the reasoning policy. The process repeats over multiple iterations, using the model's own improved generations as new training data, but now with calibrated confidence as a regularizer.

## Key Results
- EPICAR achieves Pareto-superior accuracy and calibration compared to STaR across Llama-3 and Qwen-3 (1B–8B) on GSM8K, with notable gains for ≥3B models.
- It reduces calibration error metrics (ECE, Brier Score) and improves uncertainty discrimination (AUROC) while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- When paired with confidence-informed self-consistency (weighting sample votes by confidence), EPICAR matches STaR’s K=30 performance with only K=10 samples—a 3× compute reduction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Iterative self-training risks *calibration collapse*: as models are repeatedly trained on their own correct outputs, they lose exposure to incorrect reasoning and become overconfident. EPICAR directly counteracts this by forcing the model to confront its own uncertainty. The dual-task objective ensures the model does two things simultaneously: (1) learn to produce correct reasoning via SFT on gold-labeled paths, and (2) learn to assign appropriate confidence scores to **all** its generations, including wrong ones. This internalizes epistemic uncertainty—the model learns to "know what it doesn't know"—making its confidence estimates better calibrated. By preserving uncertainty representation throughout iterations, the reasoning policy avoids the overfitting trap that leads to collapse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Calibration in LLMs**  
  *Why needed:* Ensures model confidence aligns with actual accuracy; uncalibrated models are overconfident and unreliable for high-stakes decisions.  
  *Quick check:* Are the model’s stated confidence percentages close to its true accuracy on a held-out set?  
- **Iterative Self-Training (e.g., STaR)**  
  *Why needed:* Improves reasoning by repeatedly fine-tuning on model-generated correct solutions, scaling without extra human annotation.  
  *Quick check:* Does accuracy on a held-out set improve over successive iterations?  
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**  
  *Why needed:* Captures what the model does not know due to limited knowledge or data; distinct from aleatoric noise, vital for trustworthy AI.  
  *Quick check:* Can the model identify questions it is likely to answer incorrectly?  
- **Verbalized Confidence**  
  *Why needed:* A human-readable way to extract uncertainty from LLMs; used as a training signal for calibration.  
  *Quick check:* Do natural language confidence expressions correlate with answer correctness?  
- **Self-Consistency**  
  *Why needed:* Bouces accuracy by sampling multiple reasoning paths and taking a majority vote; simple but compute-intensive.  
  *Quick check:* Does performance improve as more samples are decoded?  
- **Dual-Task Learning**  
  *Why needed:* Jointly optimizing accuracy and calibration prevents tradeoffs where one improves at the expense of the other.  
  *Quick check:* Does adding a calibration loss degrade accuracy, or does it stabilize both metrics?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Question -> Generate Reasoning & Verbalized Confidence -> Filter Correct Answers (by verification) -> Dual-Objective Training (SFT on correct paths + confidence loss on all generations) -> Updated Model -> Next Iteration

**Critical Path:** The core iterative loop where each cycle: (1) samples multiple reasoning chains from the current model, (2) verifies answers to label correct chains, (3) fine-tunes on correct chains for accuracy, and (4) applies a confidence regression loss on all chains to calibrate verbalized confidence. The updated model is then reused.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Reliance on verbalized confidence vs. using distributional entropy (simpler but may not capture all uncertainty forms).  
- Extra compute for dual-task training vs. single-task SFT (calibration gain vs. cost).  
- Scale dependency: gains diminish for very small models (<3B) due to limited capacity for dual optimization.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Confidence overfitting: model learns to output high confidence even on wrong answers if confidence loss is under-weighted.  
- Small model degradation: models <1B may show no improvement or worse calibration due to inability to handle dual objectives.  
- OOD confidence collapse: verbalized confidence may fail to transfer to novel problem styles, leading to miscalibration.

**First Experiments:**  
1. **Scale Sensitivity Test:** Train EPICAR on 1B, 3B, 8B, and 70B parameter models to confirm the ≥3B threshold and check if benefits persist or reverse at scale.  
2. **Confidence Signal Ablation:** Replace verbalized confidence with token probability entropy or sampled consistency scores during training to determine if verbalization is essential or just a proxy.  
3. **Domain Portability Check:** Apply EPICAR to non-math reasoning benchmarks (e.g., TheoremQA, BIG-Bench Hard) to test if calibration improvements generalize beyond GSM8K/MBPP.

## Limitations
- **Domain Scope:** Evaluations restricted to math (GSM8K) and code (MBPP); unknown if benefits extend to other reasoning domains (e.g., commonsense, scientific QA).  
- **Scale Dependency:** Claims of Pareto-superiority emphasize ≥3B parameters; performance on very large (>30B) or very small (<1B) models is unexplored.  
- **Uncertainty Representation:** Reliance on verbalized confidence may not fully capture epistemic uncertainty; alternative forms (e.g., distribution shifts) remain unaddressed.

## Confidence
- Calibration collapse in iterative SFT: High  
- EPICAR improves accuracy+calibration: Medium  
- Compute reduction via confidence-informed self-consistency: High  
- Generalization to OOD tasks: Low

## Next Checks
1. Test EPICAR on >30B parameter models (e.g., Llama-3.1 70B) and sub-1B models to verify scale-dependent trends and potential reversal at extreme scales.
2. Evaluate on at least three additional reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH, TheoremQA, HumanEval) to assess domain portability beyond math and code.
3. Compare verbalized confidence against alternative uncertainty estimators (e.g., entropy of next-token distributions, sampled consistency scores) to isolate whether gains are specific to natural language confidence or general to uncertainty representation.
