# GazeXPErT: An Expert Eye-tracking Dataset for Interpretable and Explainable AI in Oncologic FDG-PET/CT Scans

## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2603.00162
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00162
*   Reference count: 40
*   Primary result: Gaze-integrated nnUNet segmentation DICE improved from 0.6008 to 0.6819; gaze-correcting ViT achieved 74.95% predictions closer to tumors; intention prediction accuracy was 67.53% (AUROC 0.747).

## Executive Summary
Expert reader shortages and AI interpretability gaps hinder clinical adoption of FDG-PET/CT for tumor assessment. This work introduces GazeXPErT, a 4D eye-tracking dataset capturing expert visual search patterns and lesion annotations from 346 scans, synchronized with PET/CT slices in COCO format. Key findings demonstrate that integrating expert gaze into a 3D nnUNet segmentation model provides a powerful spatial attention prior, significantly improving performance. Furthermore, modeling the temporal sequence of gaze preceding a lesion selection enables a vision transformer to correct final fixation points and predict reader intent, offering pathways toward more interpretable and human-aligned AI systems.

## Method Summary
The GazeXPErT dataset was constructed by recording 60Hz eye-tracking data from expert and trainee readers interacting with a custom Python UI displaying 512x512 axial slices from FDG-PET/CT studies. The platform captured keystrokes marking accepted/rejected lesions with "certain/uncertain" labels. Raw gaze was synchronized with image slices via metadata, filtered for validity, and processed into two primary formats: (1) 3D volumetric heatmaps (voxel counts of gaze intersections) for segmentation, and (2) sequential (x,y) coordinate trajectories preceding lesion selections for temporal modeling. For segmentation, the heatmap was concatenated as a fourth channel to PET/CT inputs for a standard 3D nnUNet. For temporal tasks, a vision transformer (ViT) was modified with a Gaze Encoder to jointly process image slices and preceding gaze sequences for either point prediction (correction) or binary classification (intention).

## Key Results
*   **Segmentation:** 3D nnUNet with expert gaze heatmap achieved a DICE score of 0.6819 versus 0.6008 without gaze.
*   **Gaze Correction:** A ViT model predicting a corrected gaze point achieved 74.95% of predictions closer to the tumor center than the raw final fixation.
*   **Intention Prediction:** The same architecture classified intentional (lesion-targeting) vs. unintentional (general search) gaze trajectories with 67.53% accuracy (AUROC 0.747).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gaze Heatmap as Spatial Attention Prior
Expert gaze is aggregated into a 3D heatmap volume where each voxel counts gaze hits. This heatmap is concatenated as an extra channel to the PET/CT input of a 3D nnUNet. The model learns to weigh image features more heavily in regions with dense expert attention, using human visual search as distributed, weak supervision for lesion location. The core assumption is that expert gaze distribution correlates with metabolically active tumor locations even if direct fixations on lesions are brief. Evidence shows a clear DICE improvement (0.6819 vs. 0.6008). Performance breaks down if gaze patterns are systematically biased (e.g., due to calibration drift) introducing false priors.

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Gaze Sequence for Point Correction
A ViT with a Gaze Encoder takes as input the PET/CT slice at the moment of a "select" keystroke and up to 16 preceding gaze coordinates (60Hz). It is trained to predict a point minimizing Hausdorff distance to the lesion segmentation, effectively "correcting" the final raw gaze point. The mechanism leverages the idea that micro-saccades and trajectory shape in the ~0.25 seconds before a deliberate selection reflect a final visual verification process steered by learned image-gaze context. Evidence is the 74.95% correction rate. Performance is bounded by tracker accuracy (~1.176°) and fails if the last gaze point is already optimal or the preceding sequence lacks a corrective signal.

### Mechanism 3: Intent Discrimination via Gaze Dynamics
The same ViT framework models a longer (1-2 second) continuous gaze sequence paired with the current slice as a binary classification task: "intentional" (accepted lesion) vs. "unintentional" (random non-tumor hot spot). It learns temporal-spatial patterns distinguishing a focused verification trajectory from generic scanning. The core assumption is that the cognitive state of committing to a measurement produces a distinguishable temporal signature in gaze dynamics. Evidence is 67.53% accuracy (AUROC 0.747). The task may be ill-posed if "unintentional" samples are not ecologically valid, and accuracy is limited by the subtlety of intent signals and model simplicity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Raw Tobii Gaze + PET/CT Metadata -> Python Annotation UI (records gaze, keystrokes) -> Filtering/Synchronization Pipeline -> (a) 3D Gaze Heatmap / (b) Sequential Gaze Clips -> nnUNet (channel concat) / ViT+Gaze Encoder (joint input) -> Segmentation DICE / Correction % / Intention Accuracy.

**Critical path:**
1.  Replicate data processing: Filter invalid gaze (outside display, `valid=False`), synchronize timestamps to `display_sample` to link to correct 512x512 PET/CT slices.
2.  For segmentation: Bin all valid gaze (x,y,slice) into a 512x512xN volume, concatenate with PET+CT as 4th channel for nnUNet.
3.  For gaze tasks: For each accepted lesion, extract the 1-2 second gaze sequence immediately *preceding* the "select" keystroke and the corresponding slice image; format as a clip (T frames of gaze + image).
4.  Train the ViT baseline, ensuring the Gaze Encoder properly embeds coordinates and adds temporal positional encoding, with losses: Hausdorff (correction), Focal (intention).

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Temporal resolution vs. compute:** 60Hz is dense; downsampling reduces noise but may lose micro-saccades. Paper used up to 16 samples (~0.27s) for correction.
*   **Gaze aggregation:** Flattening to a 3D heatmap loses order but provides a clean volumetric prior. Sequences preserve order but need temporal models.
*   **Annotation noise:** "Certain/uncertain" keystrokes are a rich clinical signal but were not explicitly used by baseline models.
*   **Source fidelity:** Gaze is synchronized to the *displayed* image (with user-adjusted window/level), not raw DICOM; models must account for this visualization space.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Poor gaze-dependent performance:** Check per-reader calibration metrics; high inter-reader heatmap variance or low correlation with lesions suggests weak/noisy signal.
*   **Misaligned context:** Incorrect `display_sample` mapping leads to gaze-image mismatch. Validate by reconstructing mini-videos from COCO CSVs.
*   **Overfitting to idiosyncrasy:** Model trained on one reader fails on others if it learned personal scanpaths, not general strategies. Use mixed-reader training.
*   **Data leakage:** Train/test splits must be at the *study* level. Leakage occurs if gaze from the same study appears in both splits.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Replicate nnUNet gaze augmentation:** Train nnUNet (ResEncL) on PET+CT vs. PET+CT+GazeHeatmap (union of all readers) using provided folds. Validate DICE improvement matches ~0.08 gain; inspect activation maps for gaze-attended regions.
2.  **Implement Gaze Correction ViT:** Build ViT-B/32 + Gaze Encoder. Use `gaze_data_*.csv` to extract (gaze_sequence, image_slice, lesion_bbox) for samples meeting distance criteria. Train to predict point minimizing Hausdorff loss. Target >74.95% closer predictions.
3.  **Build Intention Predictor:** Reuse data format as Exp 2. Construct "intentional" (accepted lesion trajectory) vs. "unintentional" (random non-lesion hot spot) pairs. Train with focal loss. Target >67% accuracy / 0.74 AUROC; analyze failures near physiological uptake.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** Can advanced temporal modeling (e.g., Transformers with longer context) improve expert intention prediction accuracy beyond 67.53%, and what temporal gaze features are most diagnostic of "certain" versus "uncertain" assessments? *Basis:* Authors identify human intention prediction as a key direction and note baseline limitations. *Unresolved:* Baseline used fixed 1-2 sec sequences and a standard ViT without temporal specialization.
*   **Question 2:** Does using expert gaze as a reward signal in reinforcement learning produce more clinically efficient search patterns (fewer fixations, faster detection) than supervised learning from static gaze heatmaps? *Basis:* Authors list "expert gaze-rewarded modeling" as a potential application. *Unresolved:* Baseline experiments only used gaze as input/target, not as a reward in interactive settings.
*   **Question 3:** Can model attention mechanisms be regularized to align with expert gaze, creating inherently interpretable AI where explanations are directly grounded in human visual reasoning, and does this alignment improve clinical trust? *Basis:* Dataset aims to enable "models whose attention can be grounded against real expert gaze." *Unresolved:* Unclear if attention naturally aligns with gaze or needs explicit constraints, and if alignment correlates with trust.

## Limitations
*   Implementation details for nnUNet hyperparameters/folds and the ViT Gaze Encoder architecture are underspecified in the paper, hindering exact replication without the codebase.
*   "Unintentional" gaze samples for intention prediction may not represent ecologically valid pre-decision search (random hot spots vs. natural scanning), potentially limiting task validity.
*   Dataset size (346 scans) is modest for generalizability, and inter-reader variability analysis (trainee vs. expert) remains underdeveloped.

## Confidence
*   **Segmentation with gaze prior:** Medium. Clear DICE gain (0.6819 vs. 0.6008) demonstrated, but from single-site internal validation.
*   **Gaze correction:** Medium. Promising 74.95% improvement metric, but performance bounded by tracker precision (~1.176°) and short sequence context.
*   **Intention prediction:** Low. 67.53% accuracy (AUROC 0.747) suggests limited discriminative power; task may be inherently noisy or baseline model insufficient.

## Next Checks
