# One-for-All: A Lightweight Stabilized and Parameter-Efficient Pre-trained LLM for Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29756
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29756
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves SOTA accuracy with 6.8×–21× fewer trainable parameters (0.55M) and 2.2 MiB memory, enabling edge deployment.

## Executive Summary
One-for-All introduces Gaussian Rank-Stabilized Low-Rank Adapters (rsLoRA) to adapt frozen large language models (LLMs) for multivariate time series forecasting. By injecting low-rank (rank 16) trainable matrices into positional embeddings and output layers, with a mathematically derived scaling factor βr = α/√r, it ensures gradient stability even at very low ranks. Evaluated on six tasks across four benchmarks (ETT, Weather, M3, M4), it reduces trainable parameters by up to 21× compared to recent LLM-based forecasters, cuts memory footprint to 2.2 MiB (168–1,776× smaller), and matches their average MSE of 0.33 while maintaining consistent performance over horizons from 96 to 720 steps. This efficiency opens the door to deploying sophisticated forecasting on resource-constrained edge devices for healthcare, finance, and environmental monitoring.

## Method Summary
One-for-All freezes a pre-trained LLM backbone and adapts it to time series forecasting using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT). Specifically, it introduces Gaussian Rank-Stabilized Low-Rank Adapters (rsLoRA), which replace standard LoRA by scaling the adapter output with βr = α/√r to guarantee gradient stability at low ranks (e.g., rank 16). The adapters are injected at two strategic locations: the positional embedding layer (to model temporal structure) and the output projection layer (to align with forecasting targets). Input time series are first split into fixed-size patches and linearly projected into tokens. The frozen LLM processes these tokens, and the final predictions are obtained via the adapted output layer. This design drastically reduces the number of trainable parameters (as low as 0.55 M) and memory consumption while leveraging the rich representations of large pre-trained models.

## Key Results
- Parameter efficiency: 0.55 M trainable parameters (4.7% of GPT4TS), reducing parameters by 6.8× (vs. TimesNet), 21× (vs. GPT4TS), and 11.8× (vs. TIME-LLM).
- Memory footprint: 2.2 MiB, which is 168–1,776× smaller than current SOTA methods.
- Accuracy parity: Average MSE = 0.33 on par with state-of-the-art LLM-based forecasters across long-term (96–720 steps), few-shot, and zero-shot settings on ETT, Weather, M3, and M4 benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The rsLoRA adapter stabilizes gradients via the βr = α/√r scaling, allowing effective learning with very low-rank matrices (rank 16). Freezing the LLM backbone prevents catastrophic forgetting and leverages pre-trained linguistic/temporal abstractions. Injecting adapters at positional embeddings equips the model with task-specific positional biases crucial for time series, while output-layer adaptation aligns the LLM's feature space with forecasting targets. This combination yields high parameter efficiency without sacrificing representational capacity, as the frozen backbone provides a strong prior and the adapters make minimal, stable adjustments.

**Time Series Patching**: Segmenting a continuous series into fixed-length windows to create token-like structures. *Why needed*: LLMs expect sequential tokens; patching enables transformer use. *Quick check*: Given series length T and patch length P, compute number of patches.

**Frozen Backbone Adaptation**: Keeping pre-trained model parameters fixed and only training small adapters. *Why needed*: Greatly reduces trainable parameters and avoids forgetting. *Quick check*: List advantages and potential drawbacks of freezing the backbone versus full fine-tuning.

**Positional Encoding for Time Series**: Adding temporal position information to tokens. *Why needed*: Transformers are permutation-invariant; positional embeddings inject temporal order crucial for forecasting. *Quick check*: Compare absolute vs. relative positional encodings in time series.

**Multivariate Forecasting Heads**: Final linear layers mapping model representations to future steps. *Why needed*: The output adapter adapts this head to new tasks. *Quick check*: Describe how a direct linear head differs from one with an rsLoRA adapter.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map** (plain text):
Input Time Series -> Patch Split -> Linear Token Embedding -> Add Positional Embedding (with rsLoRA) -> Frozen LLM (Transformer) -> Add Output Adapter (rsLoRA) -> Forecast

**Critical Path**: Data flows from patched tokens through the frozen transformer; adapters only modify the positional addition and final projection, leaving the core transformer untouched. Training updates only the low-rank adapter matrices.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- *Freezing backbone*: Saves parameters and compute but limits adaptability to drastically different domains.
- *Low-rank (r=16)*: Minimizes parameters but may underfit complex tasks; scaling factor βr mitigates instability but does not increase capacity.
- *Adapter injection points*: Positional embeddings capture temporal patterns; output layer aligns representations. Omitting one reduces performance.
- *Fixed patching*: Simple but fails on irregular sampling; future work addresses this.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Training loss diverges → likely βr scaling misconfigured or rank too low.
- Poor long-horizon forecasts → adapters may lack capacity; consider increasing rank or adding intermediate adapters.
- Overfitting on small datasets → due to low-rank adapters over-specializing; use regularization or lower LR.
- Zero-shot transfer gaps large → distribution shift between source and target data; domain adaptation needed.

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. *Adapter Placement Ablation*: Train with only output adapter vs. only positional adapter vs. both; measure accuracy and parameter count.
2. *Rank Sensitivity*: Sweep r ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32} to validate that performance saturates by r=16 and confirm gradient stability.
3. *Frozen vs. Fine-Tuned Backbone*: Compare against fine-tuning the entire LLM (with same total parameters) to isolate the benefit of freezing and using rsLoRA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adaptive Patching for Irregular Data**  
   - **Question**: How can adaptive patching mechanisms be developed to handle irregularly sampled time series data while maintaining One-for-All's parameter efficiency?  
   - **Basis**: [explicit] "Future work will focus on enhancing zero-shot performance through: (1) optimized adaptive patching for irregular data..."  
   - **Why unresolved**: Current One-for-All uses fixed-size patching, which may not optimally capture temporal patterns in irregularly sampled series.  
   - **Evidence to resolve**: A learnable patching strategy that dynamically adjusts patch boundaries based on data density, validated on irregular benchmarks (e.g., medical ICU data) with no significant parameter increase.

2. **Frequency-Aware rsLoRA Scaling**  
   - **Question**: Can the rsLoRA scaling factor be made frequency-aware to better capture multi-scale temporal dependencies, and how does this affect forecasting accuracy on datasets with complex seasonality?  
   - **Basis**: [explicit] "Future work will focus on enhancing zero-shot performance through: (2) improved frequency-aware rsLoRA scaling..."  
   - **Why unresolved**: Uniform scaling βr = α/√r across all frequencies may ignore that different frequency bands benefit from different effective ranks.  
   - **Evidence to resolve**: Design a frequency-decomposed rsLoRA (e.g., separate low-rank adapters per frequency band) and show significant improvements (p<0.05) on multi-seasonal datasets (e.g., electricity demand) over standard rsLoRA.

3. **Asymmetric Zero-Shot Transfer Performance**  
   - **Question**: Why does One-for-All exhibit asymmetric zero-shot transfer performance (M3→M4 outperforming M4→M3), and what architectural modifications could balance this directional sensitivity?  
   - **Basis**: [inferred] Table IV shows M3→M4 sMAPE 13.27 vs M4→M3 15.10; gap unexplained.  
   - **Why unresolved**: Asymmetry suggests domain shift biases in pre-trained LLM representations or adapter training; understanding is key for reliable zero-shot deployment.  
   - **Evidence to resolve**: Ablation experiments controlling for dataset shifts, visualization of adapter activations in both directions, and a domain alignment technique that reduces gap to <0.5 sMAPE.

4. **Low-Rank Success Factors**  
   - **Question**: To what extent does rsLoRA's superior low-rank performance (Rank 16 vs. 256+ for standard LoRA) stem from inherent properties of time series data versus architectural choices (freezing attention, specific injection points)?  
   - **Basis**: [inferred] Paper claims "95% accuracy saturation by Rank 16 (vs. Rank 256+ required in standard LoRA)" but factors not decomposed.  
   - **Why unresolved**: Disentangling factors would clarify whether rsLoRA's efficiency is time-series specific or generalizable.  
   - **Evidence to resolve**: Controlled experiments applying standard LoRA (with frozen backbone) to time series vs. NLP tasks, quantifying rank reduction attributable to rsLoRA stabilization alone.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to academic benchmarks (ETT, Weather, M3, M4); no real-world deployment evidence in target domains (healthcare, finance, environmental monitoring).
- Performance gains may partly originate from the pre-trained LLM priors rather than rsLoRA alone; ablation isolating rsLoRA contribution is missing.
- Reproducibility details (code release, hyperparameters, compute environment) are not provided in the paper.

## Confidence
- Parameter/Memory Reduction Claims: **High** (quantified reductions vs. cited methods)
- Gradient Stability Guarantee: **Medium-High** (theoretical βr scaling presented, but empirical validation on diverse dynamics lacking)
- Cross-Domain Generalization (edge deployment): **Low** (no evidence from target domains)
- Accuracy Parity (MSE=0.33): **Medium** (benchmark-dependent; may degrade on noisier, high-dimensional real data)

## Next Checks

2. Ablate the LLM backbone: replace with a smaller non-LLM encoder (e.g., linear projection) to isolate rsLoRA's contribution from pre-trained LLM priors.
3. Evaluate on high-frequency, ultra-long-sequence (>10,000 steps) and high-dimensional (>1,000 variables) datasets to stress-test the rank‑16 adapter capacity and identify potential failure modes.
