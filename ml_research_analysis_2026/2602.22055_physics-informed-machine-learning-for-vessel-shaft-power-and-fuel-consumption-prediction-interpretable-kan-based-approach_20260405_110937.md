# Physics-Informed Machine Learning for Vessel Shaft Power and Fuel Consumption Prediction: Interpretable KAN-based Approach

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.22055
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22055
- **Reference count**: 34
- **Primary result**: PI-KAN consistently achieves the lowest MAE and RMSE and the highest R² for shaft power and fuel consumption across all five vessels compared to polynomial and MLP baselines.

## Executive Summary
Accurately predicting vessel shaft power and fuel consumption while maintaining physical plausibility and interpretability remains a challenge for purely physics-based or data-driven models. This paper introduces the Physics-Informed Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (PI-KAN), which combines interpretable univariate feature transformations, an auto-tuned physics-informed loss function, and a leakage-free chained prediction pipeline. Evaluated on operational data from five cargo vessels, PI-KAN outperforms polynomial and multilayer perceptron baselines across all metrics. Moreover, the learned transformations reveal physically consistent patterns (e.g., cubic speed-power relationships, cosine-like directional effects), supporting both predictive accuracy and model interpretability for maritime decision support.

## Method Summary
PI-KAN is rooted in the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem, decomposing multivariate predictions into a sum of univariate feature transformations. Each input feature is processed by a dedicated small MLP (ϕ_p) to produce a transformed feature h_p, which are linearly combined to yield the final prediction. To enforce physical consistency, a physics-informed loss term is added, measuring discrepancy against empirical physical models (e.g., resistance-based power). The loss weight λ is auto-tuned during training via a multiplicative update rule to balance data fidelity and physics compliance. Predictions are made sequentially: first shaft rotational speed (RPM), then shaft power (using predicted RPM), then fuel consumption (using predicted power). A leakage-free out-of-fold stacking strategy ensures downstream models are trained on upstream OOF predictions rather than true values, preserving causal ordering and preventing optimistic bias.

## Key Results
- PI-KAN achieves the lowest mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) and the highest coefficient of determination (R²) for both shaft power and fuel consumption on all five vessels, outperforming polynomial and MLP baselines.
- The univariate transformations learned by PI-KAN exhibit physically plausible nonlinear patterns, such as cubic speed‑power relationships and cosine‑like effects for wind and wave directions.
- The auto‑tuned physics loss weight adapts effectively to vessel‑specific conditions, maintaining a stable balance between data and physics terms across training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Decomposable univariate transformations** – By assigning each feature its own neural network and summing their outputs, PI-KAN captures nonlinear effects per feature while keeping feature interactions linear. This structure, justified by the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem, enables direct inspection of each feature’s contribution, enhancing interpretability. The approach assumes vessel performance can be approximated additively; strong higher-order interactions (e.g., draft–trim–sea state couplings) could lead to underfitting.

**Auto‑tuned physics loss weight** – The total loss combines a data term (MAE) and a physics term (MAE against empirical resistance models). The weight λ is updated as λ ← clip(λ·exp(η(L_data − L_physics)), λ_min, λ_max). This multiplicative rule increases λ when data loss dominates (underfitting) and decreases it when physics loss dominates (overfitting noise), automatically balancing both objectives. The method adapts to vessel‑specific uncertainty without manual tuning of λ. However, if the embedded physical equations are systematically inaccurate for a vessel, auto‑tuning may converge to a biased λ that enforces incorrect physics.

**Leakage‑free chained prediction** – Physical causality dictates RPM → power → fuel. To mimic deployment where future RPM/power is unknown, the pipeline uses out‑of‑fold predictions: each downstream model is trained using OOF predictions from the upstream model as inputs. This prevents the downstream model from inadvertently using true upstream targets (which would be unavailable at prediction time), avoiding overly optimistic training performance. The design assumes strict sequential dependency; if fuel could be estimated directly from speed without RPM, the chaining adds unnecessary complexity and may propagate upstream errors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov‑Arnold Representation Theorem**: Guarantees that any multivariate continuous function can be represented as a finite sum of univariate functions. In PI‑KAN this justifies decomposing predictions into independently transformed features. *Why needed*: underpins the interpretable architecture. *Quick check*: How does a KAN differ from an MLP in terms of where nonlinearities are applied?
- **Physics‑Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**: Incorporate physical laws into the loss function to guide training toward physically plausible solutions. *Why needed*: clarifies purpose of the physics loss term. *Quick check*: In a PINN, where is the physical constraint typically enforced—architecture or loss function?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input features (operational, environmental, derived) → Per‑feature univariate subnetworks φ_p (small MLP) → Transformed features h_p → Weighted sum (w_p·h_p + b) → Raw prediction → Physics loss (L_data + λ L_physics) → Final model. The full system comprises three such models in a chain: RPM → Power → Fuel.
- **Critical path**: Train RPM model on initial features, collect its out‑of‑fold predictions; use these predictions as an additional input when training the Power model; then collect out‑of‑fold power predictions to train the Fuel model. The physics loss weight λ is tuned per vessel on a validation split before final training.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Interpretability vs. interaction expressivity: Univariate transformations are transparent but may miss complex feature interactions beyond linear combination.
  - Physics guidance strength: Auto‑tuned λ adapts to vessels but depends on the accuracy of the embedded physical equations; poor equations can mislead the model.
  - Sequential chaining: Enforces causality and prevents leakage but risks error propagation from upstream models.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Large gap between training and test errors indicates possible leakage in the chained pipeline.
  - λ hitting λ_min or λ_max suggests irreconcilable data and physics losses (e.g., inaccurate physical model).
  - Learned univariate functions showing physically impossible patterns (e.g., negative power at positive speed) indicate the physics loss is not effective.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare chained PI‑KAN vs. independent models (using true upstream values) to quantify leakage impact.
  2. Ablate auto‑tuning by using fixed λ grid; see if auto‑λ converges to near‑optimal values across vessels.
  3. Introduce leakage intentionally (using in‑fold true upstream values) and measure performance drop relative to the leakage‑free version.

## Limitations
- Underspecified architecture details of the univariate subnetworks (layers, units, activation order).
- Missing exact empirical resistance model equations and key constants (engine efficiency, fuel LHV), hindering faithful reproduction.
- Unclear λ auto‑tuning hyperparameters (update rate, clipping bounds) and their interaction with vessel‑wide tuning.
- Evaluation limited to proprietary data from five vessels, lacking public benchmarks and generalizability checks.

## Confidence
- **Performance**: Medium (consistent results but on limited proprietary data and simple baselines)
- **Interpretability**: Medium (visual patterns reported, no expert validation)
- **Decomposition**: Medium (theoretically grounded, break condition untested)
- **Auto‑tuned λ**: Low (missing details, no ablation proof)
- **Leakage prevention**: Medium (method sound, implementation details insufficient)

## Next Checks
1. Replicate PI‑KAN on a public or synthetic dataset with known physics to verify both predictive performance and interpretable patterns (e.g., cubic speed‑power).
