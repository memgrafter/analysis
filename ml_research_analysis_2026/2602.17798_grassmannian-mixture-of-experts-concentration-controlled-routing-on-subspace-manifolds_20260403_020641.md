# Grassmannian Mixture-of-Experts: Concentration-Controlled Routing on Subspace Manifolds

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.17798
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17798
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: Grassmannian MoE eliminates expert collapse and enables continuous sparsity control while matching or improving perplexity over standard MoE baselines.

## Executive Summary
Standard MoE routers rely on discrete top‑k gating, which causes expert collapse, unstable training, and lacks continuous sparsity control, forcing separate models for different compute budgets. This paper introduces Grassmannian Mixture-of-Experts (GrMoE), where each expert is represented as a subspace on the Grassmannian manifold. Routing weights derive from a Matrix Bingham distribution with concentration matrix **Λ**, providing a single, interpretable knob for smooth sparsity adjustment. An amortized variational network yields uncertainty‑aware assignments, and subspace overlap regularization enforces geometrically separated experts, preventing collapse. Evaluations on language models up to 2.7B parameters show 0% expert collapse, improved perplexity and load balance, and support for post‑hoc sparsity tuning without retraining.

## Method Summary
GrMoE conceptualizes each expert as a *k*<sub>r</sub>-dimensional subspace within the *d*-dimensional representation space, represented by an orthonormal frame **U**<sub>*e*</sub> on the Stiefel manifold. The affinity of a token **x** to expert *e* is the squared norm of its projection onto that subspace: ∥**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup>**x**∥<sup>2</sup>, invariant to basis choice. Routing probabilities follow a Matrix Bingham distribution: *g*<sub>*e*</sub>(**x**; **Λ**) ∝ exp(tr(**Λ**<sub>*e*</sub> **P**<sub>*e*</sub> **x** **x**<sup>⊤</sup>)), with **P**<sub>*e*</sub> = **U**<sub>*e*</sub>**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup>. Concentration parameters *κ*<sub>*e*</sub> (scalar case) control sharpness; a global scaling *α* adjusts sparsity at inference. An amortization network *h*<sub>*ϕ*</sub>(**x**) produces token‑specific multipliers *m*<sub>*e*</sub> = *h*<sub>*ϕ*</sub>(**x**)<sub>*e*</sub>. Logits become ℓ<sub>*e*</sub> = *α · m*<sub>*e*</sub> · *κ*<sub>*e*</sub> · ∥**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup>**x**∥<sup>2</sup>, and a softmax yields gating weights. To prevent collapse, a regularization term penalizes pairwise subspace overlap: **L**<sub>subspace</sub> = Σ<sub>*e*≠*e*′</sub> max(0, ∥**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup> **U**<sub>*e*′</sub>∥<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup> − *ρ*<sub>0</sub>*k*<sub>*r*</sub>). The total loss combines language modeling and *β*·**L**<sub>subspace</sub>.

## Key Results
- **Zero expert collapse**: Across 350M (8 experts), 1.3B (16 experts), and 2.7B (32 experts) language models, GrMoE achieves 0% collapse on all random seeds, whereas baselines exhibit collapse.
- **Improved perplexity and load balance**: GrMoE matches or beats baselines in perplexity (e.g., 13.8 vs 14.2 on 1.3B) and improves load balance by 15–30% relative.
- **Post‑hoc sparsity control**: The concentration matrix **Λ** provides a smooth knob to vary sparsity at inference without retraining, maintaining a monotonic perplexity‑throughput trade‑off. Learned *κ*<sub>*e*</sub> correlate with linguistic specialization, offering interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Representing each expert as a subspace on the Grassmannian manifold (rather than a single vector) captures multi‑dimensional specialization patterns and provides invariance to basis choice, reducing routing ambiguity.
- **Mechanism**: Each expert *e* is associated with an orthonormal frame **U**<sub>*e*</sub> ∈ ℝ<sup>*d*×*k*<sub>*r*</sub></sup>, defining a projection **P**<sub>*e*</sub> = **U**<sub>*e*</sub>**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup>. The routing affinity for token **x** is ∥**P**<sub>*e*</sub>**x**∥<sup>2</sup>, measuring the energy of **x** in the expert’s subspace. This quantity is invariant under right multiplication by **O**(*k*<sub>*r*</sub>), so it is well‑defined on the Grassmannian quotient space.
- **Core assumption**: Expert specialization can be modeled as a linear subspace of the representation space; tokens belonging to an expert have most of their energy in that subspace.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "each expert can be associated with a subspace in the representation space—the subspace of inputs that the expert specializes in."
  - [section 2.2] "The Grassmannian Gr(*k*, *d*) is the set of *k*-dimensional subspaces of ℝ<sup>*d*</sup> ... The projection distance between subspaces is *d*<sub>Gr</sub> = √(*k* − ∥**U**<sub>1</sub><sup>⊤</sup>**U**<sub>2</sub>∥<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>)."
  - [corpus] Several corpus papers explore geometric aspects of MoE (e.g., "Geometry-Preserving Aggregation for Mixture-of-Experts Embedding Models", "Unraveling the Localized Latents"), but none formalize expert subspaces on the Grassmannian manifold. GrMoE is the first to do so.
- **Break condition**: If expert specializations are essentially one‑dimensional (i.e., each expert responds primarily to a single direction), then using *k*<sub>*r*</sub> > 1 introduces redundant dimensions that may not be learned and could increase subspace overlap, raising collapse risk unless regularization is very strong.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The concentration matrix **Λ** of the Matrix Bingham distribution provides a continuous, interpretable knob to control routing sparsity, replacing discrete top‑*k* selection with a smooth, mathematically grounded mechanism.
- **Mechanism**: The routing distribution is *g*<sub>*e*</sub>(**x**; **Λ**) ∝ exp(tr(**Λ**<sub>*e*</sub> **P**<sub>*e*</sub> **x** **x**<sup>⊤</sup>)). In the scalar case (**Λ**<sub>*e*</sub> = *κ*<sub>*e*</sub>**I**), this becomes *g*<sub>*e*</sub> ∝ exp(*κ*<sub>*e*</sub> ∥**P**<sub>*e*</sub>**x**∥<sup>2</sup>). A global sparsity dial *α* ≥ 0 scales all *κ*<sub>*e*</sub>: *g*<sub>*e*</sub><sup>(*α*)</sup> ∝ exp(*α κ*<sub>*e*</sub> ∥**P**<sub>*e*</sub>**x**∥<sup>2</sup>). As *α* increases, the distribution sharpens; as *α* → 0, it approaches uniform. This yields a monotonic relationship between *α* and effective sparsity (e.g., expected top‑*k* mass).
- **Core assumption**: The entropy of the routing distribution is a monotonic function of the concentration parameters, and this relationship is stable across tokens and during training.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "gating weights arise from the concentration parameters of Matrix Bingham distributions. This construction yields a single, interpretable knob — the concentration matrix **Λ** — that continuously controls routing entropy, replacing discrete top‑*k* selection with a smooth, geometrically principled sparsity mechanism."
  - [section 3.2] "We define a token‑conditioned Bingham distribution over the discrete expert index *e*: *g*<sub>*e*</sub>(**x**; **Λ**) = exp(tr(**Λ**<sub>*e*</sub> **P**<sub>*e*</sub> **x** **x**<sup>⊤</sup>)) / Σ<sub>*e*′</sub> exp(tr(**Λ**<sub>*e*′</sub> **P**<sub>*e*′</sub> **x** **x**<sup>⊤</sup>))."
  - [corpus] The corpus contains work on gating mechanisms (e.g., "Confidence‑Guided Gate", "Adaptive Gating") but none derive sparsity control from a probability distribution’s concentration parameter. This is a novel theoretical construct.
- **Break condition**: At extreme concentration values (*κ*<sub>*e*</sub> → ∞), the softmax becomes numerically unstable and may degenerate to a single expert regardless of input, effectively becoming hard top‑1 and losing continuous control. The saddle‑point approximation for the Bingham normalizer *Z*(**Λ**) may also become inaccurate (paper notes error >5% when *κ* > 10).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Regularizing expert subspaces to have bounded overlap (via **L**<sub>subspace</sub>) ensures geometric separation, which provably suppresses expert collapse by an exponential bound on load imbalance (Theorem 2).
- **Mechanism**: **L**<sub>subspace</sub> = Σ<sub>*e*≠*e*′</sub> max(0, ∥**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup> **U**<sub>*e*′</sub>∥<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup> − *ρ*<sub>0</sub> *k*<sub>*r*</sub>). This penalizes pairwise projected Frobenius norm (which equals 2*k*<sub>*r*</sub> − 2*d*<sub>Gr</sub><sup>2</sup>) above a threshold *ρ*<sub>0</sub>*k*<sub>*r*</sub>. By keeping subspaces well‑separated, routing ambiguity is minimized. Theorem 2 proves that if the concentration‑adjusted separation gap Δ = *γ*(*κ*<sub>min</sub> − *ρ κ*<sub>max</sub>) > 0, then the coefficient of variation (CV) of expected expert loads satisfies CV(ĝ) ≤ (*N*−1) exp(−*α*Δ), i.e., collapse is exponentially unlikely.
- **Core assumption**: Expert collapse is primarily caused by geometric ambiguity in the routing space—when subspaces overlap, tokens can activate multiple experts, leading to uneven specialization. Reducing overlap directly reduces this ambiguity.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "subspace overlap regularization enforces geometrically separated experts, preventing collapse."
  - [section 4, Theorem 2] "If the concentration‑adjusted separation gap satisfies Δ ≡ *γ*(*κ*<sub>min</sub> − *ρ κ*<sub>max</sub>) > 0, then the load balance coefficient of variation (CV) rigorously satisfies an exponential bound: CV(ĝ) ≤ (*N*−1) exp(−*α*·Δ)."
  - [section 6.4.3] "Removing **L**<sub>subspace</sub> increases the collapse rate from 0% to 20% on the 350M model... confirms that geometric regularization is essential and not redundant with the Bingham gating mechanism."
  - [corpus] "Spectral Manifold Regularization for Stable and Modular Routing" also uses manifold regularization but focuses on spectral properties, not explicit subspace overlap. GrMoE’s approach is distinct in using direct subspace overlap with a provable collapse bound.
- **Break condition**: If the representation space dimension *d* is too small relative to *N·k*<sub>*r*</sub>, it may be impossible to achieve the required separation *ρ*<sub>0</sub>. Also, if *ρ*<sub>0</sub> is set too high (too strict), subspaces may be forced into unnatural configurations, harming specialization and perplexity (ablation shows *ρ*<sub>0</sub>=0.1 hurts PPL).

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Token **x** → Affinity *a*<sub>*e*</sub> = ∥**U**<sub>*e*</sub><sup>⊤</sup>**x**∥<sup>2</sup> (for all experts) → Token‑specific scaling via amortization network *h*<sub>*ϕ*</sub>(**x**) → Scaled logits ℓ<sub>*e*</sub> = *α · h*<sub>*ϕ*</sub>(**x**)<sub>*e*</sub> · *κ*<sub>*e*</sub> · *a*<sub>*e*</sub> → Softmax → Gating weights *g*<sub>*e*</sub> → Weighted sum of expert outputs → Model output. Expert subspaces **U**<sub>*e*</sub> are regularized by **L**<sub>subspace</sub> and optimized via Riemannian Adam.

### Critical path
1. Geometric insight: Experts specialize in subspaces → represent each expert by a Grassmannian point.
2. Choose Bingham distribution: Its concentration parameters give continuous sparsity control.
3. Derive routing formula and show equivalence to exact Bayesian inference (normalizers cancel).
4. Prove theoretical bounds linking concentration to entropy, top‑k mass, and collapse (Theorem 1, Corollary 1, Theorem 2).
5. Add subspace overlap regularization to enforce separation, which Theorem 2 shows yields exponential collapse bound.
6. Introduce amortization network for token‑specific concentration scaling, improving uncertainty handling and load balance.

### Design tradeoffs
- *k*<sub>*r*</sub> (routing rank): Smaller reduces routing cost but may underfit; larger increases capacity but risks overlap. Heuristic: *k*<sub>*r*</sub> ≈ *d*/*N*.
- *ρ*<sub>0</sub> (overlap threshold): Lower values enforce stricter separation, improving balance but potentially harming perplexity if too strict. Ablation suggests *ρ*<sub>0</sub> = 0.1 hurts PPL.
- *α* (sparsity dial): Fixed during training (usually *α*=1) but varied at inference. Higher *α* → fewer active experts, lower latency but higher perplexity. Smooth monotonic tradeoff enables post‑hoc tuning.
- Amortization: Adds ~0.5% parameters and negligible overhead; improves PPL by 0.2–0.3 and load balance. Recommended for *N* ≥ 16.
- Bingham normalizer: Saddle‑point approximation is efficient but may lose accuracy at extreme concentrations; regularization prevents extremes.

### Failure signatures
- High collapse rate (many seeds with an expert receiving <1% tokens): indicates subspaces not sufficiently separated; check max pairwise overlap *ρ*<sub>max</sub> (should be ≤ *ρ*<sub>0</sub>). Increase *β* or decrease *ρ*<sub>0</sub>.
- Poor perplexity relative to baselines: could be due to too strict regularization (*ρ*<sub>0</sub> too low) or too small *k*<sub>*r*</sub>. Try increasing *k*<sub>*r*</sub> or relaxing *ρ*<sub>0</sub>.
- Non‑monotonic sparsity‑entropy curve when varying *α*: suggests concentration variance is low or subspaces not well learned. Check learned *κ*<sub>*e*</sub> values; they should have reasonable variance.
- Large variance across random seeds: indicates instability; try stronger subspace regularization or different initialization.
- Numerical overflow in routing logits: if *κ*<sub>*e*</sub>∥**P**<sub>*e*</sub>**x**∥<sup>2</sup> becomes too large, exponentials may overflow. Monitor max logit values and consider clipping.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Synthetic routing task**: Train GrMoE on a synthetic dataset with known ground‑truth subspaces. Verify assignment accuracy >90% and 0% collapse across 50 seeds. Validates the core mechanism.
2. **Ablate L_subspace**: Train a GrMoE model without subspace regularization. Expect increased collapse rate (e.g., 20% on 350M) and worse load balance (CV ↑ ~0.1). Confirms necessity of geometric regularization.
3. **Post‑hoc sparsity tuning**: Take a trained GrMoE model (*α*=1) and vary *α* at inference. Plot PPL vs. throughput (or entropy). Expect a smooth monotonic tradeoff; compare against separately trained softmax top‑k models. Demonstrates the key practical advantage.

## Limitations
- **Scalability**: The experiments only go up to 32 experts; scaling to larger numbers (e.g., 64, 128) may face geometric constraints as the ambient dimension *d* must be sufficiently large relative to *N·k*<sub>*r*</sub> to maintain separated subspaces.
- **Theoretical bound tightness**: Theorem 2’s exponential collapse bound depends on an idealized separation gap Δ; its practical tightness, especially with heterogeneous *κ*<sub>*e*</sub>, is not fully validated.
- **Domain generality**: All evaluations are on language modeling; effectiveness on vision, speech, or multimodal tasks remains an open question.

## Next Checks
1. **Scale study**: Train GrMoE with 64 and 128 experts on >10B parameter models; evaluate collapse rate, load balance (CV), and training stability.
