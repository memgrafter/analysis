# CoTJudger: A Graph-Driven Framework for Automatic Evaluation of Chain-of-Thought Efficiency and Redundancy in LRMs

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.07078  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07078  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** CoTJudger reveals pervasive redundancy in LRMs (e.g., Qwen3-Max 86.5%) and introduces a graph‑based Redundancy Ratio to quantify reasoning waste.

## Executive Summary
Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often produce long chain‑of‑thought (CoT) traces, yet not every step meaningfully contributes to the solution. CoTJudger provides an automated framework to evaluate reasoning efficiency by converting free‑form CoTs into directed dependency graphs and extracting the Shortest Effective Path (SEP)—the minimal set of steps that still yields a correct answer. The framework computes a Redundancy Ratio (R) that quantifies wasteful steps. Evaluated on 21 LRMs across four domains, CoTJudger uncovers widespread over‑thinking: some models exhibit redundancy over 85%, while others achieve high accuracy with minimal steps. Graph metrics further distinguish failure patterns such as cyclic recurrence and semantic verbosity. CoTJudger offers a scalable, model‑agnostic metric that disentangles true reasoning ability from computational waste, enabling targeted diagnosis and future optimization of LRMs.

## Method Summary
CoTJudger processes a CoT trace through six sequential modules. First, **Step Segmentation and Atomization** splits the raw text into atomic reasoning steps using a heuristic combined with LLM refinement. Each step is then **classified** by a universal two‑tier taxonomy (e.g., *Intermediate‑Inference*, *Reflection‑or‑Verification*). Next, **Answer Detection and Verification** locates the final answer node and checks correctness against the ground truth, employing code execution for programming tasks. Using the classifications, a **directed graph** is built with forward, backward, and self‑loop edges that encode logical dependencies. The **forward subgraph** (ignoring non‑forward edges) is explored to find all shortest paths from the root to any answer node; each candidate is validated by an LLM to ensure logical sufficiency, yielding the SEP. Finally, metrics such as Redundancy Ratio ($R = (|V| - L_{\text{eff}})/|V|$), Average Degree, and Isolated Node Ratio are computed. The pipeline is domain‑agnostic and relies on GPT‑5 for classification and validation, with structured prompts ensuring consistent outputs.

## Key Results
- **Pervasive redundancy:** Qwen3‑Max shows 86.5% Redundancy Ratio, DeepSeek‑R1 78.0%, while models like `gpt-oss-120b` achieve low redundancy with high accuracy.
- **Distinct failure patterns:** High average degree indicates cyclic recurrence; isolated node ratio reflects semantic verbosity; post‑answer looping is detectable via graph structure.
- **Distillation inheritance:** Distilled models often exhibit high redundancy, inheriting teacher verbosity without corresponding reasoning robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Graph‑based representation of CoT exposes logical dependencies and makes redundancy computationally identifiable.  
**How:** CoT is converted into a directed graph where nodes are atomic steps and edges encode logical dependencies (forward, backward, self‑loop). The Shortest Effective Path (SEP) is extracted from the forward subgraph (non‑forward edges excluded) and validated for correctness; redundancy is the proportion of nodes not on any valid shortest path.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes graph conversion and SEP extraction; Section 4.4 argues linear sequences cannot capture backtracking/repetition; neighboring work focuses on token‑length, highlighting CoTJudger’s structural novelty.  
**Break condition:** If the forward subgraph omits necessary steps (e.g., an early verification that catches an error), the SEP may be invalid; LLM validation mitigates but inherits its own reliability limits.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Functional step classification (two‑tier taxonomy) enables interpretable attribution of redundancy to specific reasoning behaviors.  
**How:** Each atomic node is labeled with a domain‑agnostic category (e.g., *Reflection‑or‑Verification*, *Correction‑or‑Refinement*). Aggregating redundancy by step type reveals patterns like “verification obsession” or “compensatory redundancy.”  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions the classification system; Section 4.2 details data‑driven induction on 2,688 CoTs; Section 5.2 links redundancy patterns to classification distributions.  
**Break condition:** Misclassification (e.g., labeling a necessary inference as redundant) distorts analysis; inter‑rater consistency was 92.1% on the dev set, but generalization to unseen domains may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Redundancy Ratio (R) derived from SEP length provides a scalable, model‑agnostic efficiency metric disentangling reasoning ability from computational waste.  
**How:** Compute $R = (|V| - L_{\text{eff}})/|V|$, where $|V|$ is total nodes and $L_{\text{eff}}$ is SEP length. This ratio is comparable across models because SEP is based on structural necessity, not token count.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract defines R; Section 4.6 reports high R values correlating with over‑thinking; Section 5.2 shows distilled models inherit high R from teachers.  
**Break condition:** If the SEP is not truly minimal (due to graph errors or validation false negatives), R may underestimate efficiency; the 95.1% answer retention rate supports validity but does not guarantee optimality.

  *Why needed here:* CoT is the input artifact being analyzed; understanding its free‑form, multi‑step nature explains why a graph conversion is necessary to capture logical dependencies and inefficiencies.  
  *Quick check:* What distinguishes a Chain‑of‑Thought from a standard LLM response, and why can it become “over‑thinking”?

- **Concept: Directed graph for reasoning processes**  
  *Why needed here:* The core representation; nodes represent reasoning steps and edges encode dependencies, enabling algorithmic path extraction and topological analysis (e.g., cycles, isolated nodes).  
  *Quick check:* How does a directed edge in CoTJudger’s graph differ from simple sequential adjacency? What does a backward edge represent?

- **Concept: Shortest Effective Path (SEP)**  
  *Why needed here:* SEP defines the minimal set of steps that still yields a correct answer; it serves as the reference against which redundancy is measured.  
  *Quick check:* Why not simply take the first N steps until the answer appears? What makes SEP “effective” and “shortest” simultaneously?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Step Segmentation & Atomization → Node Classification → Answer Detection & Verification → Graph Construction → Path Extraction & Validation → Metrics Calculation.

**Critical path:**  
Segmentation → Classification → Answer Detection → Graph Construction → Path Extraction → Metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *LLM vs rules:* Using GPT‑5 ensures adaptability but introduces cost and variability; rule‑based alternatives would be cheaper but less robust to diverse CoT styles.  
- *Graph edge heuristics:* Defining backward edges via target‑step identification is interpretable but may miss implicit dependencies or create spurious links.  
- *SEP validation:* LLM‑based validation tolerates disfluency but can reject valid paths due to overly strict interpretation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High R + low accuracy: Model generates long CoTs without gaining correctness.  
- High average degree D (>1.8) + low SEP length: Indicates cyclic loops not captured by forward shortcuts.  
- LLM validation rejects all paths: Suggests broken CoT structure, incorrect answer detection, or flawed graph construction.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Visualize graphs for a few CoTs (e.g., using Graphviz) to inspect edge patterns (self‑loops for repetition, backward for correction). Confirm that SEP is indeed a subgraph.  
2. Ablation on node normalization: Disable merging of semantically equivalent nodes and measure change in R and graph metrics to test sensitivity to repetition detection.  
3. Cross‑validate SEPs: Take SEPs from three pipeline runs and have human annotators judge whether they retain core logic (or use the paper’s rewriting experiment: rewrite SEP, re‑run inference, check answer retention).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Taxonomy coverage:** Does the universal two‑tier taxonomy adequately cover the diversity of reasoning step types produced by future large reasoning models and novel domains, or does it require systematic extension?  
   *Basis:* Taxonomy was constructed from four domains via data‑driven induction; no evaluation on unseen domains.  
   *Why unresolved:* No experiments test coverage beyond the studied domains; new reasoning patterns (e.g., multimodal, interactive planning) might not fit.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Collect CoTs from a wide range of LLMs on diverse tasks, manually annotate a sample with the taxonomy, compute coverage/agreement, and iteratively refine; possibly design an automated expansion method.

2. **SEP minimality accuracy:** How accurately does the Shortest Effective Path (SEP) extraction algorithm recover the minimal set of logically necessary steps compared to human expert annotations, and what are the failure modes?  
   *Basis:* The paper uses GPT‑5 to validate extracted paths and reports 95.1% answer retention after rewriting; this ensures correctness but not minimality.  
   *Why unresolved:* Validation does not assess whether the SEP contains unnecessary steps or omits required ones; it relies on an LLM’s judgment; human gold standards are missing.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Create a dataset with human‑generated minimal reasoning paths; compare SEPs to these pathways; measure precision/recall of step inclusion; analyze mismatches and adjust algorithm accordingly.

3. **Training with redundancy metrics:** Can the Redundancy Ratio (R) and other CoTJudger metrics be used as training objectives to incentivize LRMs to produce more efficient chain‑of‑thought without sacrificing accuracy?  
   *Basis:* The authors present R as a diagnostic but do not explore its use as a learning signal; they suggest it could guide reward modeling.  
   *Why unresolved:* Unknown whether directly optimizing for lower R will degrade reasoning robustness or accuracy; the metric might be gamed by superficial shortening; the relationship between R and task performance is not causal.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Incorporate R as a reward in reinforcement learning or as a regularization term in fine‑tuning; conduct controlled studies comparing models trained with and without efficiency incentives; evaluate trade‑offs and robustness across tasks.

4. **Distillation and redundancy:** Why do distilled LRMs often exhibit higher redundancy than their teachers, and to what extent is this due to imitation of verbose traces versus lack of capacity constraints?  
   *Basis:* The paper observes distilled models generally have Redundancy Ratios exceeding 69%, while teacher models like DeepSeek‑R1 have R=78% but with different accuracy; the authors hypothesize that distillation transfers surface‑level verbosity without corresponding verification capabilities.  
   *Why unresolved:* The underlying mechanism is not studied; it is unclear whether redundancy is a byproduct of training data (teacher outputs), objective mismatch, or capacity bottlenecks. Without this understanding, mitigation strategies remain speculative.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Analyze teacher‑student training dynamics, e.g., compare token‑level imitation losses; design distillation objectives that penalize redundancy; measure resulting redundancy and accuracy; perform controlled experiments varying student capacity and teacher output filtering.

## Limitations
- **GPT‑5 specificity:** The pipeline relies on GPT‑5’s capabilities; substituting other LLMs may alter graph structures and redundancy metrics.
- **Dataset opacity:** The exact 896‑query sample is not released, preventing full replication of the large‑scale results.
- **SEP minimality assumption:** LLM‑based validation may reject valid minimal paths or accept suboptimal ones, affecting Redundancy Ratio accuracy.

## Confidence
- **Graph‑based redundancy detection:** Medium (structural approach validated, but SEP optimality unproven).  
- **Functional classification enables diagnosis:** Medium (92.1% inter‑rater agreement on dev set, cross‑domain robustness untested).  
- **Redundancy Ratio disentangles ability/waste:** High (strong correlation with over‑thinking patterns; 95.1% answer retention supports validity).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑LLM robustness test:** Replace GPT‑5 with GPT‑4o/Claude on a diverse set of 50 CoTs and measure variance in Redundancy Ratio.  
2. **Human‑grounded SEP audit:** Recruit domain experts to judge logical sufficiency of 30 extracted SEPs; aim for >80% agreement with the paper’s rewriting validation.  
3. **Edge‑type ablation study:** Systematically remove backward and self‑loop edges from the graph and observe the impact on Redundancy Ratio to quantify each edge type’s contribution.
