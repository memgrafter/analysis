# Match Made with Matrix Completion: Efficient Learning under Matching Interference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06982
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06982
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Nuclear norm regularization enables near-optimal matrix completion under dependent matching samples, leading to efficient learning with downstream decision-making guarantees for both optimal and stable matching.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses reward learning in two-sided matching markets where the high-dimensional reward matrix is low-rank but observed samples are dependent due to matching constraints (matching interference). The authors show that standard nuclear norm regularization remains theoretically effective under such dependence, providing near-optimal Frobenius norm error bounds. They further develop a double-enhanced estimator achieving sharp entry-wise errors, which is crucial for stable matching decisions. The framework extends to online settings, yielding algorithms with regret bounds that improve upon combinatorial bandit baselines by exploiting low-rank structure. Empirical results on synthetic and real labor market data confirm the practical benefits, demonstrating substantial efficiency gains.

## Method Summary
The core of the approach is low-rank matrix completion to learn the reward matrix Θ* from n observed matchings and noisy rewards. The primary estimator solves a regularized least-squares problem with a nuclear norm penalty, with theoretical guarantees holding even when samples are dependent. For offline tasks requiring entry-wise accuracy (e.g., stable matching), a double-enhancement procedure splits the data: first, a nuclear norm estimate bΘ is obtained; second, separate linear regressions on row and column latent features, using the SVD of bΘ as proxies, refine the factors to produce eΘ with tight ∞-norm guarantees. For online optimal matching, the algorithm explores random matchings for E_h = Θ(T^{2/3}) rounds, computes bΘ, and then exploits the greedy optimal matching thereafter. For online stable matching, double-enhancement is used in conjunction with Gale-Shapley. Tuning parameters like the regularization λ and exploration length E_h are derived from theory.

## Key Results
- Nuclear norm regularization yields near-optimal Frobenius norm error ∥ bΘ−Θ* ∥_F = eO(rK/√n) even under dependent matching samples.
- A double-enhanced estimator achieves a sharp entry-wise ∞-norm guarantee ∥ eΘ−Θ* ∥_∞ = eO(r²√(K/(nN))), enabling accurate stable matching.
- Online extensions (CombLRB for optimal matching, CompLRB for stable matching) achieve regret scaling as eO(r(N+K)T^{2/3}) and improve prior stable matching regret by a factor of at least N/r^4.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Nuclear norm regularization under matching interference. Restricted strong convexity (RSC) of the nuclear norm loss is preserved despite dependent sampling, via a new linearization technique that bounds a key error term using Hadamard products and the low-rank structure. The bound relies on a minimum sampling probability ν, which holds under typical matching constraints (e.g., random injection), ensuring the estimator attains the same near-optimal Frobenius error as with independent data.

**Mechanism 2:** Double-enhancement for entry-wise accuracy. After obtaining bΘ, the method runs separate least-squares regressions for each worker row (using features from bV) and each job column (using features from bU⊤). The resulting factor estimates eU, eV are aligned via an SVD on eU to form eΘ = U₁Q₁eV⊤. This leverages the linear factorization and controls row-wise ℓ₂,∞ errors under a spikiness condition, yielding a sharp ∞-norm bound unattainable directly from bΘ.

**Mechanism 3:** Online learning with low-rank exploitation. In an initial exploration phase (E_h random matchings), the algorithm gathers data to recover Θ*. The number of effective parameters is only O(r(N+K)) instead of NK, so learning scales with rank r. Choosing E_h ∝ T^{2/3} (optimal matching) or O(log T) (stable matching) balances exploration and exploitation optimally, giving regret improvements over combinatorial bandits.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Offline: Matcher → NoisyObserver → samples → NuclearNormEstimator → DoubleEnhancer → DecisionMaker  
  Online (optimal): BanditExplorer → samples → NuclearNormEstimator → DecisionMaker  
  Online (stable): BanditExplorer → samples → NuclearNormEstimator → DoubleEnhancer → StableMatcher
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Offline: Matcher generates matchings → NoisyObserver adds noise → NuclearNormEstimator solves (3.1) → (Optional) DoubleEnhancer → DecisionMaker selects optimal/stable matching.  
  2. Online: BanditExplorer collects random matchings for E_h rounds → NuclearNormEstimator recovers bΘ → DoubleEnhancer (for stable) → DecisionMaker/StableMatcher computes target matching → exploit for remaining rounds.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Nuclear norm vs. explicit rank: Nuclear norm is convex but may be loose; explicit rank constraint can improve rates but requires tuning.  
  - Exploration length E_h: Too short yields poor estimate; too long increases regret. Theory suggests E_h ∝ T^{2/3} (optimal) or O(log T) (stable), but practical tuning may differ.  
  - Double-enhancement cost: Needs extra data split; only necessary for entry-wise guarantees; sensitive to first-stage accuracy.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - First-stage estimate not low-rank (many singular values) indicates λ mis-specification.  
  - Large condition numbers in row/column regression designs cause unstable OLS; ridge may be needed.  
  - Exploration insufficient: if ∥ bΘ−Θ* ∥_∞ > Δ_min/2, stable matching rankings may be incorrect.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Offline Frobenius validation: Compare nuclear norm estimator (Low Rank) vs. naive baseline on synthetic data; verify ∥ bΘ−Θ* ∥_F = eO(rK/√n).  
  2. Offline entry-wise validation: Run double-enhancement; confirm ∥ eΘ−Θ* ∥_∞ improves over plain nuclear norm and matches eO(r²√(K/(nN))) rate.  
  3. Online regret validation: Test CombLRB vs. CUCB for optimal matching; check CombLRB regret = eO(r(N+K)T^{2/3}) beats O(NK√T). Test CompLRB vs. CompB for stable matching; verify improvement factor ≈ N/r^4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to many-to-many matchings:** The analysis is specialized for one-to-one matching. Extensions to many-to-many or network-based interference patterns would require fundamentally new analytical tools because the dependency structure changes.  
- **Approximate low-rank robustness:** All guarantees assume exact rank r. It is unknown how methods perform if the reward matrix is only approximately low-rank (e.g., plus sparse/low-norm deviations). Real data may violate strict low-rankness; robustness analysis is absent.  
- **Optimal data-splitting for double-enhancement:** The current 50-50 split between first-stage and enhancement regressions is not theoretically justified. The trade‑off between n₁ (improving bΘ) and n₂ (quality of regressions) might be optimized adaptively.  
- **High-dimensional asymptotics:** The analysis treats N,K fixed while n → ∞. In growing platforms, N and K may also increase. The scaling of sample complexity and regret with joint growth of n, N, K, T remains open.

## Limitations
- The real-data experiment uses proprietary Revelio Labs data, preventing full replication of those results.
- Implementation details (specific solver for nuclear norm optimization, treatment of rank constraint, λ-tuning parameter α) are omitted, potentially causing deviations between reported and reproduced performance.
- Ambiguities in algorithmic choices: exploration length E_h formula (e.g., logarithm base), handling of ties in stable matching, and whether ridge regularization is needed in double-enhancement regressions are not clarified.

## Confidence
- Nuclear norm effectiveness under interference: **High** for theoretical Frobenius bound; **Medium** for practical implementation due to unspecified tuning.
- Double-enhanced entry-wise guarantee: **Medium** – mechanism is sound but relies on accurate first-stage SVD and unregularized OLS, which may be sensitive.
- Online regret improvement: **Medium** – algorithmic ideas are plausible, but missing details on E_h and Δ_min may affect measured regret.
