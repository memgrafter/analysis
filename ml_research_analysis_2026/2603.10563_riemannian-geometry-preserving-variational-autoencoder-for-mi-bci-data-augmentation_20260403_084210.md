# Riemannian Geometry-Preserving Variational Autoencoder for MI-BCI Data Augmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10563
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10563
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: RGP-VAE generates 100% valid SPD covariance matrices and improves cross-subject classification for KNN (+3.49%) while SVC degrades (-4.01%), MDM stable.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Riemannian Geometry-Preserving Variational Autoencoder (RGP-VAE) for generating synthetic EEG covariance matrices in motor imagery brain-computer interfaces (MI-BCI). The method respects the symmetric positive-definite (SPD) structure of covariance matrices by operating on the Riemannian manifold, using parallel transport for subject alignment and log/exp maps for tangent space projection. On a 12-subject dataset, it achieves 100% valid SPD outputs and statistical fidelity close to original data. However, downstream utility varies by classifier: KNN benefits while SVC suffers, indicating that synthetic data distribution may not uniformly aid all models.

## Method Summary
The RGP-VAE processes EEG covariance matrices by first aligning each subject's data to a global class reference mean using parallel transport, then projecting to the tangent space via the log map. The vectorized tangent vectors are fed to a VAE with an encoder-decoder MLP. The loss combines a tangent-space Euclidean reconstruction, a manifold reconstruction (using the Averaged Riemannian Metric, AIRM), a KL divergence term (annealed), and a diversity regularizer. During generation, noise scaling and prior sampling produce synthetic matrices, which are mapped back to the SPD manifold via the exp map and adjusted for numerical stability (eigenvalue thresholds). The training uses leave-one-subject-out cross-validation and AdamW optimizer.

## Key Results
- 100% of synthetic outputs are valid SPD matrices, whereas a standard VAE produces >40% invalid.
- Statistical variance ratio between original and synthetic is 1.06, and geometric diversity (mean intra-class AIRM) matches original (~2.03).
- Cross-subject classification: KNN balanced accuracy improves by up to +3.49% (p=0.002), SVC degrades by -4.01% (p=0.002), MDM is stable.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The RGP-VAE preserves the Riemannian geometry of the SPD manifold throughout the generative process. Parallel transport aligns subject-specific distributions to a common reference without distorting distances, enabling the VAE to learn a shared latent structure. Tangent space projection allows standard Euclidean operations while the exp map ensures outputs are valid SPD. The combined loss (tangent + manifold reconstruction) encourages fidelity to both Euclidean and Riemannian structures, and the diversity term prevents collapse to class means. This geometric awareness is why the standard VAE fails (generating invalid matrices) and RGP-VAE succeeds.

## Foundational Learning
- **SPD matrices**: Covariance matrices are symmetric positive-definite; operations must maintain positive eigenvalues. *Why needed*: Outputs must be valid covariances. *Quick check*: After exp map, compute eigenvalues; all > 1e-6.
- **Riemannian manifold of SPD**: The set of SPD matrices forms a manifold with a metric (AIRM) that accounts for curvature. *Why needed*: Distances and means are defined differently than Euclidean. *Quick check*: Use log/exp maps to move between manifold and tangent space; check that geodesic distance is AIRM.
- **Parallel transport**: Transports vectors between tangent spaces while preserving inner products, used for aligning subject data. *Why needed*: To map each subject's distribution to a common reference without distortion. *Quick check*: After transport, the mean of transported matrices should equal the reference mean when mapped back.
- **Log/exp maps**: Map between manifold and tangent space at a reference point. *Why needed*: To use standard neural networks in tangent space (Euclidean). *Quick check*: For a matrix X, log(X) at reference P should be symmetric; exp(log(X)) should recover X.
- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**: Learns a latent distribution and generates data. *Why needed*: To model the distribution of covariance matrices and sample new ones. *Quick check*: Reconstruction loss and KL divergence in ELBO decrease during training.
- **Diversity regularization**: The γ term in loss ensures synthetic outputs cover the original spread. *Why needed*: To avoid mode collapse and maintain geometric diversity. *Quick check*: Mean pairwise AIRM of synthetic data should match original.

## Architecture Onboarding
The pipeline: 
EEG -> Preprocessing (bandpass, scaling, EMS) -> Covariance -> Alignment (parallel transport to global class mean) -> Tangent projection (log) -> Vectorization -> Encoder MLP -> Latent sample -> Decoder MLP -> Symmetrization -> Exp map -> Eigenvalue adjustment -> Synthetic SPD.

Critical path: Alignment and tangent projection are essential because they make the data amenable to Euclidean operations while preserving geometric relationships. Without them, the VAE would generate invalid matrices or lose manifold structure.

Design tradeoffs:
- Tangent space projection simplifies the learning problem but discards curvature information; the manifold loss attempts to compensate.
- Parallel transport alignment may reduce subject-specific variability if the global mean over-subjects washes out individual patterns.
- Balancing the loss weights (β for KL, γ for diversity) is crucial; too high KL may cause latent collapse, too low may hurt diversity.

Failure signatures:
- Non-SPD outputs (asymmetry or non-positive eigenvalues) after exp map, indicating numerical instability or poor training.
- Degraded classification performance (e.g., SVC drop) suggests synthetic data does not capture the discriminative structure.
- High intra-class geometric similarity (low AIRM) in synthetic data compared to original indicates mode collapse.

First experiments:
1. Verify data pipeline: Preprocess one subject, compute covariances, align, project to tangent, reconstruct (without VAE) via exp map; check SPD validity and reconstruction error (AIRM).
2. Train RGP-VAE on two subjects with a small hidden size (e.g., 16) and inspect synthetic outputs: % valid SPD, variance ratio, and intra-class AIRM vs original.
3. Run LOSO-CV with MDM on original data only to establish baseline, then with augmented data (1:5 original:synthetic) and compare balanced accuracy.

## Limitations
- Parallel transport implementation and reference mean computation order are incompletely specified, risking misalignment.
- Noise scaling (ε_i=2.2) during generation is ambiguous in its exact application.
- Validation is restricted to 12 subjects from a single dataset; generalization to other cohorts is unknown.

## Next Checks
1. **Parallel transport replication**: Implement alignment using the paper's described steps and an alternative (e.g., MartINE) to quantify consistency in subject-level Fréchet mean shifts.
2. **Noise scaling ablation**: Vary ε_i (1.0, 2.2, 3.0) during generation and compute synthetic intra-class AIRM; verify that the reported diversity match occurs only at ε_i=2.2.
3. **Cross-dataset generalization**: Apply trained RGP-VAE to an external MI-BCI dataset (e.g., BCI Competition IV-2a) to assess whether SPD validity and fidelity metrics hold beyond the Faller et al. cohort.
