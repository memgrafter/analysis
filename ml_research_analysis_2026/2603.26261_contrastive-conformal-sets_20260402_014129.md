# Contrastive Conformal Sets
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26261
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26261
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Learned, minimum-volume conformal sets achieve nominal coverage while substantially increasing negative exclusion (e.g., 49.0% vs 32.4% on simulation; 77.6% vs 10.8% on CIFAR-100) and reducing log‑volume compared to ℓ2‑ball and Mahalanobis baselines.

## Executive Summary
Contrastive conformal sets extend conformal prediction to semantic feature spaces, constructing uncertainty sets that guarantee inclusion of positives with high probability while aggressively excluding negatives. The method learns a flexible, generalized multi‑norm distance metric and optimizes either set volume (when negatives are absent) or negative exclusion directly (when available). Optional joint InfoNCE fine‑tuning aligns the embedding for a better trade‑off. Under exchangeability, the resulting sets provide distribution‑free coverage; for nested families, volume minimization is provably equivalent to maximizing exclusion. Empirical evaluations on simulated 3D data and image datasets (CIFAR‑100, ImageNet‑100) confirm nominal coverage, significantly higher exclusion rates, lower volumes, and strong out‑of‑distribution detection.

## Method Summary
Starting from a pretrained contrastive encoder, the approach learns a metric \(g_{M,p}(Z,z)\) with per‑dimension scales \(m_j\) and exponents \(p_j\) (generalized multi‑norm) or a full matrix \(M\) (single‑norm). A separate training set \(D_{\text{train}}\) is used: for each anchor batch, positives and negatives are sampled and embedded. A dynamic threshold \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) is computed as the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of the batch’s positive distances (detached from the graph). The loss either minimizes the average log‑volume of the sets \(\{z: g \le \hat{q}_\alpha\}\) (Eq. 14) or maximizes a sigmoid‑approximated exclusion loss \(\sum \sigma_T(g(Z, Z_{\text{neg}})-\hat{q}_\alpha)\). Optionally, the encoder’s penultimate layer is jointly fine‑tuned with an InfoNCE term. After training, a final threshold \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) is calibrated on a held‑out set; for a test point \(Z\), the conformal set is \(\{z: g_{M,p}(Z,z) \le \hat{q}_\alpha\}\). The sets inherit distribution‑free coverage under exchangeability; for nested families, volume minimization implies maximum exclusion.

## Key Results
- Attains target coverage (\(\ge 1-\alpha\)) across simulation, CIFAR‑100, and ImageNet‑100.
- Substantially higher negative exclusion: 49.0% vs 32.4% (simulation) and 77.6% vs 10.8% (CIFAR‑100) relative to ℓ2‑ball / Mahalanobis.
- Lower log‑volume than all baselines.
- Outperforms dedicated OOD detection methods on AUROC/FPR95 without extra training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Minimum-volume sets maximize exclusion for nested families.
- **Claim:** For a nested family of sets where inclusion and volume increase with a threshold parameter \(t\), the smallest‑volume set satisfying the coverage constraint \(P(\text{positive} \in \text{set}) \ge 1-\alpha\) automatically maximizes negative exclusion.
- **Mechanism:** The proof (Proposition 1, Appendix A.3) shows that the negative exclusion probability \(q_-(t)=1-P_-(t)\) is non‑increasing in \(t\); thus the minimal \(t\) meeting coverage yields the largest \(q_-\).
- **Core assumption:** The candidate set family is nested and volume is a non‑decreasing function of \(t\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.3 (Proposition 1); Appendix A.3.
- **Break condition:** If the learned family is not nested (e.g., due to per‑dimension exponents causing non‑monotonic shape changes with \(t\)), volume minimization may fail to maximize exclusion.

### Mechanism 2: Learned generalized multi‑norm constraints adapt set geometry.
- **Claim:** Flexible metric with learnable exponents \(p_j\) and scalings \(m_j\) shapes sets that exclude more negatives while dynamic quantile calibration enforces coverage.
- **Mechanism:** The distance \(g_{M,p}(Z,z)\) defines set boundaries. During training, the threshold \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) is the empirical \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of positive distances in the batch (detached). The loss minimizes log‑volume (Eq. 19) or maximizes a sigmoid‑approximated negative exclusion loss (Eq. 16), driving the metric to pack positives tightly and push negatives outside.
- **Core assumption:** Exchangeability between training and calibration; the sigmoid approximation with temperature \(T\) is a valid differentiable surrogate; the detached quantile does not impair optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 (generalized hyper‑ball); Section 4.2 (optimization modifications).
- **Break condition:** Shifts in the positive distance distribution between training and calibration may cause the dynamic training quantile to differ from the final static threshold, violating coverage.

### Mechanism 3: Joint InfoNCE fine‑tuning improves embedding alignment.
- **Claim:** Optimizing the encoder alongside the metric via an InfoNCE term further improves the inclusion‑exclusion trade‑off.
- **Mechanism:** The combined objective \(\max_{M,p,\theta} J_{\text{neg+vol}} - \lambda_{\text{InfoNCE}} L_{\text{InfoNCE}}(\theta)\) encourages the embedding to bring positives closer and separate negatives, synergizing with the learned metric.
- **Core assumption:** The pretrained encoder can be fine‑tuned without catastrophic forgetting; alternating updates stabilize joint optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 (joint optimization equation).
- **Break condition:** Excessive fine‑tuning (high \(\lambda_{\text{InfoNCE}}\) or many epochs) may distort the space and break the exchangeability needed for coverage guarantees.

## Foundational Learning
- **Split Conformal Prediction:** A distribution‑free method that calibrates a threshold on a held‑out set to guarantee marginal coverage \(P(Y \in \hat{C}(X)) \ge 1-\alpha\). *Why needed:* Provides the theoretical coverage guarantee for the constructed sets. *Quick check:* For \(\alpha=0.1\) and a calibration set of 100 positive distances, which rank determines \(\hat{q}_\alpha\)? (Answer: \(\lceil 0.9 \times (100+1)\rceil = 91\).)
- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE):** Learns embeddings by minimizing a loss that pulls together positive pairs (e.g., augmentations of the same image) and pushes apart negatives. *Why needed:* Defines the notion of “positive” and produces the initial semantic feature space. *Quick check:* What is the effect of decreasing the temperature \(\tau\) in the InfoNCE loss? (Lower \(\tau\) increases the penalty on negatives, sharpening the distribution.)
- **Nested Families & Volume Minimization:** A set family \(\{\mathcal{B}_t\}\) is nested if \(t_1 < t_2 \Rightarrow \mathcal{B}_{t_1} \subseteq \mathcal{B}_{t_2}\). *Why needed:* Proposition 1 shows that within a nested family, the smallest set achieving coverage also maximizes negative exclusion, justifying volume minimization when negatives are unavailable. *Quick check:* Given two concentric \(\ell_2\)-balls, which has smaller volume? If both achieve 95% coverage, which excludes more negatives? (Smaller volume excludes more.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pretrained Encoder \(\theta\) → Embedding \(Z\) → Learned Metric \((M,p)\) → Distance \(g_{M,p}\) → Quantile threshold \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) → Conformal Set \(\mathcal{N}(Z)=\{z : g_{M,p}(Z,z) \le \hat{q}_\alpha\}\)

**Critical path:**
1. Split data: pretraining set, \(D_{\text{train}}\) (metric learning), \(D_{\text{cal}}\) (calibration), \(D_{\text{test}}\) (evaluation).
2. For each epoch on \(D_{\text{train}}\): compute embeddings; sample \(k\) positives/negatives per anchor; compute distances \(g\); compute dynamic \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) as the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of batch positive distances (detached); compute loss \(J\) (either \(J_{\text{vol}}\) or \(J_{\text{neg}}\) or joint); update \((M,p)\) [and optionally \(\theta\)].
3. After training, freeze parameters; compute final \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) on \(D_{\text{cal}}\) using all positive distances.
4. For a test anchor, output set \(\{z : g(Z,z) \le \hat{q}_\alpha\}\).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Metric flexibility vs. stability:** Generalized multi‑norm yields lower volume but can be unstable; single‑norm (matrix) is more stable but less expressive.
- **Negatives available:** If negatives exist, optimize \(J_{\text{neg}}\) or \(J_{\text{neg+vol}}\); otherwise rely on \(J_{\text{vol}}\) and the nested‑family guarantee.
- **Fine‑tuning:** Joint InfoNCE can improve exclusion but risks breaking coverage if embedding drifts from calibration distribution.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Coverage < 1−α** on test: likely non‑exchangeable splits, over‑fine‑tuning, or mismatch between dynamic training threshold and final calibration threshold.
- **Low exclusion:** metric failed to separate; check optimizer settings, regularization, negative sampling representativeness.
- **Log‑volume extremes/NaN:** exponents \(p_j\) diverging or scales \(m_j \to 0\); add regularization and stable log‑volume computation.
- **Poor OOD detection:** set not sharp; may indicate too strong volume regularization or weak negatives in training.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Simulation sanity check:** Train a generalized multi‑norm (volume objective only) on a 3D dataset with mixed Gaussians/banana shapes. Verify test coverage ≥ 0.95; visualize set contours to inspect nesting.
2. **Baseline comparison:** On CIFAR‑100 (split training 50/50), train Single (Neg,V ol) and Generalized (Neg,V ol). Compare negative exclusion rates and log‑volume against ℓ2‑ball and Mahalanobis; expect substantial improvements.
3. **Coverage validation:** For a trained model (e.g., Generalized (V ol) on ImageNet‑100), compute test coverage after calibrating \(\hat{q}_\alpha\) on \(D_{\text{cal}}\). Perform a binomial test to confirm coverage is not significantly different from \(1-\alpha=0.95\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the geometric properties of the learned conformal sets be directly leveraged to improve downstream tasks (e.g., retrieval) without ad‑hoc scoring?
2. Does Proposition 1’s guarantee hold when the learned set families are not perfectly nested due to optimization constraints or non‑convex geometries?
3. How sensitive are the learned metric parameters to the sigmoid temperature \(T\), and does this sensitivity degrade the distribution‑free coverage guarantee?
4. Can the framework be extended to non‑Euclidean semantic spaces (hyperbolic, spherical) where distance and volume definitions differ fundamentally?
5. Under what conditions does the learned metric overfit to the specific negative sampling scheme used during training, and how does this affect oracle negative exclusion guarantees?

## Limitations
- Theoretical optimality hinges on nested families; generalized multi‑norm sets may violate nesting during optimization, leading to suboptimal exclusion.
- Dynamic quantile use during training may introduce a distribution shift that threatens the coverage guarantee.
- Joint InfoNCE fine‑tuning lacks theoretical analysis and can break exchangeability if not carefully controlled.
- Experimental details (exact data splits, baseline hyperparameters) are incompletely specified, hindering reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Nested‑family optimality:** High for strictly nested families (proof sound), Low that the specific learned family is nested.
- **Coverage guarantee:** High for a fixed metric under exchangeability, Medium when the metric is learned/fine‑tuned due to possible shift.
- **Empirical gains (exclusion, OOD):** Medium; results are strong but lack variance estimates and full baseline configurations.

## Next Checks
1. Test whether the learned generalized multi‑norm family is nested: sample random \((M,p)\), generate sets for increasing \(t\), and verify monotonic set inclusion.
2. Run the full pipeline on 5 random data splits of CIFAR‑100/ImageNet‑100; report mean ± std for coverage, exclusion, and log‑volume to assess robustness.
3. Ablate the InfoNCE weight \(\lambda\) (e.g., 0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0) and measure coverage gap and exclusion change to quantify the trade‑off.
