# CHLU: The Causal Hamiltonian Learning Unit as a Symplectic Primitive for Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01768  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01768  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** CHLU uses a relativistic Hamiltonian with symplectic integration to guarantee phase-space volume conservation and bounded velocities, achieving infinite-horizon stability on trajectory tasks where LSTM diverges and Neural ODE collapses.

## Executive Summary
Temporal deep learning models face a stability–dissipation trade-off: LSTMs are unstable over long horizons, while Neural ODEs tend to dissipate dynamics into collapse. This paper introduces the Causal Hamiltonian Learning Unit (CHLU), a recurrent primitive built on a relativistic Hamiltonian structure and symplectic (Velocity Verlet) integration. The relativistic kinetic term enforces a strict velocity limit, while symplectic integration preserves phase-space volume, enabling stable, infinite-horizon modeling. Training uses a Wake-Sleep algorithm with Hamiltonian contrastive divergence to shape a potential energy landscape, and generation proceeds via Langevin dynamics with temperature annealing. Key results show bounded long-term orbit tracing, kinetic safety under perturbation, and qualitative digit generation from noisy initializations, demonstrating a new approach to stable, generative sequence modeling.

## Method Summary
CHLU defines a separable Hamiltonian \( H(q,p) = T(p) + V_\theta(q) + \alpha\|q\|^2 \), where the kinetic term \( T(p) = \sqrt{c^2 p^T M^{-1} p + m_0^2 c^4} \) imposes a relativistic speed limit \( c \). State updates use a Dissipative Velocity Verlet integrator (with optional friction \( \gamma \)) to maintain symplecticity. Training alternates between a Wake phase (clamped to data trajectories, minimizing MSE and a Lyapunov regularizer) and a Sleep phase (free evolution from a replay buffer, generating high-energy hallucinations). Weight updates follow a contrastive rule \( \Delta\theta \propto -\nabla_\theta H(z_{\text{wake}}) + \nabla_\theta H(z_{\text{sleep}}) \). For generation, momentum updates switch to Langevin dynamics with an annealing temperature schedule, allowing the system to thermodynamically relax into learned attractors.

## Key Results
- Bounded long-term stability: CHLU traces a lemniscate orbit indefinitely, while LSTM diverges and Neural ODE collapses.
- Kinetic safety: Under perturbations, velocity saturates at the configured causal limit \( c \), converting noise into phase shifts rather than magnitude explosions.
- Qualitative generation: Langevin dynamics with annealing produces distinct MNIST digit modes from noisy-centric initialization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Relativistic Kinetic Governor
- **Claim:** Enforces strictly bounded velocity updates, preventing kinetic explosions.
- **Mechanism:** The relativistic kinetic term \( T(p) = \sqrt{c^2 p^T M^{-1} p + m_0^2 c^4} \) yields a velocity \( \dot{q} = \nabla_p T \) that saturates at \( c \) as \( |p| \to \infty \), a structural constraint not learned.
- **Core assumption:** A configurable causal speed limit \( c \) is a sufficient prior to prevent unbounded momentum-driven instabilities.
- **Break condition:** Extremely stiff potential \( V_\theta \) (high curvature) can destabilize the integrator regardless of the governor, requiring adaptive step sizes that break symplecticity.

### Mechanism 2: Symplectic Integration
- **Claim:** Velocity Verlet integration ensures long-term phase-space volume conservation (or controlled dissipation), enabling infinite-horizon topological fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** The discrete map \( (q_t, p_t) \mapsto (q_{t+1}, p_{t+1}) \) derived from a discrete Lagrangian is exactly symplectic; with \( \gamma=0 \), it preserves phase-space volume (discrete Liouville’s theorem). Friction \( \gamma > 0 \) introduces dissipation.
- **Core assumption:** Preserving the geometric structure of Hamiltonian flow prevents accumulation of numerical errors that cause divergence/collapse.
- **Break condition:** Non-symplectic modifications (e.g., adaptive time-stepping or certain nonlinearities in \( V_\theta \)) or excessive integration noise degrade volume preservation.

### Mechanism 3: Wake-Sleep Hamiltonian Contrastive Divergence
- **Claim:** The training objective shapes \( V_\theta \) so data manifolds correspond to low-energy attractors, enabling generation via thermodynamic relaxation.
- **Mechanism:** Wake phase pulls trajectories toward data points (low energy); Sleep phase pushes hallucinations to high energy. Update \( \Delta\theta \propto -\nabla_\theta H(z_{\text{wake}}) + \nabla_\theta H(z_{\text{sleep}}) \) steepens energy wells around data modes. Langevin dynamics with annealing then relaxes into these wells.
- **Core assumption:** Contrasting physically plausible (data-like) trajectories against implausible ones via an energy-based objective learns an implicit data distribution.
- **Break condition:** If the replay buffer fills with low-energy hallucinations, the contrastive signal vanishes, preventing sharpening of the landscape; rapid annealing can trap generation in poor local minima.

## Architecture Onboarding
Hamiltonian Engine -> Integrator Core -> [Training Loop / Generative Sampler]

Critical path: Hamiltonian definition \( (H) \) → Integrator output \( (z_{t+1}) \) → Training objective \( (L_{\text{wake}}, L_{\text{sleep}}) \) → Weight update \( (\Delta\theta) \). Generative path diverges at Integrator Core with stochastic momentum updates.

Design tradeoffs:
- Stability vs. Forgetting: \( \gamma=0 \) conserves all information (including noise); \( \gamma>0 \) enables forgetfulness but sacrifices infinite-horizon guarantee.
- Symplectic Accuracy vs. Stiffness: Velocity Verlet fails for extremely stiff \( V_\theta \), requiring smaller \( \epsilon \) or adaptive stepping (breaks symplecticity).
- Contrastive Signal Strength: A diverse, high-energy replay buffer provides strong learning; buffer filled with low-energy noise weakens contrast.

Failure signatures:
1. Unbounded velocities despite perturbations: relativistic governor ineffective or \( c \) too large.
2. Phase-space volume drift over long conservative runs: broken symplecticity (e.g., from non-symplectic \( V_\theta \) gradients).
3. Collapsed generation: all outputs converge to a single low-information state; overly aggressive annealing or \( \gamma \).
4. Divergent/collapsed trajectories in inference: misconfigured \( V_\theta \) or integrator step size \( \epsilon \).

First 3 experiments:
1. Lemniscate Tracing: Train on 3 cycles, infer 50 steps; final orbit should be bounded and closed.
2. Perturbed Sine Wave (Kinetic Safety): Train on sine waves, perturb initial state; verify velocity saturates near \( c \) and perturbation causes phase shift.
3. MNIST Generation (Thermodynamic Relaxation): Train on MNIST, initialize from noisy centroid, run Langevin with annealing; expect distinct legible digits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Integration into deep architectures:** Can CHLUs be stacked while preserving symplectic stability and relativistic constraints? The paper defers comprehensive study.
- **Theoretical guarantees for training:** The Wake-Sleep/contrastive divergence update is empirical; no formal justification exists for its consistency or convergence.
- **Reconciling conservation with forgetting:** A strictly conservative CHLU lacks a natural mechanism to forget spurious correlations, limiting adaptation in non-stationary environments.
- **Benchmark performance:** Comparison to state-of-the-art temporal models on standard sequence benchmarks is deferred; the impact of geometric biases on expressivity and efficiency is unknown.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (time step \( \epsilon \), friction \( \gamma \) schedules, mass matrix \( M \) init, \( m_0 \), \( c \), \( V_\theta \) architecture) are unspecified, blocking exact replication.
- Training details (replay buffer capacity/sampling, optimizer, gradient clipping, Lyapunov \( \lambda \) schedule) are underspecified.
- The relativistic kinetic governor’s *necessity* vs. sufficiency for bounded velocities lacks external validation in the corpus.
- Generation quality depends on delicate temperature annealing; optimal schedules are dataset-specific and not provided.

## Confidence
- Relativistic Kinetic Governor (bounded velocities): **Medium** – structural guarantee is clear, but no ablation vs. non-relativistic baselines; stiff potentials may still cause instability.
- Symplectic Integration (long-term stability): **High** – strong theoretical basis and empirical support from neighboring symplectic learning papers; break conditions well-understood.
- Wake-Sleep Hamiltonian Contrastive Divergence (learned attractors): **Medium** – mechanistically plausible, but no diagnostics on replay buffer diversity or contrastive signal health; risk of mode collapse if buffer degenerates.

## Next Checks
1. **Velocity Saturation Test:** Perturb initial state, run 1000-step inference; measure max \( \|\dot{q}\| \). Expect saturation near \( c \); exceedance >5% flags relativistic gradient implementation.
2. **Phase-Space Volume Drift:** With \( \gamma=0 \), integrate for \( 10^5 \) steps; compute \( \log|\det(\text{Jacobian map})| \) periodically. Near-zero drift indicates symplecticity; sustained deviation >0.01 per 1000 steps indicates breakage.
3. **Contrastive Signal Health:** Track energy \( H(z) \) for wake vs. sleep samples during MNIST training. Expect significant margin (ΔH > 1.0) early, remaining positive (ΔH > 0.1) at convergence; ΔH → 0 early suggests degenerate replay buffer.
