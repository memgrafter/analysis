# MEME: Modeling the Evolutionary Modes of Financial Markets

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11918
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11918
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: MEME achieves an IC of 6.80% on CSI 300, significantly outperforming seven SOTA baselines (best baseline 4.85%), with strong portfolio metrics (AR 32.48%, MDD 10.76%, SR 1.5161).

## Executive Summary
MEME addresses the limitation of existing LLM-based financial methods that ignore the underlying investment logics driving market movements. It proposes a logic-oriented framework that models the market as an evolving ecosystem of competing investment narratives (“Modes of Thought”). Using a multi-agent system, MEME transforms noisy multimodal data (fundamentals, news, technicals) into structured Investment Arguments. These arguments are embedded, clustered daily via Gaussian Mixture Modeling to reveal latent modes, and tracked over time through temporal alignment. A performance score based on exponential moving averages guides portfolio construction via top-K signal aggregation. Extensive experiments on three Chinese stock pools (2023–2025) show consistent outperformance, with robust results even after the LLM knowledge cutoff.

## Method Summary
MEME processes daily multimodal data for each stock (fundamentals, news, technical indicators). A two-step multi-agent extraction first uses filter agents to distill modality-specific summaries, then a generator agent synthesizes them into structured Investment Arguments (polarity, rationale, evidence). Arguments are encoded into embeddings; a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is fit each day to discover K latent modes with soft membership probabilities. Daily modes are aligned across time by matching centroids using a linear assignment algorithm (Hungarian method). Each mode’s performance score is computed as a responsibility-weighted average of argument scores (polarity × next-day excess return) and updated via exponential moving average. For new arguments, their alignment with yesterday’s modes predicts scores; these are aggregated per stock to generate a signal, and an equal-weighted top-K portfolio is constructed daily. The backtest avoids look-ahead bias and includes transaction costs.

## Key Results
- **Predictive performance**: IC of 6.80% on CSI 300 (vs. 4.85% best baseline), ICIR of 41.71.
- **Portfolio metrics**: Annualized return 32.48%, maximum drawdown 10.76%, Sharpe ratio 1.5161.
- **Robustness**: Ablation studies confirm each component’s contribution; performance remains strong on out-of-sample data after the LLM knowledge cutoff.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1** – *Structured argument extraction*: A two-step multi-agent collaboration (filter agents per modality, then a generator agent) converts noisy raw data into concise, stance-labeled Investment Arguments. This isolates investment logic from irrelevant noise. Core assumption: raw data has low signal-to-noise ratio; human-like analytical distillation improves reasoning purity. Evidence: described in abstract and Section 3.1. Break condition: agent hallucination or omission corrupts downstream clustering.

**Mechanism 2** – *Probabilistic mode discovery*: Arguments are embedded and clustered daily via GMM, yielding K latent modes as Gaussian distributions with soft membership probabilities. This reflects the ambiguous, multi-faceted nature of market consensus. Core assumption: a single event can be interpreted through multiple, overlapping narratives. Evidence: abstract and Section 3.2.1. Break condition: if true modes are non-Gaussian or K is mis-specified, GMM fails.

**Mechanism 3** – *Temporal tracking and profitability filtering*: Daily modes are aligned across time by matching centroids (linear assignment). Each mode’s performance score is updated via EMA, combining current responsibility-weighted argument returns with its history. New arguments are aligned to historical modes to predict their scores, guiding portfolio decisions. Core assumption: investment logics have persistent cores; profitability filters robust reasoning. Evidence: abstract, Sections 3.2.2 and 3.3. Break condition: rapid semantic drift or regime shifts cause alignment errors or EMA lag.

## Foundational Learning
- **Gaussian Mixture Modeling (GMM) for soft clustering**: Assigns arguments probabilistically to overlapping investment narratives, capturing ambiguity. *Why needed*: Hard clustering forces exclusive membership, losing nuance. *Quick check*: How does responsibility-weighted scoring differ from simply averaging returns of stocks in a cluster?
- **Temporal alignment via linear assignment (Hungarian algorithm)**: Matches centroids across days to maintain consistent mode identities. *Why needed*: Enables longitudinal performance tracking. *Quick check*: Why is Euclidean distance used, and what is a limitation of this metric for semantic concepts?
- **LLM-based multi-agent collaboration for structured extraction**: Transforms heterogeneous raw data into uniform, stance-labeled arguments. *Why needed*: Provides clean, standardized input for embedding and clustering. *Quick check*: What is the risk of separate filter and generator agents versus a single monolithic agent?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input Layer (multimodal data) -> Extraction Layer (Filter Agents -> Generator Agent) -> Modeling Layer (Embedding Encoder -> Daily GMM -> Temporal Aligner) -> Evaluation Layer (Performance Calculator -> EMA Updater) -> Synthesis Layer (Alignment for new arguments -> Signal aggregation -> Top-K portfolio)

**Critical path**: Data -> Arguments -> [Embedding → GMM] → Temporal alignment → (Perf update & signal generation) → Portfolio. Temporal alignment is the linchpin connecting cross-sectional mode discovery to longitudinal performance tracking.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- GMM K vs. complexity: too small merges distinct narratives; too large fragments consensus.  
- EMA λ vs. adaptivity: high λ stabilizes but lags regime changes; low λ chases noise.  
- LLM prompt engineering: extraction quality heavily depends on prompt design.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Mode collapse: many arguments assigned to one dominant mode (K too small or GMM failure).  
- Alignment instability: frequent, non-smooth changes in permutation π (semantic drift exceeds tracking).  
- Performance decay: Perf scores become negative or volatile (extraction or clustering broken).

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Main backtest comparison on SSE50/CSI300/CSI500 (2023–2025) vs. 7 baselines using IC/ICIR and portfolio metrics.  
2. Ablation study disabling components (SAG, MOT, PM, TA) to measure contributions.  
3. Out-of-sample test on Q4 2025 data (post-LLM cutoff) to verify robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalizability**: Does MEME work on non-Chinese markets (e.g., US, Europe) or other asset classes (commodities, crypto)? Current validation is only on Chinese A‑shares.
- **LLM sensitivity**: How does the choice of backbone LLM (DeepSeek vs. others) affect argument quality and trading performance?
- **Automatic K selection**: Can the number of latent modes be determined automatically to adapt to different stock pools and improve robustness?
- **Clustering alternatives**: Is GMM the most effective method, or would other overlapping/non‑overlapping clustering techniques yield better results?

## Limitations
- **Single-market validation**: Results are limited to Chinese equities; cross‑market generalizability is unproven.
- **Proprietary LLM reliance**: Use of DeepSeek‑V3.1 and Qwen‑3 Embedding‑8B creates a black‑box risk and may hinder replication.
- **Fixed‑K GMM**: Heuristically chosen K may not adapt to evolving narrative complexity; sensitivity analysis shows performance varies with K.

## Confidence
- **Empirical Performance**: High (consistent outperformance across three indices, extensive baselines and ablations).
- **Mechanism Claims**: Medium (ablations support contributions but causal sufficiency of “investment logic” vs. latent factors not fully ruled out).
- **Generalizability & Robustness**: Low (single‑market test, proprietary model dependence, fixed‑K assumption not stress‑tested).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑market replication**: Implement MEME on S&P 500 using open‑source embedding models (e.g., all‑MiniLM‑L6‑v2) to test architectural versus model‑specific effects.
