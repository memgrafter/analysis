# KairosVL: Orchestrating Time Series and Semantics for Unified Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20494  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20494  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** KairosVL-7B achieves +13.3%–28.0% absolute accuracy gains on real-world AIOps zero-shot tasks and surpasses its base model by >30% on KairosBench across multiple reasoning subtasks.

## Executive Summary
Conventional time series analysis fails in decision-oriented tasks like AIOps fault diagnosis because it relies solely on numerical modeling and ignores contextual semantics. This paper introduces the **Semantic-Conditional Time Series Reasoning** task and proposes **KairosVL**, a two-round reinforcement learning framework that first strengthens perception of fundamental temporal primitives (noise, periodicity, out-of-distribution patterns) and then focuses on semantic-conditioned reasoning using a high-quality dataset constructed via an LLM-engaged pipeline (**KairosDataPipe**). On real-world and benchmark evaluations, KairosVL (7B) significantly outperforms its base model (Qwen2.5VL-7B) and competes with larger commercial models, demonstrating that integrating visual, temporal, and semantic understanding is crucial for robust time series reasoning.

## Method Summary
KairosVL employs a **two-round Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR)** pipeline. **Round 1** trains on a rule-generated **Primitive Dataset** (~2k samples) targeting basic temporal perception, using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a KL divergence regularization (β=1e-3) to prevent overspecialization. **Round 2** continues training on the **KairosDataset** (~2k samples) produced by the **KairosDataPipe** pipeline—an LLM-driven process that generates time series plots, multiple-choice questions, and reasoning chains, filtered by three automated judges and manual verification. In Round 2, KL regularization is removed and the clipping bound is increased (ε'=0.28) to encourage longer, diverse reasoning chains. The base model is Qwen2.5VL-7B, and training uses outcome-level rewards (answer correctness) with a format penalty.

## Key Results
- On a real-world zero-shot dataset (Dataset A), KairosVL yields absolute accuracy gains of **+13.3% (Scenario #1)**, **+17.1% (Scenario #2)**, and **+28.0% (Scenario #3)** over the base model.  
- On KairosBench, it surpasses the base model by **over 30%** in Fact-Adherent, Predictive, and Event-Aware reasoning tasks.  
- Ablation studies confirm the **two-round pipeline** and **KL loss schedule** (KL only in Round 1) are critical; mixing stages or mis-tuning KL reduces performance and causes reasoning collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Progressive Curriculum via Two-Round RL
**Claim:** The two‑round reinforcement learning pipeline progressively enhances model capabilities: first it builds accurate perception of temporal primitives, then it enables complex semantic‑conditioned reasoning.  
**Mechanism:** Round 1 uses a rule‑generated Primitive Dataset targeting noise, periodicity, and out‑of‑distribution patterns, with a KL regularization (β=1e‑3) to stay close to the reference model and avoid overspecialization. Round 2 trains on the richer KairosDataset without KL, using a larger clip ratio (ε'=0.28) to encourage longer, diverse reasoning chains.  
**Core assumption:** Correct perception of basic temporal features is a prerequisite for higher‑order reasoning; without it, deeper analyses collapse to shallow heuristics.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] Describes the two‑round framework: “the first round strengthens the model’s perception of fundamental temporal primitives, while the second focuses on semantic‑conditioned reasoning.”  
- [3.3] Provides implementation details: Primitive Dataset (§3.3.1), reward functions (§3.3.2), and loss formulations with/without KL (Eqs. 3, 4).  
**Break condition:** If the Primitive Dataset is too narrow or the KL coefficient is mis‑tuned, Round 1 may overfit or fail to establish robust primitive perception, leading to poor downstream performance.

### Mechanism 2: Verifiable Reward and Adaptive KL Constraints
**Claim:** Verifiable reward design combined with adaptive KL divergence constraints yields accurate, interpretable reasoning while preserving the model’s intrinsic dynamics.  
**Mechanism:** Task‑specific rewards compute answer correctness (e.g., MCQ accuracy, F1 for OOD intervals, relative error for periodicity). GRPO optimizes a token‑level policy using importance sampling. First round adds a KL penalty to prevent collapse to simplistic outputs; second round removes KL and widens clipping to boost exploration of longer reasoning chains.  
**Core assumption:** Outcome‑level rewards are sufficient to guide complex reasoning; KL divergence stabilizes training without hindering exploration when scheduled appropriately.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [3.3.2] Defines the reward functions (Eq. 1) and training losses for both rounds (Eqs. 3, 4), including KL terms.  
- [4.3.2] Ablation on KL settings shows that KL only in the first round yields the best accuracy; keeping KL in both rounds or removing it entirely hurts performance (Fig. 6).  
**Break condition:** Retaining KL in Round 2 overly constrains exploration, producing short generic responses; an excessively high clip ratio may cause training instability or reward oscillations.

### Mechanism 3: High-Fidelity Dataset via LLM-Engaged Pipeline
**Claim:** The KairosDataPipe pipeline produces a diverse, high‑fidelity dataset that bridges the gap between synthetic tasks and real‑world semantic‑conditional reasoning.  
**Mechanism:** A Generation Agent creates question‑answer pairs, data‑generation code, and visualization code under strict constraints. Three LLM‑based judges (Data Necessity, QA‑Data Consistency, Data & Plot Requirements) automatically filter invalid samples before manual verification. This ensures each sample truly requires visual reasoning and that the plot matches the underlying data.  
**Core assumption:** LLMs can generate realistic time series data and meaningful reasoning questions when guided by detailed prompts and iterative self‑checks.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [3.2] Fully describes KairosDataPipe workflow, the multi‑judge system, and manual verification, yielding >2k samples.  
- [4.2] KairosVL trained on this dataset achieves large gains over the base model and competes with larger commercial models, demonstrating dataset quality.  
**Break condition:** If any judge is too lenient or prompts under‑specified, trivial or inconsistent samples may leak, degrading training and causing overfitting or poor generalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Semantic-Conditional Time Series Reasoning**  
  **Why needed here:** It extends traditional numerical analysis by requiring integration of contextual semantics and operational knowledge (e.g., SOPs), which is essential for real‑world AIOps tasks.  
  **Quick check:** *What distinguishes predicting a future value from semantic‑conditional reasoning?*  
  **Answer:** The latter must interpret the time series in light of natural‑language conditions or domain rules, not just extrapolate.

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR)**  
  **Why needed here:** The training framework relies on outcome‑level correctness rather than token‑level supervision, allowing exploration of diverse reasoning chains without expensive hand‑crafted CoT data.  
  **Quick check:** *Why might RLVR be preferred over SFT for complex time‑series reasoning?*  
  **Answer:** SFT tends to produce templated responses and limits reasoning diversity; RLVR optimizes for correctness and can adaptively discover effective CoT patterns.

- **Concept: Two‑Round Curriculum Learning**  
  **Why needed here:** The staged design addresses the observed lack of basic temporal perception; the first round builds primitive skills, the second refines semantic reasoning, as shown by ablations (Figs 4, 6).  
  **Quick check:** *What would likely happen if the perception‑enhancement stage were omitted?*  
  **Answer:** The model would collapse to shallow reasoning because it cannot accurately identify basic temporal features like periodicity or noise level.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base Model (Qwen2.5VL-7B) -> Primitive Dataset -> First Round RLVR (with KL, ε=0.2) -> KairosDataset -> Second Round RLVR (no KL, ε'=0.28) -> KairosVL

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate primitive data via deterministic scripts.  
2. Run first‑round RLVR on Primitive Dataset; monitor format reward, task reward, response length, entropy.  
3. Load checkpoint; run second‑round RLVR on KairosDataset without KL.  
4. Evaluate on benchmarks; analyze failure cases.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- RLVR vs SFT: RLVR performs better on complex reasoning but is sample‑inefficient and sensitive to reward design; SFT is simpler but risks over‑constraining the model.  
- Two‑round vs Mix‑dataset: Splitting curriculum isolates primitive learning; mixing in one round reduces performance (Fig. 4).  
- KL schedule: Keeping KL in first round stabilizes perception; removing it in second round boosts exploration. Incorrect schedule yields short reasoning or instability.  
- Image vs text representation: Plot inputs drastically reduce token cost and improve accuracy (Table 3) but may sacrifice fine‑grained numeric precision.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Declining response length and entropy during training (Fig. 5b) → collapse to shallow reasoning.  
- Low format reward (< −0.2) → poor adherence to `<answer>` tags.  
- Validation reward plateau while training reward rises → overfitting.  
- Poor counterfactual performance despite gains elsewhere → model size or dataset diversity limits.  
- Temporal localization errors (approximating timestamps) → insufficient fine‑grained temporal understanding.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Fine‑tune Qwen2.5VL‑7B on KairosDataset in a single RL round; verify it matches the reported one‑row performance (Table 1, Fig. 4). Expect accuracy drop versus two‑round.  
2. **Ablation – KL schedule:** Run two‑round pipeline with KL variants: (a) KL both rounds, (b) no KL, (c) KL only in first round. Expect (c) to achieve highest accuracy, reproducing Fig. 6.  
3. **Dataset quality check:** Disable LLM judges (use raw generation outputs) and train on unfiltered data. Compare accuracy and reasoning length against the full pipeline; expect degradation, confirming the necessity of the multi‑judge filtering.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can we significantly expand the diversity and realism of time series reasoning datasets to cover more domains, time granularities, and contextual complexities?**  
  Future efforts should focus on expanding the diversity and realism of time series reasoning datasets, which are currently limited in scale and heterogeneity due to LLM biases and manual verification.

- **What training paradigms beyond the two‑round RL most effectively integrate temporal and semantic understanding in MLLMs, and how can these scale to larger models and more complex reasoning tasks?**  
  The two‑round RL is an initial proposal; its optimality and extensibility to larger models and tasks like counterfactual reasoning remain uncertain.

- **How can we evaluate the quality of model reasoning processes in time series analysis, not just final answer accuracy, to ensure interpretability and trustworthiness?**  
  RQ3 addresses "clear, faithful, and logically consistent reasoning steps", but no standardized benchmarks exist to assess reasoning step correctness, coherence, or alignment with human expert logic.

- **How can we mitigate key limitations of MLLMs for time series reasoning, such as poor fine‑grained numerical precision, inaccurate temporal localization, and logical hallucinations?**  
  These stem from the visual‑text modality gap and autoregressive generation, which are not easily addressed by existing training; without fixes, models may be unreliable in critical applications.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of dataset splits, sample counts per subtask, and manual verification criteria for KairosDataset hinders full reproducibility.  
- Opaque LLM judge thresholds and prompt tuning; substituting different LLMs may degrade quality without careful recalibration.  
- Missing training hyperparameters (total steps, LR schedule, checkpoint frequency) and hardware setup details require guesswork for replication.  
- Evaluation limited to a single base model (Qwen2.5VL‑7B); generalizability to other architectures is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Two‑round RL pipeline design:** High (strong internal ablations validate staged approach and KL schedule).  
- **KairosDataPipe dataset quality and filtering:** Medium (performance drops when judges are removed, but manual verification is irreproducible).  
- **Real‑world performance generalization:** Medium (large gains on Dataset A but based on a single small zero‑shot set).  
- **Primitive perception as prerequisite for semantic reasoning:** Medium (supported by ablation, yet causal interpretation remains indirect).

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset fidelity audit:** Re‑run KairosDataPipe with open‑source LLMs (e.g., Llama‑3.1‑70B) using provided prompts, then systematically vary judge thresholds to measure impact on downstream accuracy and reasoning diversity.  
2. **Cross‑model transfer:** Apply the two‑round training to a different base MLLM (e.g., InternVL2‑8B) and compare performance to assess whether observed gains are model‑specific.  
3. **Long‑tail robustness test:** Introduce synthetic counterfactual shifts (extreme noise, missing periods) in the evaluation set to probe the model’s temporal primitive robustness beyond the original test scenarios.
