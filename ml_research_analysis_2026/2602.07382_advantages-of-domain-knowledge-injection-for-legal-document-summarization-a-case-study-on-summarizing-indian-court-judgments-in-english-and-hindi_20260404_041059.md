# Advantages of Domain Knowledge Injection for Legal Document Summarization: A Case Study on Summarizing Indian Court Judgments in English and Hindi

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07382
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07382
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Domain-adapted models achieve 20–23% ROUGE-2 and 15–19% ROUGE-L F1 gains over SOTA for EN→EN, and 23%/19% gains for EN→HI, with statistically significant improvements in factual consistency validated by a legal expert.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that injecting legal domain knowledge via continual pre-training on bilingual (English-Hindi) Indian legal corpora substantially improves the summarization of complex court judgments. The approach is effective across extractive (using a domain-specific BERT encoder) and generative (T5, mT5, Gemma) models. Key gains are achieved on the MILDSum benchmark for both English-to-English and English-to-Hindi tasks, with notable improvements in factual consistency and coverage of legal rhetorical roles as judged by a domain expert. The use of GaLore enables memory-efficient pre-training.

## Method Summary
The core method involves two-stage adaptation. First, base models are continually pre-trained on large, unlabeled Indian legal corpora (InLegalBERT-PT for English, Bail Corpus for Hindi) using self-supervised objectives (span-corruption for T5/mT5, next-token prediction for Gemma). For extractive models, the standard SummaRuNNer architecture is modified by replacing its initial embedding layer with the domain-specific InLegalBERT encoder. Second, all models are fine-tuned on the labeled MILDSum summarization dataset. For long judgments, documents are chunked, and reference summary sentences are mapped to corresponding chunks using LaBSE sentence embeddings. Efficiency during pre-training is achieved via GaLore, and for large decoder-only models (Gemma), LoRA/QLoRA is used during fine-tuning. A critical multilingual experiment mixes English and Hindi data during pre-training to enable cross-lingual transfer for the EN→HI task.

## Key Results
- Domain-adapted generative models (T5/mT5/Gemma with PT+FT) achieve 20–23% ROUGE-2 and 15–19% ROUGE-L F1 improvements over SOTA baselines on MILDSum.
- For English-to-Hindi summarization, mixed EN+HI pre-training yields 23% ROUGE-2 and 19% ROUGE-L F1 gains.
- Expert evaluation confirms PT+FT summaries preserve significantly more information, exhibit better factual consistency, and cover key legal rhetorical roles (e.g., FAC, CIT) more effectively than non-adapted models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Continual pre-training on domain-specific legal corpora injects legal terminology, stylistic patterns, and factual knowledge into generative models, improving factual consistency and content relevance.
- **Mechanism:** Model parameters are updated via a self-supervised objective (span-corruption for T5/mT5, next-token prediction for Gemma) on unlabeled legal texts. This adapts internal representations to the statistical regularities (citations, rhetoric, jargon) of Indian law, moving parameters toward a "legal domain optimum" before fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] domain knowledge injection via continual pre-tuning; [section 4.2] pre-training procedure and corpora details; strong use of specific large-scale corpora (InLegalBERT-PT, Bail Corpus).
- **Break condition:** If the pre-training corpus is too small or lacks diversity in legal constructs (e.g., specific statutes), injected knowledge may be incomplete or biased.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Architectural modification of extractive models to use domain-specific pre-trained encoders (InLegalBERT) significantly improves sentence scoring.
- **Mechanism:** Replacing SummaRuNNer's generic word2vec layer with InLegalBERT changes the initial representation space. Sentences are encoded with embeddings already rich in legal semantics, making the subsequent classification layer more effective at identifying summary-worthy sentences.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] incorporating domain-specific pre-trained encoders; [section 4.1] explicit description of InLegalSumExt creation; strong basis in InLegalBERT pre-trained on Indian legal text.
- **Break condition:** If the domain-specific encoder's pre-training domain (e.g., Supreme Court) diverges significantly from the target data (e.g., lower courts), transfer may be ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multilingual continual pre-training (mixing English and Hindi legal texts) enables positive cross-lingual transfer for EN→HI summarization by aligning the model's latent space with legal semantics in both languages.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-training on a mixed EN+HI corpus exposes the model to parallel legal concepts and phrasing, fostering a shared representation where legal constructs are associated across languages, allowing English legal knowledge to inform Hindi generation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] gains for EN→HI; [section 4.2.2] Table 4 shows mixed PT best for EN→HI; strong use of two distinct corpora (InLegalBERT-PT EN, Bail HI).
- **Break condition:** If the EN and HI corpora are too dissimilar in style/sub-domain or the base model's multilingual pre-training (mC4) is weak, mixed training may cause negative interference.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transfer Learning via Continued Pre-training**
  - *Why needed here:* Base LMs lack specialized legal knowledge. Continued pre-training on domain text adapts parameters before fine-tuning on small labeled datasets.
  - *Quick check:* What self-supervised objective is used for T5/mT5 pre-training, and why not a summarization-specific objective during domain adaptation?
- **Concept: Architectural Adaptation for Model Paradigm**
  - *Why needed here:* Encoder-only, encoder-decoder, and decoder-only models require distinct knowledge injection strategies (layer replacement vs. full-sequence pre-training).

- **Concept: Cross-lingual Transfer in Multilingual Models**
  - *Why needed here:* The task is EN→HI. Pre-training strategy (monolingual vs. mixed) impacts the model's cross-lingual mapping ability within the legal domain.
  - *Quick check:* According to Table 4, what is the effect of Hindi-only pre-training on EN→HI performance, and what does it imply about cross-lingual transfer?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Base Models -> Pre-training Corpora -> PT Checkpoints -> Chunking & Mapping -> Fine-tuning -> Evaluation Suite
  (T5-large, mT5-large, Gemma-2-2b, SummaRuNNer) -> (InLegalBERT-PT, Bail Corpus) -> (*-PT) -> (LaBSE sentence mapping, 512/5632-token chunks) -> (*-PT-FT) -> (multilingual_rouge_scoring, BERTScore, SummaC, NEPrec)
  Domain Encoder: InLegalBERT -> InLegalSumExt (SummaRuNNer variant)
  Efficiency: GaLore for PT, LoRA/QLoRA for Gemma FT.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Select pre-training subset (EN: ~27K docs, HI: ~34K docs). Configure span-corruption (15% mask, span=3) or next-token prediction. Pre-train base model with/without GaLore. Save checkpoint.
  2. For MILDSum (doc, summary) pairs: chunk document; use LaBSE to map each summary sentence to most similar doc sentence; assign summary to corresponding doc chunk. Create chunk-wise pairs.
  3. Load PT or base checkpoint. Fine-tune on chunk pairs with specified hyperparameters. Save FT or PT-FT model.
  4. Generate test summaries. Compute ROUGE, BERTScore, SummaC, NEPrec. Perform statistical tests (Wilcoxon, Mann-Whitney U).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Pre-training corpus size: Larger (68K EN) vs. smaller (27K EN). Smaller corpus suffices, favoring efficiency.
  - Pre-training strategy: Full-parameter vs. GaLore. GaLore yields comparable performance with major memory savings.
  - Chunking: Chunk size (n) vs. summary length per chunk (t). Balance context preservation with model's max input and reference summary alignment.
  - Multilingual mix: EN-only, HI-only, or mixed pre-training for EN->HI. Mixed is best but requires managing two corpora.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low ROUGE/BERTScore + High SummaC/NEPrec: Model factually consistent but fails to match reference wording (over-extractive or different paraphrase).
  - Low SummaC/NEPrec: Hallucinations or entity errors, indicating weak domain grounding.
  - Poor EN->HI performance: If HI-only PT underperforms mixed, model relies on EN knowledge; if EN-only performs well, base mT5 has strong zero-shot transfer.
  - Expert evaluation low in "Facts (FAC)" or "Statute citations (CIT)": Model missed key legal elements, likely due to inadequate pre-training coverage.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate baseline & domain injection for extractive: Train SummaRuNNer and InLegalSumExt on MILDSum. Verify ~20% R-2 gain (Table 2) and significance.
  2. Ablate pre-training corpus size for T5: Pre-train T5-large on 27K vs 68K EN docs (with/without GaLore). Fine-tune/evaluate. Verify Table 3: small corpus sufficient, GaLore effective.
  3. Multilingual pre-training ablation for mT5: Pre-train mT5-large EN-only, HI-only, EN+HI mix. Fine-tune for EN->HI. Verify Table 4: HI-only > EN-only > baseline; mixed > HI-only.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **O1:** Does GaLore generalize beyond the tested architectures (T5, mT5, Gemma) and the Indian legal domain to other model types and domains?
- **O2:** How does domain pre-training corpus size affect performance in other low-resource legal domains or languages, given the finding that small subsets (27K) sufficed for Indian EN/HI?
- **O3:** Are the expert evaluation findings (rhetorical role coverage, factual consistency) robust across a larger, more diverse panel of legal experts with varying regional and linguistic backgrounds?
- **O4:** Does multilingual pre-training on mixed EN-HI corpora confer cross-lingual transfer benefits for other Indian language pairs (e.g., EN→TA, EN→BN) beyond EN→HI?

## Limitations
- Availability of pre-training corpora (InLegalBERT-PT, Bail) may block replication.
- Hindi NEPrec metric's NER tool is unspecified; likely uses a multilingual model with lower accuracy, introducing noise.
- LaBSE chunk-summary mapping details (threshold, many-to-one handling) are not fully specified.
- GaLore memory configuration (gradient accumulation steps) is incomplete, risking implementation mismatches.
- Human evaluation relies on a single legal expert; results may not generalize.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Pre-training injection):** High. Gains across models/metrics plus expert validation.
- **Mechanism 2 (Domain encoder):** High. Clear 20% ROUGE-2 improvement with well-defined architecture.
- **Mechanism 3 (Mixed pre-training):** Medium. Empirical support but sensitive to corpus mix; no direct representation analysis.

## Next Checks
