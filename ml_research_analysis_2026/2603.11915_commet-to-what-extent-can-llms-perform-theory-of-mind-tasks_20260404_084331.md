# CoMMET: To What Extent Can LLMs Perform Theory of Mind Tasks?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.11915
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11915
- **Reference count**: 14
- **Primary result**: Top model (Gemini-3.0-Pro) achieved 92.22% text-only accuracy but only 88.28% on visual-dependent tasks; complex belief–desire reasoning remained challenging (66.67% for Gemini-3.0-Pro); adding images did not consistently boost performance; smaller models lagged (~47% overall).

## Executive Summary
CoMMET is a new multimodal benchmark for Theory of Mind (ToM) that extends beyond prior text-only, single-round evaluations. Based on psychological ToM booklet tasks, it uses a multi-turn StoryTurn format with images and adaptive feedback to cover all ATOMS mental states plus moral reasoning. Evaluating eight LLMs, the best (Gemini-3.0-Pro) reached high story accuracy on simple tasks but struggled with complex belief–desire reasoning and non‑literal communication. Incorporating visual input often failed to improve performance, and smaller models lagged substantially. Errors on explanatory “why” questions indicate current LLMs lack robust, genuine ToM capabilities.

## Method Summary
CoMMET comprises 591 StoryTurns (text + optional images) derived from original ToM booklet tasks, organized into 31 text‑only and 8 visual‑dependent sets. The evaluation simulates a multi‑turn dialogue: after each model answer, an adaptive feedback hint is provided based on correctness, and subsequent questions depend on prior turns. Story‑level accuracy (all answers correct per StoryTurn) is the primary metric. Responses are judged by Gemini‑3.0‑Flash, with manual validation on a subset. No model training is performed; eight LLMs are tested in zero/few‑shot settings.

## Key Results
- Gemini-3.0-Pro attained 92.22% story accuracy on text‑only tasks and 88.28% on visual‑dependent ones.
- Even top models faltered on complex belief–desire reasoning (66.67%) and non‑literal communication with emotion (Claude‑Sonnet‑4.5: 79.76%).
- Adding images did not reliably enhance performance, and smaller models (e.g., LLaMA‑3.2‑11B) scored only 47.08% overall.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The StoryTurn multi‑turn format exposes LLMs’ inability to maintain and update mental state representations across a conversation, leading to inconsistent reasoning on second‑order belief–desire tasks.
- **Mechanism**: Questions are posed sequentially with adaptive feedback; a correct answer influences subsequent hints. This requires the model to integrate earlier conclusions into later reasoning. LLMs often treat each turn independently, so the final “why” question may contradict earlier correct selections, revealing a lack of persistent mental modeling.
- **Core assumption**: LLMs do not maintain an explicit internal state but rely on the context window, which is insufficient for stable belief tracking across multiple turns.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (multi‑turn format, adaptive feedback); Section 4.4.1 (most errors occur in the final turn, even for Claude Sonnet 4.5); neighboring works (e.g., GPT‑4o Lacks Core Features of ToM) report similar inconsistencies.
- **Break condition**: Future models achieve >95% story accuracy on these multi‑turn tasks and produce coherent explanations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Adding illustrative images does not reliably improve LLM performance on visual‑dependent ToM tasks, indicating that current models fail to integrate perceptual cues with mental state reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: Images provide perspective‑taking information (e.g., what a character can see). Genuine multimodal understanding should boost accuracy on tasks where the answer hinges on visual details. The mixed or negative gains suggest that vision and language pathways remain loosely coupled, preventing deep semantic alignment necessary for belief inference.
- **Core assumption**: LLMs treat images as separate inputs without grounding them into a unified representation that supports mental state attribution.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“Adding images did not consistently boost performance”); Section 4.2 (visual input can enhance performance when question depends on visual attributes, but not consistently); neighboring multimodal benchmarks (MuMA‑ToM, MOMENTS) also note limited vision‑language synergy.
- **Break condition**: A model family shows statistically significant and consistent improvement on visual‑dependent tasks when images are provided.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Model scale strongly correlates with performance on complex ToM tasks, particularly those involving multiple mental states (e.g., belief–desire, non‑literal communication).
- **Mechanism**: Larger models possess greater parameter capacity and richer training data coverage, enabling them to capture nuanced patterns of human mental state attribution and perform multi‑step in‑context reasoning. Smaller models lack this emergent ability, leading to substantial accuracy gaps.
- **Core assumption**: ToM capabilities scale with model size; beyond a certain threshold, the ability to handle complex mental reasoning emerges.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (smaller models lagged substantially, LLaMA‑3.2‑11B: 47.08% overall); Section 4.2 (LLaMA‑3.2‑11B scores ~47% while Gemini‑3.0‑Pro exceeds 92%); Section 4.3 (near‑perfect on simple desire tasks but steep drop on belief‑desire tasks for all models, larger models relatively better).
- **Break condition**: A small model (<5B) matches large‑model performance on CoMMET through targeted training.

## Foundational Learning
### Theory of Mind (ToM) fundamentals
- **Why needed**: The paper evaluates LLMs across mental states (desire, belief, emotion, etc.) defined by the ATOMS taxonomy; understanding these constructs is essential to interpret task design and results.
- **Quick check**: “What distinguishes first‑order from second‑order belief reasoning? Provide an example.”

### Multimodal evaluation in LLMs
- **Why needed**: CoMMET introduces images that sometimes provide critical perspective information; knowing the challenges of vision‑language integration helps explain performance patterns and design trade‑offs.
- **Quick check**: “What are common failure modes when LLMs process images in a question‑answering context?”

### Story‑level accuracy metric
- **Why needed**: CoMMET reports story accuracy (all questions in a narrative must be correct) rather than averaging per‑question scores; this penalizes partial understanding and reflects real‑world consistency.
- **Quick check**: “Why might a model score high on per‑question accuracy but low on story accuracy on a benchmark like CoMMET?”

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Seed StoryTurn -> LLM Data Generator -> Image Generator -> Human Validation -> Structured Storage -> Evaluation Engine (per model) -> LLM Judge -> Metrics Aggregator.

**Critical path**:
1. Acquire seed StoryTurn from original ToM booklet.
2. Prompt LLM to generate new StoryTurns covering all ATOMS states.
3. Generate corresponding images for turns requiring visual information.
4. Manually verify and correct StoryTurn texts and images.
5. Store structured data (text | feedback | question | image | answer).
6. For each target model: run sequential inference, providing feedback after each answer.
7. Collect responses and evaluate with the LLM judge (optionally human‑validated).
8. Aggregate results, compute accuracies, and perform category‑wise analysis.
9. Repeat for both text‑only and multimodal conditions.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Open‑ended vs. multiple‑choice: naturalistic but automatic evaluation harder; requires a reliable judge.
- Manual validation cost vs. dataset quality: essential for spotting image artifacts and logical errors but does not scale.
- Adaptive feedback: improves realism but introduces dependency on judge accuracy and may bias models.
- Inclusion of images: increases ecological validity but risks noise from generation artifacts.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low inter‑annotator agreement between LLM judge and human experts on sampled responses.
- High prevalence of image defects (spatial errors, duplicates, text artifacts) that could mislead models.
- Target model frequently refusing to answer or producing off‑topic replies.
- Data leakage: accidental overlap between training data and benchmark leading to inflated scores.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline replication: Run Gemini‑3.0‑Pro on the text‑only subset; expect ~92% story accuracy to confirm setup validity.
2. Image ablation: Compare model performance with and without providing images on the visual‑dependent subset to quantify multimodal gain.
3. Turn‑order sensitivity: Shuffle the order of StoryTurns or remove feedback for a subset to test the model’s reliance on sequential context and consistency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why do multimodal inputs (images) fail to consistently improve LLM performance on ToM tasks?** The paper documents mixed gains but does not investigate whether failures stem from poor integration or image artifacts.
2. **What specific architectural or training limitations cause failures in explanatory “why” reasoning for complex mental state combinations?** The paper identifies poor explanations but not the root cause (e.g., lack of explicit ToM modules, data gaps).
3. **How can ToM capabilities be efficiently transferred to smaller language models (<20B) to close the performance gap?** The gap is shown but no interventions tested.
4. **Can automated evaluation metrics reliably score open‑ended explanatory responses in multimodal ToM tasks?** Current reliance on LLM judges and manual checks is costly and may miss nuances.

## Limitations
- Incomplete reporting of evaluation details (LLM judge prompt, inference parameters) hinders exact replication.
- Limited number of visual‑dependent StoryTurns (8) may overstate image effect generalizability.
- Model selection biased toward proprietary systems; open‑source coverage sparse.

## Confidence
- **Multi‑turn inconsistency**: Medium (strong error pattern, but underlying cause uncertain).
- **Image integration**: Medium (mixed gains observed, but image artifacts and small subset limit certainty).
- **Scale correlation**: High (consistent performance gaps across model sizes, though causation not isolated).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate CoMMET with standardized prompts and open‑source multimodal models (e.g., LLaMA‑3.2‑11B‑Vision) to verify multi‑turn error patterns.
2. Remove adaptive feedback during evaluation to measure its contribution to final‑turn errors and story accuracy.
3. Compute human‑LLM judge concordance on a sample of model explanations to detect systematic biases.
