# Citation-Enforced RAG for Fiscal Document Intelligence: Cited, Explainable Knowledge Retrieval in Tax Compliance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14170
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14170
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** 94.5% citation support, 1.8% hallucination rate, 88.0% abstention accuracy, 4.2/5.0 helpfulness on 298 tax documents.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical need for transparency and auditability in AI systems for high-stakes tax compliance, where hallucinated or unverifiable outputs pose significant legal and financial risks. It introduces a multimodal, citation-enforced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework that enforces strict evidence grounding and conservative behavior. The system employs a source-first ingestion strategy to preserve document provenance, constrains generation to retrieved evidence with explicit page-level citations, and abstains from answering when retrieval confidence is low. Evaluated on a corpus of 298 real IRS and state tax documents, the system achieves a very low hallucination rate and high citation fidelity, demonstrating practical utility for analyst support while maintaining rigorous explainability.

## Method Summary
The system begins with source-first ingestion: raw text is extracted directly from PDFs (using OCR for scanned images) and tables are linearized into explicit sentences, preserving page-level metadata. Chunks are embedded using the `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5` model and indexed in a FAISS vector store. During query time, the retriever fetches top-k chunks by cosine similarity. An abstention module checks the top-1 chunk's similarity against a predefined threshold; if below threshold, the system returns an abstention message. Otherwise, the generator (Llama 3.2 3B Instruct) receives only the retrieved chunks in an evidence-only prompt, instructed to answer using solely that evidence and to cite each claim in a `[Doc ID, Page]` format. A post-generation citation validator checks that every paragraph contains at least one valid citation; failures trigger regeneration or abstention.

## Key Results
- 94.5% citation support rate (human-verified alignment between claims and cited evidence)
- 1.8% hallucination rate (generation of unsupported claims)
- 88.0% abstention accuracy (correctly declining to answer when evidence is insufficient)
- 4.2/5.0 average helpfulness score from expert human reviewers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Source-First Ingestion with Provenance Preservation
- **Claim:** Embedding only raw extracted text and preserving page-level metadata ensures retrieval returns verifiable document spans, preventing abstraction errors and enabling span-level citation.
- **Mechanism:** Multimodal extraction pulls text directly from PDFs (including OCR for scans). Tables are converted to linear sentences. Chunks are created with strict document/page identifiers and embedded without generative summarization.
- **Core assumption:** Raw extracted text is a faithful, sufficiently complete representation of source documents for compliance queries; OCR and layout parsing errors are minimal.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "adopts a source-first ingestion strategy, embedding only content extracted directly from authoritative fiscal documents while preserving document identifiers and page-level provenance."; [section] Section 4.1 ("Design Principles for Source-First Ingestion") and Section 4.2 ("Multimodal Content Extraction").

### Mechanism 2: Citation Enforcement via Evidence-Only Prompting and Validation
- **Claim:** Constraining generation to retrieved evidence and enforcing explicit citations prevents unsupported claims, reducing hallucination and ensuring auditability.
- **Mechanism:** The generator LLM receives only retrieved chunks (with metadata) and is instructed to answer using solely that evidence, with citations in a `[Doc ID, Page]` format. A post-generation validator checks citation presence/format; failures trigger regeneration or abstention.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM can be steered to produce accurate citations via prompts and validation, and the citations genuinely correspond to supporting evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Answer generation is constrained by explicit citation enforcement, which requires every generated claim to be grounded in retrieved evidence."; [section] Section 6.3 ("Citation Enforcement Mechanism") and Section 6.4 ("Answer Structure and Presentation").

### Mechanism 3: Abstention Based on Retrieval Confidence Threshold
- **Claim:** Declining to answer when retrieval confidence (cosine similarity) is low prevents generation on insufficient evidence, maintaining conservative behavior appropriate for high-stakes domains.
- **Mechanism:** After retrieval, the top-1 chunk's cosine similarity is compared to a threshold (empirically derived). Below threshold → abstention with a transparent message. This uses retrieval signals as a proxy for evidence sufficiency.
- **Core assumption:** Retrieval confidence reliably indicates whether the corpus contains explicit, semantically aligned guidance for the query. A fixed threshold effectively separates answerable from unanswerable queries.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "an abstention mechanism that declines to answer when document support is insufficient."; [section] Section 7.2 ("Abstention Criteria and Decision Policy") and Section 7.4 ("Role of Abstention in Hallucination Mitigation").

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Ingestion Pipeline (extract/linearize text with metadata) -> Vector Index (FAISS with normalized BGE embeddings) -> Retriever (cosine similarity search) -> Abstention Module (check top-1 similarity vs threshold) -> Generator (Llama 3.2 3B Instruct with evidence-only prompt) -> Citation Validator (enforce [Doc ID, Page] per paragraph). If abstention or validation failure, system exits early with message.

- **Critical path:** 1) Query embedding and top-k retrieval; 2) Compare top-1 similarity to threshold → if low, return abstention; 3) Build evidence context (chunks + metadata) and generate answer with citation instructions; 4) Validate citation format → if invalid, regenerate up to N times; if still invalid, abstain; 5) Return final answer or abstention.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  * Source-first vs. summary-based embedding: Source-first preserves verifiability but may miss semantic abstractions, potentially reducing recall for complex queries.
  * Hard citation enforcement: Guarantees traceability but may cause abstention even when evidence exists if the model fails to produce a valid citation string (model limitation vs. evidence absence).
  * Similarity-based abstention: Simple, interpretable, and tied to retrieval, but may not capture nuanced evidence insufficiency (e.g., high similarity but partial coverage).

- **Failure signatures:**
  * High abstention rate on seemingly answerable queries → could indicate poor extraction/OCR, chunking misalignment, or an overly aggressive similarity threshold.
  * Low citation support in human eval → generated claims are not actually supported by cited chunks (core failure of citation mechanism).
  * Frequent citation validator failures → model consistently outputs wrong citation format, suggesting prompt/instruction tuning needed.
  * OCR errors in chunks → inspect retrieved chunks for garbled text, especially from scanned forms/tables.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce ingestion:** Process 2 PDFs from a public tax corpus (one form, one instruction). Verify chunks match source text and have correct page metadata. Assess table linearization quality.
  2. **Retrieval confidence profiling:** For 20 known answerable queries, record top-1 similarity scores. Plot distribution to confirm separation from a set of unanswerable queries. Adjust threshold if needed.
  3. **Citation enforcement toggle:** Run system with citation validator disabled on a query with strong retrieval. Observe if model produces uncited claims. Re-enable and compare to isolate validator impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the system's citation support rate and hallucination rate generalize to tax documents from jurisdictions with significantly different regulatory structures, document formats, and linguistic conventions (e.g., non-U.S. tax codes, civil law systems)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly state the evaluation corpus is limited to the IRS and two U.S. state authorities (CA FTB, NY Tax), and they identify this as a key limitation. They also note performance "may differ across jurisdictions with substantially different document structures or legal frameworks."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's entire evaluation and design choices are situated within a specific, narrow jurisdictional context. Claims about generalizability to high-stakes compliance domains rely on an unverified assumption that the core principles transfer effectively to structurally diverse regulatory environments.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up study evaluating the identical framework on a newly curated, diverse international corpus (e.g., EU VAT directives, UK HMRC guidance, Singapore IRAS manuals). Metrics would compare citation fidelity, abstention accuracy, and retrieval confidence distributions against the U.S.-based results, controlling for document quality and language.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** For documents where critical semantic meaning is conveyed through non-linearizable visual layout (e.g., complex flowcharts, spatial relationships in forms, conditional logic diagrams), does the current text-extraction-first approach cause a measurable drop in retrieval accuracy and citation correctness for layout-dependent queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper acknowledges a design trade-off: "all retrieved evidence is ultimately represented as extracted text. This design simplifies retrieval and citation but may limit the system’s ability to reason over complex visual layouts or graphical elements that cannot be faithfully linearised." They also note OCR errors in tables as a failure mode.
- **Why unresolved:** The system deliberately avoids joint vision-language reasoning to prioritize provenance and auditability. The impact of this choice on query answerability for visually complex documents is asserted but not quantitatively measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An error analysis on a held-out set of queries explicitly targeting layout-dependent information (e.g., "What is the flow of decisions in the eligibility flowchart on page 5?"). Compare the current system's performance against a variant using a layout-aware embedding model (e.g., LayoutLMv3) that preserves 2D structure, while still enforcing citation to original coordinates (e.g., bounding boxes).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal, context-sensitive threshold for the top-1 cosine similarity score to trigger abstention, and can it be dynamically calibrated to balance the risk of hallucination against unnecessary abstentions across different query types and document topics?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The abstention mechanism uses a "predefined threshold" on the top-ranked chunk's similarity score. The paper states "the distribution of top-1 similarity scores shows a clear separation" and that this supports a threshold, but the threshold value itself is not reported or justified.
- **Why unresolved:** The threshold is a critical, underspecified hyperparameter that directly trades off answerability for safety. The paper provides no analysis of how different thresholds affect the key metrics or whether a single static threshold is optimal for all query categories.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic sensitivity analysis running the full evaluation across a range of similarity thresholds (e.g., 0.4 to 0.7). Plotting the resulting hallucination rate against the abstention rate would identify the Pareto frontier. Additionally, analyzing if thresholds calibrated per topic cluster yield better overall utility.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the citation-enforcement and abstention mechanisms be effectively integrated with a more powerful, instruction-tuned generative model (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3) without sacrificing the observed 1.8% hallucination rate, or does the observed performance depend critically on the constrained, locally-hosted Llama 3.2 3B model's inherent limitations?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses a specific, smaller open-weights model for reproducibility and control. The success of citation enforcement is attributed to the "hard constraint" and prompting, but the paper does not investigate model dependency. The low hallucination rate is a key result, but its generalizability to other models is an unstated assumption.
- **Why unresolved:** The framework's constraints are designed to be model-agnostic. However, more capable models might be more prone to "creative" adherence or subtle hallucinations that bypass simple format validation. The current results conflate the framework's design with the specific model's capability profile.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study re-running the entire evaluation pipeline with at least one state-of-the-art closed-source model (via API) and one other open-weights model of comparable or larger size. Compare hallucination rates, citation correctness, and abstention accuracy. Analyze failure cases: do larger models produce more sophisticated unsupported claims that still pass citation format checks?

## Limitations
- The precise similarity threshold for abstention is unreported; the cited "exceeding 0.55" suggests tuning but lacks a fixed value.
- Human evaluation rubric, rater instructions, and inter-rater agreement for "citation support" and "helpfulness" are unspecified, limiting assessment of metric reliability.
- No systematic error analysis is provided for OCR/linearization errors; corrupted text could silently undermine retrieval and citation fidelity.
- Evaluation corpus is limited to U.S. IRS and state tax documents; generalization to other fiscal domains or document qualities (e.g., handwritten forms) is unknown.
- Weak neighbor corpus signal (average FMR 0.435, zero citations) suggests limited direct academic precedent for the specific combination of source-first ingestion and citation-enforced RAG in compliance, increasing uncertainty about relative contribution.
