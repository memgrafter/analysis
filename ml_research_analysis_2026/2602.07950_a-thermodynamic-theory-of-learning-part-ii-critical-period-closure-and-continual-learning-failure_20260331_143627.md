# A Thermodynamic Theory of Learning Part II: Critical Period Closure and Continual Learning Failure

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.07950  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07950  
- **Reference count:** 8  
- **Primary result:** Catastrophic forgetting arises from irreversible loss of reconfigurability due to finite-time learning dynamics, not merely insufficient capacity; a threshold theorem states that forgetting is inevitable if the stable rank of a new task's Hessian restricted to the previous task's preserving manifold exceeds the residual compatible effective rank.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes a thermodynamic theory to explain catastrophic forgetting in continual learning. It models sequential learning as multiplicative transport maps in parameter space, where Jacobian matrices compose and their singular values are submultiplicative. This leads to cumulative, irreversible contraction of the dynamically accessible subspace within the task‑preserving manifold of earlier tasks. The authors derive a threshold theorem: if the stable rank of a new task’s Hessian (restricted to the old task’s preserving manifold) exceeds the residual compatible effective rank, any sufficient adaptation will necessarily cause forgetting. This establishes a fundamental capacity limit at the trajectory level, showing that forgetting can be inevitable even when multi‑task solutions exist in principle.

## Method Summary
The framework treats learning as a deterministic transport map \(\Psi_t\) that transforms initial parameters \(\theta_0\) to \(\theta_t\). The Jacobian \(J_t = \partial\Psi_t/\partial\theta_0\) captures directional sensitivity; its composition across learning phases (\(J_{t+s}=J_s J_t\)) causes submultiplicative decay of effective rank, measured as \(R(t) = \exp\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\log\det(J_t^\top J_t)] / d\bigr)\). Task performance depends on proximity to a manifold \(G_A\) of near‑optimal parameters for task A; its tangent space \(T G_A\) contains task‑preserving directions. The compatible effective rank \(R_A(t)\) restricts \(J_t\) to \(T G_A\). For a new task B, the stable rank \(m_B\) of its Hessian \(H_B\) restricted to \(T G_A\) quantifies the required reconfiguration dimensionality. If \(m_B > R_A(t)\) at any time, the theory proves forgetting unavoidable.

## Key Results
- Irreversible directional collapse occurs due to submultiplicative Jacobian rank decay under sequential transport map composition.
- Excess dissipation from finite‑time learning anisotropically contracts the task‑preserving manifold, reducing compatible effective rank even without immediate performance loss.
- Threshold theorem: Catastrophic forgetting is inevitable when \(m_B > R_A(t)\), establishing a fundamental capacity limit at the trajectory level.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism rests on three pillars:

1. **Irreversible loss of reconfigurability**: Successive learning phases compose as transport maps \(\Psi_{t+s} = \Psi_s \circ \Psi_t\), leading to Jacobian composition \(J_{t+s} = J_s J_t\). Because matrix rank and singular values are submultiplicative (\(\operatorname{rank}(J_{t+s}) \leq \min(\operatorname{rank}(J_s), \operatorname{rank}(J_t))\) and \(\sigma_i(J_{t+s}) \leq \sigma_i(J_s)\sigma_i(J_t)\)), any directional collapse induced by one phase cannot be recovered later. This causes cumulative, irreversible contraction of the dynamically usable subspace.

2. **Excess dissipation and anisotropic contraction**: Finite‑time learning deviates from optimal transport (Wasserstein geodesic), producing excess entropy production \(\Sigma_{\text{ex}} > 0\). This manifests as anisotropic drift that compresses specific parameter directions more than others, reducing the effective rank within the task‑preserving manifold. Crucially, this contraction can occur without immediate degradation in task performance, as endpoint metrics depend only on the final distribution.

3. **Threshold theorem for inevitable forgetting**: The new task B requires a number of independent curvature directions within old task A’s preserving manifold, quantified by \(m_B\) (stable rank of \(H_B\) restricted to \(T G_A\)). The system’s remaining capacity to reconfigure without forgetting is \(R_A(t)\) (compatible effective rank). If \(m_B > R_A(t)\), any learning trajectory making sufficient progress on B must exit \(G_A\), causing forgetting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transport Map and Jacobian**: Learning is modeled as a deterministic map \(\Psi_t\) from initial to final parameters, with Jacobian \(J_t\) describing infinitesimal perturbation propagation. Composition yields \(J_{t+s}=J_s J_t\), driving submultiplicative rank collapse. *Why needed*: Provides the mathematical engine for irreversible directional contraction. *Quick check*: If singular values of \(J_t\) are \([2,1,0.5]\) and those of \(J_s\) are \([1.5,0.8,0.3]\), the largest singular value of \(J_{t+s}\) is at most \(2 \times 1.5 = 3\); the product of all singular values (log‑volume) is bounded above by the product of the products of each matrix.

- **Task‑Preserving Manifold and Tangent Space**: For a task A, there exists a manifold \(G_A(\varepsilon_A)\) of parameters achieving near‑optimal performance. Its tangent space \(T_\theta G_A\) contains directions that change representation without affecting task loss (symmetries, flat directions). *Why needed*: Compatible reconfiguration must stay within this manifold; the capacity threshold compares required curvature to accessible volume inside it. *Quick check*: A perfect symmetry (e.g., swapping two hidden units) lies in \(T_\theta G_A\) because moving along it preserves task performance.

- **Effective Rank vs. Stable Rank**: Effective rank \(R(t) = \exp\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\log\det(J_t^\top J_t)] / d\bigr)\) measures multiplicative log‑volume contraction; it decays under composition. Stable rank \(m_B = \|H_B|_A\|_F^2 / \|H_B|_A\|_2^2\) (for Hessian restricted to \(T G_A\)) measures intrinsic curvature demand of a new task in a scale‑invariant way. *Why needed*: The threshold theorem compares \(m_B\) (demand) to \(R_A(t)\) (supply). *Quick check*: For singular values \([10,1,0.1]\), stable rank ≈ \((100+1+0.01)/100 = 1.01\), indicating most curvature is along a single dominant direction; for \([4,4,4]\), stable rank = 3, indicating isotropic curvature.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Task Geometry Module → Transport‑Jacobian Estimator → Compatible Effective Rank Calculator → Task Compatibility Checker → Dissipation Monitor

**Critical path**:  
1. Train to convergence on Task A → obtain \(\theta_A\).  
2. Estimate \(G_A\) and compute \(Q_A\) (orthonormal basis for \(T G_A\)).  
3. Begin sequential training on Task B. Periodically:  
   a. Estimate current transport Jacobian properties (via proxy).  
   b. Compute current \(R_A(t)\).  
   c. Compute \(m_B\) (stable rank of \(H_B\) restricted to \(Q_A\)).  
   d. If \(m_B > R_A(t)\), trigger intervention (e.g., replay, modular expansion, stop and preserve support).  
4. Correlate \(R_A(t)\) decay with eventual forgetting on a held‑out probe of Task A.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Precision vs. feasibility**: Direct Jacobian/determinant computation is intractable for large NNs; must rely on proxies (gradient covariance, ensemble finite differences) with estimation noise.  
- **Static vs. dynamic \(G_A\)**: Assuming \(Q_A\) fixed from task A may become inaccurate as parameters move; manifold may deform during later learning.  
- **Global vs. local \(m_B\)**: Computed at a point (usually \(\theta_A\)); if \(H_B|_A\) varies across \(G_A\), a single \(m_B\) may not represent global demand.  
- **Intervention timing**: Early warning requires accurate online \(R_A(t)\) estimation; delayed detection means forgetting already occurred.

**Failure signatures**:  
- **Spectral decoupling**: Rapid decay of small singular values in \(Q_A^\top J_t^\top J_t Q_A\) while task A loss remains flat.  
- **Gradient confinement**: Projection of \(\nabla L_B\) onto \(T G_A\) becomes negligible relative to total gradient norm.  
- **Threshold breach**: \(m_B > R_A(t)\) early in training on B, followed by a rise in \(L_A\).  
- **Replay ineffectiveness**: Standard replay fails to stabilize \(L_A\) even with large buffers, indicating dynamical rather than statistical interference.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Linear teacher‑student model**: Train a student network sequentially on two linear tasks; exactly compute \(J_t\), \(Q_A\), and \(H_B\); verify Theorem 1: forgetting occurs iff \(m_B > R_A\) at some \(t\).  
2. **Proxies for \(R_A(t)\) in small CNNs on Permuted MNIST**: Train a CNN on Task A; estimate \(Q_A\) via Hessian null‑space. During sequential training on Task B, track gradient covariance effective rank and Fisher participating ratio within \(Q_A\) subspace; correlate decay with final forgetting; test if \(m_B\) vs. proxy \(R_A(t)\) forecasts failure.  
3. **Intervention test**: Apply techniques to reduce anisotropic contraction (gradient clipping within \(T G_A\), isotropic noise, AdamW) during sequential training; measure whether interventions slow decay of the \(R_A(t)\) proxy and delay threshold crossing, reducing forgetting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the compatible effective rank—defined via Jacobian log‑volume contraction—be practically estimated in high‑dimensional neural networks without direct Jacobian computation?
2. Can empirical proxies such as Hessian spectral decay, gradient covariance effective rank, or structured perturbation sensitivity reliably detect the collapse of task‑preserving support and predict catastrophic forgetting?
3. How do common stochastic optimizers (e.g., SGD, Adam) influence the accumulation of excess dissipation and the decay of compatible effective rank under finite‑time learning?
4. Under what conditions does the capacity threshold theorem (\(m_B > R_A\)) fail to predict catastrophic forgetting, particularly in non‑convex loss landscapes or with complex task geometries?

## Limitations
- The task‑preserving manifold \(G_A\) and its tangent basis \(Q_A\) are assumed known; no practical method is provided for realistic, high‑dimensional networks.
- The transport Jacobian \(J_t\) is intractable to compute for modern architectures, forcing reliance on unvalidated proxies (e.g., gradient covariance).
- The theory assumes deterministic, smooth learning dynamics; stochastic effects (noise, dropout) may break submultiplicativity or even allow rank recovery.
- \(G_A\) is treated as static, but it may deform as learning on new tasks proceeds.
- Local curvature assumptions (e.g., \(\mu\)-strong convexity transverse to \(G_A\)) are not guaranteed in practice, limiting the link between Wasserstein distance and performance loss.

## Next Checks
1. **Exact threshold mapping in linear teacher‑student model**: Use analytically tractable setting to sweep task geometries and verify that forgetting occurs precisely when \(m_B > R_A(t)\).
2. **Proxy validation for \(R_A(t)\) in small CNNs**: Compute true \(R_A(t)\) via Jacobian for a small model; simultaneously record gradient covariance effective rank within \(Q_A\) subspace; test whether the proxy reliably predicts true \(R_A(t)\) decay and final forgetting.
3. **Intervention triggered by threshold**: During sequential training of a medium‑sized network, monitor \(m_B / R_A(t)\) using a feasible estimator (e.g., low‑rank Hessian approximation). When the ratio exceeds 1, apply a preservation intervention (e.g., freeze adaptation, add isotropic noise within \(T G_A\)) and compare forgetting against control.
