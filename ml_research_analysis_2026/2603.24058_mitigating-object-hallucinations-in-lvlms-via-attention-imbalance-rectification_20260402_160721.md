# Mitigating Object Hallucinations in LVLMs via Attention Imbalance Rectification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24058  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24058  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Attention Imbalance Rectification (AIR) reduces object hallucination rates by up to 35.1% (e.g., CHAIR sentence-level on LLaVA‑1.5 from 51.8 to 28.8) and improves general task performance by up to 15.9% (e.g., MM‑Vet overall from 27.6 to 32.0) without additional training.

## Executive Summary
Large Vision‑Language Models (LVLMs) often generate non-existent objects, undermining their reliability. This paper identifies imbalanced attention allocation between vision and language tokens as a key causal factor in such hallucinations. They propose Attention Imbalance Rectification (AIR), a lightweight decoding‑time intervention that selectively rectifies attention in a small set of hallucination‑sensitive heads. AIR operates through two mechanisms: (1) modality‑balanced attention reallocation, which suppresses excessive textual bias in those heads, and (2) variance‑constrained projection regularization, which homogenizes token‑wise attention distributions. Evaluated on four LVLMs with CHAIR, POPE, and MM‑Vet benchmarks, AIR achieves up to 35.1% hallucination reduction and up to 15.9% general performance improvement, outperforming seven baselines while adding negligible overhead.

## Method Summary
AIR requires a one‑time offline phase to identify hallucination‑sensitive heads (H_sens). Using an erasure‑based attribution method, each head’s effect size is computed by comparing token probabilities when the head is masked versus full model, separating hallucinated and non‑hallucinated tokens. The top‑20 heads by effect size become H_sens. During autoregressive decoding, for each generated token and each layer, the raw attention matrix A is computed for all heads. For heads in H_sens, the cumulative attention from all queries to text keys (V_text) is measured; if it exceeds a threshold τ_text (=0.3), attention is reallocated: entries attending to text keys are scaled down by λ (=0.1) and those attending to vision keys are scaled up by γ (=3.5). Regardless of trigger, the attention matrix then undergoes three‑step variance regularization: (1) zero‑trace projection (subtract mean diagonal), (2) energy‑preserving rescaling (multiply by ratio of original to projected Frobenius norms), and (3) shrinkage toward the mean matrix with coefficient β (=0.3). The regularized matrix A* is used to compute the head’s output. Additionally, all heads’ query‑key weights are pre‑scaled by a factor derived from the trace of W_QK² to mildly modulate attention logits. The intervention is hyperparameter‑driven and targets only a subset of heads, keeping computational cost low (~5‑10 ms per token).

## Key Results
- Up to 35.1% reduction in object hallucination (CHAIR sentence‑level on LLaVA‑1.5: 51.8 → 28.8).  
- Up to 15.9% improvement in general capability (MM‑Vet overall on LLaVA‑1.5: 27.6 → 32.0).  
- Consistent gains over seven baselines across four LVLMs on three benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE, MM‑Vet).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Targeting hallucination‑sensitive attention heads
**Claim**: AIR intervenes only on a small subset of heads causally linked to hallucinations, minimizing disruption to normal generation.  
**Mechanism**: An erasure‑based attribution computes each head’s “hallucination‑sensitive effect size” by measuring the change in token probabilities when that head is masked; heads with large positive effect sizes (the difference between hallucinated and non‑hallucinated token impacts) are selected as H_sens. Only these heads receive attention reallocation and regularization.  
**Core assumption**: Hallucination‑sensitive heads exhibit distinct attention patterns (e.g., higher modality imbalance) and can be reliably identified offline; intervening on them is sufficient to curb hallucinations.  
**Evidence anchors**: The paper reports that H_sens heads have higher MAI and stronger similarity to the base LLM’s attention patterns (section 4.2.2). Concurrent work (e.g., AD‑HH) also targets specific heads, supporting this paradigm.  
**Break condition**: Misidentification (e.g., due to dataset bias) may suppress useful heads, degrading performance; if hallucinations arise mostly from heads outside H_sens, the intervention will be ineffective.

### Mechanism 2: Modality‑balanced attention reallocation
**Claim**: Excessive textual bias in hallucination‑sensitive heads drives hallucinations; rebalancing cross‑modal attention improves grounding.  
**Mechanism**: For each head in H_sens, compute V_text = Σ_{i∈text_queries} Σ_{j∈text_keys} A_{ij}. If V_text > τ_text (0.3), the attention matrix is scaled element‑wise: text‑key entries multiplied by λ (0.1) and vision‑key entries by γ (3.5).  
**Core assumption**: Object hallucinations stem from disproportionate attention to language tokens relative to visual evidence; reallocation forces the model to weigh visual information more.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions “suppresses textual bias in sensitive heads”; full algorithm appears in section 5.1.  
**Break condition**: Inappropriate λ/γ or τ_text can cause visual dominance, instability, or never trigger; the threshold may need sequence‑length normalization.

### Mechanism 3: Variance‑constrained projection regularization
**Claim**: Token‑wise attention imbalance (high TAI) also contributes to hallucinations; regularizing variance yields more uniform distributions and reduces over‑concentration on spurious tokens.  
**Mechanism**: After conditional reallocation, apply sequentially: (1) zero‑trace projection: subtract (trace(A’)/L)·I; (2) energy‑preserving rescaling: multiply by ∥A’∥_F / (∥Â∥_F + ε); (3) shrinkage: A* = (1‑β)·Ã + β·mean(Ã)·1, with β=0.3.  
**Core assumption**: Reducing the variance of the attention distribution mitigates the model’s tendency to fixate on irrelevant tokens that lead to hallucinations.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract cites “homogenizes token attention distributions”; section 5.2 derives the steps; related works like DoLA manipulate attention distributions via contrastive decoding.  
**Break condition**: Excessive β oversmooths attention, hurting fine‑grained reasoning; rescaling may amplify noise if projected norm becomes too small, causing numerical instability.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Offline head selection → Compute H_sens → Decoding (for each token: compute attention for all heads; if head ∈ H_sens: compute V_text, conditional reallocation, variance regularization; else use raw attention; combine heads, add residual, FFN, generate next token).

**Critical path**  
1. Autoregressive decoding: embed current token, obtain sequence representation.  
2. For each layer ℓ and each head h: compute raw attention A(ℓ,h) from QK.  
3. If h ∈ H_sens:  
   - Compute V_text = Σ_{i∈text_queries} Σ_{j∈text_keys} A_{ij}.  
   - If V_text > τ_text: reallocate (scale text keys λ, vision keys γ) → A′; else A′ = A.  
   - Regularize: ˆA = A′ − (trace(A′)/L)·I; Ã = ˆA·(∥A′∥_F / (∥ˆA∥_F + ε)); A* = (1‑β)·Ã + β·mean(Ã).  
   - Use A* to compute head output U = W_V X A*.  
4. If h ∉ H_sens: U = W_V X A.  
5. Combine heads, add residual, apply FFN, sample next token.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Efficiency vs. coverage: Targeting only top‑20 heads keeps overhead low (~5‑10 ms per token) but may miss some hallucination sources.  
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: λ, γ, β, τ_text require model‑specific tuning; defaults work well on tested models but may not generalize.  
- General performance balance: Strong reallocation/regularization can oversuppress language or flatten attention, harming tasks needing nuance; moderate β (0.3‑0.6) recommended.  
- Modality token identification: Requires accurate index sets for vision vs. text tokens; vision tokens are typically the 576 image patch embeddings after ⟨image⟩ placeholder, but exact mapping depends on prompt template.

**Failure signatures**  
- Hallucination rates (CHAIR) do not decrease or increase relative to greedy decoding.  
- General benchmark scores (MM‑Vet) drop by >5%.  
- Attention matrices become degenerate (near‑uniform, all zeros except diagonal, or extreme values).  
- Generated text becomes repetitive, incoherent, or overly short.  
- Large variance across random seeds indicating instability.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Reallocation trigger test**: For a fixed input, compute V_text for each sensitive head; verify A is scaled only when V_text > τ_text. Check that scaling factors match λ and γ.  
2. **Variance reduction test**: On a sample batch, compute row‑wise variance of attention matrices before and after regularization; ensure variance decreases on average without collapsing to zero.  
3. **Small‑scale hallucination check**: Run AIR on 100 COCO images with a fixed prompt; compare CHAIR sentence‑level with baseline. Expect at least 10% relative reduction; if not, diagnose head selection or hyperparameters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Causality of attention imbalance** – Is the observed correlation between attention imbalance and hallucination causal, or merely a correlate? The paper uses erasure studies but does not isolate causality. Evidence would require controlled manipulation of attention while holding other factors constant.  
2. **Generalization to specialized domains** – Does AIR work on domain‑specific LVLMs (e.g., medical imaging, autonomous driving) where hallucination patterns differ? Experiments so far are limited to general‑purpose models on COCO‑derived benchmarks. Evaluation on domain‑specific models and benchmarks is needed.  
3. **Source of general performance gains** – Why does AIR improve MM‑Vet scores alongside reduced hallucinations? Is the gain a side‑effect of better visual grounding or an independent effect of attention rectification? Per‑sample correlation analysis and component ablations could clarify.  
4. **Zero‑shot head identification** – Can hallucination‑sensitive heads be identified without per‑model erasure experiments? The current method requires expensive, model‑specific attribution. Finding a generic rule or learned predictor across models would enable instant deployment.

## Limitations
- Vision/text token indices depend on prompt template and are not precisely defined, making V_text computation ambiguous.  
- Threshold τ_text is given as an absolute value (0.3) without normalization, making it sensitive to sequence‑length variations.  
- The top‑20 hallucination‑sensitive heads are selected via a costly per‑model erasure study; the exact list is not disclosed, hindering reproduction and cross‑model adaptation.  
- The regularized attention matrix is not constrained to be stochastic, potentially causing training‑inference mismatch and numerical instability.
