# Regret Analysis of Unichain Average Reward Constrained MDPs with General Parameterization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08000
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08000
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves order-optimal Õ(√T) regret and constraint violation for unichain average-reward CMDPs with general function approximation via a primal-dual actor-critic algorithm with multi-level Monte Carlo and a burn-in phase.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs) under unichain structure—allowing transient states and periodic behavior—with general parameterized policies. The authors propose a primal-dual natural actor-critic algorithm that integrates a logarithmic burn-in phase and multi-level Monte Carlo (MLMC) gradient estimation. The burn-in ensures policy updates occur only after the process enters the recurrent class, while MLMC efficiently estimates gradients despite transient dynamics. The analysis yields finite-time Õ(√T) bounds on regret and constraint violation, marking the first order-optimal guarantees for this challenging setting and highlighting the interplay between non-ergodic dynamics and function approximation.

## Method Summary
The algorithm begins with a burn-in period (of order log T) during which no policy updates occur, allowing the Markov chain to stabilize within its recurrent class. After burn-in, it employs a primal-dual framework: a critic network approximates differential value functions, and an actor network parameterizes the policy. Gradients are estimated using MLMC, which correlates samples across multiple levels to dramatically reduce variance compared to standard Monte Carlo—from O(√T) to O(log T) inner-loop complexity. The primal and dual variables are updated with adaptive step sizes to balance reward maximization and constraint satisfaction, all while relying only on instantaneous samples.

## Key Results
- Provides Õ(√T) regret and cumulative constraint violation bounds, optimal up to inherent approximation errors, for unichain average-reward CMDPs with general parameterization.
- MLMC estimator reduces inner-loop sample complexity from O(√T) to O(log T) while remaining unbiased under unichain dynamics.
- The explicit logarithmic burn-in phase ensures all updates occur within the recurrent class, mitigating biases from transient states.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MLMC reduces gradient variance by constructing correlated sample paths across refinement levels, which is particularly effective in unichain MDPs where standard Monte Carlo suffers from high variance due to transient phases and periodicity. The burn-in phase lets the chain mix into its recurrent class before learning begins, ensuring that value and gradient estimates reflect long-run average behavior rather than transient effects. The primal-dual update naturally handles constraints through Lagrange multipliers, with adaptive steps maintaining stability even when the critic and actor are imperfectly approximated. Together, these components isolate the recurrent class dynamics and produce low-bias, low-variance signals for optimization.

- **Multi-level Monte Carlo (MLMC)**: Variance reduction via telescoping sums over coarse-to-fine simulations. Why needed: Efficient gradient estimation when paths are long or mixing is slow. Quick check: How does correlation between MLMC levels reduce variance?

## Limitations
- Impact of long transient phases or small recurrent classes on burn-in duration and overall sample complexity is not quantified practically.
- Approximation error due to general function approximation is acknowledged but not characterized in terms of policy/critic network complexity for unichain dynamics.
- MLMC variance reduction relies on mixing-time bounds that may be difficult to compute and potentially loose for periodic or near-transient unichain MDPs.

## Confidence
- **Õ(√T) regret and constraint violation orders**: Medium (derivation hinges on assumptions about gradient estimator bias and mixing times lacking empirical validation in extreme non-ergodic cases).
- **MLMC reduces inner-loop complexity to O(log T)**: High (follows directly from MLMC theory; adaptation to unichain gradient estimation is plausible but requires empirical verification).
- **Burn-in ensures updates within recurrent class**: Medium (logical if recurrent class is identifiable, but online detection of recurrent class onset is not addressed).
