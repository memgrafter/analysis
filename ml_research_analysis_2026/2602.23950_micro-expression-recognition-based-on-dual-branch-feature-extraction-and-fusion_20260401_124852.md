# Micro-expression Recognition Based on Dual-branch Feature Extraction and Fusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23950
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23950
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Achieves 74.67% accuracy on CASME II, outperforming LBP-TOP by 11.26% and MSMMT by 3.36%.

## Executive Summary
Micro-expressions are fleeting and subtle, posing a challenge for optical flow-based methods. This paper introduces a dual-branch network that separately extracts global and local features using ResNet12 and Inception backbones, fusing them via a convolutional block attention mechanism (CBAM)-based adaptive fusion module (CAFFM). Evaluated on the CASME II dataset, the method reaches 74.67% accuracy. Ablation studies highlight ResNet12's suitability for the small dataset, avoiding overfitting pitfalls of deeper networks.

## Method Summary
The approach processes onset-apex frame pairs from micro-expression videos. Facial regions are detected, aligned, and resized to 231×282 pixels. A dual-branch extractor uses a shallow ResNet12 for global context and an Inception-style network for localized muscle movement patterns (e.g., brows, mouth). Their feature maps are fused through CAFFM, which sequentially applies channel and spatial attention via stacked CBAM modules to adaptively weight informative channels and spatial locations. The fused representation is pooled and classified. Training uses Adam (lr=0.001), step decay every 100 epochs, batch size 64, for 500 epochs on CASME II’s 255 samples.

## Key Results
- 74.67% accuracy on CASME II, surpassing LBP-TOP (63.41%) and MSMMT (71.31%).
- Ablation confirms ResNet12 (75.77%) outperforms deeper ResNets (e.g., ResNet18 at 74.34%) on the small dataset, mitigating overfitting.
- Full CAFFM fusion (74.67%) significantly improves over dual-branch without adaptive fusion (71.16%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A shallow ResNet12 backbone mitigates overfitting on the small CASME II dataset, enabling superior global feature extraction compared to deeper ResNets.
- **Mechanism:** The residual structure of ResNet12 provides hierarchical learning capacity while its limited depth (12 layers) reduces parameters and complexity, better matching the dataset scale. Skip connections maintain gradient flow.
- **Core assumption:** Micro-expression datasets have inherent sample scarcity, making deep networks prone to overfitting unless constrained by architecture.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV.C] Ablation shows ResNet12 (75.77% Acc) outperforms ResNet18 (74.34%) and ResNet34 (71.15%), attributed to "dataset scale mismatched with model complexity."
  - [abstract] Explicitly states ResNet12 is used and "ablation studies confirm ResNet12's suitability for the small dataset, avoiding overfitting from deeper networks."
- **Break condition:** With a much larger dataset (>10,000 samples), the overfitting advantage of ResNet12 may diminish, and deeper architectures could achieve higher upper-bound performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** An Inception-based branch captures discriminative local features from specific facial regions (e.g., mouth, brows), suppressing interference from irrelevant background or stable facial areas.
- **Mechanism:** Inception employs parallel convolutional filters of multiple scales (1x1, 3x3, 5x5) within a layer, detecting subtle, localized motion patterns at different spatial extents. Concatenated multi-scale outputs enhance fine-grained feature representation without a large parameter increase.
- **Core assumption:** Micro-expressions involve highly localized muscle movements (Action Units) better captured by multi-scale, region-focused processing than uniform global processing.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section I & II] Abstract and introduction state Inception is "constructed to enhance model representation and suppress interference from irrelevant regions."
  - [section III.A] Framework inputs "facial key regions" into the Inception branch.
- **Break condition:** If micro-expressions manifest primarily as very subtle, full-face intensity changes rather than localized AU activations, the multi-scale local advantage may be less pronounced.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The CBAM-based adaptive fusion module (CAFFM) effectively integrates global and local features by dynamically weighting their channel and spatial importance, enhancing fine-grained discrimination.
- **Mechanism:** CAFFM sequentially applies Channel Attention (global pooling + MLP) and Spatial Attention (channel-wise pooling + convolution), learning *what* to emphasize across channels (e.g., AU-responsive filters) and *where* to focus spatially (e.g., active brows vs. mouth). Embedded in fusion, it adaptively combines complementary information.
- **Core assumption:** Simple concatenation or addition of global and local features is sub-optimal; an attention-guided, learned weighting mechanism handles variable relevance across samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract & section I] Proposes a "CBAM-based feature fusion module" to address "lack of effective attention in dual-branch models."
  - [section III.A] Describes dual-branch features integrated via "three consecutive CBAM modules" for "adaptive weight adjustment."
  - [section IV.D, Table 7] Ablation: full DBFEM+CAFFM (74.67%) outperforms DBFEM only (71.16%) and incomplete CAFFM variants, indicating the importance of combined, adaptive fusion.
- **Break condition:** If the two branches produce highly correlated or redundant features, dynamic re-weighting confers little benefit and could introduce unnecessary complexity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Residual Networks (ResNet)**
  - **Why needed here:** The global branch relies on ResNet12. Understanding skip connections and gradient flow is essential to grasp why this architecture is chosen for small datasets.
  - **Quick check question:** How do identity mappings in ResNet blocks help combat gradient vanishing compared to a plain convolutional stack?
- **Concept: Inception Architecture**
  - **Why needed here:** The local branch uses an Inception structure. Its multi-scale filter design is key to capturing varied spatial extents of micro-movements.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the computational advantage of using 1x1 convolutions within an Inception module before larger (3x3, 5x5) convolutions?
- **Concept: Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)**
  - **Why needed here:** CAFFM is built from stacked CBAMs. This mechanism is core to the adaptive fusion claim.
  - **Quick check question:** In CBAM, what is the sequential order of channel and spatial attention, and what is the purpose of each stage?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Input Pre-processor: Face detection (DNN model) & alignment, cropping to facial regions, resizing to 231x282.
  2. Dual-Branch Feature Extractor: Branch A (Global): ResNet12 backbone. Branch B (Local): Inception backbone (likely Inception-v3 style). Both process cropped face images.
  3. Fusion & Refinement Engine: CAFFM: First fusion (concat/ADD) → 3× CBAM → (Concat + ReLU + Residual Add) → 2× CBAM → Activation → Max-Pooling → Classifier.
  4. Output Layer: Fully connected layer for classification into expression categories.
- **Critical path:** Face image → (Parallel paths) ResNet12 & Inception → Feature Concatenation → CAFFM (Sequence of CBAMs & residuals) → Pooling → Linear Classifier → Expression Label.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Model Complexity vs. Dataset Size:** Choosing ResNet12 (shallow) over deeper variants (18, 34) is a direct tradeoff to avoid overfitting on 255 samples, sacrificing potential representational power.
  - **Global vs. Local Focus:** Using two separate branches increases parameter count and inference time versus a single-stream network, but is posited to improve accuracy by capturing complementary cues.
  - **Fusion Sophistication:** CAFFM adds computational overhead for adaptive weighting. A simpler fusion (e.g., averaging) would be faster but less effective.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High confusion between "Surprise" and "Repression" (Figure 6 confusion matrix), indicating struggles with similar AU patterns.
  - Overall accuracy capped at ~75% on CASME II, suggesting limitations in capturing transient subtlety with purely spatial, frame-based models.
  - Performance drop with deeper ResNet (observed in ablation) is a clear signature of overfitting; training loss diverges from validation loss early.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation on Backbone Depth (Section IV.C): Compare ResNet12, 18, 34 to validate the "shallow is better" hypothesis. Metrics: Accuracy, UF1, UAR.
  2. Ablation on Fusion Module (Section IV.D, Table 7): Compare (a) Dual-branch without CAFFM (DBFEM), (b) DBFEM+full CAFFM, (c) DBFEM+incomplete CAFFM variants. Isolates adaptive attention fusion contribution.
  3. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (Section IV.D, Table 8): Benchmark against LBP-TOP, 3D-CNNs, SSSN, MSMMT, etc. on CASME II. Metrics: Accuracy, weighted Precision, weighted Recall.

## Limitations
- Small dataset: CASME II (255 samples) restricts model capacity and may inflate relative gains; overfitting risk persists despite shallow ResNet12.
- Missing architecture details: Exact ResNet12 configuration (Basic vs Bottleneck, channel dimensions) and CAFFM fusion logic (concat vs add) are unspecified.
- Evaluation scope: Results only on CASME II; no cross-dataset validation, so claim of broad superiority is unsupported.

## Confidence
- ResNet12 prevents overfitting: **High** (ablation explicitly shows deeper ResNets underperform on same small dataset).
- Inception captures local features: **Medium** (region-based input and multi-scale design are conceptually sound, but no direct ablation isolating Inception’s role).
- CAFFM adaptive fusion works: **Medium** (full model outperforms incomplete variants, but precise contribution of sequential CBAM ordering is not disentangled).
