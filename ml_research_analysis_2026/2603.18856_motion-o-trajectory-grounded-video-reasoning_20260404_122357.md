# Motion-o: Trajectory-Grounded Video Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18856  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18856  
- **Reference count:** 27  
- **Primary result:** Motion-o introduces Motion Chain of Thought (MCoT) with explicit `<motion/>` tags and trajectory-level supervision, boosting spatial-temporal reasoning (e.g., +3.9 on VideoMME, +25.0 on MotionBench) without architectural changes.

## Executive Summary
Motion-o addresses a key gap in video vision-language models (VLMs): the lack of explicit motion reasoning. It augments spatio-temporal evidence chains with structured `<motion/>` tags that summarize per-object direction, speed, and scale change between grounded observations. Training uses a trajectory-grounding dataset that densifies sparse keyframes via mask interpolation to provide continuous motion supervision. A two-stage pipeline—supervised fine-tuning to instill the MCoT format, followed by reinforcement learning with a dual reward (trajectory matching + visual grounding verification)—ensures motion descriptions are both accurate and evidence-based. Without modifying the base VLM architecture, Motion-o significantly improves performance on video reasoning benchmarks, especially those demanding fine-grained trajectory understanding.

## Method Summary
Motion-o builds on a base VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) by first constructing a trajectory-grounding dataset: starting from sparse keyframe boxes and dense masks in the PLM subset of STGR, intermediate bounding boxes are interpolated along object tracks to obtain continuous trajectories. From these, motion primitives (direction, speed, scale change) are discretized into bins and injected into reasoning traces as `<motion/>` tags. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on this augmented data to teach the model the MCoT format (grounding objects with `<obj>`, `<box>`, `<t>` tags interleaved with `<motion/>`). Stage 2 applies reinforcement learning via Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) with a composite reward: `r = r_acc + r_thk + r_fmt`, where `r_thk` includes a novel motion component `rmotion = r_traj + r_ground`. `r_traj` scores exact or adjacent bin matches against ground-truth motion; `r_ground` compares chains from the original video and a motion-masked version (frames frozen) to penalize non-visual shortcuts. This dual reward forces motion predictions to be both correct and grounded in temporal evidence.

## Key Results
- On V-STAR, Motion-o achieves 35.5 mAM (vs. 33.7 for Open-o3 Video); Open-o3+MCoT reaches 36.6 mAM.
- On VideoMME, gains are +3.9 to 67.5 (Motion-o) and +6.1 to 69.7 (Open-o3+MCoT).
- Extended benchmarks show large improvements: TVGBench +21.3, MotionBench +25.0; Open-o3+MCoT exceeds GPT-4o on MotionBench (63.0 vs. 33.0).
- Ablations confirm dense annotations boost mAM by +4.3 and discrete motion bins are essential (continuous values cause collapse).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Explicit Motion Tagging
MCoT converts implicit motion into explicit, structured descriptors that connect grounded observations into trajectory-consistent reasoning. After grounding an object at multiple timestamps, the model emits a self-closing `<motion/>` tag with discrete attributes (dir, speed, scale) summarizing the trajectory linking those observations. This forces the model to articulate dynamics rather than leave them implicit. Discrete bins (8 compass directions + stationary, 3 speed levels, 3 scale changes) are sufficiently expressive and reliably predictable. Ablation with numerical tags leads to collapse (Table 3), confirming the necessity of discrete bins.

### Mechanism 2: Dense Trajectory Supervision
Densifying sparse keyframes via mask interpolation yields continuous bounding box tracks that provide accurate ground-truth motion primitives for training. Starting from sparse keyframe boxes and dense masks in the PLM dataset, intermediate boxes are interpolated along object tracks. From these dense tracks, motion descriptors are automatically computed and injected into reasoning traces during SFT, providing trajectory-level supervision. Interpolation acts as a faithful proxy for continuous motion under typical conditions, though it may fail under heavy occlusion or highly non-linear motion.

### Mechanism 3: Dual Reward Design
Combining a trajectory reward (`r_traj` matching predicted motion to ground-truth tracks) and a visual grounding reward (`r_ground` via dual-chain verification against motion-masked inputs) ensures motion descriptions are both accurate and visually grounded. `r_traj` gives partial credit for adjacent bins; `r_ground` compares `<motion/>` tags from original and motion-masked videos—a change indicates evidence dependence. Ablation removing `r_ground` leads to significant drop in spatial grounding and overall mAM (Table 1), confirming its role in preventing shortcutting.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or Open-o3 Video checkpoint) -> Trajectory-grounding dataset (augmented STGR with dense tracks and motion descriptors) -> SFT module (instills MCoT format) -> RL module (GSPO optimizer) -> Inference (generates reasoning with `<obj>`, `<box>`, `<t>`, `<motion/>` tags). Rewards: `r_acc`, `r_thk` (includes `r_traj`, `r_ground`), `r_fmt`. Motion masking module generates motion-masked videos for `r_ground`. Track computation derives motion bins from dense tracks.

### Critical path
1. Preprocess: For each training sample, compute dense object tracks from masks and derive motion descriptors.
2. SFT: Train model on motion-augmented traces to learn `<motion/>` tag syntax and spatio-temporal grounding.
3. RL: For each (video, question) pair, sample K completions; compute rewards (answer accuracy, format, thinking including `r_traj` and `r_ground`); update policy via GSPO.
4. Inference: Model generates reasoning with structured tags and final answer.

### Design tradeoffs
- Discrete bins vs. continuous values: Discrete bins ensure learnability; continuous values cause catastrophic failure (Table 3).
- Dense annotation interpolation: Improves supervision but may introduce noise under challenging motion (occlusions, non-linear paths).
- No architectural changes: Leverages existing VLM but inherits its spatial grounding limitations (e.g., coarse boxes).
- Motion masking aggressiveness: Too weak fails to remove cues; too strong may alter static semantics.

### Failure signatures
- Low motion tag generation rate (omitted/malformed tags) indicates insufficient SFT or prompt engineering.
- `r_ground` near zero across samples suggests motion masking is ineffective or model ignores temporal cues.
- Spatial grounding poor (low Visual IoU on V-STAR Where) due to coarse base VLM boxes.
- Training instability: reward imbalance (e.g., overemphasizing `r_traj`) or poor KL tuning leads to forgetting QA accuracy.

### First 3 experiments
1. Ablation on annotation density: Compare performance with sparse keyframes vs. dense interpolated tracks (Table 3, top).

3. Ablation on `r_ground`: Train without visual grounding reward and measure drop in spatial grounding and overall mAM (Table 1, w/o `r_ground` rows).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-object interactions:** MCoT treats each object’s motion independently; extending to relative motions and interactions (e.g., chasing, handovers) requires relational motion descriptors and new datasets.
- **Non-linear trajectories:** Single motion tag per track cannot capture turns, acceleration, or depth oscillations; temporal segmentation or continuous parameterization (e.g., splines) is needed.
- **Richer motion representations:** Discrete bins are coarse but essential; hybrid schemes (discretized continuous ranges, learned codebooks) could balance expressivity and learnability without collapse.
- **Spatial grounding accuracy:** Inaccurate bounding boxes from the base VLM propagate noise into motion labels; integrating a stronger detector/tracker is necessary.

## Limitations
- Motion-masking procedure is underspecified (frame-freezing details unknown), risking poor `r_ground` calibration.
- Dense track interpolation may fail under heavy occlusion or highly non-linear motion, degrading supervision quality.
- Composite reward weights (`r_traj`, `r_ground`, `r_acc`) are not disclosed, training sensitivity remains.
