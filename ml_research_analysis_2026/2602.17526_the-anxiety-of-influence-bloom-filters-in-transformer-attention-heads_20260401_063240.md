# The Anxiety of Influence: Bloom Filters in Transformer Attention Heads

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17526  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17526  
- Reference count: 22  
- Primary result: The paper identifies three attention heads in GPT-2 small that function as Bloom filter analogs, forming a multi-resolution membership testing system that generalizes across token types and models, with ablation impacting both repeated and novel token processing.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether individual transformer attention heads implement membership testing analogous to Bloom filters. By measuring attention patterns in response to controlled stimuli that fix sequence length while varying the number of unique tokens, the authors distinguish true Bloom filter behavior from general prefix-attention artifacts. They identify three genuine Bloom filter heads in GPT-2 small (L0H1, L0H5, L1H11), while a fourth candidate (L3H0) is reclassified as a prefix-attention head after confound control. L1H11 exhibits a classic Bloom filter saturation curve (R² = 1.0) with a capacity of ~5 bits, whereas L0H1 and L0H5 maintain near-zero false positive rates (0–4%) even with 180 unique tokens—far beyond classical 64-bit Bloom capacity. These heads form a multi-resolution system with false positive rates that decay monotonically with embedding distance, consistent with distance-sensitive Bloom filters. The behavior generalizes to any repeated token type, not just names, and head ablation affects both repeated and novel token processing. The findings replicate across GPT-2 small/medium/large and Pythia-160M, and hold in naturalistic text validation.

## Method Summary
The study uses autoregressive transformer models (GPT-2 small/medium/large and Pythia-160M) accessed via TransformerLens. A suite of carefully constructed stimuli probes head behavior: (1) 100 sentence triplets (exact repeat, no repeat, synonym) to compute selectivity, miss rate, and false positive ratio; (2) fixed-length capacity contexts (200 tokens) with varying unique token counts (5–180) to measure capacity curves; (3) 1,284 similarity-graded probe sentences to map false positive rates as a function of cosine distance; and (4) WikiText-103 passages for naturalistic validation. Key metrics include selectivity (attention to repeats vs baseline), miss rate (<0.01 attention), binary false positive rate (attention >0.1 threshold), and capacity fitted to the Bloom filter formula. Confound control—especially fixing sequence length while varying unique tokens—is critical to separate Bloom-like membership testing from simple prefix attention. Ablation (mean and zero) quantifies causal impact on perplexity for repeat vs. no-repeat conditions.

## Key Results
- Three attention heads in GPT-2 small (L0H1, L0H5, L1H11) are genuine Bloom filter analogs; a fourth (L3H0) is reclassified as a general prefix-attention head.
- L1H11 follows a classical saturation curve (R² = 1.0) with capacity ~5 bits; L0H1 and L0H5 exhibit near-zero false positive rates (0–4%) even with 180 unique tokens, exceeding classical Bloom capacity.
- The Bloom heads form a multi-resolution system: false positive rates decay monotonically with embedding distance, bandwidth varies across heads, and pairwise independence is high (mean φ=0.097). Behavior generalizes across token types and models, survives naturalistic validation, and ablation impairs processing of both repeated and novel tokens.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Membership testing via QK projections
- **Claim:** Some attention heads implement membership testing via QK projections that act as locality-sensitive hash functions, producing high attention for tokens seen before and graded false positives for similar tokens.
- **Mechanism:** The QK dot product computes similarity in embedding space. For Bloom filter heads, this similarity function is tuned such that exact repeats produce near-certain high attention (zero misses), while non-repeated tokens produce attention only if their embedding lies within a head-specific threshold of a previously seen token. This creates a distance-sensitive approximate membership test.
- **Core assumption:** Attention magnitude serves as a proxy for membership decision; the head’s output distribution is functionally a membership signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "false positive rates that decay monotonically with embedding distance, consistent with distance-sensitive Bloom filters."
  - Section 4.5: Hash resolution profiles show smooth sigmoidal decay in both attention and FP rate as cosine similarity decreases; bandwidth varies across heads.
  - Corpus: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers discuss attention mechanisms and Bloom filters separately (e.g., "AttenMIA: LLM Membership Inference Attack through Attention Signals" uses attention for membership but not as inherent head computation; "Knocking-Heads Attention" addresses multi-head capacity trade-offs).
- **Break condition:** If attention-to-similar-tokens does not decay monotonically with embedding distance across multiple heads, or if the capacity curve does not hold under fixed-sequence-length controls.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-resolution system via complementary bandwidths
- **Claim:** Different Bloom filter heads operate at distinct similarity bandwidths, providing complementary membership signals from ultra-precise to broad.
- **Mechanism:** Each head’s QK projection has a different effective radius in embedding space. Precise heads (e.g., L0H5) fire only for near-identical tokens, while broader heads (e.g., L1H11) fire for a wider semantic neighborhood. Downstream layers combine these independent decisions; the AND-like combination reduces overall false positives because errors are largely independent.
- **Core assumption:** Downstream layers integrate signals from multiple heads; independence between heads’ FP decisions allows for combinatorial error reduction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "They form a multi-resolution system... with false positive rates that decay monotonically with embedding distance."
  - Section 4.5: Distinct bandwidths (L0H5 noise floor by cos 0.5, L1H11 retains 17% FP at cos 0.4); Section 4.4 low pairwise φ (mean 0.097) indicates independence.
  - Corpus: No direct anchor; related work on multi-head attention discusses capacity trade-offs but not multi-resolution membership.
- **Break condition:** If bandwidths overlap completely or pairwise FP correlations are high (>0.5), undermining functional diversity.

### Mechanism 3: Confound-controlled experimental design
- **Claim:** A genuine Bloom filter’s false positive rate depends on the number of unique items stored (n), not total sequence length. Fixed sequence length controls isolate this effect from prefix-attention artifacts.
- **Mechanism:** Under fixed sequence length, a prefix-attention head shows constant FP=100% regardless of n (attends to all prefix tokens), while Bloom heads show n-dependent FP (saturating or near-zero). Varying both n and length confounds the two.
- **Core assumption:** Attention to a probe token reflects membership testing only if it varies with unique token count after controlling for length.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.3: L3H0 reclassification: FP=100% at all n under fixed length; L1H11 shows classic saturation curve (R²=1.0).
  - Abstract: "L3H0 was reclassified as a general prefix-attention head after confound controls revealed its apparent capacity curve was a sequence-length artifact."
  - Corpus: Weak; no direct parallels, though "Surgical Repair of Collapsed Attention Heads" highlights careful head characterization.
- **Break condition:** If a head’s FP rate remains constant under fixed sequence length across varying n, it is not a membership tester.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Transformer Attention Mechanics (QK-V mechanism)
- **Why needed:** The hypothesized Bloom mechanism relies on the QK dot product as a similarity/hash function. Understanding that attention weights derive from QK⊤ is essential to interpret the hash resolution analysis.
- **Quick check:** If a head’s query vector for token *x* has high dot product with key vectors of a previously seen token *y*, what does this imply about the head’s behavior toward *x* at the position of *y*?

### Concept: Bloom Filter Theory (capacity, false positives, hash functions)
- **Why needed:** The paper’s central analogy and capacity curve fitting require understanding false positive rate dependence on load (n/m) and number of hash functions (k).
- **Quick check:** What happens to the false positive rate of a classical Bloom filter as the number of stored elements (n) approaches the bit capacity (m)?

### Concept: Confound Control in Behavioral Analysis
- **Why needed:** The reclassification of L3H0 underscores that naive capacity measurements (varying both n and length) confound two factors. Isolating variables is critical to avoid false positives in head classification.
- **Quick check:** In the capacity experiment, why is it insufficient to simply measure FP rate while varying the number of unique tokens without fixing total sequence length?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Models (GPT-2 small/medium/large, Pythia-160M) → Stimuli (sentence triplets, fixed-length capacity contexts, similarity-graded probes, WikiText-103) → Per-head attention extraction → Metrics (selectivity, miss rate, FP ratio, capacity curve, similarity decay) → Classification criteria (selectivity>3×, miss<0.01, FP decay) → Confound controls (fixed length) → Ablation (mean/zero) → Cross-model replication & naturalistic validation

### Critical path
1. **Discovery:** Run basic sentence triplets on all heads → compute selectivity, miss, FP ratio → identify top candidates (e.g., L0H1, L0H5, L1H11, L3H0).
2. **Validation:** Apply confound-controlled capacity test (fixed 200-length, vary unique tokens) → reclassify artifacts (L3H0) → confirm genuine Bloom heads.
3. **Characterization:** Conduct hash resolution sweep (graded cosine similarity) → measure bandwidth; check cross-model (GPT-2 medium/large, Pythia); perform naturalistic validation on WikiText.
4. **Causal role:** Perform mean and zero ablation → measure repeat vs. no-repeat perplexity deltas → assess functional impact.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Template vs. natural text:** Templates control confounds but lack ecological validity; WikiText validation confirms generalizability but introduces noise (function words). The paper uses both.
- **Ablation method:** Mean ablation (replace with mean activation) is more principled but may understate specialization; zero ablation simpler but introduces nonlinearities. The paper prioritizes mean ablation but reports both.
- **Threshold choices:** FP binary threshold (0.1) and selectivity cutoff (>3×) are somewhat arbitrary but justified by large effect sizes; alternative thresholds unlikely to change classifications given extreme separations.

### Failure signatures
- **L3H0 reclassification:** High selectivity on basic test but flat FP=100% under fixed sequence length → generalized prefix attention, not membership testing.
- **Saturation at very low n (<10):** Suggests capacity too low for functional membership testing in realistic contexts.
- **High miss rate (>10%):** Violates Bloom filter guarantee of zero false negatives.
- **Non-monotonic or flat similarity decay:** Inconsistent with locality-sensitive hashing; indicates head not implementing graded false positives.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Basic signature scan:** Run sentence triplets on all heads → compute selectivity, miss rate, FP ratio → identify top 4–5 candidates (expect extreme selectivity, ~0% miss, moderate FP ratio).
2. **Confound-controlled capacity test:** For each candidate, fix sequence length (200) and vary unique tokens (5, 20, 50, 100, 180) → plot FP vs. n. Genuine Bloom: L1H11 shows rising FP saturating by n=50; L0H1/L0H5 show flat near-zero FP; prefix-attention (L3H0) shows flat FP=100%.
3. **Hash resolution sweep:** For genuine Bloom heads, run similarity-graded probes (10 cosine levels) → plot normalized attention and FP rate vs. cosine. Expect monotonic decay; measure bandwidth (cosine where FP hits noise floor). Compare across heads to confirm multi-resolution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Do Bloom filter heads implement locality-sensitive hashing in their QK projections?** Only behavioral evidence exists; direct analysis of the weight matrices is needed to confirm the hash mechanism.
2. **Do false positive activations in Bloom filter heads have a functional role in generation?** The paper speculates they might foster creative misprision, but causal impact on generation (e.g., hallucinations, creativity) remains untested via selective suppression.
3. **Are Bloom filter heads present in much larger language models, and do their properties scale with model size?** The study covers models up to 708M parameters; it is unknown whether these heads emerge in frontier-scale models (>10B). Applying the identification protocol to larger pretrained models would test prevalence and scaling.

## Limitations
- The ultra-high capacity of L0H1/L0H5 (near-zero FP at n=180) conflicts with classical Bloom theory, raising questions about non-classical implementation or fixed-length stimulus artifacts.
- The QK-projected space remains uncharacterized; observed similarity decay in input embedding space may not reflect true locality-sensitive hashing.
- Ablation effects do not isolate Bloom-specific computation from general redundancy processing or other head functions; further interventions are needed.

## Next Checks
1. **Multi-head combinatorial ablation:** Abolate pairs of Bloom heads and measure joint false positive rates on capacity stimuli. Independent Bloom systems should show multiplicative FP reduction; high dependence would indicate overlapping bandwidths.
2. **Direct QK collision profiling:** For a Bloom head, sample token pairs with graded input-cosine similarities, compute their QK dot products, and correlate collision rates (dot-product > threshold) with attention FP rates. A tight correlation would support QK as a locality-sensitive hash.
3. **Naturalistic capacity stress test:** Use long, diverse natural documents (e.g., book chapters) to vary unique tokens widely without length confounds. Check whether ultra-precise heads maintain near-zero false positives or eventually saturate, testing ecological validity of claimed capacity.
