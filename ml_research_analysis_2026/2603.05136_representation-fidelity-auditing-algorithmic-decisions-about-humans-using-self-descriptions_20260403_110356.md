# Representation Fidelity:Auditing Algorithmic Decisions About Humans Using Self-Descriptions

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.05136
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05136
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces 'representation fidelity' to measure mismatch between algorithmic and self-representations; manual annotation of a synthetic loan corpus yields an average score of ~23.65, while automatic quantification with GloVe+WMD shows only weak correlation (r=0.45–0.49).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the concept of **representation fidelity** to audit whether algorithmic decision systems about people rely on representations faithful to the individuals' own self-descriptions. It operationalizes the concept through a typology of mismatches (additional, omitted, inconsistent information) and a qualitative annotation scheme. A primary case study builds the Loan-Granting Self-Representations Corpus 2025, pairing 30,000 synthetic loan application letters with feature vectors from the German Credit Dataset. Manual annotation reveals an average fidelity score of approximately 23.65 (on a mismatch-count scale). Attempts to automatically quantify fidelity using GloVe embeddings and Word Mover's Distance correlate only weakly with manual scores (Pearson's r = 0.45–0.49), suggesting current automatic methods are insufficient proxies for this nuanced construct.

## Method Summary
The core method involves defining a typology for how a decision system's input representation of a person can diverge from that person's self-description. This typology (additional, omitted, inconsistent information) guides the creation of a qualitative annotation scheme. To demonstrate, the authors generate a large-scale corpus: synthetic personal narratives (loan application letters) are algorithmically converted into structured feature vectors mimicking those used in a credit scoring model. Human annotators then apply the scheme to identify mismatches between the original letter's content and the derived feature vector, computing a manual fidelity score. For automatic quantification, text and feature vectors are embedded (using GloVe), and their semantic distance is calculated via Word Mover's Distance to produce an automatic fidelity score, which is then correlated with the manual scores.

## Key Results
*   Manual annotation of the synthetic loan corpus produces an average representation fidelity score of ~23.65, indicating a substantial prevalence of mismatches.
*   Automatic quantification using GloVe embeddings and Word Mover's Distance yields only a weak positive correlation (Pearson’s r = 0.45–0.49) with the manually annotated fidelity scores.
*   The low correlation suggests that current distributional embedding methods are limited and potentially unreliable proxies for the proposed notion of representation fidelity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism is operationalized through a two-stage validation process: first, human annotators apply the mismatch typology to establish a ground-truth benchmark of representation fidelity on a paired corpus; second, automatic quantification methods are evaluated by correlating their outputs with this benchmark. The substantial manual fidelity scores confirm the typology's ability to capture meaningful discrepancies, while the weak correlation with GloVe+WMD reveals that simple semantic distance metrics fail to align with the categorical, factual mismatches (e.g., missing binary features, inconsistent numeric values) central to the construct.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Representation Fidelity:** The core construct measuring discrepancy between a system's person-representation and self-description. *Why needed:* Provides a concrete, auditable metric for alignment in human-centered algorithmic decisions. *Quick check:* Can the concept be reliably distinguished from simple prediction error or model accuracy?
*   **Mismatch Typology:** A classification system (additional, omitted, inconsistent information) for annotation. *Why needed:* Breaks down the abstract concept of "mismatch" into observable, annotatable categories. *Quick check:* Do annotators consistently apply these three categories to the same examples?
*   **Self-Description vs. Algorithmic Representation:** The fundamental duality being compared: natural language narrative vs. structured feature vector. *Why needed:* Highlights the potential information loss or distortion in feature engineering. *Quick check:* Are the mappings from text to features in the case study grounded in a plausible, if synthetic, modeling pipeline?
*   **Qualitative Annotation Scheme:** A protocol for humans to identify mismatches based on the typology. *Why needed:* Establishes a ground-truth or benchmark for the fidelity concept. *Quick check:* Is the scheme detailed enough to be replicable by different annotator teams?
*   **Automatic Quantification (Embeddings + WMD):** The technical approach to automate scoring by measuring semantic distance. *Why needed:* Makes the audit scalable beyond manual effort. *Quick check:* Does the chosen metric (WMD) capture the categorical nature of mismatches (e.g., a missing *binary* feature)?
*   **Correlation Analysis:** Statistical method to validate automatic scores against the manual benchmark. *Why needed:* Assesses whether the automatic method is a valid proxy. *Quick check:* Is Pearson's r the appropriate metric, or would rank correlation (Spearman) or agreement measures be more informative for ordinal scores?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Data Synthesis (Generate Synthetic Letters & Feature Vectors) -> Manual Annotation (Apply Mismatch Typology) -> Automatic Scoring (GloVe Embedding + WMD Calculation) -> Correlation Analysis (Compare Manual vs. Automatic Scores)

**Critical Path:**  
The workflow is linear and data-dependent: 1) Create parallel text/feature pairs, 2) Obtain human fidelity judgments, 3) Compute automatic scores, 4) Statistically compare the two score sets. The output (correlation coefficient) determines the validity of the automatic proxy.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
*   **Corpus Fidelity vs. Scale:** Using synthetic letters allows massive, controlled data generation but sacrifices ecological validity compared to real personal statements.
*   **Annotation Depth vs. Cost:** The rich, categorical typology enables nuanced fidelity measurement but requires expensive, skilled manual labor and risks annotator fatigue/inconsistency.
*   **Embedding Simplicity vs. Expressiveness:** GloVe+WMD is a well-understood, implementable baseline but may fail to capture the precise, factual inconsistencies central to the typology (e.g., a mismatched numeric value).

**Failure Signatures:**  
*   **Low Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA):** Would indicate the typology or scheme is too ambiguous, invalidating the manual ground truth.
*   **Near-Zero Correlation (r ~ 0):** Would show the chosen automatic method is unrelated to the human concept, requiring a fundamental redesign of the quantification approach.
*   **Domain-Specific Correlation Collapse:** If weak correlation is only observed in the credit domain, it suggests the method or concept may not generalize.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Reproduce & Measure IAA:** Replicate the manual annotation process on a subset with multiple annotators to compute Cohen's Kappa or similar, validating the scheme's reliability.
2.  **Benchmark Modern Embeddings:** Replace GloVe+WMD with contextual models (e.g., BERT, SBERT) and similarity metrics (cosine similarity, BERTScore) to see if correlation improves.
3.  **Cross-Domain Validation:** Apply the entire pipeline (manual + automatic) to a new decision context (e.g., hiring resume-to-feature vectors) to test the generalizability of the fidelity concept and scoring methods.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   How does representation fidelity perform when applied to authentic human-generated self-descriptions (e.g., real loan applications) instead of synthetic text?
*   Does the fidelity concept and scoring generalize beyond credit scoring to other high-stakes domains like hiring, healthcare, or policing?
*   Can advanced contextual embeddings (e.g., BERT-based models) or tailored similarity metrics better capture the specific mismatch types (additional, omitted, inconsistent) to improve automatic quantification?
*   What are the practical challenges and costs of implementing manual annotation at scale for auditing real-world systems?
*   How should the fidelity framework account for inherent ambiguities or subjective elements in self-descriptions?

## Limitations
*   The primary evaluation corpus uses synthetic loan letters, which may not capture the linguistic complexity and authenticity of real human self-descriptions, limiting external validity.
*   The study is confined to a single domain (German credit scoring); the representation fidelity concept and findings may not generalize to other high-stakes decision contexts.
*   The manual annotation process and resulting fidelity scores lack reported inter-annotator agreement, raising significant questions about the reliability of the core benchmark.

## Confidence
*   **Representation fidelity as a conceptual framework for auditing:** Medium (theoretically sound but empirically nascent; needs more case studies).
*   **Average manual fidelity score of ~23.65:** Medium (dependent on unverified annotation quality and synthetic data).
*   **Weak correlation (r=0.45-0.49) between GloVe+WMD and manual scores:** High (statistic is clearly reported, though based on a single automatic method).
*   **Conclusion that current automatic methods are limited proxies:** Medium (inference drawn from a single modest correlation; requires testing other methods for confidence).

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate with real-world data:** Conduct the full study using authentic personal statements (e.g., actual loan applications, cover letters) paired with the corresponding feature vectors used by a real decision system.
2.  **Evaluate advanced similarity models:** Test the automatic quantification pipeline with state-of-the-art sentence embeddings (e.g., from SBERT, SimCSE) and semantic similarity metrics (e.g., BERTScore, ROUGE) to assess if correlation with human judgment improves.
3.  **Establish scheme reliability:** Perform a formal inter-annotator agreement study on the mismatch typology annotation scheme to quantify its reliability, resolve ambiguities, and produce refined guidelines.
