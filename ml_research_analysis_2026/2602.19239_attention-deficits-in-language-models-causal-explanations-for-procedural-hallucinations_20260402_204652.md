# Attention Deficits in Language Models: Causal Explanations for Procedural Hallucinations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19239  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19239  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Procedural hallucinations (failure to output computed values) are decomposed into Stage 2A (gating) and Stage 2B (binding) errors, with Stage 2B dominating; linear probes show correct values are present but not used, and oracle checkpointing nearly eliminates Stage 2B failures.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates procedural hallucinations—cases where language models fail to output values they have actually computed and retained in-context. The authors introduce a causal framework that decomposes readout-stage errors into two distinct failures: Stage 2A (gating, where the model does not even attempt to answer from the candidate set) and Stage 2B (binding, where the model attempts to answer but selects the wrong candidate). Across multiple model families and synthetic long-context binding tasks, Stage 2B accounts for the majority of errors (65–100%) in the hard regime. Surprisingly, on Stage 2B error trials, a linear probe trained on the final-layer residual stream recovers the correct value with high accuracy (e.g., 73.9% vs. ~2% chance for Qwen2.5-3B), demonstrating that the information is present but not utilized by the output layer. The paper further shows that information decays geometrically with distance along the sequence, and that an oracle checkpointing intervention (restating the true binding every 128 tokens) resets this decay and nearly eliminates Stage 2B failures. These findings provide a mechanistic explanation for attention deficits in language models and suggest practical mitigation strategies.

## Method Summary
The study builds on synthetic binding tasks where a model must retrieve a value bound to a key after a long sequence of filler tokens. The candidate set `C` is known a priori, enabling a margin-based error decomposition: `GateGap` compares the best candidate logit to the best non-candidate logit (negative indicates Stage 2A), while `ValueGap` compares the correct candidate’s logit to the next-best candidate (negative, conditional on `GateGap≥0`, indicates Stage 2B). To certify that the correct value is encoded in the residual stream on Stage 2B errors, the authors train linear probes on the final-layer hidden state; high probe accuracy above chance implies the model is not using available information (low routing efficiency). They also analyze information decay via a strong data processing inequality and test an oracle checkpointing technique that periodically re-inserts the correct binding into the context. The combination of diagnostic margins, probing, and controlled interventions provides a causal, fine‑grained picture of where and why binding failures occur.

## Key Results
- Stage 2B errors dominate in hard long-context binding tasks, constituting 65–100% of failures across models.  
- Linear probes recover the correct value on Stage 2B error trials with accuracy far above chance (e.g., 73.9% vs. ~2% for Qwen2.5-3B), proving the information is present but not used.  
- Oracle checkpointing (re‑stating true bindings every 128 tokens) raises accuracy from nearly zero to near‑perfect at long distances (e.g., 0/400 → 399/400 at `k=1024` for Qwen2.5-3B).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Stage 2A vs. Stage 2B Decomposition
**Claim:** Procedural hallucinations in long-context binding decompose into two distinct readout-stage failures: Stage 2A (gating—model does not enter answer mode) and Stage 2B (binding—model enters answer mode but selects the wrong candidate), with Stage 2B dominating errors in the hard regime.  
**Mechanism:** The model’s final-token output is determined by comparing logits over a known candidate set `C`. A *gate margin* (`GateGap`) compares the best candidate logit to the best non-candidate logit; a negative margin indicates Stage 2A. A *value margin* (`ValueGap`) compares the correct candidate’s logit to the next-best candidate; a negative margin (conditional on entering answer mode) indicates Stage 2B.  
**Core assumption:** The specification function `g(W)` is unambiguous and the correct value is deterministically recoverable from the prompt. Failures arise at the routing stage, not from missing evidence.  
**Evidence anchors:** Table 1 shows Stage 2B accounts for most errors across model families; Section 3.4 discusses that Stage 2B failures indicate the model understands the task format but routes to the wrong value.  
**Break condition:** The candidate set `C` must be known and the task unambiguous; otherwise the margin-based decomposition cannot isolate readout-stage routing.

### Mechanism 2: Information Present but Not Used (Probe Certification)
**Claim:** On Stage 2B error trials, the correct value is encoded in the final-layer residual stream (high `I_avail`) but not used by the output (low `I_used`), formalized as low routing efficiency `η = I_used / I_avail`.  
**Mechanism:** A linear probe trained on the final-layer residual stream predicts the correct token with accuracy far above chance on error trials (e.g., 73.9% vs. ~2% chance). By Proposition 4, if a probe’s mutual information with `V` exceeds that of the model’s output, then `I_used < I_avail` provably holds.  
**Core assumption:** Linear probes are sufficiently expressive to extract the target value from the residual stream. The model’s output distribution `Y` is a function of the hidden state `H_k`.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 74% probe accuracy on Qwen2.5-3B; Section 4.4 formalizes the mutual information bound and its implications.  
**Break condition:** If the value is not linearly decodable from the final-layer residual, or if the probe overfits/underfits, the certificate fails. Requires access to internal activations.

### Mechanism 3: Distance‑Dependent Decay and Checkpointing Recovery
**Claim:** Long-context binding failures are distance‑dependent due to information decay along the evidence path; checkpointing (restating the true binding) resets the distance, increasing `I_avail` and nearly eliminating Stage 2B failures.  
**Mechanism:** A strong data processing inequality (SDPI) contracts `I(V; H_k)` geometrically with distance `k` (Theorem 3). Oracle checkpointing inserts a fresh copy of the binding every 128 tokens, effectively shortening the maximum path length and boosting available information. Empirically, this converts near‑zero accuracy to near‑perfect at long distances (e.g., 0/400 → 399/400 at `k=1024` for Qwen2.5-3B).  
**Core assumption:** The evidence transmission channel behaves like a copy‑or‑noise process (Proposition 5), where SDPI contraction is tight. Checkpoint content matches the true binding.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.5 describe the geometric decay and checkpointing results.  
**Break condition:** If the model’s attention or MLP layers actively corrupt the binding (e.g., Stage 2A gating collapse at extreme distances, as seen in Llama on `PRIMACY_RECENCY`, `k=2048`), checkpointing may not recover accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Information‑theoretic bounds (Fano’s inequality):** Enables conversion of observed error rates into lower bounds on mutual information `I_used`, quantifying the “bits‑to‑trust” cost of overcoming model biases (pseudo‑priors). Without this, we cannot rigorously distinguish “doesn’t know” from “won’t use”.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input Prompt `W_k` (binding + `k` filler tokens + query) → Transformer Encoder (multiple blocks) → Final‑layer residual stream `H_k` (at readout position) → Logit Head → Output logits `z` over vocabulary. Candidate set `C` is known; margins `GateGap` and `ValueGap` computed from `z`. A separate linear probe can map `H_k` to predictions of the correct token. Interventions: Checkpoint insertion modifies `W_k`; evidence scrubbing (`do(E=∅)`) modifies `W_k`; activation patching modifies `H_k` at specific layers.

### Critical Path
1. Generate synthetic binding prompts with controlled `k`, candidate set `C`, and filler type (e.g., `decoy_heavy`).  
2. Run model (greedy decoding) to get top token `Ŷ` and candidate‑best `Y_k`; compute `GateGap` and `ValueGap`.  
3. Classify errors: `G_k=0` (Stage 2A) vs. `G_k=1` (Stage 2B).  
4. On Stage 2B errors, train/test linear probe on `H_k` to recover `V`.  
5. Apply checkpointing: insert true binding serialization every 128 tokens in filler tail; re‑evaluate accuracy.  
6. (Optional) Run activation patching to localize components.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Synthetic vs. natural tasks:** Synthetic tasks isolate binding with known `C` and `g(W)`. Natural tasks (e.g., `notes_binding`) introduce ambiguity; the stage decomposition may not fully apply.  
- **Activation access:** Probing/patching require open‑weight models. For API‑only models, one must rely on output‑level diagnostics (`GateGap`/`ValueGap`) and trace‑auditing pseudo‑priors.  
- **Checkpoint content:** Oracle checkpointing uses ground‑truth bindings; sham or wrong checkpoints can test content‑specific vs. format effects.

### Failure Signatures
- **Stage 2A:** `GateGap < 0` → `Ŷ ∉ C`. Indicates format/instruction‑following breakdown.  
- **Stage 2B:** `GateGap ≥ 0` but `ValueGap < 0` → `Ŷ ∈ C` but wrong. Probe accuracy ≫ chance certifies “present but not used.”  
- **Checkpoint failure:** If Stage 2A dominates at extreme `k` (e.g., Llama on `PRIMACY_RECENCY`, `k=2048`), checkpointing may not recover accuracy.

## Limitations
- The stage decomposition requires a known candidate set `C` and an unambiguous specification function `g(W)`; it may not apply to ambiguous or open‑ended tasks.  
- The linear probe assumption may underestimate `I_avail` if the correct value is encoded nonlinearly.  
- Oracle checkpointing relies on ground‑truth bindings and is not feasible in real‑world deployments; practical retrieval‑based checkpoints remain untested.  
- Evidence is limited to small models on synthetic tasks; scaling to large, instruction‑tuned models is uncertain.  
- The theoretical SDPI model oversimplifies attention as a copy‑or‑noise process; real transformer circuits may behave differently.

## Confidence
- Stage 2B dominates errors in hard binding tasks: **Medium** (strong synthetic results but narrow applicability).  
- Linear probe certification of “present but not used”: **High** (robust probe accuracies and formal bound).  
- Distance decay and checkpointing recovery: **High** (consistent empirical recovery across settings).  
- Overall causal explanation: **High** (multiple converging lines of evidence).

## Next Checks
1. Apply the stage decomposition to natural multi‑hop QA tasks with retrieved candidate sets to test generality beyond synthetic setups.  
2. Use nonlinear probes on Stage 2B errors to bound the information‑theoretic gap; high nonlinear accuracy would reinforce the “present but not used” conclusion.  
3. Replace oracle checkpoints with a retrieval system (e.g., periodic document re‑insertion) and evaluate Stage 2B reduction, assessing practical mitigation potential.
