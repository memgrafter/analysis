# Anti-I2V: Safeguarding your photos from malicious image-to-video generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24570
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24570
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Adversarial perturbations operating in L\*a\*b\* and DCT domains, combined with internal representation collapse (IRC) and anchor (IRA) losses, disrupt temporal coherence and identity in image-to-video diffusion models across CogVideoX, Open-Sora, and DynamiCrafter.

## Executive Summary
Anti-I2V is a defense against malicious use of personal photos in image-to-video (I2V) generation. It generates adversarial perturbations that are added to a user's photo before it is fed into a video diffusion model. These perturbations, designed to be perceptually subtle, corrupt the model's internal feature representations and disrupt temporal coherence, leading to generated videos that fail to preserve the subject's identity or maintain visual quality. The method targets both UNet and DiT-based architectures and remains effective under common image transformations like JPEG compression and blurring.

## Method Summary
Anti-I2V crafts adversarial perturbations via a dual-space optimization. Perturbations are applied in the perceptually uniform CIELAB color space (a*, b* channels) and to low-frequency DCT coefficients, making them robust to denoising and compression. The optimization maximizes a composite loss that includes: 1) the standard denoising loss (L_DM) to disrupt generation, 2) an Internal Representation Collapse (L_IRC) loss that aligns deep-layer features of the denoiser with an early, low-semantic layer, and 3) an Internal Representation Anchor (L_IRA) loss that pulls intermediate features from both the VAE encoder and denoiser toward those of an unrelated target image. An auxiliary loss (L_CLIP – L_LPIPS) preserves basic semantics and perceptual quality of the perturbed image. The perturbation is iteratively updated using projected gradient descent (AdamW) for 200 steps under an L∞ budget (Δ=16/255 in RGB).

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art protection across CogVideoX, Open-Sora, and DynamiCrafter, reducing Identity Score Matching (ISM) by >50% relative to clean inputs.
- Significantly lower CLIP-FIQA and Q-Align (frame/video) scores, indicating severe degradation in generated video fidelity and quality.
- Maintains robustness against JPEG, blur, noise, and purification defenses (e.g., DiffPure), with transferability to unseen architectures.
- Ablations confirm the dual-space (DSP) perturbation and the IRC/IRA losses are both critical for the observed attack strength.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Updating adversarial perturbations in the perceptually-uniform L\*a\*b\* color space and DCT frequency domain, rather than standard RGB space, yields perturbations that are more robust to model denoising and common image transformations while remaining imperceptible.
- **Mechanism:** The L\*a\*b\* space decorrelates luminance (L\*) from chromaticity (a\*, b\*), allowing perturbations to alter color perception without affecting brightness. Concurrently, directly modifying low-frequency DCT coefficients alters fundamental image structure information. The combined Dual-Space Perturbation (DSP) strategy targets both color perception and core structural features, making the noise less susceptible to removal during the multi-step, coherence-enforcing denoising process of video diffusion models.
- **Core assumption:** Features essential for video generation are encoded in both color-appearance channels (a\*, b\*) and low-frequency spatial structure, and disrupting these simultaneously is more effective than disrupting high-frequency RGB noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...operates in both the L\*a\*b\* and frequency domains, improving robustness and concentrating on salient pixels." [section 4.3] Details the conversion, perturbation, and integration steps, arguing that low-frequency components hold "most visually important information" and that L\*a\*b\* changes are "less perceptible."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Disrupting the propagation of high-level semantic features across intermediate layers of the denoising network (Internal Representation Collapse - IRC) degrades the model's ability to maintain temporal coherence and reconstruct identity.
- **Mechanism:** Via PCA analysis (Fig. 2), the method identifies that deeper blocks in DiT/MMDiT (e.g., layers >27 in CogVideoX, >19 in OpenSora) encode rich semantics while early layers (e.g., layer 3) do not. The IRC loss forces deeper-layer feature maps to align with those of an early, low-semantic layer. This collapses the hierarchical feature extraction process, preventing the network from developing robust, semantically-rich internal representations necessary for consistent video generation.
- **Core assumption:** The temporal coherence in I2V generation critically depends on stable, semantically meaningful features propagating through the denoising UNet/Transformer blocks. Corrupting these intermediate representations has a cascading negative effect on output quality.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.4] "Meaningful semantic features emerge after 19th layer in OpenSora and 27th layer in CogVideoX, whereas the 3rd layer contains almost no semantic information... we aim to transform the features learned in later layers to resemble those of the early 3rd layer." [section 4.4] Provides the IRC loss equation (Eq. 5).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Minimizing the layer-wise distance between features extracted from the perturbed conditional image and those from an unrelated *target* image (Internal Representation Anchor - IRA) within both the VAE and denoising modules pulls the model's internal state towards an irrelevant semantic anchor, corrupting the appearance features derived from the user's photo.
- **Mechanism:** The IRA loss (Eqs. 6-8) is applied to multiple intermediate layers of the VAE encoder and the denoising network. For a given noised latent and timestep, it minimizes the difference between the activation produced by the perturbed image (zξ) and a different, fixed target image (zψ). This "anchoring" forces intermediate representations to lose their specificity to the original input image and adopt features of the target, thereby degrading identity and fine details in the generated video.
- **Core assumption:** The fidelity of the generated first frame (and subsequent frames) depends on the distinctiveness of activation patterns in mid-level layers of the VAE and denoiser when conditioned on the input image. Forcing these patterns to match another image's pattern disrupts this conditioning.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.5] "We propose Internal Representation Anchor loss (IRA) that minimizes the layer-wise Euclidean distance between the hidden features produced when conditioning on the perturbed image and on an unrelated target image." [section 4.5] Notes that in the *untargeted* setting, xψ = x and the sign is reversed, effectively maximizing distance from the *original* clean features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Models (Denoising Process)**
  - Why needed here: The entire attack is formulated as optimizing an adversarial perturbation δ to maximize the denoising loss of the target video diffusion model. Understanding the iterative noise prediction process is fundamental to see *why* perturbing the initial latent matters and how errors can propagate.
  - Quick check question: If you add a small perturbation to the initial image's latent, how might the error at timestep *t* affect the prediction at timestep *t-1* in a DDPM-style model?
- **Concept: Adversarial Perturbation Optimization (PGD)**
  - Why needed here: The core algorithm is an iterative, projected gradient descent procedure applied in a transformed feature space (DSP). Understanding the PGD loop and the projection constraint is essential to implement the optimization correctly.
  - Quick check question: How does the choice of the *p*-norm constraint (e.g., L∞ vs. L2) and the step size α interact to control the perceptibility and strength of the adversarial noise?
- **Concept: Perceptual Color Spaces & Frequency Domain**
  - Why needed here: The DSP's novelty lies in operating in L\*a\*b\* and DCT domains. An engineer needs to understand that L\*a\*b\* separates luminance from chrominance (making color changes less noticeable) and that DCT compacts energy into low-frequency coefficients (so perturbing them changes fundamental structure).
  - Quick check question: Why might adding identical-magnitude noise to the 'a\*' channel of L\*a\*b\* be less perceptible than adding it to the 'L\*' channel? How does a low-pass filter relate to the "top-left DCT coefficients" the paper perturbs?
- **Concept: Mid-Level Feature Manipulation**
  - Why needed here: The L_IRC and L_IRA losses explicitly target intermediate feature maps of the VDM's VAE and denoiser. This requires understanding that these layers encode semantic and appearance information critical for conditioning.
  - Quick check question: Why might aligning a deep-layer feature map with a shallow-layer one (IRC) collapse the semantic representation, whereas aligning it with a different image's deep feature (IRA) pulls the representation toward an irrelevant concept?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Processor -> Perturbation Optimizer (Dual-Space Loop) -> Loss Engine (L_Anti-I2V) -> Target Video Diffusion Model (VDM)
- **Critical path:** The attack requires forward passes through the VDM's VAE encoder and denoising network to extract intermediate features for L_IRC and L_IRA. If these internal activations are inaccessible, the method defaults to a weaker attack using only DSP, L_DM, and L_aux.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Robustness vs. Perceptibility:** DSP improves robustness to purification/transformations but may yield lower SSIM/PSNR compared to pure RGB attacks, as L\*a\*b\* changes are not captured well by pixel-metric measures.
  - **Attack Strength vs. Computational Cost:** Computing L_IRC and L_IRA on multiple intermediate layers significantly increases memory footprint during backpropagation, limiting batch size.
  - **Generality vs. Specificity:** The layer selection rule ("last 3 layers" to "layer 3") is a heuristic that may not generalize to future architectures with different feature hierarchies.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1.  **No Identity Degradation:** ISM and CLIP-FIQA scores remain close to clean outputs. Indicates DSP noise is too weak or L_IRC/L_IRA are not disrupting identity-critical features.
  2.  **Artifact Pattern Consistency:** Generated videos show uniform blurring or color shifts typical of RGB-space attacks, rather than severe structural/identity corruption. Suggests DSP is not properly implemented or loss weighting is off.
  3.  **Architecture Collapse on Transfer:** Performance drops catastrophically when transferring to a new architecture family (e.g., DiT to UNet). Likely due to poor generalization of the layer-selection heuristic.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Replicate Table 1 for one model (e.g., CogVideoX) using only the vanilla L_DM loss with RGB perturbations to verify the core attack loop.
  2.  Ablate DSP contribution: With losses fixed to L_DM + L_IRC + L_IRA (untargeted), compare perturbation spaces (RGB-only, L\*a\*b\*-only, DSP). Expect DSP to show lowest DINO-SIM/Q-A(V).
  3.  Ablate layer selection for IRC: With full DSP + all losses, vary the IRC target layer mapping (e.g., "Last-1/2/3/4" to layer 3). Expect "Last-3" to match "Full" performance, confirming the simplified rule.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How effective is Anti-I2V against a broader range of Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures beyond CogVideoX and Open-Sora, particularly those with different attention patterns or scaling laws?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that "their effectiveness against Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models remains largely under-explored" and note that DiTs have "improved feature retention, and stronger temporal consistency."
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation covers only two specific DiT-based models. The underlying reasons for Anti-I2V's success on these—and whether its losses universally disrupt feature propagation in all DiTs—are not analyzed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation on a diverse suite of DiT variants (e.g., different positional encodings, attention sparsity patterns, model sizes) with layer-wise feature analysis.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the theoretical or empirical justification for selecting the "last three layers" and the "3rd layer" as the target and reference layers for the Internal Representation Collapse (IRC) loss?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper identifies layers via PCA visualization (Fig. 2) and states that "setting j to the indices of the last three layers... yields nearly identical protection performance, leading to a simple layer-selection rule." The rationale for this specific rule is not formally derived.
- **Why unresolved:** The choice seems heuristic. It is unclear if this is optimal across models, timesteps, or content types.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study varying the number and position of target/reference layers combined with metrics of feature semantic richness (e.g., via probing classifiers) to establish a generalizable selection criterion.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the computational overhead and memory footprint of Anti-I2V's dual-space perturbation optimization and multi-loss training compared to baseline RGB-space methods, and how does this scale with video resolution and length?
- **Basis in paper:** The method involves conversions to L\*a\*b\* and frequency domains and computes multiple losses on intermediate features of large video models. While all experiments ran on a single A100 40GB, runtime or memory usage comparisons are not reported.
- **Why unresolved:** The practicality of deploying Anti-I2V for real-world protection services depends on efficiency. The additional costs of domain conversions and feature extractions are unknown relative to simpler RGB-based PGD attacks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarking wall-clock time, GPU memory consumption, and FLOPs during perturbation optimization for Anti-I2V versus baselines across varying video resolutions and frame counts.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does Anti-I2V's protection generalize to non-human-centric video generation (e.g., animals, objects, scenes) and to text-to-video models without an image conditioning input?
- **Basis in paper:** The evaluation datasets (CelebV-Text, UCF101) and qualitative examples focus exclusively on human faces/bodies. The method is designed for "image-to-video generation" where the reference image is the first frame.
- **Why unresolved:** The perturbation strategy and loss functions (e.g., targeting identity-related features) are tuned for human subjects. It is unclear if they would be as effective for other domains where "identity" or "temporal coherence" have different semantics, or for pure text-to-video generation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing on diverse video generation benchmarks (e.g., VBench) covering animals, objects, and landscapes, and evaluating transfer to text-to-video models by optimizing perturbations on a dummy image or noise.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How resilient is Anti-I2V to adaptive adversaries who employ purification defenses (e.g., DiffPure, GrIDPure) or expect the dual-space perturbation strategy?
- **Basis in paper:** The robustness experiments apply fixed transformations and off-the-shelf purifiers. However, an adaptive attacker could fine-tune their own purification model specifically to remove L\*a\*b\* and frequency-domain perturbations, or use adversarial training to neutralize Anti-I2V's losses.
- **Why unresolved:** The current robustness tests are non-adaptive. The paper does not consider a game-theoretic scenario where the defender's strategy becomes known and the attacker adapts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting an adaptive evaluation where a surrogate video diffusion model is adversarially trained using Anti-I2V's loss functions to recover from the perturbations, or where a purification network is trained to invert the L\*a\*b\* and frequency modifications before generation.

## Limitations
- Exact layer indices for IRC (e.g., "3rd layer", "last three layers") are model-specific heuristics not formally derived, risking failure on future architectures.
- The protocol for selecting and fixing the unrelated target image (I_tar) for IRA is ambiguous, affecting reproducibility and performance.
- The precise low-frequency DCT mask (shape, percentage) and full set of layers used in multi-layer losses are not specified, creating implementation gaps.
- Computationally intensive due to multi-layer feature extraction and dual-space conversions, likely scaling poorly with high-resolution or longer videos.
