# RAGTurk: Best Practices for Retrieval Augmented Generation in Turkish

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03652
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03652
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** For Turkish RAG, a Pareto-optimal configuration using Cross-encoder Reranking and Previous/Next Chunk Augmentation achieves 84.6% accuracy with ~50% lower token cost than the top HyDE-based system (85.0%).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of RAG design guidance for morphologically rich languages like Turkish. The authors construct a novel Turkish RAG benchmark from Wikipedia and CulturaX and systematically evaluate seven modular pipeline stages via a genetic search. Without task-specific fine-tuning, they find that the generative HyDE method maximizes end-to-end accuracy (85.0%), but a simpler configuration combining Cross-encoder Reranking and Previous/Next Chunk Augmentation achieves near-identical performance (84.6%) with roughly half the computational token cost. Crucially, they demonstrate that stacking multiple generative modules (e.g., HyDE plus summarization and reflection) degrades performance, likely by distorting critical Turkish morphological cues. The study provides a clear, cost-effective recipe for practical Turkish RAG systems.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates a configurable RAG pipeline with seven variable stages: query transformation, initial retrieval (fixed dense model), reranking, filtering/selection, context augmentation, context condensation, and answer refinement. A novel Turkish benchmark is created with 20,459 QA pairs and 58,289 text chunks from Turkish Wikipedia and CulturaX. Fitness is a composite score balancing retrieval metrics (Recall@5, mAP, nDCG@5, MRR) and generation quality (semantic similarity, LLM-as-judge), with token cost as a secondary metric. A constrained genetic algorithm searches ~200 configurations from a design space of 21 discrete methods across the seven families, evaluating each on a stratified subset of 100 questions. The baseline is a simple dense retrieval followed by naive concatenation.

## Key Results
- HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings) achieves the highest end-to-end accuracy of 85.0%, a 6.3-point gain over the 78.7% baseline.
- A Pareto-optimal configuration (Cross-encoder Reranking + Previous/Next Chunk Augmentation) reaches 84.6% accuracy with approximately half the token consumption of the HyDE system.
- Over-stacking generative modules (e.g., HyDE, summarization, reflection) degrades performance (as low as 79.6%) by distorting Turkish morphological cues.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** HyDE maximizes accuracy for Turkish by bridging lexical gaps from rich morphology and surface-form variation.
- **Mechanism:** HyDE uses an LLM to generate a synthetic, query-focused "hypothetical" document. This document is then used as the retrieval query, better matching the semantic representation of relevant passages in the embedding space and compensating for the lexical mismatch that plagues direct retrieval in agglutinative languages.
- **Core Assumption:** The LLM-generated document captures the query's semantic intent in a form lexically closer to the corpus expression.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] HyDE achieves 85.0% vs. 78.7% baseline; [section 5.3] Top score of 85.00% with strong query expansion (HyDE).
- **Break Condition:** If the hypothetical document hallucinates or introduces corpus-irrelevant noise, precision drops and the 3.7x token cost becomes unjustified.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-encoder Reranking with Previous/Next Chunk Augmentation provides a cost-effective Pareto-optimal solution.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-encoders provide precise pairwise relevance scoring, filtering out morphologically mismatched false positives. Adjacent chunk augmentation recovers local context lost during chunking, which is critical where predicates/modifiers span boundaries in Turkish.
- **Core Assumption:** The marginal gain from precise reranking and local context recovery outweighs expensive generative query expansion.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Comparable 84.6% performance with much lower cost; [section 5.3] Cleanest recommendation: prioritize Cross-encoder Reranking and local context enrichment.
- **Break Condition:** If initial retriever recall is very low, reranking cannot recover missed chunks. Augmentation may inject noise in non-linear document structures.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Over-stacking generative modules degrades Turkish RAG performance by accumulating errors and distorting morphological cues.
- **Mechanism:** Each LLM-based transformation (HyDE generation, summarization, reflection) can paraphrase or alter Turkish inflectional suffixes, case markers, and agreement patterns essential for precise grounding.
- **Core Assumption:** For morphologically rich languages, the signal-to-noise ratio of successive LLM transformations becomes negative.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Degrades performance by distorting morphological cues; [section 5.3] Maximalist 6-module pipeline scored only 79.60%, underperforming simpler configs.
- **Break Condition:** Likely specific to morphologically complex languages; simpler morphology (e.g., English) may tolerate more stacking.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Agglutinative Morphology & Surface Form Variation**
  - **Why needed:** Turkish words concatenate multiple morphemes, creating vast surface-form variation (e.g., "gitmek" → "gidiyorum", "gitmeliydim"). This breaks exact-match and some neural retrieval models that rely on word-level similarity.
  - **Quick check:** If a query uses "yazmak" (to write) in past tense, what two common surface forms might appear in a relevant passage?
- **Concept: RAG Pipeline Modularity & Interaction Effects**
  - **Why needed:** Performance depends on the *combination* of modules, not just individual quality. Changing one component (e.g., adding HyDE) shifts the input distribution and optimal settings for downstream components.
  - **Quick check:** Why might adding a high-recall query expansion method like HyDE make a subsequent *reranker* more important, even if Recall@5 is high?
- **Concept: Cost-Accuracy Pareto Frontier & Token Economics**
  - **Why needed:** The key recommendation is a Pareto-optimal point, not the absolute max. Production constraints (token cost, latency) make understanding the trade-off curve essential.
  - **Quick check:** If a 0.5% accuracy gain requires a 3x token increase, what other factors should be considered before deployment?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:**
  Query Transformation -> Initial Retrieval (dense) -> Candidate Re-ranking -> Filtering & Selection -> Context Augmentation -> Context Condensation -> Prompt Composition -> Answer Refinement
- **Critical Path:** Minimal baseline: [Dense Retrieval] -> [Top-K] -> [Naive Concat]. First upgrade: add Cross-encoder Reranking after retrieval. Next: add Previous/Next Chunk Augmentation.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - Accuracy vs. Token Cost/Latency: HyDE/Tree-Summarize maximize score but are LLM-heavy; Cross-encoder + Adjacent Augmentation is Pareto-optimal.
  - Generative Complexity vs. Morphological Fidelity: Avoid >1 LLM-heavy generative stage (HyDE, Summarize, Reflect) due to risk of cue distortion.
  - Filtering Aggressiveness vs. Recall: Strict similarity thresholds harm Turkish retrieval; prefer reranking over hard filtering.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - Drop in Retrieval Score: Check for over-filtering (threshold too high) or failed query expansion (HyDE off-topic).
  - Drop in Generation Score despite high Retrieval: Check context condensation removing key facts or "lost in the middle" where evidence is mid-prompt.
  - High Token Cost with Low Gain: Symptom of over-stacked generative modules (e.g., HyDE + Tree-Summarize + Reflection).
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. Establish baseline: Run [Dense Retrieval] -> [Top-K] -> [Naive Concat] on 100 stratified questions. Record score and token count (~1000).
  2. Add high-value reranker: Test [Dense] -> [Cross-encoder Rerank] -> [Top-K] -> [Naive Concat]. Expect significant jump in generation score with minor token increase.
  3. Test HyDE vs. Simple Clarification: Compare [HyDE -> Dense -> Cross-encoder] vs. [Simple Query Clarification -> Dense -> Cross-encoder]. Measure score delta vs. token delta.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction Fidelity:** Key algorithmic details (genetic operator constraints, exact tokenization) and a fixed evaluation seed are unspecified, preventing exact score replication.
- **Causal Mechanism Evidence:** Claims about *why* HyDE works or over-stacking fails rely on comparative ablation, not direct probing of morphological errors; the corpus is an evaluation target, not a mechanism test.
- **External Validation:** The novel benchmark lacks cross-dataset validation; generalizability to other Turkish domains or morphologically rich languages is unproven.

## Confidence
- **Pareto-Optimal Configuration (Cross-encoder + Adjacent Augmentation):** High (directly supported by reported metrics and cost analysis).
- **HyDE as Top-End Accuracy Maximizer:** Medium (supported by scores, but extreme cost and inferred mechanism).
- **Generative Stacking Degradation:** Medium (supported by comparative ablation, but "morphological cue distortion" lacks direct linguistic evidence).
- **Reproduction Fidelity:** Medium (core pipeline is specified, but genetic algorithm details are sparse).
- **Generalizability to Other Languages:** Low (no cross-linguistic experiments conducted).
