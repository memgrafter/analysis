# PVI: Plug-in Visual Injection for Vision-Language-Action Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12772
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12772
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PVI increases average success from 35.7% to 59.7% (+24.0 pp) on 20 bimanual simulation tasks; temporal video features (V-JEPA2) achieve 69.4% success.

## Executive Summary
PVI (Plug-in Visual Injection) addresses a key limitation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the loss of geometric and temporal cues when visual representations are processed by a large vision-language model (VLM). By directly injecting auxiliary visual features—extracted from either static images (DINOv2) or video clips (V-JEPA2)—into the frozen action expert via zero-initialized residual pathways, PVI enables fine-tuning without altering the pretrained VLM or main action network. This encoder-agnostic design allows single-stage training while preserving the original VLA's behavior.

On 20 bimanual manipulation tasks in the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation environment, PVI raises average success from 35.7% to 59.7%, a 24.0 percentage point improvement. Temporal features from V-JEPA2 yield an even larger gain of 29.6 pp, achieving 69.4% success, and demonstrate strong transfer to real-robot cloth folding. Multi-task experiments further show consistent benefits across increasing task sets.

## Method Summary
PVI introduces a trainable copy of the DiT action expert that conditions on projected auxiliary visual features. The copy branch processes the same initial tokens as the main DiT but uses the projected features instead of the VLM's embeddings. At each layer, the copy’s hidden state is passed through a zero‑initialized linear injection layer and added residually to the frozen main DiT’s output. Only the copy branch, the projection layer (zero‑initialized), and the injection layers are trained; the VLM and main DiT remain frozen. The auxiliary encoder can be any frozen model (e.g., DINOv2 for static images, V-JEPA2 for video), making the interface encoder‑agnostic. Training uses the standard flow‑matching loss in a single fine‑tuning stage.

## Key Results
- On 20 bimanual simulation tasks, PVI boosts average success from 35.7% to 59.7% (+24.0 pp) and outperforms alternative injection strategies.
- Temporal video features (V-JEPA2) achieve 69.4% success (+29.6 pp), surpassing static DINOv2 features (56.8%, +17.0 pp); largest gains appear on multi‑phase tasks.
- Real‑robot cloth folding validates practical deployment; multi‑task experiments show improvements from 61.15% to 69.15% on 20 tasks and from 61.32% to 63.56% on 50 tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PVI bypasses the VLM's attenuated geometric and temporal cues by injecting auxiliary visual representations directly into the action expert's computational layers.
- **Mechanism:** A frozen auxiliary encoder provides features (e.g., from V-JEPA2 or DINOv2) that are projected and processed by a trainable copy branch of the action expert. This copy branch's hidden states are added residually to the frozen main action expert's layers via zero-initialized linear projections, allowing the auxiliary signal to influence the denoising process at every depth without altering the pretrained main pathway.
- **Core assumption:** The action expert's architecture (DiT) can accept additive residual modulation from an external pathway without disrupting its flow-matching objective, provided the modulation starts at zero.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "injecting auxiliary visual representations directly into the frozen action expert via zero-initialized residual pathways"
  - [section 3.4] "The main DiT is computed [...] +Z_i(hcopy_i), where Z_i is a zero-initialized linear injection layer"
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., SwiftVLA, MoTVLA) also explore spatiotemporal injection but with different mechanisms; PVI's specific residual design is not directly corroborated here, so corpus evidence is weak.
- **Break condition:** If the auxiliary encoder's feature space is misaligned with the DiT's expectations such that even the learned projection cannot map useful information, or if the residual addition disrupts the main DiT's pretrained dynamics (e.g., causing mode collapse), the injection fails to improve performance.

#### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Zero-initialized injection layers ensure behavior preservation during fine-tuning, allowing stable single-stage integration.
- **Mechanism:** The projection layer (W_proj) and all injection layers (Z_i) are initialized to zero. At training start, the copy branch's output is zero, so the main DiT computes exactly its pretrained function. The model thus begins from the original VLA's performance and gradually learns to incorporate auxiliary signals through gradient updates, avoiding catastrophic forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** The pretrained DiT's representations are a sufficiently strong prior that additive zero signals are benign, and the optimization landscape around zero initialization is smooth enough for stable learning.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.5] "Zero initialization ensures that training starts from behavior equivalent to the original pretrained VLA"
  - [section 4.3] Ablation "PVI@V-JEPA2 (no zero-init)" shows comparable final success (71.8% vs. 69.4%), but zero-init is retained for guaranteed preservation.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence from neighbors; this is a design choice inspired by ControlNet-style stabilization.
- **Break condition:** If the auxiliary features contain high-frequency noise or are semantically misaligned, early training gradients from non-zero projections could destabilize the main DiT's outputs before the copy branch learns meaningful modulation. The ablation "freeze projector" (55.3% vs. 69.4%) suggests the learnable projection is critical, but zero-init's specific role is inferred from design rationale.

#### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The encoder-agnostic interface decouples representation choice from injection infrastructure, enabling systematic evaluation of static vs. temporal features.
- **Mechanism:** The auxiliary encoder E is any frozen model outputting a feature sequence. A single learnable projector maps its output dimension into the DiT's cross-attention space. The copy branch and injection layers remain unchanged regardless of E, so only the frozen front-end varies across experiments (DINOv2 vs. V-JEPA2).
- **Core assumption:** Different visual encoders produce features that, once projected to the DiT's conditioning space, can be interpreted by the copied DiT architecture via the same attention/modulation mechanisms.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.3] "PVI imposes no restriction on the choice of E"
  - [section 4.2] Static (DINOv2: +17.0 pp) and temporal (V-JEPA2: +29.6 pp) both improve over baseline under identical PVI infrastructure.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "PointVLA" and "GeoVLA" use encoder-specific injection; PVI's agnosticism is a stated differentiator but not independently validated in corpus.
- **Break condition:** If an encoder's features are incompatible with the DiT's conditioning mechanism (e.g., temporal features requiring different positional/temporal embeddings than static ones), the fixed copy branch cannot process them effectively, leading to no gain or degradation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Flow-matching action expert (DiT)**
  - *Why needed:* Understanding how the action expert generates continuous trajectories conditioned on inputs is essential to see where and how PVI injects signals.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the role of the velocity field prediction in flow-matching, and how does conditioning (via z_vl) influence the denoising process?

- **Concept: Vision-Language Model (VLM) bottleneck**
  - *Why needed:* PVI's motivation rests on the claim that VLMs lose fine-grained geometric and temporal cues; one must understand what VLMs are optimized for (semantic abstraction) versus what manipulation needs (geometric precision, motion dynamics).
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a VLM pretrained on web images attenuate edge alignments or contact dynamics when its representations are used for robot control?

- **Concept: Residual connection & zero initialization**
  - *Why needed:* PVI's stability hinges on residual addition with zero-initialized weights; this is a common trick in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA, ControlNet) but must be understood in the context of preserving a frozen pretrained function.
  - *Quick check question:* If you add a zero-initialized linear layer's output to a frozen network's output at initialization, what is the network's overall output, and how does this change during training?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
- Frozen VLM: takes multi-view images + language, outputs z_vl (semantic embeddings). Do not modify.
- Frozen main DiT action expert: takes proprioception/noisy action tokens + z_vl, outputs velocity field. Do not modify.
- Frozen auxiliary visual encoder (E): takes video/image history (e.g., 8 frames at 4fps), outputs z_aux. Do not modify.
- Trainable projection W_proj: maps z_aux dimension to DiT's cross-attention dimension (2048 in GR00T N1.5). Initialize to zero.
- Trainable copy branch: exact replica of main DiT blocks, initialized from main DiT weights. Takes same initial tokens as main DiT, but conditions on projected z_aux (˜z_aux). Trainable.
- Trainable injection layers Z_i (N of them): zero-initialized linear layers mapping copy branch hidden state hcopy_i to main DiT's residual stream. Trainable.
- State/action adapters: embodiment-specific modules to bridge robot state/action to DiT interface (already present in base VLA). May be trainable depending on base policy interface.

**Critical path**
Data flow: [Observations] -> Aux Encoder E -> z_aux -> W_proj (zero-init) -> ˜z_aux -> copy branch (DiT copy) -> hcopy_i (per layer) -> Z_i (zero-init) -> added to main DiT's hmain_i at layer i. Main DiT still conditions on z_vl via its original mechanism. Loss: standard flow-matching on main DiT's final output; copy branch and injection layers trained via this loss only.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Freezing main DiT vs. jointly training: PVI freezes main DiT to guarantee pretrained knowledge retention and reduce trainable params. Alternatives like Concat or ReferenceNet-style fine-tune the main DiT, risking forgetting but potentially allowing more integration.
- Auxiliary encoder capacity: Larger frozen encoders (V-JEPA2, DINOv2-Giant) increase total params but not trainable params. Temporal encoders require video sampling strategy; static encoders use single frame. Tradeoff is computational cost vs. information richness.
- Injection location: Layer-wise residual injection (PVI) vs. input-level fusion (Concat) vs. attention-level injection (ControlVLA-style). PVI's design allows influence at every layer while preserving the main DiT's internal computations.

**Failure signatures**
1. No improvement over fine-tuned baseline: Check if auxiliary encoder is actually extracting relevant features (ablate encoder, try random features). Could be task where temporal cues are irrelevant or VLM already sufficient.
2. Degradation compared to baseline: Likely due to misaligned auxiliary features or incorrect projection dimension. Verify W_proj output dimension matches DiT's cross-attention dim. Also check if auxiliary input preprocessing matches encoder's pretraining (e.g., frame sampling, normalization).
3. Training instability (loss spikes, collapse): Could indicate zero-init not stabilizing, or auxiliary features having high variance. Try scaling auxiliary features, or temporarily unfreeze projection to align features before resetting to zero.
4. Copy branch not learned (Z_i weights remain near zero): May require longer training or lower learning rate for injection layers. Check gradient norms flowing through Z_i.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Replicate baseline:** Fine-tune GR00T N1.5 on target task(s) with standard setup. Establish baseline success rate. *Goal: Verify base VLA implementation and training pipeline.*
2. **Add PVI with static encoder (DINOv2):** Attach frozen DINOv2, zero-init projection and Z_i, freeze main DiT and VLM. Train only W_proj, copy branch, Z_i. *Goal: Confirm PVI infrastructure works with a simple spatial encoder. Expect moderate gain (+~17 pp).*
3. **Add PVI with temporal encoder (V-JEPA2):** Switch auxiliary encoder to V-JEPA2 (8 frames, 4fps). Keep all else identical. *Goal: Test if temporal dynamics provide extra benefit. Expect larger gain (+~30 pp), especially on multi-phase tasks. If not, check video sampling and encoder preprocessing.*

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does PVI generalize to auxiliary visual encoders beyond V-JEPA2 and DINOv2, such as 3D geometric or point cloud encoders?
- **Basis in paper:** PVI's encoder-agnostic design is emphasized but only two encoders evaluated in Sec. 4.2.
- **Why unresolved:** Other encoder types may provide complementary geometric cues; their integration stability remains unverified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments across a diverse set of auxiliary encoders (including 3D foundation models) on the same suite of manipulation tasks, measuring success rates and training stability.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal temporal context length for different task categories when using video encoders like V-JEPA2?
- **Basis in paper:** Ablation in Sec. 4.3 shows 2–4 frames outperform 8 frames (71.8% vs 69.4%), but main experiments use 8 frames, suggesting untuned hyperparameters.
- **Why unresolved:** Tasks with different temporal dynamics likely have varying optimal context lengths; PVI's performance may improve with per-task tuning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled study varying temporal context length across a taxonomy of tasks, analyzing performance curves and identifying task-specific optimal windows.

#### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does PVI's effectiveness scale with the number of tasks in multi-task learning, given the diminishing relative gains from 20 to 50 tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** Sec. 4.4 reports average success gains of +8.00 pp for 20 tasks but only +2.24 pp for 50 tasks.
- **Why unresolved:** Smaller improvement in larger task set may indicate interference or capacity limits, questioning scalability to broader skill collections.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extending multi-task experiments to 100+ tasks, analyzing per-task win/loss ratios, and measuring catastrophic interference or positive transfer effects.

#### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can PVI's injection mechanism be seamlessly applied to VLA architectures with non-DiT action experts (e.g., autoregressive or diffusion variants with different conditioning)?
- **Basis in paper:** PVI is instantiated on GR00T N1.5 with a DiT action expert; the dual-pathway design assumes layer-wise injection into Transformer blocks, which may not directly transfer to architectures with recurrent units or different conditioning schemes.
- **Why unresolved:** Effectiveness of zero-initialized residual injection and copy-branch initialization might depend on specific DiT properties; alternative architectures may require redesign.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing PVI on at least two other VLA backbones (e.g., an autoregressive VLA and a diffusion model with different conditioning) and comparing injection success and training stability.

## Limitations
- GR00T N1.5 and RoboTwin 2.0 are unreleased, forcing architectural assumptions (e.g., condition dimension) that may be incorrect.
- The core hypothesis that VLMs discard geometric/temporal cues is not directly measured; observed gains could arise from general feature complementarity.
- Zero‑init’s stabilizing effect is largely based on design rationale; the no‑zero‑init ablation achieved comparable final success, suggesting it may not be strictly necessary for performance.

## Confidence
- **PVI effectiveness (+24.0 pp):** High
- **Temporal features superiority (+29.6 pp vs +17.0 pp):** High
- **Zero‑init preserves pretrained behavior:** Medium
- **Encoder‑agnostic interface:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. Obtain GR00T N1.5 specifications to confirm DiT conditioning dimension and internal projection structure; adjust PVI’s projection accordingly.
2. Systematically ablate zero‑init: train identical PVI setups with zero‑init, random‑init, and no‑injection controls, monitoring loss curves, gradient norms, and early‑stage performance to quantify stabilization.
3. Causal test of VLM bottleneck: replace the VLM with an oracle that provides ground‑truth geometric (e.g., point clouds) and temporal (e.g., optical flow) cues; if PVI gains disappear, the bottleneck claim is weakened.
