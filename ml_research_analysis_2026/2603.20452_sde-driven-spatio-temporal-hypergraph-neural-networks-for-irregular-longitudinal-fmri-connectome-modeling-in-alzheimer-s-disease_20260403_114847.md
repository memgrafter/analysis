# SDE-Driven Spatio-Temporal Hypergraph Neural Networks for Irregular Longitudinal fMRI Connectome Modeling in Alzheimer's Disease

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20452
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20452
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: SDE-HGNN achieves AUC 0.777±0.030 on OASIS-3 progression prediction and 0.699±0.101 on ADNI HC vs. AD, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SDE-HGNN, a novel framework for modeling irregular longitudinal fMRI data in Alzheimer's disease progression. The method integrates stochastic differential equations to reconstruct continuous latent trajectories from irregularly sampled scans and evolves hypergraph convolution parameters over time, conditioned on inter-visit intervals. A sparsity-based interpretability mechanism identifies salient brain regions and hyperedges. Evaluated on OASIS-3 and ADNI, SDE-HGNN consistently outperforms state-of-the-art graph and hypergraph baselines. Ablation studies confirm the critical role of SDE-driven temporal modeling and show that the sparsity module yields clinically relevant, longitudinally evolving biomarkers aligned with AD pathology.

## Method Summary
SDE-HGNN processes irregular longitudinal fMRI data as follows. For each scan visit, raw ROI BOLD time series are fed into an SDE reconstruction module: an RNN encoder maps irregular observations to an initial latent state, a neural SDE (drift \(f_\theta\), diffusion \(g_\theta\)) governs continuous-time evolution, and a decoder reconstructs a complete feature matrix \(X_{tk}\) per ROI. From \(X_{tk}\), a hypergraph is constructed by connecting each ROI to its K-nearest neighbors (K=10) to form hyperedges, yielding an incidence matrix \(H_{tk}\) and normalized propagation matrix \(S_{tk}\). For temporal progression, convolution weights \(W_{tk}\) are evolved from the previous state via an SDE solver using the inter-visit interval \(\Delta t_k\), then refined with a GRU conditioned on the current \(X_{tk}\). A sparsity module learns element-wise importance probabilities to sparsify \(X_{tk}\) and the hypergraph edges. Hypergraph convolution is applied to these sparsified matrices, followed by global pooling and an MLP classifier for disease progression prediction. The model is trained with cross-entropy loss plus sparsity regularization (L1 and entropy) and mutual information preservation.

## Key Results
- **OASIS-3 progression**: AUC 0.777 ± 0.030 with up to six visits.
- **ADNI diagnostics**: AUC 0.699 ± 0.101 for HC vs. AD classification.
- **SOTA comparison**: SDE-HGNN outperforms all graph and hypergraph baseline models consistently across tasks.
- **Ablation**: Removing SDE-driven temporal modeling or sparsity significantly degrades performance, confirming their contributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Continuous latent trajectories are reconstructed from irregularly sampled fMRI time series, mitigating artifacts from missing or noisy observations.
- **Mechanism**: A neural SDE encoder–decoder maps each ROI's BOLD signal to a continuous stochastic process. The encoder produces an initial latent state; the SDE (drift \(f_\theta\), diffusion \(g_\theta\)) evolves this state through continuous time; the decoder reconstructs the complete signal. This yields temporally coherent representations for connectivity estimation.
- **Core assumption**: The latent dynamics of fMRI signals can be modeled as a continuous stochastic process governed by an SDE, and the learned function approximators are expressive enough to capture temporal structure despite sampling irregularity.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions "SDE-based reconstruction module"; Section 3.2 details the neural SDE encoder–decoder; related work uses SDEs for longitudinal brain signals.
- **Break condition**: Insufficient model capacity or extremely sparse visits (e.g., single time point) lead to high reconstruction error and corrupted connectivity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Hypergraph convolution parameters evolve continuously via SDEs conditioned on inter-visit intervals, enabling adaptive modeling of disease progression under heterogeneous follow-up schedules.
- **Mechanism**: The weight matrix \(W_{tk}\) for hypergraph convolution is evolved from \(W_{t_{k-1}}\) using an SDE solver with time step \(\Delta t_k\). A GRU then refines these weights with current ROI features and a baseline \(W^0\). This allows spatial filtering behavior to change smoothly across irregular time gaps.
- **Core assumption**: Disease progression induces changes in functional connectivity patterns that can be captured by evolving the parameters of the spatial aggregator, and the rate of change correlates meaningfully with elapsed inter-visit interval.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states "hypergraph convolution parameters evolve through SDE-controlled recurrent dynamics conditioned on inter-scan intervals"; Section 3.4 defines the SDE-driven weight evolution and GRU refinement.
- **Break condition**: If the drift function \(f_\theta\) cannot translate \(\Delta t_k\) into meaningful weight changes (e.g., due to insufficient data for long intervals), the parameter trajectories become erratic or stagnant, failing to capture progression.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A sparsity-based importance learning mechanism identifies salient brain regions and discriminative hyperedges, acting as both interpreter and regularizer.
- **Mechanism**: Learnable probability matrices \(P^X\) (node features) and \(P^E\) (hyperedges) weight features and the incidence matrix. The model is trained with L1 and binary entropy losses to encourage sparsity, alongside mutual information regularization to preserve predictive power.
- **Core assumption**: The discriminative signal for AD progression is concentrated in a sparse subset of brain regions and their higher-order interactions; forcing sparsity highlights these and suppresses noise.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions "sparsity-based importance learning"; Section 3.6 formalizes importance probabilities and the loss function.
- **Break condition**: If true discriminative patterns are not sparse, L1/entropy conflicts with mutual information, degrading performance; optimization may also produce uninformant masks (all-zeros or all-ones).

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) for continuous-time modeling
- **Why needed here**: To handle irregular inter-visit intervals and missing observations by modeling latent trajectories as continuous stochastic processes rather than discrete steps, allowing interpolation and evolution conditioned on exact time differences.

### Concept: Hypergraph Neural Networks
- **Why needed here**: To capture higher-order, group-wise interactions among multiple brain regions simultaneously, beyond pairwise functional connectivity, which better reflects coordinated neural processes implicated in AD.
- **Quick check**: In the hypergraph construction (Eq. 2), what does a single hyperedge represent about the functional organization of the ROIs it connects?

- **Why needed here**: Longitudinal fMRI data have variable follow-up intervals and missing visits; standard discrete-time models fail to account for the non-uniform passage of time, leading to biased temporal representations.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Raw fMRI → SDE Reconstruction → Hypergraph Construction → (for each subsequent visit: Weight SDE Evolution + GRU Refinement → Hypergraph Convolution) → Global Pooling → Classifier

### Critical path
The temporal loop on weights is critical: after initial hypergraph construction, each new visit triggers SDE-driven weight update based on \(\Delta t_k\), GRU refinement using current feature matrix, then hypergraph convolution on the updated weights. The outputs from all visits are pooled for final classification.

### Design tradeoffs
- *SDE complexity*: Neural SDEs provide flexible continuous-time modeling but increase computational overhead and require careful solver configuration; trade‑off: accuracy vs. training time/stability.
- *Hypergraph vs. Graph*: Hyperedges capture higher-order interactions but increase memory (O(N²) for KNN hyperedges) and may introduce noisy connections if K is poorly chosen; trade‑off: representational power vs. scalability and noise sensitivity.
- *Sparsity strength*: Strong L1/entropy regularization yields interpretable models but risks underfitting; tuning \(\lambda\) parameters is critical and task‑dependent.

### Failure signatures
- **Reconstruction failure**: SDE module produces unrealistic \(X_{tk}\) (excessively smooth or noisy), detectable by high reconstruction MSE or abnormal functional connectivity (near‑zero correlations).
- **Temporal incoherence**: Evolved weights \(W_{tk}\) show no smooth change with increasing \(\Delta t_k\) or exhibit high variance across similar‑interval visits, indicating the drift function \(f_\theta\) is not learning meaningful temporal dynamics.
- **Sparsity collapse**: Learned \(P^X\) or \(P^E\) become all‑zeros or all‑ones, typically from imbalanced \(\lambda\) tuning or poor gradient flow.
- **Overfitting on small \(T\)**: With few visits, the SDE and hypergraph modules may overfit noise; performance degrades compared to simpler baselines.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Reconstruction validation**: Isolate the SDE module; train it to reconstruct held‑out portions of ROI time series from irregular inputs. Measure reconstruction MSE and correlation against ground‑truth; success: lower MSE than linear interpolation or vanilla RNN.
2. **Hypergraph vs. Graph ablation**: Replace the hypergraph construction with a standard pairwise correlation graph while keeping all other modules identical. Compare performance on OASIS‑3 progression prediction; success: hypergraph variant shows statistically significant AUC improvement.
3. **Temporal modeling ablation**: Replace the SDE weight evolution with (a) a discrete‑time RNN that ignores \(\Delta t_k\) and (b) a deterministic ODE solver. Compare full SDE‑HGNN against these on OASIS‑3 with varying visit counts; success: full model shows consistent gains, especially with fewer visits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Under what conditions do hypergraph representations provide a statistically significant advantage over pairwise graph models for capturing AD‑related functional reorganization, and is this advantage consistent across different disease stages?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper advocates hypergraphs for higher‑order interactions but does not formally isolate the hypergraph's contribution from the SDE‑temporal component. Comparative results vary by task and visit count.
- **Why unresolved**: No controlled experiment varying only the relational inductive bias (graph vs. hypergraph) while keeping temporal modeling identical. Without such an ablation, the source of performance lift is ambiguous.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement a graph‑variant of SDE‑HGNN with standard graph convolution and identical temporal evolution; perform paired statistical tests across CV folds for all OASIS‑3 visit settings and ADNI tasks. Also analyze whether hypergraph edges align more strongly with known multi‑region AD networks than pairwise edges.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: To what extent does the stochastic component of the neural SDE (i.e., the diffusion term \(g_\theta\)) contribute to model performance under irregular sampling compared to a deterministic ODE‑based reconstruction, and is its benefit primarily from handling intra‑scan noise or inter‑visit variability?
- **Basis in paper**: The ablation (Table 3) shows SDE vs. ODE improvement, but the authors state continuous‑time stochastic modeling is important for capturing subject‑specific variability without disentangling sources.
- **Why unresolved**: The SDE module operates within each scan, while inter‑visit irregularity is handled later by weight evolution; the ablation conflates both scales, making it unclear which component benefits from stochasticity.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Separate ablations: (1) keep SDE reconstruction but replace inter‑visit weight evolution with deterministic ODE; (2) replace within‑scan SDE reconstruction with ODE while keeping inter‑visit SDE. Performance differences would clarify the contribution of stochasticity at each level. Simulated data with controlled within‑scan noise and inter‑visit irregularity could also quantify effects.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does the sparsity‑based interpretability mechanism reliably identify AD‑pathological biomarkers, or does it overfit to dataset‑specific noise by emphasizing connections that are predictive in the training cohort but lack biological plausibility or reproducibility across sites?
- **Basis in paper**: Interpretability results (Figs. 2 & 3) show evolving salient ROIs (e.g., parahippocampal early) and discriminative connections involving DAN/DMN, aligning with known AD pathology. However, analysis is limited to OASIS‑3 only, with no external validation.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper claims clinical relevance based on alignment with literature, but this is post‑hoc and not a formal validation. No test on independent cohorts or correlation with independent biomarkers (amyloid‑PET, CSF tau) is provided; selection may be driven by confounders.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Apply the trained model (with frozen sparsity masks) to an external dataset and measure overlap of top‑selected ROIs/edges with established AD networks. Perform a “dataset shuffle” control: train on OASIS‑3, test on ADNI, and compare sparsity pattern reproducibility (e.g., rank correlation). Also correlate continuous importance scores with cognitive scores (MMSE) to test monotonic relationships.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How sensitive is SDE‑HGNN's performance to the choice of brain parcellation atlas (e.g., Schaefer‑100 vs. finer or coarser atlases), and does the model's ability to capture evolving biomarkers depend on the spatial resolution and network organization of the atlas?
- **Basis in paper**: All experiments use the Schaefer‑100 atlas (100 ROIs). Hypergraph construction uses KNN based on ROI features, making topology dependent on node features derived from that parcellation. The paper does not explore atlas sensitivity.
- **Why unresolved**: Different atlases yield different node definitions and network boundaries. A biomarker identified with one atlas may be split or merged with another, affecting detection. Without sensitivity analysis, it is unclear whether advantages are atlas‑agnostic or tied to Schaefer‑100.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Repeat key experiments (OASIS‑3 progression, ADNI diagnosis) using atlases of varying granularity (e.g., Schaefer‑200, Desikan‑Killiany). Compare overall performance and spatial consistency of top‑selected ROIs (e.g., Dice overlap). If performance drops significantly with other atlases, it suggests reliance on a specific resolution; if biomarker maps remain consistent, it strengthens biological validity.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Can the SDE‑HGNN framework be effectively extended to multimodal longitudinal data (e.g., integrating structural MRI, PET, or CSF biomarkers) without compromising its handling of irregular sampling, and does fusion improve prognostic accuracy beyond fMRI alone?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion explicitly states future work will extend to multimodal integration, indicating this is an open direction.
- **Why unresolved**: The current model is designed for single‑modality (fMRI) connectomes. Multimodal data introduces challenges: different modalities have distinct temporal dynamics (PET slower, CSF sparse); fusion strategies must respect irregularity at multiple scales; hypergraph construction assumes node features are comparable, which may not hold across modalities. No pilot experiments or theoretical discussion are provided.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop an extension that integrates baseline PET amyloid load or CSF biomarkers as additional node attributes or edge modulators while preserving SDE‑based temporal modeling for fMRI visits. Evaluate on a multimodal dataset like ADNI to determine whether performance improves over fMRI‑only SDE‑HGNN and other multimodal baselines. Success would require demonstrating robust handling of irregular visits and effective fusion.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified hyperparameters**: Exact architecture details for the neural SDE (layers, hidden sizes) and the GRU weight‑refinement mechanism (Eq. 6) are not provided, making exact replication difficult.
- **Computational overhead**: The use of neural SDEs, hypergraph operations, and recurrent weight evolution likely incurs significant computational cost, but scalability and efficiency are not discussed.
- **Generalization**: The model is evaluated only on OASIS-3 and ADNI; its applicability to other datasets or acquisition protocols remains unknown.

## Confidence

- **SDE‑driven weight evolution**: Medium
- **Sparsity‑based selection**: Medium
- **Reported AUC improvements**: Low
