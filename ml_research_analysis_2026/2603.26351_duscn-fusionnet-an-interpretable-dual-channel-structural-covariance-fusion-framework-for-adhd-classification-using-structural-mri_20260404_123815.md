# DuSCN-FusionNet: An Interpretable Dual-Channel Structural Covariance Fusion Framework for ADHD Classification Using Structural MRI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26351
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26351
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** Achieved 80.59% balanced accuracy on the ADHD-200 Peking University site, with Grad-CAM identifying candidate biomarkers such as bilateral caudate and cingulum.

## Executive Summary
DuSCN-FusionNet tackles the lack of interpretable, anatomy-based biomarkers for ADHD diagnosis from structural MRI. The framework constructs dual-channel Structural Covariance Networks (SCNs) from ROI-wise mean intensity (capturing volumetric contrasts) and heterogeneity (IQR, capturing intra-regional complexity). These SCNs blend group-level covariance and subject-specific signals, then process through a CNN encoder. Auxiliary features (ROI IQR and global statistics) are fused late with CNN features for classification. Using stratified 10-fold cross-validation on the Peking University site of ADHD-200, it achieved high balanced accuracy (80.59%) and AUC (0.778). By adapting Grad-CAM to the SCN domain, the model highlights structurally relevant regions (e.g., caudate, cingulum, cerebellar vermis), enhancing clinical interpretability.

## Method Summary
The method uses T1-weighted sMRI from 78 ADHD and 116 healthy controls (Peking University, ADHD-200), preprocessed via Athena pipeline and parcellated with AAL atlas (116 ROIs). For each subject, compute ROI-wise mean intensity (MNI) and inter-quartile range (IQR). Construct two SCNs: from MNI and IQR vectors separately. Each SCN blends a group-level covariance matrix (from training data) with a subject-specific normalized outer-product, using a fixed α=0.55. Stack the two 116×116 matrices into a 2×116×116 tensor. Process this tensor via an SCN-CNN encoder (three convolutional layers with batch norm and ReLU, max-pooling after first two, adaptive average pooling) to extract relational features. Parallelly, a 119-dimensional auxiliary vector (per-ROI IQR + three global statistics) is processed by a two-layer MLP. Late fusion concatenates CNN and MLP features, followed by two fully connected layers and a softmax classifier. Training uses Adam (LR 1e-4), dropout, early stopping, and stratified 10-fold CV with a 5-seed ensemble. For interpretability, Grad-CAM is adapted to the final convolutional layer of the CNN branch: gradients w.r.t. the ADHD class score are aggregated across SCN adjacency matrix rows and columns to derive ROI importance scores.

## Key Results
- Mean balanced accuracy: 80.59%
- AUC: 0.778
- Precision: 81.66%, Recall: 80.59%, F1-score: 80.27%
- Grad-CAM identified bilateral caudate, cingulum, and cerebellar vermis as candidate ADHD biomarkers, aligning with prior neurobiological evidence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Channel SCN Construction Captures Complementary Morphometric Relationships
- **Claim:** Separate intensity-based (mean) and heterogeneity-based (IQR) SCNs provide discriminative, anatomy-grounded representations that single-channel approaches miss.
- **Mechanism:** For each subject, two adjacency matrices are created from AAL-parcellated sMRI: one from ROI mean intensities (volumetric/tissue contrast) and one from ROI IQR (structural complexity). Both matrices blend group-level (population covariance) and subject-level (normalized outer-product) information via α-blending (α=0.55), then stacked into a tensor input to a shared CNN encoder.
- **Core assumption:** Morphometric relationships (covariance) between ROIs are disorder-relevant, and mean vs. IQR descriptors reflect non-redundant aspects of ADHD structural abnormality.
- **Evidence:** Ablation study shows significant performance drop when auxiliary features (which include IQR) are removed, implying the heterogeneity channel contributes. The dual-channel design is explicit in the architecture.
- **Break condition:** If α-blending poorly weights group vs. individual signals, or if mean/IQR descriptors are individually non-discriminative, the dual-channel input becomes noisy or redundant.

### Mechanism 2: Late-Stage Fusion with Auxiliary Features Enhances Discriminative Power
- **Claim:** Integrating subject-level ROI variability (IQR vector) and global statistics via late fusion with SCN-CNN latent features improves classification over SCNs alone.
- **Mechanism:** The SCN-CNN branch outputs a feature vector `z_SCN(s)`. Separately, an MLP processes a 119-dimensional auxiliary vector (per-ROI IQR + 3 global stats) to produce `z_aux(s)`. These are concatenated before final classification layers, allowing the model to leverage both network-level relational patterns and region-/image-level summaries without early interference.
- **Core assumption:** Auxiliary features contain information about regional heterogeneity and global shifts not fully encoded in pairwise SCN structure, and late fusion prevents heterogeneous feature types from disrupting convolutional processing.
- **Evidence:** Ablation study (Table II) shows "Without Auxiliary" drops accuracy to 62.01% vs. full model 80.59%, providing strong empirical support.
- **Break condition:** If auxiliary features are highly correlated with SCN-derived features or contain site/scanner biases, fusion may add noise or overfit; benefit is data-dependent.

### Mechanism 3: Grad-CAM Adaptation to SCN Domain Enables Anatomical Biomarker Localization
- **Claim:** Adapting Grad-CAM to the final convolutional layer of the SCN-CNN branch yields ROI-level importance scores that map to candidate neuroanatomical biomarkers.
- **Mechanism:** Standard Grad-CAM computes class-discriminative activation maps on convolutional feature maps. Here, activation maps are 2D (for each SCN channel). Since input is an adjacency matrix (ROI×ROI), gradients/activations from the final conv layer are aggregated (e.g., summed) across rows and columns to derive a scalar importance per ROI. Thresholding (90th percentile) identifies salient regions.
- **Core assumption:** The final convolutional layer learns spatial patterns corresponding to meaningful ROI interactions, and row/column aggregation correctly isolates ROI contributions from adjacency matrix data.
- **Evidence:** Identified regions (bilateral caudate, cingulum, cerebellar vermis) align with known ADHD neurobiology and are supported by complementary statistical testing in the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the final conv layer learns site-specific or noise patterns, Grad-CAM importance may not generalize; the aggregation heuristic (row/column sum) is an unverified assumption for graph-structured data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structural Covariance Networks (SCNs):** Model inter-regional morphological correlations across subjects, hypothesizing that coordinated structural changes reflect disrupted neurodevelopment. **Why needed:** Moves beyond voxel-wise or single-ROI analysis to a network perspective for disorders like ADHD. **Quick check:** How does constructing an SCN from mean ROI intensities differ from one based on IQR, and why might both be informative for ADHD?
- **Explainable AI (XAI) for Graph/Network Data:** Adapting gradient-based attribution methods like Grad-CAM to non-image data such as adjacency matrices. **Why needed:** Standard Grad-CAM Designed for 2D/3D image grids; adaptation is necessary for relational data like SCNs to enable interpretability. **Quick check:** Why can't the original Grad-CAM formula be directly applied to an SCN adjacency matrix, and what does the row/column aggregation step assume about ROI importance?
- **Late-Stage Feature Fusion in Multimodal Learning:** Combining features from different processing branches after initial extraction, rather than early concatenation. **Why needed:** Here, it handles heterogeneous inputs: relational (SCN tensor) and non-relational (auxiliary vector), preventing interference during convolutional processing. **Quick check:** What is a key risk of late fusion versus early fusion when one modality is high-dimensional and spatially structured while the other is a flat feature vector?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Preprocessor (intensity normalization) -> AAL Parcellation (resample atlas, extract voxels per ROI) -> Feature Extractor (compute per-ROI mean intensity `µ_r` and IQR `IQR_r`, plus global stats) -> SCN Constructor (x2: for `µ` and `IQR` vectors, compute group covariance `C`, subject-specific `C_ind(s)` via normalized outer-product, blend `C(s) = αC + (1-α)C_ind(s)`) -> Stack to 2×116×116 tensor -> SCN-CNN Encoder (3 conv layers with BN/ReLU, max-pool after first two, adaptive avg-pool) -> `z_SCN`; Parallel: Auxiliary Vector (119-dim: IQR + global stats) -> Auxiliary MLP (2 FC layers) -> `z_aux`; Fusion: Concatenate `[z_SCN ∥ z_aux]` -> Fusion Classifier (2 FC layers) -> Softmax output.
- **Critical path:** Raw sMRI -> AAL labels -> `µ` & `IQR` vectors -> dual SCNs -> SCN-CNN -> `z_SCN`; auxiliary branch -> `z_aux`; concatenation -> classification.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Dual-channel SCNs vs. single-channel: Better morphometric coverage but doubles input channels and CNN parameters.
  - α-blending (fixed at 0.55): Simplicity vs. optimal subject-specific weighting; not learned.
  - Late fusion: Modular and easy to modify auxiliary streams, but may miss early interactions between modalities.
  - Grad-CAM aggregation heuristic: Computationally cheap, but row/column sum is an arbitrary choice for importance pooling from adjacency matrices; not theoretically grounded for graphs.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **SCNs collapse/over-smooth:** If α→1, all subjects have nearly identical `C(s)`. Detect: high similarity (e.g., cosine) between subject SCNs.
  2. **Auxiliary branch dominates:** If `z_aux` magnitude >> `z_SCN` after fusion, CNN branch is ignored. Monitor: gradient norms flowing into SCN-CNN during training.
  3. **Grad-CAM yields diffuse maps:** If >50% ROIs are above 90th percentile threshold, or maps are invariant across seeds/folds, model may not have learned localized patterns. Check: sparsity of ROI importance scores.
  4. **Poor reproducibility across seeds:** High variance in metrics across the 5-seed ensemble indicates unstable optimization or insufficient data.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **SCN Sanity Check:** Compute and visualize mean ± std of group `C` (from training set) and a few subject `C(s)`. Verify they are not identical and show plausible covariance (e.g., interhemispheric homologues positive).
  2. **Ablation Replication:** Re-run Table II experiments. Confirm "Without Auxiliary" causes major drop. Then test "Without Dual-Channel" (use only intensity-based SCN) to isolate heterogeneity channel contribution.
  3. **Grad-CAM Stability:** For the best CV fold, run Grad-CAM on models from all 5 seeds. Compute Intersection-over-Union (IoU) of top-12 ROIs (90th percentile threshold) across seeds. Low mean IoU (<0.3) indicates unstable explanations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does DuSCN-FusionNet generalize to other sites within the ADHD-200 consortium, and what site-specific factors (e.g., scanner protocols, demographics) impact performance?
2. Would incorporating a broader set of morphometric descriptors (e.g., cortical thickness, surface area, volume) improve accuracy and biomarker robustness compared to mean intensity and IQR alone?
3. How do alternative XAI methods (e.g., SHAP, attention rollouts) compare to Grad-CAM in producing anatomically coherent and clinically plausible ADHD biomarkers from SCN-based models?
4. Can the framework be extended to differentiate ADHD subtypes (inattentive, hyperactive/impulsive, combined), and what are the subtype-specific structural covariance biomarkers?

## Limitations
- **Architectural ambiguity:** Critical hyperparameters (e.g., exact FC layer dimensions, conv kernel sizes/padding) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **XAI method validity:** The Grad-CAM adaptation heuristic (summing rows/columns of an adjacency matrix) is an unverified assumption for deriving ROI importance from graph-structured data; biological plausibility is not theoretically guaranteed.
- **Dataset and generalization:** Results are from a single site (ADHD-200 PU) with a small sample (n=194); performance and biomarkers may not generalize to other cohorts, scanners, or preprocessing pipelines, with overfitting concerns.

## Confidence
- **Dual-Channel SCNs Capture Complementary Information:** Medium Confidence. Supported by ablation performance drop when removing auxiliary features, but the specific contribution of the dual-channel versus a single richer channel is not isolated experimentally.
- **Late-Stage Fusion with Auxiliary Features is Beneficial:** High Confidence. Strong empirical evidence from ablation study (62.01% vs. 80.59% accuracy).
- **Grad-CAM Adapted to SCNs Yields Valid Biomarkers:** Low-to-Medium Confidence. Identified regions align with ADHD literature, but methodological soundness of adaptation and stability of ROI rankings are not validated.

## Next Checks
1. **Single-Channel Ablation:** Replicate the "Without Auxiliary" ablation but also train a model using only the intensity-based SCN (single-channel). Compare its performance to the full dual-channel model to isolate the incremental value of the heterogeneity (IQR) channel.
2. **Grad-CAM Stability Analysis:** For the best-performing cross-validation fold, run Grad-CAM on models from all 5 random seeds. Compute the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) of the top-12 ROIs (90th percentile threshold) across seeds. A low mean IoU (<0.3) would indicate unstable explanations, questioning biomarker reliability.
3. **Sensitivity to α-Blending Parameter:** Systematically vary the blending coefficient α (e.g., 0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0) and re-evaluate model performance on a held-out validation fold within one CV iteration. This tests the robustness of the fixed α=0.55 choice and identifies if results are overly sensitive to this hyperparameter.
