# LUMINA: A Multi-Vendor Mammography Benchmark with Energy Harmonization Protocol
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14644
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14644
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A foreground-only pixel-space CDF alignment harmonization method improves multi-vendor mammography analysis, with a two-view EfficientNet-B0 achieving 93.54% AUC for benign/malignant diagnosis.

## Executive Summary
LUMINA introduces a curated multi-vendor full-field digital mammography dataset (1824 images, 468 patients, 6 acquisition systems) with pathology, BI-RADS, and breast density labels. To counter vendor- and acquisition energy-induced appearance shifts, it proposes a foreground-only pixel-space cumulative distribution function (CDF) alignment method that maps images to a low-energy reference while preserving lesion morphology. Benchmarking CNN and transformer models on diagnosis, BI-RADS classification, and density estimation demonstrates that two-view models (CC + MLO) consistently outperform single-view models, and the harmonization preprocessing consistently boosts performance across all architectures and tasks.

## Method Summary
The LUMINA dataset comprises full-field digital mammography images from six vendors, captured in both high- and low-energy styles, with annotations including pathology, BI-RADS (0-6), and breast density (A-D). Preprocessing involves (1) converting MONOCHROME1 to MONOCHROME2 and removing text overlays (for FUJIFILM images), (2) applying a foreground-only CDF matching harmonization to a low-energy reference (masking pixels with intensity >0), (3) resizing to 224² or 512² and replicating to 3 channels, and (4) random horizontal flipping during training. Models use ImageNet-pretrained backbones (EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, Swin-T). For two-view inputs (CC and MLO), backbone weights are shared; features from each view are concatenated after global average pooling and passed to a task-specific classification head. Training uses patient-level 5-fold cross-validation with AdamW optimizer, a step-decay learning rate schedule, and validation AUC for model selection.

## Key Results
- Best benign/malignant diagnosis AUC: **93.54%** with two-view EfficientNet-B0 (512², harmonized).
- Best density prediction macro-AUC: **89.43%** with two-view Swin-T (224², harmonized).
- Two-view models consistently outperform single-view models across all tasks and architectures.
- Harmonization preprocessing yields performance gains for every architecture evaluated and produces more focused Grad-CAM attention on lesions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Foreground-Only CDF Harmonization Preserves Morphology
**Claim:** Foreground-only pixel-space cumulative distribution function (CDF) alignment harmonizes vendor- and energy-induced intensity shifts while preserving lesion morphology.
**Mechanism:** The method computes intensity histograms and CDFs using only foreground (breast tissue) pixels (intensity >0), excluding the large black background. It then applies a monotonic transformation to map the source image's foreground intensities to match a low-energy reference image's foreground CDF, leaving background pixels at zero. This standardizes soft-tissue contrast and dynamic range across vendors without smoothing lesions.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "foreground-only pixel-space CDF alignment method ("energy harmonization") that maps images to a low-energy reference while preserving lesion morphology."
- [section] Section 4 details the foreground mask definition (Eqs. 1–4), CDF matching (Eqs. 5–7), and reconstruction (Eq. 8), explicitly excluding background (intensity 0). Figure 4 visually contrasts standard vs. foreground-only matching.
**Break condition:** If lesions or dense tissue have pixel intensities near zero (e.g., due to poor exposure or extreme density), they may be incorrectly treated as background and discarded, leading to loss of diagnostic information.

### Mechanism 2: Two-View Fusion Captures Complementary Projections
**Claim:** Two-view models (CC + MLO) with shared-weight backbones consistently outperform single-view models by capturing complementary anatomical projections of the same breast.
**Mechanism:** The CC and MLO views provide different tissue coverage and angles; a shared backbone learns a joint representation that fuses information from both projections. Feature embeddings from each view are concatenated before the classifier, allowing the model to combine complementary cues (e.g., a mass visible only in MLO with architectural distortion visible in CC).
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "Two-view models consistently outperform single-view models."
- [section] Table 4 shows two-view EfficientNet-B0 (512²) achieves 93.54% AUC vs. single-view 92.13%. Tables 5 and 6 show similar gains. Table 7 confirms shared-weight two-view models match independent-weight performance with 48% fewer parameters.
**Break condition:** If one view is severely compromised (e.g., poor positioning, motion artifact), the complementary signal may be insufficient, and a single high-quality view could perform better.

### Mechanism 3: Vendor/Energy Diversity and Harmonization Improve Robustness
**Claim:** Vendor and energy diversity in LUMINA exposes models to realistic domain shifts, and harmonization reduces performance variance across these shifts, improving out-of-distribution generalization.
**Mechanism:** Training on data from six vendors with both high- and low-energy styles forces the model to learn features invariant to acquisition physics and vendor-specific image processing. The foreground CDF alignment further reduces the intensity distribution gap, making the learning task easier and more stable.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "explicitly encodes acquisition energy and vendor metadata to capture clinically relevant appearance variations... To address these variations, we propose a foreground-only pixel-space alignment method..."
- [section] Table 2 lists six vendors and energy styles. Figure 8 shows harmonization improves AUC more on the minority low-energy subset when high-energy dominates, indicating reduced energy bias. Figure 6 shows harmonization improves performance across all architectures.
**Break condition:** If real-world deployment involves a vendor or energy style completely absent from LUMINA, harmonization may misalign intensities, potentially degrading performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Histogram Matching / CDF Alignment
  - **Why needed:** The harmonization method relies on matching the cumulative distribution of foreground pixel intensities to a reference. Understanding histogram specification explains why foreground masking is critical to avoid histogram bias from the large black background.
  - **Quick check:** Why would including the large black background in the histogram calculation distort the mapping between source and reference images in mammography?
- **Concept:** Multi-View Learning in Medical Imaging
  - **Why needed:** The core architectural innovation is fusing CC and MLO views. Clinically, these views provide different tissue coverage; architecturally, a shared backbone fuses complementary information.
  - **Quick check:** What is a potential failure mode if the two views are processed with independent backbones instead of shared weights?
- **Concept:** Domain Shift / Batch Effect in Medical Imaging
  - **Why needed:** The entire motivation for LUMINA and harmonization is to address vendor/energy-induced domain shift. Understanding this explains why a simple, model-agnostic preprocessing step can generalize across tasks and architectures.
  - **Quick check:** How does ComBat differ from LUMINA's harmonization in terms of the stage of application and model dependence?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Pipeline (DICOM load, MONOCHROME1→2, text removal) → Harmonization Module (foreground CDF matching to low-energy reference) → Resize & Replicate (224²/512², 3 channels) → Backbone (CNN/Transformer, ImageNet-pretrained) → Two-View Fusion (shared backbone, concatenate GAP features) → Task Head (FC layers for binary/3-class/4-class output).
- **Critical path:**
  Data loading → harmonization (if enabled) → resize/replicate → random horizontal flip (train) → backbone forward pass (per view) → feature concatenation → classification head → loss. Harmonization must precede resizing to avoid interpolation artifacts on the binary mask.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Shared vs. Independent Backbone:** Shared reduces parameters (4.34M vs 8.34M) with comparable AUC (Table 7). Independent may allow view-specific learning but risks overfitting.
  - **Resolution (224² vs 512²):** 512² preserves details, improves CNN performance (Table 4), but increases FLOPs. Swin-T's pretraining on 224² makes it less sensitive to higher resolution (Table 6).
  - **Harmonization On/Off:** Always improves AUC/ACC/F1 (Fig. 6) and Grad-CAM focus (Fig. 7) with negligible compute cost. Should be default.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No harmonization:** Model performance drops significantly on low-energy or specific vendor images; Grad-CAM activation spreads to background (Fig. 7).
  - **Incorrect harmonization reference:** If the reference is not low-energy or is low-quality, soft-tissue contrast gets distorted, potentially reducing performance (Fig. 8 shows low-energy subset benefits most).
  - **Single-view training:** Diagnosis AUC is notably lower than two-view (Table 4), especially for lesions visible in only one projection.
  - **Swin-T at 512² without fine-tuning:** Density prediction performance may suffer compared to CNNs due to resolution mismatch with ImageNet pretraining (Table 6).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Harmonization Impact:** On EfficientNet-B0 (two-view, 512²), compare raw vs. harmonized images for diagnosis AUC. Expected: harmonized > raw (Fig. 6a,b).
  2. **Single vs. Two-View:** Fix backbone (EfficientNet-B0, 224², harmonized), compare single-view and two-view diagnosis AUC. Expected: two-view significantly higher (Table 4).
  3. **Energy-Specific Evaluation:** Using best diagnosis model (two-view EfficientNet-B0, harmonized, 224²), compute AUC separately on low-energy and high-energy subsets. Expected: harmonization narrows the gap, boosting low-energy subset more (Fig. 8).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the proposed foreground-only histogram matching harmonization compare with alternative harmonization methods like ComBat or deep learning-based approaches in terms of performance and efficiency?
- **Basis:** The paper introduces the harmonization method (Section 4) and shows improvement over raw inputs (Section 6.3) but does not benchmark against other harmonization techniques mentioned in related work (Section 2).
- **Why unresolved:** Without comparative evaluation, it is unknown whether the simple histogram matching is optimal or if more advanced methods could yield further gains across tasks and architectures.
- **Resolving evidence:** Implement several harmonization methods (e.g., ComBat adapted to pixel space, CycleGAN-based style transfer) on LUMINA, train identical models, and compare accuracy, AUC, computational cost, and Grad-CAM interpretability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How well do models trained on LUMINA generalize to mammography data from unseen vendors or external institutions?
- **Basis:** LUMINA is collected from a single hospital with six vendors (Section 3, Table 2) and all experiments use random patient-level splits without leave-one-vendor-out or external validation.
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world deployment requires robustness to new scanners and populations; the current evaluation does not test this critical aspect of vendor shift.
- **Resolving evidence:** Perform leave-one-vendor-out cross-validation within LUMINA, and/or evaluate LUMINA-trained models on external datasets (e.g., INbreast, VinDR-Mammo) with appropriate preprocessing, measuring performance drop and harmonization impact.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive is the effectiveness of foreground-only histogram matching to the choice of the low-energy reference histogram?
- **Basis:** The harmonization uses a reference derived from "a representative subset" of low-energy images (Section 4) but no analysis of reference selection or its impact on results is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** An arbitrary or suboptimal reference might limit the benefits of harmonization; understanding sensitivity is key for reproducibility and optimal application.
- **Resolving evidence:** Systematically vary the reference subset (e.g., different vendors, sizes, random selections) and measure changes in model performance (AUC, ACC) across all three tasks. Optionally, learn an optimal reference via optimization.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does forcing all images to a low-energy reference via harmonization universally benefit all mammography analysis tasks, or are there tasks where high-energy characteristics are preferable (e.g., microcalcification detection)?
- **Basis:** The harmonization rationale emphasizes low-energy's soft-tissue contrast (Section 4), and evaluations focus on diagnosis, BI-RADS, and density—tasks not specifically targeting calcifications. High-energy's role in calcification visibility is not explored.
- **Why unresolved:** Microcalcifications are critical markers of malignancy (e.g., DCIS); if harmonization degrades their appearance, it could harm such tasks despite overall gains.
- **Resolving evidence:** Introduce a calcification detection/classification task using LUMINA images with calcification annotations (or curate labels), then compare performance with and without harmonization, and analyze whether low-energy mapping improves or worsens sensitivity to calcifications.

## Limitations
- **Reference histogram specification:** The exact low-energy subset and aggregation method for the harmonization reference are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- **Classification head details:** The architecture (layers, dropout, activations) of task-specific heads is unspecified, requiring assumptions.
- **Dataset scale & diversity:** With only 1824 images from 468 patients, the dataset may be underpowered for robust generalization claims, especially for rare subtypes or new vendors.
- **External validity:** The paper lacks evaluation on an *unseen* external multi-vendor dataset, so the harmonization's out-of-distribution robustness remains an assumption.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Foreground CDF Harmonization):** Medium. The method is clearly described and shows improved AUC/Grad-CAM focus. However, the break condition (lesions near zero intensity) is not empirically tested, and comparison to alternative harmonization (e.g., whole-image histogram matching) is absent.
- **Mechanism 2 (Two-View Fusion Benefit):** High. Consistently superior performance across multiple tasks and architectures is demonstrated in Tables 4–6 with patient-level CV, strongly supporting the claim.
- **Mechanism 3 (Vendor/Energy Robustness):** Medium. In-dataset energy subgroup analysis (Fig. 8) shows harmonization reduces bias. However, the critical claim of generalization to *unseen* vendors/energies is not validated experimentally.

## Next Checks
1.  **Structural Preservation Audit:** Quantify harmonization-induced anatomical distortion. Compute SSIM or LPIPS between original and harmonized images on a lesion-annotated subset. A significant drop would violate the "preserve lesion morphology" claim.
2.  **Ablate Fusion Strategy:** Train a two-view model with *independent* backbone weights (vs. shared) on the exact same folds. If performance drops significantly (e.g., >1% AUC) without the parameter savings claimed in Table 7, the shared-weight advantage is more pronounced than stated.
3.  **External Generalization Stress Test:** Apply the trained harmonization protocol (using the published reference) to a publicly available external multi-vendor dataset (e.g., DDSM, INbreast). Measure the change in a simple model's (e.g., ResNet-50) performance *with and without* harmonization. Failure to improve or a performance drop would challenge the harmonization's robustness.
