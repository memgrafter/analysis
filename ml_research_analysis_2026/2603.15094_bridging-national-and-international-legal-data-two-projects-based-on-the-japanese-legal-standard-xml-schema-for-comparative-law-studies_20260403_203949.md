# Bridging National and International Legal Data: Two Projects Based on the Japanese Legal Standard XML Schema for Comparative Law Studies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15094  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15094  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** A two-project system converts Japanese legal XML (JLS) to Akoma Ntoso (AKN) and uses multilingual embeddings with a Cross-Encoder to map provisions across jurisdictions, achieving F1=0.768 on domestic and 0.348 on cross-jurisdictional tasks, and building a network of 2,000 nodes and 3,545 edges.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of enabling computational comparative law by connecting disparate national legal data—specifically Japanese Legal Standard (JLS) XML—with international standards and identifying semantically corresponding provisions across jurisdictions. It presents two integrated projects: Project 1 develops a conversion pipeline to transform JLS into the Akoma Ntoso (AKN) standard, establishing structural interoperability. Project 2 builds on this by using multilingual embeddings (multilingual-e5-large) and FAISS for candidate retrieval, followed by a Cross-Encoder reranker, to extract cross-jurisdictional provision correspondences, visualized as a network. Project 1 validated its pipeline on 10 Japanese laws; Project 2 achieved an F1 score of 0.768 on a domestic mapping task and 0.348 on a cross-jurisdictional Japanese-German Civil Code mapping task. The resulting correspondence network for Japan, Korea, and France contained 2,000 nodes and 3,545 edges, demonstrating the feasibility of large-scale, automated candidate generation for comparative analysis.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two complementary pipelines. Project 1’s conversion pipeline parses JLS XML, applies a sequence of XSLT transformations to map hierarchical elements (e.g., `<Law>`, `<Article>`) to AKN equivalents (e.g., `<act>`, `<article>`), enforces FRBR metadata, and validates the output against the LegalDocML-AKN schema. Project 2’s semantic matching pipeline encodes each legal provision (from AKN-encoded Civil/Commercial Codes of Japan, Korea, France, etc.) using the multilingual-e5-large model, builds separate FAISS indices for foreign provisions, retrieves top candidates for each Japanese article, reranks with a Cross-Encoder, and filters results using per-country quotas and score thresholds to construct a bipartite correspondence network. The two-stage retrieval (embedding similarity then Cross-Encoder) balances speed and precision, while network visualization supports exploratory analysis.

## Key Results
- Project 1 successfully transformed 10 Japanese laws into AKN, confirming structural compatibility via schema validation.
- Project 2 achieved F1=0.768 on domestic mapping (Japanese Electricity vs. Gas Business Acts) and F1=0.348 on cross-jurisdictional mapping (Japanese vs. German Civil Code family law).
- The prototype network for Japan, Korea, and France comprised 2,000 nodes and 3,545 edges, enabling large-scale candidate generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system’s effectiveness rests on three interconnected mechanisms. First, deterministic JLS-to-AKN conversion via XSLT ensures structural interoperability by mapping hierarchical elements and enforcing metadata standards. Second, multilingual embeddings place provisions from different languages and legal traditions into a shared vector space, enabling approximate nearest-neighbor search with FAISS to retrieve semantically similar candidates at scale. Third, a Cross-Encoder reranker applies cross-attention to pairwise provision texts, refining scores beyond coarse embedding similarity and improving precision. Together, these mechanisms transform raw legal XML into a network of cross-jurisdictional correspondences, albeit with performance varying due to linguistic and doctrinal divergences.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: JLS→AKN Converter -> Embedding Encoder -> FAISS Index -> Cross-Encoder Reranker -> Graph Builder -> Visualization

Critical path: Schema conversion → AKN validation → embedding generation → FAISS indexing → candidate retrieval → reranking → network construction.

Design tradeoffs:
- Precision vs. Recall: High FAISS `topk_cand` improves recall but overloads reranker; low visualization thresholds prune edges but risk missing valid matches.
- Speed vs. Accuracy: Embedding-only retrieval is fast but imprecise; Cross-Encoder is accurate but slow for all pairs, hence the two-stage design.
- Coverage vs. Noise: Adding more jurisdictions increases connectivity but introduces linguistic/conceptual divergences that lower F1.

Failure signatures:
- Schema validation errors (e.g., missing required AKN metadata) → abort conversion.
- FAISS index returns zero candidates for a Japanese article → likely embedding outlier or corpus indexing bug.
- Reranker scores uniformly low (<0.3) across candidates → possible model mismatch or non-legal pretraining.
- Visualization shows dense clusters only for Japan–Korea but sparse Japan–France → threshold mismatch or real doctrinal divergence.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Schema Conversion Sanity Check:** Run converter on 10 sample JLS laws; pass every file through LegalDocML-AKN validator; manually verify 1–2 outputs in Akomantoso Viewer.
2. **Embedding Space Baseline:** Encode 100 random Japanese articles and 100 foreign articles; compute pairwise cosine similarities; confirm that intra-jurisdiction pairs score higher than random cross-jurisdiction pairs.
3. **Reranker Impact Test:** For 50 Japanese articles, compare top-5 FAISS candidates vs. top-5 after reranking; manually review 10 cases to assess whether reranker improves doctrinal alignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can a robust, expert-annotated gold-standard dataset for cross-jurisdictional legal provision correspondence be constructed and standardized for evaluation? The current prototype does not rely on large-scale expert-annotated datasets, which are needed for systematic evaluation.
2. What specific evaluation metrics and benchmarking protocols are most appropriate for assessing the quality of automatically extracted cross-jurisdictional legal correspondences, considering both semantic similarity and functional legal equivalence? There is no consensus on metrics or reproducible benchmarking.
3. To what extent does the performance drop in cross-jurisdictional mapping (F1=0.348) stem from fundamental conceptual/structural divergence between legal systems versus mere linguistic/translation variation? The relative contribution of these factors is not disentangled.
4. Can the prototype's one-to-many correspondence generation be reliably evaluated and refined to support practical comparative legal analysis? The methodological implications of many-to-many links for comparative analysis are unexplored.

## Limitations
- Cross-Encoder model specifics unknown: exact checkpoint and fine-tuning data are not disclosed, leading to potential score distribution mismatch.
- Corpus provenance unclear: precise AKN versions, extraction methods (e.g., inclusion of titles/items), and article count alignment across jurisdictions are undocumented.
- Embedding setup ambiguous: input prefix conventions (query:/passage:) for multilingual-e5-large and FAISS metric/normalization details are not specified, affecting retrieval results.
