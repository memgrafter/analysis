# Near-Optimal Regret for Policy Optimization in Contextual MDPs with General Offline Function Approximation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13706
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13706
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposes OPO‑CMDP, an optimistic policy optimization algorithm that achieves high‑probability regret $\widetilde{O}(H^4\sqrt{T|S||A|\log(|\mathcal{F}||\mathcal{P}|)})$ for contextual MDPs with offline function approximation, establishing the first optimal dependence on $|S|$ and $|A|$.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies stochastic contextual Markov decision processes (CMDPs) under general offline function approximation. The authors propose OPO‑CMDP, an optimistic policy optimization algorithm that constructs exploration bonuses based on counterfactual occupancy measures evaluated under the latest learned dynamics. OPO‑CMDP achieves a high‑probability regret bound of $\widetilde{O}(H^4\sqrt{T|S||A|\log(|\mathcal{F}||\mathcal{P}|)})$, which is the first to match the optimal dependence on the state and action space sizes without additional structural assumptions. This result demonstrates that policy optimization, when combined with proper bonus design, can be near‑optimal and computationally efficient in the offline function approximation setting.

The algorithm leverages offline regression oracles to obtain estimates of the loss and dynamics from all past data. For each encountered context, it updates all previously used policies via exponential mirror descent (OMD) with an optimistic Q‑function that includes context‑specific exploration bonuses. The bonuses are inversely proportional to the cumulative counterfactual occupancy of a state‑action pair under the current dynamics estimate, ensuring that under‑explored regions are promoted. A key technical innovation is the use of a single, up‑to‑date dynamics model to evaluate the counterfactual occupancy of all past policies, which stabilizes the bonus sequence and avoids reliance on complexity measures like Eluder dimension. The regret analysis decomposes into a Bellman error term and an OMD policy regret term, both controlled by the bonus design and realizability assumptions.

## Method Summary
OPO‑CMDP operates in episodes. At the start of episode $t$, a context $c_t$ is revealed. The algorithm first queries the offline loss and dynamics oracles to obtain estimates $\hat{f}_t$ and $\hat{P}_t$ based on all trajectories collected so far. Then, for every previous policy index $k$ ($1 \leq k \leq t$), it recomputes the policy $\pi^k_{c_t}$ that would have been used in this context at that round, using the same update rule: the policy is updated via exponential weighting on an optimistic $\hat{Q}^k$ built from the loss and dynamics estimates at round $k$, augmented with bonuses $b^k_{\beta\ell}$ and $b^k_{\beta P}$. The bonuses depend on the counterfactual occupancy of $(s,a)$ under policies $\pi^i_{c_t}$ for $i < k$, but crucially evaluated with the *latest* dynamics estimate $\hat{P}_t$. After computing the entire sequence of policies for $c_t$, the algorithm executes the current policy $\pi^t_{c_t}$ to collect a trajectory, which is added to the dataset for future oracle calls. The process repeats. The bonus parameters $\beta_\ell$ and $\beta_P$ are set to balance exploration and approximation error, and the learning rate $\eta$ is tuned to match the OMD regret bound. This design ensures that optimistic Q‑functions are sufficiently accurate and that the exploration bonuses decay as occupancy grows, leading to the claimed regret guarantee.

## Key Results
- **Regret bound:** $\widetilde{O}(H^4\sqrt{T|S||A|\log(|\mathcal{F}||\mathcal{P}|)})$ with high probability, matching lower bounds under realizability.
- **Optimal dependence:** First to achieve optimal dependence on $|S|$ and $|A|$, improving upon prior work that incurred extra factors (e.g., Eluder dimension).
- **Efficiency:** Demonstrates that policy optimization with simple bonuses is both theoretically sound and computationally feasible without extra structural assumptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

- **Optimistic Policy Updates with Counterfactual Bonuses:** For each context, OPO‑CMDP constructs an optimistic Q‑function by combining function approximation estimates with exploration bonuses. The loss estimate is pessimistically adjusted by subtracting a bonus $b_{\beta\ell}$ that is inversely proportional to the counterfactual occupancy of a state‑action pair under past policies and the latest dynamics model. Similarly, a dynamics bonus $2H b_{\beta P}$ is added during Bellman backups to encourage exploration of poorly modeled transitions. The policy is then updated via exponential weighting (mirror descent) on these Q‑values. This bonus design directly targets under‑explored regions in a context‑specific manner, driving the policy toward near‑optimal actions while maintaining sufficient exploration.

- **Bonus Stability via Unified Dynamics Evaluation:** A key innovation is evaluating all past policies’ counterfactual occupancies using the *same* up‑to‑date dynamics estimate $\hat{P}_t$. This avoids the drift that would occur if each policy were evaluated with its own time‑varying model. The analysis then shows that under realizability and sufficient visitation events, the approximated occupancies are within a constant factor of the true ones, bounding the total bonus sum by $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T|S||A|\log(|\mathcal{F}||\mathcal{P}|)})$. This eliminates the need for additional complexity assumptions such as Eluder dimension or minimum reachability.

- **Policy Optimization Regret Decomposition via OMD:** The exponential policy update corresponds exactly to an Online Mirror Descent (OMD) procedure with KL divergence. The regret decomposition yields a linear term $\Lambda$ that matches the OMD regret. Using the standard OMD inequality and the fact that Q‑estimates lie in $[0,H]$, one obtains $\Lambda \leq \sqrt{2 H^4 T \log|A|}$. The remaining Bellman error term is controlled by the accuracy of the loss and dynamics estimates, which is guaranteed by the offline oracles under realizability. Combining these yields the final bound.

- **Concept: Offline Function Approximation**
  - *Why needed:* The algorithm assumes access to finite realizable function classes $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{P}$ for losses and dynamics, and uses offline regression oracles to estimate them from collected data. This is the core generalization mechanism that avoids dependence on the context space size.

- **Concept: Occupancy Measure**
  - *Why needed:* Occupancy measures $q_h(s,a|\pi, P)$ represent the expected visitation of $(s,a)$ at layer $h$ under policy $\pi$ and dynamics $P$. They are used to define the exploration bonuses and to translate between policy performance and data coverage.
  - *Quick check:* Why is the counterfactual occupancy under the learned dynamics $\hat{P}_t^c$ used instead of the true dynamics?

- **Concept: Policy Optimization (Exponential Weighting)**
  - *Why needed:* The algorithm updates policies via exponential weighting on Q‑values, which is a classic OMD approach. Understanding this update rule is key to deriving the $\Lambda$ term bound.
  - *Quick check:* How does the learning rate $\eta$ affect the trade‑off between exploitation and exploration in this update?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Offline regression oracles → Loss/Dynamics estimates → Bonus calculator (uses counterfactual occupancy under latest dynamics) → Policy evaluation (builds optimistic Q) → Policy update (exponential OMD) → Execution. Occupancy computation propagates policies through the layered MDP.

- **Critical path** (per episode $t$):
  1. Observe context $c_t$.
  2. Compute latest estimates $\hat{f}_t$, $\hat{P}_t$ from all past data.
  3. For $k = 1$ to $t$ (all previous policies for this context):
     a. Compute bonuses $b^k_{\beta\ell}(c_t,\cdot,\cdot)$ and $b^k_{\beta P}(c_t,\cdot,\cdot)$ using occupancy sums $\sum_{i<k} q_h(\cdot|\pi^i_{c_t}, \hat{P}_t)$ and fixed $\beta$ parameters.
     b. Build optimistic loss $\hat{\ell}^k = \hat{f}^k - b^k_{\beta\ell} - 2H b^k_{\beta P}$.
     c. Compute $\hat{Q}^k$ via Bellman backup using $\hat{\ell}^k$ and $\hat{P}^k$.
     d. Update policy: $\pi^{k+1}_{c_t}(a|s) \propto \pi^k_{c_t}(a|s) \exp(-\eta \hat{Q}^k(s,a))$.
  4. Execute actions using $\pi^t_{c_t}$ and observe trajectory.
  5. Add trajectory to dataset for next episode.

- **Design tradeoffs**
  - *Bonus parameters ($\beta_\ell$, $\beta_P$)*: Larger values increase exploration but also increase regret; analysis sets $\beta = \Theta(\sqrt{T H^7 \log(|\mathcal{F}||\mathcal{P}|) / (|S||A|\log T)})$ to balance terms. Too small risks under‑exploration; too large inflates the bound.
  - *Learning rate $\eta$*: Controls OMD trade‑off; $\eta = \sqrt{2 \log|A|/(H^2 T)}$ balances the OMD bound; other values degrade regret.
  - *Recomputing all past policies per episode*: Guarantees bonus consistency but incurs $O(T)$ work per episode, leading to $O(T^2)$ total. Caching policies for each context could reduce overhead but must be updated when dynamics change; theoretical guarantee relies on using latest $\hat{P}_t$ for all bonuses.

- **Failure signatures**
  - *Diverging dynamics estimates*: If $\hat{P}_t$ becomes inaccurate (non‑realizable or poor coverage), counterfactual occupancies become unreliable, bonuses may not decay, and regret could grow super‑polynomially.
  - *Occupancy collapse*: For some $(s,a)$, if $\sum_{i<k} q_h(s,a|\pi^i_{c}, \hat{P}_t)$ stays very small, bonuses stay at 1, leading to nearly uniform policies and potentially linear regret in $|A|$.
  - *Q‑estimate instability*: Mis‑scaled bonuses ($\beta$ too large) cause overly optimistic Q‑values, making the policy chase phantom high‑reward regions and increasing Bellman error.
  - *Numerical underflow/overflow*: Exponential weighting with large Q‑values can cause extreme probabilities; careful log‑space implementation is needed.

- **First 3 experiments**
  1. *Synthetic sanity check:* Implement a small CMDP (e.g., 2 contexts, $|S|=5$, $|A|=3$, $H=3$) with linear realizable models. Verify convergence to near‑optimal policy and decay of bonuses as occupancy increases.
  2. *Ablation on $\beta$ parameters:* Vary $\beta_\ell$ and $\beta_P$ scaling factors. Plot cumulative regret and average bonus magnitude; confirm that too small $\beta$ increases regret due to under‑exploration, while too large $\beta$ inflates initial high‑bonus phase.
  3. *Comparison with baselines:* Compare against a UCRL‑style method or the previous state‑of‑the‑art (LOLIPOP) on a medium‑scale problem (e.g., $|S|=20$, $|A|=5$, $H=5$, 10 contexts). Measure regret scaling with $|S|$ and $|A|$ to validate the improved dependence; also test robustness to small violations of realizability.

## Limitations
- Realizability of function classes and perfect offline oracles is a strong assumption; in practice, misspecification may degrade performance and break theoretical guarantees.
- The algorithm recomputes all past policies for each new context, leading to $O(T^2)$ total time, which is prohibitive for large $T$.
- The analysis is restricted to layered, loop‑free CMDPs with bounded losses; extending to general MDPs remains open.

## Confidence
- **Regret optimality:** Medium (bound matches lower bounds under strong assumptions)
- **Bonus stability:** High (Lemma 4.7 provides solid analysis)
- **Computational tractability:** Low (quadratic per‑episode cost suggests poor scalability)
