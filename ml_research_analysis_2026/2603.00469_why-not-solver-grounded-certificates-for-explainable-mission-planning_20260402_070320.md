# Why Not? Solver-Grounded Certificates for Explainable Mission Planning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.00469
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00469
- **Reference Count**: 17
- **Primary Result**: Solver-grounded certificates provide sound, valid, and stable explanations for satellite scheduling decisions, significantly outperforming a post-hoc baseline in causality and completeness.

## Executive Summary
Satellite mission planning requires transparent justifications for scheduling decisions, yet existing post-hoc explanation methods often yield non-causal and incomplete attributions. This paper introduces solver-grounded certificates—explanations directly derived from the optimization model. For rejected requests, certificates are minimal infeasible subsets; for accepted requests, they include tight constraints and contrastive trade‑offs; for what‑if queries, they use inverse optimization. On a structurally challenging satellite scheduling instance, the certificates achieved perfect soundness (15/15 checks), counterfactual validity (7/7), and stability (Jaccard index 1.0 across 28 seed pairs). In contrast, a post‑hoc baseline produced non‑causal attributions in 29% of cases and missed necessary constraint conjunctions in every multi‑cause rejection. Extraction times are practical (sub‑second to ~15 seconds for typical batches).

## Method Summary
The authors extract explanations by directly interrogating the optimization solver. For a rejected satellite request, they compute a minimal infeasible subset (MIS) of constraints that becomes infeasible upon inclusion, ensuring the explanation is both sufficient and minimal. For an accepted request, they identify constraints that are tight at the optimal solution and compute contrastive trade‑offs by solving perturbed problems to determine which other requests must be sacrificed to accommodate an alternative. For what‑if scenarios, they solve an inverse optimization problem to modify constraints and obtain a new optimal solution that answers the query. All certificates are grounded in the original mathematical model, avoiding approximations.

## Key Results
- Perfect soundness (15/15) and counterfactual validity (7/7) on a challenging instance; stability measured by Jaccard index of 1.0 across 28 random seeds.
- A post‑hoc baseline yielded non‑causal attributions in 29% of cases and failed to capture necessary constraint conjunctions in all multi‑cause rejections (3/3).
- Certificate extraction is efficient: sub‑second to approximately 15 seconds for typical batches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Solver‑grounded certificates are effective because they originate from the exact optimization model and its solution, guaranteeing causal fidelity. Minimal infeasible subsets isolate the smallest set of constraints responsible for rejection, eliminating spurious influences. Tight constraints are those that actively shape the optimal solution, so they constitute the true reasons for acceptance. Contrastive trade‑offs are derived from actual re-optimization, providing concrete cost estimates. Inverse solves adjust the model consistently, yielding feasible and optimal outcomes for what‑if queries. This direct grounding avoids the approximations and potential biases of post‑hoc feature attribution methods, resulting in explanations that are both complete and minimal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Minimal Infeasible Subset (MIS)**: A smallest set of constraints that together render a problem infeasible. *Why needed*: To explain rejections with the most concise cause. *Quick check*: Removing any constraint from the MIS restores feasibility.
- **Tight Constraints**: Constraints active (binding) at the optimal solution. *Why needed*: To identify the limiting factors that determine the selected schedule. *Quick check*: Relax a tight constraint slightly and verify the objective improves.
- **Contrastive Trade‑offs**: The sacrifice required to incorporate an alternative request, computed by re‑optimizing after forcing its inclusion. *Why needed*: To show what must be given up for a different decision. *Quick check*: The new solution’s objective value should degrade by exactly the trade‑off amount.
- **Inverse Optimization**: Adjusting model parameters (e.g., weights, bounds) to achieve a desired solution or outcome. *Why needed*: To answer what‑if queries consistently. *Quick check*: The inverse‑derived parameters should make the desired solution optimal for the modified problem.
- **Solver‑Grounding**: Deriving explanations from the solver’s internal model and solution rather than from black‑box approximations. *Why needed*: To ensure explanations are mathematically faithful. *Quick check*: All constraints in the certificate must appear verbatim in the original formulation.

## Architecture Onboarding
The system operates on a satellite scheduling mixed‑integer program (MIP). After solving the original problem, a Certificate Extractor dispatches to specialized modules based on the query type: a Minimal Infeasible Subset Solver for rejections, a Constraint Analyzer for tightness and trade‑offs, and an Inverse Solver for what‑if scenarios. Extracted certificates are then formatted for the operator.

**Component Map**:  
Original MIP Model -> Commercial Solver -> Optimal Solution -> Certificate Extractor -> MIS Calculator  
Certificate Extractor -> Constraint Analyzer (tightness + trade‑offs)  
Certificate Extractor -> Inverse Solver  

**Critical Path**: The MIS calculation and inverse solves are the most computationally intensive; trade‑off analysis typically re‑uses the original solution with minor perturbations.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Minimality of MIS guarantees brevity but requires potentially expensive subset enumeration. Tight constraints are cheap to extract. Contrastive trade‑offs require additional solves but yield concrete costs. Inverse optimization may be ill‑posed if the desired outcome is unattainable; regularization may be needed.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- MIS module times out → returns a non‑minimal superset.
- Inverse solver fails to converge → certificate may be infeasible or suboptimal.
- Tolerance mismatches in tight constraint detection → spurious constraints may appear active.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. **Soundness Validation**: For each certificate type, verify mathematical properties (MIS minimality, tight constraints activity, trade‑off optimality, inverse feasibility).
2. **Scalability Profiling**: Measure extraction time as a function of problem size (variables, constraints) across a range of instances.
3. **Human Comprehension Pilot**: Conduct a small user study with domain experts to assess perceived usefulness and trust compared to standard solver reports.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None reported.

## Limitations
- Evaluation conducted on a single structurally challenging instance; applicability to diverse satellite scheduling configurations remains unverified.
- Baseline comparison uses an underspecified post‑hoc method; it is unclear how the results generalize to other explanation techniques.
- No user study with satellite operators; the actual impact on decision‑making trust and efficiency is unknown.
- Scalability to full‑mission planning instances (potentially thousands of variables/constraints) has not been demonstrated.

## Confidence
- **High**: Reported soundness (15/15), counterfactual validity (7/7), and stability (Jaccard=1.0) on the specific test instance.
- **Medium**: Claims that certificates are directly derived from the optimization model and are minimal; methodology is described but lacking formal minimality proofs.
- **Low**: Broad superiority over post‑hoc explanations in general, due to narrow baseline comparison and absence of multi‑method evaluation.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the experiments on a suite of at least 10–20 diverse satellite scheduling instances to assess consistency of soundness, validity, and extraction times.
2. Implement and compare against at least two established post‑hoc explanation methods (e.g., LIME, SHAP adaptations) using the same instance suite.
3. Generate scaled‑up instances (5–10× larger than typical batches) to profile certificate extraction time complexity and identify bottlenecks in MIS computation and inverse solving.
