# MDP Planning as Policy Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17375  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17375  
- Reference count: 9  
- Primary result: Formulating MDP planning as Bayesian inference over deterministic policies using VSMC yields superior performance and richer policy uncertainty estimates compared to discrete Soft Actor-Critic across multiple domains.

## Executive Summary
We address the problem of planning in finite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) by casting it as Bayesian inference over deterministic policies, where each policy’s unnormalized log-probability equals its expected return. To approximate the resulting posterior, we adapt Variational Sequential Monte Carlo (VSMC) for discrete domains. Key modifications are: (i) enforcing deterministic action selection per state via first-visit caching, and (ii) coupling transition randomness across particles through a shared transition tensor, so that particle weights differ only due to policy behavior. The resulting variational objective is optimized with Adam, and actions are selected by posterior predictive sampling—equivalent to Thompson sampling over deterministic policies. Empirical evaluations on grid worlds, Blackjack, Triangle Tireworld, and Academic Advising demonstrate that VSMC captures multimodal policy distributions, avoids entropy-seeking boundary actions, achieves higher returns in Blackjack, eliminates timeouts in Triangle Tireworld, and preserves heavy-tailed uncertainty under long horizons, contrasting with discrete Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).

## Method Summary
The method starts by defining a probabilistic model: for any deterministic policy π, its unnormalized log-density is set to the Monte Carlo estimate of expected return from a single trajectory (Eq. 7). The true posterior over policies is intractable; we approximate it with a learned proposal distribution q(a|s) (a neural network). To estimate the marginal likelihood and train q, we employ a modified Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) sweep over a fixed number of particles. Each particle traverses the MDP; on its first visit to a state s, it draws an action a from q(·|s) and caches it; subsequent visits reuse the cached a, ensuring the policy induced by each particle is deterministic. Transitions are made stochastic by drawing a successor state from the environment dynamics; crucially, for a given (s,a,visit count) we sample a single transition and share it across all particles, so that differences in particle weights arise solely from policy differences (actions). After the horizon, we compute importance weights w(i) = R + log(1/|A|) - log q(a|s) (including a uniform prior on deterministic policies). The marginal likelihood estimator log Ẑ is the log-average exponentiated weight. The objective is a stratified ELBO: L = log Ẑ + stop_gradient(log Ẑ) · Σ_{first visits} log q(a|s). This is optimized by stochastic gradient ascent on q’s parameters. At test time, actions are sampled from q(·|s) independently (posterior predictive), yielding a stochastic policy that represents Thompson sampling over deterministic policies. The approach is evaluated on discrete MDPs and compared to a discrete implementation of Soft Actor-Critic.

## Key Results
- VSMC produces multimodal policy distributions in grid worlds and avoids the boundary-directed entropy-seeking actions observed in SAC; removing shared transition randomness causes “optimistic” incorrect turns.
- In Blackjack, VSMC attains a higher average return (−0.19) than discrete SAC with the default entropy weight (−0.32).
- In Triangle Tireworld, VSMC completes all instances without timeouts, whereas SAC’s timeout rate increases with problem size.
- In Academic Advising, VSMC yields heavier-tailed return distributions, indicating preserved policy-level uncertainty even under long planning horizons.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MDP planning can be treated as Bayesian inference over deterministic policies, with policy optimality proportional to expected return.
- **Mechanism:** Define an unnormalized log probability for a deterministic policy π as the Monte Carlo estimate of its expected return from a single trajectory (Eq. 8). This makes the posterior over policies concentrate on high-return policies, with dispersion indicating policy-level uncertainty.
- **Core assumption:** The expected return is a sufficient statistic for policy optimality; uncertainty is epistemic and resides at the policy level, not in action stochasticity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "formulating it as Bayesian inference over policies, treating each deterministic policy as a latent variable with unnormalized log probability equal to its expected return."
  - [section 3] Eq. (7) formalizes log ˜p(π) = E_{τ^π}[∑ R].
  - [corpus] Weak—neighbors discuss uncertainty in MDPs but not this specific latent policy formulation.
- **Break condition:** If reward scaling is mis-specified, posterior concentration may not align with true optimal policies; the mapping from return to log-prob is monotonic but not necessarily calibrated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Variational Sequential Monte Carlo (VSMC) can be adapted to approximate the posterior over deterministic policies in discrete MDPs despite stochastic dynamics and noisy return estimates.
- **Mechanism:** Modify the SMC sweep to (i) enforce deterministic action selection per state by caching actions on first visit, and (ii) couple transition randomness across particles via a shared transition tensor ˆT, so that particle weights reflect policy differences, not independent transition noise.
- **Core assumption:** The state space is countable (for revisit detection) and action space finite; policies are deterministic to isolate policy-level uncertainty.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "enforcing deterministic action selection per state and coupling transition randomness across particles to weight policies by return alone."
  - [section 4.1] Describes the two adjustments in detail (lines 14–24 and 27–32 of Algorithm 1).
  - [corpus] Weak—neighbors use SMC/Variational methods for planning but not this exact coupling/determinism scheme.
- **Break condition:** If the state space is uncountable or transitions highly deterministic, the caching mechanism fails; shared dynamics require synchronizable RNGs across particles.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Acting via posterior predictive sampling yields a stochastic control policy that represents optimal behavior under preference uncertainty, differing fundamentally from entropy-regularized policies.
- **Mechanism:** At each step, sample an action from the variational posterior q(a|s) (Algorithm 3). This is equivalent to Thompson sampling over deterministic policies, where stochasticity arises from marginalizing over posterior policy modes, not from an intrinsic entropy term.
- **Core assumption:** The posterior q approximates the true policy posterior; reward scale encodes certainty over preferences, so mixed policy optimality depends on return separation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Acting used posterior predictive sampling, yielding stochastic behavior via Thompson sampling instead of entropy regularization."
  - [section 4.2.3] Explicitly connects posterior predictive sampling to Thompson sampling and contrasts with entropy regularization.
  - [section 7] "stochasticity here reflects uncertainty about which deterministic behavior is optimal."
- **Break condition:** If q is underdispersed (e.g., due to poor variational fit), the mixed policy will be overconfident and may ignore viable high-return deterministic policies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Bayesian inference over latent variables**  
  - *Why needed:* The framework treats policies as latent variables with density proportional to return; inference approximates the posterior over policies.  
  - *Quick check:* Why is treating the policy (rather than trajectories) as latent key for preserving the original MDP objective?
- **Concept: Variational Sequential Monte Carlo (VSMC)**  
  - *Why needed:* VSMC provides a multi-particle variational objective that handles sequential return estimation and noisy log-density estimates.  
  - *Quick check:* How does coupling transition randomness across particles prevent confounding in policy weight estimates?
- **Concept: Deterministic vs. stochastic policy representations**  
  - *Why needed:* The method infers over deterministic policies to separate epistemic uncertainty (policy-level) from aleatoric noise (dynamics), avoiding conflated randomness.  
  - *Quick check:* What is the behavioral difference between a policy sampled from the posterior (posterior predictive) and a single MAP policy extracted via annealing?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Probabilistic model (log-prob = expected return) -> VSMC sweep (first-visit caching, shared transition tensor, resampling, weight computation) -> Objective (stratified ELBO) -> Policy extraction (MAP via annealing or posterior predictive sampling).
- **Critical path:** Model specification (Eq. 7) → VSMC adaptation (Algorithm 1) → Optimization (Theorem 1) → Policy selection (Section 4.2).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Deterministic policy space gives clean interpretation but is unforgiving to early suboptimal actions; stochastic memoization is a relaxation.  
  - Shared dynamics correct weighting but requires synchronized RNG; independent dynamics yield “optimistic” inference.  
  - Annealing finds a high-return mode but discards uncertainty; sampling preserves multimodality but acts stochastically.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High variance across runs (blurry policy beams) → insufficient particles or poor proposal.  
  - Mode collapse to low-return policies (VSA) → single-particle objective.  
  - Increasing timeout rates in Triangle Tireworld with SAC but not VSMC → entropy regularization may prefer lingering over decisive risk management.  
  - Heavy-tailed return distributions in Academic Advising → posterior uncertainty over long-horizon policies, but may indicate slow convergence.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Grid worlds: Visualize policy multimodality and effect of removing shared dynamics (independent transitions cause “optimistic” wrong turns into swamps).  
  2. Blackjack: Compare outcome probabilities to known optimal policy; VSMC achieves higher return than SAC with default entropy weight.  
  3. Triangle Tireworld: Inspect timeout probabilities; VSMC has zero timeouts while SAC shows increasing timeouts on larger instances.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling to continuous state spaces:** The framework is restricted to discrete domains due to caching and exact revisit detection; it is unclear how approximations (e.g., discretization, hashing) affect the core semantics of deterministic memoization and shared transition coupling.
2. **Comparison to entropy-regularized RL:** The paper shows qualitative differences but lacks a systematic analysis linking MDP properties (reward sparsity, transition stochasticity, horizon) to when VSMC (policy-level uncertainty) outperforms SAC (entropy regularization), especially regarding risk-sensitive outcomes and robustness to timeouts.
3. **Principled reward scaling:** The reward scale λ (implicit in log ˜p(π) = λ·E[return]) encodes preference certainty, but no guidance is provided for choosing λ in new domains. Its interaction with horizon length and environmental stochasticity remains unexplored.
4. **Relaxing deterministic policy enforcement:** Strict determinism makes early suboptimal actions catastrophic. A proposed stochastic memoization (occasionally resampling on revisits) might improve robustness but its impact on posterior fidelity and multimodal policy capture is unknown.
5. **Particle count and computational trade-offs:** The paper uses N=10 particles without ablation. In large combinatorial domains (e.g., Academic Advising), the effect of particle count on approximation quality and the Pareto frontier between particles, iterations, and performance are not studied.

## Limitations
- Domain specifications for Blackjack, Triangle Tireworld, and Academic Advising are largely omitted, hindering faithful replication.  
- Key hyperparameters (learning rate schedules, resampling thresholds, SAC baseline settings) are not fully disclosed, potentially affecting empirical conclusions.  
- The method assumes discrete states and deterministic policies; scaling to continuous or large spaces may require approximations that alter the theoretical guarantees.
