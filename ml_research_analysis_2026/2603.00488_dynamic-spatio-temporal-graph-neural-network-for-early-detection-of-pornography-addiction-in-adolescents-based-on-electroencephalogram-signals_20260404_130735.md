# Dynamic Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Network for Early Detection of Pornography Addiction in Adolescents Based on Electroencephalogram Signals

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00488
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00488
- **Reference Count:** 6
- **Primary Result:** A DST-GNN model using PLI-based graphs and BiGRU achieved an F1-Score of 71.00%±12.10% and 85.71% recall in LOSO-CV, a 104% improvement over conventional machine learning baselines.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the need for objective, neurobiological detection of adolescent pornography addiction to overcome reliance on biased self-reports. The authors develop a Dynamic Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Network (DST-GNN) that processes 19-channel EEG signals by constructing spatial graphs from Phase Lag Index (PLI) connectivity and modeling their temporal evolution with a Bidirectional GRU. In a challenging Leave-One-Subject-Out Cross-Validation on a small dataset (N=14), the model significantly outperforms traditional classifiers. An ablation study confirms that both the PLI-based graph structure and temporal dynamics are critical contributors. Explainability analysis points to frontal-central brain regions (e.g., Cz) and Beta band activity as potential objective biomarkers.

## Method Summary
The study uses a public EEG dataset from 14 adolescents (7 with pornography addiction, 7 healthy controls). Raw EEG recordings are preprocessed with bandpass (0.5–45 Hz) and notch filters, then z-score normalized. Each subject's data is segmented into 30 non-overlapping 20-second windows. For each window, node features (9 per channel: 5 PSD bands + 4 Hjorth parameters) are extracted, and a spatial graph is built using the Phase Lag Index (PLI) between all channel pairs, with edges thresholded at the 50th percentile. This sequence of 30 graph snapshots is processed by a two-layer Graph Attention Network (GAT) for spatial feature enrichment, followed by a two-layer Bidirectional GRU to capture temporal dependencies. The final hidden state is classified by an MLP. Model selection and training use subject-level LOSO-CV with hyperparameter optimization via Optuna.

## Key Results
- The DST-GNN achieved an F1-Score of **71.00% ± 12.10%** and a **recall of 85.71%**, improving upon conventional machine learning baselines (LR, SVM, RF, XGBoost, MLP) by approximately **104%** in F1-Score.
- An ablation study quantified contributions: removing temporal dynamics (BiGRU) caused a **21%** absolute F1-Score drop, while replacing the PLI-based graph with a fully-connected graph caused a **57%** drop.
- Explainability analysis identified the **Beta frequency band** (58.9% contribution) and key channels/connections—**Fz, Cz, C3, C4** and **Cz-T7**—as the most discriminative neural biomarkers for addiction status.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### PLI-Based Graph Construction Provides Robust Spatial Representation
The Phase Lag Index (PLI) quantifies the consistency of phase differences between EEG channel pairs, creating a weighted adjacency matrix that is less sensitive to volume conduction artifacts than coherence. This graph structure feeds into a Graph Attention Network (GAT), which learns to weigh the importance of different connections for the classification task. The core assumption is that functional connectivity patterns, as captured by PLI, differ consistently between addicted and non-addicted adolescents and are spatially localized. Evidence includes the paper's explicit use of PLI for robustness [section, Page 7], and the ablation study showing a 57% F1-Score drop when replacing the PLI graph with a fully-connected graph, confirming its critical role. The break condition is if underlying connectivity patterns are too noisy or non-discriminative, causing performance to collapse toward baseline.

### Bidirectional GRU Captures Essential Temporal Dynamics
The sequence of graph snapshots is fed into a two-layer Bidirectional GRU (BiGRU). This recurrent architecture processes the sequence both forward and backward, learning temporal dependencies and state transitions in the functional connectivity network that static feature methods cannot capture. The core assumption is that the brain's connectivity state during/after pornographic stimulus exposure shows a characteristic, group-differentiating temporal progression. Evidence includes the paper's framing of dynamic connectivity as key [section, Page 2] and the ablation study showing a 21% absolute F1-Score decrease when removing the BiGRU. The break condition is if discriminative information is contained wholly within single static windows; then the BiGRU adds negligible value.

### Graph Attention Network Adaptively Focuses on Discriminative Connections
Within each GAT layer, attention coefficients are computed for neighbor pairs, determining how much influence one node has on another's updated representation. This allows the model to learn which specific PLI-derived connections are most salient for distinguishing groups, rather than treating all edges equally. The core assumption is that a subset of connections and brain regions carries the most signal. Evidence comes from the explainability analysis identifying specific channels (Fz, Cz, C3, C4) and connections (Cz-T7) as most important [section, Page 10-11], suggesting the attention mechanism may have learned to weight these highly. The break condition is if optimal classification can be made with uniformly weighted connections or if graph noise prevents stable attention learning.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 1. Input (Raw EEG) -> 2. Feature & Graph Construction (PSD, Hjorth, PLI adjacency) -> 3. Spatial Encoder (2× GAT layers) -> 4. Temporal Encoder (2-layer BiGRU) -> 5. Classifier (MLP with dropout)
- **Critical path:** Raw EEG → Bandpass/Notch/Filter → Z-score normalize → Windowing (2s, 30 windows) → Compute PSD/Hjorth features & PLI matrix per window → Threshold PLI for adjacency → Process graph sequence through GAT → Apply BiGRU over GAT outputs → Classify final hidden state → LOSO-CV evaluation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Graph Construction:* PLI is chosen for robustness to volume conduction but may miss zero-lag synchrony; wPLI is more noise-robust but slightly heavier. The 57% ablation drop on fully-connected graphs strongly favors a connectivity-based, not all-to-all, structure.
  - *Temporal Resolution:* 2s windows (30 per 60s task) trade off capturing sustained patterns vs. transient dynamics; this choice is data-driven but not deeply analyzed.
  - *Validation Strategy:* LOSO-CV is essential for subject-independent generalization given tiny N=14, but it inherently increases performance variance (as seen in ±12.1% F1). A random split would be overly optimistic.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Spatial Component Failure:* Model performance similar to fully-connected graph ablation (F1 ~14%) indicates PLI matrices are noise or non-informative for this task.
  - *Temporal Component Failure:* Performance similar to spatial-only ablation (F1 ~50%) suggests no useful temporal dynamics or BiGRU failure to learn them.
  - *Generalization Failure:* Very high variance across LOSO folds/seeds or ROC-AUC near 0.5 on certain seeds suggests overfitting to subject idiosyncrasies due to tiny dataset.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Replication:** Implement and run the 5 listed baselines (LR, SVM, RF, XGBoost, MLP) using the exact same feature set (PSD + Hjorth + correlation) and LOSO-CV protocol. Confirm baseline F1 scores (~20-35%) as reported.
  2. **Ablation Validation:** Systematically remove/replace DST-GNN components: (a) Remove BiGRU (spatial-only). (b) Replace PLI adjacency with all-ones (fully-connected). (c) Replace GAT with GCN. Quantify F1 change to corroborate the 21% (temporal) and 57% (PLI graph) contributions.
  3. **Explainability Reproduction:** Using the trained final model, compute Integrated Gradients for node features and gradient-based edge importance. Reproduce channel/feature importance and edge importance matrices to verify frontal-central dominance and Cz-T7 as top biomarkers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
How does the DST-GNN model generalize to larger, more demographically and culturally diverse adolescent populations beyond the current small, single-center Indonesian sample (N=14)? The authors explicitly state that the small sample size limits generalizability and causes high variability, and that cross-cultural validation is needed. This remains unresolved because reported performance shows substantial variance and the sample is homogenous; replication on independent, larger multi-regional datasets is required.

Can the identified trait-level biomarker (consistent Cz-T7 connectivity) be robustly detected in passive, stimulus-free EEG recordings, enabling screening without requiring exposure to experimental pornographic stimuli? The authors identify Cz-T7 as a potential trait biomarker observed across conditions, but all conditions involve structured tasks. It is unresolved whether this pattern is present during resting-state or naturalistic, unmonitored behavior, which is essential for practical screening; validation with resting-state EEG is needed.

To what extent are the identified biomarkers (frontal-central regions, Beta band dominance) specific to pornography addiction versus common to other impulse-control or behavioral addictions (e.g., gaming, gambling)? The paper focuses exclusively on pornography addiction and does not address disorder specificity. Without cross-disorder comparisons, it is unclear if the signature is unique or reflects a general pathological pattern of reward dysregulation; applying the model to datasets from other behavioral addictions would resolve this.

What architectural or data-processing modifications are necessary to reduce the high performance variability (F1-Score SD ±12.10%) and improve model robustness, given the constraints of a small-sample dataset? The authors acknowledge this as a limitation but do not explore mitigation strategies. Systematic studies on data augmentation, alternative validation schemes, or model simplification are needed to improve reliability for clinical use.

Can the proposed DST-GNN framework be efficiently deployed in real-time or near-real-time settings for practical screening, and what are its computational and latency requirements? The limitation states the model has not been validated on real-time data and was trained offline on a high-end GPU. The computational complexity of PLI-based graph construction and BiGRU processing may present bottlenecks; benchmarking on edge hardware and exploring model compression are required.

## Limitations
- **Small, homogenous dataset:** The study uses only 14 adolescents from a single center, limiting statistical power and generalizability; high performance variance (±12.1% F1) indicates instability.
- **Incomplete implementation details:** Exact windowing protocol (contiguous vs. condition-separated) and full GAT hyperparameters (activation, pooling) are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- **Fragile biomarker interpretation:** Explainability results (frontal-central, Beta band) are derived from a small model and may not generalize; they rely on post-hoc methods that can be unstable with limited data.
