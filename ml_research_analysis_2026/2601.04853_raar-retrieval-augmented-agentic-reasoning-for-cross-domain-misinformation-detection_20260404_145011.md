# RAAR: Retrieval Augmented Agentic Reasoning for Cross-Domain Misinformation Detection

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.04853  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04853  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** RAAR-8b and RAAR-14b achieve average macro F1 scores of 0.689 and 0.712 on AMTCele, PHEME, and COCO, substantially outperforming base Qwen3 models and advanced LLMs such as GPT-4.1 (0.696) and DeepSeek-V3.2-Reasoner (0.679).

## Executive Summary
RAAR introduces a novel retrieval‑augmented agentic reasoning framework for cross‑domain misinformation detection. The system retrieves source‑domain evidence aligned with the target sample’s semantics, sentiment, and writing style, and then orchestrates multiple specialist agents to construct verifiable reasoning paths. After supervised fine‑tuning and reinforcement learning (GRPO) on Qwen3 models, RAAR‑8b and RAAR‑14b achieve state‑of‑the‑art performance on three benchmark datasets, surpassing larger LLMs and prior adaptation methods.

## Method Summary
RAAR operates in three stages. First, for each target sample it retrieves the top‑2 most similar source samples per perspective (semantics via RoBERTa, sentiment via EmoLLama, style via StyleEmb) and builds an augmented input `x_ra`. Second, a strong LLM (e.g., DeepSeek‑v3.2‑chat) runs three specialized sub‑agents (Sentiment, Semantic, Style) that independently analyze `x_ra`; a Summary Agent integrates their outputs, and a Verifier checks answer correctness. If needed, iterative strategies (double‑check, communication, consolidation, etc.) are applied for up to three rounds, after which a natural‑language CoT and answer are produced. Third, the collected `(x_ra, CoT, answer)` pairs first supervise fine‑tuning (SFT) of a Qwen3 model, instilling the reasoning structure. Then Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) refines the model on a separate set, rewarding both accuracy and strict output format. The final RAAR model is a single, efficient LLM ready for deployment.

## Key Results
- RAAR‑8b (0.689) and RAAR‑14b (0.712) achieve the highest average macro F1 across AMTCele, PHEME, and COCO, beating GPT‑4.1 (0.696) and DeepSeek‑V3.2‑Reasoner (0.679).  
- RAAR substantially improves over the base Qwen3 models, confirming the framework’s effectiveness.  
- RAAR outperforms prior cross‑domain and adaptation methods, establishing a new state‑of‑the‑art.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RAAR’s success stems from three synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Triple‑perspective retrieval** – For a target sample, RAAR retrieves source‑domain examples that are semantically similar (RoBERTa), share emotional tone (EmoLLama), and match writing style (StyleEmb). These retrieved triples serve as in‑context demonstrations that expose the model to transferable latent structures. Statistical validation on AMTCele shows that within‑label cosine similarities are significantly higher than between‑label (p < 0.05), confirming that alignment correlates with label agreement. If source and target distributions are orthogonal across all three perspectives, retrieval may become uninformative or even harmful.

2. **Multi‑agent collaboration** – Three specialized sub‑agents (Sentiment, Semantic, Style) initially analyze the retrieved set. Their outputs are combined by a Summary Agent under the guidance of a Verifier that checks answer correctness. When the answer is wrong, iterative strategies (sub‑agent double‑check or communication; summary‑agent consolidation, reconsideration, diversification, verification, or rectification) are invoked for up to three rounds. This orchestration leverages complementary evidence and mitigates single‑perspective bias. Ablation studies (Table 2) demonstrate each agent’s contribution: removing the Style agent drops F1 on PHEME from 0.528 to 0.335, indicating its critical role in rumor detection. Potential failure occurs if agents produce persistent conflicts or the summary agent consistently ignores one perspective.

3. **Two‑stage optimization (SFT + GRPO‑RL)** – The multi‑agent process generates (CoT, answer) pairs that are first used for supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) of Qwen3‑8B/14B, instilling a reasoning prior. Then Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) refines the model on a separate set, with a reward combining format compliance and accuracy (1 for correct, 0.1 for incorrect, 0 for none). Ablations show that removing RL (“w/o RL”) reduces F1 by 0.01, while merely increasing SFT data (“SFT+Full”) does not help, highlighting RL’s specific benefit. GRPO also outperforms PPO, likely due to its memory‑efficient group updates. Misaligned rewards could encourage shortcuts; format reward alone cannot guarantee genuine reasoning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
- Retrieval module: three embedding models (EmoLLama, RoBERTa, StyleEmb) compute similarity and construct `x_ra`.  
- Reasoning agents: Sentiment, Semantic, Style sub‑agents → Summary Agent → Verifier, producing CoT and answer.  
- Training pipeline: SFT dataset builder (from `D_search`), SFT trainer, RL trainer (GRPO) using `D_RL` → final RAAR model.

**Critical path:**  
1. Input target sample `x_t`.  
2. Stage 1 retrieval → `x_ra = {target text, retrieved triples per perspective}`.  
3. Stage 2: sub‑agents analyze retrieved triples; summary agent integrates; verifier checks; iterative strategies (max N=3) if needed; final CoT and answer.  
4. Stage 3: SFT on `D_search` (`x_ra`, CoT, answer) → coarse reasoning model.  
5. Stage 4: GRPO‑RL on `D_RL` (`x_ra`, true_label) with accuracy and format rewards → refined RAAR model.  
6. Deployment: base LLM replaced by RAAR‑fine‑tuned model for inference.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Retrieval: Using three perspectives increases coverage but adds latency and memory; top‑k=2 balances signal vs. noise.  
- Agents: Fixed trio simplifies design but may miss other factors (e.g., stance); iteration depth N=3 limits reasoning steps versus time cost.  
- Optimization: SFT before RL avoids catastrophic forgetting; GRPO chosen for memory efficiency and group‑level updates over PPO.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Retrieval misalignment: low‑similarity examples → irrelevant context → reasoning drift.  
- Agent entanglement: summary agent consistently overlooks one sub‑agent, causing under‑use of that perspective.  
- RL reward hacking: model places correct label in first sentence without meaningful CoT, exploiting format reward.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Retrieval necessity:** Comparing Reason_zs (no retrieval) vs. RAAR‑8b and removing RA (random few‑shot) showed Reason_zs F1 = 0.622, RAAR‑8b = 0.689, confirming retrieval value.  
2. **Agent contribution:** Ablating each agent revealed drops: w/o sentiment on AMTCele (0.812 → 0.790); w/o style on PHEME (0.528 → 0.335), demonstrating each perspective’s importance.  
3. **Optimization impact:** “w/o RL” (SFT only) gave F1 = 0.679 vs. RAAR‑8b = 0.689; “SFT+Full” (more SFT data) gave 0.674, showing RL’s benefit and SFT overfitting risk.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How does scaling to larger LLM architectures (beyond 14B) affect RAAR’s cross‑domain misinformation detection performance?  
  *Basis:* Section 6 Limitations states computational constraints limited training to 8B/14B models.  
  *Why unresolved:* Larger models could improve reasoning and generalization but may also introduce diminishing returns or new challenges.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Train RAAR on significantly larger models (32B, 70B, or proprietary) using the same pipeline and compare performance on the three datasets.

- **Question 2:** Can incorporating additional features such as stance, argumentation structure, and topical information improve RAAR’s reasoning and detection accuracy?  
  *Basis:* Section 6 notes many potentially useful features were not analyzed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Unknown whether these dimensions provide complementary signals or introduce noise/redundancy.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Extend retrieval to include embeddings/models for stance, argument mining, and topic modeling; ablate individually and in combination; measure F1 changes and analyze errors.

- **Question 3:** How can the Summary Agent be improved to ensure more balanced integration of all retrieved perspectives?  
  *Basis:* Some cases show reasoning primarily concentrates on sentiment, semantics, style, lacking comprehensiveness.  
  *Why unresolved:* Multi‑agent design may suffer from weighting or attentional bias; summary agent might default to most salient cues.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Analyze summary agent’s prompts or internal representations to diagnose biases; experiment with modified prompts emphasizing equal consideration or replace with a trainable aggregator (e.g., attention over agent outputs); compare reasoning paths and accuracy.

- **Question 4:** How can RAAR be effectively adapted to multilingual and multimodal misinformation detection?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion mentions future enhancement to multilingual and multimodal settings.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current framework is text‑only and English‑focused, limiting real‑world applicability.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Extend retrieval to multilingual embeddings (LaBSE, multilingual BERT) and multimodal embeddings (CLIP); use or create multilingual/multimodal cross‑domain datasets; evaluate adapted RAAR on these benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Proprietary/unreleased components (EmoLLama, StyleEmb, DeepSeek‑v3.2‑chat) hinder exact replication and may affect retrieval quality.  
- Dataset splits are not provided; reconstructed splits could introduce variance and affect performance comparability.  
- Multi‑agent orchestration details (verifier prompts, diversity enforcement) and GRPO hyperparameters are underspecified, leading to potential implementation divergence and training instability.
