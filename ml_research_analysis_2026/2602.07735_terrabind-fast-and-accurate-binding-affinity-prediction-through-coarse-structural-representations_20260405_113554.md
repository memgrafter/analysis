# TerraBind: Fast and Accurate Binding Affinity Prediction through Coarse Structural Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07735
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07735
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TerraBind achieves a 26-fold speedup over diffusion-based Boltz-2 (1.04s vs 27.8s per complex) while matching its pose accuracy and improving binding affinity prediction Pearson correlation by ~20%, with well-calibrated uncertainty estimates enabling efficient continual learning.

## Executive Summary
TerraBind rethinks diffusion-based protein-ligand co-folding by demonstrating that full all-atom resolution is unnecessary for accurate pose and affinity prediction. It employs a coarse-grained representation (protein Cβ atoms, ligand heavy atoms) with frozen COATI-3 and ESM-2 encoders and a lean Pairformer trunk to predict pairwise distance distributions directly. This eliminates the computational bottleneck of iterative diffusion, enabling fast inference. The model matches state-of-the-art pose accuracy on multiple benchmarks and significantly improves affinity prediction correlation. Crucially, it provides calibrated uncertainty estimates via an epistemic neural network (epinet), which powers a hedged batch selection strategy for continual learning, achieving 6× greater affinity improvement in simulated drug discovery cycles compared to greedy selection.

## Method Summary
TerraBind uses a coarse-grained input representation: protein residues are represented by Cβ atoms and ligands by heavy atoms. Pretrained encoders (ESM-2 for protein sequences, COATI-3 for ligands) generate initial embeddings, which are kept frozen to preserve generalization. A 48-layer Pairformer trunk processes these embeddings to predict categorical pairwise distance distributions (distograms) between ligand atoms and protein residues. These distograms are used in two ways: (1) an optional gradient-based pose optimization generates coarse 3D coordinates without learned generative steps, and (2) a separate 6-layer affinity Pairformer directly consumes the structural Pairformer’s pairwise latents and distance bin probabilities, along with the frozen global embeddings, to predict binding likelihood and continuous affinity. An epinet samples from a latent-space posterior to provide well-calibrated uncertainty estimates. The model is trained in three stages: first on all interfaces, then on ligand-centered contexts with upweighted losses, and finally on high-quality PDB data. The affinity module is trained on stop-gradient structural embeddings using combined Huber and focal losses.

## Key Results
- **Speed:** 26-fold faster inference than Boltz-2 (1.04s vs 27.8s per complex).
- **Pose accuracy:** Matches diffusion-based models (Boltz-2) on FoldBench, PoseBusters, and Runs N’ Poses (e.g., ~60% of complexes with ligand RMSD < 2Å).
- **Affinity prediction:** Improves Pearson correlation by ~20% over Boltz-2 on CASP16 and a diverse proprietary dataset (18 assays), with well-calibrated uncertainty estimates enabling effective batch selection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Coarse-Grained Sufficiency
- **Claim:** Full all-atom diffusion is unnecessary; coarse-grained representations (Cβ atoms, ligand heavy atoms) suffice for accurate pose and affinity prediction.
- **Mechanism:** The Pairformer trunk predicts categorical pairwise distance distributions directly. These are used for affinity prediction and simple coordinate optimization, bypassing expensive diffusion steps.
- **Evidence:** Matches Boltz-1 pose accuracy (RMSD < 2Å) despite no diffusion; distograms capture essential geometric/chemical information.
- **Break condition:** Significant affinity degradation on highly flexible binding sites or water-mediated interactions.

### Mechanism 2: Efficient Frozen Encoders + Lean Pairformer
- **Claim:** Frozen pretrained encoders (COATI-3, ESM-2) with a streamlined Pairformer provide rich, transferable representations efficiently.
- **Mechanism:** Encoders supply initial atom- and residue-level embeddings. Pairformer (48 layers, 27M params) predicts distograms. Frozen weights avoid costly retraining and leverage vast pretraining data.
- **Evidence:** Parameter reduction from 147M (Boltz-2) to 27M; trunk latency reduced from 1.53s to 0.87s.
- **Break condition:** Encoder embeddings misalign for novel ligand chemistries or protein folds, causing representation collapse.

### Mechanism 3: Affinity from Distograms + Epistemic Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Affinity can be predicted directly from distogram probabilities and embeddings without coordinate generation; epinet provides calibrated uncertainty for decision-making.
- **Mechanism:** Affinity Pairformer (6 layers) conditions on structural Pairformer outputs (pairwise latents, distance bin probs, COATI-3/ESM-2 embeddings). Epinet samples latent-space residuals to yield posterior distributions.
- **Evidence:** Outperforms Boltz-2 on affinity; epinet IQR correlates with success rate (within 1 pIC50); EMAX batch selection yields 6× IC50 improvement vs. greedy.
- **Break condition:** Epinet becomes miscalibrated out-of-distribution, leading to overconfident or inaccurate uncertainty estimates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion-based co-folding:** State-of-the-art (e.g., Boltz-2) uses iterative diffusion to generate all-atom structures, which is computationally expensive. *Why needed:* Understands the bottleneck TerraBind eliminates. *Quick check:* What makes diffusion slow, and why doesn't scaling help?
- **Contrastive multimodal pretraining (COATI-3):** COATI-3 aligns SMILES, 2D graphs, and 3D point clouds via contrastive learning. *Why needed:* Provides robust ligand embeddings without all-atom refinement. *Quick check:* How does multimodal pretraining improve generalization to unseen ligands compared to PDB-only training?
- **Pairformer architecture:** Uses triangle attention and multiplication to encode pairwise geometric constraints without a single-sequence track. *Why needed:* Core module for distogram prediction in a lean architecture. *Quick check:* Why can Pairformer learn binding geometry without diffusion, whereas AlphaFold3 still uses a diffusion head?
- **Epistemic neural networks (epinet):** Samples epistemic indices from a unit Gaussian and adds a prior network to approximate a posterior over model uncertainties. *Why needed:* Provides calibrated uncertainty without retraining for new data. *Quick check:* How does sampling epistemic indices yield a posterior distribution over affinities?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (Protein sequence, Ligand SMILES/2D/3D) → Frozen encoders (ESM-2, COATI-3) → Structure Pairformer (48 layers) → Distograms (distance bin logits) → [Affinity Pairformer (6 layers) → Affinity prediction ± uncertainty; Pose module (gradient optimization) → Coarse 3D coordinates].

**Critical path (inference):** Protein sequence → ESM-2 → residue embeddings; Ligand → COATI-3 → atom embeddings + global embedding → Pairformer → distograms → affinity module → ŷ ± uncertainty (or optional pose optimization).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Coarse-grained vs. all-atom: 26× speed gain; loses all-atom detail, may limit free energy calculations.
- Frozen encoders: Preserve generalization, reduce training cost; may not adapt to target-specific quirks.
- No diffusion: Eliminates iterative latency; relies on Pairformer distogram quality; lower pose diversity.
- Stop-gradient: Modular training; structural improvements require retraining Pairformer, not just affinity head.

**Failure signatures:**
- High ligand-protein interface entropy (HLP > 0.7) → unreliable pose/affinity.
- Poor epinet calibration: IQR does not correlate with success rate (within 1 pIC50).
- Pocket cropping truncates key residues → context loss; monitor token counts (<200 typical).
- Chemical space shift: Novel ligands far from COATI-3 pretraining → degraded embeddings.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Structure validation:** Run TerraBind Pocket on FoldBench/PoseBusters; compute ligand RMSD < 2Å and LDDT-PLI > 0.8. Expect ~60% success.
2. **Affinity correlation:** Predict on CASP16 L3000 and proprietary assays; compute Pearson r. Expect ~0.63 on proprietary (vs Boltz-2 ~0.51).
3. **Uncertainty calibration:** Plot epinet IQR (marginal) vs. prediction success (|error| < 1 pIC50). Expect monotonic increase in success with decreasing IQR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Coarse-grained failure modes:** For which protein-ligand systems (e.g., highly flexible binding sites, water-mediated interactions) does coarse-grained representation fail, necessitating all-atom detail? *Basis:* Limitations section notes unverified for flexible/macrocyclic ligands.
- **High interface entropy:** Does high ligand-protein entropy for strong binders reflect a chemical space gap, and can synthetic data expansion reduce this uncertainty and improve affinity? *Basis:* Limitations cite pervasive failure across ligand chemical space and potential of synthetic data.
- **Epinet uncertainty shape:** How can uncertainty quantification be refined beyond Gaussian marginals to produce well-calibrated, non-Gaussian predictive distributions that better reflect real-world false-positive rates? *Basis:* Limitations note Gaussian marginals are unrealistically optimistic.
- **Assay-specific context:** Can assay type (biochemical vs. cell-based) be integrated to reduce noise from heterogeneous experimental data? *Basis:* Limitations highlight lack of conditioning on assay context introduces artifacts.

## Limitations
- Coarse-grained representation unverified for highly flexible binding sites or water-mediated interactions; affinity may degrade without all-atom refinement.
- Frozen encoders (COATI-3, ESM-2) may misalign for novel ligand chemistries or protein folds outside pretraining, risking representation collapse.
- Epinet uncertainty likely miscalibrated out-of-distribution; Gaussian marginals are overly optimistic for lower affinity ranges.

## Confidence
- **Pose accuracy matching diffusion models:** High (validated on multiple rigid benchmarks).
- **Affinity correlation improvement (~20% over Boltz-2):** Medium (strong on proprietary assays but dataset curation opaque).
- **Well-calibrated uncertainty for batch selection:** Medium (correlation shown on test set, but long-term OOD behavior untested).

## Next Checks
1. **Stress-test coarse-grained limit:** Evaluate on protein-ligand complexes with high flexibility (e.g., kinases with DFG-in/out) or explicit waters; monitor drops in RMSD and affinity correlation vs. all-atom references.
2. **Probe encoder generalization:** Test on ligands with chemotypes distant from PDB/ChEMBL (e.g., macrocycles, peptidomimetics); measure affinity prediction Pearson change vs. in-distribution baselines.
3. **Epinet OOD calibration:** Construct adversarial affinity queries (e.g., extrapolate to higher potency ranges or novel target families); plot epinet IQR vs. absolute error and check for decorrelation (IQR not increasing with error).
