# Vector Quantized Latent Concepts: A Scalable Alternative to Clustering-Based Concept Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02726  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02726  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** VQLC replaces clustering with a learnable codebook, achieving near-linear memory scaling and superior faithfulness on large datasets (e.g., 67.92% accuracy drop on RoBERTa/AG News).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Vector Quantized Latent Concepts (VQLC), a method for discovering interpretable concepts in deep learning representations that overcomes the scalability and quality limitations of clustering-based approaches. By leveraging a VQ-VAE architecture with a fixed-size learnable codebook, VQLC scales linearly with dataset size and avoids the shallow, frequency-dominated clusters produced by K-Means. An adaptive residual encoder preserves semantic alignment, while temperature-based top-k sampling mitigates codebook collapse. Evaluations on language models demonstrate that VQLC matches or exceeds hierarchical clustering in faithfulness (via probing accuracy drop) and produces more fine-grained, less noisy concepts, as judged by LLMs and qualitative analysis.

## Method Summary
VQLC extracts token representations from a target layer of a pre-trained model, applies frequency-based filtering, and learns a discrete codebook of concept vectors. An adaptive encoder maps each token representation into a quantized space via a residual connection: \( z_e = (1-\alpha)h + \alpha \cdot \text{LN}(Wh + b) \), with \(\alpha \in (0,0.5)\). A vector quantizer with exponential moving average (EMA) updates assigns each \(z_e\) to its nearest codebook vector, using top-k sampling during training to prevent collapse. A decoder reconstructs the original representation, and training minimizes reconstruction loss and a commitment loss. After training, each token is assigned to its nearest codebook vector to form concepts. The salient token for a sentence is selected by integrated gradients for intermediate layers, or by the last token (decoder-only) or [CLS] (encoder-based) by default.

## Key Results
- **Scalability:** VQLC handles 300k tokens in ~50GB memory with near-linear growth; hierarchical clustering fails (OOM) at 200k tokens.
- **Faithfulness:** On RoBERTa/AG News, VQLC achieves 67.92% accuracy drop after concept ablation, surpassing K-Means (~48%) and approaching hierarchical clustering.
- **Interpretability:** LLM judges rank VQLC consistently second, with more fine-grained, less noisy concepts than both baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** VQLC achieves near-linear memory and time complexity with dataset size, enabling scalable concept discovery.
- **Mechanism:** VQLC uses a fixed-size codebook (K vectors). Each token representation is assigned to its nearest codebook vector in O(K) time. Codebook updates use exponential moving averages (EMA) that aggregate assignments without storing pairwise distances, avoiding O(N²) computations.
- **Core assumption:** Codebook size K is constant relative to token count N; EMA updates are O(1) per assignment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "VQLC replaces clustering with a learnable discrete codebook via a VQ-VAE architecture."
  - [Section 5.1] "VQLC uses K-Means to initialize the codebook... Since both K and T are fixed constants, the concept discovery of VQLC scales nearly linearly with N."
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper *Scaling up Discovery of Latent Concepts in Deep NLP Models* (Hawasly et al., 2024) also addresses scalability of clustering-based concept discovery, indicating the problem’s importance. However, it does not directly evidence VQLC’s mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Allowing codebook size K to grow with N or storing full assignment histories would reintroduce superlinear scaling.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The adaptive residual encoder preserves semantic alignment between quantized concepts and original model representations, maintaining faithfulness.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder computes \(z_e = (1-\alpha)h + \alpha \cdot \text{LN}(Wh + b)\), mixing a linearly transformed, layer-normalized version of the token representation \(h\) with \(h\) itself via a learnable coefficient \(\alpha\) constrained to \((0, 0.5)\). This retains most of the original semantics while adjusting the space for stable quantization.
- **Core assumption:** Original token representations already encode task-relevant semantics; excessive distortion would misalign concepts from the model’s internal decision logic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "An adaptive encoder maps token representations into a space suitable for stable vector quantization."
  - [Section 3.1] "The residual connection preserves the semantic structure of the original contextual representations, ensuring that the encoder output remains faithful to the model used for prediction."
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; neighboring concept-discovery papers (e.g., *Local Concept Embeddings...*) focus on vision and do not address adaptive encoders. Weak.
- **Break condition:** If \(\alpha\) is unconstrained or the linear layer diverges, the encoder output may drift far from the original representation space, causing concepts to capture artifacts rather than semantics.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Temperature‑based top‑k sampling during training mitigates codebook collapse and encourages diverse, interpretable concept vectors.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of hard assignment to the nearest codebook vector, VQLC samples from the top‑k nearest vectors with probability \(p(j|z_e) \propto \exp(-D(z_e, v_j)/\tau)\). The temperature \(\tau\) controls randomness; this stochasticity ensures less frequent vectors are occasionally selected and updated.
- **Core assumption:** Deterministic assignment would concentrate updates on a few popular vectors, causing others to become unused (collapse).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 3.2] "To mitigate codebook collapse and encourage exploration during training, we adopt a temperature‑based sampling mechanism... We first select the top‑k closest codebook vectors and convert their distances into a probability distribution."
  - [corpus] The core VQ‑VAE paper (van den Oord et al., 2017) proposes similar sampling, but it is not among the immediate neighbors. Weak.
- **Break condition:** If \(\tau\) is too low (near 0), sampling becomes nearly deterministic and collapse may persist. If \(\tau\) is too high, assignments become random, hindering convergence to meaningful vectors.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Extractor -> Adaptive Encoder -> Vector Quantizer -> Decoder
- **Critical path (training):**  
  1. Extract token representation \(h\)  
  2. Encoder: compute \(z_e\)  
  3. Quantizer: sample index from top‑k; fetch \(z_q\)  
  4. Update EMA usage and moving averages  
  5. Decoder: reconstruct \(\hat{z}\) from \(z_q\)  
  6. Compute \(L_{\text{rec}} + \beta L_{\text{commit}}\); backpropagate through encoder/decoder
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Codebook size K:* Larger K increases concept granularity but risks fragmentation and memory. Empirically K=400 balanced performance.  
  - *Commitment weight \(\beta\):* Too high forces encoder outputs rigidly to codebook vectors, losing nuance; too low permits drift. Optimal \(\beta=0.25\).  
  - *Temperature \(\tau\):* Controls exploration; low \(\tau\) ≈ deterministic (risk collapse), high \(\tau\) ≈ random (slow convergence). Best around 1.0.  
  - *Top‑k:* Larger k improves exploration but may dilute updates; k=5 yielded best faithfulness.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Codebook collapse:* Many codebook vectors with near‑zero usage frequency (check \(n_j\) from EMA).  
  - *High reconstruction perplexity:* Decoder fails to reconstruct encoder outputs; may indicate too small K, poor training, or insufficient decoder capacity.  
  - *Noise‑dominated concepts:* Concepts containing mostly stopwords/punctuation, resembling K‑Means output; suggests encoder adaptation or codebook learning issues.  
  - *Superlinear memory growth:* Indicates naive distance computation or storing full assignments; should remain O(NK).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Scalability benchmark:* Measure peak memory and time for VQLC vs hierarchical clustering (LACOAT) on datasets of 10k–300k tokens. Expect near-linear growth for VQLC and quadratic for LACOAT.  
  2. *Faithfulness probe:* Train a linear classifier on original sentence representations, then remove the salient concept via orthogonal projection. Report accuracy drop; compare VQLC against baselines.  
  3. *LLM‑based ranking:* For a held‑out sample, extract concepts from each method and have multiple LLM judges rank them by relevance to the model’s prediction. Compute average rank and inter‑rater agreement.
