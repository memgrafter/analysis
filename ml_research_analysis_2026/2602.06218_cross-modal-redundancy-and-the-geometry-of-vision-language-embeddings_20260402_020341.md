# Cross-Modal Redundancy and the Geometry of Vision-Language Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06218
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06218
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Unimodal atoms in a shared sparse dictionary fully explain the modality gap in vision-language embeddings; removing them collapses the gap without harming retrieval performance.

## Executive Summary
The geometry of joint vision-language embeddings—notably the "modality gap" where images and texts occupy disjoint cones—is not well understood. This paper introduces the **Iso-Energy Assumption**, which posits that shared concepts exhibit equal average activation energy across modalities. To operationalize this, they propose an **Aligned Sparse Autoencoder (SAE-A)** that adds a soft penalty to encourage energy consistency while preserving reconstruction. Validated on synthetic data with known ground truth and on multiple foundational VLMs (CLIP, OpenCLIP, SigLIP), SAE-A reveals a unimodal/bimodal atom split: unimodal atoms act as modality-specific biases that fully explain the gap, while bimodal atoms carry cross-modal alignment. Interventions in the bimodal subspace yield in-distribution edits and improved recall without sacrificing reconstruction quality.

## Method Summary
The method centers on training a **Matching-Pursuit Sparse Autoencoder (MP-SAE)** with a shared dictionary \(D \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}\) and separate encoders for image and text embeddings. The key innovation is an **alignment penalty** \(L_{\text{align}} = -\frac{1}{b}\text{tr}(Z_{\text{img}} Z_{\text{txt}}^\top)\) added to the reconstruction loss (MSE), which encourages co-activation of the same atoms across modalities. The penalty weight \(\beta\) is chosen as the largest value in \(\{10^{-6}, \dots, 10^{-1}\}\) such that the \(R^2\) drop versus an unaligned SAE is \(< 0.05\). After training, atom modality is classified by the energy ratio \(\mu_i = \mathbb{E}[Z_{\text{img},i}^2] / (\mathbb{E}[Z_{\text{img},i}^2] + \mathbb{E}[Z_{\text{xt},i}^2])\), with \(|\mu_i - 0.5| \leq 0.05\) defining bimodal atoms. This split enables interventions: e.g., removing unimodal atoms to collapse the modality gap while preserving bimodal-only retrieval performance.

## Key Results
- Functional alignment \(\rho\) increases by over an order of magnitude (e.g., CLIP: 0.327 → 4.232).
- Functional and Distributional Agreement (FDA) doubles (e.g., 2.630 → 4.559).
- Interventional robustness \(\delta_r\) remains near zero after unimodal removal, indicating no performance loss.
- Reconstruction metrics (MSE, \(R^2\)) are unchanged by the alignment penalty.
- Removing unimodal atoms collapses the modality gap (DiM, Wasserstein distance) without harming recall@k on retrieval benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The **Iso-Energy Assumption** provides a normative constraint: for a shared concept, its expected activation energy (squared coefficient) should be equal in both modalities. SAE-A’s alignment penalty approximate this by encouraging positive covariance between image and text code activations. When this holds, the learned dictionary naturally partitions into:
- **Bimodal atoms** (balanced \(\mu_i \approx 0.5\)): activated by both modalities, representing shared semantic content and enabling cross-modal arithmetic.
- **Unimodal atoms** (\(\mu_i \approx 0\) or \(1\)): modality-specific biases that shift the mean embedding of each modality, creating the geometric "gap".
Removing unimodal atoms eliminates these shifts while preserving the bimodal subspace responsible for retrieval and semantic operations. Synthetic validation confirms that only when the Iso-Energy condition is met does SAE-A correctly recover a ground-truth bimodal/unimodal split.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**: Learn overcomplete dictionaries where inputs are reconstructed as sparse linear combinations. *Why needed*: Decompose high-dimensional embeddings into interpretable, additive features. *Quick check*: Does a SAE with \(K > d\) learn features with non-Gaussian activation distributions?
- **Iso-Energy Assumption**: Shared concepts have equal mean squared activation across modalities. *Why needed*: Provides a testable signal for cross-modal alignment in a shared dictionary. *Quick check*: In a trained SAE-A, do bimodal atoms show balanced energy across image/text batches?
- **Modality Gap**: Empirical observation that image and text embeddings occupy separated regions in vector space (e.g., distinct mean directions). *Why needed*: Understanding this gap is key to improving VLM alignment and interpretability. *Quick check*: Compute Difference in Means (DiM) cosine similarity; low values indicate a gap.
- **Matching Pursuit (MP)**: Iterative greedy algorithm for \(\ell_0\)-constrained sparse coding. *Why needed*: Encoder for SAE; ensures exact sparsity \(\kappa\) per sample. *Quick check*: Does MP achieve the specified \(\kappa\) atoms active per sample?
- **Functional Alignment (\(\rho\))**: Ratio of cross-modal to within-modal atom co-occurrence (weighted by cosine similarity). *Why needed*: Quantifies how much the dictionary captures genuinely shared concepts. *Quick check*: Compute \(\rho = \frac{\sum_{i \lor j} B_{\Sigma}}{\sum_{\neg i \land \neg j} B_{\Sigma}}\); high \(\rho\) indicates good alignment.
- **Optimal Transport (OT) for FDA**: Solves a matching problem between image and text energy distributions to compute Functional & Distributional Agreement. *Why needed*: Measures whether the *distribution* of energy across atoms is aligned, not just co-activation. *Quick check*: Use Sinkhorn (ε=0.1) to solve \(\min_\Gamma \langle \Gamma, C \rangle\); FDA > 1 indicates alignment.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Input Embeddings (Image/Text) → MP-SAE Encoder (shared \(D\), separate \(Z_{\text{img}}, Z_{\text{text}}\)) → Loss Calculation (MSE + β·L_align) → Dictionary Update (Adam + renormalize) → Modality Classification (\(\mu_i\)) → Metrics Computation (ρ, FDA, δr, gap metrics) → Interventions (unimodal removal, bimodal arithmetic).

**Critical Path**: Training loop with matched image-text pairs, computing codes via MP, applying alignment penalty, updating dictionary. Post-training: compute atom energy ratios to derive bimodal mask \(\delta\), evaluate metrics on held-out data, and run interventions (e.g., reconstruct using only bimodal atoms).

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- \(\beta\) selection: Larger \(\beta\) forces stronger alignment but risks reconstruction collapse if too high. Tuned via \(R^2\) tolerance rule.
- Dictionary size \(K\): Larger \(K\) increases capacity but may introduce redundant unimodal atoms.
- \(\ell_0 = \kappa\): Higher sparsity yields more interpretable atoms but may hurt reconstruction.
- OT solver for FDA: Entropic regularization (Sinkhorn) vs. exact linear programming; affects numerical stability and FDA values.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Degenerate atoms (constant or near-zero): \(\beta\) too high or poor initialization.
- Low \(\rho\) / FDA despite training: SAE-A failed to isolate bimodal atoms; check \(\mu_i\) distribution.
- NaN in OT: Cost matrix or marginals ill-conditioned; add jitter or subsample high-energy atoms.
- Reconstruction \(R^2\) drop > tolerance when increasing \(\beta\): alignment-reconstruction tradeoff limit reached.
- Unimodal removal harms recall unexpectedly: \(\delta\) threshold too strict; relax \(|\mu_i - 0.5| \leq \tau\).

**First Three Experiments**:
1. **Synthetic validation**: Train SAE-A on data generated from a known DGP with controllable \(\tau_1, \tau_2\). Measure dictionary recovery (Wasserstein between learned and true atoms) and activation pattern accuracy. Expect success only when Iso-Energy holds (\(\tau_1=1\)).
2. **CLIP ViT-B/32 training**: Extract 1M LAION embeddings, train SAE-A with \(\beta\) tuned via \(R^2\) rule. Report bimodal/unimodal split (histogram of \(\mu_i\)), reconstruction metrics, and ρ/FDA vs. baseline SAE.
3. **Modality gap intervention**: On test set, compute raw mean difference (DiM) and Wasserstein gap. Then remove unimodal atoms (mask \(\delta\)) and recompute; expect significant gap reduction. Also run retrieval recall@1 on image→text before/after removal.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter ambiguity**: Missing details on learning rate, batch size, weight decay, LR schedule, and whether an explicit L1 penalty is used besides MP’s hard \(\ell_0\). These critically affect training stability and \(\beta\) selection.
- **Metric implementation details**: Unclear whether pacc centering is over full test or per-batch; exact recall set for \(\delta_r@1\); OT solver specifics (ε, algorithm) for FDA.
- **Circularity risk**: The Iso-Energy Assumption is not independently verified on real VLMs; the bimodal/unimodal split derived from SAE-A may be an artifact of the training constraint rather than a discovery about ground-truth geometry.

## Confidence
- **Synthetic validation of SAE-A under Iso-Energy**: High – clearly demonstrates recovery of known structure when the assumption holds.
- **Unimodal atoms explain modality gap**: Medium – depends on correct implementation of \(\mu_i\) threshold and SAE-A training; some ambiguities in hyperparameters.
- **Bimodal subspace interventions improve retrieval/arithmetic**: Medium – results rely on specific metric implementations and may vary with \(\beta\) and \(K\).
- **Full explanation of modality gap by unimodal biases**: Low–Medium – risk of circularity in defining atoms via energy balance and then attributing the gap to the resulting unimodal subset.
