# Learning Progressive Adaptation for Multi-Modal Tracking

## Quick Facts
* arXiv ID: 2603.21100  
* Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21100  
* Reference count: 40  
* Primary result: PATrack achieves state-of-the-art multi-modal tracking performance (e.g., SR 0.578 on LasHeR) with only 95.51M parameters and 58.24G FLOPs.

## Executive Summary
PATrack addresses the challenge of adapting a pre-trained RGB single-modal tracker to multi-modal scenarios (RGB+Thermal/Depth/Event) with limited paired data. Existing fine-tuning methods struggle to handle modality-specific features, cross-modal interactions, and prediction head adaptation. The proposed solution introduces a progressive adaptation framework that inserts three parameter-efficient modules—Modality-Dependent Adaptation (MDA), Cross-Modality Entangled Adaptation (CEA), and Head Adaptation (HA)—into a frozen RGB backbone. Each module targets a specific aspect: MDA enhances intra-modal features via frequency decomposition; CEA enables inter-modal feature interaction through cross-attention guided by shared representations; HA adapts the prediction head to fused multi-modal inputs. Evaluations on RGB-T (LasHeR, RGBT234, GTOT), RGB-D (DepthTrack), and RGB-E (VisEvent) benchmarks show PATrack achieves new state-of-the-art results while maintaining high parameter efficiency. Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of the three modules.

## Method Summary
PATrack builds upon OSTrack, a pre-trained RGB-only Transformer tracker, and freezes its backbone to preserve robust RGB features. Three lightweight adapters are inserted into the encoder blocks:  
1. **Modality-Dependent Adaptation (MDA)**: Placed in layers {1,2,3,5,6,8,9,11,12}, MDA uses a bottleneck to split each branch’s features into high- and low-frequency components (via max-pooling and average-pooling), processes them, and transfers the complementary components to the other branch via residual connections. This strengthens intra-modal representations.  
2. **Cross-Modality Entangled Adaptation (CEA)**: Inserted in layers {4,7,10}, CEA first projects low-dimensional features from both modalities, concatenates them to form a fused query, and applies cross-attention where the fused query attends to the keys/values of each individual modality. This directly models inter-modal interactions.  
3. **Head Adaptation (HA)**: After the final encoder, search tokens from both modalities are fused (by concatenation) and passed through a bottleneck layer (down→GELU→up) with a residual connection to adapt the existing box head.  

Only the adapter and HA parameters are trained (typically 4.5M additional parameters), using AdamW (lr=4e-4, weight decay=1e-4, exponential decay 0.8) on the respective multi-modal training sets. This progressive, parameter-efficient design enables effective adaptation even with limited paired data.

## Key Results
- **RGB-T**: Success Rate (SR) 0.578 on LasHeR (+1.0% over prior SOTA), SR 0.651 on RGBT234.  
- **RGB-D**: F-score 0.600 on DepthTrack.  
- **RGB-E**: SR 0.605 on VisEvent.  
- **Efficiency**: Total parameters 95.51M, FLOPs 58.24G, significantly lower than fully fine-tuned competitors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PATrack’s progressive adaptation separately handles intra‑modal and inter‑modal learning. MDA decomposes features into frequency bands, allowing each modality to enhance its own representation and receive complementary information from the other modality—this is particularly useful because thermal/depth/event data often contain complementary details not present in RGB. CEA leverages cross‑attention guided by a fused low‑dimensional query, enabling direct communication between modalities and fostering shared semantic understanding. HA fine‑tunes the prediction head to consume the fused multi‑modal tokens effectively. By freezing the backbone, the model retains strong RGB feature extraction while the lightweight adapters learn only the necessary transformations, drastically reducing overfitting risk on small paired datasets. The staged layer assignment (MDA in early layers for intra‑modal refinement, CEA in deeper layers for fusion) ensures that each modality first builds robust individual features before interacting, leading to superior fusion and tracking performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Parameter‑Efficient Fine‑Tuning (Adapters)**  
  *Why needed*: To adapt large pre‑trained models with limited paired data without catastrophic forgetting or excessive overfitting.  
  *Quick check*: Freeze backbone; add small bottleneck modules; verify only adapter gradients are non‑zero and training converges.

- **Frequency Decomposition for Intra‑Modal Adaptation**  
  *Why needed*: Different modalities emphasize different frequency bands (e.g., thermal highlights low‑frequency objects, RGB contains high‑frequency textures). Splitting allows targeted enhancement.  
  *Quick check*: Confirm max‑pool/avg‑pool split and recombination; inspect feature maps to see high‑/low‑frequency separation.

- **Cross‑Attention for Multi‑Modal Interaction**  
  *Why needed*: Direct interaction between modalities is crucial for exploiting complementary cues (e.g., RGB color with thermal heat signatures).  
  *Quick check*: Ensure attention weights are computed between one modality’s keys/values and the other’s query formed from fused representation.

- **Progressive Layer Assignment**  
  *Why needed*: Early encoder layers focus on low‑level features that are best refined within each modality (MDA); deeper layers abstract high‑level concepts suitable for cross‑modal fusion (CEA).  
  *Quick check*: Verify MDA appears in {1,2,3,5,6,8,9,11,12} and CEA in {4,7,10} as prescribed.

- **Frozen Backbone**  
  *Why needed*: Preserves the powerful RGB feature extractor learned on large single‑modal datasets, reduces trainable parameters, and stabilizes training.  
  *Quick check*: Monitor backbone weights; they should remain constant throughout training.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
RGB tokens and X (thermal/depth/event) tokens enter a shared frozen Transformer encoder (OSTrack). Encoder layers are selectively enhanced:  
- Layers 1,2,3,5,6,8,9,11,12 contain **MDA** (intra‑modal frequency split & cross‑transfer).  
- Layers 4,7,10 contain **CEA** (cross‑attention with fused query).  
After the last encoder, search region tokens from both branches are concatenated → **HA** (bottleneck) → original box prediction head.  

**Critical path**  
1. Input tokens routed correctly to both branches.  
2. MDA performs frequency split, processing, and residual cross‑addition without altering backbone residuals.  
3. CEA projects features, forms fused query, and executes cross‑attention with correct dimensions.  
4. Search tokens from both branches are fused (concatenation) before HA.  
5. HA output dimension matches original head input (768).  
Any break here causes training divergence or performance collapse.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Adapter capacity*: Small bottleneck (8 channels) keeps parameters low but may limit expressiveness; larger (e.g., 192) could improve scores but risks overfitting on small datasets.  
- *Layer assignment*: More MDA layers strengthen individual modalities; more CEA layers increase interaction but could destabilize early features. The chosen split balances the two.  
- *Frozen vs. fine‑tuned backbone*: Freezing preserves RGB knowledge and efficiency; fine‑tuning might boost adaptation but increases cost and overfitting risk.  
- *Fusion timing*: PATrack fuses only at the end to retain modality‑specific pathways; early fusion might merge premature or noisy features.

**Failure signatures**  
- *No gain over frozen baseline*: Adapters may be missing, disabled, or not trained (check gradients).  
- *Dimension mismatches*: Crashes/NaNs due to MDA channel splits not summing to original or CEA projection sizes incompatible.  
- *Overfitting*: Training loss drops but validation plateaus; consider smaller adapters or stronger augmentation.  
- *Cross‑attention degeneracy*: Attention weights become uniform, indicating ineffective query; verify query formation (should use fused low‑dim features).  
- *HA shape mismatch*: Head expects 768 dimensions but HA outputs different size causing runtime error.

**Exactly 3 first experiments**  
1. **Sanity‑check training**: Train on 1% of LasHeR for a few epochs; ensure loss decreases and adapter gradients are non‑zero.  
2. **Ablate MDA**: Remove MDA modules (set to identity) while keeping CEA and HA; evaluate to confirm performance drops significantly.  
3. **Efficiency verification**: Count parameters (target ~95.5M) and FLOPs (~58.24G) with `thop`; run a dummy forward pass to measure inference speed.
