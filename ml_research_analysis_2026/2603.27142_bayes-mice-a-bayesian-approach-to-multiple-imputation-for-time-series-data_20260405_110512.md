# Bayes-MICE: A Bayesian Approach to Multiple Imputation for Time Series Data

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27142  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27142  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Bayes-MICE reduces imputation error on time-series data compared to standard MICE and BRITS, and provides principled uncertainty quantification via posterior credible intervals.

## Executive Summary

Bayes-MICE extends the popular Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) framework to time-series data by incorporating Bayesian inference and Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling. It respects temporal structure through lagged features and time-aware initialization. Evaluated on AirQuality and PhysioNet datasets, Bayes-MICE consistently achieved lower Normalized Mean Absolute Error (NMAE) and Normalized Root Mean Squared Error (NRMSE) than MICE and the deep imputation model BRITS. For instance, on the AirQuality CO(GT) variable, Bayes-MICE (Random Walk Metropolis) obtained an NMAE of 0.1379 (SD 0.0010) versus MICE’s 0.2622 (SD 0.0071). The MALA sampler offered faster convergence with similar accuracy. The Bayesian approach also yields full posterior distributions, enabling credible intervals for uncertainty quantification.

## Method Summary

Bayes-MICE adapts MICE to sequential data by modeling each variable with a Bayesian regression that includes lagged values of that variable and others as predictors. The imputation process initializes using a time-aware strategy that respects ordering. Missing values are then sampled from their posterior conditional distributions via MCMC (either Random Walk Metropolis or Metropolis-adjusted Langevin Algorithm). After burning and thinning, multiple imputed datasets are generated and pooled for downstream analysis. This Bayesian formulation provides a coherent way to propagate uncertainty and adapt to the temporal dependencies.

## Key Results

- **Error reduction**: Bayes-MICE outperformed MICE and BRITS on both AirQuality and PhysioNet datasets under NMAE and NRMSE metrics.
- **Sampler efficiency**: MALA achieved higher effective sample size and lower autocorrelation than RWM, with comparable imputation accuracy.
- **Uncertainty quantification**: Posterior credible intervals give principled uncertainty estimates for imputed values.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

Bayes-MICE leverages Bayesian inference to model the joint distribution of time-series variables conditionally, capturing temporal dynamics through lagged terms. By sampling from the exact posterior (via MCMC) rather than point estimates, it naturally accounts for uncertainty in imputations, which is especially important when data are missing not completely at random. The time-aware initialization and lagged predictors ensure that the sequential structure is respected, avoiding leakage of future information and improving the relevance of the conditional models. MCMC samplers like MALA adapt to the curvature of the posterior, leading to faster mixing and more efficient exploration compared to simpler random walks.

## Foundational Learning

- **Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE)**: The base iterative imputation framework Bayes-MICE extends. *Why needed*: Provides a flexible, variable-wise conditional modeling approach. *Quick check*: Explain how single-imputation cycles differ from multiple imputation.
- **Bayesian inference**: Core methodology for updating parameter posteriors. *Why needed*: Enables full uncertainty propagation via posterior distributions. *Quick check*: Derive the posterior for a Bayesian linear regression with known variance.
- **Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)**: Algorithmic tool for sampling from complex posteriors. *Why needed*: Approximates the conditional posteriors of regression coefficients in Bayes-MICE. *Quick check*: Implement a simple random walk Metropolis sampler for a 1D Gaussian posterior.
- **Time series analysis (lags)**: Incorporates temporal dependencies. *Why needed*: Ensures that the model respects sequential ordering and captures autocorrelation. *Quick check*: Given a time series, generate lag-1 and lag-2 features for a target variable.
- **Uncertainty quantification**: Using posterior credible intervals. *Why needed*: Allows assessment of imputation reliability for downstream decisions. *Quick check*: Compute a 95% credible interval from MCMC samples and compare to a frequentist confidence interval.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**  
Raw time-series input -> Data preprocessing (handle missingness, create lag features) -> Bayesian conditional model specification (likelihood, priors, regression coefficients) -> MCMC sampler (RWM/MALA) -> Posterior sample collection -> Multiple imputed datasets aggregation -> Output imputed time series.

**Critical Path**  
1. Preprocess: align time index, generate lagged predictors.  
2. For each variable, initialize its conditional model parameters (time-aware initialization).  
3. Run MCMC iterations: for each missing value, sample from its conditional posterior given observed + current imputed values.  
4. After burn-in, retain thinned samples to form multiple imputed datasets.  
5. Pool estimates (e.g., Rubin’s rules) if downstream analysis requires combined results.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Sampler choice**: RWM is simple but may mix slowly; MALA uses gradient information for faster convergence but requires tuning (step size, preconditioning).  
- **Computational cost**: MCMC is iterative and slower than deterministic imputation (e.g., EM), but provides uncertainty; tradeoff between accuracy and scalability.  
- **Model complexity**: Including many lags captures more dynamics but increases dimensionality and may worsen MCMC efficiency; regularization via priors helps.

**Failure Signatures**  
- High autocorrelation in MCMC chains (low ESS) indicating poor mixing.  
- Diverging posterior means or large variance across imputed datasets suggesting instability.  
- Validation error (e.g., NMAE) significantly higher than baseline methods on simulated missingness.

**First Three Experiments**  
1. Replicate the AirQuality benchmark: compare Bayes-MICE (RWM and MALA) against MICE and BRITS using NMAE/NRMSE.  
2. Diagnose MCMC convergence: compute effective sample size (ESS), autocorrelation, and R̂ for MALA vs RWM on the same dataset.  
3. Evaluate on PhysioNet dataset to assess generalizability across domains (environmental to clinical).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Mixed-type variables**: How can Bayes-MICE be extended to handle mixed-type variables (e.g., categorical, ordinal) in time series imputation?  
2. **MNAR mechanisms**: How can Bayes-MICE be adapted to handle missing-not-at-random (MNAR) mechanisms in spatio-temporal data?  
3. **Joint multivariate modeling**: How would a fully joint multivariate Bayesian time-series model affect imputation accuracy and uncertainty compared to the independent variable approach in Bayes-MICE?  
4. **Advanced MCMC samplers**: Can advanced MCMC samplers (e.g., Hamiltonian Monte Carlo) improve computational efficiency and convergence of Bayes-MICE without sacrificing imputation quality?

## Limitations

- **Limited evaluation scope**: Results are reported only on two datasets (AirQuality, PhysioNet); performance on other domains or missingness patterns remains unknown.  
- **Computational cost**: Bayesian MCMC, especially RWM, may be orders of magnitude slower than deterministic or deep learning imputation methods, hindering scalability.  
- **Uncertainty validation**: The paper claims principled uncertainty quantification but does not provide coverage probability or calibration metrics to verify reliability.

## Confidence

- **Accuracy superiority over MICE/BRITS**: Medium (consistent on two datasets but lacks broader validation)  
- **MALA efficiency (ESS, autocorrelation)**: Medium (only compared to RWM, not to other modern samplers)  
- **Principled uncertainty quantification**: Low (claimed but not empirically calibrated)

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset robustness test**: Replicate on at least 3 additional time-series benchmarks (e.g., electricity, traffic, medical waveforms) with varying missingness patterns (MCAR, MAR, MNAR) to assess generalizability.  
2. **Computational cost analysis**: Measure wall-clock time, memory usage, and MCMC convergence diagnostics (e.g., R-hat) across series lengths (e.g., 1k vs. 100k points) and compare against BRITS and recent deep imputation models (e.g., TimesNet).  
3. **Uncertainty calibration audit**: For imputed values, compute prediction interval coverage probability (PICP) and average credible interval width across confidence levels (e.g., 50%, 95%) on held-out test data to verify reliability of posterior uncertainty estimates.
