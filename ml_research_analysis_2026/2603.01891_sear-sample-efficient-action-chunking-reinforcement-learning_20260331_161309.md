# SEAR: Sample Efficient Action Chunking Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01891
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01891
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Approximately 90% IQM success on the 20 hardest Metaworld tasks after 1 million environment steps, exceeding SimbaV2 (~60%) and CQN-AS by over 20% IQM.

## Executive Summary
SEAR is an off-policy online reinforcement learning algorithm that enables sample-efficient training of policies that output action chunks (sequences of multiple actions). It combines a causal transformer critic trained on multi-horizon N-step returns with a random replanning data collection scheme to improve state-action coverage. This approach overcomes the exploration and stability challenges of learning with long action sequences, achieving state-of-the-art performance on challenging manipulation tasks.

## Method Summary
SEAR learns a policy π_θ that maps states to action chunks of length N. During training, a random prefix of the chunk (length k ~ Uniform(1,N)) is executed in the environment, and all individual transitions are stored in a replay buffer. The critic is a causal transformer that takes the state and a full chunk and outputs Q-values for every prefix length. It is trained on multi-horizon targets: for each prefix of length k, the target is the k-step return G(k) computed using stored rewards and the target critic at the prefix's terminal state. The actor maximizes the Q-value of the full chunk while incorporating an entropy bonus. At evaluation, a receding horizon is used: only the first k_eval actions are executed before replanning, which improves reactivity while retaining long-horizon planning benefits.

## Key Results
- On the 20 hardest Metaworld tasks, SEAR (chunk size 10) reaches ~90% IQM success after 1 million steps, significantly outperforming SimbaV2 (~60%) and CQN-AS (>20% IQM gap).
- Ablations confirm that both multi-horizon critic targets and random replanning are essential; removing either reduces performance by ~20%.
- The method remains effective with large chunk sizes up to 20, and the optimal replanning interval is around 4 steps independent of chunk size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-horizon critic targets provide dense learning signals
- **Claim:** Training the critic to predict Q-values for all prefixes of a sampled chunk (not just the full chunk) stabilizes learning with large chunk sizes by generating many training targets from each transition.
- **Mechanism:** For each state-chunk pair (s_t, a_{t:t+N}), the critic predicts Q-values for prefixes of lengths 1 to N. The target for prefix k is the k-step return G(k), which depends only on rewards up to t+k and the value at s_{t+k}. This creates N targets per transition, mitigating data scarcity.
- **Core assumption:** The Q-value of a prefix is independent of the later actions in the same chunk; the causal mask enforces this.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "multi-horizon N-step returns from chunk prefixes"; Section 4.2 describes the parallel training on all G(k); prior works like CQN-AS also use multi-target critics but not within a causal transformer for online chunking.

### Mechanism 2: Random replanning improves state-action coverage
- **Claim:** Randomly executing only a prefix of each chunk during data collection ensures the policy is trained on states that would otherwise be visited only as intermediates in full-chunk rollouts, aligning the training state distribution with the receding-horizon evaluation distribution.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of always executing all N actions, SEAR samples k uniformly from 1 to N and executes only a_{t:t+k}. The state s_{t+k} becomes a new planning point, generating a fresh chunk. This increases coverage of the state space.
- **Core assumption:** The policy's performance is sensitive to the distribution of states on which it is evaluated; fixed full-chunk execution creates a mismatch when using receding horizon at test time.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "SEAR improves the state-action coverage of the actor using random replanning"; Section 4.4 identifies the sparsity problem and introduces random replanning; Figure 4a shows a ~20% drop without it.

### Mechanism 3: Causal transformer captures temporal structure efficiently
- **Claim:** A causal transformer is essential for accurately modeling the value of action chunks because it shares computation across horizons while respecting temporal dependencies.
- **Mechanism:** The critic inputs s_t concatenated with the full action chunk. Causal self-attention ensures each action attends only to itself and previous actions. The representation at position k naturally yields the Q-value for the first k actions. This is more parameter-efficient than separate networks per horizon.
- **Core assumption:** The value of a prefix depends only on the prefix and initial state, not on future actions in the same chunk.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "causal transformer critic"; Section 4.2 explains the suitability; Figure 4a ablates the transformer with an MLP, showing a ~30% performance drop.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning (MaxEnt-RL)
- **Why needed:** SEAR optimizes a maximum entropy objective that includes a policy entropy term, which encourages exploration crucial for sparse-reward tasks like Metaworld. The entropy is computed per individual action (not per chunk) to maintain optimality regardless of chunk size.
- **Quick check question:** Why does SEAR compute the entropy bonus per action rather than using the joint entropy of the whole chunk?

### Concept: Off-Policy Learning & Importance Sampling
- **Why needed:** SEAR is off-policy: it reuses data from previous policies stored in a replay buffer. Standard off-policy N-step methods require importance sampling to correct for mismatched action distributions. SEAR avoids this by defining a Q-function Q(s_t, a_{t:t+N}) that conditions on the specific chunk, treating the whole sequence as a macro-action whose execution path (rewards, states) is already known.
- **Quick check question:** How does conditioning the Q-function on the exact chunk avoid importance sampling corrections that standard off-policy N-step methods need?

### Concept: Receding Horizon Control
- **Why needed:** Pure open-loop action chunking executes all N actions before replanning, reducing decision frequency and reactivity. SEAR adopts receding horizon execution: a full chunk is predicted but only a prefix (k < N) is executed, then a new chunk is planned. This combines long-horizon planning with closed-loop control. Random replanning during training bridges the distribution shift between training (random k) and evaluation (fixed k).
- **Quick check question:** Why would training only with full-chunk execution (k=N) fail when evaluating with a shorter replanning interval k<N?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Replay buffer D stores individual transitions; actor π_θ generates action chunks; critic Q_φ (causal transformer) predicts Q-values for all prefixes; target critic \bar{φ} provides stable targets; random prefix sampler selects execution length during data collection. Data flow: actor -> environment -> buffer -> critic/actor updates; target critic updated via EMA.

### Critical path
1. Interaction: Given state s_t, actor samples chunk a_{t:t+N}. Random k ~ Uniform(1,N) is chosen. Execute a_{t:t+k} in environment; record each transition (s_i, a_i, r_i, s_{i+1}) to D.
2. Critic update: Sample batch of chunks (s_t, a_{t:t+N}, r_{t:t+N}, s_{t+N}) from D (reconstructed). For each k in 1..N, compute target G(k) using s_{t+k} and π_θ for remaining actions. Minimize MSE between Q_φ^k(s_t, a_{t:t+k}) and G(k) for all k.
3. Actor update: Sample states s_t from D. For each, sample full chunk a_{t:t+N} ~ π_θ. Maximize Q_φ^N(s_t, a_{t:t+N}) - α log π_θ (reverse KL).
4. Target update: Soft update \bar{φ} ← τ φ + (1-τ)\bar{φ}.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Transformer vs. MLP critic:** Transformer captures sequential dependencies within chunks and efficiently shares computation across horizons; MLP is simpler but empirically ~30% worse.
- **Chunk size N:** Larger N improves long-horizon planning but increases critic difficulty and reduces decision frequency. SEAR scales robustly up to N=20.
- **Replanning interval k:** At evaluation, smaller k increases reactivity but may lose planning benefits; optimal around 4 regardless of N. Random k during training is essential.
- **Update-to-data ratio (UTD):** Paper uses UTD=1; higher UTD could improve sample efficiency but increases compute.

### Failure signatures
- **Missing multi-horizon targets:** Critic trained only on full-chunk returns leads to high variance, underfitting, and ~50% performance drop.
- **No random replanning:** Training with only full-chunk execution creates state distribution mismatch; evaluating with receding horizon causes ~20% drop.
- **MLP critic:** Replacing transformer with MLP reduces performance by ~30% due to inability to model temporal structure.
- **Transformer overfitting:** Too large a model relative to data or too high learning rate causes inconsistent per-horizon predictions; monitor variance across horizons.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation: Multi-horizon targets.** Train SEAR-10 with transformer but only predict full-chunk Q-value (no per-horizon targets). Expect ~50% drop in IQM success (from ~80-90% to ~30-40%).
2. **Ablation: Random replanning.** Train SEAR-10 with fixed full-chunk execution (k=N). Then evaluate with receding horizon k=5. Expect significant performance drop (~80%+ to ~60-70%).
3. **Chunk size & replanning sweep.** For SEAR with N=5,10,20, vary evaluation replanning interval k=1..N. Expect: (a) k=1 suboptimal; (b) performance peaks near k=4 for all N; (c) larger N yields better performance for a given k than training with that k as the chunk size.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do SEAR’s insights transfer to offline-to-online RL with demonstration data?
- Can the action chunk size be made adaptive and optimized during training?
- Can the receding horizon be made state-dependent or mid-chunk adaptable for domains like locomotion?
- What is the synergistic mechanism that makes the combination of multi-horizon targets, causal transformers, and random replanning necessary for effective receding horizon control?

## Limitations
- Implementation details (replay buffer chunk formation, transformer architecture specifics, random prefix distribution) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Evaluation limited to Metaworld tasks with proprioceptive states; generalization to image-based observations or real-world robots remains unverified.
- No theoretical analysis of the interaction between random replanning and multi-horizon targets; dependencies are established empirically but not understood mechanistically.

## Confidence
- **High:** Core performance claims (90% IQM on Metaworld) and ablations confirming necessity of multi-horizon targets and random replanning.
- **High:** Causal transformer superiority over MLP critic (demonstrated by ~30% drop).
- **Medium:** Scalability to very large chunk sizes (N > 20) or more complex tasks; theoretical break conditions exist but are not stress-tested.
- **Low:** Applicability to image-based or real-world tasks; method has only been evaluated on proprioceptive state inputs in simulated manipulation tasks.
