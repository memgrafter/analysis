# Anomaly detection in time-series via inductive biases in the latent space of conditional normalizing flows

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11756
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11756
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Outperforms standard likelihood-based methods on synthetic anomalies and competes with state-of-the-art on TSB-AD benchmark, with AUC-PR 82.1 and VUS-PR 96.0 on synthetic data (window size 64).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a novel anomaly detection method for time-series that addresses a key weakness of deep generative models: their reliance on observation likelihood, which fails to capture temporal dynamics and thus misses anomalies with high observation-likelihood. The method uses conditional normalizing flows to map observations into a latent space where trajectories are constrained to follow prescribed dynamics (e.g., linear-Gaussian). Anomalies are detected via a multivariate Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit test on these latent trajectories, which checks compliance with the expected dynamics. This approach provides a principled, label-free detector with built-in diagnostics via training compliance checks. On synthetic data, it robustly detects frequency, amplitude, and noise anomalies, and on real-world benchmarks (TSB-AD) it competes with state-of-the-art, especially when training compliance is high.

## Method Summary
The method operates in a discrete-time state-space framework. A conditional normalizing flow (CNF) maps each observation \(x_t\) (given a context window \(W_t\) of past observations) to a latent state \(z_t\) with Gaussian parameters (mean \(\mu_t\), covariance \(\Sigma_t\)). The latent means are forced to follow a prescribed deterministic dynamics, e.g., a linear-Gaussian latent dynamic model (LG-LDM): \(\mu_t = A \mu_{t-1} + b\). After training the CNF and dynamics parameters by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of the observations, latent trajectories are whitened to \(\tilde{z}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\). For a sliding window of length \(w\), a multivariate Kolmogorov-Smirnov (MV-KS) test compares the empirical distribution of \(\{\tilde{z}_t\}\) to \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\). An anomaly is flagged if the MV-KS statistic exceeds a critical value derived from the training data's distribution of statistics (i.e., when the latent trajectory deviates significantly from the prescribed dynamics). The training compliance (fraction of training windows below threshold) serves as an automatic diagnostic for model validity.

## Key Results
- On synthetic anomalies (frequency, amplitude, noise), the method achieves an AUC-PR of 82.1 and VUS-PR of 96.0 with window size 64, outperforming negative log-likelihood scoring.
- On the TSB-AD benchmark, it competes with state-of-the-art baselines, with performance strongly correlated with training compliance (FIT metric).
- The MV-KS test reliably detects anomalies that standard likelihood-based methods miss, because it evaluates temporal dynamics compliance rather than marginal density.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Anomalies are detected by testing compliance of latent representations with prescribed temporal dynamics, not by high observation-space likelihood.
- **Mechanism:** Observations are mapped via a conditional normalizing flow (CNF) into a latent space where trajectories are forced to follow a deterministic dynamical system (e.g., linear-Gaussian). Expected behavior is defined as adherence to this latent evolution; violations indicate anomalies.
- **Core assumption:** The prescribed latent dynamics (inductive bias) correctly captures the "expected" temporal patterns in the training data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...relocating the notion of anomaly to a prescribed latent space... constraints latent representations to evolve according to prescribed temporal dynamics."
  - [section 3.1] Formal state-space model: `z_t = F(x_t | W_t; θ)` with `μ_t = ψ(μ_{t-1}; ϕ)`.
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on reconstruction or generic likelihoods; this paper explicitly encodes dynamics in latent space.
- **Break condition:** If the inductive bias is misspecified (e.g., linear dynamics for inherently nonlinear processes), "expected" latent trajectories diverge from true normal behavior, causing false positives/negatives.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Anomaly detection is performed via a non-parametric multivariate Kolmogorov-Smirnov (MV-KS) goodness-of-fit test on latent whitened trajectories `̃z_t`.
- **Mechanism:** After mapping a time-series window to latent space, the MV-KS statistic compares the empirical distribution of `̃z_t` to the prescribed latent distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian). A statistic exceeding a critical value (`s ≥ τ`) flags non-compliance → anomaly.
- **Core assumption:** The MV-KS test is sensitive enough to detect deviations from the prescribed latent distribution given the window size and dimensionality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...reduced to a statistically grounded compliance test... evaluated via goodness-of-fit tests against the prescribed latent evolution."
  - [section 3.2] Explicit definition: `H(s) = unexpected behavior if s ≥ τ`.
  - [section 4.2] Empirical window size sensitivity: `w=64` optimal for D=4, `w=O(D^3)` recommended.
- **Break condition:** High-dimensional latent spaces (`D` large) or short window sizes reduce MV-KS power (curse of dimensionality), potentially missing anomalies or increasing false positives.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Model training success is diagnosed by checking if training latent trajectories comply with the inductive bias, enabling unsupervised, threshold-free AD.
- **Mechanism:** Apply MV-KS to latent representations of training subsequences. High compliance (most `s < τ`) indicates the CNF learned to map normal data into the prescribed latent dynamics, validating the AD pipeline.
- **Core assumption:** Training compliance correlates with generalization to unseen normal data; non-compliant training indicates the inductive bias was not learned.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...built-in, compliance diagnostic to identify when the DPM training procedure is successful..."
  - [section 3.2] "The MV-KS statistic computed in the training data is an automatic, model training diagnostic metric."
  - [section 4.2] Figure 3 and FIT column in Table 3 show compliance predicts reliable AD.
- **Break condition:** If training compliance is low (e.g., due to insufficient model capacity or mismatched context length), the MV-KS threshold is invalid and AD performance degrades, as seen in MITDB results.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Normalizing Flows (NFs)**
  - *Why needed here:* The CNF (`F(x|d)`) provides a tractable, invertible mapping from observations to latent space, enabling exact likelihood computation and deterministic transformation of sequences into latent trajectories.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the change-of-variable formula (`log p(z) + log|det J|`) allow training the CNF via NLL minimization?
- **Concept: State-Space Models (SSMs)**
  - *Why needed here:* The latent dynamics (`μ_t = ψ(μ_{t-1}; ϕ)`) impose temporal structure. The discrete-time SSM framework defines "expected behavior" as a distribution over latent trajectories, not static points.
  - *Quick check question:* In the LG-LDM (`μ_t = A μ_{t-1} + b`), what does `ρ(A) < 1` imply about long-term behavior?
- **Concept: Goodness-of-Fit (GOF) Tests**
  - *Why needed here:* MV-KS provides a non-parametric, threshold-free decision rule for compliance. Its critical value adapts to sample size, enabling unsupervised detection without labels.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is the MV-KS statistic preferable to parametric tests (e.g., assuming Gaussianity) in this latent space?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (sliding window) -> CNF Encoder -> Latent Dynamics -> Whitening -> GOF Test -> Decision
- **Critical path:** Data -> CNF mapping -> latent trajectory construction -> MV-KS test -> anomaly flag.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Window size (`w`):** Larger `w` improves MV-KS power but dilutes localized anomalies; recommended `w = O(D^3)`. Computationally scales with `w`.
  - **Model capacity:** Excessive CNF layers or context length can break compliance (Figure 3). Requires careful tuning (Table 1).
  - **Inductive bias choice:** LG-LDM is interpretable but may not fit all data; more complex dynamics (nonlinear, learnable covariance) increase capacity but risk overfitting.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low training compliance (high MV-KS on training subsequences) → AD unreliable.
  - MV-KS insensitive to known injected anomalies (synthetic tests) → window too small or dynamics misspecified.
  - NLL high on training data but low MV-KS compliance → CNF capacity too high, mapping to wrong latent region.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic anomaly sensitivity:** Inject frequency/amplitude/noise changes; verify MV-KS detects them even when NLL does not (Figure 2).
  2. **Window size sweep:** Test `w ∈ {20,32,40,64,100,200,400}` on synthetic data; identify `w=64` as optimal for D=4 (Table 2).
  3. **Real-world compliance check:** Run on TSB-AD subsets; record FIT (% of training sequences with `s < τ`). Expect high FIT correlates with competitive AD metrics (Tables 3, 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the framework incorporate learnable or non-linear latent dynamics, such as adaptive covariance matrices or non-Markovian models, to capture more complex temporal patterns beyond the fixed linear-Gaussian bias?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors explicitly state future work on "learning other inductive bias over-time: e.g., learnable LG-LDM covariance matrix, non-Markovian LG-LDMs, or non-linear models."
- Why unresolved: The current implementation uses a prescribed linear-Gaussian latent dynamic model, which may not generalize to all time-series with intricate or evolving dependencies, limiting detection accuracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons on diverse datasets showing improved AUC-PR or VUS-PR with adaptive dynamics, or theoretical analysis identifying conditions where complex biases outperform simple ones.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal sliding window size and alignment (e.g., centered vs. trailing) for the MV-KS test in latent space, and how do they depend on latent dimensionality and anomaly characteristics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "assessing the impact of MVN-KS window alignment" as a future avenue and empirically recommends a window size of O(D^3) but notes it may hide point anomalies.
- Why unresolved: The current guidance is empirical and heuristic; window alignment could affect sensitivity to anomalies at sequence boundaries, and the O(D^3) rule lacks theoretical justification for all cases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments with synthetic anomalies at known positions across varying dimensions and window settings, leading to a theoretically grounded or adaptive window selection method.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the proposed state-space framework with inductive biases be effectively extended to non-temporal data structures, such as graphs or spatial data, and what appropriate inductive biases would govern their evolution?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors list "extending the framework to other data types" as a future direction.
- Why unresolved: The model is designed for sequential time-series with discrete-time dynamics; other data types have different relational or topological structures requiring novel inductive biases and state representations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of analogous frameworks for graphs (e.g., using graph neural networks with prescribed graph dynamics) or images, with validation on benchmark anomaly detection tasks for those modalities.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can data-space explanations of anomalies be generated from latent-space compliance diagnostics to provide interpretable insights for domain experts about the original time-series features?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper calls for "developing data-space explanations of unexpected behavior to complement our latent-space diagnostics."
- Why unresolved: The current method flags anomalies based on latent trajectory deviations but does not attribute them to specific input variables or temporal patterns, limiting practical utility in applications requiring root-cause analysis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Methods to invert the conditional normalizing flow mapping to highlight contributing features, evaluated through case studies or user experiments demonstrating improved interpretability over latent-only diagnostics.

## Limitations
- The MV-KS test's multivariate implementation for time-series is not fully detailed, raising questions about its power and calibration in high dimensions or short windows.
- Synthetic data generation parameters are undisclosed, hindering exact replication of sensitivity results.
- The choice of inductive bias (linear-Gaussian) may not generalize to all time-series; the framework lacks an automatic mechanism for bias selection.

## Confidence
- **Core mechanism (latent dynamics vs. observation likelihood):** High confidence. Supported by synthetic experiments and clear theoretical contrast with standard likelihood methods.
- **MV-KS test robustness:** Medium confidence. Central to the method but implementation details for multivariate trajectories are sparse; power in high-D or short windows is acknowledged as a challenge.
- **Training compliance as diagnostic:** Medium confidence. Empirical correlation shown but causal link and generalizability across dynamics specifications are not proven.
- **Real-world performance on TSB-AD:** Medium confidence. Competitive results but depend on high training compliance, which is not guaranteed for all datasets.
