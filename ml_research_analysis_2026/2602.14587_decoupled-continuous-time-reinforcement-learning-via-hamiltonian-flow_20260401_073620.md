# Decoupled Continuous-Time Reinforcement Learning via Hamiltonian Flow

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14587
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14587
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CT-SAC and CT-TD3 substantially outperform existing continuous-time methods and discrete-time baselines on control tasks, with CT-SAC achieving 21% profit over a quarter in a real-world trading task—nearly double the second-best method.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles Q-function collapse and fragile martingale constraints in continuous-time reinforcement learning with irregular decision times. It introduces a decoupled actor-critic algorithm where the advantage-rate function `q` is learned from a fixed value function `V` via diffusion generators, while `V` is updated using a Hamiltonian flow that remains informative even for infinitesimal steps. A single critic network approximates `Q = V + q`, enabling practical off-policy training. Theoretical analysis proves convergence despite non-contractive operators, and empirical results on DeepMind Control Suite tasks and a minute-resolution trading task show significant improvements over prior continuous-time and strong discrete-time baselines.

## Method Summary
The approach addresses continuous-time RL with transitions `(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+u}, u)`, where `u` is an irregular holding time. The decoupled critic first estimates `q(s,a)` using a fixed `V` via `q̂_V_u(s,a) = (e^{-βu} E[V(s_u)|s,a] - V(s))/u + r`, optionally enhanced with Richardson extrapolation. Then, `V` is updated via a Hamiltonian flow: `V ← V + τ H(V)`, where `H(V)(s) = α log ∫_A exp(q̂_V(s,a)/α) da`. A single critic network with two heads outputs `V` and `q`, combined as `Q = V + q` for policy updates. Training is off-policy, sampling from a replay buffer storing these transitions, with entropy regularization in the objective `J(π)=E[∫_0^∞ e^{-βt}(r - α log π(a_t|s_t)) dt]`.

## Key Results
- CT-SAC and CT-TD3 substantially outperform existing continuous-time methods (e.g., FQI, ODE-based) and strong discrete-time baselines (SAC, TD3, PPO, TRPO, CPPO, q-Learning) on DeepMind Control Suite tasks.
- On a real-world minute-resolution trading task across 20 tickers, CT-SAC achieves 21% profit over a quarter—nearly double the second-best method—demonstrating robustness to irregular time steps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The decoupling prevents Q-function collapse by separating the learning of the advantage-rate `q` and the value `V`, avoiding the coupled optimization pitfalls of prior methods. The Hamiltonian flow update for `V` uses a non-expansive operator under the soft maximum, maintaining informativeness even for small step sizes `τ`, which stabilizes learning despite non-contractive dynamics. The single-critic parameterization `Q = V + q` simplifies implementation while preserving theoretical guarantees, as `q` becomes small near optimality, reducing representation conflicts. Together, these elements allow effective off-policy training with irregular time steps without relying on martingale constraints that are fragile in practice.

## Foundational Learning
- **Continuous-Time RL**: Models decisions at irregular intervals with holding times `u`. *Why needed*: Real-world applications like finance or robotics have event-driven timing. *Quick check*: Verify `u` is sampled from task-specific ranges (e.g., `[1,20]` minutes in trading).
- **Decoupled Critic**: Separates `q` and `V` updates to avoid collapse. *Why needed*: Coupled updates can lead to degeneracy in infinite-horizon settings. *Quick check*: Ensure `q` is estimated with fixed `V` before updating `V`.
- **Hamiltonian Flow**: Updates `V` via an ODE step `V ← V + τ H(V)`. *Why needed*: Provides stable gradient-like updates without contraction requirements. *Quick check*: Monitor `V` norms; `τ` should be small (e.g., 0.01-0.04).
- **Diffusion Generators**: Used to compute `q̂_V_u` from short-horizon expectations. *Why needed*: Approximates the infinitesimal generator for continuous-time Bellman equations. *Quick check*: Validate `q̂_V_u` formula matches Equation 28.
- **Off-Policy Replay Buffer**: Stores transitions `(s, a, r, s', u)` for experience reuse. *Why needed*: Improves sample efficiency in continuous-time with sparse events. *Quick check*: Confirm buffer samples include diverse `u` values.
- **Entropy Regularization**: Adds `-α log π(a|s)` to reward for exploration. *Why needed*: Prevents premature convergence in stochastic policies. *Quick check*: Check temperature `α` is tuned per task.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Replay buffer → Sample batch → Fix `V` → Compute `q̂_V_u` (diffusion estimator) → Update `V` via `V ← V + τ H(V)` (Hamiltonian) → Update policy using `Q = V + q` → Collect new transitions. Critic network: shared backbone with two heads (value `V(s)`, advantage-rate `q(s,a)`), combined as `Q(s,a) = V(s) + q(s,a)`.
- **Critical path**: Data collection (irregular `u`) → `q` estimation (using current `V`) → `V` update (Hamiltonian flow) → Policy gradient (using `Q`) → Iteration. Decoupling ensures `q` and `V` are not optimized jointly, avoiding instability.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Single critic reduces parameters but risks gradient conflict between `V` and `q` heads; `τ` balances step size and discretization error; `u` range trades bias (large `u`) vs. variance (small `u`) in `q̂_V_u`; Richardson extrapolation reduces bias but adds complexity.
- **Failure signatures**: `V` norm explosion (τ too large); `q` near-zero everywhere (inactive advantage, τ too small or α mis-tuned); high variance in `q̂_V_u` (u_min too small); single-critic collapse where `V` absorbs all updates (track separate losses).
- **First experiments**: 1. Train CT-SAC on `cheetah-run` with `u∈[1,20]` steps; verify learning curve exceeds SAC baseline. 2. Run on trading task; confirm profit ~$3.5k per 2 weeks. 3. Sweep `τ` (0.01, 0.02, 0.04) and monitor `V`/`q` losses for stability diagnostics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Optimal scaling constant `c` in `Q = V + c·q` for different task families; impacts stability and convergence.
- **Open Question 2**: Use of higher-order numerical integrators (e.g., Runge-Kutta) for Hamiltonian ODE to improve sample efficiency and robustness over Euler forward.
- **Open Question 3**: Robustness of convergence theory to violations of smoothness assumptions (e.g., discontinuous rewards or dynamics in contact-rich robotics or market microstructure).
- **Open Question 4**: Handling heavy-tailed or near-zero holding times (e.g., high-frequency trading); condition `E[1/U] < ∞` may break with small `u_min`.
- **Open Question 5**: Approximation errors from single-critic `Q=V+q` versus separate networks; potential gradient conflicts when `q` is large early in training.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter search spaces for baselines are incomplete; full ranges only in codebase, hindering fair comparisons.
- Trading environment specifics (feature engineering, constraints) are omitted, relying on codebase implementation.
- Network architecture details (layer sizes, activations) for `V` and `q` heads are not described; critical for `Q=V+q` stability.
- Richardson extrapolation may include hidden stabilizers not documented.
- Evaluation limited to DM Control Suite and one trading scenario; generalizability uncertain.
- Theoretical assumptions (e.g., C^2 regularity) may not hold with function approximation or real-world discontinuities.
