# I Dropped a Neural Net

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19845
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19845
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Recovered the exact ordering of 48 shuffled residual blocks in a ResNet using only 97 unlabelled linear layers and 10 000 training datapoints, achieving zero training MSE.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles a massive combinatorial reconstruction problem: given the disconnected linear layers of a 48‑block ResNet and a modest dataset, it must rediscover the original permutation of blocks and their internal input‑output projection pairs. The solution splits into two stages. First, a diagonal dominance ratio derived from the product of candidate output and input projection matrices cleanly separates true pairs from mismatches, enabling optimal bipartite matching. Second, a seed ordering—either from data‑dependent delta‑norm or data‑free Frobenius norms—is refined by greedy adjacent‑swap hill climbing to minimize prediction error. The pipeline recovers the exact original architecture with zero MSE.

## Method Summary
Reconstruction proceeds in pairing and ordering phases. For pairing, every candidate (Win, Wout) computes M = Wout·Win and the score d = |tr(M)| / ||M||_F. Correct pairs yield high scores (1.76–3.28), incorrect pairs low (≤0.58). The resulting 48×48 score matrix is solved by the Hungarian algorithm to recover the 48 matched projection pairs. For ordering, each matched block is characterized by a scalar: either the empirical delta‑norm (mean squared residual activation over the 10 000 inputs) or the Frobenius norm of its output projection Wout. A seed permutation is obtained by sorting blocks according to this scalar. Starting from this seed, greedy adjacent‑swap hill climbing repeatedly swaps neighboring blocks and accepts any swap that reduces the full‑dataset MSE against the original predictions. The process stops after a full sweep with no improvements, yielding the exact original order and zero MSE.

## Key Results
- Perfect bipartite matching of input‑output projection pairs achieved via diagonal dominance ratio and the Hungarian algorithm.
- Exact recovery of the 48‑block permutation using greedy hill climbing, resulting in zero training MSE on the provided dataset.
- Diagonal dominance scores exhibit a clear gap: correct pairs lie in [1.76, 3.28] while all incorrect pairs score ≤0.58.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Two core insights enable the reconstruction. (1) Dynamic isometry during training causes the product of a correctly paired input and output projection to possess a dominant negative diagonal; this yields a high diagonal dominance ratio that reliably separates true pairs from mismatches. (2) The residual network’s predictions are highly sensitive to block order; even small misorderings cause significant MSE, creating a landscape where greedy adjacent swaps from a reasonably good seed efficiently converge to the global optimum. The Frobenius‑norm seed, despite being data‑free, already positions most blocks near their correct locations, making the local search effective.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dynamic Isometry**: Assumption: The network exhibits dynamic isometry during training. Why needed: Stabilizes gradient flow and produces the negative diagonal pattern in paired projection products. Quick check: Compute singular values of weight matrices during training; they should remain near 1.
- **Diagonal Dominance Ratio**: Why needed: Provides a noise‑robust scalar that sharply discriminates correct from incorrect pairings. Quick check: Compute d for a known correct pair and an arbitrary incorrect pair; expect a large margin.
- **Hungarian Algorithm**: Why needed: Solves the bipartite matching problem optimally in polynomial time, guaranteeing the best pairing under the score matrix. Quick check: Apply to a small cost matrix and verify total cost is minimized.
- **Greedy Hill Climbing**: Assumption: The seed permutation is sufficiently close to the global optimum. Why needed: Explores permutation space via local swaps; efficient for near‑optimal seeds. Quick check: On a toy permutation, perform adjacent swaps until no improvement; if the seed is close, the final order should match the target.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (x ∈ ℝ⁴⁸) → [Block₁ → Block₂ → … → Block₄₈] → Final Linear Layer (1×48) → Output.  
Each Block contains: Win (96×48) → ReLU → Wout (48×96) → residual addition (adds block input to output).

**Critical path:** The forward pass through correctly ordered blocks is essential; any permutation breaks residual connections and corrupts predictions. Restoring the proper sequence is the ultimate goal.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Pairing relies on dynamic isometry; deviations (e.g., poor initialization, certain regularizers) may blur score separation.  
- Delta‑norm seed is data‑dependent and more accurate but requires a representative dataset; Frobenius‑norm seed is data‑free but yields higher initial MSE.  
- Greedy swaps are computationally cheap (O(n²) evaluations) but may get stuck in local minima; global search is infeasible for n=48.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Pairing: Overlap in diagonal dominance scores; histogram shows mixed ranges; Hungarian matching yields incorrect pairs.  
- Ordering: Greedy hill climbing plateaus with non‑zero MSE after many swaps, indicating a suboptimal local minimum or poor seed.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Score separation test: Compute the full 48×48 diagonal dominance matrix and confirm correct pairs all exceed 1.76 while incorrect pairs remain below 0.58, with no overlap.  
2. Data scaling for delta‑norm: Downsample the 10 000 inputs (e.g., 100, 500, 1000) and re‑compute delta‑norm seed ordering; report recovery rate and number of swaps needed.  
3. Random initialization resilience: Start hill climbing from 20 random permutations; measure fraction reaching zero MSE and average iterations; compare with proposed seeds.

## Limitations
- The diagonal dominance property assumes dynamic isometry, which may fail for other architectures or training regimens.  
- The data‑dependent delta‑norm seed requires a representative dataset (10 000 points) and may degrade with smaller samples.  
- The greedy ordering heuristic has no optimality guarantee and could converge to a local optimum, though it succeeded on this 48‑block network.  
- Generalization to different ResNet depths or data distributions remains unverified.

## Confidence
- **Pairing (diagonal dominance + Hungarian):** High
- **Ordering (seed + greedy swaps):** Medium
- **Overall zero‑MSE recovery:** High

## Next Checks
1. **Training regimen sensitivity:** Train ResNet-48 with different optimizers (SGD with momentum vs. Adam) and learning rate schedules; recompute diagonal dominance scores to verify the separation gap remains wide, and confirm exact ordering recovery.
2. **Weight perturbation robustness:** Add Gaussian noise of varying magnitudes (e.g., 1%, 5%, 10% of weight standard deviation) to the recovered weight matrices and re-run the pairing and ordering; measure the failure rate and number of additional swaps needed.
3. **Cross-architecture generalization:** Apply the reconstruction pipeline to a Wide ResNet (e.g., depth 48, width multiplier 2) and a ResNeXt block; evaluate whether the diagonal dominance property holds and if the greedy hill climbing still achieves exact recovery.
