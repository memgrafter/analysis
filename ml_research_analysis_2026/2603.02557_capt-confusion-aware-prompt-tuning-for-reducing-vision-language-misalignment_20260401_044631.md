# CAPT: Confusion-Aware Prompt Tuning for Reducing Vision-Language Misalignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02557
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02557
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CAPT achieves state-of-the-art few-shot performance (base 87.41%, novel 80.90%, harmonic mean 83.90%) and resolves 50.72% of confusable sample pairs across 11 benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
CAPT addresses the systematic misalignment in vision-language models like CLIP, where specific categories are persistently confused (e.g., "terrier" as "bulldog"), revealing limited fine-grained discrimination. The method learns from the model's own misalignments through confusion-aware prompt tuning. It constructs a Confusion Bank from misclassified samples and employs a Semantic Confusion Miner to identify global inter-class confusion patterns, alongside a Sample Confusion Miner to retrieve representative confusing instances and extract local features. These multi-granularity cues are fused via a Mixture-of-Experts, and prompt token clustering yields compact, discriminative prompts. In a 16-shot setting, CAPT sets new state-of-the-art results on 11 datasets and demonstrates strong cross-dataset transfer and domain generalization.

## Method Summary
CAPT introduces a pipeline that explicitly leverages model misalignments. First, a Confusion Bank is populated with misclassified samples from a base dataset using pseudo-ground-truth labels. The Semantic Confusion Miner analyzes bank statistics to capture global inter-class confusion patterns, while the Sample Confusion Miner retrieves locally confusing samples and processes them with a Diff-Manner Adapter that blends global and local cues. A Multi-Granularity Discrepancy Expert (MoE) dynamically fuses these two streams of confusion information. Finally, prompt tokens are clustered to form a compact, discriminative prompt ensemble. This end-to-end approach fine-tunes prompts to target the model's specific weaknesses in fine-grained discrimination.

## Key Results
- Achieves SOTA base (87.41%), novel (80.90%), and harmonic mean (83.90%) accuracy on 11 benchmark datasets in a 16-shot setting.
- Resolves 50.72% of confusable sample pairs by reducing systematic misalignments.
- Demonstrates robust cross-dataset transfer and domain generalization without additional training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CAPT works by directly targeting the root cause of performance drops in fine-grained recognition: the model's inherent confusion between semantically similar categories. Instead of treating misclassifications as noise, it treats them as a learning signal. The Confusion Bank provides a persistent memory of failure modes, the miners extract both macro (class-level) and micro (instance-level) confusion patterns, and the MoE fuses these complementary views to generate prompts that explicitly disambiguate confusing categories. This closed-loop process steers the model toward finer visual distinctions it originally lacked.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Confusion Bank -> Semantic Confusion Miner & Sample Confusion Miner -> Diff-Manner Adapter -> Multi-Granularity Discrepancy Expert (MoE) -> Prompt Token Clustering

**Critical path**: Misclassified samples collected in the bank feed both miners; their outputs are blended by the adapter, fused by the MoE, and finally compressed into clustered prompt tokens used at inference.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Complexity vs. performance: The multi-component pipeline improves accuracy but increases computational overhead.  
- Pseudo-ground-truth noise: Using model predictions as labels for the Confusion Bank risks amplifying errors, requiring careful filtering.  
- Global vs. local balance: Over-reliance on global patterns may miss instance-specific nuances; the adapter mediates this tradeoff.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Degraded performance if the Confusion Bank becomes noisy or unrepresentative (e.g., from poor pseudo-labels).  
- Overfitting to specific confusion pairs not generalizing to new datasets.  
- High latency due to MoE routing and sample retrieval during training.

**First experiments**:  
1. Test CAPT on a different vision-language backbone (e.g., ALIGN or Florence) to evaluate generalizability beyond CLIP.  
2. Evaluate on extreme few-shot regimes (1-shot) to assess data efficiency and bank construction stability.  
3. Measure performance on out-of-distribution domains (e.g., satellite or medical imagery) to probe domain robustness.
