# Can We Change the Stroke Size for Easier Diffusion?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26783
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26783
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MultiStroke introduces a stroke-size control operator that reduces high-frequency regression difficulty in diffusion models, yielding substantial improvements: on CIFAR-10, FID drops from 20.16 to 14.43 and one-class score rises from 46.3 to 58.2; on CelebA-HQ with 10 steps, FID falls from 183.71 to 110.44 and high-band SNR jumps from -32.73 dB to -1.65 dB.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models face challenges at high timesteps where the signal-to-noise ratio is low, making pixel-level predictions difficult and leading to artifact accumulation. This paper proposes MultiStroke, a method that systematically varies the effective stroke size (coarseness) across timesteps by applying a pooling-plus-upsampling operator. This operator attenuates high frequencies early while preserving coarse structure, both during training and sampling. Under compute-matched settings, MultiStroke consistently improves sample quality, especially in few-step sampling, by reducing target complexity and contracting detail energy in the reverse process. The approach maintains the same population minimizer as standard diffusion but reshapes the optimization landscape to focus on coarser components, leading to easier learning and fewer high-frequency artifacts.

## Method Summary
MultiStroke modifies the diffusion process with a linear mixing operator \(A_t = (1-w_t)I + w_t S_{k_{\text{max}}}\), where \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\) is a stroke operator comprising \(k_{\text{max}}\times k_{\text{max}}\) average pooling followed by nearest-neighbor upsampling (an orthogonal projector). The mixing weight \(w_t\) follows a schedule: zero for the initial "rough" fraction \(f_{\text{rough}}\) of timesteps, then linearly ramping to \(w_{\text{max}}\) at the final timestep. During training, both the network input \(x_t\) and the noise target \(\varepsilon\) are transformed by \(A_t\), and the loss is \(\mathbb{E}[\|A_t(\varepsilon_\theta(A_t(x_t), t, y)) - A_t(\varepsilon)\|^2]\). During sampling, the current state \(x_t\) is preprocessed by \(A_t\), and the injected noise is mixed as \(\tilde{\eta}_t = (1-w_{t-1})\eta_t + w_{t-1}S_{k_{\text{max}}}(\eta_t)\). The method is compute-neutral, adding minimal overhead.

## Key Results
- **CIFAR-10 (40k training steps, 500 sampling steps):** FID reduced from 20.16 to 14.43; one-class score increased from 46.3 to 58.2.
- **CelebA-HQ (10 sampling steps):** FID dropped from 183.71 to 110.44; high-band SNR dramatically improved from -32.73 dB to -1.65 dB.
- **Qualitative:** MultiStroke samples exhibit significantly fewer high-frequency artifacts and cleaner coarse structures, especially under tight step budgets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Target Complexity Reduction via Frequency-Weighted Regression
Applying \(A_t\) to both predictions and targets down-weights high-frequency components in the training objective when \(w_t>0\). The stroke operator \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\) orthogonal-projects onto a block-constant subspace, formally decomposing the image into coarse (\(Q_c\)) and detail (\(Q_d\)) parts: \(A_t x = Q_c x + (1-w_t) Q_d x\). This scales the detail-norm squared by \((1-w_t)^2\), reducing the effective multiresolution complexity measure \(C_{k,s}(x)\). Consequently, the regression problem at high timesteps becomes easier because the network is not forced to predict fine details from highly noisy inputs. The population minimizer remains unchanged, but the optimization path emphasizes coarser components.

### Mechanism 2: Sampling-Time Detail-Energy Contraction
During sampling, the reverse step is approximated by an affine surrogate \(\mu_t(A_t(x_t), \hat{\varepsilon}_t) = M_t A_t(x_t) + b_t\). The detail subspace (\(Q_d\)) of the next state then follows a recursion where propagated detail and injected noise are explicitly scaled by \((1-w_t)^2\) and \((1-w_{t-1})^2\) respectively. This contraction limits the accumulation of high-frequency errors and reduces the variance of injected detail, thereby suppressing high-frequency artifacts that typically plague diffusion at low SNR.

### Mechanism 3: Invariant Population Minimizer with Altered Optimization Path
Since \(A_t\) is invertible for \(w_t < 1\), conditioning on \(A_t(x_t)\) is equivalent to conditioning on \(x_t\). Thus the Bayes predictor in \(\varepsilon\)-space remains \(\varepsilon_\theta^* = \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon|x_t,t,y]\). However, the loss directly penalizes \(A_t(\varepsilon_\theta)\) versus \(A_t(\varepsilon)\), which reweights residuals toward coarse components. The asymptotic target is unchanged, but the optimization trajectory differs, often leading to better generalization under limited compute or steps.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Orthogonal Projections and Coarse/Detail Subspace Decompositions**  
*Why needed:* To rigorously analyze how \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\) decomposes the image space into block-constant (coarse) and within-block variation (detail) subspaces, enabling exact algebra for \(A_t\) and the transformed noise.  
*Quick check:* Given \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\) is an orthogonal projection, what are the explicit formulas for \(Q_c\) and \(Q_d\) and why does \(Q_d S_{k_{\text{max}}} = 0\)?

**Concept: Fourier Attenuation Envelopes of Discrete Pooling Operators**  
*Why needed:* To understand which spatial frequencies are suppressed by \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\) and how \(A_t\) scales mode-wise energy, linking the linear operator to its spectral behavior.  
*Quick check:* For pooling stride \(k=2\), what is the attenuation ratio \(\rho_2(\omega)\) at the highest representable Nyquist frequency? What does this imply for \(A_t\)'s effect when \(w_t>0\)?

**Concept: Affine Surrogate Models for Reverse Diffusion Dynamics**  
*Why needed:* To derive the detail-energy recursion by approximating the reverse mean as linear in the smoothed state, making the contraction effect explicit and analyzable.  
*Quick check:* In the surrogate \(\mu_t(A_t(x_t), \hat{\varepsilon}_t) = M_t A_t(x_t) + b_t\), what do the blocks \(M_{t,dc}\) and \(M_{t,dd}\) represent, and why does \(w_t\) only scale the latter and the injected noise?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Stroke operator \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\) (average pooling + nearest upsampling) -> Mixing map \(A_t(z) = (1-w_t)z + w_t S_{k_{\text{max}}}(z)\) -> Training: minimize \(\|A_t(\varepsilon_\theta(A_t(x_t))) - A_t(\varepsilon)\|^2\) -> Sampling: \(x_{t-1} = \mu_t(A_t(x_t), \hat{\varepsilon}_t) + \sigma_t \tilde{\eta}_t\) with \(\tilde{\eta}_t = (1-w_{t-1})\eta_t + w_{t-1}S_{k_{\text{max}}}(\eta_t)\).

**Critical path:**
1. Choose hyperparameters: \(k_{\text{max}}\) (pooling size), \(f_{\text{rough}}\) (fraction of timesteps with increasing \(w_t\)), \(w_{\text{max}}\) (maximum mixing weight).
2. Training: For each batch, sample \(t\) and compute \(x_t\) via forward diffusion; compute \(x^{\text{ms}}_t = A_t(x_t)\) and \(\tilde{\varepsilon} = A_t(\varepsilon)\); predict \(\varepsilon_\theta(x^{\text{ms}}_t, t, y)\) and minimize \(\|A_t(\varepsilon_\theta) - \tilde{\varepsilon}\|^2\).
3. Sampling: Initialize \(x_T = A_T(\mathcal{N}(0,I))\). For \(t = T \downarrow 1\): compute \(x^{\text{ms}}_t = A_t(x_t)\), predict \(\hat{\varepsilon}_t = \varepsilon_\theta(x^{\text{ms}}_t, t, y)\), compute \(\mu_t\) from DDPM, sample \(\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\), form \(\tilde{\eta}_t\) using \(w_{t-1}\), set \(x_{t-1} = \mu_t + \sigma_t \tilde{\eta}_t\).
4. For few-step sampling, use a subsequence of timesteps and map \(w\) to the destination index appropriately.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- \(w_{\text{max}}\): Larger values increase attenuation but risk under-specifying details; must stay \(<1\) for invertibility.
- \(f_{\text{rough}}\): Larger fraction extends the rough regime; too large may cause persistent coarse bias even after \(w_t=0\).
- Compute overhead is negligible; the dominant cost is the U-Net evaluation.

**Failure signatures:**
- Aggressive stroke control (high \(w_{\text{max}}\), large \(f_{\text{rough}}\)): Backgrounds remain blurry or missing details even after fine steps.
- Misaligned \(S_{k_{\text{max}}}\): Image dimensions not divisible by \(k_{\text{max}}\) can cause checkerboard artifacts or uneven attenuation.
- Training-inference mismatch: Using MultiStroke only during training or only during sampling degrades performance.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Bucketed training loss:** Compare loss curves across timestep buckets (low/mid1/mid2/high) between MultiStroke and DDPM; expect consistent reduction in buckets where \(w_t>0\).
2. **Compute-limited FID/OCS:** Train both models for the same number of steps (e.g., 40k on CIFAR-10) and sample with identical compute budget (500 steps); MultiStroke should achieve lower FID and higher one-class score.
3. **High-band SNR under tight sampling:** Sample high-resolution datasets (CelebA-HQ, FFHQ) with only 10–20 steps; MultiStroke should yield much higher high-band SNR (less high-frequency noise) and lower FID than DDPM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Generalization beyond images:** Can MultiStroke be applied to non-image modalities (audio, video) or latent diffusion architectures? The paper does not claim universality, and the spatial frequency decomposition via pooling may not transfer directly.
- **Mitigating bias-stability trade-off:** How can the background under-specification caused by aggressive stroke control be alleviated? The paper suggests background post-processing but lacks a solution.
- **Optimal roughness schedule:** What is the theoretically optimal schedule \(\{w_t\}\) that balances bias and variance? The paper uses a linear schedule without principled derivation.
- **Compatibility with advanced techniques:** How does MultiStroke interact with classifier-free guidance, fast samplers (e.g., DPM-Solver), or consistency distillation? The paper only evaluates basic DDPM.

## Limitations

- Reproducibility details for one-class evaluation (splits, classifier architecture) are unspecified, introducing risk.
- Aggressive stroke control introduces a bias-stability trade-off, potentially leaving backgrounds blurry.
- The orthogonal projection requires integer \(k_{\text{max}}\); non-divisible image dimensions may cause artifacts.
- The affine surrogate assumption for reverse dynamics may not hold globally, limiting theoretical guarantees.

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (target complexity reduction): **High** (rigorous spectral analysis).
- Mechanism 2 (detail-energy contraction): **Medium** (relies on local affine approximation).
- Mechanism 3 (invariant minimizer): **High** (formal proof).
- Empirical gains (FID, OCS, SNR): **Medium** (strong results but minor reproducibility uncertainties).
