# A Mathematical Theory of Agency and Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22519
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22519
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Introduces bi-predictability (P), a bounded mutual information measure for agent-environment coupling; shows current AI has agency but lacks self-monitoring (intelligence); proposes IDT to detect degradation earlier and more reliably than reward.

## Executive Summary
The paper argues that current AI systems suffer from silent degradation because they lack a feedback metric that directly measures the effectiveness of information coupling between observations, actions, and outcomes. The authors propose a theoretical framework based on bi-predictability (P), a mathematically bounded measure of shared information in the interaction loop. They prove that in classical systems P ≤ 0.5, and that agency (action selection) further lowers P and introduces predictive asymmetry (ΔH). Empirically, they demonstrate the bound in a double pendulum (P≈0.48) and in RL agents (P≈0.33, ΔH≈-0.56). They also introduce an Information Digital Twin (IDT) that monitors P and ΔH in real time.

The IDT is shown to detect perturbations (e.g., action noise, observation corruption) with 89.3% success rate compared to 44% for reward-based monitoring, and with a latency 4.4 times faster. The authors conclude that while existing AI exhibits agency (choice, effect, asymmetry), it falls short of intelligence because it does not self-monitor its coupling and adapt its state-action-outcome scope. The work provides a foundation for building more robust, self-aware AI systems.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces bi-predictability (P) as a measure of information shared among state (S), action (A), and next state (S') in an agent-environment interaction. Formally, P = MI(S,A;S') / (H(S)+H(A)+H(S')), where MI is mutual information and H denotes Shannon entropy. The authors prove that for classical finite-discrete systems, P is bounded above by 0.5, and that the presence of agency (non-trivial action selection) typically yields lower P values and a predictive asymmetry ΔH = H(S'|S,A) - H(S,A|S') ≠ 0. To operationalize these metrics, they propose an Information Digital Twin (IDT) that continuously estimates P and ΔH over sliding windows using discretized entropy estimators. The IDT compares these estimates to a baseline (mean ± 3σ) to detect anomalies in the coupling.

The methodology is evaluated in three settings: a deterministic double pendulum (passive system), reinforcement learning agents (HalfCheetah with SAC/PPO), and LLM-based dialogue (Llama-3.1 vs. Claude). Perturbations such as action noise, observation corruption, and contradictory prompts are injected. Detection performance (success rate and latency) is benchmarked against reward-based monitoring. The experiments demonstrate that P and ΔH provide earlier and more sensitive indicators of interaction degradation, supporting the theoretical claims and the IDT’s utility for monitoring AI systems.

## Key Results
- Bi-predictability (P) is theoretically bounded by 0.5 in classical information theory, and agency (action selection) typically yields lower values (e.g., RL agents achieve P≈0.33).
- The Information Digital Twin (IDT) monitoring P and ΔH detects 89.3% of perturbations, significantly higher than the 44% detection rate of reward-based monitoring, and does so 4.4× faster (median 42 vs. 184 windows).
- Empirical results confirm the classical bound: a passive double pendulum reaches P≈0.48, while RL agents show predictive asymmetry (ΔH ≈ -0.56), demonstrating that current AI possesses agency but lacks the self-monitoring required for intelligence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Classical bi-predictability (P) is fundamentally bounded by 0.5 due to mutual information constraints under Shannon entropy.
- **Mechanism:** The numerator \(MI(S,A;S')\) cannot exceed half the total capacity \(H(S) + H(A) + H(S')\) because \(MI \leq \min(H(S,A), H(S'))\) and the sum constraint forces the maximum when \(H(S,A) = H(S') = C/2\). This is a structural bound from information theory.
- **Core assumption:** Variables are discrete with finite entropies; classical (non-quantum) information theory applies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "prove regime-dependent bounds: P can reach unity in quantum systems, P ≤ 0.5 in classical systems"
  - [section 2.1] "Under classical (Shannon) information, we obtain: 0 ≤ P ≤ 1/2"
  - [section 8.2] Formal derivation showing \(P_{\text{max}} = 1/2\) from minimizing \(\min(X,Y)\) under \(X+Y=C\).
- **Break condition:** If the interaction uses quantum correlations (entanglement), the bound can approach 1. Non-finite entropies (e.g., continuous differential entropy) are out of scope per section 8.4.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Introducing agency (action selection) creates persistent predictive asymmetry (\(\Delta H \neq 0\)), distinguishing active from passive systems.
- **Mechanism:** Action adds internal degrees of freedom (\(H(A) > 0\)). Typically, \(H(S'|S,A)\) (forward uncertainty) differs from \(H(S,A|S')\) (backward ambiguity) due to stochastic policies, environmental openness, and many-to-one mappings. This yields \(\Delta H = H_f - H_b \neq 0\).
- **Core assumption:** The agent's action distribution is stochastic (\(H(A|S) > 0\)) and the environment is not fully invertible.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "lower with agency (action selection)" and "predictive asymmetry (ΔH ≈ -0.56)"
  - [section 4.1] Agency conditions: Choice (\(H(A|S)>0\)), Effect (\(MI(A;S'|S)>0\)), Asymmetry (\(|\Delta H|>0\)).
  - [section 5.1.2] RL agents show \(P \approx 0.33\), \(\Delta H = -0.56 \pm 0.22\), contrasting double pendulum's \(\Delta H \approx 0\).
- **Break condition:** A deterministic, fully invertible agent-environment dyad might show \(\Delta H \approx 0\) despite having an action channel (e.g., a perfect model with bijective dynamics).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Monitoring P and ΔH in real time (via an IDT) detects interaction degradation earlier and more reliably than reward-based monitoring.
- **Mechanism:** P and ΔH track coupling integrity at each transition, while reward integrates outcomes over many steps. Coupling breaks before cumulative returns degrade, giving earlier signal. ΔH also diagnoses failure mode (environment opacity vs. agent indistinguishability).
- **Core assumption:** Degradation first affects local state-action-outcome coupling before affecting long-horizon reward.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "detects 89.3% of perturbations versus 44% for reward-based methods, and does so 4.4× faster"
  - [section 5.1.3] "IDT detected 89.3 ± 15.1% of perturbations, compared with 44.0 ± 26.1% using reward"
  - [section 5.1.4] "Median detection latency is 42 windows for IDT versus 184 for reward"
- **Break condition:** Perturbations that alter reward without disrupting immediate coupling (e.g., external resets that preserve dynamics) may not be flagged by P/ΔH.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Mutual information and entropy (Shannon/quantum).
  - *Why needed here:* P, ΔH, H_f, H_b are defined via these quantities; understanding their properties (e.g., non-negativity, chain rules) is essential to grasp bounds and computations.
  - *Quick check question:* "Given MI(S;A) = 0.5, H(S)=1.0, H(A)=1.5, can you compute an upper bound for MI(S,A;S')?"
- **Concept:** Dynamical systems and state transitions.
  - *Why needed here:* The (S, A, S') loop models temporal evolution; properties like determinism, chaos, and invertibility affect P and ΔH (e.g., double pendulum section).
  - *Quick check question:* "Why does a chaotic double pendulum still achieve P≈0.48? What does that imply about the role of deterministic dynamics?"
- **Concept:** Agent-environment interaction loops in RL/LLMs.
  - *Why needed here:* To map real systems (e.g., HalfCheetah, dialogue) to the (S,A,S') formalism and interpret metrics in context.
  - *Quick check question:* "In the LLM dialogue setup, what constitutes S (context), A (student response), and S' (teacher prompt)? How might token-frequency discretization affect P?"

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Agent loop (S, A, S') → IDT sidecar (stream copy) → P Calculator (P, ΔH estimation) → P Controller (baseline threshold) → Modulation pathways (observation filtering, action dampening)

### Critical path
Stream acquisition → windowed joint distribution estimation → compute MI(S,A;S'), H_f, H_b → derive P, ΔH → threshold check → if exceeded, signal modulation

### Design tradeoffs
- Discretization granularity: More bins capture detail but increase variance; too few bins flatten distributions (paper uses 3 bins for RL, token frequencies for LLM).
- Window size vs. responsiveness: Longer windows stabilize estimates but delay detection; shorter windows are noisy (paper uses 300 steps for RL, turn-based for LLM).
- False positive rate: 3σ threshold balances sensitivity/specificity; may need tuning per domain.

### Failure signatures
- P collapse: Sudden drop indicates decoupling (e.g., observation noise).
- ΔH divergence: Large positive (H_f↑) suggests environment becomes unpredictable; large negative (H_b↑) suggests agent's actions become ambiguous.
- High H_f: Environment response uncertain (e.g., added force); high H_b: Agent indistinguishability (e.g., observation noise collapsing internal states).

### First 3 experiments
1. Double pendulum baseline: Verify P≈0.48, ΔH≈0 across chaotic regimes; confirms classical bound without agency.
2. RL perturbation detection: Train SAC/PPO on HalfCheetah; inject 1% action noise; confirm IDT detects within ~42 windows vs. reward at 184.
3. LLM dialogue drift: Run Llama-3.1 vs. Claude; inject contradiction; check P drops and H_b rises immediately; compare to LLM-judge scores (delayed).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Reflexive modulation engineering** – How can domain-specific mechanisms be built to adjust an agent’s observation, action, or outcome spaces when P deviates? (Basis: authors state control laws are a future engineering challenge; unresolved due to lack of algorithmic details; evidence needed: concrete architectures demonstrating automatic restructuring triggered by real‑time P monitoring.)
2. **P Controller stability** – What are the stability conditions and control parameters for the IDT’s P Controller to avoid instability or oscillations? (Basis: control laws are unspecified; unresolved due to missing control‑theoretic analysis; evidence needed: formal analysis of P signal dynamics and empirical demonstrations of a stable controller across agent types.)
3. **Token‑frequency limitations for LLMs** – Does computing P from token frequencies fail to capture semantic/pragmatic degradations that richer representations (e.g., discourse graphs) would detect? (Basis: LLM experiments show P correlates with structure but not always with judge scores; unresolved because token stats may miss subtle drifts; evidence needed: comparative experiments using structured dialogue representations.)
4. **Agency‑specific P bound** – Is the classical bound P ≤ 0.5 valid for all classical stochastic systems, or are there edge cases (non‑Markovian, redundant, deterministic cycles) where it weakens? (Basis: derivation assumes finite discrete variables; observed RL P≈0.33 is typical but not proven maximal; unresolved due to lack of systematic exploration; evidence needed: analytical tighter bound or empirical maximization across a wide class of agentic processes.)
5. **P as training objective** – Can incorporating P as an auxiliary loss improve robustness by encouraging tighter coupling? (Basis: paper frames P only for monitoring; gap between agency and intelligence suggests learning with P might help; speculative; evidence needed: training agents with P‑based regularization and evaluating perturbation resilience.)

## Limitations
- Theoretical bound P ≤ 0.5 only holds for finite‑discrete Shannon entropy; continuous or quantum settings are not covered.
- Empirical validation spans only three domains (double pendulum, MuJoCo RL, LLM dialogue), leaving generalization to other architectures and environments uncertain.
- Discretization and window size choices strongly affect P and ΔH, yet the paper provides no sensitivity analysis or selection guidelines.

## Confidence
- Classical bi-predictability bound (P ≤ 0.5): High
- Agency induces predictive asymmetry and lowers P: Medium
- IDT perturbation detection (earlier/more reliable): Medium
- Agency vs. intelligence distinction: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Bound robustness test:** Compute P across a diverse suite of classical stochastic dynamical systems (e.g., Markov chains, stochastic differential equations) with varying discretization granularity to verify the ≤0.5 bound and measure any violations.
2. **Real-world degradation benchmark:** Deploy IDT on physical robots undergoing natural wear and on dialogue systems facing genuine distribution shifts, comparing detection accuracy and latency against modern unsupervised anomaly detection methods (e.g., predictive coding, contrastive learning).
3. **Self-monitoring intelligence link:** Train RL agents with an auxiliary loss that explicitly maximizes P (or minimizes ΔH drift) and evaluate whether they maintain higher returns under perturbations and adapt faster to novel tasks than agents without such self-monitoring.
