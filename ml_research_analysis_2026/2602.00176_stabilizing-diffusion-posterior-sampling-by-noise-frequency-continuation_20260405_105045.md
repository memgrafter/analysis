# Stabilizing Diffusion Posterior Sampling by Noise--Frequency Continuation
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.00176
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00176
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces a noise-frequency continuation framework that stabilizes diffusion posterior sampling, achieving state-of-the-art results (e.g., up to +5 dB PSNR on motion deblurring) by enforcing measurement consistency only within a noise-dependent frequency band.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) for solving inverse problems often fails to recover fine image details because measurement guidance is applied uniformly across all noise levels. This uniform guidance causes early drift during sampling and introduces high-frequency artifacts. This paper proposes a noise-frequency continuation framework that constructs a sequence of intermediate posteriors. In each, measurement consistency is enforced only within a specific, noise-dependent frequency band. The method couples a standard diffusion predictor with a band-limited Gaussian likelihood and a multi-resolution strategy using Haar wavelets. This combination aggressively commits reliable coarse-scale corrections while conservatively adopting high-frequency details only when they become identifiable later in the sampling process. Evaluated on super-resolution, inpainting, and deblurring, the approach shows consistent improvements in PSNR and LPIPS, with significant gains on challenging tasks like motion deblurring. Ablation studies confirm that both the frequency continuation schedule and the Haar-based fusion are indispensable for the observed stability and detail recovery.

## Method Summary
The core innovation decouples the noise continuation (standard in diffusion sampling) from a *frequency continuation*. Instead of using the full measurement at every step, the method defines a **band-limited likelihood** that only considers image frequencies below a threshold ω(t), which increases as noise decreases. This threshold is controlled by a schedule ω_min → ω_max. To fuse the diffusion prior's output with the measurement-consistent update, the method employs a **Haar wavelet decomposition**. The low-frequency (LL) subband is updated aggressively with the measurement-based correction, while the high-frequency (LH, HL, HH) subbands are updated conservatively, only accepting changes that are consistent with the current noise level and the already-corrected low frequencies. This process is integrated into the DPS sampling loop, replacing the standard measurement guidance with the band-limited version and using the Haar coefficients for a stable multi-resolution fusion at each step.

## Key Results
*   Achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard linear inverse problems (super-resolution, inpainting, deblurring), with consistent gains in PSNR and LPIPS over strong baselines like DPS and DDRM.
*   Demonstrates a dramatic improvement on motion deblurring, increasing PSNR by up to 5 dB compared to prior methods.
*   Comprehensive ablations confirm the critical role of both components: removing the frequency continuation or the Haar fusion strategy leads to a severe drop in performance, highlighting their necessity for stability and detail recovery.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The failure of standard DPS is attributed to **uniform measurement guidance**. Applying the full measurement error signal at high noise levels (early steps) forces the sample to conform to inconsistent coarse structures, causing irreversible drift. The proposed framework addresses this by recognizing that **identifiability is frequency- and noise-dependent**. Low-frequency content is discernible even under high noise, so it is corrected early and aggressively. High-frequency content is only reliable at low noise levels, so its correction is deferred and applied cautiously. The **Haar fusion** operationalizes this: it commits the aggressively corrected LL band as a stable foundation, then only propagates high-frequency changes that are consistent with this foundation and the current, less noisy measurement model. This prevents the early corruption of the image's structural core and mitigates the introduction of high-frequency artifacts.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Diffusion Predictor (U-Net, pre-trained) -> Predicts x_{t-1} from x_t
Band-limited Likelihood (FFT + mask) -> Computes guidance ∇log p(y|x_t) using only frequencies < ω(t)
Haar Fusion (DWT + IDWT) -> Combines predictor output with guided update: aggressive LL fusion, conservative high-freq fusion
Sampling Loop -> Iterates: Predict, Apply Band-limited Guidance, Fuse via Haar

**Critical Path:** The interaction between the **Band-limited Likelihood** and the **Haar Fusion** is the central loop. The likelihood computes a correction signal in the frequency domain. This signal is transformed into the Haar wavelet domain. The LL band of this correction is added aggressively to the predictor's LL band. The high-frequency bands are added only if their magnitude exceeds a noise-dependent threshold, ensuring conservative adoption. The fused wavelet coefficients are then inverted to produce the updated sample.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Aggressive vs. Conservative Fusion:** Aggressively fitting the LL band risks overfitting early, but conservatively handling high-freq risks missing details. The schedule ω(t) and thresholds λ(t) balance this.
*   **Fixed vs. Adaptive Schedule:** The paper uses fixed ω_min/ω_max and λ decay. An adaptive schedule could improve robustness but adds complexity.
*   **Haar vs. Other Wavelets:** Haar is simple, orthogonal, and separable, but its blocky nature may not align with natural image statistics. Smoother wavelets might offer better sparsity.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Early High-Frequency Artifacts:** If ω(t) rises too quickly or λ thresholds are too low, high-frequency noise from the measurement may be injected prematurely, creating ringing or texture artifacts.
*   **Loss of Fine Detail:** If ω_max is too low or λ decay is too aggressive, the method may never incorporate identifiable high-frequency details from the measurement, resulting in blurry outputs.
*   **Drift / Mode Collapse:** If the aggressive LL fusion is too strong relative to the diffusion prior's weight, the sample can collapse to a measurement-consistent but implausible coarse structure early on.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How do biases in pretrained diffusion priors affect the fairness and accuracy of diffusion posterior sampling for inverse problems, and can the proposed noise-frequency continuation framework mitigate these biases?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The impact statement explicitly notes that the method "inherits the limitations and potential biases of the underlying pretrained diffusion prior, which may unevenly affect performance across demographics, domains, or imaging conditions."
- Why unresolved: The experiments are conducted only on FFHQ and ImageNet without demographic breakdown or bias analysis, leaving the extent of bias propagation and mitigation uninvestigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluate reconstruction quality across demographic subgroups in diverse datasets, and modify the frequency schedule or prior integration to reduce disparities, such as by incorporating fairness constraints or adaptive bandwidths.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the noise-frequency continuation framework be extended to non-linear inverse problems, such as phase retrieval or blind deconvolution, where the forward operator lacks a straightforward frequency-domain representation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The method is formulated and evaluated exclusively for linear forward operators (e.g., blur, downsampling) with spectral control relying on linear Fourier analysis; non-linear cases are not discussed.
- Why unresolved: Non-linear operators complicate band-limited likelihood definitions and the noise-frequency coupling, as the theoretical guarantees assume linear-Gaussian models, and the Haar fusion may not apply directly.
- What evidence would resolve it: Generalize the frequency-bandwidth concept to non-linear settings via local linearizations or alternative transforms, and validate on standard non-linear benchmarks with appropriate metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Is the Haar wavelet optimal for the multi-resolution consistency strategy, or would other wavelet bases (e.g., Daubechies, Coiflets) improve detail recovery and artifact suppression?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3.3 adopts Haar decomposition for simplicity and orthogonality but offers no comparison or theoretical justification for preferring it over other multi-resolution transforms.
- Why unresolved: Different wavelets have distinct sparsity and frequency localization properties that may better align with the band-limited likelihood and commitment rules, potentially affecting stability and fine-detail synthesis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct ablation studies replacing Haar with other orthogonal wavelet bases in the fusion step, measuring PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and visual artifact rates across tasks to identify performance differences.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How sensitive is the method to the hyperparameters of the frequency schedule (e.g., ω_min/ω_max, λ decay), and can adaptive or learned schedules enhance robustness and reduce tuning effort?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses fixed bounds (ω: 0.4→1.0, λ: 0.35→0 via cosine decay) without sensitivity analysis or adaptation mechanisms; the schedule is hand-tuned and not theoretically optimized.
- Why unresolved: Optimal schedules likely vary with degradation type, diffusion model, and noise level; fixed parameters may not generalize and could require per-task retuning, limiting practicality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Perform systematic sensitivity analysis by varying schedule parameters and recording performance metrics; develop an adaptive scheme that adjusts ω and λ during sampling based on real-time statistics like gradient norms or frequency energy.

## Limitations
*   **External Validation Gap:** The work is very recent (zero citations in the corpus) and has only been evaluated on FFHQ and ImageNet. Its generalizability to other domains (medical, satellite) and real-world noise models remains unverified.
*   **Generalizability of Frequency Heuristics:** The core assumption that low frequencies are always more reliably identifiable early may fail for inverse problems where critical discriminative information (e.g., fine texture, small text) resides in high frequencies.
*   **Implementation Complexity & Tuning:** The method introduces several new hyperparameters (frequency band schedule ω(t), conservative threshold λ(t)). The paper provides a fixed schedule, but the sensitivity to these parameters and the cost of per-task tuning are not thoroughly explored.
