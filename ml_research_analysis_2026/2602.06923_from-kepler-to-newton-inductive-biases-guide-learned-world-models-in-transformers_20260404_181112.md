# From Kepler to Newton: Inductive Biases Guide Learned World Models in Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.06923
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06923
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Primary result**: Generic Transformers overfit to Keplerian ellipses; only with short context (temporal locality) do they learn Newtonian force representations.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how inductive biases in Transformer architectures affect their ability to learn underlying physical laws from observational data. Using planetary motion as a testbed, the authors find that standard Transformers fail to discover Newtonian force-based dynamics, instead capturing descriptive Keplerian ellipses. They identify three failure modes: tokenization that breaks spatial smoothness, regression error accumulation, and long context lengths that favor global geometric fits. By systematically intervening—using continuous coordinates, noisy context training, and restricting context length—they show that spatial smoothness and stability yield accurate but Keplerian models, while temporal locality (context length 2) is necessary for forcing the model to represent Newtonian forces. The work highlights the critical role of architectural biases in world model learning and suggests that local temporal processing may be key to discovering causal dynamics.

## Method Summary
The authors generate synthetic trajectories of two-body planetary motion governed by Newtonian gravity. They train autoregressive Transformers to predict future states from past observations, varying three factors: tokenization (discrete vs continuous coordinates), training context (clean vs noisy trajectories), and context window length. Model representations are probed by fitting linear models to internal activations to recover either Keplerian ellipse parameters or Newtonian force variables. Predictive accuracy is measured via mean distance error, and representation quality via R² on probe targets.

## Key Results
- Standard Transformers (discrete tokens, long context, clean training) overfit to Keplerian ellipses and fail to encode Newtonian forces.
- Three failure modes: tokenization disrupts spatial smoothness, regression error accumulation, and long context encourages global geometric fits.
- With continuous coordinates and noisy context, Transformers achieve high prediction accuracy but still learn Keplerian models (R² ≈ 0.998 for ellipse parameters). Enforcing temporal locality (context length 2) yields Newtonian force representations (R² ≈ 0.999 for force variables).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Continuous coordinates preserve spatial smoothness, allowing the model to capture continuous dynamics without discretization artifacts. Noisy context training stabilizes learning by preventing the model from over-relying on precise early predictions, thus reducing error accumulation in autoregressive rollouts. However, these interventions alone still enable the model to fit the global elliptical shape (Keplerian description) because long context provides enough information to directly predict the orbit pattern. Short context (temporal locality) forces the model to infer local forces from recent relative states, which aligns with Newton's law of gravitation that depends on instantaneous positions and velocities. This shifts the learned representation from descriptive geometry to causal dynamics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input Trajectory -> Tokenizer (continuous/discrete) -> Transformer (autoregressive) -> Output Head (regression/classification)

**Critical path**: Raw state observations are tokenized into embeddings; the Transformer processes a context window to predict the next token; predictions are decoded to state coordinates for evaluation.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Tokenization: Discrete tokens are efficient but disrupt spatial smoothness; continuous coordinates preserve geometry but increase embedding complexity.
- Context length: Long context captures global patterns (ellipses) but may obscure local forces; short context enforces temporal locality.
- Training objective: Regression predicts continuous values but suffers error accumulation; classification may be more stable but less precise.
- Noise injection: Adding noise to training context improves stability but may obscure true dynamics if excessive.

**Failure signatures**:
- Overfitting to Keplerian ellipses: High R² on ellipse parameters but low on force variables.
- Error explosion in long-horizon predictions when using regression without noisy context.
- Inability to recover force representations with context length > 2.

**First three experiments**:
1. Replace discrete tokenization with continuous coordinate embeddings.
2. Train with noisy context (perturbing input states).
3. Reduce context length to 2 (temporal locality).

## Limitations
- Synthetic planetary data may not generalize to noisy real-world observations.
- Tokenization beyond continuous coordinates remains unexplored; potential biases persist.
- Probe-based assessment of Newtonian force encoding lacks mechanistic verification.
- Experiments restricted to two-body dynamics; scalability to N-body systems is unknown.
- Short context (length 2) may limit long-horizon prediction; effects not assessed.
- Model scale (size, depth) not systematically varied; conclusions may be size-dependent.
