# SD-E$^2$: Semantic Exploration for Reasoning Under Token Budgets

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17982
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17982
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Achieves 82.03% accuracy on GSM8K with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, a +27.4 pp improvement over the base model, while discovering 9.78 distinct strategies per question.

## Executive Summary
SD-E² improves reasoning efficiency in small language models (SLMs) by explicitly rewarding semantic diversity of generated reasoning strategies. It introduces a semantic diversity reward computed from a frozen sentence encoder, measuring both coverage of unique strategies (via a submodular selection with threshold δ) and average pairwise dissimilarity in embedding space. This reward is combined with outcome correctness, intermediate correctness, and format adherence in a z-score-normalized multi-objective reinforcement learning (GRPO) framework. A “collapse on success” bonus shifts the model from exploration to exploitation once any correct intermediate outcome appears, avoiding wasteful generation after a solution is found.

On GSM8K with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, SD-E² achieves 82.03% accuracy, a +27.4 pp gain over the base model, +5.2 pp over GRPO-CFL, and +1.5 pp over GRPO-CFEE, while generating an average of 9.78 distinct strategies per question. The approach transfers to MedMCQA (49.64% vs. base 38.37%) and AIME 1983–2025 (13.28% vs. base 6.74%) with comparable token usage.

## Method Summary
SD-E² fine-tunes an SLM using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a multi-objective reward. The model generates structured outputs containing multiple `<strategy>` blocks, each with `<reasoning>` and `<strategy_outcome>`, and a `<final_answer>`. The reward components are:
- **Roc**: exact match of the final answer after numeric canonicalization.
- **Rre**: binary reward if any strategy outcome matches the correct answer.
- **Rfa**: bonuses for correct number of strategies, presence of final answer, and completeness of blocks.
- **Rsd**: semantic diversity reward computed from embeddings of reasoning texts via a frozen encoder (e.g., all-MiniLM-L6-v2). It defines `Div(H)` as one minus average pairwise cosine similarity and `Uniq(H;δ)` as the size of a greedy subset where cosine similarity ≤ δ. The product `g(H)` measures diversity. Moreover, if any strategy outcome is correct, `Rsd` collapses to a fixed bonus `α`; otherwise it is scaled by `min(β, ρ·g(H))`.

All reward terms are z-score normalized within each training batch (size `BG`) before being summed with equal weights. Training uses QLoRA (4‑bit, LoRA rank 64), AdamW‑8bit (lr=5e‑6), group size `G∈{4,6}`, KL penalty tuned, gradient clipping 0.1, and cosine decay. Decoding uses temperature 0.1–0.3 and top‑p 0.90–0.95.

## Key Results
- **GSM8K accuracy:** 82.03% (Qwen2.5‑3B‑Instruct), +27.4 pp over base, +5.2 pp over GRPO‑CFL, +1.5 pp over GRPO‑CFEE.  
- **Strategy discovery:** 9.78 semantically distinct strategies per question on average.  
- **Transfer performance:** MedMCQA 49.64% (vs. base 38.37%), AIME 1983‑2025 13.28% (vs. base 6.74%) with similar token counts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The frozen sentence encoder converts reasoning text into an embedding space where semantic similarity is captured by cosine proximity, enabling a measurable reward for genuinely distinct strategies.
- **Mechanism:** For a set of generated reasoning texts `H`, compute (i) `Div(H) = 1 - avg pairwise cosine similarity` (spread) and (ii) `Uniq(H;δ)` via greedy subset selection with threshold `δ` (coverage). Their product `g(H)` quantifies semantic diversity, penalizing near-duplicates that lexical metrics miss.
- **Core assumption:** Embedding geometry from a general-purpose encoder (e.g., all-MiniLM-L6-v2) aligns with human-judged reasoning strategy distinctness for the target domain (math/medical QA).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "using a frozen sentence-embedding model... captures (i) the coverage of semantically distinct solution strategies and (ii) their average pairwise dissimilarity in embedding space".
  - Section 3.2 defines `κ(u,v)` as cosine similarity, `Div(H)` and `Uniq(H;δ)` explicitly in Equations 10–15.
  - Corpus: neighbor papers discuss "semantic diversity" but do not validate this specific encoder/metric choice for reasoning.
- **Break condition:** If the encoder's training corpus is out-of-domain (e.g., non-English, technical jargon), similarity scores may not reflect reasoning-strategy novelty, causing reward misalignment.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The "collapse on success" bonus (`α χ(a)`) pivots the model from exploration to exploitation once any strategy yields a correct intermediate outcome, preventing wasteful generation of redundant strategies after a solution is found.
- **Mechanism:** Define `χ(a) = 1[∃(r,o)∈S(a) : N(o)=N(y)]`. When `χ=1`, `Rsd` collapses to constant `α`; when `χ=0`, `Rsd` scales with `g(H)`. This creates a dynamic gate: reward semantic breadth only until first success, then incentivize consolidation.
- **Core assumption:** In a token-budget-constrained setting, discovering one correct reasoning path makes further exploration of new strategies likely low-yield unless the first was a fluke.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "upon any correct strategy, it switches to an exploitation bonus".
  - Section 3.3 Equation 17 shows `Rsd` using `χ(a)` to toggle between `α` and `min(β, ρ g(H))`.
  - Section 5 Qualitative: "Breadth when needed... Pivot to exploitation once a correct strategy appears."
- **Break condition:** If the first correct strategy is a spurious coincidence (not a generally valid approach), collapsing exploration prematurely may prevent finding a more robust strategy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Batch-wise z-score normalization of multi-objective reward components (`Roc, Rre, Rfa, Rsd`) prevents any single term from dominating optimization, stabilizing GRPO training despite vastly different scales and sparsity patterns.
- **Mechanism:** For each reward component `k` over a batch of `N=BG` trajectories, compute mean `µk` and std `σk`. Normalize: `eRk = (Rk - µk)/(σk+ε)` (unless `σk` too small). Final reward is weighted sum `∑ wk eRk`. This makes components comparable and prevents gradient explosion from e.g., sparse `Roc`.
- **Core assumption:** Within a minibatch, the distribution of each reward component is sufficiently stable to provide meaningful relative rankings; normalization does not distort inter-sample comparisons.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3: "combined with outcome correctness and solution efficiency in a z-score-normalized multi-objective objective that stabilizes training."
  - Appendix A.1 full equations (27–30) for normalization and aggregation.
  - Section 4.2: "We train with GRPO... batchwise z-score normalization."
- **Break condition:** If a batch is too small or homogeneous (e.g., all prompts equally easy), `σk` may near-zero, making normalization unstable or over-amplifying noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**  
  - *Why needed here:* SD-E² is built on GRPO, which relies on outcome-based rewards to update the policy. Understanding RLVR is essential to grasp how correctness signals drive learning.  
  - *Quick check question:* Why does GRPO use a group-relative baseline (`Rb,i - µb`) instead of an absolute reward? (Answer: removes common-mode bias, stabilizes learning from sparse rewards.)

- **Concept: Embedding Space Geometry for Semantics**  
  - *Why needed here:* The core novelty metric uses a frozen sentence encoder to map reasoning traces into an embedding space; the geometry (cosine similarity) defines semantic relatedness.  
  - *Quick check question:* If two reasoning traces use different words but the same logical steps, would their embeddings likely be close or far? (Answer: likely close; encoder generalizes over phrasing.)

- **Concept: Submodular Set Functions for Diversity Maximization**  
  - *Why needed here:* The coverage metric `Uniq(H;δ)` is a greedy approximation of a monotone submodular set function, which provides theoretical guarantees (diminishing returns) for maximizing diversity under a budget.  
  - *Quick check question:* Does adding a third strategy always increase `Uniq`? (Answer: No; if it is too similar to an already included one, `Uniq` stays the same.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map (plain text):**  
  Policy Model (πθ) → Frozen Sentence Encoder (E) → Parser (Fstrat, Fans) → Reward Calculator → GRPO Trainer

- **Critical path:**  
  Prompt `q` → sample `G` completions `a` from `πθ_old` → parse strategies `S(a)` → encode reasoning texts `H` → compute `Rsd` from `H` → aggregate all reward components `Rk` → normalize per batch → compute GRPO advantage `Ab,i` → update `πθ`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Semantic reward vs. compute:* Adding frozen encoder (~22M params) increases training step time by ~11.8% but reduces wasted exploration. Inference unchanged.  
  - *Threshold δ sensitivity:* Lower `δ` increases `Uniq` but may split semantically similar strategies; higher `δ` merges distinct ones. Requires dev-set sweep.  
  - *Structured output:* Enforcing `<strategy>` tags aids parsing but may constrain natural reasoning expression; malformed tags reduce reward signal quality.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *High S-ACC, low ACC:* Model often finds correct intermediate outcome but fails to select it as final answer → aggregation/selection problem.  
  - *High #STR, low S-ACC:* Many strategies generated but none correct → semantic diversity reward decoupled from correctness; possible gaming of superficial variation.  
  - *Low #STR, high token use:* Model generates long single strategies without breaking into blocks → parser failing or `Rfa` insufficiently incentivizing decomposition.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Parser sanity check:** Feed handcrafted outputs with known strategy counts/answers; verify `nstrat(a)`, `S-ACC`, and `Rsd` compute as expected.  
  2. **Encoder sensitivity test:** Replace `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` with `BGE` or `E5` encoder; measure `#STR`, `Div(H)`, and `ACC` on dev set to confirm robustness.  
  3. **Reward ablation on small subset:** Train 1–2 seeds with only `(Roc+Rfa)`, then add `Rsd`, then add `Rre`; confirm monotonic improvement in `ACC` and `S-ACC` as in Table 5.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How robust is SD-E2's semantic diversity reward to the choice of frozen sentence encoder, particularly regarding biases and domain/language shifts?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* The limitations section states: "its semantic signal depends on a frozen sentence encoder whose geometry and biases may distort diversity estimates, especially out of domain or in non-English settings."  
   - *Why unresolved:* The paper uses all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and briefly tests BGE/E5 families but does not systematically evaluate how encoder selection impacts performance across diverse tasks and languages. The encoder's pre-training data and architecture could fundamentally alter what counts as "semantic diversity," potentially invalidating the reward in new contexts.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* A thorough evaluation across multiple encoders (e.g., multilingual models, domain-adapted models) on a suite of benchmarks with controlled domain/language variations, measuring correlation between encoder-based diversity scores and human judgments of strategy distinctness, alongside downstream accuracy.

2. **Can the sensitivity to the similarity threshold δ and reward scales (α, β, ρ) be mitigated by an adaptive schedule that automatically adjusts based on exploration progress?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* The limitations note: "the exploration reward is sensitive to the similarity threshold δ and scales (α, β, ρ); poor settings can over/under-explore, suggesting future work on adaptive schedules or meta-gradients."  
   - *Why unresolved:* The paper sweeps these hyperparameters on a dev split but does not propose or test a dynamic mechanism. A fixed schedule may not suit varying problem difficulties or stages of learning (early exploration vs. late consolidation), leading to suboptimal token efficiency.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Developing and evaluating a meta-controller or schedule (e.g., based on rate of novel strategy discovery, S-ACC trends, or gradient signals) that adjusts δ and reward weights during training, showing improved stability and performance over broad hyperparameter ranges without extensive tuning.

3. **How does the reliance on a structured output schema (XML-like tags) limit the applicability of SD-E2, and can semantic diversity be reliably measured without such rigid parsing?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* The limitations state: "the approach relies on a lightweight output schema, so parser brittleness and malformed blocks can attenuate reward quality; more tolerant or schema-free extraction would help."  
   - *Why unresolved:* The method assumes well-formed `<strategy>` blocks. In practice, models may deviate from the schema, especially early in training or on complex tasks, leading to incomplete reward computation. A schema-free approach could improve robustness but is unexplored.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Designing an alternative method to segment reasoning traces into distinct strategies (e.g., using discourse markers, step clustering, or learned segmentation models) and comparing reward quality, training stability, and final accuracy against the schema-based approach, especially with partial or noisy outputs.

4. **Does the semantic exploration reward incentivize genuinely novel reasoning strategies, or can the model "game" it by generating superficially diverse but semantically equivalent or unhelpful traces?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* The paper argues the reward distinguishes "genuinely novel reasoning strategy" from "surface-form rephrasing." However, the limitations caution: "policies could still game the reward by producing superficially varied yet unhelpful strategies; stronger novelty criteria (e.g., causal/program structure) may further deter this." The core metric uses frozen sentence-encoder cosine similarity, which may not capture deeper logical or algorithmic differences.  
   - *Why unresolved:* There is no direct analysis of whether the discovered "distinct strategies" are truly substantively different (e.g., different mathematical approaches) versus lexical parroting in embedding space. Human evaluation of strategy novelty is absent, and the encoder's inductive biases may be exploited.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Conducting a human study where evaluators judge whether pairs of model-generated strategies for the same problem are meaningfully different. Correlate these judgments with the model's diversity scores and analyze failure cases where high diversity scores coincide with trivial differences.

5. **What is the precise trade-off between the compute overhead of SD-E2 (from sampling G completions and frozen-encoder calls) and the gains in token efficiency from reduced redundant exploration?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* The paper notes: "GRPO imposes a compute cost from sampling G completions and running the encoder during training, which rises with longer generations." It reports ∼11.8% increased wall-clock time but does not deeply analyze cost-benefit across different budgets or model scales.  
   - *Why unresolved:* The overhead is quantified but not systematically varied. The claimed "compute-efficient exploration-exploitation signal" focuses on inference-time token usage, but training cost is higher. The net efficiency gain (total compute to reach a target accuracy) is unclear, especially for larger group sizes G or longer reasoning traces.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Running controlled experiments varying G (group size), maximum generation length, and encoder cost (e.g., different model sizes) to map out the full Pareto frontier of training compute vs. final accuracy vs. inference token usage. Comparing total energy/time to achieve a fixed accuracy target against baselines would clarify the practical efficiency gains.

## Limitations
- Exact optimal hyperparameters (α, β, ρ, δ, KL β, group size G, decoding temperature/top-p) are unreported, making precise replication of the claimed gains uncertain.
- The numeric canonicalization `N(·)` for answer matching is referenced but not defined; minor implementation differences could affect reported accuracy and strategy accuracy.
- Parser resilience to malformed `<strategy>` outputs is unspecified; reward computation may be brittle under realistic generation errors, affecting robustness.

## Confidence
- **High:** Performance claims (e.g., +27.4 pp on GSM8K) are numerically explicit and benchmarked.
- **Medium:** Semantic diversity mechanism's generalizability; z-score normalization stabilizes training but depends on batch homogeneity.
- **Low:** "Collapse on success" reliably finding robust strategies; risk of premature convergence if the first correct answer is spurious.
