# Conversational Agents and the Understanding of Human Language: Reflections on AI, LLMs, and Cognitive Science

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27809
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27809
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Despite impressive practical language abilities, modern LLMs have not substantially deepened scientific understanding of human language processing.

## Executive Summary
This paper critically examines whether advances in natural language processing (NLP), particularly large language models (LLMs), have yielded deeper scientific insights into the human capacity for language. Through a comparative review of NLP paradigms—from rule-based systems to statistical models and modern Transformer-based LLMs—against theories from linguistics and cognitive science, the authors conclude that technological progress in building capable conversational agents has not translated into a commensurate advance in understanding how human minds process language. Key disconnects identified include the disembodied, data-intensive training of LLMs versus the embodied, interactive learning of children, the extreme inefficiency of LLMs compared to human cognition, and the lack of neural or cognitive analogies in Transformer architectures beyond abstract connectionism. The paper thus underscores that engineering success does not guarantee scientific comprehension.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a narrative literature review and conceptual analysis to assess the scientific value of NLP advancements. It outlines the historical evolution of NLP paradigms—rule-based, statistical, neural (including Transformers/LLMs)—and for each, compares their core mechanisms, training data, and objectives with established models of human language processing from linguistics and cognitive science. The objective is to evaluate whether these technological developments have led to a "substantial deepening" of understanding of human language capacity. The methodology involves synthesizing similarities and, more critically, disconnects between the artificial systems and biological cognition, ultimately arguing that despite performance gains, LLMs have not bridged the explanatory gap. The review is qualitative, relying on conceptual comparisons rather than empirical tests, and draws on 13 key references spanning both NLP and cognitive science.

## Key Results
- The evolution of NLP from rule-based systems to LLMs has not produced substantial scientific insights into human language processing.
- Major disconnects include: (1) disembodied, static-text training of LLMs vs. embodied, interactive child language acquisition; (2) orders-of-magnitude greater data and energy consumption compared to human learning; (3) absence of meaningful neural/cognitive analogies in Transformer architecture.
- Conclusion: Building powerful language technology does not equate to understanding the human language faculty.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper advances its negative conclusion through three central mechanisms that explain why LLM capabilities fail to inform human language cognition.

**Mechanism 1: Disembodiment and Lack of Interactive Learning**  
- **Claim:** LLMs' practical language abilities do not translate into scientific understanding because they are trained on disembodied, static text without interactive grounding.  
- **Mechanism:** Human children acquire language through sensorimotor experience and real-time social interaction, shaping embodied representations. LLMs, in contrast, learn via next-token prediction on massive corpora, lacking any coupling to physical or social context. Consequently, the internal states in LLMs are not analogous to human mental representations.  
- **Core assumption:** Language comprehension and production are fundamentally grounded in embodied experience and pragmatic interaction.  
- **Evidence:** The abstract and Section 5 contrast the "data-intensive training" of LLMs with the "embodied, interactive learning of children," noting that LLMs possess disembodied knowledge and learn through text completion while children benefit from constant speaker interaction.  
- **Break condition:** If future systems achieve human-level linguistic competency through purely textual training without any interactive or embodied component *and* their internal representations map onto known neural language circuits, this mechanism would be invalidated.

**Mechanism 2: Extreme Data and Energy Inefficiency**  
- **Claim:** The vast data and energy requirements of LLM training demonstrate a misalignment with human cognitive mechanisms, which achieve language acquisition with far less exposure and metabolic cost.  
- **Mechanism:** LLMs require training on datasets rivaling the size of major libraries and consume energy measured in GWh, whereas human children hear only a few thousand words per day and their brains use less than 1 kWh daily. This disparity suggests LLMs solve a different, brute-force optimization problem rather than capturing the efficient, structured learning processes of the human mind.  
- **Core assumption:** Efficient use of limited data and energy is a defining constraint of human cognition; any scientifically valid model should approximate this efficiency.  
- **Evidence:** The abstract highlights "data-intensive training" while Section 5 provides the quantitative contrast: children hear at most a few thousand words per day, LLM training data is library-scale, and LLM energy use (GWh) dwarfs human brain consumption (<1 kWh/day).  
- **Break condition:** An LLM or similar system achieving comparable linguistic performance to a child after exposure to developmentally plausible quantities of linguistically rich, interactive data (millions, not trillions, of tokens) and with training energy costs within an order of magnitude of human metabolic estimates would challenge this mechanism.

**Mechanism 3: Architectural Mismatch with the Brain**  
- **Claim:** The Transformer architecture lacks meaningful analogies to neural and cognitive structures involved in human language processing, limiting its value as a scientific model.  
- **Mechanism:** Beyond the very abstract connectionist principle of units and weighted activations, key features of the biological language system—specialized cortical areas, diverse synaptic dynamics, spike-timing-dependent plasticity—have no counterpart in the homogeneous, static-weight layers of a Transformer. Therefore, even if LLMs perform well, they do not employ brain-like processes.  
- **Core assumption:** A model's scientific utility for understanding a biological system depends on its architecture sharing explanatory principles with that system, not merely matching input-output behavior.  
- **Evidence:** The abstract notes the "lack of neural or cognitive analogies in Transformer architectures beyond abstract connectionist principles." Section 5 elaborates that aside from formal neurons and activation propagation, nothing in Transformers resembles brain specialization, synapse variety, or neural spiking.  
- **Break condition:** The introduction of a widely adopted neural architecture for language whose core computational primitives (e.g., attention mechanism, gating, state representation) are explicitly derived from and shown to replicate known neural dynamics or functional segregation observed in neuroimaging/lesion studies of human language would undermine this mechanism.

## Foundational Learning
Several conceptual foundations are necessary to grasp the paper's critique.

**Hierarchical Linguistic Representation** (Phonology → Morphology → Syntax → Semantics → Pragmatics)  
- **Why needed:** The paper argues that NLP historically aimed to model these distinct, interacting levels (e.g., Jackendoff, 2002). Understanding this hierarchy reveals what is missing in the flat, end-to-end learning of LLMs, despite probing studies (e.g., Tenney et al., 2019) showing some layer differentiation.  
- **Quick check:** What specific linguistic level (e.g., dependency parsing, anaphora resolution) is most poorly captured by probing intermediate LLM layers, and why might that be expected given the next-token prediction objective?

**Statistical Language Modeling** (n-grams, Smoothing)  
- **Why needed:** This paradigm directly preceded neural models and illustrates the limitations (sparsity, poor generalization) that neural networks overcame, while still sharing the core objective of predicting sequences. Understanding its constraints clarifies what neural models changed—and what they did not change—regarding cognitive plausibility.  
- **Quick check:** How does the probabilistic goal of predicting the next word in a billion-token corpus differ fundamentally from the communicative goal of a child learning to express a need to a caregiver?

**Embodied Cognition & Interactive Grounding**  
- **Why needed:** This framework posits that meaning and linguistic structure arise from physical interaction with the world and social coordination, not just text prediction. It is the core contrasting perspective the paper uses to critique LLMs; "disembodied" is a fundamental, not incidental, flaw.  
- **Quick check:** Provide an example of a linguistic concept (e.g., the verb "put" in a spatial context, or irony) whose full psychological acquisition plausibly requires non-linguistic sensorimotor or social feedback, and explain why text-only exposure would be insufficient for a child.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input text → Tokenization → Token Embedding Layer → [N identical layers: Multi-Head Self-Attention → Add & Norm → Position-wise Feed-Forward → Add & Norm] → Output Head → Generated text.

**Critical path:**  
During inference, raw text is tokenized into subword units. Token embeddings are passed through a stack of N Transformer layers, each performing self-attention to compute context-aware representations followed by a feed-forward non-linear transformation, with residual connections and layer normalization around each sub-layer. The final token representations are projected to vocabulary size and passed through a softmax to yield next-token probabilities. Sampling or greedy decoding produces the output. Training follows the same forward pass, with loss computed via cross-entropy between predicted and actual next tokens, and gradients used to update all parameters.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Model size vs. efficiency:** Increasing layers (N) or hidden dimension (d_model) improves performance but raises memory, compute (quadratic in sequence length for attention), and energy costs exponentially, moving further from human-scale efficiency.
- **Context window vs. memory/compute:** Longer contexts increase quadratic complexity of self-attention, requiring approximations (e.g., sparse attention) that may degrade long-range dependency modeling.
- **Architecture choice:** Encoder-Decoder (original Transformer) suits sequence-to-sequence tasks but is less flexible for chat. Decoder-only models (GPT) are simpler and excel at generation, but require prompting/fine-tuning for instruction following.

**Failure signatures:**
- Hallucination/Non-factuality: Generating plausible but incorrect information due to reliance on statistical co-occurrence rather than a verified world model.
- Inconsistency/Poor Persona Adherence: Contradicting earlier statements or failing to maintain a role, as there is no persistent state beyond the context window.
- Lack of robust grounding: Failing on tasks requiring physical reasoning, counterfactuals, or unstated common-sense constraints derived from embodied experience.
- Prompt sensitivity & brittleness: Drastic performance changes with minor rephrasing, indicating shallow pattern matching rather than deep, compositional understanding.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Test embodied understanding** – Present the model with vignettes requiring physical intuition (e.g., "If I put a glass on a tilted table, what happens?") and compare answers to those of children at various ages or to biomechanical simulations. A statistical model should fail on non-canonical scenarios.
2. **Measure data efficiency learning curve** – Train models on a developmentally appropriate dataset (e.g., child-directed speech transcriptions, ~10M tokens) with varying token counts, plot performance vs. tokens, and compare to known child vocabulary acquisition curves. A cognitive model should show different scaling laws.
3. **Probe for hierarchical generalization** – Create a synthetic dataset with explicit nested hierarchical rules (e.g., center-embedding sentences). Train a model and apply probing techniques (e.g., diagnostic classifiers) to intermediate layers to test if they explicitly encode syntactic depth or rule type, as predicted by psycholinguistic theories. Failure to find such structure supports the paper's claim.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** To what extent do the internal representations within large language models capture a coherent, grounded model of the physical world akin to that developed by humans through embodied experience? The paper notes LLMs are disembodied and their internal representations are poorly understood.
- **Open Question 2:** Can modifications to the training objective or architecture—such as incorporating interactive, multi-turn dialogue from early training—lead to more sample-efficient and cognitively plausible language acquisition, narrowing the gap with child development? The paper contrasts data-intensive text-only training with interactive child learning.
- **Open Question 3:** What plausible mappings, if any, exist between specific components or processing dynamics of the Transformer architecture (e.g., attention heads, layer hierarchies) and identified functional regions or neural mechanisms in the human brain during language processing? The paper points out the lack of neural analogies.
- **Open Question 4:** Is the impressive performance of LLMs on complex reasoning and instruction following primarily an emergent property of scaling (parameters, data, compute) or does it critically depend on specific architectural inductive biases that remain poorly understood? The paper remarks that the more LLMs emulate human capacity, the less we understand how.

## Limitations
- The review's conclusion is qualitative and lacks operational metrics for "scientific understanding", making the assessment somewhat subjective.
- It privileges embodied-interactive theories, potentially overlooking alternative frameworks (e.g., probabilistic or connectionist models) that might offer different perspectives.
- It does not address recent advances in multimodal and interactive LLMs (e.g., agents with sensors) that could mitigate the disembodiment critique.
- It fails to consider whether scaling itself might eventually yield scientific insights, focusing on current disconnects.

## Confidence
- Disembodiment disconnect: Medium (strong theoretical basis but untested against emerging embodied systems)
- Data inefficiency: High (clear quantitative disparity)
- Architectural mismatch: Medium (factual differences exist, but the claim that this blocks scientific insight is interpretive)

## Next Checks
1. **Developmental data efficiency:** Train decoder-only models on child-scale text (≤100M tokens) with interactive feedback; compare learning trajectories to child language acquisition data.
2. **Neural alignment probing:** Use brain imaging (fMRI/EEG) to compare representational geometries of Transformer layers to human language networks on identical stimuli.
3. **Embodied intervention:** Augment LLMs with a simulated sensorimotor body; evaluate if this reduces data needs and improves performance on physically grounded tasks, testing the disembodiment hypothesis.
