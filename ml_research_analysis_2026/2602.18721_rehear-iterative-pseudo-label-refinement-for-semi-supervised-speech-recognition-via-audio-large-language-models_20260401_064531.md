# ReHear: Iterative Pseudo-Label Refinement for Semi-Supervised Speech Recognition via Audio Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.18721
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18721
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Consistently reduces WER across benchmarks (e.g., 6.34% on Earnings-21 vs. 6.76% supervised and 8.02% pseudo-labeling).

## Executive Summary
ReHear addresses error propagation in semi-supervised ASR pseudo-labeling by iteratively refining pseudo-labels with an audio-aware large language model. The LLM conditions on both the initial ASR hypothesis and the source audio to correct phonetic errors, producing high-fidelity training targets. Across Earnings-21, Earnings-22, SPGISpeech, and AMI, ReHear yields robust WER reductions, demonstrating particular effectiveness in accent-heavy and spontaneous speech domains.

## Method Summary
ReHear implements an iterative self-training loop. First, a base ASR model generates pseudo-labels on unlabeled audio. An audio-LLM, fine-tuned on limited labeled data, then corrects these labels by jointly processing the audio and the initial hypothesis. Optionally, a filter (rule-based or learned) removes low-quality corrections. The accepted pseudo-labels are combined with gold data to fine-tune the ASR model. This updated ASR inference feeds the next cycle, progressively improving label and model quality.

## Key Results
- On Earnings-21 test, ReHear achieves **6.34% WER**, beating the supervised baseline (6.76%) and standard pseudo-labeling (8.02%).
- Gains are consistent across Earnings-22, SPGISpeech, and AMI, showing robustness in challenging spontaneous speech settings.
- Ablations confirm that **audio conditioning** is critical (text-only LLM performs poorly) and that **iterative refinement** with a decay strategy prevents degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Audio-LLM Conditioning:** The corrector receives audio embeddings plus the ASR hypothesis, enabling direct phonetic error resolution (e.g., homophones) that text-only models miss. Evidence: "Audio+Text" input significantly outperforms "Text Only" in ablations.
- **Iterative Refinement Loop:** Each cycle uses a better ASR model to produce cleaner hypotheses, which the LLM corrects more accurately, creating a positive feedback loop. Evidence: WER drops rapidly in early iterations; decay prevents later divergence.
- **Filtering:** Heuristic or model-based filters reject outlier corrections (e.g., hallucinations), stabilizing training. Evidence: Filtered variants improve stability, especially on AMI, though gains are dataset-sensitive.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Base ASR (M_A) -> Audio LLM Corrector (M_L) -> Filter (rule/model) -> Training Orchestrator -> (back to M_A fine-tuning). Critical path: Unlabeled audio → M_A inference → initial hypotheses → M_L inference (with audio) → corrected labels → filter → accepted labels → M_A fine-tuning on D_L ∪ D^f_U → repeat. Design tradeoffs: Quality vs. cost (powerful LLMs are expensive); filter aggressiveness vs. data utility; iteration count vs. diminishing returns. Failure signatures: Divergence (WER increases on D_L Val); LLM hallucination surge (high rejection rate); no improvement (WER matches supervised baseline). First experiments: 1) Replicate standard pseudo-labeling (IPL) baseline to confirm error propagation. 2) Modality ablation: compare "Audio+Text" vs. "Text Only" LLM correction. 3) Single-iteration check: compute WER of LLM-corrected pseudo-labels against gold to isolate correction quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does ReHear generalize to diverse ASR architectures (RNN-T, Conformer) and different audio-LLM backbones beyond Whisper and V oxtral?
- Can filtering be designed to avoid overfitting on limited labeled data while maintaining high pseudo-label quality?
- Does the method transfer to non-English, low-resource languages given all reported results are English-centric?
- Which specific error types (homophones, named entities, disfluencies) are most effectively corrected by the audio-aware LLM?

## Limitations
- **Language & Domain Scope:** All benchmarks are English; applicability to tonal, low-resource, or child speech is unproven.
- **Computational Cost:** Iterative LLM+ASR passes are resource-intensive; scalability to million-hour corpora is unclear.
- **Filter Dependency:** Optimal filtering is dataset-specific and requires a labeled validation set, which may not exist in true semi-SSL regimes.

## Confidence
- **Audio-LLM Conditioning:** High (strong ablation evidence from Table 3).
- **Iterative Refinement Loop:** High (consistent WER drops over 2–3 iterations; decay necessity shown in Table 5).
- **Filtering Stabilization:** Medium (improves stability on AMI but gains inconsistent across datasets).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-lingual Generalization Test:** Apply ReHear to a non-English semi-supervised benchmark (e.g., MLS) to validate transfer beyond English.
2. **Filter Impact Analysis:** On a held-out labeled set, compare oracle WER changes for samples retained vs. rejected by filters to quantify precision/recall of beneficial corrections.
3. **Training Dynamic Audit:** Track ASR WER on a clean subset of the unlabeled pool across iterations to distinguish true model improvement from transient LLM bias fade.
