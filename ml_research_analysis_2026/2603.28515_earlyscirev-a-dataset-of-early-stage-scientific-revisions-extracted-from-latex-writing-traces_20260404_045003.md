# EarlySciRev: A Dataset of Early-Stage Scientific Revisions Extracted from LaTeX Writing Traces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.28515
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28515
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Introduces EarlySciRev, a dataset of 578,440 paragraph-level revision pairs extracted from LaTeX comments of 104,023 computer science arXiv papers.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces EarlySciRev, a large-scale dataset capturing early-stage scientific revisions at the paragraph level. The dataset is mined from LaTeX source files of arXiv computer science papers by treating commented-out text as original drafts and aligning them with nearby final paragraphs using Levenshtein similarity. A Qwen3 14B model filters the candidate pairs to ensure they represent genuine revisions. This resource addresses the scarcity of revision data and enables research on scientific writing processes.

The method successfully extracts 578,440 validated revision pairs from 1.28 million candidates across over 100,000 articles, with an average of 56.85% word difference between original and revised paragraphs. The approach demonstrates that writing traces in LaTeX comments are a viable signal for large-scale revision analysis.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by downloading licensed arXiv CS LaTeX sources. After cleaning to retain only the document body (excluding figures, tables, equations, etc.), the source is parsed into consecutive blocks of commented (`%`) and non-commented lines. For each comment block, candidate final paragraphs are sought among the five preceding and five following non-comment blocks. Pairs with normalized Levenshtein difference `dnorm = lev(f,c)/max(|f|,|c|) < 0.7` are retained. A Qwen3 14B model with perplexity-based scoring (*-PLUIE) then classifies each pair as a genuine revision, using a threshold of 5.55 optimized on a 500-pair human-annotated set (Fleiss’ κ=0.54). The final dataset aggregates all pairs that pass the LLM filter.

The process is automated and scalable, producing a dataset of 578,440 revision pairs from 104,023 unique articles. The average final paragraph length is 82.42 words, and the average word difference per pair is 56.85%, reflecting substantial revision at the paragraph level.

## Key Results
- 578,440 validated revision pairs from 104,023 arXiv CS articles (1.28M candidates).
- LLM filter accuracy 82% (Fleiss’ κ = 0.54) on human benchmark.
- Average final paragraph length 82.42 words; average word difference 56.85% per pair.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Commented text preserves authentic drafts.
Authors often leave discarded or alternative formulations as comments in the LaTeX source near where the final text eventually appears. The pipeline mines these commented blocks as original drafts, assuming they reflect early versions of the nearby final paragraphs. Evidence: the high yield (578k pairs) and qualitative inspection of samples. Break condition: if authors delete comments or use version control instead, the signal is lost.

### Mechanism 2: Levenshtein similarity identifies related paragraphs.
A normalized Levenshtein distance metric (`dnorm`) quantifies lexical dissimilarity between a comment block and a candidate final block. The threshold `dnorm < 0.7` captures pairs with sufficient overlap to be plausible revisions, yet allowing for substantial changes. The dataset's average 56.85% word difference indicates the threshold effective for moderate revisions. Limitations: complete rephrasing may yield false negatives; unrelated text with coincidental overlap may yield false positives.

### Mechanism 3: LLM filtering ensures genuine revisions.
Qwen3 14B with perplexity scoring (*-PLUIE) judges whether a final paragraph qualifies as a revision of the original. The model agrees with human annotators 82% of the time (κ=0.54) on a benchmark. However, the inherent ambiguity of the task (moderate agreement) and the single-domain training limit confidence, especially for non-English text. The filter reduces noise from the candidate pool but its precision on the retained set remains unverified.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Data Collector → LaTeX Cleaner → Block Extractor → Candidate Aligner → LLM Filter → Aggregator.

### Critical Path
LaTeX Cleaner → Block Extractor → Candidate Aligner → LLM Filter.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Recall vs. Precision in Alignment**: The `dnorm < 0.7` threshold favors recall, yielding many candidates for the LLM to filter; raising it reduces load but risks missing revisions.
- **Model Size vs. Cost**: Qwen3 14B balances accuracy and computational cost; smaller models may degrade performance on ambiguous pairs.
- **Determinism vs. Flexibility**: Using perplexity-based (*-PLUIE) scoring ensures deterministic outputs but ties performance to tokenization and prompt phrasing; free-form prompting might be more flexible but stochastic.

### Failure Signatures
- **Non-English papers**: LLM prompt is English; classification likely unreliable for non-Latin scripts.
- **Equation-only revisions**: After equation masking, such pairs may have `dnorm=0` and survive to LLM; LLM should reject them but may not.
- **Paragraph merging/splitting**: If a comment block spans multiple final paragraphs (or vice versa), the ±5 block window may fail to capture the relationship.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Validate Block Extraction**: Sample 100 LaTeX files; manually verify comment block detection and block segmentation.
2. **Threshold Sensitivity Analysis**: On the 500-annotated pairs, plot precision/recall for `dnorm` filter alone across thresholds to quantify signal strength.
3. **LLM Failure Mode Analysis**: Categorize Qwen3 *-PLUIE errors (false positives/negatives) on the benchmark, correlating with paragraph length, word difference, equation presence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Authenticity of commented drafts**: Not all comments represent early drafts; some are TODOs or placeholders. *Basis*: Core assumption that comments preserve discarded drafts. *Why unresolved*: LLM filter selects revision-like pairs but does not verify comment intent. *Resolution*: Annotation study of comment purposes across a sample.
2. **Sensitivity to Levenshtein threshold**: The `dnorm < 0.7` threshold may not be optimal for all revision types or sections. *Basis*: Threshold set empirically. *Why unresolved*: No systematic optimization across contexts. *Resolution*: Sensitivity analysis on human benchmark by section and revision magnitude.
3. **Sources of annotator disagreement**: Human benchmark Fleiss’ κ=0.54 indicates moderate agreement; unclear what features cause disagreement. *Basis*: Annotators had differing selectivity. *Why unresolved*: No analysis of disagreement patterns. *Resolution*: Error analysis of benchmark codes for features like semantic shift, structural change.
4. **Cross-disciplinary generalizability**: Dataset is only CS; revision behaviors may differ in other fields. *Basis*: Authors note this limitation. *Why unresolved*: No comparative investigation. *Resolution*: Apply pipeline to other disciplines' LaTeX sources and compare.

## Limitations
- Threshold choices (`dnorm` and LLM score) are empirically tuned; generalizability to other datasets or domains is uncertain.
- Human annotation shows only moderate agreement (κ=0.54), indicating the "genuine revision" label is intrinsically noisy.
- The final dataset's precision has not been independently validated; it may contain non-revisions, non-English artifacts, or misaligned pairs.

## Confidence
- **Commented text as drafts (Mechanism 1)**: High confidence
- **Levenshtein threshold (Mechanism 2)**: Medium confidence
- **LLM filtering accuracy (Mechanism 3)**: Low confidence
- **Overall dataset utility**: Medium confidence
