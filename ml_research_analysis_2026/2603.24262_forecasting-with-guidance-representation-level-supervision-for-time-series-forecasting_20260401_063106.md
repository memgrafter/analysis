# Forecasting with Guidance: Representation-Level Supervision for Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24262
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24262
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: ReGuider achieves over 5% average improvement in MSE and MAE across seven datasets and four backbone models by aligning encoder representations with a frozen pretrained foundation model.

## Executive Summary
Time series forecasting models trained solely on prediction error often learn overly smooth representations that discard extreme patterns, leading to suboptimal forecasts. This paper introduces ReGuider, a plug-in module that injects representation-level supervision into any backbone model. ReGuider aligns the encoder’s intermediate embeddings with those of a pretrained time series foundation model (e.g., Time-MoE) via Euclidean distance, enriching the student model’s temporal semantics without altering architecture or inference cost. Extensive experiments on seven datasets and four backbones show consistent error reductions, with particularly strong gains on high-dimensional data and long forecasting horizons. Visualizations confirm that ReGuider yields more compact and discriminative latent clusters.

## Method Summary
ReGuider operates alongside any backbone forecasting model. During training, the input series is processed in parallel by two encoders: (1) the backbone’s trainable encoder producing representation \(H_f\), and (2) a frozen pretrained foundation model (Time-MoE) producing guidance representation \(H_g\). The two representations are aligned using Euclidean distance, with the gradient stopped on \(H_g\) to keep the foundation model unchanged. The total loss combines the standard forecasting loss \(L_{\text{pred}}\) and the alignment loss \(L_{\text{TSRA}}\) weighted by a hyperparameter \(\lambda\): \(L = L_{\text{pred}} + \lambda L_{\text{TSRA}}\). After training, the foundation model is discarded and only the backbone is used for inference, incurring no additional computational cost. The method is architecture-agnostic and requires only the specification of which intermediate encoder embedding to extract from the foundation model.

## Key Results
- ReGuider reduces average MSE and MAE by over 5% compared to error-only supervised baselines across seven datasets and four backbone models.
- Performance gains are largest on high-dimensional datasets (e.g., Traffic) and for long forecasting horizons (up to 720 steps).
- t-SNE visualizations demonstrate that ReGuider produces more compact and semantically separated latent clusters compared to baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Error-only supervision focuses solely on minimizing forecast error, which often down-weights rare extreme patterns because they contribute less to the average loss. As a result, the encoder learns to ignore these informative extremes and produces overly smooth representations. A pretrained foundation model, exposed to vast and diverse time series data during pretraining, has already learned to represent both common and extreme patterns robustly. By aligning the student encoder’s embeddings with the foundation model’s intermediate representations, ReGuider forces the student to preserve these extreme patterns and other rich temporal features. The Euclidean alignment acts as a regularizer that enriches the semantic content of the latent space without dictating exact predictions, thereby improving generalization and forecast accuracy. The stop-gradient ensures the foundation model remains a fixed guide, avoiding destabilizing feedback.

## Foundational Learning
- **Time Series Foundation Models**: Large pretrained models (e.g., Time-MoE) that capture universal temporal dynamics from massive corpora. *Why needed*: They provide a rich, general representation space that encodes both common and rare patterns. *Quick check*: Does the foundation model achieve competitive zero-shot forecasting results on diverse datasets?
- **Representation Alignment**: The technique of matching intermediate embeddings from two models using a distance metric. *Why needed*: It transfers knowledge at the representation level, avoiding the need to replicate the full teacher model. *Quick check*: Is the alignment computed between corresponding encoder layers/tokens and does it use stop-gradient?
- **Euclidean Distance**: A simple, differentiable metric used to measure discrepancy between embeddings. *Why needed*: Empirically found to be more effective than KL divergence or cosine similarity for this task. *Quick check*: Does Euclidean distance correlate with semantic similarity in the teacher’s latent space?
- **Error-Only Supervision**: Standard training regime where only the final prediction error is minimized. *Why needed*: It is the default but can lead to impoverished representations if rare patterns are under-represented. *Quick check*: Does the model produce overly smooth forecasts on extreme events?
- **Stop-Gradient**: An operation that detaches a tensor from the computation graph. *Why needed*: Prevents gradients from flowing into the frozen teacher, maintaining its fixed role. *Quick check*: Are all teacher parameters set to `requires_grad=False` and is stop-gradient applied to \(H_g\) in the alignment loss?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data (X) -> Backbone Encoder (trainable) -> H_f -> Prediction Head -> Forecast; Data (X) -> Foundation Encoder (frozen) -> H_g; H_f and H_g -> Euclidean Alignment (stop-grad on H_g) -> L_TSRA; Total Loss = L_pred + λ * L_TSRA.

### Critical Path
1. Forward pass through backbone encoder to obtain H_f.
2. Forward pass through frozen foundation encoder (no gradient) to obtain H_g.
3. Compute Euclidean distance between H_f and stop-gradient H_g to get L_TSRA.
4. Compute forecasting loss L_pred from backbone predictions.
5. Combine losses with weight λ and backpropagate to update backbone parameters only.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **λ selection**: Balances alignment strength vs. prediction focus; high λ may overfit alignment, low λ may yield negligible benefit. Requires tuning.
- **Embedding choice**: Which intermediate layer/token of the foundation model to use? Must be informative yet dimensionally compatible with H_f.
- **Dimensionality handling**: If H_f and H_g differ in dimension, a projection may be needed (not described, suggesting dimensions are matched by design).

### Failure Signatures
- Shape mismatch errors when H_f and H_g have incompatible shapes.
- No improvement or degraded performance if λ is poorly chosen.
- Instability if the foundation model is not properly frozen (e.g., gradients accidentally flow into it).
- Poor guidance if H_g is extracted from an unsuitable layer (e.g., too early or too late).

### First Three Experiments
1. **Baseline**: Train and evaluate the backbone model without ReGuider to establish reference metrics.
2. **ReGuider with defaults**: Integrate ReGuider using frozen Time-MoE, Euclidean alignment, and λ=1.0; train and compare to baseline.
3. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Vary λ (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0) and optionally the foundation model’s embedding layer; select best configuration on validation set.

## Limitations
- The exact intermediate encoder layer and token selection from Time-MoE used as guidance (\(H_g\)) are not specified.
- The trade-off weight \(\lambda\) and its tuning protocol are unreported, requiring practitioners to search.
- Pretrained Time-MoE checkpoints and precise architecture details are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- The claim that error-only supervision yields "semantically impoverished" representations is not directly measured; it is inferred from performance and visualization.

## Confidence
- **High**: ReGuider’s plug-in mechanism (freeze foundation model, align embeddings via Euclidean distance) is clearly described and should be reproducible in principle once checkpoints are available.
- **Medium**: Reported >5% average MSE/MAE gains across backbones/datasets; confidence reduced without access to exact training runs and hyperparameter sweeps.
- **Low**: The causal explanation that discarded extremes cause "overly smooth predictions" is not validated by direct analysis of extreme-pattern preservation in latent space.

## Next Checks

3. Extract and compare embedding dimensions from backbone encoder and Time-MoE encoder on a sample batch; if mismatched, test whether a projection or pooling layer is implicitly required.
