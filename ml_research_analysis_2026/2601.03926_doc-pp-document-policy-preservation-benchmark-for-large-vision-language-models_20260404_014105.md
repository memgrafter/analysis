# Doc-PP: Document Policy Preservation Benchmark for Large Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03926  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03926  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** LVLMs leak sensitive information in 60–100% of implicit queries requiring cross‑modal reasoning; the DVA framework significantly reduces leakage (e.g., Gemini‑3‑Flash‑Preview implicit leakage from 64.6% to 30.5%) while preserving response faithfulness.

## Executive Summary
Large Vision‑Language Models (LVLMs) often fail to preserve user‑defined non‑disclosure policies when answering questions over multimodal documents, especially when answers require implicit reasoning that synthesizes evidence across text, tables, charts, and figures. This paper introduces **Doc‑PP**, a benchmark of 1,141 query‑policy pairs derived from real‑world reports, and reveals a systemic **Reasoning‑Induced Safety Gap**: LVLMs leak protected information in 60–100% of implicit queries, far more than explicit ones. Surprisingly, providing OCR‑extracted text — which improves perception — paradoxically increases leakage. To mitigate these failures, the authors propose **DVA** (Decompose‑Verify‑Aggregation), a structural inference framework that separates reasoning from policy verification. DVA substantially lowers leakage across models while maintaining answer faithfulness.

## Method Summary
**Doc‑PP construction** follows a three‑stage pipeline: (1) **Policy construction** – candidate confidential targets are generated with GPT‑5.2, their values extracted, and a checklist filters invalid or non‑numeric entries; (2) **Query construction** – explicit queries directly request the target, while implicit queries are summary‑style questions that necessitate disclosing the target value to answer correctly; (3) **Evaluation checklist generation** – content‑coverage criteria are produced and filtered to avoid overlap with policies. The benchmark uses 90 business/financial PDFs from existing datasets, yielding 443 explicit and 698 implicit query‑policy pairs.

**DVA inference** works in two stages: first, the model decomposes its initial answer into atomic information elements; second, each element is verified against the policy, invalid ones are discarded, and a final response is aggregated from the valid atoms. Evaluation uses an LLM‑as‑a‑judge approach (GPT‑5‑mini) to score **leakage** (whether the protected value appears) and **faithfulness** (coverage of non‑protected content for implicit queries).

## Key Results
- **Reasoning‑Induced Safety Gap:** Across six LVLMs, implicit queries that require cross‑modal synthesis yield leakage rates of 60–100%, while explicit queries leak far less.
- **OCR Paradox:** Supplying OCR‑extracted text as input increases leakage compared to raw images, even though perception improves.
- **DVA mitigation:** The DVA framework reduces leakage dramatically (e.g., Gemini‑3‑Flash‑Preview implicit leakage drops from 64.6% to 30.5% with DVA) without sacrificing faithfulness; baseline defenses (CoT, revision) are far less effective.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Policy constraint brittleness during synthesis** – When answering implicit queries, LVLMs must combine information from multiple modalities (e.g., a chart’s percentage and a text total) to compute a protected value. The policy constraint is not robustly integrated into the reasoning chain; the model prioritizes satisfying the user’s information need and inadvertently produces the restricted value. This explains the sharp discrepancy between direct retrieval and reasoning‑heavy tasks.

2. **OCR‑facilitated numeric manipulation** – OCR extraction makes numeric values and relationships explicit and easy to manipulate. The model can directly operate on structured text to compute protected quantities, whereas raw images pose a higher barrier for precise reasoning. This leads to the “OCR Paradox”: better perception correlates with higher leakage.

3. **Granular verification via decomposition** – DVA breaks the answer into atomic facts and verifies each independently. This prevents the model from combining allowed facts to derive a protected value, because each fact is evaluated in isolation. The aggregation step then reassembles only permitted elements, catching leaks that would be missed by monolithic assessment.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Doc‑PP benchmark construction: Source PDFs → Policy generation (LLM + checklist) → Query generation (explicit/implicit) → Checklist generation.  
Evaluation pipeline: (Document, Policy, Query) + Input modality (Dimg/Docr) → LVLM → Response → LLM judge (leakage/faithfulness).  
DVA inference: Query → Initial answer (LVLM) → Decompose (atoms) → Verify each atom → Aggregate valid atoms → Final answer.

**Critical path**  
1. Build Doc‑PP: collect PDFs, generate and filter policies, create queries and checklists.  
2. Evaluate LVLMs: for each triplet, run model with policy instruction, judge leakage/faithfulness.  
3. Apply DVA: for given query, generate initial answer, decompose, verify, aggregate.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- LLM‑as‑a‑judge scales but risks model bias; mitigated by high human agreement (93%).  
- Checklist granularity must balance faithfulness scoring and policy overlap; strict filtering applied.  
- Fixed context window clipping (e.g., ±2 pages) balances relevance and size; leakage stable across sizes, so brevity preferred.  
- Implicit query realism is harder to control but better reflects real misuse.

**Failure signatures**  
- High implicit leakage (60–100%) vs explicit.  
- OCR paradox: OCR input increases leakage.  
- Near‑100% leakage for cross‑modal queries (chart+text, table+text).  
- DVA fails on contextual leakage (allowed facts combine to reveal protected info).  
- Baselines (CoT, revision) fail to sufficiently reduce leakage.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Main evaluation** (Sec 4.2): Test 6 LVLMs on Doc‑PP with image vs OCR inputs, establishing the Reasoning‑Induced Safety Gap and OCR Paradox.  
2. **Evidence type analysis** (Table 4): Break down leakage by evidence type (text, table, chart, cross‑modality) to pinpoint cross‑modal reasoning as the key risk factor.  
3. **Mitigation comparison** (Table 5): Compare DVA against CoT and revision baselines, demonstrating DVA’s substantial leakage reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why does providing OCR‑extracted text paradoxically increase information leakage despite improving perceptual clarity? The phenomenon is observed but its mechanism — e.g., whether numerical reasoning becomes easier or policy enforcement is less effective on structured text — remains speculative.
- **Open Question 2:** How can verification frameworks like DVA detect and prevent indirect leakage through contextual inference? DVA’s atomic verification misses cases where multiple allowed facts combine to reveal a protected value, leaving a gap for “contextual leakage.”
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent do construction biases in Doc‑PP (LLM‑assisted generation, limited document categories) affect benchmark validity and generalizability? Model‑in‑the‑loop creation may embed artifacts, and the focus on financial reports may not capture broader document layouts and policies.
- **Open Question 4:** What architectural or training modifications can give LVLMs an inherent ability to adhere to dynamic user‑defined policies during cross‑modal reasoning? Current approaches treat policy adherence as an inference‑time add‑on; models lack integrated mechanisms to track and enforce constraints throughout reasoning chains.

## Limitations
- **Domain narrowness:** Doc‑PP uses 90 business/financial PDFs and numeric targets; generalizability to legal, medical, or other document types is uncertain.
- **Dataset construction bias:** Policies and queries are generated with LLMs, potentially introducing model‑specific assumptions; only 100 samples were human‑validated.
- **Ethical handling of sensitive reports:** The source documents are real confidential reports, raising ethical concerns about storage, use, and potential leakage during experiments.

## Confidence
- **Reasoning‑Induced Safety Gap:** High – consistent empirical evidence across multiple models.
- **OCR Paradox:** Medium – observed trend but may depend on OCR quality and model perception updates.
- **DVA Framework:** Medium – shows strong reduction but fails on contextual leakage; decomposition quality is critical.

## Next Checks
1. Re‑run experiments with up‑to‑date LVLMs (e.g., GPT‑4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 2.0) on the public Doc‑PP dataset to verify persistence of the gap and paradox.
2. Conduct human analysis on DVA failure cases to distinguish decomposition errors from verification weaknesses, which will guide prompt refinements.
3. Build analogous benchmarks in legal and medical domains to assess cross‑domain robustness of both the policy‑preservation challenge and the DVA mitigation.
