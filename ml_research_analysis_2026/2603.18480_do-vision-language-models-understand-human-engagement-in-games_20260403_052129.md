# Do Vision Language Models Understand Human Engagement in Games?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18480
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18480
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Zero-shot vision-language models average ~57% accuracy on engagement prediction from gameplay videos, below the per-game majority-class baseline of 67.2%.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether vision-language models (VLMs) can infer human engagement—a latent psychological state—from first-person shooter gameplay videos. Using the GameVibe Few-Shot dataset across nine games, three VLMs are tested under six prompting strategies, including zero-shot, theory-guided, and retrieval-augmented approaches. Results show that zero-shot VLMs underperform relative to a simple majority baseline. Retrieval-augmented prompting improves pointwise accuracy for some models (e.g., Qwen3-VL reaches 75.0% with CLIP-based retrieval), while theory-guided prompting alone fails and can reinforce surface-level shortcuts. Pairwise prediction of engagement change remains near chance. Five systematic failure modes are identified, revealing a fundamental gap in current VLMs' ability to move from perceptual features to psychological state understanding.

## Method Summary
The study assesses pretrained VLMs (InternVL3.5-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, GPT-4o) on a binary classification task: predicting high or low player engagement from 1-second video windows (16 frames each). Data is drawn from the public GameVibe Few-Shot dataset, consisting of 61-second clips from nine FPS games, with labels binarized from aggregated human annotations. Six prompting strategies are compared: zero-shot (S1), theory-guided zero-shot (S2), few-shot with VLM embeddings (S3), few-shot with CLIP embeddings (S4), theory-guided few-shot with VLM embeddings (S5), and theory-guided few-shot with CLIP embeddings (S6). No model fine-tuning occurs; for retrieval-augmented settings, demonstrations are sourced from a 70% training split of the target game or from DOOM for cross-game transfer. Performance is measured via pointwise accuracy, temporal coherence metrics (flip rate, autocorrelation), and qualitative error analysis.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot VLM predictions average ~57% accuracy, below the per-game majority-class baseline of 67.2%.
- Retrieval-augmented prompting, particularly CLIP-based retrieval for Qwen3-VL, boosts pointwise accuracy up to 75.0%, with gains varying by game and visual similarity to DOOM.
- Theory-guided prompting alone does not improve performance and can amplify surface-level shortcuts; pairwise prediction of engagement change remains near chance across all strategies.
- Five failure modes are identified: visual intensity bias, reliance on surface features, post-match context blindness, temporal incoherence, and confident but wrong reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Visual Intensity Conflation**  
VLMs map high-contrast, high-motion visual features (e.g., explosions, saturation) directly to "intense = engaging" due to lacking a model of engagement as a latent psychological state. This heuristic fails when visually intense moments (e.g., routine combat, post-match scoreboards) coincide with low human engagement. Evidence: On CSGO18, VLM predictions correlate with visual intensity scores (r=0.432, p<0.001), while human labels do not (r=−0.193, p=0.143).

**Mechanism 2: Theory-Guided Shortcut Amplification**  
Structured prompts provide vocabulary for post-hoc rationalization; VLMs match visible cues (teammates, HUD elements) to theory dimensions (e.g., Flow, SDT) in a stimulus-response manner, ignoring contextual nuance like strategic tension. This relies on the false assumption that engagement theories have reliable direct visual correlates. Evidence: Error analysis of theory-guided predictions shows Challenge Misassessment (38.5%) and Relatedness Shortcuts (30.8%) dominate, accounting for over two-thirds of errors.

**Mechanism 3: Temporal Incoherence**  
VLMs process each 1-second window in isolation without memory, treating predictions as independent. Human engagement is temporally smooth (autocorrelation r=0.950), but VLMs exhibit high flip rates and low autocorrelation. Evidence: On CSGO18, VLM flip rate is 0.310—18× higher than human flip rate (0.017)—and autocorrelation is 0.275 vs. human 0.950.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Engagement as latent psychological state**  
  *Why needed:* The task requires inferring an unobservable internal state from visual cues; misunderstanding leads to treating it as a perceptual classification problem.  
  *Quick check:* Give an example where a visually intense frame might correspond to low player engagement, and explain why (e.g., post-match scoreboard with explosions but no active gameplay).

- **Concept: Prompting strategy space**  
  *Why needed:* The study systematically varies prompts to isolate how guidance and demonstrations affect performance; understanding dimensions (theory vs. examples, embedding space) is key to interpreting results.  
  *Quick check:* Why might theory-guided prompting alone harm performance on this task? (It can reinforce surface-level biases without concrete examples to correct them.)

- **Concept: Temporal dynamics of engagement signals**  
  *Why needed:* Human engagement is autocorrelated and smooth; pairwise prediction fails because VLMs lack temporal models to track state changes.  
  *Quick check:* Why is flip rate a meaningful metric for evaluating temporal coherence? (It quantifies prediction instability across adjacent windows; high flip rates indicate failure to maintain consistent state understanding.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Frame Sampling -> Prompt Construction (strategy S1-S6) -> VLM Inference (InternVL/Qwen/GPT-4o) -> Output Parsing. Retrieval branch: Frame Embedding (VLM last layer or CLIP) -> Similarity Search -> Demonstration Injection into prompt.

**Critical path:** For each evaluation window: sample 16 frames -> (optional) extract embeddings -> assemble prompt with/without retrieved demonstrations -> run VLM forward pass -> parse label and rationale -> store for accuracy/temporal analysis. Temporal metrics require stacking per-window outputs.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Zero-shot vs. few-shot: Few-shot improves pointwise accuracy but depends on demonstration balance; sensitive to label distribution.
- Theory guidance: Clarifies task structure but often amplifies biases; effective only when combined with few-shot balanced examples.
- Retrieval embedding: CLIP-based retrieval outperforms VLM embeddings for cross-game transfer (e.g., Qwen3-VL), suggesting task-agnostic semantics generalize better; scene-level visual similarity to DOUM predicts success.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Predictions correlate with visual intensity metrics (r>0.4) while human labels do not.
2. Over-prediction of "High" on games with low majority baseline (e.g., CSGO18).
3. Low temporal coherence: flip rate >0.3, autocorrelation <0.3 (vs. human >0.9).
4. Rationales cite surface features (teammates, explosions) even when ground truth contradicts.
5. Inverse confidence-accuracy: high-confidence predictions are less accurate than low-confidence ones.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate zero-shot baseline (S1) across all nine games to confirm average accuracy ~57% below majority baseline (67.2%).
2. Test CLIP-based retrieval-augmented prediction (S4) on Qwen3-VL to verify the 75.0% peak accuracy and analyze per-game variance linked to visual similarity to DOOM.
3. Compare theory-guided zero-shot (S2) vs. few-shot (S5) to isolate the effect of theory prompts: expect S2 to underperform S1 or introduce new errors, while S5 should show gains only when combined with balanced demonstrations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can temporal context be effectively incorporated into VLM architectures to improve engagement prediction consistency?
- Why do VLMs systematically conflate visual intensity with engagement, and what architectural or prompting changes can mitigate this bias?
- How do dataset characteristics (e.g., clip length, genre diversity, annotation granularity) impact the measured capabilities and failure modes of VLMs on engagement prediction?
- What modifications are necessary for VLMs to achieve reliable pairwise engagement prediction (i.e., correctly inferring engagement change between consecutive windows)?

## Limitations
- Dataset and preprocessing details are underspecified; public availability and exact label aggregation (trimmed mean) need verification.
- Retrieval-augmentation implementation (embedding layer, pooling, k-shot) lacks reproducibility guidance.
- Findings may not generalize beyond the three tested VLMs or the FPS game genre; theory-guided prompt failures could be due to wording.

## Confidence
- Zero-shot VLM underperformance vs. majority baseline: High
- Retrieval-augmentation improves pointwise accuracy for some models: Medium
- Theory-guided prompting amplifies shortcuts: High
- Temporal incoherence (high flip rates): High
- Visual intensity bias: Medium
