# Effective Reasoning Chains Reduce Intrinsic Dimensionality

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09276  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09276  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Effective reasoning strategies lower intrinsic dimensionality, which strongly correlates with better generalization on out-of-distribution math problems.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates why chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning improves language model generalization. It introduces intrinsic dimensionality—the minimum number of trainable parameters needed to reach a fixed accuracy threshold—as a measure of task compressibility. By fine-tuning Gemma-3 1B and 4B models on GSM8K with 14 different CoT strategies using LoRA, the authors find that more effective strategies yield lower intrinsic dimensionality. The inverse correlation between intrinsic dimensionality and generalization performance is strong (Spearman ρ=0.93 for 4B, ρ=0.75 for 1B). The metric outperforms alternatives like trajectory length and token perplexity, and is robust to threshold variations. Results suggest that good reasoning chains simplify the learning objective, enabling models to generalize with fewer parameter updates.

## Method Summary
The core method sweeps over low-rank adaptation (LoRA) configurations to estimate the intrinsic dimensionality of each reasoning strategy. For a fixed pretrained model (Gemma-3 1B/4B), the authors generate 14 reasoning-style datasets from GSM8K using teacher models (Gemma-27B, Gemini). For each dataset, they train LoRA adapters with varying ranks (r) and targeted module groups (attention_q_v, attention_all, mlp_all, all_layers), producing a range of trainable parameter counts. After a fixed number of training steps, they record training accuracy. A threshold τ is set as the maximum training accuracy achieved by any full-capacity LoRA configuration across all strategies. The intrinsic dimension d_int is the smallest parameter count where a strategy’s accuracy meets or exceeds τ. Separately, they train a full-capacity model per strategy and evaluate on six test splits (in-domain and OOD), computing overall accuracy as the geometric mean across splits. Finally, they compute Spearman correlation between d_int and overall accuracy.

## Key Results
- Intrinsic dimensionality inversely correlates with generalization (ρ=0.93 for 4B, ρ=0.75 for 1B).
- Executed Program-of-Thought (PoT) achieves the lowest intrinsic dimensionality and best out-of-distribution accuracy.
- Intrinsic dimensionality more strongly predicts generalization than trajectory length or token perplexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Effective reasoning chains are more compressible: they present a learning objective that can be captured by fewer parameter updates. Intrinsic dimensionality quantifies this compressibility; lower values indicate that the model can achieve a given performance level with less capacity, implying the task representation is simpler or more aligned with the pretrained knowledge. This simplicity translates to better generalization because the model adapts more efficiently without overfitting to spurious patterns in the training data.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (data and model flow):
Pretrained Gemma-3 Model → LoRA Adapter (configurable rank/group) → Training Loop (fixed steps, AdamW) → Training Accuracy Measurement → Intrinsic Dimension Determination (threshold crossing) → Full-capacity Model → Test Evaluation → Correlation Analysis.

Critical path:
1. Generate reasoning datasets for each strategy using teacher models.
2. For each strategy, perform LoRA sweep over parameter counts and record accuracies.
3. Compute threshold τ from full-capacity runs.
4. For each strategy, locate smallest parameter count achieving τ → intrinsic dimension.
5. Train full-capacity model per strategy and evaluate on all test splits.
6. Compute Spearman correlation between intrinsic dimensions and overall accuracies.

Design tradeoffs:
- LoRA rank range: Too narrow may miss threshold crossing; too wide increases compute. Chose log-spaced targets to cover orders of magnitude.
- Module groups: Targeting different subsets of layers affects efficiency and accuracy; using 4 groups balances granularity and cost.
- Fixed training steps vs. convergence: Short steps may not reach full potential for large capacities; but keeping steps constant across capacities isolates the effect of capacity.
- Threshold choice: Using max full-capacity accuracy ensures a common bar but may be unattainable for some strategies; alternative thresholds could be considered.

Failure signatures:
- Non-monotonic accuracy vs. parameter count (larger LoRA yields worse accuracy) indicates optimization instability; need more steps or LR tuning.
- No configuration reaches τ for a strategy → threshold too high or strategy fundamentally limited.
- Teacher-generated chains deviate from style (e.g., missing distractors, non-executable code) → dataset quality issue.

First three experiments:
1. **Monotonicity Check**: For a single strategy, run the full LoRA sweep and plot accuracy vs. parameter count; expect non-decreasing trend.
2. **Threshold Achievability**: After full-capacity runs, verify that every strategy’s largest LoRA configuration attains at least τ; if not, adjust τ downward.
3. **Correlation Robustness**: Replace intrinsic dimension with an alternative compressibility score (e.g., area under accuracy-parameter curve) and recompute correlation; compare strength.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can intrinsic dimensionality be estimated more efficiently without exhaustive LoRA sweeps?
2. Does the intrinsic dimensionality-generalization correlation generalize beyond GSM8K and Gemma models to other tasks, model families, and post-training methods?
3. What specific properties of reasoning chains (e.g., programmability, modularity, abstraction) cause them to have low intrinsic dimensionality, and can these be designed deliberately?
4. Why do larger models show higher intrinsic dimensionality for ineffective strategies (e.g., No CoT with distractors) compared to smaller models?

## Limitations
- Exact teacher prompts and decoding settings are unreported, preventing faithful recreation of the 14 reasoning datasets.
- Threshold τ defined as the maximum full-capacity accuracy may be unattainable for some strategies, making intrinsic dimension undefined or unstable.
- Only 14 strategies were examined, limiting statistical power of the correlation analysis.

## Confidence
- Intrinsic dimensionality inversely correlates with generalization: **Medium**
- Effective reasoning chains reduce intrinsic dimensionality: **Medium**
- Intrinsic dimensionality outperforms baseline metrics (trajectory length, token perplexity): **Low**
- Robustness to threshold selection: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. **Threshold Achievability Audit**: Re-run LoRA sweeps to obtain full accuracy curves; ensure each strategy’s largest LoRA meets or exceeds τ, or recompute τ as the minimum full-capacity accuracy across strategies.
2. **Prompt and Decoding Recovery**: Obtain or reconstruct the exact teacher prompts (including few-shot examples) and decoding parameters (temperature, top-p, max tokens) for all 14 strategies; regenerate datasets and validate style fidelity.
3. **Metric Sensitivity Analysis**: Vary LoRA sweep design (number of points, parameter bounds, module groups) and compute alternative compressibility metrics (e.g., accuracy at fixed parameter budget, AUC); test whether the inverse correlation persists.
