# MacroGuide: Topological Guidance for Macrocycle Generation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.14977
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14977
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves >99% macrocycle generation rate on pretrained diffusion models with minimal overhead.

## Executive Summary
Macrocyclic molecules (rings with ≥12 atoms) are important in drug discovery but are severely underrepresented in molecular datasets, causing standard generative models to produce almost none. MacroGuide addresses this by introducing a training‑free topological guidance module that steers pretrained diffusion models toward cyclic structures. At each reverse diffusion step, it builds a Vietoris‑Rips complex from atomic coordinates, computes persistent homology (H0 and H1), and applies gradient updates to enforce connectivity, a dominant chordless cycle, and a target ring size. The method requires no model retraining and is compatible with any diffusion‑based molecular generator. Applied to MolDiff and MolSnapper, MacroGuide lifts macrocycle generation from ~1% to >99% while preserving chemical validity, diversity, and structural quality, and it introduces only ~20% computational overhead. Users can specify desired ring size via theoretically grounded death‑time thresholds.

## Method Summary
MacroGuide is a plug‑and‑play module that modifies the sampling process of diffusion models without altering their parameters. After a standard denoising step yields intermediate atomic coordinates, the module constructs a Vietoris‑Rips filtration over these points and extracts persistent homology diagrams for H0 (connected components) and H1 (cycles). Three differentiable losses are defined: (i) *F*<sub>H1 death</sub> penalizes the death time of the most prominent H1 feature if it lies outside a user‑specified interval that encodes the desired ring size; (ii) *F*<sub>H1 birth</sub> penalizes the birth time of that feature if it exceeds a bond‑length threshold, thereby discouraging chords within the cycle; (iii) *F*<sub>H0 death</sub> penalizes H0 death times above a small distance to maintain molecular connectivity. The combined loss *F*<sub>TDA</sub> = *F*<sub>H1 death</sub> + *F*<sub>H1 birth</sub> + *F*<sub>H0 death</sub> is differentiated with respect to atomic coordinates, yielding a gradient Δ = –∇*F*<sub>TDA</sub>. The sample is updated as **x** ← **x** + λΔ, where λ is a fixed strength. For size control, Theorem 3.1 links the H1 death time to the number of atoms in an idealized crown‑shaped ring, enabling users to set the death interval according to their target ring size.

## Key Results
- Macrocycle generation rate increases from ~1% (baseline) to >99% with MacroGuide on both MolDiff and MolSnapper, for unconditional and protein‑conditioned generation.
- Chemical validity (RDKit parseability), single‑component connectivity, diversity, and structural quality (PoseBusters) are preserved or improved relative to state‑of‑the‑art methods.
- The method introduces minimal computational overhead (~20% when guiding every step) and allows specification of ring quantity and size via persistent homology thresholds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gradient‑Based Topological Optimization
MacroGuide directly optimizes the topology of the generated molecule by using differentiable persistent homology. The Vietoris‑Rips complex built from atomic coordinates yields H0 and H1 features whose persistence values reflect connectivity and cycle formation. The defined losses penalize a death time outside a target interval (to grow the ring), a birth time beyond a bond‑length threshold (to avoid chords), and H0 deaths that would fragment the molecule. Gradients from these losses are back‑propagated to the atomic positions, effectively modifying the denoising score and steering the sample toward a cyclic topology. The paper supports this with theoretical derivations (Section 3.1) and experiments showing consistent macrocycle emergence.

### Mechanism 2: Training‑Free Plug‑and‑Play Guidance
MacroGuide operates as a post‑processing step added to any pretrained diffusion model. After each denoising step, the base model’s output is corrected by a topological gradient step; the model parameters remain frozen. This separation preserves the base model’s knowledge of chemical realism while injecting the desired topological constraint. The approach stems from a conditional score decomposition and is demonstrated to work with two distinct architectures (MolDiff and MolSnapper), confirming its modularity and broad applicability.

### Mechanism 3: Theoretical Size Control
The target macrocycle size is encoded in the H1 death interval. Theorem 3.1 derives an approximately linear relationship between the death time of the dominant H1 feature and the number of atoms in an idealized equilateral crown polygon. By calibrating the death thresholds using this relation and the expected bond length, MacroGuide can steer generation toward rings of a specific size. Empirically, the median ring size follows the theoretical curve with ~3% error (Figure 5), validating the practicality of the mapping.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Base denoiser → Topological guidance module (VR complex, PH extraction, loss computation) → Integration layer (apply λΔ) → Updated sample.

**Critical path:** 1. Sample initial Gaussian noise *x*<sub>T</sub>. 2. For *t* = *T* down to 1:  
 a) Run base denoiser to obtain candidate *x*<sub>*t*−1</sub>.  
 b) If *t* is in the guidance schedule, construct Vietoris‑Rips complex on *x*<sub>*t*−1</sub>, extract H0 and H1 persistence, evaluate *F*<sub>TDA</sub>, compute Δ = –∇*F*<sub>TDA</sub>, and update *x*<sub>*t*−1</sub> ← *x*<sub>*t*−1</sub> + λΔ.  
3. Return final *x*<sub>0</sub>.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Guidance frequency:** Every step gives best macrocycle rate but ~20% overhead; applying every 5 steps cuts overhead with <15% relative drop.
- **λ strength:** Higher λ enforces constraints more strictly but may compromise chemical validity; λ=1 balanced performance.
- **H1 birth formulation:** Using ReLU penalty (not squared) avoids oscillatory gradients that harm final validity.
- **Masking for H0 death:** Gradient masking (stop gradient on the atom nearer the centroid) is necessary for stability; without it molecules can “unzip”.

**Failure signatures:**
- High rate of disconnected molecules → missing H0 death term or disabled gradient masking.
- Few or no macrocycles → H1 death interval mis‑set or H1 birth term missing/squared.
- Many steric clashes or PoseBusters failures → λ too large or guidance applied too late.
- Unstable loss/exploding gradients → incorrect masking implementation; verify centroid computation and stop‑gradient.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline check:** Generate 500 samples with base model only; confirm macrocycle rate near 1% and record validity.
2. **Full MacroGuide:** Use default parameters (λ=1, *d*<sub>min</sub>=4.45, *d*<sub>max</sub>=5.05, ℓ*=2, guidance every step) on 500 samples; expect >95% macrocycle rate and comparable validity.
3. **Ablation of H1 birth:** Remove H1 birth term; observe drop in closed macrocycle rate and potential increase in fragmented rings, confirming the term’s role.

## Limitations
- Theorem 3.1’s linear mapping between H1 death and ring size assumes an ideal crown‑shaped ring; deviations (e.g., elliptical, fused rings) cause size misestimation and non‑chordless cycles.
- Training‑free guidance may degrade for out‑of‑distribution protein pockets or when the base model’s score field is inaccurate, leading to reduced macrocycle rates or invalid structures.
- Key implementation details (exact λ scheduling, handling of bonds under position‑only updates, VR complex parameters) are underspecified, hindering exact reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Macrocycle generation rate:** High (consistent >99% on two models, extensive ablations).
- **Training‑free plug‑and‑play:** Medium (only two base models tested; generalizability to other architectures remains to be shown).
- **Size control accuracy:** Medium (theoretical mapping holds for crown geometry with ~3% error; performance may drop for distorted rings).
- **Overall chemical quality:** Medium (validity preserved but state‑of‑the‑art claims lack full independent verification).
