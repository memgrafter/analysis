# Context-free Self-Conditioned GAN for Trajectory Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08658
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08658
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Improves ADE/FDE on under-represented trajectory classes (e.g., Argoverse 'others') via cluster-based weighting from a self-conditioned GAN, while maintaining or slightly improving overall performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles mode collapse in context-free trajectory forecasting by introducing a self-conditioned GAN that discovers latent behavioral modes through clustering discriminator features. The discovered modes inform three training strategies (weighted loss, weighted batch sampling, or both) that amplify learning signals for under-represented or high-error motion patterns. Evaluated on THOR (human motion) and Argoverse (road agents) datasets, the method yields significant gains on the least-representative supervised classes—reducing ADE/FDE for the sparse 'others' class in Argoverse—and achieves overall improvement on THOR while staying competitive on Argoverse. Clustering analysis further reveals that the unsupervised modes capture semantically meaningful patterns like directional trends and trajectory length variations.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of two main stages. First, a self-conditioned GAN is trained where the generator takes an observed trajectory and a cluster ID (derived from k-Means on discriminator features of real full trajectories) to predict the future. Periodically, the discriminator’s feature embeddings for all real trajectories are clustered, assigning each training sample to a mode. Second, the intra-cluster forecasting errors (ADE/FDE) from this self-conditioned GAN are used to compute per-cluster weights Λ_i, which balance cluster size and error. A vanilla GAN (without mode conditioning) is then trained with these weights applied either to the generator’s L2 loss (wL2), to the batch sampling distribution (wB), or both (combined). This weighting scheme emphasizes under-represented or challenging modes, mitigating mode collapse.

## Key Results
- Reduces ADE/FDE for the least-representative supervised class in Argoverse ('others'): ADE 1.815→1.789, FDE 2.969→2.919.
- Improves overall ADE on THOR (0.685→0.658) while maintaining competitive performance on Argoverse.
- Clustering yields interpretable motion modes (e.g., directional trends, trajectory length variations) and the self-conditioned GAN approaches an oracle conditional GAN, validating the mode discovery.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Clustering discriminator features identifies behavioral modes.**  
- *Claim:* Clustering discriminator feature embeddings from a self-conditioned GAN reveals latent behavioral modes in trajectory data.  
- *Mechanism:* During GAN training, the discriminator learns a rich feature representation of full trajectories. These features are periodically clustered (k‑Means), and each cluster is treated as an unsupervised “mode” label. The generator is then conditioned on these labels, forcing it to learn mode‑specific mappings.  
- *Evidence anchors:* The paper explicitly states that “clustering discriminator features to identify behavioral modes” (abstract) and that “the clustering algorithm provides the respective modes based on the discriminator’s encoder features” (Section III.B.1).  
- *Break condition:* If the discriminator’s features lack semantic structure (e.g., collapse to noise), clustering will not yield meaningful modes and conditioning will not improve diversity.

**Mechanism 2: Weighting based on cluster statistics mitigates mode collapse.**  
- *Claim:* Weighting the generator’s loss and/or batch sampling by cluster size and intra‑cluster error amplifies learning signals for under‑represented or challenging clusters.  
- *Mechanism:* For each cluster *i*, a weight Λ_i combines (1) inverse cluster size to combat sample imbalance, (2) intra‑cluster ADE/FDE error from the self‑conditioned GAN to target difficult modes, and (3) tunable λ coefficients. Λ_i scales the L2 loss term or biases the batch sampler.  
- *Evidence anchors:* Tables I & II show improvements on the smallest clusters and least‑representative supervised labels, supporting that weighting targets under‑learned modes.  
- *Break condition:* If cluster assignments are noisy or misaligned with true behavior, weighting could force the generator to fit artificial groupings, harming overall performance.

**Mechanism 3: Self‑conditioned clusters approximate a lower bound of a conditional GAN.**  
- *Claim:* The self‑conditioned GAN’s performance relative to a cGAN with ground‑truth labels indicates that the discovered clusters capture meaningful motion modes.  
- *Mechanism:* The unsupervised clusters act as soft, discovered labels. Conditioning the generator on these labels yields a forecaster that approximates the performance of an ideal cGAN. The small gap on THOR (self‑conditioned ADE 0.591 vs. cGAN 0.657) supports this.  
- *Evidence anchors:* Section IV.C compares the self‑conditioned GAN to a cGAN with true labels; the gap is modest on THOR. Qualitative analysis (Fig. 4, 5) shows clusters correspond to directional trends (THOR) and trajectory length (Argoverse).  
- *Break condition:* If clusters are purely statistical artifacts with no correspondence to behavioral distinctions, the gap to a true cGAN would be large and unexplainable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Dataset → Preprocess → Self‑Conditioned GAN (Generator ↔ Discriminator) → Cluster Manager (k‑Means on D features) → Weight Calculator (Λ_i) → Vanilla GAN (Generator trained with weighting) → Evaluation.

**Critical path:**  
1. Load and preprocess dataset (e.g., pythor‑tools for THOR).  
2. Train a self‑conditioned GAN: generator G (LSTM encoder‑decoder) takes observed trajectory X and cluster label *m*; discriminator D (MLP encoder) scores full trajectories. Periodically (every *N* epochs) cluster D’s features on all real full trajectories (k=13 for THOR, 19 for Argoverse).  
3. Freeze cluster assignments; compute intra‑cluster ADE/FDE from the self‑conditioned GAN to derive weights Λ_i per Equation 2.  
4. Train a vanilla GAN (identical generator but without *m*) using one of three weighting schemes: weighted L2 loss (wL2), weighted batch sampler (wB), or combined.  
5. Evaluate per‑supervised‑label and overall ADE/FDE.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Number of clusters* *k*: too small merges distinct modes; too many creates sparse/noisy clusters; optimal *k* must be tuned per dataset (13/19).  
- *Discriminator architecture*: MLP chosen for stability vs. LSTM for richer temporal features.  
- *Weighting terms*: Balancing sample‑size penalty and error term; λ hyperparameters require tuning—over‑weighting error may cause overfitting to outliers.  
- *Context‑free constraint*: Discards scene/social context, improving flexibility but losing cues crucial for some modes (e.g., intersection behavior).

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Clustering instability: large shifts in assignments across reclustering steps indicate discriminator features are not forming stable basins.  
2. No per‑class improvement (e.g., ‘workers’ in THOR or ‘agent’ in Argoverse) suggests cluster definitions misalign with supervised labels or hyperparameters are mis‑specified.  
3. Global metrics degrade slightly (e.g., Argoverse ADE 1.912→1.923) indicates trade‑off: improving minority modes may harm majority mode performance if weighting is too aggressive.  
4. Qualitative failures: generated trajectories in “challenging” clusters still deviate significantly, meaning self‑conditioned intra‑cluster error was already high and weighting did not close the gap.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce Table I (Intra‑Class Metrics):** Train self‑conditioned GAN, derive clusters, compute Λ_i. Train Vanilla GAN baselines (no weighting) and proposed (wL2, wB, combined). Verify improvement on least‑representative class (e.g., Argoverse ‘others’) is statistically significant across ≥5 runs.  
2. **Cluster Validation (Qualitative & Semantic):** For the best run, visualize trajectories from smallest/high‑error clusters (e.g., cluster 10 in Argoverse, cluster 9 in THOR). Confirm they share a plausible, coherent behavioral pattern (direction, speed, path shape) as in Fig. 4. Compute silhouette score to sanity‑check separation.  
3. **Ablation on Λ_i Terms:** Systematically disable terms in Equation 2 (e.g., λ_ADE=0, λ_D=0). Measure impact on target class performance and overall ADE. Identify which term (sample size vs. error) contributes most to under‑represented mode improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does the number of clusters *k* impact mode discovery and forecasting accuracy?** The paper reports optimal *k* by grid search (13 for THOR, 19 for Argoverse) but shows no sensitivity study.  
2. **Can clusters be automatically associated with high‑level motion attributes (e.g., speed, intent) for interpretability?** The paper notes difficulty in understanding what each cluster represents.  
3. **Does incorporating contextual information (e.g., social interactions, scene layout) enhance mode learning and forecasting?** The work is explicitly context‑free and leaves the use of context unclear.  
4. **Does the weighted training actually increase trajectory diversity, and what metrics best capture this?** Evaluation uses only ADE/FDE (accuracy); no diversity metrics are reported.

## Limitations
- Missing key hyperparameters (λ values, learning rates, clustering frequency) hinder exact replication.  
- Discriminator feature clustering stability not evaluated; drifting assignments could invalidate weighting.  
- Limited quantitative validation of cluster semantics; no silhouette scores or robust alignment with true labels beyond a few classes.  
- Improvements on minority classes come with slight degradation on majority classes, indicating an unquantified trade‑off.  
- Statistical significance of results is unclear due to unspecified number of experimental runs.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Clustering reveals latent modes):** Medium – qualitative examples support meaningful clusters, but robust validation (stability, internal metrics) is lacking.  
- **Mechanism 2 (Weighting mitigates mode collapse):** High – consistent gains on under‑represented classes across two datasets, supported by comparative tables.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Clusters approximate cGAN lower bound):** Medium – promising THOR comparison to true cGAN, but gap not closed and interpretation remains speculative.

## Next Checks
1. **Stability Test:** Track cluster assignments over multiple reclustering steps; compute adjusted Rand index to quantify consistency. Low consistency would undermine the method’s core assumption.  
2. **Parameter Sensitivity:** Systematically vary the number of clusters *k* and λ coefficients; evaluate per‑class ADE/FDE to identify robust configurations and ensure gains are not overly sensitive.  
3. **Statistical Significance:** Run the full pipeline with ≥5 random seeds; apply paired t‑tests on minority‑class ADE/FDE (e.g., Argoverse “others”) to verify that observed improvements are statistically significant.
