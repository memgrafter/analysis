# Agenda-based Narrative Extraction: Steering Pathfinding Algorithms with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29661
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29661
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLM-driven steering achieves 9.9% higher agenda alignment than keyword matching on semantic agendas (p=0.017) with minimal coherence loss (2.2%), and counter‑agenda scores remain uniformly low (2.2–2.5/10).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the trade‑off in narrative extraction between coherence, interactivity, and multi‑perspective support. It introduces agenda‑based narrative extraction, integrating large language models into the maximum‑capacity pathfinding algorithm (Narrative Trails) to steer storyline construction toward user‑specified perspectives. Evaluated on a news corpus (418 articles on the 2021 Cuban protests) across 64 endpoint pairs and 6 agendas, LLM‑driven steering significantly outperforms keyword matching on semantic agendas while preserving coherence. Counter‑agenda experiments confirm the method cannot fabricate narratives unsupported by the source material.

## Method Summary
The method builds a coherence graph from a timestamped news corpus. Documents are embedded with Sentence‑BERT, reduced to 2D via UMAP, and clustered with HDBSCAN to obtain soft topic distributions. Edge weights combine spatial similarity (cosine on 2D vectors) and topical similarity (1 – JSD of cluster memberships), producing a directed acyclic graph. A maximum‑capacity pathfinder (modified Dijkstra) then extracts bottleneck‑optimal narratives. For agenda steering, at each step the top‑*k* neighbors by edge weight are ranked by an LLM given the agenda and context; the ranked order determines priority queue expansion. Baselines include maximum capacity (coherence‑only) and keyword matching (TF‑IDF similarity).

## Key Results
- LLM‑driven steering achieves 9.9% higher agenda alignment than keyword matching on semantic agendas (p=0.017), with a 13.3% improvement on the “Regime Crackdown” agenda (p=0.037).
- Coherence is minimally reduced by 2.2% compared to the coherence‑optimized baseline.
- Counter‑agenda scores uniformly low (2.2–2.5/10) across all methods, confirming steering cannot fabricate unsupported narratives.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach integrates three synergistic mechanisms:

- **LLM‑driven candidate ranking** replaces greedy selection with ranked expansion. The top‑*k* coherence neighbors are ranked by an LLM based on agenda alignment and narrative progress. This enables backtracking and broader exploration without abandoning coherence. Evidence: Algorithm 1 and prompt details (Section 3.2); 9.9% alignment gain (p=0.017) with only 2.2% coherence loss.
- **Coherence graph construction** creates a structured space where spatial proximity and topical similarity correlate with human‑judged coherence. The graph’s edge weight θ(u,v)=√(𝑆(𝑧̂ᵤ, 𝑧̂ᵥ)·𝑇(𝑝ᵤ,𝑝𝑣)) combines cosine similarity of 2D UMAP vectors and 1–JSD of soft cluster memberships. Evidence: Maximum capacity baseline achieves mean coherence 6.33/10; steering reduces coherence by only 0.14 points (2.2%).
- **Maximum capacity path optimization** maximizes the minimum edge weight along a path, ensuring no single transition falls below a coherence threshold. This bottleneck formulation yields globally stable narratives. Evidence: The baseline sets an upper bound on coherence; LLM‑steered paths maintain near‑baseline coherence, showing the framework tolerates agenda bias while avoiding weak links.

## Foundational Learning
- **Coherence Graph Construction** – The graph defines the search space; understanding its construction helps diagnose why transitions are coherent. *Quick check:* How would coherence scores change if you replaced UMAP with PCA, given the curse of dimensionality in high‑dimensional spaces?
- **Maximum Capacity Pathfinding** – This core algorithm determines path extraction and coherence prioritization. *Quick check:* Why does maximizing the minimum edge weight (bottleneck) typically produce more narratively satisfying sequences than maximizing the sum of edge weights?
- **LLM‑as‑a‑Judge for Ranking** – Steering relies on the LLM to evaluate candidates against the agenda, requiring careful prompt design and awareness of LLM biases. *Quick check:* What are two risks of using an LLM to rank documents for narrative alignment, and how might the paper’s prompt design mitigate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Corpus Preprocessor → Embedding Engine → Graph Builder → Pathfinder Core (with LLM Ranker Service) → Evaluation Module. The LLM Ranker is invoked repeatedly during path expansion.  
**Critical path:** Raw Corpus → Graph Builder → (for each endpoint pair + agenda) → Pathfinder with LLM Ranker → Narrative Output → Evaluation. LLM Ranker latency directly impacts total extraction time.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *k* size: Larger *k* gives the LLM more choice but increases calls per step and queue size; paper uses *k*=5.  
- LLM vs. keyword: LLM yields semantic alignment gains (9.9% on semantic agendas) but adds ~5× latency (161s vs 32s per narrative).  
- Coherence vs. alignment: Weak correlation (*r*≈0.10) suggests independence; a small coherence loss (2.2%) may be acceptable for alignment gains.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Low alignment (<3/10) on semantic agendas despite LLM steering → LLM prompt failure or inadequate graph topology.  
- Coherence drop >5% relative to maximum capacity → LLM consistently selects edges below the bottleneck threshold.  
- Counter‑agenda scores >4/10 → Steering fabricates unsupported narratives.  
- High Jaccard similarity (>0.8) between LLM and keyword paths on semantic agendas → LLM not leveraging semantic understanding.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline replication: Run maximum capacity, keyword matching, and LLM‑driven on all 64 endpoint pairs × 6 agendas; verify LLM advantage on semantic agendas and minimal coherence loss.  
2. Sensitivity sweep: Vary *k* (1–10), temperature (0.1–1.0), and model size (270M–12B) on the 17 most distant endpoint pairs; plot alignment/coherence to assess robustness and identify cost‑quality trade‑offs.  
3. Counter‑agenda stress test: Run all methods on “Protests Failing” and “Regime Popular” agendas; expect uniformly low scores (~2.2–2.5). Any method scoring >4 indicates a flaw.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**1. Chain‑of‑thought effect:** Does the improvement from chain‑of‑thought prompting stem from the reasoning steps themselves or from other prompt changes? The paper notes confounding variables and calls for an ablation to isolate the effect.  
**2. Generalization:** How do results hold across other domains, languages, and agenda types? The single‑corpus evaluation limits generalizability; testing on multiple corpora is needed.  
**3. Human validation:** Do human experts’ assessments of narrative quality agree with the LLM‑judge metrics? The authors plan human studies to evaluate practical utility for sensemaking tasks.  
**4. Optimal candidate pool size:** What is the best *k* and how does it interact with graph density or agenda specificity? The paper’s sensitivity analysis lacked power; a larger factorial experiment is needed.

## Limitations
- Reliance on an unreleased corpus and unspecified hyperparameters (e.g., UMAP settings, HDBSCAN parameters, sparsification threshold) hinders faithful replication.
- Evaluation uses speculative LLM models (Gemma 3 12B, Claude Opus 4.5, GPT 5.1) and undisclosed endpoint pairs, adding uncertainty.
- Single‑domain news corpus and limited set of six agendas restrict generalizability to other contexts.

## Confidence
- Alignment improvement: **Medium** (statistically significant but dependent on unreleased data and prompts)
- Coherence preservation: **Medium** (small drop reported, but graph construction underspecified)
- Counter‑agenda integrity: **Medium** (low scores suggest robustness, yet may reflect corpus constraints)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑corpus test:** Apply the pipeline to a public news dataset (e.g., ACE2005) using the same agendas and compare results.
2. **Graph construction ablation:** Systematically vary UMAP neighbors, HDBSCAN min_cluster_size, and sparsification threshold; measure impact on alignment and coherence.
3. **Human verification:** For 20 random endpoint pairs, obtain human ratings of coherence and agenda alignment; assess correlation with automatic scores and whether the LLM‑driven method still outperforms.
