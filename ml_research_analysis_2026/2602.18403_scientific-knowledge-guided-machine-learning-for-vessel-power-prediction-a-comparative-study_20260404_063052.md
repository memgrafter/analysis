# Scientific Knowledge-Guided Machine Learning for Vessel Power Prediction: A Comparative Study

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.18403  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18403  
- **Reference count:** 9  
- **Primary result:** Hybrid physics-guided ML models improve extrapolation stability and physical consistency of vessel engine power prediction compared to pure data-driven approaches.

## Executive Summary
Accurate prediction of vessel engine power is essential for efficiency and emissions compliance, but conventional machine learning models often violate the fundamental propeller law (power ∝ speed³) when extrapolating beyond training conditions. This study proposes a hybrid framework that combines a physics-based sea‑trial power curve (baseline) with data‑driven residual learners (XGBoost, Neural Network, Physics‑Informed Neural Network). Validated on in‑service data, the hybrid models match baseline accuracy in data‑rich regions while showing markedly smoother and physically coherent power‑speed curves in sparsely sampled operating conditions. The hybrid PINN variant achieves the best balance of accuracy and extrapolation stability, enhancing reliability for maritime decision‑support applications like weather routing where predictions in unseen conditions are critical.

## Method Summary
The method decomposes the total predicted power into a deterministic physics baseline and a learned residual:  
\[
\hat{P} = P_{\text{baseline}}(V, T) + f(\mathbf{X}),
\]  
where \(P_{\text{baseline}}\) is a calm‑water sea‑trial power law \(P = cV^n\) with coefficients \(c, n\) derived from ballast and laden trials and linearly interpolated for draft \(T\). The target residual \(R = P_{\text{measured}} - P_{\text{baseline}}\) is used to train three data‑driven models \(f\): XGBoost, a standard feed‑forward neural network, and a physics‑informed neural network (PINN). The PINN adds a physics‑regularization term to the loss function that penalizes deviation of the total derivative \(\partial\hat{P}/\partial V\) from the propeller law derivative \(3cV^2\). All models are trained on standardized features (speed, draft, trim, wind components, etc.) and evaluated on in‑service data, with emphasis on performance in extrapolation regimes.

## Key Results
- Hybrid models preserve baseline accuracy in dense data regions while producing physically plausible smooth curves during extrapolation, unlike pure ML models that often yield non‑monotonic or unphysical predictions.
- The hybrid PINN demonstrates the strongest extrapolation stability and overall performance due to its embedded derivative constraint.
- The framework enhances reliability for maritime decision‑support tasks where predictions in unseen operating conditions are essential.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Physics‑anchored decomposition.**  
Hybridizing as \(P = P_{\text{sea‑trial}} + f(\mathbf{X})\) anchors predictions to a physically consistent power‑law baseline. The propeller law captures the dominant trend; the residual learner \(f\) only models deviations caused by environment and operations. This ensures the overall power‑speed relationship remains physically grounded even when \(f\) extrapolates poorly. *Evidence:* Eq. (4) defines the decomposition; Fig. 2 illustrates it.

**Mechanism 2: Residual learning simplifies the task.**  
By subtracting the strong low‑frequency power‑law trend, the residual becomes a smoother, lower‑variance target. This regularization reduces model complexity requirements and mitigates overfitting to noise, improving generalization from sparse data. *Evidence:* Abstract and Sec. 1.3 state the simplification; Sec. 4.2 shows hybrid models yield smoother extrapolation curves.

**Mechanism 3: PINN derivative constraint enforces physics.**  
For the hybrid PINN, the physics loss penalizes \(|\partial\hat{P}/\partial V - 3cV^2|^2\) on the total prediction \(\hat{P} = P_{\text{sea‑trial}} + f(\mathbf{X})\). This explicit enforcing of the propeller law’s derivative shape on the final output further improves extrapolation consistency. *Evidence:* Sec. 2.3 derives the loss; Sec. 4.2 reports the PINN’s superior adherence.

## Foundational Learning

### Propeller Law (\(P = cV^n\))
- **Why needed:** Provides the fundamental physics baseline; the exponent \(n \approx 3\) for displacement hulls sets the dominant power‑speed scaling.
- **Quick check:** If sea‑trial data at ballast draft yields \(n=2.8\) and at laden draft \(n=3.1\), how does the paper’s linear interpolation for intermediate drafts affect the baseline’s adherence to the power law?

### Residual Learning / Delta Modeling
- **Why needed:** Core paradigm shift; the model learns deviations from the physics baseline, simplifying the learning problem and improving generalization.
- **Quick check:** If the residual target has a non‑zero mean across the training set, what does that imply about the baseline, and how should the final prediction be corrected?

### Physics‑Informed Neural Network (PINN) Loss Penalty
- **Why needed:** The hybrid PINN uses a soft constraint via automatic differentiation; understanding loss weighting (e.g., \(\lambda=100\)) is key to training dynamics and stability.
- **Quick check:** Why is the physics loss applied to the derivative \(\partial P/\partial V\) rather than directly to \(P\)? What could be a downside of directly penalizing \(|P_{\text{NN}} - cV^n|\)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Sea‑Trial Baseline Module (inputs: \(V, T\) → output \(P_{\text{sea‑trial}}(V,T)\) via Eq. 3) → features → standardized \(\mathbf{\bar{X}}\) → Residual Learner \(f(\mathbf{\bar{X}})\) → output residual \(R\) → final prediction \(\hat{P} = P_{\text{sea‑trial}} + R\). For the PINN variant: additional physics loss \(L_{\text{P.law}}\) computed from \(\partial\hat{P}/\partial V\) using autograd and added to total loss.

**Critical path:**  
1. Preprocess data, compute \(P_{\text{sea‑trial}}\) for all samples.  
2. Compute residual target \(R = P_{\text{measured}} - P_{\text{sea‑trial}}\).  
3. Train \(f\) on \((\mathbf{\bar{X}}, R)\); for PINN include \(L_{\text{P.law}}\) with weight \(\lambda=100\).  
4. Deploy as \(\hat{P} = P_{\text{sea‑trial}} + f(\mathbf{\bar{X}})\).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **XGBoost Hybrid:** Fast training/inference; robust to noisy features; less inherently physics‑aware beyond the baseline; performance highly sensitive to hyperparameters.  
- **NN Hybrid:** More flexible function approximator; requires careful tuning (learning rate, depth) to avoid overfitting the residual; simpler than PINN.  
- **PINN Hybrid:** Best theoretical physical consistency; directly enforces derivative law; training more complex (loss weighting, autograd stability) and may sacrifice in‑distribution accuracy if \(\lambda\) is poorly chosen.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Non‑monotonic \(P\text{–}V\) curves in extrapolation: indicates baseline anchoring failure or spurious residual learning.  
- Large, abrupt step changes in predictions: suggests instability in residual model, especially tree‑based, in feature space voids.  
- PINN loss not decreasing: points to poor \(\lambda\) choice or architectural inability to satisfy derivative constraint.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline sanity check:** Plot \(P_{\text{sea‑trial}}(V)\) for ballast, laden, and interpolated drafts; verify smooth power‑law curves (correct \(n\) values). Confirm training residuals have mean ≈ 0.  
2. **Residual model convergence:** Train each \(f\) variant on residual target; plot training/validation loss curves; ensure stable convergence without overfitting (validate on residual error).  
3. **Extrapolation probe:** Sweep speed (e.g., 8–17 kn) at a fixed draft/wind condition poorly represented in test data; plot predictions \(\hat{P}\) from all models. Expect baseline‑only models to show non‑physical kinks/flattening; all hybrid models should yield smooth curves anchored to the \(P_{\text{sea‑trial}}\) envelope, with the PINN producing the smoothest curve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** What architectural or training‑specific mechanisms within the Physics‑Informed Neural Network (PINN) enable its superior extrapolation stability compared to XGBoost and standard Neural Networks under the hybrid framework?  
**Basis in paper:** The results state the hybrid PINN "demonstrates the best overall performance" and "strongest physical consistency," but the analysis does not dissect whether this stems from its embedded derivative constraints, its network depth/width, or an interaction between the physics loss and residual learning structure.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper compares end‑model performance but does not perform ablation studies (e.g., removing the physics loss term from the hybrid PINN, or using the PINN’s architecture without the hybrid residual decomposition) to isolate the source of its extrapolation advantage.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments where the physics‑informed loss component, network architecture parameters, and the hybrid decomposition are varied independently during training, with extrapolation performance quantified across multiple unseen operating regimes (e.g., extreme speeds, wind directions).

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How do the characteristics (e.g., magnitude, nonlinearity, sparsity) of the residual deviations—caused by environmental and operational factors—influence the comparative performance and generalization bounds of different hybrid residual learners (XGBoost vs. NN vs. PINN)?  
**Basis in paper:** The hybrid framework assumes residual learning is simpler than direct power prediction. The paper demonstrates improved extrapolation but does not analyze the residual data distribution itself or correlate its statistical properties (e.g., heteroscedasticity, complexity) with model performance failures or successes across architectures.  
**Why unresolved:** The study validates on one dataset; it is unclear if residual patterns (e.g., stronger wind effects, fouling‑induced drift) might overwhelm certain learners (e.g., tree‑based models with limited extrapolation capacity) while others (e.g., PINNs with smoothness priors) remain robust.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A detailed residual analysis across all data segments, coupled with synthetic data experiments where residual complexity (e.g., added high‑frequency noise, nonlinear trends) is systematically increased to determine performance degradation thresholds for each hybrid model type.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** For the hybrid PINN, what is the optimal weighting coefficient (\(\lambda\)) for the physics‑based loss term to maximize extrapolation reliability without compromising in‑distribution accuracy, and does this optimal \(\lambda\) vary with operational conditions (e.g., draft, wind severity)?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper states: "\(\lambda\) is set to 100 in this work… \(\lambda\) can be treated as a tunable hyperparameter in future studies." The fixed value is admitted as a simplification.  
**Why unresolved:** The choice of \(\lambda=100\) is not justified by a sensitivity analysis. An inappropriate \(\lambda\) could either under‑constrain the model (leading to non‑physical extrapolations) or over‑constrain it (reducing its ability to learn true residual patterns from data). Its potential dependence on operating regimes is unexplored.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive hyperparameter sweep over \(\lambda\) values for the hybrid PINN, evaluating a suite of metrics that separately measure in‑distribution accuracy (e.g., MAE on test set) and out‑of‑distribution physical consistency (e.g., deviation from expected power‑law slope in extrapolation zones), possibly conditioned on draft or wind speed bins.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How does the choice of the physics‑based baseline model (beyond the calm‑water sea‑trial power law \(P=cV^n\) and linear draft interpolation) impact the overall hybrid model's accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness to data scarcity?  
**Basis in paper:** The framework relies on a specific, simplified baseline: calm‑water curves from two drafts with linear interpolation. The paper acknowledges linear interpolation is a simplification but does not test alternatives (e.g., using higher‑fidelity hydrodynamic models, non‑linear draft dependence, or incorporating hull/propeller degradation trends).  
**Why unresolved:** The baseline's fidelity and flexibility directly affect the residual learning task. A more accurate but complex baseline might reduce residual magnitude but increase computational cost or introduce its own errors. A simpler baseline might leave larger, harder‑to‑learn residuals.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative experiments using different baseline constructions (e.g., cubic spline interpolation between sea‑trial drafts, a baseline including a quadratic fouling penalty term) within the same hybrid framework, measuring changes in residual variance, model training stability, and extrapolation performance.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** To what extent can the proposed hybrid residual framework's extrapolation benefits be generalized to other vessel types (e.g., container ships vs. tankers) and other performance prediction tasks (e.g., fuel consumption, EEOI), given varying propeller law exponents (\(n\)) and dominant physical relationships?  
**Basis in paper:** Validation is on one vessel's dataset. The propeller law exponent \(n\) is vessel‑specific. The framework's core idea—decomposing into a physics‑based dominant relationship and a residual learner—may have different efficacy if the dominant relationship is less pronounced (e.g., for vessels with highly complex propulsion systems) or if the target variable has different physical scaling (e.g., fuel consumption often relates to power with an efficiency curve).  
**Why unresolved:** The paper positions the method as broadly applicable for "vessel performance monitoring" but provides evidence only for main engine power on a single ship. Generalizability to other contexts with different physical priors is an assumption.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Application of the identical hybrid modeling workflow (same baseline form, same ML architectures) to multiple, diverse vessel datasets (different sizes, types, propulsion systems) and to different target variables (e.g., daily fuel consumption), quantifying the consistency of the extrapolation improvement across contexts.

## Limitations
- **Baseline dependency:** The hybrid approach critically depends on the accuracy and relevance of the sea‑trial power curve; an outdated or inaccurate baseline introduces systematic bias that the residual learner cannot fully correct.
- **Generalization across fleet:** Validation appears limited to a single vessel type/data source; it is uncertain whether the same decomposition and residual features generalize effectively across different ship designs, sizes, or operational profiles without re‑tuning.
- **Physics constraint specificity:** The PINN’s derivative constraint assumes the propeller law holds exactly for the total power; this may be mis‑specified in conditions like extreme shallow‑water effects or strong currents, potentially harming accuracy.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid decomposition improves extrapolation stability relative to pure data‑driven models on the presented dataset** — High confidence.  
- **Residual learning simplification is the primary driver of improved generalization, not just baseline anchoring** — Medium confidence (ablation needed for full disentanglement).  
- **Hybrid PINN’s explicit derivative constraint is the optimal architecture for physical consistency** — Medium‑Low confidence (sensitivity to loss weight \(\lambda\) and truly out‑of‑distribution physics remain uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. **Baseline decay sensitivity test:** Artificially degrade the sea‑trial baseline (e.g., erroneous \(n\) or \(c\) by ± 10%) and re‑evaluate all hybrid models; quantify error inflation compared to accurate baseline.
2. **Cross‑vessel transfer learning:** Apply the trained residual models from the study vessel to a different but similar vessel, using only its baseline and minimal retraining (e.g., fine‑tuning); measure performance drop versus training from scratch.
3. **PINN loss weighting ablation:** Systematically vary the physics loss weight \(\lambda\) (e.g., {1, 10, 100, 1000}) during hybrid PINN training; plot total prediction curves in extrapolation zones against \(\lambda\) to identify the trade‑off frontier between data fit and physical consistency.
