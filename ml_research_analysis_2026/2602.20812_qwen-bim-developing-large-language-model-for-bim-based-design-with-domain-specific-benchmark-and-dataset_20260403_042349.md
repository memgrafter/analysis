# Qwen-BIM: developing large language model for BIM-based design with domain-specific benchmark and dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20812
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20812
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Qwen-BIM (14B) achieves a 21.0% average G-Eval increase over its base model and performs comparably to general LLMs with 671B parameters on BIM tasks.

## Executive Summary
General large language models (LLMs) underperform on BIM-based design tasks due to a lack of domain-specific data and evaluation benchmarks. This work introduces a dedicated BIM benchmark with 22 question templates and a methodology to generate a BIM-QA dataset by textualizing Revit/IFC models and injecting synthetic defects. A reasoning-augmented dataset (BIM-QRA) is also created. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-14B-instruct with LoRA on this data yields Qwen-BIM, which demonstrates a 21.0% improvement in the primary G-Eval metric and matches the performance of much larger generalist LLMs on BIM-specific tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors first constructed a BIM-QA dataset by converting BIM models into semi-structured textual descriptions (Header, Component Header, Component Data) and generating 3,300 question-answer pairs across five building types. Synthetic defects (e.g., incorrect dimensions, missing numbers) were systematically injected to create domain-specific problem-solving tasks. A second dataset, BIM-QRA (1,364 samples), was derived by extracting high-quality reasoning chains from a strong LLM (DeepSeek-R1) for a subset of questions. For model adaptation, Qwen2.5-14B-instruct was fine-tuned using LoRA (rank=8, alpha=32) on mixtures of QA and QRA data, with the optimal configuration using 100% QRA for 3 epochs. Evaluation was conducted on 20 held-out partitions using a standardized prompt and multiple metrics (Format Score, BLEU, ROUGE, Cosine, G-Eval), with G-Eval (via GLM-4-plus) as the primary measure.

## Key Results
- Qwen-BIM (14B) achieved a 21.0% average increase in G-Eval score over its base model (Qwen2.5-14B-instruct).
- On BIM tasks, Qwen-BIM's performance is comparable to general LLMs with 671B parameters.
- Fine-tuning with 100% reasoning-augmented (QRA) data for 3 epochs yielded the best results, improving Format Score from 0.86 to 0.90.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Domain-specific fine-tuning adapts the base model's general knowledge to BIM's unique syntax, semantics, and reasoning patterns. The textualization pipeline translates BIM's structured, hierarchical data (elements, relationships, properties) into a sequential format LLMs can process. Injecting synthetic defects creates a diverse training distribution that teaches the model to identify and resolve common BIM errors. The inclusion of explicit reasoning traces (QRA) trains the model to produce structured, step-by-step analyses, which is critical for complex tasks like design review and compliance checking. LoRA efficiently updates a subset of parameters, allowing specialization while largely preserving the base model's general capabilities and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

## Foundational Learning
- **BIM Textualization**: Serializes Revit/IFC models into semi-structured text (Header, Component Header, Component Data). *Why needed*: LLMs cannot parse binary BIM files directly. *Quick check*: Can the textual output be parsed to reconstruct key component relationships?
- **Synthetic Defect Injection**: Introduces controlled errors (e.g., non-compliant thickness, missing IDs) into BIM data. *Why needed*: Generates targeted, scalable training instances for domain-specific problem-solving. *Quick check*: Do injected defects correspond to real BIM validation rules?
- **Reasoning-Augmentation (QRA)**: Training samples include "Question-Reasoning-Answer" chains from a high-performing LLM. *Why needed*: Teaches the model to generate coherent, logical analyses before final answers, improving accuracy. *Quick check*: Are reasoning chains logically sound and aligned with BIM best practices?
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: Applies trainable rank-decomposition matrices to model weights. *Why needed*: Efficient parameter-efficient fine-tuning; reduces compute and memory costs vs. full fine-tuning. *Quick check*: Does training loss decrease monotonically with the chosen learning rate?
- **G-Eval**: LLM-based evaluation (using GLM-4-plus) with a rubric scoring answer quality. *Why needed*: Captures nuanced BIM understanding (integrality, rationality, compliance, detailing) beyond lexical overlap metrics. *Quick check*: Does the evaluation prompt explicitly assess all four BIM-specific dimensions?
- **EQS Templates**: 22 question templates categorize tasks (e.g., extraction, statistics, reasoning). *Why needed*: Provides a systematic, reproducible framework for benchmarking BIM capabilities. *Quick check*: Do templates collectively cover the full range of design review and detailing skills?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: BIM Source Models -> Textualization Engine -> BIM-QA/QRA Dataset -> LoRA Fine-Tuning (Qwen2.5-14B-instruct) -> Qwen-BIM -> Evaluation Harness (Standardized Prompt + G-Eval Judge)

Critical path: Data generation pipeline (textualization + defect injection + QRA synthesis) -> Parameter-efficient model adaptation (LoRA on QRA) -> Held-out benchmark evaluation.

Design tradeoffs: Synthetic data generation improves scalability but may not capture all real-world defect complexities; reliance on a proprietary LLM judge (GLM-4-plus) prioritizes evaluation quality over reproducibility; LoRA balances efficiency and effectiveness but may not fully capture deep domain shifts.

Failure signatures: No improvement in G-Eval after fine-tuning suggests data quality or training configuration issues; low Format Score indicates poor instruction adherence, often due to prompt mismatches; high loss on specific EQS categories (e.g., "reasoning") signals insufficient training coverage for those task types.

First experiments:
1. Replicate the fine-tuning pipeline using an alternative LLM judge (e.g., GPT-4) for G-Eval to assess metric stability.
2. Perform a data quality audit by manually inspecting 100 randomly sampled QA and QRA pairs for correctness and reasoning validity.
3. Test generalization by fine-tuning on the provided building types (mall, office, etc.) and evaluating on a completely novel type (e.g., hospital) from a different source.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the proposed BIM evaluation benchmark be systematically extended to encompass the full project lifecycle, including construction sequencing, facility management, and decommissioning tasks, beyond the current focus on design review and detailing?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion explicitly states: "Future research should focus on expanding the benchmark to cover richer BIM-based design, **construction, and operation tasks**..." This identifies the current benchmark's scope limitation to design-stage tasks as a key unresolved gap.
- **Why unresolved:** The benchmark was constructed around design-centric capability requirements (information retrieval, visualization, design review, detailing). Tasks in construction (e.g., 4D simulation, logistics planning) and operations (e.g., maintenance scheduling, energy monitoring) involve different data types, workflows, and reasoning patterns that are not captured by the current EQS or dataset generation method.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A new set of task taxonomies and question templates derived from analysis of construction and FM workflows. Validation would require generating corresponding BIM-QA datasets for these stages (e.g., from IFC files with construction schedule data) and demonstrating that the expanded benchmark can distinguish model performance on these new task categories.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does the observed performance gain from incorporating reasoning processes (QRA data) into fine-tuning generalize when using different base model architectures (e.g., decoder-only vs. encoder-decoder) or when the model is adapted for multi-modal inputs (e.g., combining text with 2D/3D visual data from BIM)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows strong gains from 100% QRA fine-tuning (Group D) for Qwen-BIM (a decoder-only model). However, the methodology and results discussion focuses exclusively on text-based BIM representations and a single model family (Qwen). The paper notes that general LLMs "cannot directly interpret BIM models" and uses a textualization method, implicitly acknowledging a modality gap.
- **Why unresolved:** The effectiveness of reasoning-augmented fine-tuning may be tied to the specific model's pre-training and the purely textual data format. Different architectures may process and benefit from explicit reasoning chains differently. Furthermore, real BIM usage often involves viewing and querying 3D models; it is unknown if the gains persist or need modification when visual/spatial data is a primary input.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments comparing fine-tuning with QA vs. QRA data across at least two distinct model architectures (e.g., a GPT-style decoder-only and a T5-style encoder-decoder). A second experiment would integrate a vision encoder (e.g., for rendered BIM views) with the LLM and repeat the QA/QRA fine-tuning comparison to measure cross-modal transfer of the reasoning benefit.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the domain-specific fine-tuning strategy, which primarily teaches task-formatted output patterns and domain knowledge, sufficiently equip the model to handle *novel* BIM design scenarios or component types not present in the training blocks (e.g., MEP systems, specialized structures), or does it risk overfitting to the synthetic defect patterns in the generated dataset?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The dataset construction method injects synthetic defects (e.g., "randomly erase the construction number," "modify the wall thickness to be outside the range of 20-600mm") into partition data to create domain-task QA pairs. The authors do not discuss the diversity or realism of these defects relative to real-world BIM errors. The evaluation uses models tested on *different* partitions from the same generated dataset, not on truly unseen, real-world BIM models.
- **Why unresolved:** The performance boost (21.0% G-Eval increase) is measured on a test set derived from the same generation pipeline. This raises the possibility that the model learns to recognize and correct the *specific synthetic error patterns* rather than acquiring robust, generalizable BIM design reasoning skills. Generalization to diverse, real-world BIM models with complex, correlated errors is an unstated but critical assumption.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A hold-out validation using a separate set of real, high-quality BIM models (e.g., from a public repository like the NBS National BIM Library) that were *not* used in any form for dataset generation or fine-tuning. The model's performance on defect detection and repair suggestions for these real models would test genuine generalization, as the defect patterns would be authentic and not part of the training distribution.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the quantitative trade-off between the improved domain-specific performance (higher G-Eval on BIM tasks) and potential catastrophic forgetting or degradation of the base model's general-purpose reasoning and instruction-following capabilities after full domain fine-tuning?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper demonstrates success on the BIM-specific benchmark but does not evaluate the fine-tuned Qwen-BIM on any general NLP benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, HellaSwag, GSM8K). The methodology section states the goal is to "adapt LLMs for BIM-based design," implying a specialization trade-off. The G-Eval prompt itself is customized for BIM answers, potentially masking losses in general coherence or factuality.
- **Why unresolved:** Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) is used to mitigate forgetting, but its effectiveness is only shown for preserving BIM-task format compliance (improved from 0.86 to 0.90). There is no evidence that Qwen-BIM retains competency on tasks outside the BIM domain. For practical deployment, a model might need to switch between BIM and general conversation, making this trade-off crucial.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate the base model (Qwen2.5-14B-instruct) and Qwen-BIM on a standardized suite of general NLP benchmarks *before and after* fine-tuning. Metrics should include accuracy on multi-subject exams (MMU), mathematical reasoning (GSM8K), and commonsense reasoning (HellaSwag). A significant drop on these benchmarks would quantify the "forgetting" cost, while stable scores would indicate successful retention of general capabilities.

## Limitations
- The BIM-QA dataset, code, and exact BIM models are not publicly released, preventing exact recreation of textualized inputs and partitioning.
- The primary evaluation metric (G-Eval) relies on the proprietary GLM-4-plus model, making results difficult to reproduce with alternative judges without recalibration.
- The benchmark scope is limited to design-stage tasks (22 templates, ~4.7k samples); its coverage of full BIM complexity and generalizability to real-world projects is uncertain.
- The comparison to 671B-parameter models lacks experimental details (e.g., which models, prompts, few-shot settings), potentially being selective.
- The small dataset size, combined with LoRA, may risk overfitting to the specific synthetic defect patterns; no comprehensive hyperparameter search is reported.
