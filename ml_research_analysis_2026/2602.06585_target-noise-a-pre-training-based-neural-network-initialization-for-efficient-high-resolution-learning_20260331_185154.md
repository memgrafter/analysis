# Target noise: A pre-training based neural network initialization for efficient high resolution learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06585  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06585  
- **Reference count:** 17  
- **Primary result:** Noise-driven pre-training accelerates convergence in implicit neural representations and Deep Image Prior tasks by attenuating the network's inherent low-frequency bias, enabling earlier recovery of high-frequency details.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a simple yet effective neural network initialization technique: pre-training the network to fit random noise before task-specific training. Unlike standard random initializations (Xavier, Kaiming), this approach induces a structured parameter configuration without requiring additional data or architectural changes. The method shows consistent convergence acceleration across implicit neural representations (INRs) and Deep Image Prior (DIP) tasks, including image representation, super-resolution, denoising, and inpainting. Empirical results demonstrate faster loss decay and higher PSNR in early training stages; for instance, in image representation, the pretrained model surpasses standard initialization within 100 iterations, and in super-resolution, it achieves consistently higher PSNR at the same iteration count. Analysis via the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) reveals that noise pre-training slows the decay of NTK eigenvalues, weakening the spectral bias that normally prioritizes low-frequency learning and thus enabling earlier high-frequency detail recovery.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves a brief pre-training phase where the network is trained to map inputs (e.g., random coordinates for INRs or fixed random tensors for DIP CNNs) to random noise targets (Gaussian or uniform distribution). This phase uses a small, fixed budget of iterations (e.g., 200 for INRs, 500–1000 for DIP tasks) with standard mean squared error loss and an Adam optimizer. The resulting weights serve as the initialization for the actual downstream task training, which proceeds with the same optimizer and hyperparameters as standard baselines. No architectural modifications or external data are needed; the only change is the weight initialization derived from fitting noise. The method is evaluated on SIREN networks for INRs and a CNN-based Deep Image Prior framework for image restoration tasks.

## Key Results
- **Faster early convergence:** Pretrained models achieve lower loss and higher PSNR within the first 100 iterations compared to standard Xavier/Kaiming initialization across multiple tasks.
- **High-frequency recovery:** Noise pre-training mitigates the low-frequency bias, allowing earlier reconstruction of fine details (e.g., textures, edges) as shown in visualizations and frequency analysis.
- **NTK spectral changes:** The Neural Tangent Kernel eigenvalue spectrum after noise pre-training decays more slowly, indicating a more uniform distribution of learning capacity across frequency modes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Attenuating low-frequency bias via NTK shaping**  
Claim: Noise-driven pre-training reduces the network's intrinsic low-frequency bias, enabling earlier high-frequency learning.  
Mechanism: Fitting white noise (a flat spectrum signal) reshapes the NTK eigen-spectrum, causing eigenvalues to decay more slowly. This distributes gradient energy more evenly across frequency components, weakening the spectral bias that normally favors low frequencies.  
Core assumption: Standard initializations in architectures like SIREN produce an NTK with rapidly decaying eigenvalues, prioritizing low-frequency modes; altering this spectrum changes learning dynamics.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract statement on attenuating bias; Section 2.3 showing slower NTK eigenvalue decay and disordered eigenvectors after pre-training; correlation with early high-frequency recovery in experiments.  
Break condition: If the downstream task is purely low-frequency (e.g., very smooth images), this benefit may be negligible or add unnecessary overhead.

**Mechanism 2: Improving optimization landscape conditioning**  
Claim: Pre-training creates a structured weight configuration that avoids poor conditioning (e.g., vanishing gradients) and yields more robust gradient signals early in task training.  
Mechanism: Optimizing on noise forces the network into a region of the parameter space with better-conditioned loss curvature, leading to faster initial loss decay.  
Core assumption: Random initialization can land in areas with unfavorable local geometry (e.g., flat plateaus); self-supervised optimization relocates weights to a more favorable basin.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract mention of "structured and non-random parameter configuration" and "more stable early-stage optimization"; Figure 5 showing pretrained model surpasses baseline within 100 iterations.  
Break condition: In architectures or scales where standard initialization already yields well-conditioned gradients (e.g., very wide networks), the effect may be minimal.

**Mechanism 3: Orthogonalizing functional modes**  
Claim: Noise pre-training prevents the network from co-adapting to its inherent prior (e.g., sinusoidal biases in SIREN), yielding a more diverse set of initial features.  
Mechanism: Fitting white noise requires simultaneous activation of diverse features, disrupting early alignment with a specific basis (like low-frequency sinusoids) and allowing faster adaptation to task-specific frequencies.  
Core assumption: Standard initialization implicitly biases the network toward a limited set of basis functions; noise pre-training deconstructs this bias.  
Evidence anchors: Section 2.3 describing eigenvectors becoming disordered and noise-like; observations of stronger locality and higher frequency selectivity in the pretrained NTK.  
Break condition: For tasks strongly aligned with the network's native bias (e.g., smooth targets), this might initially slow learning of dominant modes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Base Network (SIREN for INRs or CNN for DIP) -> Pre-training Module (generates random noise targets, optimizes MSE) -> Task Training Module (continues training on actual task) -> Diagnostic Module (NTK computation for analysis).

**Critical path**  
1. Instantiate base network with standard random initialization (Xavier/Kaiming).  
2. Run pre-training loop: for a fixed number of iterations, optimize MSE between network output (on random inputs) and random noise target.  
3. Save weights; do not re-initialize. Use these as starting point for task training.  
4. Run task training with same optimizer/hyperparameters as baseline; monitor metrics.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Compute vs. Convergence: Pre-training adds overhead (200–1000 iterations) but saves iterations on the main task; net gain if overhead < saved iterations.  
- Generality vs. Tuning: Method is architecture-agnostic but requires setting pre-training hyperparameters (iterations, LR, noise distribution).  
- Stability vs. Overfitting (DIP): Faster convergence can cause earlier overfitting in DIP; necessitates stricter early stopping.

**Failure signatures**  
- No speedup: PSNR curves identical for pretrained and random init. Likely insufficient pre-training duration or wrong learning rate.  
- Worse final performance: Pretrained model plateaus at higher loss. May have entered a bad basin or mismatched noise.  
- Numerical instability: NaNs during pre-training. Check noise scale and learning rate (SIRENs are sensitive).  
- No high-frequency gain in INRs: After 50 iterations, output still blurry. Pre-training may have been inadequate.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Reproduce Figure 5 (INR): Implement SIREN (3 layers, 256 neurons), train on a 512×512 image. Plot PSNR vs. iteration for standard vs. noise-pretrained init; verify pretrained surpasses within 100 iterations.  
2. Ablate pre-training duration: For a DIP super-resolution task, vary noise-pretrain iterations (0, 100, 500, 1000). Measure iterations to reach target PSNR; identify knee point.  
3. Test noise distribution: Compare Gaussian (mean 0, std 1) vs. Uniform [0,1] vs. structured noise (e.g., stripes) for DIP inpainting. Hypothesis: white noise (Gaussian/Uniform) works best.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to large-scale supervised learning:** Does noise pre-training accelerate convergence in standard tasks like ImageNet classification with ResNets or transformers? The paper only evaluates INRs and DIP tasks.  
2. **Optimal noise structure:** Could structured noise (colored, natural image statistics) yield better NTK shaping than white noise? The current method uses simple Gaussian/uniform.  
3. **Finite-width vs. infinite-width mechanisms:** The NTK analysis assumes infinite networks; why does the benefit persist in finite-width practical networks? The exact mechanism (e.g., gradient norm distribution, Hessian curvature) is unclear.  
4. **Interaction with architectural biases:** Is noise pre-training beneficial for networks without strong low-frequency bias (e.g., standard CNNs for classification)? Might it harm tasks aligned with the native prior?

## Limitations
- **Architecture specifics under-specified:** Exact CNN architecture for DIP tasks and noise generation details (spatial dimensions, scaling) are not fully described, hindering exact reproduction.  
- **Generality untested:** Evaluations are limited to SIRENs and a specific DIP CNN; performance on other architectures (e.g., Transformers, modern CNNs) remains open.  
- **Mechanistic evidence weak:** NTK analysis is internal and not externally validated; the corpus shows no direct corroboration (average FMR 0.47, zero citations).  
- **Task-dependent scope:** Benefits may vanish for low-frequency-dominated tasks or wide networks in the NTK regime; the boundary conditions are unclear.

## Confidence
- **Convergence speedup:** High – Strong empirical consistency across multiple experiments with clear PSNR/loss improvements in early stages.  
- **Attenuated spectral bias:** Medium – NTK eigenvalue analysis is suggestive but lacks independent verification; correlation to high-frequency recovery is inferred.  
- **Generality across tasks/architectures:** Low – Only tested on narrow set (INR + DIP CNN); no evidence for NLP/vision backbones or large-scale supervised learning.
