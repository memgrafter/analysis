# On the Optimal Reasoning Length for RL-Trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09591
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09591
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Model-dependent optimal reasoning length: Qwen3-1.7B-Base improves monotonically with output length, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B peaks at intermediate length; under-thinking occurs at short lengths, dispersion at long lengths.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies how to optimize output length for reinforcement learning (RL)-trained reasoning models to balance performance and computational cost. The authors compare three length control methods—RLOO-LP, ALP, and DRPO—on two RL-trained models (Qwen3-1.7B-Base and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) using mathematical reasoning benchmarks. They find that the relationship between output length and performance differs across models: one model benefits from longer outputs (monotonic increase), while the other exhibits a non-monotonic relationship with an optimal intermediate length. By decomposing accuracy into mode accuracy, entropy, and mode share, they identify two failure modes: short outputs lead to "under-thinking" (low mode accuracy, high dispersion), and long outputs lead to "dispersion" (high entropy, low mode share). The results suggest that length control must be tuned to avoid both extremes, and that models with strong prior reasoning capabilities can benefit from appropriate length constraints. Additionally, methods like GRPO and DRPO show instability on prompts with high within-prompt output length variance.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes two pre-trained models using reinforcement learning with rewards derived from mathematical reasoning accuracy. Three strategies for controlling output length are evaluated: RLOO-LP adds an explicit penalty on length to the reward; ALP employs an adversarial predictor to estimate and penalize length; DRPO normalizes rewards relative to a reference length. Models are assessed on a suite of math benchmarks, and performance is measured across varying output lengths. To characterize failure modes, the authors decompose overall accuracy into three metrics: mode accuracy (correctness of the most frequent answer), entropy (dispersion of answers), and mode share (fraction of outputs agreeing with the mode). This decomposition allows distinguishing between incorrect reasoning (low mode accuracy) and answer variability (high entropy, low mode share).

## Key Results
- Differential length-performance curves: Qwen3-1.7B-Base performance increases monotonically with output length, whereas DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B peaks at an intermediate length and declines beyond it.
- Failure modes: Short outputs cause under-thinking (low mode accuracy, high dispersion); long outputs cause dispersion (increased entropy, reduced mode share).
- Method instability: GRPO and DRPO exhibit instability on prompts that induce high variance in output length across rollouts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose a detailed causal mechanism linking output length to performance. Instead, it empirically demonstrates that length influences accuracy through two distinct failure modes. Under-thinking is inferred to occur when the model terminates reasoning prematurely, yielding few steps that are often incorrect and highly variable (low mode accuracy, high entropy). Dispersion arises when excessive length introduces irrelevant or contradictory reasoning, increasing answer entropy and reducing consensus (lower mode share). The model-specific curves likely reflect differences in the models' inherent reasoning capabilities; the base model may benefit from additional computation, while the distilled model has an optimal reasoning depth beyond which noise predominates. However, the analysis remains correlational, and underlying mechanisms such as how step count affects reasoning quality are not established.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input -> Policy Model -> Output -> Reward Computation (Accuracy + Length Penalty) -> Advantage Estimation -> Policy Optimizer (e.g., GRPO/PPO) -> Updated Policy. For different length control methods: RLOO-LP adds a linear length penalty to reward; ALP integrates an adversarial length predictor that modifies reward; DRPO normalizes reward relative to a reference length before advantage calculation.

Critical path: The primary flow goes from input text through the current policy to generate a reasoning chain; the reward model evaluates correctness; a length penalty (or predictor) modifies the reward; advantages are computed (often relative to a baseline); and the policy is updated via gradient descent to maximize expected reward while respecting a KL constraint.

Design tradeoffs: RLOO-LP is simple and tunable via a penalty coefficient but requires careful hyperparameter selection; ALP adapts the length penalty during training but may introduce additional instability from the adversarial predictor; DRPO uses relative rewards to remove absolute length bias but can become unstable when within-prompt output length variance is high. All methods must balance the KL penalty to avoid excessive deviation from the reference model.

Failure signatures: Under-thinking is characterized by short outputs, low mode accuracy, and high entropy (dispersed incorrect answers). Dispersion is characterized by long outputs, high entropy, and low mode share (few outputs agree). Additionally, training instability (e.g., gradient explosion, reward collapse) appears on prompts that yield highly variable output lengths when using GRPO or DRPO.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited: experiments involve only two models and mathematical reasoning benchmarks; findings may not extend to other architectures or reasoning domains.
- The decomposition of accuracy into mode accuracy, entropy, and mode share is correlational; causal claims about under-thinking and dispersion are not proven.
- No theoretical explanation exists for why models exhibit different length-performance relationships, and the observed instability of GRPO/DRPO lacks systematic investigation.

## Confidence
- Differential length-performance curves: Medium confidence.
- Failure mode decomposition: Medium confidence.
- Length control tuning & method instability: Low confidence.
