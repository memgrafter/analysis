# A Single Revision Step Improves Token-Efficient LLM Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.02828  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02828  
- **Reference count:** 35  
- **Primary result:** PACER matches or exceeds 256-sample majority voting accuracy on competitive math benchmarks while using far fewer tokens.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often improve reasoning by sampling multiple chains-of-thought and aggregating answers via majority voting. However, this treats each trace in isolation, allowing confident errors to dominate. This paper introduces Packet-Conditioned Revision (PACER), a training‑free inference method that first builds a compact consensus packet from the most stable traces and then performs a single consensus‑conditioned self‑review on each trace. The revised answers are aggregated using confidence‑weighted voting. Evaluated on AIME 2024/2025, BRUMO 2025, and HMMT 2025 with models like GPT‑OSS‑120B and DeepSeek‑R1‑Distill‑Qwen, PACER achieves parity or superiority to 256‑sample majority voting at a fraction of the token cost. For example, on HMMT 2025 with GPT‑OSS‑120B, PACER scores 28/30 versus MV@256’s 25/30; on AIME 2025 with DeepSeek, PACER reaches 27/30 versus 24/30. Against the token‑efficient DeepConf‑Online baseline, PACER gains up to +10 absolute points. Accuracy‑vs‑token curves clearly show a Pareto‑frontier improvement, demonstrating that a single consensus‑conditioned revision step can achieve large‑ensemble robustness efficiently.

## Method Summary
PACER operates in two phases without any model training. **Phase I (Stability‑filtered sampling):** Starting with a warmup of \(N_{\text{init}}=64\) full traces generated by DeepConf‑Online, the method computes a token‑level uncertainty score \(U_t = -\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log P(v_j^{(t)})\) over the top‑\(k\) token probabilities. A sliding window of \(W\) steps yields a windowed average \(\bar{U}_t\); the prefix instability \(I_t = \max_{r\le t}\bar{U}_r\) and stability \(S(\tau)=-I_T\) are recorded. The screening threshold \(s\) is set as the \((100-\eta)\)‑th percentile (with \(\eta=10\)) of warmup stabilities. For the remaining \(N_{\text{try}}-N_{\text{init}}\) attempts, tokens are generated sequentially while updating \(S_t\); if \(S_t < s\) at any point the trace is terminated immediately, otherwise it is completed and added to the stable pool \(\mathcal{T}\) if \(S(\tau)\ge s\).

**Phase II (Consensus‑conditioned revision and aggregation):** From \(\mathcal{T}\), the method computes confidence‑weighted support \(V(a)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T},\,\text{answer}=a} S(\tau)\) and selects the top \(N\) distinct answers \(\mathcal{A}_{\text{top}}\). For each \(a\in\mathcal{A}_{\text{top}}\), the most stable trace \(\tau_a\) is chosen as a representative; its rationale is truncated to \(L_{\text{sum}}\) tokens to produce a concise explanation \(R_a\). The consensus packet \(\mathcal{P}=\{(a, V(a), R_a)\}\) is constructed. Then every trace \(\tau\in\mathcal{T}\) performs a self‑review via a fixed prompt: the problem, the original solution and answer, and \(\mathcal{P}\) are provided; the model is asked to revise its answer if peer evidence contradicts it, staying within a review budget \(L_{\text{rev}}\). The revised answer \(a'\) is extracted from \(\boxed{}\). Finally, a confidence‑weighted vote over all revised answers yields the prediction \(\arg\max_a \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}} S(\tau)\cdot\mathbb{I}[a'=a]\). Token accounting includes warmup, online attempts, and review generations; packet construction incurs zero overhead when rationales are truncated.

## Key Results
- PACER matches or exceeds 256‑sample majority voting accuracy while using far fewer tokens. On HMMT 2025 with GPT‑OSS‑120B, PACER scores **28/30** vs MV@256’s **25/30**; on AIME 2025 with DeepSeek, PACER reaches **27/30** vs **24/30**.
- Against the token‑efficient DeepConf‑Online baseline, PACER gains up to **+10 absolute points**.
- Accuracy‑vs‑token curves demonstrate a clear Pareto‑frontier improvement, proving that a single consensus‑conditioned revision step can achieve large‑ensemble robustness at a fraction of the cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PACER leverages three key insights: (1) Stability scores derived from token‑level uncertainty reliably identify higher‑quality reasoning traces, allowing early discarding of low‑quality attempts and weighting votes by confidence. (2) A compact consensus packet summarizing the most supported answers and their best rationales provides peer evidence in a form that the model can efficiently process. (3) Conditioning a brief self‑review on this packet enables each trace to correct itself by comparing its conclusion to the peer consensus, effectively simulating an ensemble of reconsiderations with only one additional forward pass per trace. This meta‑cognitive step repairs many isolated errors that would persist under majority voting, while the stability weighting prevents confident outliers from dominating. The result is a method that attains the robustness of large ensembles at a fraction of the token budget.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) sampling**  
  *Why needed:* To generate multiple reasoning paths for complex problems.  
  *Quick check:* Each generated trace should contain a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}.

- **Majority voting (MV)**  
  *Why needed:* The simplest aggregation baseline; selects the most frequent answer.  
  *Quick check:* Count occurrences of each answer; choose the one with highest count.

- **Confidence‑weighted voting (CWV)**  
  *Why needed:* To discount low‑quality traces by weighting each vote with a reliability score (stability).  
  *Quick check:* Compute \(\sum_{\tau} S(\tau) \cdot \mathbb{I}[a_\tau = a]\) for each candidate \(a\) and pick the argmax.

- **Token‑level uncertainty**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a fine‑grained measure of model confidence during generation; foundation for stability.  
  *Quick check:* For each token, compute negative log‑probability over top‑\(k\) choices and verify values are sensible.

- **Stability‑based early stopping (DeepConf‑Online)**  
  *Why needed:* To terminate low‑quality traces early, saving tokens while maintaining a pool of high‑quality candidates.  
  *Quick check:* During generation, track prefix instability \(I_t\); ensure termination occurs when it exceeds the threshold \(s\).

- **Consensus‑conditioned revision**  
  *Why needed:* Allows traces to self‑correct by comparing their reasoning to a summarized peer consensus, improving accuracy with minimal extra tokens.  
  *Quick check:* After review, verify that a significant fraction of traces change their answer, and that those changes often move toward the majority of high‑stability answers.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Problem → Trace Generation (DeepConf‑Online with stability) → Threshold Determination (from warmup) → Online Sampling with Early Stopping → Stable Pool \(\mathcal{T}\) → Packet Construction (top‑\(N\) selection, confidence weighting, rationale truncation) → Review Module (consensus‑conditioned prompt) → Revised Answers → Confidence‑Weighted Aggregation → Final Answer.

**Critical path:**  
1. Warmup generation of \(N_{\text{init}}\) traces.  
2. Compute stability scores and set screening threshold \(s\).  
3. Generate remaining \(N_{\text{try}}-N_{\text{init}}\) attempts with real‑time stability checks; only completed traces with \(S(\tau)\ge s\) enter \(\mathcal{T}\).  
4. Build consensus packet \(\mathcal{P}\) from \(\mathcal{T}\).  
5. For every \(\tau\in\mathcal{T}\), invoke review with \(\mathcal{P}\) to obtain revised answer \(a'\).  
6. Aggregate revised answers using stability weights to produce final prediction.  
The total token cost is the sum of tokens produced in steps 3 and 5; packet construction is negligible.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Accuracy vs. token budget:** Increasing \(N_{\text{try}}\) or review budget \(L_{\text{rev}}\) improves accuracy but linearly raises token usage.  
- **Packet size \(N\):** Larger \(N\) increases chance the correct answer is included but also enlarges the review prompt, potentially exceeding context windows or diluting the signal.  
- **Stability threshold \(s\):** Higher \(s\) yields a pool of more stable (likely correct) traces but risks discarding potentially correct ones; too low reduces filtering benefit.  
- **Rationale truncation \(L_{\text{sum}}\):** Shorter rationales save tokens but may omit critical logical steps, weakening the packet’s usefulness.  
- **Uncertainty parameters (\(k\), \(W\)):** Smaller \(k\) or \(W\) may produce noisier stability estimates, affecting early stopping quality.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Survival rate (fraction of online attempts that complete) far from the expected range (~90% of warmup discarded plus variable online survivors) suggests mis‑computed stability.  
- Very low answer flip rate during review indicates the review prompt is ineffective or traces are already highly stable.  
- High damage rate (correct traces flipping to wrong) signals that the revision step is harming performance.  
- Frequent absence of ground‑truth answer from the top‑\(N\) packet limits revision recovery; check recall@\(N\).  
- \boxed{} extraction failures > 5% indicate parsing issues.

**Exactly 3 first experiments:**  
1. **Validate DeepConf‑Online implementation:** Generate warmup traces, compute stability distributions, set threshold \(s\), and verify that the survival rate aligns with expectations (∼\(100-\eta\)% of warmup plus online survivors).  
2. **Test packet construction:** Vary \(N\) and \(L_{\text{sum}}\); manually inspect a sample of packets to ensure rationales remain coherent and capture essential reasoning.  
3. **Run small‑scale PACER vs. baseline:** On a subset of problems (e.g., 20), compare accuracy and token usage for different \(L_{\text{rev}}\) (100, 200) with fixed \(N_{\text{try}}=64\); compute flip rates and measure any accuracy gains over DeepConf‑Online (no revision).

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (\(N\), \(L_{\text{sum}}\), \(L_{\text{rev}}\), \(k\), \(W\)) are not reported; performance may be sensitive to these choices.  
- Exact wording and formatting of the REVIEW prompt are only conceptually described; subtle variations could weaken or break the revision step.  
- The handling of correct answers that fall outside the top‑\(N\) consensus packet is unaddressed; this could prevent recovery of valid solutions.  
- Exact replication of the reported scores is unlikely without the authors’ precise configurations.

## Confidence
- **Core claim:** A single consensus‑conditioned revision improves token‑efficiency → **High**.  
- **Specific performance numbers** (e.g., +10 pts vs DeepConf‑Online) → **Medium** (depends on unreported hyperparameters).  
- **Exact replication** of reported scores without authors’ configurations → **Low**.

## Next Checks
1. Perform a systematic sweep over \(N\), \(L_{\text{sum}}\), and \(L_{\text{rev}}\) to assess robustness and identify near‑optimal values for each benchmark.  
2. Ablate the REVIEW prompt: compare variations in instruction clarity, inclusion of packet details, and token budget to measure impact on revision correctness and stability.  
3. Quantify the frequency with which the ground‑truth answer is absent from the top‑\(N\) packet (recall@\(N\)), and evaluate whether increasing \(N\) restores performance without exceeding token budgets.
