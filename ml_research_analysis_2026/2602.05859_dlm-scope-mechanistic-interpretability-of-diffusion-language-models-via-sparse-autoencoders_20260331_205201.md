# DLM-Scope: Mechanistic Interpretability of Diffusion Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.05859
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05859
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces the first SAE framework for diffusion language models (DLMs), enabling interpretable feature extraction and steering; early-layer SAE insertion can reduce cross-entropy loss, and diffusion-time steering outperforms LLM steering.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DLM-Scope, the first sparse autoencoder (SAE) framework tailored for diffusion language models (DLMs). Unlike autoregressive LLMs, DLMs involve iterative denoising with masked and unmasked tokens. The authors adapt SAE training to use activations from either masked or unmasked positions and design steering interventions that apply across multiple denoising steps. Evaluated on Dream-7B and LLaDA-8B, DLM-SAEs achieve strong sparsity-fidelity trade-offs. A key finding is that inserting SAEs into early layers can reduce masked-token cross-entropy loss—a contrast to LLMs where insertion typically harms performance. SAE features also enable effective diffusion-time steering, yielding higher concept improvement and steering scores than LLM steering. Furthermore, feature dynamics (stability/drift) across denoising steps provide an interpretable signal that correlates with decoding-order performance. Base-trained SAEs transfer well to instruction-tuned DLMs, showing stability in shallow/middle layers but divergence in deep layers.

## Method Summary
The method involves training layer-wise Top-K SAEs on activations from a DLM, with two variants for collecting training data: **MASK-SAE** uses activations from masked (to-be-predicted) positions during forward passes on noisy sequences, while **UNMASK-SAE** uses unmasked context positions. SAEs are trained with a reconstruction MSE loss and an L1 sparsity penalty on the latent activations (h = TopK(ReLU(W_E x))). For evaluation, the framework measures sparsity-fidelity via explained variance (EV) and delta LM loss (ΔL), which is the change in masked-token cross-entropy loss after inserting the SAE into the residual stream. For steering, a selected SAE feature decoder direction `v_f` is injected at a specific layer during every denoising step: `X_steer = X + α · m_f · s_k · v_f^T`, where `s_k` is a token-scope mask (ALL-TOKENS or UPDATE-TOKENS). Steering success is measured by concept improvement (C) and relative perplexity change (P), combined into a score S. To analyze decoding orders (e.g., ORIGIN, TOPK-MARGIN, ENTROPY), the framework tracks the Jaccard similarity of top-K features across denoising steps, computing pre-mask stability and post-decode drift.

## Key Results
*   Early-layer SAE insertion in Dream-7B and LLaDA-8B can achieve negative ΔL (loss reduction), contrasting with LLM baselines where insertion always increases loss.
*   DLM-SAEs enable diffusion-time steering that substantially outperforms LLM steering, with steering scores 2–10× higher in deep layers, especially using the UPDATE-TOKENS scope.
*   Feature dynamics differ by decoding order: confidence-based orders (TOPK-MARGIN, ENTROPY) show greater pre-mask stability on masked tokens and stronger post-decode drift in deep layers compared to random order (ORIGIN), correlating with higher accuracy on GSM8K.
*   Base-trained SAEs transfer to instruction-tuned DLMs with similar ΔL and EV in shallow/middle layers but diverge in the deepest layer, indicating that instruction tuning primarily alters the most abstract representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Inserting SAEs into early layers of a DLM can reduce masked-token cross-entropy loss, contrasting with LLMs where insertion typically increases loss.  
**Mechanism:** The DLM's denoising objective (Eq. 3) involves predicting masked tokens given a mixture of corrupted and clean tokens (Eq. 5). SAEs trained on activations from masked (`MASK-SAE`) or unmasked (`UNMASK-SAE`) positions learn sparse dictionaries that may capture degradation-relevant patterns. At early layers, the SAE's reconstruction might filter noise or align better with the denoising objective than the original dense activations, leading to a functional improvement.  
**Core assumption:** The loss reduction occurs because the sparsity constraint acts as a regularizer that removes representation components harmful to the denoising prediction, specific to the diffusion noise corruption structure.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "...early-layer insertion reduces cross-entropy loss (negative delta LM loss), contrasting with LLMs where insertion typically increases loss."  
- Section 3.2: "Notably, Dream shows a shallow-layer regime at small L0 where ∆LM loss is negative, meaning that SAE insertion can reduce cross-entropy loss. This effect is absent or much weaker in LLMs."  
**Break condition:** If the SAE is too dense (high L0) or inserted in deep layers, the loss typically increases, indicating the benefit is confined to specific, sparse early-layer representations.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** SAE features enable more effective diffusion-time steering interventions compared to LLM steering because the intervention can be applied repeatedly across multiple denoising steps.  
**Mechanism:** During DLM generation (Eq. 4), the same residual stream at a layer is exposed to noisy inputs repeatedly. Injecting a feature decoder direction `v_f` at every step (Eq. 7) accumulates influence, allowing the concept to steer the denoising trajectory more robustly. The `UPDATE-TOKENS` variant (Eq. 8) targets only masked positions, directing the intervention where predictions are actively formed.  
**Core assumption:** The iterative denoising process is more receptive to repeated, consistent directional pushes than a single forward pass in LLMs, leading to higher steering scores (S = C + λP).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "SAE features enable effective diffusion-time interventions, often outperforming LLM steering."  
- Section 4.2: "sparse features enable diffusion-time interventions that are effective and often outperform LLM steering. Relative to LLM-SAEs, DLM-SAEs achieve substantially higher overall steering scores, typically by 2–10× in deep layers."  
**Break condition:** Excessive steering strength (`α`) or applying to all positions can degrade fluency (negative P). Also, feature stability across steps must be sufficient; highly drifting features (Sec 5) may lead to inconsistent steering.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** SAE feature dynamics (pre-mask stability, post-decode drift) systematically differ across decoding-order strategies, providing an interpretable signal that correlates with task performance.  
**Mechanism:** Different remasking orders (ORIGIN, TOPK-MARGIN, ENTROPY) change the temporal pattern of token fixing. By tracking Top-K feature Jaccard similarity over steps (Eqs. 14, 15), we observe that confidence-based orders (higher accuracy) show larger early shifts on masked tokens and continued drift in deep layers after decoding. This indicates more active representation reorganization under effective orders.  
**Core assumption:** The extent of feature set change during denoising reflects the model's internal adjustment to the evolving context; strategies that induce more dynamic mid-to-deep-layer shifts may support better reasoning.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "SAEs can provide useful signals for DLM decoding order; [...] reveal that confidence-based orders exhibit more feature shifts."  
- Section 5: "under ORIGIN, the SAE features change only slightly... confidence-based orders show a more organized progression... deep layer features continue to adjust after tokens are fixed."  
**Break condition:** The correlation is observational from GSM8K experiments; it may not hold universally across tasks or architectures. A break would be if a low-performing order exhibited similar or more dynamics.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**  
    **Why needed:** The entire framework (training, steering, analysis) relies on SAEs to extract interpretable, sparse latents from dense DLM activations.  
    **Quick check:** Given an activation `x`, what are the roles of the encoder weights `W_E` and decoder dictionary `W_D` in the SAE objective `||x - W_D h||^2 + λ||h||_1` with `h = TopK(W_E x)`?

*   **Concept: Diffusion Language Model (DLM) denoising process**  
    **Why needed:** Understanding how tokens are corrupted (`x_t ~ q(x_t | x_0)`) and the remasking inference loop (Eq. 4) is essential for designing SAE training position strategies and diffusion-time steering.  
    **Quick check:** In Eq. (3), why is the cross-entropy applied only to masked tokens? How does the weight `w(t)=1/t` affect training?

*   **Concept: Feature steering via residual stream intervention**  
    **Why needed:** The primary application is causal intervention; you must understand how adding `α * m_f * v_f` (Eq. 7) to the residual at a layer and step changes generation.  
    **Quick check:** What is the difference between `ALL-TOKENS` and `UPDATE-TOKENS` steering in terms of the `s_k` vector, and why might one be preferred for DLMs?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
SAE Trainer (collects activations from DLM forward passes) -> SAE Module (encoder/decoder inserted at a residual stream layer) -> Steering Engine (applies intervention at each diffusion step) -> Evaluation Suite (computes ΔL, EV, steering metrics) -> Feature Analyzer (auto-interpretation and decoding-order tracker).

**Critical path:**  
1. Prepare DLM (Dream/LLaDA) and data (Common Pile).  
2. Run forward passes on masked sequences to collect activations for chosen layers.  
3. Train SAE per layer with Top-K objective and chosen position strategy (MASK/UNMASK).  
4. Validate sparsity-fidelity trade-off (EV and ΔL) on held-out masks.  
5. Interpret features via auto-interpretation and select steering candidates.  
6. Run diffusion-time steering with `token_scope` and `amp_factor`, evaluate concept/perplexity scores.  
7. For decoding-order analysis, run inference with different orders, track feature sets per step, compute stability/drift.  
8. For transfer test, splice base-trained SAE into SFT model and re-evaluate insertion metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
*   **Sparsity (L0) vs. Fidelity:** Lower L0 yields more interpretable features but risks higher ΔL and lower EV; optimal L0 is layer-dependent.  
*   **Training Position Strategy:** MASK-SAE focuses on noisy masked positions; UNMASK-SAE on clean context. Their utility for downstream tasks may differ.  
*   **Steering Scope:** ALL-TOKENS applies broadly but can dilute effect; UPDATE-TOKENS focuses on prediction sites but may miss context effects.  
*   **Steering Strength (α):** Too low has no effect; too high harms fluency and may cause degenerate repetitions.

**Failure signatures:**  
*   **Training:** SAE dead features, poor EV at any L0, or ΔL positive and increasing with L0.  
*   **Steering:** Negative concept improvement (C < 0) or large perplexity increase (P << 0); extremely repetitive or incoherent generations.  
*   **Decoding analysis:** No discernible pattern in pre-mask similarity or post-decode drift across orders; all orders yield similar dynamics.  
*   **Transfer:** Base-trained SAE shows large ΔL divergence from SFT-trained SAE in middle/deep layers on SFT model.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Train and validate DLM-SAEs: For L10 on Dream-7B, train both MASK-SAE and UNMASK-SAE at L0=80 and 160. Compute ΔL and EV on held-out. Confirm presence/absence of negative ΔL as in Figure 3.  
2. Baseline steering comparison: Pick a high-scoring feature (score >0.8). Run steering with amp_factor=3.0, dlm_steps=30, token_scope='all' (Dream) or 'topk' (LLaDA). Compute C, P, S and compare to LLM baseline from Table 2 replication.  
3. Decoding-order feature dynamics: On GSM8K with Dream-7B, run 3 orders (ORIGIN, TOPK-MARGIN, ENTROPY) with K=128 steps. Track Top-K Jaccard for L10 on a fixed prompt. Verify ORIGIN shows higher pre-mask stability and lower post-decode drift than confidence-based orders.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Why does SAE insertion reduce cross-entropy loss in early layers of diffusion LMs?** The paper reports this novel effect but provides no mechanistic explanation. Evidence: Section 3.2 and Figure 3 show negative ΔLM loss for shallow layers, absent in LLMs. Resolving would require controlled experiments varying sparsity, masked/unmasked position usage, and analyzing denoising confidence distributions.
*   **Why do base-trained SAEs transfer almost perfectly to instruction-tuned DLMs in shallow/middle layers but diverge in the deepest layer?** Evidence: Section 6 and Figure 6 show near-identical ΔL and EV for layers L1–L23 between Base-SAEs and SFT-SAEs on Dream-7B Instruct, but a clear gap at L27. Unresolved: Are deepest representations more abstract, or do tuning-specific features emerge? Requires layer-wise feature distribution and weight similarity analysis.
*   **Are SAE-based dynamics causally linked to decoding order performance?** Evidence: Section 5 observes that confidence-based orders (higher accuracy) exhibit larger pre-mask shifts and deeper post-decode drift. This is a correlation; causal role is unknown. Resolving would need interventions that artificially modulate stability/drift and measure accuracy changes, or designing orders that explicitly optimize these dynamics.

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter Ambiguity:** Exact SAE training settings (optimizer LR/betas, L1 schedule, Top-K implementation) and ΔL evaluation protocol (held-out size, mask schedule) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
*   **Steering Metric Ground Truth:** Concept improvement (C) and perplexity (P) rely on an unstated judge model and averaging procedure, making external benchmarking difficult.
*   **Generality of Dynamics Findings:** The correlation between feature dynamics and decoding-order performance is demonstrated only on GSM8K; its validity for other tasks or architectures is unproven.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Early-layer ΔL reduction):** High. Directly reported in experiments with clear break conditions (layer/L0 dependence) and contrast with LLM baselines.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Steering via repeated injection):** Medium. Logically sound and empirically supported by steering score comparisons, but effect magnitude is sensitive to α and token scope.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Dynamics as decoding-order signal):** Medium. Correlation well-documented for GSM8K confidence-based orders, but causal role and cross-task validity remain unproven.
