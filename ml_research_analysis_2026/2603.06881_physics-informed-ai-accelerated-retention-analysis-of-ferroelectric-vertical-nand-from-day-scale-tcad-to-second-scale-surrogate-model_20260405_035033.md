# Physics-informed AI Accelerated Retention Analysis of Ferroelectric Vertical NAND: From Day-Scale TCAD to Second-Scale Surrogate Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06881
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06881
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: A Physics-Informed Neural Operator (PINO) surrogate achieves >10,000x speedup over TCAD for Fe-VNAND retention analysis while maintaining high accuracy (R² > 0.998 for Vth prediction) by embedding physical laws into a Fourier Neural Operator.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a breakthrough in simulating data retention for ferroelectric vertical NAND (Fe-VNAND) by replacing prohibitively slow TCAD simulations with a fast AI surrogate. The core innovation is a Physics-Informed Neural Operator (PINO) built on a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) backbone, which is trained on a sparse set of 120 TCAD simulations. The model embeds fundamental physical constraints—Poisson's equation and monotonic decay of charge/polarization—directly into its loss function, forcing predictions to obey known physics. This approach yields a model that predicts 2D internal physics fields and final ID-VG characteristics in ~10 seconds instead of ~60 hours, with a >40% improvement in RMSE over a purely data-driven baseline. The work enables continuous, high-fidelity design space exploration for Fe-VNAND optimization.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a surrogate model using the NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo framework to learn a solution operator mapping device parameters (HZO thickness `t_HZO`, temperature `T`, retention time `τ`) to 2D physics fields (potential, polarization, trapped charge, current density) and ID-VG curves. The architecture has three phases: 1) scalar input embedding, 2) a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) that generates the 2D fields, and 3) an IV-Net that reads out the ID-VG curve. Critically, the FNO is trained with a dual loss: a standard data fidelity term (L2 vs. TCAD) and a physics-informed term that penalizes residuals from Poisson's equation and enforces monotonic decay of trapped charge and polarization over time. An additional scalar context injection pathway in the IV-Net provides global temperature and time information to the readout, as these thermodynamic effects are not fully captured by the quasi-static 2D electrostatic maps alone.

## Key Results
- **Speedup:** Inference time reduced from ~60 hours (TCAD) to ~10 seconds (surrogate), a >10,000x acceleration.
- **Accuracy:** Achieves R² > 0.998 for threshold voltage (Vth) prediction and ~0.1% relative error on 2D internal physics maps (potential, polarization, trapped charge).
- **Physics Benefit:** Enforcing physical constraints yields a >40% improvement in RMSE compared to a purely data-driven model, demonstrating the value of inductive biases with sparse training data (120 samples).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) backbone learns a continuous solution operator for the device's PDEs, enabling mesh-free, resolution-invariant inference from scalar parameters to 2D physics fields.
- **Mechanism:** The FNO transforms spatial inputs into the Fourier domain, performs global spectral convolutions via learned weight matrices, and transforms back to physical space. This approximates the solution mapping of elliptic PDEs (like Poisson's equation) directly, bypassing iterative numerical solvers.
- **Core assumption:** The underlying PDE solutions are sufficiently smooth and their integral kernels are well-approximated by a finite number of Fourier modes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "embeds fundamental physical laws... into a Fourier Neural Operator architecture to predict 2D internal physics maps."
  - Section III.A: "FNOs perform global convolutions within the Fourier domain... to learn the continuous solution operator of the governing PDEs."
- **Break condition:** If physical fields exhibit high-frequency discontinuities or sharp transitions not captured by the low-mode cutoff (modes=12), the FNO will produce smoothed, inaccurate reconstructions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Enforcing a physics-informed dual-loss function constrains the model's hypothesis space, forcing predictions to obey governing equations and known physical trends, which compensates for sparse training data.
- **Mechanism:** During training, total loss ℒ = ℒ_data + ℒ_physics penalizes deviations from TCAD ground truth (ℒ_data) and violations of Poisson's equation and temporal monotonicity (ℒ_physics). Gradient updates from ℒ_physics shape the FNO's weights to produce fields that are solutions to the embedded PDE constraints.
- **Core assumption:** The embedded physical laws (Poisson's equation, monotonic charge/polarization decay) are complete and accurate descriptions of the dominant retention physics for the given device structure and time/temperature regime.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "embedding fundamental physical laws... achieves... high accuracy... >40% improvement in RMSE compared to a purely data-driven model."
  - Section III.B: Explicit equations for Poisson residual and monotonicity constraints.
- **Break condition:** If the TCAD training data contains unphysical artifacts or the embedded physics laws are incomplete (e.g., missing a key secondary mechanism), the physics loss will enforce consistency with an incorrect model, leading to systematic yet physically plausible errors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The multi-phase architecture with scalar context injection in the IV-Net readout separates the learning of spatial physics from global thermodynamic effects, as 2D electrostatic maps alone cannot encode temperature-dependent kinetics.
- **Mechanism:** Phase 2's FNO generates spatial fields (φ, P, Q) governed by quasi-static electrostatics. Phase 3's IV-Net receives these maps *and* the original scalar inputs (t_HZO, T, log(τ)). A parallel MLP path processes the scalars directly, and its output is fused with the CNN-encoded map features. This residual injection provides the global T and τ context needed to correctly scale kinetics (detrapping rates, depolarization timescales) that are not spatially resolved.
- **Core assumption:** The spatial fields at a given (t_HZO, T, τ) are functionally dependent on T and τ in a way that cannot be fully inferred from the fields' spatial patterns alone; a separate, non-spatial contextual pathway is necessary.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section III.A: "the global thermodynamic context... cannot be extracted from purely electrostatic 2D maps. By injecting these scalars, the model preserves global context."
  - Section III.A: Shows the parallel MLP and weighted summation fusion architecture.
- **Break condition:** If temperature dependence is actually encoded in subtle spatial variations of the fields (e.g., temperature-dependent trap distributions altering local charge profiles), then scalar injection may not provide sufficient correction, and the model will fail to generalize to new T regimes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Solution Operators for PDEs**
  - *Why needed here:* The task is learning a mapping from input parameters to the solution of coupled, time-dependent PDEs. A neural operator approximates this *mapping between function spaces*—not just a function—which is why the FNO is chosen over a CNN.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a CNN (which processes discrete pixel grids) less suitable than an FNO for predicting solutions at *unseen* spatial resolutions or for continuous parameter inputs?

- **Concept: Physics-Informed Regularization**
  - *Why needed here:* With only 120 TCAD simulations, a pure data-driven model would overfit. The physics loss acts as a powerful inductive bias, projecting the solution onto the subspace of physically plausible functions, enabling generalization to unseen parameter combinations.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the risk of using a physics loss if the embedded equation (e.g., Poisson's) is only an approximation of the true TCAD solver's physics?

- **Concept: Separating Spatial State from Global Context**
  - *Why needed here:* The 2D maps represent a quasi-steady-state electrostatic snapshot. The evolution of these states over time τ and their temperature sensitivity are governed by kinetic equations not spatially local in the same way. The architecture explicitly factors this separation.
  - *Quick check question:* If you removed the scalar context injection path, what specific physical effect (T or τ dependence) would the model likely fail to capture correctly in the ID-VG curves?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input scalars (t_HZO, T, log(τ)) → Phase 1 (Embedding) → Phase 2 (FNO: 4 layers, modes=12, channels=32) → 5 co-located 2D maps (J, Q_e, Q_h, V, P) on 256x256 grid → Phase 3 (IV-Net: CNN encoder + parallel MLP on scalars → feature fusion → shallow MLP) → Predicted log(I_D)-V_G curve.
- **Critical path:** Input scalars → Phase 1 embedding → Phase 2 FNO (with ℒ_physics) → 2D maps → Phase 3 CNN encoder + scalar MLP → Fusion → ID-VG prediction. Physics loss gradient flows from PDE residual calculation back through the FNO during training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *FNO Mode Cutoff (modes=12):* Lower modes prevent overfitting on sparse data but limit maximum resolvable spatial frequency. Tradeoff: smoothness vs. fidelity of sharp interfaces.
  - *Network Depth/Width:* Very shallow networks (total params < 10^4) are mandatory to avoid memorizing 120 training samples. Tradeoff: model capacity vs. generalization.
  - *Scalar Injection:* Adds parameters but is necessary for T/τ generalization. Omitting it causes failure on unseen T (as shown by data-driven model's issues).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Training:* If ℒ_physics dominates ℒ_data, model may converge to a "physically perfect but factually wrong" solution (e.g., smooth fields that satisfy Poisson but don't match TCAD's specific trap distributions). Monitor ℒ_data/ℒ_physics ratio.
  - *Inference:* (a) Unphysical oscillations/ripples in predicted ID-VG curves → likely physics loss weight too low or model purely data-driven. (b) Systematic Vth offset that scales with T but not τ → scalar injection ineffective or FNO maps not capturing correct charge/polarization state.
  - *Generalization:* Failure on new t_HZO values beyond {5-9} nm suggests the FNO hasn't learned the correct geometric parameter sensitivity.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation - Physics Loss:** Train identical FNO+IV-Net with ℒ_physics=0. Compare ID-VG smoothness and RMSE on a held-out set (e.g., t_HZO=7nm, T=350K). Expect visible ripples and >40% higher RMSE.
  2. **Spatial Map Validation:** For a fixed (t_HZO, T, τ), compute the relative L2 error of the 5 predicted 2D maps against TCAD. Target ~0.1% error. Inspect error maps: should be uniform, not concentrated at material interfaces.
  3. **Scalar Injection Ablation:** Disable the scalar MLP path in Phase 3 (use only CNN-encoded maps). Predict ID-VG for an *unseen* temperature (e.g., 358K). Expect significant error in Vth shift magnitude compared to PINO with injection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can the PINO framework be fully validated for the program (PGM) state retention dynamics, given that only preliminary symmetric behavior assessments were provided?
  - *Basis in paper:* "While this work primarily details the ERS state for a focused physical analysis, preliminary evaluations indicate that the program (PGM) state exhibits symmetric behavior, making the proposed modeling approach equally applicable to both states."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper only presents rigorous results for the erase (ERS) state. The "preliminary evaluations" for the PGM state lack detailed validation metrics and comparative analysis, leaving the model's accuracy for PGM retention untested.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A full set of TCAD validation results for the PGM state, including 2D physics map reconstructions, ID-VG curve predictions, and Vth shift accuracy across the same parameter sweep, directly comparable to the ERS state results.

- **Open Question 2:** How can the PINO framework be extended to model full 3D Fe-VNAND array retention, considering vertical cell-to-cell coupling and thermal crosstalk not present in the single FeFET configuration?
  - *Basis in paper:* "This work establishes a foundation for full-scale 3D Fe-VNAND retention loss mechanism exploration."
  - *Why unresolved:* The current model is trained and validated on a single, isolated FeFET device. Scaling to a 3D array introduces additional phenomena (e.g., neighboring cell interference, stacked thermal gradients) not captured by the 2D gate-stack-focused surrogate or the scalar input parameters used.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Development and validation of a 3D-capable PINO surrogate. This requires generating 3D TCAD training data for multi-cell stacks, defining new input parameters (e.g., cell pitch, stack height), and demonstrating accurate prediction of retention degradation with measurable cross-cell interference.

- **Open Question 3:** Do the currently enforced physics constraints (Poisson's equation, monotonic decay) sufficiently capture all dominant retention loss mechanisms, or are additional physical laws needed for broader generalization?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The PINO embeds only two physics-informed loss terms: Poisson residual and monotonicity constraints. The paper notes retention is driven by "complex interaction between charge detrapping and ferroelectric depolarization," but the monotonicity constraint is a simplified temporal regularity condition, not a full physical law.
  - *Why unresolved:* The model's high accuracy on the sparse training set suggests these constraints are effective, but they may not universally govern all operational regimes (e.g., under high-field stress, with non-uniform trap distributions). The assumption of strictly monotonic detrapping/depolarization could break down with multi-exponential traps or back-switching events.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation studies adding more detailed physical loss terms (e.g., trap energy spectrum evolution) and testing on TCAD datasets with more complex physical models. Comparison of prediction errors, especially in regimes where monotonicity might be violated, would clarify sufficiency.

- **Open Question 4:** How robust is the PINO surrogate to extrapolation beyond the narrow training data ranges (e.g., HZO thickness <5nm or >9nm, temperatures <300K or >473K, retention times >10⁴ s)?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Training data sweeps only 3 parameters over very limited discrete values: tHZO {5-9 nm}, T {300-473K}, τ {0-10⁴ s}. The authors demonstrate interpolation but acknowledge the dataset is "highly constrained" and "sparse."
  - *Why unresolved:* Neural operators can interpolate well within training distributions but may fail catastrophically when extrapolating. The paper does not test extrapolation boundaries, which is critical for design space exploration.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic extrapolation tests: hold two parameters fixed, vary the third beyond its training min/max (e.g., tHZO=4nm), generate TCAD ground truth at a few points, and quantify PINO prediction error. Analysis of failure modes would indicate robustness limits.

- **Open Question 5:** Does the model's assumption of a uniformly distributed, smooth charge detrapping process hold for real devices with stochastic trap filling and spatial material inhomogeneity?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The TCAD model uses deterministic trapping/detrapping parameters, and the PINO enforces smooth, monotonic decay globally. The paper does not account for statistical variations in trap energy, location, or density, which cause random telegraph noise and non-uniform retention loss.
  - *Why unresolved:* The surrogate predicts deterministic 2D fields at a fixed grid resolution, but real devices exhibit localized, stochastic charge emission events. The model's ~0.1% relative error on smooth TCAD fields may not translate to accuracy when predicting statistical retention distributions.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Compare PINO predictions against TCAD simulations incorporating stochastic trap models. Measure not just mean Vth shift but also variance and distribution shape. Alternatively, validate against experimental retention data from arrays of FeFET cells to see if the deterministic surrogate captures the statistical spread.

## Limitations
- **TCAD & Dataset Black Box:** Full TCAD simulation parameters and the complete 120-sample dataset are unreproducible without access to Synopsys Sentaurus with identical physical models, preventing independent verification of the ground truth.
- **Training Implementation Gaps:** Critical hyperparameters (loss weights λ_data/λ_physics, learning rate, optimizer) and precise IV-Net layer sizes/activations are omitted. The exact numerical implementation of the monotonicity constraint is unspecified.
- **Generalization Scope:** Claims of performance across {300, 358, 400, 473} K and 5-9 nm HZO are based on reported metrics, but true out-of-distribution behavior (e.g., extreme temperatures, novel geometries) remains untested and could reveal model brittleness.
- **Physics Loss Fidelity:** The embedded Poisson equation and monotonic decay are simplifications. If the TCAD solver includes additional, unmodeled physics (e.g., complex trap kinetics), the physics loss may enforce consistency with an incomplete model, leading to *physically plausible but wrong* errors.

## Confidence
- **>10,000x Speedup (60h → 10s):** High. Direct computational timing comparison; methodology is transparent.
- **High Predictive Accuracy (R² > 0.998, ~0.1% map error):** High. Metrics are explicitly reported on test data, though test set composition is unknown.
- **>40% RMSE Improvement vs. Data-Driven Baseline:** Medium. Depends on the specific baseline architecture and training regimen, which are not fully detailed. The improvement is plausible given the physics regularization but requires replication to confirm.
- **Generalization to Unseen (T, τ, t_HZO):** Medium. Supported by presented results on a held-out set, but the range and distribution of "unseen" points are unclear. No stress-test on extreme extrapolation.
- **Effectiveness of Scalar Context Injection:** Medium.Low. The architecture is described, but the *necessity* of this pathway is inferred from comparison to a weaker model. Ablation evidence is implied but not explicitly shown in the main text.
