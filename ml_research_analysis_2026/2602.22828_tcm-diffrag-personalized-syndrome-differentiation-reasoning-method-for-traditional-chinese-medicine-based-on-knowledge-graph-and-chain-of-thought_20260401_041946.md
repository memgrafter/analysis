# TCM-DiffRAG: Personalized Syndrome Differentiation Reasoning Method for Traditional Chinese Medicine based on Knowledge Graph and Chain of Thought

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22828
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22828
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: For qwen-plus, accuracy on the challenging Jingfang-SD dataset improved from 0.038 to 0.356 by combining structured knowledge graph retrieval with chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Executive Summary
Traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods struggle with Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) diagnosis due to the domain's complex, multi-hop reasoning requirements and significant individual variations among diagnostic schools. The TCM-DiffRAG method addresses this by integrating a dual-level general knowledge graph (extracted from 580 textbooks) with personalized knowledge graphs derived from clinical case reasoning chains. A fine-tuned Chain-of-Thought model decomposes queries into precise multi-hop triplets for subgraph retrieval, effectively bridging broad medical knowledge with school-specific clinical thinking. Evaluations on three TCM benchmarks show substantial performance gains over both native large language models and other RAG approaches.

## Method Summary
The method constructs two complementary knowledge structures. First, a macro-micro general knowledge graph is built from 580 TCM textbooks: document parsing extracts a hierarchical chapter structure (macro), while an LLM extracts relational triplets from text snippets (micro). Second, a personalized knowledge graph is generated for each diagnostic school by using a strong LLM to produce Chain-of-Thought reasoning for training Q&A pairs, decomposing these into triplets, aligning them with the general graph via embedding similarity, and extracting new school-specific triplets. A Qwen2.5-7B-instruct model is fine-tuned on the combined dataset of Q&A pairs and generated reasoning chains. At inference, this fine-tuned model decomposes a new query into multi-hop triplets, retrieves relevant subgraphs from the personalized knowledge graph, maps retrieved triplets back to original text snippets, and generates the final answer.

## Key Results
- TCM-DiffRAG substantially improved accuracy over native LLMs across all datasets, with the largest gain on the school-specific Jingfang-SD benchmark (qwen-plus: 0.038 → 0.356).
- The approach outperformed supervised fine-tuned LLMs and other RAG variants, demonstrating the synergy of structured retrieval and explicit reasoning.
- Dual-level knowledge graph integration and personalized CoT decomposition were key to handling complex, individualized TCM diagnosis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TCM diagnosis involves intricate, multi-step reasoning that varies by practitioner school. Standard RAG retrieves loosely related text, which fails to capture the precise logical chains needed. TCM-DiffRAG forces the model to first articulate a diagnostic reasoning pathway (via CoT triplets), then retrieve only knowledge relevant to those explicit steps. The general knowledge graph provides a comprehensive, structured foundation of TCM concepts, while the personalized graph distills school-specific reasoning patterns from clinical cases. This two-tier structure ensures both broad coverage and diagnostic nuance, allowing the fine-tuned model to ground its generation in precisely retrieved evidence rather than generic context.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graph (KG)**: A structured network of entities and relations. *Why needed*: Encodes complex TCM relationships (e.g., syndrome→symptom→herb) for precise retrieval. *Quick check*: Can the KG answer multi-hop queries like “Which herbs treat Liver-Yang hyperactivity with Kidney-Yin deficiency?”
- **Chain of Thought (CoT)**: Intermediate reasoning steps produced by an LLM. *Why needed*: Breaks down complex diagnostic logic into retrievable units (triplets). *Quick check*: Do generated CoT sequences logically match expert TCM diagnosis processes?
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Augments LLMs with external knowledge. *Why needed*: Overcomes LLM hallucinations and knowledge cut-offs in specialized domains. *Quick check*: Does retrieved context contain the key entities needed for the final answer?
- **Personalized Medicine in TCM**: Tailoring diagnosis/treatment to individual patterns and schools. *Why needed*: TCM schools (e.g., Jingfang) emphasize different reasoning paths; a one-size-fits-all KG is insufficient. *Quick check*: Does the personalized KG for Jingfang contain distinct triplet patterns versus the general KG?
- **Multi-hop Reasoning**: Answering questions requiring several logical steps. *Why needed*: TCM syndrome differentiation often involves linking symptoms→pathologies→therapies. *Quick check*: Can the system retrieve evidence for each hop in a 3-step diagnostic chain?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Textbook Corpus → Macro-Micro General KG; Training Q&A → CoT Decomposition → Personalized KG; Combined Data (Q&A + CoT) → Fine-tuned LLM (LLM-cot-7B); Query → LLM-cot-7B Triplet Extraction → Personalized KG Retrieval (vector search, k-top) → Text Snippet Mapping → Answer Generation.

**Critical Path**: Query enters LLM-cot-7B, which outputs a series of diagnostic triplets (e.g., “patient has symptom X”, “symptom X indicates syndrome Y”). These triplets query the personalized KG; matched triplets retrieve original text snippets; snippets are fed to the LLM for final answer generation.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- General KG (broad) vs. Personalized KG (nuanced): General KG risks missing school-specific logic; Personalized KG risks overfitting to limited case data.
- Structured triplet retrieval vs. free-form generation: Triplets enforce precision but depend on extraction quality; generation is flexible but less grounded.
- Fine-tuning on CoT vs. pure Q&A: CoT improves reasoning transparency but requires high-quality synthetic chains.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Low entity recall in KG evaluation (RAGAS) → General KG snippet extraction is fragmented.
- Minimal accuracy gain on Jingfang-SD after adding personalized KG → Personalized triplets do not align with general KG or capture school patterns.
- Generated CoT chains are incoherent or clinically invalid → LLM prompt or fine-tuning data inadequate.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Replicate general KG construction: Parse sample textbooks, extract micro-triplets, evaluate macro-micro integration via RAGAS on TCM-MCQ test set.
2. Ablate personalized KG: Run TCM-DiffRAG on Jingfang-SD using only general KG vs. with personalized KG; expect significant accuracy drop without personalization.
3. Audit triplet quality: Manually review 100 CoT-generated triplets from training data; verify clinical plausibility and alignment with general KG entities.
