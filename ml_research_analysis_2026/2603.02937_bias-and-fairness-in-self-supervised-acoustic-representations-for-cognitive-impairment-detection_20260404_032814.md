# Bias and Fairness in Self-Supervised Acoustic Representations for Cognitive Impairment Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02937  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02937  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Higher-layer Wav2Vec 2.0 embeddings achieve the best CI detection performance (unweighted average recall up to 80.6%) but exhibit significant representational biases across demographic subgroups.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates performance disparities in speech-based cognitive impairment (CI) detection across demographic and clinical subgroups. It compares traditional acoustic features (MFCCs, eGeMAPS) with self-supervised Wav2Vec 2.0 embeddings on the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus. While higher-layer Wav2Vec 2.0 embeddings yield the highest CI detection accuracy (UAR up to 80.6%), they show substantial biases: females and younger participants have lower discriminative power (AUC 0.769, 0.746) and larger specificity gaps (up to 18%, 15%) compared to males and older adults. Depression detection within CI subjects performs poorly (max UAR 61.3%), with minimal generalization between CI and depression tasks. The findings underscore inherent biases in self-supervised models and the need for fairness-aware evaluation before clinical deployment.

## Method Summary
The study uses the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus, comprising 229 participants (139 CI, 90 NCI) performing the “Cookie Theft” speech task. Three feature sets are extracted: MFCCs, eGeMAPS functionals, and Wav2Vec 2.0 hidden-layer embeddings (layers 1–12). Three dataset configurations are used: imbalanced, CI-balanced, and CI-gender balanced. Classifiers include RBF-SVM, Random Forest, and MLP. Models are trained on fixed 70/30 splits (five seeds) and evaluated using unweighted average recall (UAR), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and fairness metrics (subgroup-specific Sens/Spec, intra-group imbalance δ, inter-group disparity Δ, subgroup AUC). The best CI detection uses Wav2Vec 2.0 layers 9/10; depression analysis is restricted to CI participants.

## Key Results
- CI detection peaks with W2V2 hidden layers 9/10 (UAR up to 80.6%), outperforming traditional features, but females and younger adults show lower AUC (0.769 vs. 0.824; 0.746 vs. 0.831) and specificity gaps up to 18% and 15%.
- Depression detection within CI subjects yields poor overall results (max UAR 61.3%), with W2V2 lower/mid-layers (2, 6) performing best but still inadequate.
- Cross-task generalization between CI and depression is near chance (UAR ~50–58%), indicating distinct acoustic correlates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Higher-layer Wav2Vec 2.0 embeddings capture contextual speech patterns discriminative of CI that surpass low-level acoustic features.
- **Mechanism:** Self-supervised pretraining teaches the transformer encoder to model long-range dependencies and latent phonetic-semantic patterns. Deeper layers (e.g., 9, 10) integrate broader context, aligning with subtle, distributed disruptions (circumlocution, syntactic fragmentation) in CI speech.
- **Core assumption:** CI-related linguistic and cognitive disruptions are encoded in higher-order, contextualized representations not captured by shallow features.
- **Evidence anchors:** Layer 9/10 significantly outperform baselines across configurations (p<0.05); UAR up to 80.6%.
- **Break condition:** If lower W2V2 layers (1–3) or alternative SSL models (e.g., HuBERT) match layer 9/10 UAR on CI detection.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Representational biases in W2V2 embeddings cause performance disparities across demographic subgroups in CI detection.
- **Mechanism:** Biases inherited from pretraining data (e.g., LibriSpeech) produce embeddings where CI/NCI distributions are less separable for females and younger adults, leading to higher class overlap and misclassification.
- **Core assumption:** Pretraining corpus demographics and speaking styles are not uniformly representative of all clinical populations, encoded in the latent space.
- **Evidence anchors:** Female AUC 0.769 vs. male 0.824; age-group-1 AUC 0.746 vs. group-2 0.831; substantial specificity gaps.
- **Break condition:** Subgroup AUC gaps shrink below 0.05 after rigorous debiasing (adversarial training, data re-weighting).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CI and depression detection rely on distinct acoustic representations, hindering cross-task generalization.
- **Mechanism:** CI changes (lexical retrieval failures, agrammatism) manifest in higher-level linguistic patterns; depression changes (monotone pitch, slowed rate) are more prosodic/spectral. W2V2’s hierarchy reflects this: lower layers encode surface acoustics (useful for depression), higher layers encode context (useful for CI).
- **Core assumption:** Acoustic correlates of CI and depression are sufficiently non-overlapping that a single representation cannot optimally support both.
- **Evidence anchors:** Depression detection max UAR 61.3%; lower/mid W2V2 layers (2,6) outperform higher layers; cross-condition UAR ~50–58% (chance).
- **Break condition:** A single W2V2 layer achieves UAR >65% on both tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-Supervised Learning for Speech:**  
  *Why needed:* Wav2Vec 2.0 is core to the analysis; understanding its contrastive pretraining and hierarchical outputs explains why different layers vary in utility for CI vs. depression.  
  *Quick check:* What distinguishes W2V2’s “latent” vs. “hidden” layer outputs, and which likely holds more speaker-invariant information?
- **Fairness Metrics in Binary Classification:**  
  *Why needed:* The bias analysis uses subgroup-specific Sens/Spec, δ, Δ, and AUC to expose who is harmed by model errors beyond global accuracy.  
  *Quick check:* Given subgroup A: Sens=90%, Spec=70%; subgroup B: Sens=70%, Spec=90%. Compute δ values and identify which group is more likely falsely classified as NCI.
- **Clinical Phenotypes of Cognitive Impairment and Depression:**  
  *Why needed:* Interpreting acoustic feature changes requires knowing how CI (e.g., circumlocution) vs. depression (e.g., monotone) manifest in speech tasks.  
  *Quick check:* How might “circumlocutory speech” acoustically differ from “monotone pitch” in a picture-description task?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw 16kHz Audio (DementiaBank “Cookie Theft”) → Feature Extraction Module (three parallel paths: MFCCs mean-pooled 40-D; eGeMAPS 88-D; W2V2 hidden-layer 512-D) → Classifier Module (RBF-SVM / Random Forest / MLP) → Evaluation & Bias Analysis Module (UAR, Sens, Spec, subgroup δ/Δ, AUC, density plots).
- **Critical path (CI detection):** W2V2 Layer 9/10 → MLP/SVM → global UAR & subgroup metrics → if significant Δ/δ (p<0.05), diagnose via AUC gaps and density plots.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Data balancing (CI/gender) improves fairness but does not eliminate bias, indicating need for model-level debiasing (adversarial loss).
  - Layer selection is task-dependent: higher W2V2 layers for CI, lower/mid for depression.
  - Classifier choice matters less than feature type; RF less sensitive to hyperparameters than SVM/MLP.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Task failure: Depression UAR ≤55% or cross-task UAR ~50–58%.
  - Bias failure: |Δspec| or |Δsens| >10% and subgroup AUC gap >0.1.
  - Calibration failure: High global accuracy but large intra-group |δ| (>15%).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Feature Performance Benchmark:** For each feature set (MFCCs, eGeMAPS, W2V2 layers 1–12) across dataset configurations, train classifiers and record global CI detection UAR. Identify top W2V2 layers (expected: 9,10).
  2. **Subgroup Bias Evaluation:** Using best W2V2 layer/classifier (e.g., Layer 9 + SVM on CI-gender balanced set), compute subgroup-specific Sens/Spec/AUC for gender, age, depression status; calculate δ and Δ; test significance (paired t-test).
  3. **Cross-Task Generalization Test:** Train best W2V2 models for CI detection, test on depression labels (and vice versa) using CI-gender balanced set; expect near-chance UAR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors call out the need for fairness-aware evaluation before clinical deployment and suggest future work on debiasing techniques, multi-site validation, and testing generalizability across diverse populations and tasks.

## Limitations
- Single corpus (DementiaBank, n=229) limits generalizability.
- Exact train/test splits and preprocessing details (denoising, normalization) undisclosed, hindering replication.
- W2V2 input handling (normalizer, mean-pooling) unspecified; hyperparameter search inner CV seed not fixed.
- Depression analysis restricted to CI subgroup, reducing broader applicability.
- Only one SSL architecture evaluated; findings may not generalize to others.

## Confidence
- Higher-layer Wav2Vec 2.0 for CI detection: **Medium** (results rely on undisclosed splits/preprocessing; single corpus).
- Demographic bias in W2V2: **Medium** (observations robust but causal attribution inferential; small N).
- CI vs. depression distinct representations: **High** (stable near-chance cross-task scores across feature sets).
