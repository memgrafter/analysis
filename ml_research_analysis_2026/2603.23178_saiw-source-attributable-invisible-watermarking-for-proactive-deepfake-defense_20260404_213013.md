# SAiW: Source-Attributable Invisible Watermarking for Proactive Deepfake Defense

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23178
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23178
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A framework for embedding source-attributable watermarks that achieves high visual fidelity (>50 dB PSNR) and robust bit recovery (>97%) against distortions, enabling reliable media provenance verification.

## Executive Summary
SAiW proposes a proactive defense against deepfakes by embedding invisible, source-specific watermarks at the time of media creation. Unlike reactive detection, this framework allows for later verification of a piece of content's origin. It reformulates watermarking as a source-conditioned representation learning task, using a feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) mechanism to make a single embedding network generate distinct perturbations for different sources. A perceptual guidance module, informed by simplified human visual system models, ensures these perturbations remain invisible. A dual-purpose forensic decoder then jointly extracts the watermark and identifies its source from potentially distorted inputs. Evaluations across multiple deepfake datasets show the method maintains high image quality and robust watermark recovery under various attacks while achieving strong source attribution accuracy.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two main networks: an embedding network and a forensic decoder. The embedding network takes a cover image and a source-specific binary logo. The logo is encoded into a latent vector that conditions multiple layers of a U-Net backbone via FiLM, producing source-specific feature modulations. A perceptual guidance module computes a spatial sensitivity map from the image's luminance and contrast, which scales the embedding strength to preserve visual imperceptibility. The resulting watermarked image is a small residual added to the original. The forensic decoder, a ConvNeXt backbone with two parallel heads, processes a distorted version of the watermarked image to simultaneously reconstruct the original logo and produce an identity embedding for source classification. The entire system is trained with a composite loss balancing imperceptibility, robustness, and identification.

## Key Results
- **Visual Fidelity:** Watermarked images exhibit extremely high similarity to originals (PSNR >50 dB, SSIM >0.998, LPIPS <0.001).
- **Robustness:** Bit recovery accuracy exceeds 97% across a suite of distortions including compression, noise, geometric transforms, and adversarial perturbations.
- **Source Attribution:** Achieves ~84% accuracy in identifying the originating source (generator/platform) among multiple classes, including genuine and unwatermarked sources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reformulating watermark embedding as a source-conditioned representation learning problem enables scalable, discriminative, and traceable watermark signatures for multi-source attribution.
- **Mechanism:** The watermark identity (a source-specific logo) is encoded into a latent feature vector `g_l` by a lightweight encoder (`f_logo`). This vector is used by a Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) module `f_M` to generate channel-wise scaling (`γ`) and shifting (`β`) parameters. These parameters modulate the feature maps at multiple stages (`x_i`) of the embedding network's U-Net encoder via `f_M(x_i; γ_i, β_i) = γ_i ⊙ x_i + β_i`.
- **Core assumption:** A watermark's identity, when used as a conditioning signal for network weights rather than just an additive payload, can produce source-specific transformations that are inherently discriminative and more robust.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "formulates watermark embedding as a source-conditioned representation learning problem, where watermark identity encodes the originating source and modulates the embedding process"; [section III.B.1] "Feature-wise linear modulation module `f_M` to predict parameters {(γ_i, β_i)}_K used to condition multiple layers... enabling the network to learn watermark-specific transformations".
- **Break condition:** If the FiLM parameters (`γ`, `β`) fail to produce significantly distinct modulation patterns for different sources (measured by low inter-class variance in modulated feature statistics), the mechanism fails to provide discriminative signatures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A perceptual guidance module, derived from human visual system (HVS) priors (luminance adaptation and contrast masking), adaptively allocates embedding strength to visually tolerant regions, enabling high imperceptibility without sacrificing robustness.
- **Mechanism:** From the cover image's luminance channel (`I_y`), two components are computed: 1) **Luminance Adaptation (`P_LA`)**: estimates local background sensitivity using smoothed luminance. 2) **Contrast Masking (`P_CM`)**: estimates tolerance to noise based on local edge strength (gradient magnitude). These are fused into a perceptual map `P`, rectified, and normalized to `P_n`. This spatial map `P_n` directly scales the feature representations (`h_4`) in the hybrid transformer bottleneck (`h_5 = (1 - λ_3 · P_s) . h_4`), suppressing perturbations in perceptually fragile (smooth, low-contrast) areas.
- **Core assumption:** The simplified HVS models (luminance adaptation, contrast masking) provide a sufficiently accurate spatial prior for human visual sensitivity to guide adaptive watermark embedding.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "perceptual guidance module derived from human visual system priors ensures that watermark perturbations remain visually imperceptible"; [section III.B.2] Detailed equations for `P_LA` and `P_CM` derived from standard psychovisual models.
- **Break condition:** If the perceptual map `P_n` does not correlate with ground-truth human detection thresholds (e.g., via subjective studies) or if its application leads to significantly worse imperceptibility metrics (LPIPS, SSIM) compared to a uniform embedding strategy, the HVS prior guidance is ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A dual-purpose forensic decoder, with a shared ConvNeXt backbone and parallel reconstruction/identification heads, enables simultaneous robust watermark recovery and reliable source attribution, providing complementary supervision.
- **Mechanism:** A distorted input image `I_d` is processed by a ConvNeXt backbone to produce a high-level feature map `g_map`. This shared representation feeds two parallel heads: 1) **Reconstruction Head (`f_R`)**: A transposed convolutional decoder upsamples `g_map` to reconstruct the binary watermark logo `Î_l`. 2) **Source Identification Head (`f_id`)**: `g_map` is globally pooled and projected to a 256-D identity embedding `v_i`, classified using an additive angular margin (ArcFace) loss `L_id`.
- **Core assumption:** The features optimal for recovering a distorted watermark (`g_map`) are also sufficiently discriminative for classifying its source. Joint optimization stabilizes learning for both tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "a dual-purpose forensic decoder simultaneously reconstructs the embedded watermark and performs source attribution"; [section III.C] "The extractor `f_D`... employs a ConvNeXt backbone... After the final ConvNeXt stage... a compact feature map `g_map`... is fed into two parallel branches: a watermark reconstruction head (`f_R`) and a source identification head (`f_id`)".
- **Break condition:** If training with the combined loss (`L_total = w_imp L_imp + w_rob L_rob + w_id L_id`) results in a significant trade-off where improving `L_rob` (bit accuracy) substantially degrades `L_id` (source accuracy), or vice-versa, the shared representation assumption fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)**
  - **Why needed here:** To condition the watermark embedding network on the specific source identity without proliferating per-source model parameters. It allows a single network to generate source-specific perturbation patterns.
  - **Quick check question:** If you change the input watermark logo, which part of the embedding network's computation graph is directly and efficiently affected first?
- **Concept: Perceptual Just Noticeable Difference (JND) Models**
  - **Why needed here:** To determine *where* and *how much* noise (the watermark) can be added to an image without being perceived. The paper's `P_LA` and `P_CM` are specific instantiations of JND models.
  - **Quick check question:** Would the perceptual guidance map `P_n` assign higher embedding strength to a smooth sky region or a textured tree branch? Why?
- **Concept: Additive Angular Margin (ArcFace) Loss**
  - **Why needed here:** To enforce that identity embeddings (`v_i`) from the same source are compact on a hypersphere while being maximally separable from other sources. This is crucial for reliable attribution from a degraded feature.
  - **Quick check question:** In the ArcFace loss formula, what is the purpose of the additive margin `m` in `cos(θ_yi + m)`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** 
  1. **Inputs:** Cover Image `I` (H×W×C), Watermark `I_l` (h×w×c).
  2. **Watermark Encoder (`f_logo`):** Lightweight CNN to encode `I_l` into latent `g_l`.
  3. **FiLM Generator (`f_M`):** MLP that takes `g_l` and produces `{γ_i, β_i}` for `K` encoder layers.
  4. **Main Embedding Network (`f_E`):** U-Net backbone with down/up blocks.
  5. **Perceptual Guidance (`f_P`):** Computes spatial map `P_n` from `I` (luminance/contrast).
  6. **Hybrid Transformer Bottleneck:** Swin Transformer layers modulated by `f_M` outputs.
  7. **Residual Branch:** Generates perturbation `I_r` scaled by `λ4`.
  8. **Watermarked Output:** `I_w = I + I_r`.
  9. **Distortion Module (`T(·)`):** Applies random transformations to `I_w` to yield `I_d`.
  10. **Forensic Decoder (`f_D`):** ConvNeXt backbone.
  11. **Reconstruction Head (`f_R`):** Transposed conv decoder to output `Î_l`.
  12. **Identification Head (`f_id`):** Global pool + linear layer to output embedding `v_i`.

- **Critical Path:** `I, I_l` → `f_logo` → `g_l` → `f_M` → `{(γ_i,β_i)}` → `f_E` (with FiLM at 10 encoder stages) → `f_P` modulates bottleneck → `I_r` → `I_w`; `I_w` → `T(·)` → `I_d` → `f_D` → `g_map` → (`f_R` → `Î_l`; `f_id` → `v_i`).

- **Design Tradeoffs:** 
  - *Scalability vs. Distinctiveness:* FiLM allows a single network to handle many sources without parameter explosion, but the learned modulations may become less discriminative as the number of sources grows.
  - *Imperceptibility vs. Robustness:* Perceptual guidance prioritizes visual hiding in sensitive regions, potentially reducing embedding strength in areas that might otherwise aid robustness.
  - *Task Synergy vs. Interference:* Jointly optimizing watermark reconstruction and source identification in a shared decoder improves efficiency but risks one task dominating the learned features, harming the other.

- **Failure Signatures:**
  - FiLM parameters produce near-identical feature statistics across sources (measured by low variance in `γ, β` norms), leading to poor source identification.
  - Perceptual map `P_n` shows high values (low embedding strength) in textured areas and low values in smooth regions, contradicting HVS principles, resulting in visible artifacts or poor robustness.
  - Training curves show `A_br` (bit accuracy) plateauing while `A_id` (source accuracy) declines, or vice-versa, indicating optimization conflict between the two decoder heads.

- **First Three Experiments:**
  1. **Data Pipeline Setup:** Generate a dataset with at least 6-8 source classes (unique binary logos), paired cover images (e.g., CelebA), and corresponding deepfakes (using tools like SimSwap). Implement the distortion set `T(·)` including JPEG (QF=50,75), Gaussian blur, noise, brightness/contrast, crop, rotate, and Instagram filters.
  2. **Architecture Implementation:** Build `f_E` as a U-Net with specified FiLM injection at 10 encoder stages, hybrid Swin Transformer bottleneck, and `f_P` computing `P_LA` and `P_CM` with given parameters (`p1`-`p6`). Build `f_D` as ConvNeXt with two parallel heads. Use hyperparameters: `λ1=0.3`, `λ2=1/30`, `λ3=0.5`.
  3. **Initial Training Run:** Train with composite loss `L_total = w_imp L_imp + w_rob L_rob + w_id L_id` (initialize weights equally, e.g., 1/3 each). Use `N=16` batch size for `L_id`. Monitor PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on `(I, I_w)` and bit accuracy `A_br` on clean `(I_l, Î_l)`. Validate source identification `A_id` on a held-out set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can SAiW be extended to video and multimodal media?**
  - **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "Future work will extend the framework to video and multimodal media."
  - **Why unresolved:** The current architecture processes only single images; video requires temporal coherence and multimodal content needs cross-modal alignment mechanisms.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A video extension that preserves imperceptibility and robustness across frames, and a multimodal version evaluated on audiovisual deepfake datasets with successful source attribution.

- **How robust is SAiW against generative regeneration pipelines?**
  - **Basis in paper:** The authors mention: "explore robustness against generative regeneration pipelines to support large-scale provenance tracking."
  - **Why unresolved:** Existing robustness tests cover distortions and adversarial attacks, but not the case where watermarked content is fed into generative models (e.g., GANs, diffusion) that may remove or alter the watermark.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of watermark recovery and source identification after regenerating watermarked images through various generative models; development of techniques to ensure watermark survival.

- **How does SAiW scale to a large number of source identities (e.g., hundreds or thousands of generators or users)?**
  - **Basis in paper:** The paper claims "scalable multi-source provenance encoding" via feature-wise linear modulation, but experiments only involve up to 8 source classes.
  - **Why unresolved:** Performance may degrade with many classes due to increased confusion in the identification head and reduced watermark distinctiveness; training data per class could become insufficient.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments with increasing numbers of source classes (10, 100, 1000), measure identification accuracy, bit recovery, and imperceptibility, and analyze the capacity limits of the current design.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete training specifications:** Critical loss weights (`w_imp`, `w_rob`, `w_id`) and distortion sampling probabilities are not reported, hindering faithful reproduction and trade-off analysis.
- **Unvalidated perceptual guidance:** The HVS-derived perceptual map `P_n` lacks empirical validation against human detection thresholds; its alignment with actual visual sensitivity is assumed but not tested.
- **Limited robustness evaluation:** Testing excludes advanced adversarial attacks and GAN-based editing pipelines, leaving unknown defense margins against sophisticated content manipulation attacks.

## Confidence
- **FiLM-based conditioning for scalable signatures:** **Medium** (theoretically plausible, but discriminative power of `(γ,β)` parameters not empirically dissected).
- **Perceptual guidance via JND models:** **Low** (core assumption untested; break condition—correlation with human vision—unverified).
- **Dual-purpose forensic decoder:** **High** (architecture clearly defined, joint optimization standard; supported by neighbor work).
- **Overall performance claims (PSNR >50 dB, >97% bit recovery, 84% source ID):** **Medium** (results are strong but reproducibility is compromised by missing details and limited robustness testing).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis:** Systematically vary loss weights and distortion sampling probabilities across a grid, measuring impact on imperceptibility (LPIPS), robustness (bit recovery under JPEG/noise), and attribution accuracy. Report Pareto fronts to characterize trade-offs.
2. **Human perception validation:** Conduct a two-alternative forced choice (2AFC) study with watermarked vs. original images, then correlate participants’ detection rates with the perceptual map `P_n` values (e.g., via logistic regression). A significant positive correlation would validate the HVS prior.
3. **Adversarial and editing stress test:** Evaluate SAiW under strong white-box (PGD, AutoAttack) and black-box adversarial perturbations, plus GAN-based edits (e.g., StyleGAN facial attribute edits). Measure bit recovery and source ID accuracy to reveal failure modes not captured by the paper’s distortion set.
