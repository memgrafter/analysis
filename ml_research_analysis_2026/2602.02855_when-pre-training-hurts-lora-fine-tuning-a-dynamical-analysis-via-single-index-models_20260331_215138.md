# When pre-training hurts LoRA fine-tuning: a dynamical analysis via single-index models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02855
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02855
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Excessive pre-training can slow down LoRA fine-tuning convergence for many common activation functions, and may even block escape entirely for odd-degree Hermite activations.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether pre-training always accelerates LoRA fine-tuning. Using single-index models and one-pass SGD, the authors reduce the high-dimensional optimization dynamics to two order parameters: the LoRA coefficient \(u\) and the overlap \(m\) between the student and teacher directions. They derive the population loss via Hermite expansions and analyze the initial correlated search phase through linearized ordinary differential equations. The escape time from this phase scales as \(\tau(\mu) = \frac{-B + \sqrt{B^2 + 4A^2}}{2A^2}\), where \(A\) and \(B\) depend on the pre-training strength \(\mu\) and the activation function. For linear and many nonlinear activations (e.g., error function), \(A \propto (1-\mu)\), so stronger pre-training (larger \(\mu\)) increases \(\tau\) and delays convergence. For odd-degree Hermite activations, there exists a \(\tilde{\mu} \in (0,1)\) where \(A(\tilde{\mu})=0\), making escape impossible. Transforming labels (e.g., squaring) reduces the effective information exponent, which can reverse the slowdown for some activations. These results challenge the widespread belief that pre-training universally speeds up fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The study employs a teacher-student single-index model: the teacher generates labels \(y = \phi(\omega^\star \cdot x)\) from \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)\), and the student uses a LoRA-updated predictor \(\sigma((\tilde{\omega} + u \omega) \cdot x)\) with pre-trained weight \(\tilde{\omega} = \mu \omega^\star\) (alignment \(\mu \in [0,1]\)). Training follows one-pass SGD with a spherical constraint on the trainable direction \(\omega\). The population dynamics are analyzed by reducing the infinite-dimensional gradient flow to two order parameters: the LoRA scalar \(u\) and the overlap \(m = \omega \cdot \omega^\star\). The population loss and its gradients are expressed using Hermite expansions of the activation \(\sigma\) under Gaussian measures, with the pre-activation variance \(r = \mu^2\) inducing a variance-dependent information exponent. In the initial correlated search phase (\(|u|, |m| \ll \mu\)), the dynamics are linearized, yielding a Jacobian whose largest eigenvalue gives the escape time scaling. Theoretical predictions are validated via streaming SGD simulations on synthetic data.

## Key Results
- For linear and many common nonlinear activations (e.g., erf), the escape time \(\tau(\mu)\) grows with \(\mu\) because \(A \propto (1-\mu)\); thus stronger pre-training delays convergence.
- For pure Hermite activation functions of odd degree \(k \geq 3\), there exists a critical \(\tilde{\mu} \in (0,1)\) such that \(A(\tilde{\mu}) = 0\), causing \(\tau\) to diverge and trapping the dynamics in the correlated search phase.
- Transforming labels (e.g., squaring) reduces the effective information exponent, altering \(A\) and \(B\) so that for some activations (e.g., linear, erf) the escape time now decreases with \(\mu\), reversing the pre-training slowdown.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Pre-training alignment slows escape via \(A \propto (1-\mu)\)
- **Claim:** Stronger pre-training (larger \(\mu\)) increases the characteristic escape time \(\tau(\mu)\) from the correlated search phase for linear and many common nonlinear activations.
- **Mechanism:** The linearized dynamics of order parameters \((u, m)\) have a Jacobian with eigenvalues \(\lambda_\pm = (B \pm \sqrt{B^2+4A^2})/2\). The escape time scales as \(\tau = 1/\lambda_+\). For linear activation and information exponent (IE)=1 activations, \(A \propto (1-\mu)\), so as \(\mu \to 1\), \(A \to 0\) and \(\tau\) diverges, slowing convergence.
- **Core assumption:** Linearization is valid in the correlated search phase (\(|u|, |m| \ll \mu\)) and \(A \neq 0\).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Section 3.2(a)] "For linear activation ... \(A \propto 1-\mu\) (App. E.2)."  
  - [Section 3.2(b)] "For erf and sigmoid, Fig. 2 shows that \(\tau(\mu)\) grows monotonically with \(\mu\)."  
  - [Abstract] "For linear and many common nonlinear activations, \(A \propto (1-\mu)\), so stronger pre-training (larger \(\mu\)) increases \(\tau\) and delays convergence."
- **Break condition:** When \(\mu = 1\), \(A = 0\), escape time diverges.

### Mechanism 2: Odd Hermite activations can block escape
- **Claim:** For pure Hermite activation of odd degree \(k \geq 3\), there exists \(\tilde{\mu} \in (0,1)\) such that \(A(\tilde{\mu}) = 0\), making escape from the correlated search phase impossible (\(\tau\) diverges).
- **Mechanism:** The Hermite expansion of the student activation with pre-activation variance \(r = \mu^2\) leads to coefficients for which \(A(\mu)\) has a zero at some positive \(\mu\) due to the specific polynomial structure of Hermite polynomials.
- **Core assumption:** Teacher and student share the same pure Hermite activation of odd degree.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Section 3.2(c)] Proposition 2: "For pure Hermite activation function \(\sigma(z)=\mathrm{He}_k(z)\) of odd degree \(k \geq 3\), there exists at least one \(\tilde{\mu} \in (0,1)\) such that \(A(\tilde{\mu})=0\)."  
  - [Section 3.2(c)] Figure 3 (bottom) shows \(\tau(\mu)\) diverging at specific \(\mu\) values.
- **Break condition:** When \(\mu\) equals the critical value where \(A=0\), the linearized dynamics have zero unstable eigenvalue exponent, trapping the dynamics.

### Mechanism 3: Label transformation reverses slowdown
- **Claim:** Transforming labels (e.g., squaring) reduces the effective information exponent to \(\leq 2\), altering \(A\) and \(B\) such that for some activations the escape time now decreases with \(\mu\).
- **Mechanism:** Squaring a Hermite teacher activates only \(\mathrm{He}_0\) and \(\mathrm{He}_2\) components, effectively setting the teacher's Hermite expansion to have leading nonzero component at degree 2. This changes the expressions for \(A\) and \(B\) in the linearized dynamics, leading to a different \(\mu\)-dependence.
- **Core assumption:** The loss is modified by transforming labels; the student activation remains unchanged.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Section 3.3] "Squaring enables faster escape for linear and erf" and Figure 4 shows reversal.  
  - [Appendix B.4] analytical justification for Hermite activations.
- **Break condition:** The effect varies by activation; for sigmoid, little change because its structure yields similar \(A/B\) after transformation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Single-index models**  
  - **Why needed:** The entire analysis builds on the teacher-student single-index model to capture the essence of LoRA fine-tuning on a one-dimensional relevant subspace.  
  - **Quick check:** In single-index models, what does the information exponent measure and why does it affect learning?

- **Hermite expansion**  
  - **Why needed:** The population loss and gradient are expressed in terms of Hermite coefficients of activations with respect to Gaussian measures; the mismatch in pre-activation variance \(r\) leads to variance-induced IE reduction.  
  - **Quick check:** How does the pre-activation variance \(r\) change the Hermite expansion of an activation function?

- **Order parameters (u, m)**  
  - **Why needed:** They reduce the high-dimensional SGD dynamics to a low-dimensional system; \(u\) captures the LoRA scalar magnitude and \(m\) captures the overlap with the teacher direction.  
  - **Quick check:** What is the effective alignment \(m_{\text{eff}}\) and why is it a key indicator of recovery?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Pre-trained weight vector \(\tilde{\omega} = \mu \omega^\star\) → Trainable LoRA update (scalar \(u\), orthogonal direction \(\omega\)) → Combined predictor \(\sigma((\tilde{\omega} + u\omega) \cdot x)\)

- **Critical path:** The optimization dynamics are governed by the coupled evolution of \(u\) and \(m = \omega \cdot \omega^\star\). The escape from the correlated search phase (small \(u, m\)) is controlled by eigenvalues of the linearized Jacobian \((A, B)\). After escape, exponential convergence to the global minimum occurs.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  1. Pre-training strength \(\mu\) vs convergence speed: higher \(\mu\) slows escape for most activations, but may improve initial loss.  
  2. LoRA rank: In the simple setting, rank 1; extending to higher ranks introduces cross-coupling but linear escape time unchanged.  
  3. Activation choice: The IE of the teacher and the variance-induced IE in student affect \(A\) and \(B\); odd Hermite activations exhibit singularities.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Extended plateau in training loss/overlap when \(\mu\) near singularity for odd Hermite activations.  
  - Initial rapid progress (due to high \(\mu\)) followed by extremely slow convergence for linear/ReLU when \(\mu\) close to 1.  
  - Ineffective learning if \(A\) becomes zero (theoretical impossibility to leave search phase).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Reproduce Figure 1 (Linear):** Train with different \(\mu\) values, record test MSE, \(m\), \(m_{\text{eff}}\). Expect higher \(\mu\) → slower convergence.  
  2. **Sweep \(\mu\) for Hermite-3:** Compute escape time (e.g., when \(m\) reaches 0.98) across \(\mu \in [0.1, 0.9]\). Expect a peak near predicted \(\tilde{\mu}\).  
  3. **Label transformation:** Train on squared labels for linear and ReLU teachers, compare escape times vs \(\mu\). Expect reversal: larger \(\mu\) now speeds escape.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do LoRA rank and pre-training alignment (\(\mu\)) jointly determine final test performance after fine-tuning?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly state in the Discussion: “How rank and pre-training jointly determine final performance remains an open question.”
- **Why unresolved:** The paper focuses on the escape time from the correlated search phase, showing that escape depends on \(\mu\) but not on rank in the linearized regime. The combined influence on the final converged loss and recovery quality has not been characterized.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis of fixed points for arbitrary rank \(R\) using the full order‑parameter dynamics, together with numerical experiments varying rank, \(\mu\), and activation functions. This would reveal how final error depends on both factors.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the pre‑training‑induced slowdown persist when using multi‑epoch SGD (standard fine‑tuning) rather than one‑pass SGD?
- **Basis in paper:** The analytical study and most simulations use one‑pass SGD (Section 2.1). Multi‑epoch training is not considered.
- **Why unresolved:** One‑pass SGD approximates streaming updates and emphasizes the initial search phase. Multi‑epoch revisits data, which could mitigate or worsen the slowdown, an effect not addressed in the paper.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments comparing one‑pass and multi‑epoch SGD on the same data, measuring escape times and final performance across \(\mu\). Extending the mean‑field theory to multiple passes would also provide theoretical insight.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the phenomenon generalize to deeper architectures, such as Transformers with LoRA applied to multiple layers?
- **Basis in paper:** The main results assume single‑index models; the appendix only covers two‑layer networks. Deep networks with many layers are not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** Deep networks introduce many interacting LoRA updates, potentially breaking the two‑parameter reduction. The relationship between \(\mu\) and escape time might change substantially.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a mean‑field reduction for deep LoRA models or perform large‑scale simulations on architectures resembling real transformers, varying depth and \(\mu\). Such evidence would test the robustness of the slowdown effect.

## Limitations
- Theoretical simplifications restrict applicability: rank‑1 LoRA, one‑pass SGD, spherical constraint, and Gaussian inputs are assumed.
- Critical hyperparameters (exact learning rates, batch size, total steps, Hermite polynomial normalization) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Claims are proved only for single‑index models; extension to deeper networks or adaptive optimizers like Adam is unclear.

## Confidence
- Pre‑training alignment slows escape for linear/IE=1 activations: **Medium** (relies on linearization and population limits; finite‑d effects need validation).
- Odd‑degree Hermite activations have a critical \(\mu\) where escape becomes impossible: **High** (rigorous proposition under model assumptions, strong numerical evidence).
- Label transformation (e.g., squaring) reverses the slowdown: **Medium** (empirical support for linear/erf, theory limited and activation‑dependent).
