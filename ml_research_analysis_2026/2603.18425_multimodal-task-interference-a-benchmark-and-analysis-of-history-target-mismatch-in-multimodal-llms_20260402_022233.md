# Multimodal Task Interference: A Benchmark and Analysis of History-Target Mismatch in Multimodal LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.18425
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18425
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Task interference is severe and directional in multimodal LLMs; switching from text-only to image-based tasks causes up to a -42.7% performance drop (Gemma-3n at N=5), while the reverse switch yields minimal degradation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a benchmark to study task interference in multimodal large language models (LLMs)—performance degradation that occurs when a conversation history from a different task precedes a target task. The benchmark covers six text/vision tasks and systematically varies mismatches along three axes: modality (text vs. image input), reasoning requirement, and answer format. Evaluations on four models (GPT-4.1-mini, Gemma-3n, Qwen3-VL, Pixtral) reveal that interference is large and directional: performance drops dramatically when history is text-only and target requires images, but the opposite transition causes little harm. The interference magnitude grows with history length and combines additively across mismatch dimensions, with modality differences being the strongest driver, followed by answer format changes, and barely by reasoning shifts. Qualitative analysis further shows output-style drift (e.g., verbosity) after mismatched histories.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses teacher-forcing to simulate multi-turn dialogues: each history turn consists of a paired input (text or image) and a ground-truth model response, followed by the target input. Six benchmark datasets are used (Rotten Tomatoes, MMLU, TweetQA, VQAv2, OK-VQA, COCO Captions). For each target task and history length N∈{1,3,5}, performance is measured under two conditions: same-task history (samples from the target dataset) and switched-task history (samples from other datasets). The primary metric is relative performance change Δ(%) = 100 × (E_switch - E_same)/E_same. Dataset-specific metrics include accuracy (classification), F1 (QA), VQA accuracy, and CIDEr (captioning). Experiments are repeated across five random seeds for history sampling.

## Key Results
- **Severe directional interference**: Text→image switches cause large performance drops (up to -42.7% with Gemma-3n, N=5); image→text switches cause only minimal degradation.
- **Mismatch-axis hierarchy**: Interference compounds across dimensions, ranked by impact: modality > answer format > reasoning requirement.
- **Output-style drift**: Models produce verbose answers for short-answer visual QA when preceded by text-only history, indicating anchoring on previous formatting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose an explicit underlying mechanism; instead it empirically demonstrates that interference arises from the model’s anchoring on the modality, format, and reasoning style of the preceding dialogue turns. The strong directional asymmetry suggests a bias where processing text first makes subsequent visual tasks disproportionately harder, possibly due to the model’s internal state being dominated by textual representations. Minimal effects from reasoning mismatches indicate that the model can adjust its reasoning strategy more flexibly than it can switch input modalities or output formats.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Multimodal Integration**  
   *Why needed*: To understand how models combine information from different input types (text, images).  
   *Quick check*: Can the model correctly answer questions requiring joint reasoning over an image and accompanying text?  

2. **Task Interference**  
   *Why needed*: To measure robustness in multi-turn, multi-task dialogues where the task may shift.  
   *Quick check*: Does performance on a target task degrade when the preceding history comes from a different task?  

3. **Teacher-forcing Evaluation**  
   *Why needed*: To simulate realistic dialogue histories using ground-truth responses, avoiding contamination from model errors in history.  
   *Quick check*: Are history responses drawn from the same distribution as the training data and free of target examples?  

4. **Modality Mismatch**  
   *Why needed*: To isolate the effect of switching input modalities on model performance.  
   *Quick check*: Compare performance when history and target tasks share the same modality versus when one is text-only and the other image-based.  

5. **Answer Format Sensitivity**  
   *Why needed*: To determine whether models adapt their output style to the expected format of the current task.  
   *Quick check*: Vary the expected output format (e.g., short phrase vs. long paragraph) and measure consistency in response structure.  

6. **Dialogue History Anchoring**  
   *Why needed*: To study how long preceding contexts influence current task execution.  
   *Quick check*: Increase history length and observe whether performance on the target task decays more for certain mismatches.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Image Encoder -> Vision-Language Connector -> LLM Decoder  
*(Text input is tokenized and directly fed to the LLM; images are processed by the image encoder to produce visual tokens, which are projected by the connector and interleaved with text tokens.)*

**Critical path**: The model receives an interleaved sequence of text tokens and base64-encoded image slots. During preprocessing, image slots are replaced with visual tokens from the image encoder; the combined token sequence is passed to the LLM, which autoregressively generates the output.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Efficiency vs. performance**: Larger image encoders improve visual understanding but increase inference latency.
- **Fusion strategy**: Early fusion (cross-attention) may yield better alignment but complicates training; late fusion (concatenation) is simpler but may lose fine-grained interactions.
- **Context length handling**: Longer histories risk exceeding the model’s maximum context, requiring truncation that could alter interference patterns.

**Failure signatures**:
- Model completely ignores images (text-only output) when images are present, indicating a broken vision-language connector or incorrect prompt formatting.
- Performance drops sharply for text→image switches, consistent with modality-driven interference.
- Output format becomes inconsistent (e.g., overly verbose) when answer format mismatches, showing anchoring on prior style.

**Three first experiments**:
1. **Single-turn image understanding test**: For each model, run a single image-based question with a known answer to verify that visual processing is functional.
2. **Two-turn prompt verification**: Construct a dialogue with a text-only task followed by a simple image-based task; check that the model correctly uses the image and that the baseline performance matches expected levels.
3. **Metric calibration**: On a small fixed set (e.g., 20 samples) from VQAv2 and COCO Captions, compute VQA accuracy and CIDEr using the evaluation scripts to ensure they produce scores within known ranges.

## Limitations
- Faithful reproduction is blocked by unspecified prompt templates, history-sampling details, and exact metric implementations.
- Quantitative reproducibility may be affected by differences in third-party evaluation tools (e.g., CIDEr tokenization).
- Observations about output-style drift are qualitative and lack systematic measurement.

## Confidence
- Modality-driven directional interference: High  
- Mismatch-axis hierarchy (modality > answer format > reasoning): Medium  
- Output-style drift (anchoring effect): Low
