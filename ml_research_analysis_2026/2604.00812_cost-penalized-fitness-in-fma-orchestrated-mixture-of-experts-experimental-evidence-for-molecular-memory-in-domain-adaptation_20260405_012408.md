# Cost-Penalized Fitness in FMA-Orchestrated Mixture of Experts: Experimental Evidence for Molecular Memory in Domain Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00812
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00812
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** Introduced cost-penalized fitness and a linear grace period within FMA orchestration, enabling "molecular memory" where the MoE system recovers 9–11× faster (100–150 vs. 1150–1350 steps) when returning to a prior domain, with zero expert births or replacements in the most stable configuration.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of managing a saturated Mixture of Experts (MoE) pool during domain shifts, where full expert capacity prevents effective adaptation through replacement. It introduces a cost-penalized fitness metric (Quality×Demand×Exclusivity / ComputeCost) and a linear grace period for newborn experts within a Free-Market Algorithm (FMA) orchestration framework. In round-trip domain shift experiments, the system exhibited "molecular memory": upon returning to a previously seen domain, it recovered to pre-shift performance nearly instantly without any expert births or replacements. The most stable configuration achieved zero expert churn across two domain shifts. Preliminary extrapolations suggest significant cost and energy savings for large-scale providers.

## Method Summary
The experiments use a nanoFMT framework: a 2-layer transformer with 8 experts per layer, trained sequentially on two domains (`data_char` and `data_code`). The core modification is the FMA's cost-penalized fitness calculation (e.g., Mode C: `F = (Q × D × E) / (1 + FLOPs/FLOP_avg)`). A linear grace period (`F_eff = F × min(1, age/grace_steps)`, grace_steps=50) protects newborn experts from immediate culling. Every 10 steps after a 500-step warmup, the system evaluates all experts. The worst expert is replaced (killed and a new one spawned) only if its fitness falls below `mean(F) - 1σ`. No births occur if all slots are filled and no expert crosses the replacement threshold.

## Key Results
- **Molecular memory:** On returning to the original domain, recovery was 9–11× faster (100–150 steps vs. ~1150–1350 steps from scratch) with zero expert births or replacements.
- **Stability:** Mode C achieved zero expert churn across both domain shift phases.
- **Cost implication:** Preliminary scaling estimates suggest potential annual savings of $39.1M and 27.1 GWh energy reduction for OpenAI-scale providers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The cost-penalized fitness discourages the persistence of expensive, low-quality, or low-demand experts. The linear grace period allows newborn experts a fair chance to demonstrate value before being judged, preventing premature culling that would otherwise cause replacement cascades. Together, these mechanisms maintain a stable, diverse pool where the existing expert configuration for a prior domain remains intact in the latent pool. When the domain shifts back, the pre-existing, well-adapted experts are immediately available and suitable, eliminating the need for costly birth/replacement cycles. The FMA's market dynamics naturally preserve high-fitness experts that are effective across domains or specifically for recurring domains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE) saturation:** Why needed: A full expert pool cannot add new specialists for a new domain without removing old ones. Quick check: Verify all expert slots are occupied before a domain shift and that no spare capacity exists.
- **Cost-penalized fitness:** Why needed: Prevents cheap, high-demand but low-quality experts from dominating; balances quality, demand, and computational cost. Quick check: Confirm fitness `F` decreases when an expert's FLOPs (parameter count) increase, all else being equal.
- **Exclusivity (E) as a margin term:** Why needed: Encourages specialization by rewarding experts that are the unambiguous top choice for their routed tokens. Quick check: Calculate `E` as the difference between the routed token's top expert score and the runner-up; higher `E` should increase `F`.
- **Linear grace period for newborns:** Why needed: Protects new experts from being killed by temporary bad luck during their initial evaluation window. Quick check: For an expert born at step *t*, its `F_eff` should scale linearly from 0 to 1 between steps *t* and *t+50*.
- **Replacement threshold (`mean(F) - 1σ`):** Why needed: Dynamically identifies statistically significant underperformers without a fixed cutoff, adapting to the current fitness distribution. Quick check: Compute mean and standard deviation of `F` across all experts; only experts with `F` clearly below the mean should be candidates.
- **Domain shift recovery metric:** Why needed: Measures the system's ability to leverage past learning ("memory") versus relearning from scratch. Quick check: After shifting back to Domain A, record steps until validation loss drops below the pre-first-shift level.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Input Data -> Router (per layer) -> Expert Pool (16 total experts) -> Loss Calculation -> FMA Monitor (tracks Q, D, E, FLOPs) -> Fitness Computation (with grace) -> Replacement Decision (if `F_worst < mean(F)-1σ`) -> Spawn New Expert (random init)

**Critical Path:** The evaluation loop (every 10 steps) reads per-expert statistics (tokens routed, loss on those tokens, FLOPs), computes the cost-penalized fitness, applies the grace period scaling, then decides on replacement. This loop is the sole mechanism for pool evolution.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Cost penalty term: Heavier penalty (higher exponent on FLOPs) saves compute but may discard robust, slightly more expensive experts.
- Grace period length: Longer (e.g., 100 steps) provides more stability but slows down pool adaptation to persistent new domains.
- Exclusivity weighting: Including `E` (Mode C) promotes specialization and stability but may reduce flexibility for ambiguous tokens.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **No fitness disparity:** σ(F) ≈ 0 for many steps → pool homogenizes, no replacements ever occur, adaptation stalls.
- **Runaway replacement cascade:** Many births/deaths in short span → newborns die within grace period due to aggressive threshold or insufficient grace.
- **Frozen pool on return:** Experts still being replaced when returning to original domain → grace period misapplied or fitness formula fails to preserve old-domain experts.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Baseline recovery from scratch:** Train nanoFMT on `data_char` to convergence, shift to `data_code` to convergence, then shift back to `data_char` with a fresh, empty expert pool. Measure steps to recover to original `data_char` loss. This establishes the ~1150–1350 step baseline.
2. **Grace period ablation:** Run Mode C but disable the linear grace period (`F_eff = F` for all). Expect increased churn and slower recovery on the return shift, confirming the grace period's role in "memory."
3. **Fitness disparity tracking:** Log mean and standard deviation of `F` for all experts every evaluation step during the saturated regime (post-first-shift, pre-return). A successful Mode C run should show persistent, non-zero σ(F) without collapse.

## Limitations
- **Dataset ambiguity:** Unspecified sources, preprocessing, and tokenization for `data_char` and `data_code` critically impact domain shift difficulty and absolute recovery metrics.
- **FMA mechanism opacity:** The Free-Market Algorithm orchestrates 18 mechanisms, but only fitness, birth/death, and grace are described; the role of the other 14 during experiments is unknown.
- **Extrapolation uncertainty:** The projected cost/energy savings are based on a tiny 8-expert-per-layer model and ignore architectural, communication, and serving constraints of real-world trillion-parameter models.

## Confidence
- **Molecular memory phenomenon (zero churn, fast recovery):** Medium-High. Within-paper results are specific, but confidence is reduced by unknown datasets and potential hidden FMA mechanisms.
- **Cost-penalized fitness mechanism (F = Q×D×E / FLOPs):** Medium. Formula is explicit, but precise computation of `E` and interaction with unspecified FMA mechanisms leave implementation variance.
- **Scalability extrapolation ($39.1M savings):** Low. A suggestive estimate from a toy model to massive scale, not a validated projection.

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset & baseline replication:** Train the nanoFMT architecture from scratch on standard, named character-level (e.g., WikiText-2) and code (e.g., The Stack subset) corpora. Measure the steps to reach pre-shift loss after a domain shift to validate the baseline difficulty.
2. **Grace period ablation on return phase:** Re-run Mode C but disable the grace period specifically during the return-to-original-domain phase. Confirm that re-introducing expert births/churn proves the grace period's causal role in "memory."
3. **Fitness disparity diagnostic:** During the saturated regime (after first shift), log σ(F) and range (max-min) of cost-penalized fitness per evaluation. A stable configuration should show persistent, non-zero disparity without collapse to homogeneous fitness.
