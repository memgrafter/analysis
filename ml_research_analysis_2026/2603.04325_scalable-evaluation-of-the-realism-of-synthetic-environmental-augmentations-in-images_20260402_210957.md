# Scalable Evaluation of the Realism of Synthetic Environmental Augmentations in Images

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04325
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04325
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A dual-modality framework (VLM jury + embedding analysis) shows generative AI image-editing models substantially outperform traditional rule-based libraries for simulating adverse driving conditions, with the best generative method achieving a 3.6× higher perceptual realism acceptance rate.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a scalable, automated framework for evaluating the realism of synthetic environmental augmentations (e.g., adding fog, rain) used to test safety-critical AI systems like autonomous vehicles. Addressing the lack of scalable evaluation methods, the framework employs two complementary automated metrics: a multi-model vision-language model (VLM) jury that judges perceptual realism and semantic preservation via binary decisions, and an embedding-based distributional analysis that measures statistical similarity to real adverse-condition images using a relative Mahalanobis distance. Applied to car-mounted camera data from the ACDC dataset, the evaluation reveals that modern generative AI editing models (e.g., Qwen, GPT-Image-1) far surpass traditional rule-based libraries (imgaug, albumentations) in perceived realism. Performance varies by environmental condition, with fog being easiest to simulate and nighttime remaining challenging. Notably, real adverse-condition images themselves do not achieve perfect realism scores, setting imperfect baselines that top generative methods meet or exceed for most conditions.

## Method Summary
The core method is a dual-modality realism assessment pipeline. First, a set of clear-day driving images is augmented using either rule-based image transformation libraries or generative AI image-editing models with text prompts specifying the target condition (e.g., "heavy rain"). Second, realism is evaluated via two independent metrics: (1) a **VLM jury** consisting of three large VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro) that做出 binary accept/reject decisions on image pairs (original vs. augmented) based on a prompt assessing both perceptual realism and semantic fidelity; and (2) **embedding-based distributional analysis**, where image embeddings from CLIP and DINOv3 models are used to compute a *relative Mahalanobis distance*. This distance compares an augmented image's embedding to a multivariate Gaussian fitted on real images of the target condition, while subtracting its distance to a background distribution from all adverse-condition images. This relative formulation isolates the specific environmental effect from shared scene semantics. The two metrics provide convergent evidence, with divergence (e.g., for nighttime) signaling cases needing scrutiny.

## Key Results
- Generative AI editing models (Qwen, OpenAI, Flux) substantially outperform rule-based libraries (imgaug, albumentations), with Qwen achieving a 3.6× higher VLM jury acceptance rate than the best rule-based method.
- Performance is condition-dependent: fog is the easiest to simulate realistically, while nighttime conditions remain significantly challenging for all methods.
- Real adverse-condition images set imperfect empirical ceilings (e.g., 0.933 VLM acceptance for rain), and top generative methods match or exceed these baselines for fog, rain, and snow, suggesting current generative editing can produce sufficiently realistic augmentations for scalable evaluation.
- A fundamental trade-off is identified: rule-based methods fail almost exclusively due to unrealistic appearance (∼97.5%) but preserve semantics perfectly, while top generative methods fail predominantly due to semantic alterations (66-74%), highlighting competing objectives in environmental simulation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dual-modality evaluation (VLM jury + embedding analysis) enables scalable, automated realism assessment without requiring human annotation at every iteration.
- **Mechanism:** The framework uses two independent metrics capturing complementary aspects. The multi-model VLM jury assesses perceptual realism and semantic preservation via binary decisions, mimicking human judgment. Embedding-based distributional analysis measures statistical similarity to real adverse-condition images using relative Mahalanobis distance, providing a consistent, automated signal. Convergence between these modalities builds confidence in comparative rankings, while divergence signals edge cases needing further scrutiny.
- **Core assumption:** Neither metric alone is sufficient; their agreement indicates robust realism assessment.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the use of two complementary automated metrics. Section 3 details the VLM jury binary protocol and the relative Mahalanobis formulation for near-OOD scenarios. The corpus shows this combination is unique for domain-specific safety-critical assessment.
- **Break condition:** When metrics diverge (e.g., nighttime augmentations), VLM judgments align better with intuitive realism per qualitative analysis, suggesting embedding metrics may miss perceptual criteria like scene visibility.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Relative Mahalanobis distance effectively isolates condition-specific visual features from shared background semantics in augmented images.
- **Mechanism:** For an augmented image targeting condition *k*, the method computes standard Mahalanobis distance *d<sub>k</sub>(x)* to the condition distribution, then subtracts distance *d<sub>0</sub>(x)* to a class-agnostic background distribution fitted from all adverse-condition images. The relative formulation *d<sub>rel</sub> = d<sub>k</sub>(x) - d<sub>0</sub>(x)* suppresses contributions from features common to all adverse conditions (e.g., roads, vehicles), amplifying signal from the specific environmental effect.
- **Core assumption:** Augmented and real adverse-condition images share substantial background semantics; standard Mahalanobis would be dominated by these invariant features.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4.3 justifies the relative formulation for "near-OOD scenarios" where augmented images retain original scene structure. Appendix B shows relative Mahalanobis outperforms standard distance.
- **Break condition:** For conditions requiring global scene changes (nighttime), even relative distances remain far from baseline (65–110×), suggesting embedding spaces may overemphasize low-level statistics that cannot capture contextual lighting appropriateness.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Rule-based and generative augmentation paradigms exhibit inverse failure modes due to a fundamental trade-off between semantic preservation and visual realism.
- **Mechanism:** Rule-based libraries apply deterministic transforms that perfectly preserve original scene content but produce artificial effects (e.g., uniform haze). Generative AI models achieve visually convincing effects but risk semantically altering scene elements (e.g., removing vehicles). The paper quantifies this via failure mode analysis of VLM rejection reasons.
- **Core assumption:** In environmental augmentation, perfect semantic fidelity and perfect visual realism are partially competing objectives—methods that modify pixels freely can break semantics, while rigid transforms cannot replicate complex physical phenomena.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.6 states rule-based failures are ∼97.5% realism-only, while generative failures are 66-74% semantic-only. Table 14 shows the failure type distribution.
- **Break condition:** This trade-off may shift with future generative models; some methods (e.g., Flux) show mixed patterns, suggesting the dichotomy is paradigm-dependent but not absolute.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Vision-Language Model (VLM) judging**
  - **Why needed here:** The framework relies on VLMs as automated perceptual judges to scale evaluation; users must understand their capabilities (aligning with human judgments in related tasks) and limitations (moderate inter-judge agreement, calibration issues).
  - **Quick check:** What are two documented biases in LLM/VLM scoring that make binary decisions preferable to Likert scales in this context?

- **Concept: Embedding-based distributional analysis**
  - **Why needed here:** The second evaluation modality uses pre-trained vision encoders (CLIP, DINOv3) to measure statistical similarity; practitioners must grasp why relative Mahalanobis distance is suited for near-OOD detection and how embedding choice affects sensitivity.
  - **Quick check:** Why does standard Mahalanobis distance fail for augmented images, and how does the relative formulation address this?

- **Concept: Simulation-to-reality (sim2real) gap**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire motivation is bridging the gap between synthetic test data and real-world deployment conditions; this concept underpins why realism evaluation is necessary and why baselines from real adverse-condition images are imperfect ceilings.
  - **Quick check:** Why do real adverse-condition images not achieve 100% VLM acceptance, and how does this affect interpretation of synthetic augmentation scores?

- **Concept: Near-out-of-distribution (near-OOD) detection**
  - **Why needed here:** The augmentation task creates images that are semantically in-distribution (same scenes) but visually shifted (adverse weather). The relative distance method is designed for this specific OOD scenario.
  - **Quick check:** How does subtracting a background distribution distance help in near-OOD detection compared to standard OOD methods?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Clear-day ACDC images -> Augmentation Engine (rule-based libraries OR generative AI models) -> Dual Evaluation Pipeline: (1) VLM Jury (ensemble of 3 VLMs) with binary accept/reject per image pair; (2) Embedding Extractor (CLIP/DINOv3) -> Relative Mahalanobis Distance computation against condition-specific reference distributions -> Aggregation (acceptance rates, distance metrics) -> Failure Mode Classification (LLM judges on VLM rejection texts).

**Critical path:** 1) Select 40 clear images from ACDC. 2) Apply each augmentation method to generate 160 synthetic images (4 conditions × 40). 3) Run VLM jury evaluation (480 judgments total). 4) Extract embeddings and compute relative distances. 5) Compare to real adverse-condition baselines (40 images per condition). 6) Conduct failure mode analysis on rejected samples.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- VLM jury (perceptually aligned but variable inter-judge agreement, moderate κ) vs. embedding analysis (consistent but may miss perceptual nuances like scene visibility).
- Binary decisions (robust to judge calibration) vs. scalar scores (more information but prone to bias).
- Multi-judge aggregation (wisdom of crowds) vs. single-judge efficiency.
- Prompt engineering for generative models (free-form natural language) vs. rule-based hyperparameter tuning.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Low VLM acceptance + high embedding distance = clearly poor realism across both modalities.
2. High VLM acceptance + high embedding distance = perceptual success despite distribution shift, common in nighttime (embedding limitations).
3. Rule-based methods: near-zero semantic failure rate but very low realism acceptance.
4. Generative methods: non-zero semantic failure rates with higher realism acceptance.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline calibration: Evaluate real adverse-condition images with VLM jury to establish empirical ceilings and check inter-judge agreement.
2. Main comparison: Run all 6 methods on 40 clear images across 4 conditions, collect VLM jury decisions and embedding distances, compute bootstrap confidence intervals.
3. Failure analysis: For all rejected augmentations, use LLM judges to classify rejection reasons (semantic vs. realism), following the two-stage protocol in Appendix C.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How well do the proposed automated realism metrics (VLM jury and embedding-based analysis) correlate with human judgments of augmentation realism? The current study relies solely on automated evaluation; human perception is the ultimate ground truth for realism but was not measured.
2. How reliable and controllable are generative image editing models in consistently producing desired environmental conditions across diverse input scenes? The evaluation focuses on realism but not on whether the augmentation can be precisely controlled and reproduced, which is critical for systematic evaluation pipelines.
3. Do the reported performance differences between rule-based and generative methods generalize to other autonomous driving datasets, sensors, and geographic regions? Generalizability is limited; results might be dataset-specific rather than broadly applicable.
4. Why does embedding-based distributional analysis diverge from VLM jury acceptance for nighttime augmentations, and how can embedding metrics be improved for such conditions? The discrepancy indicates embedding models may be insensitive to contextual lighting appropriateness.

## Limitations
- The evaluation uses a single dataset (ACDC) with 40 test images; performance may not transfer to other sensor modalities, geographies, or rarer conditions.
- Exact generative model versions/API parameters and full embedding distribution fitting details (e.g., covariance regularization) are unspecified, risking implementation drift.
- The VLM jury's moderate inter-annotator agreement (κ) and the embedding metric's known failure on global scene changes (nighttime) limit generalizability. Real adverse-condition image baselines are imperfect (e.g., rain acceptance 0.933), so synthetic scores are relative to noisy ceilings.

## Confidence
- **High:** The dual-modality framework (VLM jury + relative Mahalanobis) is well-specified, with clear evidence for its design rationale and convergence checks. The semantic-realism trade-off is consistently observed across methods.
- **Medium:** The claim that generative methods substantially outperform rule-based ones is supported by reported metrics but depends on the specific models tested and the ACDC conditions; replication with updated models could shift rankings.
- **Medium-Low:** Condition-specific performance rankings (e.g., "fog is easiest") are based on limited samples (n=40/condition) and embedding limitations for night; confidence is moderated by the break condition where embedding distances diverge from VLM judgments.

## Next Checks
1. **Baseline Imperfection Audit:** Re-evaluate the 120 real adverse-condition images with a fresh VLM jury composition to confirm baseline acceptance rates are stable and not judge-specific artifacts.
2. **Embedding Metric Stress Test:** For nighttime augmentations, compute distances using alternative feature spaces (e.g., RGB histograms, self-supervised models like MAE) and correlate with human evaluation to quantify the perceptual gap.
3. **Cross-Dataset Failure Mode Replication:** Apply the same augmentation pipeline and evaluation to a different driving dataset (e.g., nuScenes) to verify the semantic-realism trade-off persists across data distributions.
