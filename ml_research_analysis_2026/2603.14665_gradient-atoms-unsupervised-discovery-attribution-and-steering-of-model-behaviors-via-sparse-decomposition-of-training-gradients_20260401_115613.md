# Gradient Atoms: Unsupervised Discovery, Attribution and Steering of Model Behaviors via Sparse Decomposition of Training Gradients

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14665  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14665  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Unsupervised decomposition of training gradients into sparse "atoms" discovers interpretable task-specific directions that effectively steer model behaviors without per-query attribution.

## Executive Summary
Gradient Atoms introduces an unsupervised framework to identify shared update directions from training gradients. By projecting per-document gradients into an approximately isotropic space via EKFAC and applying sparse dictionary learning, the method extracts "atoms"—basis vectors each representing a cluster of functionally similar documents. High-coherence atoms correspond to coherent task types (e.g., refusal, arithmetic) without any behavioral labels. These atoms can be used as weight-space perturbations to significantly steer model behavior, as demonstrated by large increases in formatting adherence and near-elimination of systematic refusal. The approach eliminates the costly per-query scoring bottleneck of standard training data attribution.

## Method Summary
Given a fine-tuned model, per-document gradients are computed for all training examples. These gradients are projected and rescaled module-wise using EKFAC eigendecomposition to approximate Fisher information, yielding an approximately isotropic space where directions are comparable. Sparse dictionary learning (ℓ₁-penalized) decomposes the projected gradients into a set of atoms; each document's gradient is a sparse combination of a few atoms, ensuring each atom captures a shared pattern. Coherence—average pairwise cosine similarity of raw gradients among atom activators—validates that atoms reflect genuine functional motifs. For steering, a selected atom is unprojected to a full-parameter vector and added/subtracted to the model's weights.

## Key Results
- High-coherence atoms (coherence >0.5) align with interpretable task types (refusal, arithmetic, yes/no classification, trivia QA) without supervision.
- A bulleted-list atom increased formatting adherence from 33% to 94% (+61 percentage points).
- A refusal atom reduced systematic refusal from 50% to 0% (‑50 percentage points).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** EKFAC preconditioning normalizes curvature differences across weight directions by projecting onto eigenvectors and scaling by inverse eigenvalue square roots. This makes the gradient space approximately isotropic, so cosine similarity in the projected space meaningfully reflects functional similarity rather than curvature artifacts.

**Mechanism 2:** Sparse dictionary learning with an ℓ₁ penalty forces each atom to be activated by multiple documents and each document to use few atoms. This encourages atoms to represent single coherent patterns shared across functionally similar documents, rather than blended mixtures.

**Mechanism 3:** Coherence—computed on raw (unprojected) gradients for an atom’s top activators—confirms that the atom captures a real, high-variance computational motif in the original weight space, not an artifact of the projection.

## Foundational Learning
- **EKFAC (Eigenvalue-corrected Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature):** Provides a tractable Fisher approximation for preconditioning. Needed to handle anisotropic gradients; otherwise, decomposition would be dominated by high-curvature directions.  
  *Quick check:* Why is scaling by eigenvalues (√(λ+ε)) after projection necessary?  

- **Sparse Dictionary Learning:** Finds a basis (atoms) such that inputs are sparse linear combinations. Enforces that each atom explains multiple documents, revealing shared patterns.  
  *Quick check:* How does an ℓ₁ penalty on coefficients lead to atoms that capture single, coherent patterns instead of mixtures?  

- **Gradient-based Training Data Attribution (TDA):** Attributes model outputs to training examples, typically via per-document, query-dependent scoring. Gradient Atoms avoids this bottleneck by discovering shared directions in a single pass.  
  *Quick check:* What is the fundamental difference between per-document attribution and the shared update direction approach?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Gradient Extraction -> EKFAC Preconditioning -> Dictionary Learning -> Coherence Scoring -> Steering  
Critical path: The above sequence; coherence is offline validation, steering uses the same dictionary.  
Design tradeoffs: Projection dimensionality (k) trades off between information preservation and computational cost; sparsity penalty (α) controls atom specificity; coherence does not guarantee steerability without saturation or side effects.  
Failure signatures: Uniformly low coherence (<0.1) indicates poor decomposition; non-monotonic or ineffective steering suggests over-perturbation or fragile atoms; absence of interpretable high-coherence atoms may reflect data without clear functional clusters.  
First 3 experiments:  
1. Replicate the atom discovery pipeline (K=500, α=0.1) and plot coherence distribution; expect a long tail with some atoms >0.5.  
2. Manually inspect activating documents (top-20) for the top-5 atoms by coherence to confirm coherent task types.  
3. For a low-coherence atom (e.g., coherence ~0.1), apply perturbations with α ∈ {0.5,1,2,5,10} (both signs) and measure steering effect; expect monotonic change on target behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can multiple gradient atoms be combined to simultaneously steer multiple behaviors without mutual interference or degradation?  
2. How does scaling the number of atoms (e.g., to 1,000+) affect the proportion of high-coherence, interpretable, and steerable atoms discovered?  
3. Are gradient atoms transferable across different model architectures or fine-tuning datasets, or are they specific to a particular model and fine-tuning run?  
4. How can we determine the optimal perturbation magnitude (alpha) for steering with a given atom in a principled way, balancing effectiveness and model integrity?

## Limitations
- Exact 5k dataset mixture, evaluation prompts, and regex detectors are undisclosed, hindering precise replication of behavior metrics.  
- EKFAC hyperparameters (eigenvectors per module, ε, Fisher batch size) are underspecified; these critically affect projection quality and atom stability.  
- Coherence threshold (>0.5) appears arbitrary and may not generalize across random seeds, model scales, or data distributions.

## Confidence
- EKFAC isotropization: Low (theoretical, no direct measurement of isotropy).  
- Sparse dictionary learning yields shared atoms: Medium (standard technique but relies on correct preconditioning and α).  
- Coherence identifies interpretable task types: Medium (strong correlation for top atoms, but manual labeling may be biased).  
- Atoms steer model behaviors: Medium (large effects on two tasks shown, but limited breadth and side-effect analysis).

## Next Checks
1. Ablate EKFAC parameters (k ∈ {20,50,100}, ε, dictionary α ∈ {0.05,0.1,0.2}) and measure coherence distribution and steering magnitude to test robustness.  
2. Replicate the entire pipeline on a different fine-tuning corpus (e.g., code-focused) and a larger model (e.g., Llama-3 8B) to evaluate generality.  
3. Perform comprehensive side-effect profiling: evaluate steered models on MMLU and a diverse set of neutral prompts to quantify collateral damage and atom independence.
