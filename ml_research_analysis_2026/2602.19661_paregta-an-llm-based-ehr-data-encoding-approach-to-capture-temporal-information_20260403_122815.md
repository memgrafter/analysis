# PaReGTA: An LLM-based EHR Data Encoding Approach to Capture Temporal Information

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19661
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19661
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PaReGTA achieved up to 92.39% accuracy and 0.9524 AUC on chronic migraine prediction, substantially outperforming sparse baselines.

## Executive Summary
PaReGTA is a method for encoding longitudinal electronic health record (EHR) data that preserves temporal dynamics by converting patient visits into templated natural language sentences with explicit time cues (e.g., inter-visit gaps). These visit-level texts are processed by a fine-tuned sentence-embedding model, and the resulting visit vectors are aggregated into a fixed-dimensional patient representation via a hybrid temporal pooling mechanism. When evaluated on a cohort of 39,088 migraine patients, the approach delivered high predictive performance (AUC 0.9524) with LightGBM and provided clinically plausible factor importance rankings via its PaRégta-RSS extension.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by filtering raw EHR data to a target cohort and extracting records around diagnosis encounters. Each visit's clinical codes (medications, comorbidities) are converted into a short, templated sentence. Crucially, relative temporal information—the number of days since the previous visit—is injected as a textual token. A pre-trained sentence-embedding model (GTE-base-v1.5) is then lightly fine-tuned using unsupervised SimCSE contrastive learning on these visit texts to adapt it to the clinical domain. Each visit is encoded into a vector. Finally, a hybrid temporal pooling layer combines time-decay and attention-based weights to aggregate the variable-length sequence of visit embeddings into a single patient vector, which is used to train a standard classifier (e.g., LightGBM, Logistic Regression). For interpretability, the PaReGTA-RSS method estimates factor importance by measuring the change in the patient representation (and resulting model output) when a specific clinical factor is removed from all visit texts.

## Key Results
- PaReGTA (LightGBM, Gap tokenization) achieved 92.39% accuracy and 0.9524 AUC,大幅 outperforming one-hot (AUC 0.7634) and count Bag-of-Codes (AUC 0.8354) baselines.
- Performance remained robust even when using raw, non-standardized medication names and in settings where deep sequential models were unstable.
- The PaReGTA-RSS analysis identified botulinum toxin and CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide) therapies as top contributors for chronic migraine prediction, aligning with clinical knowledge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Visit-level textualization with explicit temporal cues preserves sequential EHR information that sparse representations discard.
- **Mechanism:** Longitudinal records are split by encounter and formatted into templated sentences (e.g., "62 days after previous, meds: ..."). This maintains visit boundaries and embeds relative temporal information (inter-visit gap) directly into the text. The sentence-embedding model then processes each visit as a semantic unit, allowing temporal dynamics to be captured in the embedding space.
- **Core assumption:** The relative time gap is a clinically meaningful proxy for recency/progression, and the LLM can map this token to a meaningful embedding direction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "converts longitudinal EHR events into visit-level templated text with explicit temporal cues"; [section 2.2.2] details textualization; related work (e.g., CEHR-BERT) supports inserting time tokens.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM fails to distinguish temporal tokens in its embedding space (e.g., "214 days" vs. "7 months"), the signal is lost.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Lightweight SimCSE fine-tuning adapts a general sentence-embedding model to cohort-specific clinical semantics without requiring labeled pairs.
- **Mechanism:** Unsupervised SimCSE creates positive pairs by applying dropout noise to the same visit text and uses in-batch negatives. This contrastive objective pulls embeddings of semantically identical clinical narratives closer and pushes unrelated ones apart, aligning the embedding space with the target cohort's distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity is preserved under dropout perturbations, and in-batch negatives suffice for domain adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "learns domain-adapted visit embeddings via lightweight contrastive fine-tuning"; [section 2.2.3] details SimCSE; [section 3.4, Table 6] shows improved uniformity/isotropy metrics.
- **Break condition:** If the pretrained model is already perfectly aligned or the cohort vocabulary is too divergent, fine-tuning may not help or could cause forgetting.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Hybrid temporal pooling aggregates visit embeddings into a patient representation that balances recency effects with attention to globally informative visits.
- **Mechanism:** Two weighting schemes are combined: (i) a time-decay factor exponentially down-weights older visits, and (ii) an attention mechanism up-weights visits similar to the global context (mean embedding). A convex combination (α) of these weights is applied to the visit embeddings before summation and L2-normalization.
- **Core assumption:** A weighted sum can compress a variable-length sequence into a fixed-dimension vector retaining predictive temporal structure, where both recent events and atypical but important history contribute.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "aggregating visit embeddings ... using hybrid temporal pooling"; [section 2.2.5] provides formulation; [section 3.7, Table 7] ablation shows complementary benefit.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal α is poorly tuned, pooling may overemphasize recency (losing long-term patterns) or global attention (losing recent urgency), harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: SimCSE Contrastive Learning**
  - **Why needed:** To adapt a general-purpose sentence encoder (GTE-base-v1.5) to clinical visit notes without costly supervised annotation.
  - **Quick check:** How does dropout noise on the same input create a valid positive pair for contrastive learning, and why is this unsupervised?
- **Concept: Hierarchical Representation Aggregation**
  - **Why needed:** EHRs have a natural hierarchy (patient → visits → events). Encoding visits first and then pooling respects this structure and allows temporal modulation at the aggregation stage, rather than force a single-pass encoding of a long, concatenated document.
  - **Quick check:** What is the primary risk of encoding a patient's entire longitudinal record as one long text instead of using a visit-level hierarchy?
- **Concept: Representation-Based Interpretability (PaReGTA-RSS)**
  - **Why needed:** Standard feature importance (e.g., SHAP) is prohibitively expensive over the text→embedding→pooling pipeline. RSS estimates factor importance by measuring the shift in the patient representation when a factor is removed, then projecting that shift through the downstream model's linear coefficients (logistic regression).
  - **Quick check:** Why is logistic regression particularly suitable as the downstream model for computing RSS scores?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Curator -> Textualizer -> LLM Encoder (GTE-base-v1.5) -> Temporal Pooler -> Downstream Classifier -> RSS Calculator.
- **Critical path:** Cohort construction -> Visit textualization (with temporal token choice) -> SimCSE fine-tuning (on sampled visit texts) -> Encode all visits -> Train pooling weights (or use fixed γ/τ) -> Generate patient vectors -> Train downstream model -> (Optional) Run RSS.
  - *Key dependency:* SimCSE fine-tuning must use a representative sample of the final visit-text distribution. The pooling module can be trained via a triplet loss or used with fixed hyperparameters.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Temporal token granularity:** "Gap" (days) outperforms "Month" (lossy) and "Last" (disrupts sequence). But very large gap values may be poorly understood; consider capping/bucketing.
  - **Medication encoding:** Raw product names leverage LLM semantics but increase sparsity. Standardized concepts (e.g., RxNorm) are cleaner but lose brand-specific signals.
  - **Pooling complexity:** Hybrid pooling adds 3 hyperparameters (γ, τ, α). Simpler time-weighted-only pooling is more robust but may miss globally salient visits.
  - **Computational cost:** SimCSE fine-tuning is lightweight. Main cost is encoding all visit texts with the LLM.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **SimCSE fails:** No improvement in uniformity/isotropy metrics or downstream AUC over "Visit-text" baseline. Check: training sample diversity, learning rate, convergence.
  - **Pooling failure:** Performance degrades vs. simple mean pooling. Check: α tuning, time-decay γ (too high loses all but last visit), attention temperature τ.
  - **Conceptual mismatch:** Using "Last" tokenization leads to severely low AUC (~0.75). Indicates model cannot learn from disrupted sequential structure.
  - **RSS instability:** Factor importance magnitudes near-zero or noisy. Check: factor prevalence threshold, LR regularization (C), whether factor influences task.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Temporal Tokenization Ablation:** Train PaReGTA with Date, Gap, Month, Last, and Without temporal tokens. Use same train/test split and LightGBM. Expected: Gap > Date > Month > Without >> Last in AUC.
  2. **SimCSE Verification:** Encode held-out visit texts with (a) original GTE, (b) GTE fine-tuned on in-domain texts, (c) GTE fine-tuned on random texts. Compute pairwise cosine similarity for semantically identical visits. Expected: (b) shows higher similarity for same-visit pairs than (a) and (c). Also check uniformity/isotropy metrics.
  3. **RSS Sanity Check - Known Factor:** Compute RSS for "botulinum toxin" on the full cohort. Expected: High positive importance for chronic migraine prediction. If near zero, factor may not be captured or LR not using relevant dimensions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can PaReGTA-RSS be extended to provide causal interpretations rather than purely predictive relevance? The current method measures predictive importance but does not imply causality and may be confounded.
- **Open Question 2:** How does PaReGTA compare to state-of-the-art deep sequential EHR models on large-scale, multi-institutional datasets? Deep models were unstable in the authors' cohort due to sparsity; the trade-off remains unclear.
- **Open Question 3:** How can PaReGTA be adapted to prevent temporal data leakage for prospective predictions? Current evaluation uses all records up to the last visit, potentially including post-outcome events, leading to optimistic performance.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the most effective architecture for combining PaReGTA's semantic visit embeddings with explicit temporal dynamics from sequence models (e.g., attention over time, recurrence)? The current hybrid pooling is simple; more expressive sequence models might capture irregular intervals better.
- **Open Question 5:** To what extent does the choice of pre-trained sentence-embedding model affect performance, and would EHR-specialized encoders lead to significant improvements? The authors used one general model (GTE-base-v1.5) and did not compare alternatives.

## Limitations
- **Replication hurdles:** Exact AoU cohort SQL, raw medication-to-class mapping for RSS, and trained pooling hyperparameters are not publicly released.
- **Interpretability methodology:** PaReGTA-RSS relies on a linear probe (LR) and additive factor removal; it may miss nonlinear interactions between factors in the embedding space or with a non-linear downstream model.
- **Generalizability:** Results are shown only on migraine (39k patients, AoU). Performance on smaller cohorts, different diseases, or other EHR sources (e.g., MIMIC) is unknown; gap tokenization effectiveness may vary with visit frequency.

## Confidence
- **Main predictive performance (AUC ~0.95):** High. Supported by large-scale test results and multiple classifier comparisons.
- **Temporal textualization mechanism:** Medium. Ablations show Gap tokenization is best, but precise linguistic grounding of gap tokens in the LLM's embedding space is not validated.
- **SimCSE adaptation benefit:** Medium. Uniformity/isotropy metrics improve, but direct causal link to downstream AUC gain vs. other fine-tuning strategies is not isolated.
- **RSS factor importance validity:** Low. Lacks clinical validation against known guidelines; prone to confounding if factors co-occur with other predictive signals.

## Next Checks
1. **Temporal token grounding test:** Compute cosine similarity between embeddings of "X days after previous" for varying X (e.g., 30 vs. 365). Expect monotonic decrease with gap.
2. **SimCSE positive-pair sanity check:** On a held-out set, measure embedding similarity for (a) two dropout-augmented versions of the same visit text vs. (b) two semantically similar but distinct visits. Expect (a) >> (b) after fine-tuning.
3. **RSS clinical sanity check for botulinum toxin:** Retrieve all patients with onabotulinumtoxinA mentions. Compute their average RSS score vs. patients without. Expect a large positive shift for chronic migraine prediction.
