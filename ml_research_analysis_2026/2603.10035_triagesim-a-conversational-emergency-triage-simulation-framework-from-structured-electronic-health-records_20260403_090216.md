# TriageSim: A Conversational Emergency Triage Simulation Framework from Structured Electronic Health Records
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10035
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10035
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Synthetic triage dialogues achieve weighted Cohen's κ 0.23–0.33 on acuity classification, indicating clinical reasoning is the main bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
TriageSim addresses the scarcity of conversational emergency triage data due to regulatory and privacy constraints by generating persona-conditioned dialogues from structured Electronic Health Records (EHRs). It employs a multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) framework where a central dialogue master orchestrates a nurse agent (following explicit triage algorithms) and a patient agent (driven by persona traits such as disfluency and verbosity). The system produces 814 synthetic conversations with synchronized audio, incorporating acoustic elements like controlled disfluency, zero-shot voice cloning with accent conditioning, and ambient noise. Evaluation demonstrates strong linguistic fidelity (disfluency control ρ=0.57), acoustic stability (WER 10.8, speaker consistency 99.98%), and medical coherence (expert-rated complaint similarity 0.83, red flag F1 0.94). However, triage classification agreement with ground truth remains modest (weighted Cohen's κ 0.23–0.33) across text, ASR, and audio modalities, suggesting that clinical reasoning from conversational data is the primary bottleneck rather than transcription noise.

## Method Summary
The framework transforms a structured EHR case (symptoms, vitals, true acuity) into a multi-turn dialogue through a three-stage pipeline: (1) A persona generator creates attribute sets for the patient (demographics, communication traits) and nurse (experience, guideline adherence). (2) A dialogue master instantiates these agents and mediates the interaction, ensuring case consistency by revealing ground-truth vitals only upon the nurse’s explicit query. The nurse agent reasons under a prompt-embedded triage algorithm (Australasian Triage Scale or Emergency Severity Index), logging decisions and red flags, while the patient agent responds within the constraints of the latent clinical state and persona. (3) The generated transcript is annotated for prosody and passed to a speech synthesizer (Qwen3-TTS) for zero-shot voice cloning conditioned on the patient’s accent/gender, after which an acoustic mixer adds background noise to simulate an emergency department environment. The resulting audio-aligned corpus can be used for training and evaluating triage classification models.

## Key Results
- Corpus scale: 814 persona-conditioned dialogues with aligned audio generated from structured EHRs.
- Linguistic & acoustic fidelity: disfluency control achieved (Spearman ρ=0.57), with stable synthesis (WER 10.8, speaker consistency 99.98%, UTMOS 3.42).
- Medical coherence: expert ratings show high chief complaint similarity (0.83) and red flag identification (F1 0.94).
- Triage classification performance: weighted Cohen's κ ranges 0.23–0.33 across synthetic text, ASR transcripts, and audio, with minimal modality differences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Persona conditioning on patient and nurse attributes induces measurable, controlled linguistic and behavioral variation in generated dialogues.
- **Mechanism:** Separate persona schemas (demographics, communication traits for patients; experience, guideline adherence for nurses) are provided as structured prompts to their respective LLM agents. These attributes modulate token generation (e.g., disfluency rate, verbosity) and decision policies (e.g., vital sign query frequency, triage confidence).
- **Core assumption:** LLM agents can reliably internalize and consistently manifest low-level persona attributes (like a specific disfluency rate) across multi-turn dialogue generation without external supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper reports a monotonic increase in measured disfluency across intended control levels (Spearman ρ=0.57) and shows that nurse experience correlates with triage confidence, while guideline adherence correlates with vital-sign check frequency.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Constraining the nurse agent's reasoning with explicit, prompt-embedded triage algorithms (ATS/ESI) aligns its intermediate decisions and red-flag identification with clinical guidelines, producing medically coherent outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** The nurse agent operates under a structured policy prompt that encodes the stepwise logic, acuity criteria, and red-flag indicators of a specific triage scale. The dialogue master only reveals ground-truth vital signs upon explicit query, forcing the nurse to reason over elicited symptoms. The nurse logs red flags and confidence at each turn.
- **Core assumption:** A text-based policy prompt is sufficient to steer an LLM's chain-of-thought toward guideline-conformant reasoning in a dynamic, multi-turn information-gathering dialogue, rather than defaulting to memorized or heuristic behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:** Expert evaluation on 50 dialogues yields high red-flag F1 (0.94) and chief complaint similarity (0.83), suggesting the nurse's identified issues align with clinical expectations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The multi-agent architecture with a central dialogue master enforces case consistency and turn-by-turn interaction stability, enabling the synthesis of coherent, multi-turn conversations from a single structured EHR snapshot.
- **Mechanism:** The dialogue master holds the complete ground-truth structured case (symptoms, vitals, true acuity). It instantiates the persona-constrained nurse and patient agents, mediates turn sequencing, provides vital signs only when formally queried, and records the nurse's logged decisions. This ensures the patient's symptoms remain consistent with the hidden truth and the nurse's knowledge state evolves based only on elicited information.
- **Core assumption:** A central orchestrator that selectively discloses ground-truth information based on dialogue acts is sufficient to model the asymmetric information dynamics of a real triage encounter.
- **Evidence anchors:** Acoustic stability metrics (speaker consistency 99.98%) indicate the voice cloning produces stable speaker identity across turns, which depends on the transcript sequence being coherent and dialogically stable.

## Foundational Learning
### Structured Electronic Health Record (EHR) Semantics
- **Why needed:** TriageSim's generation is conditioned on a structured seed case (chief complaint, vitals, pain score, true acuity). Understanding how these discrete fields map to the latent clinical state is fundamental to distinguishing ground truth from elicited dialogue content.
- **Quick check:** If the seed case has `chief_complaint="chest pain"` and `vitals.blood_pressure="90/60"`, what key symptom and red flag should the patient mention and the nurse look for?  
  (Answer: The patient should describe crushing chest pain; the nurse should recognize hypotension as a red flag for shock.)

### Triage Scale Algorithmic Logic (ATS/ESI)
- **Why needed:** The nurse agent's behavior is constrained by a prompt-embedded triage algorithm. Triage is a rule-based risk stratification focused on identifying high-acuity "red flags" and assigning an urgency level, not a differential diagnosis.
- **Quick check:** According to ESI, what are the two primary decision points?  
  (Answer: 1) Is the patient immediately life‑threatening? 2) How many resources will likely be needed?)

### Disfluency and Spoken Dialogue Phenomenology
- **Why needed:** A core contribution is the controlled generation of linguistic disfluency (filled pauses, repetitions, repairs) and its impact on ASR. Natural speech is not fluent text, and these phenomena must be understood to evaluate realism.
- **Quick check:** What is the difference between a *filled pause* (e.g., "uh", "um") and a *repetition* (e.g., "I have a a headache") as a disfluency type?  
  (Answer: A filled pause is a non‑leximal sound marking hesitation; a repetition repeats a word or phrase, often indicating reformulation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Seed Data Module → Persona Generator → Multi-Agent Dialogue Simulator (Dialogue Master, Nurse Agent, Patient Agent) → Prosodic Annotator → Speech Synthesizer → Acoustic Mixer → Evaluation Suite

**Critical path:** Seed Structured Case → Persona Generation → Multi-Agent Turn-by-Turn Dialogue (Dialogue Master mediates) → Transcript with Phrase Annotations → Voice Cloning + Noise Mixing → Final Audio Corpus.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Guideline Fidelity vs. Conversational Naturalness: Strict adherence to the triage algorithm may make the nurse sound robotic; the system prioritizes medical fidelity.
- Persona Control Granularity: Controlling many attributes (verbosity, disfluency, recall) increases complexity and risks attribute conflicts (e.g., high disfluency + low language proficiency).
- Acoustic Realism vs. Intelligibility: Adding ambient noise improves ecological validity but raises ASR WER, especially for disfluent/accented speech.

**Failure signatures:**
- Clinical Incoherence: Patient describes symptoms not present in the seed case; nurse misses a clear red flag.
- Dialogue Incoherence: Nurse repeats questions; patient contradicts earlier statements.
- Acoustic Artifacts: Robotic TTS output; obtrusive noise mixing (e.g., siren at 0 dB); inconsistent speaker identity across turns.
- Algorithm Drift: Nurse ignores the triage prompt, asks generic questions, or decides acuity without necessary vital queries.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Isolate Persona Effect:** For a fixed case, vary patient `disfluency_rate` (low/medium/high). Confirm monotonic increase in disfluency patterns and measure WER impact.
2. **Validate Nurse Algorithm Adherence:** For a case with a clear red flag, compare `nurse_guideline_adherence` set to “strict” vs. “lenient”. Scrutinize logs for correct red‑flag identification and vital‑sign queries.
3. **Test Dialogue Master's Information Control:** Parse transcripts to ensure no ground‑truth vital value appears before the nurse’s first explicit query for that parameter.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** To what extent do specific nurse persona attributes (e.g., risk tolerance, guideline adherence) quantitatively influence final triage acuity assignments and error patterns (over-triage vs. under-triage) beyond overall confidence?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper shows behavioral controllability (Figure 3) but correlates attributes with procedural outputs or confidence, not directly with final acuity distributions or error types. The modest κ scores (0.23–0.33) suggest room for error, but the contribution of controllable nurse traits to these errors is unanalyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** The multi-agent simulation allows explicit persona conditioning, but the evaluation aggregates performance across all personas. Disentangling how specific, controllable decision tendencies map to classification errors would validate the framework's utility for training robust models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale ablation study holding clinical cases constant while systematically varying nurse persona attributes (risk tolerance, experience), then measuring resulting shifts in acuity distribution, over/under‑triage rates, and disagreement with ground truth.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the incorporation of accent‑conditioned speech synthesis and disfluency in TriageSim lead to systematic biases in triage classification performance for specific patient demographics or speech profiles?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper notes higher WER for Middle Eastern (19.4) and Indian (16.7) accents and observes that "performance does not degrade with increase in patient disfluency rate (ρ=0.00)". This implies no linear effect of disfluency, but does not test for non‑linear thresholds or interactions between accent and disfluency that might cause bias.
- **Why unresolved:** The fair κ scores (0.23–0.33) are reported pooled across conditions. The potential for acoustic variability to introduce demographic bias—where certain simulated accents or disfluency patterns lead to consistent misclassification—is a critical fairness concern but is not investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of classification error metrics (e.g., per‑accent κ, confusion matrices) stratified by patient persona attributes (accent, disfluency rate). A significant finding would be if specific accent‑disfluency combinations correlate with systematic under‑triage of high‑acuity cases or over‑triage of low‑acuity cases.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do different base LLMs used for the dialogue agents (Gemini, GPT‑5, Claude) produce clinically equivalent conversational patterns and triage decisions, or do they introduce model‑specific artifacts that affect downstream task performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The corpus is generated using three models "selected uniformly at random" (Page 3), but all subsequent evaluations (fidelity, classification) aggregate across them. The paper states the nurse agent "performs triage reasoning constrained by explicit algorithmic guidelines," but does not verify if different LLMs adhere equally to these guidelines.
- **Why unresolved:** The multi‑agent framework's validity depends on consistent behavior from the nurse agent across models. If different LLMs have varying propensities to follow the triage algorithm or generate distinct dialogue styles, the synthetic corpus may contain unquantified heterogeneity that affects any downstream model trained on it.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comparative analysis holding case and persona constant across model‑generated conversations, measuring divergence in (a) nurse decision adherence to the provided algorithm (e.g., rate of correct red‑flag identification, vital sign query sequence), (b) linguistic style, and (c) impact on a fixed triage classifier's performance when trained/tested on data from individual models vs. mixed.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the limited medical fidelity evaluation (single expert, n=50) generalize to the full corpus of 814 conversations and to a broader panel of clinicians with varying experience levels?
- **Basis in paper:** Medical fidelity is evaluated via "a random subset of 50 conversations" assessed by one expert with "5+ years of experience" (Page 4). The high complaint similarity (0.83) and red‑flag F1 (0.94) are promising but based on a small, single‑rater sample.
- **Why unresolved:** Triage is a high‑stakes, subjective process. A single expert's judgment may not capture the full spectrum of clinical reasoning across different training backgrounds or institutions. The paper's conclusion about "clinically coherent" generation lacks inter‑rater reliability data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow‑up study with multiple (n≥5) experts from diverse backgrounds (e.g., Australasian vs. US‑trained, varying years) independently rating a larger, stratified sample (e.g., 200+ conversations). Reporting inter‑rater reliability (e.g., Cohen's κ for red‑flag annotation) and consensus rates would establish generalizability.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Is the modest agreement in conversational triage classification (κ 0.23–0.33) primarily due to inherent ambiguity in the triage task from conversational data, or are there specific linguistic/discourse features of the synthetic conversations that are missing or misrepresented, hindering model performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "our findings... indicate the primary bottleneck lies in clinical reasoning on conversations rather than transcription noise" (Conclusion). They base this on minimal performance differences between synthetic text, ASR, and audio modalities.
- **Why unresolved:** While modality differences are small, the absolute κ values indicate poor‑to‑fair agreement. The conclusion attributes this to task difficulty but does not analyze *what* about the conversations is hard. It could be that synthetic conversations lack critical nuance (e.g., subtle symptom descriptions, non‑verbal cues implied in text) present in real dialogues, making the task intrinsically harder for models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Error analysis of classifier predictions vs. ground truth, identifying frequent patterns of misclassification (e.g., confusing specific acuity levels, systematic failure on certain chief complaints). Comparison with classifier performance on a held‑out set of *real* (if available) triage dialogues would benchmark whether the synthetic data's utility is limited by data fidelity or by the core challenge of the task itself.

## Limitations
- **Persona‑case consistency not evaluated:** Mismatched personas (e.g., low language proficiency for a case with complex terminology) may lead to symptom omissions or incoherent descriptions, degrading medical fidelity.
- **Nurse reasoning logic unverified:** The system does not validate that the nurse agent’s chain‑of‑thought actually follows the provided triage algorithm; it only checks final outputs (red flags, acuity).
- **Dialogue master state management unaudited:** No direct evidence confirms that the orchestrator correctly controls information disclosure (e.g., no premature vital leakage, no symptom contradictions).

## Confidence
- Controlled Persona Variation: Medium Confidence
- Medical Coherence via Guideline-Constrained Nurse Agent: Medium Confidence
- Dialogue Coherence & Information Control: Low Confidence
- Acoustic Realism & Stability: High Confidence

## Next Checks
1. **Persona-Case Consistency Stress Test:** Generate dialogues for high‑acuity cases with conflicting patient personas (e.g., `language_proficiency="low"`, `medical_knowledge="high"`). Quantify symptom omissions, contradictions, or incoherence via human evaluation.
2. **Nurse Reasoning Logic Audit:** Collect the nurse agent’s chain‑of‑thought logs across diverse cases. Have clinical experts annotate each reasoning step against official ATS/ESI criteria. Compute compliance, missing‑criterion, and irrelevant‑factor rates.
3. **Dialogue Master Information Disclosure Audit:** Manually parse 100 transcripts. For each case, identify all mentions of ground‑truth vitals and verify that no vital value appears before the nurse’s first explicit query for that parameter. Flag any premature disclosures.
