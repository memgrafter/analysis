# OasisSimp: An Open-source Asian-English Sentence Simplification Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14111
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14111
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Introduces the first sentence simplification datasets for Thai, Pashto, and Tamil; reveals a large zero-shot performance gap between English (SARI >40) and low-resource languages (SARI 29–36), with few-shot prompting improving scores by up to 10 points.

## Executive Summary
OasisSimp addresses the critical lack of sentence simplification data for low-resource Asian languages by providing a human-annotated multilingual dataset for English, Sinhala, Tamil, Thai, and Pashto—the first of its kind for Thai, Pashto, and Tamil. The study evaluates eight open-weight large language models (LLMs) on this benchmark using SARI and BERTScore. Results show a stark disparity: high-resource English consistently exceeds 40 SARI, while low-resource languages range from 29 to 36 in zero-shot settings. Gemma demonstrates the strongest cross-lingual transfer. Few-shot prompting (5-shot) yields improvements of up to 10 SARI points, particularly for Gemma and Qwen, highlighting the effectiveness of in-context learning but underscoring persistent challenges in low-resource multilingual simplification.

## Method Summary
The OasisSimp dataset was constructed by sourcing complex sentences from language-specific corpora (e.g., government documents for Sinhala and Tamil, Wikipedia for Pashto, news for English and Thai). Trained annotators, following detailed guidelines, produced approximately three simplified references per complex sentence, with rigorous quality verification. The data was split into 80% test and 20% validation. For evaluation, eight LLMs (Aya, Cmd-R, DeepSeek, EuroLLM, Gemma, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen) were prompted using the BLESS strategy: zero-shot prompts were tuned across temperatures (0.1–0.9) to maximize SARI, while few-shot (1-shot, 5-shot) conditions used the best temperature and examples from the validation set. Performance was measured via SARI (evaluating add, keep, delete operations) and BERTScore (semantic similarity), averaged over references.

## Key Results
- OasisSimp is the first sentence simplification dataset for Thai, Pashto, and Tamil, directly filling resource gaps for these low-resource languages.
- Zero-shot LLM performance varies drastically: English exceeds 40 SARI, while low-resource languages range from 29 to 36 SARI; Gemma achieves the strongest cross-lingual transfer (BERTScore 74.55 on Tamil).
- Few-shot prompting (5-shot) improves SARI by up to 10 points, especially for Gemma and Qwen, and notably enhances the KEEP component across languages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Creating high-quality, human-annotated parallel datasets for specific low-resource languages directly enables measurable progress. By providing a reliable, language-specific benchmark, it isolates model capability from data-creation noise, making performance differences attributable to model knowledge rather than artifacts. The core assumption is that prior low performance is due to missing evaluation data, not inherent task difficulty. Evidence: the dataset fills gaps for Thai, Pashto, and Tamil; language-specific sourcing and manual curation ensure complexity and diversity. Break condition: if annotation fails to produce meaning-preserving, genuinely simpler sentences, the dataset would measure annotation noise rather than model simplification skill.

**Mechanism 2:** The stark zero-shot performance disparity is causally linked to the degree of language exposure during LLM pretraining. Languages extensively covered (English) allow effective zero-shot task inference; low-resource languages, likely minimally represented, force reliance on brittle cross-lingual transfer or default behaviors (e.g., deletion), leading to poor semantic alignment and unbalanced SARI components. Core assumption: zero-shot performance strongly correlates with pretraining data volume for that language. Evidence: English SARI >40, low-resource 29–36; DeepSeek and LLaMA show negative scores for Tamil and Pashto. Break condition: if a model with confirmed substantial exposure to a low-resource language still shows poor zero-shot simplification, task-specific difficulty beyond data presence is implicated.

**Mechanism 3:** Few-shot prompting mitigates zero-shot deficiencies by exemplifying the simplification operation in the target language, enabling the LLM to adapt its generation policy from general language modeling to the specific transformation. In-context examples demonstrate input-output mapping (e.g., splitting, rewording), overriding default strategies and improving both content retention (KEEP) and appropriate addition (ADD). Core assumption: LLMs can rapidly adapt when task logic is clearly exemplified. Evidence: up to 10-point SARI gain; LLaMA’s KEEP in Thai increases from 24.83 to 39.99; few-shot enhances balance between meaning preservation and simplification. Break condition: if few-shot gains are inconsistent or isolated to ADD without KEEP/BERTScore improvements, holistic task learning from examples is weak.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Pipeline (source selection → language-specific filtering → manual curation → human annotation → quality verification → dataset split) → Evaluation Pipeline (prompt construction → LLM inference → output collection → metric computation) → Analysis Pipeline (score aggregation → component breakdown → comparative analysis).

Critical path: 1) Secure native-speaking annotators with training; without this, dataset quality fails. 2) Implement robust, reproducible prompting for each model; inconsistencies cause score variance. 3) Use exact, library-verified SARI and BERTScore implementations; metric drift invalidates comparisons.

Design tradeoffs: Annotation cost vs scale (prioritized quality via trained annotators over larger size); model selection breadth vs depth (evaluated 8 models without fine-tuning, favoring generalizability); automatic metrics only vs human eval (no human evaluation due to resources, risking metric blind spots).

Failure signatures: Data-level: inconsistent simplification operations across annotators (high variance, low inter-annotator agreement). Model-level: high SARI but low BERTScore (lexical overlap without meaning preservation). Pipeline-level: temperature sweep not exhaustive; best zero-shot temperature may not transfer to few-shot.

First 3 experiments: 1) Reproduce zero-shot baselines: run exact prompt and temperature sweep; verify English SARI >40 and low-resource <36; flag model/language deviations >2 points. 2) Prompt sensitivity check: for Gemma, test 3–5 prompt phrasings in 0-shot and 5-shot; measure score variance. 3) Component isolation ablation: manually analyze 50 outputs per language; categorize errors into failed addition (ADD ~0), over-deletion (KEEP low), or semantic drift (BERTScore low despite SARI).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do language family relationships and cross-linguistic transfer affect LLM performance? The authors state this is beyond their scope; evidence needed: controlled experiments linking typological features to performance gaps.
- How does human-judged quality compare across languages and correlate with automatic metrics? No human evaluation was conducted; evidence needed: native speaker ratings for meaning preservation, fluency, simplicity, followed by correlation analysis.
- Why do LLMs exhibit very low ADD scores, and what modifications could improve addition? Not analyzed; evidence needed: error analysis to identify patterns, experiments with fine-tuning or prompting strategies encouraging lexical enrichment.
- What factors underlie Gemma’s superior cross-lingual transfer? Not explored; evidence needed: comparative analysis of Gemma’s training data/architecture, ablation studies on model size or instruction tuning.

## Limitations
- Unspecified dataset split randomness, exact prompt templates, selected temperature values, and multi-reference metric aggregation hinder faithful reproduction.
- Absence of human evaluation leaves quality blind spots regarding readability, meaning preservation, and fluency beyond automatic metrics.
