# Revisiting Gene Ontology Knowledge Discovery with Hierarchical Feature Selection and Virtual Study Group of AI Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20132
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20132
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A virtual study group of AI agents with hierarchical critique generated mostly literature-supported claims about ageing from Gene Ontology terms, demonstrating enhanced reliability but with minor hallucinations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a virtual study group (VSG) framework that employs multiple LLM-based agents in a hierarchical structure to extract ageing-related biological knowledge from Gene Ontology (GO) terms pre-selected by a hierarchical feature selection method (HIP). The system assigns specialized roles: junior researchers investigate and critique findings per model organism, senior researchers synthesize reports, and a principal investigator integrates across four species (worm, fruit fly, mouse, yeast). Validation against existing literature shows that the majority of generated scientific claims are supported, highlighting the effectiveness of the multi-layer critique mechanism in improving reliability, though some hallucinations persist.

## Method Summary
The method addresses the challenge of mining structured biological knowledge (GO terms) for ageing insights. It uses the HIP method to select a concise set of high-level, generic GO terms that are strongly associated with ageing genes across datasets, ensuring feasibility for LLM interpretation and literature validation. These terms serve as input to a multi-agent LLM framework implemented with CrewAI. For each of four model organisms, two junior agents (using DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-VL) independently investigate ageing links to the selected GO terms; one then critiques the other's report. A senior agent (GPT-OSS) synthesizes the investigation and critique into an organism-specific summary. Finally, a principal investigator (GLM-4.7-Flash) integrates the four senior summaries to produce cross-species insights. The entire process follows a bottom-up flow from organism-specific analysis to cross-species synthesis.

## Key Results
- Majority of AI-generated claims were supported by existing literature upon manual review.
- The multi-layer critique mechanism (junior B critiquing junior A, seniors synthesizing) was observed to reduce oversimplifications and unsupported causal assertions.
- Minor hallucinations occurred, including an unreal citation in the mouse report and a contested claim about Hog1 in yeast.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-layer Critique Reduces Hallucination
The system embeds a peer-review-like process: junior agents generate competing narratives (Investigator A and Critic B), and seniors must reconcile them. This forces confrontation of oversimplifications and unsupported causal claims before synthesis. The core assumption is that LLMs produce plausible but flawed biology; an adversarial structure surfaces flaws and converges on robust summaries. Evidence: The paper reports that critiques were incorporated, with seniors agreeing with criticisms, and that most claims were literature-supported. A break would occur if critiques were ignored or if juniors converged without dissent.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Structure Enables Depth and Breadth
The bottom-up flow allows deep, context-rich investigation within each organism (juniors), abstraction and critique by organism seniors, and finally meta-analysis by a principal investigator across species. This isolates organism-specific nuance while enabling cross-species pattern synthesis. Core assumption: Ageing knowledge has both species-specific details and conserved cores; the hierarchy prevents premature overgeneralization. Evidence: The PI highlighted generalizability challenges (e.g., worm-to-mammal) drawn from senior reports. A break would be if the PI merely concatenated reports without true integration.

### Mechanism 3: HIP Pre-filtering Provides Feasible Input
HIP selects top-ranked, generic GO terms that preserve hierarchical information and are frequently associated with ageing genes. This reduces the LLM's interpretation task from thousands of specific terms to a manageable set of high-level, well-documented concepts, increasing validation feasibility. Core assumption: The most informative ageing signals reside in higher-tier GO terms; validation efficacy depends on existing literature volume. Evidence: The paper explicitly chose generic terms and validated via literature review. A break would be if selected terms were overly specific or irrelevant, leading to low validation rates.

## Foundational Learning
Three concepts are essential for understanding this work.

**Gene Ontology (GO) Hierarchy**  
*Why needed*: GO is a directed acyclic graph (DAG) with "is-a" and "part-of" relationships. The paper selects parent/generic terms, not leaves/specific, to enable broad claims. Misunderstanding hierarchy obscures why certain terms are chosen and how broad interpretations are possible.  
*Quick check*: If a gene is annotated to "GO:0005576 (extracellular region)" and "GO:0045202 (synapse)", what hierarchical relationship do these terms have, and why might a method like HIP prefer one over the other for a general ageing signal?

**Hierarchical Feature Selection (HIP Method)**  
*Why needed*: HIP is a dimensionality reduction step that respects the GO DAG, selecting a subset of parent terms that collectively preserve information about all annotated genes. It trades specificity for generalizability and validation feasibility.  
*Quick check*: What is the key difference between HIP and a standard feature selection method (e.g., based on mutual information) when applied to a hierarchical feature space like GO? What does HIP try to preserve that standard methods might ignore?

**Agentic AI with Role Specialization and Critique**  
*Why needed*: The VSG framework is a multi-agent system with a social structure: junior investigators/critics, senior synthesizers, and a PI integrator. It's not parallel LLM calls; it's a designed interaction protocol where outputs are consumed by agents with role-specific prompts.  
*Quick check*: In this framework, what is the functional difference between a "Virtual Junior Researcher A" and a "Virtual Junior Researcher B"? Why have two separate agents with the same "background" instead of one agent performing both tasks sequentially?

## Architecture Onboarding
The system architecture is a pipeline of specialized agents.

**Component map**:
Input (HIP-selected GO term table per organism) → Pre-processing (formulate organism-specific prompts) → Agent Layer 1 (per organism: Jr_Investigator & Jr_Critic) → Agent Layer 2 (per organism: Sr_Synthesizer) → Agent Layer 3 (PI_Integrator) → Output (structured reports, final cross-species insights). External validation (human literature review) occurs after output.

**Critical path**: For a given organism X: HIP term → Prompt → Jr_Investigator_X output → Jr_Critic_X output (critiques Jr_Inv_X) → Sr_Synthesizer_X output (merges both) → PI_Integrator output (integrates all 4 Sr_Syn outputs).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **LLM selection**: Uses four different open-source models (DeepSeek, Qwen, GPT-OSS, GLM). This avoids single-point failure and may leverage diverse strengths, but introduces model-specific biases and complicates attribution.
- **Critique depth**: Critics produce free-form text, allowing flexibility but reducing reproducibility and systematicity compared to a constrained critique template.
- **Validation scope**: Validation is selective (only "majority" claims checked) and human-dependent, trading completeness for feasibility; automated validation (e.g., PubMed API) was not attempted.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Hallucinated citations**: Agents generate specific, unverifiable paper references (e.g., unreal citation in mouse report).
- **Role blurring**: Senior reports paraphrase juniors without engaging critique; PI output is a simple concatenation without integration.
- **Term selection mismatch**: If HIP terms were overly specific (deep leaf nodes) or irrelevant, validation rates would drop due to sparse literature.
- **Model incompetence**: A biologically uninformed model might produce nonsense that critics fail to catch.

**First 3 experiments** (proposed follow-ups):
1. **Baseline Ablation: Single Agent.** Compare full VSG to a single LLM handling all organisms without critique hierarchy.
2. **Critique Chain Depth.** Systematically remove or add critique layers (e.g., no Jr_B, double seniors) to measure marginal benefit.
3. **Input Feature Quality.** Replace HIP terms with random GO terms or the lowest-ranked HIP terms to test the pre-filtering hypothesis.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several unresolved questions:
1. How can the framework more reliably distinguish and avoid biologically unsupported claims, especially for novel hypotheses? Residual hallucinations (e.g., Hog1 claim, unreal citation) persist despite critique.
2. Can the framework effectively interpret GO terms from lower, more specific positions in the hierarchy? Evaluation was only on generic terms; scalability to specific terms is untested.
3. To what extent do insights for simple models (worm, yeast) generalize to mammals? Cross-species synthesis is limited to the PI summary; mechanisms for translation are not formalized.
4. Do the hierarchical roles and specific LLM assignments each contribute uniquely? Could alternative architectures (e.g., peer-to-peer debate) be equally effective? No ablation studies isolate component contributions.
5. Can AI-generated claims that are novel (not found in literature) be experimentally validated? The validation method only checks alignment with existing science; prospective wet-lab testing is needed.

## Limitations
- **No causal ablations**: The paper does not isolate the contribution of the multi-layer critique vs. other factors (e.g., HIP input, LLM choice).
- **Prompt and protocol opacity**: Exact system prompts, context management, and interaction logic are not detailed, hindering exact replication.
- **Subjective validation**: Literature validation was performed selectively by the authors, introducing bias and preventing reliable hallucination rate estimation.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Critique reduces hallucination)**: Medium (supported by observed critiques and majority validation, but no ablation proving causality).
- **Mechanism 2 (Hierarchical depth+breadth)**: Medium (PI provides cross-species commentary, but synthesis quality not quantified against simpler aggregation).
- **Mechanism 3 (HIP pre-filtering feasibility)**: High (logical necessity for validation; design explicitly avoids overly specific terms).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate the critique chain**: Run the pipeline with only Junior A → Senior → PI (no Junior B critique) and compare validation rates and claim depth to the full setup.
2. **Challenge HIP selection**: Replace HIP terms with (a) random GO terms of similar depth and (b) the lowest-ranked HIP terms; measure changes in validation rate and PI insight quality.
3. **Automate validation benchmark**: For a fixed sample of claims, implement automated checks: extract cited paper identifiers, query PubMed/OpenAlex to confirm existence and relevance, providing an objective baseline for "support".
