# Tri-Reader: An Open-Access, Multi-Stage AI Pipeline for First-Pass Lung Nodule Annotation in Screening CT

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.19380
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19380
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Tri-Reader reduces candidate annotations per scan by 40–55% (to 6.71–13.0 candidates/scan) while maintaining or improving lesion detection sensitivity (CPM 0.47–0.73) and achieving 90.9% sensitivity for pathologically confirmed malignancies.

## Executive Summary
Tri-Reader is an open-access, three-stage AI pipeline designed to streamline the first-pass annotation of lung nodules in screening CT scans. It integrates publicly available segmentation, detection, and malignancy classification models into a unified workflow. The system prioritizes high sensitivity while significantly reducing the number of candidate nodules that require human review. This is achieved through a consensus detection stage that leverages two complementary detectors to filter false positives, followed by a malignancy-informed recovery stage that rescues clinically important nodules missed initially. The pipeline outputs candidates with tiered confidence scores that correlate with radiologist consensus, enabling efficient prioritization for annotators.

## Method Summary
Tri-Reader orchestrates three sequential stages. Stage 1 applies two 3D RetinaNet detectors (CADe-ROIonly and CADe-FPaware) to a CT scan after lung masking with VISTA3D. Candidates found by both detectors are promoted to high confidence (1.0). Stage 2 subjects the remaining "disagreement" candidates to ensemble malignancy scoring using two ResNet50 CADx models (LUNA25-CADxr50 and DLCS24-CADxr50SWS). Those with an averaged malignancy score ≥ 0.10 are promoted to medium confidence (0.5). Stage 3 retains any final candidates with a detection score ≥ 0.20, assigning low confidence (0.2). Fixed thresholds (τCADx=0.10, τCADe=0.20) were selected via validation sweeps. The system's open-access nature ensures reproducibility but may require site-specific calibration.

## Key Results
- Reduced candidate annotations per scan by 40–55% compared to a standard detector, to a range of 6.71–13.0 candidates/scan.
- Maintained or improved lesion detection sensitivity (CPM 0.47–0.73) across multiple internal and external datasets.
- Achieved 90.9% sensitivity for pathologically confirmed malignancies on the DLCS24 test set.
- Demonstrated a strong correlation between model confidence scores and radiologist consensus, enabling efficient annotation prioritization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Consensus Detection
- **Claim:** Consensus detection with two complementary detectors reduces false positives while preserving true positive sensitivity.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 runs two detectors. Candidates found by both are promoted to confidence 1.0 and passed forward. This intersection leverages complementary error patterns: true nodules are likely detected by both, while many false positives are model‑specific.
- **Core assumption:** The two detectors have uncorrelated false positives; overlapping detections have a higher probability of being true nodules.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states it "prioritizes sensitivity while reducing the candidate list." Section 2.2 details the consensus promotion. Internal evaluations show candidate reduction (40–55%) with maintained sensitivity.
- **Break condition:** If detectors share systematic false positives (e.g., similar training data/architecture), consensus may not reduce candidates meaningfully. True nodules missed by one detector could be downgraded, risking under-review if later stages fail to recover them.

### Mechanism 2: Malignancy‑Informed Recovery
- **Claim:** Ensemble CADx rescues clinically important nodules that detectors missed.
- **Mechanism:** Candidates not promoted in Stage 1 ("disagreement" set) are scored by two CADx models. Those with averaged malignancy score ≥ τCADx (0.10) are promoted to confidence 0.5. This acts as a safety net using malignancy probability to highlight suspicious lesions with lower detection confidence.
- **Core assumption:** Malignant nodules possess image features recognizable by CADx even with low detector confidence; ensemble averaging across models improves robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 describes the ensemble scoring. Section 3.2 shows malignant prevalence was higher in Stage 2 (68.5%) than in later stages, indicating enrichment. The 90.9% malignancy sensitivity on DLCS24 test set supports effectiveness.
- **Break condition:** If CADx models are poorly calibrated or insensitive to certain subtypes (e.g., sub‑centimeter nodules), the recovery stage may fail. The fixed threshold τCADx might not generalize to new distributions without recalibration.

### Mechanism 3: Tiered Confidence Output
- **Claim:** Tiered confidence (1.0, 0.5, 0.2) enables efficient annotator prioritization, correlating with radiologist consensus and malignancy.
- **Mechanism:** The final candidate list carries tiers reflecting promotion stage. Annotators review higher tiers first, reducing initial workload while maintaining coverage of likely true nodules.
- **Core assumption:** Confidence scores are monotonic with nodule conspicuity and clinical relevance; higher tiers contain a disproportionate share of true nodules.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes confidence scores correlate with radiologist consensus. On LNDbv4, three‑reader unanimous nodules had mean detection probability 0.94 ± 0.13, while single‑reader nodules showed 0.77 ± 0.32. Section 4 discusses the correlation.
- **Break condition:** If confidence calibration drifts across datasets (e.g., different scanners), tier thresholds may no longer align with true nodule likelihood, causing either unnecessary review of low‑tier candidates or missed nodules if annotators rely exclusively on high tiers.

## Architecture Onboarding
VISTA3D -> CADe-ROIonly & CADe-FPaware -> Consensus (overlap) -> CADx ensemble (LUNA25-CADxr50, DLCS24-CADxr50SWS) -> Thresholding (τCADx, τCADe) -> Final tiered candidate list.

**Critical path:** 1. Input CT → resampling & HU clipping. 2. Apply VISTA3D → lung mask. 3. Run both detectors → candidate boxes/scores. 4. Consensus promotion: overlap → confidence 1.0. 5. Disagreement candidates → patch extraction → ensemble CADx scoring → promote if avg ≥ 0.10 → confidence 0.5. 6. Remaining: keep if CADe score ≥ 0.20 → confidence 0.2. 7. Merge all tiers into final list.

**Design tradeoffs:** *Open‑access models* ensure reproducibility but may not be optimally tuned for a specific site’s data distribution. *Three‑stage cascade* drastically reduces candidates (40–55%) but introduces error propagation; a missed nodule in Stage 1 can only be recovered in Stage 2 if malignancy is high. *Fixed thresholds* simplify deployment but may require site‑specific calibration, especially for CADx where malignancy prevalence varies.

**Failure signatures:** Sub‑centimeter nodules (< 6 mm) show consistently low sensitivity (e.g., ~0.31 on LNDbv4). If lung segmentation fails (e.g., anatomical variants), extraneous candidates may appear. If the two detectors produce highly overlapping false positives, consensus reduction may be less than expected.

**First 3 experiments:** 1. **Baseline verification:** Run on 10–20 scans with ground truth; record candidate counts and sensitivity after each stage. Confirm ~40–55% reduction vs. baseline without large sensitivity drop. 2. **Confidence calibration:** On a validation set, compute PPV for each confidence tier; expect PPV(1.0) > PPV(0.5) > PPV(0.2). Correlate confidence with radiologist consensus if available. 3. **Threshold sensitivity:** Sweep τCADx (0.05–0.20) and τCADe (0.10–0.40); plot sensitivity vs. candidates/scan. Verify chosen thresholds lie on a stable plateau.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** What specific methodologies will be employed for site-specific calibration and the integration of new open-source models into Tri-Reader?
  - **Basis:** Future work includes site-specific calibration, integration of additional open-source models. – Discussion section.
  - **Why unresolved:** No technical approaches, validation strategies, or performance criteria are detailed.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up study describing the calibration pipeline (e.g., threshold tuning protocols) and benchmarking results of integrated models.
- **Question 2:** How will prospective workflow integration impact radiologist annotation time, inter-observer agreement, and overall dataset quality in real-world screening programs?
  - **Basis:** The pipeline was evaluated on retrospective datasets; prospective workflow studies are needed. – Discussion section.
  - **Why unresolved:** Actual deployment challenges (UI/UX, human-AI interaction) and their effects on efficiency and consistency are unmeasured.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A prospective, multi-center study measuring annotation time per scan, annotator satisfaction, and agreement metrics (e.g., Cohen’s κ) with and without Tri-Reader.
- **Question 3:** To what extent do the fixed operating thresholds (τ_CADx = 0.10, τ_CADe = 0.20), selected on specific validation cohorts, limit the sensitivity-workload trade-off in geographically or demographically distinct populations?
  - **Basis:** Thresholds were determined via sweeps on validation splits from DLCS, LUNA16, LUNA25, and VLST, but robustness on external datasets like IMD-CT or LNDbv4 is not evaluated beyond overall FROC.
  - **Why unresolved:** Nodule prevalence, size distribution, and protocols vary across regions; thresholds optimized for U.S. cohorts may not balance sensitivity and candidate burden optimally elsewhere.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A study applying the same thresholds to diverse external datasets, reporting shifts in CPM and candidates/scan, followed by local re-tuning to assess improvements.
- **Question 4:** How accurate and generalizable is the rule-based report linkage module in matching radiology report descriptors to CT candidates, particularly for non-English reports or narrative styles outside the LNDbv4 protocol?
  - **Basis:** The module is described as an "extensible" rule-based NLP system, but no validation metrics (precision, recall) or error analysis are provided for the matching task.
  - **Why unresolved:** Utility for label enrichment hinges on correct matches; performance likely degrades with linguistic variability, yet this failure mode is unquantified.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A dedicated evaluation on a diverse corpus of radiology reports (multilingual, multi-institution) with gold-standard spatial links, reporting matching accuracy and common error categories.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility blockers:** Pretrained model weights and exact preprocessing details (resampling, patch extraction) are not yet publicly released.
- **Threshold generalizability:** τ_CADx and τ_CADe were tuned on validation sets; performance may degrade on sites with different scanner models or patient populations without recalibration.
- **Segmentation dependency:** VISTA3D lung mask failures or anatomical variants could lead to extraneous candidates outside lungs, increasing false positives.
- **Small nodule sensitivity:** Average sensitivity for sub-6mm nodules is low (~0.31 on LNDbv4), limiting utility for early-stage cancer detection.

## Confidence
- **Overall performance (candidate reduction, CPM):** High – consistent results across multiple internal and external datasets.
- **Malignancy sensitivity on DLCS24:** Medium – strong pathology gold standard but based on a single test set; external validation needed.
- **Confidence score calibration vs. radiologist consensus:** Medium – correlation shown on LNDbv4, but generalizability unproven.
- **Consensus detection mechanism:** Medium – candidate reduction observed, but assumes uncorrelated false positives which may not hold for all data distributions.
- **Malignancy-informed recovery:** Medium – ensemble CADx promotes malignant nodules, but fixed threshold τ_CADx may require site-specific adjustment.

## Next Checks
1. **Threshold calibration study:** On a new external site, sweep τ_CADx and τ_CADe across a validation cohort to determine operating points that preserve sensitivity while minimizing candidates; compare to original thresholds.
2. **Sub-centimeter nodule evaluation:** Test Tri-Reader on a dataset with expert-annotated nodules <6mm to quantify detection limits and assess impact on early cancer detection.
3. **Disagreement analysis:** For scans where confidence scores diverge from radiologist consensus, perform root-cause analysis (segmentation errors, atypical locations, artifacts) to identify pipeline weaknesses and guide refinements.
