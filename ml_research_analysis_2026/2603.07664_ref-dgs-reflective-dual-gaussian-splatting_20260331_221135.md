# Ref-DGS: Reflective Dual Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07664
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07664
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art rendering of reflective scenes with high-quality geometry and fast training (~12.6 minutes) without ray tracing.

## Executive Summary
Ref-DGS introduces a dual Gaussian representation to model near-field specular reflections within the Gaussian splatting framework. It separates stable surface geometry (via geometry Gaussians) from local reflection effects (via separate reflection Gaussians), while using a global spherical feature map for environment lighting. A lightweight physically-aware adaptive mixing shader combines these components, enabling efficient and high-quality novel view synthesis on challenging glossy datasets. The method outperforms prior work in both surface reconstruction (normal MAE, Chamfer distance) and rendering metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) while training orders of magnitude faster than ray-tracing based alternatives.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to represent a scene with two sets of 3D Gaussians: `G_geo` encodes surface geometry, diffuse color, roughness, and normals; `G_local` encodes local reflection features. Global specular contributions are stored in a spherical mipmap (`M`) that is queried based on the reflection direction and roughness. During rendering, two passes are performed: first rasterizing `G_geo` to obtain diffuse color, roughness, and normals; second rasterizing `G_local` to obtain local specular features. These are fused by a small MLP (`f_Θ`), conditioned on global features from `M`, local features, roughness, and the cosine of the normal and view direction. The combined color `C = C_diff + C_spec` is optimized against multi-view images using a photometric loss (L1 + SSIM), augmented with regularization terms that enforce alignment with precomputed depth/normal priors and opacity smoothness. Both Gaussian sets undergo joint densification and opacity reset similar to 2DGS. On synthetic benchmarks, Ref-DGS achieves normal MAE of 1.43° (ShinySynthetic) and 1.88° (GlossySynthetic) and PSNR of 35.21 dB and 30.63 dB respectively, with training times around 12.6 minutes, far faster than ray-tracing based competitors.

## Key Results
- ShinySynthetic: normal MAE 1.43°, PSNR 35.21 dB.
- GlossySynthetic: Chamfer distance 0.0062, normal MAE 1.88°, PSNR 30.63 dB.
- Training time: 12.6 minutes (vs. MaterialRefGS 2.42 hours).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The decoupling of geometry and reflection prevents specular highlights from corrupting surface normals and depth, which is crucial for accurate geometry recovery. Local reflection Gaussians capture near-field, view-dependent glossy effects that a global environment map alone cannot represent. The adaptive mixing shader learns to combine global and local cues in a physically plausible manner, guided by roughness and incident angle. Regularization from pretrained depth/normal priors (VGGT/Metric3D) further stabilizes geometry learning. This design avoids costly ray tracing, making the approach both efficient and effective for reflective scenes.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input images & cameras -> Dual Gaussians (`G_geo`, `G_local`) & Sph-Mip (`M`) -> Two-pass rasterization (first `G_geo`, then `G_local`) -> Specular shader (`f_Θ`) -> Color composition (`C_diff + C_spec`) -> Loss computation (`L_c`, `L_n`, `L_α`, `L_prior`) -> Backpropagation and densification -> Updated Gaussians, `M`, `f_Θ`.
- **Critical path**: (1) Initialize Gaussians from SfM points; (2) Compute priors (depth/normal) via VGGT/Metric3D; (3) For each training iteration: rasterize `G_geo` to get `C_diff`, roughness `ρ`, normals `N`; rasterize `G_local` to get `F_local`; query `M` for `F_global`; feed `[F_global, F_local, ρ, cos(N·V)]` to `f_Θ` to produce `C_spec`; combine `C_diff + C_spec`; compute loss; backpropagate; apply densification and opacity reset.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Dual Gaussians increase memory but avoid mixing signals; Sph-Mip offers efficient global lighting at cost of limited resolution; adaptive shader adds minimal overhead vs. fixed blending; splitting rendering into two passes doubles rasterization cost but allows independent optimization; regularization from priors improves geometry but requires external models.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Geometry degradation (dents, merged objects) → likely `G_local` not absorbing specular leakage or `L_n` weight too low.
  - Blurry/incorrect reflections → `G_local` failing to densify near reflective regions, or shader underfitting; check `F_local` visualization.
  - Unstable training (NaNs) → excessive loss scaling or unbounded shader outputs; clamp specular color and adjust λ.
  - Slow convergence → low learning rate for `M` or shader; monitor gradient norms; ensure `G_local` opacities are optimized.
- **First experiments**:
  1. Ablation of `G_local`: Train without the second Gaussian set; expect significant PSNR drop (>2 dB) and normal MAE increase (>5°), confirming necessity of local reflections.
  2. Ablation of adaptive shader: Replace `f_Θ` with a fixed weighted sum of global and local features; expect degraded view consistency, especially under varying roughness.
  3. Synthetic baseline verification: Train on ShinySynthetic with default hyperparameters; target normal MAE ≤1.46° and PSNR ≥34.5 dB to validate implementation before tackling real data.

## Limitations
- Major hyperparameters (loss weights, learning rates, densification thresholds) are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Real-world performance depends heavily on the quality of external depth/normal priors and may not generalize.
- The method still requires careful tuning to avoid geometry degradation or unstable training.
