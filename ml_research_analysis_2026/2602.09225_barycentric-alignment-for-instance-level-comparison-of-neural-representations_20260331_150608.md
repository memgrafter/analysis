# Barycentric alignment for instance-level comparison of neural representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09225
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09225
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a barycentric alignment framework that quotients out nuisance symmetries to construct a universal embedding space, enabling instance-level comparison with strong cross-model alignment (e.g., vision correlation >0.4, language >0.47) and consistency scores predictive of computational difficulty.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of comparing neural network representations at the level of individual instances (stimuli) across different models. Existing methods are often set-level and vulnerable to nuisance symmetries like neuron reordering and rotations. The authors propose a barycentric alignment framework that factors out these symmetries by iteratively aligning multiple models to a common barycenter using orthogonal Procrustes analysis. This yields a universal embedding space where each stimulus is represented consistently across models, enabling instance-level similarity scores.

The framework is evaluated on vision and language models, showing strong cross-model alignment (average pairwise correlation >0.4 for vision, >0.47 for language; top-10 retrieval >0.92 and >0.93). Instance-level consistency scores correlate with computational difficulty metrics (prototypicality r=0.63) but not with human memorability or recognizability. When applied to unimodal vision and language models, the approach achieves image-text retrieval comparable to CLIP and aligns with human judgments. Applied to fMRI data, alignment yields stable cortical spaces but weak correspondence with artificial models, suggesting divergence in representational variability between biological and artificial systems.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a universal embedding by aligning representations from multiple neural models on a shared stimulus set. It uses iterative orthogonal Procrustes alignment: starting from an initial mean, each model is rotated to best align with a template, the template is updated as the mean of aligned representations, and the process repeats until convergence. The resulting transformations are applied to test stimuli, yielding aligned coordinates. Instance-level consistency for a stimulus is defined as the average pairwise similarity across aligned models. This framework quotients out symmetry groups (e.g., \(O(d)\)) and enables direct comparison of individual stimulus representations across models.

## Key Results
- Cross-model alignment yields average pairwise correlation >0.4 for vision models and >0.47 for language models; top-10 retrieval accuracy exceeds 0.92 and 0.93 respectively.
- Instance-level consistency scores correlate with computational difficulty metrics: prototypicality (r=0.63) and dichotomous data difficulty.
- Cross-modal alignment of unimodal vision and language models achieves image-text retrieval comparable to CLIP (text→image top-1: 54.0% vs. 52.5%) and aligns with human judgments (Kendall’s τ=0.54).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Aligning representations by factoring out orthogonal symmetries yields a universal embedding space where instance-level comparisons become interpretable and comparable across models.
- **Mechanism:** Given representations \(X_i\) from multiple models, iterative Procrustes alignment finds orthogonal transforms \(T_i\) that minimize the distance to a common template \(M\). The template is updated as the mean of aligned representations, converging to a barycenter under the orthogonal group. This quotients out rotations/permutations.
- **Core assumption:** Differences between models are largely reducible to transformations in \(O(d)\); after removal, the underlying stimulus geometry is shared.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2 describe the algorithm and convergence; corpus context shows novelty in barycentric integration.
- **Break condition:** If models differ by nonlinear distortions beyond orthogonal transformations, alignment fails to produce high cross-model correlation; distortions remain.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Instance-level consistency scores are stable across model pool constructions and reflect stimulus-level properties like difficulty.
- **Mechanism:** After learning transformations \(T_i\), for a test stimulus \(j\), compute average pairwise similarity across aligned models \(S_j = \frac{1}{N(N-1)} \sum_{p\neq q} \text{SIM}(Y'_{pj}, Y'_{qj})\). The common coordinate system ensures model-agnostic distances.
- **Core assumption:** The universal embedding provides a common geometric frame; thus distances for individual points are model-agnostic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 defines consistency; Section 3.1 shows high correlation of scores across diverse model pools; Section 3.2 shows correlation with difficulty metrics.
- **Break condition:** Poor barycenter estimation or unstable alignment leads to noisy scores uncorrelated across pools; generalization to OOD stimuli fails (Appendix A.4).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Barycentric alignment of unimodal vision and language models yields a shared space that supports image-text retrieval and aligns with human judgments, approaching contrastive models like CLIP.
- **Mechanism:** Treat image embeddings from vision models and caption embeddings from language models as points; align all models into one universal space via Procrustes. Matching image-caption pairs become close despite no paired training.
- **Core assumption:** Internal geometries of unimodal vision and language models are approximately isometric under orthogonal transformations; shared semantic structure is linearly recoverable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3.1 shows retrieval results; Section 3.3.2 shows correlation with human ratings; abstract states post-hoc alignment yields CLIP-like performance.
- **Break condition:** If modalities are too dissimilar or unimodal objectives diverge, orthogonal alignment cannot bring matching pairs together; performance drops to chance.

## Foundational Learning
### Procrustes Analysis
**Why needed:** The core alignment solves orthogonal Procrustes problems: \(T_i = \arg\min_{R\in O(d)} \|X_i R - M\|_F\) using SVD to find optimal rotations.  
**Quick check:** Given centered matrices X, Y, the optimal orthogonal rotation R = UV^T where UΣV^T = X^TY is the SVD.

### Representational Similarity Metrics (RSA, CKA)
**Why needed:** Traditional set-level metrics like RSA yield a single similarity score for the whole stimulus set, lacking instance-level granularity.  
**Quick check:** What is a fundamental limitation of RSA that instance-level consistency overcomes? (Answer: RSA cannot assign scores to individual stimuli.)

### Barycenter / Fréchet Mean in Quotient Space
**Why needed:** The universal space is defined as the Procrustes barycenter of model representations, generalizing the mean to spaces with symmetry.  
**Quick check:** In this context, what does "universal embedding space" represent? (Answer: A common coordinate system obtained by averaging aligned representations, factoring out nuisance symmetries.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input representations (X_i, Y_i) -> Alignment module (iterative Procrustes) -> Inference module (apply T_i, compute S_j) -> Aligned coordinates (Y'_i) and consistency scores (S_j).

### Critical path
1. Gather training representations X_i for a shared stimulus set.
2. Initialize M^(0) = (1/N) Σ_i X_i.
3. Repeat until convergence:
   - For each model, solve T_i = argmin_{R∈O(d)} ||X_i R - M^(t)||_F via SVD.
   - Update X_i ← X_i T_i.
   - Recompute M^(t+1) = (1/N) Σ_i X_i.
4. For test stimuli, compute Y'_i = Y_i T_i.
5. For each stimulus j, compute S_j = (1/(N(N-1))) Σ_{p≠q} SIM(Y'_{pj}, Y'_{qj}).

### Design tradeoffs
- *Group G:* Orthogonal invariance (O(d)) is default; permutation-only (S_d) is cheaper but less expressive. Choice affects what variability is quotiented.
- *Dimensionality:* Zero-pad to max d; may add noise if some models have lower intrinsic rank.
- *Local minima:* Iterative refinement depends on initialization (arithmetic mean). Could use multiple restarts for heterogeneous pools.
- *Scalability:* Per-iteration cost O(N(n d^2 + d^3)); feasible for N~10-100, d~1000.

### Failure signatures
- Low cross-model correlation/RMS in universal space indicates poor alignment; maybe G too restrictive or models too dissimilar.
- Consistency scores uncorrelated across model pools suggest unstable barycenter.
- Cross-modal retrieval near chance: unimodal geometries lack linear isometry.
- No convergence within reasonable iterations: incompatible models or numerical issues.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Alignment quality:** Train on held-out set, compute cross-model correlation, RMS, and top-K retrieval on test stimuli. Expect vision correlation >0.4, language >0.47 (Table 1).
2. **Consistency reproducibility:** Compute instance scores from two different model pools on same test set; correlate scores. Expect r > 0.6 (Figure 2).
3. **Cross-modal retrieval:** For pool of vision-only and language-only models, evaluate image-text retrieval (top-1/5/10) on MS-COCO. Expect performance comparable to CLIP (Section 3.3.1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### What aspects of cross-modal structure are recoverable via post-hoc alignment versus requiring joint multimodal training?
**Basis:** Section 4 explicitly discusses distinguishing recoverable vs. training-required structure.  
**Why unresolved:** Paper shows unimodal alignment yields CLIP-like performance, but exact boundary unknown.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across tasks, model scales, architectures; identifying semantic dimensions where post-hoc alignment fails; comparing with contrastive training.

### Which artificial model populations exhibit representational variability patterns that best match inter-individual biological variability?
**Basis:** Section 4 motivates by weak correlation between biological and artificial consistency scores (Section 3.4).  
**Why unresolved:** Standard model pools (by architecture/scale) do not capture biological consistency patterns.  
**Evidence needed:** Compute consistency scores from diverse artificial models (different objectives, data, scales) and correlate with fMRI data across subjects/regions; determine which model factors drive alignment.

### Under what conditions do barycentric universal embedding spaces generalize beyond the training domain?
**Basis:** Section 4 discusses generalization; Appendix A.4 shows scores uncorrelated when test categories excluded from training.  
**Why unresolved:** Generalization essential for new stimuli/models; limits not understood.  
**Evidence needed:** Controlled OOD experiments varying training/test category overlap; testing with unseen architectures; assessing different invariance groups; measuring stability across domains.

## Limitations
- The core assumption that model differences reduce to orthogonal transformations may be violated by non-linear distortions, as indicated by weak fMRI correspondence.
- Instance consistency scores might depend on model pool composition; the paper only evaluated two pools, leaving stability across more diverse architectures untested.
- Cross-modal alignment is asymmetric (image→text vs. text→image) and may not generalize beyond vision–language pairs; iterative Procrustes could converge to local minima.

## Confidence
- Universal embedding (Mechanism 1): High
- Instance consistency stability (Mechanism 2): Medium
- Cross-modal alignment (Mechanism 3): Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Pool robustness test:** Randomly subsample the model pool (e.g., 50–90%) many times and correlate instance consistency scores between subsampled and full pools. Low correlation indicates sensitivity to pool composition.
2. **Non-orthogonal alignment extension:** Replace orthogonal Procrustes with affine or kernel-based alignment and measure changes in cross-model correlation and fMRI alignment. Significant improvements would challenge orthogonality assumption.
3. **Audio–text cross-modal alignment:** Apply framework to unimodal audio (e.g., YAMNet) and text (e.g., BERT) models, evaluating audio–text retrieval on AudioCaps. Above-chance performance tests generalizability beyond vision–language.
