# Mamba-VMR: Multimodal Query Augmentation via Generated Videos for Precise Temporal Grounding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22121
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22121
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SOTA R@1 (IoU=0.5)=45.20% and R@1 (IoU=0.7)=25.10% on TVR dataset.

## Executive Summary
Mamba-VMR addresses the challenge of precise temporal grounding in text-driven video moment retrieval, particularly for queries involving multiple verbs where hidden dynamics in untrimmed videos cause ambiguity. The proposed two-stage framework first uses an LLM to decompose the query and match relevant subtitle cues, generating a motion-rich auxiliary video via a text-to-video diffusion model. In the second stage, a multi-modal Mamba network fuses the generated video prior with the target video using text-controlled selection and video-guided gating, achieving linear-time processing and noise filtering. Experiments on TVR show state-of-the-art performance with R@1 (IoU=0.5) 45.20% and R@1 (IoU=0.7) 25.10%, while scaling efficiently to longer sequences compared to quadratic Transformers.

## Method Summary
Mamba-VMR operates in two stages. Stage 1 employs LLaMA‑3.1 to decompose the textual query into verb‑centered sub‑events and score subtitle sentences to select the most relevant cue sentences (top‑k = 3, threshold 0.5). The query and selected subtitles are fused into a composite prompt for CogVideoX, which generates a 6‑second auxiliary video capturing the motion dynamics. Stage 2 processes the target video frames (encoded by CLIP) and the auxiliary video (also CLIP‑encoded) through a multi‑modal Mamba network. The network augments frame features with relational embeddings from a GCN, then passes them through four bidirectional SSM layers. The SSM state matrix is conditioned on the text embedding (text‑controlled selection) and the state update is gated by a signal derived from the pooled auxiliary video (video‑guided gating), enabling noise‑filtered fusion. The model predicts start/end logits and clip‑wise relevance, trained with boundary, relevance, and contrastive losses. Training uses AdamW with a learning rate of 1e‑4 and early stopping, while inference precomputes auxiliary videos and applies beam search for refinement.

## Key Results
- SOTA on TVR: R@1 (IoU=0.5) = 45.20%, R@1 (IoU=0.7) = 25.10%, surpassing prior best (ICQ) by 1.07% and 1.02% respectively.
- High recall across all thresholds: R@100 (IoU=0.5) = 91.44%.
- Memory and computational efficiency: Mamba‑based processing scales linearly with sequence length, enabling handling of longer videos compared to quadratic Transformers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: LLM-Guided Query Decomposition and Subtitle Matching
- **Claim**: Decomposing complex multi‑verb queries into sub‑events and matching with subtitles enhances fine‑grained temporal cues, reducing ambiguity in VMR.
- **Mechanism**: Use LLM (LLaMA‑3.1) to extract verbs and segment the query; compute relevance scores for subtitle sentences; select top‑k subtitles to augment the query narrative.
- **Core assumption**: Subtitles contain dialogue cues that align with sub‑events, and the LLM accurately assesses relevance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: “LLM‑guided subtitle matching identifies relevant cues, fused with query to generate motion‑rich auxiliary videos.”
  - [section 3.2]: Details the verb extraction and subtitle scoring process.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like “Localizing Moments in Long Video Via Multimodal Guidance” also use multimodal cues, but direct evidence is weak; corpus does not provide specific validation.
- **Break condition**: If subtitles are irrelevant/noisy or the LLM misjudges alignment, augmented prompts may mislead video generation and harm grounding.

### Mechanism 2: Generated Video Priors for Motion Capture
- **Claim**: Short auxiliary videos generated from the fused query‑subtitle prompt capture implicit motion sequences, providing temporal priors that static augmentations lack.
- **Mechanism**: CogVideoX diffusion model conditions on composite prompt to produce a 6‑second clip with coherent motion.
- **Core assumption**: Generated video faithfully reflects the intended dynamics; prompts are descriptive enough to elicit accurate motion.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: “generate motion‑rich auxiliary videos via CogVideoX text‑to‑video diffusion.”
  - [section 3.3]: “capturing implicit motion information as temporal priors.”
  - [corpus]: Neighbors on AI‑generated video detection (e.g., “Learning Human‑Perceived Fakeness in AI‑Generated Videos”) show interest in video generation quality, but no direct evidence for VMR benefit.
- **Break condition**: Low‑fidelity generation or prompt deviation (e.g., unnatural transitions, missing actions) introduces noise that misguides the Mamba network.

### Mechanism 3: Multi‑Modal Controlled Mamba with Video‑Guided Gating
- **Claim**: The Mamba network extended with video‑guided gating fuses generated priors efficiently (linear time) while filtering temporal noise, enabling precise grounding over long sequences.
- **Mechanism**: Extend bidirectional SSM; gate gt = σ(Wg[eq; pooled(eg)]) modulates state transitions; gating uses pooled generated video to focus on motion‑aligned segments.
- **Core assumption**: Generated priors provide a reliable signal for gating; the SSM’s selective state updates can effectively integrate this signal over long ranges.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: “Multi‑modal Mamba network fuses generated priors with target video using text‑controlled selection and video‑guided gating, enabling linear‑time, noise‑filtered processing.”
  - [section 3.4]: Equations and architecture of gated SSM.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors on Mamba for video (e.g., “VideoMamba”) demonstrate efficiency, but the specific gating design is novel; evidence from corpus is suggestive but not direct.
- **Break condition**: Misaligned priors cause gating to suppress correct moments; Mamba’s fixed state size may limit ultra‑long dependency modeling beyond several hundred frames.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. **LLM Subtitle Matcher**: Takes query and subtitles, outputs selected subtitle set S′.
  2. **Temporal Prior Generator**: CogVideoX takes composite prompt (query + S′), outputs short video Vg.
  3. **Embedding Layer**: CLIP encoders for text (eq), target video (eo), generated video (eg); GCN produces relational embeddings ro added to eo.
  4. **Multi‑Modal Mamba**: Stacked bidirectional SSM layers with video‑guided gating (gt from eq and pooled eg).
  5. **Prediction Head**: Linear layers for start/end logits; relevance scoring; NMS refinement.
- **Critical path**:
  Input (Vₒ, S, Q) → LLM matching/decomposition → S′ → prompt fusion → Vg → embeddings (eq, eo, eg) → Mamba with gating → contextual features fo → start/end logits → NMS → output spans T.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Mamba vs. Transformer*: Mamba scales linearly to long sequences, but may underfit complex interactions; Transformer quadratic complexity limits practical length.
  - *Generated video length*: Longer Vg provides richer motion but increases sequence length and memory; 6 s chosen as balance.
  - *Subtitle selection (top‑k)*: Larger k adds contextual detail but risks noise; k=3 optimal on TVR.
  - *Gating design*: Simple concatenation of eq and pooled eg vs. more complex cross‑attention; chosen for efficiency.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - *Poor multi‑verb recall*: Check if Vg captures sequential actions (visualize generated videos).
  - *High memory usage*: If sequence length (Lo + Lg) exceeds GPU capacity, reduce Vg length or downsample frames.
  - *Low R@1 @0.7*: Imprecise boundaries; inspect gating weights—if gt near constant, gating may be ineffective.
  - *LLM irrelevance*: If selected subtitles seem off‑topic, adjust relevance threshold θ or use a different LLM.
- **First 3 experiments** (from paper):
  1. **Ablate LLM module** (Table 2): Removing it drops R1@0.5 by 5.05%, confirming its role in disambiguation.
  2. **Ablate video priors** (Table 4): Replacing CogVideoX with static images (DALL‑E) or removing priors entirely causes >5% drop at R1@0.5, showing motion cues are critical.
  3. **Ablate Mamba gating** (Table 5): Removing gating or using Transformer reduces performance and scales quadratically; validates linear‑time gated fusion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the system be made robust to low-quality or irrelevant generated video priors, which currently limit performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion states: “limitations such as sensitivity to generation quality persist; future work may explore advanced diffusion models or audio integration for richer priors.”
- **Why unresolved**: The paper acknowledges that the quality of generated videos directly impacts grounding accuracy, but does not provide solutions to mitigate degradation from poor generations.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments comparing various diffusion models (e.g., higher fidelity, temporal consistency) and robustness tests on corrupted/noisy generated videos, with correlation analysis between generation quality metrics (e.g., FVD, aesthetic scores) and VMR performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How effective is the LLM‑guided subtitle matching when subtitles are unavailable, in other languages, or significantly noisy?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method relies on extracting subtitles from TVR videos and using LLaMA‑3.1 to match them. No discussion of handling missing subtitles or cross‑lingual scenarios.
- **Why unresolved**: The framework assumes clean, aligned subtitles as a given; in real‑world videos (e.g., user‑generated content), subtitles may be absent or inaccurate, which could break the pipeline.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluation on datasets without subtitles (e.g., ActivityNet Captions) and on multilingual subtitle sets; ablation studying performance when subtitles are replaced with ASR transcripts or removed entirely.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can audio cues be effectively integrated into the multimodal Mamba network, and what is the impact on temporal grounding precision?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion explicitly suggests “audio integration for richer priors” as future work.
- **Why unresolved**: The current model only fuses video, text, and generated videos; audio could provide complementary temporal signals, but the method for fusion and its benefit remain untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Design an audio branch (e.g., using pretrained audio encoder) that is fused into the Mamba network via gating; conduct experiments on TVR (which has audio) and other audio‑visual datasets, measuring improvements especially on multi‑verb queries.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the linear complexity claim hold for extremely long videos (e.g., thousands of frames) and does performance degrade due to state size constraints?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper demonstrates memory scaling up to 1024 frames, but longer TV episodes exist; Mamba’s state size might become a bottleneck.
- **Why unresolved**: Although Mamba scales linearly in memory, the computational cost and potential state dilution for very long sequences were not examined; real‑world videos can be much longer.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Test the model on videos with frame counts >2000, reporting memory, latency, and accuracy; possibly analyze state norm growth and propose hierarchical state management.

## Limitations
- Text‑controlled selection mechanism in Mamba is unspecified, leading to high uncertainty in reproduction.
- Relational GCN and 1D convolution hyperparameters are missing, affecting local reasoning and sequence modeling.
- Sensitivity to LLM subtitle scoring consistency and auxiliary video fidelity; gating may fail under misalignment.

## Confidence
- LLM‑guided subtitle matching: Medium.
- Generated video priors for motion: Medium.
- Multi‑modal Mamba with video‑guided gating: Medium‑Low.
