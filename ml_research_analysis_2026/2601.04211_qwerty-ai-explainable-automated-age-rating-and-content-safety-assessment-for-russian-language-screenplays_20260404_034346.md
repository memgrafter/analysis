# Qwerty AI: Explainable Automated Age Rating and Content Safety Assessment for Russian-Language Screenplays
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.04211
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04211
*   **Reference Count:** 25
*   **Primary Result:** An automated system for Russian screenplays achieved 80% exact-match rating accuracy and processes 700-page scripts in under 2 minutes on a 2×A100 GPU setup, using a fine-tuned Phi-3-mini model with rule-based legal anchors for explainability.

## Executive Summary
Qwerty AI addresses the slow, subjective process of manually rating Russian-language screenplays per Federal Law 436-FZ. The system automates age-rating (0+ to 18+) and content safety assessment by first segmenting scripts into scenes, then using a fine-tuned 3.8B parameter language model (Phi-3-mini) to detect violations across five categories (violence, sexual content, profanity, substances, frightening elements). To ensure legal compliance and transparency, neural predictions are augmented with deterministic rule-based "legal anchors." On a test set of 15 full scripts, the system achieved 80% exact-match document-level rating accuracy and 80–95% scene segmentation precision, while meeting strict hackathon constraints of no external APIs and an 80GB VRAM limit.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with a parser handling multiple file formats and encodings to extract raw text. A heuristic segmenter (using regex for scene headings like "INT./EXT." and capitalization patterns) splits the script into discrete scenes. Each scene is batched and analyzed by a 4-bit quantized, LoRA-fine-tuned Phi-3-mini model. The model is prompted with Federal Law 436-FZ definitions and constrained to output JSON with scene-level violation classifications and legal justifications. A rule-based lexicon (e.g., regex for "kill|death") cross-checks predictions, adding explicit statutory references as "legal anchors" to the explanations. The final document rating is derived by taking the maximum severity rating across all scenes. A contrastive loss is applied during training to encourage coherence between consecutive scene embeddings.

## Key Results
*   Achieved **80% exact-match rating accuracy** on a test set of 15 full scripts.
*   Scene segmentation precision ranged from **80% to 95%**, depending on input format (.txt vs .docx).
*   System processes a 700-page script in **under 2 minutes** on a 2×A100 GPU setup (80GB VRAM limit).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Heuristic Scene Segmentation
*   **Claim:** Intelligent script segmentation enables efficient, context-aware analysis by isolating narrative units.
*   **Mechanism:** A heuristic algorithm (regex for standard scene headings like "INT./EXT." plus capitalization patterns) splits raw screenplay text into scenes, creating discrete analysis units.
*   **Core assumption:** Properly formatted screenplays follow predictable structural patterns; scene-level analysis is sufficient for rating decisions.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Algorithm 1 achieves 80–95% segmentation precision, varying by format.
    *   Processing scales with scene count (~0.29 sec/scene), confirming segmentation's role in throughput.
*   **Break condition:** Inputs deviating from standard formatting (e.g., minimalist prose, alternative markers like "SCENE:") cause segmentation failure, leading to incorrect text aggregation and cascading rating errors.

### Mechanism 2: Fine-tuned Small LLM with Legal Anchoring
*   **Claim:** A fine-tuned small LLM (Phi-3-mini) with legal prompt anchoring provides context-sensitive violation detection within computational constraints.
*   **Mechanism:** Phi-3-mini is fine-tuned via LoRA on 4,200 labeled Russian scenes. The system prompt embeds Federal Law 436-FZ definitions, constraining outputs to JSON with legal justifications.
*   **Core assumption:** A 3.8B parameter model, when instruction-tuned and domain-adapted, captures contextual severity (e.g., historical vs. teen alcohol use) better than pure lexicon matching.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Fine-tuned model achieves 80% rating accuracy; ablation shows +18 percentage points over base model.
    *   Contrastive loss (λ=0.1) applied to consecutive scene embeddings improves document coherence.
*   **Break condition:** Domain shift (e.g., sci-fi terminology like "disintegrate" flagged as violence) or severe class imbalance (18+ content is only 4.8% of scenes) causes context misclassification and low recall.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Neural + Rule Anchoring for Explainability
*   **Claim:** Hybrid neural+rule anchoring ensures explainability and legal alignment by augmenting model predictions with deterministic lexicon matches.
*   **Mechanism:** When the neural model flags a scene, a rule-based lexicon (e.g., regex for profanity) cross-checks for explicit legal anchor terms. Explanations combine model rationale with statutory references.
*   **Core assumption:** Legal compliance requires traceable, auditable justifications; pure neural outputs are insufficient for regulatory workflows.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Lexicon matches are explicitly added as "legal anchors" in system outputs.
    *   Ablation shows rule anchors provide a modest accuracy boost (+3.3pp) but are critical for explainability; human evaluators rated explanations 3.8/5, noting strengths in legal anchors and specific text quotes.
*   **Break condition:** Lexicon is brittle to euphemisms/metaphors (e.g., "crushed his dreams" triggers violence flag), causing false positives that require human override.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Russian screenplay formatting conventions
    *   *Why needed here:* The segmentation algorithm relies on standard cues (scene headings, character caps). Misformatting directly breaks the pipeline.
    *   *Quick check question:* How would you modify the scene-heading regex to handle a script using "SCENE:" instead of "INT./EXT."?
*   **Concept:** Federal Law 436-FZ category definitions
    *   *Why needed here:* Prompt engineering and rule lexicon are built on these legal definitions. Misinterpretation causes misalignment (e.g., confusing "frightening content" with "violence").
    *   *Quick check question:* What distinguishes a "16+" alcohol depiction from an "18+" one under Article 8?
*   **Concept:** 4-bit quantization and LoRA fine-tuning
    *   *Why needed here:* These techniques enable fitting Phi-3-mini into 80GB VRAM while processing long scripts fast. Without them, the system violates hackathon constraints.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why does 4-bit quantization not degrade accuracy here, but might in other tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Parser -> Segmenter -> (Batch) Analyzer + Anchors -> Aggregator -> Report.
*   **Critical path:** Document → Parser → Segmenter → (Batch) Analyzer + Anchors → Aggregator → Report. Any failure in segmentation or batching halts throughput.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Speed vs. accuracy:** 4-bit quantization + batch processing achieves <2 min/700 pgs but limits context window; sliding-window truncation for long scenes risks missing nuance.
    *   **Explainability vs. automation:** Rule anchors increase interpretability but lower recall for creative language (e.g., profanity euphemisms).
    *   **Generalization vs. constraints:** Phi-3-mini fits VRAM limits but lacks deep Russian fluency of larger ruGPT models.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   High scene count but low violation count → possible segmentation over-splitting.
    *   Many "violence" flags in sci-fi/historical contexts → genre shift failure.
    *   Low recall on 18+ content → class imbalance in training data.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Segmentation stress test:** Run 10 poorly formatted .txt scripts (no scene headings) and measure precision/recall vs. .docx baseline. Expect ~80% vs. 95% drop.
    2.  **Quantization impact:** Compare FP16 vs. 4-bit model on 50-scene subset; verify <0.5% accuracy change and 4× VRAM reduction.
    3.  **Rule anchor ablation:** Disable lexicon matching and re-evaluate 15 test scripts; expect ~3.3pp accuracy drop and significantly fewer legal references in explanations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How can Qwerty AI be extended to multilingual screenplays while ensuring alignment with diverse international age rating laws and cultural norms?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Future Work section explicitly states "Multilingual support: Extending to other languages (English, Spanish) by fine-tuning mBERT or XLM-R."
*   **Why unresolved:** Legal frameworks vary significantly across countries (e.g., different violation categories, severity thresholds), requiring more than direct translation; cultural nuances in content interpretation also differ.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Cross-lingual evaluation on labeled screenplays from target regions, with ratings validated by local legal experts to measure both accuracy and legal compliance gaps.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Can incorporating broader narrative context and discourse-level understanding reduce errors from metaphorical language (e.g., "killed his dreams") and subtle implications (e.g., coercive suggestion)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Limitations section identifies metaphor/context as a key challenge; Error Analysis provides concrete false positive/negative examples where sentence-level analysis fails.
*   **Why unresolved:** The current pipeline analyzes scenes in isolation without tracking character relationships, plot arcs, or authorial intent, limiting pragmatic inference.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Test context-aware architectures (e.g., hierarchical models, memory networks) on a curated benchmark of metaphorical/implicit content, comparing F1 scores against the sentence-level baseline.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What data-centric or algorithmic methods can most effectively mitigate class imbalance for rare categories (e.g., 18+ content at 4.8% of scenes) to improve recall without inflating false positives?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The Limitations section notes that "18+ content is rare... leading to lower recall" despite oversampling attempts.
*   **Why unresolved:** Existing augmentation and weighting yielded "diminishing returns"; the small absolute number of 18+ scenes (200 in dataset) limits learning of extreme-case patterns.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic comparison of advanced techniques (e.g., synthetic minority oversampling with LLM-generated 18+ scenes, focal loss, curriculum learning) on a held-out set of rare-category scenes, measuring recall-precision trade-offs.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** How can screenplay-specific structural elements (e.g., scene headings, visual cues like "FADE TO BLACK", character cues) be parsed and integrated to improve violation detection and rating accuracy?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Limitations state: "Future work could incorporate structured parsing of screenplay-specific formatting elements" and note that visual descriptions provide missing context.
*   **Why unresolved:** The current system treats scripts as plain text after basic segmentation, discarding formatting metadata that signals tone, intent, or off-screen implications.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a screenplay parser (e.g., using Final Draft/Celtx format standards) to extract structural tags, then re-evaluate accuracy on scenes where formatting changes interpretation (e.g., a "dream sequence" heading might mitigate violence).

## Limitations
*   **Data & Lexicon Secrecy:** The training dataset (4,200 scenes) and full legal anchor lexicon are hackathon-confidential and not redistributable. Any reproduction must use proxy data, risking significant distribution shift and incomplete rule coverage.
*   **Format Fragility:** The segmentation heuristic assumes standard screenplay formatting. Performance on poorly formatted, minimalist, or experimental scripts (e.g., .txt without scene headings) is predicted to degrade, threatening pipeline reliability.
*   **Generalization Boundaries:** The model is fine-tuned on a limited corpus. Its ability to handle domain shifts—such as genre-specific language, historical contexts, or creative metaphors—is a major unknown, with 18+ content recall particularly vulnerable due to severe class imbalance.

## Confidence
*   **Segmentation Mechanism:** Medium (Evidence shows 80-95% precision on test formats, but robustness to real-world variations is unproven.)
*   **Fine-tuned Phi-3-mini Performance:** Medium (Internal +18pp ablation benefit and 80% accuracy are solid, but external validity is low due to secret data and narrow domain.)
*   **Hybrid Explainability Value:** Low (Rule anchors provide traceability, but the paper admits they lower recall for creative language; net utility for legal compliance vs. automation is unevaluated in adversarial settings.)
*   **Throughput Claims:** High (Processing time is a well-defined technical metric under stated hardware, with scaling per scene documented.)

## Next Checks
1.  **Segmentation Stress Test:** Run the pipeline on 20 non-standard .txt scripts (lacking "INT./EXT." headings, using alternative markers like "SCENE:"). Measure segmentation precision/recall vs. the reported .docx baseline to quantify fragility.
2.  **Lexicon Completeness Ablation:** Disable all rule-based lexicon matching and re-evaluate the 15-script test set. Measure the drop in per-category F1 (especially profanity/substances) and the reduction in legal anchor citations in explanations to isolate the lexicon's true contribution.
3.  **Genre Shift & Euphemism Probe:** Construct a targeted adversarial set of 50 scenes from sci-fi/fantasy and historical genres containing known euphemisms for violence/sex (e.g., "disintegrated," "laid with"). Measure recall for 16+/18+ categories versus a neutral genre subset to diagnose context misinterpretation.
