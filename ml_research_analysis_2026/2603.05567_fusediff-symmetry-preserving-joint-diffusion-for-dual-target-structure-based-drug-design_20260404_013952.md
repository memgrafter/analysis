# FuseDiff: Symmetry-Preserving Joint Diffusion for Dual-Target Structure-Based Drug Design

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05567
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05567
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FuseDiff achieves state-of-the-art dual-target docking: Max Vina Dock = -8.37 (vs. -7.99 best baseline) and Dual High Affinity = 49.2% (vs. 40.5%).

## Executive Summary

FuseDiff introduces an end-to-end diffusion model for dual-target structure-based drug design, jointly generating a ligand molecular graph and two pocket-specific binding poses conditioned on two protein binding sites. The core innovation is Dual-target Local Context Fusion (DLCF), which merges local geometric context from each pocket without requiring rigid alignment, and explicit bond generation, which enforces topological consistency across the two poses. The denoiser is designed to be SE(3)-equivariant, respecting independent rigid motions of each target-ligand complex. Trained on the curated BN2-DT dataset and evaluated on the DualDiff benchmark, FuseDiff sets new records: Max Vina Dock improves by 0.38 kcal/mol and Dual High Affinity by 8.7 percentage points over prior work. In a real-world GSK3β–JNK3 case study, it yields 78 drug-like molecules compared to 15 from the strongest baseline, demonstrating its potential for practical dual-target drug discovery.

## Method Summary

FuseDiff formulates the task as conditional generation of a ligand graph \(G = (V, B)\) (atom types and bond types) and two coordinate sets \(X^1, X^2\) given two pocket point clouds \(P^1, P^2\). A forward diffusion process corrupts all elements with Gaussian noise for coordinates and categorical noise for atom/bond types. The reverse process is parameterized by a denoiser that predicts the clean variables at noise level \(t\). The denoiser employs an SE(3)-equivariant message passing scheme with DLCF. Each layer first constructs two pocket-ligand point-cloud graphs, then fuses them by merging local neighborhoods into an augmented graph where ligand nodes are shared and pocket-atom neighbors are combined. Message passing on this fused graph updates ligand node and edge embeddings using information from both pockets. Ligand coordinates are then updated separately in each original point-cloud graph via equivariant displacement prediction based on the fused embeddings, thereby preserving separate poses. The model is trained with a combined loss: MSE for coordinates and bond lengths, and KL divergences for categorical type distributions. Explicit bond generation (predicting a shared bond matrix) is crucial to maintain topological consistency. Training data come from BN2-DT, a subset of BindingNet v2 containing ligands bound to at least two distinct pockets with isomorphic graph representations. Evaluation uses the DualDiff benchmark and a GSK3β–JNK3 case study.

## Key Results

- **State-of-the-art docking:** Max Vina Dock = -8.37 kcal/mol (vs. -7.99 best baseline); Dual High Affinity = 49.2% (vs. 40.5%).
- **Balanced dual-target performance:** FuseDiff attains competitive Vina scores on both pockets without post-hoc search, enabling systematic assessment of dual-pose quality.
- **Real-world applicability:** In a GSK3β–JNK3 case, generates 78 drug-like molecules compared to 15 for the strongest baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dual-target Local Context Fusion (DLCF)

- **Claim:** Dual-target Local Context Fusion (DLCF) enables expressive cross-target coupling without enforcing a global rigid alignment between the two pocket-ligand complexes.
- **Mechanism:** For each ligand atom, DLCF constructs a fused augmented graph by merging the local neighborhoods (pocket atoms within a radius) from both target-specific point-cloud graphs. Message passing on this fused graph updates shared ligand node and edge embeddings using pocket-local context from both targets. Coordinate updates are then performed separately in each original point-cloud graph, using these fused embeddings to predict target-specific displacements.
- **Core assumption:** The local geometric environment around each ligand atom (e.g., distances to nearby pocket residues) is the primary signal for target-specific binding adaptation, and this local context can be fused from two independent pockets without requiring a shared global reference frame.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Dual-target Local Context Fusion (DLCF), which fuses each ligand atom’s local context from both pockets to enable expressive joint modeling while preserving the desired symmetries.”
  - [section 3.4] “We then build an augmented graph G_aug by fusing the local neighborhoods of G_k^1 and G_k^2 for dual-target message passing… ligand nodes remain fully connected and all intra-pocket edges are preserved. Crucially, for each ligand node u∈V, its pocket-atom neighbors are inherited from both G_k^1 and G_k^2.”
  - [corpus] Weak/absent. Neighbor papers (e.g., DualDiff) rely on rigid alignment; DLCF’s local fusion is a novel architectural pattern not directly evidenced in the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the two pockets have wildly different geometric scales or residue compositions around corresponding ligand atoms, the fused local context may become noisy or conflicting, leading to unphysical coordinate predictions or failure to capture target-specific nuances.

### Mechanism 2: Explicit Bond Generation

- **Claim:** Explicit joint generation of bond types (alongside atom types and coordinates) enforces topological consistency between the two target-specific poses, ensuring they correspond to the same molecular graph.
- **Mechanism:** The diffusion process corrupts and the denoiser predicts bond types (categorical, with a “none-type” for non-bonded pairs) for all ligand atom pairs simultaneously. This forces the model to output a single, discrete bond matrix `B` that is shared by both generated poses `X1` and `X2`. Without this, post-hoc bond inference from each pose independently would likely produce inconsistent bond assignments (e.g., a bonded pair in one pose but not the other).
- **Core assumption:** The true joint distribution `p(G, X1, X2 | P1, P2)` has a strong dependency where the bond topology `G` (specifically `B`) is a deterministic or near-deterministic function of the ligand identity and must be invariant across the two binding poses for a chemically valid molecule.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Together with explicit bond generation, FuseDiff enforces topological consistency across the two poses under a shared graph while allowing target-specific geometric adaptation in each pocket.”
  - [section 3.3] “To enhance topology consistency: generating different X1 and X2 while keeping both consistent with the generated G, FuseDiff generates G—including both atom types V and bond types B… Without explicitly generating B, bonds are inferred post hoc for each pose independently. In our ablation study… removing explicit bond-type modeling breaks topology consistency.”
  - [section D (ablation)] “W/o bond generative modeling… Dual-Validity drops from 61% in the full model to 3%… indicating that generative bond modeling is essential.”
- **Break condition:** If the training data contain numerous ligands with ambiguous or strained bonding (e.g., charges, aromaticity mismatches) or if the bond diffusion schedule is poorly tuned, the model may struggle to predict a chemically plausible, shared bond matrix, collapsing validity.

### Mechanism 3: SE(3)-Equivariant Denoiser

- **Claim:** The SE(3)-equivariant design of the denoiser (via separate coordinate updates on each point-cloud graph and use of invariant geometric features) ensures the learned joint distribution respects rigid motions applied independently to each pocket-ligand complex.
- **Mechanism:** The denoiser’s coordinate update module (Eq. 7) operates independently on `G_k^1` and `G_k^2`. The predicted displacement for a ligand atom in complex `k` depends only on invariant inputs (scalar distances `d_k,uv`, updated node/edge embeddings) and scales the relative vector `r_k,u - r_k,w`. Thus, applying a rigid transform `T_g` to `(P1, X1)` does not affect updates for `k=2`, and updates for `k=1` transform equivariantly. The fused graph’s edge features for ligand-ligand edges use symmetric sums/differences of distances from both complexes, which are invariant to swapping `k`.
- **Core assumption:** The true data distribution `p(G, X1, X2 | P1, P2)` is invariant to independent rigid translations/rotations of each target’s coordinate frame (i.e., the absolute positions of pockets are arbitrary; only relative geometry matters). This is standard in structure-based drug design.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “symmetry-preserving… FuseDiff satisfies the desired symmetries.”
  - [section 3.5] Proof of Proposition 3.2 validates R1 (permutation invariance) and R2 (SE(3) invariance) by showing the base distribution and denoiser transformations are equivariant/invariant.
  - [section 3.4] “The update is SE(3)-equivariant because φ_r uses only invariant inputs and scales a relative vector.”
- **Break condition:** If the pocket representations `P1` and `P2` are not properly centered (e.g., not translated to their center of mass) or if the model inadvertently injects absolute coordinate information into non-geometric features (e.g., via positional embeddings tied to a global frame), the equivariance property could be violated, harming generalization to new pocket alignments.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain)**  
Input Encoder → DLCF Stack (L layers) → Denoising Heads → Diffusion Scheduler

**Critical path**  
DLCF fusion → augmented graph message passing → ligand node/edge embedding fusion → separate coordinate displacement prediction per pocket

**Design tradeoffs**
- Cross-target coupling vs. Symmetry preservation: Overly strong coupling (e.g., forcing identical coordinates) breaks symmetry; DLCF uses local context fusion and separate coordinate updates to balance this.
- Explicit bonds vs. Sampling efficiency: Generating bonds discretely increases action space and can harm validity if not well-trained; the tradeoff is enforced topological consistency for dual-pose validity.
- Augmented graph size: Fusing neighborhoods doubles pocket-atom neighbors per ligand node, increasing computational cost; the 3D k-NN construction keeps local graphs sparse.

**Failure signatures**
- Low Dual-Validity (<10%): indicates bond generation failure or DLCF fusion failure; check bond prediction head and augmented graph construction.
- High Vina Score disparity between pockets: model generates a pose good for one pocket but invalid for the other; suggests insufficient cross-target coupling or training data imbalance.
- Geometrically distorted ligands (unrealistic bond lengths/angles): likely due to poor coordinate update parameterization or missing `L_{b-len}` loss; check displacement network and bond-length regularization.
- Model fails to condition on both pockets (ignores P2): could happen if DLCF is ablated; check by comparing Vina scores on both pockets.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Dual-Validity sanity check:** Sample a small batch (e.g., 100) from the trained model. Use OpenBabel to assemble a molecule from `(V^0, B^0)` and each `(X1^0, X2^0)`. Report the percentage where both assemblies yield the same valid molecule. Expected >50% (paper reports 61% on DDF).
2. **Baseline docking comparison:** Generate 10 ligands per pocket pair in the DualDiff benchmark. Compute `Max Vina Dock` and `Dual High Affinity`. Expect FuseDiff to outperform DualDiff/CompDiff on both metrics, demonstrating balanced dual-target performance.
3. **Ablation replication:** Re-run training with either (a) no bond generation (predict only atom types, infer bonds post-hoc per pose) or (b) no DLCF (sequential generation with shared graph constraint). Measure a catastrophic drop in Dual-Validity to confirm mechanism necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Question:** Does FuseDiff effectively extend to multi-target SBDD with K>2 targets?
   - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 3.4.1 states "The above design extends naturally to 𝐾 targets" but only K=2 is evaluated.
   - **Why unresolved:** The augmented graph neighbor fusion may become computationally expensive or lose expressiveness with more targets; symmetry properties (R1-R4) have not been verified for K>2.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Curate or simulate multi-target datasets (3+ targets), measure generation quality (Dual-Validity, docking scores), and verify if R1-R4 hold empirically.

2. **Question:** How does the graph-isomorphism constraint in BN2-DT dataset construction affect the diversity and real-world applicability of generated dual-target ligands?
   - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] BN2-DT requires identical ligand graphs across targets (Appendix A), yielding only 58k pairs from 197k ligands.
   - **Why unresolved:** This biases training toward ligands that maintain identical topology across targets, potentially excluding ligands with target-specific conformational adaptations common in polypharmacology.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare chemical diversity of BN2-DT vs. single-target datasets; evaluate on test cases where true dual-target ligands would have different bond types or ring systems if such data existed.

3. **Question:** What is the quantitative contribution of DLCF's specific design choices (symmetric distance statistics, separate coordinate updates) to balancing cross-target coupling (R3) and symmetry preservation (R4)?
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] DLCF uses symmetric statistics (s,δ) for ligand-ligand edges and separate coordinate updates per target (Eqs. 3,7); Appendix B proves R1-R4 with these choices.
   - **Why unresolved:** The proof shows existence but not necessity; alternative fusion mechanisms might achieve similar properties with better performance, and the relative impact on docking scores is unknown.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate DLCF components (e.g., replace s,δ with raw distances; share coordinate updates) and measure changes in Dual-Validity, Vina Dock, and direct tests of R3/R4.

4. **Question:** What are the dominant failure modes behind the 39% of samples that fail Dual-Validity?
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Dual-Validity is 61% (Section 4.3); explicit bond generation is necessary but not sufficient for full validity.
   - **Why unresolved:** Without understanding failure causes, it's unclear whether to improve bond-type prediction, coordinate generation, or DLCF's geometric consistency enforcement.
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Error analysis categorizing invalid samples by chemical rule violations (valence, bond length, stereochemistry); correlate with molecular complexity or pocket characteristics to identify systematic weaknesses.

## Limitations
