# Humans vs Vision-Language Models: A Unified Measure of Narrative Coherence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25537  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25537  
- **Reference count:** 10  
- **Primary result:** Humans consistently outperform VLMs on text-oriented coherence metrics, while some VLMs exceed humans on multimodal grounding; the composite Narrative Coherence Score (NCS) reveals a persistent human-model gap.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how vision-language models (VLMs) compare to humans in generating coherent narratives grounded in image sequences. The authors propose five complementary discourse metrics—coreference, implicit discourse relation diversity, topic switch, character persistence, and multimodal character grounding—and combine them into a unified Narrative Coherence Score (NCS). Using the Visual Writing Prompts (VWP) corpus, they generate stories from five state-of-the-art VLMs under short and long prompt conditions and evaluate them against human-written stories.

Results show humans lead on all text-oriented metrics (coreference, discourse diversity, topic switch, character persistence), while GPT-4o surpasses humans on multimodal character grounding. The composite NCS (both arithmetic and geometric means) demonstrates a consistent gap: human NCS_arith = 0.50 vs best model 0.46; human NCS_geom = 0.36 vs best model 0.32. Explicit prompting (long prompt) only partially narrows the gap, with Llama 4 Scout showing significant improvement. The findings indicate that VLMs systematically differ from humans in discourse organization despite surface fluency, highlighting the need for more nuanced evaluation beyond visual grounding.

## Method Summary
The analysis uses 60 image sequences from the VWP corpus for short prompts and 54 after quality control for long prompts. Stories are produced by five VLMs (InternVL3-78B, Qwen3-VL-235B, Claude-4.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, Llama-4-Scout) and by human writers (original VWP; additional long-prompt humans via MTurk). All stories are segmented using [SEP] tokens. Five coherence metrics are computed: Coreference (R), Implicit discourse diversity (D), Topic switch (T), Character persistence (ChP), and Multimodal character grounding (MCG). Coreference uses Link-Append; discourse relations are classified by DeDisCo; topics are derived with BERTopic trained on the combined corpus; character metrics align coreference chains with MovieNet annotations; MCG combines character continuity and the GROOViST visual grounding score. Each metric is normalized via atanh transformation. The NCS aggregates normalized metrics using arithmetic and geometric means. Paired t-tests by visual sequence assess significance.

## Key Results
- Humans score higher on coreference (R=0.77 vs best model 0.73), discourse diversity (D=0.46 vs best model 0.43), topic switch (T=0.43 vs best model 0.38), and character persistence (ChP=0.39 vs best model 0.38), with most differences statistically significant.
- GPT-4o outperforms humans on multimodal character grounding (MCG=0.58 vs human 0.45, p<.05).
- Composite NCS shows a consistent gap: NCS_arith (human 0.50, best model 0.46) and NCS_geom (human 0.36, best model 0.32).
- Under long-prompting, only Llama 4 Scout significantly narrows its NCS_geom gap; other models show limited improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Human narratives systematically outperform VLM-generated ones on text-oriented coherence metrics (coreference, discourse diversity, topic switch, character persistence).
- **Mechanism**: Humans maintain tighter referential focus (fewer, longer coreference chains), employ a more varied set of implicit discourse relations, and transition topics more frequently, creating a denser, more dynamic narrative structure.
- **Core assumption**: These metrics capture fundamental aspects of narrative coherence that are consistently expressed in human storytelling.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Humans achieve higher scores: coreference R=0.77 vs best model 0.73; discourse diversity D=0.46 vs best model 0.43; topic switch T=0.43 vs best model 0.38; character persistence ChP=0.39 vs best model 0.38.
  - [section] Tables 2–5 show humans lead on each metric, with statistical significance in most pairwise comparisons.
  - [corpus] Related work (e.g., “Are Large Language Models Capable of Generating Human-Level Narratives?”) reports similar deficits in LLM-generated narratives, supporting the pattern.
- **Break condition**: If the evaluation task prioritizes extreme visual literalism (e.g., instructional captions), text-oriented metrics may become less relevant; models could close the gap with targeted optimization for these specific measures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Some VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) exceed humans on multimodal character grounding, reflecting a strategy of visual literalism.
- **Mechanism**: VLMs are trained to maximize alignment between text and visual inputs; thus, they explicitly re‑mention characters that appear in the image sequence. Human writers abstract beyond the literal visual content, leading to lower but potentially more creative grounding.
- **Core assumption**: Multimodal character grounding (MCG) validly measures how tightly a narrative is anchored to the visual scene.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “Some models match or exceed humans on multimodal grounding (MCG: human 0.45, GPT-4o 0.58).”
  - [section] Table 6: GPT-4o MCG=0.58 vs human 0.45 (p<.05).
  - [corpus] Neighbors like “VIST-GPT” and “MedGround” highlight grounding as a key challenge in visual storytelling, confirming the metric’s relevance.
- **Break condition**: In tasks requiring high abstraction (e.g., metaphor, implicit meaning), visual literalism may degrade coherence, and humans could regain the advantage.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The composite Narrative Coherence Score (NCS) reveals a persistent human‑model gap that is less evident in individual metrics, and explicit prompting yields only partial convergence.
- **Mechanism**: Aggregating multiple metrics amplifies the cumulative advantage of humans across most text‑oriented dimensions; even models that excel in multimodal grounding (e.g., GPT‑4o) cannot offset their weaker scores elsewhere. Prompt engineering improves some metrics for some models but does not fundamentally alter this multi‑dimensional profile.
- **Core assumption**: Arithmetic and geometric means of normalized metric values reliably represent overall narrative coherence.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] NCS_arith: human 0.50, best model 0.46; NCS_geom: human 0.36, best model 0.32.
  - [section] Table 7 shows humans highest on both NCS variants. Table 8: under long prompt, only Llama 4 Scout significantly narrows its gap (geometric mean, t=2.44, p<.05); others remain similar.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like “LogicGaze” propose composite benchmarks, underscoring that single metrics are insufficient to capture coherence.
- **Break condition**: If the metric set omits critical coherence dimensions or if models are explicitly optimized for the NCS formulation, the gap could diminish.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Coreference resolution  
  - **Why needed here**: Forms the basis for coreference score R and both character metrics (ChC, ChS). Tracks how entities are referenced across segments.  
  - **Quick check question**: How does the ratio of average chain size to number of chains (R = S/C) reflect referential continuity?

- **Concept**: Implicit discourse relation classification  
  - **Why needed here**: Provides the diversity score D by labeling semantic relations between adjacent segments (e.g., Elaboration, Contrast).  
  - **Quick check question**: Why does a higher proportion of causal relations (vs. temporal) suggest more sophisticated narrative structuring?

- **Concept**: Topic modeling (BERTopic)  
  - **Why needed here**: Assigns topic labels to each segment to compute topic switch T, measuring topical progression.  
  - **Quick check question**: What does a sharper drop in topic switch when reducing nr_topics (Figure 3) indicate about human vs. model topic granularity?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Data loader → Segmenter → Coreference module (Link-Append); Discourse module (DeDisCo); Topic module (BERTopic) → Character persistence module (uses Coreference + MovieNet) → Multimodal grounding module (uses Coreference + GROOViST) → Normalizer (atanh) → Aggregator (arithmetic & geometric means) → Evaluator (paired t-tests).

### Critical path
1. Load and segment stories.
2. Run coreference extraction (slowest step; required for two downstream modules).
3. Run discourse classification on all adjacent segment pairs.
4. Assign topics with fixed BERTopic model.
5. For character metrics: align coreference chains with MovieNet character names; compute ChC/ChS.
6. For multimodal grounding: compute character continuity + GROOViST (external tool).
7. Normalize metrics (atanh).
8. Compute NCS variants.
9. Perform statistical tests.

### Design tradeoffs
- Normalization: atanh bounds scores but may distort distributions; min-max scaling is an alternative.
- Aggregation: Geometric mean penalizes imbalances more strongly, better reflecting overall coherence but sensitive to outliers.
- Topic model: Using one model across conditions ensures comparability but may not capture condition-specific topic distributions.
- Off-the-shelf models: Coreference and discourse classifiers are convenient but their errors propagate; fine-tuning on narrative data could improve accuracy but reduces generality.
- Character annotations: Limited to MovieNet’s coverage; unannotated characters are ignored.

### Failure signatures
- Coreference model returns no chains → character metrics default to zero, breaking NCS.
- Topic model assigns identical topic to all segments → topic switch ≈ 0, indicating model failure or data shift.
- Discourse classifier outputs only one relation type → diversity score near zero.
- GROOViST alignment fails → MCG scores become noisy or zero.
- Inconsistent [SEP] segmentation → all downstream metrics misaligned.
- Composite NCS masks which specific metric is underperforming; always inspect individual scores.

### First 3 experiments
1. Short-prompt replication: Generate VLM stories for 60 sequences under short prompt; compute all metrics; verify human leads in Tables 2–7.
2. Long-prompt robustness: Collect new human stories (AMT) and generate VLM stories with long prompt; compare NCS gaps to short-prompt results (Table 8).
3. Perplexity probe: Compute perplexity of human and model stories using open‑source VLMs (Qwen3‑VL, Llama4 Scout, InternVL3); confirm human texts are less predictable, supporting the coherence gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does the proposed Narrative Coherence Score (NCS) correlate with human judgments of narrative coherence?** The paper defines NCS but does not validate it against human coherence assessments. Without human ratings, it is unknown whether the weighted combination of metrics actually reflects human intuitions about narrative quality, nor whether the geometric mean’s penalty for imbalance is desirable. Collecting human coherence ratings for the same stories and computing correlation/regression with NCS would resolve this.
2. **To what extent do the observed coherence differences generalize beyond the Visual Writing Prompts (VWP) corpus to other visual storytelling datasets or genres?** The study exclusively uses the VWP corpus and acknowledges its potential prior exposure to pre‑training data, but does not test whether findings hold in different contexts. Replicating the full analysis on at least one additional visual storytelling dataset (e.g., VIST, comic panels) would reveal whether the human‑model gap is robust or dataset‑specific.
3. **Why do some VLMs achieve higher Multimodal Character Grounding (MCG) scores than humans, and does this reflect superior visual-text alignment or a lack of narrative abstraction?** Results show GPT‑4o obtains MCG=0.58 while humans score 0.45. The authors suggest that "human stories are less constrained by what is directly visible" and models "place considerable weight on staying close to the image content," but this explanation is not empirically tested. A fine‑grained analysis of character mention timing relative to visual presence, lexical diversity, and human evaluations of creativity could clarify whether high MCG reflects literal grounding or a deficit in abstraction.

## Limitations
- Exact data specifics (sequence IDs, full prompts) are undisclosed, hindering precise replication.
- The evaluation pipeline relies on off‑the‑shelf tools (Link‑Append, DeDisCo, BERTopic) whose errors propagate, and atanh normalization may distort score distributions.
- Generalizability is limited to 60/54 VWP sequences; human baseline from MTurk may lack professional quality; multimodal grounding is restricted to MovieNet‑annotated characters.

## Confidence

| Humans outperform VLMs on text‑oriented metrics | High | Consistent statistically significant leads across coreference, discourse diversity, topic switch, character persistence (p<.05 in most pairwise tests). |
| Some VLMs exceed humans on multimodal grounding | Medium | GPT‑4o shows significantly higher MCG (p<.05), but limited to one model; metric may reward visual literalism over creative abstraction. |

## Next Checks
1. **Replication with expanded data and models**: Run the pipeline on additional visual storytelling datasets (e.g., VIST, PhotoStory) and newer VLMs (e.g., GPT‑4.1, Claude 5) to test generalizability.
2. **Metric robustness analysis**: Manually annotate a subset for coreference chains and discourse relations; quantify agreement with automatic tools and assess impact on NCS. Also evaluate alternative normalization and aggregation methods.
3. **Human–model hybrid evaluation**: Collect expert human coherence judgments on the same stories and correlate with NCS to validate that the composite score approximates perceived coherence.
