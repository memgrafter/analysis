# Mixture of Predefined Experts: Maximizing Data Usage on Vertical Federated Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12708
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12708
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A novel VFL framework that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy under severe data misalignment (91.98% on CIFAR-10 with 70% missing data) while reducing communication by 200× and providing inherent robustness and interpretability.

## Executive Summary
Vertical Federated Learning (VFL) typically assumes a fully overlapping sample set across participants, which is often unrealistic. Split-MoPE introduces a method to train effectively despite incomplete alignment, where different participants possess different subsets of samples. It combines the Split Learning paradigm with a Mixture of Predefined Experts (MoPE) head, where each expert is dedicated to a specific, predefined alignment pattern (i.e., a specific subset of participants that are jointly present). By leveraging pretrained, frozen encoders for feature extraction, the system requires only a single round of communication (forward pass of embeddings) to train the classifier head located at the active participant. This design maximizes the usage of all available, misaligned data, reduces communication overhead dramatically, and yields per-sample interpretability of each participant's contribution through the router's weighting mechanism. The approach demonstrates superior robustness to noisy participants and strong empirical performance on vision and tabular benchmarks.

## Method Summary
Split-MoPE operates in a standard VFL setting with an active and multiple passive participants. Each participant uses a pretrained, frozen encoder (e.g., DINOv2 for images, Qwen3-Embedding for tabular data) to transform its local raw features into fixed-dimensional embeddings. During training, for a given batch, the active participant collects the embeddings from all passive participants for the samples they jointly possess. Missing participant embeddings for a sample are handled via zero-padding. These concatenated embeddings form a single aggregated vector `z_agg`. The core innovation is the MoPE classification head, which consists of a router (a two-layer MLP) and a set of experts (shallow MLP classifiers). The number of experts equals the number of "interesting" alignment patterns—subsets of participants that include the active party, determined privately beforehand. The router takes `z_agg` and outputs a sigmoid weight for each expert. Critically, each expert `i` is hardwired to process only the sub-vector of `z_agg` corresponding to its predefined alignment pattern (e.g., only the embeddings from the active party and Participant 1). The final prediction is a normalized weighted sum of all expert outputs. Crucially, only the MoPE head (router and experts) is trained via backpropagation at the active participant; the pretrained encoders remain fixed. This decoupling means the only communication required for training is the one-time forward pass of the embeddings from the passive parties, after which the active party can train centrally without further exchange.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10 with 70% missing samples (`p_miss=0.7`), Split-MoPE achieves **91.98% accuracy**, outperforming LASER* (90.58%) and SplitNN* (90.80%).
- On Breast Cancer Wisconsin, it reaches an **F1 score of 87.68** vs. LASER*'s 86.19 under misalignment.
- It reduces performance degradation from noisy/malicious participants by **up to 25×** compared to baselines and cuts the communication footprint for training by **200×** relative to standard SplitNN.
- The router's independent sigmoid gating provides **per-sample interpretability**, quantifying each collaborator's contribution to the prediction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Alignment-Specific Experts Eliminate Full Overlap Requirement
- **Claim:** Split-MoPE maximizes data usage in misaligned VFL by using fixed experts, each dedicated to a specific data alignment pattern (subset of participants present), thereby eliminating the need for full sample overlap.
- **Mechanism:** The Mixture of Predefined Experts (MoPE) layer replaces the standard classification head. The number of experts equals the number of "interesting" alignments (|P̃(K)|). During the forward pass, the aggregated embedding `z_agg` (from concatenated participant embeddings) is logically split into sub-vectors corresponding to each alignment pattern. Each expert processes only the sub-vector for its predefined alignment. The router (a two-layer MLP) assigns a weight via sigmoid to each expert's output, and a normalized weighted sum produces the final prediction. Padding is used for missing data, allowing all experts to be trained and interact.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States MoPE "uses predefined experts to process specific data alignments" and "maximizing data usage... without requiring full sample overlap."
  - [section 4.2] Details the expert definition: "the number of experts is strictly determined by the number of interesting alignments" and describes the forward pass logic with sub-vector decomposition.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence. Neighboring papers (e.g., PBM-VFL, pFedMoE) address VFL privacy or heterogeneous FL but do not propose predefined, alignment-specific expert routing. This mechanism is a novel architectural contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If the number of distinct alignment patterns (|P̃(K)|) is extremely large (e.g., many participants with highly sporadic participation), the number of required experts could become computationally or statistically intractable to train effectively.

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Training via Frozen Encoders Achieves Single-Round Communication
- **Claim:** Single-round training and drastically reduced communication footprint are achieved by decoupling the training of feature extractors and the classifier head, using pretrained and frozen encoders.
- **Mechanism:** Pretrained, off-the-shelf models (e.g., DINOv2 for vision, Qwen3-Embedding for tabular) are used as fixed feature extractors (`f_k`). Participants compute embeddings `Z_k = f_k(X_k)` once and transmit them. The active participant trains only the MoPE head (`h`), which consists of the router and the set of expert classifiers. No backpropagation occurs through the encoders, eliminating the need for multiple communication rounds of gradient exchange.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Mentions "leveraging pretrained encoders for target data domains, Split-MoPE achieves state-of-the-art performance in a single communication round."
  - [section 4.2] Explicitly states: "pretrained backbones require only a single forward pass per training" and contrasts with multi-round end-to-end training.
  - [section 7] Quantifies the communication reduction: "decoupled training results in a process that exchanges 38.4MB of data, yielding a 200× reduction" compared to standard SplitNN training.
- **Break condition:** If no suitable pretrained encoder exists for a participant's data modality or domain, this mechanism fails. The frozen encoders must produce embeddings that contain sufficient discriminative signal for the final classification task; otherwise, performance will be bottlenecked at the feature extraction stage.

### Mechanism 3: Independent Sigmoid Gating Enables Robustness and Interpretability
- **Claim:** Inherent robustness to noisy/malicious participants and per-sample interpretability arise from the router's independent sigmoid gating, which allows experts to collaborate rather than compete exclusively.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike standard MoE's softmax (forcing mutually exclusive, sum-to-one weights), Split-MoPE's router uses sigmoid activations, producing independent weights `g_i` in [0,1]. This permits multiple experts to contribute to a single prediction (collaborative routing). During training, if an expert's corresponding alignment consistently involves noisy data (e.g., `{Noisy, Active}`), the router learns to assign that expert a weight near zero for samples with that alignment. The final weight vector for a sample is interpretable as the proportional contribution of each participant's data (via mapping back to alignments).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Claims "inherent robustness against malicious or noisy participants" and "per-sample interpretability by quantifying each collaborator’s contribution."
  - [section 4.2] Explains the sigmoid choice: "encourages experts to collaborate rather than engage in mutually exclusive selection" and links this to achieving "Vertical Baseline Recovery" and "Local Robustness."
  - [section 7] Provides empirical evidence: Table 4 shows router weights assigned to experts for noisy participants are near zero. Table 3 shows Split-MoPE has significantly smaller performance decay with noisy parties compared to LASER* and SplitNN*.
- **Break condition:** If noise is injected precisely to mimic the statistical properties of clean data within a specific alignment pattern, the router may fail to identify and suppress that expert. Also, if the "interesting" alignment set (P̃(K)) is very large and sparse, the router may struggle to learn stable weights for all experts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Split Learning (SplitNN)**
  - *Why needed here:* Split-MoPE is built upon the Split Learning paradigm, where a model is partitioned across participants. The foundational workflow—local forward pass to generate embeddings, aggregation at active participant, and gradient return—is the baseline that Split-MoPE modifies. Understanding this baseline is essential to see how MoPE changes the aggregation/classification stage and how decoupled training alters the communication pattern.
  - *Quick check question:* In standard SplitNN with full alignment, what information is exchanged in a single training round, and why does this require multiple rounds for end-to-end training?
- **Concept: Mixture of Experts (MoE)**
  - *Why needed here:* Split-MoPE adapts the core MoE components (router + experts) but redefines their semantics. The engineer must understand standard MoE's dynamic, data-driven routing (softmax, top-k) to appreciate MoPE's key divergence: predefined, alignment-based experts and independent sigmoid gating. This conceptual shift is central to handling misalignment.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the routing function (softmax vs. sigmoid) and the expert selection strategy (learned pattern vs. predefined alignment) differ between standard MoE and Split-MoPE's MoPE?
- **Concept: Pretrained Feature Extractors & Decoupled Training**
  - *Why needed here:* This is the lever for communication efficiency. The engineer must grasp that by freezing pre-trained encoders, the feature extraction phase becomes a one-time, non-learning operation. The only trainable component is the head at the active participant. This decoupling is what enables the single-round training claim.
  - *Quick check question:* What specific change in the training computation graph (and thus communication) occurs when encoders are frozen and treated as fixed feature extractors compared to standard end-to-end SplitNN training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Participant Encoders (f_k) -> Active Participant Aggregator -> MoPE Head (Router G + Experts E_i) -> Prediction & Weight Aggregator
- **Critical path:**
  1.  **Offline (One-time):** Each participant runs their frozen `f_k` on their local dataset to generate and store embeddings `Z_k`. (This can be pre-computed or done just-in-time as the single communication forward pass).
  2.  **Training Round:** For each batch, participants with aligned samples send their `Z_k` to the active participant. Active participant:
      a.  Aggregates `z_agg` with zero-padding for missing participants.
      b.  Router `G` computes all sigmoid weights `g_i`.
      c.  Each expert `E_i` processes its designated sub-vector `z'_agg_i` (extracted from `z_agg` based on its predefined alignment pattern).
      d.  Outputs are weighted, summed, and normalized to get `y_pred`.
      e.  Loss is computed, gradients update *only* the router and all experts.
      f.  No parameters are sent back to participants; training is centralized at the active party using the received embeddings.
  3.  **Inference:** For a query sample, the active participant determines its alignment pattern (based on which participants have the sample). It requests `Z_k` from the relevant participants, then follows steps 2b-2d to predict. Per-sample contribution weights `C_k` are computed for interpretability.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Expert Count (|P̃(K)|) vs. Performance/Complexity:** More participants or more sporadic participation increases |P̃(K)|, linearly increasing the number of experts (model size) and router computation. This can harm performance if data per alignment becomes too sparse.
  - **Router Sparsity (Sigmoid Freedom) vs. Robustness:** The unsupervised router can become highly sparse (choosing 1-2 experts) or collaborative. Sparsity aids robustness (ignoring noisy experts) but may underutilize data. Collaboration may use more data but risk incorporating noise. The paper shows it self-regulates.
  - **Pretrained Encoder Capacity vs. Resource Use:** Larger pretrained encoders (ViT-L vs. ViT-S) give better and more robust performance but require more local computation and produce larger embeddings, increasing the single communication payload.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1.  **Router Collapse to Uniform/Zero:** All `g_i` become nearly identical (ignoring alignment information) or all near zero (no useful prediction). Monitor the variance of `g_i` across a batch.
  2.  **Performance Parity with Local Model at high `p_miss`:** If MoPE's accuracy drops to the local baseline even with moderate `p_miss` (e.g., 0.5), it indicates the router is failing to weight informative alignments, effectively discarding useful passive data.
  3.  **High Variance in Per-Sample Contributions:** Extremely erratic `C_k` values (Eq. 4) for samples from the same alignment pattern suggest the router is not learning stable, alignment-specific policies, leading to unreliable interpretability and potential robustness issues.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline Alignment Recovery Test:** Train Split-MoPE, LASER*, and SplitNN* with `p_miss = 0` (perfect alignment). Verify MoPE matches or slightly exceeds baselines, confirming it recovers the "Vertical Baseline" (ideal VFL performance) without penalty in the ideal case. (Anchors: Table 2, `p_miss=0` columns).
  2.  **Misalignment Robustness Curve:** Systematically increase `p_miss` (e.g., 0.0, 0.1, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9). Plot accuracy/F1 for all methods. The key pass/fail is MoPE's degradation rate being significantly shallower than LASER* and SplitNN*, especially at `p_miss >= 0.5`. (Anchors: Table 2, Figure 6).
  3.  **Noisy Participant Stress Test:** Introduce `N` participants that send Gaussian noise embeddings. With `p_miss=0.1` and `N=1..5`, measure accuracy delta vs. no-noise scenario. A passing result is MoPE's delta being consistently smaller than LASER* and SplitNN*'s, demonstrating superior robustness. Inspect Table 4 to confirm router assigns ~0 weight to experts for alignments containing noise. (Anchors: Table 3, Table 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Alternative training strategies** could further enhance MoPE's performance, especially under high data missingness or noisy participants. The current self-regulated training lacks explicit regularization that might prevent routing collapse or encourage better expert utilization.
- **Performance in heterogeneous multimodal VFL settings** (e.g., vision, text, tabular) remains unexplored. The interaction of multiple modalities within the MoPE framework and how the router learns to weight these diverse sources is an open question.
- **Scalability to a large number of participants** is a major concern. MoPE defines an expert for each alignment pattern that includes the active participant, leading to an exponential increase (2^(K-1) experts for K participants). No solution is offered for federations with many participants, which threatens feasibility.

## Limitations
- The number of experts grows combinatorially with the number of participants and the diversity of alignment patterns, posing severe scalability challenges for large federations.
- The approach is critically dependent on the availability of high-quality, domain-relevant pretrained encoders for each participant's data modality; lacking such models could cripple performance.
- The exact implementation details of the private set intersection (PSI) to enumerate alignment patterns and the precise padding/sub-vector extraction strategy are underspecified, creating a risk of implementation error.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (alignment-specific experts):** **Medium**. Experiments show improved accuracy under misalignment, but scalability to many participants remains untested.
- **Mechanism 2 (single-round training):** **High**. Decoupled design directly yields a single forward pass; communication reduction (200×) is empirically measured.
- **Mechanism 3 (robustness/interpretability):** **Medium**. Validated only on simple Gaussian noise; adaptive attacks and stability of per-sample contributions are not thoroughly evaluated.

## Next Checks
1.  **Scalability stress test:** Systematically increase participants *K* (e.g., 2→3→5→10) on CIFAR-10. Track expert count, samples per expert, and accuracy vs. missing rate *p<sub>miss</sub>*. Expect performance collapse when data per expert becomes scarce.
2.  **Padding strategy ablation:** Compare zero-padding (presumed), learned missing embeddings (trainable vector), and sentinel values. Measure impact on accuracy and robustness to Gaussian-noise participants.
3.  **Adaptive noise attack:** Introduce a malicious participant that sends embeddings correlated with clean data (e.g., clean embeddings plus small adversarial perturbation) and appears only in alignments with honest parties. Quantify accuracy drop and inspect router weight assignments to test robustness assumptions.
