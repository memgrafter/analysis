# BinaryPPO: Efficient Policy Optimization for Binary Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02708
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02708
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** BinaryPPO improves accuracy by 40–60 percentage points over supervised fine-tuning on noisy, imbalanced binary classification benchmarks, achieving up to 99% performance.

## Executive Summary
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) struggles with binary classification under realistic conditions of label noise, class imbalance, and sparse supervision. This paper recasts the problem as an offline reinforcement learning task, applying Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a novel confidence-weighted reward. The reward scales with the model’s log-probability, explicitly penalizing uncertain or incorrect predictions. Evaluated on eight domain-specific benchmarks using Qwen 2.5-3B and Gemma 2-2B, BinaryPPO dramatically outperforms SFT and vanilla PPO baselines, demonstrating robustness and generalization to out-of-distribution moderation and causal reasoning tasks while preserving truthfulness and fairness.

## Method Summary
BinaryPPO reformulates binary classification as a static-dataset, offline RL problem. The policy network outputs a probability for the positive class. The reward function is defined as the signed log-probability: `reward = sign(label) * log(p)`, where `label` is +1/-1. This construction heavily penalizes low-confidence or incorrect predictions. Training uses standard PPO with an added entropy regularization term and an equal-sampling strategy to mitigate class imbalance. The policy is updated on fixed batches without online interaction, making it suitable for supervised dataset settings.

## Key Results
- Accuracy gains of 40–60 percentage points over SFT and vanilla PPO across eight benchmarks, with near-saturation (up to 99%) on several tasks.
- Strong out-of-distribution generalization to causal reasoning and cross-dataset moderation scenarios.
- Preserved truthfulness and fairness in normative assessments, with no increase in toxic or biased outputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The confidence-weighted reward directly addresses the core failure modes of SFT in noisy/imbalanced settings. By tying reward to the model’s own predictive confidence, the method suppresses learning from low-quality or uncertain signal (e.g., mislabeled or ambiguous examples). Entropy regularization prevents premature convergence to a degenerate policy, while equal sampling ensures the minor class is not ignored during updates. This combination allows robust policy optimization from a static, imperfect dataset—a regime where standard supervised learning fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning:** Enables learning from a fixed dataset without environment interaction. *Why needed:* Avoids costly online deployment during training. *Quick check:* Does performance improve with more logged data?
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** A stable on-policy algorithm using a clipped surrogate objective. *Why needed:* Prevents destructive policy updates in the offline setting. *Quick check:* Is the policy update magnitude constrained?
- **Confidence-Weighted Reward Shaping:** Reward is scaled by model’s predictive log-probability. *Why needed:* Automatically down-weights uncertain or incorrect predictions. *Quick check:* Does reward magnitude correlate with prediction confidence?
- **Entropy Regularization:** Adds an entropy bonus to the policy loss. *Why needed:* Encourages exploration and prevents mode collapse. *Quick check:* Does policy entropy remain above a minimum threshold during training?
- **Class-Imbalance Handling via Equal Sampling:** Oversamples minority class examples per batch. *Why needed:* Ensures balanced gradient updates. *Quick check:* Is the batch class distribution approximately uniform?
- **Binary Classification as a Bandit Problem:** Each prediction is a single-step decision with immediate reward. *Why needed:* Allows applying RL tools to supervised tasks. *Quick check:* Is the reward immediate and environment-independent?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Static Dataset -> Batch Sampler (equal sampling) -> Policy Network (outputs p(y=1|x)) -> Reward Calculator (sign(label)*log(p)) -> PPO Optimizer (with entropy bonus) -> Updated Policy

**Critical path:**  
1. Sample a balanced batch from dataset.  
2. Compute log-probabilities for each example’s label.  
3. Apply sign to get confidence-weighted rewards.  
4. Perform PPO update using rewards and old policy.  
5. Repeat until convergence.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Entropy regularization vs. exploitation speed:* Higher entropy slows convergence but improves stability on noisy data.  
- *Confidence weighting aggressiveness:* Strong scaling (e.g., using raw log-prob) can over-penalize borderline cases; clipping may be needed.  
- *Equal sampling bias:* Oversampling minor class may cause overfitting to minor-class noise.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sudden drop in minor-class recall when confidence weighting is too severe.  
- High variance in policy loss across batches indicates unstable reward scaling.  
- Degeneration to random guessing if entropy bonus is too high.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Ablate the confidence-weighted reward: replace with 0/1 reward and measure accuracy on noisy vs. clean subsets.  
2. Vary the entropy coefficient (β) across orders of magnitude and plot minor-class F1-score.  
3. Test on a synthetically imbalanced dataset (e.g., 1:99) and track per-class accuracy vs. training steps.
