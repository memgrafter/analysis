# Benchmarking Long Roll-outs of Auto-regressive Neural Operators for the Compressible Navier-Stokes Equations with Conserved Quantity Correction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.22541
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22541
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** A model-agnostic conserved quantity correction technique improves long-term stability of neural operators (FNO/DPOT) in autoregressive rollouts for 2D compressible Navier-Stokes, but high-frequency spectral errors persist.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of error accumulation in autoregressive predictions of neural operators for PDEs, compounded by a failure to conserve physical quantities (mass, momentum). The authors introduce *conserved quantity correction*, a simple, model-agnostic post-processing step that enforces conservation via output scaling (magnitude for non-negative density, shift for momentum). Tested on 2D compressible Navier-Stokes from PDEBench, the correction consistently improves long-term metrics such as L2 error and correlation duration across FNO and DPOT architectures. However, spectral analysis reveals that all models inadequately capture high-frequency components, indicating a fundamental architectural limitation, especially for turbulent flows.

## Method Summary
The base neural operator (FNO or DPOT) predicts the next state from a short history during training. An autoregressive rollout of τ=5 steps is used, with an L2 relative error loss. The conserved quantity correction module T is applied at each step: for density (non-negative scalar), the output is scaled so its spatial integral matches the input integral; for momentum (a vector), a constant shift is added to each grid point to match the input momentum integral. This correction is differentiable, enabling end-to-end training without altering the base model’s architecture. For evaluation, long rollouts (up to 50 steps) are generated, and performance is measured via per-timestep L2 error, correlation duration (>90% threshold), and spectral analysis of turbulent kinetic energy and density.

## Key Results
- DPOT with correction achieves 7.8% L2 relative error at t=10 vs. 8.3% for the baseline on Mach 0.1 data.
- Correction enables >90% correlation with ground truth for 28 timesteps vs. 25 for baseline over 50-step rollouts.
- Spectral analysis shows all models inadequately capture high-frequency components, a limitation not solved by correction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1** – *Conserved quantity correction reduces error accumulation by enforcing physical conservation laws.* It scales (density) or shifts (momentum) the model output so the discrete integral of the predicted conserved variable exactly matches the integral from the previous timestep. This prevents unphysical drift in total mass or momentum that would amplify errors over many recursive steps. Core assumption: breaking conservation laws is a primary driver of long-term instability. Evidence: mathematical derivation in section III.D; experiments show improved long-term L2 and correlation. Break condition: if the base model’s prediction is catastrophically unphysical (e.g., negative density everywhere), scaling may produce degenerate outputs.

**Mechanism 2** – *The correction’s model‑agnostic design separates conservation enforcement from core dynamics learning.* Formally, H_θ = T(G_θ), where T is a simple, non‑learnable post‑processing step applied to specific output channels. This allows any base operator G_θ (FNO, DPOT) to be wrapped without modification. Core assumption: G_θ can learn local short‑term dynamics but its outputs drift in conserved quantities; T corrects this drift. Evidence: same correction wrapper improves both FNO and DPOT (section IV.B). Break condition: if G_θ fails to learn meaningful dynamics (outputs noise/constant fields), T cannot recover correct spatial structures or temporal evolution.

**Mechanism 3** – *A fundamental architectural limitation of current neural operators is the inadequate representation of high‑frequency components.* Architectures like FNO (fixed Fourier modes) and DPOT (patch decoding) inherently limit high‑frequency fidelity. Spectral analysis reveals a consistent deficit in energy density at high wavenumbers, indicating an inability to capture the forward energy cascade of turbulence. Correction manages low‑frequency integral errors but cannot invent missing high‑frequency information. Evidence: spectral energy plots (section IV.E) show sharp cutoffs. Break condition: this limitation dominates in turbulent flows (Mach 1.0), where correction provides marginal or no benefit.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base Neural Operator (G_θ) -> Conserved Quantity Correction Module (T) -> Corrected output (converted to primitive for evaluation). During training: input conserved state -> G_θ predicts next state (conserved) -> T corrects -> convert to primitive -> compute loss; rollout for τ steps.

**Critical path:**  
Integrate T as a wrapper around G_θ’s forward pass for specific output channels (density and momentum). The wrapper computes spatial sums of input and predicted conserved variables, then applies scaling (density) or shift (momentum). No change to G_θ’s weights or architecture. Retrain the entire model (G_θ + T) with the same autoregressive rollout loss; gradients flow through T.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Pros:* Immediate stability gains (lower L2, longer correlation duration) with minimal code change and no increase in model parameters.  
- *Cons:* Does not address high‑frequency spectral deficit; performance on highly turbulent (Mach 1.0) flows shows limited benefit.  
- *Tradeoff:* Correction is “free” in parameters but not in capability—it corrects integral errors but cannot generate missing small‑scale structure.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. **Early divergence:** L2 error spikes within first 5‑10 steps. Likely cause: base model G_θ too weak (e.g., insufficient modes/patches) for even short‑term dynamics.  
2. **Gradual drift despite correction:** Correlation decays linearly but slowly. Indicates correction works for integrals but high‑frequency error still accumulates.  
3. **Spectral cutoff persists:** Spectrograms show sharp drop‑off at fixed wavenumber (FNO) or patch‑related artifact (DPOT), unchanged by correction.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train FNO/DPOT on Mach 0.1 dataset without correction (τ=5). Validate short‑term L2 (~1 step) matches Table 1, confirm long‑term degradation (error growth, correlation drop).  
2. **Mass‑only correction:** Apply only magnitude correction to density channel. Measure change in long‑term L2 slope and correlation duration vs. baseline; expect improvement but less than full correction.  
3. **Full correction + spectral probe:** Apply both mass (magnitude) and momentum (shift) correction. Generate 50‑step rollouts for 100 samples. Plot L2 vs. timestep (like Fig. 3) and compute spectrograms for density (like Fig. 6). Confirm improved stability but persistent high‑frequency deficit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** *How can neural operators be enhanced to capture high‑frequency features and overcome spectral cutoffs?*  
  *Basis:* Authors explicitly call for incorporation of high‑frequency components (Conclusion).  
  *Why unresolved:* Spectral analysis reveals all tested models lack high‑frequency energy, limiting accuracy in turbulent flows.  
  *Resolving evidence:* An architecture that matches ground‑truth spectral density at high frequencies and reduces rollout errors on high‑Mach datasets.

- **Open Question 2:** *Does the conserved quantity correction technique generalize to other neural operator architectures and PDE systems?*  
  *Basis:* Conclusion states need to test on additional architectures and datasets; only FNO and DPOT evaluated.  
  *Why unresolved:* Model‑agnostic claim remains unverified; unseen architectures may respond adversely.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Systematic application to diverse operators (e.g., GNOT, OFormer) and PDE benchmarks, with quantified conservation errors and rollout performance.

- **Open Question 3:** *Why does conserved quantity correction yield large gains for FNO on Mach 1.0 flows but not for DPOT?*  
  *Basis:* Table 1 shows FNOc reduces error vs. FNO baseline on M=1.0, while DPOTc shows no improvement; paper offers no explanation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Understanding interaction between correction and architecture is essential for reliable deployment.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Ablation studies separating mass and momentum corrections, combined with analyses of internal features and patch‑size variations in DPOT.

## Limitations
- Correction only enforces integral conservation; it does not address the fundamental architectural limitation of high‑frequency representation, which is critical for turbulent flows.
- Effectiveness on highly turbulent (Mach 1.0) data is limited; gains are small or absent for DPOT.
- The technique relies on the base model learning sufficient short‑term dynamics; if the initial prediction is too unphysical, correction may produce degenerate outputs.

## Confidence
- **High:** Conserved quantity correction improves stability (L2, correlation) on Mach 0.1 data for both FNO and DPOT; mechanism is clearly defined and differentiable.
- **Medium:** Correction is model‑agnostic (evidence from two architectures) but untested on others.
- **Low:** Correction resolves the high‑frequency spectral deficit; spectral deficit persists and is not addressed by the method.
