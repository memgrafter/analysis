# Emergence is Overrated: AGI as an Archipelago of Experts

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07979
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07979
*   **Reference count:** 7
*   **Primary result:** Human expertise is largely domain-specific and brittle, and major creative breakthroughs follow stochastic processes; therefore, AGI need not exhibit KKM's "emergent intelligence" and could instead be realized as a system of many narrow, specialized modules—an "archipelago of experts."

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the core thesis of Krakauer et al. (2025, "KKM") that genuine intelligence requires emergent, analogical, and compressive representations. It argues, based on cognitive science evidence, that human expertise is predominantly narrow and brittle, with spontaneous analogical reasoning being rare and difficult. Expert performance flexibility is posited to arise not from unifying principles but from vast repertoires of specialized patterns. Furthermore, creative breakthroughs are characterized as stochastic outcomes of a Blind Variation and Selective Retention (BVSR) process, not principled insight. Accepting this account of human cognition as genuinely intelligent, the author concludes that Artificial General Intelligence could be built as an "archipelago of experts"—a collection of specialized modules—without needing the kind of emergent representations KKM deem essential.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a conceptual synthesis and critical analysis of existing cognitive science literature. It does not present novel experiments or datasets. The method involves re-interpreting established findings on expertise (e.g., surgical skill, chess mastery), analogical transfer (e.g., Gick & Holyoak's radiation problem), and creativity (BVSR theory, the equal-odds rule) to argue against the necessity of KKM's proposed emergent, compressive representations. The argument proceeds by establishing the brittleness and domain-specificity of human competence, then proposing an architectural analogy for AGI based on this characterization.

## Key Results
*   **Human expertise is fundamentally brittle and domain-specific**, with performance collapsing on minor variations of familiar tasks (e.g., chess experts on randomized boards, surgeons on different simulator tasks).
*   **Spontaneous, productive analogical reasoning is rare** (e.g., 20-43% success in classic studies without hints), often requiring explicit cues to recognize and map deep structural similarities.
*   **Major creative breakthroughs follow stochastic BVSR processes**, where impact correlates with output volume (variation) and selective retention, not with the consistent application of deep, unifying principles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Domain-Specific, Brittle Expertise
*   **Claim:** Human expertise is not flexible but is finely tuned to narrow task contours, leading to catastrophic failure on minor novelties.
*   **Mechanism:** Cognitive resources are allocated to mastering high-dimensional, specific details rather than extracting low-dimensional, abstract principles for reuse. Performance flexibility is an illusion created by a large repertoire of specialized patterns.
*   **Core assumption:** The cost of maintaining and applying general principles outweighs the benefit for most real-world task environments.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "human expertise is predominantly domain-specific and brittle... Expert performance flexibility arises from vast repertoires of specialized patterns." [section 4] Evidence from surgical expertise, radiology search, chess with randomized positions, and accounting.
*   **Break condition:** Consistent, spontaneous cross-domain transfer becomes the *norm* for experts within their broad field.

### Mechanism 2: Rarity of Spontaneous Analogy
*   **Claim:** Productive analogical transfer, the supposed core of emergent intelligence, is exceptionally rare without explicit support.
*   **Mechanism:** Analogical transfer fails at two knowledge-intensive stages: (1) recognizing deep structural similarity across surface-dissimilar domains, and (2) correctly mapping and applying the source solution. Both stages are cognitively demanding and error-prone.
*   **Core assumption:** The cognitive load of cross-domain structural alignment prevents spontaneous, reliable transfer.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "only 20-43% spontaneous transfer in Gick & Holyoak studies." [section 3] Detailed review of radiation problem (20-43% vs. 76-100% with hints), LCM transfer, and Gardner's physics students.
*   **Break condition:** High-rate (>70%) spontaneous analogical transfer is consistently observed across diverse novel contexts in expert populations.

### Mechanism 3: Stochastic Creativity (BVSR)
*   **Claim:** Creative breakthroughs are not driven by principled insight but by a blind variation and selective retention process.
*   **Mechanism:** The "equal-odds rule" states the probability of any single creative attempt being a hit is constant. Thus, total creative impact is a function of prolific output (variation) and effective filtering (selection), not superior generative insight.
*   **Core assumption:** The generation of novel, valuable ideas is fundamentally probabilistic; prior knowledge only sets the neighborhood of search, not the specific successful outcome.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Creative breakthroughs follow stochastic... Blind Variation and Selective Retention processes." [section 5] Argument that KKM's examples may be the "lucky tail"; citations of BVSR theory (Campbell, Simonton) and equal-odds rule evidence (Jung et al., Sinatra et al.).
*   **Break condition:** Longitudinal analysis shows the impact of an individual's most influential work is *not* randomly distributed within their sequence of outputs.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Domain-Specificity of Expertise**
    *   **Why needed here:** It is the foundational empirical claim that directly contradicts KKM's emphasis on general, compressive representations. It is necessary to evaluate the "archipelago" proposal.
    *   **Quick check:** According to the paper, why does a surgeon's expertise in Procedure A not reliably predict their skill in closely related Procedure B?
*   **Concept: The Einstellung Effect (Functional Fixedness)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Provides a mechanistic explanation for how pre-existing, specialized patterns can actively block superior solutions, supporting the claim that expertise often involves rigid routines.
    *   **Quick check:** In the Bilalić et al. (2008) chess study, why did experts miss a better solution on the fifth puzzle after recognizing a pattern in the first four?
*   **Concept: Blind Variation and Selective Retention (BVSR)**
    *   **Why needed here:** It is the proposed alternative mechanism for creativity, replacing KKM's focus on analogical reasoning and unifying principles with a probabilistic, evolutionary model.
    *   **Quick check:** What does the "equal-odds rule" imply about the relationship between the quantity of a scientist's publications and the expected impact of their most famous paper?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Specialized Expert Modules (Islands) <- Minimal Bridges (Straits) -> Meta-Critic (Selection Pressure) -> Pattern Accumulator (Variation Generator) -> New Specialized Expert Modules.
*   **Critical path:** 1) Define a taxonomically fine-grained set of narrow tasks. 2) Train a separate model to superhuman performance on each task in isolation. 3) Develop lightweight routing/arbitrage mechanisms (Minimal Bridges) to dispatch novel inputs to candidate experts. 4) Integrate a Meta-Critic to evaluate outputs and feed signals to the Pattern Accumulator (standard training loop) for creating/refining experts.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Storage/Compute Brittleness vs. Peak Performance:** Each island achieves peak efficiency by ignoring data outside its narrow distribution, leading to system-wide massive redundancy and no graceful degradation.
    *   **No Transfer vs. Rapid Specialization:** Gives up cross-domain generalization to enable rapid creation of new competent modules via training on new narrow data.
    *   **Architectural Simplicity vs. Integration Overhead:** Modules are simple; complexity shifts to defining the task taxonomy and tuning the brittle bridging/routing logic.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Bridge Failure:** Minimal Bridges route inputs to irrelevant experts (e.g., legal docs to medical diagnosis), producing nonsensical outputs.
    *   **Island Isolation:** Modules perform perfectly on training data but catastrophically fail on any real-world perturbation or minor variant, confirming brittleness.
    *   **Critic Gaming:** The Meta-Critic's signal is gamed; modules produce confident, plausible, but incorrect outputs that pass evaluation.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Transfer Stress Test:** Train 100 specialized experts on 100 distinct narrow datasets. Systematically evaluate performance on each expert's own test set vs. all others. **Hypothesis:** Near-zero transfer (>95% of non-native tasks).
    2.  **Bridging Efficacy Under Distribution Shift:** For a held-out task "Z," train an expert. Create test inputs with surface variations (font, wording). Measure routing accuracy of Minimal Bridges and consequent performance. **Hypothesis:** Bridge accuracy and performance degrade sharply with surface variation.
    3.  **BVSR Analogue in Module Creation:** Monitor creation of new expert modules over time. Correlate final module performance/impact with number of attempts made to create it (training runs, hyperparameter configs). **Hypothesis:** Positive correlation supports a variation-and-selection model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Why does human expertise often appear flexible and generalizable despite being based on brittle, domain-specific pattern recognition?** The paper suggests an "illusion of generality" from accumulated patterns but lacks direct empirical validation against genuine analogical reasoning.
*   **Can an artificial system composed of isolated, specialized modules—without shared representations or analogical reasoning—achieve general intelligence comparable to human performance across diverse domains?** The thesis is conceptual and does not define operational metrics for "general intelligence" or empirically test such architectures.
*   **To what extent do creative breakthroughs in science and art arise from stochastic blind variation and selective retention (BVSR) versus from analogical reasoning and unifying principles?** BVSR is presented as an alternative, but the paper does not quantify the relative contributions or resolve tension with principle-driven discoveries.
*   **What architectural principles enable an "archipelago of experts" to learn new domains efficiently, and how does this learning differ from human domain-specific expertise acquisition?** The focus is on characterizing existing expertise, not mechanisms for expansion or scaling.

## Limitations
*   **Evidence Base:** Relies heavily on classic, potentially dated cognitive studies (e.g., Gick & Holyoak, 1980) with concerns about replication and publication bias; raw data for key statistics is often inaccessible.
*   **Definitional Fluidity:** Boundaries for "domain," "spontaneous transfer," and "minor variation" are not uniformly operationalized, allowing for selective interpretation of brittleness evidence.
*   **Conceptual Scope:** The thesis trades one philosophical definition of intelligence (emergent/compressive) for another (brittle/domain-specific). Whether human-like brittleness is a *requirement* for AGI remains a value-laden assumption.

## Confidence
*   **Human Expertise is Domain-Specific & Brittle:** **High** (supported by robust, multi-field evidence; break condition rarely observed).
*   **Spontaneous Analogical Reasoning is Rare:** **Medium** (well-cited statistics are dependent on problem design and population; "with hints" boost is expected but magnitude is key).
*   **Creative Breakthroughs Follow Stochastic BVSR:** **Low** (equal-odds rule correlation is intriguing but correlational; does not preclude rare, principle-driven insights).

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate Core Transfer Statistics:** Obtain and re-analyze original data from Gick & Holyoak (1980) or a high-fidelity replication to confirm spontaneous vs. cued solution rates, controlling for expertise and surface similarity.
2.  **Systematize Brittleness Evidence:** Conduct a structured literature review to tabulate cross-domain failure cases (e.g., expert performance collapse on randomized/delayed tasks) and quantify the magnitude of performance degradation.
3.  **Audit LLMs for Archipelago Signatures:** Empirically test state-of-the-art LLMs on a battery of narrow, specialized benchmarks. Introduce minor, naturalistic distribution shifts (paraphrasing, context noise) to measure whether degradation is all-or-nothing (island-like) or graceful (unified representation).
