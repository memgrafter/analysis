# Do LLM Self-Explanations Help Users Predict Model Behavior? Evaluating Counterfactual Simulatability with Pragmatic Perturbations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03775
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03775
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** LLM self-explanations improve counterfactual simulatability for humans and LLM judges, with gains varying by perturbation type and larger for pragmatic counterfactuals like lexical substitution and presupposition flips.

## Executive Summary
This study assesses whether LLM-generated self-explanations (chain-of-thought or post-hoc) aid users in predicting model responses to counterfactual questions. Using the StrategyQA dataset, the authors generate perturbed questions via LLM automation and a novel Gricean maxims-based taxonomy. Human participants and LLM judges predict answers with and without explanations. Results show explanations consistently boost prediction accuracy but the magnitude depends heavily on the perturbation strategy, with pragmatic types yielding more stable improvements. Explanations also reduce anchoring bias, especially when the original answer is incorrect.

## Method Summary
The study leverages StrategyQA, where LLMs answer multi-hop questions. Counterfactual questions (Q′) are created using two approaches: (1) LLM-driven generation and (2) a pragmatic taxonomy applying Gricean maxims (lexical substitution, presupposition flip, scalar adjustment, contextualization). For each original question-answer pair, self-explanations (CoT or post-hoc) are collected from the same model. Human participants (N=28) and LLM judges (two tiers) then predict the model's answer to Q′ in two phases: without explanation (Phase 1) and with explanation (Phase 2). Accuracy gains (Δacc) and bias metrics (e.g., anchoring on original answer) are computed. Qualitative analysis of human rationales identifies patterns like criterion matching.

## Key Results
- Self-explanations improve counterfactual simulatability for both human and LLM judges.
- Gains are largest for pragmatic counterfactuals (lexical substitution, presupposition flip) compared to LLM-generated ones.
- Explanations reduce anchoring bias, particularly when the model's original answer is incorrect.
- Human accuracy improved modestly overall (Δacc = +0.020), with contextualized questions showing the highest gain (+0.060).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Explicit Decision Criteria**  
Explanations verbalize reasoning steps, enabling users to extract stable criteria (e.g., entity disambiguation) and apply them to counterfactuals. This transforms implicit heuristics into extrapolatable rules, but fails if the perturbation alters unaddressed features.

**Mechanism 2: Anchoring Mitigation**  
Explanations provide a rationale that helps users override the tendency to repeat the original answer. By highlighting why the original answer was given, users can identify when a perturbation invalidates that justification, leading to correct "flip-back" predictions, especially if the original answer was wrong.

**Mechanism 3: Perturbation-Explanation Alignment**  
The utility of explanations is contingent on the counterfactual perturbation strategy. Perturbations that align with explanation content (e.g., scalar adjustment matching a scalar condition in the explanation) yield larger Δacc, while mismatches result in minimal gains or negative effects due to ceiling or distraction.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Counterfactual Simulatability**  
*Why needed:* It operationalizes explanation usefulness as the accuracy improvement in predicting model behavior on perturbed inputs.  
*Quick check:* If a judge’s accuracy on Q′ improves by 0.15 with an explanation, this implies the explanation provided useful, generalizable reasoning. A key limitation is that high Δacc may reflect increased confidence rather than correctness if Phase 1 guess rates are low.

**Concept: Gricean Maxims**  
*Why needed:* They underpin the pragmatic perturbation taxonomy, ensuring linguistically motivated and controlled counterfactuals that challenge specific conversational assumptions.  
*Quick check:* Scalar Adjustment targets the Maxim of Quantity by modifying degree terms (e.g., "some" to "all"), challenging assumptions about informativeness and precision in communication.

**Concept: Anchoring and Flip-back Bias**  
*Why needed:* Anchoring is a measured bias where users echo the original answer; flip-back is the corrective prediction when that answer is wrong. Explanations’ ability to reduce these biases links mechanism to outcome.  
*Quick check:* Negative Δbias (change in bias metric) indicates reduced anchoring, meaning explanations help users override the original answer. This is critical when the original answer is incorrect, as it enables accurate model simulation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
StrategyQA dataset → Counterfactual Generators (LLM-based and pragmatic engine) → Q′ pairs → Explanation Collectors (CoT/post-hoc prompts) → X (explanations) → Judges (human participants and LLM-as-a-judge) → Predictions (with/without X) → Metrics Engine (computes Δacc, bias) → Analysis; Qualitative Coder supports human rationale analysis.

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate Q′ for each (Q, A) using perturbation strategies.  
2. Collect self-explanations X from the model for original Q.  
3. Run simulation: Judges predict A′ in Phase 1 (no X) and Phase 2 (with X).  
4. Aggregate metrics per explanation type, cfq_type, and judge.  
5. Analyze Δacc patterns, bias reduction, and qualitative rationales.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Human vs. LLM judges: Humans offer ecological validity but are costly and small-N; LLM judges are scalable but may not mirror human cognition (moderate agreement ~0.6).  
- Perturbation diversity: LLM-generated Q′ are fluent but uncontrolled; pragmatic Q′ are principled but limited to four types, allowing comparison but confounding difficulty.  
- Explanation format: CoT vs. post-hoc showed minimal differences, suggesting even brief justifications may suffice, but task complexity may matter.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Ceiling effect: Phase 1 accuracy near >0.8 caps Δacc potential.  
- Negative Δacc: Explanations worsen prediction, indicating misleading or distracting content.  
- Low Phase 1 guess rate: May inflate Δacc if explanations boost confidence without correctness.  
- Anchoring persists: Positive or small Δbias suggests explanation failed to override original answer bias.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline simulatability:** Run Phase 1 only for all (Q, Q′) pairs to establish base accuracy and guess rates, assessing task feasibility without explanations.  
2. **Perturbation sensitivity sweep:** For a fixed explanation type, compare Δacc across all cfq_types to verify pragmatic types yield larger/more stable gains than LLM-generated.  
3. **Bias reduction check:** Compute Δbias_orig and Δbias_orig_wrong per cfq_type to confirm explanations reduce anchoring, especially on wrong-original instances; investigate if explanations mention flipped elements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Do LLM verbalized self-explanations contribute to model understanding as measured by counterfactual simulatability?  
*Basis:* Explicit research question (i). *Why unresolved:* Results show variable effect sizes depending on perturbation and judge; generalizability to other tasks and real-world scenarios is unclear. *Resolution evidence:* Replication across diverse tasks, models, and formats; long-term user studies; analysis of explanation faithfulness.

**Open Question 2:** How do different counterfactual perturbation strategies affect the assessment of LLM self-explanations' usefulness?  
*Basis:* Explicit research question (ii). *Why unresolved:* Pragmatic perturbations yield stable gains but linguistic/cognitive reasons are unexplained; taxonomy is incomplete. *Resolution evidence:* Map perturbation characteristics (semantic distance, logical flips) to simulatability; user studies on "good" counterfactuals; expand taxonomy.

**Open Question 3:** Can self-explanations reduce users' anchoring bias toward the model's original answer, especially when that answer is incorrect?  
*Basis:* Explicit research question (iii). *Why unresolved:* Anchoring reduction is inconsistent across conditions; mechanisms not investigated. *Resolution evidence:* Process-tracing (think-aloud, eye-tracking); vary explanation content to isolate effective features; study user expertise effects.

**Open Question 4:** How do self-explanations affect human confidence and accuracy in predicting model behavior, and how well do LLM-as-a-judge evaluations align with human judgments?  
*Basis:* Explicit research question (iv). *Why unresolved:* Human gains are modest with small N; LLM-judge agreement with humans is moderate (~0.6–0.8). *Resolution evidence:* Larger human studies with diverse demographics; systematic LLM judge comparisons; analyze disagreement cases.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Results are specific to StrategyQA and a limited pragmatic perturbation taxonomy; unknown if gains hold for other domains (e.g., math, coding) or naturalistic counterfactuals.
- **Ecological validity of LLM judges:** Moderate inter-rater agreement (~0.6) with humans questions whether LLM judges reliably simulate human reasoning.
- **Sample constraints:** Human study small (N=28, 100 items/type), leading to noisy estimates for smaller effect sizes.

## Confidence
- **Core claim (explanations improve counterfactual simulatability):** Medium – within-study evidence strong but external validity limited.
- **Perturbation-sensitivity claim:** High – empirically robust across judges; pragmatic types yield stable improvements.
- **Anchoring reduction claim:** Medium – quantitative metrics show negative Δbias but causal links to explanation features are inferred.
- **Faithfulness-agnostic utility:** Low – paper acknowledges but does not test if "useful but unfaithful" explanations drive gains.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset replication:** Re-run core experiment on at least two additional datasets (e.g., mathematical reasoning, factual QA) to verify Δacc pattern generalizability.
2. **Faithfulness vs. utility disentanglement:** For a subset, collect multiple explanations varying in faithfulness metrics; test if high-utility explanations correlate with higher faithfulness or simpler heuristics.
3. **Human judge scaling and diversity:** Conduct larger human study (N>200) with stratified perturbation types and original answer correctness; measure individual differences (expertise, cognitive reflection) to test benefit uniformity.
