# Zero-Shot Cross-City Generalization in End-to-End Autonomous Driving: Self-Supervised versus Supervised Representations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11417
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11417
- **References:** 40
- **Primary result:** Domain-specific self-supervised visual backbones (e.g., DINOv2, I-JEPA) enable near-parity in zero-shot cross-city transfer, while supervised backbones fail catastrophically.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates zero-shot cross-city generalization for end-to-end autonomous driving. Models trained on one city (e.g., Boston) fail severely when deployed to a geographically distinct city (e.g., Singapore) due to domain shifts in road topology and traffic conventions. Integrating self-supervised visual representations pretrained on driving data drastically reduces this performance gap, achieving near-parity on key metrics like displacement error and collision rate. The findings establish self-supervised representation learning as essential for robust geographic transfer and propose zero-shot cross-city evaluation as a critical stress test for autonomous driving systems.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct experiments on two benchmarks: nuScenes (open-loop) with Boston/Singapore splits, and NAVSIM (closed-loop) with Boston, Pittsburgh, Las Vegas, and Singapore. They compare supervised backbones (Swin, ResNet34) against self-supervised ones (I-JEPA, DINOv2, MAE), including both generic ImageNet-pretrained and domain-specific nuScenes-pretrained ViT models. For each source city, they train planning frameworks (LAW for open-loop, TransFuser for closed-loop) and evaluate zero-shot on held-out cities. Metrics include L2 displacement error and collision rate (open-loop) and Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS, closed-loop). All backbones are evaluated in both frozen and fine-tuned configurations.

## Key Results
- Supervised backbones exhibit catastrophic directional degradation (e.g., Boston→Singapore L2 ratio 9.77×, collision ratio 19.43×).
- Domain-specific self-supervised pretraining reduces cross-city gaps to near-parity (e.g., L2 ratio 1.20×, collision ratio 0.75×).
- In closed-loop NAVSIM, self-supervised backbones improve average out-of-distribution PDMS by up to 4% compared to supervised counterparts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Self-supervised pretraining on driving-specific data (nuScenes) aligns visual representations with the latent structure of driving scenes (e.g., road geometry, traffic object semantics). This yields features that are more invariant to geographic shifts in topology and traffic conventions (e.g., right-hand vs. left-hand driving). In contrast, supervised ImageNet-pretrained or randomly initialized backbones encode biases from their training distribution, which are misaligned with the target city's visual and operational norms. Domain-specific SSL thus provides a robust, transferable foundation that generalizes better under strict geographic distribution shifts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-supervised learning (I-JEPA, DINOv2, MAE):** Learns representations from data without labels via objectives like masked prediction or distillation. *Why needed:* Avoids supervision bias from datasets like ImageNet and captures driving-relevant semantics. *Quick check:* Linear probe accuracy on nuScenes validation set.
- **Domain generalization:** Training on one distribution and evaluating on another without adaptation. *Why needed:* Addresses real-world deployment where cities differ. *Quick check:* Compute error ratio (target/source) on a held-out city.
- **End-to-end autonomous driving:** Mapping raw sensor inputs directly to control commands via a learned policy. *Why needed:* Bypasses hand-designed modules; evaluates holistic system performance. *Quick check:* Closed-loop PDMS in simulation.
- **Zero-shot transfer:** Applying a model to a new domain without further training. *Why needed:* Simulates deployment to unseen cities. *Quick check:* Performance drop when training and testing cities differ.
- **Visual backbone:** CNN or Transformer extracting features from camera images. *Why needed:* Provides perception front-end for planning. *Quick check:* Feature visualization (e.g., attention maps) for driving-relevant regions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Camera Input -> Backbone (SSL/Supervised ViT) -> Feature Projection -> Transformer Encoder (LAW) / Fusion Module (TransFuser) -> Trajectory Decoder -> Control Output.

**Critical path:** Raw images are processed by the visual backbone; extracted features are consumed by a planning module (LAW or TransFuser) that predicts future trajectories, which are converted to control commands.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Frozen vs. fine-tuned backbone: Frozen SSL features are more stable but may not adapt fully to planning task; fine-tuning risks overfitting to source city.
- Input resolution: Rectangular (224×392) vs. square (224×224) affects performance; rectangular better captures driving scene aspect ratio.
- Computational cost: SSL pretraining (especially video-based) is expensive; image-based methods trade temporal context for efficiency.

**Failure signatures:** 
- Exploding L2 displacement error (>10× in-domain) in Boston→Singapore transfer.
- High collision rate (>20× increase) on left-hand traffic when trained on right-hand.
- Directional asymmetry: degradation worse from right-hand to left-hand traffic cities.
- Poor closed-loop PDMS (<50% of in-domain baseline).

**First three experiments:**
1. Train LAW with supervised Swin on Boston nuScenes; evaluate zero-shot on Singapore. Expect severe L2 and collision degradation.
2. Pretrain ViT-S with DINOv2 on nuScenes (rectangular inputs); freeze and integrate into LAW; repeat Boston→Singapore transfer. Expect reduced error ratios.
3. In NAVSIM, train TransFuser with domain-specific SSL backbone on Boston; evaluate average OOD PDMS across Pittsburgh, Las Vegas, Singapore. Compare frozen vs. fine-tuned backbone.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What drives directional asymmetry in cross-city degradation (e.g., Boston→Singapore vs Singapore→Boston)? Traffic side conventions vs other city attributes are not isolated.
- **Open Question 2:** How does training on more cities (greater geographic diversity) affect the zero-shot generalization gap when using SSL?
- **Open Question 3:** Can video-based self-supervised pretraining (e.g., V-JEPA) further reduce the gap by capturing temporal driving patterns?
- **Open Question 4:** How can multimodal self-supervised learning (joint camera–LiDAR) improve zero-shot transfer compared to supervised LiDAR with self-supervised images?

## Limitations
- Undisclosed hyperparameters for domain-specific SSL pretraining (masking ratios, augmentation, LR schedule) hinder exact replication.
- NAVSIM closed-loop evaluation protocol (simulation parameters, episode count, PDMS weighting) is not detailed, making verification of reported PDMS gains difficult.
- Evaluation limited to 2–4 cities and a subset of SSL methods (I-JEPA, DINOv2, MAE); generalizability to broader geographies and other objectives is unknown.

## Confidence
