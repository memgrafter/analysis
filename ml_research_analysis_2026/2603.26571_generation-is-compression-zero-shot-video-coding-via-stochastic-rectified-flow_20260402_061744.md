# Generation Is Compression: Zero-Shot Video Coding via Stochastic Rectified Flow

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.26571
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26571
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: Zero-shot video coding framework that converts a pretrained rectified‑flow model into a codec via ODE‑to‑SDE conversion, achieving ultra‑low bitrates (<0.002 bpp) with superior perceptual quality (LPIPS reduced by 70.3% vs. DCVC‑RT) and 97% user preference.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces GVC, a zero‑shot video compression framework that repurposes a pretrained rectified‑flow video model (Wan 2.1) as the codec itself. Unlike existing generative approaches that rely on conventional codecs with post‑hoc refinement, GVC converts the model’s deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE) sampling process into an equivalent stochastic differential equation (SDE) at inference time. This conversion introduces per‑step noise injection points that are replaced by reproducible codebook selections, enabling bitrate control solely through a hyperparameter. Three conditioning strategies—T2V (text‑to‑video), I2V (image‑to‑video), and FLF2V (first‑last‑frame‑to‑video)—span a trade‑off space between spatial fidelity, temporal coherence, and compression efficiency. Experiments on standard benchmark datasets show that GVC achieves high‑quality reconstruction below 0.002 bpp, reduces LPIPS by 70.3% compared to DCVC‑RT at similar bitrate, and receives a 97% preference in a user study.

## Method Summary
GVC’s core method begins with a pretrained rectified‑flow video model that defines a deterministic probability‑flow ODE: \(d\mathbf{x}_t = u_t(\mathbf{x}_t)dt\). Using the Score‑SDE equivalence theorem, the authors derive an SDE with drift  
\(f_t(\mathbf{x}_t) = u_t(\mathbf{x}_t) + \frac{g_t^2}{2}\bigl[-(1-t)u_t(\mathbf{x}_t) + \frac{\mathbf{x}_t}{t}\bigr]\)  
and diffusion coefficient \(g_t = g_{\text{scale}}\cdot t^2\). This SDE shares the same marginals as the ODE but adds per‑step stochasticity.  

During encoding, for each SDE timestep the encoder computes the denoising residual \(r = \mathbf{x}_0 - (\mathbf{x}_t - t\cdot u_t(\mathbf{x}_t))\) using the ground‑truth latent \(\mathbf{x}_0\). A deterministic codebook \(\mathcal{C}\) (generated from a shared seed) supplies \(K\) random vectors; the encoder selects the top‑\(M\) atoms with largest absolute inner product with \(r\), combines them with their signs, and normalizes to unit variance to form a replacement noise \(z^*\). The decoder, sharing the model, seed, and codebook, reconstructs \(z^*\) from the transmitted indices and signs and applies the identical SDE step to recover the latent.  

Three conditioning variants build on this backbone:
- **T2V** uses no reference frames, yielding minimal bitrate but higher risk of drift.
- **I2V** uses the first frame of each GOP as a free anchor and autoregressively chains GOPs; it supplements this with adaptive tail‑frame atom allocation and tail latent residual correction to mitigate error accumulation.
- **FLF2V** uses both first and last frames as anchors and employs boundary‑sharing GOP chaining to reduce overhead and ensure continuity.
Bitrate is controlled by \(g_{\text{scale}}\) and \(M\); the approach requires no retraining.

## Key Results
- **Ultra‑low bitrate operation**: GVC functions below 0.002 bpp on standard benchmarks (UVG, DDD, etc.), far below traditional codecs and prior generative methods.
- **Perceptual quality advantage**: At ~0.0017 bpp, LPIPS is reduced by 70.3% relative to DCVC‑RT; a user study shows 97% preference over DCVC‑RT.
- **Flexible bitrate control**: A single hyperparameter (\(g_{\text{scale}}\)) directly tunes the rate‑distortion trade‑off; the three conditioning strategies further navigate the fidelity‑coherence‑efficiency space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: ODE‑to‑SDE Conversion for Stochastic Injection
The rectified‑flow model defines a deterministic ODE that transports noise to data. By invoking the Score‑SDE equivalence theorem, there exists a family of reverse‑time SDEs sharing the same marginal distributions. The authors choose a quadratic diffusion coefficient \(g_t = g_{\text{scale}}\cdot t^2\), which concentrates stochasticity early in the trajectory. The resulting SDE drift \(f_t(\mathbf{x}_t) = u_t(\mathbf{x}_t) + \frac{g_t^2}{2}\bigl[-(1-t)u_t(\mathbf{x}_t) + \frac{\mathbf{x}_t}{t}\bigr]\) is computed solely from the pretrained velocity field \(u_t\) and requires no additional training. This conversion re‑introduces per‑step noise injection points, a prerequisite for codebook‑based compression. *Break condition*: If the learned velocity field violates the theorem’s assumptions, the SDE marginals may diverge, causing trajectory mismatch.

### Mechanism 2: Codebook‑Driven Trajectory Control
At each SDE step, the stochastic noise \(z\) is replaced by a reproducible vector \(z^*\) selected from a shared codebook. The encoder computes the residual \(r = \mathbf{x}_0 - (\mathbf{x}_t - t\cdot u_t(\mathbf{x}_t))\) using the ground‑truth latent, then identifies the top‑\(M\) codebook atoms with largest absolute inner product with \(r\). These atoms are combined with their signs and normalized to unit variance to approximate the required noise magnitude. Transmitting only the \(M\) indices and signs allows the decoder to reconstruct the exact same \(z^*\) and thus follow an identical generative trajectory. This mechanism adapts the Turbo‑DDCM idea to SDEs derived from rectified flow. *Break condition*: If \(r\) cannot be well approximated by a linear combination of the top‑\(M\) atoms (e.g., for complex textures), the trajectory diverges, causing blur or flicker.

### Mechanism 3: Conditioning Strategies for Trade‑offs
The three conditioning variants systematically explore the trade‑off space by varying the number and placement of spatial/temporal anchors within the unified SDE‑codebook backbone:
- **T2V** (no anchors) relies purely on the generative prior, achieving the lowest bitrate but with higher drift/flickering risk.
- **I2V** autopropagates with the first frame as a free anchor; it adds *adaptive tail‑frame atom allocation* (more atoms for temporally distant frames) and *tail latent residual correction* (lightweight residual transmission for the last latent before it becomes the next GOP’s reference) to combat error accumulation.
- **FLF2V** uses both first and last frames as anchors and employs *boundary‑sharing GOP chaining* (reusing the last frame of one GOP as the first of the next), reducing anchor overhead by ~50% and ensuring seamless splice continuity.
Empirical results validate the expected trade‑offs: I2V yields highest PSNR (but with residual cost), FLF2V offers best marginal efficiency, and T2V operates at the lowest bitrate. *Break conditions*: T2V flickering without anchor; I2V progressive degradation without tail correction; FLF2V boundary artifacts without proper sharing.

## Foundational Learning

- **Rectified Flow**  
  *Why needed*: Provides the deterministic ODE backbone (\(d\mathbf{x}_t = u_t(\mathbf{x}_t)dt\)) that GVC repurposes. Understanding RF’s training objective (matching a linear interpolation path) explains why it yields a deterministic sampler.  
  *Quick check*: What is the key difference between the training objective of a rectified flow model and a standard diffusion model’s score matching, and why does this lead to a deterministic ODE solver at inference?

- **Score‑SDE Equivalence**  
  *Why needed*: The theorem (Song et al.) guarantees that any probability‑flow ODE has an equivalent family of reverse‑time SDEs with identical marginals. GVC uses this to convert the deterministic ODE into a stochastic one that allows controllable noise injection.  
  *Quick check*: Given an ODE \(d\mathbf{x}_t = f(\mathbf{x}_t)dt\) defining marginal distributions \(p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)\), write the general form of the equivalent reverse‑time SDE and identify the term that introduces stochasticity.

- **Zero‑Shot Codebook Compression (DDCM/Turbo‑DDCM)**  
  *Why needed*: GVC adapts the codebook mechanism from these image compression works to the video domain. The paradigm—replacing a source of randomness in a generative process with a codebook selection—enables compression without retraining.  
  *Quick check*: In the original DDCM framework for diffusion models, what specific part of the reverse diffusion process is replaced by the codebook, and why does transmitting only indices allow perfect reconstruction?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map** (encoder flow, decoder mirrors with shared modules):
Input Video -> VAE Encoder -> Latent \(x_0\) -> Initialize \(x_T\) -> [SDE Loop: Compute residual \(r\) -> Codebook Manager (top‑\(M\) selection) -> Form \(z^*\) -> Apply SDE step] -> (I2V: Tail Residual Correction) -> (FLF2V: Anchor Encoding) -> Bitstream Formatter -> Bitstream

**Critical path**  
- *Encoding*: For a GOP, start from noise \(x_T\). For \(t = T, T-1, ..., 1\): compute \(\hat{x}_0|t = x_t - t\cdot u_t(x_t)\) and residual \(r = x_0 - \hat{x}_0|t\); select top‑\(M\) codebook atoms to form \(z^*\); update \(x_{t-1} = x_t + f_t(x_t)\Delta t + z^*\sqrt{g_t^2\Delta t}\). For the last \(N\) steps, skip codebook (deterministic ODE). For I2V, apply tail residual correction after the loop; for FLF2V, encode the last frame as next GOP’s anchor.  
- *Decoding*: Receive bitstream (indices, signs, possible residuals). Identical initialization. For each \(t\), reconstruct \(z^*\) from the shared codebook and received indices/signs; apply the same SDE/ODE step. After the loop, apply any received residuals and decode reference frames to condition the model; finally, decode latents to video frames.

**Design tradeoffs**
- **\(M\) (atoms/step)**: higher improves quality but linearly increases bits; diminishing returns and potential LPIPS degradation at very high \(M\).
- **\(g_{\text{scale}}\)**: controls injected stochasticity; too low limits correction, too high overwhelms model signal; sweet spot ~2.0–3.0 (Fig 2d).
- **Conditioning variant**: T2V minimal side‑info but lowest fidelity; I2V high fidelity but costly tail residuals; FLF2V balances with boundary sharing.
- **GOP length**: longer lengths improve temporal consistency but risk drift; 33 frames optimal (Fig 2e).

**Failure signatures**
- Temporal flickering or drift (T2V or mis‑configured I2V).
- Progressive degradation along I2V autoregressive chain without tail residual correction.
- Visible seams at GOP splices in FLF2V if boundary sharing misaligned.
- Oversmoothing/blur at very low \(M\) or high compression.
- Catastrophic collapse when \(g_{\text{scale}}\) too high.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Hyperparameter Sweep** (M, K, T, \(g_{\text{scale}}\), GOP length): Identify stable operating regions; watch for quality collapse at high \(g_{\text{scale}}\) and steep PSNR gain from \(T=5\) to \(T=20\).
2. **Cross‑Variant Comparison on UVG 720p** (Table 1): Measure fidelity‑coherence‑bitrate trade‑offs; validate I2V highest PSNR, FLF2V best marginal efficiency, T2V lowest bitrate.
3. **State‑of‑the‑Art Comparison at Matched Bitrate Tiers** (Table 2): Test superiority against HEVC, VVC, DCVC‑RT; success shown by LPIPS reductions (e.g., 70.3% vs. DCVC‑RT at 0.0017 bpp) and user preference.

## Limitations
- **Model specificity**: Claims are tied to Wan 2.1’s architecture; other rectified‑flow video models may not achieve similar performance due to differences in latent space or conditioning.
- **Codebook approximation**: The top‑\(M\) selection heuristic and normalization are not theoretically derived for video RF; their sufficiency for complex motion/textures remains uncertain.
- **Theoretical gap**: The Score‑SDE equivalence is invoked for the learned marginals but not empirically verified; the derived SDE might deviate from the ODE’s true data distribution.
- (Optionally: Scalability evaluated only on short GOPs; long sequences may suffer unbounded drift or overhead.)

## Confidence
- **High**: ODE‑to‑SDE conversion is mathematically sound given Score‑SDE theory and supported by the \(g_{\text{scale}}\) ablation (Fig 2d).
- **Medium**: Codebook‑driven trajectory control yields strong results, but the assumption that the residual is a sufficient statistic is not directly validated; failure modes (flickering, blur) indicate its limitations.
- **Low**: Specific engineering choices for conditioning variants (adaptive tail allocation, boundary‑sharing) lack controlled ablations; the claimed “principled trade‑off space” remains empirical.

## Next Checks
1. **Marginal distribution sanity check**: For a held‑out video, compute the marginal distributions \(p_t(\mathbf{x}_t)\) using both the deterministic ODE sampler and the proposed SDE sampler at multiple timesteps. Compare them with a statistical distance metric (e.g., MMD). *Expected*: distributions should be close if the equivalence holds.
2. **Codebook approximation stress test**: On high‑motion UVG sequences, measure the fraction of residual energy \(\|r\|^2\) captured by the top‑\(M\) codebook atoms as \(M\) varies and correlate with LPIPS degradation. *Expected*: sharp LPIPS increase when captured energy drops below a threshold (e.g., 80%).
3. **Conditioning variant ablation**: Disable I2V’s tail residual correction and adaptive tail allocation separately. Run on long sequences (e.g., 300 frames, 9 GOPs) and track per‑GOP PSNR. *Expected*: Without tail correction, PSNR decays progressively; uniform \(M\) allocation causes later GOPs to degrade more.
