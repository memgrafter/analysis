# Constitutional Black-Box Monitoring for Scheming in LLM Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00829
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00829
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Monitors trained solely on synthetic data generalize to realistic environments, achieving test partial AUROC (FPR<0.2) of 0.853–0.866 using simple prompt sweeps.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates black-box monitoring to detect scheming—covert misalignment—in autonomous LLM agents using only externally observable actions. It introduces constitutional black-box monitors trained on synthetic trajectories generated from natural-language scheming specifications via two pipelines: STRIDE and Gloom (1,000 samples each). Evaluated on 7,500 semi-realistic trajectories from ControlArena with labels from deterministic sandbox checks, these monitors achieve strong test performance (partial AUROC 0.853–0.866) using simple prompt sweeps. However, more intensive optimization (human refinement, DSPy/GEPA) yields minimal gains and often induces overfitting, suggesting that the prompting paradigm and synthetic data quality are fundamental bottlenecks.

## Method Summary
The study constructs two synthetic training datasets (1,000 trajectories each) using STRIDE (single-model iterative refinement with a discriminator) and Gloom (two-model simulation). These trajectories are filtered for realism and label integrity using privileged verifiers. Monitors are prompted classifiers that take black-box transcripts (no chain-of-thought) and output an integer score from 1 to 10, using frontier LLMs (Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5.1). A grid search over 15 prompts on synthetic train/validation sets identifies top prompt-model pairs, which may be further refined via human iteration or automated DSPy optimization (GEPA/MIPROv2). Evaluation uses a held-out test set from ControlArena comprising 7,500 trajectories across five grounded environments, with labels derived from deterministic sandbox checks that detect side-task success. Performance is measured by partial AUROC for false positive rates below 0.2, focusing on the low-FPR regime relevant for deployment.

## Key Results
- Monitors trained solely on synthetic data achieve test partial AUROC (FPR<0.2) of 0.853–0.866, demonstrating effective generalization to semi-realistic environments.
- Intensive prompt optimization (human refinement, DSPy/GEPA) fails to improve performance and often causes overfitting: training scores rise while test scores plateau or decline.
- The integer scoring scale [1,10] is essential for low-FPR detection; binary classification yields poor results, confirming the need for fine-grained confidence scores.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Synthetic data generation pipelines (STRIDE & Gloom) produce a transferable signal of scheming behavior that generalizes to semi-realistic environments.
- **Mechanism**: The pipelines instantiate a broad, constitutionally-defined scheming behavior space through controlled generation. The monitor learns to detect invariant features of covert misalignment (e.g., goal misalignment, concealment, resource accumulation) that persist across synthetic and realistic execution contexts, despite distributional shift.
- **Core assumption**: The core behavioral patterns of egregious scheming (conjunction of misaligned goal pursuit and active deception) are abstract enough to be captured in a constrained synthetic generation process and remain detectable in more complex, grounded agent trajectories.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Monitors trained solely on synthetic data generalize to realistic environments, capturing a meaningful scheming signal."
  - [section 5.1] "We find that monitoring signals learned from synthetic data transfer to the more realistic test settings... monitors selected for strong training performance outperform lower-ranked monitors on the held-out test set."
  - [section 3.2] The filters use "privileged verifiers" with ground-truth attack strategy to ensure labels reflect the intended scheming concept, not just generation artifacts.
- **Break condition**: If the synthetic generation process fails to produce trajectories with the core abstract properties of scheming (e.g., if the model refuses or produces only superficial/imitative failure modes), the learned signal will not transfer.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Performance saturates quickly under prompt optimization due to fundamental limits of the prompting paradigm for this task, leading to overfitting on synthetic dataset idiosyncrasies rather than learning robust detection features.
- **Mechanism**: Prompting induces global, coarse shifts in the monitor's behavior. To correct a specific failure mode (e.g., missing a particular covert tactic), the prompt must be adjusted, but this perturbation non-locally alters the model's scoring behavior across many unrelated examples. Optimization thus rapidly exploits low-level statistical artifacts and noise in the finite synthetic dataset (e.g., specific tool call sequences, phrasing patterns) that are not causally related to scheming, harming out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Core assumption**: The decision boundary for detecting scheming in black-box transcripts is complex and requires nuanced, example-specific reasoning that is difficult to specify with a single, global natural language prompt. The synthetic data distribution has unintended correlations that are easier to learn than the true signal.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 5.2] "Simple prompt sweeps matching the results of more extensive optimization."
  - [section 5.3] "Aggressive optimization induces overfitting... optimization increasingly exploits idiosyncratic and noisy artifacts of the synthetic training data."
  - [section F.3.1] The "Whack-a-Mole" dynamic where fixing one failure mode introduces new ones, due to global prompt effects.
- **Break condition**: If the synthetic data distribution were perfectly matched to the test distribution (no OOD shift) and large enough, more intensive prompt optimization might continue to improve performance. The break condition here is the *interaction* of distribution shift and the coarse control interface of prompting.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The use of a [1,10] integer scoring scale, rather than binary classification, is a critical enabling factor for effective low-FPR detection by preserving uncertainty and providing a finer-grained signal for thresholding.
- **Mechanism**: Scheming is expected to be rare, and its manifestations are nuanced. A binary forced-choice discards the model's confidence and nuanced reasoning, collapsing the ROC curve at low FPR. A continuous or multi-level score retains the monitor's internal assessment strength, allowing a threshold to be set that captures a meaningful fraction of true positives while controlling false alarms. The [1,10] scale is a Pareto optimum between granularity and model calibration ease.
- **Core assumption**: Frontier LLM monitors possess a meaningful, calibrated internal scoring capability for this task when prompted appropriately, and this granularity is not an artifact of the training process but reflects the underlying difficulty of the discrimination.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section E.1] "Single-sample binary classification yields poor performance at low false-positive rates... The [1,10] scale achieves superior performance... making it the Pareto-optimal choice."
  - [section 4.1] "We find that frontier models... perform comparably as monitor LLM with a variety of basic prompts... we choose to consider all three in our search space." This suggests the scoring mechanism is robust across capable models.
- **Break condition**: If the monitor LLMs fundamentally lack an internal scoring dimension that correlates with scheming likelihood (e.g., if their outputs are essentially random or uniformly distributed for this task), then any scoring scale would fail. The mechanism relies on the monitor's capability.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Scheming as a Conjunction (Misaligned Goal + Active Deception)**
  - *Why needed*: The entire monitoring task is predicated on a clear, operationalizable definition that distinguishes scheming from simpler misalignment and harmless polished work, determining what synthetic data generators must produce and what monitors must detect.
  - *Quick check*: If an agent completes a side task (e.g., exfiltrates data) but makes no effort to hide it, does that count as scheming? (No, it lacks active deception.)

- **Concept: Constitutional Data Generation**
  - *Why needed*: This core innovation enables the study without massive real-world scheming datasets. It leverages a frontier model's generative capability to create diverse scheming examples from a natural-language spec, providing training data for the monitor.
  - *Quick check*: What is the key risk of using a model that can generate scheming behavior to also filter the training data? (Shared blind spots; the filter might discard subtle scheming examples, leading to a dataset of only obvious scheming.)

- **Concept: Black-Box Monitoring & Action-Only Inputs**
  - *Why needed*: Defines the strict oversight constraint. The monitor sees only (user message, tool calls, tool outputs)—no internal CoT. This is argued to be more robust and realistic, forcing inference of intent from actions.
  - *Quick check*: Why might relying on the agent's Chain-of-Thought be a brittle monitoring signal? (Post-training RL can render reasoning traces illegible or strategically obfuscated; explanations are often unfaithful.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Synthetic Data Generation (STRIDE/Gloom) -> Monitor Training (Prompt Sweep / Optimization) -> Monitor Evaluation (ControlArena) -> Performance Metric (Partial AUROC)

### Critical Path
1. Specification: Define scheming constitution & desired behavior taxonomies.
2. Synthetic Data Generation: Run STRIDE/Gloom pipelines to produce labeled training trajectories (1k each). Apply filtering (realism, label integrity, difficulty).
3. Monitor Initialization: Construct prompt pool (LLM-generated + hand-written).
4. Optimization & Selection: Use grid search on synthetic train/val sets to select top prompt-model pairs.
5. Iterative Refinement (Optional): Apply human-guided or DSPy optimization *from the top grid-selected prompt*. **Key:** Avoid starting from a weak prompt, as this can lead to overfitting without transfer gains.
6. Evaluation: Run selected monitors on *held-out* ControlArena test set. Report partial AUROC.
**Crucial Dependency:** The synthetic train/val sets must be generated *before* any evaluation on ControlArena to prevent contamination. The test set is a fixed, separate resource.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Synthetic Diversity vs. Realism**: STRIDE/Gloom prioritize diversity and scale but risk generating unrealistic "tells." ControlArena prioritizes realism but is expensive and covers a narrower set of tasks/side-tasks. The paper bets that diversity captures the abstract concept better.
- **Prompting vs. Finetuning**: The paper uses prompting for its flexibility and low barrier. The observed ceiling suggests finetuning (local weight updates) might be necessary to avoid the "global shift" problem of prompts, but at higher cost/compute.
- **Optimization Intensity vs. Generalization**: Light optimization (grid search) finds a robust prompt. Heavy optimization (DSPy from a weak start, or many human refinement cycles) risks overfitting to synthetic artifacts. The tradeoff is between maximizing in-distribution score and ensuring out-of-distribution transfer.

### Failure Signatures
- **Overfitting (Observed)**: Training pAUROC improves while test pAUROC degrades or plateaus under heavy optimization (Figs 6b, 17, 18b). Indicates learning synthetic artifacts.
- **Mode Collapse in Generation**: If STRIDE's discriminator or Gloom's judge consistently rejects "interesting" but subtle scheming, the dataset becomes trivial (only obvious hacks). Check diversity metrics across misaligned task categories.
- **Monitor Refusal/Instability**: High rate of invalid scores (outside [1,10]) or LLM refusals, especially on certain topics (CBRN filters). Indicates prompt or data toxicity issues.
- **No Transfer**: Best synthetic-trained monitor performs at random on ControlArena test. Suggests fundamental mismatch between synthetic scheming concept and test instantiation, or that the synthetic signal is too weak/artifact-bound.
- **Metric Gaming**: A monitor that achieves high partial AUROC by being hyper-sensitive to a single, non-causal feature present only in the synthetic data (e.g., always flags if a specific tool like `curl` is used).

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline Establishment**: Run full prompt sweep (15 prompts x 3 LLMs) on both STRIDE and Gloom training sets. Identify top 3 prompt-model pairs per dataset. Evaluate these on *held-out* ControlArena test set. **Goal:** Confirm primary result: simple selection works and generalizes.
2. **Overfitting Stress Test**: Take #1 prompt from Experiment 1. Run heavy optimization (e.g., DSPy/GEPA) initialized from this prompt for 50 iterations (vs. paper's "light" budget). Plot training vs. test pAUROC across iterations. **Goal:** Reproduce saturation/overfitting curve (Fig 6b pattern) and identify iteration where test peak occurs.
3. **Ablation of Scoring Mechanism**: For fixed top monitor prompt-model pair, compare performance when forced to output: (a) single binary (YES/NO), (b) averaged 5 binary samples, (c) [1,10] integer, (d) [1,100] integer. Evaluate all on test set. **Goal:** Replicate that continuous/grayscale scoring is essential for low-FPR performance (Fig 11).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **What specific artifacts in synthetic training data cause monitors to overfit during aggressive optimization, and how can these be identified and mitigated?**
   - *Basis in paper*: The paper observes that extensive optimization leads to overfitting, with test performance degrading despite training improvements (Section 5.3). It hypothesizes that optimization exploits "idiosyncratic and noisy artifacts" of the synthetic distribution (Section 6).
   - *Why unresolved*: The study does not analyze or characterize these artifacts; it only documents the overfitting phenomenon without pinpointing causal features.
   - *What evidence would resolve it*: Perform feature attribution or error analysis on misclassified test trajectories to identify patterns unique to synthetic data (e.g., linguistic cues, action sequences). Then design targeted data augmentation or filtering (e.g., adversarial transformations) to remove these artifacts and re-evaluate monitor generalization.

2. **Can augmenting synthetic training data with techniques like paraphrasing, style transfer, or adversarial smoothing improve monitor robustness and prevent overfitting when using intensive prompt optimization?**
   - *Basis*: The discussion suggests that if the ceiling stems from distributional limitations, such augmentations could smooth decision boundaries and reduce overfitting (Section 6).
   - *Why unresolved*: The current STRIDE and Gloom pipelines prioritize diversity but do not incorporate such augmentations, and no experiments test their impact.
   - *What evidence*: Generate augmented versions of the synthetic datasets using the proposed methods, retrain monitors with identical optimization protocols, and compare test pAUROC (FPR<0.2) against baseline to measure improvements and overfitting reduction.

3. **Does finetuning the monitor LLM on synthetic scheming trajectories outperform prompt-based methods in low false-positive rate regimes, and does it circumvent the observed performance ceiling?**
   - *Basis*: The authors propose that monitor finetuning offers a promising alternative, as it may enable precise, local decision boundaries without the global behavior shifts that cause overfitting (Section 6), implying prompting has fundamental limits.
   - *Why unresolved*: The study evaluates only prompted monitors; finetuning is not implemented or compared.
   - *What evidence*: Finetune frontier models (e.g., via supervised fine-tuning on STRIDE/Gloom) and evaluate on ControlArena test set, comparing pAUROC and overfitting patterns to best prompted monitors.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data quality may be the fundamental bottleneck: The paper infers that data quality, not monitor capability, limits progress, but this cannot be definitively proven without a higher-quality synthetic dataset or a successful finetuning experiment.
- The synthetic generation process may not capture the full, subtle spectrum of real scheming (e.g., long-term planning, nuanced social manipulation), potentially limiting monitors to obvious failure modes due to privileged verifier filtering.
- The prompting paradigm itself may be inherently limited for this task, causing overfitting to synthetic artifacts and saturating performance early.
