# Chart-RL: Generalized Chart Comprehension via Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06958  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06958  
- Reference count: 8  
- Primary result: Chart-RL improves vision-language models' chart comprehension via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, achieving +16.7% on MultiChartQA and +11.5% on ChartInsights.

## Executive Summary
Chart-RL introduces a reinforcement learning (RL) approach for training vision-language models (VLMs) on chart comprehension tasks. Instead of supervised fine-tuning, it uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with two mathematically verifiable rewards: an accuracy reward checking the correctness of the answer and a format reward enforcing a structured output with `<thinking>` and `<answer>` tags. Trained on hard, multi-step chart queries, Chart-RL significantly outperforms supervised methods on multiple benchmarks, exhibits robustness across diverse chart perturbations, and achieves remarkable data efficiency—surpassing models trained on thousands of simple examples with only ten complex ones. Moreover, it transfers to out-of-domain visual mathematics, improving MathVerse by +55.6% without math-specific training, highlighting that reasoning complexity rather than data quantity drives generalization.

## Method Summary
Chart-RL builds upon a base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) and applies parameter-efficient fine-tuning using LoRA while keeping the vision modules trainable. The training loop employs GRPO: for each chart-question pair, the model generates \(N=8\) responses, which are evaluated by two binary rewards—accuracy (exact match with ground truth) and format (strict adherence to prescribed tags and JSON structure). These rewards are normalized within the group to compute advantages. The policy is updated via a clipped surrogate objective with a KL penalty against the reference model, avoiding a separate value critic. The training data consists of two sets: an "easy" set of 6,200 samples from PlotQA (simple numeric extraction) and a "hard" set of 448 samples from CharXiv (multi-step reasoning). Evaluation uses exact match accuracy on MultiChartQA, ChartInsights, RobustCQA, and MathVerse.

## Key Results
- +16.7% absolute improvement on MultiChartQA over SFT.
- +11.5% on ChartInsights.
- Outperforms SFT on 18 of 25 perturbed chart categories in RobustCQA.
- With only 10 complex training examples, surpasses a model trained on 6,000 simple ones.
- Transfers to visual mathematics: +55.6% relative improvement on MathVerse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Chart-RL succeeds through three interconnected mechanisms. First, it uses mathematically verifiable rewards—accuracy and format—to guide policy optimization toward correct and well-structured responses. GRPO samples multiple responses per query, normalizes their rewards within the group to form advantages, and updates the policy with a clipped objective and KL penalty, stabilizing learning under sparse binary signals. Second, training on hard, multi-step chart queries provides rich exploratory learning signals that cultivate transferable reasoning skills such as data extraction, comparison, and aggregation, which generalize to both simpler subtasks and out-of-domain visual math. Third, the group-relative advantage estimation eliminates the need for a learned value critic and reduces variance, making RL feasible with deterministic rewards. The method relies on the assumption that chart questions have verifiable ground truth and that a prescribed output structure improves interpretability and evaluation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (chart image + query) → Base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) → Generate N responses per prompt → Reward module (accuracy verifier + format checker) → GRPO optimizer (advantage computation, policy update with KL penalty) → Updated VLM weights (LoRA adapters; vision modules unfrozen).

- **Critical path:**  
  For each RL iteration: sample multiple responses → compute binary rewards → normalize advantages per-query group → perform gradient step with clipped objective and KL constraint → repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • Verifiable rewards restrict training to tasks with deterministic answers.  
  • LoRA reduces memory but may limit adaptation depth.  
  • Unfrozen vision modules allow visual feature tuning but increase compute.  
  • Group size (N=8) balances reward signal quality vs. sampling cost.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  • Accuracy reward plateauing early (tasks too easy or insufficient exploration).  
  • Format reward dropping (model ignoring structural constraints).  
  • Degradation on perturbed charts (e.g., log scale) suggests missing reasoning patterns.  
  • Catastrophic forgetting if KL penalty β is too low.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Compare Chart-RL against SFT and CoT-SFT on MultiChartQA and ChartInsights.  
  2. Test robustness across 25 perturbed chart categories in RobustCQA.  
  3. Study data efficiency by training with 10, 100, and 448 hard examples to demonstrate complexity over quantity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to non-deterministic tasks**: How can Chart-RL be extended to chart comprehension tasks that lack deterministic answers or require subjective interpretation? The current reward design relies on mathematically verifiable ground truths, limiting real-world applicability.
2. **Mechanism behind hard-task transfer**: Why does training on complex, multi-step chart reasoning tasks enable better generalization to simpler tasks and out-of-domain visual math compared to training on easy tasks? The paper shows transfer but does not analyze the underlying causal reasoning patterns.
3. **Robustness to mathematical transformations**: Why does Chart-RL degrade on perturbed charts involving log scale and scaling, and how can the reward or training data be modified to mitigate this? The robustness analysis reveals weaknesses but offers no explanation or remedy.
4. **Scaling with model size**: How does Chart-RL's effectiveness change with larger or different base VLMs? The study uses only Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct; generalizability to other architectures and scales remains unknown.
5. **Multi-stage curriculum**: Would a curriculum alternating SFT and RL improve accuracy and generalization beyond pure RL? This suggestion is untested and the optimal scheduling is unclear.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (learning rate, KL coefficient β, clipping range ε, group size N, generation settings) are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- Transfer to visual math (MathVerse) is not isolated; it may reflect dataset similarity rather than true generalization of reasoning skills.
- Verifiable rewards limit the method to tasks with deterministic answers, excluding subjective or ambiguous chart queries.

## Confidence
- Improvement via verifiable rewards: **High**  
- Transferability from hard multi-step training: **Medium**  
- GRPO stabilization of learning: **Medium**
