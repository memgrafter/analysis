# ZACH-ViT: Regime-Dependent Inductive Bias in Compact Vision Transformers for Medical Imaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17929  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17929  
- Reference count: 19  
- Primary result: ZACH-ViT demonstrates a regime-dependent advantage in few-shot medical image classification, achieving MacroF1 0.600±0.071 on BloodMNIST and remaining competitive on datasets with stronger spatial priors, despite only 0.25M parameters and no pretraining.

## Executive Summary
ZACH-ViT is a compact vision transformer (0.25M parameters) tailored for medical imaging under strict few-shot conditions (50 samples per class). It discards the standard positional embeddings and [CLS] token, opting for permutation-invariant processing via global average pooling over patch tokens. To ensure training stability in this ultra-compact regime, it introduces adaptive residual projections that initialise to zero when dimensions change. Evaluated across seven MedMNIST datasets, ZACH-ViT performs best on datasets with weak spatial structure (e.g., BloodMNIST) and sees diminishing relative gains as spatial priors strengthen (e.g., OCTMNIST, OrganAMNIST). Ablations confirm that positional encodings are beneficial only when spatial cues are strong, while the [CLS] token is consistently detrimental. This work highlights the importance of aligning architectural inductive biases with the underlying data regime, particularly when data and model size are severely limited.

## Method Summary
ZACH-ViT processes an input image by first splitting it into non-overlapping patches (size 16×16). Each patch is flattened and linearly projected to an embedding; no positional encodings are added, preserving permutation invariance. The resulting sequence of patch tokens passes through a series of transformer blocks. The block design incorporates adaptive residual projections: when the input and output dimensions differ (e.g., transition between stages), a zero‑initialised linear layer is applied to the residual branch to maintain gradient flow without adding significant parameters. After the final transformer stage, a global average pooling aggregates all patch tokens into a fixed‑size vector, which is fed to a linear classifier. The entire architecture is constrained to ~0.25 M parameters, with hyperparameters such as patch size 16, embedding dimensions [128, 64] across two stages, 8 attention heads, and MLP inner dimensions matching the stage widths. Training follows a strict 50‑shot‑per‑class protocol on MedMNIST datasets, using Adam (lr = 1e‑4, batch = 16) for 23 epochs from random initialisation. Identical preprocessing is applied across all models, though the exact resizing and normalisation steps are not fully specified.

## Key Results
- ZACH-ViT achieves the highest MacroF1 on BloodMNIST (0.600 ± 0.071), where spatial layout is diagnostically weak.
- Its relative advantage decreases on datasets with stronger anatomical priors (e.g., OCTMNIST, OrganAMNIST), confirming a regime‑dependent performance pattern.
- Ablations demonstrate that reintroducing positional embeddings helps only when spatial structure is strong, while adding a [CLS] token consistently degrades performance; global average pooling is the most robust aggregation method across all regimes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Removing positional embeddings and the [CLS] token yields a permutation‑invariant architecture that aligns with the inductive bias required for medical images where spatial layout is diagnostically weak or inconsistent.
- **Mechanism:** By omitting positional encodings, patch order becomes irrelevant; replacing the [CLS] token with global average pooling (GAP) aggregates patch representations without creating an ordered summary token. This forces the model to rely on local feature composition and set statistics rather than learning unstable spatial correlations.
- **Core assumption:** Spatial arrangement carries limited or non‑diagnostic information for the target task; discriminative signal resides in unordered patch collections.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "removes both positional embeddings and the [CLS] token, achieving permutation‑invariant patch processing via global average pooling"  
  [section 3.1] "no positional embeddings are added, preserving invariance to patch permutations"  
  [corpus] The neighbor paper on ZACH‑ViT (2510.17650) also removes positional encodings, suggesting an emerging design pattern, but direct validation for this mechanism is primarily from the present paper's internal ablations.
- **Break condition:** When spatial structure is strong and diagnostically relevant (e.g., OCTMNIST, OrganAMNIST), the lack of positional priors becomes a limiting factor, as shown by decreased relative advantage.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adaptive residual projections (zero‑initialised linear projections when residual and transformed branch dimensions differ) preserve gradient stability and prevent optimisation collapse in ultra‑compact transformer configurations.
- **Mechanism:** During training, if the dimensionality changes between the input and output of a transformer block, a learnable projection `W_proj` (initialised to zero) is applied to the residual branch. This ensures identity mapping at initialisation, avoiding sudden gradient shocks while adding negligible parameters.
- **Core assumption:** Standard residual connections fail when block input/output dimensions change under strict parameter budgets, causing instability in small models.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.2] "Adaptive residual projections preserve gradient flow across dimensional transitions while introducing minimal additional parameters, enabling stable training within the 0.25M‑parameter regime."  
  [section 6.2] The variant "- Adaptive Residuals" on BloodMNIST showed no consistent degradation, suggesting this mechanism supports stability without major accuracy trade‑offs in the weakest‑structure regime.
- **Break condition:** If model capacity is increased beyond the strict 0.25M regime (e.g., via wider MLPs), this mechanism may become unnecessary; however, removing it in the target compact configuration risks training instability.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Global average pooling (GAP) is the most robust token aggregation strategy for permutation‑invariant compact transformers across a spectrum of spatial structures.
- **Mechanism:** GAP computes a deterministic, symmetric average over all patch tokens, producing a fixed-size representation irrespective of patch order. This avoids bias toward any specific token (as in [CLS]) and avoids hard selection (as in max pooling).
- **Core assumption:** Distributed evidence across patches is more reliable than learned attention weights for aggregation in low‑data, parameter‑constrained settings.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 6.3] Table 5: GAP achieves the best or tied best MacroF1 on BloodMNIST, PathMNIST, and OCTMNIST.  
  [section 6.3] "GAP is the strongest and most robust default aggregation strategy overall."  
  [corpus] Evidence from corpus is indirect; related work on set‑based models (e.g., SONIC) also favours symmetric aggregations, but no direct citation supports GAP specifically for this regime.
- **Break condition:** As spatial structure increases, attention pooling shows marginal gains on PathMNIST and OCTMNIST, indicating GAP is not universally optimal, though the gains are small.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Patchify → Linear Projection → Transformer Blocks (L layers) → Global Average Pooling → Linear Classifier

### Critical path
The removal of positional embeddings and [CLS] token happens before transformer blocks. Adaptive projections are an internal block mechanism. GAP is the final aggregation step.

### Design tradeoffs
- *Invariance vs. spatial modeling:* Removing positional info grants permutation invariance but may discard useful anatomical layout cues.  
- *Compactness vs. capacity:* 0.25M parameters forces aggressive capacity allocation (e.g., no dedicated spatial modeling parameters).  
- *Simplicity vs. flexibility:* GAP is simple and stable but lacks learned token weighting (attention pooling).

### Failure signatures
- Performance collapse on datasets with strong anatomical priors (e.g., OrganAMNIST) relative to models with positional embeddings.  
- Training instability (loss spikes, NaN) without adaptive projections in the compact regime.  
- Increased variance across random seeds if patch size is too large (loss of local detail) or model too shallow/deep for the few‑shot regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalisation to larger clinical datasets**  
   - **Basis:** The authors state in the limitations that additional validation on larger clinical datasets beyond MedMNIST would clarify generality.  
   - **Why unresolved:** Evaluation is confined to seven MedMNIST datasets under a strict few‑shot protocol; real‑world data may have different distributions and complexity.

2. **Quantitative metric for spatial structure strength**  
   - **Basis:** The paper orders datasets on a qualitative "spatial‑structure strength index" based on domain knowledge, but no objective measure is provided.  
   - **Why unresolved:** Without a quantitative metric, selecting between positional and non‑positional architectures for new tasks remains qualitative and inconsistent, limiting practical deployment.

3. **Performance as training data increases**  
   - **Basis:** The paper uses a fixed 50‑shot protocol; interaction between data scale and inductive bias alignment is unexplored.  
   - **Why unresolved:** The regime‑dependent hypothesis suggests that with limited data, architectural alignment matters more. With abundant data, positional models might learn robust spatial patterns, potentially narrowing ZACH‑ViT's advantage or shifting the spectrum.

## Limitations
- **Architectural specifics:** The exact configuration (number of blocks, MLP expansion ratios) needed to achieve the 0.25M parameter budget is not fully detailed, hindering precise replication.  
- **Preprocessing details:** The "identical preprocessing" pipeline (resize target, normalisation, augmentations) is undefined, introducing potential performance variance not accounted for.  
- **Spatial‑structure index:** The core regime‑dependent hypothesis relies on a qualitative ordering of datasets; an objective, quantifiable metric for spatial structure strength is lacking.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Removing positional embeddings and the [CLS] token yields a permutation‑invariant architecture that benefits datasets with weak spatial diagnostics (e.g., BloodMNIST).  
- **Medium confidence:** Adaptive residual projections enable stable training in the ultra‑compact regime; global average pooling is the most robust pooling strategy across varying spatial structures.  
- **Low confidence:** Claims of competitive performance against larger pretrained models are context‑dependent; the advantage may not hold on larger, more diverse clinical datasets.
