# CosyAccent: Duration-Controllable Accent Normalization Using Source-Synthesis Training Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19166  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19166  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** A model trained solely on synthetic L2 source data and authentic native targets achieves a 12.96% WER and 3.04 UTMOS, significantly outperforming a strong token-based baseline trained on real L2 data (16.21% WER, 2.86 UTMOS) while matching accentedness reduction.

## Executive Summary
Accent normalization (AN) systems traditionally suffer from unnatural outputs and content distortion due to artifacts in synthetically generated target speech and rigid duration control. This paper introduces CosyAccent, which reverses the standard data pipeline: instead of synthesizing native targets, it synthesizes accented *sources* from a clean native corpus using a prompt-based TTS. This source-synthesis strategy provides authentic, high-quality native speech as training targets, eliminating the need for real L2 data. CosyAccent is a non-autoregressive model that uses a position-scaled cross-attention decoder to implicitly model rhythm while offering explicit total duration control. Evaluated on L2-ARCTIC, it achieves superior content preservation and naturalness compared to baselines trained on real L2 data, demonstrating the viability of a fully synthetic training paradigm for high-quality accent conversion.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is a source-synthesis training data pipeline. Starting from a native English corpus (LibriTTS-R), the method pairs each native utterance with a prompt of non-native accented speech (from L2-ARCTIC). A prompt-based TTS (CosyVoice2) then synthesizes a *source* audio sample that imprints the accent of the L2 prompt while preserving the timbre and text of the native utterance. The resulting parallel dataset has authentic native targets and synthetic L2 sources. The CosyAccent model converts the synthetic L2 source into native-sounding speech. It uses a frozen Whisper encoder for robust linguistic features, a separate speaker embedder (Resemblyzer) to preserve timbre, and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) decoder trained with flow matching. A key technical contribution is "position scaling": during training and inference, the positional indices of the source content features are linearly stretched to match the target Mel-spectrogram length, enabling explicit, stable duration control without sacrificing prosodic naturalness.

## Key Results
- **Content Preservation:** Achieves 12.96% WER, significantly lower than the 16.21% of a strong token-based baseline (TokAN-2) trained on real L2 data.
- **Naturalness:** Attains a UTMOS score of 3.04, superior to the baseline's 2.86.
- **Accent Reduction:** Matches the accentedness reduction performance of the real-data baseline while providing explicit duration control.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Authentic Targets via Source-Synthesis Avoid TTS Artifact Learning
The model trains on parallel pairs where the *target* is authentic, artifact-free native speech (from LibriTTS-R), and the *source* is a synthetic L2 accent generated by a high-quality TTS (CosyVoice2). This prevents the model from learning noise, prosodic quirks, or spectral discontinuities that would be present if the target itself were synthetically generated. The core assumption is that the primary quality limiter in prior AN systems is the gap between synthetic and authentic native speech. Bridging this gap by using real L1 as the target eliminates this ceiling, leading to improved content preservation and naturalness. Ablations are not provided to isolate this effect directly, but the overall superiority over a model trained on real L2 data (which has authentic sources but potentially imperfect targets) supports this mechanism.

### Mechanism 2: Position Scaling Enables Robust Explicit Duration Control
The DiT decoder uses cross-attention over source content features. During training, the positional indices of these features are scaled so their endpoint aligns perfectly with the target Mel-spectrogram's length. This establishes a coarse, length-agnostic alignment cue. At inference, manually specifying a target output length automatically rescales these positions, guiding the decoder to generate the correct number of frames without retraining. This decouples the exact timing of the output from the linguistic content, allowing the decoder to generate holistic Mel-spectrogram chunks (via flow matching) that implicitly model natural rhythm. The ablation in Table 3 shows model collapse without this scaling, proving its necessity for training stability and functional duration control.

### Mechanism 3: Separate Speaker Embedding Compensates for Whisper's Timbre Suppression
The frozen Whisper encoder provides robust, noise-robust linguistic features but is known to suppress speaker-specific timbre information. To ensure high-fidelity voice mimicry, the model explicitly conditions the decoder and duration predictor on a speaker embedding vector extracted by a dedicated model (Resemblyzer) from the *native target* speech. This two-pronged approach ensures the decoder receives clean linguistic content from Whisper and a distinct, high-fidelity timbre signal from the speaker embedder. The ablation in Table 3 shows a sharp drop in speaker similarity (SECS) when the speaker embedding is removed, confirming both Whisper's timbre suppression and the effectiveness of this solution.

## Foundational Learning

### Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) Loss
*Why needed here:* Applied as an auxiliary loss on the encoder's output to force the extracted content features to form a valid, monotonic alignment to the auxiliary text transcript. This stabilizes the content encoder's learning from the L2 source, which is critical because the decoder relies on these features for mapping. The ablation shows WER spikes dramatically without it.
*Quick check question:* How does a CTC loss on the encoder's output differ in purpose from the main speech generation loss on the decoder, and why is it particularly important when using a frozen frontend like Whisper?

### Flow Matching for Non-Autoregressive Generation
*Why needed here:* The DiT decoder is trained with flow matching to generate the velocity field of the Mel-spectrogram. This is the core non-autoregressive technique that allows parallel, high-quality spectrogram generation, avoiding error accumulation of autoregressive methods while maintaining coherence over long-range dependencies.
*Quick check question:* In the context of CosyAccent, what is the "source" distribution and the "target" distribution that the learned flow is connecting during training?

### Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) with Scaling
*Why needed here:* RoPE provides relative positional information within attention layers. The key innovation is applying a length-dependent scaling factor to the source sequence's positions *before* RoPE is applied. This is the technical implementation of "position scaling," creating a dynamic, length-aware alignment cue that links source content positions to the target output length.
*Quick check question:* If you double the desired output duration at inference, what happens to the scaled positional indices fed into the cross-attention over the source content features?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
L2 Source Audio -> Frozen Whisper Encoder -> Content Encoder (Transformer + CTC Head) -> Content Features (c) & CTC Loss. Parallel: Native Target Audio -> Speaker Embedder (Resemblyzer) -> Speaker Vector (s). (Optional: Duration Predictor from c & s -> Scaling Ratio r). Decoder (DiT with Flow Matching) takes Noisy Mel (x_t), Timestep (t), **Scaled** c, s -> Predicted Velocity. Final Mel -> HiFTNet Vocoder -> Output Waveform.

### Critical Path
1.  Source audio passes through Whisper to extract initial features.
2.  Content encoder processes features, produces content representations `c` and computes CTC loss against transcript.
3.  Speaker embedder extracts vector `s` from the *native target* audio.
4.  (Optional) Duration predictor estimates total duration scaling ratio `r` from `c` and `s`.
5.  Positions of `c` are scaled by `r` (or true ratio from data) to align with target Mel length.
6.  Decoder iterates (e.g., 32 Euler steps): takes `x_t`, `t`, scaled `c`, `s` -> predicts velocity -> updates `x_t`.
7.  Final Mel-spectrogram is converted to waveform by HiFTNet.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **NAR vs. AR:** Chose Non-Autoregressive (DiT) for fast, parallel generation and holistic spectrogram modeling, sacrificing some fine-grained temporal granularity. Position scaling mitigates NAR's inherent weakness in precise alignment.
- **Explicit vs. Implicit Duration:** Prioritized explicit total duration control (via predicted or inherited scaling ratio) for dubbing applications. Implicit rhythm is a byproduct of spectrogram-level NAR generation, which is flexible but less precisely controllable than phoneme-level predictors.
- **Data Source:** Sacrificed exposure to real L2 acoustics (using only TTS-synthesized L2) for access to perfect, authentic L1 targets. Assumes the TTS can generate plausible L2 speech and that the target quality ceiling is the dominant factor.

### Failure Signatures
- **Model Collapse:** Training instability or NaN loss if position scaling is omitted or misimplemented, as the decoder cannot learn variable-length mapping.
- **Content Distortion:** Sharp increase in WER if the CTC loss weight is too low or the content encoder is not properly constrained, leading to drifted linguistic representations.
- **Timbre Loss:** Drastic drop in speaker similarity (SECS) if the speaker embedding is ablated, confirming Whisper's timbre suppression and the necessity of the explicit condition.
- **Rhythmic Artifacts:** Unnatural prosody and potential content errors if the specified/ predicted duration scaling ratio is extreme (too short or too long), exceeding the model's implicit rhythm adaptation capacity.

### First 3 Experiments
1.  **Replicate Data Pipeline:** Generate a minimal source-synthesized dataset (e.g., 50 LibriTTS-R sentences paired with 5 L2-ARCTIC prompts). Verify synthetic L2 sources have measurable accent (using an accent classifier) and native targets are clean.
2.  **Test Position Scaling Necessity:** Train two tiny models (e.g., small DiT) on the minimal data: one with scaled positions, one with fixed absolute positions. Expect the unscaled model to fail to converge or produce degenerate output.
3.  **Ablate Speaker Condition:** Train the minimal model with and without the speaker embedding input to the decoder. Compute SECS on a held-out validation set. Expect the "- speaker" model to have a significantly lower SECS, confirming the timbre gap from Whisper and the embedder's role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does CosyAccent's performance degrade when applied to real-world, noisy L2 speech, given it was trained exclusively on clean, source-synthesized data?
- **Open Question 2:** What is the upper limit of the total duration scaling ratio that CosyAccent's position scaling mechanism can robustly handle before output quality deteriorates?
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent does the choice and accent diversity of the L2 prompts used in the source-synthesis pipeline affect the quality and accentedness of the resulting synthetic training data and, consequently, the final CosyAccent model?
- **Open Question 4:** What specific linguistic or prosodic representations from the L2 source speech are preserved by the frozen Whisper encoder and utilized by CosyAccent to achieve accent normalization, given that timbre is explicitly re-added via a speaker embedding?
- **Open Question 5:** Why does the source-synthesis training strategy, which introduces a domain gap between synthetic L2 source and real L2 test speech, still yield superior accentedness reduction and content preservation compared to models trained on real L2 data?

## Limitations
- The synthetic training data is very clean; robustness to acoustic noise, channel variations, and recorder quality found in real-world L2 speech is not established.
- The exact implementation details for position scaling and the duration predictor's training objective are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- The source-synthesis pipeline's reliance on CosyVoice2's capability to generate plausible L2 accents is a single-point potential failure; if its artifacts are systematic, the net benefit could be reduced.
- The model's performance on extremely aggressive duration scaling (compression or expansion) beyond the natural range of the training data (~1.3x) is unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Authentic targets improve quality):** *Medium*. Supported by comparative results but not by a direct ablation isolating target quality from source quality.
- **Mechanism 2 (Position scaling enables duration control):** *High*. Ablation shows complete training collapse without it, demonstrating necessity.
- **Mechanism 3 (Whisper + speaker embedding preserves timbre):** *High*. Ablation shows sharp SECS drop without speaker embedding, confirming the timbre gap and solution.
- **Overall claim (source-synthesis > real-L2-data training):** *Medium*. Results are strong but depend on the specific TTS used and the quality of the prompt set; generalizability to other TTS or languages is not proven.

## Next Checks
1.  **Extreme Duration Scaling Test:** Systematically vary the specified or predicted duration scaling ratio (e.g., 0.3x to 3.0x) and measure WER, UTMOS, and subjective naturalness to map the operational boundaries of the position scaling and implicit rhythm mechanism.
2.  **Artifact Profiling:** Compute objective metrics (e.g., Mel-Cepstral Distortion, periodicity) between: (a) synthetic L2 source, (b) authentic native target, and (c) a fully synthetic pair (synthetic L2 source & synthetic native target). This quantifies the "quality gap" assumed by Mechanism 1 and tests if source artifacts could become a new limiter.
3.  **Speaker Encoder Robustness:** Replace the Resemblyzer speaker embedder with a different architecture (e.g., ECAPA-TDNN) and re-evaluate SECS and SIM. This tests whether the timbre preservation result is specific to Resemblyzer or a general property of the explicit conditioning approach.
