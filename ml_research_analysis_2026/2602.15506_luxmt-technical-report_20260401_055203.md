# LuxMT Technical Report
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15506
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15506
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** LuxMT fine-tuned on filtered LuxAlign data shows statistically significant gains over the Gemma 3 baseline for LB→FR and LB→EN, with additional improvement for LB→DE despite no German training data.

## Executive Summary
LuxMT is the first dedicated machine translation system for Luxembourgish, addressing a critical low-resource gap. It fine-tunes the strong multilingual Gemma 3 27B model on a high-quality subset of the LuxAlign parallel corpus, filtered using the LuxEmbedder sentence similarity metric. Evaluated on a new human-translated benchmark (Luci, 500 pairs per direction), LuxMT achieves statistically significant improvements over the base model for all three target languages (French, English, German). The cross-lingual transfer to German from French/English fine-tuning is a notable finding. The study also explores LuxEmbedder as a reference-less quality estimator, finding strong correlation with reference-based metrics but calling for further validation.

## Method Summary
The method involves acquiring parallel Luxembourgish data from two sources: the existing LuxAlign news corpus and parliamentary transcripts (monolingual Luxembourgish machine-translated to French/English). This raw data is filtered using LuxEmbedder, a Luxembourgish sentence encoder, applying a high cosine similarity threshold (≥0.99 for LuxAlign, ≥0.98 for transcripts) to remove low-equivalence segment pairs. The remaining high-quality pairs are used to fine-tune a Gemma 3 27B Instruct model for 1 epoch at a learning rate of 2e-5, using a simple prompt template. The resulting system, LuxMT, is evaluated on the held-out Luci benchmark using an ensemble of automatic metrics (BERTScore, BLEURT-20, XCOMET XL) with statistical significance testing.

## Key Results
- LuxMT significantly outperforms the Gemma 3 baseline on LB→FR and LB→EN according to multiple metrics (p<0.05).
- LuxMT also shows significant improvement for LB→DE, a zero-shot direction with no explicit training data.
- Estimated human pairwise accuracy for metric deltas exceeds 90%, suggesting substantial real-world quality gains.
- LuxEmbedder cosine similarity correlates strongly with reference-based metrics but its utility as a standalone quality estimation metric requires further research.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Fine-Tuning with High-Quality Filtered Parallel Data
- **Claim:** Filtering LuxAlign with a high LuxEmbedder cosine similarity threshold (≥0.99) removes low-equivalence pairs, creating a cleaner fine-tuning dataset.
- **Mechanism:** Model parameter updates are confined to semantically aligned source-target pairs, preventing the model from learning from noisy translations and leading to better convergence.
- **Core Assumption:** Sentence embedding cosine similarity is a reliable proxy for translation equivalence at the segment level.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Use of LuxEmbedder for filtering; [section 6.2.1] Threshold of 0.99 yields best performance despite 85.2% data reduction.
- **Break Condition:** If the threshold eliminates too much data or valid linguistically diverse constructions, performance may degrade due to insufficient coverage.

### Mechanism 2: Leveraging a Strong Multilingual Base Model (Gemma 3 27B)
- **Claim:** Starting from a powerful, broadly multilingual LLM provides latent cross-lingual knowledge that fine-tuning efficiently specializes for low-resource Luxembourgish.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-training already establishes robust cross-lingual representations; fine-tuning adapts these to Luxembourgish-specific patterns using a small, clean dataset.
- **Core Assumption:** A large modern multilingual LLM possesses transferable knowledge for low-resource pairs that can be unlocked via targeted fine-tuning.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 5] Gemma 3 outperforms other local LLMs initially; [section 7] Fine-tuned version (LuxMT) significantly beats Gemma 3 baseline.
- **Break Condition:** If the base model's pre-training lacks relevant Luxembourgish/related language content or its architecture is unsuitable for SFT, gains would be minimal.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Lingual Transfer to Zero-Shot Language Pairs
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning on LB→FR and LB→EN transfers to improved LB→DE quality despite no German training data, due to linguistic proximity and the model's existing cross-lingual knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** Fine-tuning refines Luxembourgish representations and general translation mechanisms; combined with pre-trained German knowledge, this enhances zero-shot German generation. The Germanic relationship between LB and DE facilitates this.
- **Core Assumption:** Linguistic similarity and the model's pre-trained multilingual space enable positive transfer from FR/EN fine-tuning to DE generation.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Improvement for LB→DE despite no DE data; [section 7] Finding aligns with literature on LLM cross-lingual capabilities.
- **Break Condition:** If LB is not similar enough to DE in the embedding space, or if FR/EN fine-tuning causes negative interference (catastrophic forgetting), LB→DE performance might degrade.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Noise-Robust Data Filtering for Low-Resource MT**
  - **Why needed:** Raw Luxembourgish parallel data (LuxAlign) contains non-equivalent sentence pairs from news articles. Filtering is essential to create a viable training set.
  - **Quick Check:** If you lowered the LuxEmbedder filter threshold from 0.99 to 0.90, what is the predicted trade-off between data quantity and expected model performance, based on the paper's preliminary results?

- **Concept: Reference-Based vs. Reference-Less (QE) Evaluation Metrics**
  - **Why needed:** The paper relies on automatic metrics. Understanding the differences between semantic similarity metrics (BERTScore, BLEURT), surface-overlap metrics (BLEU), and the novel reference-less LuxEmbedder is key to interpreting results.
  - **Quick Check:** Why did the authors advise caution when interpreting LuxEmbedder scores as a QE metric, even though it correlated strongly with other metrics?

- **Concept: Statistical Significance Testing in MT Evaluation**
  - **Why needed:** With a modest test set (N=500), observed metric differences could be due to chance. Paired bootstrap resampling determines if improvements are reliable.
  - **Quick Check:** What does a statistically significant delta (p<0.05) for BLEURT-20 on LB→EN tell you about the likelihood that the observed improvement reflects a real quality difference?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Gemma 3 27B Instruct (Base Model) <- Fine-Tuning <- Filtered Parallel Data (LuxAlign + Transcripts, filtered by LuxEmbedder) -> LuxMT -> Evaluation (BERTScore, BLEURT-20, XCOMET) on Luci Benchmark
- **Critical path:**
  1.  Acquire/construct parallel data (LuxAlign + augmented transcripts).
  2.  Apply LuxEmbedder filtering (threshold selection via preliminary test).
  3.  Fine-tune Gemma 3 for 1 epoch at LR=2e-5 on filtered data (LB-FR & LB-EN mixtures).
  4.  Evaluate on held-out Luci benchmark for all three directions (LB-FR, LB-EN, LB-DE).
  5.  Analyze metric correlations and statistical significance.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Filter threshold (0.99 vs 0.98):** Higher threshold ensures clean data but discards ~85% of LuxAlign. Lower threshold retains more but risks noise. A precision/recall trade-off for training signal quality.
  - **Benchmark domain:** Luci is a tourist magazine. This controls for domain but limits generalizability to news or parliamentary text.
  - **Metric selection:** An ensemble of neural metrics mitigates bias but increases cost. Surface-overlap metrics are reported but de-emphasized.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Training instability:** If fine-tuned model perplexity on a clean validation set does not decrease or degrades sharply, filtered data may be too small or threshold too high.
  - **Directional failure:** No improvement on LB→DE could indicate failed cross-lingual transfer, possibly due to insufficient base model German knowledge or negative interference from fine-tuning.
  - **QE metric discrepancy:** If LuxEmbedder scores show poor correlation with neural metrics on new systems, its utility as a QE metric is not generalizable.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Model Selection Baseline:** Run all candidate base models (Gemma 3, Llama, etc.) on Luci with a fixed prompt to confirm Gemma 3's superiority.
  2.  **Filter Threshold Sweep:** Fine-tune separate Gemma 3 models on LuxAlign filtered at thresholds 0.90, 0.95, 0.99. Evaluate on a held-out Luci subset to find optimal quality/quantity balance.
  3.  **Training Schedule Check:** Fine-tune the chosen model for 1, 2, and 3 epochs. Evaluate to confirm one epoch is optimal and detect overfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How suitable is LuxEmbedder as a quality estimation metric for Luxembourgish MT, and how robust are its correlations with reference-based metrics under human validation?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states: "We call for further research to fully assess the metric's utility and advise using it with caution."
- **Why unresolved:** Only correlations with automatic metrics are reported; no human judgments confirm if LuxEmbedder aligns with perceived quality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale human evaluation of MT outputs, measuring agreement (e.g., pairwise accuracy) between LuxEmbedder scores and human preferences across diverse systems and domains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the small size (500 segment pairs) and single-domain nature of the Luci benchmark yield unreliable or high-variance performance estimates for LuxMT, potentially overfitting to the test set?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations section notes the benchmark is "limited in its domain-scope" and "limited to a rather small size of 500 segment pairs per language pair which may impact the results’ reliability," citing Kocmi et al. (2024).
- **Why unresolved:** All improvements are based on this modest benchmark; without larger/more diverse test data, generalizability is unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-evaluate on a larger, multi-domain Luxembourgish MT test set; perform power analysis; compare effect sizes to establish stability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do translation artifacts (translationese) in the Luci benchmark, due to the unknown original source language, bias the evaluation of LuxMT's translation quality?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper notes: "It is not clear what the source language of Luci is... the LB text could contain translation artifacts."
- **Why unresolved:** If the source LB is itself a translation, the test data may not reflect natural Luxembourgish, potentially biasing metric scores or favouring models trained on translated data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform translationese detection on Luci LB segments and compare performance on subsets with/without artifacts; or build a new benchmark from native LB texts.

## Limitations
- **Data and Model Access Blocked:** The `Luci` test set and `LuxEmbedder` model are not publicly released, preventing exact replication and external validation of filtering/evaluation.
- **Domain Specificity:** Evaluation is on a tourist magazine corpus; performance may not generalize to news or parliamentary domains despite training on such data.
- **Transfer Mechanism Uncertainty:** The LB→DE zero-shot improvement is attributed to linguistic proximity and model knowledge, but without controlled ablation (e.g., training on only FR or EN), the precise pathway remains inferred.

## Confidence
- **Filtering Efficacy:** High (Strong internal evidence from threshold sweep and drastic data reduction supports cleaner data; tempered by unknown generalizability of LuxEmbedder as a proxy).
- **Fine-Tuning Gains:** Medium-High (Statistically significant improvements on a novel benchmark are compelling; limited by benchmark's narrow domain and lack of human evaluation beyond metric deltas).
- **Cross-Lingual Zero-Shot Transfer:** Low-Medium (Result is consistent with LLM literature but is a single observation without isolating contributing factors; requires direct validation).
- **QE Correlation:** Medium (Strong correlation in this study is noted, but correlation does not guarantee utility for quality estimation on unseen, noisy data).

## Next Checks
1.  **Ablate Fine-Tuning Data Directions:** Train separate models on *only* LB→FR and *only* LB→EN data. Evaluate all three directions (including LB→DE) to isolate whether transfer to German is driven more by French, English, or their combination.
2.  **Test Filter Threshold Robustness:** Fine-tune models with lower `LuxEmbedder` thresholds (e.g., 0.95, 0.90) on the same `LuxAlign` corpus. Measure performance on a held-out *in-domain* validation set to confirm the predicted trade-off (more data vs. noise) and validate the optimal 0.99 choice.
3.  **External QE Validation of LuxEmbedder:** Compute `LuxEmbedder` scores on an existing, publicly available noisy parallel corpus for a related language pair (e.g., DE-EN). Correlate these scores with human judgments of translation quality (if available) or with established reference-based metrics to test generalizability as a quality estimation metric.
