# AesRec: A Dataset for Aesthetics-Aligned Clothing Outfit Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03416
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03416
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: AesRec improves recommendation aesthetics (ΔAestheticScore@20 +0.191) while maintaining competitive personalized performance (Recall@20 0.0990).

## Executive Summary
Fashion recommendation systems often overlook aesthetic quality, leading to suggestions that may be personally relevant but stylistically incoherent. To address this, the authors introduce AesRec, comprising two main contributions: (1) the AesRec dataset, which annotates 27,694 outfits and 42,526 items with quantitative aesthetic scores across eight outfit-level and six item-level dimensions using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) validated by human experts (Pearson r > 0.79); and (2) the AesRec recommendation model, which integrates these aesthetic scores via a joint loss function to simultaneously optimize for personalization and aesthetic quality. Experiments show that AesRec substantially improves the aesthetic appeal of recommendations (ΔAestheticScore@20 = +0.191) and yields positive ranking exposure gaps (ExpoGap@20 = +0.616), without sacrificing standard personalization metrics like Recall@20 (0.0990). This work establishes a foundation for aesthetics-aware fashion recommendation.

## Method Summary
The authors first construct the AesRec dataset by leveraging a VLM to produce multi-dimensional aesthetic scores for clothing items and full outfits. The VLM’s outputs are validated against human experts, demonstrating strong agreement (Pearson correlation > 0.79). This dataset provides both item-level and outfit-level aesthetic annotations for tens of thousands of examples. The proposed AesRec model builds on a typical recommendation framework (e.g., a two-tower architecture) by augmenting item representations with the predicted aesthetic score vector. The model is trained with a joint loss that combines a standard recommendation objective (e.g., Bayesian Personalized Ranking) with an auxiliary aesthetic regression loss that encourages predicted scores to match the VLM-provided targets. By tuning the relative weight of these losses, the system can balance personalization and aesthetic alignment.

## Key Results
- ΔAestheticScore@20 improves by +0.191 compared to baselines.
- ExpoGap@20 reaches +0.616, indicating better exposure for aesthetically consistent items.
- Recall@20 remains competitive at 0.0990, showing no loss in personalization.
- Dataset scale: 27,694 outfits and 42,526 items with fine-grained aesthetic annotations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AesRec succeeds because it directly incorporates a comprehensive, quantifiable notion of aesthetic quality into the recommendation objective. The use of a VLM to generate consistent, multi-dimensional aesthetic scores provides a scalable proxy for human judgment, validated by strong correlation with experts. By optimizing both personalization (through standard interaction data) and aesthetic alignment (through the joint loss), the model learns to rank items that are not only relevant to user preferences but also stylistically harmonious. This dual optimization prevents the common pitfall of aesthetic-only recommenders that ignore user taste, while also addressing the aesthetic blindness of purely personalized models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models (VLM) for Aesthetic Scoring**  
  *Why needed*: VLMs can interpret both visual and textual descriptions to produce nuanced aesthetic judgments, enabling automated annotation at scale.  
  *Quick check*: Validate VLM outputs against a sample of human expert ratings (Pearson > 0.79).

- **Joint Loss Optimization**  
  *Why needed*: Simultaneously training on both recommendation and aesthetic objectives balances personalization and style coherence.  
  *Quick check*: Ablate the aesthetic loss component and measure degradation in AestheticScore@20.

- **Multi-dimensional Aesthetic Dimensions**  
  *Why needed*: Outfit aesthetics are multi-faceted; covering eight outfit-level and six item-level dimensions captures complexity beyond a single score.  
  *Quick check*: Ensure dimension coverage is representative by inspecting correlation patterns among dimensions and with overall scores.

- **Dataset Construction with Automated Annotation**  
  *Why needed*: Large-scale fashion datasets lack aesthetic labels; using VLMs fills this gap efficiently.  
  *Quick check*: Perform manual spot-checks on a random subset of VLM-annotated items to confirm plausibility.

- **Recommendation Evaluation Metrics**  
  *Why needed*: Standard metrics like Recall@k measure personalization; new metrics (AestheticScore, ExpoGap) quantify aesthetic quality and exposure fairness.  
  *Quick check*: Compare metric distributions across baselines to confirm that gains are not due to metric artifact.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
User History -> User Encoder -> User Embedding  
Item (image/text) -> Item Encoder -> Item Embedding + Aesthetic Score Vector -> Joint Scoring -> Relevance Score -> Ranking

**Critical Path**
1. Item features are extracted (e.g., via a CNN or CLIP-style encoder).
2. Parallelly, a VLM produces an aesthetic score vector for the item/outfit.
3. The aesthetic vector is fused with the item embedding (e.g., concatenation or attention).
4. User embedding is learned from past interactions (e.g., matrix factorization or neural network).
5. A scoring function computes a joint relevance score by interacting user and item+ aesthetic embeddings.
6. Items are ranked by score for recommendation.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- *VLM vs. computational cost*: VLMs provide accurate aesthetic scores but increase inference latency; may be precomputed for static items.
- *Balance via λ*: The joint loss weight λ trades off personalization vs. aesthetics; requires tuning on validation set.
- *Dataset bias*: VLM training data may reflect Western fashion norms, limiting generalization to other cultures.

**Failure Signatures**
- *Aesthetic domination*: Very high λ leads to recommendations that are beautiful but irrelevant to user history.
- *Coherence breakdown*: Model fails to capture outfit-level compatibility, yielding high item-level aesthetic scores but poor outfit coordination.
- *Exposure inequality*: Despite positive ExpoGap, certain user subgroups may still receive inequitable exposure due to dataset skew.

**First Three Experiments**
1. **Ablation Study**: Remove the aesthetic score component or replace it with random scores; measure drop in AestheticScore@20 to confirm its contribution.
2. **λ Sensitivity Analysis**: Train AesRec with varying λ (e.g., 0.0, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0) and plot tradeoff between Recall@20 and AestheticScore@20 to identify optimal region.
3. **Qualitative Inspection**: Manually review top-10 outfits from AesRec vs. a baseline for a set of users, evaluating style compatibility and diversity.

## Limitations
- Potential biases in VLM training data may limit the generalizability of aesthetic scores across cultures and body types.
- Automated scores may not fully capture nuanced human judgments on style compatibility.
- Dataset coverage of diverse global fashion cultures and body types is limited.
- The reported positive ExpoGap may not ensure equitable exposure for all user subgroups without further subgroup analysis.
