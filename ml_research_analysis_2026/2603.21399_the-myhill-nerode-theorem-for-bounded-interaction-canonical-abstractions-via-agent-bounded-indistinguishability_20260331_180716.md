# The Myhill‑Nerode Theorem for Bounded Interaction: Canonical Abstractions via Agent‑Bounded Indistinguishability

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21399  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21399  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A bounded‑interaction analogue of the Myhill‑Nerode theorem: finite POMDPs admit canonical state abstractions via probe‑family‑parametrized Wasserstein indistinguishability, with exact value preservation for observation‑action measurable objectives and provable approximation bounds for latent rewards.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a theoretical foundation for state abstraction in partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) when the observer is capacity‑limited (e.g., governed by finite‑memory controllers). It defines a closed‑loop Wasserstein pseudometric on observation histories, parameterised by a fixed probe family of finite‑state controllers. Two histories are equivalent if no controller in the probe family can distinguish their future observation distributions. This equivalence yields a unique minimal quotient POMDP—a bounded‑interaction Myhill‑Nerode theorem. The authors prove three core results: (i) For clock‑aware probes and objectives measurable on joint observation‑action trajectories, the exact quotient preserves expected return for all clock‑aware policies. (ii) For rewards that depend on latent states, an observation‑Lipschitz constant guarantees an additive error scaling linearly with horizon and merge tolerance. (iii) Operational deterministic‑stationary probes define a coarser but tractable quotient; a measured cross‑family pseudometric gap δ_clk certificates value transfer from the clock‑aware to the operational quotient. Experimental validation on Tiger, GridWorld, and RockSample benchmarks demonstrates substantial state‑space reduction (up to 94%) with negligible value loss when goals align with observations.

## Method Summary
The construction proceeds as follows. Given a finite POMDP, a memory bound `m`, a horizon `T`, and a probe family (either time‑indexed “clock‑aware” or deterministic stationary), compute for every observation history `h` the distribution over future observation sequences under each probe. The history‑level pseudometric `d_Π(h,h')` is the supremum over probes of the 1‑Wasserstein distance between these distributions (using a ground metric on observations). Histories are then clustered with complete linkage: two histories merge if their pseudometric ≤ `ε`. The quotient POMDP uses equivalence classes as states, with transitions derived from uniform averaging over class members’ one‑step observation probabilities and canonical aggregated beliefs. For exact theorems, `ε=0` and the clock‑aware family are used; for scalability, the operational family is employed and the cross‑family gap `δ_clk` measured on small instances to certify approximate guarantees.

## Key Results
- **Exact sufficiency:** For the clock‑aware family and any objective in `G_obs` (measurable on joint observation‑action sequences), the quotient POMDP preserves expected return for all clock‑aware policies (Theorem 4.6).
- **Approximate bound:** For rewards that are `L_R`‑Lipschitz in observations, merging histories with `d_Π ≤ ε` incurs an additive value error `|V^π - Ṽ^π| ≤ L_R · T · ε` (Theorem 3.6). This can be vacuous if `L_R` is large.
- **Operational certification:** The deterministic‑stationary quotient is a coarsening of the clock‑aware one. If the cross‑family gap `δ_clk = ∥d_clk - d_op∥_∞` is measured, then for all `π ∈ Π_clk` the value error on `Q_op` is bounded by `L_R T (ε + δ_clk)` (Theorem 4.12). Greedy controller‑subset selection reduces the probe family size while controlling `δ_S`.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Bounded‑indistinguishability equivalence.**  
Histories are equivalent if no controller in the probe family can distinguish their future observation distributions. This defines a canonical right‑invariant relation. The quotient POMDP merges histories that are `ε`‑close under the closed‑loop Wasserstein pseudometric `d_Π = sup_{π∈Π} W1`. For `ε=0`, the partition is minimal and unique. *Break condition:* if the probe family is too coarse, distinguishably different histories merge.

**Mechanism 2: Exact sufficiency for agent‑accessible objectives.**  
For the clock‑aware family and any reward `G` measurable on the joint observation‑action trajectory (`G^obs`), the exact quotient inherits the full observation law, and because actions are measurable functions of histories, also the joint observation‑action law. Integrating `G` over these laws yields identical values for all clock‑aware policies (Theorem 4.6). *Break condition:* latent‑state‑dependent rewards violate the assumption and fall back to the approximate bound, which may be loose if `L_R` is large.

**Mechanism 3: Cross‑family value transfer via measured gaps.**  
The operational deterministic‑stationary family is a subset of the clock‑aware family, so `d_op ≤ d_clk` (Prop. 4.11). If histories are `ε`‑merged under `d_op`, their `d_clk`‑diameter is at most `ε + δ_clk`, where `δ_clk = ∥d_clk - d_op∥_∞` is explicitly computed on a small exact benchmark. Theorem 4.12 then gives the additive value bound for all `π∈Π_clk`. *Break condition:* If `δ_clk` is not measured (e.g., in scaling experiments), the operational quotient has no formal guarantee.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
POMDP + probe family → distance computation (pairwise `W1` per history/probe) → clustering (complete linkage with threshold `ε`) → quotient construction (state classes, aggregated beliefs, transitions) → policy evaluation (compute values) → (optional cross‑family certification via measured `δ_clk`)

**Critical path**  
1. Choose probe family (`Π_clk` for theorem; `Π_op` for scalable).  
2. Enumerate or sample histories (depth `T`).  
3. Compute pairwise distances `d_Π(h,h') = max_π W1(...)` (LP or simulation).  
4. Apply complete‑linkage clustering to obtain equivalence classes.  
5. Materialise the quotient POMDP using canonical aggregated beliefs.  
6. For cross‑family transfer, compute `δ_clk` on a small exact instance and invoke the error bound.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Probe family:** `Π_clk` gives exact sufficiency for `G^obs` but is expensive; `Π_op` is tractable but coarser (Prop. 4.11).  
- **Exact vs approximate merger:** `ε=0` yields minimal quotient; increasing `ε` compresses more but incurs `L_R T ε` error.  
- **Layering vs monolithic:** Horizon decomposition (Theorem 5.6) reduces `|O|^T` to `L·|O|^τ` but accumulates wrapper distortions.  
- **Subset selection:** Greedy controller selection (Alg. 2) reduces `|Π|` drastically with a posteriori gap control; the trade‑off is the risk that a small subset may miss some distinguishing behaviors.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Value bound vacuity:** `L_R` large (e.g., standard Tiger reward `L_R=110`) makes `L_R T ε` exceed the value range; ensure reward is `L_R`‑bounded.  
- **Unmeasured cross‑family gap:** Scaling experiments using `Q_op` without measuring `δ_clk` have no formal guarantee; interpret results as empirical diagnostics.  
- **Exponential blowup:** History tree size `|O|^T`; even `m=1` FSC enumeration (`|A|^T`) can become costly. Mitigate via sampling, layering, or `δ_O`‑coarsening.  
- **LP bottleneck:** `W1` solves dominate runtime (>99% on small POMDPs). Parallelize or use faster LP solvers.

**First three experiments (reproduction order)**  
1. **Exact sufficiency validation:** Compute `Q_clk` on Tiger for `T=2–10` and an observation‑action objective (e.g., constant reward). Verify `V_M^π = V_{Q_clk}^π` exactly (Table 3).  
2. **Cross‑family gap measurement:** On small Tiger and GridWorld (`T≤4`), separately build `Q_clk` and `Q_op` with `ε=0`. Compute `δ_clk = ∥d_clk - d_op∥∞`; check it matches values in Table 2.  
3. **Operational scaling with certification:** Apply greedy FSC subset selection to `Π_op` on GridWorld `3×3` (`m=2`) and confirm that a small subset (`k=3`) achieves `δ_S=0` (Table 5). Then use the measured `δ_clk` to bound value loss for `Π_clk` policies on `Q_op`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1 – Computational complexity of the quotient construction.**  
What is the complexity of computing the exact probe‑exact quotient for a finite POMDP? The known algorithm is exponential in `T`; is the problem NP‑hard, or does it admit a polynomial‑time algorithm? (Section 8, Limitations; Appendix F)

**Open Question 2 – Distinguishability lower bounds.**  
Given two non‑equivalent histories, how large must their probe distance `D_Π(h, h')` be? Currently no general lower bound is known (Section 8, Limitations).

**Open Question 3 – Predicting the cross‑family gap `δ_clk`.**  
Can `δ_clk` be upper‑bounded a priori without explicitly computing the clock‑aware family? This would enable certified use of the operational quotient at scale (Future Directions).

**Open Question 4 – Relation to predictive complexity.**  
Conjecture 8.1 posits that the minimal controller subset size needed to achieve error `δ` is polynomially controlled by the Hankel rank `r_H(M,T)` together with `(m,|A|,δ^{-1})`. Is this true? This would connect bounded‑interaction abstraction to PSR/OOM theory.

## Limitations
- **GridWorld specification missing.** The exact observation metric, transition dynamics, initial state, and reward functions used in the GridWorld experiments are not provided; any reproduction must assume these details.
- **Probe enumeration ambiguity.** The reported count of deterministic stationary FSCs (e.g., 147 for Tiger `m=2`) depends on subtleties of the enumeration rule; a different count yields different probe tensors and could affect `δ_S` results.
- **Reward‑function dependence.** The exact reward functions used in Tables 3‑4 are unspecified; we infer a constant reward for Tiger from the numbers, but GridWorld values differ, indicating a non‑trivial reward. Without them, numeric replication of those tables is impossible.

## Confidence
- **Exact quotient theorem (Theorem 4.6):** High – proof and Tiger verification confirm value preservation.
- **Approximate bound for latent rewards (Theorem 3.6):** Medium – proof appears sound but requires testing on actual latent rewards.
- **Cross‑family `δ_clk` transfer (Theorem 4.12):** Medium – mechanism is clear; empirical gaps need exact GridWorld details for confirmation.
- **Empirical compression claims:** Medium‑Low – depend on unspecified environment details; trends likely hold but exact class counts may vary.
