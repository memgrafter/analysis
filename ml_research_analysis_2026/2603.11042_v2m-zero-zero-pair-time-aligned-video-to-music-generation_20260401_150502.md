# V2M-Zero: Zero-Pair Time-Aligned Video-to-Music Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11042  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11042  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Zero-pair video-to-music generation with event curves outperforms paired-data baselines on alignment and quality metrics.

## Executive Summary
V2M-Zero addresses video-to-music generation without requiring paired video-music training data. It introduces intra-modal event curves—1D signals capturing when and how much change occurs within a modality—as a modality-agnostic temporal synchronization mechanism. By fine-tuning a text-to-music model with music event curves and swapping in video event curves at inference, the system generates music that aligns with video events. Evaluations on OES-Pub, MovieGenBench-Music, and AIST++ show significant gains over prior work in objective metrics (FAD, CLAP, Scene Cut Hit, Beat Hit) and human evaluations (68–77% win-rates), with lightweight fine-tuning costs.

## Method Summary
The method first extracts feature sequences from audio (using MusicFM) or video (using DINOv2 or CoTracker). From these, it computes event curves: pairwise cosine dissimilarity between consecutive features, followed by standardization (zero mean, unit variance), resampling to a fixed length, and Hann-window smoothing. A rectified flow diffusion model (DiT-based) is fine-tuned on music-text pairs, with the music event curve concatenated to the diffusion latent as an extra conditioning channel. At inference, for a given video, its event curve replaces the music curve while the model weights remain fixed; a text prompt is auto-generated from the video (via Whisper and an LLM), and music is generated conditioned on both the text and video curve.

## Key Results
- Surpasses paired-data baselines: FAD improves 5–21%, CLAP 13–15%, Scene Cut Hit 21–52%, Beat Hit Score 28% on dance.
- Human evaluations show 68–77% win-rates for quality and temporal alignment.
- Generalizes across video types by selecting appropriate visual encoders, with fine-tuning costs of 192–768 GPU hours.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Intra-modal event curves as temporal synchronization signals**  
Temporal alignment is achieved by matching the timing and magnitude of changes within each modality independently. Event curves are derived from intra-modal dissimilarity (1 − cosine similarity) of high-level features, yielding 1D peaks at moments of change (e.g., beats, scene cuts). The core assumption is that the temporal pattern of change is modality-agnostic—music and video may differ in *what* changes but share *when* changes occur. Break conditions include fundamentally misaligned change patterns or poor feature encoders. Evidence: abstract and section 3.2 emphasize matching "when and how much change occurs, not what changes"; cited references [22,39,76] support self-similarity for rhythm/shot detection.

**Mechanism 2: Modality gap mitigation through standardization, resampling, and smoothing**  
Music and video event curves originate from different feature spaces and must be aligned for zero-shot swap. Standardization removes scale/offset differences; resampling matches the music latent temporal dimension; Hann smoothing suppresses modality-specific details while preserving peak locations. These operations yield curves with similar statistical properties. Core assumption: crucial temporal structure (relative timing and magnitudes) is retained. Break conditions: excessive smoothing blurs fine events; inadequate standardization if distributions are non-Gaussian. Evidence: section 3.2 describes these steps; section 5.3 ablations show smoothing kernel trade-offs (Figure 4) and encoder impacts (Table 4).

**Mechanism 3: Test-time conditioning swap for zero-pair transfer**  
A text-to-music model fine-tuned with music event curve conditioning can generate video-synchronized music by simply replacing the conditioning signal with a video event curve at inference, without cross-modal training. The model learns to modulate generation based on the temporal structure of the curve, which is assumed to be modality-invariant. Break conditions: overfitting to music-curve distributions or conflicts between text prompt and video curve. Evidence: abstract and section 3.4 describe the swap; section 5.1 shows V2M-Zero outperforms paired-data baselines, validating the approach.

## Foundational Learning
- **Event curves as novelty signals**: Needed to bridge modalities by capturing *when* changes happen, independent of semantics. Quick check: How does the choice of feature encoder (e.g., DINOv2 vs. V-JEPA) affect robustness to non-semantic perturbations?
- **Rectified flow diffusion for audio generation**: Foundation for the T2M backbone; understanding conditioning injection and fine-tuning is essential. Quick check: What is classifier-free guidance’s role, and how might event curve conditioning interact with it?
- **Modality gap and zero-shot transfer**: Central to swapping without joint training; understanding why distributions differ and how standardization/smoothing helps is key. Quick check: Why is resampling to a fixed length necessary, and what happens if skipped?

## Architecture Onboarding
Pretrained encoders (MusicFM, DINOv2/CoTracker) → Event curve module (dissimilarity, standardization, resampling, smoothing) → DiT backbone (modified input projection) → VAE decoder → waveform.
Critical path:
- Training: Music audio → MusicFM → music-event curve → fine-tune DiT (text + curve).
- Inference: Video frames → DINOv2/CoTracker → video-event curve → generate with text prompt (from LLM) → output music.
Design tradeoffs: Smoothing kernel size (balance FAD vs. SCH), encoder selection (domain-specific vs. general), text vs. curve (semantics vs. timing).
Failure signatures: Low SCH (poor alignment), high FAD (audio quality), low CLAP (semantic mismatch), inconsistent outputs (curve-text conflict).
First 3 experiments:
1. Visualize event curves to verify peaks correspond to beats/scene cuts.
2. Compare SCH of base T2M (no curve) vs. fine-tuned V2M-Zero.
3. Ablate smoothing kernel sizes (e.g., {9,31,63}) on validation set; plot FAD vs. SCH.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How do human experts stylistically synchronize music to video events, and how can these principles be modeled?
2. Can limited paired video-music data improve V2M-Zero over zero-pair fine-tuning?
3. How can we better align music and video event curves to mitigate the modality gap?
4. What metrics better capture human perception of temporal alignment in video-music generation?

## Limitations
- Generalization to out-of-distribution videos remains uncertain.
- Performance is sensitive to encoder choice and frame rate.
- Reproducibility is hindered by unreleased components and large proprietary training data.

## Confidence
- Overall performance: Medium
- Mechanism 1 (event curves): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (gap mitigation): Low
- Mechanism 3 (test-time swap): Medium
