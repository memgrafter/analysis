# Decentralized Federated Learning With Energy Harvesting Devices

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14051
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14051
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A fully decentralized policy iteration algorithm using only two-hop neighbor information achieves near-optimal test accuracy (e.g., 84.2% for i.i.d. FMNIST), matching centralized baselines and significantly beating myopic approaches.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses sustainable decentralized federated learning (DFL) under energy constraints by jointly optimizing device scheduling and transmission power control for energy harvesting devices. The core contribution is a multi-agent Markov decision process (MDP) formulation that captures stochastic energy arrivals and channel conditions, enabling long-term optimization of learning convergence. To solve this intractable problem scalably, the authors propose a decentralized policy iteration algorithm where each device uses only local two-hop neighborhood state information. Theoretical analysis proves asymptotic optimality, and experiments on FMNIST and CIFAR-10 demonstrate that the algorithm attains performance comparable to a centralized policy iteration baseline while drastically reducing communication and computational overhead.

## Method Summary
The method frames the joint scheduling and power control problem as a multi-agent MDP. Each device’s state comprises its local channel conditions (to neighbors) and battery level; actions are discrete transmit power levels. The one-step cost is derived from a federated learning convergence bound that penalizes packet drops and partial participation. To compute policies without central coordination, each device performs localized policy iteration on its two-hop neighborhood’s state-action space. During policy improvement, devices exchange their localized Q-values with two-hop neighbors and average them, enforcing partial consensus. This truncation avoids the exponential complexity of the global MDP while theoretical results show that repeated improvement iterations close the gap to the centralized optimum.

## Key Results
- The proposed two-hop decentralized algorithm achieves test accuracy statistically indistinguishable from the centralized policy iteration baseline (e.g., 84.2% vs. 84.3% on i.i.d. FMNIST).
- It significantly outperforms myopic baselines (centralized SCA and uncoordinated) by 3–5% absolute accuracy on both FMNIST and CIFAR-10.
- Ablation studies confirm that a two-hop neighborhood scope balances coordination benefit and overhead; performance saturates after ~20 policy improvement iterations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: MDP Formulation for Long-Term Optimization
Formulating joint device scheduling and power control as a multi-agent MDP enables optimization over the long-term convergence of DFL under stochastic energy and channel conditions. Each device treats its local channel state and battery level as the MDP state, and the policy selects transmission power to minimize the expected cumulative cost derived from the convergence bound. This captures temporal correlations in fading channels and energy arrivals. The core assumption is that battery evolution and channel states follow finite-state Markov chains. Evidence: The MDP tuple is defined in Section IV.A, with the one-step cost based on packet error rate.

### Mechanism 2: Decentralization via Two-Hop Localization
The decentralized policy iteration uses only two-hop neighbor information to avoid exponential complexity while maintaining near-optimal performance. Each device computes a localized Q-value function based on its two-hop neighborhood state-action space. During policy improvement, devices exchange these localized Q-values with two-hop neighbors and average them, enforcing partial consensus without global coordination. This truncates the global state space to a manageable local size. The core assumption is that the two-hop neighborhood contains sufficient information to approximate the global optimum. Evidence: Algorithm 1 (Line 10) implements the averaging step; the paper’s ablation shows two-hop outperforms one-hop.

### Mechanism 3: Asymptotic Optimality via Contraction
Theoretical analysis guarantees that increasing policy improvement iterations closes the gap between decentralized and centralized solutions. The iterative policy improvement is a contraction mapping in a suitably defined metric. Theorem 2 bounds the cost gap as decaying geometrically with the number of iterations \( R \), dependent on algorithm parameters and the neighborhood interaction matrix. The core assumption is that localized cost functions are Lipschitz and the interaction matrix has bounded entries. Evidence: Theorem 2 in Section IV.C provides the bound \( J(\pi_{\text{loc}}) - J(\pi^*) \leq O(DR) \).

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Device node -> Two-hop neighborhood manager -> Localized MDP solver -> Communication module <- Energy harvester & battery

### Critical path
1. Offline phase: Initialize Q-values for each device’s two-hop state-action space (Algorithm 1 Lines 3–6, 8). Run \( R \) policy-improvement iterations with two-hop Q-value averaging (Lines 9–13).
2. Online phase: At each time slot \( t \), each device reads its two-hop neighborhood state, looks up action \( p_{i,t} \) from the learned policy \( \pi_{i,t} \), and executes local training/transmission if powered.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Neighborhood scope (k-hop):** Larger \( k \) improves coordination but increases local state-action space size (\( O(d^k) \) where \( d \) is degree). Paper finds two-hop optimal.
- **Policy improvement iterations \( R \):** Higher \( R \) improves accuracy but increases offline communication. Suggests \( R=20 \) as practical.
- **Discretization resolution:** More battery/channel states improve MDP accuracy but blow up memory; tradeoff between precision and tabular storage.

### Failure signatures
- **Energy depletion:** Frequent battery drops to zero despite EH → policy too aggressive or EH model mis-specified.
- **Poor convergence:** Final test accuracy significantly below centralized baseline → two-hop neighborhood too small or \( R \) too low.
- **High variance:** Inconsistent learning curves → insufficient averaging in policy improvement or unrealistic channel/EH variability.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Convergence bound validation:** Compare theoretical bound (Theorem 1) with empirical test accuracy over time slots.
2. **Algorithm comparison:** Plot test accuracy vs. time slots for proposed vs. centralized policy iteration, myopic SCA, and uncoordinated baselines on FMNIST/CIFAR-10.
3. **Ablation study:** Vary neighborhood scope (0,1,2,3 hops) and policy iterations \( R \) to demonstrate two-hop and \( R=20 \) as sweet spots.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive neighborhood scope:** How to design an adaptive mechanism for selecting the optimal neighborhood scope (e.g., one-hop, two-hop, three-hop) in EH-DFL to balance communication cost and convergence speed under varying network densities and link reliability? (Section IV-B3)
- **Online incremental updates:** How to achieve online, incremental policy updates in EH-DFL to handle slow changes in channel statistics and energy harvesting patterns without restarting the full offline policy iteration? (Inferred from Section IV-B3 and Remarks)
- **Modeling energy correlation:** How does temporal correlation in energy harvesting (e.g., solar patterns) affect optimal scheduling and power control, and how can the MDP model be extended to capture such correlations? (Explicit: Section II-D assumes i.i.d. energy arrivals)

## Limitations
- Exact network topology (number of devices, mixing matrix) and discretization parameters (channel states, battery levels, power levels) are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on Markov assumptions for energy/channel and Lipschitz cost functions; violations in real-world settings could degrade performance.
- The two-hop neighborhood scope’s optimality is shown only on limited experimental settings; performance may degrade in sparse or highly irregular topologies.

## Confidence
- **MDP formulation validity:** Medium. Strong theoretical framing and prior evidence, but Markov assumptions may not hold for all real-world energy/channel traces.
- **Two-hop decentralization sufficiency:** Medium-High. Ablation supports two-hop optimality, but untested in extreme topologies.
- **Asymptotic optimality guarantee:** Medium. Proof depends on smooth cost and bounded interaction matrix; continuous actions or non-smooth costs could break guarantees.
