# LEMON: Local Explanations via Modality-aware OptimizatioN

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02786
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02786
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: LEMON provides model-agnostic, hierarchical explanations for multimodal models with 35–67× fewer black-box evaluations and 2–8× faster runtime than baselines, while matching faithfulness and aligning with radiologist annotations in clinical tasks.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal black-box models lack efficient, unified explanation methods that disentangle modality and feature contributions. LEMON addresses this by fitting a single sparse group-lasso surrogate to perturbed inputs, producing hierarchical explanations that attribute both modality-level importance and feature-level relevance. Evaluated on vision-language QA and a clinical tri-modal task, LEMON achieves faithfulness comparable to strong baselines (e.g., AOPC) while drastically reducing computational cost. In the clinical setting, its image evidence aligns well with radiologist annotations, demonstrating both efficiency and clinical relevance.

## Method Summary
LEMON takes a multimodal input partitioned into interpretable units per modality (e.g., superpixels for images, tokens for text). It samples binary masks over all units, constructs perturbed inputs by replacing masked units with modality-specific baselines, and queries the black-box model to obtain target outputs. Each perturbation is weighted by a locality kernel that downweights masks far from the original input, using a modality-aware Hamming distance. A weighted sparse group-lasso regression is then fit to these samples; group penalties correspond to modalities, enforcing joint selection or exclusion of entire modalities while allowing sparsity within selected groups. The resulting linear surrogate yields hierarchical attributions: modality importance as the ℓ₂ norm of group coefficients, and feature importance as absolute coefficient values. Hyperparameters (λ, ρ, αₘ) are tuned on a validation set. The entire process requires only a single surrogate fit, avoiding the multiplicative query overhead of per-modality or coalition-based methods.

## Key Results
- **Faithfulness**: Matches strong baselines in deletion-based metrics (e.g., AOPC 1.099±2.838 vs. 1.279±2.859 for DIME on CLIP-VQA).
- **Efficiency**: Reduces black-box evaluations by 35–67× (2.714k vs. 180.857k forward calls) and runtime by 2–8× (7.5s vs. 61.3s) compared to baselines.
- **Clinical alignment**: In a tri-modal clinical task, image evidence aligns with radiologist annotations (PixelAP+ median 0.497, CH-z 2.289) and provides clear modality-level attributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: LEMON produces unified hierarchical explanations by fitting a single sparse group-lasso (SGL) surrogate that jointly enforces modality-level and feature-level sparsity.
- **Mechanism**: The surrogate model `g(z) = β₀ + zᵀβ` is optimized with a composite regularizer: a group-lasso term (`λ(1-ρ) Σₘ τₘ ‖β_{gₘ}‖₂`) that encourages entire modality groups to be selected or discarded together, and an ℓ₁ term (`λρ‖β‖₁`) that induces sparsity within selected modalities. This structure yields both modality contributions (`Aₘ = ‖β_{gₘ}‖₂`) and feature attributions (`aⱼ = |βⱼ|`) from a single optimization.
- **Core assumption**: The black-box model's local behavior around the input instance `x` is well-approximated by a linear function in the interpretable binary space `z`. The group structure correctly reflects the intended modality partition.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states "fits a single modality-aware surrogate with group-structured sparsity..."; Section 3.3 defines the objective and `Aₘ`, `aⱼ`.
- **Break condition**: If group penalty weights `τₘ` are improperly normalized (e.g., due to large or low-variance modalities), modality attributions may be misleading.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: LEMON reduces computational cost (queries and runtime) compared to multimodal baselines by requiring only a single, structured surrogate fit rather than multiple disentangled models or Shapley coalition sampling.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of decomposing explanations via multiple unimodal surrogates (e.g., DIME) or enumerating coalitions (e.g., Shapley), LEMON generates one local dataset `D = {(zᵢ, yᵢ, πᵢ)}` of `N` perturbed samples and fits a single SGL model. This avoids multiplicative query overhead.
- **Core assumption**: A single linear surrogate with appropriate group regularization can capture first-order, modality-aware contributions within the local neighborhood.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract reports 35–67× fewer evaluations and 2–8× speedup; Section 3.2 makes query complexity explicit; Section 4.5 provides concrete forward call and runtime numbers.
- **Break condition**: If local linearity is poor (highly non-linear decision boundaries), the surrogate may be unfaithful even with many samples, eroding the cost advantage.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: LEMON's modality-aware locality weighting stabilizes explanations by downweighting perturbations that are far from the original instance in a modality-sensitive way.
- **Mechanism**: For each sampled perturbation mask `z`, per-modality Hamming distance `dₘ(z)` from the all-ones mask is computed. These are scaled by IQR, aggregated with modality weights `αₘ`, and transformed via an exponential kernel `π(z) = exp(-D(z)²/(2σ²))`. This penalizes masks that alter many units in one modality more heavily.
- **Core assumption**: Perturbations that modify a modality more heavily move further from the local linear approximation region for that modality. The aggregated distance `D(z)` is a valid measure of overall perturbation "farness."
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions "remaining faithful under repeated perturbations"; Section 3.2 defines `dₘ`, `d̃ₘ`, `D`, `σ`, `π`; Section 4.5 reports stability (Spearman ρ) competitive with DIME at far lower cost.
- **Break condition**: If the modality partition is poorly defined (units within a modality have heterogeneous importance and Hamming distance isn't predictive of output shift), weighting may over- or under-penalize useful perturbations, harming fidelity or stability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Group Lasso (SGL)**  
  *Why needed*: SGL simultaneously enforces group-level (modality) and element-level (feature) sparsity in a single linear model, enabling hierarchical attributions without post-hoc aggregation.  
  *Quick check*: How does the group-lasso term `‖β_{gₘ}‖₂` differ from a constraint that sets all coefficients in a group to exactly zero together?

- **Perturbation-based Local Surrogates (LIME paradigm)**  
  *Why needed*: LEMON treats the predictor as a black box and constructs a local weighted dataset by perturbing the input in the interpretable binary space and querying the model. This is the standard model-agnostic post-hoc explanation approach.  
  *Quick check*: Why is a locality kernel (e.g., exponential on Hamming distance) necessary when fitting a local surrogate?

- **Modality Partitioning and Interpretable Units**  
  *Why needed*: The definition of "modality" and the finest-grained "feature" (superpixels, tokens) is user-defined via a partitioner. This design choice directly determines the explanation's structure and granularity.  
  *Quick check*: What are the trade-offs between using fine-grained (e.g., individual pixels) vs. coarse-grained (e.g., image quadrants) interpretable units in the context of SGL?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1.  **Partitioner (`pₘ`)**: Modality-specific function `x^{(m)} → {x_k^{(m)}}`. Outputs interpretable units and defines binary mask space.
  2.  **Perturbation Sampler**: Draws binary masks `z ∈ {0,1}ᴷ`. Number of samples `N` is a key budget.
  3.  **Perturbation Composer (`P(·,·)`)**: Applies masks to raw inputs using modality-specific baselines `¯x^{(m)}` to create `˜x`.
  4.  **Black-box Querier**: Calls `f(˜x)` and applies transformation `ϕ(·)` to get target `y`.
  5.  **Locality Weight Calculator**: Computes modality-aware Hamming distances `dₘ`, aggregates to `D(z)`, and computes kernel weights `π(z)`.
  6.  **SGL Optimizer**: Solves weighted objective with group penalties `τₘ` (size/variance corrected).
  7.  **Output Formatter**: Aggregates `|βⱼ|` for feature heatmaps/highlights and `‖β_{gₘ}‖₂` for modality shares.

- **Critical path**:  
  `Raw input x` → **Partitioner** → `Units {x_k}` → **Define groups gₘ** → **Sampler** → `{zᵢ}` → **Composer** → `{˜xᵢ}` → **Querier** → `{yᵢ}` → **Weight Calculator** → `{πᵢ}` → **SGL Optimizer** → `{βⱼ}` → **Output Formatter** → `Heatmaps, Modality shares`.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Sparsity (`λ`, `ρ`) vs. Faithfulness**: Higher sparsity yields more compact explanations (`L₀↓`) but risks underfitting the local model and lower deletion AOPC.
  - **Perturbation count `N` vs. Cost**: More samples improve surrogate fidelity and stability but increase black-box calls and runtime linearly.
  - **Partitioner granularity**: Fine partitions (many small superpixels) allow precise localization but increase `K`, making SGL optimization harder; coarse partitions reduce cost but blur attributions.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Unstable modality shares**: Large variance in modality contribution `Aₘ` across repeated runs with same `N`. Suggests perturbation budget too low or modality distances not discriminative.
  - **Poor alignment with known evidence (e.g., radiologist ellipses)**: PixelAP+, CH-z, or IoU-AUC+ scores below thresholds. Could indicate poor partitioner or that the black-box model does not use image evidence.
  - **All-zero or near-zero surrogate coefficients**: SGL λ too high, or locality kernel σ too small (weights too concentrated on the original point).

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1.  **Sanity check on synthetic data**: Construct a toy multimodal predictor with known sparse, modality-grouped weights. Run LEMON and verify recovered modality contributions and top features match ground truth under controlled noise.
  2.  **Baseline comparison under matched query budget**: On 50 VQA instances, run LEMON, DIME, and MM-SHAP with identical `N`. Compare deletion AOPC, `L₀`, and wall-clock time. Verify LEMON achieves comparable AOPC with lower `L₀` and cost as reported.
  3.  **Clinical alignment pilot**: On 5–10 REFLACX cases with clear findings (e.g., cardiomegaly), generate LEMON explanations. Visually inspect image evidence overlap with radiologist ellipses and compute PixelAP+ and CH-z for this small set to validate pipeline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What is the most effective approach to improve the stability (run-to-run consistency) of LEMON's explanations?  
  *Basis*: The authors identify robustness and stability as key limitations, noting higher variability in Spearman’s ρ. They propose three remedies (perturbation aggregation, bootstrap stabilization, group-normalized coefficient aggregation) but did not evaluate them.  
  *Why unresolved*: These remedies remain unimplemented; stability is critical for high-stakes applications.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Systematic experiments comparing the proposed strategies (and combinations) on stability, faithfulness, and cost across multiple datasets and models.

- **Open Question 2**: How does designing perturbations with modality stratification and semantic constraints affect LEMON’s explanation quality and efficiency?  
  *Basis*: Future Work suggests “stratified, modality-balanced, semantically constrained sampling.”  
  *Why unresolved*: LEMON currently uses random masking; the impact of informed perturbations is unexplored.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Controlled studies comparing random vs. constrained perturbations on AOPC, L₀, and stability across tasks.

- **Open Question 3**: Can LEMON scale to multimodal settings with more than three modalities or with fundamentally different data types (e.g., audio, video, time-series)?  
  *Basis*: The authors state they will “extend evaluation to richer modality configurations and broader tasks.”  
  *Why unresolved*: Current evaluation is limited to image–text and tri-modal clinical tasks; scaling poses challenges in interpretable unit definition and interactions.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Applying LEMON to datasets with 4+ modalities or diverse types (e.g., video+audio+text) and measuring fidelity and scaling behavior.

- **Open Question 4**: In the clinical tri-modal setting, how well do LEMON’s text and tabular attributions align with clinical evidence or expert judgment?  
  *Basis*: The clinical evaluation only assessed image alignment due to lack of ground truth for text/vitals.  
  *Why unresolved*: Without validation for non-image modalities, claims about trustworthy explanations in healthcare are incomplete.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Clinician studies evaluating highlighted text tokens and vital signs, or creating annotated rationales for non-image modalities to compute alignment metrics.

## Limitations
- Partitioner parameters (e.g., superpixel count) and modality baselines are dataset-dependent and not fully specified, limiting reproducibility.
- Hyperparameter search spaces (λ, ρ, αₘ) and validation criteria are vague; reported values may not generalize to other tasks.
- Group penalty τₘ correction details are high-level; implementation subtleties could alter sparsity and stability.

## Confidence
- **Hierarchical SGL explanations**: Medium (mechanism clear but critical implementation details for τₘ and hyperparameters are missing).
- **Computational efficiency**: High (35–67× fewer forward calls and 2–8× speedup are both reported and inherent to the single-surrogate design).
- **Faithfulness & clinical alignment**: Medium (competitive metrics shown but dependence on partitioner/baseline choices not fully isolated).
