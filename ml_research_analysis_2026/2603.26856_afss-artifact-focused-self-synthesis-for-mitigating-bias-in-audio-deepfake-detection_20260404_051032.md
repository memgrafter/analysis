# AFSS: Artifact-Focused Self-Synthesis for Mitigating Bias in Audio Deepfake Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26856
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26856
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art cross-domain audio deepfake detection with average EER 5.45% (1.23% on WaveFake, 2.70% on In-the-Wild) without using pre-collected fake data.

## Executive Summary
Audio deepfake detection systems often suffer from dataset bias, failing to generalize to unseen audio sources. This paper introduces Artifact-Focused Self-Synthesis (AFSS), a novel training framework that generates pseudo-fake audio from real bonafide samples using same-speaker voice conversion and vocoder reconstruction. By ensuring identical speaker identity and semantic content between real and pseudo-fake samples, AFSS forces detectors to focus exclusively on synthesis artifacts. A learnable reweighting loss dynamically emphasizes these synthetic samples during training, while balanced batch sampling stabilizes optimization. Trained solely on real audio from ASVspoof 2019 LA, AFSS demonstrates exceptional cross-dataset generalization, achieving an average Equal Error Rate (EER) of 5.45% across seven evaluation datasets, significantly outperforming prior work.

## Method Summary
AFSS requires only real (bonafide) audio for training. It creates two types of pseudo-fake samples per real audio: (1) *self-conversion*: a random acoustic transformation (e.g., pitch shift) is applied to the real audio to create a target, then a same-speaker voice conversion model (kNN-VC) converts the original real audio to match that target, preserving speaker and content but introducing VC artifacts; (2) *self-reconstruction*: the real audio is processed by a neural vocoder (e.g., HiFiGAN) and optionally RawBoost, preserving speaker and content but adding vocoder artifacts. The resulting pseudo-fakes, together with the original reals, form a training set where all samples share speaker and content characteristics, thus any detection cue must come from synthesis artifacts. A detector is trained with a balanced batch sampler (equal numbers of real and pseudo-fake per batch) and a loss that uses learnable sample weights: synthetic samples receive weight between 1 and 2, real samples between 0 and 1, encouraging the model to prioritize artifacts. The detector uses a pretrained XLS-R model as a frozen (or fine-tuned) feature extractor, followed by a simple classifier (linear projection, mean pooling, ReLU, dropout, dense layer). Training uses AdamW with differential learning rates and early stopping.

## Key Results
- Average EER of 5.45% across seven test datasets (ASVspoof 2019/2021 LA/DF, WaveFake, In-the-Wild).
- Particularly low EER on WaveFake (1.23%) and In-the-Wild (2.70%).
- Eliminates the need for manually collected or generated fake audio in training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Speaker and Content Locking
By design, both self-conversion and self-reconstruction branches produce pseudo-fake audio that is **identical in speaker identity and semantic content** to the original real sample. This eliminates speaker and linguistic content as confounding features, forcing the detector to learn only **synthesis artifacts** (e.g., spectral smoothing from VC, phase inconsistencies from vocoders). The assumption is that these artifacts are consistent across speakers and datasets, thus generalizable. Evidence: [abstract] explicitly states the goal; [section III.A/B] formalize the same-speaker constraint; prior work (ALDEN [16], DSVAE [26]) identifies speaker identity as a major bias source. *Break condition:* If the VC fails to preserve speaker identity, confounding cues remain and bias persists.

### Mechanism 2: Learnable Reweighting Loss
The loss function assigns **higher weight to pseudo-fake samples** via learnable parameters: \(w_{\text{fake}} = 1.0 + \sigma(\tilde{w}_{\text{fake}})\) (∈(1,2)) and \(w_{\text{real}} = \sigma(\tilde{w}_{\text{real}})\) (∈(0,1)). This amplifies gradients from synthetic samples, pushing the model to focus on their artifacts while suppressing overfitting to patterns in real data. Evidence: [section III.C] derives the loss; [section V.C.3] shows ablation removing the reweighting (Alpha) worsens performance; learning rate sensitivity experiments ([section V.C.3], Table VI) indicate optimal LR \(10^{-6}\) for weight stability. *Break condition:* Unstable weight updates (e.g., diverging to boundaries) cause training collapse; also if synthetic artifacts are not representative, over-emphasis harms generalization.

### Mechanism 3: Balanced Batch Sampling
Each mini-batch contains an equal number of real and pseudo-fake samples. This **stabilizes training** for the learnable reweighting loss by preventing class imbalance from distorting the effective weighting signal across batches. Without balance, random batch composition would cause large fluctuations in the gradient contribution of each class, interfering with optimization of \(\tilde{w}_{\text{real}}, \tilde{w}_{\text{fake}}\). Evidence: [section III.D] motivates the need; [section V.C.1] ablation shows adding balanced sampling (Bal) improves performance (E.g., Table IV: S-VC+S-Rec+Alpha EER 6.61% → 2.72% on ASV19-eval with +Bal). *Break condition:* Forcing balance might remove useful natural statistics if the underlying distribution is imbalanced; also if the reweighting loss is inherently robust, balancing could be redundant.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bias in Audio Deepfake Detection**  
  *Why needed:* AFSS directly targets dataset bias where detectors learn spurious correlations (e.g., speaker identity, recording conditions) instead of universal forgery artifacts. Understanding this bias explains why same-speaker constraints are crucial.  
  *Quick check:* If a model trained on female studio recordings fails on male phone recordings, what likely happened?
- **Voice Conversion (VC) Fundamentals**  
  *Why needed:* The self-conversion branch relies on same-speaker VC. Knowing that VC maps a source speaker’s vocal characteristics to a target speaker while preserving linguistic content clarifies how identical speaker/content can be maintained.  
  *Quick check:* In VC, what information must be preserved to maintain semantic content, and what is altered to change speaker identity?
- **Neural Vocoder Artifacts**  
  *Why needed:* Self-reconstruction introduces vocoder-specific artifacts. Recognizing typical artifacts (e.g., over-smoothing, phase errors) explains the detection cues the model must learn.  
  *Quick check:* How might a neural vocoder’s reconstruction differ from natural speech in the time-frequency domain, and why is this a consistent artifact across speakers?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Real audio → {Self-Conversion branch (Transform → kNN-VC), Self-Reconstruction branch (Vocoder (+RawBoost))} → Pseudo-fake set → BalancedBatchSampler → XLS-R front-end → Linear projection (1024→128) → Mean pooling → ReLU → Dropout (0.5) → Dense (1) → Weighted BCE loss (with learnable weights) → Optimizer (AdamW, diff LR)
- **Critical path:** 1. Start with real bonafide audio. 2. Generate two pseudo-fake samples per real via the two branches. 3. Combine real and pseudo-fake into training set. 4. Sample balanced mini-batches (6 real + 6 fake). 5. Extract features with XLS-R. 6. Pass through classifier to get predictions. 7. Compute loss with sample-specific weights derived from learnable parameters. 8. Backpropagate and update classifier and reweighting parameters (with differential LRs). 9. Repeat until early stopping.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Same-speaker constraint vs. Artifact diversity:* Too weak transformations (Intensity 0) yield insufficient VC driving signal; too strong (Intensity 2/3) add transformation artifacts that confuse learning. Optimal Intensity 1 per Table V.
  - *Learnable weighting range vs. Stability:* Constraining weights (fake ∈(1,2), real ∈(0,1)) prevents runaway but may limit adaptability; requires careful LR tuning (optimal \(10^{-6}\) for reweighting params, Table VI).
  - *Vocoder selection vs. Generalization:* Using multiple vocoders (4) increases artifact diversity but adds computational cost.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High EER on cross-domain datasets (e.g., >20% on In-the-Wild) suggests speaker identity leakage in pseudo-fakes.
  - Unstable training loss with large oscillations indicates learnable weights diverging; reduce LR or check batch balance.
  - Learnable weights collapsing to boundaries (e.g., \(w_{\text{fake}} \to 2\), \(w_{\text{real}} \to 0\)) means the model ignores real samples entirely.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate branches: train with only self-conversion, only self-reconstruction, and both; expect both together to perform best (Table IV).
  2. Sweep transformation intensity (0–3) on WaveFake and In-the-Wild; expect a U-curve with Intensity 1 optimal.
  3. Replace same-speaker VC with cross-speaker VC; expect severe degradation on out-of-domain sets (Table VII), validating same-speaker design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does VC model choice affect AFSS?**  
   *Basis:* Paper uses only kNN-VC and claims compatibility with any VC, but no alternatives are evaluated. Different VC architectures may yield distinct artifacts or imperfect identity preservation, impacting generalization.  
   *Resolution:* Test multiple VC models (VAE, GAN, diffusion) while holding other components constant, measure cross-dataset EER/AUC and speaker identity preservation.
2. **Can learnable reweighting be made LR-robust?**  
   *Basis:* Performance degrades when reweighting LR is increased to \(10^{-4}\) or \(10^{-5}\) despite similar weight values, indicating instability.  
   *Resolution:* Explore alternative parameterizations (e.g., bounded updates, adaptive weighting based on batch statistics) and evaluate stability across a wide LR range.
3. **Do pseudo-fake artifacts match real deepfake artifacts?**  
   *Basis:* AFSS assumes VC/vocoder artifacts are universal and similar to unseen attack artifacts, but no direct comparison is made. A mismatch could cause detectors to learn irrelevant cues.  
   *Resolution:* Compare feature distributions (e.g., XLS-R embeddings) of AFSS pseudo-fakes and real deepfakes via KL divergence or t-SNE; train on AFSS-only data and test on real deepfakes to quantify any domain gap.

## Limitations
- **Speaker identity preservation not verified:** No quantitative evidence that same-speaker VC (kNN-VC) maintains identity; failure could leave confounding speaker cues.
- **Artifact representativeness uncertain:** Assumes VC/vocoder artifacts are universal and overlap with real deepfake artifacts; no direct evidence of such overlap.
- **Training stability sensitive:** Learnable reweighting requires careful LR tuning; weight divergence can collapse training.

## Confidence
- Speaker/content locking mechanism: **Medium** (supported by ablation but no direct identity verification).
- Learnable reweighting mechanism: **High** (stable improvement, principled design).
- Balanced batch sampling: **High** (standard technique, ablation confirms benefit).
- Overall SOTA performance: **High** on standard benchmarks, **Medium** for real-world due to artifact specificity concerns.
