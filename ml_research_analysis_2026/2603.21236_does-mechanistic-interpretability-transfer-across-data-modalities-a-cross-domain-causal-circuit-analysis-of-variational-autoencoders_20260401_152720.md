# Does Mechanistic Interpretability Transfer Across Data Modalities? A Cross-Domain Causal Circuit Analysis of Variational Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21236
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21236
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Mechanistic interpretability findings from image-based VAEs do not generalize to tabular data, with tabular VAEs showing lower circuit modularity and severe causal effect strength (CES) collapse that strongly correlates with reconstruction error.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether mechanistic interpretability techniques developed for image-based variational autoencoders (VAEs) are transferable to tabular data. The authors extend a four-level causal intervention framework with novel metrics—posterior-calibrated Causal Effect Strength (CES), path-specific activation patching, and Feature-Group Disentanglement (FGD)—and evaluate five VAE architectures across four tabular datasets and dSprites. Results reveal significant cross-modality differences: tabular VAEs exhibit lower circuit modularity, β-VAE suffers catastrophic CES collapse, and CES emerges as the most discriminative metric. The study challenges direct transfer of image-domain mechanistic insights to tabular applications and highlights architectural limitations in causal decomposition for sequential encoders.

## Method Summary
The study employs a four-level causal intervention framework applied to fully-connected VAEs (Standard, β-VAE, β-TC-VAE, FactorVAE, DIP-VAE-II). Level 1 uses multi-scale input perturbations to compute a latent importance matrix R. Level 2 sweeps each latent dimension across a posterior-calibrated range to measure CES. Level 3 performs path-specific activation patching to isolate per-layer contributions in sequential encoders. Level 4 applies clamped causal mediation with the Nonlinear Interaction Strength (NIS) diagnostic. Derived metrics include Modularity, FGD (adapted from DCI completeness), and Specificity. Five datasets (four tabular, dSprites) are used with 3 random seeds each, totaling 75 runs. Statistical analysis uses Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ( Holm–Šídák correction) and Pearson correlations.

## Key Results
- Tabular VAEs have lower circuit modularity (0.092) than dSprites VAEs (0.125).
- β-VAE shows severe CES collapse on tabular data (0.043) vs. image (0.133), strongly correlated with reconstruction MSE (r = –0.886).
- CES is the most discriminative metric, capturing 9 of 11 significant architectural differences.
- Intervention Specificity predicts downstream linear probe AUC (r = 0.460).
- Causal mediation analysis saturates (NIS = 0) in all sequential encoders, limiting layer-level insights.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Posterior-calibrated CES accurately measures a latent dimension’s causal influence on decoder output across modalities by normalizing the perturbation range to each dimension’s effective posterior variance.
- **Mechanism:** CES sweeps each latent dimension d across μ<sub>d</sub> ± 3σ<sup>eff</sup><sub>d</sub> (51 points), where σ<sup>eff</sup><sub>d</sub> combines posterior mean spread and per-sample uncertainty. This prevents overestimation for dimensions with narrow posteriors (common under strong KL regularization) and underestimation for broad posteriors.
- **Core Assumption:** The decoder is locally linear around the posterior mean, so output change scales linearly with perturbation.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Proposition 1 proves CES<sub>cal</sub>(d) ≈ (‖J<sub>d</sub>‖<sub>1</sub>/n) · σ<sup>eff</sup><sub>d</sub>; ablation shows fixed-range CES severely biases results when posteriors vary.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Path-specific activation patching with direct contribution decomposition isolates the unique information added at each encoder layer in purely sequential architectures.
- **Mechanism:** Compound(l) = ‖μ<sub>patched</sub>(l) – μ<sub>target</sub>‖ measures total effect of patching layer l. Then direct(l) = compound(l) – compound(l+1) yields the incremental contribution of layer l via a telescoping sum, with compound(L) = 0.
- **Core Assumption:** The encoder is strictly sequential (no skip connections/residuals) so the decomposition is exact.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Proposition 2 formally proves the telescoping property; Section 5.7 reports flat layer profiles consistent with sequential encoding.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Feature-Group Disentanglement (FGD) adapts DCI completeness to tabular data by treating semantically defined feature groups as generative factors.
- **Mechanism:** FGD = ∑<sub>d</sub> (w<sub>d</sub>/∑<sub>d</sub> w<sub>d</sub>) · (1 – H(P<sub>d</sub>)/log G), where P<sub>d</sub> is the normalized column of importance matrix R, H is entropy, and w<sub>d</sub> is responsiveness. Low entropy indicates a dimension primarily responds to one group.
- **Core Assumption:** Semantic feature groups capture independent sources of variation analogous to generative factors.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Proposition 3 shows FGD reduces to DCI completeness for singleton groups; random grouping ablation yields lower FGD, validating the metric.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**  
  *Why needed:* VAEs are the model family under study; understanding the encoder-decoder structure, latent bottleneck, and regularization losses (β, TC, DIP) is essential to interpret CES, modularity, and disentanglement results.  
  *Quick Check:* How does increasing the β weight in β-VAE affect the KL divergence term and potential latent space capacity?  
  *Answer:* Raising β strengthens the KL penalty, pushing the posterior toward the prior, which can reduce latent capacity and cause posterior collapse.

- **Concept: Causal Intervention Framework**  
  *Why needed:* The four-level intervention approach (input perturbations, latent sweeps, activation patching, mediation) is the core methodology for circuit analysis; knowing how to design interventions and interpret effects (TE, RE, MR) is critical.  
  *Quick Check:* What is the difference between compound and direct patching, and what does each measure about encoder layer contributions?  
  *Answer:* Compound(l) measures the total effect of replacing layer l’s activations; direct(l) isolates the unique contribution of layer l after removing the effect of subsequent layers via telescoping.

- **Concept: Disentanglement Metrics (DCI, MIG, SAP)**  
  *Why needed:* FGD adapts DCI completeness; comparing CES, modularity, FGD, and MIG requires understanding that DCI measures factor alignment and completeness, MIG measures gap between top two mutual informations, and SAP measures alignment vs. informativeness.  
  *Quick Check:* Why might MIG be low for a model with high CES, as observed?  
  *Answer:* High CES indicates latent dimensions strongly influence outputs, but if many dimensions respond to the same factor (low completeness), the mutual information gap (MIG) between the top two factors per latent may be small.

## Architecture Onboarding
Training module (5 VAEs, FC encoder/decoder, latent dim=10) -> Intervention engine (Levels 1–4) -> Metric calculator (FGD, Modularity, Specificity, MIG) -> Validation suite (random grouping, linearity check, NIS) -> Statistical analyzer (Wilcoxon, Pearson)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Train model, verify convergence via reconstruction MSE.  
2. Run Level 1 input interventions → compute R.  
3. Compute posterior statistics (μ<sub>mean</sub>, σ<sup>eff</sup>) → run Level 2 CES sweeps.  
4. Perform activation patching (source/target pairs) → compute direct(l) via telescoping.  
5. Run Level 4 mediation → compute MR and NIS.  
6. Derive FGD, Modularity from R; compute Specificity from CES.  
7. Compare to baselines (random grouping) and across datasets.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Computational cost dominated by CES sweeps (51 × 10 dims) and patching (O(layers × test samples)); 75 runs required substantial GPU time.  
- Semantic feature group definitions rely on domain expertise; arbitrary groupings change Modularity/FGD scores, necessitating ablation.  
- Results specific to fully-connected sequential encoders; CNNs/transformers would yield different patching/mediation patterns.  
- Hyperparameters fixed (β=4.0, latent dim=10); collapse severity likely β‑dependent.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- **CES collapse:** Near‑zero CES across dimensions (e.g., β‑VAE on Adult: 0.0003) indicates capacity bottleneck; check reconstruction MSE and posterior σ².  
- **Mediation saturation:** All MR ≈ 1.0 and NIS=0 indicates sequential encoder limitation; no layer‑level discrimination.  
- **Linearity violation:** R² < 0.9 for Level 1 perturbation responses suggests nonlinear encoder; intervention interpretations may not hold.  
- **Random grouping parity:** Semantic Modularity/FGD ≈ random scores suggests model did not learn group‑specific structure.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. Train all architectures on Adult Income and dSprites; record reconstruction MSE to confirm β‑VAE underfitting on tabular.  
2. For Standard VAE, compute R matrix via Level 1; check linearity (R² > 0.96); compute Modularity/FGD for semantic vs. random groupings.  
3. For β‑VAE on tabular, compare fixed‑range CES vs. posterior‑calibrated CES; expect large overestimation (factor ≈ 1/σ<sup>eff</sup>) with fixed range.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do the observed cross-modality differences generalize when comparing tabular data to real-world image datasets (e.g., CelebA, 3DShapes) rather than synthetic dSprites?  
   *Basis:* Limitation noted in Section 6.6; dSprites is synthetic and factor‑independent.

2. Is the β‑VAE capacity bottleneck a continuous function of β or a phase transition? How does latent dimensionality interact with this modality‑dependent effect?  
   *Basis:* β and latent dim fixed; paper acknowledges possible β‑specific collapse.

3. Does the strong CES–MSE correlation (r = –0.886) hold for broader generative model families (hierarchical VAEs, VQ‑VAEs, normalizing flows)?  
   *Basis:* Correlation limited to studied architectures; external validity untested.

4. For encoder architectures with non‑sequential connectivity (skip connections, residuals, attention), do causal mediation ratios remain saturated or provide meaningful layer discrimination?  
   *Basis:* Mediation saturated in all sequential encoders; paper recommends testing non‑sequential architectures.

5. Do circuit metrics predict domain‑specific downstream utility beyond classification (e.g., imputation error, synthetic fidelity, anomaly detection)?  
   *Basis:* Downstream evaluation limited to linear probe; tabular VAEs have broader use cases.

## Limitations
- Exact formula for intervention specificity (Roy & Misra 2025) is missing, preventing replication of that metric.
- MIG computation on tabular data is underspecified; it is unclear how discrete generative factors are defined for continuous features.
- Downstream evaluation details (classifier type, hyperparameters, splits, robustness/parity definitions) are not fully described.
- Causal framework assumes a sequential encoder without skip connections; results may not generalize to modern architectures.
- CES and Level‑1 linearity assumptions rely on the decoder being locally linear within the perturbation range; strong nonlinearities could invalidate these measures.

3. **Linearity stress test:** For each trained model, regress decoder output changes against latent perturbations across multiple scales; report R² distributions to flag potential CES reliability issues (R² < 0.9).
