# A Multi-Prototype-Guided Federated Knowledge Distillation Approach in AI-RAN Enabled Multi-Access Edge Computing System

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09727
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09727
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Proposes MP-FedKD, a federated learning method for non-IID data in AI-RAN MEC, achieving up to ~28.70% accuracy improvement on EuroSAT (10 clients) over baselines like FedProx and FedProto.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical challenge of non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) data in federated learning (FL) within AI-RAN enabled Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC) systems. Conventional FL methods suffer significant performance degradation under such data heterogeneity. The authors introduce MP-FedKD (Multi-Prototype-guided Federated Knowledge Distillation), a comprehensive approach combining self-knowledge distillation, a novel Conditional Hierarchical Agglomerative Clustering (CHAC) method to generate multiple class prototypes per client, a prototype alignment scheme, and a new LEMGP loss function. Through extensive experiments across multiple image datasets under various non-IID conditions, MP-FedKD demonstrates consistent and substantial superiority over state-of-the-art FL baselines in accuracy and error reduction, proving its robustness and scalability.

## Method Summary
MP-FedKD operates in the standard FL paradigm with a central server and distributed edge clients. Each local client trains a model using a composite loss function. To handle non-IID data, the method first uses CHAC on per-class local embeddings to generate multiple prototypes (cluster centroids) instead of a single average, preserving intra-class diversity. A prototype alignment (PA) loss then aligns the current round’s global prototypes with embeddings from the previous round’s local model, mitigating semantic drift during aggregation. The core LEMGP loss shapes the embedding space by attracting samples to their class’s global prototypes while repelling them from other-class prototypes. Local model updates are sent to the server, which aggregates both model weights (via FedAvg) and the multiple prototypes per class (weighted average) for distribution in the next round.

## Key Results
- MP-FedKD consistently outperforms baselines (FedProx, FedProto, etc.) in test accuracy and average accuracy across CIFAR-10, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, EuroSAT, and combined datasets under Dirichlet non-IID settings.
- It achieves a maximum accuracy improvement of approximately 28.70% on the EuroSAT dataset with 10 clients compared to the best baseline.
- The method reduces error metrics (RMSE, MAE) and demonstrates enhanced robustness and scalability across varying client counts and heterogeneity levels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multi-Prototype Generation via CHAC
- **Claim:** Generating multiple prototypes per class using Conditional Hierarchical Agglomerative clustering mitigates the information loss inherent in single-prototype averaging under non-IID FL.
- **Mechanism:** On each client, CHAC hierarchically clusters the per-class embedding vectors using Ward's criterion for merging clusters. The resulting cluster centroids serve as multiple local prototypes. A conditional stopping rule determines the final number of clusters per class.
- **Break condition:** If a class has very few local samples (`|D_m,c|` below threshold `bζ`), CHAC defaults to one prototype per sample, negating the benefit and increasing complexity.

### Mechanism 2: Prototype Alignment (PA)
- **Claim:** Aligning current global prototypes with embeddings from the previous round's local model reduces semantic drift during prototype aggregation.
- **Mechanism:** The global prototype `P_c` for class `c` is trained (via MSE loss) to match the embeddings `ϱ_{m,c}^{t-1}` generated by the *previous round's* local model `ω_m^{t-1}` on the same client's data.
- **Break condition:** Severe concept drift between rounds makes previous-round embeddings a poor guide, potentially corrupting global prototypes.

### Mechanism 3: LEMGP Loss
- **Claim:** The LEMGP loss jointly attracts embeddings to same-class global prototypes and repels them from other-class prototypes, improving class separation in the embedding space.
- **Mechanism:** LEMGP combines an attractive weighted MSE term (pulling an embedding toward its class's global prototype) and a repulsive log-exponential term (pushing it away from all other-class prototypes), balanced by coefficient `Λ`.
- **Break condition:** If global prototypes become poor class representatives (e.g., from severe non-IID aggregation), the attraction/repulsion signals become misleading.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hierarchical Agglomerative Clustering (HAC)**
  - **Why needed:** To generate multiple, structured prototypes per class that capture feature sub-modalities without pre-specifying the number of clusters.
  - **Quick check:** How does the dendrogram in HAC provide more interpretable structure for per-class embeddings than a flat k-means clustering?

- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD) & Self-Knowledge Distillation (SKD)**
  - **Why needed:** To transfer "dark knowledge" (soft label relationships) from a teacher (previous local model) to the student (current local model), mitigating client drift in non-IID FL.
  - **Quick check:** Why is SKD (using the model's own previous state as teacher) preferred over standard KD with a separate teacher network in edge FL?

- **Concept: Metric Learning with Attractive-Repulsive Loss**
  - **Why needed:** To explicitly shape the embedding space so samples cluster tightly around their class prototype while being clearly separated from other prototypes, directly combating feature confusion from non-IID data.
  - **Quick check:** What is the effect of the logarithmic term in the repulsive loss on gradients for "easy" vs. "hard" negative samples?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Central Server (CU) <-> Distributed Unit (DU) <-> Client Edge Device (MEC node)
  CU: Global Aggregator (weights ω), Prototype Aggregator (prototypes P)
  DU: Relay for global (ω, P) to clients and local (ω_m, P_m) to server
  Client: Local Model (ω_m), CHAC module, Loss calculator (CE, SKD, PA, LEMGP)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Server initializes global ω and P.
  2. DU relays (ω, P) to selected clients.
  3. Client runs CHAC on local embeddings to generate P_m.
  4. Client computes total loss L = CE + μ₁*SKD + μ₂*PA + μ₃*LEMGP.
  5. Client updates local model ω_m via gradient descent.
  6. Client sends (ω_m, P_m) to DU.
  7. DU relays updates to server.
  8. Server aggregates ω (FedAvg) and P (weighted average).
  9. Repeat from step 2.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Complexity vs. Robustness:** CHAC's O(|D_m,c|³) complexity is high for large local datasets but provides nuanced prototypes; trade-off managed via `bζ` to cap clusters.
  - **Global vs. Local:** Transmitting multiple prototypes per class increases communication overhead (∝ ζ_m) but retains more heterogeneity information.
  - **Loss weighting:** Hyperparameters (μ₁, μ₂, μ₃, Λ, λ) require careful tuning; invalid settings (e.g., μ₃=0) collapse to weaker baselines.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Cluster collapse:** CHAC produces ≈1 cluster for a class despite `bζ>1`, indicating overly homogeneous embeddings or too few samples.
  - **Prototype divergence:** Global P_c variance increases across rounds, or L_PA_m does not decrease, indicating PA alignment failure.
  - **Loss explosion:** L_LEMGP_m or L_rep_m becomes NaN/inf, often from λ misconfiguration causing numerical instability in exponential/log terms.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation - CHAC vs K-Means:** Replace Algorithm 1 (CHAC) with K-Means (fixed k=bζ). Compare accuracy; expect CHAC ≥ K-Means due to hierarchical structure.
  2. **Ablation - PA Impact:** Set μ₂=0 (remove PA loss). Compare full MP-FedKD; expect performance drop, especially under high non-IID (Dir=0.3).
  3. **LEMGP Weight Sensitivity:** Vary Λ in {0.1, 0.5, 0.9} while fixing λ. Plot accuracy vs. rounds; expect an optimal Λ balancing attraction/repulsion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the computational overhead of CHAC scale with increasing local dataset sizes and number of classes in large-scale AI-RAN MEC deployments?
- **Open Question 2:** Can MP-FedKD maintain robustness under continuous, non-stationary data streams typical in MEC environments, where client data distributions shift over time?
- **Open Question 3:** How does intermittent client participation (due to connectivity, mobility, or battery constraints) affect the convergence stability and final accuracy of MP-FedKD?
- **Open Question 4:** What is the communication efficiency trade-off between transmitting multiple prototypes per class and traditional model-only updates in bandwidth-limited MEC networks?

## Limitations
- CHAC's O(|D|³) complexity may hinder scalability to large local datasets; not evaluated on such scales.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (μ₁, μ₂, μ₃, Λ, λ) is largely unexplored; reported tuning is limited.
- Prototype alignment assumes historical embeddings remain stable; untested under concept drift.
- Experiments are on image datasets only; transfer to other MEC data modalities (e.g., sensor streams) is uncertain.
- Communication cost of multi-prototype exchange is not quantified for resource-constrained MEC.

## Confidence
- **CHAC multi-prototype mitigates information loss:** Medium (novel but has break condition risk).
- **Prototype alignment reduces semantic drift:** Medium (evidence present but drift scenarios untested).
- **LEMGP loss improves embedding separation:** High (theoretically grounded, strong empirical gains).
- **Overall MP-FedKD superiority over baselines:** High (consistent across 5 datasets, large improvements).
