# SpecMoE: Spectral Mixture-of-Experts Foundation Model for Cross-Species EEG Decoding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16739
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16739
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SpecMoE achieves state-of-the-art results on nine diverse EEG decoding tasks, including 0.8527 balanced accuracy on drug classification (MACO, +7.2% relative), 0.8655 on seizure detection (SIENA, +9.9%), 0.6230 on pharmacological effects (+6.3%), and 0.1522 RMSE on vigilance estimation (halved error vs. previous best), while maintaining strong cross-species generalization with only 4.3M parameters.

## Executive Summary
SpecMoE introduces a novel EEG foundation model that overcomes biases in existing pretraining approaches by employing Gaussian-smoothed masking on time-frequency spectrograms. The method combines a U-shaped hierarchical dual-path convolutional architecture (SpecHi-Net) with a spectral mixture-of-experts (SpecMoE) fine-tuning framework that routes inputs to pretrained experts based on power spectral density. Across nine downstream tasks spanning drug classification, seizure detection, sleep staging, and more, SpecMoE achieves significant gains over prior art, demonstrating the effectiveness of spectral-aware pretraining and hierarchical feature integration for cross-species EEG decoding.

## Method Summary
SpecMoE consists of three key stages: (1) Self-supervised pretraining on ~27k hours of TUEG data using aggressive Gaussian masking applied to STFT spectrograms. Masks are smooth, jointly cover time, frequency, and time-frequency planes, and are biased toward low-frequency EEG bands to force the model to learn smooth oscillations. (2) The SpecHi-Net backbone uses a U-shaped hierarchy with three downsampling and upsampling stages; each stage employs dual-path convolutions (small kernels for transients, large kernels for rhythms) interleaved with transformer layers to capture multi-scale features. Reconstruction is guided by a composite loss (temporal MSE, spectral loss, auxiliary downsampling losses). (3) For downstream tasks, three independently pretrained SpecHi-Net experts are combined via a spectral gating MoE: the input's power spectral density (via differentiable Welch's method) generates gate values that weight each expert's embedding before concatenation and prediction. Full-parameter fine-tuning is applied.

## Key Results
- Balanced accuracy 0.8527 on MACO drug classification (+7.2% over prior).
- Balanced accuracy 0.8655 on SIENA seizure detection (+9.9%).
- AUPRC 0.6230 on DA-Pharmaco pharmacological effects (+6.3%).
- RMSE 0.1522 on vigilance estimation (halved error vs. previous best).
- Consistent gains across all nine evaluated tasks with only 4.3M trainable parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Gaussian-smoothed masking forces learning of smooth neural oscillations
- **Claim**: Gaussian smoothing of masks on STFT spectrograms prevents biases toward high-frequency edge artifacts and low-frequency trivial interpolation, compelling the model to learn globally smooth, physiologically plausible oscillations across all bands.
- **Mechanism**: Standard rectangular masks create sharp boundaries that generate high-frequency components during reconstruction; the model overfits to these artifacts. Gaussian masks introduce soft transitions, making reconstruction dependent on broader context. Joint time/frequency/joint masks with a 50% bias toward δ,θ,α,β bands ensure no low-frequency bin is ever fully visible, eliminating leakage from neighbors.
- **Core assumption**: Neural oscillations are smooth and correlated over long windows; destroying continuity at multiple scales forces internalization of the underlying manifold.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "forces the model to learn intricate neural patterns across both high- and low-frequency domains."
  - Sec. 3: "we ensure that our model develops a high-fidelity understanding of a broad range of clinically relevant biomarkers."
- **Break condition**: Using rectangular masks instead of Gaussian causes >20% accuracy drop on complex tasks (e.g., BCIC2020-3) and catastrophic failure in the "Non-Gaussian" ablation.

### Mechanism 2: SpecHi-Net's hierarchical dual-path architecture captures multi-scale dynamics under aggressive masking
- **Claim**: The U-shaped hierarchical design with dual-path convolutions and interleaved transformers is necessary to recover signals when large spectral-temporal regions are masked, simultaneously modeling fast transients and slow oscillations.
- **Mechanism**: Aggressive masking removes contiguous chunks. A shallow/ flat architecture cannot infer missing rhythms from distant context. SpecHi-Net downsamples and upsamples across three stages. Each stage has two convolutional paths: one with small kernels (k=4) for micro-states, another with large/dilated kernels (k=65) for long-range rhythms. Their features are concatenated and fed to transformer layers, enabling cross-talk. Skip connections preserve details.
- **Core assumption**: EEG contains overlapping processes across timescales; explicit multi-scale separation is required.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "enables high-fidelity recovery under aggressive masking."
  - Sec. 3: architecture description.
  - Sec. 5 ablation: replacing SpecHi-Net with CBraMod collapses performance (e.g., 0.6262 → 0.2448 on BCIC2020-3).
- **Break condition**: Using a non-hierarchical transformer (e.g., CBraMod) yields near-random accuracy on complex tasks.

### Mechanism 3: PSD-gated MoE enables task-specific specialization without catastrophic interference
- **Claim**: The spectral mixture-of-experts routes inputs to pretrained experts based on power spectral density, allowing dynamic assembly of spectral feature sets tailored to downstream tasks and species.
- **Mechanism**: Three SpecHi-Net experts are pretrained on disjoint TUEG subsets. During fine-tuning, the input's PSD (computed via differentiable Welch) is transformed into three gate values (sigmoid). Each expert's embedding is weighted by its gate before concatenation and prediction.
- **Core assumption**: Different tasks emphasize distinct frequency-band signatures; a fixed representation is suboptimal. PSD provides a robust, low-dimensional summary for routing.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "routes inputs to three pretrained experts via power spectral density gating."
  - Sec. 3: gating tensor generation.
  - Sec. 5: "Utilizing a raw-data-driven encoding network consistently underperforms... gating without PSD degrades."
- **Break condition**: Removing the PSD prior and using a learned router on latent features reduces performance (e.g., -12% AUPRC on BCIC2020-3).

## Foundational Learning
### Self-supervised masked reconstruction in the time-frequency domain
- **Why needed**: Pre-training must learn general neural manifold representations without label bias. Masking on STFT aligns objective with physiological oscillations, prevents low-frequency leakage, and Gaussian smoothing increases difficulty to promote deeper features.
- **Quick check**: If rectangular masks on raw EEG were used, what two biases would dominate? (Answer: High-frequency edge artifact bias; low-frequency trivial interpolation bias.)

### Hierarchical multi-scale feature extraction
- **Why needed**: EEG spans fast spikes to slow waves; single-scale models cannot disentangle masked overlapping components. Explicit multi-scale modeling is necessary.
- **Quick check**: What two complementary receptive fields do the dual convolutional paths target, and what EEG phenomena do they map to? (Answer: Small kernels → transient micro-states/spikes; large kernels → long-range rhythmic oscillations.)

### Spectral-domain routing for mixture-of-experts
- **Why needed**: Foundation models must adapt to heterogeneous downstream tasks (sleep vs. seizure). A static representation is suboptimal; routing based on PSD allows dynamic assembly of the right feature toolkit.
- **Quick check**: Why is using the input's PSD as a gating signal more interpretable and robust than using the output of the first transformer layer as a router? (Answer: PSD is a direct, low-variance summary of dominant frequency content; latent-based routers may conflate spectral content with noise or irrelevant patterns.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Raw EEG -> STFT -> Gaussian mask generator (time/freq/joint) -> masked STFT -> iSTFT -> corrupted EEG -> SpecHi-Net backbone (3x dual-path conv+transformer down -> bottleneck transformers -> 3x upsample+skip+dual-path conv -> multi-scale outputs) & PSD calculator (Welch) -> gating network (linear+sigmoid) -> expert weighting (gate_i * expert_i output) -> concatenation -> spatial pooling -> MLP predictor -> task output.
- **Critical path**: Input `x` → [Corrupter] → `x̂` → [SpecHi-Net Encoder] → expert embeddings `O_i`; Parallel `x` → [PSD Calc] → `PSD_x` → [Gating] → `G_i`; Then `G_i * O_i` → concat → pool → MLP → prediction.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Hierarchical depth vs. parameters: depth needed for aggressive masking but increases compute.
  - PSD-based gating vs. learned router: PSD is simple and tied to spectral hypothesis but may lack flexibility for non-spectral cues.
  - Full fine-tuning vs. frozen experts: full-parameter fine-tuning beneficial, but risks catastrophic forgetting and cost.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Masking failure: >20% accuracy drop on complex tasks if masks not properly smoothed; check mask sigma.
  - Architecture failure: collapse to near-random if hierarchical depth missing; ensure 3 downsample stages and dual-path concatenation.
  - Gating failure: One expert dominates; monitor gate entropy; verify PSD differentiable and numeric stability.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Masking sanity: train on 10% TUEG with Gaussian vs rectangular masks; visualize masks; check reconstruction quality and downstream accuracy.
  2. Hierarchy necessity: on PhysioNet-MI, compare SpecHi-Net to a flat transformer backbone (same params) under identical masking; expect >5% absolute drop for flat.
  3. Gating robustness: on a spectral task (e.g., MACO), replace PSD router with MLP on first transformer output; expect ~2-10% accuracy drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Zero-shot transfer**: Can SpecMoE achieve competitive downstream performance without fine-tuning, and what modifications enable true foundation model behavior?
2. **Optimal masking hyperparameters**: What mask ratio and Gaussian kernel widths maximize pretraining efficiency and downstream accuracy? Current values (50% ratio, 5% sigma) are inherited and not tuned.
3. **Domain-specific expert pretraining**: Would pretraining each expert on a specific EEG domain (sleep, pathology, motor) improve spectral gating and downstream performance versus random data partitioning?
4. **Multi-scale feature dissection**: What specific hierarchical features are critical for reconstruction under Gaussian masking, and how does each stage contribute to recovering low-frequency rhythms?
5. **Cross-species feature invariance**: What spectral-temporal features are shared between human and murine EEG that enable SpecMoE's generalization? Are there inherent limits for certain pharmacological/pathological states?

## Limitations
- Core implementation details (mask generator, differentiable Welch, gating fusion) are only in code, not fully described in text.
- Some downstream datasets (MACO, DA-Pharmaco) are private, preventing full external replication.
- Model requires full-parameter fine-tuning for each task; not a true zero-shot foundation model.
- No external validation on completely unseen species or acquisition systems; cross-species claims limited to provided murine tests.

## Confidence
- **Gaussian masking**: Medium – ablations show strong effect but mask generator details not documented, and optimal parameters unexplored.
- **SpecHi-Net hierarchy**: Medium–High – catastrophic collapse when replaced suggests necessity, but depth-parameter tradeoff unclear.
- **PSD-gated MoE**: Medium – gating without PSD degrades performance; however, Welch parameters omitted and alternative routers not thoroughly explored.
- **Cross-species generalization**: Medium – results on two murine tasks positive but not exhaustive; may not generalize beyond tested setups.
- **Reproducibility**: Low – key implementation details only in code; private data limits replication; hyperparameters not fully tuned.

## Next Checks
1. **Masking verification**: Retrieve the code's Gaussian mask generator; visualize masks to confirm smoothness, joint geometries, and 50% low-frequency bias. Pretrain two SpecHi-Net models on 10% TUEG: one with Gaussian, one with rectangular masks; compare reconstruction loss convergence and downstream accuracy on a medium task (e.g., PhysioNet-MI).
2. **Hierarchy necessity**: On PhysioNet-MI, replace SpecHi-Net with a flat transformer backbone of comparable parameter count, keeping all other settings (masking, loss) identical. Evaluate balanced accuracy; anticipate >5% absolute drop if hierarchy is critical.
3. **PSD gating robustness**: For a spectral task like sleep staging, replace the PSD-based router with a 2-layer MLP that takes the first transformer layer's pooled output as input. Compare weighted F1-score and gating entropy (to check for expert collapse across tasks). Also vary Welch window length (e.g., 2s vs 4s) to assess stability.
