# Measuring and Mitigating Post-hoc Rationalization in Reverse Chain-of-Thought Generation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.14469
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14469
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Structural Skeleton-guided Reasoning (SSR) reduces post-hoc rationalization across lexical, entropic, and probabilistic metrics and, when distilled (SSR-D), yields up to 10% absolute gains on downstream benchmarks while restoring out-of-distribution generalization.

## Executive Summary
Reverse Chain-of-Thought Generation (RCG), where a model produces reasoning to justify a pre-committed answer, is inherently prone to post-hoc rationalization—where the answer anchors and distorts the reasoning. This paper formalizes this anchoring via three complementary metrics: lexical (surface overlap), entropic (information density dynamics), and probabilistic (mutual information). Simple instruction-based suppression (ignoring the answer) fails paradoxically, increasing internal anchoring due to an "ironic process" of active monitoring. The proposed solution, Structural Skeleton-guided Reasoning (SSR), decouples reasoning structure from answer content by first generating an answer-invariant functional skeleton (e.g., operation tags) and then using it to guide trace generation. SSR consistently lowers all three anchoring metrics, and its distilled variant (SSR-D) improves downstream performance and out-of-distribution generalization, where suppression methods collapse.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a three-level measurement framework for anchoring in RCG: **lexical (Alex)** via ROUGE-L overlap with the answer; **entropic (Aent)** via the geometric mean of global entropy uniformity and local entropy jump non-uniformity across reasoning steps; and **probabilistic (Aprob)** via normalized pointwise mutual information measuring how much the trace reduces uncertainty about the answer. To mitigate anchoring, **Structural Skeleton-guided Reasoning (SSR)** employs a two-phase process: (1) generate a functional skeleton `S` of tags and content summaries that describe operations, not results; (2) generate the full reasoning trace `R` conditioned on `S`. The skeleton's content invariance is key to absorbing the anchoring load. For practical deployment, **SSR-Distilled (SSR-D)** fine-tunes a student model on high-quality `(Q, A) → (S, R)` pairs from a teacher model.

## Key Results
*   SSR reduces anchoring metrics relative to neutral prompting: -36.5% lexical (Alex), -15.6% entropic (Aent), and -7.8% probabilistic (Aprob) on Qwen3-4B-Thinking.
*   Semantic suppression reduces lexical anchoring but increases entropic and probabilistic anchoring, confirming an ironic monitoring effect.
*   Distilled SSR (SSR-D) achieves up to 10% absolute gains on in-distribution benchmarks (e.g., ArenaHard) and partially restores out-of-distribution performance (e.g., AIME 2025) lost due to standard training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-Level Anchoring Measurement
Post-hoc rationalization manifests across three distinct levels: lexical (direct token copying), entropic (unnatural flattening or spiking of information density), and probabilistic (latent dependence between reasoning and answer). A single metric is insufficient; faithful reasoning requires low scores across all three. Evidence comes from formal definitions (Eqs 1, 3-6) and the construction of behavioral zones (Reason, Encode, Cloze, Copy) via controlled trace generation experiments.

### Mechanism 2: Ironic Failure of Semantic Suppression
Instructing a model to ignore the answer triggers an "ironic process" from cognitive psychology. To obey the constraint, the model must actively monitor its output for forbidden answer tokens. This active monitoring keeps answer-related representations cognitively salient in latent space, paradoxically deepening probabilistic dependence and disrupting natural entropy dynamics. Evidence shows suppression reduces Alex but increases Aent and Aprob, shifting traces into the "Cloze" and "Encode" zones.

### Mechanism 3: SSR's Decoupling via Functional Skeletons
SSR reduces anchoring by separating reasoning topology from answer content. The answer-invariant skeleton `S` (a sequence of functional tags like `[PLAN]` and operation-focused summaries) absorbs the anchoring effect during the planning phase. The subsequent reasoning generation `R` attends to `S`'s structural plan rather than directly to `A`, redirecting focus from answer monitoring to structural adherence. A theoretical bound links anchoring reduction to functional invariance (`I(A;S|Q)`). Empirically, SSR lowers all three metrics and increases the proportion of traces in the "Reason" zone.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Query-Answer Pair (Q, A) -> Skeleton Generator -> Functional Skeleton (S) -> Reasoning Generator -> Reasoning Trace (R) -> Anchoring Metric Calculators (Alex, Aent, Aprob). Distillation Pipeline: Teacher (L_S, L_R) -> Student Fine-Tuning -> SSR-D Model.
*   **Critical path:** For any input `(Q, A)`, generation of a valid, content-invariant skeleton `S` must complete before reasoning generation `R` begins. The skeleton's quality is the primary dependency.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Skeleton Abstraction vs. Faithfulness: Overly abstract summaries lose logical connections; too concrete leaks answer content, breaking invariance.
    *   Tag Set Size: A fixed, small set (8 tags) ensures learnability but may not capture nuances of all reasoning problems.
    *   Suppression (simple, one-step) vs. SSR (complex, two-phase): SSR avoids ironic monitoring at the cost of architectural complexity.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Suppression: Low `Alex` but high `Aent`/`Aprob`; traces in "Cloze"/"Encode" zones; degraded multi-turn performance.
    *   SSR Prompting: Inconsistent skeleton adherence or leaked answer summaries in `S`; partial metric improvement.
    *   SSR-D: No anchor reduction or OOD gain → poor teacher skeleton quality or insufficient student capacity.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Behavioral Zone Construction:** Generate traces under controlled conditions ("Real CoT", "+Prob Anchor", etc.) to empirically define the four zones in the (Aprob, Aent) plane.
    2.  **Anchoring Metric Comparison:** Run NEU, SUP, and SSR on a fixed model/dataset. Verify suppression increases Aent/Aprob and SSR reduces all three.
    3.  **Downstream Benchmark Evaluation:** Fine-tune models on traces from each method. Evaluate on in-distribution (ArenaHard) and OOD (AIME, GPQA) benchmarks to correlate anchor reduction with accuracy and generalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **OQ1:** To what extent do the three anchoring metrics comprehensively capture all forms of post-hoc rationalization? Are there anchoring phenomena outside this hierarchy? The metrics are evaluated on specific tasks/models; their exhaustiveness and applicability to other domains (e.g., math proofs, code) are unknown.
*   **OQ2:** What is the precise mechanism by which semantic suppression triggers ironic monitoring in LLMs, and can this be directly observed in internal representations? The claim is supported indirectly by output metrics; no analysis of attention patterns or activations is provided.
*   **OQ3:** How optimal is the fixed set of functional tags in SSR? Could adaptive or learned skeleton structures further improve mitigation and downstream performance? The tag set is manually designed and not empirically optimized for different reasoning problem types.
*   **OQ4:** Why does SSR-D not fully restore OOD generalization to the "without training" baseline, and what limits the transferability of decoupled reasoning structures? The gap is attributed to "training data coverage" but the specific aspects (topic diversity, pattern variety) and residual factors (knowledge vs. anchoring) are unclear.
*   **OQ5:** Does ironic monitoring under suppression manifest as increased computational cost or latency, and can this be a diagnostic signal? The paper does not measure inference efficiency; if suppression increases internal monitoring load, it might be measurable via token generation speed or state revisits.

## Limitations
*   Model diversity is limited to the Qwen family; generalizability to other architectures (Llama, Mistral) is not established.
*   Key teacher traces rely on proprietary models (Qwen3-Max, Qwen3-235B), hindering full reproducibility.
*   Skeleton generation's content invariance is enforced via prompting heuristics ("operation not result"), not automatically verified.
*   The anchoring metrics are novel and lack a gold-standard benchmark for absolute validation against human notions of faithful reasoning.

## Confidence
*   **Three-level anchoring metrics:** Confidence: **High**. Formal definitions and behavioral zone construction show distinct capture. Weakness: no external validation against human judgments.
*   **Ironic effect of semantic suppression:** Confidence: **Medium**. Empirical pattern (↓Alex, ↑Aent/Aprob) is consistent with theory. Weakness: causal mechanism (active monitoring) is inferred, not directly measured.
*   **SSR's decoupling via functional skeletons:** Confidence: **Medium**. Consistent metric reductions and downstream gains. Weakness: contribution of teacher quality vs. skeleton design is unclear; content invariance heuristic is not automatically enforced.

## Next Checks

2.  **Human fidelity evaluation:** Conduct a blinded annotation study where experts rate traces for "genuine reasoning" vs. "post-hoc rationalization." Correlate human scores with the three anchoring metrics to validate the measurement framework.
