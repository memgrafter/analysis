# CogCanvas: Verbatim-Grounded Artifact Extraction for Long LLM Conversations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.00821
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00821
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** On LoCoMo, CogCanvas achieves 32.4% accuracy as the best training-free method, outperforming RAG by +7.8pp and delivering decisive gains on temporal reasoning (+20.6pp to 32.7%) and multi-hop questions (+1.1pp to 41.7%). In controlled benchmarks, it attains 97.5% recall versus summarization's 19.0%, with BGE reranking alone contributing +7.7pp.

## Executive Summary
Conversation summarization, commonly used to condense long dialogues, often discards critical constraints and details that are essential for accurate downstream reasoning (e.g., "use type hints everywhere"). CogCanvas addresses this by extracting verbatim‑grounded artifacts—atomic cognitive objects like decisions, facts, and todos—while preserving their exact source quotations. These artifacts are organized into a temporal‑aware graph, enabling precise retrieval for complex queries.

In evaluations on the LoCoMo benchmark, CogCanvas reaches 32.4% accuracy, the best among training‑free methods, and surpasses RAG by 7.8 percentage points. It shows especially strong improvements on temporal reasoning (+20.6pp to 32.7%) and multi‑hop questions (+1.1pp to 41.7%). On a controlled extraction benchmark, it achieves 97.5% recall compared to only 19.0% for summarization. Ablation studies identify BGE reranking as the single largest contributor (+7.7pp), highlighting the importance of neural reranking in the pipeline.

## Method Summary
CogCanvas processes long conversations by extracting atomic artifacts (Decisions, Facts, Todos, etc.) with their verbatim source quotes using a single‑pass LLM prompt per turn. Each artifact (CanvasObject) includes a summary, grounding quote, timestamps, and confidence. Extracted artifacts are stored in an in‑memory temporal graph; edges are added based on semantic similarity, causal type pairs, and temporal heuristics linking recent KeyFacts/Reminders to subsequent Decisions.

At inference, the system performs hybrid retrieval (semantic vector search + keyword) to fetch 20 candidate artifacts, then re‑ranks them with the BGE neural reranker. Depending on query type (multi‑hop, temporal, simple), an adaptive *k* (15/12/10) is chosen, and a greedy selection under a token budget picks the final context. The selected artifacts, together with the query, are fed to the LLM backend to produce the response. The entire pipeline operates stateless per query, rebuilding the graph on‑the‑fly for simplicity and confidentiality.

- **Controlled extraction recall:** 97.5% vs. summarization's 19.0%.
- **Ablation:** BGE reranking provides the largest single gain (+7.7pp).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Verbatim‑grounded artifact extraction preserves critical constraints and details lost by summarization.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of compressing dialogue, the system identifies discrete cognitive objects (Decisions, Facts, Todos, etc.) and stores their exact source quotation (`q`) alongside a summary (`c`). This prevents paraphrase‑induced loss of modifiers like "everywhere".
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can reliably identify and extract these atomic artifacts with their verbatim quotes in a single pass (improved by gleaning).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "extracts verbatim‑grounded artifacts… preserving exact source quotations"
  - [section 3.1] Definition of `CanvasObject` with fields `(τ, c, q, s, e, t, γ)`; `q` is the "grounding quote (verbatim excerpt)".
  - [section 4.2] Controlled benchmark: summarization 19.0% exact match vs. CogCanvas 93.0%.
  - [corpus] Weak; neighbors discuss memory/retrieval but not specific verbatim grounding mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the extraction LLM consistently fails to identify a fact as salient (see error analysis, §4.8), the artifact is never created and cannot be retrieved.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A temporal‑aware semantic graph connects related artifacts, enabling retrieval for complex reasoning (especially temporal) beyond pure vector similarity.
- **Mechanism:** Artifacts are linked via three rules: (1) reference edges on high semantic similarity, (2) causal edges for specific type pairs satisfying temporal constraints, (3) *temporal‑heuristic causal edges* for recent `KeyFact`/`Reminder` influencing subsequent `Decision` regardless of semantic similarity. This explicitly encodes time‑sensitive dependencies.
- **Core assumption:** Causal and temporal relationships can be approximated via heuristic rules (type pairs, turn indices) and enhanced by vector similarity, without full relational reasoning at storage time.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] Describes "Temporal‑heuristic causal edges" and rules for linking.
  - [section 4.4] LoCoMo: +20.6 pp on temporal questions (32.7% vs. RAG 12.1%).
  - [section 4.9] Ablation: Removing graph expansion drops overall accuracy by –6.6pp and multi‑hop by –8.4pp.
  - [corpus] Weak; GraphRAG neighbor uses community summarization, not temporal heuristics for fine‑grained edges.
- **Break condition:** If the conversation contains complex temporal logic ("before the meeting scheduled for last Tuesday") that isn’t captured by simple turn‑index heuristics or isn’t extracted as a `KeyFact` with a precise timestamp.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A two‑stage retrieval with neural reranking (BGE) and query‑adaptive top‑*k* selection maximizes the precision of context injection for the given token budget.
- **Mechanism:** (1) Hybrid retrieval (semantic + keyword) fetches top‑20 candidates. (2) BGE reranker re‑scores for precise relevance. (3) Adaptive *k* (10/12/15) based on detected query complexity. This combination yields the largest single‑component gain.
- **Core assumption:** A generic neural reranker (BGE) trained on general retrieval can effectively re‑score domain‑specific conversation artifacts, and simple keyword/query‑length heuristics can reliably classify query complexity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Ablation studies reveal that BGE reranking contributes the largest single gain (+7.7pp)."
  - [section 3.3] Details adaptive top‑*k*: multi‑hop (*k*=15), temporal (*k*=12), simple (*k*=10).
  - [section 4.10] Ablation: Removing reranking causes –11.5pp drop overall, especially –15.7pp on multi‑hop.
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct anchor on BGE reranking in neighbor papers.
- **Break condition:** If the query complexity classifier (heuristic) misroutes queries, or if the BGE reranker is mismatched to the domain (e.g., conversational vs. document retrieval), precision gains diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Conversation → Extraction Module → Graph Store ↔ Vector DB
Query → Hybrid Retrieval → BGE Reranker → Injection Controller → LLM Backend → Response

### Critical path
User Query → Hybrid Retrieval (Vector DB + keyword) → Top‑20 Candidates → BGE Reranking → Top‑k Selection (adaptive) → Greedy Token‑Budget Selection → Context Injection → LLM Generation → Response

### Design tradeoffs
- **Stateless vs. Stateful:** CogCanvas is stateless per query (re‑extracts/re‑builds graph on‑the‑fly). This trades peak accuracy for zero infrastructure and simplicity.
- **Generality vs. Efficiency:** The artifact taxonomy (Decision, Todo, etc.) is optimized for task‑oriented dialogue. Deploying on informal chat would likely cause extraction gaps.
- **Precision vs. Recall in Graph:** `θ_ref=0.5` and `θ_causal=0.45` are fixed thresholds tuned on a dev set. Higher thresholds increase precision but may miss valid causal links; lower increases recall but adds noise.

### Failure signatures
- **Extraction Gaps (78.8% of errors):** LLM misses a salient fact. Monitor: low `γ` scores or missing expected artifact types in a domain.
- **Temporal Blindness (14.9%):** Relative time ("last Tuesday") not anchored. Monitor: `KeyFact` artifacts lack ISO dates or precise `t` offsets.
- **Reasoning Chain Breaks (6.4%):** Multi‑hop fails due to missing intermediate `CanvasObject` links. Monitor: graph density and specific edge‑type distribution (e.g., few `Caused By` edges).
- **Reranker Drop:** If BGE reranking reduces performance (check ablation table), the reranker model or its domain match is suspect.

### First 3 validation experiments
1. **Controlled Extraction Recall:** Run the standard benchmark (N=50). Measure recall/exact match. If <<97.5%, the extraction LLM or prompt is the primary bottleneck.
2. **Graph Expansion Utility:** Compare retrieval‑only recall (Table 6) with and without graph expansion. If the +46pp gap doesn't appear, the linking rules (θ thresholds, edge types) or embedding model (`ϕ`) are failing.
3. **Reranker Ablation:** Remove BGE reranking and re‑evaluate on LoCoMo. Expect ~–11.5pp drop. If drop is smaller, the reranker is less critical for your query distribution; if larger, it's essential.

## Limitations
- Stateless extraction: Rebuilding the graph per query is inefficient and may miss cross‑session dependencies (confidentiality trade‑off).
- Extraction reliability: Single‑pass LLM extraction may fail on implicit or nuanced constraints, especially in informal dialogue (§4.8 error analysis).
- Temporal heuristics: Simple turn‑index rules may not handle complex relative time expressions (“last Tuesday”) or non‑linear narratives.

## Confidence
- Verbatim grounding & extraction recall: High
- Temporal graph reasoning: Medium
- BGE reranking impact: High
