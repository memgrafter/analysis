# Cross-Modal Rationale Transfer for Explainable Humanitarian Classification on Social Media

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.18611
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18611
- References: 40
- Primary result: VLTCrisis yields interpretable multimodal crisis classification, improving Macro-F1 by 2–35% on CrisisMMD, with high rationale fidelity (Token-F1 0.826) and strong zero-shot adaptation (80% accuracy).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the lack of interpretability in multimodal crisis tweet classification. It introduces VLTCrisis, an interpretable-by-design framework that extracts text rationales via a multi-task ViLT-based model and transfers them to image regions using cross-modal optimal transport (IPOT), avoiding manual image annotation. Classification relies solely on the extracted rationales, ensuring transparency. On the CrisisMMD dataset, VLTCrisis outperforms baselines with Macro-F1 gains of 2–35%, achieves Token-F1 of 0.826 for text rationales, and retrieves image patches rated 12% better by human evaluators. The model demonstrates high faithfulness (51% performance drop when rationales are removed) and generalizes to unseen datasets with 80% zero-shot accuracy.

## Method Summary
VLTCrisis processes a tweet (text and image) through a ViLT encoder to obtain token and patch embeddings. It jointly trains a text rationale extractor (GRU + FC) and a tweet-level classifier in a multi-task setup, balancing rationale extraction and classification losses. The predicted text rationales are aligned to image patches via IPOT, producing a heatmap of image rationales. Non-rationale text tokens are masked with '`*`' and non-rationale image patches are blurred. The masked multimodal input is fed to the same ViLT encoder followed by a classification head, ensuring predictions derive exclusively from the rationales. This design yields an interpretable model without requiring paired text-image rationale annotations.

## Key Results
- Macro-F1 improvement of 2–35% over strong baselines on CrisisMMD.
- Token-F1 of 0.826 for text rationale extraction, indicating high token-level agreement with human rationales.
- Faithfulness confirmed by a 51% drop in performance when rationales are removed (comprehensiveness), and only a 7.2% drop when using rationales alone (sufficiency).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-task learning improves text rationale quality
- **Claim**: Multi-task learning with an auxiliary classification objective improves the quality and relevance of extracted text rationales.
- **Mechanism**: The ViLT encoder is trained on two simultaneous tasks: (1) token-level binary classification to predict rationale tokens, and (2) tweet-level classification to predict humanitarian labels. The combined loss (Eq. 3) weights these objectives, encouraging the shared representations to capture both fine-grained rationale cues and coarse class-discriminative features. The auxiliary classification task acts as a regularizer, guiding the model to focus on tokens that are predictive of the humanitarian class.
- **Core assumption**: The humanitarian class label provides a supervisory signal that helps distinguish rationale tokens from non-rationale tokens. This assumes that tokens important for the class label are likely to be valid rationales.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "extracts text rationales via a multi-task ViLT-based model"
  - [section 4.2]: Explicit formulation of the multi-task loss (Eqs. 1–3) and training procedure.
  - [section 6.1]: Average alignment scores of 4.1 (Qwen-VL) and 3.8 (LLaVA) indicate strong text-image correspondence, supporting the rationale transfer.
- **Break condition**: If the auxiliary classification task dominates (e.g., α too large), the model may ignore subtle rationale patterns. Conversely, if the rationale task dominates, classification performance may suffer. The weighting α requires careful tuning.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-modal alignment via IPOT transfers text rationales to images
- **Claim**: Cross-modal alignment via IPOT transfers text rationales to image rationales without manual image annotation, leveraging inherent text-image correspondences in crisis tweets.
- **Mechanism**: For each image patch, the model computes alignment scores with all predicted text rationale token embeddings using the inexact proximal point method (IPOT) for optimal transport. The maximum alignment score across text rationales for each patch yields a heatmap (Eq. 4), highlighting image regions that semantically match the text rationales. This soft matching avoids the need for paired text-image rationale annotations.
- **Core assumption**: Crisis tweets exhibit strong text-image alignment such that textual mentions (e.g., "damaged bridge") correspond to visually evident regions in the image.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "transfers these to image rationales using cross-modal alignment (IPOT)"
  - [section 4.2]: Detailed description of IPOT alignment and heatmap computation.
  - [section 6.1]: LLM-based scoring yields average alignment ~4/5, validating the assumption.
- **Break condition**: If text and image are misaligned (e.g., sarcastic, metaphorical, or unrelated content), IPOT may produce incorrect heatmaps. Errors in text rationales also propagate to image rationales.

### Mechanism 3: Rationale-only classification ensures interpretability and faithfulness
- **Claim**: Classification based solely on extracted rationales yields an interpretable-by-design model with high faithfulness, as predictions rely exclusively on the provided explanations.
- **Mechanism**: After extracting rationales, the model masks non-rationale text tokens (replaced with '`*`') and blurs non-rationale image patches (using the inverse heatmap). The ViLT encoder processes only these masked inputs, and a final linear layer predicts the class. This forces the decision to be derived entirely from the rationales, making the process transparent.
- **Core assumption**: The extracted rationales contain sufficient and necessary information for accurate classification. Faithfulness metrics (comprehensiveness and sufficiency) are used to verify this.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Finally, tweets are classified based on extracted rationales."
  - [section 4.3]: Description of masking rationales and classification.
  - [section 6.4]: Comprehensiveness = 0.514 (51.4% drop when rationales removed) and sufficiency = 0.072 (only 7.2% drop when using only rationales), indicating high faithfulness.
- **Break condition**: If rationales are incomplete or noisy, classification accuracy may degrade significantly. The model may also discard useful contextual cues outside rationales, potentially harming performance on ambiguous tweets.

## Foundational Learning
### Vision-Language Transformers (ViLT)
- *Why needed here*: ViLT is the core encoder that processes tokenized text and image patches jointly, providing embeddings for each input unit. Its token- and patch-level outputs are essential for rationale extraction at fine granularity.

### Optimal Transport for Cross-Modal Alignment
- *Why needed here*: IPOT computes a soft matching between text rationale tokens and image patches, transferring rationale information without paired supervision. It approximates the optimal transport plan efficiently.
- *Quick check*: What problem does optimal transport solve in multimodal alignment, and how does the inexact proximal point method (IPOT) make it computationally feasible for large patch sets?

- *Why needed here*: The text rationale extractor shares a ViLT encoder with an auxiliary tweet classification task. The multi-task loss balances rationale extraction and classification, aiming to produce task-relevant rationales.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input Preprocessing -> ViLT Encoder -> Text Rationale Extractor -> Image Rationale Extractor -> Rationale Masking -> Classification Head

**Critical path**: Raw tweet → ViLT embeddings → text rationales → image rationales → masked inputs → class prediction. The cross-modal transfer (text→image) is the key bridging step.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- ViLT selected for speed and token-level outputs vs. larger VL models (e.g., CLIP) that may have higher accuracy but lack fine-grained embeddings.
- Multi-task loss weight α balances rationale and classification objectives; requires dev-set tuning.
- IPOT offers efficient alignment but may be less precise than full optimal transport; tradeoff between speed and alignment fidelity.
- Hard masking of non-rationales improves interpretability but risks losing supporting context.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low Token-F1 (<0.5) on text rationales indicates poor rationale extraction.
- Low LLM-based text-image alignment scores (<3) suggest cross-modal transfer may fail.
- High sufficiency (>0.2) or low comprehensiveness (<0.3) implies rationales are not faithful.
- Significant performance drop on rare classes (e.g., 'affected individuals') reveals data imbalance issues.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline replication: Train VLTCrisis on CrisisMMD and verify reported Macro-F1 (0.822), Token-F1 (0.826), and human evaluation gains (12%).
2. Ablation on α: Vary the multi-task loss weight (e.g., 0.01, 0.1, 1) and measure impact on Token-F1 and Macro-F1 to identify optimal balancing.
3. Alignment validation: Compute LLM-based text-image alignment scores (using Qwen-VL/LLaVA) on a held-out sample; if mean score <3.5, investigate IPOT heatmap quality.

## Limitations
- Generalization to diverse crisis datasets remains unclear; zero-shot results lack breadth.
- IPOT-based transfer may fail on sarcastic, metaphorical, or otherwise misaligned text-image pairs.
- Human evaluation covers only image rationales; text rationale quality has not been validated by humans.

## Confidence
- Multi-task text rationale extraction: Medium (Token-F1 0.826 on a single dataset).
- IPOT cross-modal transfer: Medium (LLM alignment ~4/5 but proxy-based).
- Rationale-only classification faithfulness: High (comprehensiveness 0.514, sufficiency 0.072).

## Next Checks
1. Cross-dataset generalization: Evaluate on at least two additional crisis datasets (e.g., DISASTERIMAGE, CrisisMMD v2) in zero-shot and fine-tuned settings; report Macro-F1 and rationale metrics.
2. Human rationale validation: Annotate a held-out sample of CrisisMMD for both text tokens and image patches (multiple annotators); compute token-level Token-F1 and patch-level IoU against model rationales.
3. Hyperparameter sensitivity: Ablate multi-task weight α and image patch resolution across random seeds; assess stability of Token-F1, Macro-F1, and alignment scores.
