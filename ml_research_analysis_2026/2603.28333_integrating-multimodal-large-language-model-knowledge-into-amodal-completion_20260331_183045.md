# Integrating Multimodal Large Language Model Knowledge into Amodal Completion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28333  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28333  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves 75.09% mIoU on COCO-A (+10.08% over prior) and reduces MLLM calls by 48.73% via selective invocation.

## Executive Summary
Amodal completion reconstructs occluded regions of objects but existing methods lack real-world knowledge. This paper introduces **AmodalCG**, a framework that selectively invokes a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to provide two types of guidance for heavily occluded objects: **geometric guidance** resizes the inpainting mask to match the predicted full object extent, preventing extraneous generation; **semantic guidance** generates a detailed text prompt describing the occluded parts. A multi-scale expansion strategy iteratively refines the result. On COCO-A, AmodalCG achieves a 10.08% absolute mIoU gain (75.09%) over the best prior method. For occluded object recognition, it improves Top-1 accuracy by 8.86%. The selective invocation mechanism reduces unnecessary MLLM calls by nearly half while maintaining high guidance call rates for difficult samples.

## Method Summary
AmodalCG is a modular pipeline. First, an occluder detection stage (SAM + occlusion order predictor) unionizes all foreground objects occluding the target to produce the inpainting mask. A lightweight Guidance Decision Module (InternVL3.5-8B) classifies whether the sample requires expensive MLLM guidance based on an isolated crop of the target. If “yes”, a larger MLLM (GPT‑4o) is queried: (i) **Geometric Guidance** predicts three bounding boxes (tight, moderate, coarse) for the fully visible object; (ii) **Semantic Guidance** produces a detailed text description of the hidden parts after the occluders are visually masked in the image. The inpainting module (Stable Diffusion v2) then runs a multi-scale process: for each predicted scale, the mask is the intersection of the predicted bbox and the occluder mask; diffusion inpainting is performed; the result is validated by checking if the generated object touches the bbox boundary. The first scale that does *not* touch is accepted. If no guidance is needed, a fixed 10% margin expands the modal bbox, and the category name is used as the prompt. Finally, SAM extracts the completed object mask, which is combined with the visible part.

## Key Results
- **75.09% mIoU** on COCO-A, an absolute improvement of **10.08%** over the previous state-of-the-art.  
- **8.86% higher Top‑1 accuracy** on the Separated COCO benchmark for occluded object recognition.  
- **Selective invocation** reduces MLLM calls by **48.73%** with high guidance call rates on hard samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Resizing the inpainting mask to match the MLLM-predicted full object extent prevents the generation of extraneous content beyond the target object's boundaries.  
- **Mechanism:** The Geometric Guidance Module uses an MLLM to predict tight, moderate, and coarse bounding boxes for the fully visible target object, given an isolated visual prompt and the visible bounding box. The inpainting mask is redefined as the intersection of the original occluder union mask and the predicted bounding box, providing explicit spatial constraints for the diffusion inpainting process.  
- **Core assumption:** An inpainting mask significantly larger than the true occluded region gives the diffusion model too much freedom, causing it to generate unrelated objects or over-extend the target.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "geometric guidance resizes the inpainting mask to match the predicted full object extent, preventing extraneous generation"  
  - [section 3.3] "A major reason existing amodal completion methods often generate undesired objects beyond the target object is the use of an excessively large inpainting mask"  
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on MLLM reasoning but do not directly address mask resizing; evidence is primarily internal.  
- **Break condition:** If the MLLM consistently underestimates the true object extent (e.g., for elongated objects like fences), the resized mask may remain too small, leading to incomplete reconstruction that touches the mask boundary during multi-scale verification.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Providing a detailed, object-centric textual description of the occluded regions (generated by an MLLM with occluders visually masked) guides the diffusion model to generate a naturally completed appearance consistent with the object's identity and pose.  
- **Mechanism:** The Semantic Guidance Module prompts an MLLM to describe *only* the hidden parts of the target object. The visual prompt masks out the occluders (while preserving their shape/context) to prevent the MLLM from incorporating occluder details into its description. This description becomes the text prompt for Stable Diffusion inpainting.  
- **Core assumption:** Category-level text prompts are insufficient for reconstructing specific occluded details (pose, texture). MLLMs can infer plausible details from context if distractors (occluders) are visually suppressed.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "semantic guidance generates a detailed text prompt describing the occluded parts"  
  - [section 3.4] "By removing the occluders’ appearance, this approach allows the MLLM to easily distinguish the occluders from the target object"  
  - [section 8] Qualitative comparison (Fig. 14) shows masking occluders yields descriptions focused on the target object vs. descriptions that mention occluders.  
- **Break condition:** If the MLLM succumbs to co-occurrence bias and includes descriptions of non-visible but commonly associated objects (e.g., "sliced fruits" with a plate), the diffusion model may generate those extraneous items.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Selectively invoking the computationally expensive MLLM guidance only for samples with heavy occlusion reduces overall system latency while maintaining high performance on difficult cases.  
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight Guidance Decision Module (smaller MLLM) assesses an isolated crop of the target object and outputs a binary flag. If "yes", the larger MLLM is invoked for geometric and semantic guidance. If "no", a heuristic (fixed margin expansion) is used instead.  
- **Core assumption:** Many real-world amodal completion instances involve only minor occlusion and can be solved adequately by the diffusion model's prior with a simple mask adjustment, not requiring expensive MLLM reasoning.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "selective invocation reduces unnecessary MLLM calls by 48.73% on COCO-A while maintaining high guidance call rates for difficult samples."  
  - [section 3.2] "samples with minimal occlusion can be adequately reconstructed without additional guidance"  
  - [section 4.3, Table 3] Reports high Guidance Call Rate (GCR) for hard samples and Guidance Skip Rate (GSR) for easy ones; cites specific percentages.  
- **Break condition:** If the occlusion assessment fails (e.g., an object is truncated by the image border but has a low computed occlusion ratio), the system may skip guidance for a sample that actually needs it, leading to incomplete generation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Amodal Completion**  
  - *Why needed here:* The entire task is amodal completion—reconstructing the full shape and appearance of an occluded object. Understanding its goal (vs. modal segmentation) and challenges (ambiguity, lack of ground truth) is foundational.  
  - *Quick check:* What is the key difference between predicting an amodal mask (shape) and generating the amodal appearance (pixels)?

- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Inpainting**  
  - *Why needed here:* The Inpainting Module uses Stable Diffusion. Knowledge of how inpainting works (conditioning on masked image + text prompt), its sensitivity to mask size, and its tendency to fill large masks with plausible but uncontrolled content is critical.  
  - *Quick check:* How does the size and shape of the inpainting mask qualitatively affect the output of a diffusion inpainting model?

- **Concept: MLLM Prompting for Spatial & Descriptive Tasks**  
  - *Why needed here:* The framework hinges on prompting an MLLM for two non‑standard tasks: (1) predicting a bounding box scale, and (2) describing an occluded region while ignoring visible occluders. Understanding prompt engineering, visual marking, and output formatting is essential.  
  - *Quick check:* Why is masking the occluders in the visual prompt necessary for generating a clean description of the target's hidden parts?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (I, M_modal) → Occluder Detection (SAM + occlusion predictor) → M_inpaint → Guidance Decision (InternVL3.5-8B) → [if requires_guidance = yes] → Geometric Guidance (GPT-4o bbox preds) + Semantic Guidance (GPT-4o prompt) → Multi-scale Inpainting (SDv2) → Output (I_amodal, M_amodal); [else] → Heuristic (fixed margin + category prompt) → Single-scale Inpainting → Output.

- **Critical path:**  
  For a heavily occluded object, the system invokes GPT‑4o twice to obtain a set of three candidate bounding boxes and a detailed text description. The inpainting mask for each scale is the intersection of the current bbox with the occluder mask. The first scale where the generated object does not touch the bbox boundary is selected. For lightly occluded objects, a simple 10% margin expansion and the category name suffice.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Accuracy vs. Compute:** Selective invocation trades off potential accuracy on medium‑occlusion cases for massive MLLM call reduction. Multi-scale expansion trades off extra inference steps for robustness to size estimation errors.  
  - **Guidance Specificity:** Semantic guidance (detailed prompt) improves appearance but may introduce co‑occurrence bias. Geometric guidance (mask resizing) is always applied (with heuristic if no MLLM) as it’s low-cost and addresses a primary failure mode.  
  - **Modularity:** Occluder detection and final mask extraction (SAM) are plug-and‑play; the paper uses specific models but the pipeline is model‑agnostic.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Extraneous object generation:** Mask `M_inpaint*` is too large → check if geometric guidance failed or if decision module incorrectly skipped guidance.  
  - **Incomplete object:** Generation touches mask boundary → indicates mask too small (tight prediction failed), system should have escalated to moderate/coarse.  
  - **Unnatural appearance / wrong identity:** `P_long` is off‑context or includes occluder details → check semantic guidance visual prompting (occluder masking) and MLLM prompt.  
  - **High latency, no quality gain:** Decision module incorrectly invoking guidance for easy samples → examine occlusion ratio heuristic or decision module training/prompting.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation of Selective Invocation:** Run full pipeline vs. always‑invoke‑MLLM vs. never‑invoke‑MLLM on COCO‑A. Measure mIoU and number of MLLM calls. Expected: Always‑invoke gives marginal gain on easy samples at high cost; never‑invoke collapses on hard samples; selective matches always‑invoke on hard with ~50% fewer calls.  
  2. **Mask Size Validation:** For a sample where GT full object bbox is known, compare IoU between `M_inpaint*` (our resized mask) and `GT_bbox ∩ M_inpaint`. Also test against the original `M_inpaint`. Expected: Our mask has significantly higher IoU with GT than original mask.  
  3. **Semantic Prompt Quality Test:** Manually inspect `P_long` outputs for 50 high‑occlusion samples. Rate: (a) describes only target object (not occluders), (b) matches visible context/pose, (c) introduces unrelated co‑occurring objects. Expected: High score on (a) and (b) due to occluder masking; some failures on (c).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1:** How can co‑occurrence bias in multimodal LLM outputs be reduced to prevent extraneous object generation during amodal completion?  
- **OQ2:** How can geometric guidance more accurately estimate the full extent of objects that are unbounded or truncated by the image boundary (e.g., long fences)?  
- **OQ3:** Can the binary guidance‑invocation decision be refined into a spectrum of guidance levels to better balance computational cost and completion quality?

## Limitations
- The occlusion order predictor [9] details are missing; errors in `M_inpaint` propagate and cannot be corrected.  
- Ambiguous definitions for “10% margin” and “touch‑boundary” criterion prevent faithful reproduction and may affect scale selection.  
- GPT‑4o is a black‑box model with API cost and prompt sensitivity; it suffers from geometric underestimation (elongated objects) and semantic co‑occurrence bias.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Geometric guidance):** Medium‑High – Strong mIoU gain (+10.08%) but relies on accurate GPT‑4o bbox; break condition for undersized masks.  
- **Mechanism 2 (Semantic guidance):** Medium – Qualitative evidence (Fig. 14) but lacks quantitative ablation; risk of co‑occurrence bias remains.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Selective invocation):** High – 48.73% call reduction with high GCR on hard samples is concrete and validated (Table 3).
