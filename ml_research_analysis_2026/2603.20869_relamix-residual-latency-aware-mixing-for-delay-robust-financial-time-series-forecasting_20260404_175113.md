# ReLaMix: Residual Latency-Aware Mixing for Delay-Robust Financial Time-Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.20869  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20869  
- **Reference Count:** 22  
- **Primary Result:** ReLaMix achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy under 15%–35% Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) latency on second-resolution crypto data (PAXGUSDT/BTCUSDT), with lower MSE/MAE and higher R² than TimeMixer and Transformer baselines while using only ~14K parameters (e.g., MSE 0.02928 vs. TimeMixer 0.07898 at 15% delay, k=1).

## Executive Summary
Financial time-series forecasting at high frequencies is severely degraded by latency artifacts such as Zero-Order Hold (ZOH), where repeated stale values create step-function distortions in input sequences. ReLaMix addresses this by extending the TimeMixer architecture with two key innovations: a learnable bottleneck compression layer that explicitly filters redundancy from repeated values, and a dense residual refinement pathway that preserves critical market dynamics. Trained end-to-end from corrupted observations to clean future targets, ReLaMix demonstrates robust performance across varying delay ratios (15%–35%) and prediction horizons (k=1,5,7,10) on cryptocurrency markets, outperforming several strong baselines with an order-of-magnitude fewer parameters.

## Method Summary
ReLaMix modifies the TimeMixer framework to handle ZOH-corrupted inputs. The architecture begins with a bottleneck projection that compresses the high-dimensional input (OHLCV features) into a low-dimensional latent space (db=32), discarding task-irrelevant repetition. This compressed representation is then processed by a stack of Efficient Mixing Blocks, each performing temporal mixing (via a linear MLP on the transposed sequence) and feature mixing (expand-compress MLP) with pre-normalization and residual connections. Multi-level skip connections aggregate outputs from all previous layers, scaled by a fixed coefficient (α=0.1), to create an information highway that mitigates information loss through the bottleneck. The model is trained end-to-end with mean squared error loss on clean future targets, avoiding separate imputation stages.

## Key Results
- ReLaMix significantly reduces forecasting error (MSE, MAE) and improves R² compared to TimeMixer and Transformer-based baselines under simulated ZOH delays of 15%–35% on PAXGUSDT at second resolution.
- At 15% delay and horizon k=1, ReLaMix achieves MSE 0.02928 versus TimeMixer's 0.07898, with only ~14K parameters.
- Cross-asset generalization to BTCUSDT yields consistent best performance under 25%–35% delays.
- Ablation studies confirm both the bottleneck compression and residual refinement components are essential; removing either causes drastic performance degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Bottleneck Compression Filters ZOH-Induced Redundancy
A low-dimensional bottleneck projection (Din→db, db≪Din) explicitly suppresses repeated-value artifacts caused by ZOH latency, reducing representational redundancy and preventing overfitting to stale patterns. This design follows the Information Bottleneck principle, compressing features to discard task-irrelevant noise while preserving predictive signals. The core assumption is that ZOH corruption introduces high correlation (repetition) in the observed sequence, which is more efficiently handled by aggressive early compression than by processing full-dimensional inputs. Evidence: The paper states the method "explicitly suppresses redundancy from repeated stale values" [abstract] and adopts the Information Bottleneck principle for this purpose [section III.B]. Ablations removing the bottleneck lead to higher error, confirming its role. Risk: If db is too large, compression fails; if too small, essential dynamics are lost.

### Mechanism 2: Residual Refinement Preserves Nonlinear Market Dynamics
Pre-activation residual connections within and across mixing blocks allow low-level predictive signals (e.g., genuine price changes) to bypass bottleneck processing, preventing information loss and stabilizing gradient flow under severe ZOH corruption. Each block adds residuals after time and feature mixing, and multi-level skips aggregate all prior layer outputs, forming an information highway. The core assumption is that ZOH stagnation can obscure true dynamics, so once clean signals are extracted, they must not be degraded by subsequent transformations. Evidence: The abstract notes residual refinement "preserves market dynamics"; [section III.C.3] describes the dense residual pathway as an information highway; ablations show MSE spikes (e.g., 0.029→0.237 at k=1) when residuals are removed [section IV.C]. Risk: High residual scaling may propagate stale signals; too low scaling diminishes the highway effect.

### Mechanism 3: End-to-End Corrupted-to-Clean Learning Avoids Two-Stage Error Propagation
Training directly from ZOH-corrupted inputs to clean future targets (fθ(X_obs)→Y) in a single model avoids distribution shifts and cumulative errors from separate imputation and forecasting stages, enabling joint optimization for latency-robust recovery. The model is supervised with MSE loss on clean targets, forcing it to learn mappings that filter staleness artifacts while predicting true dynamics. The core assumption is that a unified objective allows bottleneck and residual mechanisms to cooperate optimally; decoupling introduces latency and mismatch. Evidence: The abstract contrasts with "naive models" and "two-stage pipelines"; [section I] argues ZOH requires suppressing redundancy, not standard imputation. While direct comparative experiments are not provided, the principle is supported by related work on end-to-end learning for missing values [Ref 8]. Risk: Simulated ZOH during training must match deployment latency distribution; insufficient capacity may hinder joint recovery and forecasting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Information Bottleneck Principle:** Compresses input to a latent representation by minimizing mutual information I(X;Z) while retaining information relevant to the target. *Why needed:* explains why aggressive early compression (db=32) can discard ZOH redundancy without hand-crafted filters. *Quick check:* How does minimizing I(X_obs; Z) encourage discarding repeated stale values while preserving predictive factors for future prices?
- **Mixer Architectures (MLP-based Temporal/Feature Mixing):** Replaces self-attention with simple linear layers applied across temporal and feature dimensions (e.g., transposing sequence for time mixing). *Why needed:* to understand why mixers are efficient alternatives to attention for high-frequency, ZOH-corrupted data. *Quick check:* Why might a linear mixer on transposed sequences be more latency-robust than self-attention when inputs contain step-function artifacts?
- **Residual Learning in Deep Networks:** Adds identity mappings (y = F(x) + x) to ease gradient flow and preserve information through deep stacks. *Why needed:* to grasp why dense skip connections are critical when using bottlenecks (which risk information loss) and modeling volatile financial streams. *Quick check:* In a pre-norm residual block, why is the residual added *after* the mixing operation rather than before?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (L=20, Din OHLCV) → LayerNorm → Bottleneck Projection (Linear(Din→db=32) + GELU) → [Stacked EfficientMixingBlock × N] with multi-level skips → Prediction Head (Linear) → Output (k steps).  
**Critical path:** Bottleneck → Time Mixing (L×L MLP) → Feature Mixing (Expand-Compress MLP) → Output head. Multi-level skips are auxiliary but essential for depth.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Bottleneck size db: smaller (16) increases compression (risk losing signal), larger (64) reduces efficiency (risk overfitting ZOH). Feature mix expansion ratio (32→64→32): higher (128) increases capacity/params, lower (32→32→32) may underfit nonlinear dynamics. Skip scaling α: balances highway signal vs. gradient stability (paper uses 0.1).  
**Failure signatures:**  
- **Over-smoothing:** Predictions become flatlines; inspect bottleneck activations—if latent variance is very low, db may be too small or training too aggressive.  
- **Residual collapse:** Performance close to "w/o Residual" ablation; check if skip signals dominate outputs (α too high) or gradients vanish in deep blocks.  
- **Latency mismatch:** Good validation loss but poor live performance; indicates simulated ZOH (random s_t) doesn't match real market latency bursts.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation - Bottleneck:** Train ReLaMix without Compression (remove W_in, keep full Din). Expect parameter count ↑ (~46K) and MSE ↑, confirming compression necessity.  
2. **Ablation - Residual:** Train ReLaMix without Residual (remove additions in mixing blocks). Expect severe MSE ↑ (e.g., 0.029→0.237 at k=1), confirming residual pathways.  
3. **Delay Ratio Sweep:** Test on held-out PAXGUSDT with delay ratios {0%, 15%, 25%, 35%, 50%} for k=1. Expect ReLaMix's MSE to degrade gracefully vs. baselines (e.g., TimeMixer) which degrade sharply beyond 25% delay.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can ReLaMix be extended to handle more complex latency patterns beyond Zero-Order Hold (ZOH), such as stochastic delays, variable hold periods, or mixed staleness patterns observed in real trading systems?** *Basis:* Conclusion states future work will extend to more complex latency patterns. *Why unresolved:* Current study only evaluates simulated ZOH; real-world latency involves stochastic durations, partial updates, or correlated disruptions. *Resolving evidence:* Evaluate on datasets with real latency events or simulate other models (e.g., geometric hold distributions); measure performance with artifact-complexity metrics.
- **What are the minimal computational requirements and hardware-specific optimizations needed for ReLaMix to achieve sub-millisecond inference latency in live, ultra-low-latency trading environments?** *Basis:* Conclusion mentions exploring deployment on hardware-efficient platforms. *Why unresolved:* Paper reports parameter count and GPU speed; real HFT requires microsecond responses on specialized hardware (FPGAs/ASICs) with deterministic timing. *Resolving evidence:* Benchmark on FPGA/ASIC or optimized CPU kernels under realistic streams; measure end-to-end latency, jitter, throughput vs. baselines.
- **Does ReLaMix maintain its performance advantage when evaluated on real-world high-frequency data with naturally occurring latency events, rather than synthetically injected ZOH corruption?** *Basis:* Inferred from methodology using random repetition simulation. *Why unresolved:* No experiments use actual latency logs; simulation may not reflect statistical properties of real staleness. *Resolving evidence:* Obtain timestamped data with documented latency incidents; apply ReLaMix directly to corrupted observations; compare to baselines using staleness-aware metrics.
- **How does the choice of bottleneck dimension (db) affect the trade-off between ZOH artifact suppression and preservation of informative market dynamics across different delay ratios and financial assets?** *Basis:* Inferred from fixed db=32 without sensitivity analysis. *Why unresolved:* Optimal compression level may vary with delay severity and asset volatility; paper does not explore this. *Resolving evidence:* Systematically vary db (e.g., 8–128) on multiple assets/delays; plot accuracy vs. parameter count to find Pareto-optimal ranges.
- **To what extent does ReLaMix generalize to traditional financial assets (e.g., equities, futures, forex) with different microstructures, trading hours, and liquidity profiles, beyond the cryptocurrency markets tested?** *Basis:* Inferred from experiments limited to PAXGUSDT/BTCUSDT (24/7 crypto). *Why unresolved:* Generalizability to markets with discrete sessions, auction mechanisms, or lower frequencies is unexplored. *Resolving evidence:* Replicate protocol on diverse asset classes (e.g., E-mini S&P 500, EUR/USD) with appropriate resampling; compare relative improvements.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified implementation details:** Exact model depth (N), training schedule (epochs, early stopping), prediction head structure, and full baseline hyperparameters are not reported, hindering exact replication.
- **Evaluation scope:** Experiments are restricted to two cryptocurrency assets (PAXGUSDT, BTCUSDT) at second resolution; generalizability to equities, futures, forex, or lower frequencies is unknown.
- **Simulation realism:** Latency is simulated via independent random value repetition; real exchange latency exhibits burstiness, cross-asset correlation, and out-of-order arrivals that may not be captured.
- **Baseline tuning:** Baselines may not have been optimized for ZOH settings, potentially overstating ReLaMix's relative gains.

## Confidence
- **Bottleneck filters ZOH redundancy:** Medium confidence. Ablation shows benefit vs. no bottleneck, but no direct evidence that compression discards only redundancy; may lose predictive signal.
- **Residual refinement preserves dynamics:** High confidence. Ablation removing residual connections causes dramatic MSE increase (e.g., 0.029→0.237), confirming critical role.
- **End-to-end learning avoids two-stage errors:** Medium confidence. Conceptual argument and figure provided, but lacks quantitative comparison with a two-stage pipeline under identical conditions.
- **SOTA performance under delay:** Medium confidence. Results are strong on tested crypto assets but limited in asset diversity and frequency; external validity untested.
