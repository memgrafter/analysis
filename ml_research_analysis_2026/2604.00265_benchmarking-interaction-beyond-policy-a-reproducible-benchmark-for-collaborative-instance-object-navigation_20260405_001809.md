# Benchmarking Interaction, Beyond Policy: a Reproducible Benchmark for Collaborative Instance Object Navigation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00265
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00265
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces QAsk-Nav benchmark and Light-CoNav model, which achieves state-of-the-art navigation success (9.63% on Val Unseen) and superior question-asking performance while being 3× smaller and 70× faster.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the entanglement of navigation and interaction in existing CoIN benchmarks by proposing a disentangled evaluation framework called QAsk-Nav, comprising separate protocols for question-asking and navigation. It introduces a dataset of 28k reasoning traces and a lightweight unified model, Light-CoNav, which outperforms larger modular baselines in both question-asking and navigation. The work enables reproducible, efficient research on collaborative interaction through reduced computational overhead and higher-quality target descriptions.

## Method Summary
The authors construct QAsk-Nav, a benchmark that splits collaborative instance object navigation into two distinct tasks: (1) a question-asking protocol where an agent responds to a sequence of distractor images with decisions or questions, and (2) a navigation protocol that retains full episodes but uses improved, diverse target descriptions generated by Gemini-3-pro and validated by humans. The core model, Light-CoNav, is a single multimodal transformer (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) fine-tuned in two stages: first to predict reasoning and an uncertainty score, then to also generate questions given context. This unified approach replaces complex modular pipelines, yielding a model that is three times smaller and seventy times faster than prior methods while achieving superior performance.

## Key Results
- Question-asking: Light-CoNav achieves 45.1% Success Rate (SR) and 33.2% Finish Rate (FR), outperforming AIUTA and larger VLMs.
- Navigation: It attains state-of-the-art SR on Val Seen (12.2%) and Val Unseen (9.63%) with better Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).
- Efficiency: The model is 3× smaller and 70× faster than previous modular systems, enhancing reproducibility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Separate protocols enable lightweight, reproducible assessment of collaborative interaction without full navigation overhead.
- Mechanism: The question-asking protocol isolates interaction reasoning using distractor images, avoiding time-consuming simulations. The navigation protocol retains full episodes but uses better descriptions.
- Core assumption: Question-asking performance in isolation correlates with interaction quality in full CoIN.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions separate protocols; [section 3.2] and [section 3.3] detail protocols; no direct support in corpus.
- Break condition: Low correlation between isolated question-asking scores and full CoIN navigation performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High-quality, diverse target descriptions reduce ambiguity and improve navigation outcomes.
- Mechanism: Synthetic descriptions from Gemini-3-pro, validated by humans, replace noisy prior annotations, offering six specificity levels.
- Core assumption: Better instructions boost agent's ability to distinguish target from distractors.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] and [section 3.3] describe generation and human validation (>80% preference); [supp. mat. Sec 2.1, Tab 7] shows quantitative improvement over CoIN-Bench.
- Break condition: Navigation success does not increase with new descriptions (holding model constant). Both AIUTA and Light-CoNav improve, supporting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Two-stage supervised fine-tuning on reasoning traces yields a compact model that outperforms larger modular systems.
- Mechanism: Stage 1 trains VLM to output reasoning R and uncertainty S; Stage 2 adds question generation Q with context C. Unified approach avoids complex modular pipelines, reduces latency.
- Core assumption: Explicit supervision on reasoning/uncertainty is necessary; staged curriculum prevents catastrophic forgetting.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 4] (architecture/training), [section 5.1] (results vs baselines), [supp. mat. Sec 5.1, Fig 14] (ablation on reasoning).
- Break condition: Single-stage training or removing reasoning supervision yields comparable performance. Ablations disagree, supporting.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Habitat simulator → Navigation policy (VLFM) with object detector (Grounding-DINO) → Light-CoNav VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) → Oracle (Gemini-3-VL-30B-A3B) for question answering → QAsk-Nav-dataset (training tuples). Metrics computed from simulator logs.

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Navigation policy explores until object of target category is detected.  
  2. Observation O and description D (plus context C) are fed to Light-CoNav.  
  3. Light-CoNav returns reasoning R, score S, and question Q (if any).  
  4. If S=2, candidate is accepted; policy navigates to it.  
  5. If S=0, candidate rejected; navigation continues.  
  6. If S=1, question Q is sent to oracle; answer A updates context C; repeat step 2 with same O and new C.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Unified VLM vs modular: Simpler, faster, fewer parameters but may sacrifice specialized performance.  
  - Two-stage training: More stable than end-to-end but requires curated datasets and may not align representations perfectly.  
  - Lightweight model (7B) vs larger (30B+): Enables real-time deployment but caps accuracy ceiling.  
  - Discrete uncertainty scores: Simplify decisions but lose nuance.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - High NQ + low SR: Irrelevant or excessive questions.  
  - Low FR: Premature wrong conclusions (e.g., always S=0 or S=2).  
  - Large performance drop on Val Unseen: Poor generalization.  
  - Latency >1s per interaction: Breaks real-time use (Light-CoNav ~1.15s vs AIUTA 177s).  
  - Incoherent reasoning traces R: Model not truly understanding task.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Question-asking replication: Run Light-CoNav on QAsk-Nav test episodes with fixed oracle (Gemini-3-flash), compute SR/FR/NQ/Time, compare to Tab 1.  
  2. Navigation replication: Integrate Light-CoNav with VLFM in Habitat, evaluate on navigation splits (Val Seen, etc.), compute SR/SPL/NQ, compare to Tab 3.  
  3. Training ablation: Train variants without Stage 1, without Stage 2, and without reasoning in output. Evaluate on question-asking to show each stage’s contribution (see Supp. Fig 14).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: How much explicit supervision from reasoning traces is actually needed to learn effective question asking?  
  Basis: Explicitly posed in Conclusion; unresolved because minimal required amount and relative importance unknown; evidence would be systematic ablation varying reasoning supervision quantity.

- **Question 2**: Does coupling question generation with calibrated uncertainty estimates improve ask-or-act decisions and robustness under noisy observations or missing detections?  
  Basis: Explicitly listed as key question; unresolved because uncertainty calibration quality and robustness to noise not thoroughly evaluated; evidence would be controlled experiments with synthetic noise and analysis of score-error correlation.

- **Question 3**: How well do VLM-based oracles approximate real human interaction, and what biases might they introduce in evaluation?  
  Basis: Inferred; QAsk-Nav relies on VLMs as oracles; human validation limited (Supp. Mat. Sec 1.2) and not systematic; unresolved due to potential answering style biases; evidence would be large-scale human evaluation across models and description types.

- **Question 4**: Why does navigation performance remain low even with improved question-asking, and what are the primary bottlenecks in generalization to unseen environments?  
  Basis: Inferred; absolute SR on Val Unseen only 9.63% indicates persistent challenges; paper attributes gains to interaction but doesn't decompose failures; unresolved; evidence would be comprehensive error analysis categorizing failure modes.

## Limitations
- Disentanglement relies on unverified correlation between isolated question-asking and full CoIN navigation performance.
- Critical implementation details (hyperparameters, VLFM setup, episode splits) are missing, blocking exact replication.
- Synthetic target descriptions may not fully represent natural human language variation.

## Confidence
- Disentanglement claim: **Low**
- Description quality claim: **Medium**
- Two-stage training claim: **Medium**
