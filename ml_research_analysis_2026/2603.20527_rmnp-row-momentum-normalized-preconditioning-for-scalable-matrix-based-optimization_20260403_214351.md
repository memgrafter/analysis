# RMNP: Row-Momentum Normalized Preconditioning for Scalable Matrix-Based Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20527
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20527
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RMNP achieves 13-44× faster preconditioning while matching or improving perplexity (e.g., 22.60 vs. 22.71 on GPT-2 Small).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the high computational cost of Muon's Newton-Schulz iteration for matrix-based preconditioning in deep learning. By replacing this iterative orthogonalization with a simple row-wise ℓ2 normalization, RMNP reduces per-iteration complexity from O(mn·min(m,n)) to O(mn). This efficiency gain is motivated by the empirically observed strong diagonal block dominance in Transformer Hessians, where within-row parameter interactions dominate cross-row ones. Across GPT-2 (125M-770M) and LLaMA (60M-350M) pre-training, RMNP achieves 13-44× speedup in wall-clock preconditioning time while matching or improving perplexity relative to Muon. Theoretical analysis provides convergence guarantees with minimax optimal sample complexity, aligning with Muon's best results.

## Method Summary
RMNP operates on the momentum matrix \(V_t\) (accumulated gradients) from an SGD-like update. Instead of computing the full matrix square root via Newton-Schulz iterations (as in Muon), RMNP normalizes each row of \(V_t\) by its ℓ2 norm. This row-wise operation yields a preconditioned direction \(D_t\) that approximates the full preconditioner under the assumption of diagonal dominance in the Gram matrix \(VV^\top\). The method integrates into a mixed-update framework: 2D weight matrices (e.g., Linear layers) are updated with RMNP, while all other parameters use AdamW. A learning rate scaling factor \(\sqrt{n/m}\) is applied to the matrix learning rate to balance dimensions. The algorithm avoids constructing the full Gram matrix, requiring only row norms (cost O(mn)), thus achieving linear complexity in the matrix size.

## Key Results
- **Efficiency**: 13-44× speedup in wall-clock preconditioning time compared to Muon across GPT-2 sizes (60M to 1.5B).
- **Effectiveness**: Matches or improves pre-training perplexity on GPT-2 and LLaMA (e.g., 22.60 vs. 22.71 on GPT-2 Small).
- **Theoretical**: Convergence guarantees with minimax optimal sample complexity \(O(m^2 L_F \sigma^2 \Delta \epsilon^{-4})\) under Frobenius smoothness and \(O(m L_{\infty,2} \sigma^2 \Delta \epsilon^{-4})\) under row-wise smoothness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Transformer layer-wise Hessians exhibit strong row-wise block diagonal dominance, where within-row parameter interactions dominate cross-row interactions. The diagonal blocks capture the primary curvature information. Muon’s preconditioner based on momentum outer product approximates the full curvature, but RMNP simplifies by retaining only the diagonal blocks via row-wise operations. Evidence: empirical verification in Section 3.2 shows \(r_{\text{min}} > 1.6\), \(r_{\text{avg}} > 4.9\) across GPT-2 scales. Break condition: if \(r_{\text{min}}\) approaches 1, off-diagonal interactions become significant, and RMNP may underperform.

### Mechanism 2
Row-wise ℓ2 normalization serves as an effective structured approximation of the Muon preconditioner by aligning with the diagonal block dominance. RMNP computes \(D = \text{diag}(VV^\top)^{-1/2} V\), normalizing each row independently. This corresponds to a Kronecker product with a diagonal matrix, retaining only row-level scaling and dropping cross-row coupling. Evidence: Section 3.1 derives this from Equation 3 and 4. Break condition: when row norms vary widely or cross-row effects are strong, the diagonal approximation may distort preconditioning.

### Mechanism 3
RMNP reduces computational complexity from \(O(mn \cdot \min(m,n))\) to \(O(mn)\) per iteration by eliminating iterative matrix operations. Row-wise normalization requires computing row norms (cost \(O(mn)\)) and scaling, while Muon’s Newton-Schulz involves multiple matrix multiplications (each \(O(mn \cdot \min(m,n))\)). Evidence: Section 4.2, Table 2 shows 13–44× speedup. Break condition: for very thin matrices (\(\min(m,n)\) small), the speedup diminishes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Momentum matrix \(V_t\) (from SGD) -> Row-wise ℓ2 normalization -> Preconditioned direction \(D_t\) -> Weight update. Integrates with AdamW for non-matrix parameters.

**Critical path:**
1. Compute momentum: \(V_t = \beta V_{t-1} + (1-\beta) G_t\).
2. Compute Gram matrix row norms: \(\text{diag}(VV^\top)_i = \|V_{i,:}\|_2^2\) (avoid full \(VV^\top\) construction).
3. Invert square roots: \(\text{diag}(VV^\top)^{-1/2}\).
4. Scale rows: \(D_{i,:} = V_{i,:} / \|V_{i,:}\|_2\).
5. Update weights: \(W_{t+1} = W_t - \eta_t D_t\).

**Design tradeoffs:** RMNP sacrifices off-diagonal curvature information for order-of-magnitude speedup. Based on empirical dominance, this is acceptable for Transformers, but may fail for architectures with stronger cross-row coupling.

**Failure signatures:**
- Perplexity degradation vs. Muon, especially on later training stages.
- Diagonal dominance metrics (\(r_{\text{min}}\)) dropping near 1.
- Instability in gradient norms if row norms become near-zero (add epsilon).
- No speedup on small matrices (\(\min(m,n)\) tiny) due to overhead.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Diagonal dominance validation: Compute \(r_{\text{min}}\), \(r_{\text{avg}}\) on small Transformer models (e.g., GPT-2 Small) to confirm dominance holds across layers.
2. Wall-clock timing: Benchmark preconditioning time per step on varying model sizes (60M to 1.5B) to verify 13–44× speedup.
3. Pre-training comparison: Train GPT-2/LLaMA with RMNP vs. Muon and AdamW on standard corpora (OpenWebText, C4), tracking perplexity to ensure no performance loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability of diagonal dominance**: Does it hold for non-Transformer architectures? The paper only tests on GPT-2/LLaMA.
2. **Theoretical origin of dominance**: What causes diagonal dominance in Transformer Hessians? No causal theory is provided.
3. **Optimal momentum for RMNP**: How does RMNP's optimal momentum β differ from Muon's default β=0.95? No sensitivity study is conducted.
4. **Comparison to other matrix preconditioners**: How does RMNP compare to Shampoo or SOAP in the accuracy-efficiency trade-off? The paper benchmarks only against Muon and AdamW.

## Limitations
- Diagonal dominance evidence is limited to GPT‑2 and LLaMA Transformers; may not generalize to other architectures or later training stages.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on idealized assumptions (Frobenius/row‑wise smoothness, isotropic noise) that may not hold exactly in practice.
- Hyperparameter details for LLaMA (e.g., AdamW learning rate) are missing, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- Diagonal block dominance: **Medium** (empirical support strong but narrow in scope).
- Row-wise ℓ2 normalization as effective preconditioner: **High** (empirical parity with Muon and theoretical convergence under smoothness assumptions).
- Computational complexity reduction: **High** (clear algorithmic improvement; speedups confirmed empirically).
- Convergence guarantees/sample complexity: **Medium** (proofs rely on idealized assumptions; empirical alignment needs further validation).
- End‑to‑end pre‑training results: **High** for GPT‑2, **Medium** for LLaMA due to incomplete hyperparameter reporting.
