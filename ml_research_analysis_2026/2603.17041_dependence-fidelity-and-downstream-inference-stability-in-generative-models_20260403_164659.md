# Dependence Fidelity and Downstream Inference Stability in Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17041
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17041
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: Marginal distribution fidelity does not guarantee preservation of multivariate dependence structures; covariance-level dependence fidelity (Frobenius norm of covariance difference) is necessary and sufficient for stability of downstream tasks governed by covariance (e.g., linear regression, PCA).

## Executive Summary
Generative models are typically evaluated on marginal distribution fidelity, but this does not ensure preservation of multivariate dependence structures crucial for downstream inference. This work introduces covariance-level dependence fidelity—measured by the Frobenius norm between covariance matrices—as a practical criterion to assess joint structure preservation. Three theoretical results establish a hierarchy: (1) Exact marginal matching is compatible with arbitrarily large dependence divergence, including distinct copulas and covariance matrices. (2) Covariance discrepancies induce quantitative downstream instability; for linear regression, the difference in population coefficients is bounded by the normalized Frobenius norm, and example constructions yield regression sign reversal despite identical marginals. (3) Explicit covariance control provides stability guarantees for dependence-sensitive tasks: eigenvalue errors are bounded by the Frobenius norm, and principal subspace error obeys a bound involving the eigengap. Minimal synthetic examples illustrate that marginal-only diagnostics fail to detect these dependence-induced errors, highlighting dependence fidelity as a necessary and sufficient (for covariance‑governed tasks) diagnostic for trustworthy generative modeling.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a theoretical and empirical analysis of dependence fidelity in generative modeling. The core methodological contribution is the definition of a dependence fidelity metric, D_Σ(P,Q) = ||Σ_P - Σ_Q||_F, where Σ denotes the population covariance matrix. The authors prove three theorems linking this metric to downstream stability: Theorem 1 shows that marginal fidelity (exact matching of all univariate distributions) places no constraint on Σ, so D_Σ can be arbitrarily large; Theorem 2 provides an upper bound on the difference in linear regression coefficients between two distributions in terms of D_Σ, and constructs examples where the sign of the coefficient reverses; Theorem 3 uses matrix perturbation theory to bound eigenvalue errors and principal subspace errors for PCA in terms of D_Σ and the eigengap. The theoretical results are illustrated with synthetic bivariate Gaussian examples and a real-data case using the Huntley Alzheimer's gene expression dataset, where independent marginals matching yields large D_Σ and unstable regression coefficients.

## Key Results
- **Theorem 1 (Marginal vs. Dependence)**: Matching all univariate marginals does not constrain the joint dependence structure; the copula and covariance matrix can differ arbitrarily, yielding unbounded D_Σ.
- **Theorem 2 (Regression Instability)**: For linear regression with equal feature variances, |β(P)-β(Q)| ≤ (1/√(2σ_X^2)) · D_Σ(P,Q). Moreover, there exist distributions with identical marginals but opposite regression coefficient signs.
- **Theorem 3 (PCA Stability)**: Covariance perturbations of size D_Σ bound eigenvalue errors: |λ_i(Σ_P) - λ_i(Σ_Q)| ≤ D_Σ. For principal subspaces, the sin Θ error is bounded by D_Σ / γ, where γ is the eigengap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Exact matching of all univariate marginals does not constrain the multivariate dependence structure (copula and covariance).
- **Mechanism:** Univariate distribution matching only ensures that the marginal cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) agree. The joint distribution is defined via Sklar’s theorem as the combination of marginals and a copula. Matching marginals leaves the copula completely free, so dependence structures (e.g., correlation, tail dependence) can differ arbitrarily. The covariance divergence ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_F can be made arbitrarily large while all P^j = Q^j.
- **Core assumption:** Distributions have continuous marginals (so copula is unique) and finite second moments (so covariance exists).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Theorem 1: “distributions can match all univariate marginals exactly while exhibiting substantially different dependence structures.”
  - [section] §2–§4.1: formal definitions of marginal fidelity vs. dependence fidelity and construction using bivariate Gaussians with identical N(0,1) marginals but opposite correlations.
  - [corpus] Neighbors discuss copula-based modeling (e.g., “GPDFlow”) and distributional fidelity, but none directly prove the non-identifiability result; thus corpus evidence for this precise mechanism is weak.
- **Break condition:** If any marginal distribution differs (P^j ≠ Q^j), even worse dependence ambiguity occurs, but the core point stands that marginal matching alone is insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Covariance-level dependence divergence directly induces quantitative instability in downstream inference, e.g., sign reversal of population regression coefficients even when marginal variances match.
- **Mechanism:** For centered variables, the population least-squares slope β = Cov(X,Y) / Var(X). If Var_P(X)=Var_Q(X), then |β(P)–β(Q)| ≤ (1/√2 σ_X²) ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_F. A perturbation confined to off-diagonal covariance entries (correlation sign flip) achieves equality, reversing β’s sign while all univariate marginals and marginal variances remain identical.
- **Core assumption:** Equal marginal variances (σ²_X) and finite second moments; the bound is tight when covariance perturbation is purely off-diagonal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Theorem 2: “for linear regression, |β(P)–β(Q)| ≤ (1/√2 σ_X²) ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_F, and example constructions yield regression sign reversal despite identical marginals.”
  - [section] §4.2: proof using expansion of population risk and direct substitution of covariance difference.
  - [corpus] Neighbors on “counterfactual inference” and “synthetic data” mention downstream utility but do not derive the specific regression coefficient bound.
- **Break condition:** The bound assumes equal marginal variances; if Var_P(X) ≠ Var_Q(X), a more complex relationship emerges that may not yield sign reversal with only covariance perturbation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Explicit control of covariance-level discrepancy (D_Σ) provides quantitative stability guarantees for dependence-sensitive tasks like PCA, under an eigengap condition.
- **Mechanism:** Matrix perturbation theory (Weyl’s inequality, Davis–Kahan sin Θ theorem) shows that eigenvalue and principal subspace errors are bounded by ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_2 and D_Σ(P,Q)/γ respectively, where γ is the eigengap. Thus, small D_Σ ensures stability of spectral quantities.
- **Core assumption:** Small perturbation relative to the eigengap γ (D_Σ(P,Q) ≪ γ); distributions have finite second moments; PCA subspace is defined by top‑r eigenvectors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Theorem 3: “eigenvalue errors are bounded by ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_F, and principal subspace error obeys ‖sin Θ(U_P,U_Q)‖_2 ≤ ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_F/γ.”
  - [section] §4.3: explicit application of Weyl and Davis–Kahan, and discussion of informative regime D_Σ ≪ γ.
  - [corpus] Neighbors on “stability of statistical procedures” and “trustworthy AI” reference perturbation theory but not the specific D_Σ-based bounds.
- **Break condition:** If the eigengap γ is very small (nearly duplicate eigenvalues), even tiny D_Σ may cause large subspace errors; the bound becomes loose.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Copula Theory**
  - Why needed here: Separates marginal distributions from dependence structure, enabling construction of distributions with identical marginals but different joint dependence (Theorem 1).
  - Quick check question: Given two distributions with identical continuous marginals, what mathematical object uniquely determines their dependence structure?
- **Concept: Covariance Matrix Perturbation**
  - Why needed here: D_Σ(P,Q) = ‖Σ_P – Σ_Q‖_F is the proposed fidelity metric; stability bounds for regression and PCA are expressed in terms of this quantity.
  - Quick check question: How does the Frobenius norm of a covariance difference bound the difference in population regression slopes when marginal variances are equal?
- **Concept: Eigengap**
  - Why needed here: Determines sensitivity of PCA subspace to covariance perturbations; Theorem 3’s subspace bound includes γ in the denominator.
  - Quick check question: Why does a larger eigengap (γ) lead to a tighter stability guarantee for the principal subspace under a fixed covariance discrepancy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The “architecture” here is an evaluation framework, not a generative model. Components: (1) target distribution P (real data), (2) candidate generative distribution Q (synthetic data), (3) metric D_Σ(P,Q) = ‖Σ_P - Σ_Q‖_F, (4) downstream task T (e.g., linear regression, PCA), (5) stability bound linking D_Σ to |T(P)–T(Q)|.
- **Critical path:** 1) Estimate Σ_P and Σ_Q from samples (use regularized estimator if high-dimensional). 2) Compute D_Σ. 3) For a given task (e.g., regression), check whether D_Σ is small enough relative to task-specific scales (e.g., σ_X² for regression, γ for PCA). 4) If D_Σ is large, expect potential instability; inspect downstream T(Q) directly.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Frobenius norm is computationally simple but scale-sensitive; one might use correlation-based or whitened versions. High dimensionality requires regularization (e.g., Ledoit–Wolf) for stable covariance estimates. The eigengap γ may be unknown in practice; empirical estimation adds variance.
- **Failure signatures:** (i) Small marginal KS distances but large D_Σ. (ii) Large changes in regression coefficients or PCA subspaces despite good marginal fidelity. (iii) For Gaussian-like data, opposite signs in sample correlation between real and synthetic data.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Theorem 1: Generate bivariate Gaussians with unit marginals but correlations ρ and –ρ; verify marginals match (KS test) and D̂Σ = ||Σ̂_P - Σ̂_Q||_F (≈2√2|ρ|≈1.414). Verify marginal histograms overlap.
  2. Validate Theorem 2: For the same pair, compute regression slopes β̂ = Cov(X,Y)/Var(X). Expect β̂_P≈ρ=0.5, β̂_Q≈-0.5. Check |β̂_P-β̂_Q| ≈ (1/√2) D̂Σ.
  3. Test Theorem 3: Take a high-variance Gaussian with clear eigengap, add small Gaussian noise to its covariance, compute D̂_Σ, and measure ‖sin Θ‖ between true and perturbed top‑2 eigenvectors; verify it scales with D̂_Σ/γ.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1: How to measure covariance-level dependence fidelity for modern generative models (e.g., diffusion models, GANs)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states that evaluating covariance-level dependence diagnostics on modern generative models “is an important next step.”
- **Why unresolved**: Current evaluation focuses on marginal metrics; the behavior and impact of dependence fidelity for these architectures remain unstudied.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic benchmarking of DΣ across model families, correlated with downstream task stability, to identify common distortion patterns.

#### Open Question 2: Can the dependence fidelity framework be extended to large language models (LLMs)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The abstract mentions “potential extensions to large language models as future work.”
- **Why unresolved**: LLMs generate discrete tokens with contextual dependencies; it is unknown whether covariance in embedding spaces captures task-relevant structure.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Define appropriate dependence metrics for LLM outputs, evaluate on dependence-sensitive language tasks, and correlate with performance.

#### Open Question 3: What dependence criteria beyond covariance are necessary for higher-order tasks like tail-event estimation?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper notes: “These guarantees apply specifically to procedures governed by covariance structure; tasks requiring higher-order dependence such as tail-event estimation require richer criteria.”
- **Why unresolved**: Covariance does not capture tail dependence or nonlinear copula effects, which are crucial for extreme-value analysis.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop and validate copula-based or other higher-order dependence metrics, and demonstrate their ability to predict joint extreme-event probabilities.

## Limitations
- **Second-order limitation**: D_Σ captures only linear (covariance) dependence and may miss non-linear structures like tail dependence, which are important for extreme events.
- **Estimation error**: In high dimensions, sample covariance estimation is noisy; regularization can help but theoretical bounds do not account for this error, potentially leading to unreliable D_Σ.
- **Eigengap sensitivity**: For PCA, the subspace error bound scales as D_Σ/γ; when the eigengap γ is tiny, even minuscule D_Σ can cause large instability.

## Confidence
- **Theorem 1** (marginal matching ≠ dependence fidelity): **High** confidence (rigorous copula construction).
- **Theorem 2** (covariance divergence → regression instability): **High** confidence under equal marginal variances; bound is tight for off-diagonal perturbations.
- **Theorem 3** (covariance control → PCA stability): **High** confidence (matrix perturbation theory), caveated on non-negligible eigengap.
- **Overall claim** that D_Σ is necessary and sufficient for covariance‑governed tasks: **High** confidence.
