# Vision Verification Enhanced Fusion of VLMs for Efficient Visual Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12669
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12669
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: V3Fusion improves accuracy on MMMU (+8.09%), MMMU‑Pro (+4.87%), A‑OKVQA, and OCR‑VQA, outperforming individual VLMs and prior ensemble methods while maintaining competitive cost and low data dependency.

## Executive Summary
Vision‑Language Models often exhibit complementary strengths, but effectively combining them is challenging due to architectural heterogeneity and hallucinations. V3Fusion introduces a three‑stage pipeline: (1) pruning the VLM pool using Focal‑CKA (visual diversity on failure cases) and focal error diversity, optimized via a genetic algorithm; (2) fusing outputs with task‑specific models (MLP for multiple‑choice, LED for open‑ended); and (3) rectifying uncertain predictions based on epistemic uncertainty and an adaptive threshold. Extensive evaluation on four benchmarks shows consistent improvements over strong baselines, even when the majority of models are wrong, while maintaining efficiency and low data dependence.

## Method Summary
V3Fusion addresses VLM ensemble fusion through a systematic approach. First, to select a complementary subset, it computes visual diversity via Focal‑CKA: for each model, it extracts visual token embeddings on samples where that model errs, then measures pairwise CKA similarity among models; low similarity (high 1‑CKA) indicates diverse representations. Additionally, it measures output‑level error complementarity through focal error diversity (λ_focal_error), which penalizes joint failures. A genetic algorithm maximizes a fitness function combining these diversity scores and a proxy accuracy (plurality voting) to identify high‑performing sub‑ensembles. Second, the chosen ensemble’s outputs are combined: for MCQ, each model’s choice probabilities are concatenated and fed to a shallow MLP; for open‑ended, generated answers are concatenated with the question and processed by a Longformer Encoder‑Decoder (LED) to produce a final answer. Third, an epistemic uncertainty estimate (mutual information derived from ensemble logits) is compared against a dataset‑specific threshold τ, automatically selected via a Gaussian vs. GMM likelihood‑ratio test on validation data. If uncertainty exceeds τ, the fused answer is rejected and a fallback (e.g., averaged logits for MCQ) is used instead. This two‑tiered correction mechanism helps mitigate hallucinations.

## Key Results
- +8.09% on MMMU and +4.87% on MMMU‑Pro over the best single VLM.
- Outperforms prior ensemble methods (LLM‑Blender, RouteLLM, MAD) on all benchmarks, including cases where majority vote fails.
- Achieves these gains with competitive inference cost and low dependence on training data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Focal‑CKA Visual Diversity**  
The claim is that computing CKA on visual embeddings extracted from erroneous samples quantifies visual understanding diversity among VLMs, enabling selection of complementary ensemble members. The mechanism: for each VLM, visual token embeddings are mean‑pooled; on held‑out samples where a focal model fails, pairwise CKA similarity is computed across models. The average of (1–CKA) over focal models yields the Focal‑CKA score, representing visual dissimilarity on mistakes. The core assumption is that disagreement in visual representations on incorrect predictions indicates that the models perceive the image differently, which fusion can exploit. Evidence includes the abstract’s mention of “focal error diversity and a CKA‑based metric,” Section 4.1’s definition of Focal‑CKA (Equation 3), and the novelty shown by the lack of such metrics in neighboring papers. The break condition arises if all models fail on the same visual patterns; then Focal‑CKA remains low (high similarity) even for heterogeneous architectures, causing the pruning step to discard potentially useful combinations or select a subset lacking true diversity.

**Mechanism 2: Focal Error Diversity and GA Pruning**  
The claim: The focal error diversity metric (λ_focal_error) captures output‑level error complementarity; optimizing it with a Genetic Algorithm efficiently identifies high‑performing sub‑ensembles from an exponential pool. Mechanism: For a candidate ensemble, ρ_focal for each model is 1 – (P_joint_fail / P_fail), where P_joint_fail is the probability of simultaneous failures weighted by model counts. λ_focal_error averages ρ_focal across ensemble members. The GA uses a fitness function that combines λ_focal_CKA, λ_focal_error, and plurality voting accuracy to evolve toward optimal subsets. The core assumption is that ensembles whose component models err on different samples (low joint failure probability) exhibit complementary errors, increasing the likelihood that fusion yields the correct answer. Evidence: abstract states “focal error diversity to capture complementary reasoning across VLMs” and “applied a Genetic Algorithm to effectively prune out those component VLMs that do not add value;” Section 4.2 defines ρ_focal and λ_focal_error; Section 5 describes GA pruning and reports Figure 3 (ensemble surface). No existing work applies a GA to prune VLM ensembles with a focal diversity metric. Break condition: the GA fitness landscape may contain local optima if diversity metrics are noisy or the validation set is small; the algorithm could converge to a suboptimal subset, particularly when many models have similar performance distributions.

**Mechanism 3: Task‑Specific Fusion with Uncertainty Rectification**  
The claim: Task‑specific fusion models (MLP for multiple‑choice, LED for open‑ended) learn to combine heterogeneous VLM outputs, and an adaptive epistemic uncertainty threshold prevents hallucination by rejecting uncertain predictions—even when majority vote fails. Mechanism: For MCQs, concatenate each VLM’s probability vector over choices and feed to an MLP that outputs a final choice distribution. For OEQs, concatenate each VLM’s generated answer (with separators) together with the question and feed to a Longformer Encoder‑Decoder that autoregressively generates the final answer. Epistemic uncertainty is estimated as the mutual information between model parameters and output, approximated by the entropy of the ensemble’s softmax logits minus the average per‑model entropy. An adaptive threshold τ is selected per dataset via a likelihood‑ratio test between a single Gaussian and a two‑component GMM fitted to validation uncertainties. If uncertainty > τ, the fused output is rejected and a fallback (e.g., averaged logits for MCQ) is used. Core assumption: The distribution of logits or generated texts from diverse VLMs encodes sufficient complementary cues for a learned fusion to recover the correct answer; high epistemic uncertainty (disagreement) correlates with incorrect predictions and hallucinations. Evidence: abstract describes “V3Fusion‑MLP leverages the probability distribution … even when the majority of candidate models are incorrect,” “V3Fusion‑LED … learns to generate answers … by comparing and contrasting base‑model outputs,” and “reflection mechanism based on the epistemic uncertainty … with a dynamic adaptive threshold;” Section 6 details architectures; Section 7 formulates uncertainty (Eq. 6) and presents Algorithm 1 for threshold selection; Table 2 shows gains; Figure 4 demonstrates success when majority is wrong; Figure 5 validates the threshold. No existing work combines learned fusion with adaptive uncertainty for VLM ensembles. Break condition: If fusion models overfit to the training ensemble distribution (which may not reflect test‑time diversity), generalization to new VLM combinations may degrade. Also, if the uncertainty distribution is unimodal, the GMM may still be selected but produce a threshold that does not separate certain from uncertain samples well, reducing rectification benefits.

## Foundational Learning
- **Ensemble Diversity**: Why needed here? V3Fusion’s pruning and fusion rely on the principle that diverse models make different errors, enabling correction through combination. Quick check: Why is high correlation among model errors detrimental to ensemble performance, even if individual models are accurate?
- **Central Kernel Alignment (CKA)**: Why needed? CKA is the foundation of Focal‑CKA, allowing comparison of visual embeddings from VLMs with different architectures and dimensions. Its invariance to orthogonal transformations and scaling makes it suitable for cross‑model similarity. Quick check: How does CKA differ from cosine similarity when comparing neural network representations, and why is this important for heterogeneous VLMs?
- **Epistemic Uncertainty in Deep Ensembles**: Why needed? The rectification stage uses epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty) estimated from VLM disagreement to detect hallucinations. Distinguishing epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty is key. Quick check: In a VLM ensemble, what does a high mutual information (I[y,θ|x]) tell you about the prediction’s reliability?
- **Vision‑Language Model Architecture**: Why needed? V3Fusion interacts with VLMs at the level of visual encoders and language decoders (hooking embeddings, reading logits). Understanding how images are tokenised and processed alongside text is necessary. Quick check: What are the two main components of a standard VLM and how do they exchange information during forward pass?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Processor → VLM Pool → (Analysis Module for offline pruning) → Fusion Models (V3Fusion‑MLP/V3Fusion‑LED) → Uncertainty Estimator → Adaptive Threshold Module → Rectification Module → Output Formatter. The Analysis Module includes Focal‑CKA calculator, Focal‑Diversity calculator, and Genetic Algorithm optimizer; the VLM Pool provides visual embeddings (for diversity) and outputs (logits or text). The Adaptive Threshold Module uses validation uncertainties to set τ; during inference, the Uncertainty Estimator computes I(y,θ|x) from ensemble logits.
- **Critical path**:  
  1. *Offline selection*: On a held‑out validation set, compute focal metrics for all candidate subsets (or GA search) to identify the best subset per task. Save selected VLM IDs and train the fusion model (MLP/LED) on the training split.  
  2. *Inference*:  
     a. Load the pruned VLM subset.  
     b. Forward each VLM to obtain its output (logits or text).  
     c. Run the corresponding fusion model to get a preliminary prediction.  
     d. Compute epistemic uncertainty from the VLM logits.  
     e. Compare uncertainty to τ; if above, trigger rectification (fallback).  
     f. Return final answer.  
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Ensemble size vs. latency*: Larger subsets increase diversity but linearly increase memory/time; pruning aims to keep only high‑value models, but GA may drop a model useful under distribution shift if validation set is narrow.  
  - *Fusion model capacity*: MLP is simple/efficient but limited to fixed‑choice tasks; LED handles arbitrary answers but needs longer context and more training data; sliding‑window attention mitigates complexity but adds overhead.  
  - *Threshold selection*: The GMM approach assumes bimodal uncertainty; if test uncertainties are unimodal, the threshold may be too permissive or strict.  
  - *Static vs. dynamic diversity*: Focal metrics computed offline may become outdated if test distribution drifts; online re‑evaluation not supported.  
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Very low Focal‑CKA/Focal‑Diversity across many subsets → all models behave similarly, limiting fusion gains.  
  - Large discrepancy between validation and test uncertainty distributions → poor threshold calibration, high false‑accept/false‑reject rates.  
  - Overfitting of the fusion model (especially LED) when training data is limited → poor performance on new queries or unseen VLM combinations.  
  - GA fails to improve fitness over many generations → may need to adjust population size, mutation rate, or fitness weighting.  
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Core result replication: Run V3Fusion on MMMU, MMMU‑Pro, A‑OKVQA, OCR‑VQA with the provided model pool; reproduce Table 2, confirming gains over best single VLM and baselines (LLM‑Blender, RouteLLM, MAD).  
  2. Ablation study: Disable pruning (use full pool) and disable fusion (replace MLP/LED with plurality voting) separately; reproduce Table 5 to show each stage’s contribution and full pipeline’s highest gain.  
  3. Diversity‑performance correlation: For a small pool (N=6), enumerate all subsets, compute Focal‑CKA/Focal‑Diversity, plot against subset accuracy; replicate Figure 7 to verify positive correlation and that GA‑selected subset lies near Pareto frontier.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **O1**: How does V3Fusion’s performance and efficiency scale when incorporating larger VLMs (>16B parameters) or closed‑source models like GPT‑4V? (Hardware constraints limited experiments to <16B open‑source models.)
- **O2**: Is Focal‑CKA the most effective metric for quantifying visual disagreement in VLM ensembles, or could alternative measures yield better pruning performance? (The paper does not compare with other visual diversity metrics.)
- **O3**: Can the adaptive epistemic uncertainty threshold be refined to improve rectification, especially for the rejected samples that currently require larger models? (The GMM assumption may not capture complex uncertainty distributions.)

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter details for the Genetic Algorithm and fusion model training are missing, which could affect reproducibility and optimal performance.
- Focal‑CKA depends on validation “negative episodes”; if all models fail on the same samples, diversity scores may not reflect true complementarity.
- The adaptive uncertainty threshold assumes a bimodal distribution; under dataset shift this may lead to mis‑calibrated rejection.
