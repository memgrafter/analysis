# Can Vision-Language Models Solve the Shell Game?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08436
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08436
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Standard VLMs fail at visual entity tracking due to reliance on static frame features; SGCoT fine-tuning achieves >90% accuracy on the synthetic VET-Bench.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with tasks requiring persistent tracking of visually identical objects across time, such as the shell game. The authors create VET-Bench, a synthetic benchmark where objects are indistinguishable, forcing models to rely on spatiotemporal continuity rather than visual shortcuts. They prove the underlying tracking problem is NC¹-complete for five or more objects, explaining why fixed-depth transformers fail without intermediate supervision. Their solution, Spatiotemporal Grounded Chain-of-Thought (SGCoT), fine-tunes Molmo2 to output explicit object trajectories as intermediate reasoning states, enabling high-accuracy tracking.

## Method Summary
VET-Bench generates videos of identical objects (e.g., cups) moving continuously with a minimum separation constraint, ensuring object correspondence between frames. The task is to report the final position of a tracked object after a series of swaps. Standard VLMs are evaluated via direct-answer prompting. SGCoT reformulates the problem by fine-tuning a VLM (Molmo2) to produce a structured intermediate output: a timestamped list of (x,y) coordinates for the target object. The model is trained on synthetic text-only trajectory data, with loss masked on the trajectory tokens and applied only to the final answer. This forces the model to leverage its pre-trained grounding ability to generate a coherent trajectory, then reason from it to the final answer.

## Key Results
- All evaluated VLMs (including large reasoning models) perform near random chance (~33% for 3-cup games) on VET-Bench when using direct-answer prompting.
- SGCoT fine-tuning of Molmo2 achieves >90% accuracy on VET-Bench.
- Error analysis shows failures in SGCoT arise from perceptual grounding errors (e.g., trajectory "jumps") rather than reasoning from the trajectory.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Static Frame Processing
Standard VLMs ingest video as independent frames without a mechanism to bind a single entity across time. They rely on per-frame features and aggregate statistics, which fail when objects are visually identical. The failure mode collapses only when visual shortcuts (e.g., unique appearances, transparency) are present.

### Mechanism 2: Theoretical Expressivity Limit
For k≥5 indistinguishable objects, the visual entity tracking problem (TRACKₖ) is NC¹-complete. Fixed-depth transformers (corresponding to TC⁰ circuit class) cannot efficiently solve NC¹-complete problems on arbitrary sequence lengths without intermediate states acting as working memory. Empirical loss stagnation during direct-answer training confirms this limitation.

### Mechanism 3: SGCoT's Grounded Intermediate States
SGCoT succeeds by transforming the problem into grounded reasoning: the model outputs a precise, discretized trajectory (`<tracks coords="timestamp object_idx x y;...">`) before the answer. The trajectory serves as an explicit intermediate state that encodes temporal coherence. Fine-tuning on synthetic text-only data aligns Molmo2's pre-trained tracking ability to the QA task without degrading perceptual grounding. The final answer is supervised, forcing the model to learn that a correct trajectory determines the answer.

## Foundational Learning

- **Spatiotemporal Continuity Constraint**  
  The condition `2d < Δ` (where `d` is max object speed per frame and `Δ` is minimum inter-object distance) guarantees a unique object correspondence between frames, making tracking well-posed.  
  *Quick check:* Three cups moving at 5 pixels/frame with 20px separation require >1 FPS to satisfy `2*5=10 < 20`; any frame rate works as long as motion per frame ≤10px.

- **NC¹-Completeness for Entity Tracking**  
  TRACKₖ for k≥5 is NC¹-complete via reduction from the WORD problem for S₅, meaning it requires inherently sequential computation beyond fixed-depth transformers.  
  *Quick check:* For k=2, the problem reduces to parity (TC⁰), but models may still struggle due to lack of low-order statistical correlations in the label.

- **Grounded vs. Descriptive Chain-of-Thought**  
  SGCoT uses fine-grained, unambiguous states (discrete timestamps, absolute coordinates) rather than coarse natural language descriptions, which accumulate temporal misalignment errors.  
  *Quick check:* A description like "between 1 and 2 seconds, the left cup swaps" is ambiguous; "at 1.5s, left cup at (200,500)" defines a single verifiable snapshot.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Video frames → Vision Encoder (SigLIP backbone, frozen) → Visual tokens → Projection → LLM backbone (Molmo2, fine-tuned via QLoRA) → Structured output generation (`<tracks>` block followed by "Answer: X") → Token prediction head.

**Critical path:**  
The model must generate a temporally dense and spatially precise trajectory in `<tracks>` that, if extracted, uniquely determines the final answer. The loss is masked on trajectory tokens, so the model must rely on its pre-trained grounding to produce a useful trajectory.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Trajectory density vs. efficiency:* More timestamps improve precision but increase sequence length; SGCoT uses 0.5s intervals.  
- *Precision vs. generalization:* Normalized coordinates (0-1000) risk overfitting but match synthetic data distribution.  
- *Supervision scope:* Masking trajectory loss preserves perceptual grounding but may fail if the base model's tracking ability is weak.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Trajectory jumps:* Sudden coordinate changes between adjacent timestamps.  
- *Trajectory flatlines:* Long sequences of identical coordinates during swaps.  
- *Answer mismatch:* Final answer does not match terminal coordinate in `<tracks>`.  
- *Malformed output:* Missing `<tracks>` block, reverting to direct answer.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Establish baseline: Evaluate Molmo2 with direct-answer prompting on VET-Bench; expect ~33% random accuracy.  
2. Test zero-shot SGCoT: Prompt Molmo2 to generate `<tracks>` then answer; measure accuracy when answer is derived *from the terminal coordinate* (not the stated answer) to check if the model can produce a correct trajectory.  
3. Ablate SGCoT training: Compare masked loss (trajectory tokens ignored) vs. full supervision on both trajectory and answer; assess impact on accuracy and trajectory smoothness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. Are fixed-depth transformer VLMs fundamentally incapable of solving general visual entity tracking (k≥5 indistinguishable objects) without intermediate supervision, regardless of scale or size?  
2. Can SGCoT transfer to VLMs lacking inherent tracking ability, and what minimal supervision is needed?  
3. For which real-world video complexities (occlusions, blur, cluttered backgrounds) does SGCoT remain robust, and what are the failure modes?  
4. Does NC¹-completeness imply all practical video QA tasks requiring multi-step temporal reasoning are intractable for fixed-depth transformers without CoT, or can some admit efficient shortcuts?  
5. Can SGCoT's spatiotemporal grounding be replaced by coarser natural language descriptions without significant accuracy loss, and what does this imply about necessary intermediate state granularity?

## Limitations
- VET-Bench is synthetic; real videos with noise, occlusion, and distractors may degrade SGCoT performance.
- Results are limited to Molmo2; generalization to other VLMs is unproven.
- Theoretical NC¹ result assumes idealized constant-depth transformers; practical models have bounded depth and architectural biases.
- SGCoT relies on pre-trained grounding; trajectory errors ("jumps") indicate perceptual failures within the CoT.

## Confidence
- **Failure mechanism (static frames):** Medium – supported by internal error analysis but no direct causality test.  
- **Expressivity limit (NC¹):** Medium – proof is solid but assumptions may not hold for practical VLMs; empirical loss stagnation supports it.  
- **SGCoT efficacy for Molmo2 on VET-Bench:** High – achieved >90% accuracy in experiments.  
- **SGCoT generalization to other VLMs:** Low – not tested in the paper.

## Next Checks
1. Test SGCoT on real-world tracking datasets (e.g., MOT17) to assess robustness under occlusion and appearance variance.
2. Apply the SGCoT pipeline (synthetic text-only fine-tuning) to other video VLMs (e.g., Qwen-VL, GPT-4o) and measure VET-Bench accuracy.
3. Ablate trajectory timestamp frequency (per frame, 0.5s, 1s) to determine minimal density needed for high accuracy and its computational cost.
