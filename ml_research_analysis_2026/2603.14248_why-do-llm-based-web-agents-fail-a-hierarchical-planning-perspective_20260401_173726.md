# Why Do LLM-based Web Agents Fail? A Hierarchical Planning Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14248  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14248  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Low-level execution is the main bottleneck; structured planning (PDDL) improves alignment; one round of replanning yields large performance gains.

## Executive Summary
LLM-based web agents often fail on complex, long-horizon tasks, and coarse end-to-end success metrics do not reveal where breakdowns occur. This paper introduces a hierarchical planning framework to dissect agent performance into three layers: high-level planning, low-level execution, and replanning. The authors extend the Mind2Web-Live benchmark with human-annotated high-level plans and compare two planning representations—natural language (NL) and structured PDDL—across three LLMs. They find that PDDL produces more aligned and compact plans, but low-level execution remains the dominant failure source: even with perfect human plans, gpt-5-nano achieves only ~38% subgoal completion and ~36% task success. A single environment-triggered replanning round substantially improves outcomes, highlighting the importance of feedback-driven plan revision. The study advocates for separating planning from execution, improving perceptual grounding, and adopting process-based evaluation.

## Method Summary
The evaluation pipeline consists of: (1) a high-level planner that generates a sequence of subgoals in either NL or PDDL; (2) a low-level executor that translates each subgoal into concrete UI actions (clicks, typing, navigation) using DOM observations; (3) a postcondition checker (LLM-as-judge) that assesses whether the subgoal’s intended effects were achieved; and (4) a replanner that revises the plan upon subgoal failure. The authors use an extended Mind2Web-Live dataset (104 tasks) with human-validated high-level plans derived from key navigation nodes. Three LLMs are tested (gpt-5-nano, claude-haiku-4.5, gemini-flash-2.5) under different action spaces (expanded vs. primitive). Metrics include plan alignment (Perfect Match, Missing, Unmatched), subgoal/task completion rates, and fine-grained failure modes (redundant actions, hallucinated links, out-of-domain navigation). Ablations compare performance with and without a single replanning round.

## Key Results
- PDDL yields higher plan alignment (67.7% perfect match) and more compact plans than NL (60.6% perfect match).
- Low-level execution is the primary bottleneck: with human-provided plans, gpt-5-nano reaches only 38.5% subgoal completion and 36.4% task success.
- One replanning round significantly improves performance; PDDL subgoal completion jumps from 70.3% to 93.3% after replanning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Structured representations (PDDL) produce high-level plans that are better aligned with human intentions and more compact than natural language.
- **Mechanism:** PDDL’s formal syntax and explicit preconditions/effects impose symbolic constraints that reduce verbosity, irrelevant elaboration, and overspecification, yielding plans that match human abstractions more closely.
- **Core assumption:** Enforcing structured output mitigates the LLM’s tendency to generate overly detailed or hallucinated steps in free-form text.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Alignment metrics: PDDL perfect match 67.7% vs NL 60.6%; missing steps 2.2% vs 4.2%; unmatched 15.4% vs 29.4% (Section 5.1).
  - PDDL domain generation explicitly defines action preconditions and effects, ruling out unknown inputs/links (Section 4.3).
- **Break condition:** Tasks requiring flexible, context-rich descriptions that exceed PDDL’s rigid templates without extensive engineering.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Low-level execution is the dominant performance bottleneck, not high-level planning quality.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs fail to ground abstract subgoals into precise UI interactions due to perceptual grounding errors, poor state tracking, and inadequate world models.
- **Core assumption:** The gap between symbolic subgoals and concrete DOM actions is larger than the planning capability gap; current LLMs lack fine-grained perceptual and control skills.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - With human-authored plans, subgoal completion is only 38.5% and task success 36.4% (abstract).
  - Failure breakdown: 34.2% of actions are redundant; 32% of `goto` actions lead to hallucinated links; 10.4% repetition failures; 16.7% out-of-domain navigation (Section 5.2).
- **Break condition:** If perceptual grounding (e.g., multimodal inputs, refined accessibility trees) or training improves markedly, execution may cease to be the bottleneck.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A single round of environment-triggered replanning substantially improves subgoal completion and task success.
- **Mechanism:** Replanning uses execution feedback (e.g., failed postconditions) to revise the high-level plan, either by continuing from the last successful state or generating a new plan, leading to more executable and context-aware plans.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can interpret execution failures and produce revised plans that better align with actual website dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - After replanning, PDDL subgoal completion rises from 70.3% to 93.3% (abstract, Section 5.3).
  - Figure 3 shows consistent gains across NL and PDDL; NL alignment slightly degrades but success improves.
- **Break condition:** If replanning causes catastrophic drift (e.g., NL alignment drops) or environments are too stochastic; multiple rounds may be needed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hierarchical Task Network (HTN) Planning**  
  *Why needed:* The paper’s decomposition of tasks into high-level subgoals and low-level actions mirrors HTN’s abstraction hierarchy, providing a principled separation of planning and execution.  
  *Quick check:* How does HTN differ from classical (STRIPS) planning in terms of action decomposition and reuse?

- **Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL)**  
  *Why needed:* PDDL is the structured representation contrasted with NL; its action definition components directly constrain LLM plan generation in the experiments.  
  *Quick check:* What are the key components of a PDDL action definition, and how do they restrict an LLM’s output?

- **Perceptual Grounding**  
  *Why needed:* Low-level failures (hallucinated links, redundant clicks) stem from poor mapping between symbolic subgoals and DOM elements; grounding bridges abstract plans to concrete UI interactions.  
  *Quick check:* What causes a “hallucinated link” failure, and how might improved grounding prevent it?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
High-level Planner -> Low-level Executor -> Postcondition Checker -> (on failure) Replanner -> (revised plan) -> back to Planner/Executor. Action Spaces (Expanded/Primitive) feed into Executor.

**Critical path:**  
1. User task → High-level Planner generates subgoal sequence.  
2. For each subgoal: Low-level Executor produces actions until postcondition checker returns YES or max steps.  
3. If subgoal fails → Replanner invoked → new plan (full/partial) → return to step 2.  
4. All subgoals completed? → End-to-end Evaluator judges task success.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- NL vs. PDDL: NL flexible but underspecified/overspecified; PDDL concise but requires domain engineering and may lack expressiveness.  
- Expanded vs. Primitive action spaces: Expanded reduces invalid actions but risks off-domain navigation; Primitive (Action ID) reduces hallucination but forces arbitrary choices when uncertain.  
- Single vs. multiple replanning rounds: One round gives gains with minimal complexity; more rounds may compound errors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High-level: High unmatched/decomposed rates vs. human plan; overly long plans.  
- Low-level: Redundancy rate >30%, `goto` hallucination >30%, out-of-domain navigation >15%.  
- Replanning: Alignment degrades without success gains, or minimal improvement (e.g., gemini-flash-2.5).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **High-level alignment comparison:** Generate NL and PDDL plans; compute alignment metrics (Perfect Match, Missing, Unmatched) against human reference. Expect PDDL superior.  
2. **Execution with human plans:** Supply human-authored plans to executor; measure subgoal completion, plan completion, task success. Expect low rates (~38.5% success).  
3. **Replanning efficacy:** Run full pipeline with and without one replanning round; compare subgoal completion and success. Expect significant improvement (e.g., PDDL subgoal completion from 70.3% to 93.3%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Do structured planning representations like PDDL consistently improve alignment of LLM-generated high-level plans with human intentions and enhance executability across a wider range of web tasks and domains?**  
   *Basis:* Paper explicitly compares NL vs PDDL (RQ2, RQ3) and finds PDDL advantages on Mind2Web-Live.  
   *Why unresolved:* Evaluation limited to one benchmark and three LLMs; generalizability unclear.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Extensive cross-benchmark experiments (e.g., WebArena, Mind2Web) with diverse LLMs and formalisms.

2. **How can perceptual grounding in LLM-based web agents be significantly improved to reduce failures such as redundant actions, hallucinated links, and navigation outside the target domain?**  
   *Basis:* Identified as primary failure mode (Section 5.2); Recommendation 2 calls for better grounding.  
   *Why unresolved:* No effective solutions proposed; current DOM representations insufficient.  
   *Resolving evidence:* New grounding techniques (multimodal inputs, improved element descriptions, explicit state tracking) that demonstrably lower failure rates.

3. **Should action spaces be modified to allow low-level executors to express uncertainty or request replanning, rather than forcing a concrete UI action at every step, and would this improve overall task success on long-horizon web tasks?**  
   *Basis:* Recommendation 3 argues for uncertainty-aware action spaces; paper uses fixed action spaces forcing concrete actions.  
   *Why unresolved:* No method for explicit uncertainty expression tested; uncertainty currently leads to random errors.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Integrate an “uncertainty” or “abstain” action and compare success rates across baselines.

4. **How can the reliance on human-annotated reference high-level plans for evaluation be reduced, enabling scalable, automated process-based assessment of web agents' planning capabilities?**  
   *Basis:* Limitations state dependence on expert annotations; need scalable, automated assessment.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current framework requires human key-node annotations per task, which does not scale.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Develop automatic/semi-automatic reference plan generation (e.g., LLM with verification) that correlates with human judgments.

## Limitations
- **Human plan availability:** Human-annotated high-level plans for Mind2Web-Live are not confirmed public, preventing exact replication of alignment metrics.
- **Model specifics:** Undisclosed details for “gpt-5-nano” API, sampling temperatures, and hyperparameters hinder full reproducibility.
- **Postcondition checker ambiguity:** Exact state representation (DOM abstraction) used to judge subgoal completion is underspecified, affecting failure diagnostics.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (PDDL vs NL alignment):** High – backed by clear metrics and compactness evidence.  
- **Mechanism 2 (Execution bottleneck):** High – supported by human-plan results and detailed failure breakdown.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Replanning gains):** Medium – improvements shown but NL alignment degrades and single-round limits noted.

## Next Checks
1. **Verify human plan accessibility:** Inspect the Mind2Web-Live repository for release of expert-annotated subgoal sequences; if unavailable, regenerate using the paper’s prompt protocol and compute variance against published numbers.
2. **Test replanning robustness:** Run multi-round replanning (2–3 iterations) for both NL and PDDL; check if gains plateau or alignment further erodes.
3. **Probe execution bottleneck source:** Ablate DOM input fidelity (e.g., raw HTML vs simplified accessibility tree) while holding human plans fixed; a significant success increase would confirm grounding as the primary bottleneck.
