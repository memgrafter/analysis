# LIDS: LLM Summary Inference Under the Layered Lens

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00105  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00105  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LIDS distinguishes high-quality GPT-5 summaries from benchmarks (scores ~0.95 vs ≤0.87, no overlap), correlates with human evaluations (Pearson 0.904), and is faster than BERTScore (25.5s vs 158.5s for 50 summaries).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LIDS, a novel framework for evaluating summaries generated by large language models. LIDS integrates BERT embeddings with singular value decomposition (SVD) to compute a latent direction similarity metric, and employs SOFARI to extract theme-specific keywords with false discovery rate (FDR) control. This combination aims to provide both a robust evaluation score and interpretable insights into summary content.

Empirical results demonstrate that LIDS effectively separates high-quality GPT-5 summaries from lower-quality baselines, showing no overlap in scores. The metric correlates strongly (Pearson 0.904) with human judgments, and outperforms BERTScore in computational efficiency. LIDS also exhibits robustness across multiple domains including news, NASA articles, legal documents, and novel chapters.

## Method Summary
LIDS processes a reference text and one or more summaries through a defined pipeline. First, BERT token embeddings are extracted for each text, forming a matrix. SVD is applied to this matrix to obtain singular values and left/right singular vectors, which are sign-resolved for consistency. For each possible number of layers \(k\), a layered direction vector \(d(k)\) is constructed by weighting token embeddings with singular values and left singular vectors. The LIDS score (MACS) for a summary is the maximum absolute cosine similarity between its \(d(k)\) and the reference’s \(d(k)\), selecting the optimal \(k\). The summary embedding \(d(k^*)\) representing the best alignment is retained.

For interpretability, SOFARI provides p‑values for components of the left singular vectors in the top \(k^*\) layers. Applying the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure at a chosen FDR level yields sets of significant keywords per layer, which can be visualized as word clouds. This dual output—a quantitative score and qualitative keyword layers—enables both evaluation and diagnosis of summary fidelity.

## Key Results
- Clear separation: GPT‑5 summaries achieve mean LIDS ~0.95, while naive and random‑topic benchmarks score ≤0.87, with no overlap.
- Strong human correlation: Pearson correlation coefficient \(r = 0.904\) between LIDS scores and human evaluations.
- Efficiency gain: LIDS computes similarity for 50 summaries in 25.5 seconds vs. 158.5 seconds for BERTScore.
- Domain robustness: The method performs consistently across news, NASA, legal, and novel domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Latent SVD‑based Direction Metric via BERT Embeddings
- **Claim:** LIDS computes similarity by aligning low‑dimensional thematic structures from SVD layers, using weighted BERT token embeddings.
- **Mechanism:** For each text, BERT embeddings → SVD decomposition → directional vectors \(d(k)\) constructed by weighting token embeddings with singular values and signed left singular vectors. The LIDS score \(MACS_j\) selects the layer \(k\) that maximizes absolute cosine similarity between paired layers of reference and summary.
- **Core assumption:** Leading SVD layers capture semantically coherent themes; a good summary preserves these themes in aligned layers despite brevity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states use of latent SVD‑based direction metric; Section 2.1 details construction and layered theme concept.
- **Break condition:** If summary themes are orthogonal to reference (e.g., random topic), \(MACS_j\) remains low even if grammatically fluent.

### Mechanism 2: Statistical Uncertainty Quantification via Repeated Prompts
- **Claim:** Repeated LLM prompts yield an empirical distribution of LIDS scores, quantifying summary stability.
- **Mechanism:** The same prompt is issued multiple times (e.g., 50) to the LLM; variance of resulting \(MACS_j\) values measures uncertainty in summary quality.
- **Core assumption:** LLM outputs are non‑deterministic; repeated sampling reveals distribution of faithfulness.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 describes repeating prompts 50 times; Section 4.5 uses Sharpe ratio (mean/SD) to compare LLM robustness.
- **Break condition:** Deterministic LLM (temperature = 0) yields point estimates, no distribution.

### Mechanism 3: FDR‑Controlled Keyword Selection via SOFARI
- **Claim:** SOFARI provides p‑values for left singular vector components; Benjamini–Hochberg controls false discoveries when extracting theme‑specific keywords per SVD layer.
- **Mechanism:** For each SVD layer \(l\), SOFARI debiases left singular vector estimates → z‑scores → p‑values → BH selects keywords at FDR \(q\). Visualizations (word clouds) show layered themes.
- **Core assumption:** Significant left singular vector components correspond to tokens defining the latent theme; noise components will not survive FDR control.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3 describes SOFARI and BH procedure; Section 4.4 shows word cloud plots with coherent theme progression.
- **Break condition:** If SVD layers are not sparse or SOFARI assumptions violated, p‑values may be invalid, leading to spurious keywords.

## Foundational Learning
- **BERT Token Embeddings**  
  *Why needed here:* LIDS operates on contextual embeddings that capture token meaning, order, and polysemy; naive word counts fail on semantic similarity.  
  *Quick check:* Why might the embedding magnitude of a token like “bank” differ from “the”? (Answer: Context‑specific tokens have larger norms after averaging across diverse contexts.)

- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**  
  *Why needed here:* SVD decomposes the embedding matrix into orthogonal thematic directions; singular values indicate theme importance.  
  *Quick check:* How does weighting by singular values (\(\lambda\)) in \(d(k)\) differ from uniform averaging? (Answer: Larger \(\lambda\) amplifies dominant themes, filtering noise from lower‑ranked layers.)

- **False Discovery Rate (FDR) Control**  
  *Why needed here:* With many tokens per layer, multiple testing occurs; Benjamini–Hochberg procedure limits expected proportion of false keywords among selected ones.  
  *Quick check:* If you select 10 keywords at \(q=0.05\), what is the expected number of false positives? (Answer: Up to 0.5 on average.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input → BERT encoder → SVD module → Direction calculator → SOFARI interface → BH selector

**Critical path:**  
Reference/summary text → BERT → SVD → layered direction vectors \(d(k)\) → cosine similarity maximization → SOFARI p‑values → Benjamini–Hochberg → keyword sets

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Regular SVD (fast, dense) vs. sparse SVD (slower, more interpretable keywords)  
- FDR level \(q\) controls balance between keyword quantity and certainty

**Failure signatures:**  
- \(MACS_j\) near 0 for all summaries: likely SVD layer misalignment; check BERT model version or token filtering.  
- Identical keyword sets across all layers: SVD not separating themes; increase \(k\) or regularize SVD.  
- Extremely few/no keywords selected: FDR too strict or SOFARI not converging; relax \(q\) or check SOFARI tuning.  

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity check:** Compute \(MACS_j\) between identical texts (should ≈ 1) and between random texts (should ≈ 0).  
2. **Layer validation:** For a thematic text, inspect top 3 SVD layer keyword sets without FDR; expect coarse thematic separation.  
3. **FDR calibration:** Run SOFARI on a synthetic matrix with known signal; verify selected keywords match true signal at nominal FDR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** Can the LIDS framework be effectively extended to incorporate more general embedding models (e.g., time‑series adapted BERT) or graph neural network architectures leveraging text knowledge graphs?  
*Basis:* Explicit (Section 5).  
*Why unresolved:* Current implementation is built specifically on standard BERT token embeddings and does not explore alternative embedding spaces or structural graph‑based representations.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Empirical evaluations comparing LIDS performance (accuracy, efficiency, interpretability) when using alternative foundational models on diverse summarization tasks, alongside theoretical analysis of embedding‑SVD interactions.

**Open Question 2:** How can one optimally determine the number of latent SVD layers (\(k\)) used in the LIDS direction metric for a given summary task, beyond the current maximization over a range?  
*Basis:* Inferred (the heuristic of maximizing absolute cosine similarity lacks universal justification).  
*Why unresolved:* No theoretical or empirical guidance is provided on whether this maximization is optimal; alternative selection criteria (e.g., based on explained variance, stability) remain unexplored.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Systematic studies analyzing the distribution of optimal \(k\) across diverse texts, summary qualities, and LLMs; comparison of LIDS scores and human correlation under different \(k\) selection rules; possibly a cross‑validated or information‑criterion‑based method.

**Open Question 3:** To what extent do subword tokenization and subsequent recombination in BERT affect the fidelity and interpretability of LIDS visualizations, particularly for languages with rich morphology or different writing systems?  
*Basis:* Inferred (Section 4.4 acknowledges tokenization artifacts but only tests English).  
*Why unresolved:* The methodology for handling subword tokens is heuristic and not formally validated; impact on SOFARI p‑values and FDR‑controlled keyword selection is unassessed.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Experiments applying LIDS to multilingual or morphologically complex texts (e.g., Finnish, Arabic, Chinese), evaluating consistency of keyword extraction, correspondence to human‑identified themes, and sensitivity to tokenization choices.

**Open Question 4:** How robust and generalizable is LIDS across a broader spectrum of text domains, genres, and languages, particularly for highly structured or creative texts?  
*Basis:* Inferred (only four English, non‑dialogue, formal domains tested).  
*Why unresolved:* Limited domain scope leaves open whether LIDS’s layered SVD approach generalizes to texts with different statistical properties (e.g., high repetition in technical manuals, nonlinear narratives in novels).  
*What evidence would resolve:* Comprehensive benchmarking across dozens of domains and languages, including social media, scientific papers, scripts, and literary works, with domain‑tailored evaluation criteria beyond human scores.

**Open Question 5:** Can LIDS be effectively used for ensemble or interactive summarization, such as aggregating summaries from multiple LLMs or guiding LLM generation iteratively based on LIDS feedback?  
*Basis:* Explicit (Section 5 proposes this future direction).  
*Why unresolved:* LIDS is currently a post‑hoc evaluative metric; its potential as an interactive or generative tool remains untested.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Implementation of interactive systems where LIDS scores or keyword clouds are fed back to an LLM to improve summaries; studies measuring whether such guided summarization yields higher human‑rated quality or better alignment with reference texts.

## Limitations
- The method for handling texts exceeding BERT’s 512‑token limit (e.g., truncation, sliding window) is not described, which is essential for reproducing results on long documents.
- SOFARI, the tool used for FDR‑controlled keyword extraction, is not publicly available; its algorithmic details cannot be independently verified.
- The specific reference texts and GPT‑5 summaries used in the experiments are not provided, preventing direct replication of the reported domain results.
