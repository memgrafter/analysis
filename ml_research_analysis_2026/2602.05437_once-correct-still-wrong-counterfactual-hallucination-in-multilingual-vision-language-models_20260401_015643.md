# Once Correct, Still Wrong: Counterfactual Hallucination in Multilingual Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05437
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05437
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CFHR rises sharply in Arabic dialects while true-statement accuracy remains high, exposing a conditional hallucination failure masked by standard metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a failure mode in multilingual vision-language models (VLMs) where models accept visually incorrect but culturally plausible statements even after correctly identifying the true visual fact. To measure this, the authors introduce M2CQA, a benchmark with images from 17 MENA countries and contrastive true/counterfactual statements in English, Modern Standard Arabic, and two Arabic dialects. They propose the CounterFactual Hallucination Rate (CFHR), which measures counterfactual acceptance only when the model first succeeds on the true statement. Results show CFHR increases dramatically in dialectal Arabic, that "Answer then Reason" prompting mitigates the issue while "Reasoning First" exacerbates it, and that scaling helps but Arabic-focused models show divergent patterns.

## Method Summary
The core method is the M2CQA benchmark, which pairs each image with a true statement (Q+) and two culturally plausible counterfactual statements (Q−) across four languages. Evaluation uses three prompt templates: direct binary judgment, "Answer then Reason," and "Reasoning First." The primary metric is CFHR, defined as the proportion of samples where the model correctly answers Q+ but fails to reject at least one Q−. This conditional metric isolates hallucination from general knowledge failure. Models are evaluated on specific VLMs (Qwen3-VL, Gemma-3-VL, Fanar, AIN) with outputs parsed for binary decisions, and scores are aggregated per language and prompt.

## Key Results
- CFHR increases sharply in Arabic dialects (Egyptian, Levantine) while Q+ accuracy remains relatively high, revealing a dialect-specific vulnerability.
- Prompting order significantly affects CFHR: "Reasoning First" increases hallucination compared to "Answer then Reason," especially in Arabic.
- Model scaling generally lowers CFHR, but Arabic-focused models diverge: AIN shows high CFHR despite high Q+ accuracy, while Fanar shows lower CFHR but reduced Q+ accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CFHR isolates conditional counterfactual hallucination by measuring false acceptance only when the model already succeeded on the true statement (Q+).
- **Mechanism:** By computing `CFHR = (Acc(Q+) - Acc(combined)) / Acc(Q+)`, the metric discards cases where Q+ failure might confound counterfactual acceptance due to lack of knowledge. It focuses on samples where visual grounding of the true statement was successful, thus revealing failure to maintain grounding against culturally familiar distractors.
- **Break condition:** If a model never achieves high Q+ accuracy, CFHR becomes unstable and may not isolate hallucination from poor knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Prompting order significantly modulates CFHR; committing to an answer before justification ("Answer then Reason") reduces counterfactual hallucination compared to eliciting reasoning first ("Reasoning First").
- **Mechanism:** "Answer then Reason" anchors the initial decision to an image-grounded perception, with subsequent evidence generation rationalizing that perception. "Reasoning First" encourages exploration of alternative interpretations before commitment, allowing culturally informed priors to override correct visual perception.
- **Break condition:** If the model's internal reasoning process is not sequentially influenced by output format, this effect would not manifest.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Linguistic uncertainty in lower-resource dialects amplifies counterfactual hallucination rates (CFHR), even when true-statement accuracy (Q+) remains high.
- **Mechanism:** Dialectal Arabic, being underrepresented, creates greater linguistic ambiguity and weaker language-visual grounding. This increases reliance on culturally plausible priors when faced with a visually discriminable but culturally ambiguous scene.
- **Break condition:** If dialectal queries were simpler or cultural priors less varied, this amplification might not occur.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Evaluation Design**
  - **Why needed:** Evaluating a model's ability to *reject* a culturally plausible but visually incorrect statement is more diagnostic of visual grounding than just evaluating acceptance of the correct statement, as it tests robustness against plausible distractors.
  - **Quick check:** Why is rejecting a plausible falsehood harder than accepting a truth for measuring visual grounding?
- **Concept: Conditional Metric (CFHR)**
  - **Why needed:** CFHR as a conditional probability (hallucination *given* Q+ success) isolates a specific failure mode that unconditional accuracy can mask.
  - **Quick check:** If Model A has Q+ = 0.90 and Acc(combined) = 0.70, and Model B has Q+ = 0.70 and Acc(combined) = 0.68, which model shows a more severe *conditional* hallucination problem? (Calculate CFHR: A = (0.90-0.70)/0.90=0.22, B = (0.70-0.68)/0.70=0.03 → A is worse.)
- **Concept: Culturally Grounded Benchmarking**
  - **Why needed:** Hallucination benchmarks must cover diverse cultural contexts to avoid Western-centric blind spots; M2CQA uses MENA imagery and dialectal Arabic to surface culture-specific failures.
  - **Quick check:** How might a counterfactual involving a region-specific landmark (e.g., "The historical city is Saida") be a better diagnostic than one about a globally known site (e.g., "The mosque is located in Mecca") for a model trained on Western datasets?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Curation Pipeline -> Metric Computation Engine -> Evaluation Harness

**Critical path:** Clean dataset (M2CQA) -> Model inference with structured prompts -> Parse binary decisions -> Compute Q+ & Q− accuracies -> Calculate Acc(combined) and CFHR.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Binary vs. Graded Judgment:** Binary T/F provides clean contrast but misses model uncertainty or partial support.
- **Translation Quality:** Using GPT-4.1 for dialects is scalable but risks subtle meaning shifts affecting counterfactual plausibility.
- **Counterfactual Plausibility:** Generated from MCQ distractors ensures cultural plausibility but may not capture all real-world confusions.

**Failure signatures:**
- **High CFHR + High Q+:** Model knows the true fact but is easily swayed by cultural priors (e.g., AIN in dialects).
- **Dialectal CFHR Spike:** CFHR jumps from MSA to dialects while Q+ stays high.
- **Reasoning-First CFHR Increase:** CFHR(C) >> CFHR(B).
- **Low CFHR + Low Q+:** Model rejects both Q+ and Q− conservatively (e.g., Fanar in dialects).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **CFHR Calculation Sanity Check:** On a hand-checked subset (n=100), manually compute Acc(Q+), Acc(combined), and CFHR. Verify automated parser matches human judgment.
2. **Dialect Impact Isolation:** Evaluate Qwen3-VL-32B on M2CQA in EN, MSA, and Egyptian Arabic under Prompt A only. Hypothesize: CFHR(ARZ) > CFHR(MSA) > CFHR(EN) with minimal Q+ degradation.
3. **Prompting Strategy A/B Test:** For AIN and Qwen3-VL-32B, run evaluation under Prompt B and Prompt C. Compute ΔCFHR = CFHR(C) - CFHR(B); expect positive Δ across models/languages, especially Arabic.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What training or inference-time methods can effectively reduce counterfactual hallucination as measured by CFHR?
- Does the CFHR metric generalize to open-ended generation tasks (e.g., image captioning, visual dialogue) beyond discriminative true/false QA?
- How do underrepresented Arabic dialects (e.g., Gulf, Maghrebi, Iraqi) affect counterfactual hallucination rates compared to Egyptian and Levantine Arabic?
- Does the binary true/false judgment format in M2CQA mask graded visual uncertainty, and would a more nuanced assessment alter model rankings on CFHR?

## Limitations
- Dataset release timing and format are unspecified; exact image/statement mappings are inaccessible until public release.
- Model checkpoints and inference parameters (temperature, max tokens) for Arabic-focused models (Fanar, AIN) are unclear.
- CFHR interpretation is unstable when Q+ accuracy is low (<0.70), conflating poor grounding with conditional hallucination.
- Output format non-compliance in dialectal Arabic may skew parsing; GPT-4.1–generated counterfactuals/translations may introduce subtle cultural/linguistic mismatches.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (CFHR isolation design):** High confidence. The conditional metric is clearly defined, novel, and mathematically sound if Q+ accuracy is sufficient.
- **Mechanism 2 (Prompting order effect):** Medium confidence. Empirical trends are consistent, but direct corpus evidence is weak; effect may hinge on model-specific reasoning dynamics.
- **Mechanism 3 (Dialect amplification):** High confidence. Strong alignment with prior work on multilingual hallucination; results show sharp CFHR rise in dialects despite stable Q+ accuracy.
