# Navigating Ideation Space: Decomposed Conceptual Representations for Positioning Scientific Ideas

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.08901
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08901
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A decomposed ideation space with dimension-specific contrastive learning improves retrieval Recall@30 by 16.7% and novelty assessment correlates 0.37 with expert judgments.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the conflation of distinct conceptual aspects in scientific literature retrieval and novelty assessment. It introduces the Ideation Space, which decomposes scientific knowledge into three orthogonal dimensions—research problem, methodology, and core findings—each learned via contrastive training on citation networks. This enables fine-grained similarity measurement and models ideation transitions between dimensions. The Hierarchical Sub-Space Retrieval framework achieves a Recall@30 of 0.329, and the Decomposed Novelty Assessment algorithm correlates 0.37 with expert judgments while identifying which specific aspects of an idea are novel.

## Method Summary
The method begins by extracting dimension-specific text (research problem, method, findings) from paper abstracts using a fixed LLM prompt. Citation relationships are mined to construct positive pairs; an LLM classifies each citation pair’s functional relationship (problem/method/findings) to assign papers to dimension-specific sub-graphs. Three contrastive encoders (initialized from SPECTER2) are fine-tuned separately on these sub-graphs using an InfoNCE loss with hard negative sampling based on cross-dimensional scores. Embeddings from each subspace are stored in FAISS indexes. Transition vectors (method−problem, findings−method) are computed and indexed. For a query paper, its three dimension embeddings are retrieved from respective indexes, and a novelty score is computed by constructing a reasoning graph, mapping nodes to subspaces, retrieving nearest neighbors per node, and aggregating similarity weighted by graph centrality (degree/betweenness).

## Key Results
- Hierarchical Sub-Space Retrieval achieves Recall@30 of 0.329, a 16.7% improvement over monolithic baselines like SPECTER2.
- Transition retrieval (using TPM/M2K vectors) reaches Hit Rate@30 of 0.643, outperforming concatenation baselines.
- Decomposed Novelty Assessment attains a 0.37 correlation with expert novelty judgments and can pinpoint novel dimensions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Decomposing scientific papers into three orthogonal dimensions (problem, method, findings) enables fine-grained similarity measurement and avoids conflation of conceptual aspects. The framework learns separate embedding spaces via contrastive training, where each space optimizes for functional similarity within one dimension. Positive pairs are selected based on citation relationships validated by an LLM classifier to ensure dimension-specific relevance. The core assumption is that citation context indicates which conceptual dimension primarily connects two papers, and this signal guides specialized encoder training. Evidence includes margin improvements >0.64 in Table 1, far exceeding base model margins ~0.1. If the LLM classifier systematically misassigns relationships or citation context does not reflect the primary connection, subspace training signals become corrupted.

### Mechanism 2
Contrastive training on dimension-specific citation sub-graphs produces embeddings where proximity reflects genuine conceptual similarity within that dimension, not just topical overlap. The InfoNCE loss uses hard negatives that are structurally independent but cross-dimensionally confusing (e.g., high problem-similarity but low method-similarity), forcing the encoder to focus on dimension-relevant features. The assumption is that citation-coupled, dimension-classified pairs provide reliable positives, and cross-dimensional scores effectively regularize. Evidence: Table 2 shows subspace retrievers often outperform monolithic baselines on node retrieval. If hard negative sampling is noisy or SPECTER2 weights are too rigid, encoders may not isolate dimension-specific signals.

### Mechanism 3
Modeling "ideation transitions" (vectors between subspace embeddings) captures reasoning patterns (how problem leads to method, method leads to findings), enabling retrieval of papers with analogous logical structures across topics. Transition vectors TPM(p) = E_method(p) – E_problem(p) and TMF(p) = E_findings(p) – E_method(p) are computed and stored. The assumption is that vector differences meaningfully represent the "intellectual bridge" within a paper and are recognizable across contexts. Evidence: Table 3 shows Transition Space achieves highest Recall@90 (0.275) and Hit Rate@90 (0.762). A case study surfaces pattern-similar but topically distant papers. If embeddings are not aligned (different scales/rotations) or reasoning patterns are non-linear, subtraction fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Learning:** The subspace encoder training relies on contrastive objectives to cluster dimensionally similar papers and separate dissimilar ones. Understanding positive/negative pair construction, temperature scaling, and hard negative mining is crucial. *Quick check:* Why would in-batch negatives alone be insufficient, and how do "hard conceptual negatives" address that?
- **Citation Network Semantics:** Training data’s positives and dimension labels derive from citation links and contextual analysis. One must understand how bibliographic coupling/co-citation indicate intellectual connection and how LLM classification assigns a functional dimension. *Quick check:* What is the risk of using LLM-classified citation context as ground truth, and how might it bias subspaces?
- **Graph-Based Importance Weighting (Betweenness Centrality):** The Decomposed Novelty Assessment weights node importance in the reasoning graph using degree and betweenness centrality to emphasize structurally central claims. *Quick check:* Why might a high-betweenness node (a bridging claim) be more critical for novelty assessment than a high-degree peripheral node?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input Processor -> Dimension Classifier (offline) -> Subspace Encoders (E_problem, E_method, E_findings) -> Vector Transition Modules (TPM, TMF) -> FAISS Databases (RP, MA, KF, P2M, M2K) -> Novelty Assessor (reasoning graph + centrality weighting). Critical path: Encoder training → Database construction → Query-time retrieval (node and transition) → Novelty scoring. Design tradeoffs: dimension independence vs. holistic understanding; LLM-based ground truth vs. scalability; transition vector simplicity vs. expressivity. Failure signatures: encoder margins remain low (~0.1) after fine-tuning; baselines match subspace retrievers; novelty scores show no correlation with humans; transition retrieval at random. First 3 experiments: 1) Embedding space sanity check (validate margin >0.6). 2) Node retrieval ablation (compare subspaces vs. monolithic). 3) Transition retrieval validation (hand-curated pattern-similar pairs).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to other domains:** Does the framework transfer beyond AI/ML to fields like biology or physics? Basis: [explicit] "exploring how well our decomposed ideation space transfers to other scientific disciplines... remains an interesting direction." Unresolved: different fields have distinct publication cultures and conceptual structures. Evidence needed: Apply pipeline to other corpora (e.g., PubMed) and measure against domain benchmarks.
- **Sufficiency of three dimensions:** Is the decomposition sufficient for interdisciplinary/theory-heavy work, or are finer-grained dimensions needed? Basis: [explicit] "certain interdisciplinary or theory-heavy works may exhibit more nuanced structures." Unresolved: Oversimplification may miss complex interactions. Evidence needed: Qualitative analysis of interdisciplinary papers; extend decomposition (e.g., add "theoretical framework") and evaluate.
- **LLM-assisted ground truth alignment:** How well do LLM-generated novelty-relevant citation labels align with human judgments across dimensions? Basis: [explicit] "our evaluation relies on citation relationships and LLM-assisted ground truth... may not capture all dimensions." Unresolved: LLM biases may cause noise/incompleteness. Evidence needed: Human annotation study comparing LLM vs. expert labels.
- **Subspace orthogonality:** Are the three subspaces orthogonal, or do significant correlations exist? Basis: [inferred] Training assumes functional orthogonality, but ideas often have interdependencies. Unresolved: Correlation might imply redundancy or overlap. Evidence needed: Analyze pairwise cosine correlations; test joint modeling enhancements.

## Limitations
- **LLM-as-ground-truth risk:** Training signal depends on an LLM classifier for dimension assignment, which may encode biases (e.g., conflating method/findings) and contaminate subspace separation.
- **Hard negative sampling validity:** The core regularization uses cross-dimensional scores to mine "hard conceptual negatives"; if these scores are noisy, the contrastive objective is weakened.
- **Transition vector assumption:** Linear subtraction (E_method − E_problem) assumes embeddings are perfectly aligned and intellectual leaps are linearly decomposable—a strong, unvalidated assumption.

## Confidence
- **High:** Decomposition + retrieval gains (Recall@30 +16.7% on clear baselines).
- **Medium:** Contrastive training mechanism (margin improvements support separation but causal link to dimension-specific learning is indirect).
- **Low:** Transition retrieval & novelty correlation (Hit Rate@90 promising but lacks ablation on "pattern" capture; 0.37 novelty correlation is modest and may reflect retrieval quality).

## Next Checks
1. **Subspace Ablation & Alignment Check:** For a held-out set, compute within-dimension vs. cross-dimension similarity distributions per encoder. Successful decomposition requires within-dimension similarities significantly higher. Also, test subspace alignment (isotropy/rotation) via Procrustes analysis.
2. **Transition Vector "Pattern" Validation:** Hand-curate 50 pairs of papers with analogous reasoning (e.g., "adversarial training for robustness") but topically distant. Evaluate whether transition retrieval surfaces these pattern-similar pairs more effectively than monolithic baselines or random transition vectors.
3. **LLM Classifier Sensitivity Analysis:** Re-train a subspace using a heuristic (e.g., dimension assignment via keyword overlap in citation context) instead of the LLM classifier. Compare resulting embedding margin (Table 1 metric) and retrieval performance. A large drop would confirm reliance on LLM classification quality.
