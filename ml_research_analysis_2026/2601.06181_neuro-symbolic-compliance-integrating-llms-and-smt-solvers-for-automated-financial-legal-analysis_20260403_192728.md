# Neuro-Symbolic Compliance: Integrating LLMs and SMT Solvers for Automated Financial Legal Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2601.06181
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06181
- References: 31
- Key result: 86.2% SMT generation accuracy; 100% compliance restoration accuracy; >100× reasoning speedup.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a neuro-symbolic framework that combines large language models (LLMs) with satisfiability modulo theories (SMT) solvers to automate compliance analysis in financial regulation. LLMs translate legal texts and case facts into SMT constraints, while SMT solvers verify legality and compute minimal factual corrections for violations. Evaluated on 87 real Taiwan Financial Supervisory Commission enforcement cases, the system achieved 86.2% accuracy in generating correct SMT code and perfect 100% accuracy in restoring compliance, with over 100× improvement in reasoning efficiency compared to LLM-only approaches.

The framework demonstrates how the flexibility of LLMs in interpreting natural language can be coupled with the precision of symbolic solvers to ensure logical consistency and efficient decision-making in high-stakes regulatory domains.

## Method Summary
The proposed system employs a multi-agent neuro-symbolic pipeline. Legal documents are first parsed into a structured corpus and indexed in a vector database. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) engine retrieves relevant statutory provisions using hybrid BM25 and embedding search, followed by reranking and LLM filtering. An LLM then translates the retrieved laws (as hard constraints) and case facts (as soft constraints) into SMT-LIB format. The Z3 solver checks satisfiability of hard constraints alone (to ensure legal consistency) and then with case facts plus `Penalty=False` to detect violations (UNSAT indicates non-compliance). To compute minimal corrections, a weighted MaxSMT optimization treats all soft constraints equally and flips the smallest set to satisfy hard constraints. The system orchestrates multiple agents: Rewriter (query expansion), Execute (solver runs), Coding (patch generation on errors), and Analysis (report generation).

## Key Results
- 86.2% accuracy in generating correct SMT constraints from legal texts and case facts.
- 100% accuracy in computing minimal factual corrections to restore legal compliance.
- Over 100× improvement in reasoning efficiency compared to pure LLM analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** An LLM can generate syntactically and semantically valid SMT constraints from unstructured legal texts and case facts with sufficient accuracy for downstream verification.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM is prompted (few-shot) to extract statutory provisions and case-specific facts, then translates them into propositional/arithmetic SMT formulas. A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) module supplements base articles with contextually relevant clauses to improve constraint completeness.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs possess adequate language-to-logic mapping capability for a constrained legal domain when guided by structured prompts and enriched legal context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "The LLM interprets statutes and enforcement cases to generate SMT constraints."
  - [section III-A4a]: Detailed process of building law and fact constraints with concrete examples from the Insurance Act.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence—none of the highly related neuro-symbolic papers identified in the corpus (e.g., "Delta1 with LLM") are cited or discussed in this work, so cross-study validation is absent.
- **Break condition:** If LLM-generated SMT code falls below a correctness threshold (e.g., <80% in pilot testing), the pipeline fails; error sources include omitted variables, logical inconsistencies, or misencoded regulatory conditions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** SMT solvers can reliably detect legal non-compliance (violations) by identifying unsatisfiable cores when case facts conflict with statutory constraints.
- **Mechanism:** Statutory provisions are encoded as hard constraints (H_law), case facts as additional constraints (H_case). The solver checks H_law ∪ H_case ∪ {Penalty=False} for satisfiability. An UNSAT result indicates a violation; the minimal unsat core pinpoints conflicting statutory clauses.
- **Core assumption:** Regulatory statutes can be formally modeled as a consistent set of logical constraints that capture necessary and sufficient conditions for compliance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "SMT solver verifies legality... when penalties arise."
  - [section III-B2]: Explicit formulation of the case illegality check (Eq. 1) and use of unsat cores for violation diagnosis.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence—no direct citation of foundational SMT-for-law works like Judson et al. [21] or Feng et al. [22] in this paper’s text, though they appear in Related Work.
- **Break condition:** If the statutory constraint base H_law is internally inconsistent (yields UNSAT alone), the model cannot distinguish case-driven violations from encoding errors; iterative refinement of legal encoding is required.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Weighted MaxSMT optimization can compute the minimal factual modification needed to restore legal compliance, aligning with the "minimal intervention" principle.
- **Mechanism:** Case-specific fact predicates are treated as soft constraints with uniform weights. The solver maximizes soft constraint satisfaction while all hard statutory constraints hold. The minimal set of violated soft constraints (Δ) defines the correction trace.
- **Core assumption:** The smallest set of fact changes that resolves statutory conflicts corresponds to the legally appropriate remediation, and this can be captured by cardinality minimization in the SMT model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Computes the minimal factual correction to restore legality."
  - [section III-B3]: Formal problem (Eqs. 2–3), Algorithm 3, and example (flipping "improvement plan executed").
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence—optimization-based compliance correction is not a common theme in the retrieved corpus; this paper’s approach is positioned as novel.
- **Break condition:** If multiple, equally minimal correction sets exist but have different legal implications (e.g., changing different factual variables), the system may produce ambiguous guidance without additional weighting or domain rules.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: SMT Solving & MaxSMT**
  - **Why needed here:** The core verification and optimization engine relies on SMT solvers (Z3) to check satisfiability and compute minimal soft-constraint relaxations. Understanding hard vs. soft constraints, unsat cores, and optimization modes is essential to interpret system outputs and debug failures.
  - **Quick check:** "If H_law ∪ H_case is UNSAT, what does the unsat core represent, and how would you use it to identify a specific statutory violation?"
- **Concept: Legal Constraint Formalization**
  - **Why needed here:** Translating natural language regulations into precise logical formulas (implications, thresholds, classifications) is the bridge between legal text and symbolic reasoning. Errors here propagate to all subsequent stages.
  - **Quick check:** "Given an insurance regulation: 'If capital adequacy ratio < 100%, then submit a remediation plan within 30 days,' how would you encode this as an SMT constraint with variables `car` (ratio) and `plan_submitted`?"
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for Legal Context**
  - **Why needed here:** The RAG module (LegalSearchEngine) expands the initially cited statutes with related provisions, ensuring the constraint set captures the full regulatory context and avoids omissions due to incomplete case citations.
  - **Quick check:** "Why is hybrid BM25 + vector search with LLM re-filtering used instead of a single retrieval method for legal clause expansion? What risks does each component mitigate?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw legal PDFs → Parsed statutes (vector DB) → RAG retrieval (hybrid BM25/embedding) → LLM constraint generation → Z3 SMT solver → Multi-agent orchestration (Rewrite/Execute/Coding/Analysis) → Compliance report.
- **Critical path:** For a new case: RAG enriches relevant provisions → LLM generates SMT constraints (hard laws, soft facts) → Z3 checks satisfiability; if UNSAT with penalty false, optimization computes minimal soft flips → agents handle errors and produce final analysis. For precedent cases: Rewriter Agent retrieves similar SMT templates → Execute Agent runs solver → on errors, Coding Agent patches → Analysis Agent outputs report.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - LLM flexibility vs. SMT precision: LLMs interpret ambiguous text but may generate flawed code; SMT ensures soundness but requires correct input.
  - RAG recall vs. noise: Broad retrieval increases coverage but may introduce irrelevant clauses; LLM filtering aims to balance.
  - Hard vs. soft constraint balance: Over-constraining risks UNSAT from minor variations; under-constraining weakens regulatory rigor.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Generation: SMT syntax errors, unbound variables, missing constraints in LLM output.
  - Verification: H_law alone UNSAT → inconsistent legal encoding; H_law ∪ H_case SAT → potential false negative (non-compliant case not flagged).
  - Optimization: Multiple equally minimal correction sets → ambiguous guidance; solver timeout on large constraint sets (>40 variables).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **SMT Generation Sanity Check:** Run LLM on 10 pre-validated cases; manually verify each generated SMT line against ground truth. Target: >85% line-level correctness.
  2. **RAG Ablation Study:** Disable RAG enrichment for 5 cases; measure constraint completeness (coverage of relevant provisions) and final SMT satisfiability status. Expect significant drop in coverage for sparsely cited statutes.
  3. **Optimization Calibration:** For 5 satisfiable cases, flip one soft constraint (e.g., `plan_executed`) and re-run optimization; confirm Δ exactly matches the introduced flip, validating minimal-change identification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross-jurisdictional adaptation:** How can the framework be adapted for legal systems with different statutory structures and languages? The authors propose future work on multilingual and precedent-driven adaptations but have only validated on Taiwan FSC cases. Evidence needed: Testing on common law jurisdictions (e.g., US, UK) with modified RAG corpus and prompts.
2. **LLM generation error improvement:** What causes errors in LLM-generated SMT constraints, and how can accuracy be raised beyond 86.2%? The paper identifies omission and inconsistency errors but does not analyze root causes or systematic improvements. Evidence needed: Detailed error taxonomy and evaluation of enhanced prompting, fine-tuning, or iterative verification loops.
3. **Scalability limits:** How does the system scale to hundreds of variables and constraints? Current evaluation averages ~13 variables and 31 constraints (max 40). Evidence needed: Benchmarking on synthetic problems of increasing size to measure solve time, memory, and agent coordination overhead.
4. **Temporal and probabilistic extensions:** Can the framework handle temporal dynamics, probabilistic conditions, or nested hierarchies? Current encoding uses Boolean/numerical constraints only; richer legal reasoning may require temporal or probabilistic logics. Evidence needed: Extensions to SMT with temporal/probabilistic operators and evaluation on cases featuring such elements.

## Limitations
- LLM model, version, and exact prompt templates are undisclosed, hindering reproducibility.
- Ground truth labeling procedure for "correct" SMT code and inter-annotator agreement are not reported.
- Evaluation is limited to Taiwan FSC cases; no cross-jurisdiction validation.
- Soft constraint weights are uniform; regulatory prioritization scheme unspecified.
- Statutory constraint base consistency (H_law) not independently verified.
