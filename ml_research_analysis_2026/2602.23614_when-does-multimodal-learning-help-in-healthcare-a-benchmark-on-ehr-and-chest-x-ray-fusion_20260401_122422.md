# When Does Multimodal Learning Help in Healthcare? A Benchmark on EHR and Chest X-Ray Fusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23614
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23614
- **Reference Count:** 18
- **Primary Result:** Multimodal fusion improves outcomes for complementary diseases but degrades under realistic missingness unless models handle incomplete inputs, without improving fairness.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces CareBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multimodal learning that fuses electronic health record (EHR) data and chest X-rays (CXR) across 17 models and three clinical tasks (phenotyping, mortality, length-of-stay). Using MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-CXR, it finds that fusion provides measurable gains when both modalities are present, particularly for conditions requiring complementary signals (e.g., congestive heart failure). However, under realistic clinical missingness (~75% of ICU stays lack CXR), performance collapses unless the model is explicitly designed to handle incomplete inputs. Furthermore, adding CXR does not mitigate racial disparities in model performance; fairness gaps persist, driven primarily by unequal sensitivity across subgroups. The work provides actionable guidance for developing reliable multimodal clinical systems.

## Method Summary
The authors construct two cohorts from MIMIC: a base cohort (26,947 ICU stays, ~25% with CXR) reflecting realistic missingness, and a matched subset (7,149 stays with paired CXR within ±24h). Structured EHR features (hourly resampled vitals, labs) are processed via LSTM/Transformer encoders; CXR images use a ResNet-50. 17 fusion models are benchmarked, ranging from naive concatenation (Late Fusion) to advanced cross-modal methods (DrFuse, AUG, InfoReg) and those natively supporting missing data (MedFuse). Evaluation uses AUPRC/AUROC for classification tasks, with additional fairness metrics (AUPRC gap, TPR gap, ECE gap) across race subgroups. Hyperparameter optimization is standardized via Bayesian search, and patient-level splits ensure no data leakage.

## Key Results
- With both modalities complete, advanced fusion (e.g., InfoReg) outperforms unimodal and naive fusion, achieving AUPRC 0.495 for phenotyping vs. 0.472 best unimodal, with gains concentrated in diseases like CHF and liver disease.
- Under base cohort missingness (~75% CXR absent), performance drops sharply for models not designed for incomplete inputs; only MedFuse (AUROC 0.874 for mortality) retains clear multimodal benefits.
- Multimodal fusion does not inherently improve fairness; across race subgroups, disparity gaps (AUPRC, TPR) remain large, primarily due to unequal sensitivity, and can even widen compared to unimodal baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multimodal fusion improves performance when EHR and CXR provide complementary signals for a clinical task.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-modal learning mechanisms (e.g., attention, disentanglement) enable the model to learn conditional dependencies between modalities—such as interpreting CXR findings in the context of EHR vitals—that simple concatenation cannot capture.
- **Core assumption:** The target clinical condition has manifestations distributed across both data types (e.g., CHF shows longitudinal fluid overload in EHR and pulmonary edema in CXR).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Multimodal fusion improves performance when modalities are complete, with gains concentrating in diseases that require complementary information from both EHR and imaging."
  - [section 5.2] Disease-wise AUPRC gains for CHF, liver disease, and COPD; InfoReg/AUG/DrFuse outperform Late Fusion.
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; neighboring papers focus on general multimodal fusion or fairness, not specific to EHR-CXR complementarity.
- **Break condition:** When one modality (typically CXR) is missing for most samples, or when the condition is primarily detectable via one modality (e.g., sepsis via lab trends).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Under realistic missingness (~75% CXR missing), multimodal benefits degrade unless the model is explicitly designed to handle incomplete inputs.
- **Mechanism:** Models like MedFuse treat modality representations as a variable-length sequence (e.g., via LSTM fusion), allowing them to ignore absent modalities during inference. Others (e.g., DrFuse) impute shared latent representations from available data.
- **Core assumption:** Missingness is non-random and pervasive in clinical workflows; zero-imputation of missing CXR for non-adaptive models leads to poor representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Under realistic missingness, multimodal benefits rapidly degrade unless models are explicitly designed to handle incomplete inputs."
  - [section 5.4] MedFuse achieves AUROC 0.874 on mortality in base cohort; Figure 2 shows specialized models (AUG, InfoReg) degrade slower as missing ratio increases.
  - [corpus] Not directly addressed in corpus; neighboring works (MoE-Health, EMERGE) tackle missing modalities but not in this specific EHR-CXR setup.
- **Break condition:** If missingness exceeds ~80% and the model lacks a flexible fusion layer (e.g., simple concatenation), performance falls below unimodal EHR baseline.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multimodal fusion does not inherently improve fairness; disparities (especially in TPR) persist because unequal sensitivity across racial subgroups is not mitigated by adding a second modality.
- **Mechanism:** The CXR modality may inherit biases from imaging protocols or disease prevalence patterns; without fairness constraints, fusion amplifies subgroup gaps, particularly in AUPRC and calibration (ECE).
- **Core assumption:** Bias sources in EHR (e.g., treatment disparities) and CXR (e.g., acquisition differences) are not automatically canceled out by multimodal integration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Multimodal fusion does not inherently improve fairness... disparities mainly arising from unequal sensitivity across demographic groups."
  - [section 5.5] Figure 3 shows multimodal models have larger AUPRC/TPR gaps than unimodal Transformer; TPR gaps > FPR gaps across all models.
  - [corpus] Corroborated by "Fairness at Every Intersection" and "FairEHR-CLP" which also find multimodal fairness requires explicit intervention.
- **Break condition:** If subgroup TPR differences exceed 0.1 (absolute), the model is unlikely to be fair regardless of modality addition.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Modality Complementarity**
  - **Why needed here:** Fusion only helps if each modality contributes unique, condition-relevant signals. This determines *which diseases* benefit most (e.g., CHF vs. sepsis).
  - **Quick check question:** For a target condition, does current clinical guidelines require interpreting both longitudinal vitals/labs *and* imaging? If yes, complementarity likely exists.

- **Concept: Modality Imbalance**
  - **Why needed here:** EHR’s dense temporal sampling inherently dominates over single-timepoint CXR. Without balancing (e.g., AUG’s boosting, InfoReg’s adaptive learning rates), the CXR pathway fails to learn useful features.
  - **Quick check question:** During training, are gradient norms for CXR encoder significantly smaller than for EHR encoder? If yes, imbalance is suppressing CXR learning.

- **Concept: Missing Modality Robustness**
  - **Why needed here:** ~75% of ICU stays lack CXR. Models must flexibly ingest 1 or 2 modalities without retraining or imputing raw CXR pixels.
  - **Quick check question:** If you randomly replace CXR inputs with zero tensors during validation, does model performance drop >5% relative to full-modality performance? If yes, it lacks missing-modality robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  EHR Encoder (LSTM/Transformer) -> CXR Encoder (ResNet-50) -> Fusion Module (varies: concat/attention/disentangle) -> Classifier (task-specific head).  
  For missing CXR: Fusion Module must accept `h_cxr = None` or zero vector.

- **Critical path:**  
  Preprocessed EHR (sequential) -> EHR encoder -> `h_ehr`  
  Preprocessed CXR (image) -> CXR encoder -> `h_cxr`  
  Fusion module(`h_ehr`, `h_cxr`) -> `h_fused`  
  Classifier(`h_fused`) -> predictions.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Simple concatenation (LateFusion):** Low compute, but fails under missingness and ignores cross-modal dependencies.
  - **Cross-attention (UTDE, DAFT):** Captures fine-grained interactions but assumes both modalities present; sensitive to imbalance.
  - **Disentanglement (DrFuse, ShaSpec):** Better missing-modality handling via shared latent space, but adds complexity.
  - **Modality balancing (AUG, InfoReg):** Explicitly mitigates EHR dominance; may slow convergence but improves CXR utilization.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Missing-modality collapse:** Model performance on base cohort (75% missing CXR) is worse than EHR-only Transformer.
  2. **Imbalance symptom:** CXR encoder gradients near zero; disease-wise gains only for modality-distributed phenotypes (CHF, liver disease) but not for EHR-dominant ones (sepsis).
  3. **Fairness failure:** TPR gap across race subgroups > 0.1 even on matched cohort; calibration (ECE) gap > 0.05.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Matched vs. base cohort comparison:** Train/evaluate all models on matched subset (complete CXR) and base cohort (realistic missingness). Expect >5% AUROC drop for non-adapted models; MedFuse/AUG should degrade slower.
  2. **Controlled missingness sweep:** Artificially set CXR missing ratio from 0% to 80% in matched cohort. Plot AUPRC vs. missing ratio; specialized models (AUG, InfoReg) should show gentler slopes.
  3. **Fairness subgroup analysis:** On matched cohort, compute AUPRC/TPR gaps between White and Black patients. Check if adding CXR reduces gaps; expect no systematic improvement and possible widening per paper findings.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Under what specific clinical conditions and disease phenotypes does multimodal fusion of EHR and chest X-rays provide a significant predictive advantage over unimodal models?
- **Open Question 2:** How do different multimodal fusion strategies compare in terms of performance, robustness to missing data, computational efficiency, and scalability to new tasks?
- **Open Question 3:** What architectural designs or training objectives can effectively mitigate the modality imbalance introduced by the temporal richness of EHR, thereby enabling the imaging modality to contribute meaningfully without being dominated?
- **Open Question 4:** Why does multimodal fusion often fail to improve, and sometimes even worsen, fairness across demographic subgroups, and how can we design multimodal models that maintain both high performance and equitable outcomes?

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to EHR-CXR fusion for specific ICU prediction tasks; generalizability to other modality pairs, diseases, or settings (e.g., outpatient) is unknown.
- The 75% CXR missingness is dataset-specific; breakpoints for other clinical workflows or imaging modalities are not established.
- Fairness analysis identifies persistent TPR gaps but does not disentangle whether biases originate from EHR, CXR, or the fusion process itself; no fairness interventions were tested.

## Confidence
- **Complementarity Benefits (disease-specific gains):** Medium-High – Supported by disease-wise AUPRC improvements but limited to phenotyping tasks.
- **Missing-Modality Degradation (performance drop under missingness):** Medium – Robust base-cohort results, but missingness thresholds are dataset-dependent.
- **Fairness Persistence (disparities not mitigated):** Medium-Low – Strong subgroup correlation, but causal drivers (EHR bias vs. CXR bias) are not disentangled.

## Next Checks
1. **Controlled missingness sweep:** Replicate Fig. 2’s AUPRC-vs.-missing-ratio curve for MedFuse/AUG vs. LateFusion using *synthetic* missingness (0–80%) on the matched cohort. Verify that specialized models show statistically gentler performance degradation (slope difference p<0.05).
2. **Gradient norm inspection:** During training on the base cohort, log per-epoch gradient norms for EHR vs. CXR encoders. Confirm that models without explicit balancing (e.g., UTDE) exhibit EHR gradients >5× larger than CXR gradients, while AUG/InfoReg show near-parity.
3. **Fairness intervention ablation:** For a top-performing model (e.g., MedFuse), add a fairness constraint (e.g., equalized odds) and re-evaluate TPR gaps on the matched cohort. Expect: if gaps persist without constraint but shrink with it, the original fairness failure is architectural rather than data-inevitable.
