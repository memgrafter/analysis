# FlatLands: Generative Floormap Completion From a Single Egocentric View
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.16016
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16016
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Stochastic generative models with oracle best-of-K selection significantly outperform deterministic baselines for single-view BEV floor completion, with FM+XAttn providing superior calibration by concentrating uncertainty at layout boundaries.

## Executive Summary
FlatLands establishes a new benchmark for indoor floorplan completion from a single egocentric RGB image. The dataset aggregates over 270k observations across 17,656 real indoor scenes, providing precise BEV ground truth, visibility masks, and validity regions. The task is formulated as a generative completion problem: given an observed floor region and an unobserved mask, models must predict multiple plausible completions. The work evaluates a spectrum of approaches, from deterministic (U-Net, LaMa) to stochastic (Diffusion, Flow Matching). Key findings show that stochastic methods, when combined with oracle best-of-K selection, achieve the highest fidelity. Crucially, the FM+XAttn variant demonstrates superior probabilistic calibration, learning to place predictive variance primarily at layout boundaries rather than uniformly. In end-to-end RGB inference, performance gaps narrow due to front-end perception noise, shifting the primary bottleneck to the RGB→BEV mapping. The benchmark enforces a hard evidence-clamping constraint to ensure observed floors are never altered.

## Method Summary
The task is defined as conditional generation of an unobserved BEV floor region \(F_{\text{uno}}\) given an observed region \(F_{\text{obs}}\) and an unobserved mask \(U\). Formally, models learn \(q_\theta(\hat{F}_{\text{uno}} \mid F_{\text{obs}}, U)\). At inference, \(K\) samples are drawn and merged with the observed evidence via a deterministic clamping operation: \(F_{\text{comp}} = F_{\text{obs}} + U \odot \hat{F}_{\text{uno}}\), which absolutely preserves \(F_{\text{obs}}\). The benchmark evaluates both deterministic single-hypothesis predictors and stochastic multi-hypothesis generators. The conditioning input is a concatenated binary map of \([F_{\text{obs}}, U]\). For real-world use, a perception front-end (Depth Pro + SegFormer + back-projection) extracts \(F_{\text{obs}}\) and \(U\) from an RGB image.

## Key Results
- Stochastic generators (Diffusion, Flow Matching, FM+XAttn) with oracle best-of-K (K=4) surpass all deterministic models (U-Net, PConv-UNet, LaMa) in IoU on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (ScanNet++) test sets.
- FM+XAttn achieves the best probabilistic calibration (lowest Masked Energy Score) and concentrates predictive variance at layout boundaries (interior/boundary variance ratio ~600×), unlike LaMa-Ensemble's uniform variance.
- In end-to-end RGB-to-floorplan inference, the stochastic advantage diminishes substantially, indicating that front-end perception errors dominate the error budget and shift the system bottleneck.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Generative Multi-Modal Completion
- **Claim:** Generative models capture the multi-modal posterior distribution over plausible unobserved layouts, enabling higher-fidelity predictions via oracle best-of-K selection.
- **Mechanism:** A conditional generative model (e.g., diffusion, flow matching) is trained to approximate \(p(F_{\text{uno}} \mid F_{\text{obs}}, U)\). At inference, \(K\) samples \(\hat{F}_{\text{uno}}^{(k)}\) are drawn from the learned distribution and combined with the observed evidence via hard clamping. Oracle selection picks the sample with the highest IoU against ground truth.
- **Core assumption:** The observed floor region provides sufficient contextual constraints; the distribution of valid indoor layouts is learnable from data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "The task is cast as generative completion: given observed floor and unobserved mask, predict multiple plausible completions."
  - [Section 5] "With oracle best-of-K selection (K=4), all stochastic generators surpass every deterministic predictor."
  - [Corpus] Weak; related works apply generative models to BEV tasks, but FlatLands is the first dedicated benchmark for indoor floor completion comparing stochastic vs. deterministic approaches.
- **Break condition:** Under extremely sparse conditioning (\(r_{\text{cond}} < 0.02\)), generative models may produce boundary leakage or disconnected islands. End-to-end front-end noise also diminishes the stochastic advantage.

### Mechanism 2: Hard Evidence Clamping
- **Claim:** A post-sampling hard merge guarantees the observed floor is preserved exactly, which is a non-negotiable requirement for downstream robotic planning safety.
- **Mechanism:** The final completion is computed as \(F_{\text{comp}} = F_{\text{obs}} + U \odot \hat{F}_{\text{uno}}\). Where \(U=0\) (observed region), the output is forced to equal \(F_{\text{obs}}\) irrespective of the sample.
- **Core assumption:** The provided \(F_{\text{obs}}\) is authoritative; any deviation would invalidate the map for collision checking.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "The benchmark enforces evidence clamping to preserve observed floor exactly."
  - [Section 3.3] "This evidence-clamping step enforces a posterior support constraint: returned completions agree with \(F_{\text{obs}}\) on all observed cells."
  - [Corpus] Common in image inpainting (e.g., RePaint); FlatLands formalizes it for BEV floor completion.
- **Break condition:** If \(F_{\text{obs}}\) contains errors (from segmentation or depth miscalibration), clamping propagates them. With end-to-end perception, clamping can cause artifacts at frustum edges where \(F_{\text{obs}}\) is fragmented.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Attention for Calibrated Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Injecting cross-attention conditioning (FM+XAttn) improves probabilistic calibration by teaching the model to concentrate predictive variance at layout boundaries, where genuine ambiguity exists, rather than uniformly across the unobserved region.
- **Mechanism:** Flow Matching is augmented with a condition encoder on \([F_{\text{obs}}, U]\). Cross-attention tokens are injected at coarse resolutions (64×64, 32×32), allowing the denoiser to globally attend to structural cues. This guides samples to vary mainly at floor-non-floor transitions.
- **Core assumption:** Interior floor regions are structurally determined by surrounding context; uncertainty is primarily a boundary phenomenon.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "FM+XAttn achieves superior calibration (lowest Masked Energy Score 0.095 OOD) and concentrates uncertainty at layout boundaries, while LaMa-Ensemble exhibits uniform variance."
  - [Section 6] "FM+XAttn attains \(\bar{\sigma}^2_{\text{int}} = 6\times10^{-5}\), \(\bar{\sigma}^2_{\text{bnd}} = 0.037\) (ratio \(\sim600\times\)), concentrating uncertainty at boundaries. LaMa-Ensemble yields \(\bar{\sigma}^2_{\text{int}}=0.052\) and \(\bar{\sigma}^2_{\text{bnd}}=0.125\) (ratio 2.4×)."
  - [Corpus] Cross-attention is standard in transformers; FlatLands adapts it for BEV conditioning, but no direct corpus evidence for boundary concentration.
- **Break condition:** Under very sparse conditioning, FM+XAttn may still over-extend floor (boundary leakage). The added cross-attention also increases inference latency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Bayesian inverse problems**  
  **Why needed:** The completion task is fundamentally ill-posed; a single egocentric view admits many valid completions. Framing it as estimating a posterior \(p(F_{\text{uno}} \mid F_{\text{obs}}, U)\) clarifies why stochastic, multi-modal outputs are necessary.  
  **Quick check:** What does "inverse problem" mean in floor completion, and why is the solution space multi-modal? (Answer: Inferring an unobserved scene property from limited sensor data; multiple layouts explain the observation equally well.)

- **Concept: Hard evidence clamping (conditional generation)**  
  **Why needed:** Guarantees the observed floor is never altered—a hard constraint critical for safe robotic planning. This distinguishes the task from standard soft conditioning where the generator might ignore or modify the input.  
  **Quick check:** How does evidence clamping differ from simply masking the loss during training? (Answer: Training masking allows the model to output anything in observed regions; clamping post-processes any sample to exactly match \(F_{\text{obs}}\).)

- **Concept: Uncertainty calibration metrics (MES, boundary variance)**  
  **Why needed:** Evaluating multi-hypothesis generation requires metrics beyond single-sample accuracy. Masked Energy Score (MES) balances fidelity and diversity; analyzing variance separated by interior/boundary regions reveals whether uncertainty is placed meaningfully.  
  **Quick check:** Why is a low Masked Energy Score desirable, and what does a high interior variance (vs. boundary) likely indicate? (Answer: Low MES means samples are both accurate and diverse. High interior variance suggests the model is uncertain where it should be confident, indicating poor calibration.)

- **Concept: Best-of-K selection**  
  **Why needed:** Provides an oracle upper-bound on the performance of a generative model's sample set. It answers "what is the best possible outcome we could hope to select from K draws?" and is used to quantify the ceiling for stochastic approaches.
- **Concept: Out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation**  
  **Why needed:** Tests model robustness to unseen scene layouts and sensor characteristics. Using ScanNet++ as a held-out dataset checks whether learned priors generalize beyond the FlatLands training distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  RGB image -> Perception front-end (Depth Pro + SegFormer + back-projection) -> Observed BEV (\(F_{\text{obs}}\)) & Unobserved mask (\(U\)) -> Completion model (Deterministic: U-Net/LaMa; Stochastic: Diffusion/FM[+XAttn]; Ensemble: LaMa-Ensemble) -> Sample K completions -> Evidence clamping (\(F_{\text{obs}} + U \odot \hat{F}_{\text{uno}}\)) -> Output BEV completions -> Evaluation on \(R_{\text{eval}} = U \odot V\) vs. \(F^\star\).

- **Critical path:**  
  Input processing yields \((F_{\text{obs}}, U)\). The completion model produces \(K\) samples of \(\hat{F}_{\text{uno}}\). A final deterministic clamping step merges each with \(F_{\text{obs}}\) to yield \(K\) full BEV completions. Performance is measured on the unobserved-but-valid region \(R_{\text{eval}}\).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Deterministic vs. Stochastic:* Deterministic models are fast (1 NFE) but output a single, potentially over-smoothed completion. Stochastic models capture multi-modality but require many NFEs (50–200) for good sampling.
  - *Conditioning:* Simple channel concatenation is computationally cheap. Cross-attention (FM+XAttn) improves calibration and boundary-focused variance but adds compute and latency.
  - *Dataset:* Aggregating ZInD (panoramic) and pinhole sources increases layout diversity but introduces heterogeneity in viewpoints and noise characteristics.
  - *Metrics:* IoU is simple but can be inflated by floor prevalence. Masked Energy Score (MES) is better for stochastic calibration but requires drawing multiple samples.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Complete collapse to all-floor or all-obstacle predictions when conditioning is extremely sparse (very low \(r_{\text{cond}}\)).
  - Uniform variance across interior and boundary regions (as seen in LaMa-Ensemble), indicating seed divergence without structured uncertainty.
  - "Boundary leakage," where the model over-extends floor into obstacle areas, especially when most of the BEV is unobserved.
  - Significant performance drop in end-to-end (RGB→BEV) vs. GT-conditioned settings, highlighting perception noise as the dominant error source.
  - Poor results on challenging subsets: low conditional ratio (\(r_{\text{cond}} \leq 0.2\)), low floor prevalence, complex layouts.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Reproduce Table 3 (GT-conditioned):** Run the official test split. Confirm stochastic models (K=4) achieve higher IoU/UMR than all deterministic baselines. Check FM+XAttn's competitive fidelity.
  2. **Reproduce Table 4 (Calibration):** For stochastic models, compute Masked Energy Score (MES) and measure variance separately in interior vs. boundary regions of \(R_{\text{eval}}\). Verify FM+XAttn has the lowest MES and by far the highest interior/boundary variance ratio.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-view integration:** How can observations from multiple viewpoints be fused within the FlatLands framework to reduce completion ambiguity and improve robustness?
- **Graded traversability:** What is the impact of incorporating richer traversability semantics (stairs, slopes, roughness) on BEV completion quality and its utility for robots with different mobility capabilities?
- **Closed-loop navigation:** Does using uncertainty-aware, multi-hypothesis floorplans improve closed-loop navigation performance (success rate, safety, efficiency) under partial observability compared to single deterministic maps?
- **Front-end robustness:** What architectural improvements can make the completion model robust to the noise and errors inherent in monocular depth and segmentation, mitigating the end-to-end performance drop?

## Limitations
- Stochastic models require 50–200 NFEs, posing a challenge for real-time deployment on resource-constrained robots.
- In end-to-end RGB inference, the stochastic advantage largely vanishes because errors from the monocular depth and segmentation front-end dominate the overall error budget.
- The dataset aggregates sources with different camera models (panoramic vs. pinhole), which may introduce subtle biases in layout distributions and visibility patterns.
- Hard evidence clamping irrevocably includes any errors present in the perception front-end's \(F_{\text{obs}}\), potentially propagating systematic mistakes.
- Under extremely sparse conditioning (\(r_{\text{cond}} < 0.02\)), all models struggle, exhibiting boundary leakage or disconnected floor regions.

## Confidence
- **Stochastic models (best-of-K) surpass deterministic ones:** **High** – Supported by extensive GT-conditioned results on 270k+ samples; ranking persists in OOD (ScanNet++) tests.
- **FM+XAttn provides superior calibration and boundary-focused uncertainty:** **High** – Quantitatively demonstrated via MES and interior/boundary variance ratios; reproducible from provided tables.
- **End-to-end bottleneck shifts to perception, diminishing stochastic gains:** **High** – Evident in Tab. S1; the gap between stochastic and deterministic narrows significantly when using noisy front-end outputs.
- **Evidence clamping is a necessary hard constraint:** **High** – Fundamental to the problem formulation for robotic safety; the mechanism is straightforward and empirically enforced.
- **Dataset scale and diversity support generalizable learning:** **Medium** – 17k scenes is large, but aggregation from two sources could hide distribution gaps; OOD tests suggest some generalization, but unknown unknowns remain.
