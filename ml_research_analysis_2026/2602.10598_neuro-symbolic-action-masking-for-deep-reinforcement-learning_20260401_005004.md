# Neuro-symbolic Action Masking for Deep Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10598
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10598
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** NSAM uses PSDD-based symbolic grounding to dynamically mask infeasible actions, significantly improving sample efficiency and reducing constraint violations in constraint-heavy domains compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agents often struggle in environments with hard constraints because they explore infeasible actions, leading to poor sample efficiency and safety violations. This paper introduces Neuro-symbolic Action Masking (NSAM), a framework that integrates symbolic reasoning into DRL without requiring full symbolic state annotations. NSAM automatically learns a symbolic state representation using probabilistic sentential decision diagrams (PSDDs), guided by minimal supervision from transition outcomes (whether an action leads to a constraint violation). This representation evaluates action preconditions to generate dynamic masks that filter invalid actions during policy execution. Evaluated on four constraint-heavy domains (Sudoku, N-Queens, Graph Coloring, Visual Sudoku), NSAM achieves substantially higher rewards and near-zero violation rates compared to strong baselines. The core insight is that the PSDD's structure guarantees constraint satisfaction by design, while the end-to-end training creates a mutually reinforcing loop between symbolic grounding and policy learning.

## Method Summary
NSAM operates by compiling domain constraints φ into a Sentential Decision Diagram (SDD) structure, which defines a Probabilistic SDD (PSDD). A neural gating network g(s) maps the high-dimensional state s to parameters Θ for the PSDD. The PSDD then computes a distribution Pr(m|Θ, m|=φ) over symbolic models m that satisfy φ. At each decision step, the most likely model m̂ is obtained via a MAP query. Action preconditions φ(a) are evaluated on m̂ to produce a binary mask for each action. The policy network's raw output is multiplied by this mask and renormalized to produce a feasible action distribution π⁺. The policy is trained with PPO on these masked actions. The gating network g is trained in a minimally supervised manner using transition tuples (s,a,s') stored in a buffer; a label y=1 is assigned if both s and s' satisfy φ, and g is updated to maximize the likelihood that explorable actions (those with φ(a) true under the PSDD) predict positive outcomes. This process repeats periodically, with improved masking encouraging policy exploration of valid regions, which in turn generates better data for the PSDD.

## Key Results
- In 5×5 Sudoku, NSAM achieves a reward of 2.7 with a 4.3% violation rate, while baselines exhibit negative rewards and near 100% violations.
- NSAM significantly improves sample efficiency across all domains, reaching high rewards orders of magnitude faster than baselines.
- Ablating the PSDD grounding with a standard neural network leads to unstable learning and poor performance, confirming the necessity of the symbolic component.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PSDDs automatically learn symbolic representations that satisfy domain constraints φ, bridging the numerical-symbolic gap without manual grounding.
- **Mechanism:** Domain constraints φ are compiled offline into an SDD circuit structure that defines the PSDD. A neural gating network g(s) produces parameters Θ for the PSDD, which computes state-conditioned distributions Pr(m|Θ, m|=φ). The SDD structure guarantees Pr(m|Θ, m|≠φ)=0 by construction.
- **Core assumption:** Domain constraints φ can be expressed as a propositional formula and compiled into an SDD of manageable size.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "PSDDs serve two purposes: they guarantee that any learned symbolic model satisfies domain constraints... and allow the agent to represent probability distributions over symbolic models conditioned on high-dimensional states."
  - Section 3.1: Detailed SDD/PSDD compilation process showing how zero probability is enforced for violating models.
  - Corpus: Corpus shows related neuro-symbolic RL work but no direct evidence specifically validating this mechanism; weak external corroboration.
- **Break condition:** If constraint compilation yields an SDD too large to handle, or if domain knowledge cannot be expressed propositionally.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Action feasibility is learned from minimal supervision (transition outcome labels) rather than full symbolic state annotations.
- **Mechanism:** Collect experience tuples (s,a,s',y) where y=1 iff both s and s' satisfy domain constraints φ. Train g(s) to maximize likelihood that actions leading to non-violating transitions have high predicted explorability probability P̂(a|s)=Σ_{m|=φ(a)} Pr(m|g(s)). Loss: Lg = -[y·log(P̂) + (1-y)·log(1-P̂)].
- **Core assumption:** Whether an action is explorable can be inferred from whether its execution leads to constraint violations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "...in a minimally supervised manner during the DRL process."
  - Section 3.2: Equation (1) defines y from constraint satisfaction; Equation (3) shows cross-entropy loss using y.
  - Corpus: Corpus identifies sample-efficient neuro-symbolic RL as related but does not provide direct validation for this particular minimal-supervision approach.
- **Break condition:** If environment cannot reliably detect constraint violations to compute y, or if action preconditions φ(a) are not well-aligned with transition outcomes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** End-to-end training creates mutual reinforcement between symbolic grounding (PSDD) and policy learning, where each improves the other.
- **Mechanism:** Algorithm 1 periodically updates g using buffer D of transition tuples. At each step, use current PSDD to compute most likely symbolic model m̂ via MAP query; evaluate preconditions φ(a) on m̂ to get binary mask Cφ(a)(m̂); renormalize action distribution π⁺ = π·C / Σ; train policy with PPO on masked actions. As policy explores better, it generates more informative (s,a,s') tuples that improve PSDD; better PSDD yields better masks.
- **Core assumption:** The feedback loop is stable: improved masking leads to policies that generate sufficiently diverse and high-quality transition data to continue improving PSDD.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "improvements in symbolic grounding and policy learning mutually reinforce each other."
  - Section 5: Algorithm 1 explicitly details periodic PSDD updates and masked policy steps.
  - Section 7.7: Experiment shows NSAM generalizes from single transition to multiple related actions using precondition structure.
- **Break condition:** If either component stagnates (e.g., PSDD early misstep leads to overly restrictive masks), the loop may degrade rather than reinforce.

## Foundational Learning

**Probabilistic Sentential Decision Diagrams (PSDDs)**
- *Why needed here:* Core representation linking neural networks to symbolic reasoning while preserving constraint satisfaction and enabling efficient inference.
- *Quick check question:* "Can a PSDD assign non-zero probability to a model that violates domain constraints φ?" (Answer: No—by structural design, violating models get probability zero.)

**Action Masking in Deep Reinforcement Learning**
- *Why needed here:* Used to exclude infeasible actions during action selection, improving sample efficiency and safety.
- *Quick check question:* "Does masking invalid actions break the policy gradient theorem?" (Answer: No, if renormalization is applied, as shown in prior work.)

**Neuro-symbolic Integration**
- *Why needed here:* Enables DRL agents to incorporate explicit symbolic knowledge (constraints) without losing the ability to learn from high-dimensional perceptual inputs.
- *Quick check question:* "What is the primary role of the SDD circuit in NSAM?" (Answer: To enforce that all sampled symbolic models satisfy domain constraints φ by construction.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Input Encoder -> Policy Network π(s,a) & Gating Network g(s) -> Compiled PSDD (Θ, structure) -> MAP query gives m̂ -> Action Preconditions AP evaluate Cφ(a)(m̂) -> Masking Module computes π⁺(s,a,φ) -> Environment -> Constraint Checker provides y -> Replay Buffer D -> Updaters (PPO for π, Cross-entropy for g)

**Critical path:**
1. Observe state s.
2. Compute Θ = g(s); inject into PSDD.
3. Compute m̂ = MAP(Θ).
4. For each action a: evaluate Cφ(a)(m̂).
5. Renormalize: π⁺(s,a,φ) = π(s,a)·Cφ(a)(m̂) / sum.
6. Sample action a ~ π⁺; execute; receive (r, s').
7. Get y = 1 iff s and s' satisfy φ.
8. Append (s,a,s') to D.
9. Periodically (every freq_g steps): sample from D; compute P̂(a|s) = Σ_{m|=φ(a)} Pr(m|g(s)); minimize Lg; update g.
10. At episode end: collect trajectory; update π with PPO on masked actions.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- PSDD vs MLP grounding: PSDD enforces constraints by structure; MLP cannot guarantee this. Tradeoff: PSDD compilation cost vs constraint safety.
- MAP vs sampling: MAP gives deterministic, interpretable m̂. Sampling would introduce stochasticity in masks, possibly aiding exploration but harming stability.
- Update frequency: Updating g every step is expensive; periodic updates (e.g., every 1K steps) balance performance and cost. Too infrequent: PSDD lags; too frequent: overhead.

**Failure signatures:**
- High violation rates: Check PSDD accuracy—may need more data or larger g network; check y computation—ensure φ detection is correct.
- Policy collapse/stagnation: Masks may be too restrictive early if PSDD is poor. Consider annealing mask application.
- PSDD parameters uniform: Insufficient exploration diversity in D; ensure ε-greedy or entropy regularization in π.
- Slow convergence: PSDD might be too large; check SDD compilation size. Also verify AP preconditions are correct and complete.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ground truth verification:** On 2x2 Sudoku, enumerate all states/actions, compute P̂(a|s) from trained PSDD, compare with ground truth from AP/φ. Expect near-perfect match.
2. **Ablation study:** Replace PSDD with standard MLP (same size). Expect unstable policy learning and higher violation rates.
3. **Mask necessity test:** Train NSAM without masking (use π instead of π⁺). Expect lower final reward and higher violations, confirming masks are essential.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can NSAM be extended to use richer symbolic knowledge (e.g., temporal logics) for action preconditions? The authors state in the conclusion: "A promising research direction for future work is to investigate richer forms of symbolic knowledge as action preconditions, such as temporal logics..." NSAM currently only supports propositional logic preconditions; temporal logics involve sequential dependencies and more complex semantics that are not trivially compilable into the PSDD framework while preserving differentiability and efficient masking.
2. How can NSAM handle situations where domain constraints are unknown, incorrect, or partially specified? The authors mention "to design learning framework when constraints are unknown or incorrect" as a future direction. NSAM assumes the domain constraint φ is provided and correct; if φ is missing or flawed, the PSDD may enforce wrong invariances.
3. Can NSAM be effectively scaled to complex, high-dimensional real-world domains (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving) and what architectural modifications are needed? The authors state "Extending NSAM to broader real-world domains is also an important future direction." Current evaluations are on small synthetic domains; real-world settings involve high-dimensional sensory inputs, continuous actions, and intricate constraints that may cause PSDD compilation to blow up.

## Limitations
- Unspecified hyperparameters (`freq_g`, buffer size for D), exact training steps per domain, and Visual Sudoku’s image preprocessing pipeline hinder exact replication.
- PSDD compilation scalability to larger domains may suffer exponential blowup, limiting real-time applicability.
- The minimal-supervision signal `y` assumes constraint checks are available and aligned with action preconditions; this may fail in partially observable or stochastic environments where violations are latent or delayed.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (PSDD symbolic grounding):** High. SDD structure mathematically guarantees zero probability for violating models; core design is sound and empirically validated via ablations.
- **Mechanism 2 (Minimal supervision from transitions):** Medium. Relies on accurate `y` labels from constraint satisfaction, feasible in benchmarks but may fail in more complex settings.
- **Mechanism 3 (End-to-end mutual reinforcement):** Medium. Experimental results show improvement, but the feedback loop’s stability is not theoretically guaranteed; early PSDD errors could cause irreversible over-restrictive masking.
