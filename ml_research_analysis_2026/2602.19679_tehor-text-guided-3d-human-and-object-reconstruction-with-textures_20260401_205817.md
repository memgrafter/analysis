# TeHOR: Text-Guided 3D Human and Object Reconstruction with Textures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19679
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19679
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TeHOR achieves state-of-the-art textured 3D human-object reconstruction from a single image, significantly improving Chamfer distance and contact accuracy on Open3DHOI and BEHAVE datasets by using text-guided optimization with 3D Gaussians.

## Executive Summary
TeHOR solves the problem of reconstructing a textured 3D human and object in interaction from a single RGB image. Existing methods that rely on contact region priors fail for non-contact interactions and ignore global appearance context. TeHOR leverages text descriptions of the interaction, extracted by a vision-language model (GPT-4), to guide a joint optimization of human and object geometry, pose, and texture. Both are represented as 3D Gaussians—a flexible, topology-free format—and refined using a combination of reconstruction, appearance (via score distillation from a diffusion model), contact, and collision losses. This approach yields coherent, physically plausible reconstructions even for non-contact scenarios and sets new performance records on standard benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two stages. First, initial 3D representations are independently obtained: the human via a feed-forward model (LHM) fed a segmented, object-removed image, and the object via InstantMesh applied to an isolated object crop, both converted to 3D Gaussians. The background is inpainted. Text prompts (`P_holistic` describing the overall interaction, `P_contact` listing body parts in contact) are generated by GPT-4. Second, a joint optimization refines the Gaussian attributes and pose parameters of both human and object. At each step, multi-view renderings are evaluated against the input image (`L_recon`), guided by the holistic text via diffusion model score distillation (`L_appr`), constrained to attach at predicted contact regions (`L_contact`), and penalized for interpenetration (`L_collision`). Finally, the optimized Gaussians are converted to meshes for quantitative evaluation by aligning contact regions between the Gaussian surface and the base meshes via a local vertex shift.

## Key Results
- **Chamfer Distance (cm):** Human: 4.941 vs. 5.111; Object: 16.701 vs. 19.363 (improvement over prior state-of-the-art).
- **Contact F1-score:** 0.412 vs. 0.348.
- **Collisions:** Significantly reduced interpenetration compared to baselines.
- Robust performance on non-contact interactions where contact-based methods fail.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Text-Guided Semantic Alignment via Diffusion Prior
- **Claim:** Text descriptions from a VLM provide semantic guidance that enables accurate reconstruction of non-contact and holistically plausible human-object interactions (HOI).
- **Mechanism:** The framework optimizes 3D Gaussians by distilling visual knowledge from a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) using Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). Gradients conditioned on the holistic text prompt (`P_holistic`) update the Gaussians to align rendered novel views with the semantic content of the text.
- **Core assumption:** A powerful diffusion model encodes the joint distribution of images and text; optimizing 3D assets to minimize SDS loss moves their renderings toward this plausible appearance prior.
- **Evidence:** Abstract states text enforces semantic alignment for a wider spectrum of interactions including non-contact. Section 3.3 defines the appearance loss as aligning with the diffusion network's learned distribution. The approach builds on DreamFusion and TeCH but uniquely applies SDS to joint HOI reconstruction.
- **Break condition:** If the diffusion model lacks training examples of the described HOI, or the text prompt is ambiguous/noisy, SDS gradients become misaligned, leading to implausible appearances.

### Mechanism 2: Flexible 3D Gaussian Representation
- **Claim:** Representing human and object as 3D Gaussians provides a high-fidelity, topology-free representation better suited for absorbing appearance and geometric updates from the diffusion prior during joint optimization.
- **Mechanism:** 3D Gaussians are defined by position, covariance, opacity, and appearance features. They are differentiable, model fine textures and soft edges, and lack fixed topology, allowing non-rigid surfaces (e.g., cloth) to deform freely. During optimization, Gaussian attributes (`ϕ_h`, `ϕ_o`) are updated alongside pose parameters, enabling direct conformity to appearance guidance.
- **Core assumption:** A splat-based representation with per-Gaussian features can be optimized more effectively via image-space losses (like SDS) than a mesh constrained by fixed topology and parameterized textures.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.1 defines Gaussians following 3DGS. Table 3 shows Gaussians significantly outperform mesh representation due to high-fidelity appearance modeling and flexible structure for spatial relationship optimization.
- **Break condition:** Gaussians can become disordered or bloated without regularization; the non-manifold output requires conversion for applications needing watertight meshes.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Loss Joint Optimization
- **Claim:** A multi-loss scheme combining reconstruction, appearance, contact, and collision losses jointly refines geometry, pose, and texture for a globally coherent and physically plausible HOI.
- **Mechanism:** Total loss `L = L_recon + L_appr + L_contact + L_collision` balances competing objectives. `L_recon` matches the front-view rendering to input RGB and mask. `L_appr` provides global semantic guidance via text. `L_contact` enforces geometric attachment at predicted contact parts. `L_collision` discourages interpenetration.
- **Core assumption:** The four losses are complementary and optimized simultaneously via gradient descent to achieve visual consistency with the input, semantic alignment with text, physical contact, and non-penetration.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.3 define the losses mathematically. Ablation in Table 2 shows removing `L_appr` or `L_contact` degrades Chamfer and Contact metrics, validating their necessity.
- **Break condition:** Loss weighting is critical; improper balancing (e.g., over-weighting `L_appr`) may ignore precise image matching. The method also assumes the initial reconstruction is reasonable to avoid poor local minima.

## Foundational Learning

### Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Zero/Few-Shot Captioning
- **Why needed:** The entire guidance signal (`P_holistic`, `P_contact`) originates from a VLM (GPT-4). Understanding its prompt design, capabilities (in-context learning, spatial reasoning), and failure modes (hallucination) is crucial for predicting text quality and its impact on reconstruction.
- **Quick check:** If the VLM's `P_contact` output is "none" for a clear interaction, what are the two most likely causes (model limitation vs. prompt flaw)?

### Score Distillation Sampling (SDS)
- **Why needed:** `L_appr` is implemented via SDS. Prerequisites include how gradients from a frozen diffusion UNet optimize a 3D asset, the role of timestep sampling and classifier-free guidance, and inherent artifacts like the "Janus problem."
- **Quick check:** In the SDS loss formula, what is the effect of using a higher classifier-free guidance scale (`w_t`) on the strength and potential overfitting of the appearance guidance?

### 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
- **Why needed:** The core 3D representation is based on 3DGS. Understanding Gaussian definition, splatting, differentiable rasterization, and the role of covariance/opacity is essential.
- **Quick check:** Why is a "topology-free" representation advantageous for optimizing human-object spatial relationships compared to a mesh-based representation like SMPL-X?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Text Captioning Module (GPT-4) -> Initial Reconstruction Module (LHM, InstantMesh, SmartEraser) -> Joint HOI Optimization Module (Adam optimizer with `L_recon`, `L_appr`, `L_contact`, `L_collision`) -> Gaussians-to-Mesh Converter (local shift for contact alignment).

**Critical path:** The text-guided HOI optimization loop is the core innovation. It takes independently reconstructed human and object Gaussians and refines them jointly. Dataflow: `(current 3D Gaussians + background) → multi-view rendering → SDS loss computation (via diffusion model) → gradient backpropagation to 3D Gaussians and pose parameters`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Gaussians vs. Meshes: Gaussians enable easier appearance optimization but yield non-manifold outputs; meshes are standard for evaluation but harder to deform/texture. Tradeoff: optimize with Gaussians, convert to mesh for final output.
- Holistic Text vs. Contact Loss: `L_appr` provides global semantics but is noisy; `L_contact` offers precise local signal but is sparse/error-prone. Tradeoff: balance via loss weights (`λ_appr`, `λ_contact`).
- Single-Image Input: Severe ambiguity. The system injects strong priors (LHM, InstantMesh, diffusion) to resolve it, limiting generalization to highly unusual compositions.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Text misalignment:** Wrong/vague `P_holistic` → `L_appr` optimizes toward incorrect appearance. Symptom: renderings match text but not input image.
- **Contact prediction error:** Incorrect `P_contact` → `L_contact` pulls object to wrong human region. Symptom: object penetrates non-contact part or detaches from intended part.
- **Gaussian disorder:** Over-optimization with high `L_appr` weight → Gaussians blob, lose coherent surface. Symptom: blurry renderings, poor silhouette matching.
- **Collision persistence:** Insufficient `L_collision` weight or poor initial depth → human-object interpenetration. Symptom: high Collision metric, visible z-fighting.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablate Text Conditioning:** Run optimization with `P_holistic` set to empty or generic phrase. Expect severe degradation in semantic plausibility and object orientation, especially non-contact. Validate via `CD_object` and qualitative gaze/action alignment.
2. **Ablate 3D Gaussians:** Replace Gaussians with meshes (SMPL-X, InstantMesh) during optimization, keeping all else identical. Expect worse `CD_object` and `Contact` scores due to less flexible updates. Tests Gaussian advantage claim.
3. **Ablate Contact Loss:** Set `P_contact = "none"` or remove `L_contact`. Expect improved non-contact performance but degraded contact fidelity and increased collisions in contact scenarios. Measure Contact F1-score and Collision % change.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can fine-grained, region-specific supervision be incorporated into the text-guided optimization to reconstruct local details such as small accessories and subtle surface deformations? *Basis:* Section S8 notes the appearance loss lacks fine-grained supervision; localized text-driven supervision is a future direction.
- **Open Question 2:** How can TeHOR be extended to video input to achieve temporally coherent 3D human-object reconstructions across frames? *Basis:* Section S8 highlights maintaining temporal consistency as a key challenge for video extension.
- **Open Question 3:** How robust is TeHOR to errors or ambiguities in the text descriptions generated by the vision-language model? *Basis:* The method heavily relies on GPT-4 for prompts, but sensitivity to caption errors is unexamined.
- **Open Question 4:** To what extent does the choice of pre-trained diffusion model affect the quality and diversity of TeHOR's reconstructed HOI? *Basis:* No experiments compare different diffusion models; their varying semantics and biases could influence results.

## Limitations
- Reliance on VLM prompt quality; hallucinations or vague prompts misguide `L_appr` and `L_contact`.
- Diffusion prior bias; rare HOI categories may be uncovered, and SDS gradients are noisy, causing instability.
- Gaussian disorder without careful regularization; mesh conversion may lose contact details.
- Loss weighting sensitivity; suboptimal weights impair convergence.
- Single-image ambiguity; fails on atypical poses/occlusions.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Text-guided SDS):** Medium. Ablations support necessity, but VLM/diffusion dependence lowers certainty.
- **Mechanism 2 (3D Gaussians):** High. Direct mesh vs. Gaussian comparison (Table 3) shows clear gains.
- **Mechanism 3 (Multi-loss):** Medium-High. Ablations confirm each loss contributes; weighting robustness not fully explored.
- **Overall SOTA claim:** High. Consistent CD and Contact improvements on two benchmarks; dataset diversity limited.
