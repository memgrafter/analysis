# Learning Response-Statistic Shifts and Parametric Roll Episodes from Wave--Vessel Time Series via LSTM Functional Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.24431
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24431
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** An LSTM surrogate trained on URANS-simulated wave-motion data predicts vessel responses and captures parametric-roll-induced shifts in roll statistics (Gaussian to heavy-tailed PDFs), with amplitude-weighted MSE offering the best trade-off between time-domain accuracy and tail fidelity.

## Executive Summary
This study aims to create a data-driven surrogate model that predicts full vessel motion time series from incident wave elevation histories. A core challenge is capturing nonlinear phenomena like parametric roll, which induces regime-dependent changes in response statistics (e.g., heavy-tailed roll distributions). The authors train stacked LSTM networks on URANS simulation data for one vessel across three sea states, using different loss functions to balance temporal accuracy and statistical fidelity without explicit sea-state parameters. The surrogate successfully reproduces parametric roll onset and growth in held-out simulations and learns the associated shift in roll probability density functions. An amplitude-weighted MSE loss is found to provide the most balanced performance across sea states and motion components.

## Method Summary
The authors use paired time series of incident wave elevation and vessel motions (roll, pitch, heave) from high-fidelity URANS simulations. Data spans three sea states (SS–1, SS–2, SS–3), each with 49 realizations. Stacked LSTM networks are trained to map wave histories to motion time series, conditioned only on the wave input sequence and not on explicit sea-state parameters (e.g., significant wave height, peak period). Three loss functions are compared: standard MSE for point-wise accuracy, a relative-entropy-inspired loss to match output distributions, and an amplitude-weighted MSE that penalizes errors in larger amplitudes more heavily. Performance is evaluated on held-out time series via time-domain error metrics and distributional comparisons (e.g., PDFs, tail behavior).

## Key Results
*   The trained surrogate accurately reproduces the onset and growth of parametric roll oscillations in held-out simulated wave-motion pairs.
*   The model captures the sea-state-dependent shift in roll PDFs: near-Gaussian distributions in SS–1 and SS–2 transition to a distinctly heavy-tailed distribution in SS–3, aligning with the training data.
*   The amplitude-weighted MSE loss yields the most balanced performance, improving tail fidelity relative to standard MSE with a manageable increase in average error, and outperforming the relative-entropy-inspired loss in several metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LSTMs are inherently suited for learning long-range temporal dependencies in sequential data, allowing them to internalize the nonlinear coupling between wave excitation and vessel response dynamics from the training pairs. The amplitude-weighted MSE loss explicitly guides the model to prioritize accuracy during large-motion events (e.g., parametric roll episodes), which are responsible for the heavy tails in the response distribution. By avoiding explicit sea-state parameters, the model must learn to condition its predictions directly on the wave history, potentially encoding implicit representations of the underlying sea state that drive the statistical shifts.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Networks:** A recurrent architecture designed to capture long-term dependencies in sequence data, essential for modeling the memory effects in wave-vessel interactions. *Why needed:* To map variable-length wave elevation histories to corresponding motion time series. *Quick check:* Evaluate if prediction error accumulates over long horizons compared to shorter sequences.
*   **Unsteady Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes (URANS) Simulations:** A computational fluid dynamics method used to generate high-fidelity, physics-based training data of wave-vessel systems. *Why needed:* To provide realistic examples of parametric roll and associated statistical shifts for supervised learning. *Quick check:* Compare key simulated outputs (e.g., RAOs, roll decay) against available experimental or theoretical benchmarks.
*   **Parametric Roll:** A nonlinear instability phenomenon where a vessel's roll amplitude grows exponentially under certain wave encounter frequencies due to periodic variations in stability. *Why needed:* It is the primary regime where response statistics shift dramatically, posing a key challenge for prediction. *Quick check:* Identify in time series by checking for sustained, growing roll oscillations under following/quartering seas with specific wavelengths.
*   **Statistical Shifts in Response Distributions:** Changes in the probability density function (PDF) of vessel motions across sea states, particularly the emergence of heavy tails indicating a higher likelihood of extreme roll angles. *Why needed:* The goal is to reproduce not just average behavior but the full distribution, including rare dangerous events. *Quick check:* Compare empirical PDFs and tail statistics (e.g., 95th/99th percentiles) of predicted vs. actual roll motions.
*   **Loss Function Design for Distributional Fidelity:** Choosing training objectives (MSE, relative entropy, amplitude-weighted) that directly influence whether the model learns mean behavior, full distribution matching, or tail accuracy. *Why needed:* Standard MSE is insufficient for capturing tail events. *Quick check:* For each loss, plot predicted vs. actual PDFs and compute metrics like KL divergence or tail probability ratios on a validation set.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input (Wave Elevation Time Series) -> Stacked LSTM Layers -> Fully Connected Layer -> Output (Motion Time Series: Roll, Pitch, Heave)

**Critical Path:** The raw wave elevation sequence is fed into the first LSTM layer. Hidden states propagate sequentially through subsequent LSTM layers, allowing information to persist and be transformed over time. The final hidden state at each time step is passed through a dense output layer to produce the predicted motion components for that time step.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The core tradeoff is between point-wise temporal accuracy and distributional/tail fidelity. Standard MSE optimizes for low average error but often underestimates variance and extreme values. The relative-entropy-inspired loss aims to match the entire output distribution but can be unstable to train and may compromise time-domain alignment. The amplitude-weighted MSE introduces a scalar penalty on larger errors, explicitly encouraging better tail capture while largely preserving the simplicity and stability of the MSE framework.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   Under standard MSE, predicted roll time series show damped oscillations where true parametric roll grows, and the output roll PDF lacks heavy tails.
*   The relative-entropy-inspired loss may produce outputs with correct marginal distributions but misaligned temporal phases (e.g., incorrect timing of roll peaks relative to waves).
*   All models may fail to generalize to sea states with significantly different spectral characteristics or to vessel geometries not seen in training, producing unrealistic motion magnitudes or frequencies.

**First Experiments:**
1.  Train a baseline model using standard MSE loss. Evaluate on held-out sea states: compute RMSE for each motion component and visually/quantitatively compare roll PDFs (e.g., using kernel density estimates).
2.  Train a model using the amplitude-weighted MSE loss. Compare its performance against the baseline on tail metrics (e.g., 95th percentile roll error, kurtosis of predicted roll) and overall RMSE to quantify the trade-off.
3.  Perform an ablation study: train a model using the relative-entropy-inspired loss. Assess whether it improves PDF matching (e.g., lower KL divergence) compared to MSE and amplitude-weighted MSE, and at what cost to time-series phase accuracy (e.g., using cross-correlation).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalization beyond training data:** The paper does not test transfer to other vessel types or significantly different sea spectra, leaving open how universally the learned statistical shifts apply.
*   **Physical plausibility:** Without explicit conservation laws or hydrodynamic constraints, it is unknown whether predicted motions obey fundamental physical principles outside the training distribution.
*   **Loss function trade-offs:** The optimal balance between time-domain accuracy and tail fidelity via amplitude-weighted MSE is empirically determined; a theoretical understanding of this trade-off is lacking.
*   **Scalability of training data:** The study uses 49 realizations per sea state; the minimum required data volume for robust learning of parametric roll statistics is not established.

## Limitations
*   The study relies exclusively on URANS simulation data for a single vessel hull form and three specific sea states, limiting generalizability to real-world conditions and other ship types.
*   There is no explicit verification of the physical plausibility of the predicted motions (e.g., conservation of energy, expected damping characteristics), raising questions about whether the surrogate learns true dynamics or spurious correlations.
*   The reported trade-off between average error and tail fidelity is qualitative; the precise Pareto frontier across loss functions and metrics is not fully quantified.

## Confidence
*   **Claim:** The surrogate reproduces parametric roll onset and growth in held-out simulated series. **Confidence:** Medium. Supported by within-distribution testing on simulated data but lacks external validation.
*   **Claim:** The model captures the regime-dependent shift in roll PDF from near-Gaussian to heavy-tailed. **Confidence:** Medium. Based on visual PDF comparisons and loss function design; formal statistical tests for tail differences are not emphasized.
*   **Claim:** Amplitude-weighted MSE provides the most balanced performance. **Confidence:** Medium. Conclusion is relative to two other losses on a limited set of sea states and metrics; broader validation is needed.

## Next Checks
1.  **External Validation on Real Data:** Test the trained surrogate (or a retrained version) on held-out experimental model-test or full-scale measurement data containing documented parametric roll episodes to assess predictive capability beyond simulations.
2.  **Generalization Probe:** Evaluate the model's performance on a wider envelope of sea states (varying significant wave height, peak period, directional spreading) and, if possible, on a different vessel geometry to test transferability of the learned statistical shifts.
3.  **Physical Consistency Audit:** Compute derived physical quantities from the predicted motions (e.g., roll energy spectrum, estimates of linear and nonlinear damping) and compare them against values from the URANS training data and established hydrodynamic theory to identify implausible outputs.
