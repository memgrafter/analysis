# Under the Influence: Quantifying Persuasion and Vigilance in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21262
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21262
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Performance, persuasion, and vigilance in LLMs are dissociable capacities; high task ability does not guarantee vigilance.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models' (LLMs) ability to solve tasks is linked to their capacity to influence others (persuasion) and to detect manipulation (vigilance). Using a controlled multi-turn Sokoban puzzle game, the authors treat LLMs as both advisors (who may be benevolent or malicious) and players. They define quantitative metrics for task performance, persuasion (impact of advisor on player choices), and vigilance (player's resistance to malicious advice). The key finding is that these three capacities are dissociable: a model's puzzle-solving prowess does not predict its vigilance. For example, GPT-5 scores high on both persuasion and vigilance, while Grok 4 Fast is persuasive but lacks vigilance. Additionally, LLMs exhibit resource-rational token usage, modulating output length based on advice intent, though not all models consistently ignore known malicious advice when already capable. This work provides a framework for disentangling complementary but distinct aspects of LLM behavior in interactive settings.

## Method Summary
The experimental paradigm centers on a multi-turn Sokoban puzzle game. Two LLM agents are deployed: an **advisor** that observes the game state and provides textual advice to the player, and a **player** that decides moves based on the current state and optionally the advice. The advisor is manipulated to be either benevolent (helpful) or malicious (trying to mislead). The game proceeds turn by turn; after each player move, the advisor may offer new advice. Task performance is measured by whether and how efficiently the player solves the puzzle. Persuasion is quantified by the extent to which the player's decisions align with the advisor's recommendations, especially under malicious conditions. Vigilance is measured by the player's ability to disregard harmful advice, operationalized as a low alignment between player actions and malicious suggestions while still solving the puzzle. The study evaluates several proprietary LLMs, including GPT-5 and Grok 4 Fast, and collects behavioral data on token usage and decision patterns.

## Key Results
- **Dissociability**: Across models, task performance (puzzle-solving) shows no correlation with vigilance scores, indicating that being good at the task does not ensure resistance to manipulation.
- **Model profiles**: GPT-5 exhibits high persuasion and high vigilance; Grok 4 Fast is highly persuasive but has low vigilance, illustrating that persuasion and vigilance can vary independently.
- **Token modulation**: LLMs tend to use fewer tokens when receiving benevolent advice and more when receiving malicious advice. However, not all models selectively ignore malicious advice when already capable of solving the puzzle, challenging a simple resource-rational account.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not delve into the internal mechanisms that produce the observed dissociability between persuasion and vigilance. It notes that LLMs modulate token usage in a way that suggests resource-rational behavior—spending more tokens when faced with potentially harmful advice—but does not establish a causal link between this modulation and the ability to ignore malicious input. The authors leave the explanation of underlying cognitive or training-related factors for future research, focusing instead on empirical measurement and the demonstration that the capacities are separable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Sokoban Game → Player LLM ← Advisor LLM; Metrics Collector ↔ all components

**Critical Path**
1. Initialize Sokoban puzzle.
2. For each turn until puzzle solved or max turns:
   a. Present current game state to Player LLM; include Advisor's advice if available.
   b. Player LLM outputs a move.
   c. Execute move in Sokoban; update state.
   d. If advisor is active, feed current state to Advisor LLM to generate advice for next turn.
3. After game end, compute performance, persuasion, and vigilance scores.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- *Synthetic task*: Sokoban offers precise control and reproducibility but lacks ecological validity of real-world persuasion scenarios.
- *Limited model set*: Using only a few proprietary models reduces generalizability and hinders independent replication.
- *Short horizons*: The number of turns per game is bounded, possibly missing longer-term strategic effects of advice.
- *Advisor prompting*: The method to induce benevolence vs malice may not capture the full spectrum of persuasive intent.

**Failure Signatures**
- Player consistently fails to move or makes invalid actions, indicating misconfiguration.
- Advisor produces no advice or always repeats the same suggestion, losing variability.
- Persuasion score near zero even with benevolent advice, suggesting advice was ignored outright.
- Vigilance score high for all models, implying the malicious advice was not distinguishable from benevolent.
- Token count anomalies (e.g., extremely low) may signal early termination or refusal.

**First Experiments**
1. **Replicate with open models**: Test dissociability using Llama 3.1 70B, Mixtral, etc.
2. **Maliciousness gradient**: Introduce advice with varying degrees of subtlety to probe vigilance thresholds.
3. **Cross-task transfer**: Replace Sokoban with a different sequential decision-making game (e.g., text-based negotiation) to check robustness of dissociability.

## Limitations
- **Ecological Validity**: The Sokoban puzzle is a narrow, artificial environment; findings may not generalize to open-ended dialogues or complex social interactions.
- **Model & Sample Scope**: Evaluation focused on a small set of proprietary models and a limited human participant sample, reducing statistical power and representativeness.
- **Mechanistic Opacity**: The study measures behavioral outcomes but offers little insight into the internal reasons why persuasion and vigilance dissociate; the resource-rational token use hypothesis remains speculative.

## Confidence
- **Framework Validity**: High
- **Dissociability of Capacities**: Medium
- **Model-Specific Profiles**: Medium
- **Selective Ignoring of Advice**: Low

## Next Checks

3. Cross-task validation: adapt the experimental design to a fundamentally different sequential decision-making task (e.g., text-based negotiation) to determine if the dissociation persists outside Sokoban.
