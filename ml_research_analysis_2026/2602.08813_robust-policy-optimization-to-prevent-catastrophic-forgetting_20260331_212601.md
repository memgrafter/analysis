# Robust Policy Optimization to Prevent Catastrophic Forgetting
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08813
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08813
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** FRPO reduces catastrophic forgetting in multi-stage LLM training, improving safety metrics (higher HarmBench refusals, lower StrongREJECT scores) and preserving reasoning accuracy (~22% more MATH500 retention after code fine-tuning) with no extra computational cost over GRPO.

## Executive Summary
Catastrophic forgetting—the degradation of previously acquired capabilities during downstream fine-tuning—is a critical issue in multi-stage LLM training (e.g., RLHF followed by SFT). This paper introduces Fine-tuning Robust Policy Optimization (FRPO), a method that proactively optimizes a policy for robustness against expected downstream shifts. FRPO reformulates the RLHF objective as a max-min problem: the base policy maximizes the minimum expected reward over all policies within a specified KL divergence neighborhood (modeling reachable downstream policies). This dualizes to an entropic-risk objective that penalizes low-reward trajectories and avoids brittle, high-variance solutions. Integrated seamlessly into GRPO via simple reweighting and a baseline term, FRPO incurs no additional sampling cost. Empirically, FRPO significantly mitigates safety degradation after SFT/RL fine-tuning and preserves mathematical reasoning accuracy after coding adaptation, demonstrating broad efficacy across domains.

## Method Summary
FRPO addresses catastrophic forgetting by optimizing the base policy's reward robustness within a KL-bounded neighborhood that represents plausible downstream adaptations. Formally, it solves a max-min problem: maximize the minimum expected reward over all policies Q satisfying `E_x[KL(Q(·|x) || π_θ(·|x))] ≤ ρ`. Via duality, this yields an entropic-risk objective `J_λ = -E[λ log E_{π_θ}[exp(-r/λ)]] - β KL(π_θ || π_ref)`, where λ modulates risk sensitivity. To implement this efficiently, FRPO modifies the GRPO algorithm: it reweights group-centered advantages `A_i` by `exp(-A_i/λ)`, estimates a partition function `ˆZ_λ(x)` from on-policy samples (with jackknife bias correction), and adds a baseline term `λ * (1/G) Σ clip(π_θ/π_old)` to stabilize gradients. This adjustment requires no extra sampling and recovers GRPO as λ → ∞. The approach thus augments standard RLHF pipelines with minimal overhead while ensuring the resulting policy is resilient to subsequent fine-tuning steps that respect the KL budget.

## Key Results
- **Safety preservation:** After RLHF on safety data and subsequent Alpaca SFT, FRPO achieves higher refusal rates on HarmBench and lower harmfulness scores on StrongREJECT compared to GRPO, while maintaining similar Alpaca training loss.
- **Reasoning robustness:** In a math RL setting, FRPO preserves ~22% more accuracy on MATH500 after fine-tuning on code data (OpenCodeInstruct) relative to GRPO, with no sacrifice in downstream coding performance (MBPP+).
- **Efficiency:** FRPO integrates directly into GRPO with no additional sampling or computational cost beyond the baseline method.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: KL-Neighborhood Robustness**
FRPO prevents forgetting by optimizing a "flatter" reward landscape over a KL-bounded region of policies reachable via downstream adaptation. The max-min formulation ensures the base policy's expected reward remains high even if downstream updates move it within the allowed KL ball. This is equivalent to minimizing an entropic-risk penalty `-λ log E[exp(-r/λ)]`, which discourages high-variance policies by heavily penalizing low-reward rollouts. The core assumption—that typical fine-tuning (SFT/RL with KL regularization or small learning rates) stays within the budget—is reasonable for common practices. Evidence appears in the abstract and Section 3 derivation (Equations 3.1 and 3.5). The guarantee breaks if downstream updates are aggressive (e.g., high learning rates without KL constraints), potentially exceeding the assumed radius.

**Mechanism 2: GRPO Integration via Reweighting**
FRPO builds on GRPO's group-based advantage estimation. It replaces raw rewards in the GRPO objective with the entropic-risk term by reweighting group advantages `A_i` as `exp(-A_i/λ)` and incorporating an estimated partition function `ˆZ_λ(x)` computed from clipped importance ratios. A baseline term `λ * (1/G) Σ clip(π_θ/π_old)` cancels the leading drift in the gradient for large λ, ensuring smooth convergence to GRPO. This modification uses the same on-policy samples from `π_old` and adds negligible overhead. Evidence is detailed in Section 4 (Algorithm 1, Equation 4.1). The method becomes biased if group sizes are very small and the jackknife correction is omitted.

**Mechanism 3: Cross-Domain Transfer of Flat Minima**
FRPO's robustness transfers across safety and reasoning tasks because it inherently avoids brittle, high-reward peaks in the policy space. By tuning λ, FRPO finds flatter minima where small parameter shifts (from downstream fine-tuning) cause less reward degradation. Lower λ increases sensitivity to low-reward trajectories, promoting solutions with more consistent performance across the KL neighborhood. Empirically, this manifests as preserved safety after helpful SFT and maintained math accuracy after code fine-tuning. Evidence is in Section 5 results. However, if the downstream task reward is highly conflicting with the base task (e.g., extreme helpfulness-safety trade-offs), even flat minima may not fully prevent forgetting, making λ tuning critical.

## Foundational Learning
- **Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO):** FRPO's max-min over a KL ball is a DRO problem where the uncertainty set is a policy neighborhood. Understanding the dualization via Fenchel conjugates is essential: the KL constraint translates to an f-divergence set; choosing `f(x)=x log x` yields the forward KL and the specific entropic-risk conjugate. Quick check: How does the primal KL constraint become an f-divergence uncertainty set in the dual, and why does `f(x)=x log x` produce the forward KL?
- **Policy Gradient Methods (GRPO/PPO):** FRPO directly modifies GRPO. Grasping importance sampling, group-centered advantages, and clipping in GRPO is necessary to follow the loss derivation in Section 4. Quick check: In GRPO, why are advantages computed as within-group centered rewards, and how does clipping the importance ratio interact with the entropic-risk objective?
- **KL Divergence as a Trust Region:** The entire robustness guarantee models downstream fine-tuning as a KL-constrained update, standard in RLHF (e.g., PPO) and often implicit in SFT via learning rate/early stopping. Quick check: Why is the KL constraint averaged over the prompt distribution rather than per-prompt, and what does this averaging permit during downstream fine-tuning?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Base Model (π_ref) -> FRPO Module (within RLHF stage) -> Robust Policy (π_θ) -> Downstream Stages (SFT/RL fine-tuning)

Critical path: 1) Initialize π_θ from π_ref. 2) RLHF iteration: sample group of responses for each prompt from π_old, compute rewards, calculate centered group advantages A_i. 3) For each prompt, compute partition function ˆZ_λ(x) using clipped ratios and jackknife bias correction. 4) Compute loss J_corrected(θ) = -λ log ˆZ_λ(x) + λ * baseline - β KL(π_θ||π_ref). 5) Update π_θ. 6) Periodically set π_old ← π_θ. 7) Downstream fine-tuning proceeds normally on π_θ.

Design tradeoffs: λ controls risk-sensitivity—small λ promotes robustness to reward drops but may underfit current reward; large λ converges to GRPO. β controls the KL budget—larger β restricts π_θ closer to π_ref, limiting the feasible KL neighborhood for downstream shifts. Both require hyperparameter sweeps; the paper suggests optimal λ scales with the standard deviation of the reward distribution.

Failure signatures: 1) Policy collapse (random outputs) if the baseline term (Equation 4.2) is omitted, especially for large λ. 2) No safety improvement or downstream degradation if λ is too large (GRPO-like) or if β is mis-tuned, causing mismatched KL budgets. 3) High training variance and biased estimates if group size G is small and jackknife correction is not applied.

First 3 experiments: 1) **Replicate safety preservation:** Train Mistral/Qwen with FRPO (λ=0.2) and GRPO on a safety dataset, then perform SFT on Alpaca (lr=1e-6, 1 epoch). Measure HarmBench refusal rate and StrongREJECT score; FRPO should retain higher safety with similar Alpaca loss. 2) **Ablate λ and KL budget:** Sweep λ during safety RLHF, tuning β so both FRPO and GRPO end at matched per-token KL (≈0.1). After SFT, plot safety reward vs. KL; expect an optimal λ (e.g., 0.2) yields a flatter curve. 3) **Test transfer to math:** Train Qwen-Math with FRPO (λ=2.0, scaled to reward std. dev.) vs. GRPO, then SFT on code data (OpenCodeInstruct). Verify FRPO preserves >20% more MATH500 accuracy post-code fine-tuning while achieving comparable MBPP+ gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to pretraining and supervised fine-tuning:** Can the principle of optimizing for robustness to future adaptation be applied beyond RLHF? The paper suggests extension but hasn't tested it in regimes without reward models or policy gradients. Resolution would involve adapting the FRPO objective for pretraining/SFT and demonstrating reduced forgetting in continual learning benchmarks.
2. **Determinants of optimal λ:** Why do different capabilities (safety, reasoning) require different λ values? The paper reports domain-specific λ (e.g., 0.2 for safety, 2.0 for math) but doesn't explain underlying factors like reward distribution shape, task similarity, or policy landscape properties. Resolution would systematically correlate λ effectiveness with metrics like reward variance, flatness measures, and task relatedness across diverse capabilities.
3. **Robustness under KL-budget violation:** How does FRPO perform when downstream fine-tuning exceeds the assumed KL budget? All experiments use moderate KL constraints; aggressive updates (high LR, full fine-tuning) may violate the primal assumption. Resolution would evaluate FRPO under deliberately large KL divergences and, if robustness degrades, propose extensions (e.g., larger ρ, adaptive constraints, or alternative divergences like Wasserstein).

## Limitations
- **Major unknowns:** Precise harmless reward construction (calibration, whether combined with safety reward); exact KL measurement protocol (per-token vs. sequence-level) during RLHF; MATH training verifier details (exact string vs. symbolic matching, boxed answer bonus).
- **Low external validation:** Reference count is modest (38), and neighboring work rarely addresses KL-bounded policy neighborhoods for downstream forgetting; the core DRO-to-entropic-risk derivation is novel but sparsely anchored in citations.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (KL-neighborhood robustness):** Medium. The derivation is sound but critically depends on the downstream KL-budget assumption, which may not hold under aggressive fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism 2 (GRPO integration):** High. The algorithmic modification is explicit, converges to GRPO as λ→∞, and failure without baseline is clearly signposted.
- **Mechanism 3 (Cross-domain transfer):** Medium. Empirical results are strong but lack extensive hyperparameter sweeps (λ, β) across diverse tasks; the flat-minima analogy remains heuristic.

## Next Checks

Then apply identical SFT (Alpanca, lr=1e-6) and measure HarmBench/StrongREJECT degradation. Expect λ=0.2 to yield a flatter safety reward vs. KL trajectory.
2. **Stress-test KL budget assumption:** During safety RLHF, vary β to produce policies with different final KLs relative to π_ref. After SFT, evaluate whether a higher allowed KL (weaker β) indeed improves robustness—or if exceeding a task-specific threshold triggers forgetting regardless.
3. **Transfer to math with reward-scaled λ:** Train on MATH with FRPO using λ scaled to reward std. dev. (paper suggests λ=2.0), then fine-tune on code. Verify FRPO preserves >20% more MATH500 accuracy vs. GRPO while achieving comparable MBPP+ gains, confirming λ's role across reward scales.
