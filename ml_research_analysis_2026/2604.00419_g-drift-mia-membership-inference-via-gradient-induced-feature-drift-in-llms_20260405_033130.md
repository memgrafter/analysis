# G-Drift MIA: Membership Inference via Gradient-Induced Feature Drift in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00419
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00419
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a white-box MIA that achieves AUC up to 0.9998, substantially outperforming existing methods (best baseline ~0.88 AUC) under same-distribution conditions.

## Executive Summary
G-Drift MIA addresses the poor performance of existing membership inference attacks (MIAs) for large language models when member and non-member data come from the same distribution. The method is white-box, requiring full model access. It applies a single targeted gradient-ascent step to increase a candidate sample’s loss, then measures the resulting changes (drift) in internal representations—logits, hidden states, and feature projections. These drift signals train a lightweight logistic classifier that robustly separates members from non-members. Evaluated across multiple models (LLaMA, Gemma, GPT-Neo) and datasets, G-Drift yields near-perfect AUC scores, demonstrating that memorized samples exhibit smaller, more structured feature-projection drift than non-members.

## Method Summary
For each candidate sample, G-Drift first performs a forward pass in training mode to record pre-update states: loss \(L\), logit for the true answer token \(z_y\), final-layer hidden state \(h\), and a feature projection \(\alpha = h^\top v\) (where \(v\) is a fixed random unit vector). A single gradient-ascent step (\(\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta \nabla_\theta L\)) with \(\eta = 10^{-2}\) is applied to increase loss. A second forward pass with the updated parameters yields post-update states \(L'\), \(z'_y\), \(h'\), \(\alpha'\). Drift features include Euclidean hidden-state change \(\Delta h = \|h' - h\|_2\) and the six pre/post components. After processing all samples, a logistic regression classifier is trained on these feature vectors and labels. Crucially, original parameters are restored after each sample to prevent cumulative updates. The method assumes white-box access and that memorized samples occupy locally stable regions in the loss landscape.

## Key Results
- G-Drift achieves AUC up to 0.9998 on WikiMIA with LLaMA-3.1B, outperforming the strongest baseline (Min-k%) by over 0.11 AUC.
- Ablation studies show feature-projection drift (\(\Delta\alpha\)) is the most critical signal; removing it causes the largest AUC drop (e.g., LLaMA-3: 0.9600 → 0.9040).
- Memorized samples consistently show smaller average feature-projection drift than non-members (e.g., members \(\mu=0.332\) vs. non-members \(\mu=0.505\)), providing a clear separation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
A single targeted gradient-ascent step induces measurable changes in internal representations that differ systematically between member and non-member samples. The method computes \(\nabla_\theta L\) and applies \(\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta \nabla_\theta L\), then measures drift between pre- and post-update states. The core assumption is that memorized (member) samples occupy locally stable regions in the loss landscape; thus, a small parameter perturbation causes a structured, measurable shift in feature space that is distinguishable from the shift for non-members. Evidence anchors: the abstract describes measuring changes in internal representations; section 3.3 details the seven collected features.

### Mechanism 2
Feature-projection drift (\(\Delta\alpha = (h' - h)^\top v\)) is particularly discriminative, with members showing smaller average drift than non-members. Projecting hidden-state changes onto a fixed random direction reveals that memorized samples’ representations are more “anchored” along certain axes, resisting the perturbation more. The core assumption is that memorized data points are encoded in more polysemantic or robust feature directions. Evidence anchors: section 5.3 reports lower \(\Delta\alpha\) for members; section 5.2 ablation shows removing projections causes the largest AUC drop.

### Mechanism 3
Memorization is linked to representation stability under controlled gradient interventions; the geometry of feature drift provides a mechanistic signal. By measuring how representations shift in response to a deliberate “unlearning” perturbation, G-Drift probes the model’s reliance on that sample. Stronger, structured resistance (smaller drift) indicates the sample influenced training. Evidence anchors: the abstract states memorized samples exhibit smaller, more structured feature drift; section 5.3 analysis shows hidden-state drift is larger for members but projection drift is clearer.

## Foundational Learning

  - *Why needed:* Understands the goal of distinguishing training from non-training data and why classical MIA signals (e.g., confidence, likelihood) fail under same-distribution LLM settings.
  - *Quick check:* Why do confidence-based MIAs degrade to near-random performance on same-distribution LLM data?

- **Concept: Gradient-based Model Interrogation**
  - *Why needed:* G-Drift relies on computing \(\nabla_\theta L\) and applying a parameter update; understanding backpropagation and loss landscape geometry is essential.
  - *Quick check:* What is the effect of a small gradient-ascent step on a model’s parameters and internal representations?

- **Concept: Representation Geometry in Transformers**
  - *Why needed:* The method projects hidden states onto arbitrary directions; knowledge of activation spaces, residual streams, and linear probes is key to interpreting feature-projection drift.
  - *Quick check:* How might memorized information be distributed across neurons (polysemanticity), and why would a random projection axis still capture meaningful drift?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Target LLM -> Pre-update recorder -> Gradient perturbation module -> Post-update recorder -> Drift calculator -> Membership classifier. Critical path: forward pass (pre) -> gradient ascent -> forward pass (post) -> feature extraction -> logistic regression prediction. Parameters must be restored after each sample to avoid cumulative effects. Design tradeoffs: white-box vs. black-box limitation; single step chosen for efficiency and stability; random \(v\) for simplicity vs. potential SAE-based improvement. Failure signatures: AUC near 0.5 (non-separable drift); huge \(\Delta h\) variance (wrong \(\eta\) or instability); zero logistic coefficients (uninformative features); parameter reset failure; incorrect \(v\) shape; tokenization mismatch for multi-token answers. First 3 experiments: 1) Baseline comparison on WikiMIA with LLaMA-3.2-1B; 2) Ablation removing feature groups; 3) \(\eta\) sensitivity sweep (1e-4 to 1e-1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can G-Drift’s performance be improved by replacing random feature projections with directions from sparse autoencoders or other interpretability methods?
- Does gradient-induced feature drift generalize as an effective membership signal in multi-modal models (e.g., vision-language models)?
- Can aggregated drift evidence across many samples enable reliable dataset-level membership inference?
- Why do feature-projection drifts (\(\Delta\alpha\)) provide the most discriminative signal compared to loss or hidden-state norm?
- How effective is G-Drift against models trained with differential privacy, and does the attack signal persist under different privacy budgets (\(\epsilon\))?

## Limitations
- White-box requirement restricts applicability to open-source models; black-box APIs are excluded.
- Performance depends on the learning rate \(\eta\); the paper uses a fixed 0.01 without systematic sensitivity analysis.
- Random projection vector \(v\) may be suboptimal; theoretical justification for its effectiveness is weak.
- Fine-tuning details for inducing memorization are vague; results might not hold for models with minimal overfitting.
- Experiments are limited to small LLMs (\(\leq\)4B) and specific QA datasets; generalization to larger models and other domains is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **High:** G-Drift substantially outperforms existing MIAs (AUC up to 0.9998 vs. ~0.88) across multiple models and datasets.
- **Medium:** A single gradient-ascent step yields discriminative drift signals; feature-projection drift (\(\Delta\alpha\)) is the most critical component per ablation studies.
- **Medium:** The claim that memorized samples exhibit smaller, more structured drift is empirically supported but lacks a full mechanistic explanation.
