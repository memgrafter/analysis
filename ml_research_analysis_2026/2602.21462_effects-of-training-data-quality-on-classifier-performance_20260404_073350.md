# Effects of Training Data Quality on Classifier Performance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21462
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21462
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Training data quality critically affects classifier performance, with a sharp breakdown threshold where small degradations cause catastrophic, congruent failure across multiple classifiers.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates how training data quality impacts the performance of metagenomic read classifiers. Four classifiers—Bayes, neural network, partition model, and random forest—are trained on simulated Illumina reads from three virus genomes, with training data degraded via SNP corruption, mislabeling, selective degradation, and addition of irrelevant data. Performance is evaluated on a fixed validation set.

A universal breakdown threshold is observed near an SNP corruption probability of 0.75. Below this threshold, classifier correctness declines gradually and congruence remains high due to correct predictions. At the threshold, all classifiers experience a precipitous drop in correct classification rate, with predictions collapsing to a single class ("Adeno"). Above the threshold, correctness stays extremely low but congruence rises again because classifiers fail in the same way. Similar qualitative behavior appears for other degradation mechanisms, though thresholds differ. The findings demonstrate extreme sensitivity to training data quality and a critical regime where minor degradations lead to catastrophic, congruent failures.

## Method Summary
The study uses a controlled simulation framework. Base Illumina reads are generated from three virus genomes, providing a pristine dataset. Four degradation mechanisms are applied to the training data: (1) SNP corruption—random nucleotide substitutions at a specified probability; (2) mislabeling—random flipping of class labels at a given rate; (3) selective degradation—targeted corruption of one or more specific viruses; and (4) addition of irrelevant data—mixing in reads from unrelated organisms. For each degradation type and level, the four classifiers are trained on the resulting dataset and evaluated on a fixed validation set that remains unchanged across all conditions. Performance metrics include correct classification rate, classifier congruence (agreement among different models), and Boundary Status (a measure of decision boundary degeneration). This systematic approach quantifies the impact of incremental data quality reductions on classifier behavior.

## Key Results
- A universal breakdown threshold near SNP corruption probability 0.75: below it, correctness declines gradually with high congruence; at it, a sharp drop occurs and predictions collapse to a single class; above it, correctness remains very low but congruence rises again as all classifiers fail similarly.
- The breakdown is accompanied by a shift in the Boundary Status distribution, indicating severe degeneration of decision boundaries at the critical corruption level.
- Similar qualitative breakdown patterns are observed for mislabeling and selective degradation, though the precise threshold varies; addition of irrelevant data also yields comparable behavior.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The breakdown likely stems from a phase transition in the learning problem: as data integrity falls below a critical level, the class distributions become too ambiguous to support discriminative boundaries. Classifiers then revert to a simplistic, high‑prior or majority‑class strategy, explaining the collapse to a single prediction and the high congruence across diverse models. The sharp threshold indicates that the task abruptly becomes unsolvable beyond that point. The observed Boundary Status shifts confirm that the internal partitioning of the feature space degenerates at the threshold, rendering the classifiers effectively non‑functional.

## Foundational Learning
- **Training Data Quality**  
  *Why needed:* It is the manipulated factor that determines the amount of usable signal for learning.  
  *Quick check:* Verify that degradation parameters (e.g., SNP rate) are correctly applied to the training set and that the validation set remains unaltered.

- **Label Noise (Mislabeling)**  
  *Why needed:* Common in real-world datasets, it directly corrupts the target variable and tests model robustness.  
  *Quick check:* Introduce a known mislabeling fraction and inspect a sample to ensure the correct proportion of flipped labels.

- **Decision Boundary Degeneration**  
  *Why needed:* Quantifies how the classifier's ability to separate classes erodes with data degradation, directly linked to the breakdown.  
  *Quick check:* Monitor Boundary Status values across corruption levels; expect a sharp shift at the breakdown threshold.

- **Classifier Congruence**  
  *Why needed:* Measures agreement among different models; high congruence during failure indicates a shared simplistic failure mode.  
  *Quick check:* Compute pairwise prediction agreement (e.g., Cohen's kappa) for each degradation level; expect a spike at the threshold.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Read Simulator -> Degradation Module -> Training Set -> Classifier -> Validation Set -> Metrics

**Critical path**:  
1. Generate simulated reads from virus genomes.  
2. Apply selected degradation to produce a training set.  
3. Train the classifier on this set.  
4. Evaluate the trained model on the fixed validation set.  
5. Compute performance metrics (accuracy, congruence, Boundary Status).  
These steps must be executed sequentially for each degradation condition.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Classifier diversity*: Using a mix of simple (Bayes) and complex (neural net) models provides breadth but may limit in‑depth analysis of any single architecture.  
- *Degradation realism vs. control*: Simulated corruptions (SNP, mislabel) offer precise control but simplify real‑world noise complexities.  
- *Fixed validation set*: Ensures comparability across conditions but risks overfitting to that split and does not capture validation variability.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Sharp drop in correct classification rate at a specific degradation level.  
- Predictions collapse to a single class (e.g., "Adeno").  
- Increase in classifier congruence (high agreement among different models).  
- Marked shift in Boundary Status distribution.

**Three first experiments**:  
1. Sweep SNP corruption probability from 0 to 1 in fine increments and locate the threshold where correctness plummets and congruence spikes.  
2. Increase mislabeling rate systematically to determine if a similar breakdown threshold exists.  
3. Perform selective degradation by corrupting only one virus genome at a time, testing whether the threshold depends on which class is affected.

None.

## Limitations
- The experiments rely on simulated reads from only three virus genomes, limiting biological diversity and the generalizability of the breakdown threshold to broader microbial populations.
- Evaluation was performed on a single fixed validation set, which may overfit to that split and does not account for potential variability across different validation samples.
- Only four traditional classifiers were studied; modern deep learning methods (e.g., transformers) were not considered, leaving open whether the observed phenomenon extends to state‑of‑the‑art architectures.

## Confidence
| Claim | Confidence | Reason |
|-------|------------|--------|
| Classifier performance is highly sensitive to training data quality | High | Consistent, dramatic drops in correctness observed across all four classifiers and multiple degradation types. |
| A sharp “breakdown” threshold exists near specific corruption levels (e.g., SNP corruption ~0.75) | Medium | Supported by experimental trends, but exact value may vary with conditions; not tested across many replicates. |
| Above the threshold, classifiers exhibit congruent failure (predictions collapse to a single class) | Medium | Evident in measured congruence and class distribution; underlying cause not deeply analyzed. |
| Similar qualitative breakdown behavior occurs across different degradation mechanisms | Medium | Pattern reported for mislabeling and selective degradation, but precise thresholds differ; requires systematic comparison. |

## Next Checks
- **Threshold stability analysis**: Replicate the SNP corruption experiment with multiple random seeds, varied training/validation splits, and different training set sizes/hyperparameters to quantify variability in the breakdown threshold.
- **Real‑world validation**: Apply analogous degradation to a real metagenomic dataset with known quality issues or use naturally corrupted data to determine whether the threshold and congruent failure persist outside simulation.
- **Broader classifier and data diversity**: Test state‑of‑the‑art deep learning classifiers (e.g., transformers) and a large, phylogenetically diverse panel of microbial genomes to evaluate the generality of the observed phenomenon.
