# Causality in Video Diffusers is Separable from Denoising

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10095
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10095
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SCD reduces per-frame latency by 2-4× while maintaining or improving video quality metrics across TECO-Minecraft, UCF-101, and VBench.

## Executive Summary
Video diffusion models typically apply causal attention at every layer and denoising step, leading to redundant computation. This paper introduces Separable Causal Diffusion (SCD), which decouples temporal reasoning from frame-wise rendering. SCD employs a causal transformer encoder to compute a context latent once per frame, and a lightweight diffusion decoder that iteratively denoises each frame independently using that fixed context. This design exploits two observed regularities: high feature redundancy across denoising steps in early layers and sparse cross-frame attention in deep layers. Experiments demonstrate 2-4× speedups with equal or better perceptual and fidelity metrics compared to prior causal video diffusion models.

## Method Summary
SCD separates temporal reasoning and denoising via two components: a causal encoder \(E_\phi\) that processes clean history frames to produce a context latent \(c_i\) for each frame \(i\), and a diffusion decoder \(D_\theta\) that denoises the current frame using only \(x_i^t\) and \(c_i\) with no cross-frame computation. Training uses teacher forcing or diffusion forcing, with amortized multi-sample decoding: the encoder runs once per frame, while the decoder is applied multiple times with different noise samples. For fine-tuning from a bidirectional pretrained model, the architecture splits: early layers form the encoder, while a combination of early and late layers form the decoder. Input corruption and context injection are adjusted to match the teacher's distribution.

## Key Results
- On TECO-Minecraft (128×128), SCD-M is 4× faster (0.52 sec/frame vs 2.4 sec/frame) than Causal DiT-M, with improved LPIPS (0.179 vs 0.196), SSIM (0.525 vs 0.512), PSNR (19.3 vs 18.9), and FVD (36.1 vs 38.7).
- On UCF-101 (64×64), SCD-B is 2.2× faster (1.1 sec/frame vs 3.2 sec/frame) than FAR-B, with comparable or better metrics (LPIPS 0.037, SSIM 0.829, PSNR 26.00, FVD 158.7).
- For text-to-video (VBench, 832×480), fine-tuned SCD (1.6B) achieves 11.1 FPS vs 8.9 FPS for Self-Forcing (1.3B), with similar overall scores (84.03 vs 84.26) and 35% lower latency (0.29s vs 0.45s).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Amortized Early-Layer Computation via Context Encoder**  
Early and middle transformer layers in autoregressive video diffusion produce highly similar features across denoising steps for a given frame (cosine similarity >0.95), indicating redundant computation. SCD's causal encoder \(E_\phi\) runs once per frame to compute a context latent \(c_i\) that summarizes historical information, which is then reused across all denoising steps for that frame. This eliminates repeated temporal reasoning. The approach assumes early-layer features stabilize quickly and depend more on context than noise level. Evidence includes high cosine similarity in layer 15 across 50 steps and PCA showing first-step features capture global structure. However, similarity drops to ~0.8 in the last 10 steps, so amortization may not capture late refinements.

**Mechanism 2: Intra-Frame Rendering via Shallow Decoder**  
Deep transformer layers in causal video diffusion primarily perform intra-frame attention with sparse cross-frame interactions. SCD's diffusion decoder \(D_\theta\) processes only the current frame's tokens and the fixed context \(c_i\) using bidirectional self-attention, removing all cross-frame computation. This is justified by attention mass visualizations showing deep layers (e.g., layers 25-29) attend minimally to past frames, and fine-tuning with a frame-diagonal mask in the last 5 layers preserves quality. The assumption is that temporal dynamics are sufficiently encoded in \(c_i\). This may fail for videos requiring dense, step-sensitive cross-frame interactions.

**Mechanism 3: Decoupled Training and Inference Pipeline**  
The separation allows amortizing compute, efficient KV caching, and flexible capacity allocation. During training, \(E_\phi\) encodes once per clean frame, and \(D_\theta\) is run multiple times with different noises (multi-sample decoding). During inference, \(E_\phi\) uses cached KV for efficient rollout, and \(D_\theta\) denoises without cross-frame access. The context \(c_i\) acts as a sufficient statistic for frame \(i\)'s content and motion. This design enables the observed speedups while maintaining quality, as shown on multiple benchmarks. If \(E_\phi\) is too shallow or the task requires iterative cross-frame updates, consistency may degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Causal Encoder \(E_\phi\) -> context latent \(c_i\) -> Diffusion Decoder \(D_\theta\)

**Critical path:** For each frame \(i\):  
1. Run \(E_\phi\) on history frames \(x_{<i}\) (with KV cache) to get \(c_i\).  
2. Initialize noise \(x_i^T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\).  
3. For \(t = T\) down to \(1\): run \(D_\theta(x_i^t, t, c_i)\) to predict velocity \(\hat{v}_i^t\), sample \(x_i^{t-1}\).  
4. Store clean frame \(x_i^0\) for future context.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Encoder depth (\(\ell\)): More layers improve consistency but increase per-frame cost (amortized).  
- Decoder depth (\(m\)): More layers improve per-frame detail but cost multiplies by denoising steps \(S\).  
- Context corruption (\(\eta_t, \eta_{cfg}\)): Mild noise on \(c_i\) during training improves robustness; CFG at inference can boost adherence but may over-constrain.  
- Input reparameterization for encoder: During fine-tuning, feed highly corrupted current frame (top 20% \(t\)) to match teacher's input; at inference use Gaussian noise.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Degraded long-horizon consistency if \(E_\phi\) is too shallow (FVD spikes).  
- Poor rendering quality (high LPIPS/SSIM loss) if \(D_\theta\) is too shallow.  
- Artefacts (blurring, motion loss) from excessive context corruption.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Feature similarity profiling: Compute cosine similarity of hidden states at middle layers (e.g., layer 15) across denoising steps for the same frame. Expect >0.95 to validate amortization.  
2. Attention sparsity visualization: For deep layers (last 5), compute cross-frame attention mass (queries at frame \(i\) to keys at frames \(<i\)). Expect <10% to validate intra-frame decoder.  
3. Skip-layer finetuning: Finetune baseline by skipping middle layers on all but first few denoising steps. If quality recovers (LPIPS/FVD similar to baseline), it confirms step-wise redundancy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What are the scaling laws for SCD's encoder size versus inference efficiency and generation quality, and how do they compare to autoregressive language models on next-frame prediction tasks?  
2. Can SCD's decoupled architecture accelerate rollout-based training frameworks like Diffusion Forcing or Self-Forcing, and what training protocols best leverage its structure?  
3. How can SCD retain the small but non-zero cross-frame attention in deep layers (which may be critical for high-resolution quality) while preserving its computational advantages?

## Limitations
- **Feature similarity decay:** Cosine similarity drops to ~0.8 in final denoising steps, suggesting amortization may not fully capture late-stage refinements, risking detail loss.  
- **Cross-frame attention sparsity assumption:** Deep layer sparsity observed on TECO-Minecraft and UCF-101 may not generalize to videos with complex physical dependencies, potentially harming long-term consistency.  
- **Reproduction gaps:** Critical details like exact token dimensions and full layer-split configurations for all model scales are unspecified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- Amortized early-layer computation: High for early denoising steps (empirical support), Medium for full trajectories due to observed decay.  
- Intra-frame rendering via shallow decoder: High on sparsity evidence from attention analysis, Medium for tasks requiring dense temporal interactions.  
- Overall efficiency and quality gains: High on reported benchmarks, but generalizability to unseen video domains remains Medium.
