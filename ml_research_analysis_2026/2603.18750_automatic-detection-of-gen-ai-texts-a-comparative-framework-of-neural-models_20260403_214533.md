# Automatic detection of Gen-AI texts: A comparative framework of neural models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18750
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18750
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Supervised neural detectors (e.g., MobileNet CNN) provide more stable and robust AI-text detection across languages and domains than commercial tools, with MobileNet achieving 91.67% accuracy on a balanced English dataset.

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks four supervised neural architectures (MLP, CNN 1D, MobileNet CNN, Transformer) against eight commercial AI-detection tools. Evaluated on multilingual (English, Italian) and thematic datasets, the supervised models demonstrated superior stability and robustness. On a balanced English test set, the MobileNet CNN achieved the best accuracy trade-off (91.67%). For Italian single-class detection, lightweight models (MLP, CNN 1D) reached 100% accuracy while many commercial tools degraded significantly. On a challenging thematic dataset (Art & Mental Health), the proposed models maintained 98.3% accuracy, whereas a commercial tool failed completely (0% detection). The findings indicate that transparent, supervised learning with curated data yields more reliable detectors than proprietary heuristic-based systems.

## Method Summary
The study implements a unified binary classification pipeline: raw text is tokenized, embedded, and processed by one of four neural feature extractors. These are a simple MLP with masked pooling, a 1D CNN for local n-gram patterns, an efficient MobileNet CNN using depthwise-separable convolutions, and a Transformer for global context. A shared global pooling layer and a sigmoid classifier head produce a probability score, which is thresholded (τ) via validation-set calibration. Models are trained on three datasets: a balanced English corpus (dtEN), an Italian GenAI-only set (dtITA), and an Italian thematic Art & Mental Health corpus (ART&MH). Performance is evaluated on held-out 60-sample test sets using overall accuracy and per-class recall rates. Commercial tools are benchmarked via their public interfaces on the same test samples.

## Key Results
- The MobileNet CNN achieved the best balance on dtEN with 91.67% accuracy, 83.33% human recall, and 95.24% GenAI recall.
- On the Italian single-class dtITA, lightweight models (MLP, CNN 1D) attained 100% GenAI detection, while several commercial tools fell below 60%.
- Proposed models maintained 98.3% accuracy on the challenging thematic ART&MH dataset, in stark contrast to the Writer tool's 0% GenAI detection rate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*Claim:* Specialized neural architectures learn hierarchical textual features (local patterns, long-range dependencies) that consistently distinguish AI-generated from human text, outperforming heuristic-based commercial tools.
*Mechanism:* Each model's feature extraction block (MLP, CNN, MobileNet, Transformer) processes token embeddings to capture different inductive biases (e.g., n-grams vs. contextual relationships). The core assumption is that AI text exhibits learnable statistical deviations. Evidence: Abstract asserts stable performance; Section 3 details architectural differences; cross-dataset results show consistent model superiority.
*Break condition:* Performance degrades under extreme domain/language shifts or if AI generation techniques evolve to mimic the training distribution's statistical signature.

### Mechanism 2
*Claim:* Supervised learning on balanced, curated datasets allows for transparent threshold calibration, enabling controllable trade-offs between sensitivity and specificity, unlike opaque commercial systems.
*Mechanism:* A validated threshold (τ) is set per model on a validation split, explicitly balancing false positives and false negatives. Commercial tools use fixed, black-box scores. Evidence: Section 4.1 shows calibration-driven trade-offs (e.g., MLP high human recall, CNN 1D high GenAI recall); Section 4.2 shows commercial tool instability on dtITA.
*Break condition:* Poorly representative, small, or poisoned training data leads to non-generalizable calibration, as hinted in cross-domain analysis.

### Mechanism 3
*Claim:* Training on linguistically and thematically diverse data (e.g., ART&MH) builds more generalized feature representations, enhancing robustness to distributional shifts.
*Mechanism:* Exposure to varied styles reduces overfitting to spurious correlations in a single dataset, improving transferability. Evidence: Table 5 shows ART&MH-trained models outperformed dtEN-trained models on the Italian dtITA; models maintained high accuracy on ART&MH while commercial tools failed.
*Break condition:* Extremely radical shifts (e.g., essays to poetry/code) may exceed the learned manifold's coverage.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Text Embeddings and Sequence Tokenization**
  *Why needed:* The pipeline starts by converting raw text into a fixed-length sequence of dense vectors via tokenization and an embedding layer. This continuous representation is what neural blocks process.
  *Quick check:* How does the choice of maximum sequence length (L) and padding strategy impact a model's ability to handle documents of varying lengths, and what biases might this introduce?
- **Concept: Architectural Paradigms for Sequential Data (MLP, CNN, Transformer)**
  *Why needed:* The comparative analysis hinges on how each architecture (feed-forward, local-convolutional, global-attentional) extracts features from the embedded sequence, making different inductive biases about the discriminative signal.
  *Quick check:* What is the computational and representational trade-off between a 1D CNN with a small kernel and a Transformer with self-attention when modeling long text sequences for binary classification?
- **Concept: Binary Classification Calibration and Evaluation Metrics**
  *Why needed:* The paper emphasizes threshold tuning (τ) and reports class-wise recall alongside accuracy, highlighting that a single metric is insufficient to assess detector reliability in high-stakes applications.
  *Quick check:* In AI text detection for academic integrity, why might a system with high Human recall (low false positives) be prioritized over one with high GenAI recall, and how does this affect the choice of decision threshold?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Raw Text → Tokenizer → Token ID Sequence → Pad/Truncate (Length L) → Embedding Layer (Matrix E ∈ ℝ^(L×d)) → Neural Feature Extractor (Variant: MLP/CNN1D/MobileNet/Transformer) → Feature Maps H ∈ ℝ^(L×k) → Global Pooling → Fixed Vector h ∈ ℝ^k → Dropout → Dense Layer → Sigmoid Output ŷ → Compare to Threshold τ → Predicted Label
- **Critical path:** 1. Prepare balanced/single-class training data. 2. Select model architecture based on dataset scale (e.g., avoid Transformer for tiny dtITA). 3. Train with regularization (dropout, weight decay). 4. Calibrate decision threshold τ on validation set. 5. Evaluate on fixed test set, compute accuracy and per-class recall.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Model Complexity vs. Data Size (Transformer needs more data); Bias Trade-off (τ controls sensitivity-specificity); Transparency vs. Performance (proposed models are transparent, commercial tools are not but may appear higher on some tests).
- **Failure signatures:** CNN 1D on dtEN predicts all GenAI (0% Human recall) indicating feature overfit; Transformer (dtEN) drops on dtITA showing language sensitivity; Writer tool predicts all Human on ART&MH (0% GenAI recall) indicating mode collapse; commercial tools show large performance swings across datasets.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate MLP baseline on dtEN: Train with embedding dim 128, hidden 128, dropout 0.2, τ∈[0.35,0.40]. Expect ~85% accuracy with high Human recall (~97%).
  2. Evaluate MobileNet on dtEN: Use embedding dim 256, max len 1024, batch 192, τ=0.36. Expect best trade-off: 91.67% accuracy, 83.33% Human recall, 95.24% GenAI recall.
  3. Test cross-domain generalization: Evaluate MLP and CNN 1D trained on ART&MH (Italian) on dtITA (Italian). Expect >90% GenAI detection, confirming domain/language-aligned training improves robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do detection architectures generalize across diverse languages and subdomains, and what linguistic or stylistic factors underlie robustness under language shift?
- Can ensemble or hybrid detection strategies that combine heterogeneous neural architectures (e.g., CNN, Transformer) improve robustness and generalization under complex distributional shifts?
- How can decision thresholds be adaptively calibrated per language or domain to minimize false positives in sensitive applications without significantly reducing sensitivity?
- Does incorporating uncertainty estimation (e.g., via Bayesian methods or ensemble variance) enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of detectors by providing well-calibrated confidence scores?

## Limitations
- Exact tokenizer/vocabulary, training/validation split strategies, optimizer/scheduler details, and random seeds are not specified, blocking full replication.
- Commercial tool internals are opaque; their performance benchmarking is a black-box comparison subject to change.
- Generalizability is limited to tested languages (English, Italian) and one thematic domain; claims about robustness across broader contexts remain unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **Supervised models outperform commercial tools across languages/domains:** High. Strong internal evidence from consistent multi-dataset experiments.
- **Specialized architectures capture discriminative AI-text features:** Medium. Comparative framework shows differing behaviors but lacks ablation isolating architectural effects from dataset confounders.
- **Domain/language-aligned training enhances robustness:** Medium. Cross-domain results support this, but language and domain shifts are conflated; a controlled single-factor study is absent.
