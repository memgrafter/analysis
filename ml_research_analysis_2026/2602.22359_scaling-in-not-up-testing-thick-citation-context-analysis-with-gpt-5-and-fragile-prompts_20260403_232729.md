# Scaling In, Not Up? Testing Thick Citation Context Analysis with GPT-5 and Fragile Prompts

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.22359
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22359
*   **Reference Count:** 35
*   **Primary Result:** Prompt scaffolding and nudges systematically shaped GPT-5's interpretative hypotheses about a single hard citation case, revealing 21 recurring moves and demonstrating that LLMs can function as guided co-analysts for thick citation context analysis.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models, specifically GPT-5, can support *interpretative* citation context analysis—a "thick" reading of a single, complex citation case—as an alternative to traditional "scaling up" approaches that classify many citations with simple labels. Using a canonical footnote from science studies, the authors implement a two-stage prompting pipeline: first, a stable surface classification; second, a cross-document interpretative reconstruction. Across 90 experimental runs, they find that prompt design (4-step scaffolding vs. 1-step, and thematic nudges) critically shapes the distribution of 21 inductively derived interpretative moves. The model reliably detects textual mismatches between documents but resolves them preferentially as lineage or positioning moves rather than admonishment. The study concludes that LLMs can widen the space of text-grounded interpretations, but their outputs are highly sensitive to prompt scaffolding, positioning them as tools for guided exploration rather than automated analysis.

## Method Summary
The study employs a two-stage pipeline on a single hard case: footnote 6 in Chubin & Moitra (1975), citing Gilbert & Woolgar (1974) and Price (1970). **Stage 1** uses a citation-text-only prompt to perform a stable classification (Chubin & Moitra's scheme) and generate expectations about the cited works. Its outputs are sampled to seed **Stage 2**, where the full texts of all papers are provided. Stage 2 uses a 2x3 prompt design: a base prompt (either 4-step scaffolding or 1-step) combined with one of three nudges (Toward, Away, No-nudge). Each run generates five interpretative hypotheses. The 450 resulting hypotheses are inductively coded for 21 binary thematic "moves." Analysis uses linear probability models (clustered by run) to test prompt effects on code frequencies and lexical echo.

## Key Results
*   GPT-5 generated 450 hypotheses revealing a repertoire of 21 recurring interpretative moves, from lineage establishment to social/didactic readings.
*   Prompt scaffolding had a systematic effect: 4-step prompting increased lineage/positioning moves (e.g., Canon, Priority-Positioning) and decreased pragmatic/agile readings.
*   Nudges steered the model toward specific thematic clusters (e.g., "Toward" increased muting and bridging; "Away" increased social/teaching moves) and produced detectable lexical echo in the generated text.
*   The model almost always detected a key textual hinge—that Gilbert & Woolgar omit Price at a definitional moment—but resolved it as a lineage/positioning issue rather than an admonishment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Structured scaffolding biases toward lineage and authority moves
*   **Claim:** The 4-step prompt, by breaking work into expectation checks, cue analysis, extended context, and hypothesis generation, increases frequency of genealogical and positioning readings (e.g., Canon, PrioP, UseGW) and decreases pragmatic/agile readings.
*   **Mechanism:** The stepwise structure forces explicit documentation of mismatches and cues, which in this case highlight reiterated phrasing and the missing Price citation. These become evidentiary hinges for hypotheses that recast the citation's role as managing lineage or authority.
*   **Core Assumption:** The model uses intermediate outputs to inform final hypotheses; scaffolding makes certain evidence more salient.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "4-step scaffolding increased lineage/positioning readings"
    *   [section 4.3] "The scaffolded procedure makes the model less likely to foreground pragmatic and flexible handling ... and more likely to foreground genealogical or authority work"
*   **Break Condition:** If intermediate steps are ignored (e.g., due to context limits), the scaffolding effect may vanish.

### Mechanism 2: Nudging primes lexical and thematic clusters
*   **Claim:** Illustrative paragraphs in nudge conditions steer the model by providing storyline templates and vocabulary, which then appear in hypotheses (lexical echo), shifting the distribution of interpretative moves.
*   **Mechanism:** Nudge examples activate related concepts and phrasing, making those moves more likely. The "Toward" nudge's emphasis on "lineage" and "muted critique" increases corresponding codes.
*   **Core Assumption:** LLMs are sensitive to prompt phrasing and will reuse provided lexical items and narrative frames.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "nudges steered toward specific themes (e.g., Toward increased priority/muting, Away increased social/didactic readings)"
    *   [section 4.3] "Toward increases PrioP, MuteGW, Bridge; Away increases SSS and Teach"; also lexical echo analysis.
*   **Break Condition:** If the model is highly diversified or the nudge conflicts with textual evidence, the priming effect may be negligible.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-document expectation checking grounds hypotheses in documentary mismatches
*   **Claim:** Providing full texts and instructing the model to check initial expectations against them enables detection of mismatches (e.g., G&W not citing Price at the definitional moment), which become anchors for alternative hypotheses.
*   **Mechanism:** Stage 1 produces expectations from the citation string alone. Stage 2 compares these to actual content, revealing discrepancies that complicate the surface reading.
*   **Core Assumption:** The model can reliably parse/compare across documents and incorporate comparisons into hypotheses.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "GPT-5 detected the same textual hinges yet more often resolves them as lineage and positioning"
    *   [section 4.4] "In expectation checks, the model almost always noticed that G&W do not cite Price when defining the ref–cit distinction (41 of 45 runs)"
*   **Break Condition:** If texts are too long or extraction fails, the checking step may not yield useful mismatches.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Interpretative pluralism in citation context analysis**
    *   **Why needed:** The scaling-in approach accepts multiple plausible readings for a single citation, especially with indirect cues, contrasting with traditional CCA seeking a single correct label.
    *   **Quick Check:** What are two different ways to interpret footnote 6 in Chubin & Moitra (1975), and what textual features support each?
*   **Concept: Prompt chaining and stepwise decomposition**
    *   **Why needed:** The two-stage pipeline and 4-step substeps break a complex interpretative task into manageable pieces, allowing sequential focus.
    *   **Quick Check:** How does the 4-step prompt differ from the 1-step prompt, and what might be the benefit of each?
*   **Concept: Lexical echo and nudge sensitivity**
    *   **Why needed:** Prompt examples can bias output vocabulary and themes, crucial for designing prompts that widen rather than narrow the interpretative space.
    *   **Quick Check:** To encourage lineage-focused readings without forcing a conclusion, what nudge phrase would you include? Give an example.
*   **Concept: Cross-document expectation checking**
    *   **Why needed:** Grounding interpretations in comparisons between a citation's surface presentation and the actual content of cited documents prevents shallow readings.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why might comparing Stage 1 expectations (from citation string alone) with Stage 2 (full texts) reveal more nuanced interpretations?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input -> Stage1 (Surface Classification + Expectations) -> Sampling (15 outputs) -> Stage2 (Reconstruction with Full Texts) -> Coding (21 binary codes) -> Analysis (LPMs)

*   **Component Map:**
    *   **Input:** Citation context text, full-text PDFs of citing and cited papers.
    *   **Stage 1 module:** Prompt for surface classification (Chubin & Moitra categories) and expectation generation. Output: JSON with classification and expectations.
    *   **Sampling:** Random sample of Stage 1 outputs to seed Stage 2.
    *   **Stage 2 module:** Variant prompts (4-step or 1-step) with optional nudge paragraphs. Inputs: Stage 1 outputs + full texts. Outputs: Five hypotheses with justifications; for 4-step also intermediate outputs.
    *   **Coding scheme:** 21 binary codes derived from close reading of hypotheses.
    *   **Analysis:** Linear probability models (LPMs) with cluster-robust SEs by run to test prompt effects on code frequencies and lexical echo.

*   **Critical Path:**
    1. Prepare Stage 1 prompt and run 30 times to assess stability.
    2. Sample 15 Stage 1 outputs for use as Stage 2 seeds.
    3. For each of the 6 experimental conditions (2 base × 3 nudges), run Stage 2 for every seed (90 runs total).
    4. Collect all hypotheses (450) and perform inductive close reading to develop the 21 codes.
    5. Code each hypothesis for the 21 subthemes and for nudge-related vocabulary.
    6. Estimate LPMs (`Y ~ Base + Toward + Away + Base:Toward + Base:Away`) clustering by run.

*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **4-step vs 1-step:** 4-step provides interpretable intermediates but may constrain final hypothesis space; 1-step allows freer generation but risks less grounded output.
    *   **Nudges:** Steering explores specific interpretative regions but risks reducing diversity and introducing bias.
    *   **Sample size:** 90 runs (450 hypotheses) balances depth for coding with manageable close reading; larger scale sacrifices nuance.
    *   **Model choice:** Proprietary GPT-5 ensures capability but limits reproducibility and may have alignment biases.

*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   Stage 1: High variance in surface classifications (should be stable, mostly supplementary).
    *   Stage 2: Low hypothesis diversity (many duplicates); failure to use full texts; JSON parsing errors.
    *   Nudge effects: No detectable difference in code frequencies or vocabulary across conditions.
    *   Instruction non-compliance: Model not producing exactly 5 hypotheses or not grounding in evidence.

*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1. Reproduce Stage 1 stability: Run surface classification 30 times; expect >90% Supplementary-Perfunctory or Additional-Information.
    2. Test prompt variation effects: Run all 6 conditions; use LPMs to see if 4-step increases Canon/PrioP and decreases Agile/Pragma; Toward increases MuteGW/Bridge; Away increases Teach/SSS.
    3. Verify lexical echo: Check if hypotheses under Toward contain more "genealogy", "slippage", etc., and under Away contain "student", "social", etc., using FDR correction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Does the two-stage prompting pipeline produce similarly structured interpretative spaces when applied to a diverse range of citation contexts beyond this single hard case?
*   **Basis in Paper:** The authors state the pipeline is a proof of concept needing testing on other citation contexts; the study centers on one canonical case.
*   **Why Unresolved:** It is unknown whether the approach generalizes to routine citations, different domains, or other referencing forms; it may yield implausible or shallow readings for less complex cases.
*   **Resolving Evidence:** Systematic replication across varied citation contexts, measuring hypothesis diversity, textual grounding, and code frequency distributions; testing if the 21-code repertoire holds or adapts.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How do different LLMs (e.g., open-weight vs. proprietary, varied alignment objectives) influence the distribution of interpretative moves and susceptibility to prompt framing effects?
*   **Basis in Paper:** The authors used only GPT-5 and note its agreeableness tendencies may bias against conflict-forward readings; they suggest other models' alignment regimes might yield a different balance.
*   **Why Unresolved:** Model-specific training and tuning could fundamentally alter which interpretative space regions are explored, raising portability questions.
*   **Resolving Evidence:** Running the same 2×3 prompt design on multiple LLMs, comparing frequency and co-occurrence of key codes, and analyzing lexical echo patterns for model-dependence.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What concrete practices and tools enable human analysts to curate LLM-generated hypotheses effectively, avoiding over-reliance on model outputs and mitigating prompt-induced blind spots?
*   **Basis in Paper:** The framework positions analysts as curators and critics but offers no specific guidelines; the authors warn prompts can amplify analyst bias and require LLM literacy.
*   **Why Unresolved:** The model-human division of labor is conceptually clear but practically underspecified; analysts need strategies to evaluate plausibility, select readings, and maintain critical distance.
*   **Resolving Evidence:** Developing/testing curation protocols (e.g., textual anchoring checklists, bias-probing prompts), observing scholars using the pipeline in real tasks, and measuring output quality against expert judgments.

## Limitations
*   **Proprietary model dependency:** Findings rely on a specific `gpt-5-2025-08-07` snapshot. Model updates or API changes could alter outputs, limiting reproducibility and causal claims.
*   **Single-case fragility:** All tests center on one footnote. Effects may not generalize to other citation contexts (e.g., routine citations, different disciplines).
*   **Prompt engineering as intervention:** The "mechanisms" are induced by crafted prompts. The paper shows *that* scaffolding/nudging shifts outputs, but not *why* the model's internal decision-making changes; lexical echo is correlational.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 3 (Cross-document checking → hinge detection):** High. Model consistently detected the missing Price citation (41/45 runs); this is a concrete, observable mismatch.
*   **Mechanism 1 (Scaffolding → lineage/positioning):** Medium. Evidence shows significant code frequency shifts and expert rationale, but reduced by single-case design and lack of ablation on intermediate-step utility.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Nudging → lexical/thematic echo):** Medium. Lexical echo is empirically shown (FDR-corrected), but causality is inferred; model might echo without deeper thematic alignment.
*   **Core claim (LLMs as guided co-analysts):** Low-Medium. Supported by output diversity and prompt effects, but "co-analyst" implies reliability/insightfulness beyond pattern mimicry.

## Next Checks
1.  **Model generalizability test:** Replicate the 2x3 prompt design using a state-of-the-art open-source model (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Llama 3.1 405B). Check if the same code frequency shifts occur.
2.  **Multi-case replication:** Apply the full pipeline to at least two additional "hard" footnote citations from STS or sociology. Test whether scaffolding consistently increases lineage/positioning codes and nudges produce lexical echo.
3.  **Ablation of cross-document grounding:** Run Stage 2 4-step prompt *without full-text PDFs* (only citation string and citing excerpt). Measure drop in "lineage/positioning" codes and hinge-detection rates.
