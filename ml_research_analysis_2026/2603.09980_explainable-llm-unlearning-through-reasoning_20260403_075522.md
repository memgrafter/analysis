## Executive Summary
LLM unlearning suffers from a loss‑of‑control problem: the unlearning scope and desired post‑unlearning responses are often poorly specified, leading to incomplete removal of unwanted knowledge or incoherent outputs. This paper introduces Targeted Reasoning Unlearning (TRU), which defines unlearning targets as structured reasoning traces generated by a strong reasoning model. Each target consists of a step‑by‑step explanation of why the query belongs to the forgotten scope, ending with a coherent refusal. TRU trains the model with a combined loss: (1) cross‑entropy on the reasoning‑trace sequences to teach the abstract scope and refusal behavior, and (2) a gradient‑ascent‑based loss (e.g., GradDiff) to suppress memorization of the original data. On WMDP and MUSE benchmarks, TRU substantially outperforms strong baselines (UQ > 6.5 vs. near 0) while preserving retention quality (RQ drop <4%). Ablations confirm that reasoning traces are essential for balancing unlearning and retention, and TRU demonstrates robustness against cross‑lingual, jailbreak, and relearning attacks.

## Method Summary
TRU operates on a forget set $D_u$ (data to be removed) and a retain set $D_r$ (data to preserve). For each sample $x_u \in D_u$, a strong external reasoning model (Deepseek‑reasoner) is prompted to produce a chain‑of‑thought reasoning trace (enclosed in `<think>` tags) that explains why $x_u$ belongs to the scope to be forgotten, followed by a safe refusal response (enclosed in `<answer>` tags). The backbone model’s tokenizer is extended with the special tokens `<think>`,`, `<answer>`, and `</answer>`. Training sequences are constructed as `[x_u] <think> [reasoning]  <answer> [refusal] </answer>`. The loss function combines two terms:  
- $L_{\text{target}}$: cross‑entropy on the entire target sequence, supervising the model to produce the reasoning and refusal for in‑scope queries.  
- $L_{\text{GA‑based}}$: a gradient‑ascent‑style loss (e.g., GradDiff) applied to the original forget data, which reduces the model’s probability on memorized outputs.  
The total loss is $L = L_{\text{target}} + \alpha L_{\text{GA‑based}}$, where $\alpha$ balances the two objectives. The model is fine‑tuned with AdamW. After training, the model should reason about whether a query falls into the unlearned scope; if so, it outputs a refusal, otherwise it retains normal generative abilities. Evaluation uses LLM‑as‑a‑Judge metrics for Unlearning Quality (UQ) and Retention Quality (RQ) across multiple benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reasoning‑based unlearning targets explicitly define the unlearning scope and desired post‑unlearning response, solving the loss‑of‑control problem.  
- **Mechanism:** Generate for each forget sample a reasoning trace (via a strong reasoning LLM) that explains the underlying knowledge and a coherent refusal. Fine‑tune the model with cross‑entropy loss on these targets. The trace teaches the model to recognize the abstract unlearning scope (generalizing beyond surface forms), while the refusal pattern ensures readable, logical outputs.  
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge is parameterized; the reasoning trace captures the latent concept to be forgotten and allows the model to distinguish in‑scope queries even if rephrased or translated. The refusal pattern is learned as a safe template.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “TRU combines cross‑entropy supervised loss on reasoning traces (which capture underlying knowledge and coherent refusals) with a gradient ascent loss.”; [section] Section 4.1 details target construction; [corpus] neighbor papers (e.g., *Concept Unlearning via Self‑Constructed Knowledge Triplets*) lack explicit reasoning traces.  
- **Break condition:** If reasoning traces are misaligned with the true unlearning scope or refusal pattern inconsistent, the model may fail to generalize and produce gibberish.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The joint objective (cross‑entropy on reasoning targets + gradient‑ascent‑based loss) balances thorough knowledge removal with general capability preservation.  
- **Mechanism:** $L = L_{\text{target}} + \alpha L_{\text{GA‑based}}$. $L_{\text{target}}$ steers the model toward desired behavior (reasoning → refusal) while $L_{\text{GA‑based}}$ (e.g., GradDiff) reduces the probability of original forget data to erase memorization. The hyperparameter $\alpha$ controls the trade‑off; gradients from $L_{\text{target}}$ counteract over‑unlearning from GA.  
- **Core assumption:** Unlearning solely via negative gradients causes model collapse; adding a positive behavioral target prevents this and guides the model to maintain out‑of‑scope performance.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “We employ the target using a cross‑entropy supervised loss combined with a GA‑based loss, enabling the model to learn reasoning ability for precise knowledge removal while preserving unrelated abilities.”; [section] Equation (4) defines overall objective; [section] Ablation studies (Table 2) show removing $L_{\text{target}}$ collapses both UQ and RQ.  
- **Break condition:** Poor $\alpha$ (too high) lets GA dominate, causing excessive forgetting; $\alpha$ too low leaves residual knowledge.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The reasoning ability acquired through $L_{\text{target}}$ makes unlearning robust to cross‑lingual, jailbreak, and relearning attacks.  
- **Mechanism:** The model learns an abstract representation of the unlearning scope via reasoning traces, not just surface token suppression. This allows it to recognize in‑scope queries across languages, under obfuscating prompts, and even after few‑shot fine‑tuning on forget data.  
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning encodes a semantic notion of the forbidden knowledge; surface attacks cannot easily circumvent a model that internally verifies whether a query relates to that notion.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “TRU also demonstrates robustness under cross‑lingual, jailbreak, and relearning attacks.”; [section] Section 5.3 (Figure 3) shows only minor UQ drops under those attacks.  
- **Break condition:** If reasoning traces are not diverse or the unlearning scope ill‑defined, attacks that exploit reasoning shortcuts may still succeed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What specific characteristics or structural properties of reasoning‑based targets (e.g., step depth, coherence, length) most directly determine the trade‑off between unlearning quality (UQ) and retention quality (RQ) in TRU?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] – The ablation study (w/o Reasoning) shows that removing reasoning traces drastically reduces RQ while affecting UQ, indicating reasoning’s critical role. However, the analysis does not dissect which trace attributes are decisive.  
- **Why unresolved:** Pinpointing these factors could enable more efficient target design, reduce unnecessary computation, and improve generalizability across tasks.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying trace properties (e.g., logical steps, detail level) while holding other variables constant, then measuring UQ/RQ across multiple benchmarks and correlating with trace quality metrics (e.g., logical consistency scores).

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the reliance on external reasoning models (e.g., Deepseek‑reasoner) for target generation be replaced by self‑supervised or in‑domain fine‑tuning without significant performance drop, and what are the associated costs?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] – The paper uses an API to generate reasoning traces but does not discuss efficiency, scalability, or alternatives. This omission raises questions about practical deployment at scale.  
- **Why unresolved:** External APIs introduce latency, cost, and dependency issues. A self‑contained method would improve accessibility and allow continual unlearning.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare TRU’s performance when targets are generated via (a) the original API, (b) a smaller model fine‑tuned on reasoning examples, and (c) a self‑supervised approach that generates traces during training. Evaluate UQ/RQ and operational costs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does TRU perform against adversarial attacks that specifically target the reasoning interface of the unlearned model (e.g., prompt injections that corrupt the reasoning process)?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] – Section 5.3 tests cross‑lingual, jailbreak, and relearning attacks. However, the jailbreak prompts are generic and not tailored to exploit reasoning‑based defenses.  
- **Why unresolved:** TRU’s core claim rests on reasoning providing controllable unlearning; if an attacker can manipulate or bypass reasoning, the method’s robustness guarantee is undermined.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop adversarial prompts that simulate reasoning corruption (e.g., injecting false premises, truncating reasoning), then measure UQ/RQ degradation. Compare with baselines to see if TRU’s reasoning mechanism indeed adds resilience.
