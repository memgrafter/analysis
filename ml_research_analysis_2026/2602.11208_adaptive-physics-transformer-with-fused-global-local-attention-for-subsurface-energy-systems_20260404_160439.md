# Adaptive Physics Transformer with Fused Global-Local Attention for Subsurface Energy Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11208
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: APT achieves up to 4× lower error than interpolated baselines on adaptive mesh refinement data and shows strong cross-dataset generalization, enabling scalable subsurface foundation models.

## Executive Summary
Subsurface simulations for energy systems face high computational cost due to geological heterogeneity and tightly coupled physics. This paper introduces the Adaptive Physics Transformer (APT), a mesh‑agnostic neural operator that fuses a global perceiver encoder and a local graph neural operator encoder through a gated fusion mechanism. This design captures both long‑range pressure propagation and fine‑scale heterogeneity while learning directly from adaptive mesh refinement data without interpolation. Across five benchmarks, APT significantly improves accuracy and efficiency, and demonstrates robust cross‑dataset performance, positioning it as a scalable backbone for subsurface foundation models.

## Method Summary
APT processes unstructured point clouds via a parallel encoder. The global perceiver branch uses cross‑attention on a fixed set of learnable supernodes to aggregate domain‑wide context. The local graph neural operator (GNO) builds radius graphs and aggregates neighborhood features via mean pooling to model local differential operators. A gated fusion mechanism adaptively combines the two embeddings token‑wise. The fused latent tokens are evolved to the target time through a stack of transformer blocks with time modulation. A decoder then cross‑attends from arbitrary spatial‑temporal query points to the latent tokens to predict field values. Training uses a direct one‑step relative Lp loss, enabling evaluation at any time without autoregressive rollout. The mesh‑agnostic design allows training on adaptive mesh outputs without preprocessing.

## Key Results
- On 2D faulted CO₂ storage, APT attains 0.11% pressure error and 0.32% saturation error (1.8× and 3.75× lower than MGN‑LSTM).
- On adaptive‑mesh aquifer thermal energy storage, relative L2 = 0.011 (99% R²), a 4× reduction versus interpolated U‑Net/FNO.
- On basin‑scale nested grid CO₂, APT matches specialized models with 0.56% pressure error using 97% fewer parameters; cross‑dataset training yields an R² gain of 54.7%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Multi‑Scale Fusion
APT’s encoder fuses a global perceiver and a local GNO via a gated mechanism. The global branch projects inputs onto supernode queries using cross‑attention, approximating integral operators and capturing long‑range pressure waves. The local branch constructs radius graphs and aggregates neighbor features via mean pooling, modeling differential operators and fine‑scale heterogeneity. A learnable gate adaptively blends the two representations per token, balancing global trends against local constraints. The core assumption is that subsurface physics inherently involves multi‑scale interactions. Evidence: Ablation shows fusion outperforms single‑pathway variants; gating weights learn meaningful combinations. Break condition: If gates saturate at extremes, the model degenerates to one branch, losing multi‑scale benefits.

### Direct Adaptive Mesh Learning
APT treats simulation data as an unstructured point cloud; the local GNO builds graphs based on current mesh distances, automatically adapting to topology changes. The decoder can query arbitrary locations. Thus, the model trains directly on adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) outputs without interpolation, avoiding regridding errors and preserving sharp fronts. Core assumption: Interpolation discards geometric information and smooths features. Evidence: On ATES, APT achieves relative L2 0.011 vs. 0.084/0.046 for interpolated U‑Net/FNO (4× improvement). Break condition: Extremely sparse meshes may yield too few neighbors in the local graph, weakening aggregation.

### Cross‑Dataset Generalization
APT’s encoder produces a fixed‑size latent token set independent of input resolution, allowing samples from different datasets (varying mesh types, node counts) to be mixed during training. The permutation‑equivariant, resolution‑invariant operations enable learning from heterogeneous sources. Core assumption: Combining diverse subsurface data improves generalization and data efficiency. Evidence: Joint training on Gaussian LGR and Channelized LGR boosted R² from 32.0% to 86.7% on the Channelized test set. Break condition: Incompatible normalizations or large dataset size disparities may cause gradient interference; unified normalization or balancing may be needed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Neural Operators**: Why needed: APT learns mappings between function spaces, enabling mesh invariance and evaluation at arbitrary resolutions. Quick check: How does a neural operator differ from a conventional neural network? Answer: It maps functions to functions, allowing prediction at any continuous point, not just a fixed grid.
- **Graph Neural Networks (Message Passing)**: Why needed: The local GNO encoder aggregates information from neighboring cells on unstructured meshes, essential for representing local heterogeneity. Quick check: In the local GNO branch, what is the role of the radius graph, and how is aggregation performed? Answer: It connects nodes within a specified radius; features are aggregated via mean pooling over neighbors.
- **Transformer Attention (Cross‑Attention and Self‑Attention)**: Why needed: Both the global perceiver (cross‑attention) and latent dynamics (self‑attention) capture dependencies; cross‑attention keeps cost fixed by using supernode queries. Quick check: Why does the global perceiver use cross‑attention instead of self‑attention? Answer: Cross‑attention with learnable supernode queries avoids quadratic cost over all nodes and binds memory to the fixed supernode count, independent of input size.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Input (node features: permeability, porosity, well type, coordinates; plus time/scalar conditioning) -> Encoder (parallel Global Perceiver and Local GNO) -> Gated Fusion -> Approximator (stacked transformer blocks with time modulation) -> Decoder (cross‑attention from (x,t) query points) -> Output (field predictions)

Critical path:
1. Parallel encoder: Global Perceiver creates supernode embeddings via cross‑attention; Local GNO builds radius graph and aggregates neighbor features.
2. Gated Fusion combines the two embeddings per token using learned gate values.
3. Approximator applies temporal modulation and self‑attention on fused latent tokens to evolve state to target time.
4. Decoder attends from arbitrary spatial‑temporal query points to latent tokens; a linear head produces field values.

Design tradeoffs:
- Supernode count: More supernodes improve global context but increase memory in cross‑attention.
- Fusion strategy: Gated fusion adds parameters but allows adaptive weighting; simpler sum/concat may be less expressive.
- Training objective: Direct one‑step avoids error accumulation and handles irregular timesteps but requires diverse temporal queries; autoregressive may be more data‑efficient but suffers from compounding errors.
- Latent size: Fixed latent size bounds capacity; larger sets handle complex heterogeneity but raise memory use.

Failure signatures:
- High errors in heterogeneous regions (faults, sharp fronts) indicate insufficient local graph radius or too few supernodes.
- Poor generalization to new well configurations suggests over‑reliance on fixed input features; ensure well controls are properly conditioned.
- Super‑resolution degradation often stems from training data lacking fine details; APT cannot extrapolate beyond simulation fidelity.
- Cross‑dataset instability may arise from mismatched normalizations or huge dataset size imbalances; monitor loss balancing and gradient norms.

First 3 experiments:
1. Ablation of fusion mechanisms (global‑only, local‑only, fused) on 2D faulted CO₂ dataset shows necessity of both pathways.
2. Direct learning from AMR: Train APT on ATES DMO dataset and compare against interpolated U‑Net/FNO; APT reduces error by 4×.
3. Cross‑dataset learning: Pre‑train on Gaussian LGR and Channelized LGR jointly; test on Channelized set yields R² jump from 32% to 86.7%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can neural operators achieve reliable super‑resolution for subsurface systems when training data lacks mesh convergence? The paper cautions that performance is bounded by the mesh convergence of the underlying simulations, and current super‑resolution assumes mesh‑converged data which is prohibitively expensive for large‑scale subsurface cases.
2. Can APT scale to true foundation model pre‑training across dozens of heterogeneous subsurface datasets? The paper demonstrates promise with two datasets, but scaling to dozens with incompatible meshes, varying physics, and different fidelities may reveal architectural instability, catastrophic forgetting, or data heterogeneity issues.
3. What is the full scope of APT’s out‑of‑distribution generalization capabilities for realistic subsurface uncertainty? The paper tests only permeability shifts; broader OOD scenarios such as unseen boundary conditions, well patterns, or coupled physics remain unexplored.

## Limitations
- Implementation gaps: Radius graph parameters, perceiver compression details, and DiT modulation specifics are not provided, risking replication variance.
- Break conditions: Gate saturation, local graph failure on extremely sparse meshes, and normalization mismatches in cross‑dataset training could undermine robustness.
- Scope limits: Cross‑dataset experiments only on two datasets; super‑resolution bounded by training mesh fidelity; global perceiver cost may hinder very large supernode counts.

## Confidence

- Direct adaptive mesh learning advantage: High

## Next Checks
