# Standing on the Shoulders of Giants: Rethinking EEG Foundation Model Pretraining via Multi-Teacher Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04478
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04478
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** MTDP uses a two-stage multi-teacher distillation process (vision + time-series FMs) to pretrain EEG models, achieving superior downstream performance with only 25% of the pretraining data compared to state-of-the-art self-supervised methods.

## Executive Summary
EEG foundation model pretraining is hindered by limited and noisy data, making standard masked reconstruction objectives ineffective. This work proposes Multi-Teacher Distillation Pretraining (MTDP), which leverages frozen vision (DINOv3) and time-series (Chronos) foundation models. A learnable gating network fuses their latent representations via a masked denoising objective, creating a rich target that is then distilled into an EEG-specific student model (CBraMod). Evaluated on 12 datasets across 9 tasks, MTDP significantly improves performance and data efficiency over existing self-supervised approaches.

## Method Summary
MTDP operates in two distinct stages. **Stage 1** trains a small gating network and per-teacher prediction heads. For a batch, masked and unmasked representations are extracted from frozen teacher models. The gating network produces weights to fuse the *masked* teacher representations into a single latent, which each prediction head uses to reconstruct its corresponding *unmasked* teacher latent via a denoising loss. **Stage 2** freezes the trained gate and teachers. It computes a fused target for each EEG sample by applying the gate to the teachers' *unmasked* latents. The student EEG model (CBraMod) is then trained to match this fused target using a cosine similarity loss, with a projection head aligning dimensions.

## Key Results
- **Superior performance:** With full pretraining data, MTDP improves balanced accuracy by 1.86% on FACED and 8.80% on BCIC2020-3 over SOTA self-supervised baselines.
- **Data efficiency:** Using only 25% of the pretraining corpus, MTDP matches or exceeds the performance of models trained on the full dataset.
- **Generalizable representations:** Linear probing confirms MTDP yields more transferable features, outperforming the baseline on 10 of 12 downstream datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:** A learnable gating network automatically weights and fuses complementary latent representations from heterogeneous teachers (vision and time-series FMs) without task labels. It is trained via a masked latent denoising objective: given masked teacher latents, the fused output must predict each teacher's full (unmasked) latent. This forces the gate to combine signals such that the masked fusion retains sufficient information to reconstruct each teacher's view, implying a comprehensive summary of the underlying neural dynamics.

**Mechanism 2:** The fused teacher representation (`h_fused`), computed with the trained gate on unmasked inputs, acts as a high-quality distillation target. It synthesizes cross-modal prior knowledge (e.g., spatial coherence, temporal patterns) from large, generalist models that is difficult for an EEG model to learn from scarce, noisy data via reconstruction alone. The student model aligns its representations to this target.

**Mechanism 3:** The two-stage decoupling of fusion (Stage 1) and distillation (Stage 2) stabilizes learning and improves efficiency. Stage 1 only updates the small gate and prediction heads while teachers remain frozen. Stage 2 then freezes the gate and pre-computes fused targets, training only the student. This separates the problem of learning to combine teacher signals from learning an EEG-specific encoder, preventing conflicting gradients and reducing memory overhead.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Distillation (KD):** Transferring "dark knowledge" from a large teacher to a smaller student, typically by matching outputs or intermediate representations. *Why needed:* MTDP is a multi-teacher KD framework; the student EEG model learns by mimicking a fused teacher representation. *Quick check:* If teacher and student latent dimensions differ, what is the purpose of the projection head `q_ζ`?
- **Masked Modeling Objectives:** Self-supervised pretraining (e.g., MAE) where parts of the input are masked and the model reconstructs them. *Why needed:* MTDP's core critique is that masked *reconstruction* fails on noisy EEG; it replaces this with masked *latent denoising* (predicting teacher latents). *Quick check:* How does a low signal-to-noise ratio in EEG cause a masked reconstruction pretext task to learn artifacts instead of true neural dynamics?
- **Cross-Modal Transfer:** Leveraging models pretrained on one modality (e.g., images) for another (e.g., EEG). *Why needed:* MTDP's central hypothesis is that generalist vision/time-series FMs provide useful priors for EEG. The paper's linear probing experiment directly tests this transferability. *Quick check:* What two structural or statistical properties of images/time-series might *not* generalize to EEG signals?
- **Representation Learning for Time Series:** Learning meaningful features from sequential data. *Why needed:* Chronos is a time-series foundation model teacher; understanding its pretraining (e.g., forecasting) helps reason about what temporal priors it might contribute to EEG. *Quick check:* If a time-series model is pretrained on global forecasting, which aspects of its learned representations could be relevant for EEG classification?

## Architecture Onboarding
Teachers (DINOv3, Chronos) -> Gating Network `g_ψ` (Stage 1) -> Fused Latent `h_fused` -> Student EEG FM `f_θ` (CBraMod) + Projection Head `q_ζ`.

**Critical Path:**
1. **Stage 1 (Fusion Training):** For each batch, extract masked/unmasked latents from frozen teachers. Compute fusion weights `w` via `g_ψ`. Form fused masked latent `˜h_fused`. Each prediction head `p_ξₖ` predicts `hₖ` from `˜h_fused`. Minimize `L_denoise` to update `g_ψ` and `p_ξₖ`.
2. **Stage 2 (Distillation):** Freeze `g_ψ` and teachers. Pre-compute or online compute `h_fused` (using `g_ψ` on unmasked teacher latents). Pass EEG through `f_θ`, project via `q_ζ`. Minimize cosine similarity `L_distill` between student output and `h_fused` to update `f_θ` and `q_ζ`.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Teacher Choice vs. Fidelity:* More/stronger teachers could improve `h_fused` but increase pre-computation/storage cost.
- *Gate Complexity vs. Stability:* A more complex `g_ψ` might capture non-linear teacher interactions but risks overfitting or unstable weights. A simple MLP is used.
- *Pre-computation vs. Flexibility:* Pre-computing `h_fused` saves memory but makes the target static per epoch, decoupling it from the student's learning dynamics.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Gate collapse:** Softmax weights `w` become one-hot for one teacher across >90% of samples, indicating failed fusion.
- **Denoising loss plateau:** `L_denoise` fails to drop, meaning `˜h_fused` cannot predict any unmasked `hₖ`.
- **Distillation loss divergence:** `L_distill` increases or oscillates, suggesting a poor target or insufficient student capacity.
- **Linear probing parity:** Student's frozen features perform no better than a self-supervised baseline on downstream linear evaluation.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Replicate the preliminary linear probing (Section 3): freeze CBraMod (self-supervised) and DINOv3, extract features from identically preprocessed EEG, and train logistic regression heads. Verify DINOv3's strong transfer performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Which modalities beyond vision and time series provide the most complementary representations for EEG when used as teachers in MTDP?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper uses DINOv3 (vision) and Chronos (time series) but does not explore other modalities (e.g., language or audio models).
- **Why unresolved:** Optimal teacher modalities for capturing diverse EEG dynamics (spatial, spectral, temporal) are unknown; different modalities might offer unique priors.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of additional teacher models (e.g., audio transformers, LLMs) across all 12 downstream tasks, measuring their individual and combined contributions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the learnable gating network compare to alternative fusion mechanisms (e.g., attention-based weighting, MoE-style routing) in terms of robustness and performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The gating network is central but only compared to single-teacher and summed-loss baselines; no alternative fusion architectures are benchmarked.
- **Why unresolved:** The design choice for fusing heterogeneous representations is critical; other mechanisms might yield better adaptability or stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and compare alternatives (e.g., transformer fusion, dynamic routing) using identical pretraining data and evaluate on all tasks, including sensitivity to teacher combinations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What specific properties of the teacher representations (e.g., spatial, temporal, spectral features) are most transferred to the EEG student, and how do they differ from features learned via masked reconstruction?
- **Basis in paper:** MTDP improves linear probing but provides no analysis of the transferred features' nature or differences from reconstruction-based pretraining.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding transferred knowledge could guide better teacher selection and explain MTDP's advantage.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct representational similarity analysis (RSA) or CCA between teacher-student representations, and design probing tasks isolating specific EEG characteristics (e.g., motor imagery patterns, sleep rhythms).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the computational overhead of MTDP (storing precomputed teacher representations, I/O burdens) be reduced without sacrificing performance?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations explicitly state that using DINOv3 and Chronos incurs additional data processing and storage costs.
- **Why unresolved:** This overhead hinders scalability and accessibility, especially for large-scale pretraining or resource-constrained settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark cost-reduction strategies (distilled smaller teachers, on-the-fly inference with caching, representation dimensionality reduction) and measure trade-offs between cost and downstream performance.

## Limitations
- **Teacher complementarity assumption:** Fusion relies on heterogeneous FMs having complementary latents; high correlation could cause gate collapse. Ablation shows gating helps but latent similarity is not deeply analyzed.
- **Static gating:** The gate trained in Stage 1 is frozen during distillation, assuming optimal fusion is independent of the student's state. Joint training might improve results but is not tested.
- **Scalability & storage:** Precomputing teacher representations for large EEG corpora incurs significant memory/storage costs; the paper only uses 25% of available data.

## Confidence
- **Superior performance on 12 tasks:** High (direct empirical validation across diverse datasets).
- **Data efficiency (75% reduction):** High (quantified results on FACED, BCIC2020-3, and others).
- **Improved linear probing representations:** Medium (wins on 10/12 datasets, but not universal and linear probe may not capture all transfer qualities).
- **Gated fusion mechanism necessity:** Medium (ablation supports benefit over naive fusion/single teacher, but complementarity of teacher latents is not fully proven).
- **Two-stage decoupling efficiency:** Medium (computational rationale is plausible but lacking direct runtime/comparison studies).

## Next Checks
1. **Analyze gating weight distribution:** Track per-sample weights `w` throughout Stage 1 and Stage 2. Quantify collapse (e.g., % samples where one teacher weight >0.9) and correlate weight patterns with downstream task types.
2. **Test frozen vs. joint gate:** Re-run MTDP with the gate unfrozen in Stage 2 (recomputing `h_fused` each step with current `g_ψ`). Compare downstream performance to the published frozen-gate version to validate the decoupling assumption.
3. **Quantify cross-modal latent alignment:** Compute CCA or regression R² between each teacher's latent space and ground-truth EEG task labels (on a held-out set). Check if alignment strength correlates with the performance gains observed per dataset when that teacher is included in MTDP.
