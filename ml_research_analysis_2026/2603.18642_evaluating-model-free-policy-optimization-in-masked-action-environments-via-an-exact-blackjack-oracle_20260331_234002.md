# Evaluating Model-Free Policy Optimization in Masked-Action Environments via an Exact Blackjack Oracle

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18642
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18642
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** An exact infinite-shoe blackjack oracle reveals that model-free optimizers (REINFORCE, SPSA, CEM) achieve smooth aggregate reward convergence but suffer high cell-conditional regret due to sparse state visitation and tabular representation.

## Executive Summary
This study constructs an exact dynamic programming oracle for infinite-shoe blackjack across 4,600 decision cells, providing ground-truth policy and expected value (EV ≈ -0.00161). Three model-free optimizers—masked REINFORCE with a per-cell baseline, SPSA, and CEM—are evaluated on policy recovery. REINFORCE is most sample-efficient, yet all methods exhibit substantial local policy errors (mean cell-conditional regret > 0.25) despite converging aggregate EV. A negative control confirms that under i.i.d. draws without counting, optimal bet sizing collapses to the table minimum. The work highlights persistent challenges in masked-action environments, where dynamic action masking creates severe visitation sparsity that cripples tabular learning even as overall performance appears to improve.

## Method Summary
The authors first derive an exact dynamic programming oracle for the infinite-shoe blackjack Markov Decision Process, computing optimal Q-values and policies for 4,600 canonical decision cells via closed-form Bellman equations. This oracle serves as ground truth for evaluating model-free policy optimization. Three tabular optimizers are then tested: masked REINFORCE (with a per-cell exponential moving average baseline), SPSA (simultaneous perturbation stochastic approximation), and CEM (cross-entropy method). Performance is measured by action-match rate (AMR) against the oracle policy and cell-conditional regret (the difference in Q-value between the learned and optimal actions per state). A separate bet-sizing module, informed by the Kelly criterion, validates that under i.i.d. draws and negative expectation, the minimum bet is optimal.

## Key Results
- REINFORCE achieves a 46.37% action-match rate and EV of -0.04688 after 10^6 hands, outperforming CEM (39.46% AMR, 7.5×10^6 evaluations) and SPSA (38.63% AMR, 4.8×10^6 evaluations).
- All methods exhibit high mean cell-conditional regret (>0.25), with worst-case regret ~2.96, indicating persistent errors in rarely visited states.
- A negative control confirms optimal bet sizing collapses to the table minimum under i.i.d. draws; larger wagers increase volatility and ruin without improving expectation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** An exact dynamic programming oracle provides verifiable ground truth for policy optimization in a masked-action environment.
- **Mechanism:** The infinite-shoe model renders dealer outcomes a finite Markov chain with a stationary distribution computable by recursion. Player expected values for each legal action (Stand, Hit, Double, Split) are calculated via closed-form Bellman equations over 4,600 canonical decision cells, yielding precise optimal action labels and expected values.
- **Core assumption:** The infinite-shoe (i.i.d.) model isolates strategic decision-making from card-counting dynamics and enables exact computation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the oracle yields ground-truth values; Section 2.2 details recursive computation of dealer probabilities and Bellman equations (Eqs. 1–4); neighbor literature treats blackjack as a strategic benchmark.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A per-cell exponential moving average baseline in masked REINFORCE reduces gradient variance compared to global or rollout-based baselines, improving sample efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** The baseline b(s) is updated locally per decision cell via an exponential moving average of past returns. This anchors the advantage estimate (G – b(s)) to the typical outcome of that specific state, filtering out high-variance global reward noise (e.g., dealer blackjacks) that swamps subtle policy signals.
- **Core assumption:** State-conditional returns have lower variance than trajectory returns, and a localized baseline can track them effectively with a suitable decay rate (αb = 0.02).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.3 describe the masked REINFORCE algorithm with per-cell baseline; Section 4.3 attributes REINFORCE's superiority to variance reduction from strict localization; neighbor work on masked actions discusses variance challenges.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Dynamic action masking creates a highly asymmetric state-action topology with severe visitation sparsity, crippling tabular model-free learning even when aggregate reward appears to converge.
- **Mechanism:** The legal action set changes combinatorially based on hand state (pairs, soft totals, doubling eligibility). Actions like Split create recursive sub-states (up to depth 4), leading to many rarely visited cells. Tabular methods must learn each cell independently; sparse visitation prevents convergence in low-frequency cells, causing high cell-conditional regret even as average EV over frequent states improves.
- **Core assumption:** The tabular parameterization (Θ ∈ ℝ^{|S|×5}) precludes generalization between structurally similar states.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes sparse visitation and dynamic masking challenges; Section 4.1 links high regret to tabular representation and sparse states; regret heatmaps (Figures S1–S6) show errors concentrated in pair-split and deep-split cells.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Simulator -> Oracle Solver -> Policy Parameterization (Tabular logit matrix) -> Optimizers (REINFORCE, SPSA, CEM) -> Evaluation Engine -> Bet-sizing Module
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Validate oracle solver: Check EV ≈ -0.00161 for S17 rules (Table S2 consistency).  
  2. Run baseline REINFORCE with small budget (e.g., 10^4 hands) to confirm learning signal exists (EV should improve from random).  
  3. Execute full optimizer comparison with fixed random seeds; regenerate regret heatmaps (Figures S1–S6) to diagnose error patterns.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Tabular vs. Neural: Tabular gives exact per-cell diagnostics but cannot generalize; neural nets enable generalization but lose interpretability and exact oracle comparability.  
  - Per-cell vs. Global baseline: Per-cell baseline reduces variance but requires sufficient state visitation; global baseline is simpler but may not decorrelate noise in highly state-specific rewards.  
  - Budget allocation: REINFORCE uses hands directly; SPSA/CEM use evaluations (multiple hands per perturbation/candidate). Comparing "hand" vs. "evaluation" budgets requires careful normalization.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High regret with smooth EV curve: Indicates learning in high-frequency states but failure in sparse states; check heatmaps for concentrated error.  
  - EV plateau above oracle gap ~0.045: Suggests optimizer stuck in local policy; may need lower learning rate or increased entropy decay.  
  - Bet-sizing module selecting b > 1: Violates Theorem 1; indicates simulator bug (e.g., allowing card-counting or non-i.i.d. draws).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Oracle sanity check: For a known cell (e.g., hard 16 vs. 10), manually compute Q*(Stand) and Q*(Hit) using Eq. 1–2; compare to solver output.  
  2. Baseline ablation: Run REINFORCE with αb = 0 (no baseline) vs. αb = 0.02; expect dramatically higher variance and slower convergence.  
  3. Sparse state probe: Evaluate learned policy only on split-depth = 3 cells; AMR should be near random if visitation sparsity is the bottleneck.

## Limitations
- Oracle external validity: The exact infinite-shoe DP oracle may not generalize to finite-shoe or rule variations (e.g., dealer hits soft 17).
- Representation constraint: Tabular policy parameterization prevents generalization between structurally similar states, likely causing high cell-conditional regret.
- Baseline robustness: The per-cell exponential moving average baseline’s efficacy in extremely sparse states (e.g., deep split hands) is not isolated; poor estimates there could reintroduce high variance or bias.

## Confidence
- Exact oracle and its EV (-0.00161): **High**
- REINFORCE sample efficiency superiority: **Medium**
- Sparse visitation → high cell-conditional regret: **Medium-High**
- Minimum-bet optimality under i.i.d. draws (Theorem 1): **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Oracle stress-test:** Recompute the oracle for common rule variations (e.g., S17 vs. H17, double-after-split allowed). Quantify EV and policy label shifts to assess robustness.
2. **Representation ablation:** Replace the tabular policy with a small neural network (e.g., 2 hidden layers) and rerun REINFORCE. Measure change in cell-conditional regret and action-match rate to isolate the effect of generalization.
3. **Sparse-state baseline stability:** During REINFORCE training, explicitly track the per-cell baseline error (|b(s) – V*(s)|) for the 1% rarest visited cells. Correlate baseline error magnitude with local regret to validate the variance-reduction mechanism.
