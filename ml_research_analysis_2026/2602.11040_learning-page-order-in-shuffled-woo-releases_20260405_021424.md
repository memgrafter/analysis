# Learning Page Order in Shuffled WOO Releases
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11040
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11040
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: A specialized pairwise ranking transformer ensemble achieves Kendall’s τ up to 0.95 on short (2–5 page) documents and 0.72 on 11–15 page documents, while seq2seq transformers fail on long documents (τ ≈ 0.014) and curriculum learning underperforms direct training by 39%.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of恢复 the chronological order of pages in shuffled Freedom of Information (FOI) PDF documents. The dataset consists of 5,461 Dutch FOI PDFs of lengths 2–25 pages, where semantic cues between successive pages are often weak or absent. The authors compare a range of approaches, from heuristic similarity-based methods to deep learning models. Their best performing method is a specialized ensemble of pairwise ranking transformers, which decouples the permutation problem into independent binary comparisons. This approach yields strong performance on documents up to 15 pages (Kendall’s τ 0.95–0.72), but faces limitations on longer documents. Two notable failures are identified: seq2seq transformers catastrophically degrade on long documents, and curriculum learning actively harms performance due to incompatible local (short) and global (long) attention strategies.

## Method Summary
The top-performing method uses a pairwise ranking transformer encoder. Each page is represented by a 3072‑dimensional text embedding (OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large`). The encoder produces contextualized representations for all pages in a document. For every ordered pair of pages (i, j), a 4‑layer network scores the difference `encoded_j - encoded_i` to predict whether page j comes after page i. The global position score for a page is computed as the in‑degree minus out‑degree in the resulting pairwise graph: `score_i = (1/N) * Σ_j (s_ji - s_ij)`. Pages are sorted by descending score to obtain the predicted order. An ensemble of five models is trained, each specialized for a specific document‑length range (e.g., 2–5, 6–10, …, 21–25 pages). Specialization is achieved by scaling model capacity (layers, hidden dimension) according to the target range and applying a 5× loss weight to examples within that range. At inference, a document is routed to the model corresponding to its length.

## Key Results
- Specialized pairwise ranking ensemble: Kendall’s τ = 0.95 (2–5 pages), 0.86 (6–10 pages), 0.72 (11–15 pages), dropping to 0.38 on 21–25 page documents.
- Seq2seq transformers fail catastrophically on long documents: τ declines from 0.918 on short docs to 0.014 on 21–25 page docs across all positional encoding variants.
- Curriculum learning underperforms direct training by 39% on long documents due to a mismatch between the local attention required for short documents and the global attention needed for long ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The specialized pairwise ranking transformer ensemble achieves strong performance (τ up to 0.722) on documents up to 15 pages by decoupling the permutation problem into independent pairwise comparisons.
- **Mechanism:** For every ordered page pair (i, j), the model predicts a "comes-after" score sᵢⱼ using contextualized embeddings from a transformer encoder. These scores are aggregated into a global position score for each page (in-degree minus out-degree in the pairwise graph), then sorted. This non-autoregressive design avoids error accumulation from sequential generation and scales better with sequence length than seq2seq or pointer networks.
- **Core assumption:** The correct global ordering can be derived from aggregated binary "i before j" judgments, even when semantic cues between adjacent pages are weak.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...pairwise ranking models... Kendall’s τ ranging from 0.95 for short documents (2–5 pages) to 0.72 for 15-page documents."
  - [section 4.1] "The pairwise ranking architecture outperforms pointer networks... by +0.150 τ on 11–15 page documents."
  - [section 5] Describes the scoring aggregation formula.
- **Corpus:** No direct corpus evidence; neighbor papers focus on document splitting or procedural ordering, not heterogeneous page reordering.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades on documents longer than 15 pages (τ drops to 0.380 on 21–25 pages), suggesting the pairwise aggregation strategy becomes insufficient for capturing longer-range dependencies or that the model capacity is mismatched for very long permutations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Seq2seq transformers fail catastrophically on long documents (τ from 0.918 to 0.014) due to a combination of positional encoding limitations and architectural depth interacting with the training data distribution.
- **Mechanism:** The autoregressive decoder must generate a permutation step-by-step, heavily relying on positional encodings to track progress. Learned positional encodings poorly represent high positions (e.g., position 24) because they appear rarely in the training data (skewed toward short docs). Sinusoidal encodings improve but do not solve the issue, indicating additional factors: the deep encoder-decoder stack may develop length-specific biases, and training instability (high epoch-to-epoch variance) prevents convergence on a robust strategy for long sequences.
- **Core assumption:** The seq2seq formulation is inherently sensitive to positional representation and training dynamics when the task difficulty changes with length.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "seq2seq transformers fail to generalize on long documents (Kendall’s τ drops from 0.918 on 2–5 pages to 0.014 on 21–25 pages)... degradation persists across all encoding variants."
  - [section 4.3] "Learned positional encodings... position 24 appears in about 1/45 as many documents as position 0... Sinusoidal encodings moderately improve... but model still fails... degradation persists across all encoding variants."
  - [section 4.3] "Training dynamics show considerable instability... sinusoidal encodings never reach negative validation performance, while learned and no-position variants occasionally produce τ < 0."
- **Corpus:** Neighbor papers (e.g., "The impact of positional encoding on length generalization") support that positional encoding affects extrapolation, but not specifically for this permutation task.
- **Break condition:** Any document longer than ~15 pages triggers near-complete failure, regardless of positional encoding scheme used.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Curriculum learning underperforms direct training by 39% on long documents because short and long documents require incompatible ordering strategies (local vs. global attention patterns), so learning short-document strategies first harms long-document performance.
- **Mechanism:** Curriculum learning starts training on short documents (2–5 pages), where the optimal solution relies on highly local attention (77.9% of attention within ±2 positions). This trains the model to adopt a local pattern. When later exposed to long documents (21–25 pages), which require global attention (only 20.8% local), the model's parameter initialization and learned inductive bias resist switching strategies. Direct training on mixed lengths avoids this negative transfer.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal attention pattern for ordering is length-dependent and mutually exclusive; learning one impedes learning the other.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "curriculum learning underperforms direct training by 39% on long documents. Attention pattern analysis reveals that short and long documents require fundamentally different ordering strategies."
  - [section 4.5] "Short models exhibit highly local attention (77.9% within ±2 positions)... long models attend globally (20.8% local)... a 4.96× difference."
  - [section 4.5] "trained a model exclusively on short documents (τ=0.8817) and tested on long documents, achieving τ=0.1618, demonstrating weak transfer."
- **Corpus:** Neighbor papers discuss curriculum learning theory (e.g., "When do curricula work?") but do not address this specific incompatibility.
- **Break condition:** When the curriculum's initial stage covers documents with a local-attention optimum that differs fundamentally from the target long-document global-attention optimum.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Kendall’s tau (τ) as evaluation metric**
  - *Why needed here:* Measures rank correlation between predicted and true page order, rewarding partially correct orderings and tolerant to exact position errors. Essential because the factorial number of permutations makes exact match meaningless.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is Kendall’s tau more appropriate than exact match accuracy for this task?
- **Concept: Pairwise ranking decomposition**
  - *Why needed here:* Reduces permutation learning to independent binary comparisons, which scales more naturally to longer sequences than autoregressive generation and avoids error accumulation.
  - *Quick check question:* How does aggregating pairwise "comes-before" scores into a global ranking avoid the sequential error problem of pointer networks?
- **Concept: Positional encoding and length generalization**
  - *Why needed here:* Transformers require positional information to differentiate sequence positions. Learned encodings fail on rare positions; sinusoidal help but don't fully resolve long-sequence degradation, indicating architectural limits.
  - *Quick check question:* Why do learned positional encodings fail on long documents even when the model sees some long examples during training?
- **Concept: Curriculum learning pitfalls**
  - *Why needed here:* Standard curriculum assumes skills transfer from simple to complex. Here, short-document (local attention) and long-document (global attention) strategies conflict, causing negative transfer.
  - *Quick check question:* What evidence in the paper shows that the attention patterns for short and long documents are incompatible?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Heuristics -> BiLSTM Position Classifier -> Pointer Networks -> Seq2seq Transformer -> Pairwise Ranking Transformer
- **Critical path:** Start with heuristics → test BiLSTM/pointer for baseline autoregressive performance → evaluate seq2seq with positional ablations to diagnose length failure → implement pairwise ranking and test specialization/curriculum → analyze attention patterns to explain curriculum failure.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Autoregressive (pointer, seq2seq) vs. non-autoregressive (pairwise): tradeoff between sequential dependency handling and error accumulation/scalability. Universal vs. specialized models: tradeoff between parameter efficiency and performance on underrepresented lengths. Positional encoding choice: tradeoff between training stability (sinusoidal) and potential representation flexibility (learned).
- **Failure signatures:** Seq2seq: τ collapses to near zero on documents >15 pages; high training instability (epoch-to-epoch variance). Curriculum: Specialized curriculum model significantly underperforms direct training on target long lengths. Pairwise universal: Moderate τ on long docs (0.175) vs. specialized (0.380), indicating capacity/length mismatch.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline establishment: Run all heuristic methods (Random, Greedy NN, TSP NN) to confirm that simple similarity-based ordering fails (τ < 0.17), proving semantic similarity is not a reliable cue.
  2. Autoregressive comparison: Train Pointer MLP and Pointer LSTM; expect graceful degradation with length, contrasting with seq2seq’s catastrophic drop. This isolates architecture depth as a factor.
  3. Positional encoding ablation: Train the three seq2seq variants (learned, sinusoidal, none) on the mixed-length training set. Expect all to fail on long docs, but sinusoidal to show moderate improvement and more stable training, confirming positional encoding is a contributing but not sole cause.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What are the precise architectural and training factors beyond positional encoding that cause seq2seq transformers to fail catastrophically on long WOO documents?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the complete explanation for the seq2seq transformer’s catastrophic failure on long documents remains unclear" (Page 12), despite ablations showing positional encodings contribute.
- **Why unresolved:** Training instability and deep architecture are suspected but not isolated; multiple interacting causes (data imbalance, attention mechanisms) remain unquantified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying encoder/decoder depth, attention span, and training data distributions while holding positional encoding constant; detailed failure analysis (e.g., attention rollback, error propagation patterns).

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can curriculum learning be modified to accommodate the shift from local to global attention strategies required by long documents, thereby closing the performance gap with direct training?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Attention pattern analysis reveals that short and long documents require fundamentally different ordering strategies, explaining why curriculum learning fails" (Abstract and Page 12), with evidence showing local vs. global attention divergence.
- **Why unresolved:** Standard curriculum forces early learning of local attention, which is incompatible with long-doc needs; no curriculum explicitly adapts to strategy evolution.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a curriculum that gradually increases receptive field (e.g., via attention regularization or staged architectural changes) and evaluate Kendall’s tau on long documents versus direct training.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent do visual elements (e.g., charts, tables) in WOO documents provide recoverable ordering signals, and can multimodal embeddings improve performance on documents rich in such content?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Limitation: "Our embedding pipeline processes only textual content, excluding visual elements such as charts, graphs, and tables that may contain important ordering signals" (Page 12); future work suggests multimodal integration.
- **Why unresolved:** Visual cues might encode continuity (e.g., flow in charts, table structures) missing from text; their impact on ordering heterogeneous PDFs is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend page embeddings with visual features (e.g., from document image models), retrain the best model (specialized pairwise ranking) on a visually-rich subset, and measure change in Kendall’s tau compared to text-only.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters: exact training settings (batch size, learning rate, epochs, regularization) are unreported.
- Inference routing ambiguity: ensemble routing mechanism and whether models are trained jointly or separately is unclear.
- Pairwise network details: the 4‑layer scorer’s hidden dimensions, activations, and weight‑sharing strategy are unspecified.
