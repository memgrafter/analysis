# Beyond performance-wise Contribution Evaluation in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22470
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22470
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Client contribution rankings are highly metric-dependent, with performance and trustworthiness scores (fairness, reliability, resilience) showing near-zero correlation, and no client excelling across all dimensions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how federated learning (FL) clients contribute to multiple aspects of model trustworthiness beyond mere accuracy. Using Shapley value approximations, it independently scores clients on four dimensions: performance, fairness (demographic parity), reliability (Gaussian noise tolerance), and resilience (PGD adversarial attack resistance). Experiments across datasets (ADULT, CIFAR-10, IMDB) reveal that performance rankings correlate poorly with trustworthiness scores (Spearman ρ < 0.1), indicating that clients beneficial for accuracy may harm fairness or robustness. Furthermore, score distributions widen under non-IID data heterogeneity, implying that single-metric evaluation risks unfair reward allocation and fails to capture the diverse value clients bring. The work advocates for multi-dimensional client valuation in trustworthy FL systems.

## Method Summary
The authors implement two Shapley approximation methods—Guided-Truncated-Gradient (GTG) and Leave-One-Out (LOO)—to assign contribution scores per client for each trustworthiness dimension. For a given metric *v* (e.g., fairness), the score aggregates the client’s marginal impact on *v* over FL rounds. GTG samples client permutations and truncates negligible contributions to reduce computational cost, while LOO simply compares the full federation’s *v* to that without the client. The evaluation spans three datasets with IID/non-IID splits, using FedAvg over 10 rounds (discarding round 1). Four metrics are normalized to [0,1]: accuracy, 1 minus demographic parity disparity, noise-consistency, and PGD-robustness. Scores are accumulated across rounds and analyzed for correlations and distributions.

## Key Results
- Performance and trustworthiness scores exhibit near-zero Spearman correlation (|ρ| < 0.1) across datasets, confirming statistical independence.
- No client ranks in the top tier for all four dimensions; trade-offs are evident (e.g., high-performance clients often reduce fairness).
- Score variance increases under non-IID data heterogeneity, highlighting instability in client contributions when data distributions diverge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: GTG Shapley Approximation for Efficient Valuation**
Claim: GTG reduces computational cost while preserving attribution accuracy by sampling permutations and truncating small marginal contributions. Mechanism: It skips rounds with minimal model improvement (ϵ₁), samples a fraction (ϵ₂) of client permutations, and only counts marginal contributions exceeding threshold ϵ₃. Core assumption: Negligible contributions don’t affect fairness; early-round changes can be ignored if small. Evidence: [section 3] details the three efficiency enhancements; [abstract] cites state-of-the-art Shapley approximation. Break condition: Poorly tuned ϵ thresholds may omit meaningful effects in highly non-IID settings where small client impacts accumulate.

**Mechanism 2: LOO Heuristic for Lightweight Client Scoring**
Claim: LOO provides a cheap contribution estimate via the utility drop when excluding a client. Mechanism: Score = *v*(all) − *v*(all without *k*); requires one full run plus *K* evaluations. Core assumption: Client contributions are additive, so exclusion effect approximates marginal contribution. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 3] define LOO explicitly. Break condition: Fails with synergistic client interactions, leading to noisy scores in non-IID regimes (as seen in higher variance).

**Mechanism 3: Orthogonal Trustworthiness Dimensions via Metric-Specific Valuation**
Claim: Evaluating clients across independent metrics reveals trade-offs because these dimensions capture orthogonal aspects. Mechanism: Apply same Shapley/LOO framework but with utility functions *v* that measure fairness, reliability, or resilience separately. Core assumption: These metrics are disentangled from accuracy; a client’s impact on fairness isn’t merely a byproduct of its accuracy impact. Evidence: [abstract] reports |ρ| < 0.1; [section 4] tables/figures show low cross-correlations. Break condition: If metrics are confounded (e.g., high accuracy implies robustness), independence fails. Also, metric definitions (e.g., noise level, attack parameters) can alter correlations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Shapley Value**: Provides axiomatic foundation (efficiency, symmetry, dummy, additivity) for fair allocation. Needed to ensure each client’s score reflects average marginal contribution across all coalitions. Quick check: What are the four Shapley axioms, and why does satisfying them guarantee a unique allocation?
- **Demographic Parity**: Measures fairness as statistical independence between predictions and a sensitive attribute. Needed to operationalize the fairness dimension. Quick check: Write the definition of demographic parity and explain why it may conflict with accuracy optimization.
- **Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) Attack**: Generates worst-case adversarial examples by iteratively perturbing inputs to maximize loss, with a projection step to stay within a norm ball. Needed to measure model resilience. Quick check: How does PGD iteratively perturb an input, and what does the "projected" step ensure?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Clients -> Server -> Evaluation Module. Clients hold local data, train locally, send updates. Server coordinates FL rounds, aggregates updates (FedAvg), and computes scores. Evaluation Module runs GTG/LOO for each metric on the global model using held-out validation data, requiring intermediate model states.

**Critical path**:
1. Initialize global model *M⁰*.
2. For each round *t*=1..10:
   a. Server broadcasts *M<sup>t−1</sup>*.
   b. Clients train local *M<sub>k</sub><sup>t</sup>*.
   c. Server aggregates to *M<sup>t</sup>*.
   d. After aggregation, for each metric *v*, compute contribution scores for all clients using GTG or LOO.
3. Accumulate scores across rounds (typically skip *t*=1).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- GTG vs LOO: GTG more accurate but computationally heavy; LOO cheap but noisy and misses coalition effects.
- Metric comprehensiveness vs overhead: Adding dimensions increases evaluation cost; each needs its *v*(·) definition.
- Single-metric vs multi-objective rewards: Single-metric simple but may incentivize harm in other dimensions; multi-metric fairer but requires weighting.

**Failure signatures**:
- High score variance in non-IID settings: unstable contributions; may need more rounds or adjusted ϵ thresholds.
- Synthetic fairness groups (CIFAR/IMDB): artificial sensitive attributes limit fairness score validity.
- Metric confoundation: if reliability/resilience correlate strongly with performance (not here, but possible elsewhere), independence assumption breaks.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Score evolution tracking: Plot *v*(*M<sup>t</sup>*) over rounds; expect performance rises, others may not.
2. Metric correlation analysis: Compute Spearman ρ between performance and each trust score; expect |ρ| < 0.1.
3. Distribution comparison: Visualize score histograms for IID vs non-IID; expect wider, skewed distributions under non-IID.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Question 1**: How can the independently computed multi-dimensional trustworthiness scores be aggregated into a unified, holistic reward allocation mechanism?
Basis: Conclusion states future work should explore unified reward allocation for truly dependable AI.
Why unresolved: No client excels across all dimensions; naive aggregation (e.g., weighted sum) may be unfair; design space unexplored.
Evidence to resolve: Develop and evaluate aggregation strategies (multi-objective optimization, lexicographic ordering, stakeholder-preference weighting) in simulated FL, measuring participation incentives, overall trustworthiness, Pareto efficiency.

**Question 2**: To what extent are client contribution rankings sensitive to the specific quantitative definitions chosen for each trustworthiness dimension?
Basis: Limitations propose sensitivity analysis to different metric choices (e.g., fairness beyond demographic parity).
Why unresolved: Study uses one canonical metric per dimension; alternatives (equalized odds, different corruption types, attacks) could yield different rank orders.
Evidence to resolve: Replicate core experiments across several alternative metrics per dimension, compare score distributions and rank correlations (Spearman’s ρ, L2) to quantify instability.

**Question 3**: Do other critical trustworthiness dimensions (e.g., privacy) exhibit the same statistical independence from performance and from each other?
Basis: Limitations propose measuring privacy via membership inference attack success rate.
Why unresolved: Privacy interacts uniquely with data utility; its contribution profile and correlations with other dimensions are unknown, crucial for complete client valuation.
Evidence to resolve: Extend framework to include privacy score, compute Shapley-based scores, analyze pairwise correlations with performance, fairness, reliability, resilience.

## Limitations
- Metric definitions: synthetic fairness (grayscale), single-trial reliability/resilience, unspecified PGD α for IMDB risk confounding.
- Approximation methods: GTG thresholds may bias in non-IID; LOO ignores coalition synergy → noisy scores.
- Synthetic sensitive attributes for CIFAR/IMDB limit realism of fairness findings.
- Reproducibility gaps: local training hyperparameters (epochs, batch, Adam βs), GTG permutation sampling details, feature scaling for PGD.

## Confidence
- Orthogonality of trustworthiness dimensions: High
- Metric-dependent rankings and trade-offs: High
- GTG efficiency and accuracy: Medium
- LOO heuristic validity: Medium
