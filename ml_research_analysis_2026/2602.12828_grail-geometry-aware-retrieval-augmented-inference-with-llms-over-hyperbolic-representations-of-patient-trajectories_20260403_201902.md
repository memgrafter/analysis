# GRAIL: Geometry-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Inference with LLMs over Hyperbolic Representations of Patient Trajectories
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12828
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12828
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** On MIMIC-IV, GRAIL improves multi-modal next-visit prediction (R@10: 0.410→0.618) and reduces hierarchical inconsistency in primary diagnosis forecast (tree distance: 3.62→1.98).

## Executive Summary
Predicting future clinical events from longitudinal electronic health records (EHRs) is challenging due to data sparsity, multi-modality, and the need for hierarchical consistency. GRAIL tackles these issues by constructing a unified clinical graph that combines deterministic coding hierarchies (e.g., ICD, ATC) with data-driven lagged cross-modal associations (e.g., diagnosis → medication). The graph is embedded in hyperbolic space, which naturally represents tree-like hierarchies, and patient visits are summarized into probabilistic "Central Events" via hyperbolic barycenters. At inference time, GRAIL retrieves a geometry-aware "Risk Horizon" of candidate future events, filtered by a directional cone to maintain hierarchical plausibility, and optionally reranks candidates with an LLM. Evaluated on MIMIC-IV, GRAIL substantially outperforms baselines on multi-modal next-visit prediction and yields more hierarchy-consistent forecasts.

## Method Summary
GRAIL operates in three phases. First, it builds a directed, typed graph from EHR data: each modality (diagnoses, procedures, medications, labs) forms a tree via deterministic hierarchies; cross-modal edges are added based on lagged pointwise mutual information (PMI) computed from patient trajectories, with stability filtering to reduce noise. Second, all graph nodes are embedded into the Poincaré ball using a hyperbolic graph embedding objective that reconstructs typed edges. Third, each visit’s codes are compressed into a “Central Event” by computing the hyperbolic barycenter of their embeddings, trained via masked reconstruction. For inference, given a patient’s current Central Event, GRAIL retrieves candidate next events by considering descendants, associates, and predictive neighbors, then restricts to a risk cone around the downstream direction derived from the most likely current concept. The top candidates per modality are optionally reranked by an LLM that considers synthesized patient history.

## Key Results
- Multi-modal next-visit prediction (Recall@10) improves from 0.410 to 0.618 with LLM reranking on MIMIC-IV diagnoses.
- Hierarchical consistency in primary diagnosis prediction improves markedly, reducing tree distance from 3.62 to 1.98.
- Gains are especially large for medications and labs, which have long-tail distributions and complex hierarchies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Hyperbolic embedding provides a geometrically faithful representation of medical code hierarchies, enabling more effective summarization of sparse, multi-modal visits and semantically coherent retrieval.
**Mechanism:** Deterministic coding hierarchies (e.g., ICD, ATC) are embedded into the Poincaré ball. Hyperbolic space's exponential growth naturally allocates more representational capacity to fine-grained child nodes while preserving parent-child containment relationships via radial distance. Visit summaries ("Central Events") are computed as hyperbolic barycenters (Fréchet means), which denoise sparse code sets by aggregating them into a single point that respects the underlying hierarchical geometry.
**Core assumption:** Medical code hierarchies are tree-like and benefit from a geometry where distance grows exponentially with depth, allowing finer distinctions among specialized concepts.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions embedding in hyperbolic space and summarizing visits into Central Events. Section 3.4 discusses hyperbolic geometry for hierarchical data. Section 5.1 shows larger improvements for medications/labs, which have heavier long-tail distributions and pronounced coarse-to-fine abstraction, aligning with hyperbolic inductive bias.
**Break condition:** If clinical hierarchies are extremely shallow or flat, hyperbolic geometry may offer no advantage over Euclidean and could underperform due to optimization difficulties.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Lagged cross-modal PMI stitching creates data-driven, trajectory-consistent edges between modalities, encoding temporal dependencies (e.g., diagnosis → medication) that deterministic hierarchies alone miss.
**Mechanism:** For each pair of modalities and lag Δ, co-occurrence statistics PΔ(a→b) are computed from training trajectories. Edges are retained if their pointwise mutual information (PMI) exceeds a threshold τ and has minimum support κ. Stability filtering via bootstrapping removes dataset-specific noise. These edges "stitch" otherwise disjoint modality trees into a single graph, capturing empirical temporal progression.
**Core assumption:** Clinically meaningful, lagged associations between different event types are recoverable from population-level EHR data and stable enough to generalize.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes combining deterministic hierarchies with data-driven temporal associations. Section 3.3.2 details the PMI edge retention. Ablation "Hyperbolic (no stitch)" shows performance drops largest for medications and labs, which are rarely predictable from within-modality history alone.
**Break condition:** In settings with extremely short patient sequences or massive missingness, lagged PMI estimates become unreliable, leading to noisy cross-modal edges.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Structure-aware "Risk Cone" retrieval restricts candidate next events to those that are both geometrically proximate and directionally consistent with hierarchical progression, reducing implausible predictions.
**Mechanism:** From the current visit's Central Event, the "downstream direction" d_T is defined as the vector from the root ancestor to the most likely current concept v_T in tangent space. Candidates are filtered to those within an angular cone (∠ ≤ φ) of this direction, favoring specialized/descendant concepts over abstract ancestors or unrelated branches. Final ranking combines hyperbolic proximity to the visit barycenter with cone membership.
**Core assumption:** Clinical progression follows a hierarchical "downward" specialization pattern more often than abstraction or lateral jumps; this directionality is approximately preserved in the hyperbolic embedding.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions retrieving a structured set of clinically plausible future events aligned with hierarchical and temporal progression. Section 3.7.2 defines the risk cone. Section 5.2 shows GRAIL achieves more hierarchy-consistent forecasts, with tree distance dropping significantly, indicating errors are within clinically related subtrees.
**Break condition:** If clinical trajectories frequently involve upward abstraction or lateral modality jumps not captured by the graph, the fixed cone aperture may exclude valid future events. The cone parameter φ is a critical hyperparameter.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (longitudinal EHR sequences) -> Graph Builder -> Hyperbolic Encoder -> Visit Denoiser -> Risk Horizon Retriever -> LLM Reranker (optional)

**Critical path:** Graph construction → hyperbolic embedding training → Central Event training → inference (retrieval → optional LLM). The graph topology is fixed after training; embeddings and Central Event parameters are learned jointly.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Sparsity vs. Connectivity:* High PMI thresholds τ reduce noisy edges but may miss weak valid associations; stability filtering q trades sensitivity for robustness.  
- *Geometry fidelity:* Hyperbolic optimization is sensitive to initialization and learning rate; poor convergence can destroy hierarchical structure.  
- *Candidate set size:* Risk cone aperture φ directly controls precision/recall trade-off. Too narrow → low recall; too wide → noisy candidates.  
- *LLM dependency:* LLM reranking adds cost but can resolve semantic ambiguities; however, it inherits the LLM's biases if the candidate set is poor.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Poor cross-modal edges:** If PMI stitching fails (e.g., insufficient data), retrieval for secondary modalities collapses to within-modality neighbors, hurting performance as shown in the "no stitch" ablation.  
- **Central Event collapse:** If masked-visit pretraining fails, barycenters may not retain predictive signal (e.g., mask ratio too high or training unstable), leading to noisy retrieval anchors.  
- **Cone misalignment:** If the downstream direction is poorly estimated (e.g., v_T noisy or hierarchy broken), the risk cone may exclude true future events, manifesting as a drop in Recall@k despite good nDCG.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Graph sanity check:** Visualize hyperbolic embeddings of a small, known subtree (e.g., a branch of ICD-10 codes). Verify parent nodes are radially inward of children and distances grow with depth.  
2. **Central Event reconstruction:** Train only the masked-visit objective and measure reconstruction accuracy of masked codes. A high recovery rate indicates Central Events preserve predictive signal.  
3. **Ablation sweep on risk cone φ:** Fix all else, vary φ and measure multi-modal Recall@10 on validation. Identify the elbow point where gains plateau or reverse due to over-restriction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does hyperbolic geometry provide the optimal embedding space for modeling hierarchical clinical relationships and cross-modal temporal associations, or could alternative non-Euclidean geometries yield further improvements?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors introduce the central modeling question and argue for hyperbolic geometry based on prior work, but only compare against Euclidean baselines.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not systematically explore other non-Euclidean geometries or curvature settings. It remains unknown whether more exotic geometries could better capture the combination of strict hierarchies and data-driven associations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a comprehensive geometry ablation study embedding the graph in various manifolds (hyperbolic with different curvatures, spherical, product manifolds) under identical protocols on multiple datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the Central Event summarization be further improved by modeling uncertainty or learning a more expressive probabilistic visit representation, rather than using a single hyperbolic barycenter?
- **Basis in paper:** GRAIL uses a deterministic Fréchet-mean barycenter and does not compare against alternative summarization methods nor quantify uncertainty.
- **Why unresolved:** The barycenter approach may not capture multimodality in patient states or provide calibrated uncertainty estimates, which could be critical for risk assessment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the Central Event with probabilistic formulations (e.g., hyperbolic mixtures, generative models) and evaluate on accuracy, calibration, and robustness to missing data.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does incorporating the LLM into the training loop (e.g., fine-tuning or gradient-based alignment) improve performance beyond using it as a frozen reranker at inference time?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that LLM reranking is strictly at inference time; they do not backpropagate through the LLM.
- **Why unresolved:** The LLM may be suboptimal for this specific medical retrieval task without adaptation; fine-tuning or using its gradients could tighten integration and potentially recover more clinically relevant candidates.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a version where the LLM is fine-tuned or its outputs used for training signals (e.g., distillation, RL), and compare performance of fine-tuned vs frozen rerankers.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How well does GRAIL generalize to other healthcare systems and coding practices, given the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (MIMIC-IV)?
- **Basis in paper:** Only MIMIC-IV results are reported. The dataset-specific characteristics may affect the learned graph and embeddings.
- **Why unresolved:** The success relies on stability of cross-modal PMI associations and reliability of coding hierarchies. Different hospitals may use different coding versions, test nomenclatures, and protocols. Without validation on diverse datasets, broader applicability is uncertain.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate GRAIL on additional EHR datasets from different countries/networks after code mapping, and perform domain adaptation experiments to measure performance drops and mitigation strategies.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility:** Critical hyperparameters (risk cone angle φ, directional weight η, LLM blend λ, loss weights α, mask ratio ρ) and exact LLM prompting templates are unreported, hindering faithful replication.
- **LLM Dependency:** The benefit of LLM reranking may be sensitive to prompt engineering; the primary diagnosis extraction method from discharge summaries is unspecified, affecting the "grounded mention" metric.
- **Robustness:** Stability of cross-modal PMI edges is sensitive to minimum support (κ) and bootstrap threshold (q); their values and sensitivity analysis are not provided, raising concerns for smaller datasets.
