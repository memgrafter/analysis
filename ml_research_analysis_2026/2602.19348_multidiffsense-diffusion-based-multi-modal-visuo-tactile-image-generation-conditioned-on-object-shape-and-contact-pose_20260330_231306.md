# MultiDiffSense: Diffusion-Based Multi-Modal Visuo-Tactile Image Generation Conditioned on Object Shape and Contact Pose

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19348
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19348
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Multi-modal visuo-tactile image synthesis via diffusion, conditioned on CAD depth maps and 4-DoF contact pose, outperforming cGAN baseline in SSIM

## Executive Summary
MultiDiffSense introduces a diffusion-based generative model to synthesize aligned visuo-tactile images for three sensor modalities (ViTac, TacTip, ViTacTip) given CAD-derived depth maps and structured text prompts specifying sensor type and 4-DoF contact pose. The method addresses the high-cost data collection bottleneck by enabling controllable multi-modal data synthesis, which is validated both via image quality (SSIM) and downstream 3-DoF pose estimation tasks. Evaluated on 8 objects with seen and novel poses, the model significantly outperforms a Pix2Pix cGAN baseline, with SSIM gains ranging from +36.3% to +134.6% on unseen objects. Notably, mixing 50% synthetic with 50% real data halves the real data needed for competitive pose estimation performance.

## Method Summary
MultiDiffSense uses a diffusion model conditioned on CAD-derived depth maps and structured text prompts (sensor type, contact pose) to generate aligned visuo-tactile images. The method supports three sensor modalities: ViTac, TacTip, and ViTacTip. Depth maps serve as geometric conditioning, while text prompts specify sensor type and 4-DoF contact pose. The model is trained on aligned multi-modal visuo-tactile datasets and evaluated on both seen and unseen objects, as well as novel poses. Outputs are assessed using SSIM for image quality and in downstream pose estimation tasks, where synthetic data is mixed with real data to reduce labeling requirements.

## Key Results
- MultiDiffSense significantly outperforms Pix2Pix cGAN baseline in SSIM: +36.3% (ViTac), +134.6% (ViTacTip), +64.7% (TacTip) on unseen objects
- For 3-DoF pose estimation, mixing 50% synthetic with 50% real data halves the required real data while maintaining competitive performance (e.g., ViTac R² 0.940 vs. 0.919 real-only)
- Results validated on 8 objects, 5 seen during training, with novel poses and objects tested

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages diffusion models' strong generative capabilities, conditioning on structured CAD depth maps and text prompts to synthesize realistic, pose- and sensor-specific visuo-tactile images. By aligning visual and tactile modalities and explicitly encoding contact pose and sensor type, MultiDiffSense ensures controllability and consistency across outputs. The use of diffusion allows for high-quality synthesis, while structured conditioning enables targeted generation for downstream tasks like pose estimation, where mixed synthetic/real data proves effective.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Text Prompt (sensor + pose) -> CAD Depth Map -> Diffusion Model -> Visuo-Tactile Images (ViTac, TacTip, ViTacTip)

**Critical Path**
Input conditioning (text + depth) -> Diffusion denoising steps -> Output image synthesis -> Downstream pose estimation

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Depth map vs. RGB image conditioning: depth is more robust to lighting and texture variation
- Text prompt vs. numerical pose encoding: text is more flexible but may require careful parsing
- Diffusion vs. GAN: diffusion offers better quality and stability at the cost of training speed

**Failure Signatures**
- Conditioning mismatch: synthetic images do not reflect specified pose or sensor type
- Mode collapse: repetitive or unrealistic outputs for novel objects
- Poor alignment: visuo-tactile outputs are inconsistent in geometry or timing

**First Experiments**
1. Generate visuo-tactile images for a held-out object with known CAD model and compare SSIM to real data
2. Perform 3-DoF pose estimation using 50% synthetic + 50% real data and compare to real-only baseline
3. Ablate conditioning: remove depth map or pose info and observe impact on output quality and controllability

## Limitations
- Dataset scale limited to 8 objects, 5 seen during training; generalization to truly novel objects uncertain
- Evaluation focused on controlled, known objects and poses; real-world variability not tested
- Perceptual quality metrics beyond SSIM not reported; potential gaps in realism assessment
- No analysis of temporal dynamics or cross-view consistency, critical for real manipulation

## Confidence
- **High**: Synthetic data utility in pose estimation (clear quantitative improvement over real-only)
- **Medium**: Synthesis quality (SSIM gains shown but perceptual quality unverified)
- **Low**: Generalization to novel objects or uncontrolled environments

## Next Checks
1. Test synthesis and downstream task performance on a larger set of held-out objects with varied geometries and surface properties
2. Evaluate perceptual image quality using established benchmarks (e.g., FID, LPIPS) and conduct human preference studies
3. Assess robustness by adding realistic sensing noise to both synthetic and real inputs and measuring impact on downstream task performance
