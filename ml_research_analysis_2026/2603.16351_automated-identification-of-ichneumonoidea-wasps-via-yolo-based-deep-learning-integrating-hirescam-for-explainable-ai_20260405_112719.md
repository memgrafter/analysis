# Automated identification of Ichneumonoidea wasps via YOLO-based deep learning: Integrating HiresCam for Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.16351
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16351
*   **Reference count:** 38
*   **Primary result:** A YOLOv26-based model achieved 96.14% Top-1 Accuracy in classifying Ichneumonoidea families, with HiResCAM visualizations confirming biologically plausible feature attention.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the expert-dependent, labor-intensive process of manually identifying Ichneumonoidea wasps by developing a deep learning framework for automated family-level classification. The authors train YOLO-based models on a curated dataset of 3,556 high-resolution specimen images and integrate HiResCAM explainable AI to visualize model focus. The top-performing model, YOLOv26, achieves high accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Critically, the XAI component validates that the model attends to diagnostically relevant morphological features like wing venation, enhancing trust and transparency for biodiversity monitoring applications.

## Method Summary
The method uses the DAPWH dataset containing 3,556 images across 12 Hymenoptera families. Images are resized to 512x512 pixels. The study compares two YOLO variants (yolov12n-cls and yolo26n-cls) under a standard classification setup, splitting the data 70% train, 15% validation, 15% test. Training occurs on a specified hardware configuration (AMD Ryzen 9, NVIDIA RTX 4090). Model interpretability is achieved by post-hoc integration of HiResCAM, which generates class activation maps from the final convolutional layer to highlight image regions influencing predictions.

## Key Results
*   The YOLOv26 model achieved a Top-1 Accuracy of 96.14%, Precision of 93.43%, Recall of 97.04%, and an F1-score of 95.20% on the test set.
*   Confusion matrices show strong diagonal performance, with most families exceeding 93% accuracy, though smaller families like Colletidae and Halictidae were less separable.
*   HiResCAM visualizations consistently highlighted taxonomically relevant features (e.g., specific wing veins, metasomal structures), aligning with entomological keys and supporting biological plausibility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** YOLO's convolutional backbone learns hierarchical morphological features from high-resolution images, enabling discrimination between visually similar Hymenoptera families.  
**Mechanism:** Stacked convolutional layers progressively abstract local edge and texture patterns into complex shape detectors (e.g., wing venation, metasomal segmentation) through backpropagation. The architecture's design for object detection inherently localizes salient regions.  
**Core assumption:** Key taxonomic differentiators (e.g., wing vein presence/absence) are spatially consistent and statistically prevalent within family-level image distributions.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes the architecture "captures discriminative patterns across convolutional layers...emphasizing structural contours." Section 3.2 states feature activation maps "reveal hierarchical encoding...from low-level edge detection to higher-level morphological descriptors." Direct corroboration from the citation corpus is weak, as neighboring papers focus on other taxa or non-XAI methods.  
**Break condition:** If specimen images exhibit non-standard orientations, extreme occlusion, or damage obscuring diagnostic structures, the learned convolutions may fail to activate on relevant features, causing misclassification.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** HiResCAM visualizations provide faithful, high-resolution maps that validate the model's reliance on taxonomically correct anatomical regions, establishing biological plausibility.  
**Mechanism:** HiResCAM computes gradient-weighted activations from the final convolutional layer without upsampling approximations, preserving spatial correspondence between input pixels and class-specific importance scores. This reveals whether prediction logic aligns with entomological keys.  
**Core assumption:** Human-annotated morphological keys (e.g., vein 2m-cu presence in Ichneumonidae) correspond to visually discriminative regions that a well-performing model must attend to.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states HiResCAM confirms the "model focuses on taxonomically relevant anatomical regions." Section 3.3-3.5 and figures (e.g., Fig. 9, 13, 16-20) provide specific examples of highlighted wing venation and metasomal features. No direct evidence exists in the citation corpus, which does not address Ichneumonoidea or HiResCAM validation.  
**Break condition:** If HiResCAM highlights background or irrelevant body regions (e.g., mounting pins, labeling artifacts), it indicates the model learned spurious correlations, undermining trust despite high accuracy metrics.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The curated, high-resolution DAPWH dataset's intra-family diversity and inter-family morphological variance enable the model to learn robust, generalizable features beyond dataset-specific artifacts.  
**Mechanism:** Exposure to varied specimens (different species, sexes, angles, lighting) within each family forces the network to capture invariant traits. Balanced sampling across 12 families prevents overfitting to class-imbalanced cues.  
**Core assumption:** Families possess unique combinations of morphological traits that are more consistent within-group than between-group, even with intraspecific variation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1 details dataset composition (3,556 images across 12 families, e.g., Ichneumonidae n=786, Braconidae n=648). Section 3.1's confusion matrices show strong diagonal performance, indicating learned inter-class boundaries. The citation corpus offers weak support, lacking directly comparable datasets for Ichneumonoidea at this scale or family focus.  
**Break condition:** If new specimens from under-sampled families (e.g., Colletidae, n=51) or novel environments exhibit trait shifts (e.g., regional color morphs), model accuracy may degrade due to insufficient representation of the feature space.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (512x512 RGB) -> YOLO Backbone (CSP/ELAN blocks) -> Neck (PANet) -> Head (Classification branch, 12-class softmax) -> Loss (Cross-entropy). Post-hoc: HiResCAM attached to final convolutional layer.  
**Critical path:** Data loading & resizing -> Backbone feature extraction -> Neck multi-scale fusion -> Classification head -> Loss calculation -> HiResCAM overlay on validation samples.  
**Design tradeoffs:** Resolution vs. memory (512x512 balances fine detail and GPU memory). Nano variant prioritizes speed/compactness over peak accuracy. HiResCAM adds negligible computation but requires storing intermediate gradients.  
**Failure signatures:** Quantitative: Low recall (>5%) for small families in confusion matrix. Qualitative: HiResCAM heatmaps on background/non-diagnostic regions. Training: Validation loss divergence after ~epoch 50 suggests overfitting.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1.  Train on only Ichneumonidae vs. all others; verify HiResCAM highlights wing venation consistently across Ichneumonidae specimens.  
2.  Artificially mask wing regions in validation images; measure accuracy drop to quantify model reliance on wings.  
3.  Initialize both YOLOv12n and YOLOv26n with same ImageNet pretraining; track validation F1-score per epoch to compare convergence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Can the framework be extended to subfamily or genus levels?** The paper's conclusion mentions this as future work. Current training is at family level with limited intrafamilial variation; finer levels require more subtle distinctions and more data.
*   **How does performance generalize across geographically diverse specimens?** The dataset is sourced primarily from one collection (DCBU) with potential regional bias. No explicit cross-institutional or geographic generalization test is reported.
*   **Are HiResCAM visualizations fully reliable for validating learned features?** The paper shows alignment with morphological keys but lacks systematic error analysis or comparison with expert feature importance maps, leaving open the possibility of missed spurious correlations.

## Limitations
*   Key training hyperparameters (learning rate, optimizer, augmentation pipeline) are unreported, hindering exact replication.
*   HiResCAM implementation specifics (target layer, gradient handling) are vague, weakening the strength of the XAI validation.
*   Dataset imbalance exists (e.g., Colletidae n=51), risking poor generalization for under-sampled families.

## Confidence
*   **Performance metrics (96.14% Accuracy, etc.): Medium.** Strong numbers but depend on unreported training choices and dataset constraints.
*   **HiResCAM explanations: Medium.** Visual focus on expected features is encouraging but lacks quantitative ground truth from experts; risk of spurious correlations remains.
*   **Dataset generalizability: Low.** Limited intra-family diversity and under-sampled families threaten out-of-sample robustness without external validation.

## Next Checks
1.  **Exact replication:** With the provided code and DAPWH dataset, retrain YOLOv26n-cls using the same 70/15/15 split. Metrics matching within ±0.5% would confirm reproducibility.
2.  **Feature ablation:** Systematically mask diagnostically critical regions (e.g., wings, metasomal segments) in validation images. A sharp accuracy drop *only* when those features are occluded validates reliance on biologically relevant structures.
3.  **Cross-dataset generalization:** Test the trained model on an independent collection of Ichneumonoidea specimens from a different museum/source. Performance degradation >5% would indicate overfitting and need for more diverse data.
