# Statistical Roughness-Informed Machine Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09304  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09304  
- Reference count: 36  
- Primary result: SRAGU consistently achieves the closest alignment to gold retraining (ORTR) across multiple datasets and deletion scenarios, significantly reducing behavioral and membership leakage while maintaining retained accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Machine unlearning seeks to efficiently remove data influence from a model without costly retraining. However, current methods often fail under large or adversarial forget sets due to pronounced layer-wise heterogeneity in stability. SRAGU addresses this by enhancing Adaptive Gradient Unlearning (AGU) with layer-wise statistical roughness: it estimates the heavy-tailed spectral exponent of each layer’s weight Gram matrix, maps it to a bounded stability weight via an in-range gate, and reweights AGU’s parameter sensitivities accordingly. This modulates update magnitudes across layers, concentrating motion in stable layers while damping brittle ones. Comprehensive experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, UCI Adult, and ImageNet100 under random, class-specific, and adversarial deletions show that SRAGU achieves near-optimal alignment with full retraining, reducing behavioral leakage by 3–6× and membership leakage to near-random levels while preserving retained accuracy. With only ~50% extra runtime over AGU and ~15× speed‑up versus retraining, SRAGU offers a practical and robust unlearning solution.

## Method Summary
SRAGU builds upon Adaptive Gradient Unlearning (AGU) by introducing layer‑wise stability modulation. First, for each layer, it computes the heavy‑tailed exponent \(\xi_l\) of the top eigenvalues of the Gram matrix \(C_l = \frac{1}{m} W_l^\top W_l\). This exponent characterizes the spectral distribution. Then \(\xi_l\) is passed through an in‑range gate \(\nu_l = \sigma(d_1(4-\xi_l)) / \sigma(d_2(\xi_l-2))\), where \(\sigma\) is the sigmoid, yielding a bounded weight that is large for moderate \(\xi_l\) (\(2<\xi_l<4\)) and small otherwise. Meanwhile, AGU computes per‑parameter sensitivities \(R_j\) as the mean squared gradient on the forget set. SRAGU reweights each sensitivity by its layer’s \(\nu_l\): \(R'_j = R_j \cdot \nu_{l(j)}\). After max‑normalization across all parameters, these reweighted sensitivities drive the standard AGU update \(\phi_j \leftarrow \phi_j - \alpha R'_j \nabla_{\phi_j} L(B;\phi)\) over minibatches from the forget set. The process stops when the \(\ell_2\) drift from the original model exceeds a threshold or a maximum step count is reached. This mechanism allocates more update magnitude to layers with stable spectra, improving robustness especially under adversarial deletions.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR‑10 with 30% influence‑style adversarial deletion, SRAGU attains \(\epsilon_{\text{pred}} = 0.030\) vs AGU’s 0.162, \(D_{\text{KL}} = 0.051\) vs 0.258, and MIA AUC = 0.530 vs 0.667, while retaining accuracy within 0.5% of full retraining.
- Across datasets (MNIST, CIFAR‑10/100, UCI Adult, ImageNet100) and deletion types (random, class‑specific, adversarial), SRAGU reduces behavioral leakage by 3–6× and brings membership leakage close to random guessing.
- SRAGU incurs only ~50% additional runtime over AGU and is ~15× faster than full retraining, offering a favorable trade‑off between unlearning quality and efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** SRAGU stabilizes unlearning by modulating parameter updates according to layer‑wise spectral stability.  
**Mechanism:** It first computes AGU sensitivities \(R_j\) (mean squared gradient over forget set). Then it multiplies each sensitivity by a layer‑wise stability weight \(\nu_l\) derived from the heavy‑tail exponent \(\xi_l\) of that layer’s weight matrix Gram spectrum. The reweighted sensitivities \(R'_j = R_j \cdot \nu_{l(j)}\) are max‑normalized and used in the AGU update rule.  
**Core assumption:** Layers with heavy‑tailed spectra in the nominal band (\(2 < \xi_l < 4\)) are more stable and can safely absorb larger unlearning‑induced parameter changes without harming retained accuracy or causing catastrophic forgetting.  
**Evidence:** Abstract and Section 4.1 describe the reweighting as a “speed controller” across layers; neighboring work does not directly address this spectral‑reweighting mechanism.  
**Break condition:** If \(\xi_l\) is estimated with high variance (e.g., for very small layers or degenerate spectra), \(\nu_l\) may become unreliable, causing misallocation of update mass and potential utility collapse.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The heavy‑tailed exponent \(\xi_l\) is a practical proxy for a layer’s optimization geometry and training maturity.  
**Mechanism:** For each layer, SRAGU forms the Gram matrix \(C_l = \frac{1}{m} W_l^\top W_l\), extracts the top \(h = \lfloor \tau s \rfloor\) eigenvalues (\(\tau\) is a fit fraction), and fits a power law \(p(\lambda) \propto \lambda^{-\xi_l}\) to obtain the exponent.  
**Core assumption:** The slope of the top eigenvalue spectrum correlates with how well‑trained or overfit a layer is; heavy tails (moderate \(\xi_l\)) indicate implicit self‑regularization and stability.  
**Evidence:** Section 3 details the spectral estimation; Section 4.3 links heavy‑tailed spectral structure to training dynamics and implicit self‑regularization.  
**Break condition:** The power‑law fit may be unstable for layers with few eigenvalues (thin layers) or non‑power‑law spectra, leading to inaccurate \(\xi_l\) and thus poor \(\nu_l\).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The bounded in‑range gate on \(\xi_l\) implements a band‑pass filter that concentrates unlearning update mass in layers with moderate heavy‑tailedness.  
**Mechanism:** \(\nu_l = \sigma(d_1(4-\xi_l)) / \sigma(d_2(\xi_l-2))\), where \(\sigma\) is the sigmoid. For \(\xi_l \in (2,4)\), both numerator and denominator are moderate, yielding higher \(\nu_l\). For \(\xi_l \leq 2\) or \(\xi_l \geq 4\), one gate closes, shrinking \(\nu_l\).  
**Core assumption:** Layers with \(\xi_l \leq 2\) are under‑trained/brittle and should be damped; layers with \(\xi_l \geq 4\) are overfit/rigid and also should be damped; only the intermediate band is safe for significant update motion.  
**Evidence:** Section 3 (Eq. 8) and Section 4.3 explain the gating design.  
**Break condition:** The chosen stability band \((2,4)\) may not generalize to all architectures or datasets; if the true stable regime differs, the gate could misclassify layers and allocate updates suboptimally.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
AGU sensitivity module → (R_j)  
Spectral estimator → (ξ_l) → Stability gate → (ν_l)  
(R_j) + (ν_l) → Reweighting engine → (R'_j) → Unlearning updates → Stopping controller

### Critical Path
1. Load pre‑unlearning model \(\phi_0\).
2. For each layer, compute \(\xi_l\) from weights \(W_l\) (one‑time cost).
3. Compute \(\nu_l\) from \(\xi_l\) (fixed during unlearning).
4. Compute sensitivities \(\{R_j\}\) on forget set at \(\phi_0\).
5. Form reweighted sensitivities \(\{R'_j\}\) using \(\nu_l\).
6. Iterate over forget‑set minibatches: compute gradient, apply update \(\phi_j \leftarrow \phi_j - \alpha \bar{R}^{(SR)}_j \nabla_{\phi_j} L(B;\phi)\).
7. Check stopping condition (\(\|\phi - \phi_0\|_2 < 10^{-4}\) or max steps \(T\)).

### Design Tradeoffs
- Spectral fit fraction \(\tau\): smaller \(\tau\) reduces compute but increases noise; larger \(\tau\) includes more eigenvalues that may not follow power law.
- Damping constants \(d_1, d_2\): control gate sharpness; higher values yield more binary decisions but may overfit to a particular \(\xi_l\) distribution.
- Runtime vs. robustness: SRAGU adds ~50% overhead over AGU due to spectral estimation, but gains stability under adversarial deletions.

### Failure Signatures
- High variance in \(\xi_l\) across seeds for same layer → unstable \(\nu_l\).
- Mismatch between \(\nu_l\) and actual layer‑wise update allocation \(A_l\) (e.g., high \(\nu_l\) but low \(A_l\)) → gating not translating to updates.
- Utility collapse at high deletion ratios despite SRAGU → spectral signal insufficient to guide updates in extremely hard regimes.
- Oscillatory unlearning trajectories (overshoot in \(\epsilon_{\text{pred}}\)) → update reweighting not sufficiently stabilizing.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Layer‑wise profiling:** Compute \(\xi_l\), \(\nu_l\), and update diagnostics \(Q_l, A_l\) to verify band‑pass allocation pattern.
2. **Adversarial deletion robustness:** Evaluate performance across four adversarial forget‑set constructions (high‑loss, low‑margin, high‑gradient, influence‑style) to ensure gains are not cherry‑picked.
3. **Cross‑dataset generalization:** Test on MNIST, CIFAR‑10/100, UCI Adult, ImageNet100 to confirm modality and scale invariance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Can SRAGU's stability weighting be combined with differential privacy to provide certified unlearning guarantees?  
**Basis in paper:** Future Work (Section 5.16): “Toward certified unlearning and stronger privacy notions… connect to formal guarantees, e.g., certified removal… or privacy accounting when combined with DP training.”  
**Why unresolved:** SRAGU currently relies on empirical alignment to a gold retrained model and lacks theoretical privacy guarantees that could certify removal under adversarial audits.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis deriving bounds between SRAGU's updates and a retrained model, or an SRAGU variant that satisfies \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-differential privacy while preserving utility on retained data.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Does replacing exact spectral exponent estimation in SRAGU with randomized methods (e.g., Hutchinson, randomized SVD) maintain performance while improving scalability?  
**Basis in paper:** Future Work: “Faster and more robust spectral estimation… randomized or streaming spectrum estimators (e.g., randomized SVD, Hutchinson trace estimators).”  
**Why unresolved:** SRAGU's per‑layer SVD/eigendecomposition may become computationally prohibitive for very large models (e.g., transformers), limiting practical deployment.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical comparison of approximate vs. exact spectral exponents and unlearning metrics (\(\epsilon_{\text{pred}}, D_{\text{KL}}\)) across large‑scale models, demonstrating speed‑ups with negligible degradation.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** For transformer architectures, which weight matrices (e.g., attention Q/K/V projections, MLP weights) are most predictive of layer stability for SRAGU's spectral weighting?  
**Basis in paper:** Future Work: “Architecture‑specific definitions of \(W_l\)… systematically studying which matrices best reflect layer stability (e.g., attention projections vs. MLP blocks).”  
**Why unresolved:** The current formulation treats each layer as a matrix, but transformer blocks contain multiple distinct parameters; the choice of \(W_l\) could significantly affect derived stability weights.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying \(W_l\) definitions on multiple transformer models and tasks, measuring correlation between \(\nu_l\) and unlearning success (gold alignment), and identifying a consistent best practice.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Would finer‑grained (sub‑layer or channel‑level) stability weights improve SRAGU's unlearning efficacy beyond layer‑level granularity?  
**Basis in paper:** Future Work: “Beyond layer‑wise weights: finer granularity… use sub‑layer or channel‑wise stability weights.”  
**Why unresolved:** Layers may contain heterogeneous parameter groups with different stability; coarse layer‑level weighting might under‑ or over‑penalize certain subsets, limiting precision.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a channel‑wise SRAGU variant (e.g., per‑output‑channel exponents for convolutions), evaluate against layer‑wise SRAGU on benchmarks, focusing on leakage (\(\epsilon_{\text{pred}},\) MIA) and utility under high deletion ratios.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** Is the heavy‑tailed exponent estimation reliable for very small or highly structured layers (e.g., depthwise convolutions), and how can we mitigate potential instability?  
**Basis in paper:** Inferred from Limitations (Section 5.15): “Spectral diagnostics can be noisy for small or highly structured layers… the power‑law fit may be unstable or less meaningful.”  
**Why unresolved:** Unreliable \(\xi_l\) estimates could produce misleading \(\nu_l\) values, leading to poor update allocation and degraded unlearning quality.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Profile variance of \(\xi_l\) across seeds and initializations for problematic layers; develop robustification (e.g., Bayesian fitting, smoothing across neighboring layers) and demonstrate improved unlearning outcomes.

## Limitations
- Spectral estimation stability: The heavy‑tail fit may be noisy for thin layers or degenerate spectra, risking unreliable \(\nu_l\).
- Gating hyperparameter sensitivity: Fixed band \((2,4)\) and sigmoid constants \((d_1,d_2)\) may not generalize across architectures/datasets.
- Architectural generality: Unclear how “layer” is defined for complex modules (e.g., transformers’ attention/MLP weights).

## Confidence
- Empirical effectiveness (SRAGU outperforms baselines): High  
- Mechanism explanation (spectral stability ↔ update allocation): Medium  
- Theoretical guarantees (bounds on forgetting/retention): Low
