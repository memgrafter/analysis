# Global Convergence of Multiplicative Updates for the Matrix Mechanism: A Collaborative Proof with Gemini 3

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19465  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19465  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: The multiplicative update $\phi(v)=\operatorname{diag}\big((D_v^{1/2} M D_v^{1/2})^{1/2}\big)$ converges globally to the unique maximizer of $J(v)=2\|M^{1/2}D_v^{1/2}\|_*-\sum_i v_i$ for any positive initialization, proven via a majorization‑minimization framework and connected to Bures‑Wasserstein geometry.

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves the long‑standing open problem of global convergence for a multiplicative update rule that arises in the matrix mechanism for differential privacy in machine learning. The update $\phi(v)=\operatorname{diag}((D_v^{1/2} M D_v^{1/2})^{1/2})$ optimizes the potential function $J(v)=2\|M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2}\|_*-\sum_i v_i$ where $M\succ0$ is fixed and $D_v=\operatorname{diag}(v)$. The key insight is to exploit a variational characterization of the nuclear norm to rewrite $J(v)$ as a supremum over unitary matrices. By freezing the unitary factor from the polar decomposition of $M^{1/2}D_v^{1/2}$ at the current iterate, the authors construct a majorization‑minimization (MM) surrogate that is separable and whose maximizer coincides exactly with the update. This surrogate lower‑bounds $J$ and matches its gradient at the current point, guaranteeing monotonic ascent. Consequently, the iterates converge to the unique global optimum from any positive starting point. The analysis further reveals that the update corresponds to a half‑step ($\eta=1/2$) in the Bures‑Wasserstein geometry of positive‑definite matrices, and a full‑step variant $\psi(v)=\phi(v)^2\oslash v$ achieves twice the asymptotic convergence rate.

## Method Summary
The problem is to prove global convergence of the fixed‑point iteration $\phi(v)=\operatorname{diag}((D_v^{1/2} M D_v^{1/2})^{1/2})$ for maximizing the potential function
\[
J(v)=2\bigl\|M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2}\bigr\|_* - \sum_i v_i,
\]
with $M$ symmetric positive definite and $v>0$. The algorithm requires only matrix square roots and a polar decomposition per iteration.

The proof uses the majorization‑minimization (MM) principle. The nuclear norm variational formula $\|A\|_*=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{U}(N)}\operatorname{Tr}(U^\top A)$ rewrites the trace term as a supremum over unitaries. For the current iterate $v^{(k)}$, compute $A_k=M^{1/2} D_{v^{(k)}}^{1/2}$ and its polar decomposition $A_k=U_kP_k$. Freeze $U_k$ to obtain the separable surrogate
\[
G(v;v^{(k)})=2\operatorname{Tr}(U_k^\top M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2})-\sum_i v_i
= \sum_i\bigl(2c_i\sqrt{v_i}-v_i\bigr),
\]
where $c_i=[U_k^\top M^{1/2}]_{ii}$. This surrogate lower‑bounds $J$ and satisfies $G(v^{(k)};v^{(k)})=J(v^{(k)})$ and $\nabla G(v^{(k)};v^{(k)})=\nabla J(v^{(k)})$. Its elementwise maximizer is $v_i^{\text{opt}}=c_i^2$, and the update $v_i^{(k+1)}=\sqrt{v_i^{(k)}v_i^{\text{opt}}}$ is the geometric mean of the current value and the surrogate optimum. Monotonic ascent follows:
\[
J(v^{(k+1)})\ge G(v^{(k+1)};v^{(k)})\ge G(v^{(k)};v^{(k)})=J(v^{(k)}).
\]
Strict concavity and coercivity of $J$ then guarantee convergence to the unique global maximizer from any $v^{(0)}\succ0$.

## Key Results
- **Global convergence**: The update $\phi$ converges to the unique maximizer of $J(v)$ for any positive initialization.
- **Monotonic ascent without line searches**: The MM surrogate provides a strict lower bound and gradient matching, ensuring $J$ strictly increases each iteration.
- **Bures‑Wasserstein connection**: $\phi$ is a half‑step in the Bures‑Wasserstein metric; the full‑step $\psi(v)=\phi(v)^2\oslash v$ doubles the asymptotic convergence rate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The nuclear norm of $A = M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2}$ can be expressed as $\sup_{U\in\mathcal{U}(N)} \operatorname{Tr}(U^\top A)$, enabling a separable surrogate.
- **Mechanism**: Lemma 1 rewrites $2\operatorname{Tr}((D_v^{1/2} M D_v^{1/2})^{1/2})$ as $\sup_U 2\operatorname{Tr}(U^\top M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2})$. Freezing $U$ to the polar factor $U_k$ at iterate $v^{(k)}$ yields a linear function in $\sqrt{v_i}$.
- **Core assumption**: $M \succ 0$ and $v > 0$ ensure $A$ is well‑defined and polar decomposition $A_k = U_k P_k$ exists with unitary $U_k$.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “variational characterization of the nuclear norm to diagonalize the problem”
  - [section 2.1] Lemma 1
  - [corpus] weak: neighbors discuss majorization‑minimization but not this specific lemma
- **Break condition**: If $M$ is not positive definite or $v$ has zero entries, the square root or polar decomposition may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A majorization‑minimization (MM) surrogate $G(v; v^{(k)})$ constructed from $U_k$ guarantees monotonic ascent $J(v^{(k+1)}) \ge J(v^{(k)})$.
- **Mechanism**: $G(v) = 2\operatorname{Tr}(U_k^\top M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2}) - \sum_i v_i$ is separable. Its maximizer is $v_i^{\text{opt}} = c_i^2$ with $c_i = [U_k^\top M^{1/2}]_{ii}$. The update $\phi(v^{(k)})$ computes $v_i^{\text{new}} = c_i \sqrt{v_i^{(k)}}$, the geometric mean of $v^{(k)}$ and $v^{\text{opt}}$. The gain $G(v^{\text{new}})-G(v^{\text{old}})$ reduces to $c_i^2 \cdot z\cdot(z-1)^2\cdot(z+2) \ge 0$ after substituting $v_i = c_i^2 z^4$. Since $J \ge G$ and equality holds at $v^{(k)}$, ascent follows.
- **Core assumption**: Surrogate $G$ lower bounds $J$, matches $J$ and $\nabla J$ at $v^{(k)}$ (tangency), and the scalar polynomial is non‑negative for all $z>0$.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “majorization‑minimization framework constructs a surrogate function whose maximizer coincides with the update, establishing monotonic ascent”
  - [section 2.3.1] definition of $G$ and its properties
  - [section 2.3.2] scalar factorization and ascent proof
- **Break condition**: Failure of tangency (e.g., mis‑identified $U_k$) could make $G$ not a lower bound; a negative polynomial region would break monotonicity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: $\phi$ is equivalent to a half‑step ($\eta=1/2$) in the Bures‑Wasserstein geometry; the full‑step $\psi(v)=\phi(v)^2 \oslash v$ achieves twice the asymptotic convergence rate.
- **Mechanism**: $J(v) = \operatorname{Tr}(M) - d_{\text{BW}}(D_v, M)$, where $d_{\text{BW}}$ is the Bures‑Wasserstein distance. The optimal transport map $T = D_v^{-1/2}(D_v^{1/2} M D_v^{1/2})^{1/2} D_v^{-1/2}$ projects onto diagonal matrices via $T_{\text{diag}} = \operatorname{diag}(T)$. Applying this map with step 1 yields $D_{v^{\text{new}}} = T_{\text{diag}} D_v T_{\text{diag}}$, which simplifies to $\psi(v) = \phi(v)^2 \oslash v$. This is a full gradient step in the Bures‑Wasserstein metric. $\phi(v) = \sqrt{v \odot \psi(v)}$ corresponds to $\eta=1/2$, halving the local contraction factor.
- **Core assumption**: The Bures‑Wasserstein geometry is valid for positive definite matrices and the diagonal projection is correct.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “connects the iteration to optimal transport’s Bures‑Wasserstein geometry”
  - [section 2.4] derivation of $\psi(v)$ and rate analysis
  - [corpus] neighbor paper on Bures‑Wasserstein distance (BJL19) provides supporting literature
- **Break condition**: If the Bures‑Wasserstein distance is not geodesically convex on the diagonal manifold, the full‑step may not converge globally; the factorization relies on $v>0$.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Nuclear norm variational characterization (Lemma 1)  
  - *Why needed*: It rewrites the nonlinear trace term as a supremum over unitaries, which becomes linear in $\sqrt{v_i}$ after freezing the unitary, enabling separation.  
  - *Quick check*: “State the variational formula for $\|A\|_*$ and explain why the supremum is attained by the unitary factor from the polar decomposition of $A$.”  
- **Concept**: Majorization‑Minimization (MM) principle  
  - *Why needed*: MM provides a general framework to prove global convergence of $\phi$ without requiring closed‑form progress; constructing a surrogate that lower bounds $J$ and matches its gradient yields monotonic ascent.  
  - *Quick check*: “What three conditions must a surrogate $G(v; v^{(k)})$ satisfy for the MM algorithm to guarantee $J(v^{(k+1)}) \ge J(v^{(k)})$?”  
- **Concept**: Bures‑Wasserstein distance and optimal transport  
  - *Why needed*: Recognizing $J(v)$ as (up to constant) the Bures‑Wasserstein distance explains the algorithm’s geometric origin, yields a faster variant, and connects to established convergence guarantees.  
  - *Quick check*: “Given $d_{\text{BW}}(A,B) = \operatorname{Tr}(A)+\operatorname{Tr}(B)-2\operatorname{Tr}((A^{1/2} B A^{1/2})^{1/2})$, show how maximizing $J(v)$ is equivalent to minimizing $d_{\text{BW}}(D_v, M)$.”

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Core elements: potential $J(v)=2\|M^{1/2} D_v^{1/2}\|_* - \sum_i v_i$; update $\phi(v)=\operatorname{diag}((D_v^{1/2} M D_v^{1/2})^{1/2})$; polar decomposition $A_k = M^{1/2} D_{v^{(k)}}^{1/2} = U_k P_k$; surrogate $G(v; v^{(k)}) = \sum_i (2 c_i \sqrt{v_i} - v_i)$ with $c_i = [U_k^\top M^{1/2}]_{ii}$; scalar substitution $v_i = c_i^2 z^4$ for gain analysis.  
- **Critical path**: (a) Prove $J$ strictly concave and coercive → unique maximizer $v^*$. (b) Derive gradient $\nabla J(v) = \phi(v) \oslash v^{-1}$. (c) Introduce Lemma 1 to express trace term as $\sup_U 2\operatorname{Tr}(U^\top A)$. (d) Fix $U_k$ to form $G$, show $G \le J$ and $\nabla G(v^{(k)}) = \nabla J(v^{(k)})$. (e) Show $\phi(v^{(k)})$ is the geometric mean of $v^{(k)}$ and the surrogate maximizer $v^{\text{opt}}$. (f) Factorize scalar gain $\Delta G_{\text{scalar}} = c_i^2 z (z-1)^2 (z+2) \ge 0$. (g) Chain $J(v^{(k+1)}) \ge G(v^{(k+1)}) \ge G(v^{(k)}) = J(v^{(k)})$. (h) Use monotonicity + boundedness → convergence to $v^*$. (i) Identify Bures‑Wasserstein link for acceleration.  
- **Design tradeoffs**: MM gives a closed‑form, parameter‑free update with global convergence, avoiding line searches. Cost: each step needs a full polar decomposition ($O(N^3)$). The half‑step $\phi$ is conservative but robust; the full‑step $\psi$ doubles the local rate at same cost. Choosing $\phi$ balances simplicity and stability; $\psi$ is preferable when asymptotic speed matters.  
- **Failure signatures**: (1) $J$ decreases at some iteration → broken monotonicity, likely incorrect $U_k$ or non‑PD $M$. (2) Any $v_i \to 0$ violates positive invariance and may cause numerical issues in square roots. (3) Coefficients $c_i$ become complex/negative, indicating $U_k^\top M^{1/2}$ not properly computed. (4) Scalar gain polynomial negative for some $z>0$ signals algebraic error in factorization.  
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Monotonic ascent test**: Generate random PD $M$ (e.g., $M = LL^\top$) and random $v>0$. Run $\phi$ for 500 steps, record $J(v)$ each step. Verify $J(v^{(k+1)}) - J(v^{(k)}) \ge -10^{-12}$ for all $k$.  
  2. **Rate comparison test**: Using same setup, run $\phi$ and $\psi$ until $\|v - v^*\| < 10^{-8}$ (or $|J(v^{(k+1)})-J(v^{(k)})| < 10^{-12}$). Count iterations; confirm $\psi$ uses roughly half as many as $\phi$ near convergence.  
  3. **Surrogate validation test**: At each iteration, compute $c_i$ and $G(v; v^{(k)})$. Check $J(v) \ge G(v; v^{(k)})$ for random perturbations around $v^{(k)}$, with equality at $v^{(k)}$. Also verify $\nabla J(v^{(k)}) = \nabla G(v^{(k)})$ elementwise within numerical tolerance.

## Limitations
- Polar decomposition diagonal entries `c_i = [U_k^⊤ M^{1/2}]_{ii}` may vanish; positivity is required for the surrogate maximizer and the geometric mean update.
- The MM argument relies on scalar factorization to separate variables; a rigorous proof that `J(v) ≥ G(v; v^{(k)})` for all `v ≻ 0` is missing.
- The Bures‑Wasserstein identification assumes the diagonal projection is a valid retraction; boundary behavior when `v_i → 0` is not fully analyzed.

## Confidence
- Convergence of φ: **High** – MM yields monotonic ascent; J strictly concave and coercive.
- Surrogate properties: **Medium** – gradient tangency and lower bound are plausible but need formal verification.
- Bures‑Wasserstein link: **Medium** – derivation aligns with OT literature; empirical rate tests remain to confirm the 2× speedup.
