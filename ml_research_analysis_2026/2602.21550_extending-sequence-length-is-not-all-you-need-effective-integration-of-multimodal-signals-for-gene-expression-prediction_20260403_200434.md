# Extending Sequence Length is Not All You Need: Effective Integration of Multimodal Signals for Gene Expression Prediction
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.21550
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21550
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Prism achieves state-of-the-art gene expression prediction using only 2k bp sequences by applying causal backdoor adjustment to mitigate spurious correlations from background epigenomic signals, reducing MSE and increasing Pearson correlation on multiple cell lines with a minimal parameter increase (~11K).

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing trend of scaling sequence length for gene expression prediction. It demonstrates that naively integrating multimodal epigenomic signals (e.g., DNase-seq, Hi-C) with long DNA sequences leads to performance degradation because these signals contain confounding "background" patterns that create spurious correlations with expression. The authors propose **Prism**, a method that applies a causal backdoor adjustment by learning multiple representations of these background chromatin states and intervening on them. Prism uses a short 2k bp input window and a lightweight confounder encoder atop a Caduceus backbone. It achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple cell lines (e.g., K562, GM12878) with significant improvements in MSE and Pearson correlation, while adding only ~11,000 parameters. The work underscores that effective multimodal integration requires addressing confounding, not merely extending context.

## Method Summary
Prism introduces a causal intervention module to a standard sequence predictor (Caduceus). It takes two inputs: a one-hot DNA sequence \(X\) (2k bp) and raw epigenomic signal tracks \(S\) over the same region. A signal encoder \(g_\theta\) maps \(S\) to a high-dimensional feature tensor \(H\). A lightweight confounder encoder \(g_\omega\) (a 1D-CNN) processes \(S\) to produce \(n\) normalized weight vectors \(a_1,...,a_n\). Each \(a_i\) element-wise multiplies \(H\) to simulate a specific background chromatin state \(C_i\). The sequence predictor \(h_\phi\) (Caduceus) fuses \(X\) with each weighted \(H \odot a_i\). The final prediction averages the \(n\) context-specific outputs to approximate an interventional quantity \(\hat{Y}_{do}\). Training uses three losses: \(L_1\) (standard prediction on \(X\) and unweighted \(H\)), \(L_2\) (Huber loss on \(\hat{Y}_{do}\)), and \(L_3\) (a uniform diversity loss on the normalized weights \(a_i\) to prevent collapse). The total loss is \(\mathcal{L} = L_1 + \alpha L_2 + \beta L_3\).

## Key Results
*   On K562 with 2k bp input, Prism reduces MSE to **0.1789** (from 0.1856) and increases Pearson \(r\) to **0.8751** (from 0.8723) compared to the previous best, Seq2Exp-soft.
*   Similar consistent improvements are observed on GM12878 and the held-out cell line H1 across MSE, MAE, and Pearson correlation.
*   The method adds only **~11K parameters** to the Caduceus backbone, demonstrating parameter efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Background epigenomic signals act as confounders that create spurious correlations with gene expression, degrading model performance when naively integrated.
*   **Mechanism:** The Structural Causal Model posits an unobserved confounder \(C\) (background chromatin states) affects both the observed epigenomic features \(H\) and expression \(Y\). Simple concatenation of \(H\) into the model learns associations via the backdoor path \(H \leftarrow C \rightarrow Y\) instead of the causal path \(H \rightarrow Y\).
*   **Core assumption:** Proximal epigenomic signals are a mixture of causal foreground information and confounding background patterns that co-vary with expression due to genome-wide chromatin state properties.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] Explicitly states simple concatenation leads to spurious associations and confounding effects.
    *   [section 1] Figure 1(f) shows severe performance drop when background signals are removed at test time from a model trained with them, despite minimal standalone benefit (Figure 1(e)), indicating learned spurious correlations.
*   **Break condition:** If background signals are independent of expression or confounding effects are negligible, backdoor adjustment would not help. The test-time removal degradation contradicts this.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Representing the confounder \(C\) as multiple learned linear combinations of epigenomic feature dimensions allows stratification and intervention via backdoor adjustment.
*   **Mechanism:** The confounder encoder \(g_\omega\) maps raw signals \(S\) to weight vectors \(A = [a_1,...,a_n]\). Each \(a_i\) re-weights the feature tensor \(H\) element-wise (\(H \odot a_i\)), simulating intervention under a specific background state \(C_i\). Averaging predictions across these weighted inputs approximates \(P(Y|do(H))\).
*   **Core assumption:** Background chromatin states can be represented as distinct, gene-specific linear combinations in the high-dimensional feature space of epigenomic signals, and adjusting for these combinations blocks confounding pathways.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.4] Formal definition of \(g_\omega\) and the interventional prediction \(\hat{Y}_{do} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} h_\phi(X, H \odot a_i)\).
    *   [section 4.4] Figure 5 visualizes learned weight vectors as distinct and complementary per gene.
*   **Break condition:** If the linear weighting scheme cannot capture meaningful variation in background states (e.g., states are nonlinear or positional), the intervention is ineffective. The diversity loss helps but doesn't guarantee biological interpretability.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A uniform diversity loss on normalized weight vectors \(\{\tilde{a}_i\}\) prevents collapse, ensuring multiple distinct background states for effective adjustment.
*   **Mechanism:** Loss \(L_3 = \log \left( \sum_{i,j} \exp(2t \cdot \tilde{a}_i^T \tilde{a}_j - 2t) \right)\) penalizes similarity between vectors, encouraging orthogonality/diversity.
*   **Core assumption:** Diverse weight vectors correspond to distinct confounding strata; without diversity, the intervention degenerates to a single (possibly biased) adjustment.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.5] Introduction of the uniform loss to encourage distinct weight vectors.
    *   [section 4.3] Ablation shows performance peaks at \(n=4\) and degrades when \(L_3\) is removed or over-weighted, confirming its role.
*   **Break condition:** If \(L_3\) is too weak, weights collapse; if too strong, it may force artificial diversity that harms generalization.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Structural Causal Models (SCMs) and Backdoor Adjustment**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper frames the confounder problem causally. Understanding \(P(Y|do(H))\) vs. \(P(Y|H)\) and the backdoor criterion is essential to grasp why intervention is necessary and how Prism approximates it.
    *   **Quick check:** If a confounder \(C\) affects both \(H\) and \(Y\), what does the backdoor adjustment formula \(P(Y|do(H)) = \sum_c P(Y|H,C=c)P(C=c)\) accomplish, and why isn't simply including \(C\) as a model input sufficient?
*   **Concept: Chromatin State Combinatorics**
    *   **Why needed:** The confounder \(C\) is defined as "combinations of high-dimensional epigenomic features" reflecting background states. This draws from bioinformatics (ChromHMM) where chromatin states are combinatorial patterns of histone marks.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might a linear combination of epigenomic signal features (via learned weights) be a plausible representation of a "background chromatin state," even though biological states are often discretized (e.g., "active," "repressed")?
*   **Concept: State Space Models (SSMs) for Genomics**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper's critique of long-sequence modeling targets SSMs (Caduceus, Seq2Exp). Understanding SSMs' recency bias and fixed hidden states explains why extending length doesn't help.
    *   **Quick check:** Beyond computational efficiency, what inherent architectural limitations of SSMs (as cited in Section 2) conflict with the goal of modeling gene regulation from distal enhancers?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Input (DNA sequence \(X\), epigenomic signals \(S\)) -> Signal encoder \(g_\theta\) -> Encoded features \(H\)
    Input \(S\) -> Confounder encoder \(g_\omega\) -> Weight vectors \(A = [a_1,...,a_n]\)
    For each \(a_i\): \(H \odot a_i\) -> Caduceus backbone \(h_\phi\) (with \(X\)) -> prediction \(\hat{y}_i\)
    Average \(\{\hat{y}_i\}\) -> Final prediction \(\hat{Y}_{do}\)
    Losses: Huber(\(\hat{Y}_{do}\)), Huber(\(\hat{Y}_{\text{direct}}\)), Diversity(\(A\))
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Encode \(S \rightarrow H\) (via \(g_\theta\)) and \(S \rightarrow A\) (via \(g_\omega\)).
    2.  For each \(a_i \in A\), compute element-wise product \(H \odot a_i\).
    3.  Fuse DNA sequence \(X\) with each \(H \odot a_i\) and pass through Caduceus backbone \(h_\phi\).
    4.  Average the \(n\) backbone outputs (or predictions) to get \(\hat{Y}_{do}\).
    5.  Compute total loss \(\mathcal{L} = L_1(\hat{Y}_{\text{direct}}, y) + \alpha L_2(\hat{Y}_{do}, y) + \beta L_3(A)\).
    6.  Update all modules (\(g_\theta, g_\omega, h_\phi\)) jointly via backpropagation.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Confounder encoder capacity:** The 1D-CNN is lightweight (11K params) but may oversimplify state representation; deeper networks could capture more complex patterns but risk overfitting.
    *   **Number of states \(n\):** More states allow finer adjustment but increase computation and may dilute diversity without enough data. Paper finds optimal \(n \approx 2-4\).
    *   **Intervention weight \(\alpha\):** Balances causal regularization vs. primary task; too low ignores confounding, too high distorts learning (Table 2).
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Weight collapse:** All \(a_i\) become nearly identical (low pairwise dot product). \(L_3\) failed; intervention degenerates.
    *   **No performance gain:** Prism's MSE/MAE similar to Caduceus w/signals. Either confounding is minimal or \(g_\omega\) failed to learn meaningful states.
    *   **Instability with longer sequences:** Performance degrades sharply as \(L\) increases (unlike Seq2Exp), may indicate over-correction or that confounding worsens with length (Appendix J).
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Ablation on \(n\) and \(\alpha\):** Train with \(n \in \{0,1,2,4\}\) and \(\alpha \in \{0,0.1,1,10\}\) on K562 (2k input). Expect: \(n=0\) (no intervention) underperforms; \(\alpha=0\) (no \(L_2\)) underperforms; \(n=2\) or \(4\) with \(\alpha=1\) best (Table 2).
    2.  **Visualize learned weights:** For a few genes, plot the \(a_1, a_2\) vectors. Expect: intra-gene diversity (different patterns) and inter-gene magnitude variations (Figure 5). If all weights identical, \(L_3\) ineffective.
    3.  **Test-time signal removal:** Train Caduceus w/all signals, then test with H3K27ac dropped. Expect: large performance drop (Table 7). Then train Prism and repeat; expect smaller drop, showing confounder adjustment mitigates over-reliance on background signals.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** What is the optimal representation for background chromatin states in the backdoor adjustment framework, and can more complex (e.g., non-linear) encoders improve performance? The paper uses a lightweight CNN with linear weight outputs without theoretical justification; exploring deeper or attention-based encoders could yield gains and better biological alignment.
*   **Open Question 2:** How does the selection and combination of multimodal epigenomic signals impact the confounding effect and overall prediction accuracy, and is there a principled way to choose signals? The study uses a limited set; a systematic analysis across the full ENCODE catalog is needed to understand which signals are most confounding and which provide true causal signal.
*   **Open Question 3:** To what extent can Prism generalize to completely unseen cell types or tissues, given that training data is cell-type specific? Experiments are limited to 2-3 cell lines with separate models. Evaluating on a held-out panel of diverse cell types is necessary to assess transferability of the confounder adjustment.
*   **Open Question 4:** Does the assumption that DNA sequence \(X\) is independent of epigenomic features \(H\) hold in practice, and if not, how does it affect the validity of the backdoor adjustment? The model assumes this independence for simplification, but biologically, sequence influences chromatin marks. Violation could bias the adjustment and requires either empirical testing or SCM extension.

## Limitations
*   **Data preprocessing ambiguities:** Exact specifications for epigenomic signal binning resolution, normalization, and missing value imputation are not fully detailed, hindering exact replication.
*   **Baseline comparison fairness:** Key baselines (Seq2Exp) are reported with 200k bp inputs, while Prism uses 2k. The paper provides a 2k Caduceus baseline, but a full re-evaluation of all baselines at 2k is needed for a fair comparison.
*   **Generalization scope:** Evaluation is restricted to a small number of cell lines. The confounding effect and Prism's efficacy may vary substantially across different tissues and developmental stages, limiting claims of broad applicability.

## Confidence
*   **Background signals as confounders:** **High** - Strong empirical evidence from test-time signal removal experiments showing severe degradation.
*   **Linear confounder representations + backdoor adjustment:** **Medium** - Ablations validate the intervention mechanism, but the assumption that linear weight combinations capture true biological chromatin states lacks external biological validation.
*   **Diversity loss necessity:** **High** - Ablation studies consistently show performance drops when \(L_3\) is removed or misweighted.
*   **Overall SOTA with short sequences:** **Medium** - Gains are consistent across metrics and cell lines, but tempered by uncertainty regarding the fairness of baseline comparisons (potential input length mismatch).

## Next Checks
1.  **Baseline parity experiment:** Retrain Caduceus and Seq2Exp with the identical 2k bp input, data splits, and hyperparameters used for Prism to confirm whether the reported improvement persists under a fair, controlled comparison.
2.  **Biological interpretability of confounders:** Project the learned weight vectors \(a_i\) onto established chromatin state annotations (e.g., ChromHMM states) and test whether distinct \(a_i\) correspond to known background states (e.g., active, repressed, quiescent).
3.  **Cross-cell-line robustness:** Train Prism exclusively on K562 and evaluate zero-shot on GM12878 and H1. Compare the performance drop to the within-cell-line results to quantify how well the confounder adjustment transfers across different epigenomic landscapes.
