# FinSheet-Bench: From Simple Lookups to Complex Reasoning, Where LLMs Break on Financial Spreadsheets

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07316
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07316
*   **Reference Count:** 7
*   **Primary Result:** Evaluated ten LLM configurations on a synthetic financial spreadsheet benchmark; the best model achieved only 82.4% overall accuracy, with performance dropping below 50% on the most complex files and ~20% on the hardest reasoning tasks, indicating no model is production-ready.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FinSheet-Bench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate Large Language Models (LLMs) on the challenging task of question answering over complex, real-world financial spreadsheets. Due to data confidentiality, the authors created a synthetic dataset derived from private equity fund structures, comprising 24 Excel files with varied layouts and 16 question templates ranging from simple lookups to multi-step numeric aggregations. Testing ten state-of-the-art model configurations reveals a stark performance cliff: while simple lookups are handled relatively well (~89%), complex aggregations and sorting tasks see accuracy plummet to ~20%. Even the top model, Gemini 3.1 Pro, makes an error on approximately one in six questions overall and fails on half the questions in the largest, most structurally complex spreadsheet. The results point to fundamental limitations in LLMs' ability to preserve two-dimensional spreadsheet structure from linear text serialization and perform deterministic multi-step calculations, suggesting that production systems must decouple document understanding from computational execution.

## Method Summary
The study constructs a synthetic benchmark from eight real private equity fund template structures. Each template is scaled and perturbed to create three variants (A, B, C) that differ in size (number of companies/funds) and structural complexity (e.g., merged cells, fund dividers, column shuffling), yielding 24 unique Excel files. These files are serialized to comma-separated text (CSV) via `openpyxl`, preserving cell values and blank rows but discarding all formatting, borders, and merged cell semantics. Sixteen question templates (e.g., counting funds, summing values, computing medians across sections) are parameterized and evaluated in a zero-shot setting with minimal prompts. Accuracy is measured against a ground truth derived from manually extracted standardized tables using a three-tier cascading verification system (exact match, fuzzy numeric/list matching, and an LLM adjudicator). Ten model configurations from leading providers are tested, including variants with reasoning/thinking modes enabled.

## Key Results
*   **Overall ceiling:** The best model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) achieves 82.4% accuracy, equating to roughly one error every six questions—unsuitable for production.
*   **Complexity sensitivity:** Accuracy on the largest spreadsheet (152 companies, 8 funds) averages only 48.6% across all models, compared to 86.2% on the simplest file.
*   **Task-specific collapse:** Performance on complex aggregation tasks falls to ~20% for top models, while simple lookups reach ~89%. Reasoning modes provide significant gains (e.g., +22.8 pp) but do not close the gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structure Loss in Serialization
- **Claim:** LLMs fail to preserve the two-dimensional semantic structure of spreadsheets when they are serialized as linear text.
- **Mechanism:** Tokenization disrupts positional relationships; merged cells, formatting cues (bold, underlines, colors), and spatial grouping are lost, making it difficult to distinguish fund sections, identify correct column headers, and associate values with their intended categories. This leads to row misidentification and column selection errors.
- **Core assumption:** Visual and spatial layout in financial spreadsheets carries essential semantic meaning beyond the raw cell values.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions "complex layouts, fund dividers, and multi-line column names"; [section 4.5.1] details lost information (merged cells, colors, borders, visual distinctiveness of dividers).
- **Break condition:** If models achieve uniformly high accuracy across all structural variants using alternative input modalities (e.g., rendered images or structured JSON), this mechanism would be invalidated.

### Mechanism 2: Lack of Deterministic Computation
- **Claim:** Standalone LLMs lack the deterministic numerical computation required for reliable multi-step financial aggregation and sorting.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs generate token sequences rather than executing symbolic math; they are prone to arithmetic errors, incorrect sorting, and miscalculation of statistics (e.g., median, percentiles) that require precise intermediate value handling.
- **Core assumption:** Financial extraction requires separation of document understanding (interpretation) from deterministic calculation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes "complex aggregations fell to ~20%... fundamental LLM limitations in... multi-step numeric reasoning"; [section 5.2] Table 5 shows Aggregation (53.7% all models), Sorting (37.5%), Complex Aggregation (19.6%); [section 6.3] "brute-force extraction" hypothetical shows per-value lookups achieve near-perfect accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If single-call LLM responses consistently match ground truth for complex aggregations with >95% accuracy across diverse spreadsheets, this mechanism is falsified.

### Mechanism 3: Complexity Scaling
- **Claim:** Accuracy degrades with file complexity (number of companies/funds) and structural variation due to limited effective context utilization.
- **Mechanism:** Longer, more complex spreadsheets increase cognitive steps needed to locate relevant data and maintain coherence; attention disperses, and the model may lose track of hierarchical groupings, especially after structural modifications.
- **Core assumption:** LLM reasoning capacity is bounded by context window quality, not just length; structural noise exacerbates this.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "largest spreadsheet... average accuracy of just 48.6%... compared to 86.2% on the easiest"; [section 5.6] Figure 9 shows moderate negative correlation (−0.23pp per 1K chars, R²=0.30); per-file accuracy varies widely, with some shorter variants underperforming longer ones due to layout complexity.
- **Break condition:** If accuracy remains stable across files with varying numbers of companies/funds and structural variants, file complexity is not a primary driver.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Tabular Data Serialization**
  - Why needed here: Understanding how Excel spreadsheets are converted to text (CSV) and what semantic information is lost (merged cells, formatting, spatial cues) is essential to diagnose why LLMs misinterpret fund boundaries and column headers.
  - Quick check question: What specific visual formatting cues in a financial spreadsheet typically indicate a fund divider or subtotal row, and how does CSV serialization treat them?

- **Concept: Numeric Reasoning vs. Deterministic Computation**
  - Why needed here: The paper's core finding is that LLMs excel at lookup but fail at calculation. Distinguishing between "retrieving" a cell's value and "computing" an aggregate across cells is critical for designing pipelines.
  - Quick check question: Why might an LLM incorrectly compute the average enterprise value across multiple funds even if it correctly identifies every individual company's entry EV?

- **Concept: Schema Discovery in Semi-Structured Tables**
  - Why needed here: Financial spreadsheets lack a fixed schema; the system must first infer fund boundaries, column semantics, and header layouts before any reliable extraction. Q1 ("How many funds?") and Q2 ("How many companies per fund?") are themselves challenging.
  - Quick check question: What makes counting the number of funds in a serialized spreadsheet difficult for an LLM, even without any arithmetic?

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map:* Serializer -> Schema Discoverer -> Row Extractor -> Deterministic Calculator -> Verifier

*Critical path:* Schema discovery must succeed with high confidence; errors here propagate to all downstream steps. Row extraction accuracy must be near-perfect for the calculator to produce correct results.

*Design tradeoffs:*
- *Single-call QA:* Low latency/cost but high multi-step reasoning failure rate.
- *Brute-force per-cell:* Near-perfect lookup accuracy but prohibitive cost/latency (~1,500 API calls per file).
- *Staged pipeline* (schema → row extraction → calculation): Balances cost (~100 calls for 100-company file) and accuracy; produces auditable intermediate table.

*Failure signatures:*
- Low accuracy on "Aggregation," "Sorting," "Complex Aggregation" categories.
- Performance drop on files with fund dividers, merged headers, or shuffled rows.
- Inconsistent results across version B/C structural variants of the same base file.
- High token usage with reasoning mode not translating to proportional accuracy gain on numeric tasks.

*First 3 experiments:*
1.  **Serialization sensitivity test:** Convert a subset of FinSheet-Bench files to alternative formats (structured JSON preserving 2D grid, tabular HTML, rendered spreadsheet images) and evaluate top models. Hypothesis: Visual/structured formats improve structural parsing but may not resolve numeric reasoning failures.
2.  **Two-stage pipeline prototype:** Implement schema discovery (1–2 LLM calls) followed by row-level extraction (1 call per company) and deterministic calculation. Compare accuracy on complex questions (Q11–Q16) against single-call baseline. Measure cost/latency tradeoff.
3.  **Failure mode ablation:** Manually analyze a sample of errors from top models on "Sorting" and "Complex Aggregation" tasks. Classify errors as: (a) wrong value identification (parsing), (b) wrong arithmetic (calculation), (c) incorrect ordering logic. This quantifies the relative contribution of Mechanism 1 vs. Mechanism 2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Do vision-language models achieve higher accuracy on complex financial spreadsheet reasoning tasks when provided with rendered images instead of text-serialized CSV input?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note that text serialization discards visual formatting cues (bold, colors, borders) that carry semantic meaning in financial spreadsheets and suggest that vision-capable models could recover these cues, though core numerical reasoning failures might persist (Section 7, Future Work).
- **Why unresolved:** The benchmark exclusively used CSV text; no multimodal experiments were run. The potential trade-off between improved parsing from visual cues and persistent reasoning errors remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate top vision-capable LLMs (e.g., Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.2) on the same FinSheet-Bench questions using rendered spreadsheet images versus CSV text, measuring accuracy differences on complex aggregation and sorting tasks while controlling for model and token budget.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do synthetic data perturbations that break real-world accounting identities (e.g., inconsistent asset-liability-equity relationships) affect LLM accuracy on multi-step numeric reasoning?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The data generation scheme scales entry and exit values by different factors and adds independent perturbations, which may break accounting identities, though the authors note real data also contains minor inconsistencies and exact equations are preserved where they exist (Section 3.1.2).
- **Why unresolved:** The impact of these introduced inconsistencies on reasoning tasks has not been isolated; the observed accuracy drop could be partly due to synthetic data artifacts rather than inherent model limitations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Generate a controlled version of FinSheet-Bench where accounting identities are strictly enforced during synthetic data creation. Compare model performance on identity-dependent calculations (e.g., net debt ratios) between the enforced and original perturbed datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent are FinSheet-Bench’s difficulty patterns (accuracy degradation with file size and complexity) generalizable to financial spreadsheets from other domains (e.g., corporate budgeting, banking)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The benchmark is explicitly derived from private equity fund structures; the limitations section acknowledges that other financial domains may present different challenges, and related work focuses on public filings unlike private equity spreadsheets.
- **Why unresolved:** Without cross-domain evaluation, it is unclear whether the observed limitations reflect fundamental LLM constraints on all complex spreadsheets or domain-specific structural features.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Curate a small set of confidential or synthetic spreadsheets from other financial domains (e.g., loan portfolio tracking, corporate forecasting), apply analogous question templates, and evaluate top models. Compare accuracy gradients by file size and task complexity to FinSheet-Bench results.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does a staged pipeline that separates spreadsheet schema discovery, row-level extraction, and deterministic computation improve accuracy and cost-efficiency for complex spreadsheet QA compared to single-call end-to-end approaches?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors propose such a pipeline as a practical middle ground between per-cell brute force and end-to-end QA, motivated by the gap between high lookup accuracy and low aggregation accuracy (Section 6.3). This is listed as key future work.
- **Why unresolved:** No empirical evaluation of pipeline architectures exists; the hypothesis is based on error analysis and a conceptual brute-force argument but remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement the three-stage pipeline using LLMs for schema discovery and row extraction, then perform calculations deterministically. Evaluate on FinSheet-Bench complex aggregation/sorting tasks, comparing accuracy, token usage, and latency against single-call QA with reasoning enabled.

## Limitations
- **Dataset access:** The 24 proprietary Excel files and ground-truth tables are unreleased, blocking exact replication and independent validation.
- **Model parity:** Tests include unreleased future models (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6), preventing live re-evaluation with current APIs.
- **Serialization bias:** All tests used CSV text; findings may not generalize to image-based or structured JSON inputs that preserve spatial layout.
