# Glove2Hand: Synthesizing Natural Hand-Object Interaction from Multi-Modal Sensing Gloves

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20850  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20850  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Translates multi-modal sensing glove videos into photorealistic bare-hand HOI videos with SOTA quality (FID 30.1, FVD 19.5) and enables robust downstream tasks.

## Executive Summary
Conventional hand-object interaction (HOI) videos captured by standard cameras lack physical signals such as contact forces and inertial data, and often suffer from occlusions that obscure hand-object contact. Glove2Hand introduces a system that translates egocentric videos taken with a multi-modal sensing glove into photorealistic bare-hand HOI videos, preserving synchronized tactile and IMU data. The core of the approach is a novel 3D Gaussian hand model that ensures temporally consistent and geometry-aware rendering, combined with a diffusion-based hand restorer that refines hand-object boundaries and wrist connectivity. This pipeline enables the creation of the HandSense dataset—the first to provide paired glove-to-hand videos along with tactile and IMU signals. Extensive evaluation shows that Glove2Hand achieves state-of-the-art video quality (FID 30.1, FVD 19.5) and human evaluations close to real-hand scores. Moreover, the dataset significantly boosts downstream tasks like video-based contact estimation (85.3% IoU) and robust hand tracking under heavy occlusion (MKPE reduced to 16.6 mm).

## Method Summary
Glove2Hand processes an input egocentric video from a multi-modal sensing glove, along with hand pose estimates (from MoCap or the glove's IMU). The pipeline begins by optimizing a subject-specific 3D Gaussian hand model: a canonical hand mesh with surface-anchored 3D Gaussians that are deformed along with the mesh using a skinning-based approach. The model renders a hand-only video conditioned on per-frame poses. In parallel, a segmentation module (Grounding DINO + SAM-2) extracts masks for the glove and the interacted object. The glove region is erased from the original video using Propainter inpainting, while object pixels are preserved. The rendered hand (with a dilated mask and a wrist hole) is then overlaid onto this glove-removed video, forming a composite that serves as input to a diffusion-based hand restorer. The restorer, built on ControlNet and AnimateDiff, is trained on pairs of such corrupted composites and real bare-hand videos; it inpaints the hand region to produce a photorealistic bare-hand output. The entire system operates in a sliding window for long sequences.

## Key Results
- **State-of-the-art video quality:** FID 30.1, FVD 19.5 on HandSense test set.
- **Near-real human evaluation:** Mean opinion scores approach those of real bare-hand videos.
- **Downstream task boosts:** Contact estimation IoU 85.3%; hand tracking under occlusion MKPE reduced from 19.2 mm to 16.6 mm.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Temporally Consistent 3D Gaussian Hand Model
The 3D Gaussians are anchored to a canonical hand mesh via barycentric coordinates and deformed by transforming the mesh vertices with linear blend skinning; Gaussians are recomputed on the deformed surface using a deformation gradient (section 3.2.1). This maintains surface integrity, provides a stable geometric prior, and enables relighting via spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients predicted from hand pose. The normals derived from the mesh avoid albedo-illumination ambiguity. Break condition: noisy pose estimates or mismatched canonical mesh degrade consistency.

### Mechanism 2: Diffusion-Based Hand Restorer for Boundary Refinement
The restorer integrates the rendered hand into the scene by conditioning on a composite video: the glove-removed background with the overlayed rendered hand (wrist masked, hand mask dilated). Using ControlNet for spatial guidance and AnimateDiff for temporal coherence, it learns to inpaint the hand region back to the real bare-hand distribution (section 3.2.2). Ablations confirm its distinct contribution (e.g., Table 2 FD improvements with "+Diffusion"). Break condition: severe geometry errors in the Gaussian render may cause the restorer to produce implausible corrections.

### Mechanism 3: Inference-Time Glove Removal and Object Masking
Before overlaying the rendered hand, the glove region is erased using Propainter inpainting based on a temporally consistent glove mask from SAM-2 and Grounding DINO. The object mask ensures foreground object pixels remain intact (section 3.2.2). This cleanup yields a cleaner composite, allowing the diffusion restorer to focus on hand-specific details. Ablations show performance drops when removing either glove removal or object masking (Table 2). Break condition: segmentation failures or inpainting artifacts introduce new errors.

## Foundational Learning
Understanding these core concepts is essential for replicating Glove2Hand:

- **3D Gaussian Splatting**: A rendering primitive that represents scenes with anisotropic Gaussians. *Why needed*: Forms the hand rendering core; surface anchoring and affine transformation enable stable deformation and relighting. *Quick check*: How does anchoring Gaussians to a mesh surface improve lighting estimation and deformation stability compared to free-space Gaussians?
- **Diffusion Model Conditioning for Video**: Latent diffusion models conditioned on spatial cues (ControlNet) and temporal adapters (AnimateDiff) to generate coherent video. *Why needed*: Powers the hand restorer that refines boundaries and motion. *Quick check*: Why is AnimateDiff necessary instead of applying a single-image diffusion model frame-by-frame?
- **Segmentation Mask Generation for HOI**: Promptable segmentation (SAM-2) and open-vocabulary detection (Grounding DINO) produce accurate masks for hand, object, and glove. *Why needed*: Critical for glove removal and object preservation during inference. *Quick check*: How does the pipeline select the correct interacting object from multiple detections, and why is temporal propagation of masks important?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map (data flow):**  
Glove video + MoCap/IMU → Pose Extractor & Refiner → 3D Gaussian Hand → Segmentation Pipeline (Grounding DINO + SAM-2) → Glove Removal (Propainter) → Hand Overlay (with object mask) → Diffusion Restorer (ControlNet + AnimateDiff) → Output bare-hand video.

**Critical Path:** Pose extraction → Gaussian render → glove/object masks → glove removal → composite creation → diffusion → output. Failures in pose or segmentation break downstream steps.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Speed vs. Quality:* Gaussian training heavy (~12h/subject) but inference fast (50 FPS render); diffusion is slow bottleneck (~0.5 FPS).
- *Generalization vs. Identity:* Subject-specific Gaussians ensure identity fidelity; shared diffusion leverages HOT3D for generalization.
- *Simplicity vs. Flexibility:* Surface-grounded Gaussians sacrifice deformation expressivity for stability and relighting; avoids learning complex skinning weights.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Temporal flickering or inconsistent hand geometry (Gaussian training failed or pose noisy).
- Hand-object penetration or floating after diffusion (composite mask errors or restorer underfitting).
- Wrist discontinuity or ghost glove artifacts (glove removal incomplete or object mask wrong).
- Lighting mismatch between hand and scene (SH prediction ineffective).

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Gaussian reconstruction sanity check:** Render hand-only video from training poses; evaluate geometry, relighting, temporal smoothness against ground-truth hand crops.
2. **Diffusion single-frame restoration:** Feed a single composite frame to the trained restorer; assess hand boundary quality and wrist connection against real hand images.
3. **End-to-end video pipeline:** Run full pipeline on a held-out glove clip; compute FID/FVD against real hand clip; subjectively check motion stability and HOI plausibility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Generalization to unseen hand identities. The paper uses subject-specific Gaussian models and observes performance drop in "In-the-Wild" settings; it hypothesizes scaling data could help but does not explore universal modeling. Evidence: human evaluation degradation on unseen subjects; no experiment on shared parameters. To resolve: train a single universal Gaussian model across multiple subjects and compare quality metrics (MOS, FID/FVD) against subject-specific models.

**Open Question 2:** Zero-shot generalization to novel object categories. HandSense contains only 10 object categories; the diffusion restorer is trained on these. It is unclear whether the method can handle completely new objects (e.g., deformable items) without retraining. Evidence: no held-out object evaluation. To resolve: evaluate on a set of held-out object categories not present during training, measuring FID/FVD and human plausibility.

**Open Question 3:** Long-term temporal consistency over minutes or hours. The paper only reports FVD-long for 10–30 second clips and uses a sliding window for longer videos, but no validation beyond 30 seconds. Sliding windows may accumulate errors or lose global coherence. Evidence: absence of long-sequence experiments. To resolve: generate hour-long HOI videos and compute consistency metrics (feature trajectory smoothness, segment-wise FID) and human evaluation over time.

**Open Question 4:** Real-time inference feasibility. The pipeline runs at ~0.5 FPS due to the diffusion bottleneck; no optimization explored for interactive applications. Evidence: Appendix D states inference speed. To resolve: implement optimizations (distillation, pruning, efficient architecture) and report quality–speed trade-offs.

## Limitations
- **Data dependency:** HandSense dataset is not publicly available, requiring the same glove hardware and calibration; this limits reproducibility and may cause domain gaps for novel hand shapes/lighting.
- **Pose accuracy critical:** The pipeline assumes accurate glove-derived hand poses; noisy MoCap/IMU inputs likely break the Gaussian model's temporal consistency.
- **Segmentation fragility:** The reliance on SAM-2 and Grounding DINO means that inaccurate or temporally inconsistent masks propagate errors to glove removal and diffusion conditioning.

## Confidence
- **3D Gaussian hand model (Mechanism 1):** Medium confidence. The design is well-described but details of adaptive Gaussian reassignment and pose-noise robustness are not fully validated.
- **Diffusion hand restorer (Mechanism 2):** Medium–High confidence. Ablations confirm its distinct contribution, but its performance depends on plausible Gaussian input; extreme geometry errors remain untested.
- **Inference cleanup (Mechanism 3):** Medium confidence. Ablations show necessity, but failure modes under segmentation drift or dynamic backgrounds are not thoroughly analyzed.

## Next Checks
1. **Pose-noise sensitivity test:** Add controlled Gaussian noise to ground-truth hand poses and measure degradation in Gaussian render quality (temporal flicker, geometry error) before diffusion.
2. **Segmentation failure simulation:** Replace SAM-2 masks with temporally inconsistent or inaccurate masks (e.g., occasional glove pixels retained) and quantify impact on final FID/FVD.
3. **Cross-subject generalization:** Train Gaussian model on one subject and test on unseen subjects without fine-tuning; evaluate identity preservation and relighting plausibility.
