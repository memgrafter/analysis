# Detached Skip-Links and $R$-Probe: Decoupling Feature Aggregation from Gradient Propagation for MLLM OCR

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20020  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20020  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Detached Skip-Links and R-Probe improve OCR metrics (e.g., average score from 65.2 to 68.3 on Perception Encoder) while enhancing training stability.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal LLMs often fuse multi-layer ViT features via skip connections, which inadvertently create direct back‑propagation paths from high‑level semantic losses to early visual layers. These shallow layers encode fine‑grained geometric patterns, and the mismatched gradients destabilize training and degrade OCR performance. To address this, the authors propose **Detached Skip‑Links**: a forward‑pass reuse of shallow features with gradient stopping on the skip branch, decoupling feature aggregation from gradient optimization without added parameters. They also introduce **R‑Probe**, a reconstruction‑based diagnostic that measures pixel‑level recoverability of visual tokens using a shallow LLM‑initialized decoder, serving as a proxy for fine‑detail preservation. Evaluated across multiple ViT backbones and up to 7 M training samples, the method yields consistent improvements on OCR‑centric benchmarks and general multimodal tasks, and R‑Probe loss correlates strongly with downstream OCR performance.

## Method Summary
Training follows a two‑stage procedure. First, *adapter pre‑training* freezes the ViT backbone and LLM; only a lightweight MLP adapter is trained to project visual tokens into the LLM space. The key modification is in the fusion: intermediate ViT features are sampled at a regular stride \(S\); the shallowest \(D\) of these are detached (stop‑gradient) before concatenation with deeper features and the main‑path output. Second, *supervised fine‑tuning* (SFT) optionally adds an auxiliary R‑Probe reconstruction loss and may unfreeze parts of the backbone. R‑Probe attaches a shallow Transformer decoder (initialized from the first quarter of the LLM) to the frozen encoder+adapter. Given a context sequence (background image + optional text), it reconstructs a target image region containing text; the per‑token mean‑squared error quantifies how well fine details survive the projection.

## Key Results
- OCR average score improved from 65.2 to 68.3 on the Perception Encoder benchmark.
- Gains observed across diverse ViT backbones (e.g., InternViT‑300M, AimV2‑L, SigLip2‑So400M) with up to 7 M training samples.
- R‑Probe reconstruction loss exhibits strong negative correlation with downstream OCR performance, confirming its diagnostic sensitivity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gradient Interference from Multi‑Layer Skip Fusion
In MLLMs that concatenate intermediate ViT features, skip connections create direct back‑propagation paths from the LLM’s semantic loss to early visual layers. Shallow layers encode low‑level geometric structures (edges, strokes) whose optimization should be guided by low‑level objectives, not high‑level semantics. Gradients flowing through the skip branch exhibit high variance, weak alignment with the main‑path gradient, and negligible cross‑covariance, effectively acting as noise that overwrites pre‑trained spatial priors and destabilizes joint training. Evidence includes gradient statistics measurements (Fig. 3) and observed training instability when skip gradients are present.

### Mechanism 2: Detached Skip‑Links Decouple Feature Aggregation from Gradient Propagation
By applying stop‑gradient to the shallow skip features before fusion, the model retains their fine‑grained information in the forward pass without exposing early layers to disruptive high‑level gradients. Only the main‑path and deeper skip branches receive gradients from the LLM loss. This reduces total gradient variance and aligns updates with the main branch’s lower‑variance signal. Theoretical analysis (Section 4) shows that under assumptions of variance dominance and weak mean alignment, the detached estimator improves expected one‑step progress (Proposition 4.3). Empirically, detaching shallowest layers consistently improves OCR and overall performance (Section 6.3).

### Mechanism 3: R‑Probe as a Reconstruction‑Based Diagnostic of Detail Preservation
R‑Probe attaches a shallow LLM‑initialized decoder to the frozen vision encoder and adapter. Given a context sequence, it reconstructs a target image region containing text. Because the decoder shares initialization with the LLM’s early layers, its success depends on the visual tokens retaining sufficient spatial detail and being embedded in a space interpretable by the LLM. The per‑token MSE thus measures fine‑detail fidelity. Lower R‑Probe loss correlates with higher downstream OCR scores across multiple ViT backbones (Table 8), validating its sensitivity as an early proxy for detail preservation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: ViT backbone (produces patch embeddings at multiple depths) → Adapter (MLP that fuses main‑path, deep skips, and detached shallow skips) → LLM (processes visual tokens alongside text). R‑Probe head (shallow Transformer decoder + MLP projector) attaches to the frozen backbone+adapter for reconstruction diagnostics.

**Critical path**:  
1. Forward: input image → ViT → extract features at selected blocks (stride = \(S\)).  
2. Shallow features (first \(D\)) → `stop_gradient` → stored as \(h_{\text{shallow}}\); deeper features retained as \(h_{\text{deep}}\) and optionally \(h_{\text{main}}\).  
3. Fuse: \([h_{\text{main}}, h_{\text{deep}}, \text{sg}(h_{\text{shallow}})]\) → MLP adapter → visual tokens → LLM → loss.  
4. Backward: LLM loss backpropagates through LLM and adapter, but gradients reaching the skip branch are cut; only main/deep branches propagate to the ViT (if unfrozen). R‑Probe operates on the same frozen backbone+adapter with a separate reconstruction loss.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Detachment depth*: Only the earliest layers are detached; deeper skips remain trainable.  
- *Sampling stride (S)*: Denser fusion (small \(S\), e.g. 3–6) provides more information but increases memory; too sparse (\(S\approx12\)) underutilizes details.  
- *R‑Probe auxiliary loss*: Can improve OCR but may slightly harm abstract reasoning.  
- *ViT freezing*: The ViT is typically frozen during adapter pre‑training; selective unfreezing during SFT may yield further gains.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Detaching too many shallow layers eliminates the model’s ability to adapt visual features, causing performance drops across tasks.  
- Using no detachment leads to training instability (oscillating loss, degraded attention maps) and OCR gains vanish.  
- High R‑Probe loss despite detachment indicates inadequate adapter projection or shallow features for detail preservation.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Replication of main result**: Train a baseline adapter with full skip gradients and a detached variant on a small subset (e.g., 100 k samples); evaluate on an OCR benchmark (DocVQA) to confirm stability and gain.  
2. **R‑Probe sensitivity check**: With a frozen backbone+adapter, run R‑Probe across fusion strides and detachment depths; verify that the configuration with lowest reconstruction loss yields the best OCR after a few fine‑tuning steps.  
3. **Gradient statistics**: Implement double‑backward to measure \(g_{\text{main}}\) and \(g_{\text{skip}}\) norms and cosine similarity early in training; confirm the described patterns (variance dominance, orthogonality) as a diagnostic of correct detachment behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What principle should guide the selection of ViT layers to detach?**  
   - *Basis*: Ablations show “shallow‑only detachment is robust” but no general rule across architectures/tasks.  
   - *Why unresolved*: Optimal detachment likely depends on encoder depth, pretraining objective, and downstream characteristics; trial‑and‑error remains costly.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: A broad empirical study across ViT families and vision‑language tasks to correlate encoder properties with detachment boundaries, yielding practical guidelines.

2. **How does the depth of the R‑Probe decoder affect its diagnostic sensitivity?**  
   - *Basis*: R‑Probe uses the first quarter of LLM layers justified by modality‑agnostic early layers; no depth ablation.  
   - *Why unresolved*: Different LLMs may require different depths; the default may be suboptimal and limit generalizability.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Systematically vary decoder depth (e.g., 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, full LLM layers) and measure reconstruction quality and its correlation with OCR/perception scores across multiple LLMs.

3. **Can R‑Probe be integrated as an auxiliary training objective without sacrificing reasoning?**  
   - *Basis*: Appendix shows joint training improves OCR but degrades abstract reasoning (e.g., CharXiv RQ).  
   - *Why unresolved*: The trade‑off may stem from imbalanced loss weighting or an overly OCR‑specific reconstruction target; it is unknown whether a better multi‑task regime could yield overall gains.  
   - *Resolution evidence*: Multi‑task experiments varying R‑Probe loss weight, curriculum schedule, and reconstruction targets on a balanced benchmark suite that includes both perception and reasoning tests, aiming for a Pareto‑optimal configuration.

## Limitations
- Theory scope limited: gradient interference assumptions validated only on a single ViT backbone; may not hold for other architectures or when fine‑tuning from scratch.
- Gains are modest (+3.1 OCR average) and may stem primarily from training stability rather than enhanced representations; generalization to non‑OCR tasks or larger models remains unclear.
- R‑Probe’s correlation with downstream OCR is empirical, not causal; its sensitivity to adapter design, LLM initialization, and context construction is under‑explored.

## Confidence
- Gradient interference exists: **High** – supported by gradient statistics and observed instabilities.
- Detached Skip‑Links improve performance: **Medium** – benefits are consistent but rely on heuristic detachment depths and theoretical assumptions that may not universally hold.
- R‑Probe as diagnostic: **Medium** – strong correlation shown, but causal link and generalizability are not fully established.

## Next Checks
1. Test detached skip‑links across additional ViT families (e.g., DeiT, Swin, ConvNeXt) with varied initializations to verify robustness of interference patterns and performance gains.
2. Compare stop‑gradient against alternative gradient‑modification methods (e.g., low‑pass filtering, auxiliary shallow reconstruction loss) to isolate the specific contribution of gradient decoupling.
3. Evaluate R‑Probe on non‑OCR vision‑language tasks (VQA, captioning) and with different context constructions to assess its generalizability as a detail‑preservation metric.
