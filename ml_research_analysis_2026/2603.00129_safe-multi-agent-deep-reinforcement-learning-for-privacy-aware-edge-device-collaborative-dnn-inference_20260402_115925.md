# Safe Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning for Privacy-Aware Edge-Device Collaborative DNN Inference
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00129
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00129
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** HC-MAPPO-L achieves a combined user cost of 131.35 while meeting a 3s delay constraint (2.74s average) with >98.5% success rate, outperforming baselines by 12-21%.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of privacy-aware collaborative deep neural network (DNN) inference across edge devices and servers. It jointly optimizes inference delay, energy consumption, and privacy cost under dynamic resource constraints, formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). The proposed HC-MAPPO-L is a hierarchical multi-agent safe reinforcement learning framework that decomposes the CMDP into three layers: a slow-timescale model deployment policy, a per-slot user association and partitioning policy enhanced with Lagrangian multipliers, and an attention-based resource allocation policy. Extensive evaluations show HC-MAPPO-L consistently satisfies stringent delay constraints (e.g., 2.74 seconds vs. 3-second target) while achieving the lowest combined user cost among baselines, with service success rates above 98.5% across varying scales.

## Method Summary
HC-MAPPO-L employs a hierarchical decomposition of the CMDP into three decision layers, each with its own policy network and timescale. At the slow timescale (every ΔT slots), deployment agents on each server use an auto-regressive GRU policy to select which DNN models to cache, respecting storage limits and anticipating future requests. At each slot, user agents decide which server to associate with and how to split the DNN computation (partition point) based on their request and the current deployment matrix; these agents are trained with Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) using a shared adaptive Lagrangian multiplier to enforce a long-term average delay constraint. Concurrently, for each server, allocation agents use an attention mechanism to distribute compute and bandwidth resources among its associated users. Training follows a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm: critics have access to global information during training (e.g., all users' requests, deployment matrix) while actors execute with local observations. The Lagrangian multiplier is updated via dual ascent to adaptively penalize delay violations, and separate value networks estimate reward and cost for the constrained policy update.

## Key Results
- Achieves combined user cost of 131.35, beating baselines by 12-21%.
- Meets delay constraint: average 2.74s (target 3s).
- Service success rate >98.5% across varying scales.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hierarchical Temporal Decomposition
- **Claim:** Decomposing the CMDP into three layers aligned with distinct decision timescales (slow-timescale deployment, fast-timescale operational decisions) ensures tractability while preserving necessary coordination.
- **Mechanism:** The deployment agent updates every ΔT slots (slow timescale), making caching decisions that shape the feasible action space for user agents. User agents act each slot, handling association and partitioning within the cached model constraints. Allocation agents act per-slot to distribute resources among associated users. This separation prevents the joint combinatorial explosion of all decisions and mirrors the physical system's natural timescales.
- **Core assumption:** Decisions at different timescales exhibit a semi-Markovian structure where slow-timescale decisions define quasi-static constraints for fast-timescale optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "decomposes the CMDP into three hierarchically structured policy layers: an auto-regressive based model deployment policy, a Lagrangian-enhanced user association and model partitioning policy, and an attention-based resource allocation policy."
  - [section IV.A] "The framework is further strengthened by an adaptive Lagrangian mechanism... To ensure tractability while maintaining coordination, we decompose the CMDP into three hierarchically structured policy layers..."
- **Break condition:** If the timescale separation ΔT is misaligned with actual system dynamics (e.g., deployment changes faster than user demand patterns), the hierarchical decomposition may introduce suboptimality by creating stale constraints.

### Mechanism 2: Lagrangian Relaxation for Long-Term Constraint Enforcement
- **Claim:** Integrating a shared adaptive Lagrangian multiplier λ into the user agents' policy optimization (via PPO-Lagrangian) enforces the long-term average delay constraint more reliably than reward penalization, enabling stable training under stringent QoS.
- **Mechanism:** Each user agent's policy maximizes the Lagrangian objective: reward minus λ times delay cost. λ is updated via dual ascent (λ ← max(0, λ + αλ·(ˆJ_C_usr − ¯J_C))). This decouples constraint satisfaction from reward maximization: when average delay exceeds the threshold, λ increases, penalizing delay-inducing actions in the policy gradient; when below, λ decreases, allowing reward-seeking exploration. The shared λ ensures all user agents cooperate toward the global constraint.
- **Core assumption:** The long-term average constraint (C8) can be enforced via a per-agent sufficient condition with a shared multiplier, and the dual variable converges faster than policy updates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "enhances Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) with adaptive Lagrangian dual updates to enforce long-term delay constraints."
  - [section IV.B.2] "The Lagrangian for the user agents’ problem is: L_usr(πθ_usr, λ) = J_R_usr(πθ_usr) − λ(J_C_usr(πθ_usr) − ¯J_C)... concurrently, λ is updated via projected gradient ascent."
  - [section V.B] Convergence results: "All Lagrangian-based methods maintain delays below the required threshold: HC-MAPPO-L: 2.74 s... while unconstrained baselines exhibit significant violations: H-MAPPO: 4.38 s."
- **Break condition:** If the constraint threshold ¯τ is too aggressive relative to the system's physical limits (e.g., minimum achievable delay > ¯τ), λ may diverge or cause policy collapse as the constraint becomes infeasible.

### Mechanism 3: Specialized Policy Architectures for Heterogeneous Decision Spaces
- **Claim:** Tailoring policy networks to each layer's decision structure—auto-regressive for combinatorial deployment, attention-based for dynamic resource allocation—and using CTDE with layer-specific critics enables effective coordination despite heterogeneous action spaces and partial observability.
- **Mechanism:** 
  - **Deployment (combinatorial):** Auto-regressive policy with GRU encoder and masked action selection sequentially picks models until storage fills, factoring the exponential action space into a polynomial sequence. Critic shares encoder for step-wise baseline.
  - **User (joint association+partitioning):** Standard MAPPO actor-critic with decentralized execution, but constrained via λ.
  - **Allocation (dynamic variable-sized user sets):** Attention mechanism generates allocation weights by comparing a server's query vector (from its observation) to per-user key vectors (from embedded features). Dual heads handle compute/bandwidth separately.
  - **CTDE:** All critics use augmented global observations during training to resolve coupling (e.g., global deployment matrix, all user requests), while actors use local observations at execution.
- **Core assumption:** Each layer's decision space has a structure (sequential, combinatorial, dynamic set) that can be exploited by specialized architectures without losing optimality, and that global value functions can be learned from augmented observations despite partial observability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV.B.1] "To manage this combinatorial complexity, we design an auto-regressive policy that decomposes the joint decision into a sequence of individual model selections."
  - [section IV.B.3] "The policy employs two parallel attention branches for computation and bandwidth resources... This process is detailed with a diagram in Appendix A."
  - [section IV.B.4] "The HC-MAPPO-L algorithm is trained using a CTDE paradigm tailored to the three-layer architecture."
- **Break condition:** If the deployment models I become very large (>100), the auto-regressive sequential selection may become too slow or myopic. If user set sizes per server vary extremely (e.g., 1 vs. 50), the fixed-dimension attention input masking may lose expressiveness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)**
  - **Why needed here:** The problem jointly optimizes multiple objectives (energy, privacy) under a hard long-term delay constraint. A standard MDP cannot express constraints; a CMDP formalizes this as maximizing reward subject to a constraint on expected cumulative cost.
  - **Quick check question:** How does converting a constrained problem into an unconstrained Lagrangian loss affect the optimality gap compared to primal methods?
- **Concept: Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE)**
  - **Why needed here:** User agents have partial observations (only their own request and local deployment status) but must coordinate (avoid overloading a server, balance privacy-delay trade-offs). CTDE allows critics to see global state during training to learn coordination signals while keeping execution decentralized and scalable.
  - **Quick check question:** What specific global information is provided to each layer's critic, and why might that information be sufficient for value estimation despite partial observability?
- **Concept: Lagrangian Duality in RL**
  - **Why needed here:** Directly incorporating constraint violation into the reward (e.g., large penalty for delay > ¯τ) causes high-variance gradients and training instability. Lagrange multipliers provide a separate, adaptive penalty channel that stabilizes learning by decoupling constraint satisfaction from reward shaping.
  - **Quick check question:** Under what conditions might the primal-dual updates in HC-MAPPO-L fail to converge to a feasible policy even if one exists?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Agents:** 3 agent types per server (deployment, allocation) + per-user agents. Parameter sharing within each type.
  - **Policy networks:** 
    - `π_θ_dep`: Auto-regressive GRU policy (outputs sequence of model indices).
    - `π_θ_usr`: MLP policy (outputs server index + partition point).
    - `π_θ_alloc`: Attention-based policy (outputs normalized resource fractions for users of that server).
  - **Critic networks:** 
    - `V_φ_dep`: MLP critic (estimates value of deployment macro-action).
    - `V_φ_usr` & `V_φ_cost`: MLP critics (estimate reward value and cost value for user agents).
    - `V_φ_alloc`: MLP critic (for allocation agents).
  - **Shared:** Lagrange multiplier λ (scalar, updated globally).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Every ΔT slots: Deployment agents execute auto-regressive policy → update deployment matrix X.
  2. Every slot: 
     - User agents observe (model request, input size, X) → select (server, partition).
     - Allocation agents for chosen servers observe (users' model, input, partition) → allocate (f_j,k, B_j,k).
     - Environment computes cost (energy+privacy), delay τ_k, rewards, updates state.
     - Trajectories stored in layer-specific buffers: ξ_dep (per server), ξ_usr (per user), ξ_alloc (per server).
  3. After N episodes: 
     - Compute GAE advantages for all buffers.
     - Update λ via ˆJ_C_usr (average delay cost across all user trajectories).
     - Update θ_dep via PPO with ˆA_dep.
     - Update θ_usr via PPO-Lagrangian with ˆA_R_usr, ˆA_C_usr, and current λ.
     - Update θ_alloc via PPO with ˆA_alloc.
     - Update all critics via MSE on value targets.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Auto-regressive vs. monolithic deployment policy:** Sequential selection is tractable but may be suboptimal if early choices severely restrict later ones; however, the informed baseline (shared GRU critic) mitigates this.
  - **Per-agent vs. global constraint handling:** Using per-user sufficient condition with shared λ simplifies multi-agent credit assignment but may be overly conservative if some users can tolerate higher delays. The paper assumes homogeneous delay sensitivity.
  - **Attention vs. MLP for allocation:** Attention handles variable user counts but increases complexity; they use masked fixed-size input to keep MLP dimension constant.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **λ divergence:** λ → max (100) persistently indicates constraint is infeasible under current policy network capacity.
  - **High variance in user cost distribution:** Dark clusters in Fig. 6 indicate unfairness; likely due to inadequate coordination in user agents' value function (V_φ_usr not properly leveraging global info).
  - **Service failure rate > 1.5%:** Cache misses (x_i,j=0) or resource exhaustion (C5/C6 violated); suggests deployment policy is not adapting to request distribution or allocation policy is starved.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate Lagrangian enforcement:** Train HC-MAPPO-L with varying ¯τ (2.0s to 4.0s). Check that average delay (Fig. 11b) meets each threshold, while unconstrained H-MAPPO violates. Verify λ increases when delay > ¯τ.
  2. **Test hierarchical decomposition:** Ablate to single-layer MAPPO (all decisions by user agents) with same parameters. Expect training instability, higher cost, and constraint violations due to non-stationarity from deployment coupling.
  3. **Isolate allocation policy:** Fix deployment (popularity-based) and user policy (greedy partition), then train only allocation agents with HC-MAPPO-L. Should improve delay (Fig. 5) but not achieve lowest cost, proving attention-based allocation’s contribution.

## Limitations
- The exact SSIM-based privacy leakage lookup tables (per model, partition depth) are not provided, making precise privacy cost calculation irreproducible.
- The attention-based allocation policy's normalization mechanism to enforce server capacity constraints (C5-C6) is unspecified.
- Critical hyperparameters (embedding dimensions, GRU sizes, attention head counts) and the exact handling of variable-length deployment macro-actions are omitted, hindering faithful implementation.

## Confidence
- **High**: Hierarchical decomposition and Lagrangian mechanism design—clearly described and theoretically sound.
- **Medium**: Reported quantitative performance (e.g., 131.35 combined cost, 2.74s delay)—depends on unreported implementation details and privacy profile.
- **Low**: Privacy metric fidelity—without SSIM profiles, the claimed privacy-accuracy trade-off cannot be externally validated.
