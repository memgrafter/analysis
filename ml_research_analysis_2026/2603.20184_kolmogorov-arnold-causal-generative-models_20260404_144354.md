# Kolmogorov-Arnold causal generative models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20184
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20184
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A causal generative model using Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs) achieves joint expressiveness and interpretability by enabling direct inspection, pruning, and symbolic extraction of structural equations while maintaining competitive performance on continuous and mixed-type benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Causal generative models in high-stakes domains require both accurate causal query answering and transparent, auditable mechanisms. This paper introduces KaCGM, which replaces standard neural network parameterizations in additive noise models (ANMs) with Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs). KANs decompose each structural equation into univariate spline functions, making learned mechanisms directly inspectable and amenable to pruning and symbolic approximation. The authors demonstrate competitive performance against state-of-the-art causal generative models on synthetic continuous and mixed-type benchmarks. A sensitivity analysis shows that model misspecification (e.g., non-additive noise) can be detected via independence tests on residuals using only observational data. Finally, a real-world cardiovascular case study extracts interpretable equations (e.g., quadratic effects of age and systolic pressure on ischemia probability) while preserving observational fit, illustrating the trade-off between expressiveness and functional transparency.

## Method Summary
KaCGM builds an additive noise model (SCM: \(x_j = f_j(\text{pa}(j)) + u_j\)) where each mechanism \(f_j\) is parameterized by a Kolmogorov–Arnold Network (KAN). For mixed-type data, discrete variables use a Logistic-KAN to model conditional probabilities. Training proceeds node-by-node in topological order, minimizing prediction error (e.g., MSE) for each KAN. After fitting, residuals \(\hat{u}_j\) are inferred in reverse topological order. Model validity is assessed via two complementary criteria: (1) **falsification tests**—residuals should be independent of parents (tested with HSIC/dHSIC); (2) **observational distribution matching**—generated samples should match the real data (measured by MMD and C2ST). To enhance interpretability, a simplification pipeline prunes unimportant KAN edges and applies symbolic regression to approximate remaining univariate splines with closed-form expressions (e.g., polynomials). The simplified model is re-validated; if it passes the same criteria, the symbolic equations are extracted for auditing and query answering. Interventional and counterfactual queries are answered by ancestral sampling from the learned structural equations.

## Key Results
- KaCGM (using KAAMs—KANs with zero hidden layers) matches or exceeds state-of-the-art (ANM, DBCM, CFlow) on continuous and mixed-type synthetic benchmarks for observational and interventional metrics, while enabling symbolic extraction.
- Residual independence tests (HSIC/dHSIC) effectively detect misspecification due to non-additive noise; under additive regimes KaCGM residuals pass tests, while more flexible models (CFlow) only show advantage when additivity is violated.
- On a real cardiovascular dataset, the interpretability pipeline extracts a simplified, closed-form equation for ischemia probability showing quadratic age/systolic effects, with observational distribution match (MMD, C2ST) comparable to the full KAN model.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Replacing standard neural network layers with Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs) in structural equations enables direct inspection and symbolic extraction of causal mechanisms.
- **Mechanism:** KANs parameterize each univariate transformation on edges with learnable spline functions, structuring the functional decomposition such that the learned equation for each node can be written as a composition of interpretable univariate functions. This replaces opaque scalar-weight layers with inspectable functions.
- **Core assumption:** The true data-generating process admits relatively low-complexity functional decompositions that KANs can approximate efficiently.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "each structural equation is parameterized by a Kolmogorov–Arnold Network (KAN). This decomposition enables direct inspection of learned causal mechanisms, including symbolic approximations and visualization of parent–child relationships."
  - Section IV.A: "KaCGM is an additive noise model (ANM) in which each mechanism ... is approximated by a KAN."
- **Break condition:** When the true mechanism is highly nonadditive or entangled, KANs may fail to capture necessary complexity without deep architectures, reintroducing opaqueness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Model misspecification (e.g., nonadditive noise) can be detected using only observational data via independence tests on inferred exogenous residuals.
- **Mechanism:** After fitting the ANM, residuals (estimated exogenous noise) are tested for independence from parent variables using Hilbert–Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) and multivariate dHSIC. Significant dependence suggests the additive structure is inadequate.
- **Core assumption:** Under causal sufficiency and a correctly specified graph, the true exogenous variables are independent of parents; violation indicates functional misspecification.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "sensitivity analysis shows misspecification detectable via independence tests on residuals using only observational data."
  - Section IV.A: "Test the independence of the exogenous noise: \(u_j \perp\!\!\!\perp \text{pa}(j)\). ... we propose to use the statistic of the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence test (HSIC)."
- **Break condition:** Hidden confounders or graph misspecification can also cause residual dependence, confounding the test.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An interpretability pipeline (pruning + symbolic approximation) can extract closed-form structural equations from trained KANs while preserving observational fit within acceptable thresholds.
- **Mechanism:** After training, edges with low importance are pruned, then the remaining univariate splines are symbolically approximated (e.g., to polynomials/trig functions). The simplified model is validated against the original via distributional metrics (MMD, C2ST) and residual independence; simplification is accepted only if these do not degrade beyond a user threshold.
- **Core assumption:** Pruning and symbolic substitution do not fundamentally alter the causal semantics for queries of interest; the simplified function remains a good approximation of the true mechanism.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "extraction of simplified structural equations and interpretable causal effects."
  - Section IV.C: "Prune the edges ... yielding \(\tilde{f}_j^{(p)}\). Fit a symbolic substitution ... approximates the function to a combination of atoms."
- **Break condition:** Over-pruning or poor symbolic fit may degrade predictive fidelity, especially for complex interactions; the validation step is meant to catch this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Additive Noise Models (ANMs)
  - **Why needed here:** KaCGM is built on the ANM assumption (\(x_j = f_j(\text{pa}(j)) + u_j\)). Understanding ANMs is necessary to grasp the residual-based validation and the scope of causal queries answered.
  - **Quick check question:** If residuals from a fitted ANM are dependent on parents, what does that imply about the model's functional form?
- **Concept:** Kolmogorov–Arnold Representation Theorem
  - **Why needed here:** The theorem justifies that any continuous multivariate function can be represented as a composition of univariate functions, which is the architectural basis of KANs. This underpins the promise of interpretability via univariate decomposition.
  - **Quick check question:** How does representing functions as sums of univariate splines (instead of weighted activations) change the interpretability trade-off?
- **Concept:** Causal Graph Structure & Sufficiency
  - **Why needed here:** KaCGM assumes a known, correct causal graph and causal sufficiency (no hidden confounders). These assumptions define the factorization and independence properties used for validation and query answering.
  - **Quick check question:** How would a misspecified causal graph affect the independence tests on residuals?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Data D and Graph G -> Train KANs per node in topological order -> Infer residuals u_j -> Validation: HSIC/dHSIC and MMD/C2ST -> If pass: Prune edges -> Symbolic regression -> Re-validate -> If pass: Extract symbolic equations and PDP/PRP; else reject. Query answering: Interventional sampling (Algorithm 4) and counterfactual inference (Algorithm 5) via structural equation evaluation.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Given dataset D and causal graph G, train KANs for each node in topological order (minimize MSE on x_j vs. KAN_j(pa(j))).
  2. Infer residuals û_j and estimate their distributions ˜p(u_j).
  3. Run falsification tests (Algorithm 3): HSIC(û_j, pa(j)) and MMD between real vs. generated observational data.
  4. If tests pass, proceed to simplification: prune KAN edges, symbolically approximate remaining univariate functions, re-test.
  5. If simplified model passes, extract closed-form equations and visualizations (PRP/PDP).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Expressiveness vs. interpretability:** Deeper/wider KANs capture more complex functions but become harder to simplify/symbolicate. Shallow KAAMs are preferred if they pass validation.
  - **Additivity assumption:** KaCGM assumes additive noise; nonadditive DGPs require more flexible models (e.g., CFlow) but lose interpretability.
  - **Discrete handling:** Logistic-KANs handle mixed data but implicit u_j prevents direct inspection; counterfactual identifiability may be limited.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High RF accuracy in C2ST (synthetic vs. real) → poor observational fit.
  - Significant HSIC/dHSIC → residual dependence → likely nonadditive or missed parents.
  - Simplified model fails validation tests (MMD/HSIC degrade) → over-pruning or bad symbolic fit.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sensitivity analysis on synthetic DGP** (Fig. 2): Vary additivity parameter α; show that when α>0 (nonadditive), KaCGM’s residual independence tests fail while more flexible models (CFlow) succeed.
  2. **Benchmark on synthetic continuous/mixed datasets** (Tab I, Fig. 3): Compare KaCGM (KAN/KAAM) vs. ANM, DBCM, CFlow across observational, interventional, counterfactual metrics.
  3. **Real-world cardiovascular case study** (Fig. 5, 8): Apply full pipeline (train → test → prune/symbolicate) on real data; demonstrate extraction of closed-form equation for ischemia and interpretable PDP/PRP visualizations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can KaCGM be extended to handle strongly non-additive, highly entangled, or heteroscedastic mechanisms without sacrificing interpretability?
  - **Basis in paper:** The authors identify that KaCGM relies on low-complexity functional decompositions and may suffer approximation bias when the true data-generating process is non-additive; they suggest extending the architecture with KAN-based normalizing flows or alternative bases while preserving interpretability.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current KANs enforce structural simplicity but are outperformed by flexible deep causal generative models on non-additive regimes. No existing method simultaneously achieves high expressivity and explicit symbolic extraction.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of novel KAN-based architectures (e.g., KAN-normalizing flows) that match state-of-the-art density approximation and counterfactual accuracy on both additive and non-additive benchmarks, accompanied by interpretability tools to audit learned mechanisms.
- **Question 2:** How can the trade-off between interpretability (via pruning and symbolic approximation) and predictive fidelity be quantified and managed in practice?
  - **Basis in paper:** The authors acknowledge that simplification improves transparency but may degrade performance, and their validation pipeline cannot guarantee that simplified mechanisms match the true structural equations, especially under misspecification.
  - **Why unresolved:** The validation pipeline provides necessary but not sufficient conditions for model adequacy; there is no theoretical framework to bound how much simplification affects the accuracy of causal queries.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis linking simplification metrics (e.g., sparsity, symbolic complexity) to estimation error bounds for causal queries, supported by empirical studies measuring this trade-off across diverse datasets and simplification levels.
- **Question 3:** How can KaCGM be extended to settings with hidden confounders or incorrectly specified causal graphs?
  - **Basis in paper:** The methodology assumes causal sufficiency and a correct graph; the authors list extending beyond causal sufficiency as an open challenge, proposing proxy-based adjustments or graph transformations.
  - **Why unresolved:** The current additive noise formulation requires independent exogenous variables; hidden confounding violates this assumption and invalidates counterfactual estimates. Existing approaches for hidden confounders often sacrifice interpretability.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Integration of identification strategies (e.g., proxy variables, instrumental variables) into the KAN framework that retain additive noise assumptions and interpretability, validated on semi-synthetic data with known hidden confounders.
- **Question 4:** How can symbolic extraction and visual diagnostics scale to larger causal graphs and more complex mechanisms?
  - **Basis in paper:** The authors concede that although KaCGM works for moderate-dimensional tabular data, extracting symbolic expressions and visual diagnostics becomes increasingly challenging as graph size and mechanism complexity grow.
  - **Why unresolved:** The interpretability pipeline is demonstrated only on small graphs; computational and algorithmic complexity likely increases with the number of variables and parents.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Scalable algorithms for KAN pruning and symbolic regression that handle hundreds of variables, perhaps via hierarchical decomposition or sparse representations, accompanied by user studies demonstrating usability on larger real-world causal graphs.
- **Question 5:** What alternative functional parameterizations beyond splines could be incorporated into KANs to better capture periodic, oscillatory, or domain-specific patterns?
  - **Basis in paper:** The authors propose exploring Fourier expansions as an alternative basis to splines, noting that splines may not be optimal for periodic or oscillatory functions.
  - **Why unresolved:** Splines are the default in KANs and work well for smooth functions, but their efficiency for periodic functions is untested; exploring other bases is still in early stages with no empirical comparisons.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical evaluations on synthetic and real datasets with known periodicities, comparing Fourier-KANs or other specialized bases against spline-KANs in terms of predictive performance, interpretability, and symbolic extraction quality.

## Limitations
- The approach assumes additive noise and a perfectly specified causal graph; violations (non-additive interactions, hidden confounders) invalidate residual independence checks and counterfactual guarantees.
- Interpretability diminishes if KANs require depth or wider layers to fit complex functions, and the simplification pipeline may degrade predictive fidelity if pruning or symbolic approximation is too aggressive.
- Crucial experimental details (final hyperparameters, baseline training protocols, counterfactual evaluation specifics) are omitted, hampering independent replication.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (KAN parameterization enables direct inspection):** Medium – architectural decomposition is sound, but evidence is on limited benchmarks; complex functions may force opaque designs.
- **Mechanism 2 (Residual tests detect misspecification):** Medium – rooted in standard ANM diagnostics, yet susceptible to graph errors and hidden confounding.
- **Mechanism 3 (Pruning & symbolic extraction preserve fit):** Medium – pipeline demonstrated on one real dataset; scalability and robustness remain open.
- **Overall empirical competitiveness:** Medium – reported numbers are promising but await independent verification.
