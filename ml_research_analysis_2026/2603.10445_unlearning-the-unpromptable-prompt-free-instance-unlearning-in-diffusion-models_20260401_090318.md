# Unlearning the Unpromptable: Prompt-free Instance Unlearning in Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10445
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10445
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The proposed prompt-free unlearning method achieves effective forgetting (SSCD < 0.4) while preserving generation quality (e.g., LPIPS 0.35, SSIM 0.87 on CelebA-HQ), significantly outperforming baseline approaches.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for prompt-free instance unlearning in diffusion models, targeting undesired outputs that cannot be expressed via text prompts (e.g., specific individuals or culturally inaccurate depictions). The approach constructs surrogate images of the target using editing techniques such as TediGAN and SDEdit, then optimizes a combined loss incorporating timestep-aware weighting and gradient surgery to forget the target while preserving overall model integrity. Evaluated on unconditional (DDPM-CelebA) and conditional (Stable Diffusion 3) models, the method meets the forgetting criterion (SSCD < 0.4) and significantly outperforms prompt-free baselines (NegGrad, EraseDiff, SISS) in preservation metrics, achieving LPIPS 0.35 and SSIM 0.87 on CelebA-HQ. Theoretical analysis shows that surrogate unlearning can induce smaller parameter shifts than exact unlearning, and empirical results confirm a Pareto-optimal trade-off between forgetting and integrity, enabling ethical compliance without reliance on prompts.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two main stages: (1) generation of surrogate images representing the undesired content, using editing tools like TediGAN or SDEdit; (2) fine‑tuning of the diffusion model via a combined loss that includes a forgetting component (driving the model away from the surrogate) and a preservation component (maintaining performance on clean data). The forgetting loss is weighted by a timestep‑aware schedule λ(t)=1−βt, which focuses learning on diffusion steps where unlearning is most effective. Gradients from the two loss terms are combined using gradient surgery (projection) to mitigate interference. This prompt‑free strategy avoids the need for explicit textual descriptions and is applicable to both unconditional and conditional diffusion models. The paper supports the approach with a theoretical argument (under a ridge regression surrogate) showing that surrogate‑based unlearning requires a smaller parameter shift than exact unlearning, offering an explanation for its empirical efficiency.

## Key Results
- The method achieves the forgetting criterion (SSCD < 0.4) on both DDPM‑CelebA (unconditional) and Stable Diffusion 3 (conditional).
- On single‑instance unlearning from CelebA‑HQ, it substantially outperforms baselines with LPIPS 0.35 and SSIM 0.87.
- Ablations confirm a Pareto‑optimal balance between forgetting and integrity, and demonstrate that gradient projection yields the best outcomes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method succeeds because surrogate images provide a practical stand‑in for the target content, enabling unlearning without access to original data or textual prompts. The timestep‑aware weighting concentrates unlearning efforts on the most influential diffusion steps, while gradient surgery prevents the forgetting updates from degrading the model’s general capabilities. Together, these design choices ensure that parameter changes remain minimal and targeted, preserving overall model performance. The theoretical analysis reinforces this by showing that, in a simplified setting, surrogate‑based unlearning leads to smaller parameter shifts than exact unlearning, which aligns with the observed empirical robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Target Instance → Surrogate Generator → Forgetting Loss; Clean Data → Preservation Loss; Loss Combiner (with λ(t)) → Combined Loss → Gradients → Gradient Surgery → Parameter Update (Diffusion Model).

**Critical Path**  
1. Generate surrogate images for the target instance.  
2. For each training iteration, sample a timestep t, compute forgetting loss (model output vs. surrogate) and preservation loss (model output vs. clean data).  
3. Combine losses using λ(t) and backpropagate to obtain gradients.  
4. Apply gradient surgery to the gradients.  
5. Update the diffusion model parameters.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Surrogate quality vs. computational overhead.  
- Forgetting strength vs. preservation of generative capabilities.  
- Choice of β in λ(t) vs. training stability.  
- Projection dimensionality in gradient surgery vs. mitigation of interference.

**Failure Signatures**  
- SSCD remains >0.4 (forgetting insufficient).  
- LPIPS/SSIM drop sharply (generation quality degraded).  
- Loss instability or explosion (hyperparameter mismatch).  
- Overly conservative updates leading to underfitting on clean data.

**First Experiments**  
1. Single‑instance unlearning on CelebA‑HQ: measure SSCD, LPIPS, SSIM.  
2. Direct comparison with baseline methods (NegGrad, EraseDiff, SISS) under identical conditions.  
3. Ablation study on timestep weighting parameter β and gradient surgery configuration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does surrogate unlearning generalize to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT) and closed‑source models?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Limitation VI.D notes only one model per setting was tested.
- **Why unresolved**: Architectural differences may change forgetting dynamics; closed‑source models may have extra constraints.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Test on diverse architectures (UNet, Transformer) and report SSCD/LPIPS/SSIM.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can surrogate construction be automated to optimize the forgetting‑integrity trade‑off?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Section IV‑C uses manual CLIP weight selection.
- **Why unresolved**: Manual tuning is not scalable; optimal surrogate varies per instance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Build an automatic hyperparameter search or learned generator and show Pareto gains.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does the theoretical advantage of surrogate‑based unlearning (smaller parameter shift) hold for non‑convex deep models like diffusion networks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Theorem 2 (Sec. IV‑B) proves this for ridge regression.
- **Why unresolved**: Deep models are non‑linear; convex analysis may not apply.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extend theory to overparameterized networks or measure parameter shifts in diffusion models.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How does the timestep‑aware weighting schedule λ(t)=1−βt interact with different samplers and step counts, and can β be chosen adaptively?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Ablation (Table III) tested only a few β values.
- **Why unresolved**: Schedule may depend on sampler; ad‑hoc β may be suboptimal.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Vary β across multiple samplers (DDIM, DPM‑Solver) and step counts to derive guidelines.

## Limitations
- **Surrogate dependence**: Imperfect surrogate images may leave residual traces of the target.
- **Limited evaluation**: Tests only on single‑instance CelebA‑HQ and Stable Diffusion 3; generalizability to multi‑instance, diverse datasets, and architectures is unknown.
- **Metric concerns**: The SSCD threshold ( <0.4 ) is arbitrary and may not reflect true forgetting; LPIPS/SSIM overlook semantic fidelity.
