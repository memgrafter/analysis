# Data-Driven Graph Filters via Adaptive Spectral Shaping

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.03698
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03698
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Learns a reusable baseline spectral kernel modulated by Gaussian factors to handle heterogeneous spectra, enabling few-shot cross-graph transfer with improved reconstruction accuracy.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Adaptive Spectral Shaping (ASS) to overcome the limitation of fixed-prototype graph wavelets in handling heterogeneous, multi-peak spectra of real-world graph signals. ASS learns a smooth baseline spectral kernel via an MLP and modulates it with a small set of learnable Gaussian factors (centers, bandwidths, amplitudes), forming an interpretable multi-peak filter bank. Scalability is achieved through Chebyshev polynomial approximations, avoiding expensive eigendecompositions. The Transferable ASS (TASS) decouples a universal baseline from graph-specific shaping parameters, enabling few-shot transfer to new graphs by freezing the baseline and adapting only the Gaussian parameters. Experiments on synthetic benchmarks show reduced reconstruction error and spectral discrepancy compared to fixed-prototype wavelets and learned linear banks, with TASS yielding consistent positive transfer when source and target graphs share structural similarities.

## Method Summary
The core of ASS is a spectral response defined as \(G(\lambda) = \sum_{k=1}^K a_k \, g_\theta(\lambda) \exp(-\gamma_k (\lambda - \mu_k)^2)\), where \(g_\theta\) is an MLP that serves as a smooth baseline, and each Gaussian component contributes a peak at \(\mu_k\) with bandwidth \(\gamma_k\) and amplitude \(a_k\). This design allows the filter to adapt to multi-peak, multi-scale signal spectra while maintaining interpretability through the Gaussian parameters. For scalability, \(G(\lambda)\) is projected onto a Chebyshev polynomial basis to obtain coefficients \(c_r\); the filtered signal \(G(L)x\) is then computed via recursive application of the scaled Laplacian \(\tilde{L}\) with \(O(R|E|)\) time. In the TASS variant, \(g_\theta\) is pretrained on a source graph (or family) and then frozen; only \((\mu_k, \gamma_k, a_k)\) are adapted on a target graph using limited target data. Training minimizes mean squared reconstruction error plus regularizations: a smoothness penalty on \(g_\theta\) (finite differences on a spectral grid) and a shape regularizer. The method is implemented without eigendecomposition, relying solely on sparse matrix operations.

## Key Results
- ASS learns an interpretable multi-peak filter bank that adapts to heterogeneous signal spectra, outperforming fixed-prototype wavelets and learned linear banks on synthetic reconstruction tasks (lower MSE and spectral discrepancy).
- TASS enables effective few-shot cross-graph transfer by decoupling a universal baseline from graph-specific shaping parameters; under matched compute, it yields consistent positive fractional improvement when source and target graphs share structural similarities.
- The Chebyshev implementation scales to large graphs without eigendecomposition, achieving \(O(R|E|)\) complexity and demonstrating robustness across various polynomial degrees.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multi‑peak shaping via baseline modulation
- **Claim:** Adaptive spectral shaping enables multi‑peak, multi‑scale filter responses by combining a learned smooth baseline kernel with data‑driven Gaussian modulations.
- **Mechanism:** A baseline spectral kernel \(g_\theta(\lambda)\) (parameterized as an MLP) is multiplied by \(K\) Gaussian factors \(\exp(-\gamma_k(\lambda-\mu_k)^2)\) with learnable centers \(\mu_k\), bandwidths \(\gamma_k\), and amplitudes \(a_k\). Summing these components yields \(G(\lambda) = \sum_{k=1}^K a_k g_\theta(\lambda) \exp(-\gamma_k(\lambda-\mu_k)^2)\), which allocates energy to heterogeneous spectral regions.
- **Core assumption:** A single smooth baseline can serve as a universal template, and deviations from it can be captured by localized Gaussian bumps.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "learns a reusable baseline spectral kernel and modulates it with a small set of Gaussian factors (controlling center and bandwidth). This forms a multi‑peak, multi‑scale filter bank". Section 2 Eq. (3) formalizes the sum of shaped components. The corpus contains related work on adaptive spectral filters, though not this exact shaping mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Fails if the target spectra contain non‑Gaussian peaks (discontinuities, high‑order oscillations) or if the baseline MLP lacks capacity. Excessive overlap of Gaussian components reduces interpretability.

### Mechanism 2: Few‑shot transfer via frozen baseline
- **Claim:** Transferable Adaptive Spectral Shaping (TASS) enables few‑shot cross‑graph transfer by decoupling a universal baseline from graph‑specific shaping parameters.
- **Mechanism:** The baseline \(g_\theta\) is learned on a source graph (or family). For a target graph, \(g_\theta\) is frozen and only the Gaussian parameters \((\mu, \gamma, a)\) are adapted using limited target data. This separates universal spectral structure from graph‑specific emphasis.
- **Core assumption:** The baseline spectral template \(g_\theta\) generalizes across graphs with similar Laplacian spectra, and only the spectral peak locations/widths need per‑graph adjustment.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes TASS: "the baseline kernel is learned on source graphs and, on a target graph, kept fixed while only the shaping parameters are adapted, enabling few-shot transfer". Section 2 notes: "This design decouples universal spectral structure (learned in \(g_\theta\)) with graph‑specific emphasis (via \(\mu_{m,k}, \gamma_{m,k}, a_{m,k}\))". Corpus reference [11] on transferability of spectral GCNs supports cross‑graph filter transfer.
- **Break condition:** Transfer fails when source and target graphs have dissimilar Laplacian spectra (e.g., different generators, extreme density/path length differences) or when signal regimes shift drastically. A poorly fitted baseline becomes a harmful prior.

### Mechanism 3: Scalable filtering via Chebyshev expansion
- **Claim:** Scalability is achieved by approximating spectral filters with Chebyshev polynomial expansions, avoiding costly eigendecompositions.
- **Mechanism:** \(G(\lambda)\) is projected onto Chebyshev polynomials of the scaled Laplacian \(\tilde{L} = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}}L - I\). The filter operation becomes \(G(L)x \approx \sum_{r=0}^R c_r T_r(\tilde{L})x\), computed via recursive polynomial evaluation with \(O(R|E|)\) time and \(O(N)\) memory.
- **Core assumption:** The Chebyshev expansion accurately approximates \(G(\lambda)\) over \([0, \lambda_{\max}]\) with moderate degree \(R\), especially when passbands are not extremely narrow.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "To scale, we implement filters with Chebyshev polynomial expansions, avoiding eigendecompositions." Section 2 Eq. (5) and discussion confirm \(O(R|E|)\) time and \(O(N)\) memory. The technique is standard in graph signal processing [3,8] and used in neighboring papers [14,18].
- **Break condition:** Approximation error becomes unacceptable for very sharp transitions (narrow peaks) requiring high polynomial degree, or when \(\lambda_{\max}\) is ill‑conditioned. Gibbs oscillations may occur without damping (Jackson‑Chebyshev).

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
- Baseline module \(g_\theta\): MLP mapping normalized \(\lambda\) to a scalar.
- Shaping module: stores \(K\) sets of parameters \((\mu_k, \gamma_k, a_k)\).
- Filter response composer: computes \(G(\lambda) = \sum_{k=1}^K a_k \, g_\theta(\lambda) \exp(-\gamma_k (\lambda-\mu_k)^2)\).
- Polynomial approximator: projects \(G(\lambda)\) onto Chebyshev basis to obtain coefficients \(c_r\) and applies recursion to compute \(G(L)x\).
- TASS controller: orchestrates two‑phase training (source: update all; target: freeze \(g_\theta\), update shaping only).

Critical path:
1. Preprocess: compute \(\lambda_{\max}\) and scaled Laplacian \(\tilde{L}\) for each graph.
2. For each batch of node signals \(x\), compute \(G(L)x\) via Chebyshev recursion: initialize \(T_0 = x\), \(T_1 = \tilde{L}x\), then iterate \(T_{r+1} = 2\tilde{L}T_r - T_{r-1}\) weighted by \(c_r\).
3. Compute reconstruction loss \(\|G(L)x - y\|^2\) plus regularizers.
4. Backpropagate; during target adaptation, mask gradients to freeze \(g_\theta\).
5. Iterate until convergence.

Design tradeoffs:
- Number of shaping components \(K\): more components increase capacity for multi‑peak spectra but add parameters and risk overfitting.
- Chebyshev degree \(R\): higher \(R\) improves accuracy for narrow bands but raises computational cost \(O(R|E|)\).
- Shared baseline vs. per‑graph adaptation: TASS reduces target data needs but may suffer if source and target spectra diverge significantly.
- Smoothness regularization on \(g_\theta\): prevents erratic baselines but may oversmooth necessary variations.

Failure signatures:
- High spectral discrepancy \(E_{\text{spec}}\) despite low MSE: indicates poor spectral match, possibly insufficient \(K\) or \(R\).
- Negative transfer (TASS): target MSE higher than from‑scratch; check spectral similarity between graphs.
- Training instability: exploding gradients when \(\gamma_k\) becomes too small; enforce minimum via softplus initialization.
- Overfitting on small target sets: monitor validation loss; reduce \(K\) or increase smoothness penalty.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Single‑graph reconstruction:** Train ASS on synthetic graphs with known multi‑peak ground‑truth filters. Vary \(K\) and compare MSE and \(E_{\text{spec}}\) against fixed‑prototype wavelet banks and learned linear banks. Visualize learned \(G(\lambda)\) against ground truth.
2. **Cross‑graph transfer (TASS):** Pretrain baseline \(g_\theta\) on a source graph family (e.g., ER). Adapt shaping on a target graph (e.g., BA) with limited samples. Compute fractional improvement vs. from‑scratch training. Test across generator pairs and signal regimes.
3. **Ablations and scalability:** Vary polynomial degree \(R\) on larger graphs (\(N=1000\)) to measure runtime/memory vs. spectral error. Also vary target sample size in TASS to demonstrate few‑shot efficiency.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Missing details on MLP architecture, regularization weights, Chebyshev degree selection, and initialization hinder exact replication.
- **Synthetic‑only validation:** Evaluation is limited to synthetic graphs and Gaussian‑sum ground‑truth filters; performance on real‑world data with arbitrary spectra remains unproven.
- **Transfer boundary unclear:** TASS shows positive transfer for similar graphs, but the threshold of spectral dissimilarity causing negative transfer is not quantified, risking failure on divergent structures.
