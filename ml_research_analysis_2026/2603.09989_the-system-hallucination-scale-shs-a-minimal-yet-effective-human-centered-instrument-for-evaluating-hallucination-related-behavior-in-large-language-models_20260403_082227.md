# The System Hallucination Scale (SHS): A Minimal yet Effective Human-Centered Instrument for Evaluating Hallucination-Related Behavior in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.09989
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09989
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A 10-item, 5-point Likert scale measuring five hallucination-related dimensions demonstrated high clarity (87.2%) and strong internal consistency (α=0.87) in a user study.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the System Hallucination Scale (SHS), a human-centered psychometric instrument designed to evaluate how large language model (LLM) hallucinations are perceived by users during interaction. Addressing a gap in user-focused evaluation tools, the SHS condenses assessment into 10 items across five paired dimensions (factual accuracy, source reliability, logical coherence, deceptiveness, responsiveness). A validation study with 210 participants confirmed the scale's feasibility, showing high item understandability and reliability. The paired-item structure provides an inherent consistency check, enabling comparative analysis and deployment monitoring. The instrument aims to complement objective metrics by capturing subjective user experience of hallucination-related behavior.

## Method Summary
The research involved a two-phase development process. First, the SHS was constructed as a 10-item questionnaire using a 5-point Likert scale, with each of the five dimensions represented by two complementary items (e.g., one positively and one negatively keyed). Second, a real-world validation study was conducted where 210 participants interacted with an LLM and subsequently completed the SHS to rate their experience. The collected data were analyzed for item clarity, internal consistency (Cronbach’s α), and inter-dimension correlations to assess the scale's psychometric properties.

## Key Results
- **High Item Clarity:** 87.2% of participants found the SHS items understandable.
- **Strong Internal Consistency:** Cronbach’s alpha reached 0.87, indicating excellent reliability.
- **Significant Inter-Dimension Correlations:** All dimension pairs showed statistically significant correlations (p < 0.001), supporting the scale’s construct coherence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The SHS works by targeting the subjective perception of hallucination through paired items per dimension, which serves a dual purpose. First, it captures a nuanced user experience by assessing both the presence and absence of hallucination-related traits (e.g., accuracy vs. inaccuracy). Second, the paired design creates a built-in consistency diagnostic—discrepancies between paired responses for the same dimension can flag inattentive or unreliable responding, enhancing data quality for comparative and monitoring use cases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Likert Scaling:** A psychometric method using ordered categories to measure attitudes or perceptions. *Why needed:* Provides structured, quantifiable user feedback. *Quick check:* Verify scale points are equidistant and anchors are clear.
- **Internal Consistency (Cronbach’s α):** Measures how closely related a set of items are as a group. *Why needed:* Ensures the scale reliably measures a single construct. *Quick check:* α > 0.7 is typically acceptable.
- **Construct Validity:** The extent to which a test measures the theoretical construct it intends to. *Why needed:* Confirms the SHS actually measures “hallucination-related behavior” from a user view. *Quick check:* Correlate with related and unrelated scales.
- **Paired-Item Design:** Using complementary items within a dimension (e.g., positive and negative phrasing). *Why needed:* Enables response consistency checks and reduces acquiescence bias. *Quick check:* Analyze within-subject agreement for each pair.
- **User-Centered Evaluation:** Assessing system performance through the lens of end-user experience. *Why needed:* Objective hallucination metrics may not reflect real-world usability. *Quick check:* Compare SHS scores with user task outcomes or behavioral logs.
- **Psychometric Instrument Development:** The systematic process of creating, testing, and refining measurement tools. *Why needed:* Ensures scales are valid, reliable, and practical. *Quick check:* Follow established guidelines (e.g., item generation, piloting, validation).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Item Generation -> Expert Review -> Pilot Testing -> Validation Study (210 participants) -> Reliability & Correlation Analysis -> Scale Finalization

**Critical Path:**  
1. Define five hallucination-related dimensions from user perspective.  
2. Write two items per dimension (10 total).  
3. Conduct validation study with LLM interaction.  
4. Compute Cronbach’s α and inter-dimension correlations.  
5. Implement paired-item consistency check.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Brevity vs. Coverage:** A 10-item scale is quick to administer but may omit nuanced aspects of hallucination.  
- **Paired Items vs. Response Burden:** Two items per dimension add length but provide diagnostic consistency.  
- **Generalizability vs. Context Specificity:** Developed for general LLM interaction; may need adaptation for domain-specific use.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low Cronbach’s α (<0.7) indicating poor item cohesion.  
- Non-significant or inconsistent inter-dimension correlations.  
- Low item clarity scores (<70%) in pilot.  
- High discrepancy between paired items without clear rationale (e.g., genuine user ambivalence vs. inattention).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Test-Retest Reliability:** Administer SHS to the same users after a delay to assess stability.  
2. **Factor Analysis:** Conduct exploratory/confirmatory factor analysis to verify the five-dimension structure.  
3. **Cross-Sample Validation:** Replicate the study with a demographically different user group and a different LLM.

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed Hypotheses & Analysis Plan:** No preregistration; increases risk of outcome-driven analysis.
- **Sample Representativeness Unknown:** Lack of demographic details for 210 participants limits generalizability.
- **Construct Validation Incomplete:** No convergent/discriminant validity tests against other hallucination or trust metrics.

## Confidence
- **Feasibility & Initial Psychometrics:** Medium. Supported by study data but limited by sample transparency.
- **"Human-Centered" Evaluation Utility:** Low. No comparison with alternative methods or linkage to behavioral outcomes.
- **Built-in Consistency Diagnostic & Comparative Use:** Medium. Design is described but practical diagnostic value unproven.
- **Instrument as a "Minimal" Effective Solution:** Low. Asserted without benchmarking against longer instruments.

## Next Checks
1. **Demographic & Sampling Audit:** Verify full participant demographics (age, tech proficiency, LLM experience) and recruitment sources to assess sample bias.
2. **Construct Validation Against Behavioral Metrics:** Correlate SHS scores with objective user behavior logs (e.g., task completion, correction rates, reliance on LLM outputs) in a controlled interaction study.
3. **Comparative Criterion Validity Test:** Administer SHS alongside established scales (e.g., System Usability Scale, trust questionnaires) during the same LLM interaction tasks to establish convergent/discriminant validity and measure "minimalism" benefits.
