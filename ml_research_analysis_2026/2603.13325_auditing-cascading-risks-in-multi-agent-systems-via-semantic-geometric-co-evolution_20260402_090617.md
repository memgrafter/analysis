# Auditing Cascading Risks in Multi-Agent Systems via Semantic-Geometric Co-evolution

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.13325
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13325
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** SCCAL achieves AUROC 0.88 (adversarial) and 0.83 (natural failures) for cascading risk detection, providing lead times of 10.2 and 3.4 interaction turns before semantic violations, and outperforms all baselines while reducing false positives via semantic-geometric coupling.

## Executive Summary
Multi-agent systems (MAS) built on large language models (LLMs) can experience cascading risks—small instabilities that amplify across interactions into harmful outcomes. Traditional content-based auditing reacts only after semantic violations appear, missing the chance for early intervention. This paper introduces the Semantic–Curvature Co-evolutionary Auditing Loop (SCCAL), a proactive framework that jointly models the semantic content of messages and the geometric structure of the agent communication graph. SCCAL computes Ollivier–Ricci Curvature (ORC) per edge to capture local topological properties (e.g., redundancy, bottlenecks) and learns a coupled dynamics model from normal behavior. Deviations from the expected co-evolution of semantics and geometry generate early warnings, often tens of turns before overt risk. On synthetic benchmarks, SCCAL achieves AUROC scores of 0.88 for adversarial cascades and 0.83 for natural failures, substantially surpassing semantic-only, curvature-only, and GNN baselines. The local nature of ORC also enables interpretable root-cause attribution, pinpointing suspicious agents or links.

## Method Summary
SCCAL processes a log of MAS interactions, where each turn provides agent messages. A frozen sentence transformer (SBERT) encodes each message into a node embedding. For each turn, a directed semantic flow graph \(G_t\) is constructed: edge weights combine transmissibility (cosine similarity between sender’s current embedding and receiver’s previous embedding) and credibility (exponential of negative perplexity of the message). On \(G_t\), Ollivier–Ricci Curvature is computed per edge to quantify the local geometric tendency toward redundancy (positive curvature) or fragility (negative curvature). A coupled predictor, comprising a GRU with curvature-gated attention for semantic forecasting and a MLP for curvature evolution, is trained on normal trajectories to predict \((Y_{t+1}, \kappa_{t+1})\) from \((Y_t, \kappa_t)\). The anomaly score at time \(t\) is the residual \(A_t = \|Y_t - \hat{Y}_t\|^2 + \lambda \|\kappa_t - \hat{\kappa}_t\|_1\). Thresholding \(A_t\) on a validation set yields early warnings; the edges with largest curvature residual identify candidate culprits. SCCAL thus treats risk as a deviation from a learned semantic-geometric manifold rather than a pointwise anomaly.

## Key Results
- **High detection accuracy:** AUROC 0.88 (adversarial) and 0.83 (natural failures), beating semantic-only (0.71/0.62), curvature-only (0.77/0.69), and structural GNN (0.75/0.77) baselines.
- **Substantial lead time:** Risks are flagged 10.2 turns (adversarial) and 3.4 turns (natural) before the first semantic violation, enabling earlier mitigation.
- **False positive reduction:** The semantic–geometric coupling lowers false positive rate to 0.07 vs. 0.32 for curvature-only, by distinguishing benign high-curvature collaboration from malicious distortion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Geometric instability precedes semantic violations
- **Claim:** Cascading risk is preceded by detectable structural instability in the interaction topology, not immediate semantic violations.
- **Mechanism:** Ollivier–Ricci Curvature (ORC) quantifies local geometric properties of the agent communication graph. In trusted collaboration, ORC values remain within a stable range tied to task roles. As risk develops (e.g., echo chambers or bottlenecks form), ORC deviates systematically—positive ORC increases for redundant amplification, negative ORC increases (in magnitude) for fragile bottlenecks—*before* semantic content turns explicitly harmful.
- **Core assumption:** The interaction topology is a meaningful substrate where latent instability accrues; curvature is a sensitive leading indicator of this accrual.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "Cascading risks... manifest as structural instabilities in interaction topology before explicit semantic violations."
  - Section 3.3: ORC definition and interpretation linking positive/negative curvature to redundancy/bottlenecks.
  - Section A.3: Time-lag analysis showing curvature anomalies 4–7 rounds before semantic risk onset across risk types.
- **Break condition:** If a cascading risk scenario shows no significant ORC deviation prior to semantic violation, or if benign high-collaboration scenarios (e.g., rapid brainstorming) naturally produce identical ORC patterns without subsequent risk, the leading-indicator property is invalid.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic–geometric decoupling as early-warning signal
- **Claim:** Semantic–geometric *decoupling* (inconsistency between semantic flow and geometric evolution) is the reliable early-warning signal, not semantic or geometric anomalies alone.
- **Mechanism:** SCCAL learns a coupled predictor Ψ that forecasts next-step semantic states *and* curvature from current coupled state. A consistency residual (difference between predicted and observed) measures deviation from the learned co-evolutionary manifold. Risk is flagged when this joint residual exceeds a threshold. This suppresses false positives from benign high-curvature collaboration (where geometry alone would alarm) and captures risk when semantic signals are still clean.
- **Core assumption:** Trustworthy MAS exhibits a stable, learnable relationship between *what* is communicated (semantic vectors) and *how* the communication structure evolves (curvature). Cascading risk disrupts this relationship.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Deviations from learned co-evolutionary consistency provide early warnings."
  - Section 3.4: Formal definition of coupled predictors ψ and ϕ and the joint residual A_t.
  - Table 2: ORC-only has FPR 0.32 vs. consistency-based 0.07, demonstrating decoupling's role in suppressing geometric false positives.
- **Break condition:** If ablating the coupling loss (Table 3 "– Coupling") does not degrade performance significantly, the coupling is not essential. If semantic-only or curvature-only methods occasionally outperform the coupled model in specific regimes, the universality of the co-evolution assumption is questionable.

### Mechanism 3: Local ORC enables principled root-cause attribution
- **Claim:** Local Ollivier–Ricci Curvature enables principled, interpretable root-cause attribution for cascading risk initiation.
- **Mechanism:** Because ORC is computed per directed edge, edges with the largest curvature deviation (κ_ij - κ̂_ij) in the early warning phase directly point to the problematic agent interactions (source/target nodes). Positive deviation indicates an emerging echo chamber; negative deviation points to a fragile bottleneck where a single point of failure can cascade.
- **Core assumption:** The "source" of cascading instability is localized in the topology at the level of specific edges/agents *before* global failure. ORC's locality captures this.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "The local nature of Ricci curvature provides principled interpretability for root-cause attribution, identifying specific agents or links."
  - Section 3.5: "Edges with maximal curvature deviation directly identify agents responsible."
  - Section 5: "By localizing curvature distortions, auditors can identify specific agents or interaction links that catalyze cascading instability."
- **Break condition:** If in failure post-mortems, the edges with highest early curvature deviation do not correlate with the actual origin of the failure chain (e.g., the first malicious agent node), the attribution power is coincidental or misleading.

**Why needed here:** ORC is the geometric primitive that quantifies local properties of a graph. Understanding its definition (1 - W1(m_i, m_j)/d(i,j)), interpretation (positive = redundancy/sink, negative = bottleneck/fragility), and computational sensitivity to neighborhood probability measures is essential.

The decoupling into transmissibility (τ) and credibility (χ) is critical for the co-evolution model.

### Co-evolutionary system modeling & consistency residuals
**Why needed here:** This is the core theoretical framing—the idea that two state variables (semantic Y_t, geometric G_t) evolve in a coupled, predictable manner in healthy systems. Risk is defined as a trajectory that violates this learned joint dynamic. Understanding this frames anomaly detection as a trajectory stability problem rather than a point-wise classification problem.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Message → Semantic Encoder (frozen SBERT) → Node embeddings (y_i^t) → Flow Induction (τ·χ) → Graph G_t → Geometric Profiler (ORC) → Coupled Dynamics Model (ψ, ϕ) → Residual A_t → Threshold → Alert. For attribution: edges with largest |κ - κ̂|.

### Critical path
1. Receive raw MAS interaction log (agent, message, recipients) per turn.
2. Encode messages with frozen SBERT to obtain node embeddings y_i^t.
3. Build directed graph G_t: for each sender i and receiver j, compute τ_ij = cos(y_i^t, y_j^{t-1}) and χ_i = exp(-PPL(s_i^t)); set weight w_ij = τ_ij * χ_i^t.
4. Compute Ollivier–Ricci Curvature κ_ij for all edges using POT/GraphRicciCurvature.
5. Feed (Y_t, κ_t) to coupled predictor: ψ (GRU with curvature-gated attention) predicts Ŷ_{t+1}; ϕ (MLP) predicts κ̂_{t+1}.
6. Compute residual A_t = ||Y_t - Ŷ_t||^2 + λ ||κ_t - κ̂_t||_1.
7. Compare A_t to threshold τ; flag if exceeded.
8. For flagged turns, rank edges by |κ_ij - κ̂_ij| to identify suspicious agents/links.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Fixed vs. Trainable Semantic Encoder:** Freezing SBERT ensures representation stability and prevents overfitting to dynamics, but cannot adapt to domain-specific jargon, possibly degrading performance on specialized MAS.
- **Local (ORC) vs. Global Geometry:** ORC provides per-edge interpretability and micro-level early detection but may miss global phase transitions; spectral or topological invariants are unexplored.
- **Sliding Window vs. Full Trajectory:** SCCAL uses a sliding window for computing G_t and predictions. Shorter windows increase reactivity but amplify noise; longer windows smooth fluctuations but may delay detection.

### Failure signatures
- **False Positive Spike in Dense Collaboration:** If ORC-only is used, benign brainstorming sessions with high positive curvature trigger alerts. In SCCAL this is suppressed when the semantic residual A_t remains low (demonstrating decoupling).
- **Detection Lag:** If A_t rises only after semantic risk signals, the predictor may be mis-calibrated or the coupling weight λ is inappropriate, indicating poor model training or threshold setting.
- **Numerical Instability in ORC:** Wasserstein-1 computation via POT can be noisy on sparse or rapidly changing graphs, leading to jittery κ values. Symptom: highly volatile A_t on otherwise normal trajectories.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Simulation-to-Reality Gap:** How does SCCAL generalize from synthetic benchmarks to real-world LLM-MAS with human-in-the-loop and asynchronous dynamics? The current evaluation relies on controlled simulations that may not capture production noise and latency.
- **Global Geometric Invariants:** Can global descriptors (e.g., spectral curvature, persistent homology) improve early risk detection beyond local ORC? The paper notes this as an open frontier.
- **Causality and Adversarial Robustness:** Are curvature anomalies causal triggers or mere correlates? Could adversaries manipulate the interaction topology to mask ORC deviations and evade detection? No adversarial robustness analysis is provided.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic data dependency:** All reported results stem from a simulated MAS benchmark; performance on genuine multi-agent traces with natural failures is untested.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Key hyperparameters (λ, GRU size, optimizer), graph construction details (edge pruning, sliding window), and exact risk induction procedures are omitted, hindering exact replication.
- **Attribution validation missing:** The claim that early curvature deviations pinpoint root causes lacks empirical evidence linking curvature rankings to actual malicious agents in failure post-mortems.

## Confidence
- **Cascading risk manifests as structural instability before semantic violations:** Medium confidence (supported by internal time-lag analysis but lacking external validation).
- **Semantic–geometric decoupling enables early warning:** High confidence (consistent AUROC improvements and ablation results across adversarial and natural scenarios).
- **Local ORC provides principled root-cause attribution:** Low confidence (theoretically plausible but not directly verified against known failure origins).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Perform a systematic sweep over λ, GRU hidden dimension, and learning rate on a held-out validation set; report distributions of AUROC and detection lead time to assess robustness and proper calibration.
2. **Cross-dataset transfer:** Train SCCAL on the synthetic dataset and evaluate on a real MAS interaction dataset (e.g., from MAS-Shield or SentinelAgent) without retraining. Compare detection performance against the same baselines to gauge generalization.
3. **Attribution precision test:** In adversarial trajectories with a known malicious agent, compute edge rankings by early-warning curvature residual. Measure precision@k and recall to directly evaluate the root-cause attribution claim.
