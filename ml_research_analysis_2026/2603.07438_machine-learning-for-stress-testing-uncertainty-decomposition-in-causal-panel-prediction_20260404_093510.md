# Machine Learning for Stress Testing: Uncertainty Decomposition in Causal Panel Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07438
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07438
- **Reference count**: 34
- **Primary result**: A three-layer framework that decomposes uncertainty in causal stress testing into estimation, confounding, and extrapolation components, enabling valid prediction bands under hypothetical macro scenarios.

## Executive Summary
Regulatory stress testing requires projecting outcomes under hypothetical macroeconomic scenarios, but standard predictive methods ignore endogeneity and thus yield biased causal estimates. This paper introduces a framework that rigorously separates three sources of uncertainty: (1) estimation uncertainty of the observational path contrast, (2) confounding uncertainty due to unobserved factors, and (3) extrapolation uncertainty when stress scenarios lie outside historical support. The first layer identifies an observational path contrast via iterated regression, requiring only pre-period data under stationarity and state sufficiency. The second layer, under a bounded confounding assumption, yields a sharp identified set for the causal effect with an interpretable breakdown value. The third layer uses importance-weighted conformal calibration to produce distribution-free confidence bands, accompanied by diagnostics (e.g., extrapolation cost and effective sample size) that signal when the stress scenario is too extreme and abstention is warranted. Theoretically, an oracle inequality shows that recursive rollout error compounds with a horizon-dependent amplification factor ρ, guiding the choice between recursive and direct multi-horizon estimation. Simulations across contracting, near-critical, and expanding regimes confirm the error bounds, coverage aligns with theory despite moderate values (50–79%), and set identification contains the true effect across confounding strengths. The framework abstains in a COVID retrospective due to high extrapolation cost, demonstrating the practical value of the diagnostics.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of three interconnected layers. First, an observational estimator learns the conditional outcome state model m(ι, a) from pre-intervention data. Under stationarity and state sufficiency, the observational path contrast τ^obs_h is obtained by recursively applying the g-computation formula starting from the observed initial state. Second, a causal set wrapper assumes bounded confounding on the potential outcomes, which implies |τ^do_h - τ^obs_h| ≤ 2c_h for a sensitivity parameter c_h. This yields a sharp interval [τ^obs_h - 2c_h, τ^obs_h + 2c_h] and a breakdown value c^*_h that quantifies how strong confounding must be to overturn the sign of the effect. Third, a calibration module splits the pre-period into training and calibration blocks, computes rollout errors on rolling origins, and reweights these errors by the likelihood ratio of the macro transition density between historical and stress paths. The weighted quantile of the absolute errors gives a conformal band Δ^est_h with coverage guaranteed to be at least 1-α minus terms capturing temporal dependence (R_mix) and extrapolation severity (R_weight). Diagnostics such as R_weight and effective sample size B_eff flag high extrapolation cost. The three layers are aggregated to produce the final uncertainty decomposition. The theoretical core is an oracle inequality (Theorem 2) that bounds the error of recursive rollout by ϵ_n Γ_h, where Γ_h grows with ρ^h if ρ>1, signalling when direct multi-horizon estimation becomes preferable.

## Key Results
- Three-layer framework: observational path contrast, causal set bounds, importance-weighted conformal calibration with extrapolation diagnostics.
- Oracle inequality reveals recursive rollout error amplification factor ρ that depends on macro regime; high ρ (>1) rapidly degrades reliability and triggers switch to direct estimation.
- Empirical simulations show coverage within theoretical bounds (50–79%), diagnostics correctly identify severe stress scenarios, set identification covers true effects across confounding strengths, and the framework abstains on COVID retrospective due to excessive extrapolation cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Observational Path Contrasts
- **Claim**: Observational path contrasts for continuous macro paths are identified from pre-period data without requiring a control group or causal assumptions.
- **Mechanism**: Under state sufficiency, conditional stationarity, and deterministic state update, the conditional mean m(ι,a) is time-invariant. The observational path contrast is obtained by iterating the g-computation formula starting from the observed state I_i,t0.
- **Core assumptions**: Stationarity (Assumption 3) and state sufficiency (Assumption 2).
- **Evidence anchors**: Proposition 1 and its proof; abstract mentions "observational identification of path-conditional means via iterated regression".
- **Break condition**: Violation of stationarity (e.g., structural breaks like COVID stimulus) or failure of state sufficiency (hidden state variables).

### Mechanism 2: Causal Set Bounds under Bounded Confounding
- **Claim**: A sharp identified set for the causal path contrast τ^do_h is obtained by bounding the observational–causal gap with a sensitivity parameter c_h, yielding interpretable breakdown values.
- **Mechanism**: Under bounded confounding (Assumption 1), |τ^do_h - τ^obs_h| ≤ 2c_h by triangle inequality. The bound is sharp via construction of a binary confounder that saturates endpoints. The breakdown value c^*_h = |τ^obs_h|/2 is the minimum confounding strength that would nullify the effect.
- **Core assumptions**: Bounded confounding (Assumption 1) and path-level consistency (Appendix B).
- **Evidence anchors**: Theorem 1 and its sharpness proof; abstract states "causal set identification under bounded confounding, yielding sharp identified sets with interpretable breakdown values".
- **Break condition**: Confounding structure violates the pointwise bound (e.g., time-varying confounding strength that exceeds c_h non-uniformly across paths).

### Mechanism 3: Importance-Weighted Conformal Calibration
- **Claim**: Importance-weighted conformal calibration provides distribution-free coverage bands for stress-path extrapolation, with diagnostics (R_weight, B_eff) quantifying extrapolation cost and triggering abstention.
- **Mechanism**: Split pre-period into training/calibration. For each rolling origin, compute rollout error scores S_b. Weight these scores by macro transition density ratios w_b(a^S) to correct for distribution shift between historical and stress paths. The weighted quantile yields a band with coverage ≥ 1-α - R_mix - R_weight, where R_weight depends on extrapolation severity.
- **Core assumptions**: Score path-homogeneity (Assumption 12) and bounded likelihood ratios (Assumption 11).
- **Evidence anchors**: Proposition 2 and its coverage guarantee; abstract mentions "importance-weighted conformal calibration bands with diagnostics that quantify extrapolation cost and trigger abstention".
- **Break condition**: Stress path lies far outside historical support such that w_b(a^S) → ∞ (unbounded likelihood ratios) or score homogeneity fails (e.g., nonlinearities that make error distribution path-dependent beyond likelihood ratio).

## Foundational Learning
- **Partial Identification (Causal Set Bounds)**
  - *Why needed*: The framework accepts that confounding is present and cannot be bought away; partial identification trades point estimates for honest uncertainty sets.
  - *Quick check*: Given a sensitivity parameter c_h and observational contrast τ^obs_h, what is the identified set for τ^do_h? → [τ^obs_h - 2c_h, τ^obs_h + 2c_h].

- **Recursive Rollout vs. Direct Multi-Horizon Estimation**
  - *Why needed*: Recursive rollout shares a model across horizons but compounds estimation error; direct estimation avoids compounding but requires high-dimensional inputs. Understanding the trade-off is key to the oracle inequality and the crossover horizon h^*.
  - *Quick check*: When does the oracle inequality recommend switching from recursive to direct estimation? → When ρ > 1 and ϵ_n Γ_h > ϵ^dir_{n,h}, i.e., error amplification exceeds direct estimator risk.

- **Conformal Prediction under Covariate Shift**
  - *Why needed*: Stress scenarios lie outside historical macro support; standard conformal bands fail. Importance weighting corrects for distribution shift using macro transition density ratios.
  - *Quick check*: What diagnostic quantifies extrapolation severity in the weighted conformal band? → R_weight = W_max / (∑_b w_b + W_max) and effective sample size B_eff.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Observational Estimator → Causal Set Wrapper → Calibration Module → Aggregation.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Pre-processing: construct state variables ι and macro actions a.
  2. Train conditional mean model m(ι,a) on pre-period data.
  3. Recursive rollout: compute τ^obs_h by iterating m from observed initial state.
  4. Specify sensitivity parameter c_h; form identified set [τ^obs_h ± 2c_h] and breakdown value c^*_h.
  5. Split pre-period into training and calibration blocks.
  6. For each rolling origin in calibration, compute rollout error scores S_b.
  7. Compute importance weights w_b(a^S) based on macro transition densities.
  8. Compute weighted quantile to obtain calibration band Δ^est_h; derive diagnostics R_weight and B_eff.
  9. Aggregate: final uncertainty decomposition includes estimation band (Δ^est_h) and confounding envelope (2c_h).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Recursive vs. direct estimator*: recursive is parsimonious but error compounds with ρ; direct avoids compounding but requires H separate models.
  - *Calibration block size (B)*: larger B improves nominal coverage but increases temporal dependence term R_mix; too small B yields wide bands.
  - *Sensitivity parameter (c_h)*: larger c_h yields conservative sets but may obscure actionable conclusions; c^*_h provides a single-number robustness summary.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High R_weight (>0.5) or B_eff < 5: severe extrapolation; coverage guarantees degrade → abstain.
  - ρ > 1 with large Γ_h: recursive rollout unreliable; switch to direct estimator beyond h^*.
  - Placebo tests (faket0) yield non-zero τ^obs_h: violates stationarity → framework invalid.
  - Mean-state bias |b_h| large: indicates nonlinear Update function; direct estimator preferred.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate oracle inequality (Theorem 2): across ρ regimes, verify |bµ^rec_h - µ^obs_h| ≤ ϵ_n Γ_h; determine crossover horizon h^* where recursive error exceeds direct estimator risk.
  2. Validate calibration coverage (Proposition 2): across stress severities, empirical coverage should be ≥ 1-α - R_mix - R_weight; confirm R_weight and B_eff correlate with stress severity.
  3. Validate set identification (Theorem 1): across confounding strengths (γ_A, γ_Y), verify true τ^do_h lies within [τ^obs_h ± 2c_h]; check monotonicity of gap |τ^do_h - τ^obs_h|.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How to extend the framework to non-stationary environments with structural breaks? The paper notes that extending to regime-switching dynamics is an important direction; stationarity is essential for identification and error bounds, so violations (e.g., during COVID) cause large unaddressed errors. Resolution would require a version with time-varying parameters or change-point detection validated on crisis periods.
- **Open Question 2**: What is the empirical performance of the framework on real loan data and its impact on regulatory stress testing? The authors mention applying the framework to real loan-level data (e.g., Fannie Mae) as a natural next step. Real data complexities may affect uncertainty decomposition and calibration diagnostics. Resolution would involve such an application and comparison to benchmarks, analyzing how the three-layer uncertainty influences capital decisions.
- **Open Question 3**: Can c_h be calibrated objectively from data while preserving honest identification? The authors note that c_h choice currently rests on domain judgment, limiting reproducibility and regulatory trust. A data-driven procedure to bound confounding (e.g., using instrumental variables) with validated coverage would resolve this.

## Limitations
- Core assumptions (stationarity, state sufficiency, bounded confounding, bounded likelihood ratios) may not hold in extreme macro stress, threatening identification or coverage.
- Recursive rollout error compounds with horizon, and weighted conformal coverage is modest (50–79%) due to high extrapolation cost and temporal dependence.
- Empirical validation is limited to simulation; real-world performance remains untested.
