# DeepPresenter: Environment-Grounded Reflection for Agentic Presentation Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22839  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22839  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** DeepPresenter (Gemini‑3‑Pro) averages 4.44 / 5 across 128 tasks, beating Gamma (4.36) and all open‑source baselines; its distilled 9B variant (4.19) nearly matches GPT‑5 (4.22) at a fraction of the cost.

## Executive Summary
DeepPresenter tackles the inflexibility of current presentation agents—limited by predefined workflows and templates—by proposing an agentic framework with a Researcher and a Presenter that collaborate through a shared environment. The core innovation is environment‑grounded reflection: agents use an `inspect` tool to view rendered slides and manuscript diagnostics, enabling them to detect and fix post‑render defects (e.g., layout issues, low contrast) that internal reasoning misses. During training, extrinsic verification—an independent critic—supplies unbiased reflection signals to mitigate self‑verification bias. Evaluated on 128 diverse tasks, DeepPresenter with Gemini‑3‑Pro attains an average score of 4.44 (0–5), outperforming Gamma (4.36) and all open‑source baselines. The distilled DeepPresenter‑9B achieves 4.19, competitive with GPT‑5 (4.22) at a fraction of the cost. Ablation studies underscore the importance of both the dual‑agent structure and the grounded reflection mechanism.

## Method Summary
The system consists of two specialized agents sharing a filesystem. The **Researcher** autonomously retrieves and synthesizes topic‑aligned manuscript content using tools like `search_web` and writes `manuscript.md`. The **Presenter** reads this file and generates HTML slide decks from scratch, guided by a content‑driven design plan. After creating a slide, it calls `inspect_slide` to obtain a rendered image, then enters a reflect‑revise loop: it reasons about the perceptual output, identifies defects (e.g., overflow, contrast), and edits the source code accordingly. For training, trajectories are synthesized by sampling strong backbone models (e.g., Gemini‑3‑Pro). An independent **Extrinsic Verifier** critiques intermediate artifacts (manuscript or slide renders) in isolation, and its reasoning trace is injected into the agent’s context as a `think` call, guiding fixes. Trajectories are filtered for constraint compliance, reflection–action consistency, and output quality, yielding ~802 high‑quality demonstrations. A base model (e.g., GLM‑4.6V‑Flash) is fine‑tuned on these trajectories via standard supervised fine‑tuning.

## Key Results
- DeepPresenter (Gemini‑3‑Pro) average score 4.44/5 on 128 tasks, surpassing Gamma (4.36) and all open‑source baselines.
- Distilled DeepPresenter‑9B achieves 4.19, nearly matching GPT‑5 (4.22) while being far cheaper.
- Ablations confirm the dual‑agent decomposition and environment‑grounded reflection are both critical to performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Specialized dual‑agent decomposition
- **Claim:** Separating content research (Researcher) from visual design (Presenter) enables adaptive planning and content‑driven design.
- **Mechanism:** The Researcher autonomously explores sources and synthesizes a coherent manuscript, while the Presenter converts it into slides from scratch using a content‑driven plan. They coordinate through a shared file system, avoiding rigid template‑filling.
- **Core assumption:** Each agent can develop specialized tool‑use patterns and long‑horizon planning suited to its sub‑task, leading to higher quality and narrative coherence.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “coordinates two specialized agents through a shared observation space... Researcher autonomously explores... Presenter converts it into visually coherent slides via content‑driven design.”  
  - Section 2.2: Details Researcher’s “autonomously plans its exploration” and Presenter’s “generates slides from scratch... content‑driven approach.”  
  - Corpus: Weak direct evidence; neighboring works (e.g., SlideGen, PreGenie) explore multi‑agent slide generation but do not explicitly validate the specialization decomposition via ablation.

### Mechanism 2: Environment‑grounded reflection
- **Claim:** Using the `inspect` tool to obtain perceptual artifact states (rendered slide images, manuscript diagnostics) lets agents correct defects invisible to internal reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Agents call `inspect` to see the actual rendered output, then engage in a reflect–revise loop. Defects such as low contrast or overflow are identified based on the perceptual state, not on code or reasoning traces alone.
- **Core assumption:** The user’s perception aligns with the final rendered artifact; therefore, correction signals must be grounded in that perceptual state to avoid “state mismatch” between internal reasoning and reality.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “environment‑grounded reflection conditions the generation process on perceptual artifact states... enabling the system to identify and correct presentation‑specific issues during execution.”  
  - Section 2.3: Contrasts with self‑reflection, states “many defects manifest only in perceptual states... leaving introspective reflection operating in a mismatched observation space.”  
  - Corpus: Strong support; CuES and related agentic works emphasize environment‑grounded signals. Reflact also uses “world‑grounded decision making.”

### Mechanism 3: Extrinsic verification during trajectory synthesis
- **Claim:** An independent critic provides unbiased reflection signals, mitigating self‑verification bias and strengthening reflective behavior learning.
- **Mechanism:** After an `inspect` call, an extrinsic verifier (a separate LLM) evaluates the intermediate artifact in isolation, producing a critique JSON with identified defects and suggested fixes. This trace is injected into the agent’s context as a `think` call, guiding a targeted revision before the rollout continues.
- **Core assumption:** Verification signals generated within the same trajectory state are biased toward rationalizing defects; decoupling verification (different model/state) yields more objective, actionable signals.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “extrinsic verification (an independent critic) supplies unbiased reflection signals, mitigating self‑verification bias.”  
  - Section 3.2: “self‑verification bias... verification signals are produced in an isolated context... decoupling verification from the agent’s own trajectory state.”  
  - Corpus: Moderate support; works like “Self‑[in]correct” and “On the self‑verification limitations” study the bias, but extrinsic verification as a training data synthesis technique appears novel to this paper.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Agentic Workflows with Tool Augmentation**  
  *Why needed here:* The system is not a monolithic model but a coordinated process using tools (`search_papers`, `inspect_slide`, `write_file`) to interact with a dynamic environment. Understanding how to decompose a goal into tool‑mediated steps is core.  
  *Quick check question:* Given the goal “create a slide about a recent AI breakthrough,” list the first three tools the Researcher agent should likely invoke and why.

- **Concept: Multimodal Perception for Code Generation**  
  *Why needed here:* The Presenter generates HTML/CSS, but `inspect` returns a *pixel image*. The agent must map visual defects (e.g., “text is blurry”) back to code fixes (e.g., “increase font‑weight”).  
  *Quick check question:* If `inspect_slide` returns an image where text overflows its container, what are two possible CSS property adjustments the agent should consider?

- **Concept: Verification Bias in RL/LLM Trajectories**  
  *Why needed here:* Training data synthesis relies on the insight that an agent cannot reliably critique its own intermediate outputs. Understanding this bias explains why extrinsic verification is necessary.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might an agent, after writing HTML, conclude “the layout looks fine” when a human would immediately see overlapping elements?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
User -> Researcher -> Shared Filesystem -> Presenter -> Inspect Tools (inspect_manuscript, inspect_slide) -> Reflection Loop -> Final Slides. Training adds: Trajectories -> Extrinsic Verifier -> Critic Traces -> Trajectory Filter.

### Critical path
1. Receive user instruction `I`.  
2. Researcher executes trajectory `τ_R`: plans, retrieves, writes `manuscript.md` to shared filesystem (may loop with `inspect_manuscript`).  
3. Presenter reads `manuscript.md` and executes trajectory `τ_P`: plans design, writes `slide_01.html` to filesystem.  
4. Presenter calls `inspect_slide` on `slide_01.html`, receives rendered image `O`.  
5. **Grounded Reflection:** Agent `think`s based on `O`, identifies defect (e.g., low contrast), `edit_file` to fix.  
6. Repeat steps 4‑5 for each slide until finalize.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Free‑form vs. Templates:** Free‑form HTML enables topical visual themes but risks layout defects; templates guarantee stability but limit diversity and topic alignment.  
- **Tool Overhead:** Each `inspect` call requires rendering (CPU/GPU cost, latency). More reflection cycles improve quality but increase cost and context length.  
- **Constraint Compliance:** Enforcing user constraints (e.g., “14 slides”) can conflict with narrative naturalness. The system prioritizes content integrity but uses rule‑based verification for scoring.

### Failure signatures
- **Environment Failure:** `inspect` tool timeout, file system permission errors, context window overflow (warning at 50%/80%).  
- **Agent Failure:** Researcher produces manuscript with missing asset references (`inspect_manuscript` flags); Presenter generates slides with overflow/contrast issues (`inspect_slide` shows them) but `think` fails to propose correct fix.  
- **Training Failure:** Trajectories with “reflection–action inconsistency” (agent ignores extrinsic critic’s advice) or low output quality (overlap, blank slides) are filtered out.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Tool Sanity Check:** Run the Presenter on a trivial 1‑slide manuscript. Verify `inspect_slide` returns a valid image and that a deliberate low‑contrast error (white text on white bg) is detected.  
2. **Dual‑Agent Ablation:** Replace Researcher+Presenter with a single agent doing both roles. Compare manuscript depth (# of unique sources cited) and visual style diversity (Vendi Score) against the full system on 10 PersonaHub tasks.  
3. **Extrinsic Verification Validation:** On a held‑out set of 50 trajectories, compare the type and quantity of defects identified by self‑verification (agent’s own `think` post‑`inspect`) vs. extrinsic verification (independent critic). Expect extrinsic to find significantly more “layout” and “render” defects per Figure 4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Inference‑time extrinsic verification:** Can the extrinsic verification mechanism be effectively deployed at inference time to mitigate self‑verification bias without introducing reflection–action inconsistency or prohibitive overhead?  
2. **Robustness and cost of multi‑step rollouts:** To what extent does the framework’s reliance on multi‑step, tool‑using rollouts limit its robustness and cost‑effectiveness in real‑world, long‑horizon scenarios with unstable environments?  
3. **Generality of environment‑grounded reflection:** Is the environment‑grounded reflection mechanism, as implemented via the `inspect` tool, generalizable to detecting defects in presentation artifacts beyond rendered slides and manuscript diagnostics (e.g., audio narration scripts, speaker notes, interactive elements)?  
4. **Data efficiency of learned behaviors:** What is the minimal‑sufficient quality and quantity of demonstration trajectories required for DeepPresenter‑9B to learn effective agentic behaviors, and how does this scale with backbone model capacity?

## Limitations
- Exact implementation of `inspect_slide` (browser, viewport, asset handling) and rule‑based constraint verifiers is unspecified.  
- Full system prompts for agents and extrinsic critic are missing, limiting reproducibility.  
- Vendi Score feature extraction pipeline (DINOv2 preprocessing) details are not provided.

## Confidence
- Dual‑Agent Decomposition: **High**  
- Environment‑Grounded Reflection: **Medium**  
- Extrinsic Verification for Training: **Low**
