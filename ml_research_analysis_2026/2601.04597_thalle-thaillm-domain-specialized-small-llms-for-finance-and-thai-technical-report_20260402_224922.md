# THaLLE-ThaiLLM: Domain-Specialized Small LLMs for Finance and Thai -- Technical Report
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04597
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04597
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Merging Qwen-8B with ThaiLLM-8B improves Thai exam performance; further merging a finance-specialized model yields multi-domain gains while maintaining consistency.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the problem of efficiently developing multi-capability LLMs for on-premise use in finance and Thai language, avoiding the cost of retraining a single model for multiple domains. It employs linear model merging via MergeKit to combine independently pre‑trained specialist models (Qwen‑8B, ThaiLLM‑8B for Thai, THaLLE‑CFA‑8B for finance) into a unified model. Evaluations demonstrate that merging Qwen‑8B with ThaiLLM‑8B enhances Thai performance on M3/M6 O‑NET exams. A three‑way merger further improves reasoning scores, CFA results, and Thai instruction‑following consistency, showing that model merging can be a cost‑effective alternative to joint training.

## Method Summary
The authors adopt a two‑stage pipeline: first, they fine‑tune separate base models on domain‑specific data—ThaiLLM‑8B on Thai language tasks, THaLLE‑CFA‑8B on finance, while keeping Qwen‑8B as a generalist foundation. In the second stage, they merge the checkpoints of these models using MergeKit, a toolkit for linear combinations of model parameters. The merging process is governed by configurable coefficients that weight each specialist’s contribution. The resulting merged model, THaLLE‑0.2‑ThaiLLM‑8B‑fa, inherits capabilities from all constituent models without further training. This approach leverages the efficiency of parameter‑space arithmetic to create a multi‑domain LLM while avoiding the computational expense of joint training or multi‑task fine‑tuning.

## Key Results
- Merging Qwen‑8B with ThaiLLM‑8B yields a measurable improvement in Thai language performance on the M3/M6 O‑NET exams.
- The three‑way merge (Qwen‑8B + ThaiLLM‑8B + THaLLE‑CFA‑8B) in reasoning mode achieves a 12.6% O‑NET score increase, a 5.7% CFA score gain, and a 40% Thai‑IC score boost over the base Qwen‑8B.
- The merged model also exhibits higher consistency in Thai output generation as measured by IFEval‑TH.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Model merging via linear combination in parameter space allows the strengths of individually fine‑tuned models to be combined without catastrophic forgetting. Each specialist model contributes task‑specific features that are additive in the weight space, so the merged model retains generalist knowledge from Qwen‑8B while gaining Thai proficiency from ThaiLLM‑8B and finance expertise from THaLLE‑CFA‑8B. The MergeKit framework provides a controlled way to balance these contributions through coefficients, which the authors tune to maximize performance across domains. The observed gains suggest that the domains are sufficiently orthogonal that interference is minimal, enabling simultaneous improvement.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Qwen‑8B → ThaiLLM‑8B → THaLLE‑CFA‑8B → MergeKit → THaLLE‑0.2‑ThaiLLM‑8B‑fa
- **Critical path**: Independent domain fine‑tuning → Selection of checkpoints → Configuration of MergeKit coefficients → Merging → Evaluation on M3/M6 O‑NET, CFA, Thai‑IC, IFEval‑TH.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Independent training vs joint multi‑task training (cost vs potential synergy); fixed merging coefficients (simplicity) vs learned interpolation (flexibility); risk of domain interference vs additive gains.
- **Failure signatures**: Drop in generalist performance on non‑target tasks; increased variance across evaluation benchmarks; degraded output consistency (IFEval‑TH score decrease).
- **First three experiments**:

## Limitations
- Insufficient methodological detail (MergeKit config, seeds, coefficients) and lack of statistical tests impede reproducibility.
- Evaluation limited to a few benchmarks with unknown coverage and possible data leakage; no comparison to joint training or alternative merging methods.
- Evidence restricted to the Qwen‑8B family; no human or real‑world validation; paper not peer‑reviewed.

## Confidence
- Merging efficacy: Medium – plausible but method‑sensitive.
- Thai gain (Qwen+ThaiLLM): Medium – single evaluation, no error bars.
- Combined multi‑domain gains: Low – simultaneous improvements may mask trade‑offs.
