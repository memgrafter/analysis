# Multimodal Emotion Recognition via Bi-directional Cross-Attention and Temporal Modeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11971
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11971
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Macro F1 of 0.32 on ABAW 10th EXPR test set, surpassing baseline (0.25). Validation best: 0.367 with 30-frame window.

## Executive Summary
Facial expression recognition in unconstrained videos is notoriously difficult due to large variability and dynamic affective states. This paper introduces a multimodal framework that leverages frozen foundation models—CLIP for visual information and Wav2Vec2.0 for audio—augmented with a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) to model temporal dynamics. The visual and audio streams are fused via bi-directional cross-attention, enabling symmetric interaction, while an audio adapter aligns audio features to the visual dimensionality. A text-guided contrastive loss aligns visual representations with emotion-related text prompts, providing semantic regularization. On the ABAW 10th EXPR test set, the model achieves a Macro F1 score of 0.32, outperforming the baseline of 0.25. Validation experiments show the best configuration (30-frame window) reaches 0.3670 Macro F1.

## Method Summary
The framework processes video frames through a frozen CLIP image encoder to obtain visual embeddings. These embeddings are fed into a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) to capture temporal context. Audio is encoded using a frozen Wav2Vec2.0 model; an adapter then projects the audio features into the same dimension as the visual tokens. Both modalities interact through bi-directional cross-attention, allowing each to selectively attend to the other. The fused representation is used for emotion classification. Additionally, a contrastive loss aligns visual embeddings with CLIP-encoded text prompts corresponding to the target emotion, encouraging semantic consistency. All components are trained end-to-end except the frozen backbones.

## Key Results
- Macro F1 on ABAW 10th EXPR test set: **0.32** (baseline: 0.25).
- Best validation performance: **0.367** Macro F1 with a 30‑frame window.
- The proposed combination of TCN, cross‑attention, and text‑guided loss yields a clear improvement over the baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Bi‑directional cross‑attention** enables each modality to attend to salient features of the other, creating a balanced multimodal representation that leverages complementary cues.
- **Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)** aggregates information across frames, capturing the evolution of facial expressions while maintaining computational efficiency.
- **Frozen CLIP and Wav2Vec2.0** provide strong, generic feature extractors trained on massive datasets, reducing the need for large‑scale annotation and mitigating overfitting.
- **Audio adapter** aligns the audio feature space with the visual space, facilitating effective cross‑modal attention without distorting either representation.
- **Text‑guided contrastive loss** anchors visual embeddings to an semantically meaningful text space defined by emotion labels, smoothing the decision boundaries and improving generalization to unseen expressions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Fusion**: Combines visual and audio cues to overcome limitations of single‑modality approaches. *Why needed*: Unconstrained videos contain complementary signals; fusion improves robustness. *Quick check*: Evaluate unimodal versus multimodal performance.
- **Cross‑Attention**: Allows each sequence to attend to the other in a symmetric fashion. *Why needed*: Enables mutual reinforcement of features. *Quick check*: Visualize attention weights to ensure both modalities are utilized.
- **Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)**: Uses dilated convolutions to model long‑range dependencies in sequences. *Why needed*: Expressions evolve over time; TCN captures dynamics without recurrence. *Quick check*: Ablate TCN and test on variable‑length sequences.
- **Contrastive Learning**: Pulls together embeddings of matching modalities (e.g., visual and text for same emotion) while pushing apart others. *Why needed*: Provides semantic regularization beyond standard cross‑entropy. *Quick check*: Perform nearest‑neighbor search in the joint embedding space.
- **Frozen Foundation Models**: Pre‑trained CLIP and Wav2Vec2.0 act as fixed feature extractors. *Why needed*: Leverages large‑scale pre‑training to boost representation quality without enormous training costs. *Quick check*: Compare with fine‑tuned counterparts to measure benefits and stability.
- **Audio Adapter**: A simple linear projection aligning audio feature dimensions to visual tokens. *Why needed*: Ensures dimensional compatibility for attention. *Quick check*: Analyze distribution similarity (e.g., cosine similarity) after projection.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: CLIP backbone → TCN → Visual tokens; Wav2Vec2.0 backbone → Audio adapter → Audio tokens; Bi‑directional cross‑attention (visual↔audio) → Concatenation → Classifier; Emotion‑label prompts → CLIP text encoder → Contrastive loss.
- **Critical Path**: Input video/audio → Visual and audio encoding → Temporal modeling (TCN) → Cross‑modal fusion → Feature aggregation → Classification.
- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - Frozen backbones offer stability but may miss emotion‑specific fine‑grained patterns.
  - TCN provides efficient temporal processing but has a limited receptive field; very long‑range dependencies might be missed.
  - Symmetric cross‑attention treats modalities equally but can be noisy if one modality is unreliable (e.g., occluded face).
  - Fixed text prompts are simple but may not fully capture the diversity of in‑the‑wild expressions.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Severe performance degradation on samples with noisy audio or heavily occluded faces.
  - Large gap between validation and test scores, indicating overfitting or dataset shift.
  - Uneven performance across emotion classes, particularly ambiguous or subtle ones.
- **First Three Experiments**:
  1. Ablate the TCN by replacing it with simple frame averaging to quantify the temporal modeling contribution.
  2. Replace bi‑directional cross‑attention with late fusion (concatenation after independent encoders) to assess the necessity of cross‑modal interaction.
  3. Systematically vary the temporal window size (e.g., 15, 30, 60 frames) to study the trade‑off between context length and accuracy/efficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What more effective temporal modeling strategies beyond TCN could better capture long-range dependencies in facial expression sequences without sacrificing computational efficiency?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion states: "For future work, we plan to explore more effective temporal modeling strategies..."
- **Why unresolved**: The TCN shows diminishing returns beyond 30 frames on validation, suggesting limited receptive field; the paper does not compare with alternatives like transformers or hierarchical models.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic comparison of TCN, transformer‑based temporal encoders (e.g., TimeSformer), and hybrid approaches on ABAW, measuring Macro F1 vs. window size and computational cost.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can adaptive multimodal fusion mechanisms that dynamically weigh visual and audio contributions improve robustness under modality-specific noise (e.g., occluded faces or noisy audio)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion mentions exploring "stronger multimodal fusion mechanisms."
- **Why unresolved**: The bi‑directional cross‑attention treats modalities symmetrically but does not address scenarios where one modality is unreliable; no analysis of fusion behavior under simulated noise.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies injecting noise (e.g., blur on faces, additive background noise) and evaluating fusion variants (e.g., gated attention, modality confidence weighting) with degradation analysis.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Why does the optimal temporal window size differ between validation (30 frames) and test (60 frames), and does this indicate dataset bias or label inconsistency?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Tables 1 and 2 show best validation at 30 frames but test at 60 frames; the paper does not analyze this discrepancy.
- **Why unresolved**: Unclear if due to distribution shift, varying expression durations, or annotation noise in test set; could affect model selection and reported performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Statistical comparison of expression duration distributions across splits, label consensus metrics, and cross‑validation with alternate train/test splits.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Are fixed emotion‑label prompts sufficient for CLIP‑based semantic regularization, or would learnable prompts better align visual features with affective semantics in unconstrained videos?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper uses static prompts (e.g., "A face expressing [Emotion]") without optimization; related work (CoCoOp) suggests learnable prompts improve downstream adaptation.
- **Why unresolved**: Static prompts may not capture in‑the‑wild expression nuances or CLIP's textual space; contrastive loss impact is not ablated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Compare fixed prompts with prompt‑tuning (e.g., CoCoOp) and analyze per‑class performance, especially for ambiguous expressions, via visualization of CLIP embedding alignment.

## Limitations
- Single‑dataset evaluation (ABAW EXPR) limits generalizability.
- Frozen backbones may not capture emotion‑specific features.
- Validation‑test performance gap (0.367 vs 0.320) hints at overfitting or dataset shift.
