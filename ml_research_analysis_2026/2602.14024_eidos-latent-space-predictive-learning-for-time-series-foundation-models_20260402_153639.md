# EIDOS: Latent-Space Predictive Learning for Time Series Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.14024  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14024  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art zero‑shot forecasting on GIFT‑Eval (normalized MASE 0.757, CRPS 0.547) and exhibits superior robustness to noise.

## Executive Summary
EIDOS is a time series foundation model that reimagines pretraining through latent‑space predictive learning. Instead of directly predicting future values, it trains a causal Transformer to forecast future latent embeddings, using a lightweight aggregator to produce stable targets. A joint objective combines three losses: latent alignment (predictor vs. aggregated target), observational grounding (reconstructing future values from the target via a frozen forecasting head), and standard forecasting supervision. This design yields a more structured latent space, improved noise robustness, and strong zero‑shot performance with a compact 6‑layer backbone. Ablations confirm the necessity of the grounding loss, and probing reveals linearly separable representations enabling controllable steering.

## Method Summary
EIDOS processes univariate time series with a context length of 4096 and a forecast horizon of 64 during pretraining. Inputs are tokenized by a SiGLU layer (point‑wise GLU with sine activation) into embeddings. A 6‑layer causal Transformer (hidden dimension 384, 12 heads, FlashAttention, RoPE) acts as the predictor \(f_\theta\); when given historical embeddings \(z_{\leq t}\), it outputs a predicted future embedding \(\hat{b}_{t+1}\). The target aggregator \(h_\phi\) is a depthwise 1D convolution (kernel size = horizon) applied to the actual future segment \(z_{t+1:t+l}\), producing a target embedding \(h_{t+1}\). Two forecasting heads are used: a trainable head on \(\hat{b}_{t+1}\) for forecasting loss \(L_{\text{pred}}\), and a frozen copy (the grounding head \(g_\psi\)) on \(h_{t+1}\) for grounding loss \(L_{\text{gnd}}\). The latent loss \(L_{\text{latent}}\) measures negative cosine similarity between \(\hat{b}_{t+1}\) and \(\text{sg}(h_{t+1})\). The total loss is \(L = L_{\text{latent}} + 0.1\,L_{\text{gnd}} + 1.0\,L_{\text{pred}}\). Training data mixes real series (Chronos and GIFT‑Eval pretrain; TSMixup augmentation) with synthetic CauKer data (1:1 ratio). Optimization uses AdamW (warm‑up + cosine LR, batch 256, bfloat16) for 100k steps. Zero‑shot evaluation on GIFT‑Eval and fev‑bench uses median forecasts.

## Key Results
- **SOTA on GIFT‑Eval:** Normalized MASE 0.757 and CRPS 0.547, outperforming prior foundation models.
- **Enhanced noise robustness:** Under high Gaussian noise, relative CRPS degradation is ~1.31× for EIDOS vs. >1.5× for Chronos‑2.
- **Structured latent space:** Linear probing shows higher Linear Discriminant Ratio (LDR) for trend/periodicity, and latent steering can controllably modify forecasts.
- **Ablation importance:** Adding SiGLU improves results; adding latent loss (\(L_{\text{latent}}\)) helps; removing grounding loss (\(L_{\text{gnd}}\)) degrades performance despite lower \(L_{\text{latent}}\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Latent‑space prediction forces internalization of temporal dynamics
The predictor \(f_\theta\) is trained to match a future latent embedding \(h_{t+1}\) aggregated from the actual future segment, with stop‑gradient on the target. This prevents trivial solutions and forces the model to capture predictable, temporally coherent patterns rather than noise. The core assumption is that a well‑structured latent space—where similar dynamics are nearby—generalizes better. Evidence includes higher LDR in probing experiments and strong robustness to input noise. Ablations show that removing latent loss harms performance.

### Mechanism 2: Grounding loss prevents representation collapse
The frozen forecasting head \(g_\psi\) reconstructs future observations from the aggregated target embedding \(h_{t+1}\). This anchors the latent target to the observation space, ensuring it remains informative and degenerate (constant) solutions are penalized. The core assumption is that an embedding reconstructable into future values must encode forecasting‑relevant information. The ablation removing \(L_{\text{gnd}}\) produces variants with lower latent loss convergence but worse CRPS/MASE, confirming collapse prevention.

### Mechanism 3: Lightweight depthwise aggregator stabilizes targets
The depthwise 1D convolution in \(h_\phi\) distills a future segment into a single embedding, allowing each latent channel to learn its own temporal kernel. This captures channel‑specific dynamics more effectively than uniform averaging or a linear projection across channels. Ablations (Figure 8b) show depthwise Conv1D achieves lower grounding loss (better convergence) than alternatives, indicating more stable and informative targets.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept 1: Predictive Coding / Joint‑Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**  
*Why needed here:* The core paradigm—predicting future latent representations with an asymmetric predictor/target architecture and stop‑gradient—directly follows JEPA. Understanding this is key to avoiding representation collapse.  
*Quick check question:* What is the purpose of the stop‑gradient operation on the target embedding in the latent loss?

**Concept 2: Latent Space Geometry and Separability**  
*Why needed here:* EIDOS claims that latent‑space prediction yields "linearly separable" and "structured" representations. Probing results (Linear Discriminant Ratio) are central evidence; one must understand why linear separability indicates coherent feature organization.  
*Quick check question:* Why would a model trained to predict future latents produce higher LDR for trend/periodicity than an observation‑space model?

**Concept 3: Representation Collapse in Self‑Supervised Learning**  
*Why needed here:* The grounding loss explicitly prevents collapse. Recognizing collapse symptoms (e.g., trivial constant embeddings that minimize latent loss but lose informativeness) is essential for designing stable objectives.  
*Quick check question:* In the ablation, why does the variant without \(L_{\text{gnd}}\) achieve lower \(L_{\text{latent}}\) but worse forecasting performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Input x → SiGLU Tokenizer → embeddings z  
z_context → Causal Transformer \(f_\theta\) (predictor) → \(\hat{b}_{t+1}\)  
z_future → Target Aggregator \(h_\phi\) (depthwise 1D conv) → \(h_{t+1}\)  
\(\hat{b}_{t+1}\) → Forecasting Head (trainable) → forecast → \(L_{\text{pred}}\)  
\(h_{t+1}\) → Frozen Forecasting Head \(g_\psi\) → forecast → \(L_{\text{gnd}}\)  
\(\hat{b}_{t+1}\) and \(\text{sg}(h_{t+1})\) → Latent Loss \(L_{\text{latent}}\)

**Critical path**  
For each training window: tokenize context and future; run Transformer on context to get \(\hat{b}_{t+1}\); aggregate future embeddings to get \(h_{t+1}\); compute three losses (latent, grounding, forecasting) with respective heads; sum and backpropagate.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Simplicity vs. flexibility:* EIDOS omits an auxiliary target encoder, simplifying training but relying on \(h_\phi\) for stable targets; the depthwise conv is lightweight but may be less expressive than a learned encoder.  
- *Latent vs. observation supervision:* Pure latent loss risks collapse; adding grounding re‑introduces observation‑space pressures.  
- *Parameter efficiency:* A small 6‑layer backbone works well, indicating the latent objective provides strong signal without massive scale.

**Failure signatures**  
- *Latent collapse:* \(L_{\text{latent}}\) approaches its lower bound (near −1) while forecasting metrics sharply drop.  
- *Grounding head updating:* Non‑zero gradients on \(g_\psi\) parameters; \(L_{\text{gnd}}\) decreases unrealistically fast.  
- *Aggregator shape mismatch:* Dimensions of \(\hat{b}_{t+1}\) and \(h_{t+1}\) do not align, breaking cosine loss.  
- *Data scaling mismatch:* \(L_{\text{pred}}\) dominates and \(L_{\text{latent}}\) remains high, suggesting inconsistent normalization between real and synthetic data.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Ablation replication:** Train four variants: baseline (no JEPA), +SiGLU, +\(L_{\text{latent}}\) only, full (with \(L_{\text{gnd}}\)). Verify full EIDOS outperforms others and that removing \(L_{\text{gnd}}\) degrades performance. Monitor \(g_\psi\) gradients to confirm they are zero in the full model.  
2. **Noise robustness replication:** Inject Gaussian and impulse noise at varying levels into GIFT‑Eval inputs; compute relative CRPS degradation. Confirm EIDOS degrades less than Chronos‑2/Moirai2, especially at high noise.  
3. **Latent steering check:** Use linear probing on the final Transformer layer to extract trend and periodicity direction vectors. Apply injection (e.g., ±0.2, ±0.5) to hidden states of a neutral input; compare forecasts—systematic trend/oscillation changes should appear for EIDOS but not for baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the latent‑space predictive framework be extended to multivariate time series and effectively incorporate static or dynamic covariates? The paper acknowledges this as a natural direction but leaves it unaddressed.  
- How can multimodal signals (e.g., text descriptions, metadata) be incorporated into the framework to enable richer context and interpretable interactions? The conclusion suggests this as a future possibility.  
- Why does scaling model capacity not yield consistent improvements in forecasting performance? The paper notes scaling benefits are unclear and likely depend on data, objectives, or architecture rather than size alone.  
- Can the structured latent space be effectively leveraged for downstream tasks beyond forecasting (e.g., anomaly detection, classification, retrieval) and does it provide interpretable advantages? The conclusion hints at broader applications but offers no validation.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete specifications:** Critical implementation details—quantile head architecture, aggregator initialization, evaluation protocol nuances (context length adaptation, autoregressive generation), and data normalization strategy—are not fully described, hindering faithful replication.  
- **Weak validation of latent structure claims:** The assertion of more structured, linearly separable representations relies heavily on JEPA analogies and probing; direct evidence within the time series domain remains limited.  
- **Ambiguity in scaling:** The paper reports no clear monotonic relationship between model size and performance, but does not diagnose the underlying causes (optimization, objective, or data limitations).

## Confidence
- **Latent‑space prediction yields structured representations:** Medium  
- **Grounding loss prevents collapse:** High  
- **Depthwise aggregator superiority:** High  
- **Overall SOTA and noise robustness:** Medium
