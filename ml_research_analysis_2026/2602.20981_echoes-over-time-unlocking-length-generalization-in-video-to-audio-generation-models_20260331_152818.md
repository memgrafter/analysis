# Echoes Over Time: Unlocking Length Generalization in Video-to-Audio Generation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20981
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20981
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MMHNet achieves state-of-the-art long-form video-to-audio generation, significantly outperforming prior methods on metrics like FD-PANNs (↓5.29), IS-CPANNs (↑8.10), IB-Score (↑36.27), and DeSync (↓0.410), while generating coherent audio beyond 5 minutes without training on such durations.

## Executive Summary
Video-to-audio (V2A) generation models typically fail to generalize from short training clips (8–10 seconds) to long test sequences (>60 seconds) due to architectural constraints like positional embeddings in Transformers and a lack of long-duration training data. This paper introduces MMHNet, a hierarchical multimodal network that integrates a non-causal Mamba-2 state-space model (which removes the need for positional embeddings), dynamic token routing (temporal and multimodal), and compressed latent space processing. This design efficiently aligns video, text, and audio tokens while reducing redundancy and preserving global context. MMHNet demonstrates robust length generalization, generating high-quality, synchronized audio for videos up to 5 minutes long and significantly surpassing prior state-of-the-art methods on long-video benchmarks UnA V100 and LongVale across multiple fidelity and alignment metrics.

## Method Summary
MMHNet employs a hierarchical architecture that first compresses multimodal inputs (video, text, audio) into a latent token space. In this compressed space, learned routing mechanisms—temporal routing (discarding redundant consecutive audio tokens) and multimodal routing (selecting tokens with high similarity across modalities)—dynamically downsample the sequence. The core processing stack consists of non-causal Mamba-2 blocks, which provide a global receptive field without positional embeddings, interleaved with multimodal and single-modality blocks. After processing, the sequence is upsampled via a straight-through estimator (STE) to restore original resolution before final audio synthesis. The model is trained with a conditional flow matching objective in latent space, using pretrained encoders (CLIP, Synchformer) and a VAE-based audio decoder.

## Key Results
- MMHNet lowers FD-PANNs to 5.29 (vs. 7.50 for MMAudio) and IS-CPANNs rises to 8.10 (vs. 6.18) on UnA V100.
- Multimodal alignment (IB-Score) improves to 36.27 (vs. 30.71) and temporal de-sync (DeSync) drops to 0.410 (vs. 0.593).
- The model generates coherent audio for videos up to 5 minutes, where prior methods degrade significantly, demonstrating successful length generalization without training on longer sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Non-Causal Mamba-2 for Position-Embedding-Free Generalization
- **Claim:** Removing explicit positional embeddings via non-causal Mamba-2 enables robust length generalization from short training clips (8s) to long test sequences (>60s) without performance degradation.
- **Mechanism:** Replaces Transformer attention with a state-space model (SSM) where the transition matrix avoids cumulative sequential products (non-causal). This avoids modulation decay and provides a global receptive field, making fixed positional encodings unnecessary for sequence modeling.
- **Core assumption:** The SSM's structured matrix formulation implicitly captures temporal dependencies through adaptive token processing, eliminating the need for explicit position indices.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "integrating non-causal Mamba-2 (removing positional embeddings) ... maintaining global context for long sequences."
  - Section 3: Pilot study shows Transformer-based models (MMAudio) fail with longer sequences due to positional embedding constraints.
  - Section 4.3: Non-causal Mamba-2 avoids cumulative products, making it more stable for long-range dependencies.
  - Corpus: Neighbors like "LD-LAudio-V1" and "SALSA-V" target long-form V2A but do not replace positional embeddings with a non-causal SSM.
- **Break condition:** If SSM parameters (A, B, C) cannot be learned on short sequences to model long-range dependencies, global context degrades, causing quality drops in long outputs.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Token Routing for Redundancy Reduction and Alignment
- **Claim:** Hierarchical token routing (temporal and multimodal) in a compressed latent space reduces redundancy and improves cross-modal alignment for efficient long-sequence processing.
- **Mechanism:** Uses learned cosine similarity between token sets to mask and select salient tokens. Temporal routing discards homogeneous audio by keeping tokens with low similarity to previous frames; multimodal routing selects tokens with high similarity across modalities (e.g., audio-text). This chunking/downsampling occurs in early layers (compressed space), reducing computational load while preserving critical information.
- **Core assumption:** Long streams contain redundant information, and multimodal tokens can be selectively aligned based on semantic similarity, allowing a sparse subset to represent essential content.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "token routing (temporal and multimodal) and dynamic chunking in a compressed latent space ... reducing redundancy."
  - Section 4.4: "Long video and audio recordings often include a significant amount of redundant information ... we propose a hierarchical framework designed to selectively route only the most important tokens."
  - Section 5.2, Table 4: Hierarchical version outperforms non-hierarchical on UnA V100 (FD-PANNs 5.29 vs 6.31; IB-Score 36.27 vs 35.00) and LongVale.
  - Corpus: "SALSA-V" uses masked diffusion but lacks a learned token routing mechanism in compressed space.
- **Break condition:** If similarity thresholds (e.g., 0.5 for MM routing) are poorly calibrated, token selection may discard critical events or retain noise, harming alignment and coherence.

### Mechanism 3: Compressed Latent Space Processing for Multimodal Alignment
- **Claim:** Processing multimodal conditions in a compressed latent space before dechunking enables efficient alignment of tokens from different modalities with varying lengths, supporting coherent long-duration generation.
- **Mechanism:** Early layers operate on downsampled token sequences for all modalities. Routing reduces token count and aligns representations based on similarity. Later layers (after upsampling) process in the original token space for detail refinement. This two-stage hierarchy separates coarse alignment from fine synthesis.
- **Core assumption:** Coarse-grained multimodal alignment can be achieved with fewer, more informative tokens; fine-grained details can be recovered later via upsampling without losing global structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "operates in a compressed space during early layers, where multimodal alignment occurs to effectively integrate tokens from different sources and reduce redundancy."
  - Section 4.4: Describes downsampler compressing encoder outputs and direct selection of boundary-marked vectors.
  - Section 5.2, Table 4: Hierarchical method with compressed space consistently beats non-hierarchical across metrics.
  - Corpus: Limited direct evidence; "FoleyGRAM" uses aligned encoders but not explicit hierarchical compressed-space processing.
- **Break condition:** If the compressed space loses critical modality-specific details (e.g., precise audio transients), upsampling may fail to reconstruct them, leading to blurry or misaligned outputs.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Flow Matching for Generative Modeling**
  - **Why needed here:** MMHNet trains using conditional flow matching in a latent space. Understanding this continuous-time generative framework is essential to grasp how the model learns a velocity field to map noise to audio latents.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the training objective (Eq. 1) ensure the model learns to generate audio that matches the conditional distribution p(data|condition) without explicit likelihood computation?

- **Concept: State-Space Models (SSMs) and Mamba**
  - **Why needed here:** The core replacement for Transformer attention is the non-causal Mamba-2 SSM. Understanding SSM parameterization (A, B, C), discretization, and the structured mask matrix M is crucial to see why it avoids positional embeddings and handles long sequences.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key difference in the mask matrix M between causal and non-causal Mamba-2 that enables omnidirectional information flow and prevents modulation decay?

- **Concept: Multimodal Routing via Learned Similarity**
  - **Why needed here:** The hierarchical framework relies on temporal and multimodal routing layers that use cosine similarity to select tokens. This is a learned form of attention/compression that must be distinguished from standard attention mechanisms.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the token selection probability pℓ differ in what it aims to accomplish between temporal and multimodal routing, and why is cosine similarity chosen over other metrics?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input Encoders (CLIP, Synchformer, 1DConv+ConvMLP) -> Hierarchical Router (Temporal + Multimodal routing with chunking) -> Main Network (Non-Causal Mamba-2 blocks, interleaved MM/single-modal blocks) -> Dechunking/Upsampler (STE-based) -> Output Block (VAE decoder + BigVGAN).

- **Critical path:** Video/Text conditioning → Feature extraction → Chunking & Routing (compressed space) → Non-Causal Mamba-2 processing → Dechunking/Upsampling → Audio waveform. The routing step is critical: it decides which tokens proceed to the heavy SSM processing.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Compressed space vs. detail preservation: Aggressive routing reduces compute but risks losing subtle events; threshold tuning (e.g., 0.5) balances this.
  - Non-causal vs. causal Mamba: Non-causal provides global context and avoids decay but loses sequential inductive bias; causal might be better for strictly temporal tasks but fails for long-form here.
  - Routing overhead: Similarity computation adds cost; however, it's done on smaller compressed tensors, netting a speedup vs. full Transformer attention on long sequences.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1. Homogeneous/repetitive audio: Indicates temporal routing failed to discard redundant tokens or non-causal Mamba decayed.
  2. Audio-video desynchronization (high DeSync): Multimodal routing thresholds too low/high, failing to align salient events; or Synchformer features poorly injected.
  3. Degraded quality on long sequences (high FD, low IS): Compression lost critical details; or model capacity insufficient for max test duration.
  4. Training instability: Routing probabilities become near 0 or 1, starving the main network; requires gradient flow via STE and careful threshold initialization.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate core network: Replace Non-Causal Mamba-2 with Transformer (no pos. emb.) and Causal Mamba-2. Validate that Non-Causal Mamba-2 alone gives best long-sequence metrics (IB-Score, DeSync) on UnA V100/LongVale.
  2. Test routing strategies: Compare no routing, temporal-only, and temporal+MM routing. Expect temporal+MM to yield best IB-Score and DeSync due to better cross-modal alignment.
  3. Threshold sweep: Vary similarity threshold (0.3 to 0.7) for token selection. Identify optimal threshold (0.5) that balances FD-PANNs, IB-Score, and DeSync.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** Is train-short/test-long generalization fundamentally limited to a specific maximum duration (e.g., 5 minutes), or can the hierarchical non-causal Mamba-2 architecture scale to arbitrarily long video inputs without degradation?
  - *Basis:* Authors demonstrate up to 5 minutes but do not test beyond. Architectural designs like non-causal Mamba-2 theoretically support longer contexts, but empirical validation beyond current benchmarks is absent.
  - *Why unresolved:* Experiments stop at ~500 seconds (LongVale). Potential issues like state-space model memory constraints or routing threshold saturation for ultra-long sequences remain unexplored.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic evaluation on videos exceeding 10 minutes, measuring metrics across increasing durations and stress tests on memory/coherence for hour-long inputs.

- **Open Question 2:** To what extent do positional embeddings actually limit length generalization in V2A models, given that ablations adding positional embeddings to MMHNet's conditions show minimal performance change?
  - *Basis:* Paper identifies positional embeddings as a core failure mode in Transformers and avoids them via Mamba-2. However, Table A7 shows negligible difference when adding positional embeddings to *condition* tokens.
  - *Why unresolved:* Ablation tests positional embeddings only on *condition* tokens, not on the *audio token sequence itself*. The core claim targets audio sequence modeling, but the test setup may not isolate the critical path.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation reintroducing positional embeddings *within the audio generation stream* (e.g., in Mamba-2's input projections) and measuring performance decay over increasing durations. Comparative studies with other positional-free architectures would disentangle Mamba-specific effects.

- **Open Question 3:** Is the fixed token similarity threshold (0.5) in the multimodal and temporal routing mechanisms universally optimal, or does it require per-dataset or per-content adaptation to balance information retention and redundancy removal?
  - *Basis:* Authors set threshold 0.5 based on pretrained encoder expectations and validate only on UnA V100 (Table 5). No cross-dataset threshold analysis provided.
  - *Why unresolved:* Threshold sensitivity tested only on UnA V100 with a narrow range (0.3–0.7). LongVale uses same threshold without justification. Video content varies widely (static scenes vs. fast action), likely affecting token similarity distributions.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate MMHNet with threshold values learned per video or dynamically adjusted based on input statistics. Compare performance across diverse video genres to see if threshold robustness holds.

- **Open Question 4:** Does the hierarchical compressed-space processing systematically discard perceptually important audio details (e.g., transient sounds, spatial cues) critical for realism, even if global metrics improve?
  - *Basis:* Routing aims to reduce redundancy by selecting tokens with high cross-modal similarity. However, audio realism often depends on transient, low-similarity events (e.g., a door creak in a silent scene). Paper shows improved distribution matching but lacks subjective evaluation or fine-grained audio quality metrics.
  - *Why unresolved:* All metrics are classifier-based (PANNs, CLIP, Synchformer), which may not capture nuanced audio artifacts. The compressed space could smooth over brief but salient sounds. Visual examples (Fig. 4) show improved synchronization but do not analyze spectral details or conduct listening tests.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled listening studies comparing MMHNet vs. baselines on events with low video-audio similarity. Use audio-specific metrics like Mel-Cepstral Distortion for transient accuracy or spectral convergence. Ablate routing to measure its impact on such events.

- **Open Question 5:** Can the non-causal Mamba-2's omnidirectional state updates truly prevent modulation decay across very long sequences, or does decay manifest in deeper layers or with specific audio patterns (e.g., rhythmic vs. stochastic sounds)?
  - *Basis:* Authors claim non-causal Mamba-2 mitigates modulation decay (Sec. 4.3) and show activation maps for one layer (Fig. A6) where causal Mamba-2 decays after ~250 tokens. Decay analysis is limited to a single early layer.
  - *Why unresolved:* Figure visualizes only the first single-modal block. Decay could re-emerge in later layers due to compounding effects. Audio patterns with long-term dependencies might still suffer. No analysis of layer-wise decay or correlation with audio content types.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Measure activation magnitudes or state norms across all layers for sequences of varying lengths and audio types. Compare with causal Mamba-2 in a depth-controlled study. Introduce synthetic audio with controlled long-range dependencies to isolate decay effects.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity:** Routing and dynamic chunking hyperparameters (thresholds, chunk size, upsampling factor, STE gradients) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- **Compression artifact risk:** The routing mechanism may discard perceptually important transient sounds or spatial cues that are critical for realism, as no fine-grained audio analysis (e.g., transient preservation) is provided.
- **Training-test distribution gap:** Model trains only on short clips (8 s) but tests up to 5 minutes. It is unclear if true generalization occurs or if the model merely interpolates within the short-clip distribution.
- **Evaluation scope:** Benchmarking is limited to UnA V100 and LongVale; domain diversity is insufficient to claim broad robustness across video genres or acoustic environments.
- **Computational overhead:** The cost of similarity computations in routing layers is not reported; scalability to extremely long sequences (e.g., hour-long videos) remains unclear.

## Confidence
- **Non-causal Mamba-2 for length generalization:** Medium. Supported by ablations but mechanistic link to positional-embedding removal not rigorously isolated (e.g., condition token positional embeddings show minimal effect).
- **Hierarchical token routing for redundancy reduction and alignment:** High. Ablation shows large gains (e.g., IB-Score +1.27, DeSync −0.183) and clear design rationale.
- **Compressed latent space processing:** Medium. Benefits are entangled with routing; no separate ablation of compression alone isolates its contribution.

## Next Checks
1. **Backbone ablation:** Replace non-causal Mamba-2 with causal Mamba-2 and Transformer (no positional embeddings) while fixing other components. Evaluate long-sequence metrics on UnA V100 and LongVale to isolate non-causality's contribution.
2. **Routing strategy comparison:** Train three models: no routing, temporal-only routing, and full multimodal routing. Compare multimodal alignment (IB-Score) and temporal sync (DeSync) to validate each routing branch's impact.
3. **Threshold sensitivity analysis:** Vary multimodal routing cosine similarity threshold (0.3–0.7). Measure trade-offs among distribution matching (FD-PANNs), alignment (IB-Score), and sync (DeSync) to confirm robustness of the chosen 0.5 default.
