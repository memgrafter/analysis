# Long-Term Visual Localization in Dynamic Benthic Environments: A Dataset, Footprint-Based Ground Truth, and Visual Place Recognition Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04056
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04056
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a multi-year benthic dataset, a footprint-based ground truth method, and shows state-of-the-art VPR methods achieve significantly lower recall (e.g., MegaLoc 21.9% R@1) compared to terrestrial benchmarks, with distance-based GT overestimating performance in rugged terrain.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the scarcity of long-term visual localization data and inadequate ground-truthing in dynamic underwater benthic environments. The authors provide a curated dataset of AUV stereo imagery from five photic-zone sites revisited over up to six years, including calibrations and sub-decimeter registered poses. They propose a novel footprint-based method that estimates 3D seafloor footprints for each image and defines true positives based on overlapping footprints rather than simple location proximity. Benchmarking eight visual place recognition (VPR) models reveals that performance is substantially lower than on terrestrial or stable underwater benchmarks (e.g., MegaLoc achieves 21.9% Recall@1 vs. 33.7% on the Eiffel Tower). The study also demonstrates that distance-threshold ground truth inflates recall at sites with terrain relief, validating the need for content-aware evaluation.

## Method Summary
The method pipeline begins with curated AUV stereo imagery and navigation logs from five sites. Each visit undergoes independent structure-from-motion (SfM) and multi-view stereo (MVS) processing to obtain camera poses, calibrations, and dense point clouds. These per-visit reconstructions are aligned to a common reference frame via a four-stage point cloud registration (FPFH+RANSAC followed by colored ICP) achieving sub-decimeter consistency. For every camera view, a dense range map is produced by fusing stereo disparity (HitNet) and monocular depth (Depth Anything V2) via robust linear regression. The image’s seafloor footprint is then estimated by projecting image corners along rays using the fused range and converting the resulting 3D polygon to a global 2D footprint. Ground truth links between query and database views are formed if their footprint intersection-over-union exceeds a conservative threshold (τ_f ≈ 0.07). Finally, eight state-of-the-art global descriptor models are evaluated by extracting single-image descriptors, performing nearest-neighbor search with FAISS, and computing Recall@K and IRRecall@K against the footprint-based links.

## Key Results
- VPR methods exhibit significantly lower performance on benthic data than on terrestrial benchmarks; MegaLoc reaches only 21.9% Recall@1 on one site versus 33.7% on the Eiffel Tower.
- Distance-based ground truth overestimates Recall@K at sites with rugged terrain, confirming the footprint approach provides a more accurate, content-aware definition of place.
- Successful recognitions are spatially clustered around distinctive, persistent features (e.g., coral, boulders), while large areas of homogeneous sediment yield few matches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A curated multi-year, multi-site dataset with sub-decimeter registration enables systematic study of long-term VPR in dynamic benthic environments.
- **Mechanism:** The dataset provides synchronized raw/color-corrected stereo imagery, calibrations, and precisely registered poses across revisit intervals up to six years. This creates a controlled testbed where algorithmic development can be disentangled from the logistical and geometric challenges of underwater data collection.
- **Core assumption:** The geometric registration pipeline (SfM/MVS + similarity alignment via colored ICP) achieves sufficient (<0.16 m) consistency across visits to treat them as sharing a common reference frame for VPR evaluation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "curated dataset... sub-decimeter registered camera poses"
  - [section 2.3] "sub-decimeter alignment... 99th percentile error remains below 0.16 m"
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers confirm multi-year underwater datasets are exceptionally rare, supporting the novelty claim.
- **Break condition:** If temporal habitat changes (e.g., storm damage, species turnover) introduce geometric differences that exceed the sub-decimeter registration tolerance, the cross-visit ground truth links become invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Footprint-based ground truth (overlapping 3D seafloor projections) provides a more accurate and content-aware definition of "place" for near-nadir AUV imagery than location-based distance thresholds.
- **Mechanism:** For each calibrated camera view, a 3D polygon footprint is estimated by intersecting rays from image corners with a dense, fused range map. A query-database pair is a true positive only if these polygons intersect by more than a conservative IoU threshold (τ_f ≈ 0.07), ensuring shared visual content despite terrain relief and altitude variations.
- **Core assumption:** The fused range maps provide sufficiently accurate, dense, and metrically consistent depth estimates across all image borders to define footprints that reflect actual seafloor coverage.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "estimates 3D seafloor image footprints and links camera views with overlapping footprints"
  - [section 2.4.3] Equation (11) defines links via footprint IoU > τ_f; Section 3.2 quantifies the conservative threshold.
  - [section 3.4] "location-based ground-truthing can overestimate VPR Recall@K at sites with rugged terrain"
- **Break condition:** Failure of the range fusion pipeline—e.g., stereo failure in low-texture regions or monocular scale drift—leads to inaccurate footprint polygons, causing false positives/negatives in the ground truth.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Benchmarking SOTA VPR methods on this dataset reveals that single-image descriptor-based approaches suffer severe performance degradation in dynamic photic-zone benthic environments compared to terrestrial or stable hydrothermal vent settings.
- **Mechanism:** The benchmark evaluates eight global descriptor models (CNN- and ViT-based) using Recall@K, with ground truth defined by footprint overlap. The results show consistently lower recall and high spatial variability (clusters of success only over distinctive, persistent features).
- **Core assumption:** The VPR models, trained primarily on terrestrial data, lack inductive biases or representations robust to the combined challenges of underwater image degradation (color shift, backscatter) and genuine multi-year habitat changes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Recall@K is significantly lower on our dataset than on established terrestrial and underwater benchmarks"
  - [section 3.3.1] Figure 7 and Table 3 show cross-site variability; MegaLoc's best is 21.9% R@1 vs. 33.7% on Eiffel Tower.
  - [section 3.3.2] Spatial clustering of successful recognitions over coral/boulder features.
- **Break condition:** If VPR models are specifically retrained on this dataset's domain, the reported performance gap may close, altering the conclusion about inherent difficulty.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS)**: Needed to generate the initial, self-consistent 3D reconstruction and camera poses for each visit, which are then aligned across visits via similarity transforms.  
  *Quick check:* What specific failure modes in SfM (e.g., low texture, dynamic content) would necessitate the local similarity transform correction described in Section 2.3?

- **Pinhole Camera Model and Inverse Projection**: Forms the mathematical basis for mapping pixel coordinates (image corners) to 3D rays, which are then intersected with range data to estimate the seafloor footprint polygon.  
  *Quick check:* If the camera intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy) are miscalibrated, how would that error propagate to the footprint estimation and subsequent IoU calculation?

- **Information Retrieval Metrics vs. VPR-Specific Recall**: Needed to correctly interpret benchmark results. VPR's Recall@K (a query is "recognized" if *any* top-K retrieved image is correct) can be inflated by permissive ground truth. IRRecall@K (classical recall over all relevant query-db pairs) penalizes false negatives and exposes discrepancies between ground truth definitions.  
  *Quick check:* Why does a location-based ground truth (large distance threshold) increase R@10 but decrease IR@10 compared to the footprint-based ground truth?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data Ingestion → Preprocessing (color correction) → Single-Visit Reconstruction (SfM/MVS) → Multi-Visit Registration → Range Map Generation → Footprint Estimation → Ground Truth Linkage → VPR Benchmark (descriptor extraction, retrieval, metrics)

- **Critical path**: Raw images → Color correction → SfM/MVS per visit → Visit registration → Range map fusion → Footprint generation → Ground truth links → Descriptor extraction → Retrieval → Metrics.  
  *Key dependency*: Accurate camera calibrations and poses from steps 3-4 are essential for valid footprint estimation (step 6).

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Footprint IoU threshold (τ_f): Higher value increases recall *opportunity* but risks false positives from registration errors; τ_f=0.07 is deliberately conservative.  
  - Range fusion: Stereo provides metric accuracy but has holes; monocular provides completeness but is scale-ambiguous. Fusion trades local detail for global consistency.  
  - Geometric registration: Stage 1 (FPFH) is robust but coarse; subsequent ICP stages refine but can get stuck in local minima if initial alignment is poor.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - **High registration error** (99th percentile >0.16m): Indicates structural changes or missing overlap, invalidating cross-visit comparisons.  
  - **Sparse/fragmented footprints**: Fusion failure leads to incomplete polygons; IoU calculations become unreliable.  
  - **Extreme cross-site performance variance**: Suggests dataset diversity exposes model weaknesses or that τ_f may need per-site tuning.  
  - **Large gap between R@K and IR@K**: Signals that the ground truth definition is overly permissive (location-based) or overly strict (footprint-based with high τ_f).

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Validate geometric registration**: Compute point cloud correspondence error (Equation 1) across all visit pairs. Confirm 99th percentile < 0.16m. If not, debug registration pipeline parameters (voxel sizes, ICP thresholds).  
  2. **Compare ground truth strategies**: For a single visit pair, compute both footprint-based links (IoU>τ_f) and location-based links (distance < 95th pctile of footprint-link distances). Visualize discrepancies to understand where terrain/altitude breaks the distance assumption.  
  3. **Baseline VPR benchmark**: Run a single model (e.g., NetVLAD) on one visit pair. Plot Recall@K curve. Manually inspect top-5 retrievals for recognized vs. unrecognized queries to correlate success with seafloor texture and trajectory overlap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can image footprint estimation be refined to provide more accurate and robust ground-truth for visual localization in benthic environments?** Future work could refine the footprint estimates by sampling more image points or using more advanced range map estimation methods.
- **How can sub-map or cluster-based map representations improve the efficiency, accuracy, and robustness of long-term visual place recognition in dynamic benthic environments?** The authors propose to exploit clustered successful recognitions by exploring map representations that decompose habitats into sub-maps or place representations formed from spatially and visually coherent image clusters.
- **How effective is multi-image contextual retrieval using trajectory segments for improving long-term visual localization compared to single-image VPR?** Using information from multiple query images along trajectory segments could increase spatial context and reduce reliance on high overlap between individual query–database image pairs.
- **To what extent can integrating dead-reckoning odometry with visual place recognition reduce false positives and improve overall localization reliability for underwater vehicles?** Accurate short-term odometry from dead-reckoning sensors can provide relative pose estimates to geometrically reject or verify VPR candidates.

## Limitations
- **Registration pipeline parameters**: Exact FPFH/ICP hyperparameters (radii, thresholds, iterations) are unreported, risking alignment variability across sites.
- **VPR model implementation details**: Backbones, input resolutions, normalization, and descriptor post-processing are unspecified, potentially invalidating performance comparisons.
- **Dataset accessibility**: The curated dataset’s exact download location and file formats are ambiguous; reliance on the IMOS portal may require additional undocumented processing.
- **Footprint threshold tuning**: The fixed τ_f=0.07 may be suboptimal per site; derivation parameters (FOV, altitude) lack site-specific justification.
