# Escaping the BLEU Trap: A Signal-Grounded Framework with Decoupled Semantic Guidance for EEG-to-Text Decoding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03312
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03312
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Achieves SOTA on ZuCo with +36.4% 24-way retrieval accuracy and +54.4% lower Fréchet Distance vs. GLIM, while eliminating BLEU-trap hallucination by producing gibberish on noise input.

## Executive Summary
EEG-to-text decoding suffers from three critical failures: semantic bias (repetitive templates), signal neglect (hallucination from linguistic priors), and the BLEU trap (metric inflation via stopword overlap). This paper introduces SemKey, a two-stage framework that first decouples high-level semantic attributes (sentiment, topic, length, surprisal) from the EEG encoder’s representation, then forces a frozen LLM decoder to actively retrieve neural information via a Q-K-V injection mechanism. By replacing BLEU with N-way Retrieval Accuracy and Fréchet Distance, evaluation shifts from superficial fluency to true signal-grounding, yielding significant gains in semantic fidelity and diversity.

## Method Summary
SemKey operates in two stages. **Stage 1** trains a Prior-Guided Conformer encoder on raw EEG with parallel multi-task heads predicting decoupled semantic attributes (sentiment, topic, length, surprisal), alongside alignment and reconstruction losses. This forces the EEG embedding (`v_eeg`) to encode disentangled semantic properties. **Stage 2** freezes these heads and the LLM decoder (Flan-T5-Large). The EEG sequence embeddings (`E_eeg`) are projected and served as Key-Value banks, while a multi-perspective prompt derived from the predicted attributes acts as the Query. Cross-attention is structurally constrained so the LLM must attend to neural signal (`E_eeg`) to generate text, preventing reliance on internal priors. Multiple Text Variants (MTV) augment training targets to improve diversity.

## Key Results
- On ZuCo, SemKey achieves **+36.4% higher 24-way retrieval accuracy** and **+54.4% lower Fréchet Distance** compared to GLIM, while Dist-2 (diversity) improves by **+179.0%**.
- Despite lower BLEU (21.3% vs. GLIM’s 26.3% with MTV), SemKey shows drastically better semantic alignment, exposing the BLEU trap.
- With Gaussian noise input, SemKey generates incoherent text (Dist-2→99.6%), while baselines produce fluent but meaningless output, confirming strict signal dependency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Semantic Attribute Extraction
- **Claim:** Parallel attribute prediction (sentiment, topic, length, surprisal) forces the EEG encoder to produce semantically disentangled global embeddings, mitigating semantic bias and mode collapse.
- **Mechanism:** Auxiliary classification/regression heads attached to the encoder’s global embedding are trained concurrently with alignment and reconstruction losses. This multi-task setup prevents the encoder from collapsing into a generic latent space by requiring it to encode specific, orthogonal high-level properties.
- **Core assumption:** These attributes form robust, decoupled representations stably extractable from EEG and can serve as effective "semantic anchors" for decoding.
- **Evidence:** [section 3.1] Heads force encoder to encode specific properties; [section 4.4, Table 4] attribute accuracy is non-chance (Topic 83.5%, Sentiment 90.7%).
- **Break condition:** If the EEG signal is too noisy or training corpus lacks diversity for these attributes, head performance degrades, leading to noisy guidance and potentially increased bias.

### Mechanism 2: Q-K-V Injection Active Retrieval
- **Claim:** Structuring cross-attention with semantic prompts as Queries and EEG embeddings as Key-Value pairs forces the LLM decoder to actively retrieve from neural signals, preventing hallucination.
- **Mechanism:** The decoder’s Queries come from the semantic prompt and generated token history; Keys/Values are strictly from the projected EEG sequence and a static vector. This makes next-token prediction dependent on similarity between linguistic context (Query) and neural content (Key), with output informed by EEG-derived Values.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM’s strong priors can be overridden by a hard architectural constraint removing self-attention over pure text tokens as sole K/V source.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "strictly forcing the model to attend to neural inputs"; [section 4.2, Table 3 & Figure 4] On noise, SemKey’s performance collapses (Dist-2→99.6%, FD↑1.27) while baselines hallucinate fluently.
- **Break condition:** If projected EEG embeddings lose discriminative information during training, the K/V bank becomes uninformative, and the LLM may still produce generic text by attending to the least noisy parts of the now-useless K/V.

### Mechanism 3: Escape from the BLEU Trap via New Metrics
- **Claim:** N-way Retrieval Accuracy and Fréchet Distance measure true semantic alignment and distributional fidelity, unlike BLEU which rewards template repetition and stopword overlap.
- **Mechanism:** N-way retrieval tests if EEG embedding can identify correct text among distractors based on semantic similarity. Fréchet Distance compares distributions in a semantic embedding space. Together, they penalize models generating safe, high-frequency templates that score well on BLEU but fail on unique semantics.
- **Core assumption:** A true EEG decoder will produce outputs whose semantic distribution matches the reference manifold and can be distinguished from random text, whereas a prior-based model yields a narrow, template-heavy distribution.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] SOTA on retrieval/FD despite lower BLEU; [section 4.1, Table 1 & 2] SemKey’s lower BLEU (21.3% vs GLIM 26.3% MTV) but far superior retrieval (+5.2% 24-way) and diversity (Dist-2 22.6% vs 8.1%), while GLIM shows high Self-BLEU (90.8%).
- **Break condition:** If evaluation metrics are misaligned (e.g., retrieval reference set too easy, embedding space poorly aligned), they may not correlate with true fidelity. The divergence between BLEU and other metrics, plus noise-input results,provide evidence against this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-Task Learning & Loss Balancing**  
  Why needed: The framework simultaneously optimizes alignment, reconstruction, and attribute prediction. Understanding weighted loss sums and potential conflicts (e.g., reconstruction vs. disentanglement) is key for stability.  
  Quick check: If attribute head losses dominate total loss, what happens to reconstruction/alignment objectives, and how would it manifest in N-way retrieval scores?

- **Concept: Cross-Attention as Information Retrieval**  
  Why needed: The Q-K-V injection re-frames cross-attention from conditioning to explicit retrieval. Engineers must grasp standard Transformer attention to see why swapping K/V source from decoder history to external signal is a hard constraint.  
  Quick check: In a standard Transformer decoder, where do Key/Value vectors come from during cross-attention? How does SemKey change this source?

- **Concept: Metric Design for "Faithful" vs. "Fluent" Generation**  
  Why needed: The paper argues BLEU rewards fluency/templates, not signal grounding. Understanding why Fréchet Distance (distributional) and N-way retrieval (alignment) are proposed alternatives, and what failure modes they target, is essential.  
  Quick check: Why would a model always generating "The movie was..." achieve high BLEU but near-zero N-way retrieval accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** EEG signal -> Spectral Whitening -> Prior-Guided Conformer Encoder -> `E_eeg` (sequence) & `v_eeg` (global) -> [Stage 1] Parallel attribute heads (Sentiment, Topic, Length, Surprisal) + Aligner -> [Freeze heads] -> Trainable Projector -> Multi-perspective prompt (from predicted attributes) -> Frozen Flan-T5-Large (Q-K-V Injection) -> Output text.
- **Critical path:** Trainable EEG Encoder -> Projector -> Frozen LLM Decoder (with modified cross-attention). Gradients from final cross-entropy loss bypass the LLM to update encoder/projector.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **LLM Frozen vs. Fine-tuned:** Fully frozen LLM works best; fine-tuning risks overfitting tiny EEG corpus and destroying LM priors.  
  - **MTV Augmentation:** Improves diversity but risks introducing template bias if paraphrases are homogeneous.  
  - **Attribute Head Timing:** Heads are frozen after Stage 1, preventing adaptation to generative objective that might cause overfitting.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Semantic bias collapse:** Repetitive outputs (e.g., "He was..."). Diagnose: Low Dist-2, high Self-BLEU.  
  - **Signal neglect/hallucination:** Good BLEU on noise input. Diagnose: Replace EEG with Gaussian noise; if fluency persists, model ignores signal.  
  - **BLEU trap:** High BLEU but poor retrieval/FD. Diagnose: Disconnect between n-gram and distributional metrics.  
  - **Training instability:** Multi-task loss conflicts (e.g., regression dominating). Monitor individual losses.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Verify Stage 1 attribute extraction:** Train only Stage 1. Report attribute head accuracy (Topic, Sentiment) and regression MAE (Length, Surprisal) on held-out set. Must significantly exceed chance (Table 4).  
  2. **Test Q-K-V injection causality:** After full training, run inference with Gaussian noise. Output should be semantically incoherent (near-zero Content Recall, FD >1.0). If BLEU >10%, constraint is leaky.  
  3. **Isolate MTV & prompt impact:** Ablate: (a) without MTV, (b) without prompt (empty). Expect major drops in 24-way retrieval and Dist-2 (Table 5). Confirms both components are necessary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can EEG-to-text models be improved to accurately decode low-frequency proper nouns and other fine-grained semantic details that currently remain challenging?
- **Open Question 2:** What architectural or training innovations can enable robust cross-subject EEG-to-text decoding without requiring impractically large amounts of per-subject training data?
- **Open Question 3:** Do the proposed N-way Retrieval Accuracy and Fréchet Distance metrics fully capture semantic fidelity in EEG-to-text generation, and how well do they correlate with human judgments of output quality?
- **Open Question 4:** How can data augmentation for EEG-to-text be designed to increase linguistic diversity without introducing template biases that artificially inflate evaluation scores, as seen with the Multiple Text Variants (MTV) strategy?

## Limitations
- **Spectral whitening specifics:** Exact preprocessing parameters (window size, normalization) are unspecified and performance-sensitive (+8.64% 24-way accuracy difference reported).
- **MTV generation protocol:** Paraphrasing algorithm for creating Multiple Text Variants is not detailed; template bias in augmentations may reinforce repetition.
- **Prompt engineering:** Exact template structure, attribute ordering, and tokenization are assumed but not validated; variations could affect guidance.

## Confidence
- **Decoupled Semantic Attributes (Mechanism 1):** High confidence. Supported by non-chance attribute accuracy (Table 4) and ablation showing dramatic drop when removed (Table 5).
- **Q-K-V Injection / Active Retrieval (Mechanism 2):** Medium confidence. Noise-input ablation strongly suggests signal dependency, but the "hard constraint" claim is inferred; gradient flow and K/V corruption stress-tests are not detailed.
- **Evaluation & BLEU Trap (Mechanism 3):** High confidence. Consistent divergence between BLEU and retrieval/FD across datasets, plus noise test, demonstrates the trap.

## Next Checks
1. **Attribute head sanity check:** Train Stage 1 in isolation. Verify that Topic accuracy exceeds 65% (6 classes) and Sentiment exceeds 80% on held-out set.
2. **Signal causality stress test:** Replace EEG with Gaussian noise at inference. Confirm Content Recall near zero and Fréchet Distance >1.0; if BLEU remains >10%, the Q-K-V constraint is leaky.
3. **Guidance component ablation:** Systematically remove (a) MTV augmentation, (b) multi-attribute prompt. Expect combined drop >15% in 24-way retrieval accuracy compared to full model.
