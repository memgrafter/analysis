# Multi-Level Causal Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22287  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22287  
- **Reference count:** 32  
- **Primary result:** Consistent α-embeddings enable merging heterogeneous causal models at different resolutions, solving the multi-resolution causal marginal problem and improving joint distribution estimation in simulations.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the integration of multiple causal models that operate at varying levels of detail—a frequent challenge in complex systems such as ecosystems. It introduces “causal embeddings,” a generalization of causal abstractions, which allow several detailed models to be mapped into subsystems of a coarser causal model via non-surjective α-embeddings. Graphical consistency is enforced through mediated adjacencies and confounders, and functional consistency is measured by Li-error. Theoretically, the authors prove that a set of consistent embeddings yields a solution to the multi-resolution causal marginal problem, bridging statistical and causal integration across heterogeneous resolutions. In a simulated ecosystem case study, merging two marginal datasets (2000 and 4000 samples) using the proposed embeddings dramatically improved joint distribution estimation, reducing KL divergence from 0.77 and 0.34 to 0.22 relative to ground truth. Moreover, the approach enabled the estimation of a cross-resolution distribution (Predators, Humans) that was unattainable from either marginal model alone.

## Method Summary
The method begins by specifying a high-level structural causal model (SCM) \(M^*\) over a variable set \(V^*\). For each low-level model \(M_i\), an α-embedding \((\varphi_i, \alpha_i)\) is defined, where \(\varphi_i\) maps a relevant subset \(R_i \subseteq V^{M_i}\) to a subset \(S \subseteq V^*\), and \(\alpha_i\) provides surjective range mappings for each target variable. Graphical consistency requires that for any two variables in \(R_i\), a mediated directed path or confounding exists in \(M_i\) if and only if the corresponding mapped variables have such a relationship in \(M^*\) over \(S\) (checked via the Cluster DAG condition). Functional consistency (zero Li-error) demands that for any intervention on \(S\), the distribution induced by \(M^*\) matches the distribution from \(M_i\) after applying the embedding mappings. When all embeddings are consistent, \(M^*\) is synthesized by combining the functions and distributions from the marginal models in a manner that respects the mappings. For data fusion, embeddings transform each dataset to the \(V^*\) space, after which missing values are imputed and quantities of interest are estimated under the joint model.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical solution:** A set of Li-consistent α-embeddings provides a solution to the multi-resolution causal marginal problem (Theorem 5).
- **Empirical improvement:** In a simulated ecosystem, merging two marginal datasets via embeddings reduced KL divergence for joint distribution estimation from 0.77/0.34 to 0.22 versus ground truth.
- **Cross-resolution inference:** Enabled estimation of the distribution \(P(\text{Predators}, \text{Humans})\), which was impossible from either marginal model alone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Multiple detailed causal models can be mapped into subsystems of a single coarser model via non-surjective α-embeddings.
- **Mechanism**: The paper generalizes causal abstraction by defining an α-embedding as a non-surjective mapping φ from relevant variables R in detailed models to a subset S of variables in a high-level model, coupled with surjective range mappings α_V'. This allows several low-level models to cover different parts of the high-level model.
- **Core assumption**: Causal relations (mediated adjacencies and confounders) between relevant variables are preserved under the mapping.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Mentions mapping multiple detailed models into subsystems of a coarse model and non-surjective α-embeddings.
  - [section 4, Def.10–11] Formalizes non-surjective α-abstraction and defines α-embedding with projection and CDAG conditions.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows strong related work on causal abstractions (FMR~0.55), but direct evidence for *multi-model* embeddings is primarily from this paper; neighbors focus on pairwise abstractions.
- **Break condition**: If the projection of the high-level graph over S is not a Cluster DAG (CDAG) of the projection of a low-level graph over R, the graphical consistency fails, invalidating the embedding.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Graphical consistency for embeddings is enforced via mediated adjacencies and confounders, ensuring causal pathways align across resolutions.
- **Mechanism**: An α-embedding requires that a mediated directed path (X ⇝ Y) or confounding (X ⥽ Y) exists in the high-level model (over S) if and only if it exists in the low-level model (over R). This check bypasses non-relevant variables.
- **Core assumption**: Preservation of *mediated* causal structure (paths/confounders not involving intermediate relevant variables) suffices for consistency across resolutions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4, Def.12–14] Defines mediated adjacencies/confounders and provides the alternative graphical consistency condition for α-embeddings.
  - [section 4, Lemma 1] Proves equivalence between projection-based (Def.11) and mediated-edge-based (Def.14) definitions.
  - [corpus] Neighbor "Aligning Graphical and Functional Causal Abstractions" (arxiv:2412.17080) studies graphical vs. functional consistency, supporting the importance of graphical conditions.
- **Break condition**: If a mediated adjacency or confounder present in the low-level model over R is absent in the high-level model over S (or vice versa), graphical L2-consistency fails, and the embedding is invalid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A set of Li-consistent α-embeddings provides a solution to the multi-resolution causal marginal problem, bridging statistical and causal integration across heterogeneous models.
- **Mechanism**: The multi-resolution marginal problem seeks a joint SCM M* over a shared variable set V* from multiple marginal models with different resolutions. Consistent embeddings φ_i: V^{M_i} → V* and functional consistency (Li-error = 0) ensure that M* respects all marginal models' causal and distributional properties.
- **Core assumption**: Consistency (zero Li-error) of all embeddings guarantees that the joint model M* generates interventional/observational distributions consistent with each marginal model after mapping.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 5, Theorem 5] States that consistent embeddings (Li-consistent, R = V^{M_i}) yield a solution M*.
  - [section 5, Lemma 6] Shows embeddings reduce the multi-resolution problem to a single-resolution one.
  - [section 6, Examples 5–6] Demonstrates improved joint distribution estimation (KL reduction) and estimation of previously unattainable distributions after merging datasets via embeddings.
  - [corpus] Neighbor "Causal inference through the structural causal marginal problem" (Gresele et al., cited) introduces the single-resolution causal marginal problem; this paper extends it to multi-resolution.
- **Break condition**: If any embedding has non-zero Li-error, the joint model M* may not reproduce the marginal models' interventional/observational distributions, violating consistency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Causal Models (SCMs)**  
  *Why needed here*: The entire formalism operates on SCMs (endogenous/exogenous variables, functions, distributions). Understanding SCMs, interventions (do-operator), and Pearl's Causal Hierarchy (L1–L3) is essential to grasp what consistency means.  
  *Quick check question*: What is the difference between observational (L1) and interventional (L2) distributions in an SCM, and how does an intervention modify the graph?
- **Concept: Causal Abstraction (α-framework)**  
  *Why needed here*: Embeddings generalize abstractions. The α-abstraction (surjective φ, surjective α_V') is the starting point; embeddings drop surjectivity of φ. Knowing abstraction's error measure (Li-error) and CDAGs is prerequisite.  
  *Quick check question*: In an α-abstraction, why are both φ and α_V' required to be surjective, and what does surjectivity prevent?
- **Concept: The (Causal) Marginal Problem**  
  *Why needed here*: The paper's application is a multi-resolution version of this problem. Understanding the statistical marginal problem (combining overlapping marginals) and its causal extension (finding joint SCMs) is key to appreciating the paper's contribution.  
  *Quick check question*: Why can't the standard causal marginal problem (Def.8) handle cases where overlapping variables have different resolutions or representations across models?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Model input module -> Embedding specification module -> Graphical consistency checker -> Functional consistency estimator -> Marginal problem solver -> Data merging utility

Critical path:
1. Define high-level variable set V* (or let S from embeddings define it).
2. For each low-level model M_i, specify embedding α_i (φ_i, α_V') with R_i ⊆ V^{M_i}.
3. **Verify graphical consistency** (Def.14) for all α_i—this is a hard requirement.
4. **Estimate functional consistency** (Li-error) for each α_i; if Li-error > tolerance, adjust mappings or functions.
5. If all consistent, synthesize M* by ensuring for each V* ∈ V*, its distribution under interventions matches all α_i-mapped marginals.
6. For data merging: apply embeddings to datasets, align on V*, impute missing values, estimate quantities of interest.

Design tradeoffs:
- *Non-surjective φ vs. surjective*: Non-surjectivity allows multiple models to embed into different subsystems of V*, but risks underconstraining M* if relevant variables are missing from all R_i. Surjective φ (abstraction case) fully covers V* with one model but cannot combine multiple models.
- *Graphical vs. functional consistency*: Graphical L2-consistency (Def.11/14) is necessary but not sufficient for functional L2-consistency (Theorem 4). One can have graphically consistent embeddings with non-zero Li-error. Prioritize graphical checks first, then functional validation.
- *Resolution of V*: Finer resolution (more variables in V*) increases model complexity but may improve consistency; coarser resolution simplifies but may lose information. Choose V* to balance coverage of R_i and parsimony.

Failure signatures:
- **Graphical inconsistency**: Mediated adjacency X ⇝ Y in low-level over R is absent in high-level over S (or vice versa). Check Def.14 manually or via CDAG projection mismatch.
- **Functional inconsistency**: Non-zero Li-error (Eq.3). Compute via sampling from models: for many X',Y' ⊆ S, compare P_{M'}(Y'|α_X'[L_i(φ^{-1}(X'))]) vs α_{Y'}(P_M(φ^{-1}(Y')|L_i(φ^{-1}(X'))). Large divergence indicates mismatch.
- **Underdetermined M***: If relevant variable sets R_i do not collectively cover all parents of V* variables in any consistent way, M* may not be uniquely specified. Ensure each causal mechanism in M* is constrained by at least one embedding (Remark 1).
- **Imputation failure** (data merging): After embedding, missingness pattern may be too sparse; KNN or other imputation may produce biased estimates if MCAR/MAR assumptions violate the causal structure.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Toy graphical consistency test**: Construct two low-level models with known mediated edges (e.g., X→Z→Y with Z ∉ R). Define α with R={X,Y}. Verify mediated adjacency X ⇝ Y exists in low-level (via Z) and check high-level has direct X→Y edge per Def.14. Should pass.
2. **Ecosystem dataset merging (Example 5–6)**: Replicate simulated data generation (App.C.2). Apply embeddings φ_i (sum for deer/predators) and merge 2000+4000 samples. Estimate ˆP(Deer, Squirrels) and compare KL to ground truth; expect improvement over marginals. Also estimate ˆP(Humans,Predators), which is impossible from marginals alone.
3. **Consistency vs. non-consistency**: Deliberately break an embedding (e.g., remove confounding edge in high-level). Attempt to solve multi-resolution marginal problem; observe that inferred M* will have non-zero Li-error and fail to reproduce marginal interventional distributions (e.g., do(Deer) effects mismatch).

## Limitations
- The framework requires manually specified embeddings (φ, α) and an appropriate high-level model; no automatic discovery method is proposed.
- Graphical consistency conditions (mediated adjacencies/confounders) may not guarantee functional consistency in high-dimensional or heavily confounded settings.
- Empirical validation is restricted to a single simulated ecosystem; real-world data could violate SCM assumptions and challenge scalability.

## Confidence
- **Formalism (α-embeddings, consistency conditions):** High  
- **Theoretical solution to the multi-resolution causal marginal problem:** High  
- **Practical utility (improved joint distribution estimation):** Medium
