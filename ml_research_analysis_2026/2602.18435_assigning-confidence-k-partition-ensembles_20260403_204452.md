# Assigning Confidence: K-partition Ensembles
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18435
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18435
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CAKE assigns per-point confidence scores by combining assignment stability and geometric consistency from clustering ensembles; removing low-confidence points improves ARI, AMI, and ACC.

## Executive Summary
Clustering algorithms typically lack per-point confidence estimates, making it difficult to judge the reliability of individual assignments. CAKE (Confidence from k-partition Ensembles) addresses this by fusing two ensemble-derived statistics: (1) assignment stability, measuring label agreement across runs after alignment, and (2) geometric consistency, aggregating Silhouette scores (mean minus standard deviation). The resulting score in [0,1] ranks points by assignment reliability. Experimental results on synthetic and real datasets show that discarding low-confidence points before reclustering significantly improves external validation metrics (ARI, AMI, ACC) compared to using all points. CAKE also outperforms entropy-based agreement and bootstrap stability in distinguishing correctly assigned instances (higher AUROC/AUPRC) and yields monotonic coverage-accuracy curves. The method is model-agnostic, computationally efficient with a centroid-based Silhouette approximation, and requires a moderate ensemble size (R≈30-40) for stable scores. Theoretical analysis further supports separation of stable points from noise.

## Method Summary
CAKE operates on a dataset and a base clustering algorithm (e.g., k-means). It first generates an ensemble of R clusterings using varied random seeds or parameters. For each run, it obtains cluster labels and computes Silhouette scores (either exact or using a centroid-based approximation for efficiency). To compare partitions, pairwise label alignments are performed via the Hungarian algorithm on contingency matrices, ensuring label correspondence across runs. Two per-point statistics are then aggregated: (i) stability \(c_i\) is the fraction of aligned run pairs where point i receives the same label; (ii) geometric consistency \(\tilde{S}_i = (\mu_i - \sigma_i)_+\) where \(\mu_i\) and \(\sigma_i\) are the mean and standard deviation of the Silhouette scores across runs. The final CAKE score combines these via product (PR) or harmonic mean (HM). Optionally, points with the lowest scores are filtered out, and the base clustering is reapplied to the retained subset. For non-convex cluster shapes, a kernelized Silhouette variant is available. Theoretical results provide conditions under which stable points are separable from ambiguous ones.

## Key Results
- Removing low-confidence points (e.g., the bottom 30%) improves clustering quality (ARI, AMI, ACC) compared to the full-data baseline.
- CAKE achieves higher AUROC and AUPRC than entropy-based agreement and bootstrap stability in identifying correctly assigned points.
- Coverage-accuracy curves are monotonic, and per-point scores stabilize with an ensemble size of approximately 30-40 runs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Assignment Stability via Aligned Pairwise Agreement
Claim: CAKE estimates per-point assignment stability by measuring how consistently a point receives the same cluster label across ensemble runs after label alignment.
Mechanism: For each unordered pair of clustering runs, compute a contingency matrix and find the optimal label permutation (Hungarian algorithm) to align one partition to the other. Then, for each point, the agreement indicator is 1 if the aligned labels match. The stability score \(c_i\) is the fraction of pairs where the point agrees.
Core assumption: The ensemble captures assignment variability due to initialization; alignment is necessary because cluster indices are arbitrary.
Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "assignment stability (pairwise agreement across runs after label alignment)"
  - [section 3.3]: Full derivation, including contingency matrix, Hungarian algorithm, and U-statistic interpretation.
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighboring papers on clustering ensembles (e.g., "Efficient Multi-View Graph Clustering...") focus on consensus partitions, not per-point stability.
Break condition: If the ensemble is heterogeneous (different algorithms or \(k\)), alignment may be ill-defined or stability may conflate different cluster structures.

### Mechanism 2: Geometric Consistency via Silhouette Aggregation
Claim: CAKE measures local geometric support by aggregating Silhouette scores across runs, rewarding points with high average fit and low variability.
Mechanism: For each point, compute mean \(\mu_i\) and standard deviation \(\sigma_i\) of its Silhouette scores across runs. The geometric component is \(\tilde{S}_i = (\mu_i - \sigma_i)_+\) (clipped to non-negative). This captures both quality and consistency of local cluster fit.
Core assumption: Silhouette is a valid measure of cluster membership; consistency across runs indicates robust geometric placement.
Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "geometric consistency (mean minus std of Silhouette scores)"
  - [section 3.2]: Defines Silhouette, aggregation, and centroid-based approximation; extends to kernelized Silhouette for non-convex data.
  - [corpus]: Silhouette is a standard internal validation metric (Rousseeuw, 1987). CAKE's cross-run aggregation is novel; corpus does not address this.
Break condition: Silhouette favors convex, isotropic clusters; for non-convex structures, Euclidean distances may mislead unless a suitable kernel is chosen.

### Mechanism 3: Score Fusion and Filtering for Improved Clustering
Claim: Fusing stability and geometric scores via product or harmonic mean yields a reliability ranking that, when used to filter low-confidence points before reclustering, improves global clustering quality.
Mechanism: CAKE\(_i = c_i \cdot \tilde{S}_i\) (product) or harmonic mean. Points are ranked; removing the lowest-scoring fraction (e.g., 30%) reduces ambiguous points. Reclustering on the retained set yields higher ARI/AMI/ACC than using all points.
Core assumption: Points with both high stability and high geometric consistency are reliably assigned; ambiguous points degrade partition quality.
Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "These are fused into a [0,1] score ... removing low-confidence points improves clustering quality ... coverage-accuracy curves are monotonic."
  - [section 5.2]: Results across synthetic/real datasets (Tables 2,6; Figures 7,8,9) show CAKE outperforms entropy agreement and bootstrap stability baselines.
  - [corpus]: Related work on ensemble uncertainty in supervised learning (e.g., "Confidence Before Answering...") uses disagreement; CAKE adapts to clustering with a geometric signal. Corpus lacks clustering-specific confidence fusion.
Break condition: The fusion rule may unbalance signals; optimal filtering threshold is dataset-dependent. CAKE is robust but may not always dominate a single-component signal if one aspect is highly informative.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Clustering Ensemble and Label Alignment  
  Why needed here: CAKE aggregates multiple clustering partitions; alignment (Hungarian matching) is required because cluster labels are arbitrary per run.  
  Quick check question: Given two clusterings with labels \(\{1,2,3\}\) and \(\{A,B,C\}\), how do you determine which labels correspond? What algorithm is used?
- **Concept**: Silhouette Score  
  Why needed here: The geometric component aggregates Silhouette scores to assess local cluster fit. Understanding its computation and interpretation is essential.  
  Quick check question: For point \(x_i\), what are \(a_i\) (intra-cluster distance) and \(b_i\) (nearest-cluster distance) in the Silhouette formula \(s_i = (b_i - a_i)/\max(a_i,b_i)\)? What does \(s_i = 0.8\) indicate?
- **Concept**: Ensemble Variability as Uncertainty Proxy  
  Why needed here: In clustering, ground-truth confidence is unavailable; CAKE leverages that consistent assignments and fit across runs indicate reliability, analogous to ensemble disagreement in supervised learning.  
  Quick check question: If a point switches clusters across k-means runs, what does that suggest about its assignment confidence? How does CAKE capture this?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Ensemble Generator -> Silhouette Calculator -> (Label Aligner -> Stability Aggregator; Geometric Aggregator) -> Score Fuser -> Filtering & Reclustering (optional)

Critical path:
1. Ensemble generation (parallelizable).
2. Silhouette computation per run (parallelizable).
3. Pairwise label alignment (independent pairs can parallelize; each Hungarian O(k^3); often bottleneck for large R).
4. Aggregation (stability: O(R^2 n); geometric: O(nR)).
5. Fusion (O(n)).

Design tradeoffs:
- Exact vs. approximate Silhouette: exact accurate but O(n^2); centroid-based O(nkd) for centroidal clusterers.
- Homogeneous vs. heterogeneous ensemble: same algorithm vs. mixing algorithms; heterogeneous alignment may be noisier.
- Ensemble size R: larger R stabilizes scores but increases compute; R≈30-40 sufficient.
- Fusion rule: product stricter; harmonic mean smoother; both perform similarly empirically.
- Kernelized Silhouette: necessary for non-convex data; requires choosing kernel parameters (e.g., RBF bandwidth).

Failure signatures:
- All CAKE scores near 0 or 1: ensemble lacks diversity or all points uniformly stable/unstable; check pairwise agreement distribution.
- Negative correlation between CAKE and accuracy: signals inverted or pathological base clustering; inspect component scores.
- Many points with μ_i - σ_i < 0: after clipping, many get 0; consider affine scaling or check if Silhouette scores are mostly negative.
- Hungarian alignment instability: ties in contingency matrix cause arbitrary permutations; inspect matrix sparsity or add tie-breaking.
- Runtime explosion: exact Silhouette infeasible for large n; use centroid-based. Pairwise alignment O(R^2 k^3) may dominate for large R.

First 3 experiments:
1. Sanity check: On synthetic S1 (k=7, clear clusters), run CAKE with R=20. Visualize points colored by CAKE; expect high scores within clusters, lower at boundaries. Compute coverage-accuracy curve—should be monotonic increasing.
2. Convergence test: Vary R from 5 to 70 on Iris. Compute CAKE for each R and measure per-point standard deviation across sub-ensembles (like Fig. 6). Confirm stabilization by R≈30-40.
3. Baseline comparison: On noisy S3 with outliers, compute CAKE, entropy agreement, bootstrap stability. Evaluate AUROC/AUPRC for predicting consensus correctness. Expect CAKE to outperform.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Calibration**: How can CAKE confidence scores be calibrated to reflect true assignment error probabilities when only a small amount of labeled data is available?
2. **k selection**: Can the distribution of CAKE scores across a clustering ensemble reliably guide the selection of the number of clusters k?
3. **Multi-k ensembles**: How should CAKE be extended to ensembles where different runs use different numbers of clusters (varying k), and can such an extension improve robustness to misspecification?
4. **Semi-supervised integration**: Can CAKE effectively integrate into semi-supervised or active learning pipelines by using confidence to weight pseudo-labels or query points, and does this improve sample efficiency?
5. **Kernel sensitivity**: How sensitive is CAKE's confidence ranking to the choice of kernel parameterization in kernelized Silhouette for non-convex clusters, and can a parameter-free variant maintain performance?
