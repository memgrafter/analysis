# The Triangle of Similarity: A Multi-Faceted Framework for Comparing Neural Network Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17093
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17093
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Introduces a tripartite framework (static, functional, sparsity views) revealing architecture as the main driver of representational similarity, divergent metric behaviors under pruning, and significant CKA-Procrustes disagreement in 19% of model pairs.

## Executive Summary
Existing methods for comparing neural network representations offer a narrow perspective, often focusing on a single metric. This paper proposes the Triangle of Similarity, a framework integrating three complementary views: static representational similarity (e.g., CKA, Procrustes), functional similarity (e.g., Linear Mode Connectivity), and sparsity similarity (robustness under pruning). Analyzing CNNs, Vision Transformers, and Vision-Language Models, the study finds that architectural family is the strongest predictor of static similarity, forming well-defined clusters. Under pruning, representational geometry (CKA) degrades more slowly than functional connectivity (LMC) and task accuracy. Furthermore, CKA and Procrustes disagree for nearly one-fifth of model pairs, highlighting the necessity of a multi-faceted approach to avoid misleading conclusions.

## Method Summary
The framework evaluates pairs of neural networks across three views. The **static view** computes pairwise CKA (linear kernel) and Procrustes distances on activations from a fixed subset (5k CIFAR-10 or 2k ImageNetV2 images). The **functional view** uses Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) barrier for same-architecture pairs (interpolating weights, measuring max accuracy drop) or Predictive Similarity (JSD) for cross-architecture pairs. The **sparsity view** applies iterative global magnitude pruning (up to 40% sparsity), tracking each model’s accuracy and cross-model layer-wise CKA. Synthesis involves correlating metrics across a suite of 21 model pairs from CNN, ViT, and VLM families.

## Key Results
- Architectural family (CNN vs. Transformer) is the primary determinant of static representational similarity, forming distinct clusters in CKA/Procrustes matrices.
- Under pruning, CKA self-similarity and task accuracy are strongly correlated (Pearson’s r = 0.882), but accuracy degrades more sharply than representational geometry.
- For 19.0% of model pairs, CKA and Procrustes metrics show significant disagreement (>0.15), justifying the multi-faceted approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Architectural Primacy**  
*Claim:* Architectural identity (e.g., CNN vs. Transformer) is the primary determinant of static representational similarity, forming distinct clusters. *Mechanism:* Inductive biases and computational primitives (e.g., convolutional locality vs. self-attention) impose a structural prior on activation geometry, captured by CKA/Procrustes. *Core assumption:* Geometric structure is a stable proxy for architectural similarity across datasets. *Evidence anchors:* Clear within-family clusters and low cross-family similarity in Figure 2 (OOD CIFAR-10). *Break condition:* High cross-family similarity would weaken the claim of architecture as primary.

**Mechanism 2: Pruning Brittleness Hierarchy**  
*Claim:* Under pruning stress, a model’s representational core (CKA self-similarity) is more robust than its functional integrity (LMC barrier) and task accuracy. *Mechanism:* Representational geometry is distributed and tolerant to weight loss, while functional basins are narrow and accuracy depends on precise weights. *Core assumption:* Loss landscape has a flat representational basin but narrow functional basins. *Evidence anchors:* LMC barrier rises faster than CKA self-similarity drops (Figure 4). *Break condition:* If LMC remains near zero while CKA drops sharply for a family, the hierarchy is challenged.

**Mechanism 3: Pruning-Induced Core Revelation**  
*Claim:* For some architecturally dissimilar pairs, pruning reveals a shared robust computational core via non-monotonic cross-similarity curves. *Mechanism:* Mid-pruning removes architecture-specific parameters, causing divergence; extreme sparsity leaves only generic high-level features, driving transient alignment. *Core assumption:* Universal sparse features exist for a given task. *Evidence anchors:* Non-monotonic cross-CKA curves at high sparsity (Page 4–5). *Break condition:* Strictly monotonic curves for all dissimilar pairs would refute the mechanism.

## Foundational Learning
- **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)**  
  *Why needed:* Static view cornerstone; measures similarity of activation Gram matrices, invariant to orthogonal transforms and scaling, enabling cross-architecture comparison.  
  *Quick check:* What property of CKA makes it more suitable than simple correlation for comparing features from different layers/models?

- **Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) Barrier**  
  *Why needed:* Functional view for same-architecture pairs; quantifies loss landscape barrier via max accuracy drop along linear weight interpolation.  
  *Quick check:* If LMC barrier between two independently trained ResNet-50s is zero, what does that imply about their trained weight configurations?

- **Global Magnitude Pruning**  
  *Why needed:* Sparsity stressor; systematically removes smallest-magnitude weights globally to test robustness and expose shared representational cores.  
  *Quick check:* Why is *global* (unstructured) pruning used instead of layer-wise or structured pruning for this comparative analysis?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Similarity Metrics Module -> Functional Analysis Module -> Pruning & Stress Test Module -> Synthesis & Visualization Module
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Select model pair & dataset  
  2. Extract activations for static view panels  
  3. Compute functional metric (LMC or JSD)  
  4. Run pruning loop, recording accuracy & cross-similarity  
  5. Compute cross-view statistics & generate Triangle plots
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Accuracy vs. Speed: CKA is O(N²) in samples; use fixed subset (5k).  
  - Generality vs. Specificity: Functional view requires architecture-specific logic (LMC vs. JSD).  
  - Stress Depth: Pruning to 40% is cheap but may destroy signal; 80% risks collapse.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - CKA/Procrustes disagreement >0.15 destabilizes static view.  
  - Wildly oscillating cross-similarity under pruning indicates instability.  
  - Constant near-zero LMC barrier suggests ineffective pruning or unusually large basin.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate architectural clustering (Figure 2): Compute CKA/Procrustes for CNN vs. Transformer pairs on CIFAR-10; expect distinct within-family clusters.  
  2. Validate pruning self-similarity (Figure 3 top): Prune ResNet-50 to 40% sparsity; expect CKA(self) > 0.8.  
  3. Cross-view correlation sanity check (Table 1): Correlate mean static similarity with sparsity robustness AUC across 21 pairs; expect r ~ 0.88.

## Limitations
- Key methodological details are underspecified (exact layer mapping, pruning schedule, model variants, LMC steps), hindering exact replication.
- Evidence for the "shared computational core" mechanism is primarily the paper’s own observation, with limited direct support from the small corpus (2 references).
- Findings rely on a narrow set of models (5 CNNs, 5 ViTs, 3 VLMs); broader validation across architectures and scales is needed.

## Confidence
- Architectural family as primary determinant of static similarity: **Medium** (strong internal evidence, limited external validation)
- Pruning brittleness hierarchy (Accuracy < LMC < CKA): **Medium** (novel empirical finding, dependent on implementation)
- 19% CKA-Procrustes disagreement: **High** (directly computed, low uncertainty)
- Pruning reveals shared computational core: **Low** (intriguing but speculative, weak corpus support)
