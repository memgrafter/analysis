# NeuroWeaver: An Autonomous Evolutionary Agent for Exploring the Programmatic Space of EEG Analysis Pipelines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13473
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13473
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Generates lightweight EEG pipelines (<1.2M parameters) that consistently outperform task-specific methods and match large foundation models in accuracy.

## Executive Summary
NeuroWeaver addresses key limitations in EEG analysis: foundation models demand excessive data and compute, while general AutoML ignores neurophysiological priors. It reformulates pipeline engineering as a discrete constrained optimization problem. By initiating the search within a neuroscientifically plausible subspace and employing multi-objective evolutionary optimization, it balances accuracy, novelty, and efficiency. Evaluated on five benchmarks, NeuroWeaver synthesizes compact pipelines that exceed state-of-the-art task-specific methods and rival much larger foundation models, with clear performance gains across evolutionary generations.

## Method Summary
NeuroWeaver searches over a compositional space of EEG processing modules (filters, transforms, classifiers) under explicit domain constraints. The search begins with a Domain-Informed Subspace Initialization, which restricts the initial population to pipelines respecting neurophysiological principles (e.g., valid frequency bands, montage handling). A multi-objective evolutionary algorithm then iteratively evolves this population, evaluating each pipeline on accuracy, novelty (structural diversity), and efficiency (parameter count). This closed-loop process avoids the resource intensity of foundation models and the uninformed exploration of generic AutoML, yielding optimized, interpretable pipelines.

## Key Results
- Achieved 0.7737 balanced accuracy on HMC vs. 0.7286 for the previous best task-specific method.
- Scored 0.7391 on Workload vs. 0.7069 for prior state-of-the-art.
- Matched or exceeded large foundation models (e.g., LaBraM-Base 5.8M, NeuroLM-B 254M) while using <1.2M parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach succeeds by integrating three synergistic elements: (1) Domain-informed constraints bound the search to scientifically valid pipelines, preventing overfitting to noise or biologically impossible transformations. (2) Multi-objective optimization explicitly balances accuracy with novelty (avoiding redundant solutions) and efficiency (ensuring compactness), which single-metric searches neglect. (3) Evolutionary search effectively navigates the large, discrete, non-differentiable space of pipeline compositions where gradient-based methods fail. The iterative refinement allows incremental improvements that compound over generations, leading to superior final solutions.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Domain Constraints → Subspace Initialization → Evolutionary Population → Fitness Evaluation (Accuracy, Novelty, Efficiency) → Selection & Variation → New Population

Critical path:
1. Initialize population within constrained subspace.
2. Evaluate each pipeline on all objectives (accuracy on validation set, novelty via structural distance, efficiency via parameter count).
3. Select parents via non-dominated sorting (e.g., NSGA-II) to maintain Pareto front.
4. Apply variation (crossover/mutation) to generate offspring, respecting domain constraints.
5. Form new population from parents and offspring via environmental selection.
6. Repeat until termination condition (e.g., max generations or convergence).

Design tradeoffs: Tight domain constraints improve plausibility but risk missing unconventional valid solutions; novelty metric promotes diversity but may slow convergence; multi-objective scalarization requires careful weighting.

Failure signatures: Population collapse (extinction of diverse solutions), pipelines violating domain rules (e.g., impossible filter sequences), stagnant fitness across generations, or overfitting to specific validation splits.

First experiments:
1. Ablation: Compare performance with/without domain-informed initialization.
2. Objective analysis: Contrast multi-objective vs. accuracy-only evolution.
3. Resource profiling: Measure per-generation runtime and memory usage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not have a dedicated open questions section. Potential open questions inferred from the discussion and limitations include:
- How does performance generalize to EEG datasets with different pathologies, acquisition protocols, or real-world noise conditions beyond the five benchmarks?
- What specific neurophysiological principles are encoded in the domain constraints, and could these constraints inadvertently bias the search against viable but atypical pipeline structures?
- How does the novelty metric quantitatively affect diversity maintenance and convergence speed over the evolutionary timeline?
- What is the computational budget (wall-clock time, GPU/CPU resources) required for the evolutionary search, and how does it scale with the size of the module library?

## Limitations
- Generalizability uncertainty: Only five EEG benchmarks tested; performance on other datasets or real clinical scenarios unknown.
- Statistical rigor lacking: No significance testing or confidence intervals across multiple random seeds.
- Insufficient detail on domain constraints and novelty metric, potentially introducing hidden biases.
- Search efficiency not quantified: Runtime and computational cost of evolution are unreported.

## Confidence
- Method design and search: Medium (conceptually sound, but missing hyperparameter details and independent replication).
- Performance superiority: Medium (consistent gains across five tasks, but limited benchmark diversity and no statistical tests).
- Efficiency advantage: High (orders-of-magnitude parameter reduction is objectively measured and reproducible).
