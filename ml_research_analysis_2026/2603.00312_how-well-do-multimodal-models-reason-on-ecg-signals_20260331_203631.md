# How Well Do Multimodal Models Reason on ECG Signals?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00312
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00312
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: **ECG ReasonEval** reveals that frontier LLMs achieve strong deduction (up to 0.48 Precision@5) but weak signal perception (<0.15 Acc@Thresh100%), while time-series language models show moderate perception (~0.30) but low deduction (<0.25); Gemini 3.1 Pro Plot uniquely balances both, and final accuracy correlates strongly with deduction (r=0.70) but weakly with perception (r=0.18).

## Executive Summary
Multimodal models that interpret ECG signals often produce reasoning traces that are difficult to evaluate automatically. This paper introduces ECG ReasonEval, a framework that decomposes reasoning into two verifiable components: Perception, which grounds claims in raw signal features, and Deduction, which aligns them with established clinical knowledge. Perception is assessed via an agentic system that generates and executes Python code to verify signal characteristics; Deduction uses a retrieval-based approach against a curated database of ECG diagnostic criteria. The framework enables scalable, reproducible evaluation without relying on manual expert review.

Evaluated on zero‑shot multimodal models, time‑series language models (OpenTSLM, QoQ‑Med) achieve moderate Perception (up to ~0.30 Acc@Thresh100%) but low Deduction (Precision@5 <0.25). Frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro) demonstrate higher Deduction (up to 0.48 Precision@5) but weak Perception (<0.15 Acc@Thresh100%). Notably, Gemini 3.1 Pro Plot balances both, attaining relatively high Perception (~15‑16%) alongside top Deduction. Final prediction accuracy correlates strongly with Deduction (r=0.70) and weakly with Perception (r=0.18), indicating that high predictive performance does not guarantee semantically correct reasoning. The agentic Perception pipeline also flagged potential errors in ~17% of cardiologist notes, suggesting utility for auditing human annotations.

## Method Summary
ECG ReasonEval operates on 12‑lead ECG signals (10 s, 500 Hz) from MIMIC‑IV‑ECG and ECG‑QA, along with cardiologist reports and model‑generated reasoning traces. For Perception evaluation, an agentic system parses each trace into discrete findings and generates a Python verification function `verify_perception(ecg_signal, ecg_features_dict)`. This function uses pre‑computed ECG features (derived from a segmentation model that provides wave delineations) to check whether the claimed signal characteristics hold. A self‑correcting debugging agent handles code execution failures, and the process yields binary judgments for each finding, which are aggregated into metrics such as Acc@ThreshP% and Global Accuracy.

Deduction is measured by building a retrieval database of ECG diagnostic criteria. The authors scrape unstructured medical literature from LITFL, Wikipedia, ECGpedia, and WikiEM, then apply two text‑cleaning agents (Exact Quote and Structured Synthesis) using Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.2 Pro to extract criterion clusters for each diagnosis. These criteria are embedded with a Gemini model and indexed (e.g., FAISS). For a given model trace with the diagnosis masked, the system retrieves the top‑k criteria and computes Precision@k against the true label. The overall framework allows zero‑shot evaluation of multimodal models, including OpenTSLM, QoQ‑Med, Claude Opus 4.5 (array and plot versions), and Gemini 3.1 Pro Plot.

## Key Results
- Time‑series language models (OpenTSLM, QoQ‑Med) achieve moderate Perception (up to ~0.30 Acc@Thresh100%) but low Deduction (Precision@5 <0.25).
- Frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro) demonstrate higher Deduction (up to 0.48 Precision@5) but weak Perception (<0.15 Acc@Thresh100%).
- Gemini 3.1 Pro Plot uniquely balances both (Perception ~15‑16%, Deduction 0.48 P@5); final prediction accuracy correlates strongly with Deduction (r=0.70) and weakly with Perception (r=0.18).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ECG ReasonEval works because it breaks down the complex task of reasoning correctness into two orthogonal, machine‑checkable sub‑problems. Perception validation is operationalized through executable Python code that directly queries the ECG signal and its derived feature dictionary, providing an objective, reproducible assessment of whether a model’s claim is grounded in the data. Deduction validation uses a retrieval‑augmented approach to check whether the model’s inferred diagnosis aligns with established clinical criteria, leveraging a large, automatically constructed knowledge base. This separation allows independent scoring of signal grounding and clinical logic, revealing trade‑offs between them that would be invisible in aggregate prediction accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **ECG feature extraction**  
  Why needed: Provides structured measurements (intervals, amplitudes) for the Perception agent to verify signal‑grounded claims.  
  Quick check: Can a segmentation model output QRS duration and ST elevation for each lead?

- **Agentic code generation**  
  Why needed: Translates natural‑language reasoning into executable Python, enabling automated, scalable verification of perceptual claims.  
  Quick check: Does the agent produce a syntactically correct function that references the expected feature dictionary keys?

- **Retrieval‑augmented knowledge base**  
  Why needed: Offers a large, up‑datable source of clinical criteria; retrieval aligns model deductions with consensus without hand‑crafted rules.  
  Quick check: For a query about "atrial fibrillation", do the top‑5 retrieved snippets contain mentions of irregular RR intervals and absent P waves?

- **Evaluation metrics design**  
  Why needed: Distinguishes between grounding (Perception) and logic (Deduction), enabling trade‑off analysis and correlation with final accuracy.  
  Quick check: How does Acc@Thresh100% differ from a simple accuracy metric in measuring perception?

- **Correlation analysis**  
  Why needed: Reveals whether end‑task performance is driven by correct signal interpretation or by shortcuts in textual reasoning.  
  Quick check: If Deduction correlates strongly with final accuracy, what might that imply about the model's reliance on clinical knowledge versus signal features?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text)**  
ECG Signal → Segmentation → Feature Dictionary; Model Reasoning Trace → Perception Agent (Parse → Generate Python → Execute → Verify) and Deduction Module (Mask → Embed → Retrieve → Score); Knowledge Base (embedded criteria) supports Deduction; Metrics Aggregator produces Perception and Deduction scores.

**Critical path**  
1. Compute feature dictionary for each ECG using the segmentation tool.  
2. For each reasoning trace: (a) Perception agent parses trace, generates verification code, executes it with the feature dictionary, and records a binary outcome per claim; (b) Deduction module censors the diagnosis, embeds the trace, queries the knowledge base for top‑k criteria, and computes Precision@k.  
3. Aggregate results over the dataset to obtain final metrics.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Perception via executable code*: flexible and directly verifiable, but depends on reliable code generation and execution; added debugging loop increases latency.  
- *Deduction via retrieval*: scalable to many diagnoses and easy to update the knowledge base, but performance is tied to coverage and extraction quality; missing criteria penalize models unfairly.  
- *Pre‑computed features*: speeds repeated evaluations, but locks the pipeline to a specific segmentation model’s outputs; using a proprietary tool reduces transparency.

**Failure signatures**  
- Perception: frequent code syntax errors, missing feature dictionary keys, or consistently low boolean match rates indicating mis‑alignment between claims and features.  
- Deduction: near‑zero recall of correct criteria in top‑k results, or uniformly low retrieval similarity scores, suggesting gaps in the knowledge base or poor query embedding.  
- Overall: large gaps between Perception and Deduction scores across models without clear performance trends.

**First experiments**  
1. Validate the Perception agent on a hand‑crafted trace with known feature values to ensure code generation and boolean logic are correct.  
2. Measure knowledge‑base coverage by querying all ground‑truth labels and verifying that each appears within the top‑5 retrieved criteria for a representative set of traces.  
3. Replicate the evaluation using an alternative open‑source segmentation model to assess the sensitivity of Perception metrics to feature extraction choices.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability to other modalities** – The framework assumes well‑structured signal features (e.g., ECG waves); it is unclear whether it can be adapted to domains like radiology or pathology where features are less standardized.
2. **Automated correction of gold‑standard annotations** – The pipeline flagged ~17% of cardiologist reports as potentially erroneous, but scaling this to large datasets and adjudicating corrections without bias remains unresolved.
3. **Source of the Perception‑Deduction balance** – Frontier models like Gemini 3.1 Pro achieve a better trade‑off between the two dimensions; the paper does not investigate architectural or training reasons for this difference.
4. **Spurious textual correlations** – The weak correlation between Perception scores and final accuracy suggests models may rely on text‑diagnosis shortcuts; controlled perturbation experiments are needed to confirm.

## Limitations
- Use of a proprietary segmentation model (Park et al. 2025) impedes reproducibility and may bias feature extraction.  
- Knowledge base construction via LLM agents may suffer from coverage gaps and extraction noise, limiting Deduction reliability.  
- Evaluation is restricted to five models on two datasets, limiting statistical power; the 17% error‑flagging claim lacks expert validation.

## Confidence
- Framework decomposition into Perception and Deduction: **High**  
- Time‑series language models have higher Perception but lower Deduction than frontier LLMs: **Medium**  
- Deduction correlates strongly with final prediction accuracy (r=0.70): **Medium**  
- Gemini 3.1 Pro uniquely balances Perception and Deduction: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. Replace the proprietary segmentation model with an open‑source alternative (e.g., torchEc) and recompute Perception metrics to evaluate robustness to feature extraction differences.  
2. Perform a human expert review of the flagged cardiologist notes to quantify true error rates and refine the auditing process.  
3. Expand the study to include at least five additional multimodal ECG models across three or more independent datasets to test the generalizability of the Perception–Deduction trade‑off.
