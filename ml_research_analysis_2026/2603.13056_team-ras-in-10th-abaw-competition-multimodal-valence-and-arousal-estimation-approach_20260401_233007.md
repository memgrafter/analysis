# Team RAS in 10th ABAW Competition: Multimodal Valence and Arousal Estimation Approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13056  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13056  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Reliability-Aware Audio-Visual (RAAV) fusion achieves CCC 0.6576 (dev) and 0.62 (test) for continuous valence-arousal estimation on Aff-Wild2.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles frame-level continuous valence-arousal (V-A) estimation from in-the-wild video by combining three complementary modalities: facial expressions, behavioral context, and audio. The face branch uses GRADA features with a Transformer, behavior leverages Qwen3-VL embeddings with Mamba, and audio employs WavLM with attention-statistics pooling. Two fusion strategies are explored: Directed Cross-Modal Mixture-of-Experts (DCMMOE) and Reliability-Aware Audio-Visual (RAAV). RAAV, which fuses visual features frame-wise and uses audio as contextual evidence, yields the best reported results on the Aff-Wild2 dataset, demonstrating the strength of adaptive multimodal integration for affect recognition.

## Method Summary
The method processes video through three parallel pipelines. The face branch detects faces with YOLO, extracts GRADA features, and models temporal dynamics via a Transformer over sliding 4-second windows (stride 2 sec). The behavior branch uniformly samples 16 frames per segment, prompts Qwen3-VL to obtain visual and multimodal embeddings, and applies Mamba for temporal modeling. The audio branch filters 4-second segments using MediaPipe mouth dynamics, encodes with fine-tuned WavLM, and pools features. For each window, fusion occurs either via DCMMOE—which computes ordered cross-modal attention experts gated by a multimodal state—or RAAV, which first fuses face and behavior tokens frame-wise using reliability masking, then conditions this sequence on an audio bottleneck via cross-attention. Overlapping predictions are averaged to produce frame-level outputs.

## Key Results
- RAAV fusion achieves CCC 0.6576 on the Aff-Wild2 development set and 0.62 on the test set, the highest reported.
- Both multimodal fusion strategies (DCMMOE and RAAV) significantly outperform unimodal baselines, confirming the benefit of combining face, behavior, and audio.
- The audio-only model alone yields modest CCC (~0.40), but contributes positively when fused, especially after cross-modal filtering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Combining face, behavior, and audio modalities improves performance by capturing complementary affective signals.
- **Mechanism:** Face provides frame-level expression details; behavior (Qwen3-VL) encodes contextual body and scene cues; audio captures prosody. Fusion integrates these diverse representations.
- **Core assumption:** Modalities are largely independent; their joint use yields a more robust representation than any single one.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states the combination of three complementary modalities; [section 3] details each branch; corpus neighbors show multimodal dominance in ABAW, with this paper uniquely adding VLM-based behavior.
- **Break condition:** If one modality is consistently corrupted (e.g., occluded face, no speech) and fusion lacks reliability weighting, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Reliability-Aware Audio-Visual (RAAV) fusion effectively integrates modalities by fusing visual features frame-wise while using audio as contextual evidence.
- **Mechanism:** RAAV combines face and behavior tokens at each frame via masked reliability-aware gating, producing a visual sequence. This sequence attends to a window-level audio bottleneck, allowing audio to resolve visual ambiguities without strict temporal alignment.
- **Core assumption:** Visual modalities directly relate to frame-level affect with higher temporal resolution; audio provides complementary but less precise cues.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes RAAV as frame-centric visual fusion with audio context; [section 3.4] formalizes gating and audio bottleneck; Table 1 shows RAAV (ID 8) outperforms DCMMOE (ID 7).
- **Break condition:** If audio is highly misleading (e.g., noise) and gating fails to downweight it, contextual modulation may harm performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Cross-modal filtering of audio segments using visual mouth dynamics improves audio robustness by discarding unlikely speech segments.
- **Mechanism:** Audio segments are retained only if MediaPipe mouth-opening metrics exceed predetermined thresholds, approximating speech presence.
- **Core assumption:** Speech segments are more informative for affect, and visible mouth movements correlate with speech in Aff-Wild2.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] describes the filtering; [section 4.2] audio-only CCC is modest (~0.40) but fusion helps, suggesting filtering reduces noise.
- **Break condition:** Valid audio may be discarded if speech occurs off-screen or mouth moves without speech.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Face: YOLO-face detection → GRADA encoder (EfficientNet-B1) → Transformer temporal regressor  
Behavior: 16-frame sampling → Qwen3-VL prompt → Mamba temporal model (visual embeddings: 4 layers, dim 128; multimodal: 12 layers, dim 256)  
Audio: 4-sec segments (2-sec overlap) → MediaPipe mouth filtering → WavLM-Large (top 4 layers fine-tuned) → attention-statistics pooling → regression head  
Fusion: DCMMOE (cross-modal experts with gating) or RAAV (frame-level visual gating + audio bottleneck cross-attention)

**Critical path:**  
1. Input video → face detection/tracking → GRADA frame features.  
2. Video segments (16 frames) → Qwen3-VL prompt → segment embeddings.  
3. Audio extraction → mouth-based filtering → WavLM encoding → pooled chunk features.  
4. For each sliding window (4 sec, stride 2 sec): align modality features → apply chosen fusion → frame-level VA predictions.  
5. Overlapping frame predictions averaged.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- DCMMOE vs. RAAV: DCMMOE models explicit window-level cross-modal interactions; RAAV is frame-centric with audio as context, yielding better performance but asymmetric temporal resolution.  
- Qwen3-VL size (4B) vs. speed: large VLM captures rich context but is heavy; visual-only embeddings underperform, highlighting prompt importance.  
- Cross-modal filtering: thresholds trade speech recall vs. precision; too aggressive discards valid speech.  
- Window length: longer windows capture more context but increase latency; stride controls sample density vs. continuity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Valence CCC much lower than arousal: check face branch generalization (lighting, pose) or behavior context encoding.  
- Audio model alone poor (CCC ~0.4) but fusion helps: indicates audio is noisy; verify filtering thresholds and WavLM fine-tuning.  
- RAAV outperforms DCMMOE: suggests frame-level visual fusion is key; ensure audio bottleneck dimension and cross-attention heads are adequate.  
- High subject variance: subject-specific expression patterns not captured; consider subject-specific batch norm or test-time adaptation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Train unimodal branches separately; validate expected ranking (face > behavior > audio) per Table 1.  
2. Implement naive late fusion (e.g., weighted average) of unimodal predictions; confirm multimodal gain before complex fusion.  
3. Ablate RAAV components: remove audio bottleneck, replace with global average, or disable gating; measure impact on CCC to confirm each design’s contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can we improve the reliability of cross-modal filtering for audio segments?** The current mouth-opening heuristic is not fully reliable; alternative audio-visual correlation methods (e.g., lip movement models, multimodal VAD) on Aff-Wild2 with speech annotations could show improved CCC.
- **To what extent do VLM embeddings capture behavior complementary to faces, and how does prompt design and segment length affect this?** The paper uses fixed prompt and 16-frame segments; ablation varying prompt wording, segment length, and VLM architecture is needed to understand gains.
- **How do directed fusion strategies compare to conventional fusion (e.g., cross-attention, concatenation) in performance and robustness?** The paper introduces DCMMOE/RAAV but does not benchmark against other methods or test robustness under modality degradation.
- **Does RAAV’s frame-centric design underutilize audio’s temporal resolution?** Audio is treated as a window-level bottleneck; frame-level audio integration might align better with facial dynamics and could improve performance.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters for DCMMOE/RAAV and cross-modal filtering thresholds are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- The incremental value of VLM-based behavior embeddings versus visual-only features is not ablated; prompt design may critically influence results.
- Generalization beyond Aff-Wild2 is unproven; break conditions (e.g., occluded faces, misleading audio) could degrade performance due to gating reliability.

## Confidence
- Multimodal integration yields superior valence-arousal estimation: **High** (unimodal baselines consistently underperform fused models in Table 1).
- RAAV fusion outperforms DCMMOE: **High** (best reported CCC on dev/test directly demonstrates advantage).
- Cross-modal filtering improves audio robustness: **Medium** (audio-only CCC is modest and fusion helps, but no controlled ablation exists).

## Next Checks
1. Ablate RAAV components: remove audio bottleneck or masked gating; measure CCC drop to isolate each design’s contribution.
2. Sensitivity to mouth-opening thresholds: systematically vary filtering thresholds and record audio-only and fused CCC to establish robustness and optimal setting.
3. Cross-dataset validation: apply the trained RAAV model to an external in-the-wild VA dataset (e.g., Aff-Behavior) to assess generalization and avoid dataset-specific bias.
