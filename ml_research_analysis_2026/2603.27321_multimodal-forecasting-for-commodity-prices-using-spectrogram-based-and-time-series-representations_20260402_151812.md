# Multimodal Forecasting for Commodity Prices Using Spectrogram-Based and Time Series Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27321  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27321  
- Reference count: 6  
- Primary result: SEMF outperforms seven baselines in multi-horizon commodity price forecasting, with stable errors and notably low RMSE/MAE/MAPE for Gold.

## Executive Summary
Accurately forecasting commodity prices is challenged by complex cross-variable dependencies and non‑stationary market behavior. The paper introduces Spectrogram‑Enhanced Multimodal Fusion (SEMF), a dual‑path framework that combines a Vision Transformer processing Morlet wavelet spectrograms of the target price series with a Transformer encoder handling exogenous financial and macroeconomic indicators. These modalities are fused via a bidirectional cross‑attention mechanism, enabling mutual information exchange.

Evaluated on Coal, Gold, and Steel across forecasting horizons of 1 to 35 days, SEMF consistently achieves lower errors than seven competing time series and image‑based baselines. For Gold, it yields a mean RMSE of 107.05, RMAE of 0.0361, and MAPE of 0.0358. Importantly, SEMF maintains stable performance across both short and long horizons, whereas baseline errors increase monotonically. Ablation studies confirm the essential contributions of the Morlet spectrogram, the Transformer‑based exogenous encoder, and the bidirectional fusion.

## Method Summary
SEMF processes two distinct data streams. The first stream converts the univariate target price series into a time‑frequency representation using Morlet wavelet transformation, producing a spectrogram image. A Vision Transformer (ViT) then encodes this image into a contextual embedding. The second stream directly feeds multivariate exogenous variables (financial and macroeconomic indicators) into a Transformer encoder, capturing temporal dependencies and cross‑variable interactions. 

The two embeddings are fused through a bidirectional cross‑attention module: the ViT embedding attends to the Transformer output and vice versa, allowing each modality to condition the other. The fused representation is passed to a forecasting head that produces predictions for all specified horizons simultaneously. This design leverages the strengths of image‑based time‑frequency analysis and sequence modeling while ensuring tight integration.

## Key Results
- SEMF surpasses seven baselines (both classical and deep learning) on three commodities over horizons 1–35 days.
- Gold forecasting: mean RMSE 107.05, RMAE 0.0361, MAPE 0.0358.
- Error stability: SEMF’s RMSE grows only marginally with horizon, while baselines exhibit monotonic error increase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Morlet wavelet spectrogram transforms the price series into a 2D time‑frequency map, revealing periodicities and transient patterns that raw time series may obscure. The Vision Transformer excels at extracting hierarchical spatial features from such images. Meanwhile, the Transformer encoder on exogenous variables models complex temporal dynamics and external influences. Bidirectional cross‑attention enables each pathway to query the other, so spectral patterns can be contextualized with macro variables and vice versa. This synergy yields robust representations that generalize across horizons and commodities, explaining the consistent accuracy and stability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Morlet Wavelet Spectrogram**: A continuous wavelet transform using Morlet wavelets to produce a time‑frequency energy distribution. *Why needed*: Captures non‑stationary oscillations and multi‑scale patterns in price series. *Quick check*: Verify that spectrogram shows clear frequency bands and time localization for known cycles (e.g., weekly, monthly).
- **Vision Transformer (ViT)**: Splits an image into patches, linearly embeds them, and processes with self‑attention. *Why needed*: Leverages powerful spatial feature extraction from images, here applied to spectrograms. *Quick check*: Confirm that patch embedding dimension matches spectrogram dimensions after flattening.
- **Transformer Encoder for Sequences**: Uses self‑attention to model dependencies in variable‑length sequences. *Why needed*: Handles heterogeneous exogenous indicators with varying lags and interactions. *Quick check*: Ensure positional encoding is added to preserve temporal order.
- **Bidirectional Cross‑Attention**: Two attention layers where queries from one modality attend to keys/values of the other (both directions). *Why needed*: Enables mutual conditioning, avoiding one‑sided fusion that may lose information. *Quick check*: Check that attention weights are computed for both directions and summed or concatenated.
- **Multimodal Fusion**: Combining representations from different data sources. *Why needed*: Integrates complementary signals (spectral patterns and exogenous variables) for a holistic view. *Quick check*: Validate that fused vector dimension is consistent with downstream predictor input.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map (plain text)**:
  Raw Price Series -> Morlet Wavelet -> Spectrogram Image -> ViT Encoder -> Spectra Embedding -> \
    <-- Bidirectional Cross‑Attention <-- Exogenous Variables -> Transformer Encoder -> Exog Embedding --> \
  Fusion Output -> Prediction Head -> Forecasts (multiple horizons)

- **Critical Path**:
  1. Load price series and exogenous variables.
  2. Generate Morlet spectrogram from price series.
  3. Encode spectrogram with ViT to obtain spectral embedding.
  4. Encode exogenous sequence with Transformer to obtain exog embedding.
  5. Apply bidirectional cross‑attention: spectral embedding attends to exog embedding, and exog embedding attends to spectral embedding.
  6. Combine attended features (e.g., sum or concatenate).
  7. Pass through prediction head to produce forecasts for all horizons.

- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - *Spectrogram vs Raw Series*: Spectrograms add computational overhead and require wavelet parameter tuning, but reveal time‑frequency patterns; raw series are simpler but may miss multi‑scale dynamics.
  - *Bidirectional vs Unidirectional Fusion*: Bidirectional increases parameters and risk of overfitting, but provides richer interaction; unidirectional is lighter but may underutilize one modality.
  - *ViT vs CNN for Spectrogram*: ViT handles long‑range dependencies better, but needs more data to train; CNNs are parameter‑efficient but may struggle with global context.

- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Degraded performance when Morlet wavelet parameters (width, scale) are poorly chosen, leading to uninformative spectrograms.
  - Cross‑attention producing uniform weights, indicating failed modality interaction (often due to mismatched embedding dimensions or insufficient training).
  - Overfitting on training set with large error gap between train and validation, especially with limited data.
  - Model instability on highly volatile periods (e.g., crises) if exogenous variables are missing or noisy.

- **Exactly 3 First Experiments**:
  1. **Ablation of Spectrogram**: Replace Morlet spectrogram with raw price input to the ViT (or use a simple linear projection) to measure the contribution of time‑frequency representation.
  2. **Fusion Directionality**: Compare bidirectional cross‑attention against two unidirectional variants (spectral→exog only and exog→spectral only) to validate the necessity of mutual attention.
  3. **Wavelet Comparison**: Substitute Morlet wavelet with other continuous wavelet transforms (e.g., Paul, DOG) or with Short‑Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to confirm that Morlet provides a distinct and beneficial signal.
