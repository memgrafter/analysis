# Query Disambiguation via Answer-Free Context: Doubling Performance on Humanity's Last Exam

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04454
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04454
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AFC-based query rewriting doubles gpt-5-mini accuracy on Humanity's Last Exam from 0.14 to 0.37

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for improving language model accuracy by rewriting questions using answer-free context (AFC), which provides relevant background information without revealing the answer. The approach significantly boosts benchmark performance, notably doubling the accuracy of gpt-5-mini on Humanity's Last Exam. The study demonstrates that query disambiguation through AFC-based rewriting achieves better results than simply prepending AFC to the original question, and requires a distinct rewriting phase rather than in-situ rewriting during inference. The method is particularly effective for factual queries, while complex reasoning tasks benefit more from direct context access.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a query disambiguation approach that uses answer-free context (AFC) to rewrite questions before passing them to language models. The method involves generating AFC relevant to a question without including the answer, then using this context to rewrite the original query in a way that disambiguates it. The rewritten questions show higher cosine similarity to the context, which correlates with improved performance. The study found that this rewriting needs to be done as a distinct phase rather than during inference, and that the benefits exceed those of simply prepending AFC to the original question.

## Key Results
- AFC-based rewriting doubled gpt-5-mini accuracy on Humanity's Last Exam from 0.14 to 0.37
- Rewritten questions showed significantly higher cosine similarity to context compared to original questions
- Performance gains were greater than simply prepending AFC to the original question
- The method was particularly effective for factual queries, while complex reasoning tasks benefited more from direct context access

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by improving semantic alignment between questions and context through rewriting. By providing AFC that is relevant but answer-free, the rewriting process helps disambiguate queries and align them more closely with the available context. This semantic alignment is measurable through increased cosine similarity and translates to improved model performance on benchmark tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Answer-free context (AFC)**: Context that provides relevant background without revealing the answer; needed because it helps disambiguate queries without biasing the model toward specific answers
- **Query rewriting**: The process of reformulating questions using AFC; quick check: does the rewritten question maintain the original intent while being more aligned with context?
- **Semantic alignment**: The degree to which questions match context in meaning; why needed: better alignment leads to improved model performance
- **Cosine similarity**: A measure of semantic similarity between text embeddings; quick check: does increased similarity correlate with performance gains?
- **Distinct rewriting phase**: Performing rewriting as a separate step rather than during inference; why needed: ensures consistent and thorough disambiguation
- **Benchmark performance measurement**: Using standardized datasets like Humanity's Last Exam to evaluate improvements; quick check: are results reproducible across different benchmarks?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
AFC Generator -> Query Rewriter -> Language Model -> Performance Evaluator

### Critical Path
The critical path involves generating AFC, rewriting the query using that context, and then evaluating the rewritten query's performance. The rewriting phase is essential and cannot be effectively replaced by in-situ rewriting during inference.

### Design Tradeoffs
The paper found that AFC-based rewriting outperforms simply prepending AFC to the original question, suggesting that active rewriting is more effective than passive context provision. However, this approach requires an additional rewriting phase, increasing computational overhead.

### Failure Signatures
- If AFC is too specific or reveals the answer, it may bias the model
- Poor quality or irrelevant AFC can lead to ineffective rewriting
- Complex reasoning tasks may not benefit as much as factual queries

2. Compare performance with and without AFC on various question types to understand limitations
3. Measure cosine similarity between original vs. rewritten questions and context to quantify alignment improvements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal size or capability of a language model for performing query rewriting using answer-free context?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tested three models (gpt-oss-20b, gpt-oss-120b, and Qwen3-235B) for rewriting but did not systematically analyze performance across different model sizes.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluated a limited set of models for rewriting, leaving uncertainty about whether smaller models could perform comparably or if there's a minimum capability threshold.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic benchmarking of multiple model sizes and capabilities on the rewriting task, measuring both accuracy and efficiency trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of answer-free context (in terms of relevance and comprehensiveness) affect the performance gains from query rewriting?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper used automatically generated answer-free context but did not analyze how different quality levels impact rewriting effectiveness.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumed a fixed quality of AFC without exploring the relationship between context quality and rewriting performance, which could vary significantly in real-world applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the relevance, completeness, and specificity of AFC while measuring rewriting accuracy and downstream performance gains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the exact mechanism by which rewriting improves semantic alignment between questions and context?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observed that rewritten questions have higher cosine similarity to context but did not explain the specific linguistic or cognitive mechanisms.
- Why unresolved: While the paper measured the effect (increased cosine similarity), it didn't investigate whether improvements come from specific word choices, structural changes, or other factors in the rewriting process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed linguistic analysis of rewritten vs. original questions, identifying specific patterns or features that correlate with improved alignment and performance.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness is primarily demonstrated on gpt-5-mini and Humanity's Last Exam, leaving questions about generalizability
- Performance gains are greater for factual queries compared to complex reasoning tasks, suggesting limitations with sophisticated question types
- The methodology requires a distinct rewriting phase rather than in-situ rewriting during inference, potentially impacting practical deployment
- The specific reasons why AFC-based rewriting outperforms simple AFC prepending are not fully explored

## Confidence

- High: The core finding that AFC-based rewriting significantly improves benchmark performance on factual queries is well-supported by the reported accuracy gains from 0.14 to 0.37
- Medium: The claim that performance improvements stem from better semantic alignment between rewritten questions and context is plausible but could benefit from additional validation
- Low: The assertion that complex reasoning tasks benefit more from direct context access rather than AFC-based rewriting is based on limited evidence and requires further investigation
